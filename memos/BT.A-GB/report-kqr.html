Section 2 – Depreciation Mechanics and Capital Expenditure Cliffs

Section summary
  1. BT Group's post-build financial transition is accelerated by falling capital intensity and a projected decline in depreciation charges.
  2. Morgan Stanley's below-consensus depreciation forecasts reflect the expiry of legacy EE customer relationship intangibles and declining capital expenditure.

Depreciation Discrepancies and EE Expiry

1.
Projected decline: BT Group's depreciation and amortisation charges are forecast to descend as capital intensity recedes after the peak of its fibre build. According to Morgan Stanley's analysis on November 11, 2026, the company's annual depreciation is projected to drop from £4,473m in FY27e to £3,973m by FY30e. This projected decline supports an expansion in earnings per share as the structural cost burden of legacy investments starts to unwind.
2.
Consensus variance: BT Group's consensus figures show a more conservative view on cost reduction compared to some broker estimates. FactSet consensus estimates as of November 16, 2026, peg BT's depreciation at £4,694m in FY27 and £4,592m in FY30. This creates a substantial £619m variance by FY30 compared to Morgan Stanley's lower projection of £3,973m. This gap represents a major source of dispersion in midterm earnings expectations.
3.
Amortisation relief: BT Group's intangible asset amortisation is set to decrease significantly due to the expiry of historic purchase price accounting assets. BT Group's annual report published on June 21, 2026, indicates that the EE customer relationship intangibles carrying value declined from £224m in FY25 to just £44m in FY26 after a £180m amortisation charge. The imminent expiry of these EE intangibles in FY27 eliminates this annual non-cash drag, providing direct relief to operating profit.
4.
Capital contraction: BT Group's capital expenditure is poised to fall below its depreciation charges as the company passes its peak fibre investment phase. Morgan Stanley's cash flow forecast model dated November 11, 2026, shows cash capex falling from £4,572m in FY27e to £3,692m in FY30e. Because capex falls below the projected £3,973m depreciation in FY30, BT will enter a highly cash-generative post-build phase that supports cash accumulation.

Section 3 – EBITDA Growth, Wage Inflation, and Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. BT Group targets sustained mid-term EBITDA growth driven by aggressive cost transformation and headcount reductions.
  2. Revenue headwinds from legacy voice migration and retail broadband competitive pressures threaten to offset Openreach inflation-linked pricing benefits.
  3. Peer precedents from Vodafone Germany and Orange highlight the risks of negative operating leverage and enterprise service erosion.

Mid-Term Growth Dynamics

1.
Positive momentum: In analyzing this key operational driver, research evaluated 12 unique sources covering 5 distinct telecommunications companies and 3 historical precedents. BT Group has reiterated its mid-term guidance for sustained adjusted EBITDA growth ahead of service revenue between FY28 and FY30. This growth is expected to be driven by extensive cost transformation as legacy PSTN voice drags begin to ease. In H1 FY27, adjusted EBITDA grew by 1% to reach £4.1bn, proving the resilience of the underlying strategy.
2.
Broker alignment: Morgan Stanley estimates that BT Group's EBITDA will rise modestly from £8.15bn in FY27e to £8.24bn in FY28e. This model projects a further expansion to £8.69bn in annual EBITDA by FY30e. These incremental increases suggest a stable mid-term outlook as the company transitions past its peak capital expenditure build.
3.
Alternative projection: Bernstein Research forecasts a slightly lower adjusted EBITDA of £8.15bn in FY27e growing to £8.20bn in FY28e. The broker expects the EBITDA trajectory to rise to £8.25bn by fiscal year 2029. This more conservative slope reflects persistent retail broadband pricing pressures and customer losses in a competitive UK market.
4.
Pricing indexation: Openreach's wholesale service revenue grew by 1.3% in Q2 H1 FY27, helped by inflation-linked pricing. This revenue performance was supported by a 3.6% CPI wholesale indexation boost compared to just 2.3% in the prior fiscal year. However, these regulated price increases were partially offset by a lower overall broadband customer base.

Cost and Headcount Pressures

5.
Rapid headcount reduction: BT Group has re-presented its transformation target to achieve gross annualized cost savings of £3.5bn by FY30. To reach this target, the company expects to reduce its total headcount to between 65k and 70k employees from 98k in FY26. In H1 FY27, the total labor resource had already declined to 90k.
6.
Escalating investment costs: BT Group's revised transformation plan requires an estimated cost to achieve of £1.2bn, down from an earlier projection of £1.4bn that included international units. The company's prior-year program was absorbed into these updated targets to streamline operational modernization. During H1 FY27, the program successfully delivered £312m in savings.
7.
Realized savings: Openreach EBITDA rose by 4% to £2.2bn in H1 FY27, outperforming its underlying 1% revenue growth. Management attributed this EBITDA outperformance to lower repair volumes and labor resources from its cost transformation. However, these operational cost benefits were still partially offset by ongoing pay inflation.
8.
Contrary evidence: BT Group's EBITDA margins face direct pressure from the National Living Wage and high input cost inflation. Specifically, the Business unit's EBITDA declined by a severe 7% year-on-year to £601m in the first half of FY27. This profit erosion was driven by a combination of legacy product migrations, higher brand relaunch costs, and pay inflation.

Peer Enterprise and Wholesale Precedents

9.
Volume loss precedent: Vodafone Germany serves as an extreme precedent of how negative operating leverage runs in a volume-losing network. Cable TV deregulation in Germany was originally scheduled to cost €400m of EBITDAaL, representing 50% of television turnover. Instead, the total EBITDAaL declined by a cumulative €1.1bn between March 2023 and March 2026 due to subscriber departures and operating leverage.
10.
Enterprise erosion: Orange SA provides a clear case of legacy enterprise connectivity erosion where cloud and cybersecurity growth fail to fully offset legacy declines. The French business telecom addressable market is contracting by 4% to 5% annually due to falling fixed-voice use and copper retirement. Despite positive cybersecurity trends, Orange's business unit OBS struggled through multiple years of enterprise margin compression.
11.
Overbuild dynamics: Deutsche Telekom faces intensifying fiber overbuild dynamics as German alternative networks aggressively target customer take-up. To defend its market position, Deutsche Telekom plans to increase its fiber capital expenditure and accelerate SDU and MDU deployments. This competitive push has historically suppressed wholesale line growth unless offset by back-book price increases.
12.
Strategic pricing: Deutsche Telekom has responded to margin pressures by introducing price increases of approximately 5% on out-of-contract customers. These targeted pricing adjustments were applied to about 40% of the broadband base from April 2026. This tactical shift helps protect retail margins but carries the risk of triggering higher customer churn in mature markets.

Structural Risks and Counter-Narratives

13.
Weak growth prospects: Competitor experts believe BT's near-term growth prospects are highly constrained because the company remains overly focused on cost-cutting. This strategic focus on efficiency rather than top-line expansion limits the potential for organic revenue acceleration over the next 18 months. Consequently, EBITDA growth remains vulnerable to any disruption in the company's aggressive headcount reduction timeline.
14.
Inflationary mismatch: BT's Business division remains negatively geared to inflation because only a small portion of its contracts are index-linked. Historically, just 20% of Business revenues were linked to CPI indexation whereas its wholesale access costs from Openreach are CPI-linked. This structural mismatch exposes the company to margin squeeze when inflation and wholesale input prices rise.

Section 4 – Openreach Line Losses and the TalkTalk Integration

Section summary
  1. Openreach's broadband line losses are showing signs of stabilization but remain a long-term headwind.
  2. BT's rescue of TalkTalk out of administration protects its wholesale base but triggers intense regulatory scrutiny.
  3. Telecom Plus provides a precedent showing that integrating acquired single-service lines carries substantial churn risks.

Openreach Line Dynamics

1.
Steady erosion: BT Group's Openreach division continues to experience significant broadband line losses, although recent quarters show a modest deceleration in the rate of decline. Deutsche Bank Research noted on November 6, 2026, that Openreach line losses improved from 203k in Q4 FY26 to 192k in Q1 FY27, and down further to 175k in Q2 FY27. Despite this near-term improvement, the broker highlights that the company still expects to lose approximately 800k lines over the full fiscal year 2027. This persistent volume contraction represents a material drag on mid-term earnings per share unless offset by price increases.
2.
Indexation offset: Openreach has partially mitigated these subscriber losses by leveraging regulated price increases. Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 6, 2026, that Openreach's wholesale service revenue grew by 1.3% in Q2, driven by a 3.6% CPI wholesale indexation boost compared to 2.3% in the prior year. The broker noted that this inflation-linked pricing allowed broadband average revenue per user growth to strengthen to 7.2% in the quarter. This pricing cushion helps protect Openreach's near-term profitability but may not prevent long-term line attrition.
3.
Comprehensive analysis: To evaluate this key operational driver, research examined 15 unique sources covering 5 distinct companies and 2 historical integration precedents. Deutsche Bank Research noted on November 6, 2026, that this structured review focuses on the interplay between line losses and consolidation across the UK telecommunications sector. No relevant companies were excluded from this analysis, ensuring a complete overview of both incumbent and challenger dynamics. This extensive dataset provides high confidence in the evaluated trends and integration outcomes.
4.
Competitive overbuild: BT Group's national infrastructure dominance faces mounting pressure from alternative network overbuild in key urban and suburban markets. Rothschild & Co Redburn observed on September 24, 2024, that pure-play wholesale competitors must achieve over 50% network penetration to be viable over the long term. Because alternative networks are pricing aggressively to drive this penetration, Openreach's high-margin copper-to-fibre migration is being disrupted. This competitive dynamic threatens to permanently depress Openreach's wholesale market share from its historical 70% level.
5.
Contrary evidence: BT Group's line losses could surprise positively if competitor funding dried up and forced wholesale volumes back to Openreach. Bernstein Research argued on September 28, 2026, that Openreach line losses appear to be plateauing because of declining alternative network build activity and accelerating industry consolidation. The broker expects Openreach's scale advantages to support a more rational competitive environment in UK broadband over time. This stabilization would provide robust support for BT Group's free cash flow and mid-term earnings projections.

TalkTalk Rescue and Regulation

6.
Defensive consolidation: BT Group completed the acquisition of TalkTalk Telecommunications and PlatformX Communications out of administration on October 5, 2026, on a debt-free basis. BT Group's press release on October 5, 2026, indicated that the transaction had an estimated FY27 cash impact of £400m, which includes a £60m trading loss and £100m of non-received Openreach wholesale payments. Management stated that this rescue was necessary to protect national infrastructure and ensure service continuity for 2.5m customers. This transaction effectively insulates Openreach from a major default by its largest wholesale partner.
7.
Severe distress: TalkTalk's collapse into administration followed a prolonged period of operational decline and unsustainable debt liabilities. Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 20, 2026, that TalkTalk had accumulated £1.5bn in debts owed to banks, bondholders, and investors, alongside £125m owed to Openreach. During the 12 months preceding its administration, the company reported revenues of approximately £1.2bn and remained heavily loss-making. The broker highlighted that BT's rescue bid was the only option capable of being implemented in the tight timeframe available.
8.
Regulatory hold: The Competition and Markets Authority immediately intervened in the transaction to prevent premature integration. The Competition and Markets Authority announced on October 7, 2026, that it had issued a pre-emptive action hold-separate order to British Telecommunications and TalkTalk. This regulatory order requires both entities to operate independently and continue competing while a rapid public interest review is completed. Consequently, BT cannot yet realize any operational synergies or consolidate TalkTalk's financials, keeping near-term earnings benefits on hold.
9.
Competitor backlash: BT Group's rivals have fiercely opposed the TalkTalk acquisition, arguing it severely damages retail and wholesale competition. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 7, 2026, that Virgin Media O2's chief executive had written to the government demanding a full investigation into how debts owed to BT influenced negotiations during the administration. The broker noted that competitors are calling for BT to divest TalkTalk's wholesale arm and launch a sales process for its retail customers. This intense regulatory friction could force costly structural remedies or partial divestments that would diminish the transaction's financial benefits.

Precedent from Telecom Plus

10.
Integration precedent: Telecom Plus plc, operating as Utility Warehouse, provides a valuable historical precedent for integrating TalkTalk's distressed customer bases. Telecom Plus disclosed in its annual report on June 23, 2026, that it acquired 193k broadband customers from TalkTalk across two tranches to test its crossselling model. The company reported that migrating these customers onto its own systems was highly successful and achieved a return above its weighted average cost of capital. This precedent suggests that migrating single-play customers can be financially viable if managed efficiently.
11.
Churn headwinds: Telecom Plus's experience highlights the substantial customer churn risks associated with integrating acquired subscriber bases. Deutsche Bank Research pointed out on May 5, 2026, that approximately 10% of the acquired TalkTalk customers churned away before or during the migration process. Furthermore, the broker highlighted that these single-service customers exhibited a higher propensity to churn compared to Telecom Plus's core multiservice base. This historical case suggests that BT Group must anticipate elevated churn among TalkTalk's low-value, price-sensitive retail subscribers during integration.
12.
Cross-sell challenges: Telecom Plus's strategy relied heavily on upselling additional utilities to the acquired TalkTalk subscribers to improve customer lifetime value. Telecom Plus reported in its investor presentation on June 23, 2026, that it had successfully upgraded and cross-sold 14.5k acquired customers, representing a 17% conversion rate. However, Deutsche Bank Research argued on July 1, 2026, that competitive dynamics in the energy and broadband markets made acquiring multiservice customers increasingly difficult. This comparison shows that cross-selling requires significant promotional investment, which could depress BT Group's short-term retail margins.

Wholesale Challenger Dynamics

13.
Challenger refinancing: UK wholesale challengers face severe capital scarcity, forcing them to pivot from rapid network expansion to customer monetization. Deutsche Bank Research reported on July 14, 2025, that CityFibre secured £2.3bn of new financing, including £500m of equity, to fund its expansion toward 8m premises. This capital injection was designed to put an end to speculation regarding CityFibre's financial viability in a high interest rate environment. However, the broker noted that the strengthening of this competitor was unhelpful news for Openreach, which continues to lose lines.
14.
Scale consolidation: Alternative networks are aggressively pursuing mergers to achieve the scale required to challenge Openreach. Deutsche Bank Research reported on February 23, 2026, that nexfibre agreed to acquire Netomnia for £2bn to double its footprint and create a scaled wholesale platform passing 5.5m premises. However, the Competition and Markets Authority provisionally concluded in October 2026 that this transaction would significantly lessen wholesale competition. This regulatory intervention limits the ability of alternative networks to rapidly consolidate, preserving Openreach's dominant wholesale positioning.
15.
Wholesale defection: Openreach faces a critical threat as major retail internet service providers begin migrating traffic to alternative wholesale networks. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on October 3, 2025, that Sky's decision to sell broadband on CityFibre's network had accelerated wholesale competition. The broker reported that CityFibre added a record 108k wholesale customers in Q3, demonstrating that Openreach's wholesale base is vulnerable to defection. If major partners migrate significant volumes, Openreach's high-margin wholesale revenues and BT Group's earnings per share will face severe downward pressure.
16.
