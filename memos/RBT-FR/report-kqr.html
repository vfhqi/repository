Section summary
  1. Understanding Robertet's financial inputs over the next four years is critical because these variables will determine whether the company can successfully transition from an artisanal natural ingredients producer to a high-scale global leader.

Section 2 – Currency Dynamics and Foreign Exchange Sensitivities

Section summary
  1. Robertet remains highly exposed to currency translation risks because the US Dollar represents over thirty-one percent of its sales.
  2. Peers like Symrise and Sensient use formal cash flow hedge accounting while Robertet manages transaction risks using short-term unhedged forwards.

Robertet Currency Risk Profile

1.
Unhedged translation: Robertet faces significant foreign exchange volatility because the US Dollar represents 31.7% of total sales. The group operates with limited formal hedging, meaning fluctuations directly translate into reported revenue. During the first half of 2026, the appreciation of the Euro created a negative three percent currency headwind on top-line performance. This unhedged translation risk remains a primary driver of earnings volatility over the medium term.
2.
Accounting translation: Robertet translates income and expenses in foreign currencies at the average monthly exchange rate. Monetary assets and liabilities are translated at the year-end closing rate, with resulting differences recognized directly in shareholders' equity under conversion reserves. This accounting treatment isolates translation volatility in balance sheet reserves, but transactional gains and losses continue to impact the financial result directly.
3.
Increasing derivatives: Robertet manages transactional currency risk through forward foreign exchange contracts without applying formal hedge accounting. The nominal value of its outstanding US Dollar forward hedges rose to seventeen million dollars in 2025 from ten million dollars in 2024. These derivative contracts had a recorded fair value of 14.48 million euros at the end of December 2025. These short-term instruments help stabilize transaction margins but do not protect the group against broader translation risks.

Peer Hedging Benchmarks

4.
Natural hedging: Givaudan relies primarily on its vast geographical spread to create natural hedges against currency movements. The Swiss group reports in Swiss Francs, which exposes its global sales to significant translational foreign exchange headwinds when foreign results are consolidated. Givaudan does not apply hedge accounting for general foreign exchange risk management, although it centrally hedges its largest cost-effective exposures. This operational offset helps protect the group's consolidated EBITDA margin from severe currency swings.
5.
Severe sensitivity: Givaudan faces substantial absolute translation exposure, where each ten-cent move in the US Dollar impacts group EBITDA by forty to fifty million Swiss Francs. This leverage makes Givaudan highly sensitive to the US Dollar relative to smaller peers. To mitigate specific transactional risks, the group manages foreign exchange activities centrally against the functional currencies of its subsidiaries.
6.
Disciplined hedging: Symrise implements a structured currency risk management program that utilizes active rolling hedges to protect cash flows. The company hedges currency risk over a period of up to eighteen months with a maximum hedging ratio of 75% of open items. Unlike Robertet, Symrise designates these forward contracts as cash flow hedges to prevent earnings volatility. This proactive hedging policy significantly reduces the short-term impact of exchange rate fluctuations on its operating results.
7.
Reduced exposure: DSM-Firmenich actively manages a significant currency mismatch between its European production costs and global sales. The group successfully reduced its absolute US Dollar exposure to thirteen million euros per cent in 2025, down from sixteen million euros previously. Its Swiss Franc exposure was also lowered to 600 million Swiss Francs through active portfolio restructuring. These currency optimization efforts help stabilize margins against sudden translation shocks.
8.
Extensive forwards: DSM-Firmenich utilizes large-scale forward contracts, hedging 859 million US Dollars of projected net cash flows against the Euro in 2025. The group also hedged 322 million Swiss Francs of obligations at an average rate of zero point ninety-two Swiss Francs per Euro. These forward contracts allow the company to lock in favorable exchange rates for its projected operations.

Translational Peer Risk Profiles

9.
Translational pressure: Croda experiences significant translational currency volatility due to its reporting in British Sterling. The specialty chemical company operates with substantial translation exposure, meaning that sterling strength creates immediate headwinds for consolidated earnings. This unhedged translation risk makes Croda highly vulnerable during periods of sterling appreciation against the US Dollar.
10.
Dollar sensitivity: Croda operates under a simple rule of thumb where a one cent move in the US Dollar impacts its adjusted operating profit by one million British pounds. A similar one cent movement in the Euro also has a one million pound annualized impact on adjusted operating profit. This clear metric highlights how unhedged currency translation directly drives Croda's underlying profit volatility.
11.
Dollar overexposure: Treatt remains highly exposed to the US Dollar, which represents approximately 50% of its total business operations. This concentration creates substantial transaction and translation risks for the sterling-reporting entity. Although Treatt utilizes matched currency hedging and a local-for-local production and sales strategy, prolonged dollar weakness remains a top-line headwind. A depreciating dollar complicates forecasting and can depress reported margins when translated back to sterling.
12.
Designated hedges: Sensient Technologies relies on designated cash flow hedges to mitigate foreign exchange risks across its global manufacturing network. The company held 29.3 million dollars of outstanding forward exchange contracts designated as cash flow hedges as of September 30, 2026. Sensient generally uses foreign exchange contracts with durations of less than 18 months to offset fluctuations on non-functional currency purchases. This hedge accounting program delays derivative gains and losses until the underlying transaction hits earnings.
13.
Inverse correlation: Interparfums faces an inverse relationship between currency shifts and margins because about 50% of its European segment sales are in US Dollars. While its sales are denominated in dollars, its operational costs are almost entirely incurred in Euros. This mismatch means a weaker dollar boosts reported revenues through translation but simultaneously compresses operating margins. This unique operational structure creates significant margin volatility during periods of dollar depreciation.

Outlook and Contrary Evidence

14.
Contrary evidence: Robertet's reported currency headwinds in traditional markets were offset by twenty-one percent organic growth in South America during the first half of 2026. This expansion was driven primarily by robust sales momentum in Brazil, which benefited from a new creation center in São Paulo. Consequently, high regional growth in emerging markets can successfully counter severe translation drag from a stronger Euro.
15.
Favorable modeling: Deutsche Bank Research expects foreign exchange to transition from a headwind into a modest tailwind for ingredients producers in 2027. This outlook assumes that current spot rates prevail, leaving the US Dollar approximately four percent stronger against the Euro and Swiss Franc compared to the 2026 average. For unhedged or partially hedged players like Robertet, this currency shift could support reported margins and boost earnings per share over the next eighteen months.
16.
Other side of the story: Although unhedged translation risk exposes Robertet to short-term earnings volatility, the company's highly diversified global manufacturing footprint provides a natural operational buffer. By producing ingredients locally within major currency zones, Robertet aligns its costs with local revenues, reducing transactional risks. Therefore, the long-term impact of exchange rate fluctuations on net margins is far less severe than raw top-line figures suggest.
