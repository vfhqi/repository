Cost Splits and Operating Gearing

1.
Asymmetrical cost splits: Carlsberg’s cost base is highly weighted toward materials and packaging inputs, which limits immediate supply-chain flexibility during sudden volume drops. Jefferies estimates on 4 March 2026 that materials account for 57% of Carlsberg's cost of goods sold, whereas direct production labor represents only 4%. This material-heavy structure means that while direct variable inputs adjust to volume, indirect production overheads at 13% and depreciation at 6% create nearterm fixed-cost drag during periods of under-utilization, ultimately impacting EPS.
2.
Fixed operating expenses: Operating expenses at Carlsberg carry a high portion of fixed commitments that cannot be easily scaled down without damaging long-term brand equity. Jefferies Research shows on 4 March 2026 that distribution represents 30% of Carlsberg's operating expenses, followed by marketing at 28%, sales at 24%, and administration at 19%. Because roughly 50% of these operating expenses are contractually fixed in the near term, any sudden volume decline in Western Europe immediately triggers negative operating leverage unless offset by administrative cuts.
3.
Operational gearing limits: Carlsberg’s business model exhibits a high level of operational gearing that translates sales volatility directly into magnified profit swings. Jefferies Research estimated on 31 March 2020 that Carlsberg's operational gearing after mitigating factors was 1.8x, driving a projected 19.6% EBIT decline on a 10.8% revenue decline. This structural gearing means that when volumes contract, fixed-cost absorption worsens, making EPS highly sensitive to rapid volume changes.

Historical Downturns and Inflationary Cycles

4.
Pandemic volume stress: Carlsberg's response to the 2020 COVID-19 downturn demonstrated that tight cost execution can protect operating margins even during severe volume contractions. Carlsberg’s annual report on 5 February 2021 showed that total organic volumes fell 3.8% and revenue fell 8.4%, yet organic operating profit only declined 3.1%. Management achieved this by using its operating cost management toolkit to cut marketing, travel, and administration expenses by 14% organically, proving that fixed costs are partially cuttable in a crisis to support EPS.
5.
Inflationary margin squeeze: The severe cost inflation of 2022 and 2023 tested Carlsberg's pricing power as rising input costs outpaced the execution of price increases. Jefferies Research noted on 8 February 2023 that Carlsberg’s cost of sales per hectolitre rose by 13% in 2022 due to energy and commodity spikes, which compressed gross margins by 190bp to 45.6%. This lag in price pass-through shows that fixed-cost leverage becomes highly dilutive when transactional hedges roll over, reducing EPS.
6.
Moderate volume recovery: Carlsberg's performance during the 2025 volume decline demonstrated the structural shift in its portfolio toward soft drinks following major acquisitions. Carlsberg reported on 4 February 2026 that its organic volumes declined 2.0% in 2025, but organic operating profit still grew by 5% due to the inclusion of Britvic. This category rebalancing helped offset the structural decline in mainstream European beer, protecting EPS from deeper operating deleverage.
