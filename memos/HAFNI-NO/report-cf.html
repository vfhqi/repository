EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of Change Hafnia executed an orderly CEO transition effective September 2026, appointing internal successor Søren Steenberg Jensen while preserving operational continuity through outgoing CEO Mikael Skov's board nomination. The company successfully redomiciled from Bermuda to Singapore on October 1, 2024, securing permanent tax exemptions under the Maritime Sector Initiative and optimizing its operational structure.

Strategically, Hafnia is systematically exiting the legacy Handy segment and winding down commercial pool platforms during 2026, refocusing capital on larger LR2 and MR ship classes while executing a substantive newbuilding program. The company acquired a 13.97% strategic stake in competitor TORM for $311 million in December 2025, signaling potential industry consolidation while contributing dividend income and establishing a platform for future strategic optionality.

Financially, Hafnia delivered peak cyclical net profit of $774 million in FY 2024 driven by Red Sea disruptions, followed by normalization to $340 million in FY 2025 as trade flows stabilized, before recovering to nearly triple year-on-year net profit of $180 million in Q1 2026. The balance sheet deleveraged decisively from 64% net LTV in early 2022 to 20.2% in Q1 2026, unlocking elevated dividend payout thresholds while management contracted $405 million in MR newbuilds for delivery through 2029.

Top Drivers Ranked by Impact and Plausibility Spot TCE Rate Volatility [HIGH IMPACT | HIGH PLAUSIBILITY]: Spot TCE rates represent the single largest driver of shipping margins, with Hafnia's Q1 2026 LR2 spot rates reaching $51,869 per day and fleet-wide bookings averaging $29,979 per day, outperforming cash breakevens below $13,000 per day. DNB Carnegie estimates Q2 2026 fleet-wide TCE will reach $43,200 per day before normalizing, with 2027-2028 adjusted EPS projected to decline to $0.71 and $0.69 as rates soften.

Downside risk materializes if sudden Middle East peace resolutions drag spot rates down toward cash breakeven levels, causing immediate cash flow erosion and dividend coverage pressure.

Geopolitical Chokepoint Dynamics [HIGH IMPACT | MEDIUM PLAUSIBILITY]: Persistent Suez and Strait of Hormuz transit disruptions force vessel rerouting, adding 3% to 3.5% to annual tonne-mile demand, while the Strait of Hormuz closure directly impacts approximately 15% of product tanker demand. Hafnia vessels capture elevated spot premiums as military threats reduce effective global fleet capacity, though an eventual ceasefire would eliminate the seaborne transit risk premium and trigger rapid rate correction. Prolonged closures risk severe oil demand destruction as Chinese and Indian refiners restrict seaborne exports to protect domestic markets.

Product Tanker Supply Growth [MEDIUM IMPACT | HIGH PLAUSIBILITY]: The global product tanker orderbook-tofleet ratio climbed to 18.8% in August 2026, introducing future supply risk, though near-term fleet growth is projected to average a modest 3% over the next three years due to constrained global shipyard capacity. Supply growth is mitigated as over 45% of the global product tanker fleet will exceed 20 years of age by 2030, while approximately 25% of global capacity operates in the shadow market, locking non-compliant tonnage away from core trades.

Commercial Pool Fees and Scaling [LOW IMPACT | HIGH PLAUSIBILITY]: Hafnia generates consistent nonoperating income by charging third-party pool participants fixed fees of $271.5 to $296.5 per day plus 2.25% to 2.75% commission on net TCE, contributing $7.8 million in fee income during Q1 2026. Management's decision to exit commercial Handy pools in 2026 reduces long-term fee diversity and limits operating scale.

Environmental Carbon Mandates [LOW IMPACT | HIGH PLAUSIBILITY]: The IMO Carbon Intensity Indicator mandates annual carbon emissions intensity reductions of 2% through 2026, penalizing non-compliant ships, while scrubber-equipped vessels capture higher daily TCE spreads as the VLSFO-HSFO fuel spread reached $202 per metric ton in August 2026. Hafnia targets a 40% carbon intensity reduction by 2028, insulating modern assets from regulatory operating limits, while underperforming vessels rated D or E face mandatory slowsteaming rules that effectively reduce active global shipping capacity.
