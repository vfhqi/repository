Simultaneously, complex deepwater execution risks at Rosebank could drive material capital cost overruns, with project inflation increasing development expenditure and borrowing requirements at precisely the moment when production revenues remain deferred.

These unstable domestic tax regimes simultaneously restrict the company's ability to attract farm-in partners needed for the massive undeveloped Cambo field, permanently blocking a critical growth avenue.

Bear-case valuations from skeptical brokers underscore the severity of downside scenarios, with Morgan Stanley modeling a target as low as 32 pence under stressed commodity pricing and Jefferies outlining a downside scenario of 165 pence representing a 38% decline from current levels.

High future capital expenditure requirements on greenfield developments could severely constrain dividend capacity even under base-case commodity assumptions, threatening the company's cash return proposition to investors. The least-discussed risk centers on accelerating mature-field retirements and escalating decommissioning cash outflows, which the market significantly under-monitors despite representing a high-likelihood failure pathway.

Steep declines at mature fields require rising abandonment spending that drains group liquidity, with the simultaneous cessation of production at Stella and Alba in 2026 triggering substantial decommissioning obligations. Group decommissioning liabilities have already climbed to $2,753.9 million during 2025, creating a mounting cash burden that compounds operational failure scenarios.

Currency translation risks represent an underappreciated amplification mechanism for operational failures, as year-end monetary liabilities denominated in Sterling nearly doubled to 107 million pounds in 2025, with a 10% strengthening of Pounds Sterling triggering a 14.1 million dollar loss before tax. A rising Pound Sterling directly inflates converted Rosebank capital costs while simultaneously reducing net operating cash flows available for debt servicing when combined with elevated cash tax payments, creating a synchronized pressure point that worsens both greenfield execution and dividend capacity failures.

Governance weaknesses compound these operational risks, as the company operates under non-independent Executive Chair leadership with external auditors stating that the control environment is still maturing and cannot yet be relied upon. Historical concerns about value destruction through international expansion during peak cycles and poor asset integration post-merger with Eni UK create additional uncertainty around management's ability to execute the complex operational turnaround required to offset these multiple failure pathways.
