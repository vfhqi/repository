Section 3 – Nordic Contract Manufacturers After Peak Growth

Section summary
  1. Valuation multiples for Nordic contract manufacturers collapsed during the 2024 destocking before stabilizing at lower ranges.
  2. Rapid multiple recoveries were heavily dependent on exposure to defensive end-markets and swift capacity adjustments.

Peak Valuations and the Destocking Crisis

1.
Premium valuation peaks: Nordic contract manufacturers achieved historically high valuation multiples during the 2022-2023 industrial boom. According to a Nordea Commissioned Research note on May 3, 2023, the 12-month forward average P/E for Kitron and HANZA reached approximately 18.5x. This peak valuation reflected strong underlying end demand and persistent component shortages. Consequently, investors priced these companies as secular growth compounders, establishing a high bar for future financial delivery.
2.
Customer concentration shock: The high valuation framework was severely disrupted when major customer destocking materialized. A DNB Carnegie broker report on January 16, 2025, noted that Incap's largest customer, Victron Energy, saw its share of the sales mix grow from 43% to 67% between 2020 and 2022. When Victron Energy initiated aggressive destocking in 2023, Incap was hit by a dramatic freeze in new orders, proving that extreme customer concentration remains a critical risk for EMS multiples.
3.
Severe revenue contraction: Operational deceleration immediately triggered sharp multiple contraction across the Nordic contract manufacturing sector. Nordea Commissioned Research reported on February 23, 2024, that Incap's net sales plummeted by 46% year-over-year in the fourth quarter of 2023. Although its EBIT margin remained relatively decent at 12.7%, the company's valuation suffered as investors re-rated forward multiples downward. This severe down-cycle demonstrates how quickly peak growth expectations can dissolve when key industrial clients stop placing orders.
4.
Multiple de-rating duration: The de-rating cycle for compressed EMS valuations lasted longer than most analysts originally projected. Nordea Commissioned Research noted on March 3, 2025, that Incap's 2025e EV/EBIT multiple fell to 7.7x, down from 13.2x in 2022. This prolonged valuation compression spanned approximately 18 to 24 months as destocking took longer than expected to clear. Investors refused to re-rate these shares until visible signs of organic order re-acceleration emerged.

These painful actions aimed to save approximately twelve million euros annually.
