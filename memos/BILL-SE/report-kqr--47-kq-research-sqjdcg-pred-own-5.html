Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Billerud AB's strategic resolution of European capacity actions will dictate its mid-term margin recovery and group profitability in 2027 and 2028.

Financial Implications and Strategic Relevance

1.
Critical inquiry: "Billerud AB (BILL-SE), a Swedish producer of virgin-fibre paper and packaging materials (liquid packaging board, cartonboard, sack and kraft paper, containerboard and fluting, market pulp), reporting two regions: Region Europe (six mills in Sweden and Finland) and Region North America (the Escanaba and Quinnesec mills in Michigan, mainly coated graphic and label paper, part converting to packaging board under the Evolution programme), plus Other. How likely is Billerud AB (BILL-SE) to announce a permanent European capacity closure, asset swap or consolidation step in 2027 and 2028, which mills or machines are most exposed (for example Karlsborg and Pietarsaari, named by Jefferies in September 2023, and cartonboard machines), what would such a step cost and add, what precedents exist (Metsä Board Tako, Stora Enso Skutskär, Smurfit Westrock Birmingham, Reno de Medici Barcelona, UPM), and how does the departure of the EVP Legal & Strategic Projects at the end of 2026 and the Mayr-Melnhof Karton packaging sale (November 2026) bear on it?"
2.
Urgent necessity: Billerud faces severe European margin contraction due to high Nordic wood fiber costs and persistent overcapacity across major product lines. Resolving these structural imbalances through capacity closures or consolidation is crucial because Europe's current underperformance impedes the group from reaching its medium-term targets. Achieving the targeted group EBITDA of over SEK 6bn remains highly dependent on a European margin recovery.
3.
Severe pressure: Nordic pulpwood costs doubled compared to historical averages, creating a structural disadvantage that Billerud is trying to combat through efficiency and pricing. While Scandinavian wood raw material costs are expected to decline moderately, they will remain structurally higher than historical norms. This cost inflation directly threatens Billerud's long-term European competitiveness and profit margins unless strategic rationalization is pursued.
4.
Direct impact: Billerud's earnings per share are highly sensitive to raw material cost relief and mill system utilization rates. For example, a 10% reduction in Nordic wood costs would translate to an EBITDA benefit of SEK 0.9bn, significantly boosting earnings. Successfully executing capacity actions in 2027 and 2028 would eliminate unprofitable capacity and directly improve the group's EPS trajectory over the forecast horizon.

High Nordic Wood Cost Pressures

1.
Severe inflation: Nordic wood raw material costs have structurally escalated by more than 50% compared to 2020 levels, creating a persistent margin squeeze for Billerud's Scandinavian operations. Jefferies Research notes that this cost inflation is driven by the ban on Russian wood imports, competition for biomass, and reduced sawmill activity. Consequently, these elevated raw material costs have permanently shifted Nordic mills up the cost curve relative to international competitors. This structural disadvantage will continue to depress European profit margins unless Billerud pursues capacity rationalization or aggressive pricing.
2.
Critical shortages: Billerud purchases approximately 7.7 million cubic meters of wood raw material annually from Swedish forests to feed its integrated European mill system. The company's management acknowledged that supply constraints in the Nordic market are unprecedented, as regional pulpwood availability is insufficient to meet planned industry production. Because Billerud's self-sufficiency in Scandinavian wood is extremely low, it remains highly exposed to these high-cost domestic wood markets. These ongoing supply pressures threaten the company's long-term European fiber competitiveness over the next several years.
3.
Persistent oversupply: European packaging and paperboard markets continue to face severe oversupply, which restricts Billerud's ability to fully pass through these inflated raw material costs. Jefferies Research indicates that European mill operating rates are projected to hover around 85% to 86% through 2028, far below historical averages. This structural imbalance means that product price increases alone cannot permanently offset the wood cost headwinds. Therefore, Jefferies expects that permanent pulp and packaging capacity closures will be required to restore regional supply-demand balance.
4.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie argues that Billerud possesses sufficient pricing power in its key European markets to completely offset elevated Nordic wood raw material costs. The broker projects that robust price hikes and a more favorable sales mix will drive gradual margin recovery in 2025 and 2026 without requiring major closures. Consequently, the likelihood of immediate permanent capacity closures could be lower than the wider market expects.
