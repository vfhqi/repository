EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Key Question examines how comprehensively the current narrative of sell-side and investor commentary articulates Volati's post-spin dynamics following the distribution of Salix Group on 15 June 2026. Volati distributed all shares of its highly profitable subsidiary Salix Group to ordinary shareholders, leaving the company with five remaining business platforms including Ettiketto, Communication, Corroventa, S:t Eriks, and Tornum.

The market narrative struggles to fully capture the complex operational realities that have emerged after this separation, particularly regarding leverage definitions, earnings volatility, liquidity constraints, and the prospects for capital reallocation. The portfolio composition debate centers on whether Volati distributed its best operational asset through the Salix spin-off. Salix generated a robust pro-forma EBITA margin of 10.0% on net sales of SEK 4,115 million in 2025, representing a highly cash-generative compounding engine with superior historical growth relative to Volati's lumpy industrial segments.

The remaining portfolio exhibits higher cyclicality and earnings volatility, with lumpy platforms like Tornum and S:t Eriks exposing earnings per share to infrastructure and agricultural cycles. However, Ettiketto Group also acts as a key operational pillar, representing one of the two most important segments carrying the highest multiples in sum-of-the-parts valuation models and supporting strong margin expansion for the continuing group.

Leverage calculations reveal substantial mismatches between company definitions and analyst frameworks that obscure the true financial flexibility available to management. Volati reported a net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio of 3.1x in its third-quarter interim report, sitting slightly above the long-term target range of 2.0x to 3.0x but below the maximum ceiling of 3.5x. However, DNB Carnegie argues that including lease liabilities, earn-outs, and preference shares pushes adjusted net debt to EBITDA to between 4.5x and 4.8x, with the listed preference shares alone accounting for approximately 2.0x of the adjusted leverage ratio. This elevated gearing significantly hampers near-term acquisition capacity compared to lightly leveraged Nordic peers.

The preference share structure creates an ongoing cash drain and strategic optionality that remains underdiscussed in current commentary. Volati's listed preference shares carry a fixed priority dividend of SEK 40 annually requiring SEK 64 million in total cash outflows, with redemption possible at a fixed price dropping to SEK 625 per share after May 2025. Sdiptech successfully executed a capital restructuring by redeeming its preference shares in Q1 2026 at SEK 105 per share for approximately SEK 184 million, which streamlined the capital structure and cleared the path for cheaper debt refinancing.

Following redemption, Sdiptech's leverage stood at 2.95x while successfully reaccelerating its M&A engine, demonstrating that clearing expensive equity-like instruments can significantly improve strategic flexibility. Redeeming Volati's preference shares would require approximately SEK 1.0 billion in cash, which DNB Carnegie deems highly unlikely in the near term given elevated adjusted leverage without prior operational recovery.
