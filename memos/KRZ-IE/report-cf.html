Plausible problems to monitor

Kerry's adjusted EBITDA margin of 16.7% in H1 2026 remains materially below peer Symrise's 21.8% margin, representing a persistent profitability gap of over 500 basis points despite the ongoing Accelerate 2.0 efficiency program. This structural margin discount reflects Kerry's historically lower concentration of high-margin IP-rich flavor molecules compared to pure-play flavorist competitors and suggests that the company's broader technology portfolio, while valuable for integrated solutions, carries an inherent margin dilution penalty relative to specialized peers. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7]

& Co Redburn H1 2026 cash conversion dropped to 76% of net income due to working capital outflows and capex timing, representing a significant deterioration from FY24's 95% conversion rate and falling materially short of both the near-term target of 80% and the 2030 goal of 85%+. The H1 free cash flow miss of EUR 262 million versus consensus of EUR 371 million represented a 29% shortfall, the largest miss among tracked financial metrics and substantially worse than peer working capital efficiency benchmarks. This signals potential structural deterioration in cash generation quality that could constrain future shareholder returns and M&A capacity. [Cross- ref: Section 4, Section 6]

Outliers worth noting

Kerry's H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of 16.7% positions the company in the bottom quintile relative to direct taste and nutrition peers, notably trailing Symrise's 21.8% by over 500 basis points, despite comparable exposure to high-growth reformulation and foodservice end markets. While management targets convergence toward 39%+ gross margins by 2030 to approximate Symrise's 40% benchmark, the magnitude of the current profitability gap remains an unexplained structural anomaly given Kerry's ostensibly similar biotechnology platform capabilities and its self-described leadership position in fermentation-enabled taste solutions [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7].

The severe H1 2026 cash conversion collapse to 76% of net income represents a dramatic statistical deviation from Kerry's historical performance, falling well below both the company's own FY24 benchmark of 95% and sector median conversion rates that typically stabilize above 85% among mature ingredient manufacturers. The cash leakage, attributed to elevated average working capital outflows of €51 million, remains inadequately explained by management's narrative of strategic raw material securing and capital expenditure phasing, particularly when peer companies navigating identical agricultural commodity markets maintained stable working capital profiles during the same period [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 10].
