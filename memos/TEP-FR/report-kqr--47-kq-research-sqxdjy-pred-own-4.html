Section 2 – Status of the Portfolio Review for Non-Core Assets

Section summary
  1. Teleperformance launched a strategic review of its specialized portfolio in March 2026 to focus on core value-creating operations.
  2. Potential divestments of highly profitable niche assets like LanguageLine could dilute the company's margins and reduce its growth diversification.

Strategic Launch and Core Mandate

1.
Strategic mandate: Teleperformance officially initiated a comprehensive strategic review of its global business portfolio in March 2026 to identify potential asset sales and optimization opportunities. The company aims to focus resources on core operations such as back-office tasks, sales lines, and data services for artificial intelligence. This wide-ranging corporate re-evaluation is expected to determine the future structure of both the Core Services division and the Specialized Services division.
2.
Accelerating execution: Morgan Stanley reported in March 2026 that incoming Chief Executive Officer Jorge Amar has a clear mandate from the board of directors to conduct this comprehensive strategic review. The broker highlighted that every single business segment remains under active consideration for potential disposal. This strategic pivot has immediately reignited debates among institutional investors regarding the potential valuation upside of assessing the customer-experience provider on a sum-of-the-parts basis.
3.
Diverse capabilities: An industry expert explained in December 2025 that Specialized Services entities like LanguageLine, TLScontact, and Health Advocate have almost no operational overlap with traditional call centers. These specialized entities function as high-margin growth engines and diversification plays that insulate the broader company from core business headwinds. Consequently, a full divestment of these high-value niche operations is highly feasible from an operational standpoint because they currently operate as standalone organizations.
4.
Contrary evidence: Teleperformance management asserted in early 2026 that they were not looking to divest any further operational segments beyond two small completed transactions. The company previously restricted disposals to small and low-margin non-core units that lacked long-term strategic value to the group's development. This historical reluctance suggests that the newly launched strategic review represents a sudden pivot forced by severe public market pressure.

Operational Contributions and Profitability Benchmarks

5.
Superior margins: Teleperformance reported that its Specialized Services segment generated €1,485 mm in revenue during fiscal year 2025. This specialized division delivered an impressive 29.4% operating EBITA margin before non-recurring items. This high-margin performance significantly outperforms the company's core BPO call center contracts, which generally target historical operating margins of approximately 14% once fully ramped up.
6.
Profitable divergence: The Specialized Services segment maintains a high-value margin profile that is more than double the wider corporate group's targets. Teleperformance confirmed its recurring group EBITA margin target of 14.7% to 15.0% at constant exchange rates for fiscal year 2025. Divesting these high-margin assets would automatically trigger immediate operational profit dilution for the remaining business unless offset by massive cost-saving programs.
7.
Softening performance: Teleperformance experienced lower profitability in both PSG Global Solutions and Health Advocate throughout fiscal year 2025. These specialized divisions were severely affected by labor market headwinds and challenging operating environments within the United States. The company previously identified these specific cash-generating units as carrying potential goodwill impairment risks requiring sensitivity analyses in mid-2024.
8.
Growing portfolio: Teleperformance expanded its Specialized Services division by acquiring language provider ZP Better Together in February 2025. Management plans to leverage its global footprint to expand ZP's sign language and video relay services across international markets. This strategic acquisition almost completely offset the negative profitability impact of losing a major visa application management contract during the same year.

Section 3 – Capital Allocation Strategy and Saham Group Influence on Proceeds

Section summary
  1. Teleperformance faces significant strategic choices in balancing debt reduction against public investor demands for cash returns.
  2. The Saham Group exerts substantial influence over corporate governance to accelerate capital distributions and asset portfolio optimization.
  3. Potential divestment proceeds could generate massive capital returns but management increasingly prioritizes balance sheet deleveraging.

Strategic Allocation Framework

1.
Structured framework: Teleperformance outlined its new Future Forward financial plan during the Capital Markets Day in June 2025. The company aims to generate approximately €3.0 bn in cumulative net free cash flow over the period from 2026 through 2028. Under this plan, management intends to return 50% of this cash to public shareholders through regular dividends and share buybacks. This framework establishes a clear baseline of one and a half billion euros for shareholder distributions.
2.
Elevated leverage: Teleperformance currently balances its long-term financial stability against rising public investor demands for capital returns. The company previously allocated 61% of its total capital expenditure to mergers and acquisitions between fiscal years 2020 and 2023. Morgan Stanley noted in January 2025 that these major transactions forced the group to rapidly re-lever its balance sheet. Consequently, management is heavily focused on reducing leverage below two times net debt to EBITDA to maintain market confidence.
3.
Substantial accretion: Potential divestments within the Specialized Services segment present a major opportunity to optimize the capital structure and boost earnings. Bernstein Research calculated in August 2026 that executing these disposals could support share buybacks of up to 10% annually. This scenario is modeled to deliver a 14% upside to normalized EPS compared to base-case estimates. Thus, asset sales remain the most powerful mechanism for driving near-term earnings accretion.
4.
Contrary evidence: Teleperformance management has historically resisted large-scale asset disposals, stating that they are much more focused on improving underlying operational delivery than executing divestments. Morgan Stanley reported in July 2025 that the company preferred to prioritize organic execution over structural transactions. This suggests that the board may delay or entirely avoid crystallizing the value of its specialized assets. Consequently, expected capital returns from major transactions might not materialize as quickly as optimistic market participants assume.

Board Influence and Saham Strategy

5.
Accelerating influence: The Casablanca-based Saham Group has rapidly increased its strategic influence over Teleperformance's corporate governance and capital allocation decisions. Saham entered a total return equity swap in March 2026 to increase its total exposure to the company. Morgan Stanley reported that this swap is expected to raise Saham's equity stake from 4% to approximately 15% by September 2026. This aggressive stake expansion positions Saham's founder, Moulay Hafid Elalamy, to exert unprecedented control over the strategic portfolio review.
6.
Transitioned governance: Teleperformance executed a comprehensive revamp of its executive leadership team under the direct guidance of Chairman Moulay Hafid Elalamy. The company announced the appointment of AI-transformation expert Jorge Amar as the new Chief Executive Officer in February 2026. In this process, long-time leaders Daniel Julien and Thomas Mackenbrock decided to step down from their executive positions. This leadership transition highlights Saham's growing power to shape the customer-experience provider's strategic direction.
7.
Growing tension: The board of directors faces escalating pressure from institutional investors to optimize its capital structure and increase cash distributions. Bernstein Research noted in February 2026 that the board is actively studying the possibility of returning additional capital to shareholders. However, some investors remain skeptical about whether the current framework allocates enough cash to share buybacks. This ongoing debate continues to weigh on the company's public market valuation.
8.
Divestment trade-offs: Sell-side analysts remain focused on the potential use of any cash proceeds generated from the strategic review of non-core assets. Morgan Stanley highlighted in May 2026 that investors require much more clarity on the exact division between deleveraging and returns. While management intends to maintain a strong balance sheet to support vendor consolidation, some investors prefer direct share buyback acceleration. This strategic tension must be resolved to restore market confidence.

This transaction is estimated to generate up to €2.52 bn in gross cash proceeds. The company would likely retain approximately 30% of these proceeds, or €750 mm, to sustain a comfortable leverage ratio of 1.8x. This allocation would still leave €1.77 bn available for a potential partial or whole return to shareholders.
10.
Deferred distributions: Teleperformance has increasingly shifted its near-term capital allocation priorities away from aggressive share buyback programs. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that the prospect of substantial share buybacks has shifted further to the right. The broker noted that the company continues to prioritize balance sheet deleveraging over the next two fiscal years. This conservative approach means that large-scale share buybacks are unlikely until leverage falls toward 1.2x.
11.
Strict targets: Teleperformance management remains committed to driving its leverage ratio down toward its long-term strategic targets. Chief Executive Officer Jorge Amar emphasized that financial strength represents a key competitive advantage during major client vendor consolidations. The group aims to reduce its net debt to EBITDA ratio down toward 1.2x by the end of fiscal year 2028. Consequently, excess cash flow will likely be funneled into debt repayment rather than immediate equity repurchases.
12.
Resilient dividends: Teleperformance continues to support its capital return profile through a stable and predictable annual dividend payout policy. The company's board of directors proposed a gross dividend of €4.50 per share for approval at the general meeting in May 2026. This dividend represented a notable increase from the four euros and twenty cents paid during the previous year.
