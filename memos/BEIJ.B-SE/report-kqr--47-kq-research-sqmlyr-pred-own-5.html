  1. Accelerated European environmental mandates starting in 2027 will ban R-32 refrigerants in split air-to- water heat pumps, introducing near-term inventory discounting risks for distributors.
  2. Beijer Ref is wellpositioned to mitigate these legacy equipment risks through its rapidly growing and highly prepared green OEM natural refrigerant manufacturing franchise.

Regulatory Bans and Deadlines

1.
Prescriptive restrictions: The European Union has implemented highly prescriptive product-level restrictions that fundamentally shift the regulatory landscape away from gradual quota reductions to hard product bans. Jefferies Research reported on October 13, 2026, that Regulation (EU) 2024/573 establishes a series of effective dates for specific air-conditioning and heat-pump categories. Specifically, split air-to-water heat pumps of up to 12kW and certain self-contained systems face a GWP threshold below 150 starting January 1, 2027. This regulatory shift accelerates the transition toward natural refrigerants across the European continent.
2.
Non-compliant refrigerants: Current market-standard refrigerants face immediate obsolescence in new European installations due to these strict GWP thresholds. Jefferies Research reported on October 15, 2026, that R-32 has a GWP of approximately 675. Because this value exceeds the new limit of 150, R-32 is no longer allowed in new equipment split air-to-water heat pumps up to 12kW from January 1, 2027. This productlevel prohibition forces distributors to quickly adjust their product portfolios.
3.
Future expansion: The regulatory roadmap extends these low-GWP mandates to larger comfort cooling and heating systems over the next decade. Jefferies Research reported on October 15, 2026, that mainstream split air-to-air systems below 12kW will face the same 150 GWP threshold from January 1, 2029. This subsequent deadline means that the critical 2027 heat-pump transition serves as a critical operational test run before the much larger room air conditioner transition in 2029. 4.
Segmented exposures: Beijer Ref's exposure to these regulatory changes is heavily concentrated within its European comfort cooling and heating operations. DNB Carnegie reported on September 16, 2025, that HVAC products, primarily air-conditioning units, represent approximately 55% of the distributor's group sales. Within EMEA, which accounts for 60% of total group turnover, these regulatory milestones are driving an accelerated replacement cycle for legacy heat pumps. Consequently, the group must carefully manage its inventory mix to prevent the accumulation of obsolete non-compliant equipment.

Inventory Risks and Discounting

5.
Channel discounting: Distributors in key European markets are currently experiencing significant margin pressure as they rush to clear non-compliant inventory before the ban. Morgan Stanley reported on March 25, 2026, that the upcoming regulatory step has prompted manufacturers and distributors to discount legacy products ahead of scheduled phase-outs. Channel checks indicate that distributors are aggressively discounting R- 32 equipment in Europe. This aggressive destocking behavior is creating localized pricing pressure and compressing short-term distributor margins.
6.
Evolving pricing: The rapid evolution of the European refrigerant landscape has introduced rolling pricing pressure across the entire HVAC-R distribution channel. Morgan Stanley reported on March 25, 2026, that the EU's Fgas regulations are accelerating the phase-down of high-GWP refrigerants. Distributors face heightened inventory write-down risks if they cannot clear remaining R-32 stocks before the January 1, 2027 deadline. This regulatory cutoff leaves distributors with limited options except to sacrifice margin for volume.
7.
Delayed clearance: The transition dynamics in the European market differ from previous regulatory shifts where older stock could be installed post-deadline. DNB Carnegie reported on July 3, 2025, that while distributors historically held substantial pre-buy inventories that could be installed after sell-in bans, the current F-gas rules are much more restrictive. If distributors carry excessive legacy stock into 2027, they will face severe operational and financial friction. This structural change limits the distributor's ability to slowly phase out old equipment.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some industry reviews indicate that R-32 will remain highly relevant and unproblematic for a substantial portion of the existing market. Jefferies Research reported on March 10, 2025, that R-32 will continue to stay unproblematic as the market standard for systems that are under regular maintenance without leaks. This indicates that aftermarket demand for legacy gases will continue to support distributor revenues for several years. Therefore, the risk of immediate and complete inventory obsolescence is lower than the raw product ban suggests.

The distributor has successfully expanded its green OEM branded portfolio of sustainable solutions. Refra has a strong European position in custom-engineered cooling and heating solutions using CO2 and propane. This acquisition enhances Beijer Ref's OEM segment and addresses growing demand for sustainable alternatives ahead of 2027.

Growing backlogs: The group's existing OEM brands continue to demonstrate strong momentum and expanding backlogs for eco-friendly systems. Installer Challenges and Regional Shifts

13.
Flammability concerns: The physical properties of natural alternatives like propane introduce significant installation and warehousing complexities for the industry. Jefferies Research reported on October 15, 2026, that propane is highly flammable, requiring specialized safety controls, leak-detection systems, and certification processes. Because of these hazards, the transition to propane systems represents a complex manufacturing and supply-chain upgrade cycle rather than a simple refrigerant swap. Consequently, manufacturers must qualify suppliers at greater volumes to ensure safe deployment.
14.
Storage complexity: Storing and handling a diverse range of next-generation synthetic and natural refrigerants creates substantial operational friction for distributors. An expert from Heritage Distribution noted on October 10, 2023, that commercial refrigeration requires distributors to stock upwards of 12 different refrigerants. Stricter safety and fire hazard regulations add substantial warehousing complexity and raise overall operational costs. These rising logistics expenses could potentially erode the gross margin premiums expected from compliant equipment sales.
15.
Other side of the story: While the January 2027 ban on R-32 units poses immediate discounting risks, Beijer Ref's strong OEM green portfolio should easily offset these headwinds. Jefferies Research reported on October 15, 2026, that the transition beyond R-32 represents a massive industrialization milestone that benefits scaled technical distributors. High-margin sales of proprietary propane systems from Refra and Fenagy are wellpositioned to capture structural demand under F-gas rules. Consequently, the regulatory transition remains a powerful long-term catalyst for group earnings.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A slower European transition driven by policy easing or persistent black markets would protect legacy distributor margins while delaying green equipment sales.
  2. Severe installation labor deficits and flammability concerns present major structural bottlenecks that could defer the transition to natural refrigerants.

Regulatory Loosening and Alternative Trajectories

1.
Muted transition: The US federal government's planned easing of transition rules for next-generation systems could inspire similar regulatory flexibility within Europe. Morgan Stanley reported on May 22, 2026, that the easing of GWP rules would create a much longer demand tail for traditional HFC gases. A slower migration would keep legacy refrigerant sales highly profitable for longer than expected, delaying the transition toward natural alternatives.
2.
Sustained demand: Delayed compliance timelines would prolong the life of legacy HFC systems and support firmer aftermarket pricing. Expert insights from a former board member at Heritage Distribution on October 10, 2023, indicated that safety and fire codes increase warehouse operational complexity for next-generation gases. If regulatory pressure eases, distributors would continue to run legacy equipment networks without facing immediate write-down risks.
3.
Widespread smuggling: Persistent illegal imports of F-gases present a major headwind to the enforcement of the scheduled 2027 quota halving. The United States Trade Representative reported on November 6, 2026, that insufficient oversight by EU member states has allowed widespread illegal HFC imports that bypass official quota channels. This illicit supply chain undermines legitimate distributors by deflating prices and slowing down the commercial adoption of natural alternatives.

Severe Installation and Component Bottlenecks

4.
Labor deficits: The physical transition to natural refrigerants faces severe human capital bottlenecks that are ignored by optimistic market forecasts. Industry news from Spain on November 7, 2026, highlighted an estimated deficit of 10,000 specialized technicians required to adapt supermarket cooling systems to new standards. Without a trained workforce to install complex propane and carbon dioxide systems, the replacement rate of non-compliant equipment will slow down significantly.
5.
Flammability bottlenecks: Safety concerns surrounding highly flammable propane systems could severely restrict their building-level deployment in urban areas. Jefferies reported on September 5, 2023, that building regulations often restrict the use of propane because propane is highly volatile and flammable. These physical limitations mean that a complete phase-out of synthetic refrigerants remains technically unfeasible for many commercial properties.
6.
Technical challenges: Adopting natural alternatives requires expensive equipment redesigns that pose significant challenges to smaller, independent retailers. Copeland reported on June 17, 2026, that smaller retailers are the least equipped to absorb pricing shocks and supply reductions linked to rapid transitions. If manufacturers fail to reduce the initial costs of compliant systems, commercial customers will delay upgrading their legacy systems.
