EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business model transformation and ecosystem positioning

1.
Prosus has evolved from a passive investment holding company into an active regional technology operator, concentrating operations across Latin America, Europe, and India through high-growth consumer internet platforms. The massive Tencent associate stake remains the primary economic engine, with Prosus holding a 22.66% economic interest that generates highly predictable cash dividends anchoring the group's valuation. Scaled food delivery assets include 100% of iFood and 22.31% of Swiggy, complemented by the October 2025 full acquisition of Just Eat Takeaway and consolidated Classifieds operations under OLX Group. Revenue is diversified across transactional commissions, online sales, and advertising, with food delivery revenue expanding from 20% to 34% of total sales in fiscal year 2026. The group deploys over 70,000 AI agents globally, utilizing a proprietary Large Commerce Model trained on transaction data from 2 billion customers to unlock operational capacity. Fabricio Bloisi, former iFood CEO, was appointed Group chief executive in July 2024, placing entrepreneurship and active AI-first operations at the strategic core.

Financial performance and capital allocation excellence

2.
Consolidated revenue expanded by 57.30% to US$9.71 billion in fiscal year 2026, with organic Ecosystem revenue growing 12% year-on-year and outperforming slow European internet peers. Ecosystem adjusted EBITDA surged by 84% to US$1.27 billion, reflecting a 13.06% segment EBITDA margin, while consolidated group adjusted EBITDA reached US$1.05 billion at a 10.85% margin. Group free cash flow increased to US$1.51 billion, with free cash flow excluding Tencent reaching a record positive US$275 million, proving unlisted assets are structurally self-funding. The open-ended share repurchase program has retired 32% of the Prosus free-float since June 2022, delivering 16 percentage points of NAV accretion through highly tax-efficient capital returns. Share buybacks were funded by trimming the Tencent holding by 0.90% to a 22.66% economic stake, generating US$4.70 billion in capital transaction gains. Capital allocation was supported by US$2 billion of non-core divestments, US$3.67 billion from JD.com disposals, and EUR 4.20 billion to fund the full acquisition of Just Eat Takeaway. Prosus traded at a persistent 28% to 37% discount to NAV, with valuation discounts effectively pricing unlisted assets and secondary listed holdings at zero value.

Industry consolidation and competitive intensity

3.
Global food delivery consolidation accelerated dramatically, with Uber launching a massive EUR 13.0 billion takeover offer for Delivery Hero on July 16, 2026 at a 34% premium, divesting 14 overlapping markets for EUR 1.4 billion to secure regulatory clearance. iFood operates as a virtual monopoly in Brazil, controlling approximately 80% to 90% of food delivery market share and processing over 1 billion orders in FY25. Intense competition erupted as Chinese giants Meituan and DiDi pledged $1 billion and BRL 2 billion respectively to aggressively subsidize their Brazilian market entries through mid-2026. iFood is defending its position through heavy promotional spend, reducing FY27 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $100m-$150m while petitioning Brazil's CADE regulator alleging predatory pricing and corporate espionage. India's quick commerce is dominated by Zomato's Blinkit, capturing 46% of order volume and 52% of net order value while achieving unit economics breakeven via strict avoidance of deep discounting. Unlisted Zepto aggressively secured the number-two spot at 35% order volume share via everyday low pricing, suffering INR 59 adjusted EBITDA loss per order, while Swiggy's Instamart lags at 19% volume share.

Value chain positioning and margin distribution

Prosus captures outsized economic value at the marketplace orchestration layer, with OLX delivering a 48% adjusted EBITDA margin and iFood maintaining 27% adjusted EBITDA margins in its core marketplace business. Downstream logistics operations face structural margin constraints, with own-delivery services forced to absorb heavy infrastructure and rider model costs, compressing margins to -10% to 3% at the fulfillment stage. Payment gateway operations achieve moderate margins, with best-in-class peer Adyen demonstrating a 53% EBITDA margin, while PayU's transaction-heavy business reported a low 2% adjusted EBITDA margin of US$12 million. Tencent's stable credit terms reveal highly favorable upstream power, with 58.44% of total receivables aging in the 0-30 days category and over-90-day receivables remaining low at just RMB 3,496 million.

4.
Swiggy's restaurant partners fund 95% of the 17% to 18% average cart-value discounts, demonstrating powerful downstream pricing leverage, while quick commerce discount rates were reduced from 31.30% to 28% in Q4FY26.

Customer retention and platform lock-in mechanisms

5.
Tencent's WeChat commands unmatched network lock-in with 1.432 billion Monthly Active Users as of March 2026, representing a steady 2% year-on-year increase and anchoring China's digital ecosystem. iFood's Clube loyalty program accounted for 45% of total food delivery volume by March 2026, with members ordering 5.20 times monthly versus 3.50 times for non-Clube users and achieving 75% retention versus 65% for non-members. The iFood Pago fintech arm grew revenue by 219% to US$463 million, providing critical financial lock-in for restaurant merchants through integrated billing, cash management, and credit facilities. Swiggy expanded monthly transacting users by 37% year-on-year to 24.3 million, with food delivery monthly transacting users growing 21% to 18.3 million and total GMV expanding 46% in calendar year 2025. Swiggy's cohort maturation has accelerated dramatically, with the FY23 cohort doubling its gross order value per customer in Year 2 compared to 4 years for the FY19 cohort. The Swiggy One subscription program reached 17.37 million members as of September 2025, with members spending 3 times more than non-members and ordering 7.40 times monthly versus the platform average of 4.50 times.

Operator quality and mechanical valuation arbitrage

6.
Chief executive Fabricio Bloisi's incentive features a special once-off award of approximately US$100 million in shares, triggering if combined Naspers and Prosus market capitalization doubles between July 2024 and June 2028 and is maintained for at least 12 months. The open-ended share repurchase program has emerged as one of the largest capital return initiatives globally, repurchasing 32% of the Prosus free-float and delivering an estimated 16 percentage points of incremental NAV per share accretion. Capital allocation strategy underwent a major shift in late 2025, transitioning buyback funding from 100% Tencent pass-through to 50% Tencent and 50% cash resources, maintaining a disciplined annual run-rate of approximately US$5 billion. Flagship asset iFood possesses a near-monopoly in Brazil, serving over 55 million active customers and commanding an estimated 80% to 90% market share of total food delivery volumes. The unlisted eCommerce segment has transitioned into a structurally self-funding business model, with free cash flow excluding Tencent dividends reaching a record inflow of US$275 million in FY26, while Ecosystem adjusted EBITDA grew 44% organically to US$1.27 billion. A highly reliable, structural capital return framework drives automatic equity value creation, with selling Tencent shares at full market price to buy back discounted Prosus equity mechanically increasing NAV independent of underlying operational performance.

Disruption threats and long-term structural evolution

7.
AI disruption has de-rated European classified peers to 10x EV/EBITDA, down from their 18x historical average, with investors penalizing platforms due to perceived disintermediation risks from conversational search engines. In a bear case, LLM search engines could displace up to 6.60% of organic classified traffic, forcing platforms to increase paid marketing spend to sustain lead volumes, though OLX estimates a 50% reduction in organic search traffic would compress EBITDA margins by only 3%. Quick commerce has emerged as a disruptive channel, eroding traditional brick-and-mortar FMCG distribution systems, with major consumer brands reporting on-demand retail now contributes over 10% of total urban sales. On-demand delivery platforms face a structural cost floor of INR 95 to 100 per order in India, with the industry's current annualized cash burn rate exceeding US$2.50 billion across the top five Indian players. Quick commerce is expanding beyond groceries, with non-grocery categories accounting for 65% of listed SKUs and carrying superior profit margins and higher average order values for delivery platforms. China's instant retail market is structurally poised to exceed RMB 2 trillion by 2030, with high-frequency food demand catalyzing a structural shift toward immediate delivery for cosmetics and electronics.

Outliers worth noting

Prosus's 22.66% economic interest in Tencent represents an extraordinary concentration outlier that dwarfs the scale and economic contribution of all organic operating assets combined. The associate stake generated US$1.2 billion in cash dividends during fiscal year 2026, while the entire unlisted ecosystem portfolio produced just US$275 million in free cash flow excluding Tencent. This structural imbalance positions Prosus as fundamentally a Tencent proxy vehicle with ancillary operating businesses rather than a diversified technology holding company, creating a 4.4-to-1 cash generation ratio heavily skewed toward the passive associate holding.

The operator has not provided a convincing structural explanation for why this extreme capital allocation asymmetry persists despite the stated strategic pivot toward active regional ecosystem operations. Management's narrative emphasizes the transformation from an investment holding company into an operating technology business, yet the overwhelming economic dependence on Tencent dividends directly contradicts this repositioning story and suggests the group remains functionally tethered to a single Chinese internet asset.

The second genuine outlier emerges in iFood's mature Brazilian food delivery corridors, where the platform reportedly delivers returns on invested capital exceeding 25%, supported by a robust 27% adjusted EBITDA margin on its core marketplace operations. These profitability metrics place iFood in the top 5% globally among scaled consumer internet platforms and significantly above peer delivery operators. Delivery Hero operated at a thin 1.8% adjusted EBITDA/GMV margin, while Indian quick commerce leader Zepto recorded a steep INR 59 adjusted EBITDA loss per order. The twenty-fold margin differential between iFood's mature marketplace and global delivery peers remains structurally unexplained beyond generic references to monopolistic market share and scale economies. Management has not disclosed the precise geographic segmentation, cohort maturity profiles, or merchant subsidy mechanics that enable such exceptional unit economics in a sector characterized globally by razor-thin logistics margins and intense promotional competition.

The persistence of these outlier returns raises fundamental questions about the sustainability of iFood's pricing leverage amid aggressive Chinese entrants pledging over $1 billion in combined promotional capital.

(Spanish) The third structural outlier is the sheer magnitude and duration of Prosus's share repurchase program, which has retired 32% of the free-float shares since June 2022 and delivered 16 percentage points of NAV per share accretion. This buyback scale and intensity places Prosus within the top 2% of global listed equity repurchase programs by relative float reduction over a comparable timeframe, surpassing even aggressive U.S. technology buyback benchmarks.
