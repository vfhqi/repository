Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section 2 – ACIP Recommendations and MMWR Publication Status

Section summary
  1. The MMWR publication of Vimkunya’s ACIP recommendation remains delayed into late 2026, creating a temporary roadblock for broad commercial uptake in the United States. Administrative uncertainty at the CDC prevents automatic insurance reimbursement and retail pharmacy stocking, forcing Bavarian Nordic to employ alternative commercial strategies. Strategic wholesale stocking and retail pharmacy partnerships help mitigate this administrative bottleneck and stabilize near-term Travel Health segment earnings.

Administrative Recommendations and Delays

1.
Successful approval: Bavarian Nordic received FDA marketing approval for its chikungunya vaccine Vimkunya in February 2025. Following this milestone, the CDC Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices voted on April 16, 2025, to recommend the vaccine. This regulatory progress was crucial for initiating the commercial rollout in the US travel market. Delayed MMWR publication of this recommendation has since emerged as the primary administrative bottleneck for US commercialization.
2.
Target populations: The April 2025 recommendation specifies that Vimkunya is indicated for US travelers aged 12 and older. Under this guidance, the vaccine is recommended for individuals visiting regions with active outbreaks or elevated chikungunya risk. Additionally, laboratory workers with potential viral exposure are included in the primary recommendation group. This defined market segment is crucial because it forms the basis of all commercial travel health sales in the United States.
3.
Pronounced delays: The standard administrative timeline for publishing an ACIP recommendation in the Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report is approximately two months. However, Bavarian Nordic has experienced severe delays, with the recommendation remaining unpublished as of late 2026. Management noted that the administrative timeline within the CDC remains highly unpredictable. These extensive delays hinder travel health providers who rely on official publications to establish standardized clinical protocols for immunization.
4.
Distribution hurdles: US distributors and large retail pharmacy chains are generally unwilling to purchase or stock travel vaccines in the absence of an official MMWR publication. Jefferies reported on November 20, 2025, that this lack of publication prevents insurers from offering reimbursement. Consequently, commercial traction is severely restricted because out-of-pocket costs remain too high for average travelers without insurance coverage. This barrier will continue to suppress near-term EPS growth until the CDC finalizes the publication.
5.
Clinical protocols: Family medicine providers closely monitor CDC advisory updates when making clinical recommendations to their patient populations. Experts note that changes or delays in official schedules can disrupt standard clinical protocols and increase vaccine hesitancy among patients. In the absence of an official publication, providers lack the standardized guidelines needed to routinely recommend new travel vaccines like Vimkunya. Consequently, provider awareness remains low, which restricts organic demand during the initial launch phase.
