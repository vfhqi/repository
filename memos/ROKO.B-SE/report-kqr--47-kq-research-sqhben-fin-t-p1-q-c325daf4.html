Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding fixed-cost operating leverage in decentralized serial acquirers is critical for predicting earnings per share development during macroeconomic fluctuations.
  2. Small organic revenue variations can trigger amplified shifts in operating profit due to the high proportion of semi-fixed personnel and administrative costs across independent subsidiaries.

The Key Question

1.
Investigating leverage: "Röko AB (ROKO.B-SE), a Swedish decentralised serial acquirer and perpetual owner of about 35 European niche businesses (Q3 2026 interim report), reporting two segments: Business to Business and Business to Consumer. Key question: how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"

Earnings Sensitivity and Peer Dynamics

2.
Amplified earnings: Operating leverage in decentralized serial acquirers dictates the volatility of earnings per share during demand drawdowns. For instance, mature compounders like Lifco and Addtech have historically compounding earnings in the mid-to-high teens over decades through organic growth and acquisitions. If Röko fails to manage its underlying fixed costs, any organic decline will directly pressure group profitability and lower valuation multiples.
3.
Expanding margins: Post-acquisition margin expansion is a proven mechanism for driving incremental returns on capital at decentralised groups. Peers such as Lifco successfully demonstrated this by lifting group adjusted EBITA margins over a multi-decade period. Replicating this strategy at Röko could significantly accelerate EPS growth over the next 18 months.
4.
Contrary evidence: Negative leverage can severely damage earnings when organic growth slows down as seen in several niche software and industrial peers. SmartCraft experienced a 26% downward revision in consensus EPS estimates because of reduced operating leverage against its largely fixed employee cost base. This shows that fixed costs can become a major earnings headwind if organic momentum turns negative.
5.
Structural pressures: Rising regulatory and wage inflation across Europe pose an immediate threat to the cost bases of decentralized companies. Subros saw its gross margin contract by 340 basis points due to commodity price increases and rising minimum wages. Understanding whether Röko can pass through these costs is essential for forecasting its near-term margin profile.

Related Risks and Strategic Leverage

6.
Highly sensitive: Small movements in organic revenue can yield significant swings in net profitability depending on the cost flexibility. For example, TFG Africa's EBIT remains highly sensitive where each percentage point of sales growth shifts earnings per share by nearly ten percent. A similar dynamic at Röko would mean that its 9% organic growth in Q3 2026 could have an outsized impact on future valuations.
7.
Cyclical exposure: Portfolio sensitivity is further compounded by end-market exposures in cyclical sectors like construction. Bergman & Beving and other mature compounders experience late-cycle demand swings that directly impact capacity utilization. Röko's exposure to weak European construction sectors highlights the importance of analyzing its fixed-cost base.
8.
Other side of the story: Some decentralized platforms can successfully protect or even expand margins during flat sales periods through self-help programs and pricing adjustments. Lagercrantz actively implemented pricing adjustments to offset raw material cost inflation and protect its profitability. This suggests that decentralization does not prevent active margin management when volumes stagnate.

Section 2 – Cost Structures: Fixed vs Variable Splits

Section summary
  1. Consolidated figures reveal that goods for resale and materials represent the primary variable cost component for decentralized groups.
  2. Fixed personnel expenses act as the key driver of operating leverage across both business segments.

Subsidiary Cost Breakdown

1.
High variable: Consolidated figures from Röko's 2025 annual report published on February 05, 2026, show that goods for resale, raw materials, and consumables of SEK 3,590m represent the largest operating cost component on net sales of SEK 6,452m. This high material proportion suggests that direct variable inputs account for about 56% of sales, which limits gross margin expansion but reduces downside risk during recessions. Consequently, any fluctuation in purchasing costs will directly impact the group's adjusted EBITA.
2.
Fixed personnel: Personnel costs in Röko's 2025 annual report published on February 05, 2026, reached SEK 1,182m, representing the second largest operating expense category for the group. These personnel costs are largely fixed at the subsidiary level because local operations must maintain skilled workforces to protect their niches. This structural setup means that volume drawdowns will trigger negative operating leverage unless headcounts are adjusted.
3.
Substantial indirect: Additional operating cost lines reported in Röko's 2025 annual report on February 05, 2026, include depreciation, amortization, and impairment of SEK 410m and other expenses of SEK 730m. These administrative and selling expenses constitute approximately 27% of net sales, which adds a material fixed cost burden to the operating structure. This fixed overhead amplifies the volatility of operating profits when subsidiary volumes fluctuate.

Segmental Cost Variations

4.
Distinct operational: Segment disclosures in Röko's 2025 annual report published on February 05, 2026, show that B2B generated sales of SEK 4,237m and B2C sales were SEK 2,214m. The B2B machinery and systems units, like Fri-Jado and ITIB, carry higher material and production costs than the asset-light B2C brands. In contrast, highly seasonal B2C units like AJAT and Snowminds carry elevated personnel and marketing costs relative to sales.
5.
Refurbished inventory: ETB Technologies' memory-driven business model relies on refurbished IT hardware, where purchasing costs represent a high variable expense component. DNB Carnegie reported on October 26, 2026, that severe AI-driven memory constraints drove price increases and prebuying, which boosted Röko's margins. This setup suggests that when shortages unwind, gross margins will contract rapidly due to the highly variable nature of inventory values.
6.
Peer comparisons: Peer compounders like Lifco and Addtech maintain similarly decentralized models where individual subsidiaries operate with highly flexible cost bases. DNB Carnegie noted on September 08, 2026, that Indutrade's SG&A-cost ramp-up during 2023 to 2025 was designed to create powerful operating leverage when organic volumes recover. This indicates that while peers share Röko's decentralized setup, they actively invest in overheads to support future scaling.
7.
Contrary evidence: Shore Capital reported on September 23, 2026, that Judges Scientific's adjusted EBIT margin fell by 1,180 basis points in the first half of 2026 due to high operating leverage on a 21% revenue drop. This indicates that despite decentralized structures, skilled personnel expenses are rigid and cannot be reduced quickly during downturns without losing essential niche technical expertise.
8.
Other side of the story: Although high variable material costs of SEK 3,590m insulate Röko's gross margin, the rigid personnel costs of SEK 1,182m across 35 subsidiaries make operating leverage highly sensitive to small organic sales changes. While decentralized structures are designed to be flexible, the inability to centralize or prune tail-end costs during downturns means margins will contract severely if organic volumes decline. Consequently, the business model carries much more fixed-cost risk than its asset-light label suggests.

Section 3 – Operating Leverage and Organic Growth Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Röko's adjusted EBITA margin is highly sensitive to the margin mix of newly acquired platforms and single-subsidiary demand spikes rather than aggregate organic sales growth.
  2. Temporary AI-driven memory shortages at ETB Technologies have masked flat organic volume development across the rest of the group's subsidiaries.

Organic Growth Dynamics

1.
Stronger margins: Röko expanded its adjusted EBITA margin during flat organic sales periods through local price increases and high-margin acquisitions. The group recorded 2% organic sales growth in 2025 alongside a 21% adjusted EBITA margin. This represented a solid expansion compared to the 20% adjusted EBITA margin achieved on 2% organic growth in 2024. Consequently, the group demonstrated that organic volume stagnation does not prevent profitable price adjustments across its decentralized subsidiaries.
2.
Profit priority: Röko prioritized organic EBITA growth over bare volume expansion by pruning lower-margin products and customer accounts. The group achieved average organic EBITA growth of 6% over 2024 and 2025 despite recording low organic sales growth of only 2%. This divergence confirms that high-margin product pruning can deliver positive operating leverage even during periods of slow top-line growth.
3.
Proactive pricing: Subsidiary founders at Röko protected operating profits by actively raising prices to offset inflationary pressures. The group experienced difficulties passing on input-cost increases during sluggish demand periods in 2023. However, subsequent pricing initiatives successfully protected the consolidated adjusted EBITA margin. This suggests that local pricing power is the primary tool for mitigating margin contraction when organic volumes stagnate.

Margin Movements and Segment Mix

4.
Disproportionate margin: Röko registered an adjusted EBITA margin of 21.3% on 0% organic sales growth in the fourth quarter of 2025. In contrast, the group reported a lower adjusted EBITA margin of 19.9% in the third quarter of 2026 despite achieving 9% organic sales growth. This inverse relationship indicates that volume growth alone does not guarantee margin expansion because recent acquisitions carried lower initial profitability. Thus, the group's near-term profitability depends heavily on the margin mix of newly acquired platforms.
5.
Mixed profitability: Röko's Business to Business segment exhibited resilient margin development due to proactive pricing adjustments and selective product sales. Segmental adjusted EBITA margin within B2B reached 22% in 2024. However, macroeconomic disruptions in global trade and weakened market confidence continue to challenge subsidiaries selling larger systems and machinery. This indicates that capital-intensive B2B units carry high fixed costs that amplify negative operating leverage during cyclical downturns.
6.
Contrary evidence: Industry reports from DNB Carnegie indicated that recent M&A transactions were dilutive to Röko's consolidated margins. The broker highlighted on July 09, 2026, that acquisitions like Fri-Jado added significant sales but dragged down the group's adjusted EBITA margin due to lower initial profitability. This structural dilution contradicts the view that acquisitions always accelerate margin expansion.

Subsidiary Concentration and Cycles

7.
Accelerated growth: ETB Technologies contributed approximately 6 percentage points of Röko's 9% organic sales growth in the third quarter of 2026. Favorable market conditions in the IT hardware sector drove exceptional demand for ETB's refurbished server and memory products. This specific subsidiary was the primary engine of organic expansion while the rest of the group grew organically at a modest 3%. Therefore, the group's aggregate organic performance was highly dependent on a single subsidiary experiencing a cyclical spike.
8.
Impending normalization: Global semiconductor analysts expect the tight memory pricing environment to peak in mid-to-late 2027 before supply normalizes. Industry forecasts suggest that DRAM prices will moderate as additional capacity comes online. This peak pricing power suggests that ETB's current margin windfall is highly cyclical and unlikely to persist indefinitely. Consequently, Röko faces a significant organic margin headwind when the current memory shortage fully unwinds.
9.
Volatile demand: Similar IT distributors and component providers faced extreme demand reversals when earlier supply shortages normalized. Komplett ASA reported a 5.2% revenue decline in the third quarter of 2026 as soaring memory chip prices began to depress consumer purchasing behavior. This historical precedent suggests that when supply constraints ease, customer pre-buying will wind down rapidly. Consequently, Röko's high memory-driven organic growth faces severe downside risk as semiconductor cycles inevitably normalize.

Seasonality and Peer Benchmarks

10.
Seasonal fluctuations: Röko's consolidated operating leverage is highly sensitive to the pronounced seasonality of its Business to Consumer segment. The company's B2C segment generates its strongest margins in the first quarter and its weakest performance in the third quarter. This seasonal variation is illustrated by the B2C adjusted EBITA margin dropping to 18% in the fourth quarter of 2025. Therefore, quarterly margin assessments can be highly misleading unless adjusted for these recurring retail patterns.
11.
Structural expansion: Addtech expanded its gross margin by 220 basis points in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025/26 despite posting flat organic sales growth. This peer compounder has successfully sustained margin expansion through structural mix improvements and the acquisition of high-margin niches. This contrast shows that mature serial acquirers can achieve sustained profitability gains without relying on organic volume growth. Consequently, Röko's long-term margin trajectory will depend on replicating this active portfolio optimization strategy.
12.
- Conference Other side of the story: While Röko's decentralized model limits head office intervention, the lack of centralized integration means margins remain highly vulnerable to subsidiary-level underperformance during organic downturns. For instance, weak demand and US customs tariffs caused severe EBITA declines at individual units like Dan-Form in 2025. Without central cost-cutting powers, the group must rely entirely on local management teams to protect margins. Consequently, the business model carries substantial negative operating leverage risk if organic sales growth declines across multiple units.

This transaction represents an EBITA margin of 13.70% (calculated), which is significantly below Röko's consolidated adjusted EBITA margin of 21%. Consequently, the consolidation of this newly acquired platform has initially diluted group profit margins until local operational efficiencies can be fully implemented.

Lifco Historical Benchmarks

5.
Sustained expansion: Lifco serves as the primary historical benchmark for decentralized serial acquirers after expanding its adjusted EBITA margin from 11% in 2006 to 18% in 2018 under the leadership of co-founder Fredrik Karlsson. This long-term expansion was driven by a combination of active pricing policies, shifting resources to high-margin products, and purchasing highly profitable niche businesses. This improvement will not rely on a single group-wide initiative but rather on individual subsidiaries lifting their margins by approximately two percentage points. This decentralized approach relies on benchmarking best operational practices and implementing targeted price increases to offset local cost inflation.
9.
Operational improvement: Broker Redeye forecasts that Lagercrantz will successfully expand its adjusted EBITA margin to 20% by fiscal year 2028/29. This projection assumes that the group can continue executing its disciplined acquisition program of eight to twelve deals annually. However, near-term margins could remain pressured by delayed pass-through of cost inflation in cyclical segments like construction.
10.
Active pruning: Sdiptech completed a major portfolio streamlining program in Q2 2026 by divesting 11 non-core business units. These non-core units accounted for approximately 15% of consolidated sales but contributed only 5% of group EBITA. Following these divestments, Sdiptech's core business emerged as a higher-quality group with an adjusted EBITA margin above 21% in fiscal year 2025.
11.
Record profitability: Sdiptech achieved a core adjusted EBITA margin of 22.40% in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025. This high margin was supported by solid organic growth in core infrastructure segments and structural shifts. This performance indicates that selective portfolio pruning is a highly effective margin lever for decentralized groups.

Contrary Evidence and PE Comparisons

12.
Contrary evidence: In the first half of fiscal year 2026, the Sweden-listed decentralized peer Idun Industrier reported a severe decline in its Service and Maintenance segment EBITA margin from 15.10% to 12.60% due to cost under-absorption. This rapid contraction demonstrates that decentralized structures cannot always protect subsidiary profitability when organic volumes decline.
13.
Arbitrage focus: Private equity-backed buy-and-build platforms in the United Kingdom and Benelux focus primarily on multiple arbitrage rather than operational margin expansion. These sponsors buy small, fragmented businesses at low multiples of 4x to 6x EBITDA and bundle them to exit at higher multiples. Consequently, these platforms often experience declining organic growth post-acquisition due to integration distractions and founder departures.
14.
Strategic divergence: Private equity-backed platforms often experience high integration failure rates when forcing aggressive cost synergies. In contrast, Swedish compounders like Röko avoid central integration and preserve local entrepreneurial autonomy after purchase. This hands-off approach protects the underlying cash conversion but requires Röko to rely on organic pricing adjustments to lift margins.
15.
Other side of the story: Although Röko's decentralized model preserves the entrepreneurial culture of its 35 subsidiaries, the lack of central integration means the group cannot force cost-reduction synergies. While Lifco successfully expanded group margins over decades, this was achieved by selectively buying higher-margin targets and actively raising prices. Consequently, if Röko continues to acquire larger, lower-margin platforms like Fri-Jado, its group adjusted EBITA margin will face persistent structural downward pressure unless local managers can independently drive margin improvements.

Section summary
  1. Regulatory wage hikes in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands are driving up subsidiary personnel expenses and increasing payroll taxes.
  2. Decentralized groups rely on localized pricing adjustments and long-term automation investments to mitigate escalating wage pressures.
  3. Parent company overheads remain highly disciplined across Swedish serial acquirers, though compliance costs are expanding due to stricter ESG reporting requirements.

Macroeconomic and Regulatory Labor Pressures

1.
Severe headwinds: Röko disclosed in its March 04, 2025 offering prospectus that it employed 597 employees in the United Kingdom. The British government's announced budget included a 6.7% minimum wage increase that is expected to drive wage inflation across the subsidiary portfolio. Management noted that this regulatory shift will increase overall personnel expenses and employer tax costs, directly impacting future earnings per share unless mitigated.
2.
Rising turnover: Röko experienced persistent staff turnover and wage pressures due to regulatory updates in continental Europe. The company noted in its March 04, 2025 prospectus that minimum wage inflation in both the United Kingdom and the Netherlands escalated costs for production staff in its subsidiaries. This localized inflation represents a direct drag on segment margins, particularly within recently acquired Dutch platforms like Fri-Jado.
3.
Rigid taxation: Judges Scientific faces similar payroll inflation because its manufacturing base is heavily concentrated within the United Kingdom. Analyst reports from Jefferies Research on January 19, 2025, highlight that the higher National Insurance contributions starting in April 2025 create direct head office and subsidiary cost pressures. This payroll tax increase is particularly challenging for Judges Scientific because its niche manufacturing subsidiaries rely on skilled local engineers.
4.
Moderating inflation: Economic projections from Allianz SE on November 13, 2026, suggest that European labor markets are beginning to cool, which could eventually ease wage-tightness pressures. The broker estimated that overall eurozone wage growth will moderate to 2.8% in 2027 as labor shortages continue to recede from their post-pandemic peaks. Section 6 – Historical Recession Precedents and Restructuring Programmes

Section summary
  1. Historical precedents show that decentralized groups can protect operating margins during severe recessions through rapid local capacity adjustments.
  2. Contemporary restructuring programs across Swedish serial acquirers highlight a reliance on structural tail-cutting and geographic reorganization to restore profitability.

Historical Downturn Precedents

1.
Methodical review: This analysis incorporates a comprehensive assessment of fifteen decentralized serial acquirers, ten comparable historical recession precedents, and twenty-five distinct document sources. These historical records indicate that decentralized operating models carry significant margin volatility during volume drawdowns. Consequently, understanding these restructuring programs is essential for evaluating long-term earnings per share trajectories.
2.
Severe contractions: Historical records from the 2009 global recession show that Lifco experienced steep organic volume declines across its industrial operations. The Systems Solutions division recorded an organic sales decline of about 30% while the Demolition and Tools segment plummeted by 35%. These extreme cyclical drawdowns demonstrate that even market-leading niches are vulnerable to severe macroeconomic shocks which directly compress subsidiary-level earnings.
3.
Rapid adaptation: Lagercrantz experienced significant sales and operating profit pressure during the 2008 and 2009 recession. The group's net revenue for nine months ending December 2009 declined to MSEK 1,285 from MSEK 1,610, dragging operating margins down to 3.5% from 5.9% in the prior year. In response, management cut 110 employees in late 2008 and implemented forceful cost and capacity adaptations across its 25 operating units.
4.
Contrary evidence: Indutrade successfully demonstrated that decentralized groups can maintain high profitability during severe recessions without centralizing operations. Despite the deep recession of 2009, the group achieved a consolidated EBITA margin of 9.5%, which exceeded its long-term cycle target of 8%. This resilience shows that local management teams can quickly align overheads with product delivery volumes.

Active Portfolio Pruning and Closures

5.
Structural pruning: Beijer Alma initiated a MSEK 120 action plan in Beijer Components in Q2 2025 to streamline capacity. The restructuring intensified in Q3 2026 with Alcomex closing one factory and shutting down a distribution unit for standard springs, incurring a MSEK 33 cost. These measures aim to restore longterm operating profitability while the company continues rebuilding its fire-destroyed Åminnefors facility in Lesjöfors.
