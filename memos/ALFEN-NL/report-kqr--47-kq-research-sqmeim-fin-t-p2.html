Section 2 – Smart Grid Solutions: Frameworks, Indexation, and Bottlenecks 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Alfen's pricing power in Smart Grid Solutions is constrained by concentrated Dutch operators who demand substantial volume discounts.
  2. Real like-for-like price increases remain difficult to achieve because operator installation bottlenecks limit overall substation deployment volume.

Dutch DSO Frameworks and Installation Bottlenecks

1.
Concentrated buyer leverage: Alfen's Smart Grid Solutions business is highly exposed to three major Dutch distribution system operators that control about 95% of the domestic market. These publicly owned operators leverage their massive purchasing volume to demand substantial discounts during framework contract negotiations and renewals. Consequently, Alfen faces significant margin constraints because it cannot easily push through discretionary like-for-like price increases. This concentration limits Alfen's ability to maximize its segment profitability unless private microgrid sales expand
2.
Operator execution bottlenecks: Dutch grid operators are experiencing severe skilled labor shortages and regulatory permitting constraints that hamper their physical deployment speed. These labor bottlenecks forced operators to scale down their substation order quantities, leading to a 10.2% drop in Alfen's divisional revenue in 2025. SPIE project managers confirm that labor shortages for technical roles represent a critical gating factor that delays grid construction projects. Thus, Alfen cannot achieve volume-driven pricing leverage because installation capacity, rather than equipment supply, remains the primary industry bottleneck
3.
Substation delivery deceleration: Alfen reduced its substation manufacturing pace to match the actual installation speed of its primary utility customers. The company adjusted its production rate down from 100 substations per week in late 2024 to approximately 70 systems per week in 2025. This deceleration reflects the slow execution speed of Dutch operators who are constrained by land availability and nitrogen emission permits. Consequently, Alfen's revenue generation remains capped by external physical bottlenecks, which ultimately weakens its operating leverage and segment earnings growth

47

Cost Inflation, Indexation Mechanics, and Margin Targets

4.
Indexation contract clauses: Alfen incorporates indexation clauses in its larger project contracts to mitigate the price risks associated with raw material and subcontracting costs. These clauses allow Alfen to pass through normal inflationary pressures to grid operators, but they operate with built-in time lags that delay margin recovery. Furthermore, unusual costs such as the Pacto substation moisture remediation expenses are completely excluded from these inflation indexation formulas. This structural lag means that volatile input costs can temporarily depress Alfen's gross margins before contract adjustments take effect

51

5.
Smart grid margin targets: Alfen targets a long-term Smart Grid gross margin range of 20% to 30%, which is significantly lower than its historical levels. The segment adjusted gross margin stood at 22.4% in 2025 and slightly improved to 22.9% in H1 2026. ING notes that margins remain capped because large grid operators demand discount prices, whereas higher-margin private domain projects are often delayed by grid congestion. This unfavorable customer mix prevents Alfen from achieving the high-20s gross margins recorded back in 2023
6.
Pass-through contract precedents: Comparable grid equipment suppliers utilize diverse price variation clauses to shield their profitability from raw material volatility. For instance, Time Interconnect employs automatic copper price quotation mechanisms that directly pass through metal inflation to customers, though this alters its reported gross margin percentage. Similarly, Supreme Power Equipment covers 70% to 80% of its order book with price variation clauses to manage material inflation. Alfen's lack of automatic pass-through on smaller public tenders exposes it to greater margin volatility than these global niche peers
