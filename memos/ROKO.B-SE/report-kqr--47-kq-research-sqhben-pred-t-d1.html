Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The plausibility of Röko sustaining its high acquisition pace under leverage constraints is the critical factor determining its future EPS growth and valuation.
  2. Equity investors face substantial valuation risks if the company cannot execute acquisitions without breaching its leverage covenants or diluting organic performance.

Core Key Question and Scope

1.
Primary question: "Röko AB (ROKO.B-SE), a Swedish decentralised serial acquirer and perpetual owner of about 35 European niche businesses (Q3 2026 interim report), listed on Nasdaq Stockholm since 11 March 2025, reporting two segments: Business to Business (about two-thirds of sales: niche manufacturers and distributors such as Fri-Jado, ETB Technologies, Lambda, ATEMAG, Oppold, Pureoptics, CHP, Renovotec, Access Building Products) and Business to Consumer (about one-third: brands and retailers such as AJAT, Golfshopen, Golf Experten, Snowminds, Topa/Brauer, CyTech, Dan-Form). Key question: how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Significant implications: Röko's future earnings per share are heavily dependent on whether it can sustain its historical acquisition pace under tightening leverage constraints. If the company successfully continues its strategy, DNB Carnegie expects adjusted EPS to grow from SEK 85 in 2026e to SEK 95.50 in 2027e and SEK 103.40 in 2028e. Conversely, any operational or financial deceleration would directly impair the expected compounding of EPS over the next three years.
3.
Valuation compression: Röko's current market valuation reflects optimistic assumptions about its scalable M&A model and portfolio quality. According to DNB Carnegie, the stock's target price of SEK 2,150 is based on a 2027e EV/EBITA of 18.4x, which leaves very little room for operational error. If acquisition momentum slows due to leverage or competition, investors face a severe de-rating of multiples to historical norms.

Capital Allocation and Model Sustainability

4.
Leverage limitations: Röko's recent rapid expansion has pushed its financial net debt including option liabilities to 2.6x adjusted EBITDA. In its third-quarter interim report of 2026, Röko reported that this leverage ratio approaches its self-imposed limit of 3.0x. This high leverage restricts near-term acquisition capacity unless internal cash generation improves significantly to fund new platforms.
5.
Organic growth risks: Röko's underlying organic performance remains a key concern for long-term sustainability when inorganic expansion is constrained. DNB Carnegie reported that organic sales grew 9% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, but noted that this was heavily driven by a one-off memory product shortage at its ETB Technologies subsidiary, which contributed 6 percentage points. Excluding this event, the portfolio's underlying organic sales growth remains moderate and uncertain.
6.
Management continuity: Röko is navigating its first major leadership transition following its stock listing, which poses execution risks. Johan Bladh took over as chief executive officer on April 22, 2026, having previously served as Chief Financial Officer and participating in 32 of 33 acquisitions. Investors are closely watching whether the new management can replicate the successful track record of Fredrik Karlsson, the co-founder who built Lifco's model.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

7.
Contrary evidence: Röko's high M&A pace and leveraged model might be unsustainable if interest rates rise or private-market competition increases, as peer precedents suggest. RedEye noted in August 2026 that several Swedish serial acquirers have experienced severe multiple contraction from their 2021 peaks, and highlighted that increased competition in M&A processes can drive up paid multiples and reduce overall returns.
8.
Other side of the story: Röko is well positioned to double its earnings over the next five years without needing to raise additional equity or increase net leverage. According to DNB Carnegie, Röko's solid operational cash conversion of approximately 60% and scalable M&A platform support sustained long-term EPS compounding through its massive acquisition universe. While near-term headroom is limited, incremental cash flow should quickly deleverage the balance sheet to enable further self-funded platform transactions.

Section 6 – Governance and Scale: Leadership Transitions and Head Office Capacity

  1. Röko's transition to CEO Johan Bladh in April 2026 preserves operational continuity due to his long- standing track record.
  2. Precedents like Lifco's 2019 CEO transition show that decentralized structures successfully mitigate key-person risks despite temporary transition costs.
  3. Managing 35 to 50 subsidiaries with a lean eight-person headquarters requires rigorous monthly controls to prevent operational slippages.

Executive Leadership and Transition Continuity

Internal promotion: Röko filled its vacant chief financial officer position internally to maintain administrative consistency. Kressner had previously managed the accounting procedures for the company's initial public offering in 2025. Successful transition: Lifco provides an encouraging historical precedent where a decentralized compounder successfully navigated a sudden chief executive transition. Following the departure of Fredrik Karlsson in early 2019, Per Waldemarson took over as CEO. Waldemarson managed to maintain the group's acquisition momentum and organic expansion without operational disruptions. This transition demonstrates that decentralization minimizes key-person risk over the long term.

The company reported transition-related costs of SEK 56 million during the first quarter of consolidation. These non-recurring items were adjusted in EBITDA calculations to reflect underlying performance. Under CEO Per Waldemarson, Lifco consolidated six new businesses with total annual sales of SEK 1,277 million in 2019. Head Office Capacity and Oversight Risks

9.
Lean oversight: Röko's business model relies on a highly decentralized structure with an exceptionally lean head office. The company's eight-person headquarters must provide strategic guidance and financial oversight for its 35 European units. While this structure keeps overhead costs remarkably low, it raises concerns regarding central control capacity. As the group expands toward 50 units, oversight capacity will be severely tested by increased operational complexity.
10.
Scalability limitations: Former CFO perspectives on compounder models suggest that extremely small headquarters face natural transaction bottlenecks. At peer compounder Teqnion, a lean head office of two or three people managed the entire M&A sourcing process. While a small team ensures high cultural alignment, it naturally limits the volume of opportunities they can evaluate. This suggests that head office scale limits acquisition speed unless additional personnel are hired.
11.
Operational failures: Peer compounders have experienced integration issues when rapid growth outpaces the oversight capacity of a lean headquarters. For instance, acquiring lower-quality units can lead to severe operational disruptions and margin dilution. This risk is particularly acute for Röko because its B2C segment has shown highly volatile developments. Without expanding central resources, unmonitored subsidiary underperformance could drag down the group's overall profitability.
12.
Oversight breakdowns: Teqnion's early operational setbacks demonstrate the risks of expanding a portfolio without sufficient central controls. The group's housing and home-building subsidiaries suffered severe losses from poorly managed facility moves. Because local managers operated with high autonomy and limited central support, integration failures remained undetected for several quarters. This comparative case suggests that excessive decentralization risks operational slippages during rapid expansions.
13.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts argue that a lean headquarters does not compromise governance because local managers are heavily incentivized to maintain performance. In decentralized groups, the relationship between subsidiary CEOs and the head office is built on trust and constructive involvement. Rather than central control, successful compounders use structured board representation to guide strategic decisions. Consequently, eight people can effectively govern fifty units as long as local management remains aligned.

Operational Controls and Governance Resilience

14.
Rigorous monitoring: Röko's internal control framework utilizes regular self-assessments to monitor financial performance and identify subsidiary risks. Every subsidiary CEO is responsible for conducting annual reviews of internal processes and reporting findings directly to the group CFO. These self-assessments are audited annually to ensure compliance with IFRS. This standardized reporting framework helps detect operational slippages before they escalate into material losses.
15.
Strategic oversight: Röko's senior executives maintain close contact with subsidiaries by serving on their individual boards of directors. This dual role allows the central team to monitor strategic matters while preserving local operational autonomy. However, as the portfolio grows, this board representation will consume a significant portion of management's time. This scheduling bottleneck could eventually hinder new transaction sourcing efforts or reduce due diligence quality.
16.
Financial discipline: Röko's decentralization model is supported by performance-linked compensation systems designed to align local and central interests. Local managers are heavily incentivized to create shareholder value through continuous earnings improvements. This financial alignment reduces the need for intrusive head office intervention. Maintaining this discipline is crucial to ensuring sustained compounding of earnings per share over the next three years.
17.
Other side of the story: The transition to Johan Bladh and a lean head office represents a major structural risk that could compromise Röko's expansion plans. If the eight-person head office fails to provide adequate oversight, subsidiary integration failures or internal control breakdowns will occur. While decentralized structures minimize operational friction, they cannot prevent localized fraud or operational slippages if central oversight is too weak. Consequently, any control failure would severely damage the strategic narrative and lead to a multiple contraction.

Section 7 – What Is Known and What Is Not

Section summary
  1. A divergent scenario of slowed acquisitions and multiple contraction would severely impair Röko's long- term EPS compounding.

The Key Question asks how plausible the expected acquisition and EPS growth is, given Röko's record and the strengths it demonstrably has. The evidence supports a medium overall confidence. It is firm on the current balance sheet and cash generation. It is weaker on whether the pace can be repeated, because the report does not show it has been stress-tested. The ratings below are given in prose rather than as a table.

1.
Acquisition capacity and leverage: medium confidence. The current position is well documented. Röko spent SEK 1,979 million on acquisitions in the first nine months of 2026, adding five platforms and SEK 1,543 million of annual net sales. Financial net debt including option liabilities reached 2.6x adjusted EBITDA by September 2026, which leaves narrow headroom below the 3.0x long-term target. Cash generation supports the model: operating cash flow rose to SEK 973 million, and cash conversion is approximately 60%. Leverage corresponds to approximately 60% of the bank covenant. Röko's net debt to EBITDA is also reported as higher than the peer average. Peers such as Lagercrantz and Addtech fund deals more comfortably from internal resources, which sharpens that contrast.

Operating performance and margin lift: low-to-medium confidence. Headwinds include weak UK construction and B2C organic declines in 2025.

Governance and scale: medium confidence. Continuity is a real strength. Bladh was the first employee hired and participated in 32 of 33 acquisitions, and Karlsson continues to lead the investment committee. Lifco maintained its momentum after its 2019 CEO change. Against that, an eight-person head office must oversee 35 units, and Teqnion's integration failures went undetected for several quarters under high local autonomy.
