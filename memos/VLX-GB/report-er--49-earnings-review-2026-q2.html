Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Volex has transitioned from a commodity power cord maker to a high-margin complex connectivity specialist over the last three years.
  2. Fundamental performance has consistently outpaced the broader sector, driven by exposure to high-growth segments like data centres and electric vehicles.
1.
Business definition: Volex is a leading global supplier of critical power and data connectivity solutions, operating across diverse end-markets including medical, industrial, and consumer electronics.
2.
Sector positioning: The company operates within the electronic components and manufacturing services sector, focusing on high-specification, mission-critical applications rather than mass-market commodities.
3.
Historical transformation: Over the last three years, Volex has moved from a low-margin provider to a partner for advanced technology firms, significantly increasing its value-add.
4.
Growth trajectory: Revenue has grown from approximately $440 million to over $1 billion annually, outperforming sector averages by double digits through both organic growth and M&A.
5.
Margin evolution: Operating margins have expanded from roughly 7% to nearly 10% as the mix shifted toward complex industrial and medical applications.
6.
Data centre pivot: The company has successfully capitalized on the AI revolution by providing specialized high-power cabling for large-scale data centres.
7.
Strategic acquisitions: Recent history is defined by the acquisition of Murat Ticaret, which expanded Volex's footprint in the off-highway and industrial harness markets.
8.
Share price performance: The stock has significantly outperformed the market over the last 12 months, reflecting growing investor confidence in the "Volex 2.0" strategy.
9.
Recent momentum: In the six months prior to these results, the share price appreciated by over 25% , as early indicators of data centre strength began to surface.
10.
Peer comparison: Volex has maintained superior organic growth rates compared to peers such as Amphenol or TE Connectivity in specific high-growth niches.
11.
Broker sentiment: Analysts have consistently raised price targets over the last year, moving from a cautious stance to a strong "buy" consensus.
12.
Forecast revisions: Earnings estimates were revised upward by 10% to 15% in the six months leading up to the H1 FY2026 reporting period.
13.
Global footprint: The company operates a highly diversified manufacturing base across 20+ countries, providing resilience against geopolitical and supply chain shocks.
14.
End-market mix: The portfolio now includes five distinct business units, with Complex Industrial Technology becoming the largest and fastest-growing contributor.
15.
Management stability: The leadership team has remained consistent, executing on a five-year plan that targets $1.2 billion in revenue by 2027.
16.
Other side of the story: Despite the successful transformation, Volex still carries higher sensitivity to consumer cycles than some pure-play industrial connectivity competitors, which could lead to volatility.

Section 4 – Financial delivery

Section summary
  1. Volex delivered a comprehensive beat on revenue and earnings, driven by unprecedented 80% growth in its data centre business.
  2. The results fully validated the Murat Ticaret acquisition, with strong organic growth and margin contribution from the off-highway division.

H1 FY2026 reporting period and highlights

1.
Reporting period: This review covers the six months ended September 2025 (H1 FY2026), which were reported on November 14, 2025.
2.
Revenue performance: Volex reported revenue of $584 million, a 13% organic increase compared to the $538 million delivered in H1 FY2025.
3.
Data centre surge: The Data Centre segment grew revenue by 80% year-over-year, a massive acceleration from the 20% growth rate seen in the prior period.
4.
Complex industrial growth: Organic growth in this division reached 48%, far exceeding market growth rates of roughly 5% to 8% for industrial electronics.
5.
Profitability beat: Adjusted operating profit was $57.2 million, surpassing the consensus expectation of $52 million by approximately 10%.
6.
Operating margin: The margin reached 9.8%, an improvement over the 9.5% reported in H1 FY2025 and trending toward the company's 10% target.

Earnings and cash flow metrics

7.
EPS growth: Adjusted basic EPS was $0.197, up 30% from the $0.152 reported in the comparative period, beating consensus of $0.17.
8.
EBITDA delivery: Group EBITDA reached $73.6 million, a 20% increase over the $61.3 million recorded in H1 FY2025.
9.
Return on Capital: ROCE was maintained at 20% for the period, matching the high performance of the previous full year.
10.
De-leveraging: The covenant net debt ratio dropped to 1.1x from 1.3x at the start of the year, showing strong cash generation.
11.
Capex investment: Capital expenditure rose to $21.3 million, up from roughly $15 million in H1 FY2025, to expand high-growth capacity.
12.
Consumer segment drag: Consumer Electricals revenue was $126 million, a 6% organic decline that slightly exceeded the 4% decline expected by some analysts.
13.
Medical weakness: Medical sales fell 10% organically, a sharper miss than the 5% decline the market had been modelling for the half.
