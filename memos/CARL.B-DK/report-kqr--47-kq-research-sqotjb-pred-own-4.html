Section 2 – Carlsberg Hedging Policy and Raw Material Costs

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg faces rising commodity costs in 2027 that will exceed 2026 levels, driven primarily by packaging materials and unhedged energy exposures.

Hedging policy framework

1.
Predictable cost planning: Carlsberg coordinates its commodity risk management centrally to achieve predictable purchase prices in the medium term. For barley and aluminium, which represent its two most significant commodity exposures, the group policy mandates a minimum of 70% hedged for the following year by the end of the third quarter of the prior year. The company then targets a 90% hedge ratio at the start of the fiscal year. This structured strategy ensures that the majority of near-term transactional exposure is locked in before the fiscal year begins.
2.
Regional hedging limitations: Carlsberg utilizes rolling 15-month forward hedges in Western Europe, where developed markets allow extensive use of derivative financial instruments. Conversely, most energy and utility markets across Asia and Central & Eastern Europe are heavily regulated, leaving no possibility to execute derivative hedges for electricity or natural gas. Consequently, Carlsberg remains exposed to localized price changes in these emerging regions. One notable unhedged exposure is Laos, which carries a significant transaction risk due to capital expenditures and raw material purchases denominated in US dollars.
