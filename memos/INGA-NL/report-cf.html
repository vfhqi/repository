Plausible problems to monitor

The third plausible problem to monitor emerges from intensifying digital deposit competition in Germany, where JP Morgan Chase launched a competitive savings product offering a 4% teaser rate exceeding the ECB's deposit rate. While Germany retail deposits grew 5% sequentially in Q2 2026, demonstrating that ING's brand and personalized offers can successfully defend its EUR 160 billion German deposit book, the defensive nature of this success masks underlying structural pressure.

ING selectively offers 3.2% for German savings to preserve margins while using a 4% promotion in Italy, indicating that promotional pricing concessions are already required to maintain deposit franchise strength. This competitive intensity exceeds the deposit battles faced by Dutch peer ABN AMRO or Belgian peer KBC Group in their home markets, as detailed in Section 3's competitive dynamics and Section 7's peer positioning analysis.

A fourth regulatory plausible problem to monitor centers on punitive Polish tax hikes, with the Corporate Income Tax rate increasing from 19% to 30% for 2026 and the countercyclical capital buffer legislated to increase from 1% to 2% effective September 2026. These dual fiscal and prudential pressures uniquely target the Polish banking sector, where ING maintains material operations through its retail and SME franchises. Unlike peers with minimal Polish exposure such as KBC Group or Santander's diversified geographic footprint, ING faces disproportionate earnings drag from this single-country regulatory deterioration, as explored in Section 3's regulatory analysis and Section 4's profitability trajectories.

The fifth operational plausible problem to monitor involves persistent earnings quality volatility from adverse hedge ineffectiveness and hyperinflation charges, with treasury operations suffering EUR 81 million of negative hedge ineffectiveness results in Q1 2026 and Turkish operations generating a EUR 17 million hyperinflation charge in Q2 2026. While these non-core items are typically excluded from adjusted earnings, unpredictable swap rate fluctuations could trigger further negative hedge ineffectiveness if treasury derivatives mismatch underlying deposits.

European banking peers with simpler hedge structures or minimal emerging market exposures experience lower earnings volatility, positioning ING as an outlier in reported profit stability as documented in Section 5's earnings quality analysis and Section 6's recent delivery assessment.

The final macro plausible problem to monitor involves the risk that a prolonged Eurozone economic downturn could trigger a simultaneous contraction in corporate loan demand, a sharp rise in consumer defaults, and rapid deposit flight to higher-yielding assets, unwinding the replicating portfolio's interest rate defense. The Eurozone economic expansion is expected to soften with heightened geopolitical tensions and energy shocks weighing on real consumption, while aggregate European corporate revenue growth hovers around 0%.

This triple-threat scenario would simultaneously compress net interest income, elevate risk costs, and force aggressive deposit promotional campaigns that offset the structural replicating portfolio tailwinds central to the investment thesis, as analyzed in Section 3's macro forces, Section 9's driver synthesis, and Section 10's stress scenarios.

These six plausible problem to monitor collectively represent emerging vulnerabilities that could materially constrain ING's ability to deliver on its upgraded sixteen percent ROTE target for 2027, particularly if multiple concerns materialize concurrently rather than in isolation. The interconnected nature of these signals–spanning strategic execution risks, competitive pressures, regulatory headwinds, operational transitions, earnings volatility, and macro sensitivities–suggests that forward guidance credibility depends heavily on management's ability to navigate a narrowing margin for error across multiple simultaneous challenges.

Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive analysis of ING Groep's operational and financial profile across the 2023-2026 period reveals four material statistical outliers that warrant focused attention due to their deviation from both peer norms and historical baseline patterns. The first notable outlier concerns the scale of ING's replicating portfolio hedge relative to its market capitalization, which reached 6.7 times market capitalization in the euro-denominated retail segment.

This represents the largest replicating portfolio relative to market capitalization among European banking peers, creating an unprecedented structural sensitivity to interest rate repricing that fundamentally differentiates ING's net interest income trajectory from comparable institutions. While peer ABN AMRO maintains a cost-to-income ratio of 58% and experiences more conventional liability margin dynamics, ING's massive hedge position generates mechanical income tailwinds that appear statistically detached from operational efficiency improvements.
