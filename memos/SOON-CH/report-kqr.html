Tax Reform under Pillar Two

6.
Structured step-up: Sonova is adjusting its financial models to account for a permanent tax rate increase under the global minimum tax framework. Under OECD Pillar Two, the transition from single-digit rates to a sixteen to eighteen percent ETR will act as a permanent drag on net profit margins. This tax step-up represents a headwind that will constrain EPS growth even as operating margins expand, a dynamic that remains underdiscussed in early sell-side models.
7.
Peers comparison: Straumann has already detailed the impact of Pillar Two legislation, reporting an effective tax rate of 11.9% for its Swiss entities. Due to its low statutory tax rate, the group is not eligible for safe harbor rules, which led to higher short-term tax provisions of CHF 21.4 million in 2025 compared to CHF 15.2 million in 2024. This trend highlights how Swiss-headquartered medtech multinational groups are losing their historic low-tax advantages under the global minimum tax ordinance.

Section 4 – Balance Sheet Deployment and Operational Savings

Section summary
  1. Sonova's leverage is projected to drop rapidly below its target corridor, creating significant capacity for capital deployment.
  2. The group's choice between larger acquisitions and share buybacks will determine if it can achieve its ambitious sales target.
  3. Operational transformation savings will be partially diluted by heavy reinvestment into digital infrastructure and retail expansion.
  4. The consolidation of retail networks is driving a structural shift in manufacturer supply agreements that will eliminate historic revenue streams.
  5. Independent clinic backlash and rising customer acquisition costs present near-term operational headwinds that squeeze retail margins across the sector.

Rapid deleveraging: Sonova Holding AG is projected to experience a swift reduction in its leverage ratio over the coming years due to strong cash generation. According to estimates by DNB Carnegie in November 2026, the group's net debt to EBITDA ratio is modeled to fall from 1.1x in 2026 to 0.8x in 2027e, and further down to 0.5x in 2028e. This rapid decline will pull the company well below its target corridor of 1.0x to 1.5x, suggesting substantial unutilized balance sheet capacity that could eventually support higher earnings per share.

Deferred buybacks: Sonova Holding AG has decided to put share repurchases on hold to prioritize strategic expansion. Analysts at Bernstein Research reported in November 2026 that management does not plan to initiate a new share buyback in the second half of the fiscal year. Instead, the company remains focused on allocating CHF 80 million to CHF 100 million for retail bolt-on acquisitions while maintaining its dividend payout ratio above forty percent.

Acquisition reliance: Sonova Holding AG is highly dependent on executing larger transactions to achieve its long- term corporate ambitions.
