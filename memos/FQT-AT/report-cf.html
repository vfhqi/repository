EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 24 findings

Materiality of CHANGE

1.
Accelerating strategic transformation: Frequentis is executing a fundamental shift from hardware to software-centric business models across both Air Traffic Management and Public Safety & Transport segments, expanding addressable markets while temporarily depressing operating margins through intensive upfront research and development outlays
2.
Exceptional revenue momentum inflection: Group revenues surged 44.8% organically in H1 2026, driven by unprecedented project milestone pull-forwards that shifted typical second-half seasonality patterns, though management emphasizes this temporal acceleration cannot be extrapolated as a sustainable run rate
3.
Structural governance non-compliance persists: The founding Bardach family maintains approximately 68% voting control through parallel holding structures, creating material violations of Austrian independence standards across Supervisory Board composition and key committees, shielding management from activist-led restructuring pressure
4.
Software platform scaling achieved: The flagship X10 voice communication system and MCX emergency networks now represent the core growth engines, replacing legacy hardware installations with high-margin recurring software licenses and multi-year maintenance contracts across sovereign infrastructure customers

Top Key Drivers (Ranked and Assessed)

Driver #3 – FRMCS Railway Modernization (20% impact, MEDIUM plausibility): European rail operators face mandatory replacement of GSM-R infrastructure with Future Railway Mobile Communication Systems by the early 2030s, creating a multi-billion euro addressable opportunity contingent on coordinated public procurement timelines across fragmented national railway authorities
8.
Driver #4 – US Remote Digital Towers (15% impact, HIGH plausibility): The successful delivery of 15,000 protocol converters for the FAA VoICE program demonstrates operational readiness for scaled deployments, though broader market penetration depends on Federal Aviation Administration certification approvals and state-level airport infrastructure budgets
9.
Driver #5 – Drone Traffic Management Systems (10% impact, MEDIUM plausibility): Sweden's selection of Frequentis for nationwide uncrewed traffic management infrastructure positions the company at the forefront of European drone integration, though regulatory framework development timelines remain uncertain and customer adoption curves are difficult to forecast
10.
Driver #6 – AI-Assisted Controller Tools (5% impact, MEDIUM plausibility): Natural language processing and computer vision optimization tools are being integrated into core platforms to enhance controller efficiency, though customer trust building and safety certification processes create extended adoption timelines that limit near-term revenue contributions
