Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating structural pricing power across electrification segments determines if Nexans can achieve its 2028 Adjusted EBITDA target of €1,150M.
1.
Uncertain pricing power: "Nexans (NEX-FR), a French-listed cable maker that completed its exit from non- electrification businesses in July 2026; it reports three electrification segments, PWR-Transmission (high-voltage subsea and land cable systems with installation, including the Halden mass-impregnated line in Norway, the Charleston plant in the United States and three cable-laying vessels), PWR-Grid (medium- and low-voltage cables and accessories for distribution utilities) and PWR-Connect (low-voltage cables for buildings, industry and data centres, including Republic Wire in the United States since June 2026), plus a shrinking metallurgy segment (Other Activities). Key question: how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: an industry sold out of high-voltage capacity (2022-2028); a high-voltage capacity overhang and price trough (2015-2020); supply and demand reaching parity or oversupply as new capacity lands, with newly qualified entrants bidding (about 2029-2031); transmission system operators prioritising cost over speed (TenneT's re-phasing, Elia's postponement and Energinet's pauses in 2026); sharp copper price rises and volatility (2021-2022 and 2025-2026); input shocks without escalation clauses (polymers, compounds, energy and freight after the 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption, and the 2010 polymer spike); a prolonged European and Nordic residential construction downturn; US low voltage after the Section 232 tariffs and incumbent consolidation (Prysmian with Encore Wire and Atkore); a data-centre demand surge and a possible pause?"

Section 3 – Contract Terms and Indexation in High-Voltage and Grid Portfolios

Section summary
  1. Contract indexation mitigates raw material risk by passing metal price fluctuations directly to utility customers.
  2. Turnkey transmission agreements provide structural protection through non- refundable reservation fees and milestone down-payments.
  3. Project delay risks are financially neutralized as demonstrated by the Great Sea Interconnector payment structures.

Pass-Through Mechanics and Indexation

1.
Pervasive indexation formulas: Nexans protects its operating profitability by employing extensive raw material indexation across the majority of its business. Outgoing management stated during an earnings call on 29 April 2021 that close to 83 to 85 percent of total group sales are protected by automatic pass-through methodologies on both metals and polymers. This structural contract engineering means that sharp copper or aluminum price spikes will not dilute earnings per share because the incremental cost is passed directly to the customer.
2.
Metal cost protection: Non-ferrous metals are completely de-risked by Nexans through back-to-back hedging contracts established at the point of project award. Nexans's former Canadian CFO explained in an interview on 14 July 2022 that copper and aluminum are backed by financial derivatives on the London Metal Exchange to neutralize commodity price volatility. This hedging approach insulates the group from margin erosion because the material costs are locked in when production begins.
3.
Non-metal inflation vulnerability: Non-metal inputs like polymers and compounds present a greater margin risk because they lack automatic indexation terms in standard delivery contracts. Nexans reported during its 2010 earnings call on 14 February 2011 that petroleum-based polymers, PVC, energy, and transport represented about 17 percent of total costs. The company was only able to pass through 70 percent of the 25 percent average polymer price spike in 2010. This exposure means unindexed non-metal inflation will dilutively impact earnings per share unless manual catalog price adjustments can be pushed through distributors.
4.
Long-term miner partnerships: Nexans further de-risks its raw material supply chains by securing multi-year agreements with key upstream producers. Bernstein Research reported on 26 May 2026 that Nexans has a seven-year supply agreement with a Tier 1 copper miner to insulate the business against supply chain disruptions. This longterm contract secures raw material access while insulating the business against force majeure events in the Middle East. These secure sourcing terms protect future EPS by avoiding expensive spot-market purchases during periods of extreme global geopolitical volatility.

Commercial Safeguards and Contract Terms

5.
Substantial advance down-payments: High-voltage subsea projects require significant working capital funding which cable manufacturers offset using milestone payments. Jefferies Research reported on 21 April 2024 that major turnkey project contracts in high voltage are backed by initial down-payments of 10 to 15 percent of the total value. These payments support a highly favorable negative working capital position for cable makers. This funding structure protects cash generation during the multi-year manufacturing cycle, ensuring earnings per share are supported by minimized interest expenses.
6.
Robust cancellation protections: Turnkey engineering contracts contain strong cost recovery terms that protect cable makers if developers cancel or delay projects. Jefferies Research highlighted on 21 April 2024 that developers face expensive termination fees, including non-refundable capacity reservation fees and the full payment of all accepted work. Additionally, customers must compensate manufacturers for all raw materials ordered and costs incurred to start production. These termination clauses protect corporate earnings because only the unbilled project margin remains uncompensated during a project cancellation.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts claim that high complexity in execution can easily overwhelm these contractual protections. A former CFO of Prysmian Group noted in an expert interview on 12 December 2024 that errors in production or delivery scheduling trigger severe liquidated damages with customers. These penalties can easily turn a project with a 30 percent margin into a loss of several million euros very quickly. This reveals that despite indexation and escalation clauses, execution failures represent a persistent risk to earnings per share.
8.
Hyperscaler capacity reservation: Peer companies are extending successful transmission contract models into high-growth data center electrification segments to secure margins. Prysmian's CEO Massimo Battaini explained on 26 February 2026 that the company is negotiating frame agreements and capacity reservation fees with hyperscalers. These upfront capacity reservation fees provide strong visibility and funding for new manufacturing lines. This contractual strategy protects peer EPS because it shifts capacity underutilization risk directly onto tech developers before capital expenditure is fully deployed.
