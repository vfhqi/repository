EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report examined whether non-operating items (net financing costs, minorities, associates and tax) will absorb Vodafone's operating growth, and what that means for adjusted EPS over the next 18 months to 3 years. It reviewed 102 relevant documents from four main source types. The verdict is that Answer A (consensus too high and likely to fall) best explains the near-term path. The report rates Answer A as carrying medium plausibility. My confidence in that verdict is moderate (medium). The direction is well supported, but the timing and size of any revision are uncertain. The ten findings below summarize the basis for it.

1.
Financing costs: Adjusted net financing costs rose to 869 million euros in the half-year, against net financing income of 27 million euros a year earlier, a sharp swing in financing overhead.
2.
Tax cushion: The adjusted effective tax rate fell to 23.3 percent from 27.4 percent, which partly cushions the financing burden. The cushion is smaller than the financing increase.
3.
Non-cash charges: Vodafone wrote down 2.247 billion euros of UK deferred tax assets, a heavy non-cash hit to reported earnings. It does not by itself change the cash trajectory.
4.
Safaricom minorities: Safaricom has been consolidated since 1 July 2026, but Vodafone owns only a 36 percent economic interest, so a large share of earnings leaks to minority partners.
5.
Cash leakage: Vodacom paid 5.4 billion rand in dividends to non-controlling interests in the half-year, which drains cash from the parent.
6.
Legal risk: Kenya's High Court voided Vodacom's extra 15 percent Safaricom stake, and an adverse final ruling would cut economic interest back to 40 percent.
7.
Buybacks delayed: Vodafone halted buybacks to fund the UK minority buyout, and analysts expect them back on the agenda in fiscal 2028 so mechanical EPS accretion is deferred.
8.
Operational offset: African organic service revenue growth stepped up to 14 percent and organic EBITDAaL growth reached 18 percent, giving real but insufficient mitigation of non-operating drags.
9.
Peer context: Telefonica's leverage is projected at 3.5x net debt to EBITDAaL for 2028, and its minority profit rose to 252 million euros, which shows that minority leakage is a sector pattern.
10.
Definitional caveat: Brokers treat minority leakage and the Safaricom consolidation date differently, so consensus is partly non-comparable, which keeps Answer C a live secondary explanation.
11.
Horizon split and limits: Near-term pressure is expected to give way to recovery as the 1 billion euro European opex programme and deleveraging below the target range in fiscal 2028 take effect, so the outer years look more constructive than the next 18 months. This view depends on the Nairobi appeal, broker definition harmonization and emerging-market currency stability. Rapidly stabilizing local currencies would be the earliest sign that the negative view is wrong. Several source figures are broker estimates that later disclosures could revise.
