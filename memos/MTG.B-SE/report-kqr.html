Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The direct-to-consumer migration theme is resolvable and unlocks material valuation multiple expansion across undervalued European gaming peers as operating cash flow margins structurally rise through bypassing the 30% platform fee, though execution risks remain concentrated in platform retaliation, genre-specific adoption friction, and ambiguous reporting that prevents precise long-term earnings modeling. The core investment question evaluates whether publishers can successfully bypass the 30% store commission through direct-to-consumer web shops, aided by regulatory authorities aligned on their side, which is critical for near-term EPS trajectories as transferring transaction volume out of mobile ecosystems expands gross profit growth and offsets organic declines. Transitioning a transaction dollar from mobile stores to direct billing expands gross margins from roughly 70% to 95%, net of Stripe and PayPal processing fees of 2.5% to 3%, creating an immediate and measurable economic benefit that flows directly to enterprise valuation multiples. Every 5% of direct payment adoption translates to a 3% EBITDA margin uplift for Playtika and 2.1% for Roblox, demonstrating the sensitivity of operating leverage to channel migration. The empirical evidence across leading mobile publishers confirms rapid adoption momentum and tangible financial outcomes. Playtika has consistently grown its direct-to-consumer platform revenue share from 25.27% in Q2 2025 to 39.24% in Q2 2026, bypassing third-party fees, and is run-rating at close to $1.2 billion in annualized direct-to-consumer revenue. Modern Times Group expanded its Midcore District direct-to-consumer revenue share to 51% in Q2 2026, up significantly from 31% in Q2 2025, led by RAID: Shadow Legends and Warhammer 40,000: Tacticus. Stillfront successfully expanded its direct-to-consumer bookings share from 26% in 2023 to 39% in Q2 2025, driven by the rollout of direct billing across key midcore franchises. These quarterly progressions represent structurally superior unit economics that traditional sell-side models have not fully integrated into forward estimates.

Genre affinity represents a critical non-consensus edge that differentiates portfolio quality and long-term adoption ceilings. Midcore strategy and RPG genres exhibit exceptionally high web-shop affinity because players with deep progression profiles are motivated by high-value web currency bundles, while casual word games operated by MTG's PlaySimple show very low direct-to-consumer affinity, diluting group-level direct payment progress. This bifurcation creates asymmetric valuation opportunities where midcore-heavy portfolios command premium multiples relative to casual-diluted peers, even when headline booking growth appears comparable. Regulatory interventions provide structural tailwinds but introduce significant implementation complexity. The European Commission designated iOS as core services under the Digital Markets Act in March 2024, forcing unprecedented platform opening, and Apple's initial compliance plan lowered standard App Store commissions to 17% for one-time purchases and 10% for recurring subscriptions. However, Apple introduced a Core Technology Fee of €0.50 per first annual install above 1 million, and game developers warn that the Core Technology Fee penalizes free-to-play business models by charging for non-paying users, creating massive downside risks. Google restructured its Play Store fees in the US, UK, and Western Europe to charge 20% on link-outs, and Apple's June 2025 revisions introduced a 5% to 15% steering fee on transactions completed outside the App Store. These defensive pricing mechanisms compress the net economic benefit of off-platform migration and create jurisdiction-specific profitability variance. 7 ranking clarity emerges when isolating pure mobile exposure and midcore genre concentration. Modern Times Group ranks as the purest listed European direct play because its Midcore District achieved 51% direct-toconsumer sales share in Q1 2026, while Stillfront Group represents a highly leveraged play, raising its direct-toconsumer bookings share to 33% but facing top-line headwinds from player discounts. Ubisoft Entertainment remains a highly diluted play because its proprietary distribution platform, Ubisoft Connect, represents only a minority of its PC sales compared to Steam. The PC gaming counter-case demonstrates platform power durability, where Steam's 30% platform fee remains secure against any developer-led direct launcher alternative due to Valve's massive social and community network, combined with entrenched player wishlists and centralized asset libraries.

Critical data gaps prevent definitive long-term earnings precision. Publishers fail to disclose the precise breakdown of transactions processed via cheap enterprise engines versus expensive localized systems, limiting gross margin forecasting accuracy, and mobile gaming firms do not publish separate user acquisition metrics for web versus mobile store platforms, masking true customer acquisition efficiency. Structured primary expert research and tracking of quarterly operational disclosures are required to bridge these informational gaps, particularly around cohort-level retention, promotional intensity, and reinvestment rates into performance advertising channels.

Second-order implications demonstrate that margin expansion benefits accrue asymmetrically across the value chain. Mobile publishers continuously reinvest their off-platform savings back into performance user acquisition, benefiting high-ROI networks like AppLovin, and moderating store fees allow developers to expand performance advertising budgets, supporting Unity's underappreciated commerce runway. This dynamic creates potential margin leakage where consolidating adtech platforms capture the surplus through elevated cost-per- install bidding, neutralizing publisher-level profitability gains. The landscape remains resolvable with material rerating potential, but execution visibility, regulatory stability, and genre mix determine which specific equities offer asymmetric risk-reward profiles over the 18-month to 3-year investment horizon.

Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. Migrating transaction volumes to direct-to-consumer web shops bypasses the 30% platform fee, driving substantial gross margin expansion and a highly visible EPS re-rating trajectory over the next 18 months.
  2. This economic mechanism unlocks material valuation multiple expansion across undervalued European gaming peers as operating cash flow margins structurally rise.

Defining the Verbatim Driver and EPS Trajectory

1.
Verbatim driver defined: The core investment question evaluates whether publishers can successfully bypass the 30% store commission through direct-to-consumer web shops, aided by regulatory authorities aligned on their side.
2.
EPS trajectory significance: Direct payment adoption is critical for near-term EPS trajectories, as transferring transaction volume out of mobile ecosystems expands gross profit growth and offsets organic declines.
3.
App store fee comparison: Standard purchases via the App Store or Google Play Store lose 15% to 30% in platform fees, whereas payment processors like Adyen, PayPal, and Stripe generally involve fees of 5% or less.
4.
/A Inherent user redirection friction: Sceptics note that friction in the user experience and lower consumer trust could prevent a meaningful proportion of gamers from shifting to web checkout channels.

Economic Mechanisms and Multiple Re-rating Dynamics

5.
Gross margin expansion mechanism: Transitioning a transaction dollar from mobile stores to direct billing expands gross margins from roughly 70% to 95%, net of Stripe and PayPal processing fees of 2.5% to 3%.
6.
Incremental margin flow-through: According to Morgan Stanley's 28 September 2026 research, every 5% of direct payment adoption translates to a 3% EBITDA margin uplift for Playtika and 2.1% for Roblox.
7.
EBITDA valuation multiple impact: Bypassing app store fees supports Playtika's transition toward direct platforms, with its valuation supported by a 5x multiple on forward EBITDA as margins recover.
8.
MTG stub valuation discount: DNB Carnegie's 9 October 2026 preview highlights that divesting a minority stake in PlaySimple would imply that the MTG stub trades at only 4x to 5x EV/EBIT.
9.
Other side of the story: Sceptical analysts warn that if the direct-to-consumer theme is overhyped due to platform retaliation, such as Google's June 2026 fee restructuring of 20% on link-outs, anticipated gross margin expansions will fail to materialize for publishers.

Section 2 – Thematic Mechanism and Economics of DTC Migration

Section summary
  1. Shifting transactions off-platform drives immediate gross margin expansion by replacing 30% store fees with 3% processing costs.
  2. Direct channels allow publishers to enhance monetization through customized loyalty rewards and exclusive progression bundles.

Microeconomics of Payment Processing Costs

1.
Massive commission delta: Native app stores impose a restrictive 30% tax, whereas direct-to-consumer payment alternatives typically incur fees under 5% of purchase value, as stated in Bending Spoons SpA's July 2026 prospectus.
2.
Transparent processor pricing: Enterprise payment processors like Adyen offer transparent Interchange++ models at scale to minimize transaction markups for large-volume publishers, according to March 2026 expert interviews.
3.
Favorable processing rates: Transactional processing fees across proprietary direct-to-consumer web platforms typically average 3% to 4% net of store margins, based on Playtika's August 2026 quarterly filing.
4.
30 10Q Stiff interchange base: A September 30, 2026, Jefferies study reveals blended card processing costs of 1.95% to 2.35%, proving that interchange fees represent the absolute floor of off-platform transactional expenses.
5.
Unrivaled enterprise scale: Expert insights from former product leads in May 2026 suggest Adyen achieves an all-in take rate of 17 basis points, representing the lowest-cost option for multi-billion-dollar game publishers.
6.
Aggressive bidding wars: Payment processors like Stripe are shifting from flat-rate pricing to tiered volume- based models to defend their enterprise volume against aggressive merchant pricing, according to January 2026 expert interviews.

Trajectories of Web Shop Adoption and Incentives

7.
Widespread web adoption: An August 18, 2026, Bain & Company report reveals that 75% of top-grossing mobile games operate web stores, up from a base of only 12% in 2019.
8.
Accelerating consumer migration: Third-party tracking from Morgan Stanley on September 28, 2026, estimates that 17% of US mobile gaming spend has migrated to direct payments since early 2025.
9.
Dilutive booking growth: Stillfront's strategy of using consumer discounts to drive direct-to-consumer adoption suppresses reported absolute bookings growth even as underlying gross margins expand, as noted in Stillfront's October 2024 quarterly report.
10.
Lock-in loyalty rewards: Game publishers like PLAYSTUDIOS utilize tiered loyalty points and special currency bundles to drive player retention and maintain high lifetime value direct-to-consumer, according to its May 2026 annual filing.
11.
Valuable consumer ownership: Direct platforms provide publishers with more comprehensive data and monetization tools, enabling them to improve player unit economics structurally, as stated in Playtika's May 2026 prepared remarks.
12.
Other side of the story: : Sceptical software experts caution that web checkouts suffer from severe conversion friction because mobile players must leave active gameplay sessions and complete cumbersome external browser logins, based on September 2026 expert interviews.

Section 4 – Regulatory Environment and Platform Policy Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Regulatory interventions structurally support off-platform billing but gatekeepers introduce complex fee layers to protect their revenues.
  2. Apple and Google deploy defensive pricing mechanisms like steering fees and API tracking to limit the profitability of alternative payment networks.

European Digital Markets Act Compliance Framework

1.
Accelerating platform intervention: The European Commission designated iOS as core services under the Digital Markets Act in March 2024, forcing unprecedented platform opening.
2.
Adjusted commission terms: Apple's initial compliance plan lowered standard App Store commissions to 17% for one-time purchases and 10% for recurring subscriptions, representing a substantial nominal cut.
3.
Restructuring transaction fees: Under Apple's March 2024 EU framework, developers using Apple's payment system must pay an additional 3% fee.
4.
Aggressive alternative charges: To offset commission cuts, Apple introduced a Core Technology Fee of €0.50 per first annual install above 1 million.
5.
Prohibitive download penalty: Game developers warn that the Core Technology Fee penalizes free-to-play business models by charging for non-paying users, creating massive downside risks.
6.
Evolving regulatory pressure: In response to European Commission investigations, Apple revamped its steering guidelines in June 2025 to allow multiple outbound links.
7.
Defensive commission tiering: Apple's June 2025 revisions introduced a 5% to 15% steering fee on transactions completed outside the App Store.

Google's Fee Restructuring and Alternative Billing

8.
Imposing steering fees: On June 30, 2026, Google restructured its Play Store fees in the US, UK, and Western Europe to charge 20% on link-outs.
9.
Limited transaction relief: Under Google's June 2026 terms, developers are charged 25% for existing installs and 20% for new installs on off-platform steered transactions.
10.
Mandating platform tracking: Google tracks transactions by requiring integration with monitoring APIs, creating technical barriers for independent shops.
11.
Pioneering regulatory pushback: South Korea's August 2021 legislation pioneered alternative billing mandates, but Google offered only a 4% discount.
12.
Preserving platform economics: Despite regulatory interventions, tech platforms successfully repack-aged their fee structures to retain underlying economics, limiting immediate financial relief for developers.
13.
Escalating antitrust scrutiny: The Competition Commission of India investigated Apple's mandatory billing, confirming anti-competitive platform behavior.

Legal Precedents and Platform Resistance Tactics

14.
Legal catalyst verified: The long-running Epic Games v. Apple litigation proved that platforms violated unfair competition laws by restricting anti-steering practices.
15.
Tailored judicial relief: In December 2025, the Ninth Circuit upheld Apple's right to require size and placement parity for external checkout links.
16.
68 10Q Protracted legal disputes: Legal experts note that Epic v. Google and Epic v. Apple have entered extended phases, delaying final resolution until 2027.
17.
Escalating consumer litigation: In October 2026, Google faced a £1 billion UK consumer lawsuit alleging excessive commissions on developers and consumers.
18.
Targeted regulatory expansion: The UK Competition and Markets Authority designated Apple and Google with strategic market status in mobile in October 2025, paving the way for local interventions.
19.
side of the story: Sceptical mobile developers warn that aggressive platform pricing like the €0.50 Core Technology Fee makes alternative EU storefronts economically unviable for games with large nonpaying user bases, as highlighted by G5 Entertainment in February 2024.
