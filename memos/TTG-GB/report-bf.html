EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Operational structure and footprint realignment

1.
Product-led divisional pivot: reorganising operations into Power, EMS, and Components divisions replaces the former regional structure, enhancing market accountability while Components undergoes strategic review for potential disposal.
2.

4.
Leadership renewal and governance: Eric Lakin appointed Chief Executive Officer alongside Chair Phil Swash and Senior Independent Director Mary Waldner, establishing clear strategic direction toward high-reliability power electronics and operational discipline.

Financial performance and peer positioning

5.
Revenue contraction and margin resilience: group revenue contracted 7.6% statutorily and 2.7% organically in 2025 to £481.4 million, yet organic adjusted operating profit grew 2.2% in 2025 to reach £37.2 million, demonstrating underlying margin expansion despite top-line headwinds.
6.
Severe statutory losses masked by restructuring: statutory operating loss reached £28.2 million in 2025 driven by £41.4 million in non-cash impairments against North American assets and goodwill, distorting GAAP results while adjusted metrics show operational progress [#R]
7.
Tax rate inflation from deferred asset derecognition: the adjusted effective tax rate escalated to 57.1% in 2025 due to US tax losses, yet if US deferred tax assets were recognized, the adjusted effective tax rate would be 25.4%, artificially depressing statutory net earnings [#R]
8.
Exceptional cash generation and deleveraging: operating cash conversion reached 150% in 2025 while net debt excluding leases reduced to £50.3 million at year-end 2025 from £80.1 million in 2024, significantly strengthening balance sheet resilience.
9.
Return metrics lag quality peers: post-tax return on invested capital expanded to 13.3% in 2025 from 10.0% in 2024, yet peer Volex delivered superior returns with a 21.0% ROCE and Oxford Instruments leads with 20.0% EBITDA margins, exposing operational execution gaps.
10.
Working capital unwind drives cash outperformance: disciplined working capital management generated a £12.7 million cash inflow during 2025, with net inventory levels decreasing by £14.8 million, converting operational improvements into tangible cash returns.

Industry structure and competitive positioning

11.
Fragmented HMLV market with consolidation tailwinds: the overall value chain exhibits an hourglass margin distribution where upstream materials/OSAT and downstream system integrators capture bulk profits, leaving midstream assembly squeezed while Components and Power divisions manufacture custom resistors, power convertors, and magnetics operating in harsh performance-critical environments, defending 8-12% EBIT margin pools.
12.
Customer concentration remains low with deep lock-in: no individual customer accounts for more than 10% of total group revenues yet all top 10 customer relationships exceed 10 years of continuous partnership, while design-in solutions and strict regulatory approvals bind top OEMs for 15-30 year lifecycles, creating multi-decade revenue visibility.
13.
Direct OEM dominance limits disintermediation risk: direct sales to tier-1 OEMs represent 83% of total group revenues while distributor sales contracted to 17% of group sales, significantly dampening channel disintermediation threats despite short-cycle distribution volatility.
14.
Contractual inflation pass-through protects margins: indexed pricing clauses allow TT to pass raw material inflation directly to OEMs and successfully recovered 40 million pounds of input inflation via contractual adjustments and surcharges, insulating gross dollars against raw input volatility.

Operator quality and foundation assessment

15.
Leadership establishes clear strategic priorities: Eric Lakin was appointed Chief Executive Officer in August 2025, establishing four core strategic priorities spanning divisional realignment, cost reduction, sales transformation, and portfolio optimization, while management stabilized the Cleveland facility through specialist external operational support, with factory productivity expanding from 57% in Q3 2024 to 81% by January 2026, restoring operational discipline.
16.
Prudent capital allocation and deleveraging trajectory: management reduced net debt excluding leases to £50.3 million at year-end 2025 from £80.1 million in 2024, with covenant leverage ratio dropping to 1.1x adjusted EBITDA, while management paused dividend payments in 2024 and 2025 to prioritize balance sheet deleveraging and operational stabilization, demonstrating disciplined capital management.
17.
Deep customer platform lock-in creates structural moat: design-in solutions embed TT onto defense and medical platforms for 10 to 30 years, with customer requalification requiring 18 to 24 months of rigorous validation, while manufacturing facilities maintain FDA registrations, ISO 13485 medical cleanroom standards, and AS9100D defense accreditations, creating high structural switching costs.

Operating leverage vulnerability in Components: components division exhibits high operating leverage, where volume declines cause sharp unhedged overhead under-absorption, exposing near-term margin compression risk if distributor destocking extends through 2027 [#R]

Section 4 – Value Chain Map + Company Position + Margin Distribution

Section summary
  1. TT Electronics occupies two distinct value-chain stages–specialist discrete components/assemblies and midstream EMS–where margin pools vary dramatically between high-barrier magnetics (12-15% EBIT) and commoditised board assembly (3-5% EBIT).
  2. the overall value chain exhibits an hourglass margin distribution where upstream materials/OSAT and downstream system integrators capture bulk profits, leaving midstream assembly squeezed by raw material inflation.

Value chain stage mapping and TT Electronics position

1.
Discrete component and specialist assembly stage: Components and Power divisions manufacture custom resistors, power convertors, and magnetics operating in harsh performance-critical environments.
2.
Magnetics and conversion focus: Power division builds high-spec power supplies and transformers tailored for aerospace, defence, and medical OEMs.
3.
Passive component exposure: Components division delivers specialized resistors, sensors, and microelectronics primarily serving industrial distribution networks.
4.
EMS and board assembly stage: EMS division performs complex printed circuit board assembly (PCBA) and box-build integration for life sciences and industrial clients.
5.
High-mix low-volume orientation: focuses on complex low-volume builds rather than commodity high- volume consumer assembly.
6.
Upstream integration profile: lacks raw semiconductor fabrication or substrate manufacturing, relying on external foundries and material suppliers.
7.
Pass-through material dependency: raw silicon, copper, and precious metals are procured externally under contractual pass-through clauses.
8.
Downstream integration boundary: supplies sub-assemblies and control modules to tier-1 defense and industrial primes without owning final end-market platforms.
9.
Long-term platform design-in: secures 10 to 30 year platform presence by co-engineering custom power solutions with OEMs.
