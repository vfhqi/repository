What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: a predictable business inside an unpredictable security, whose FY2027 EPS the sell-side still has too high because the margin story stops at EBITDA, while the heart, the hubs and cash make the two-to-three-year outcome better than the next three reports. Six judgements decide whether, and when, it is a buy again.

  1. Judgement 1, the fourth quarter and FY2026. In July the company steered FY2026 EBITDA to "in line with consensus" (then 206); both houses now have 199 to 200, as do we. Fourth-quarter sales forecasts run from about 245 implied by RedEye's full year (our arithmetic) to DNB Carnegie's 273 (ours about 260), with the customer's 10 of stock to unwind and the 8.5 of lease revenue not repeating. A fourth quarter at the top of the range with heart above 35 and lung above 140 is the memo's 10% counter-case that holds FY2027 consensus; at the bottom, a net cut in January, which the memo puts at 70% likely, becomes more likely still.
  2. Judgement 2, the customer in 2027. Our base is a fall of 65 (range 40 to 100) from about 150 this year; DNB Carnegie's 5% to 6% of its 1,276 is 64 to 77; RedEye warns it could be worse. Each 35 either way is about 0.65 of FY2027 EPS.
  3. Judgement 3, European heart and the hubs. Our heart is 155 in 2027 against the sell-side's 180 to 194; ex-customer lung grows 25% to 30% in our base on the hubs. Each 35 of heart is about 0.63 of FY2027 EPS; heart at the sell-side's level is what lets management keep the targets another quarter.
  4. Judgement 4, the lines below EBITDA. CE-mark amortisation of 39 a year, our 20% tax rate against DNB Carnegie's 15%, and the end of 2026's currency gains. Aligning the sell-side to them takes 8% to 10% off FY2027 consensus EPS before any operating number changes; on our arithmetic it is about half of the gap between DNB Carnegie's 5.70 and our 3.82, the rest being EBITDA (319 against 279).
  5. Judgement 5, operating costs and the 2027 targets. We have costs excluding D&A up 13% to about 562 and an EBITDA margin of 24.0%; the houses have 23.4% (RedEye) and 25.0% (DNB Carnegie) against a target above 30%. Cost growth of 17% takes EPS to about 3.3; a re-dating in January to a year Xvivo can beat is a negative headline and a positive set-up.
  6. Judgement 6, cash and capitalisation. We have capitalisation falling toward 100 by 2028 and free cash flow of +15 in 2027; DNB Carnegie has capitalisation rising to 204 and 239 and free cash flow of (80). If DNB Carnegie is right, cash builds more slowly and a one-year FDA delay costs more; if we are, positive free cash flow in 2027 is the one favourable surprise in the window.
