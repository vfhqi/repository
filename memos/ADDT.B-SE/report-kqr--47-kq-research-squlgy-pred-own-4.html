Section 4 – Floating Debt Reference-Rate Mix and Hedges

Section summary
  1. Addtech relies entirely on variable floating-rate debt to finance its operations, creating high sensitivity to short-term STIBOR movements.
  2. Peer comparisons show that while Lifco and Indutrade have used derivative swaps, Addtech maintains a purely unhedged floating debt structure.

Addtech Rate Exposure

1.
Variable rate focus: Addtech structures its capital financing primarily through short-term variable overdraft facilities and external loans that expose it directly to rate volatility. The group reports that its entire debt portfolio of credit facilities carried variable interest terms of zero to three months as of 31 March 2026. This floating structure allows the company to quickly benefit from rate cuts but increases operational cash flow volatility during central bank tightening cycles.
2.
Elevated sensitivity metrics: Addtech faces a larger potential impact on its net earnings from floating interest rates as its absolute debt load has expanded. The group reported that a one percentage point change in interest rates would impact post-tax profit by SEK +/- 57 million as of June 2026. This sensitivity has increased from SEK +/- 41 million in March 2024, reflecting a larger consolidated debt footprint from continuous programmatic acquisitions.
3.
Minimal derivative protection: Addtech's financial risk management relies on cash management rather than active interest rate derivative positions to mitigate debt service volatility. The company's annual disclosures for the period ending 31 March 2025 showed zero carrying values for active interest rate hedging derivatives on its balance sheet. Instead of swaps, the group utilizes currency swaps from time to time to reduce overall financing costs and optimize internal liquidity pools.

Peer Financing Comparison

4.
Floating peer profile: Lifco manages its substantial debt portfolio by issuing floating-rate bonds while maintaining a high sensitivity to broad STIBOR movements. The group reported total borrowings of SEK 10,964 million as of 31 December 2025, with 100 percent of this debt subject to variable interest rates. A one percentage point interest rate shift would impact Lifco's annual net profit by SEK +/- 87 million, which exceeds Addtech's sensitivity of SEK 57 million.
5.
Specific swap utilization: Lifco employs structured currency interest rate swaps to hedge specific currency debt exposures rather than general interest rate risk. The company reported outstanding swaps with nominal values of EUR 63 million and NOK 713 million as of 31 December 2024. These derivatives help align foreign net assets with borrowings but do not eliminate the variable risk of its primary STIBOR-linked bonds.
6.
Sizable interest burden: Redeye reported in October 2024 that Lifco typically pays three-month STIBOR or a two- year mid-swap rate plus a 1.0% margin for its outstanding bond loans. According to Redeye, the elevation of STIBOR from near zero in early 2022 to approximately 3.0% in late 2024 reduces the profits and free cash flows attributable to equity owners. This rate headwind represents a direct drag on returns on equity unless leverage is reduced.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some serial acquirers historically utilized derivative contracts to fix their interest expenses even when their wider peer group operated with unhedged debt. Indutrade reported that its parent company maintained an interest rate swap to hedge SEK 250 million of variable borrowings against fixed rates. Although this specific contract expired in 2023, it demonstrates that Swedish compounders can deploy active hedging programs when macro environments dictate.

Lagercrantz Debt Dynamics

8.
Structured borrowing guidelines: Lagercrantz Group regulates its borrowing risk by combining short-term credit facilities with strict internal guidelines on fixed interest durations. The company's debt portfolio consists of committed credit facilities with a fixed interest term of one year alongside external loans carrying fixed terms of three to six months. Lagercrantz dictates in its financial policy that up to 50 percent of its borrowings can be fixed for terms of one to five years.
9.

A one percentage point change in the weighted average interest rate is estimated to affect the group's annual pre-tax interest expense by MSEK 40. This pre-tax impact is lower than the profit sensitivities reported by Addtech and Lifco.
10.
Favourable rate shifts: Redeye reported in April 2025 that Lagercrantz is poised to experience an earnings boost as central bank reference rates enter a cutting cycle. Because Lagercrantz carries floating-rate debt, lower interest rates will directly boost its net income margin and support earnings per share. However, Redeye expects absolute financial expenses to remain elevated at SEK 175 million in 2025/2026 due to higher absolute debt levels from rapid acquisitions.
11.
Other side of the story: Maintaining a completely variable debt portfolio without derivative hedges represents a deliberate strategy to align borrowing costs with macroeconomic trends. If inflation declines and central banks cut reference rates rapidly, unhedged compounders like Addtech will reap immediate interest expense savings that boost net income. Conversely, peers with fixed-rate lock-ins will suffer from uncompetitive financing costs during rate-cutting cycles.

Consequently, excluding these obligations from standard leverage metrics can understate the true debt burden of the consolidated groups.
