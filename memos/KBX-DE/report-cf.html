EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Knorr-Bremse achieved its BOOST operating EBIT margin target early in Q2 2026 at 14.2% and launched its Growth Beyond 2030 strategy, targeting EUR 10 billion revenue, 16% operating margin, and greater than 25% ROCE by 2030. The company delivered a 3% revenue beat and 4% operating EBIT beat versus Vara consensus in Q2 2026, with earnings per share increasing 24.3% year-on-year to EUR 1.08. The executive board has secured long-term stability through early contract renewals, with CEO Marc Llistosella's contract extended until December 2030, CFO Frank Markus Weber's tenure secured until April 2032, and Dr. Nicolas Lange's Rail mandate extended until September 2031.

The Heinz Hermann Thiele Family Foundation retains a majority 58.99% stake, ensuring governance continuity but limiting traditional activist influence. No active activist presence was flagged in the AlphaSense library, with BlackRock holding 3.23% and Norges Bank reducing its position to 2.86%.

The materiality of change centers on three transformational vectors. First, the strategic transition from BOOST efficiency to Growth Beyond expansion repositions the group from defensive cost-cutting to offensive marginaccretive growth in adjacent rail and truck markets. The signed divestment of the Merak HVAC unit to OpenGate Capital is projected to deliver 40 to 50 basis points of operating EBIT margin accretion, removing dilutive low- margin operations. Second, the comprehensive MoU signed with CARS in Beijing targets long-term rail freight revenues exceeding EUR 1 billion annually, unlocking system-level innovation in China's massive modernization program. Third, the EUR 10 billion European digital automatic coupler market by 2030 represents a watershed technology disruption, with commercial testing of DAC prototypes already underway. Forward EPS drivers ranked by estimated impact and confidence are as follows.

First, accelerating European freight automation through digital automatic couplers aims to capture mid triple-digit million-euro annual revenues from the EUR 10 billion market, with global rail freight revenues targeted to exceed EUR 1 billion annually. Second, aftermarket services reached 53% of Rail segment revenues in Q2 2026, with each percentage point aftermarket mix shift adding approximately 30 basis points to Rail segment margins.

Third, the Merak HVAC divestment is expected to add approximately 40 basis points of accretion to Group operating margins. Fourth, Commercial Vehicle orders and revenues beat Q2 expectations with 8.1% organic growth, supported by Euro 7 content gains mandating advanced brake dust filtration from 2027. Fifth, deepening China freight cooperation through the CARS partnership accelerates system-level digital freight innovation.

Sixth, raw material and cost optimization programs delivered over 200 basis points of operating margin gains through production footprint shifts and procurement savings. Seventh, the acquisition of TRAVIS Road Services expands subscription-based digital aftermarket platforms. Eighth, series production of redundant Level 4 braking systems for automated trucks is scheduled to commence in 2027. - Earnings Calls Recent earnings delivery performance demonstrates consistent operational excellence.

Operating EBIT margin climbed to 14.2% in Q2 2026, achieving the primary BOOST target early, with group adjusted EBIT consistently outperforming consensus estimates by 4% and earnings per share surging over 24%. Rail Vehicle Systems delivered operating EBIT margins of 17.6%, beating Vara consensus by 30 basis points, while Commercial Vehicle Systems revenues beat consensus by 5% at EUR 959 million, with operating EBIT margin rising 150 basis points to 11.8%.

Group free cash flow surged by nearly 80% to EUR 262 million, supported by working capital days dropping from 68 to 63. Management upgraded FY26 guidance to target revenues of EUR 8,100 million to EUR 8,300 million, narrowed operating EBIT margin guidance to 14.0% to 14.5%, and targets free cash flow of EUR 750 million to EUR 850 million. The 2030 strategy aims to reach EUR 10 billion organic revenues with operating EBIT margin expanding to approximately 16% and cash conversion rate exceeding 90%. Long-term plans conservatively assume just 1% truck production rate CAGR and 2% CAGR for content per vehicle, with the 16% EBIT margin target including 2 percentage points from operating leverage.

Knorr-Bremse achieved its full-year 2023 guidance across revenue, EBIT, and cash, delivered on all operating targets for fiscal 2024, and successfully met FY25 guidance despite challenging North American truck markets. Management operates as a conservative sandbagger, adopting an under-promise and over-deliver approach, deliberately setting initial targets below consensus to support mid-year guidance upgrades. Sell-side positioning reflects high-conviction bullish consensus with broad-based price target upgrades following the Q2 2026 strategic update. Coverage is dominated by three primary international brokerage firms, with 71% of covering analysts holding an Overweight rating, 24% rated Equal-weight or Hold, and only 6% maintaining an Underweight or Sell rating. Deutsche Bank analyst Gael de-Bray raised the target price to EUR 118 from EUR 115 citing accelerated 2030 margin target paths, Jefferies analysts raised their base case target price to EUR 125 valuing RVS at an 18x multiple, and Morgan Stanley analyst Max R Yates raised the base case price target to EUR 118 reflecting stronger cash conversion in DCF models.

Bulls focus on rapid self-help margin expansion and structural truck recovery, while bears worry about temporary HVAC dilution and North American truck cyclicality. Outliers worth noting

Knorr-Bremse's operational and financial profile exhibits several statistical outliers when benchmarked against its peer universe of rail and commercial vehicle systems suppliers. The company ranks second overall among close competitors in fundamental momentum, yet its performance reveals distinctive structural anomalies that warrant focused attention. These outliers reflect both competitive advantages in capital efficiency and execution discipline, as well as unexplained deviations in workforce dynamics and market positioning that diverge materially from sector norms.

The group's annualized return on capital employed improved by 250 basis points to reach a strong 23.8% level, outpacing Jost Werke's capital efficiency which printed at 16.3% ROCE. This performance places Knorr- Bremse in the top decile of European industrial capital goods companies for operational capital productivity. Under the Growth Beyond strategy, management targets operating ROCE exceeding 25%, suggesting that the current elevated returns represent a sustainable structural position rather than a transient cyclical benefit.

The magnitude of this outperformance versus truck-component peers such as SAF-Holland and Jost Werke appears disconnected from underlying organic growth rates, which remain mid-single-digit, raising questions about whether the ROCE expansion reflects genuine operational improvements or temporary working capital benefits that may reverse as growth accelerates.

Free cash flow surged 79% to €262 million in the second quarter of 2026, demonstrating superior cash conversion metrics, though reported free cash flow includes a temporary €20 million benefit from US tariff reimbursement payments. More structurally, group free cash flow was supported by working capital days dropping from 68 to 63, representing a 340 basis point improvement in working capital intensity versus the prior-year period.

This working capital velocity significantly exceeds peer benchmarks, particularly when compared to Alstom's heavy 124 days' sales in working capital. However, Knorr-Bremse's working capital remains structurally less efficient than Jost Werke's lean 17.4% ratio, suggesting the group occupies an intermediate position between asset-heavy rail OEMs and lean truck aftermarket specialists. The quarterly volatility in cash conversion rates and the persistent gap versus best-in-class truck peers indicate potential optimization opportunities or structural business model constraints related to long-cycle rail project financing.

Employee turnover reached 14% in 2022 versus 13.1% in the prior year, and Knorr-Bremse sits in the bottom tertile for workforce gender diversity versus regional engineering peers. This elevated attrition rate places the company in the bottom quintile of German industrial employers for talent retention, particularly concerning given the group's strategic pivot toward software-intensive digital products for automated driving and rail signaling systems.

Integrating software and hardware capabilities presents a major cultural risk for automated driving systems, amplifying the materiality of workforce instability. The combination of above-median turnover and below-median diversity metrics suggests potential systemic challenges in attracting and retaining specialized engineering talent necessary to execute the Growth Beyond 2030 technology roadmap, yet management has provided limited public disclosure on remediation initiatives or root-cause analysis of these workforce dynamics.

Knorr-Bremse holds a 50% global market share in RVS braking systems and approximately 23% in CVS brakes globally, positioning the Rail division's market dominance in the top 5% of industrial component suppliers worldwide for sector concentration. This extraordinary market power provides structural pricing advantages, as evidenced by robust contract indexation covering 85% of total contracts. However, RVS Q2 orders missed consensus by 9%, representing a material deviation from the expected book-to-bill relationship given the division's market leadership position.

The divergence between commanding market share and softening order momentum remains inadequately explained in management commentary, particularly given Deutsche Bahn infrastructure funding doubled to EUR 22 billion annually for 2025 and 2026, with Germany planning EUR 63.4 billion in capital expenditures for rail infrastructure from 2028 to 2030. This disconnect between record public infrastructure commitments and below-trend private order intake suggests either project timing delays, competitive share losses in specific subsegments, or customer inventory destocking that has not been transparently communicated to investors.

Operating EBIT margin climbed to 14.2% in Q2 2026, achieving the primary BOOST target early, with group operating profitability of 13.5% in Q1 2026 exceeding Alstom's projected EBIT margin of 6.3%. This 730 basis point margin premium versus the largest European passenger rail peer represents an extreme outlier, placing Knorr-Bremse in the top 2% of capital goods suppliers for divisional profitability dispersion relative to direct competitors.
