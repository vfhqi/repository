Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding historical cyclical recoveries is crucial for predicting Billerud's earnings per share trajectory.
  2. The strategic shift in North America and structural wood cost inflation redefine Billerud's valuation multiples.
  3. Historical valuation cycles reveal that Nordic paper producers experience dramatic multiple expansion at earnings troughs.
  4. Rising consensus estimates during recoveries typically last twelve to eighteen months before cost inflation terminates expansion.

Framework of the Investigation

1.
Precise investigation: The central focus of this research is "How have the share prices, forward price-to- earnings and price-to-book multiples, and consensus estimates of Billerud AB (BILL-SE) and its Nordic and European peers (Stora Enso, Metsä Board, SCA, Holmen, Mondi, UPM-Kymmene, Mayr-Melnhof Karton and Smurfit Kappa, now Smurfit Westrock) behaved through past recoveries from an earnings trough, for example 2009 to 2010, 2013 to 2014, 2016 to 2017, 2020 to 2021 and the second half of 2023 to 2024: how long did the phase of rising estimates last in each case, what multiple did each stock trade on at the trough, in mid-recovery and at the peak, what ended each recovery (wood or pulp costs, new capacity, currency or demand), and how did each stock perform over the 6, 12 and 18 months after its first two consecutive quarterly earnings beats; and, for each year since 2018, how did the consensus forecast of EBITDA and earnings per share for the following fiscal year, as it stood in November, compare with the outturn, for Billerud and for each of these peers?"
2.
Strategic shift: Billerud is gradually evolving its North American operations under the Evolution program to transition from graphic paper into packaging materials. By upgrading the Escanaba and Quinnesec mills, the company aims to reduce exposure to declining graphic demand and improve utilization. This strategic redirection directly alters Billerud's long-term margin potential and risk profile, which ultimately determines its earnings per share.
3.
Valuation compression: Billerud historically traded at a premium to European paper and packaging peers, but its multiple has contracted due to product mix dilution. Post-acquisition of Verso, trading multiples have been discounted to reflect the lower-multiple graphic paper business in the sales mix. This research utilizes findings from over 50 relevant sources, covering Billerud and nine of its direct peers across five comparable past recovery cycles.

Cyclical Dynamics and Financial Profiles

4.
Cyclical sensitivity: European paper and packaging producers operate in a highly capital-intensive sector that is heavily exposed to structural supply and demand imbalances. During cyclical recoveries, operating leverage can drive significant margins and earnings expansion as mill operating rates recover. However, persistent industry overcapacity and high Nordic wood costs have historically threatened the sustainability of these earnings recoveries.
5.
Macro questions: This investigation is closely linked to critical macroeconomic questions, such as how macro factors impact packaging demand. Key industry uncertainties include whether necessary capacity rationalization occurs to balance European supply and demand. Additionally, the volatility of regional wood fiber and energy input costs remains a central factor shaping sector profitability.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some historical recovery periods did not result in expected multiple expansion because structural cost increases offset pricing power. For instance, the Swedish wood cost surge in 2022 and 2023 severely squeezed Billerud's profit margins even as product prices remained near peak levels. This shows that raw material cost cycles can decouple from demand cycles and disrupt typical valuation patterns.
7.
Other side of the story: While historical precedents suggest that cyclical recoveries lead to robust earnings and multiple expansion, structural shifts in the European forestry sector may prevent a return to historical averages. The permanent removal of Russian timber imports and increased regional competition have structurally raised Nordic pulpwood costs, potentially capping the mid-cycle EBITDA margin potential of Billerud and its peers. Consequently, the sector may remain in a value trap unless permanent capacity closures occur, regardless of demand improvements.

Section 3 – Catalysts, End of Recovery Drivers, and Post-Beat Performance

Section summary
  1. Severe raw material cost inflation and sudden demand slowdowns have historically terminated cyclical earnings recoveries for Billerud and its peers.
  2. Upward momentum in European pulp and wood costs has repeatedly offset paperboard price hikes, triggering down-cycles and compressing multiples.
  3. Post-beat stock performance is highly sensitive to regional wood cost dynamics and structural capacity additions across the European packaging sector.

Historical Termination Drivers

1.
Elevated fiber: Billerud's recovery following the 2009 financial crisis was ultimately terminated by severe wood cost inflation and falling pulp prices. Annual reported figures for 2011 show variable costs rose by SEK 243 mm, with wood costs accounting for SEK 212 mm of this increase. This cost surge compressed Billerud's fourthquarter operating margin to a low of 4% in late 2011. Consequently, the raw material cost spike offset positive packaging pricing gains, severely denting earnings per share.
2.
Subdued demand: Billerud's volume recovery in late 2011 was further pressured by destocking and market-related production shutdowns. Total sales volumes declined by 6% in the fourth quarter of 2011 due to shutdowns at the Gruvön mill. Customers aggressively reduced inventory levels as economic growth slowed across Europe. This combination of falling demand and price pressure abruptly ended the post-crisis expansion phase.
3.
New capacity: Stora Enso and its peer group faced a prolonged market imbalance during the 2013 to 2014 cycle. Billerud's annual reports reveal that linerboard prices fell gradually in 2014. This pricing erosion was driven primarily by new containerboard capacity entering the market. This added supply outpaced regional packaging demand, preventing sustained margin expansion.
4.
Muted volumes: Billerud's 2014 containerboard segment suffered from lower sales volumes despite favorable currency exchange rates. While the operating profit rose by 31% in 2014 due to cost controls, sales volumes remained suppressed. Increased market competition forced Billerud to underutilize its paper machine assets. This volume headwind eventually capped the recovery cycle and led to subsequent earnings contraction.
5.
Severe shortages: Billerud's 2016 to 2017 recovery cycle was terminated by a combination of severe wood shortages and operational issues. The company suffered from a regional pulpwood shortage in late 2017 and early 2018. This shortage reduced production volumes and forced Billerud to transport logs across long distances, adding significant logistics expenses. These supply issues lowered operational efficiency and hurt earnings.

Total fiber costs surged by SEK 530 mm in the third quarter of 2018 compared to the previous year, split equally between wood and market pulp. Additionally, caustic soda prices rose by 30% during the same period. This input cost inflation neutralized the positive pricing momentum Billerud achieved in its packaging segments.
