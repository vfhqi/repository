Outliers worth noting

The bear-case evidence against Beijer Alma reveals three statistical outliers that distinguish the company from its Swedish peer group, each representing a quantifiable deviation from sector norms that warrants further scrutiny. The first and most striking anomaly concerns capital allocation efficiency, where Beijer Alma's group return on incremental capital employed averaged a poor 9% across prior three, five, and seven-year durations, positioning the company as a material underperformer relative to premium Swedish serial acquirers.

This poor historic allocation stands out when compared to premium serial peers like Addtech, which compounded without leverage spikes, and suggests a structural weakness in the company's decentralized acquisition model that has persisted across multiple business cycles. The severity of this outlier is compounded by net debt to EBITDA rising from 0.6x in 2018 to 2.3x in 2024, indicating that management deployed increasingly aggressive leverage to achieve returns that dramatically lagged both cost of capital and peer benchmarks.

The second outlier emerges in the margin deterioration trajectory of the core Beijer Components division, where historical EBITA margins fell from a peak of 20% in 2021 to 15.9% in 2024 due to low-margin additions. This 490 basis point compression represents an unprecedented erosion for a supposedly defensive industrial compounder, particularly when peers maintained or expanded margins during the same period. The margin dilution was driven by the Alcomex acquisition, which printed lower-than-average margins and faced subsequent

US operational headwinds, demonstrating a systematic failure in due diligence and integration execution. The persistence of this margin drag across multiple quarters, despite restructuring efforts, suggests the problem is structural rather than cyclical and distinguishes Beijer Alma from peers who successfully maintained pricing discipline and operational leverage through similar macroeconomic conditions. The third statistical anomaly concerns executive transition volatility, where the company underwent two rapid CEO transitions between early 2025 and January 2026, creating a level of leadership instability that is highly unusual for Swedish industrial compounders.

Permanent successor Oscar Fredell departed within two months of joining, forcing acting CEO Johnny Alvarsson to return, revealing either profound cultural dysfunction or strategic misalignment at the board level. While peer companies experienced occasional leadership changes, the velocity and abrupt nature of Beijer Alma's transitions represent a clear outlier that introduces execution risk precisely when the company needs stable stewardship to navigate margin recovery and capital allocation discipline.

This pattern becomes even more concerning when viewed alongside long-time CFO Johan Dufvenmark leaving in late 2024, suggesting a broader exodus of institutional knowledge during a critical operational inflection point.

Plausible problems to monitor

Sustained margin dilution in the core Beijer Components division constitutes a third structural weakness, with acquisitions consistently diluting operating margins within the former Lesjöfors business, driving historical EBITA margins down from a peak of 20% in 2021 to 15.9% in 2024. The margin recovery to 20% remains the most load-bearing pillar of the investment thesis, as any failure to rebound severely invalidates the compounder valuation multiple. [Cross-ref: Section 3 + Section 6 + Section 11] 361 Pro-cyclical hedging architecture emerges as a fourth operational vulnerability, where the formulaic rolling hedging policy acts pro-cyclically by locking in unfavorable exchange rates at cyclical peaks during demand downturns, while simultaneously delaying the positive earnings impact of favorable spot rate movements as cyclical recovery begins.

Beijer Alma stands as an active hedging outlier among Swedish serial acquirers, systematically hedging transaction flows up to 18 months, whereas peers predominantly rely on operational natural hedges and pricing adjustments. [Cross-ref: Section 19 + Section 21 + Section 23] 363

Elevated translation exposure dominance represents a fifth cross-cutting weakness, with translation risk overwhelming transaction exposure in absolute accounting sensitivity, registering MSEK 6 impact on profit before tax for every 1% fluctuation in the Swedish Krona. Foreign exchange acted as a negative 3% drag on group net revenue growth during full-year 2025, with broker forecasts trimming near-term adjusted earnings estimates to reflect persistent foreign exchange translation drag through fiscal 2026. A stronger Krona compounds the 77% general industrial demand cyclicality by shrinking translated export revenues from core German markets.
