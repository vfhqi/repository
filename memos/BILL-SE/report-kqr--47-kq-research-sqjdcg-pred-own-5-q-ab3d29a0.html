Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. This section defines the Key Question evaluating Billerud's potential European restructuring and its critical implications for the company's long-term profitability and capital allocation.
  2. Billerud faces a medium probability of European capacity closures or asset swaps in 2027 and 2028 because persistent overcapacity and elevated wood costs depress regional margins.
  3. Our confidence in this assessment is medium, based on Billerud's official evaluation of strategic options and aggressive capacity rationalization by its Nordic peers.

Defining the Strategic Inquiry

1.
Critical inquiry: "How likely is Billerud AB (BILL-SE) to announce a permanent European capacity closure, asset swap or consolidation step in 2027 and 2028, which mills or machines are most exposed (for example Karlsborg and Pietarsaari, named by Jefferies in September 2023, and cartonboard machines), what would such a step cost and add, what precedents exist (Metsä Board Tako, Stora Enso Skutskär, Smurfit Westrock Birmingham, Reno de Medici Barcelona, UPM), and how does the departure of the EVP Legal & Strategic Projects at the end of 2026 and the Mayr-Melnhof Karton packaging sale (November 2026) bear on it?"
2.
Strategic division: Billerud divides its business into Region Europe and Region North America. Region Europe produces virgin-fibre paper and packaging materials across six mills, but faces severe profitability pressure from high wood costs and cartonboard overcapacity. Addressing these asset challenges is crucial as Region Europe targets EBITDA of SEK 4.3 bn to SEK 4.8 bn, which is currently questioned by analysts.
3.
Extensive database: The scope of this research covers a total of 12 distinct companies, 5 historical precedents, and 24 relevant sources. This exhaustive study analyzes Billerud alongside competitors like Stora Enso, UPM, and Metsä Board. Evaluating these historical actions provides a reliable framework to project the financial and strategic path for Billerud.

Assessing Earnings and Cost Dynamics

4.
Financial leverage: Resolving Billerud's capacity questions is estimated to severely impact earnings per share over the next 18 months to 3 years. Implementing mill closures or swaps would trigger immediate restructuring cash costs and asset write-downs. However, eliminating high-cost capacity like Pietarsaari or Karlsborg could improve long-term margins and significantly support EPS recovery.
5.
Capital priority: Billerud's capital allocation focuses on completing its US Evolution investment program with SEK 500 mm remaining in 2027. Beyond 2027, regional capital spending is guided to return to a base level of approximately SEK 2.2 bn. Strategic capacity rationalization would avoid substantial future maintenance capital expenditures and free up capital for shareholder distributions.
6.

Operating with this volume deficit is equivalent to running an entire mid-sized paper machine idle. This structural underutilization severely degrades fixed cost absorption across the European manufacturing network.
7.
Related challenges: Closely aligned with the primary query are questions regarding European wood fiber availability and the execution of the North American conversion. Specifically, investors must evaluate if Billerud can secure cost-competitive pulpwood without reducing its production footprint. Another critical issue is how the European packaging market will absorb new containerboard capacity from industry peers.

Section 3 – Mill and Machine Exposure: Karlsborg, Pietarsaari, and Cartonboard

Section summary

High Exposure Nordic Assets

1.
Vulnerable Nordic assets: Jefferies research from September 2023 conservatively assigned zero value to Billerud's Karlsborg and Pietarsaari mills to calculate the replacement value of Region Europe's assets. This valuation method highlighted the structural vulnerability of these two specific facilities under conditions of elevated Nordic wood costs. The analysis suggested that closing these assets would help optimize the regional cost curve and support long-term earnings per share.
2.
High integration advantages: Billerud's Karlsborg mill in Sweden operates with 335,000 tonnes of capacity and utilizes a highly integrated pulp structure. The facility houses a first-cost quartile bleached sack paper machine that secures Billerud's leading global market position. Jefferies noted in September 2023 that closing Karlsborg is highly unlikely because Billerud would be forced to shift these profitable niche volumes to other manufacturing sites.
3.
Nonintegrated cost pressures: Billerud's Pietarsaari mill in Finland possesses 200,000 tonnes of capacity dedicated entirely to producing sack and specialty kraft paper. This asset is highly exposed because it lacks integrated pulp production and must purchase its raw fiber at market rates. Jefferies indicated in September 2023 that the lack of pulp integration typically results in a higher cost structure compared to Billerud's integrated Swedish facilities.
4.
Peer production downtime: Peer company UPM-Kymmene's management of its own 800,000 tonnes Pietarsaari pulp mill illustrates the severe margin pressures in the region. Jefferies reported in June 2026 that UPM planned temporary downtime at Pietarsaari to optimize production levels and wood sourcing. This comparable past case suggests that Billerud's non-integrated paper mill at the same location faces prolonged high operating cost risks during market downturns.
5.
Softwood capacity shutdowns: Peer competitor Stora Enso addressed uncompetitive Nordic fiber lines by permanently closing 100,000 tonnes of softwood pulp production at its Skutskär mill. Morgan Stanley reported in May 2026 that this decision was driven by negative margins and elevated Swedish wood costs. This industry action indicates that Nordic producers must permanently retire unprofitable capacity to protect their corporate profit margins.

This persistent overcapacity means Billerud's consumerboard segment, which represents 7% of its sales substrate, cannot achieve normal operating rates without significant industry capacity rationalization.
7.

This uncompetitive position is driven by high Nordic wood costs and unfavorable foreign exchange movements between the Swedish Krona and the Euro. Peer companies are facing similar pressures, with Stora Enso experiencing disadvantages in liquid packaging board due to its high-cost Finnish mill orientation.
