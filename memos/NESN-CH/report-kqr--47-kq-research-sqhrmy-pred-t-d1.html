Part 1: Core Analytical Questions and Sector-Wide Synthesis

1.
Growth Sustainability: Achieving $\ge$ 2% Real Internal Growth (RIG) Sustaining real internal growth (volume and mix) of at least 2.0% annually–the medium-term target for a mature food group like Nestlé–becomes exceptionally difficult when mechanical pricing tailwinds fade and pricing falls toward 1.0%. Evidence from peer precedents indicates that volume recovery is highly non-linear and extremely sensitive to price-volume elasticity. For example:
2.
Elasticity Shockwaves: During retail price negotiations in late 2025, JDE Peet's experienced high retailer elasticities of -0.4x to -1.0x in Europe, resulting in a dramatic 13.0% volume decline in January 2025.
3.
Pricing Give-backs: Mondelez was forced to selectively lower European chocolate pricing by -3.7% in Q3 2026 to restore volume penetration and regain lost market share.
4.
Transitory Volatility: Nestlé’s sequential RIG acceleration to +2.8% in Q3 2026 (up from +1.2% in Q1 and +1.8% in Q2) was heavily supported by temporary factors, including a European heatwave that boosted Waters and transitory confectionery phasing.
5.
Reasonable Inference: Once mechanical tailwinds pass, a baseline of 2.0% RIG cannot be sustained passively. It requires continuous, active promotional and price reinvestments, which structurally limits near-term margin expansion unless compensated by massive gross efficiencies.
6.
The Operating Margin Squeeze: Raising UTOP to 17%+ Concurrent with Higher A&M Nestlé aims to expand its Underlying Trading Operating Profit (UTOP) margin from 16.1% (2025) to a 17.0%+ medium-term target while simultaneously increasing advertising and marketing (A&M) spend from 8.6% of sales (2025) to 8.9% (H1 2026) and beyond. Peer precedents show that raising margins while increasing brand reinvestment is a high-wire act:
7.
The Reinvestment Trap: Colgate-Palmolive successfully expanded its gross margin to 60.3% in Q3 2026 via its Funding-the-Growth program, but was forced to immediately reinvest these savings into global brand equity, driving double-digit increases in advertising (which reached 14.4% of net sales) and compressing segment-level margins. 5 10Q The Structural Reset: Nestlé’s own history of margin targets reveals a series of resets: 17.5% to 18.5% (set in 2017), followed by an 18.5% ambition, which was ultimately reset to "at or above 16%" for 2025 at its November 2024 Capital Markets Day (CMD).
8.
Reasonable Inference: A simultaneous expansion of operating margins and A&M is only plausible if a company can capture structural, non-consumer-facing overhead savings (such as white-collar headcount reductions or back-office centralization) rather than cutting raw material quality or trade support. Part 2: Corporate Core Strengths: Demonstrated vs. Needed

To execute its turnaround, Nestlé must leverage its existing structural strengths while systematically building or acquiring capabilities it currently lacks.

Demonstrated Organizational Strengths Procurement Scale: Demonstrated by JDE Peet's (sourcing ~8.0% of global coffee production to secure pricing leverage) Procurement Scale (cont.): and Mondelez (navigating a massive 500,000-ton cocoa surplus to optimize margins).

1.
Emerging-Market Distribution: Demonstrated by Colgate-Palmolive (45.0% of sales in emerging markets with resilient, double-digit pricing power) Emerging-Market Distribution (cont.): and Unilever (where massive EM momentum successfully offset developed-market declines in Q3 2026). Petcare Science and Therapeutic Innovation: Demonstrated by Colgate’s Hill's Pet Nutrition, which grew 5.7% (6.4% ex-private label exits) by scaling Science Diet and Prescription Diet therapeutic lines. Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) and Owned Logistics: Demonstrated by Keurig Dr Pepper’s owned Direct Store Delivery (DSD) network, which serves as a structural shield against third-party logistics cost inflation. Medical and Specialized Nutrition Barriers: Demonstrated by Danone, where 55.0% of total group profits are protected inside high-barrier Specialized Nutrition (such as Kate Farms and gut health formats), isolating the business from private-label competition. Strengths Nestlé Needs to Acquire or Build Bargaining Power with European Retail Alliances: Consolidated buying groups hold immense scale, pushing private-label penetration in European food categories to 40%–45%, which limits a supplier's ability to pass through costs and increases the risk of severe retail delistings.
2.
Performance-Driven Cost Culture: Needs to be built inside Nestlé to mirror the strict zero-based budgeting and cost-containment models utilized by JAB Holding and 3G Capital (which successfully unlocked massive productivity gains at ABInBev and are being applied to secure Peet's €500m savings program). & Co Redburn Digital Commerce and Retail-Media Execution: Demonstrated as a needed capability by General Mills (which historically ranked in the bottom 10% of CPGs in digital capabilities and is actively leveraging DTC board expertise to optimize search performance and online assortments) and Colgate-Palmolive (which utilized its connection-led marketing model to drive India e-commerce sales at gross margins 400 bps higher than company averages).
