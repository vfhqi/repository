EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 finding

Naturenergie holding AG operates a dual-model business balancing a low-risk regulated German grid monopoly against highly volatile Swiss hydropower generation, under consolidated leadership and 67% EnBW control, though massive grid capex requirements could depress free cash flow if regulatory returns fail to outpace inflation. The financial profile reveals stable regulated earnings and strong return on capital, with ROCE reaching 19.6% in 2025 and an equity ratio of 66.0%, despite net revenue declining 8.8% to €1,558 million and reported EBIT falling to €214 million.

The German and Swiss energy sectors face unprecedented network expansion demand growing at a 10% CAGR through 2030, while municipal partnerships act as critical defensive moats securing 37 partners, though EnBW's dominant 67% shareholding restricts free-float liquidity.

Business model and operational structure

Regulated grid operator naturenergie netze GmbH manages 13,120 kilometers of distribution lines in South Baden, providing system-relevant infrastructure. Renewable generation consists of 642 MW of installed capacity focused on sustainable hydro and solar assets across Switzerland and Germany. The retail business serves 226,378 private and commercial accounts, with all commercial customers automatically migrated to 100% green power. Leadership transition occurred in October 2024 when long-time executive Michel Schwery replaced Dr. Jörg Reichert as CEO. The naturenergie vernetzt program includes 37 municipal partners, with 50% to 60% of total capex dedicated to grid expansion.

5

Financial performance and capital allocation

Net revenue reached €1,558 million in 2025, representing an 8.8% decline from €1,708 million in 2024 after peaking at €1,966 million in 2023. Reported EBIT declined to €214 million in 2025 from €224 million in 2024, while Adjusted EBIT stood at €209 million and €211 million respectively. Return on Capital Employed contracted to 19.6% in 2025 from 21.5% in 2024 due to expanding capital base, compared to just 10.7% in 2021.

Gross capital expenditure rose to €174 million in 2025 from €147 million in 2024, reflecting aggressive grid infrastructure investment. Free cash flow reached €91.1 million in 2025, dropping from €115.3 million in 2024 and €199 million in 2021 on high capex. The company maintained a solid equity ratio of 66.0% representing €1,340 million in equity, while net financial assets rose to €184 million.

Competitive position and structural moats

The broader grid sector is projected to expand at a 10% CAGR through 2030, dramatically exceeding regional GDP growth rates. The naturenergie vernetzt program enables communities to actively shape local grid topics, growing from 35 to 37 municipal partners with €500,000 annually distributed for climate projects. EnBW holds 67% of outstanding shares with board representation overlap, while NEAG uses EnBW's framework agreements to lower procurement costs.

Upstream renewable generation captures a 30% EBIT margin depending on hydrological volumes, though wholesale energy prices dropped 26% and squeezed upstream profits. Distribution grid operations capture a 11.84% EBIT margin via regulated municipal concessions, though regulators reduced the distribution grid WACC to 3.98%.

Customer dynamics and channel strategies

Retail contracts expanded to 226,378 in H1 2026, while business customer contracts contracted sharply by 13.6% to 58,551 accounts. Total energy sales volume slipped by 9.8% to 3,362 million kWh during H1 2026, with German Hochrhein sales falling 28.7% but Swiss Valais growing 19.4%. The municipal network retained a 100% renewal rate across critical regional concession bids, with Immendingen and Honstetten signing twentyyear contracts starting January 2027. Digital customer engagement scaled to 52,000 registered portal users in 2025, with over 12,000 opting for paperless electronic communication. The group acquired 100% of bacher & schmidt Elektro AG in May 2026 and Studer Söhne Elektro AG in 2023 to strengthen Swiss installation capacity.

Operator quality and foundation verdict

Capital returns demonstrate exceptional performance with ROCE reaching 19.6% in 2025, expanding from just 10.7% in 2021 before the pricing upcycle. The company projects total investments of €1.4 billion over 2022 to 2030, with net investment projected to increase significantly as grid infrastructure expands. Free cash flow reached €91.1 million in 2025, though massive ongoing capital expenditure plans could severely depress future cash flow if power price deflation continues. Balance sheet strength remains robust with a 66.0% equity ratio and net financial assets of €184 million providing deep defensive buffers. Technology initiatives include pioneering 1 MW hydrogen plant in Wyhlen being expanded by 5 MW and an 8 MW lithium battery at Ackersand.
