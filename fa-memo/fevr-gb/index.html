The reasoning behind the inputs

Three conventions the model must apply, or the forecasts will be wrong:

  1. Revenue is brand revenue (the company's "adjusted revenue"), not FactSet's statutory revenue. The FY2025 base is £375.3m (statutory £325m). Half-year bases: H1 2025 about £171.6m, H2 2025 about £203.7m (our calculation from H1 2026 brand revenue of £183.6m, about +7% reported). Applying these growth rates to £325m would understate revenue by about 13% every year.
  2. The EBITDA margin is the company's adjusted EBITDA (before share-based payment and exceptional items) over brand revenue, the basis of guidance (£49.9m for FY2026) and of FactSet consensus (£49.9m, £63.9m, £77.5m). FY2025 base: £42.4m, 11.3%; H1 2025 about £18.4m, H2 2025 about £24.0m.
  3. The "Depreciation and amortisation" input includes the share-based payment charge (FY2026-30: £6.0m, £6.2m, £6.5m, £6.7m, £7.0m) on top of depreciation and amortisation excluding acquired intangibles (£5.5m, £6.0m, £6.3m, £6.5m, £6.8m). The brokers' EBIT and normalised EPS carry share-based payment as a cost, so this makes the model's EBIT and EPS comparable with consensus while its EBITDA stays comparable with guidance; because share-based payment is non-cash, cash flow is unaffected.

Per-share figures are in pounds (0.177 = 17.7p), as in the FactSet financials block.

The operating drivers behind the inputs

  • US revenue (the swing line): a distribution step under Molson Coors (400 distributors, 500,000 accounts, sales teams incentivised on Fevertree volume in 2026) plus the first national campaign (April 2026). Circana off-trade +6% (Q1), +11% (Q2), +16% (July-August 2026), but tracked channels are 35-40% of US sales and H1 shipments (+11% cc) ran slightly ahead of tracked takeaway. We take +15% for H2 2026, +12% for FY2027 (the step continues; the campaign laps in April 2027), then fading to +8% by FY2030 as the step saturates. US price/mix −1% to 0 while cans and eight-packs grow.
  • US EBITDA (Fevertree's royalty): profits shared broadly equally at EBITDA, with an undisclosed absolute minimum royalty for 2026-30 that steps up and, per Deutsche Bank (6-Oct-26), delivers profit consistent with full localisation even though only cans localise in 2027. Drivers: tariffs gone from H2 2026 (about £2.5m a year to Fevertree; one-off refund about £3.5m in H2 2026), can localisation in 2027 (about £2.5-3m), partial marketing normalisation from about 20% to about 15% of US revenue by FY2028 (about £4m; Redburn models 11%), and volume. We take £20m (FY2027) and £27m (FY2028), between Jefferies (about £20m) and Deutsche Bank (about £29m).

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
