Section 2 – Effective Tax Rate and Drivers

Section summary
  1. Kainos's effective tax rate remains anchored above the UK statutory rate of 25% due to higher-tax overseas jurisdictions, despite temporary H1 FY2027 relief. Sustainable tax structures are heavily dependent on share option deferred tax credits and geographic earnings mix rather than structural tax planning.

Baseline Tax Exposures

1.
Elevated tax profile: Kainos Group plc reported an effective tax rate of 27% in its 2026 annual report, matching the 27% rate from the previous fiscal year. This baseline rate exceeds the standard UK corporation tax rate of 25% because of higher foreign tax jurisdictions. Consequently, the company's geographical footprint acts as a structural drag on adjusted earnings per share.
2.
Historical tax progression: Kainos's ETR stood at 23% in FY2023 when the UK statutory corporation tax rate was 19%. The subsequent rise in the UK statutory rate to 25% and an expanding US presence drove the rate up to 27% in FY2025. This history demonstrates that the group's tax baseline is sensitive to UK legislative shifts as well as international geographic expansions.
3.
Neutral tax impact: Kainos operates in jurisdictions where the effective tax rate generally exceeds the 15% global minimum tax threshold under OECD Pillar Two rules. This matches peer trends, as Computacenter plc estimated that its effective tax rates exceed 15% in all material jurisdictions and does not expect any material impact on its ETR from Pillar Two. Therefore, global minimum tax regulations represent a neutral factor for Kainos's future effective tax rate.
4.
Comparative peer benchmarks: Kainos's tax rate is high relative to UK IT services peer Bytes Technology Group plc, which reported a full-year effective tax rate of 26.6% in May 2026. Similarly, Softcat plc recorded a total tax charge of £52.5m on profit before tax of £202.8m for the year ended July 31, 2026, which represents an effective rate of 25.89% (calculated). These peer figures indicate that Kainos faces a slightly higher tax burden than peers with lower international software operations.
5.
Global peer pressures: European IT peer Computacenter plc recorded an adjusted effective tax rate of 30.8% in 2025, up from 29.3% in 2024, due to profits shifting to Germany and the United States. Meanwhile, Italian peer Reply SpA experienced a tax rate of 31.8% in 2024, compared to 30.6% in 2023. These comparisons show that broader geographic expansion inevitably drives effective tax rates higher across the European IT services landscape.

R&D Credits and Patent Box

6.
Corrected credit cashflows: Kainos restated its H1 FY2026 cash flow statement to properly account for £2.1m of Research & Development Expenditure Credits (RDEC) as an operating cash flow adjustment. For the full year, the company recognised RDEC income of £6.0m in FY2026, up from £5.1m in FY2025. Although these credits support cash inflows, they do not fully offset the corporation tax increases implemented in the UK.

Overseas Profit and Geography Mix

9.
Adverse tax drag: Kainos's international operations, particularly in North America, create upward pressure on the group's effective tax rate. The company noted that its ETR remains above the UK rate of 25% due to higher statutory corporate tax rates in the United States and non-deductible acquisition expenses in Canada. Consequently, any accelerated growth in US Workday Services will exacerbate this negative tax mix effect.
10.
Geographic expansion dynamics: Kainos announced in December 2025 that it is expanding its Workday Services operations in Poland to support Workday's growing European customer base. Poland provides a highly skilled workforce, but the regional expansion shifts the geographic profit distribution. Because foreign tax jurisdictions impose varying rates, this expansion will complicate the future global tax rate reconciliation.
11.
Volatile regional taxes: Kainos's geographic mix exposes its bottom line to the differing corporate tax environments of its operating subsidiaries. In contrast, peer Reply SpA operates under Italian tax laws with a theoretical corporate tax rate of 24%, which rises to 31.8% once regional taxes and foreign rate differentials are applied. This highlights that geographic profit shifts are the primary driver of tax rate volatility for expanding IT providers.

H1 FY2027 Tax Structures

12.
Temporary interim benefits: Kainos reported a lower effective tax rate of 24% for the six months ended September 30, 2026, down from 27% in the prior year period. Management clarified that this decrease reflects a higher deferred tax credit arising from the increase in the company's share price. This temporary share option benefit was what was meant by "efficient tax structures," meaning it is highly vulnerable to equity market volatility.
13.
Contrasting deferred taxes: Kainos's share option deferred tax impact contrasts with Bytes Technology, where a reduction in deferred tax asset value relating to outstanding share options actually pushed their tax rate to 26.6%. Similarly, Softcat plc recognised deferred tax movements on share options of £3.0m in FY2026, illustrating how equity scheme accounting drives volatility in effective tax rates. This confirms that share price fluctuations remain a key swing factor for IT consulting tax rates.
14.
Stable tax expectations: Kainos's management previously guided in November 2024 that a 26% tax rate was a reasonable, maintainable level for the group. This maintainable baseline is slightly higher than Softcat's calculated 25.89% rate for 2026. Consequently, Kainos's long-term tax rate expectations are consistent with standard UK-centric peers despite temporary interim dips.
15.
Normalising tax expectations: Kainos expects its full-year effective tax rate for the year ending March 31, 2027, to normalize at 26%, which is higher than the UK statutory rate of 25%. Management guided that the rate remains elevated primarily due to the impact of higher tax rates in North America. Because the interim 24% rate was driven by a temporary share price deferred tax credit, the lower rate is unlikely to persist long-term.
16.
Other side of the story: Sustained operational delivery and double-digit growth in high-margin Workday Products could easily offset minor tax rate headwinds. For instance, Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 9, 2026, that Kainos's H1 FY2027 revenue grew 37% to £268.8m, demonstrating massive commercial momentum. If the business continues to scale rapidly, marginal changes in the effective tax rate will have a negligible impact on long-term EPS progression.

Section 3 – Share Count, Buybacks, and Dilution

Section summary
  1. Kainos completed ninety million pounds of share buybacks to compress its equity base, but further repurchases are paused to preserve cash.
  2. Employee share schemes introduce persistent dilution that offsets buyback gains, while peer consultancies favor progressive dividends over active share repurchases.

Buyback History and Equity Impact

1.
Substantial equity contraction: Kainos completed its third consecutive share buyback program on May 15, 2026, returning a cumulative £90.0 million to shareholders since November 2024. The final phase of this buyback resulted in the purchase of 3,729,068 shares for £30.2 million, including transaction costs. This execution successfully reduced issued ordinary shares to 116,263,722 by September 30, 2026. This program permanently reduced the group share count to bolster earnings per share.
2.
Falling average sharecount: Kainos's share buybacks significantly lowered the weighted average number of basic ordinary shares to 115.6 million for the six months ended September 30, 2026, compared to 121.1 million in the prior period. This reduction directly boosted adjusted basic earnings per share, which rose 45% to 27.7 pence in the same period. This shows how repurchases magnify bottom line earnings growth during periods of strong operational execution.
3.
Contrary evidence: Kainos's share buyback program was completely paused in May 2026 as cash reserves were prioritized for other business needs. Deutsche Bank Research noted on May 18, 2026, that the group's cash reserves of £89.0 million would be retained for office construction rather than additional buybacks. This pause halts the near term EPS accretion that investors experienced over the preceding eighteen months.
4.
Prudent capital preservation: Kainos chose to complete its buyback program to maintain a robust and debt-free balance sheet. Management reported in their November 2025 earnings call that the primary allocation of capital must always support organic growth rather than continuous share repurchases. Consequently, the group is prioritizing physical infrastructure development over immediate returns of surplus capital to institutional equity holders.

Conditions for Buyback Restart

5.
Explicit restart parameters: Kainos's board stated that any future purchase of own shares would only be executed if the directors believe it improves earnings per share and serves the best interests of shareholders. Furthermore, the company requires surplus cash over and above the capital needed to fund organic growth and targeted acquisitions. Therefore, a restart is unlikely until current capital expenditure obligations are satisfied and cash conversion improves.
6.
Severe cash constraints: Kainos experienced a decline in its period-end cash and treasury deposits to £86.5 million at September 30, 2026, down from £105.5 million a year earlier. This cash reduction was driven by £12.5 million of buybacks in H1 FY27 and £7.1 million of cash outflows for building the new Belfast headquarters. This cash drain constrains the board strategic flexibility to approve a new buyback program before late FY2028.
7.
Demanding working capital: Kainos requires a minimum working capital cushion of £20.0 million to £25.0 million to protect the group against operational shocks. Operating cash flow is also under pressure because the group's massive revenue growth in H1 FY27 has expanded trade receivables and accrued income by 10% to £91.9 million. This operational cash requirement prevents the recycling of capital into near-term equity repurchases.
8.
Heavy headquarter outlays: Kainos has committed substantial financial resources to its new Belfast headquarters at One Bankmore Place, with capital commitments reaching £34.3 million. Management expects to spend in the high £20 millions on this development, which directly consumes free cash flow. This capital intensity means a buyback renewal remains highly dependent on completing the physical office construction.

Dilution from Employee Schemes

9.
Ongoing option dilution: Kainos's employee incentive schemes continue to introduce new shares that dilute the ownership of existing shareholders. The company issued 160,462 ordinary shares in H1 FY27 due to the exercise of vested employee options, at a weighted average price of £1.06 per share. These issuances partially offset the share count contraction achieved by the board's completed buyback initiatives.
10.
Persistent trust holdings: Kainos utilizes an Employee Benefit Trust to hold shares for the UK Share Incentive Plan and the Irish Restricted Share Scheme, with 983,000 shares held in trust at September 30, 2026. These share awards must be held in trust until specific vesting and service conditions are met over three to five years. This equity-settled compensation structure creates a steady dilutive drag on the group's diluted earnings per share.
11.
Post acquisition equity awards: Kainos issued 47,411 ordinary shares in H1 FY27 for post-acquisition remuneration to employees staying after the Davis Pier business combination. Under the deal's structure, 30% of the total acquisition cost is held back and expensed as compensation over three years to prevent perverse incentives. This post-acquisition remuneration amplifies the dilutive share issuance beyond standard employee option plans.
12.
Diluted sharecount buffer: Kainos's basic share count of 115.6 million in H1 FY27 expands to a weighted average diluted share count of 117.2 million after adjusting for outstanding share options. At September 30, 2026, the company also excluded 546,158 options from the dilution calculation because their effect was antidilutive. This indicates that future share price appreciation will increase the dilutive impact of outstanding employee options.

Section 4 – Adjusting Items and Share-Based Payments

Section summary
  1. Kainos's operating profit adjustments reveal a widening gap between statutory and adjusted performance driven by rising share-based compensation and post-acquisition remuneration.
  2. Peer comparisons with Endava, Netcompany, and Made Tech highlight that adjusting items are highly standard in IT consulting but vary in dilution risk.

Reconciling Statutory and Adjusted Profits

1.
Widening profit gap: Kainos reported a substantial difference between its adjusted pre-tax profit of sixty-seven point one million pounds and its statutory pre-tax profit of fifty-eight point one million pounds in fiscal year 2026. This discrepancy of nine point zero million pounds was driven by share-based payment costs, amortisation of acquired intangibles, and post-combination services compensation. This variance indicates that statutory measures do not fully reflect the underlying business performance as presented by management.
2.
Consistent adjustment policy: Kainos has maintained a highly uniform methodology for calculating its adjusted profit before tax across multiple reporting periods. The company systematically excludes non-cash items and non-recurring expenses such as restructuring costs and acquisition expenses to aid period-on-period comparability. Consequently, these adjustments allow investors to facilitate closer comparisons with peer companies that utilize similar adjusted metrics.
3.
Interim profit variance: Kainos's statutory profit before tax for the six months ended September 30, 2026, rose twenty-six percent to thirty-five point eight million pounds. However, adjusted pre-tax profit increased thirty percent to forty-one point seven million pounds after adding back five point nine million pounds of adjusting items. This variance shows that the gap between reported and adjusted earnings continues to expand during periods of rapid growth.

Share Based Compensation Trends

4.
Rising compensation expenses: Kainos recorded share-based payment expenses of five point four million pounds in fiscal year 2026, representing a slight decrease from five point nine million pounds in the previous year. In the first half of fiscal year 2027, these expenses accelerated to three point eight million pounds, compared to two point nine million pounds in the prior period. This upward trajectory suggests that equity-settled compensation will remain a persistent headwind for statutory earnings per share.
5.
Market value dependencies: Kainos structures its share incentive plans as both equity-settled and cash-settled schemes for employees. The financial charge from these schemes is heavily influenced by factors outside management's control, such as share price volatility and option forfeiture rates. As a result, sudden equity market fluctuations can introduce significant statutory profit volatility despite stable core trading.

Accounting for the Davis Pier Acquisition

6.
Deferred consideration structures: Kainos completed the acquisition of Canadian consultancy Davis Pier on September 15, 2025, for a total consideration of eleven point two million pounds. The purchase was satisfied by seven point four million pounds in cash and three point eight million pounds in ordinary shares. Under the terms of the transaction, thirty percent of the purchase cost is treated as post-combination remuneration rather than capitalised goodwill.
7.
Post combination pay impact: Kainos recognized a post-combination services charge of two point one million pounds in fiscal year 2026, which rose from zero point nine million pounds in fiscal year 2025. For the first half of fiscal year 2027, this charge reached one point four million pounds, compared to zero point three million pounds in the prior year period. These service-conditioned payouts will continue to depress statutory profits through September 2028.
8.
Strategic alignment choices: Kainos's management structured the Davis Pier deal to align staff incentives and prevent perverse behaviors post-acquisition. Although international financial reporting standards dictate that these contingent payments must be expensed through the income statement, management views them as an economic cost of acquisition. Therefore, excluding these charges from adjusted profits helps preserve comparability with previous organic investments made by the group.

However, the business contributed a net loss of zero point one million pounds for the period ending March 31, 2026, despite generating four point six million pounds in revenue. This negative contribution indicates that acquisition integration can temporarily depress group-level profit margins.

Comparative Peer Adjustment Trends

10.
Consultancy metric variances: Made Tech Group plc reported a pre-tax profit of four point seven million pounds and share-based payments of zero point eighteen million pounds for the year ended May 31, 2026. The peer's adjusted EBITDA calculations also add back depreciation, amortisation, and exceptional items to present underlying performance. This lower reliance on share-based pay makes Made Tech's statutory earnings higher quality than peers with larger equity incentive schemes.

Morgan Stanley Research highlighted that Netcompany's earnings quality has been on a deteriorating trend due to these restructuring and M&A integration charges. These special adjustments suggest that heavy restructuring can distort true profitability for large European digital service providers.
12.
Massive equity dilution: Endava plc reported a loss before tax of eight point five million pounds and share-based compensation of seven point seven million pounds for Q1 fiscal year 2026. The group regularly excludes share-based compensation, amortisation of acquired intangibles, and contingent consideration movements to calculate its adjusted pre-tax profit. This highlights that Endava relies far more on adjusted metrics to mask statutory losses compared to Kainos.

Long Term Adjustments Outlook

13.
Other side of the story: Kainos's practice of adjusting out share-based payments and acquisition remuneration may obscure the true cost of doing business and inflate perceived earnings power. Section 5 – Cash Balance Interest, Capital Expenditure, and Currency Exposure

Section summary
  1. Kainos's interest income on cash remains sensitive to central bank interest rate cuts, which act as a headwind to pre-tax profitability.
  2. Significant capital expenditure on the One Bankmore Place headquarters is reallocating cash into property assets, which increases future depreciation charges and introduces rental leasing opportunities.
  3. Unhedged foreign currency revenues introduce earnings volatility, while progressive dividend payouts are maintained at sustainable levels alongside peer distributions.

Interest Sensitivity on Cash

1.
Declining cash reserves: Kainos Group plc reported that its period-end cash and treasury deposits decreased to eighty-six point five million pounds as of September thirty, 2026. This cash depletion was driven by ninety million pounds of cumulative share buybacks and ongoing capital outlays. Consequently, the reduced balance directly limits the company's interest-earning potential in a falling rate environment.
2.
Sensitive interest earnings: Kainos's bank deposit interest income fell to three point seven million pounds for the fiscal year ended March thirty-one, 2026, compared to six point four million pounds in the prior period. The group's sensitivity model shows that a one percent decrease in interest rates would reduce pre-tax profits by eighty-nine thousand pounds based on year-end cash. Therefore, future Bank of England rate cuts represent a direct risk to non-operating finance income.
3.
Comparative peer assets: Kainos's cash position of eighty-six point five million pounds remains lower than other cash-generative UK-listed IT providers. For comparison, peer Softcat plc operates with a highly cashgenerative model that supports substantial liquidity while keeping its revolving credit facility completely undrawn. This difference demonstrates that Kainos faces higher balance sheet pressure from capex commitments relative to peers.

Bankmore Place Headquarters Capex

4.
Escalating office construction: Kainos expanded its non-current assets through a seven point one million pound investment in its new Belfast headquarters during the first half of fiscal year 2027. This capital expenditure includes four point zero million pounds categorized as property, plant and equipment, and three point one million pounds classified as investment property. This physical expansion reallocates liquid cash into fixed assets before the building's late 2026 opening.

This strategy allows the company to generate rental yields, offsetting some of the high construction costs.
6.
Rising depreciation headwind: Kainos recorded depreciation charges on property, plant and equipment of one point five million pounds for the six months ended September thirty, 2026. The completion of the One Bankmore Place headquarters will increase future annual depreciation charges significantly once the asset is ready for use. This non-cash expense will create a persistent statutory drag on earnings per share over the next three years.

Belfast lease commitments: Kainos maintains some lease obligations for other properties alongside its physical headquarters construction. The company reported right-of-use assets of five point two million pounds and lease liabilities of six point one million pounds in 2026. These lease obligations will continue to incur minor finance expenses as the group transitions.

Unhedged Currency Translation Risk

8.
Unhedged currency exposures: Kainos is exposed to significant foreign exchange volatility because approximately a quarter of its revenue is denominated in US dollars without active hedging. The finance function regularly converts surplus foreign currency balances into sterling rather than using forward foreign exchange contracts. Consequently, the group's translated profitability remains highly vulnerable to sterling strengthening against the dollar.
9.
Translation sensitivity parameters: Kainos reported that a one percent weakening of the US dollar against sterling would decrease group profits by thirty-five thousand pounds. The company faces similar exposure to other regional operating currencies, including a forty-three thousand pound profit reduction from a weak Canadian dollar. This indicates that unhedged translation adjustments create earnings volatility despite stable underlying regional margins.
10.
Contrary evidence: Kainos's management maintains that currency risk is naturally mitigated because regional operations incur most of their associated costs in the same foreign currency. However, the group still reported a translation difference loss of seventy-seven thousand pounds in its foreign currency translation reserve for the period ended September thirty, 2026. This demonstrates that natural hedging is insufficient to prevent foreign currency translation losses.
11.
Seasonal currency influences: Kainos's currency translation risks are also subject to regional cost seasonality across its overseas development centers. The group's staff costs, particularly bonus accruals, are seasonal and denominated in Polish zloty. Consequently, regional currency fluctuations can disrupt short-term margin planning parameters during the year.

Dividend Policy and Cover

12.
Sustainable dividend increases: Kainos declared a six percent increase in its interim dividend to ten point four pence per share for H1 fiscal year 2027. This progressive dividend distribution is supported by improved cash conversion, which rose to sixty-two percent from forty-eight percent in the prior period. This distribution demonstrates that management intends to maintain progressive returns to shareholders even during heavy capital expenditure.
13.
Comparative peer distributions: Kainos's progressive dividend policy aligns closely with the capital allocation strategies of its UK-listed technology peers. For example, peer Softcat plc maintains a progressive policy targeting an annual ordinary dividend of forty to fifty percent of its profits after tax. These similar distributions suggest that orderly capital return remains standard across the UK IT sector.
14.
Contrasting dividend strategies: Kainos's capital return model relies entirely on progressive ordinary dividends, which contrasts with the dividend profiles of peers. Peer Computacenter plc increased its final dividend by seven point six percent, delivering a total annual dividend of seventy-four point six pence per share. These variations show that larger peers can provide higher dividend growth metrics than Kainos during construction cycles.
15.
Other side of the story: Kainos's robust cash conversion and large backlog could easily offset any non-operating headwinds from interest rates or currency translations. The company's contracted backlog rose twenty-six percent to eight hundred and fifty-three million pounds, which secures long-term revenue visibility. If organic revenue growth continues at its current rapid pace, these non-operating items will have only a negligible impact on long-term adjusted earnings per share.
