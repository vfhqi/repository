Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Rising competitive intensity in Greece during 2026 directly threatens operator margins and cash flows unless joint ventures stabilize the market.
1.
Crucial question: "Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE) (HTO-GR): Greece's incumbent fixed and mobile operator, majority-owned by Deutsche Telekom. How has competitive intensity in Greek fixed broadband and mobile changed during 2026, what is driving it, and is it stabilising or still building into 2027?"

Financial Impact and EPS Volatility

2.
Vulnerable earnings: OTE's earnings per share are highly sensitive to fixed broadband pricing because retail fixed service revenues represent a significant portion of its total turnover. Morgan Stanley updated its forecasts on October 30, 2026, estimating OTE's 2026 earnings per share at €1.53 compared to €1.23 in 2025. If retail pricing degrades due to aggressive campaigns by utility entrant Public Power Corporation, this projected earnings expansion could be severely derailed.
3.
Disrupted margins: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in October 2025 that Public Power Corporation entered the retail broadband market with prices approximately 50% below established players. This aggressive retail pricing by the utility entrant threatens to erode the stable value-oriented pricing structure of the Greek telecom market. If these competitive pressures persist, OTE's ability to sustain its historically strong free cash flow yields could be compromised.
4.
Depressed valuations: Morgan Stanley notes that OTE's current valuation remains supported by its best-in-class network and superior shareholder returns, which screen well against European peers. However, the broker's bear case scenario of €15.20 per share is explicitly tied to more intense pricing competition and a successful fiber overbuild by Public Power Corporation. Investors must monitor these competitive dynamics because a structural escalation in retail price wars would contract OTE's earnings multiples.

Industry Risks and Overbuild Dynamics

5.
Escalating risk: Vodafone Greece has experienced fixed broadband customer losses, reporting a decline of 14,000 subscribers in the fiscal year ending March 2026. To counter this trend and mitigate fiber overbuild risks, Vodafone and Public Power Corporation proposed a 50-50 fiber joint venture in June 2026. The success of this joint venture will determine whether Vodafone can stabilize its fixed-line earnings or if it must continue sacrificing average revenue per user to retain subscribers.
6.
Strategic uncertainty: United Group has been reviewing a potential sale of Nova, which introduces consolidation risk into the Greek telecommunications sector. This potential transaction could reshape the market if the new owner decides to compete aggressively on price rather than value. Any shift in Nova's commercial strategy directly impacts OTE's wholesale and retail revenues, making the resolution of this sale a key driver of 2027 competitive intensity.
7.
Accelerated rollout: Infrastructure rollout questions are closely related to competitive intensity, as Public Power Corporation aims to reach 3.8 million households with fiber by 2028. OTE is countering this threat by expanding its own fiber-to-the-home footprint, targeting 3.5 million premises by 2027. The speed of these deployments and the take-up of government fiber vouchers will dictate the future wholesale volumes challengers buy from OTE.
8.
Regulatory headwinds: The Greek telecom regulator, Hellenic Telecommunications and Post Commission, has proposed changes to wholesale pricing that could squeeze challenger margins. These pending decisions, which include a projected 10% to 13% decrease in fiber-to-the-home wholesale charges, will shape the wholesale revenues OTE generates from its rivals. If these regulatory changes fail to stimulate consumer migration to high-speed fiber, OTE's retail and wholesale average revenues per user will remain under pressure.

Tactical upgrades: Vodafone Greece expanded its retail tariff adjustments in February 2026 by expanding its retail tariff adjustments on entry-level pay-TV services and Fixed Wireless Access services. The reduction in consumer taxes helped operators cushion the impact of retail price increases on household budgets.

Strategic partnership: OTE Group strengthened its retail value proposition in October 2025 by launching a new MagentaONE promotional bundle in partnership with energy provider Metlen. This joint offering gave OTE customers a monthly five euro discount on their fixed-line telecom bill for two years when signing up for Metlen's energy service. This cross-industry promotional bundle was designed to lock in subscribers and reduce monthly churn.

Rebranded identity: OTE initiated a comprehensive rebranding campaign in September 2026 to transition its commercial operations from Cosmote to the international Telekom brand. This branding transition included renaming its consumer products to Telekom 5G, Telekom Fiber, and MagentaTV. The management team expects this international alignment to bolster consumer trust and provide access to global technology partnerships.
