Section 2 – Pillar Two Implementation in Key Jurisdictions

United Kingdom Legislative Timeline

1.
Enacted legislation: The United Kingdom has established its domestic and multinational top-up taxes to enforce a 15% global minimum rate. The UK government introduced the Multinational Top-up Tax and Domestic Top-up Tax under the Finance Act 2023, which became effective for accounting periods beginning on or after December 31, 2023. These domestic rules ensure that UK profits are subject to minimum taxes rather than being ceded to other jurisdictions.
2.
Backup rules: The United Kingdom has also implemented the Undertaxed Profits Rule to capture low-taxed foreign income when other jurisdictions fail to apply the minimum tax. Under the Finance Act 2025, which was substantively enacted on March 20, 2025, the protective rule applies to fiscal years beginning on or after December 31, 2024. This ensures the UK can collect top-up taxes from foreign subsidiaries if their parent jurisdiction has not implemented Pillar Two rules.
3.
Muted impact: Large corporate entities in the United Kingdom indicate that the new domestic and multinational rules will not significantly raise their immediate tax expenses. For instance, both Together Financial Services and J Sainsbury PLC note that their prevailing effective tax rates are comfortably above 15% in their main operating regions. This suggests that Pillar Two top-up tax liabilities will remain immaterial for groups with primarily UK-centric footprints.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some UK entities face immediate top-up tax obligations despite overall compliance under the transitional country-by-country safe harbor guidelines. A UK multinational group notes that while its other operations qualify for safe harbors, a top-up tax will arise in respect of the Isle of Man for the current year. This shows that localized low-tax jurisdictions still trigger liabilities even when the consolidated group meets the safety thresholds.

Bermuda and Norway Minimum Taxes

5.
Sweeping reform: Bermuda has abandoned its historic status as zero-tax by implementing its Corporate Income Tax Act 2023. Enacted on December 27, 2023, the legislation imposes a 15% corporate income tax on inscope entities for fiscal years starting on or after January 1, 2025. This new regime supersedes previous tax protection assurances that were originally scheduled to shield exempted undertakings until March 31, 2035.
6.
Mitigating allowances: Bermudian entities have utilized transitional tax assets to defer the immediate cash impact of the newly introduced corporate income tax. Companies like Brookfield Wealth Solutions and RenaissanceRe have recognized substantial deferred tax assets resulting from economic transition adjustments. These assets allow entities to reduce future cash tax payments over a multi-year amortization period as the 15% rate is phased in.
7.
Norway regime: Norway maintains a statutory corporate tax rate of 22% which already exceeds the 15% minimum threshold. In addition to this standard rate, Norway has enacted domestic top-up tax rules to align with the global Pillar Two framework. This statutory corporate tax is higher than Poland's 19% corporate tax rate but remains the dominant tax exposure for Norwegian marine operators.
8.
Tax methods: Other European countries have implemented different corporate tax systems to align with international Pillar Two requirements. Sweden, for example, uses a flat levy on imputed returns under its domestic tax framework. These varying national systems illustrate how countries adapt local tax laws to meet global standards while attempting to minimize the administrative burden on multinational corporations.
9.
Tax liability: Companies operating in Bermuda are preparing for increased tax reporting and payment obligations as the domestic CIT takes full effect. Multi-national groups expect to incur higher global tax expenses unless they can offset the 15% rate with qualifying domestic credits. These adjustments are expected to raise the effective tax rate for previously zero-tax entities over the next 3 years.
