Carl Zeiss Meditec's nine-month fiscal year 2025/26 results delivered revenue of €1,553.7m, flat on a constantcurrency basis, alongside an adjusted EBITA margin of 8.0%, which matched the lower bound of consensus but

- Earnings Calls The single most important change revealed by these earnings is the permanent erosion of Carl Zeiss Meditec's structural margin profile and competitive moat in China. The Ophthalmology SBU, which historically generated over three-quarters of total revenue, reported a revenue decline of -4.8% on a reported basis and -2.9% on a constant-currency basis, while EBITA collapsed by -52.9% to €62.3m, with the division's margin shrinking to 5.2% from 10.6% in the prior year.

The upcoming round of Volume-Based Procurement is estimated to drive 30% to 40% pricing headwinds across premium and monofocal categories, while local Chinese manufacturers EyeBright and Vision Pro are entering the premium intraocular lens segment, challenging Carl Zeiss Meditec's historical dominance. This competitive deterioration, combined with persistent weak US capital equipment demand and back-ended ProfitUp savings realization, fundamentally undermines EPS predictability over the next 12 to 18 months and renders a 25%+ IRR investment case unfeasible without radical positive deviations from current trajectories.

The company initiated the comprehensive "ProfitUp" savings program targeting an annual gross earnings improvement of over €200 million by fiscal year 2028/29 compared to the 2025/26 baseline, designed to restore long-term competitiveness by targeting massive structural cost reductions across R&D, G&A, and operations. However, management will have to reinvest approximately €40 million annually out of the gross savings to offset rising corporate infrastructure costs, diluting the net profitability benefit to €160 million.

The realization of ProfitUp benefits remains skewed heavily towards the final years of the program, making the upcoming fiscal year 2026/27 a transition phase rather than a period of margin expansion. This back-ended execution timeline creates a fundamental tension between the market's demand for near-term proof points and management's assertion that structural improvement will materialize only by fiscal year 2028/29.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the prevailing sell-side pessimism is that Carl Zeiss Meditec could experience a rapid operational inflection if global refractive procedures rebound faster than expected. Management's focus on structural cost control and the deployment of premium systems like the VISUMAX 800 could trigger a rapid operational inflection if global refractive procedures rebound faster than expected, driving immediate margin expansion ahead of the back-end-weighted schedule.

The rollout of 25 Zeiss VISUMAX 800 femtosecond lasers to Aier Eye Hospital Group, scheduled to progress from the second half of 2026 across Aier's domestic and international hospital networks, represents a major commercial catalyst. The ongoing commercial rollout of the VISUMAX 800 has accelerated SMILE Pro adoption, with premium consumables commanding a 10% average selling price uplift, priced at €165 per eye versus €150 for legacy SMILE.

If Chinese private clinic refractive volumes recover swiftly following macroeconomic stabilization, and if the newly registered AT LISA 809 successor lens captures meaningful public hospital market share in the upcoming nationwide Volume-Based Procurement tender, the company could deliver double-digit organic growth and margin expansion well ahead of current conservative consensus models. The precise trigger that would change the investment thesis is quarterly sequential evidence of Ophthalmology SBU margin stabilization above 8% by the end of fiscal year 2026/27. Specifically, investors should monitor Q1 and Q2 fiscal year 2026/27 results (scheduled for February and May 2027) for three critical data points: first, whether China refractive procedure volumes return to mid-single-digit growth following the summer 2026 trough; second, whether the company can defend adjusted EBITA margins above 9% excluding one-off tariff refunds; and third, whether order intake in the Americas returns to flat or positive growth on a constant-currency basis, signaling that private clinic capital expenditure cycles are stabilizing.

The outlier call-out is that Carl Zeiss Meditec's deeply embedded corporate relationship with Carl Zeiss AG could become a major strategic liability. Carl Zeiss Meditec is deeply integrated with its parent company Carl Zeiss AG, which holds a majority 59% stake in the listed entity, with the close contractual relationship including shared infrastructure, brand licensing, and corporate service agreements subject to related-party regulations under Section 111c of the German Stock Corporation Act.
