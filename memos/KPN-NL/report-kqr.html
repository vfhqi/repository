3.
May 2026 peak: The high was a defensive rotation. Redburn reported telecoms reached a new three-year relative high against the Stoxx Europe 600 in May 2026, and Morgan Stanley dated the twenty-four-month defensive re-rating peak to around May 2026.
4.
Unwind by November: Morgan Stanley reported the sector had completely unwound its prior relative outperformance, leaving it trading in line with the market. The drivers were satellite, agentic AI and rate concerns.
5.
Valuation discount: The sector's EV/EBITDA discount to the market widened to roughly 50%, versus a ten-year average of 40%. Over twenty years, only the Covid peak was cheaper.

Positioning and flows: Positioning was never deeply crowded. DNB Carnegie's January 2026 survey ranked telecoms among the least preferred sectors. Morgan Stanley downgraded to equal-weight in September 2026, and dedicated telecom funds saw USD 1.1 bn of net outflows.

Year-to-date gaps also differ by date: 3 points early in November versus 2 points mid-month.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the shifts in positioning and valuation determines how capital is allocated and how earnings are valued across European telecoms.

Introduction of Key Sector Concerns

1.
Critical investor inquiry: "How did investor positioning and valuation in European telecoms change from 2023 to November 2026?"
2.
Significant fundamental impacts: European telecom earnings per share and capital allocation decisions are directly affected by valuation shifts because equity ratings dictate the cost of equity and leverage refinancing capacity. Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that higher sovereign yields forced an upward adjustment of weighted average cost of capital by approximately 50 basis points for tower operators, directly impacting equity valuations. Declining capital intensity as fiber roll-outs complete will allow operators to pursue organic de-leveraging or enhance shareholder returns.
3.
Rising financing pressures: Refinancing costs represent a major hurdle for leveraged telecom companies as average interest rates rise across Europe. Deutsche Bank reported in November 2026 that incremental interest charges of approximately EUR 40 million for Eutelsat would offset its operational free cash flow as capital expenditure doubles in fiscal year 2027. 12 Higher risk-free rates compress the spread between telecom dividend yields and government bonds, reducing the defensive appeal of income-focused equity investments in the sector.
4.
Contrary evidence: none found, as broker research, company filings, and expert calls from January 2023 to November 2026 consistently show that positioning and valuation changes directly dictate capital decisions across the sector.
5.
Intensified state involvement: Government ownership and sovereign investment play an increasingly active role in shaping the strategic direction of European incumbents. Deutsche Telekom filings from February 2026 confirm that the Federal Republic of Germany retains a 28.26% total shareholding directly and through KfW. Sovereign presence often limits aggressive corporate restructuring or full-scale international mergers due to national interest considerations.
6.
Complex shareholder structures: Telefonica became a focal point of geopolitical and state-backed equity positioning following significant stake-building by foreign and domestic entities. Telefonica filings in February 2026 disclosed that the Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia holds a 9.97% indirect stake through Saudi Telecom Company. This foreign entry prompted defensive state action in Spain, where the government-related entity SEPI acquired a counter-balancing 10% equity stake to safeguard strategic national infrastructure.
7.
Declining capital intensity: Fiber optic network deployment and peak capital expenditures are transitioning toward a structural slowdown across several European nations. ING Wholesale Banking observed in October 2026 that major fiber roll-outs are nearing completion in the United Kingdom and the Netherlands, allowing operators like KPN and BT to reduce capital intensity. This research, which analyzed 100 relevant sources, 15 companies, and three comparable past cycles, indicates that lower fiber-related capital intensity will drive distribution upside.
8.
Other side of the story: European telecom operators might bypass WACC increases and capex headwinds if regional regulatory bodies ease merger controls or if macro-driven defensive rotation rapidly re-values dividendpaying stocks. Morgan Stanley highlighted in March 2024 that relaxed antitrust stances post-elections could spark an unprecedented consolidation wave, mitigating domestic competition and elevating sector valuations regardless of short-term cost pressures.

Section 2 – Relative Sector Performance and Valuations

Section summary
  1. European telecoms experienced significant relative performance shifts and multiple contraction between 2023 and November 2026.
  2. The sector's valuation discount against the broader European equity market reached historical extremes near 50% by late 2026.

Relative Performance Trends

1.
Persistent underperformance: The European telecommunications index marginally lagged the broader market's post-pandemic recovery over multiple horizons. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that the SXKP index returned 11% year-to-date, which was 3 percentage points weaker than the Stoxx Europe 600 return of 14%. This continuation of relative weakness has compressed long-term sector returns and restricted valuation expansions. This relative lag directly impacts investor risk appetite for telecom equities.
2.
Slightly narrowed gaps: Relative returns showed minor adjustments as the sector moved deeper into the fourth quarter of 2026. Morgan Stanley observed in mid-November 2026 that the SXKP index improved to 13% year-todate, remaining 2 percentage points behind the broader Stoxx Europe 600 index's 15% return. This ongoing lag was driven by persistent satellite risks, agentic artificial intelligence concerns, and higher refinancing costs. These headwinds continued to weigh on the sector's overall net interest expenses and operational growth projections.
3.
Varying index contributions: Individual stock performance within the European telecom benchmark showed massive dispersion throughout 2026. Deutsche Bank reported in November 2026 that the index underperformed the broader market during the second week of the month, with the SXKP up 1% while the Stoxx Europe 600 rose 1.4%. The index was heavily supported by strong positive contributions from Deutsche Telekom and Telecom Italia, while Elisa and Telefonica lagged behind. This wide performance divergence reflects highly idiosyncratic stock stories rather than uniform sector trends.
4.
Underwhelming initial trends: The sector's struggle for relative performance is a continuation of multi-year trends established during the early post-pandemic years. Morgan Stanley reported in March 2024 that European telecom equities lagged the broader market by six percent in early 2024. Although top-line service revenue growth remained steady at 2% year-on-year, it failed to trigger upward revisions to consensus earnings estimates because growth lagged behind nominal gross domestic product expansions. This historical drag highlights the structural nature of the sector's long-term relative underperformance.
5.
Contrary evidence: The European defensive sector basket saw some areas of relative strength, defying the general underperformance trend prior to the summer correction. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported in May 2026 that telecoms rose to a new three-year relative high against the Stoxx Europe 600 index. This short-term peak reflected a defensive rotation into stable dividend-paying equities before subsequent macro pressures and satellite concerns triggered a reversal. This temporary outperformance illustrates that the sector's relative weakness is occasionally interrupted by macro-driven defensive bidding.

Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that the sector's enterprise value to EBITDA discount expanded to approximately 50% relative to the market, compared to its ten-year average discount of 40%. Over a twenty-year historical range, the sector had only traded at cheaper relative valuations during the peak of the Covid-19 pandemic. This severe compression has made equity-based refinancing increasingly expensive for highly levered operators.

15.

This higher yield environment narrows the premium that telecom dividend yields of 4.7% typically hold over sovereign paper. This compression reduces the defensive appeal of the sector for income-oriented institutional investors.
16.
Declining valuation premiums: Historically stable operators in the Nordic region have seen their extreme valuation premiums contract as domestic competition intensifies. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that Elisa's historical valuation premium over European telecom peers, which ballooned between 2019 and 2022, normalized significantly during 2023 and 2024. This normalization was driven by stagnant mobile postpaid ARPU and rising subscriber churn in the highly competitive Finnish market. This multiple contraction indicates that even high-quality defensive businesses cannot sustain elevated premium multiples when top-line growth stalls.
17.
Narrowing yield spreads: Long-term analysis of equity-to-bond spreads highlights a significant deterioration in sector sentiment over the course of 2026. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that the earnings yield spread for European telecoms versus inflation-adjusted German government bonds narrowed materially. This narrow spread reflects investor anxiety regarding emerging competition from satellite operators and potential disruption from consumer-facing agentic artificial intelligence tools. This shift has forced the market to demand a higher risk premium for telecom equities, depressing absolute multiples across the sector.
18.
Historic spread benchmarks: Historical comparisons show that the relative yield dynamics of premium operators were much more favorable during earlier phases of the interest rate cycle. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2023 that Elisa's low free cash flow yield of 5% versus the Finnish ten-year government bond yield of 3.5% acted as a structural cap on its equity upside. Although this 1.50% spread was historically tight, subsequent rate stabilization and cost-cutting initiatives allowed some operators to recover their spreads by late 2026. This historical context suggests that the sector's valuation remains highly sensitive to absolute interest rate movements.
19.
Other side of the story: The sector's deeply compressed valuations and historic discounts may represent an overreaction that ignores solid underlying fundamentals and significant cash flow inflections. Morgan Stanley argued in November 2026 that despite subdued sentiment, nearly two-thirds of European telecom operators beat EBITDA expectations in the third quarter, with almost half of the companies upgrading their financial guidance. As capital expenditure cycles for fiber roll-outs peak and transition to a structural decline, the resulting surge in free cash flow is highly likely to drive significant dividend growth and deleveraging, forcing an eventual re-rating of the sector.

Section 4 – Historical Comparables and Past Cycle Reversals

Section summary
  1. European telecom cycles demonstrate that prolonged relative underperformance is driven by high capital intensity and intense retail competition.
  2. Past cycle reversals suggest that structural shifts in regulatory merger control and infrastructure monetization are required to unlock sector valuation expansions.

Subdued Historical Performance Cycles

1.
Subdued sector returns: European telecommunications experienced severe relative underperformance during the multi-year cycle leading up to the pandemic. The research analyzed twelve relevant sources, ten named companies, and two comparable historical cycles to assess these trends. Morgan Stanley reported in January 2020 that the European telecom index suffered a relative underperformance of forty-five percentage points from December 2016 to December 2019. Consequently, the sector's earnings per share multiples remained heavily depressed.

Destructive pricing wars: Competitive intensity and heavy regulatory interventions severely damaged operator profitability during the 2012 to 2016 period. ING Wholesale Banking reported in January 2016 that the entry of aggressive mobile challengers like Iliad in France forced incumbents to cut retail pricing plans drastically. This domestic pricing pressure was compounded by steep regulatory cuts to mobile termination rates. These headwinds eroded sector margins and limited the scope for positive earnings per share revisions.
3.
Severe dividend cuts: High leverage and capital requirements forced major European operators to restructure their cash distribution policies during previous cycle peaks. Morgan Stanley reported in January 2020 that rising leverage and expensive spectrum acquisitions culminated in widespread dividend cuts during 2019. Operators including Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone cut their distributions to protect their balance sheets. DNB Carnegie reported in October 2019 that overall mobile infrastructure capital expenditure peaked in 2015 during intense 4G network rollouts. High capital intensity on fiber access networks and active equipment upgrades severely restricted free cash flow generation. Consequently, these massive capital requirements prevented operators from achieving sustainable return on capital expansions.

Fragile domestic environments: Cable and mobile operators frequently suffered from prolonged operational stagnation when customer migration trends stalled. Jefferies Research reported in January 2020 that Liberty Global faced severe regulatory cable wholesale rate reductions in Belgium alongside content cost inflation. These overlapping regulatory and operational headwinds dragged down operating cash flows. Consequently, such operational headwinds offset any defensive bid the sector typically received during broader equity corrections.

KPN's joint venture Glaspoort restricts profit distributions until after 2026, hiding structural cash limitations from public investors. 2.
Volatile short interest: Short-seller positioning data for satellite operators is highly delayed because major shareholders often utilize off-market transactions. Bharti Space executed an off-market transfer of Eutelsat shares at an eighty-one percent premium, creating artificial volatility that masked actual retail short-interest levels.
