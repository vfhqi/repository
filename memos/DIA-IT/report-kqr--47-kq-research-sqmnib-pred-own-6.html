Section 2 – PAMA Clinical Laboratory Fee Cuts and CMS Proposals

Section summary
  1. The proposed Protecting Access to Medicare Act cuts in 2027 present a material pricing risk for diagnostic players.
  2. Bipartisan legislative efforts or further delays represent the primary mechanism to mitigate these severe pricing reductions.

Mechanism and Severity of Proposed Cuts

1.
Severe reduction: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services published preliminary rate proposals in September 2026 that outline an average 16% overpayment relative to commercial rates. These proposed cuts under the Protecting Access to Medicare Act of 2014 will affect nearly 1,200 laboratory tests. The pricing pressure will be capped at a maximum of 15% annually from 2027 through 2029. This structural shift represents a severe diagnostic pricing headwind across the laboratory industry.
2.
Repeated deferrals: Congress has historically acted to delay PAMA implementation multiple times to protect laboratory margins. Bipartisan legislation in early February 2026 suspended scheduled cuts for the remainder of the year and froze rates. This persistent legislative intervention has repeatedly deferred the impact of the original 2014 law. The historical pattern suggests that future delays remain the primary mitigation tool for diagnostic suppliers.
3.
Expected headwind: Labcorp reported in October 2026 that the proposed fee cuts represent an estimated $125 million top-line pricing headwind for fiscal year 2027. Management intends to offset a portion of this impact through operating efficiencies and business development. This $125 million exposure represents roughly an 8% drag on earnings per share based on Deutsche Bank estimates assuming a 25% offset. This pricing reduction could significantly compress future operating margins if mitigation efforts fall short.
4.
Similar exposure: Quest Diagnostics faces an estimated $100 million to $120 million gross PAMA headwind in fiscal year 2027. Morgan Stanley projects that this gross revenue pressure will translate to approximately 1% of total revenues in 2027. Management intends to offset 20% to 50% of this anticipated headwind through productivity initiatives. These cost-saving programs are crucial for offsetting the anticipated reimbursement cuts in 2027.
5.
Varying impact: Sonic Healthcare updated its estimated fiscal year 2027 PAMA impact to approximately 24 million Australian dollars in November 2026. This updated estimate is significantly higher than the previous projection of 15 million Australian dollars. Sonic's exposure is lower than Labcorp's $125 million headwind due to its smaller US market presence. This lower absolute exposure highlights differing geographic sensitivities to US reimbursement.

Legislative and Industry Countermeasures

6.
Significant shift: Morgan Stanley reports that hospital laboratories represented 13.6% of the reporting sample in the 2026 collection cycle compared to only 1.1% in the original 2017 dataset. This increased hospital representation was driven by updated CMS reporting definitions under PAMA. Despite this increase, the American Clinical Laboratory Association argues the data remains distorted and underrepresents high-cost providers. This data distortion could lead to artificially low pricing benchmarks across the sector.
7.
Uneven impact: Morgan Stanley reports that preliminary payment rates suggest varying cuts across different diagnostic test categories. Molecular pathology and genomic sequencing codes face potential weighted reductions of 22% and 23% respectively. In contrast, chemistry and immunology tests face lower cuts capped at 16% and 19.3% respectively. These unequal rate reductions will disproportionately squeeze molecular segment revenues compared to core clinical chemistry.
8.
Legislative push: Bipartisan lawmakers are backing the RESULTS Act to permanently freeze rates and reform the data collection process. The proposed bill has secured more than 135 congressional co-sponsors as of late October 2026. This alternative legislation would replace PAMA's flawed survey methodology with a fairer claims database to prevent rate distortions. Passing the RESULTS Act is the most definitive path to permanent relief from annual clinical laboratory fee cuts.
9.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies notes that another legislative delay remains the most likely outcome with a 40% probability while the RESULTS Act has less than a 10% chance of passing by late 2026. The Congressional Budget Office has not finalized its scoring of the reform bill. If Congress simply delays the cuts again, the industry will face continued pricing uncertainty in 2028 without permanent reform.
10.
Downward pressure: Industry experts suggest that large commercial reference laboratories will try to pass PAMA cuts entirely to suppliers. A former science director at Quest Diagnostics noted that commercial payers tend to follow Medicare reimbursement trends, amplifying the margin squeeze. This pass-through pressure will directly target diagnostic manufacturers like DiaSorin. These price concessions will likely erode reagent margins over time.
