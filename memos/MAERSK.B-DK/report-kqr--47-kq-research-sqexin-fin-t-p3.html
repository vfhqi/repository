Section 2 – Capital Allocation Loops: Profit-to-Orderbook and Scrapping Lags

Section summary
  1. Record container shipping profits have fueled massive orderbooks that will trigger a structural oversupply through 2028.
  2. Industry cash buffers of 26 to 30 billion dollars are delaying essential ship scrapping by enabling a prolonging game of chicken.

Profit to Orderbook Flywheel

1.

Deutsche Bank reported in October 2026 that the global container ship orderbook has ballooned to 44% of the active fleet due to aggressive post-conflict ordering. This massive influx of capital ensures that effective capacity growth remains highly elevated through 2028. Consequently, the severe vessel oversupply will significantly drag down Maersk's future earnings per share.
2.
Extreme ordering: The current capacity expansion mirrors previous structural downturns where high profitability triggered uncontrollable ship ordering. Contract Shortening Mechanics

Rapid transmission: Shorter contract structures are accelerating the speed at which spot market rate shifts impact ocean carrier profitability. Jefferies Research observed in September 2025 that container lines are increasingly exposed to highly volatile spot freight rates because shipper contracts are resetting more frequently. Shippers have transitioned from traditional annual commitments to flexible quarterly or spot-linked contracts to capitalize on falling rates. This structural shift ensures that any downward spot movement immediately erodes Maersk's contract rate protections, directly compressing near-term earnings per share.
2.
Frequent renegotiations: Logistics buyers are leveraging market overcapacity to bypass long-term pricing commitments. Expert insights from a Senior VP of Finance at Echo Global Logistics in March 2026 indicate that approximately 20% of major shippers have transitioned from annual contracts to quarterly bids. This frequent resetting of pricing terms allows cargo owners to exploit near-term spot rate declines very quickly. Consequently, Maersk and its alliance partners must constantly adjust their pricing baselines, which increases the volatility of corporate cash flows.

Costly flexibility: Shippers are refusing to honor fixed contract rates when spot options drop significantly below agreed levels. A Former Sales and Operations Manager at RXO observed in February 2026 that modern contract rates are treated as may-haul rather than must-haul agreements. Shippers routinely demand mid-cycle rate renegotiations or divert volumes to cheaper spot carriers during soft markets.
