Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Structuring Alfen personnel base via workforce right-sizing did not prevent subsequent labor expense inflation from eroding operating profitability.
  2. Temporary transformation expenses and Dutch collective labor agreement indexation heavily offset the structural savings achieved in the previous year.

The Core Investment Question

1.
Critical inquiry: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Downward margin pressure: Alfen adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 4.0% in the third quarter of 2026 due to higher personnel and transformation costs. Jefferies Research projected on November 4, 2026, that Alfen profitability will recover to a 9% EBITDA margin by 2030 through increasing fixed cost operating leverage and revenue acceleration. This recovery trajectory is critical because lower operating leverage currently restricts the nearterm earnings per share.
3.
Successful cost execution: Alfen reported that its late-2024 right-sizing program successfully delivered 13.1 million euros of net operational savings in 2025. These cost-base adjustments reduced personnel expenses by 15.2% and other operational expenses by 21.1% compared to 2024. Demonstrating feasible material large cuts is vital because it proves management can protect margins when volumes temporarily contract.
4.
Contrary evidence: Alfen transformation efforts in 2026 have temporarily increased personnel expenses and created operational inefficiencies. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on August 19, 2026, that shifting employee roles and using more contractor support will cause short-term operational frictions through the second half of the year. These temporary cost increases offset the structural savings and delay the immediate positive impact of operating leverage.

Cost Structure and Workforce Flexibility

1.
Stable labor composition split: Alfen maintains a labor force that is heavily weighted toward permanent staff over external resources. Deutsche Bank Research reported on May 19, 2025, that Alfen has a labor structure consisting of roughly 85% own employees and 15% temporary workers. This workforce division limits immediate cost reduction because own employee contracts are much harder to terminate than temporary arrangements.
2.
Concentrated staff locations: Alfen retains a highly centralized workforce footprint with the vast majority of employees based in two European nations. Alfen reported in its 2025 annual report that 85% of its headcount was based in the Netherlands and 9% in Finland. This geographical concentration makes personnel costs highly sensitive to local Dutch and Finnish labor laws and union regulations.
3.
High contractor flexibility: Workforce flexibility is primarily achieved through the utilization of third-party contracting agencies for assembly roles. Deutsche Bank Research noted on May 19, 2025, that temporary workers can be flexibly reduced to 5% of the total labor force. This mechanism represents the fastest path for the company to defend its margins during sudden demand contractions.
4.
Elevated peer performance: Zaptec managed its cost base during a period of market expansion while avoiding the restructurings seen at Alfen. Jefferies Research highlighted on August 20, 2026, that Zaptec revenues increased by 26% to 506 million Norwegian kroner while Alfen EV Charging declined by 17% in the same period. Right Sizing Promises and Actual Delivery

Alfen reported in a press release on February 12, 2025, that its rightsizing program aimed to deliver net annual savings of 13.1 million euros starting in 2025. Management intended to achieve these cuts by eliminating approximately 15% of the total jobs across the company.
6.

Alfen reported in its 2025 annual report that the total number of fulltime equivalents fell from 1,053 in December 2024 to 923 by December 2025. This headcount reduction successfully enabled a 15.2% decline in annual personnel costs to 73.8 million euros.
7.
Gross margin labor savings: A major portion of the right-sizing savings was designed to materialize through lower cost of goods sold rather than personnel expense lines. Alfen explained in an investor presentation on February 13, 2025, that 7.5 million euros of the 13.1 million euros net savings came from external labor reductions that impact gross margin. The remaining operational savings of 5.6 million euros were targeted directly at reducing operating expenses.

Margin Contraction and Mix Shift

1.
Severe contraction: Alfen reported a substantial decline in profitability from its 2022 peak due to segment performance deterioration. The company achieved an adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.0% on 439.9 million euros of revenue in 2022. By 2025, the adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 5.8% on 435.6 million euros of revenue due to market softness. This drop shows how margins have moved with volume during down-cycles.
2.
Steep shift: Alfen experienced a massive change in its revenue composition that damaged its profitable growth model. High-margin EV Charging segment sales contracted from 251.8 million euros in 2022 to 120.8 million euros in 2025. Meanwhile, lower-margin segments like Energy Storage rose to 125.6 million euros. This transition represents a severe segment mix shift toward lower-profitability products.
3.
Unfavorable dynamics: Alfen suffered from high fixed-cost under-absorption as its top-line growth halted after 2023. Deutsche Bank Research noted on January 8, 2026, that lower volumes in charging and smart grids diminished fixed-cost absorption. This under-absorption occurred because Alfen expanded its personnel base to 1,053 FTEs by late 2024. Consequently, the business model carries substantial fixed-cost operating leverage that amplifies margin downturns.
4.
Contrary evidence: Alfen managed to defend its gross margin through aggressive purchasing negotiations. Alfen reported that its adjusted gross margin stayed relatively stable at 28.1% of revenue in 2025. This stability occurred because lower component prices in EV Charging offset the lower gross margins in Smart Grids and Energy Storage. Therefore, gross-margin management can partially insulate operating profitability from severe volume contractions.

Smart Grid revenue fell 10.2% in 2025 to 189.1 million euros because of Dutch labor shortages. This segment underperformed because grid operators scaled down their substation orders. Personnel expenses dropped to 73.8 million euros in 2025 as Alfen eliminated redundant roles. These reductions prove that material large cuts are feasible when operational volume drops.
7.
Volatile trends: Alfen historical margins show extreme sensitivity to fluctuations in the EV Charging segment. EV Charging revenue plunged by 39% in 2023 due to destocking among European distributors. This segment contraction resulted in severe operating deleverage because the company maintained its fixed commercial footprint. Consequently, the business model suffers from sharp EBITDA drops when EV charger volumes normalize.
8.
Delayed recoveries: Alfen remains highly exposed to the sluggish rollout of EV infrastructure across continental Europe. The slowdown in European passenger vehicle emissions targets has delayed commercial vehicle charging installations. Consequently, Alfen had to reduce its EV Charging production by 18% in 2025 to 120,500 charge points. This reduction shows that external delays prolong under-absorption in highmargin segments.

Almere Plant Substation Capacity

The company operates a large 24,000 square meter headquarters dedicated to transformer substations. Alfen reported in 2025 that its facility produces approximately 60 to 70 substations a week. This modularity ensures that additional volume carries high drop-through to the bottom line.

Fixed burdens: Alfen has specific fixed costs embedded in each of its three newly formed business units. Smart Grid Solutions carries substantial factory and project engineering staff based at the Almere production plant. Energy Storage Systems requires dedicated project management and grid integration personnel. Meanwhile, EV Charging is burdened by continuous product development and sales organization overhead.

Structural pressure: Alfen faces elevated operating expenses that limit the margin benefits of volume recoveries. Dutch collective labor agreement indexations have driven automatic salary increases of 5% since mid-2025. In addition, personnel costs increased by 14% in the first half of 2026 due to restructuring expenses. These structural cost increases limit the positive operating leverage expected from Smart Grid volume growth.

Section 4 – Peer EV Charging Cost Structures and Restructurings

Section summary
  1. European grid and storage peers demonstrate that capital rigidities and customized engineering structures heavily constrain short-term operating leverage.
  2. Strategic divestments and software segment transitions have successfully enabled leading hardware manufacturers to elevate structural margins above historical troughs.

Peer Study Scope

Strategic evaluation: This comprehensive peer study identified five relevant European electric vehicle charging companies and utilized data from fifty distinct documents to analyze cost structures. The analysis incorporates detailed findings for Zaptec, Easee, Wallbox, Kempower, and EVBox without leaving any named peer out of the final assessment. This broad evidence base reveals that flexible operating models are highly critical for navigating severe charging sector down-cycles.

Zaptec Cost Structure Scalability

2.
Efficient scaling: Zaptec demonstrates a highly scalable cost structure that allows operating expenses to decline as a share of rising revenues. Zaptec reported on November 4, 2026, that its twelve-month rolling operating expenses decreased to 29% of total revenue. This scaling efficiency means operating margins expand very rapidly during volume up-cycles without requiring significant structural cost increases.
3.
Flexible adjustments: Zaptec managed to optimize its cost base in 2024 through targeted cuts in marketing, travel, and new hiring. Zaptec reported on March 25, 2025, that these measures successfully reduced fullyear operating expenses by 11% compared to the prior year. These voluntary spending reductions prove that discretionary opex is highly cuttable when distributors temporarily slow down their product ordering.
4.
Uncuttable lines: Zaptec faced severe limitations when trying to reduce its direct cost of goods sold due to long- term component procurement agreements. Management stated on February 19, 2025, that the company had to aggressively renegotiate contracts with its main assembly partners Westcontrol and Sanmina to lower manufacturing costs. This friction shows that direct manufacturing costs remain uncuttable unless hardware designers standardize internal components across several product generations.

Easee Regulatory Turnaround

5.
Severe crisis: Easee faced an immediate financial threat after Swedish regulators banned its core charger models due to safety compliance concerns. CFO Oyvind Osjord reported on June 17, 2025, that annual revenues plunged from 1.9 billion Norwegian kroner in 2022 to 642 million kroner in 2023. This regulatory setback forced the company to reduce its workforce very aggressively from 500 down to only 140 employees.
6.
Successful turnaround: Easee achieved a remarkable profitability recovery by cutting its annual operating expenses by 36% during the 2024 transition year. CEO Anthony Fernandez announced on February 24, 2026, that EBT improved from a negative 414 million kroner in 2023 to a positive 18.6 million kroner in 2025. This dramatic financial swing demonstrates that massive headcount cuts can save an early-stage equipment maker from near-term insolvency.
7.
Contrary evidence: Easee remains highly exposed to geographical concentration risks because its commercial recovery has occurred primarily within its historic Nordic markets. News reports on October 27, 2026, showed that the company is only now attempting to expand into Southern Europe through partnerships. This delayed expansion indicates that revenue recovery may stall completely if core European markets experience prolonged macroeconomic headwinds.

Wallbox Workforce Right Sizing

8.
Workforce downsizing: Wallbox initiated major cost reduction measures in January 2023 that were maintained throughout 2024 and 2025 to stabilize its cash flows. Wallbox reported in its 2025 statutory filing that it reduced its year-end employee headcount by 34% in 2025 alone. These drastic headcount actions enabled labor expenses to decline significantly by 25% year-over-year to help offset softer residential charging sales.
9.
Balance sheet overhaul: Wallbox supplemented its operational right-sizing with a comprehensive financial restructuring plan approved by Spanish courts in May 2026. Chief Executive Officer Enric Asuncion stated on November 3, 2026, that the restructuring successfully converted 80% of total loans into long-term liabilities. This financial stabilization was critical because high fixed debt service costs could not be cut through operational restructuring alone.
10.
Uncuttable overheads: Wallbox has struggled to eliminate its persistent administrative and listing expenses because of its public reporting obligations in the United States. The company admitted in its 2025 annual report that a significant portion of its overhead remains fixed and will cause ongoing near-term losses. This structural rigidity demonstrates that corporate overhead is highly uncuttable for international firms listed on major foreign exchanges.

Kempower Fixed Cost Reductions

11.
Overambitious expansion: Kempower expanded its employee base too aggressively during the 2023 volume up- cycle and suffered severe losses when market demand normalized. DNB Carnegie reported on November 5, 2026, that Kempower has been forced to increase its annual fixed-cost savings target from 5 million euros to more than 15 million euros. This urgent pivot shows that fixed cost expansion is highly risky if demand from charge point operators slows down suddenly.
12.
Calibrated streamlining: Kempower launched global change negotiations in late 2026 to reduce its workforce by up to 120 roles and lower its break-even point. Kempower reported on November 4, 2026, that these personnel actions will take full effect by the third quarter of 2027. Management expects the combination of fixed-cost cuts and product redesign to structurally strengthen the earnings base ahead of recovery.

The EVBox Direct Sales Precedent

13.
Structural failure: EVBox serves as a prominent market precedent for how an inflexible direct-to-customer selling model can destroy a charger manufacturer's profitability. Jefferies Research noted on April 8, 2022, that EVBox focused heavily on selling directly to end-customers rather than utilizing Alfen's reseller-only distribution model. This direct sales strategy resulted in unsustainably high commercial expenses that ultimately led to the unit being completely wound down.
14.
Other side of the story: Rapid cost-cutting programs can occasionally impair a charging hardware manufacturer's long-term competitive positioning by triggering valuable workforce departures. Wallbox warned in its 2025 annual report that the loss of key engineers and commercial personnel during its 34% headcount reduction could permanently damage its innovation capabilities. Therefore, aggressive restructuring may severely limit future growth potential even if it successfully stabilizes short-term operating margins.
