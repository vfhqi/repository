Section 2 – Accounting Policies and Segment Allocation of Contingent

Section summary
  1. Teqnion AB records contingent consideration revaluations under other operating income and expenses in the income statement rather than within financial items.
  2. Segment reports show that these earn-out revaluations are adjusted at the group level under central functions rather than allocated to operational business areas.
  3. Peer comparisons show that Teqnion's accounting treatment of contingent considerations is highly standardized and aligns with other major Swedish serial acquirers.

Considerations

Teqnion accounting policy and income statement treatment

1.
Standardized accounting: Teqnion AB applies International Financial Reporting Standards to measure contingent considerations at fair value through profit or loss. The company states in its definitions that any subsequent adjustments to the estimated earn-out liability are recorded under other operating income and expenses in the income statement. Because these non-cash revaluations are recorded within operating profit, they directly affect reported EBITA and EBIT metrics rather than financial items. This treatment means that fluctuations in earn-out estimates can introduce substantial volatility into the company's operating performance.
2.
Complex discounting: Teqnion AB discounts its long-term contingent considerations to present value using a market-based interest rate. During an earnings call, Chief Executive Officer Johan Steene noted that IFRS rules force the company to add a fictional cost through discounting adjustments on these liabilities. This noncash accounting effect does not represent an actual operational cash outflow but impacts reported book values over time. Consequently, these non-cash interest adjustments are managed centrally to prevent distortion of underlying subsidiary results.
3.
Non-cash presentation: Teqnion AB highlights that earn-out revaluations have absolutely no impact on cash flows. The company's annual report states that these adjustments are recorded purely as accounting entries on the consolidated level. While they can significantly alter reported net profits in any given quarter, they do not affect the liquidity available for future acquisitions. Therefore, analysts must strip out these non-cash movements to evaluate the group's actual cash-generating capacity.

Segment presentation and corporate allocation

4.
Centralized management: Teqnion AB presents segment results for its newly organized business areas, Teqnion Nord and Teqnion Väst, from the start of 2026. The company's third-quarter report for 2026 clarifies that segment information includes IFRS adjustments like earn-out revaluations under central lines. This indicates that regional operating segment results are kept insulated from the volatility of group-level accounting adjustments. As a result, the operational performance of Teqnion Nord and Teqnion Väst is reported on a normalized basis.
5.
Central allocation: Teqnion AB allocates the accounting effects of lease treatments and earn-out adjustments within its corporate or central lines. In the third quarter of 2026, Central Group Functions reported an EBITA loss of 9.5 MSEK compared to a loss of 6.0 MSEK in the prior year's quarter. This unallocated central line serves to absorb corporate expenses, intercompany eliminations, and the net impact of fair value revaluations. This structure allows investors to evaluate the core regional operating segments without the noise of nonoperational corporate adjustments.
6.
Contrary evidence: Teqnion AB experienced a severe reduction in its first-quarter 2024 accounting profit due to balance sheet revaluations of earn-outs and acquisition loans rather than operational issues. The company disclosed that the depreciation of the Swedish krona created an accounting cost for earn-out revaluations on the consolidated income statement during that period. This highlights that operating segment earnings are still exposed to significant group-level translation and adjustment volatility despite the centralized segment reporting.

This shows that Lagercrantz follows a identical income statement line presentation to Teqnion AB. Consequently, both companies keep these adjustments inside operating profit rather than booking them in net financial items.

The broker reports that the peer company's adjusted EBITA margin excludes these non-operational items affecting comparability. This analytical adjustment ensures that the operational run-rate of the divisions is not distorted by accounting adjustments. Therefore, broker models treat these earn-out revaluations as nonrecurring items when forecasting peer earnings.

This method ensures that the five operating segments remain focused on pure operational performance.

Since these write-downs are excluded from segment EBITA, regional managers might appear highly profitable even as the group experiences economic destruction. Consequently, relying exclusively on regional segment EBITA may lead investors to overlook systemic operational underperformance in newly acquired businesses.
