0. Momentum now, and where 2026 lands

0.1 ★ "Deutsche Bank has 6.2% organic EBIT growth for 2026, a little above the top of your 4% to 6% range, and Morgan Stanley and consensus about 5.8%. With October and most of November behind you, what are your thoughts on 6.2%?"
- Why: each point of 2026 organic growth is about DKK 137m of EBIT. FactSet's EBIT (DKK 14,472m) sits at the guidance midpoint; Viewforth expects 5.5% to 6.0% (DKK 14,554m). The beat over the initial midpoint has shrunk from about 300bp (2024) to 200bp (2025).
- Tell: no pushback on 6.2% raises the odds of a small beat in February and of the sell-side holding its 2027 numbers through a conservative opening; "the range is the range" or talk of a tough fourth quarter lowers them and says the beat is shrinking faster than the memo's 150bp to 200bp.

0.2 ★ "Chongqing's third-quarter sales fell 9.6%, and Sichuan's beer output fell 26.8% in October. How has China's fourth quarter traded so far against the third quarter's fall of about 10%, and is sell-out now running ahead of what you ship to distributors?"
- Why: China is about a fifth of group EBIT (about DKK 2.7bn to 2.9bn). Jefferies writes that distributor inventories normalised in September; the memo would treat a Chongqing fourth quarter worse than (5)% as turning the spring's cuts from composition into fundamentals. Viewforth has China volume +2% in 2027.
- Tell: "better than the third quarter, sell-out ahead of shipments" supports the easy comparisons from the second quarter of 2027 and the sell-side's numbers; "similar or worse" lowers the odds of China's 2027 recovery and raises the odds of revenue cuts in the spring.

0.3 ★ "The Kazakhstan plant opened on 1 October. Morgan Stanley describes the step as 'a step-up to 10% organic EBIT growth in 2027'. How is the ramp-up going against plan, and how large is the profit step in 2027 as toll-filling ends, in kroner?"
- Why: Jefferies says the plant build was a 40bp drag on gross margin in the first half of 2026. Viewforth puts the 2027 step at about DKK 200m (1.4 points of group EBIT growth), against a group EBIT increase of DKK 730m to 790m. A Kazakh sugar-drinks tax, dropped from the new tax code, could still pass in 2026 (Beverages Sector Digest, 20-Nov).
- Tell: on plan and DKK 200m or more supports a February opening near consensus; a slower ramp, or a step nearer DKK 100m, lowers the odds of 2027 landing at consensus (5.7% to 5.9%).

0.4 ★ "Soft-drink volumes in Central and Eastern Europe and India rose 41.2% in the third quarter on the PepsiCo ramp-up, and Jefferies flags tough comparisons in 2027 there and in Western Europe. - Tell: "flat to slightly positive" makes consensus revenue of 3.7% to 3.8% look high, the memo's first cut; "1% or more" supports it.

0.5 "You expect about 80% of the GBP 110 million Britvic synergies by the end of 2026. How much of the remainder lands in 2027, and is the target still conservative?"

- Tell: most of the remainder in 2027, or a hint the target will rise again, raises the odds of 2027 opening near consensus; "largely done in 2026" lowers them.

Driver 1. Price against cost in 2027

1.1 ★ "In April the Chief Financial Officer told us the base rate of inflation was 1% to 2%, with some items running four to five times that, and that you would raise price rather than eat into margins. Jefferies now expects 2027 input costs to be much less pronounced than in 2022 to 2023, while Royal Unibrew talks of 8% to 12% cumulative industry inflation over two years. Where does Carlsberg's 2027 cost per hectolitre sit after hedges, and how much is already locked?"
- Why: Viewforth has input inflation of about 5.4%, about 160bp of gross margin, with more than 80% covered in money. If revenue per hectolitre rises only 1.5% against inflation above 5%, 2027 EPS is about DKK 68.5 (3.4% below Viewforth's base); if price holds at 3%, about DKK 72.9.
- Tell: below 4% and mostly locked raises the odds of 2027 landing at or above consensus; "higher than last year's view" points to the downside branch and a February opening below consensus.

1.2 ★ "Redburn thinks soft-drink mix caps your net price and mix at about 1% a year. What price and mix should we expect in 2027, and is 1% too low?"
- Why: Redburn estimates about 30bp of negative category mix a year. Viewforth has pure price about 2.6% and revenue per hectolitre about +2.1% after mix. Each 0.5 point of price and mix is DKK 300m to 450m of EBIT.
- Tell: "nearer 2% than 1%" supports consensus EBIT for 2027; agreement with Redburn's 1% means the sell-side above 5.5% organic EBIT growth is too high.

1.3 "In the United Kingdom supermarkets are fighting on standard lager, and in Poland competitors are narrowing the gap between mainstream and economy beer. For 2027, are you following price down in either market, or giving up volume to protect margin?"
- Why: Redburn sees a visible volume drag on standard lager in British supermarkets; an industry expert (an AlphaSense Expert Interview of October 2026) expects Carlsberg to become more price-aggressive in Poland. Carlsberg leads in only 24% of its volume. Viewforth's base assumes it concedes volume rather than price.
- Tell: "protect margin, accept volume" keeps price over cost intact but cuts revenue; "follow price" lowers the odds on gross margin and on 2027 EBIT.

1.4 "Heineken has cited long negotiations with European retail groups, and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners lost volume in Germany in September while it agreed 2027 terms. Are any of your 2027 negotiations open where a delisting is a real risk?"
- Why: Morgan Stanley reports retailers more willing to accept gaps on shelf than concede price. Price against cost is the widest swing in 2027 EBIT; a delisting in a large Western European market would hit the first quarter.
- Tell: "terms largely agreed" raises the odds that 2027 price lands; "still negotiating in several markets" is a first-quarter risk to volume and to the opening range.

1.5 "If price runs ahead of cost in 2027, does the surplus go to margin, or into marketing towards your 8.5% framework?"
- Why: the company plans marketing at about 8.5% of revenue. Viewforth has gross margin flat to (30)bp and EBIT +5%. A surplus spent on marketing caps the upside to consensus.
- Tell: "to margin" is the route to a raise above the initial range in 2027; "reinvested" caps it.

Driver 2. China in 2027: a cycle or a share loss

2.1 ★ "In September and October, Heineken's brand grew 31% in China through China Resources Beer while China Resources Beer's total sales fell 4%, and Budweiser APAC's China volume fell 16.8% in the third quarter. Where did Carlsberg's premium brands sit in that range, and are you losing share in the on-premise channel?"
- Why: Morgan Stanley reports Chongqing's high-end sales down 10.9% in the third quarter and its operating margin down 2.5 points. China's above-scale beer output fell 6% in January to September. Viewforth reads 2026 as part weather and destocking, part share and channel loss, with 2027 recovering on easy comparisons.
- Tell: "the whole market, premium holding share" supports a cyclical recovery in 2027; "Heineken is winning in premium" says part of the fall is share and lowers the odds of China's 2027 recovery and of the sell-side's revenue for 2027.

2.2 ★ "Jefferies has your 2027 organic revenue at 2.3%, partly on lower premium beer growth in China. Does your 2027 plan assume China volume grows on the easy comparisons from the second quarter, and by how much?"
- Why: China's second and third quarters earn about two-thirds of its profit. Viewforth: China volume +2% and China EBIT +3% to 5% (about +DKK 100m). A second weak summer costs about DKK 320m of EBIT (2.1%) but only DKK 1.1 of EPS, because 40% of Chongqing belongs to others.
- Tell: "modest growth on easy comparisons" fits the sell-side's base; "we plan China flat" makes Jefferies' 2.3% look closer to right and the consensus 3.7% too high.

Driver 3. The self-help runway after 2027 (not the thrust of this call; ask if time allows)

Driver 4. Capital return: leverage, the buyback and India

4.1 ★ "On 3 November the Chief Financial Officer said the March dividend makes a buyback announcement at the annual results unlikely, and Bernstein expects buybacks to resume in the second half of 2027. Is the half-year in August the natural point, and what would bring it forward?"
- Why: the buyback is in no consensus model. Viewforth has DKK 2bn from August 2027 and DKK 4bn to 5bn a year from 2028, 2.5 to 3.5 points of EPS growth a year; a one-year delay costs about 3.7% of 2028 EPS.
- Tell: "August, subject to conditions" is the memo's base and its case to add after the spring; "India's proceeds could change the timing" raises the odds of an earlier start; "not before 2028" lowers them.

4.2 ★ "Jefferies has leverage at 2.43 times at the end of 2026 on the rating agency's basis, and you have called 2.5 times fit for the future business. If you finish the year near there, with India possibly on top, is 2.5 times a level you hold, with cash beyond the dividend and capital spending returned, or a waypoint lower?"
- Why: Viewforth has about 2.5 times at end-2026 if Sapporo closes (about 2.7 times if not). Holding 2.5 times frees the DKK 4bn to 5bn a year from 2028 that no consensus model carries.
- Tell: "a level we hold" supports the buyback path and upgrades to 2028 EPS from August 2027; "lower" or "investment first" means smaller buybacks and a weaker case.

4.3 "Reports since 13 November describe an India roadshow, a valuation near USD 4 billion and up to USD 600 million of proceeds as early as January. Where does the listing stand, and would the proceeds go to buybacks or to debt?"
- Why: Bernstein estimates up to USD 700m. The memo puts DKK 4bn to 5bn bought back at about 2% added to 2028 EPS, net of the new Indian minority, and gives about 15% to a buyback announced in February.
- Tell: "January, proceeds available to shareholders" reverses the memo's sequence and argues for adding before the spring; "debt first" or "no date" leaves it as an option.

4.4 "Is Sapporo still on track to close this quarter? Where does leverage land at the year end with and without it?"
- Why: the research report notes regulatory approvals still pending in local markets (citing Carlsberg's press release of 6-Jul-2026); Redburn puts the deal at 0.2 times of leverage. It sets whether 2.5 times is reached on time.
- Tell: "December" keeps the buyback timetable; a slip into 2027 delays it.

4.5 "In April the Chief Financial Officer told us no large acquisition was likely in the next one to two years. With leverage expected to reach target by the year end, does that still hold, ahead of buybacks?"
- Why: a large acquisition is the one use of cash that would break the buyback leg of the case; the April notes also say 3.3 times net debt was then a constraint.
- Tell: "still holds" protects the buyback; any opening towards acquisitions is a reason to reconsider the case's capital-return leg.

Driver 5. What reaches EPS below EBIT in 2027

5.1 ★ "Morgan Stanley has net finance costs of DKK 1.86 billion for 2026, near consensus of DKK 1.85 billion, falling to DKK 1.51 billion in 2027; Jefferies, in its June preview, was at DKK 2.05 billion, which it tied to the hybrid coupons moving out of interest. - Tell: "nearer 1.5 than 1.9, coupon separate" means EPS upgrades for most houses in February; "nearer 1.9" means cuts.

5.2 ★ "Where will the hybrid coupon sit in the 2026 accounts, and is your adjusted EPS before or after it?"

The memo calls this the single mechanic the market most mis-models.
- Tell: "after, and below net income" means cuts of DKK 1 to 3.6 at the houses that have not deducted it, the spring dip the memo expects; "before" leaves consensus EPS intact but overstates what shareholders receive.

5.3 ★ "Bernstein has about DKK 40 million a year for Sapporo's share of Southeast Asia's profit. Is that the right order for 2027?"
- Why: minorities were DKK 1,023m in 2025 (14.65% of profit, the highest among European brewers), and brokers' 2027 range is DKK 1,004m to 1,293m. Viewforth has DKK 1,280m, including DKK 100m for Sapporo; DKK 0.8 of EPS lies between the extremes for Sapporo alone.
- Tell: "several times that" means a further EPS cut at the houses below DKK 1,100m; "about right" favours the higher-EPS houses.

5.4 "Will you publish restated 2025 figures on the IFRS 18 basis before 9 February, so the market can correct its models before the results?"
- Why: the research report names the restated comparatives as one of the main gaps; the reclassification is about DKK 150m to 250m of the cut in finance-cost guidance.
- Tell: "yes, in January" means corrections arrive before results and February surprises less; "with the results" concentrates the composition cuts on results day.
