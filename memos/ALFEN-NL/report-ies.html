ALFEN-NL (Alfen): what the Q3 2026 results and the response to them do to the existing case

The results leave every judgement the case makes about the business where it was: the reported quarter is better than the case's path by almost exactly the size of a one-off, and the underlying quarter is the case's trough. The response to them has weakened the momentum side by a full grade on the pinned sell-side letter, and the share price is lower than before the print.

What changed

Alfen reported Q3 2026 on 4 November 2026.

  • Group. Revenue €99.1m, about 5% below €104.1m in Q3 2025 and 2% below consensus of €101.4m. Adjusted EBITDA €4.0m (4.0% margin) against €6.9m a year earlier and consensus of €2.3m. Adjusted gross margin 30.8% against consensus of 27.7%.
  • The beat is a release. About €2.5m of project contingencies were released on completion of storage projects, taking the storage gross margin to 37.1%. Without it, adjusted EBITDA is about €1.5m, a 1.5% margin (our arithmetic). The case's path has €1.9m for the whole of H2 2026.
  • Grid. Smart Grid Solutions revenue €54.0m, +10% on €49.1m; 870 substations; a record quarter in Finland. The case's own test called 850 to 950 "in line" and above 950 positive. This is in line.
  • Storage. Revenue €18.3m, -30% and 21% below consensus of €23.1m. Backlog €109m at 30 September 2026 against €93m at 30 June, of which €23m is for the rest of 2026 and €86m for FY27. The review gives no order-intake figure; backlog growth plus revenue recognised implies about €34m (our arithmetic), against the case's assumption of about €35m a quarter and its tests of above €40m (positive) and below €20m (negative).
  • Charging. Revenue €26.8m, 7% above consensus of €25.1m and about 7% below €28.7m a year earlier (one table in the review shows €32.9m and -19% for the prior year; the text and the quarterly table both give €28.7m). Charge points 25,850, -11% on 28,900. Average selling price +4% on mix toward business chargers. Revenue per charge point is about €1,037 (our arithmetic).
  • Cost and cash. Operating expenses €26.7m, +13%; net personnel expenses €19.6m against €17.8m. Operating cash flow €1.2m against €8.1m. The €50m revolving facility and the €80m guarantee facility were extended to October 2029 on identical terms.
  • Guidance. FY26 revenue of €435m to €475m held, now "upper half"; adjusted EBITDA margin narrowed to the lower half of 4% to 7%. FY27 remains qualitative: improvement in revenue and margin, with detail in early 2027.

The response. Jefferies kept Hold and cut its target to €12 from €14, while raising FY26 adjusted EBITDA 10% to €20.7m (4.4% margin, revenue €464.8m) on the release; it models Q4 at about breakeven, a FY27 margin of 5.7%, and expects FY26 consensus to stay broadly unchanged. Deutsche Bank kept Hold and €13, models about €460m at 4.5%, says the Q4 margin could fall below 2%, and says FY27 growth "needs further explanation". The share price was €10.42 on 3 November 2026, before the print, and €10.09 on 10 November 2026, about 3% lower; it is 15.6% below its 200-day average, with relative strength at the 12th percentile. Consensus target €10.665, down 2% over one month; buy share 0%.

Set against the case: it expected reiteration (about 85%), an H2 trough, and the first of three consensus cuts (FY26 EBITDA down about 15%, about 75% likely by February). The first two happened. The third did not, and for a reason that carries no information about FY27.

The challenge

Reading one: the release is evidence about how storage projects are priced, and the case's storage margin is too low. The case holds storage at a 21.5% gross margin, describes pricing as back-to-back on cells plus a fee, and carries a 10% to 20% tail of a fixed-price overrun. The first hard evidence since then ran the other way: two record-sized Dutch projects completed with contingency unspent. Storage gross profit in the quarter was about €6.8m; without the release it is about €4.3m, roughly 23% on €18.3m (our arithmetic), still above the case's figure in the weakest revenue quarter of the year. If contingency is built into every tender and execution holds, completions against the €86m FY27 book release margin again. That would be lumpy, but it would not be the "non-recurring" item both brokers call it. On the case's FY27 storage revenue of €140m, each point of storage gross margin is €1.4m of EBITDA (our arithmetic), against a base of €24.9m. What would have to be true: contingency is standard practice and not specific to these two projects; the review's reference to execution cost efficiencies describes a habit; storage gross margin stays above 21.5% in halves with no large completion. The limit of this reading is that a release moves margin between periods of the same project. It shows the all-in margin was higher than booked, but it does not show that future tenders, in a market the review describes as compressed by Chinese integrators, are won at the same price.

Reading two: the release has deferred the reset the case is waiting for, and made it larger. The case's posture is to let consensus reset on 17 February 2027 and then buy the grid-volume upgrade. Of the €4.2m gap between its FY27 EBITDA and consensus, €3.7m was inherited from the FY26 base. That base is now closing from the other side. Nine-month adjusted EBITDA is about €20.5m (our arithmetic from the review's quarterly margins), against the case's €18.3m for the full year and a guidance floor of €18.2m to €19.0m on upper-half revenue; the case's 30% branch of a miss below the floor now needs a Q4 loss of more than €1.5m. If brokers carry a FY26 of about €20.7m into FY27 without removing the €2.5m, February resets only the depreciation line, and the EBITDA cut waits for the H1 2027 print in August 2027, against a comparison half that held the front-loaded storage deliveries. The price would then be set later and by a worse print than the case assumed. What would have to be true: brokers leave FY27 margins near 5.7% after the FY26 results; any FY27 range given in February brackets consensus. Against it: Jefferies cut its target while raising FY26, and Deutsche Bank already questions FY27, so part of the sell-side is looking through the base.

Reading three, weaker: charging is shrinking into a business-charger niche, which neither branch of the case's test describes. The case's test paired volume and price: a decline narrowing to single digits with price at or above €950 meant stabilising; a decline above 10% with price below €930 meant the price war had not plateaued. Volume fell 11% and price rose 4%. The case's FY27 has units +7% on price -4%, the opposite sign on both. In the strong form of this reading, Alfen is ceding residential volume while Dutch battery-electric registrations rose 51%, holding revenue on mix, and the home charger due for mass production in Q2 2027 re-enters the price-led segment at a lower price and margin. What would have to be true: business-charger growth continues, and segment gross margin, which the review does not give for Q3, rises with mix. We hold it loosely, because one quarter of mix does not show it.

The readings put up on the morning of the results.

  • The price was marking an invalidation. This has lost force and we drop it in that form. It needed storage intake below €30m and substations below 800; implied intake is about €34m and substations were 870. Its core survives as the underlying €1.5m. It also rested on a re-opening price of about €10.50, which the case as it now stands does not state: the case puts fair value at about €11.5 for end-2027, the trough at €8.7, and the turn in the asymmetry at €8 to €9. At €10.09 the price has crossed none of them.
  • A sector de-rating on intact estimates. On the business, this gained: revenue in line, guidance narrowed but not cut, FY26 consensus broadly unchanged. Fourteen of sixteen sector peers are pinned D or F on technical momentum. The return it claimed did not follow. The shares fell on an EBITDA beat, and the case reads the earnings-multiple return as an artefact.
  • The personnel overlap is partly a run-rate. Neither gained nor lost. Personnel cost rose 10% in the quarter; management expects it to ease through Q4 and normalise in early 2027.

What it does to the case

  • Scope for upgrades (15, F): a little less pressure. The first of the three cut steps has not happened and is now unlikely before February. The depreciation reset and the FY27 EBITDA steps stand, and reading two says they may be later and larger.
  • Guidance and track record (24, F): the same. The narrowing to the lower half matches the case's reading that the margin midpoint was not conservative. The floor is now close to secure on arithmetic. There is still no raise.
  • Current momentum (27, D): more pressure. The pinned sell-side earnings momentum letter is D against C at the memo, and technical momentum F against D. The case already read the C as an artefact, so the letter has moved toward the case's view.
  • Riskiness (20, F) and Case Riskiness (20): slightly less pressure. The case listed a revolver to refinance in 2027; it now runs to October 2029. The guarantee facility stayed at €80m, so the cap the case places on fixed-price storage intake is unchanged while the backlog has grown.
  • Storage intake judgement: less pressure. The FY27 book is €86m against the €56m the case carried and its trigger of €90m or more by December.
  • Grid volume judgement: the same. Nine-month segment revenue of €165.6m (our arithmetic) leaves €46.4m in Q4 to reach the case's €212m, against €43.0m in Q4 2025. Three quarters at €54m to €56m and 870 substations show a steady pace, short of the 950 the case wanted.
  • Charging judgement: more pressure on volume, less on price.
  • Opex judgement and the internal change rating (58, D): the same. The finance director is still interim.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (54), Foundations' Robustness (25), Setups' Fit/Crispness (34): the same.
  • Fair multiple (38, D) and total return (41, D): the same. Fair value of about €11.5 is about 14% above €10.09 and the trough about 14% below (our arithmetic).

We re-rate nothing.

What would settle it

What this review settles. Revenue and EBITDA against the pre-results preview; guidance; substations; the dating of the storage book (€23m for 2026, €86m for FY27); charging units and price; the brokers' first views and the price reaction. It does not give Q3 storage order intake as a figure, the charging gross margin, or any split of personnel cost into permanent and transitional.

Still open, with dates.

  • Whether the release recurs: the storage gross margin at the FY26 results on 17 February 2027 and the H1 2027 results in August 2027, as the FY27 book completes.
  • Whether the reset is deferred: broker FY27 EBITDA after 17 February 2027, and whether numerical FY27 guidance is given then. The company has said only "early 2027".
  • Whether Q4 is as weak as modelled: the same date. The lower half of the range allows a Q4 well above breakeven, and the brokers have chosen the bottom of it.
  • The 2027 storage book against €90m: the December backlog, reported in February 2027.
  • Grid pace: operator 2027 installation plans, which the case expects between December and February; Q4 substations.
  • Charging: Q4 units and price in February 2027; the home charger from Q2 2027.
  • Personnel: H1 2027 against H2 2026, in August 2027.

Nothing on the calendar answers how much contingency sits in the backlog, what the segments earn at EBITDA level, or when a permanent finance director is named. The company has committed to none of these.
