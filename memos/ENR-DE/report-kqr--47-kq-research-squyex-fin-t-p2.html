Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Favourable pricing conditions and record order backlogs will drive massive group earnings upgrades for Siemens Energy over the next three years.

Defining the core inquiry

1.
Core inquiry: "Siemens Energy AG (ENR-DE), a German power-equipment group (to be renamed Omterra) with four segments: Gas Services (gas and steam turbines and their long-term service), Grid Technologies (HVDC, transformers, switchgear, grid systems), Transformation of Industry (compressors, steam turbines, electrolysers; separation under way) and Siemens Gamesa (onshore and offshore wind turbines and service). Key question: how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: - Sold-out capacity with multi-year lead times (2024 to 2027). - An order downturn after a peak: the large gas turbine downturn of about 2016 to 2020, and the grid slowdown of about 2010 to 2013. - Raw material spikes: copper, grain-oriented electrical steel and aluminium in 2021 to 2022 and in 2026. - New competitor capacity arriving, such as GE Vernova's plan to reach 30 GW of annual gas turbine output by 2030 (company disclosure, July 2026) and new US transformer plants. - Customer financing stress among data-centre developers and offshore wind developers. - Large US dollar moves against the euro?"
2.
Profitability transmission: Siemens Energy converts its massive order book into reported earnings with a lag because large equipment deliveries can take up to several years. According to Siemens Energy's pre-close call on 30 September 2026, gas turbine installations typically take 12 to 24 months, followed by a two-year warranty phase before full service revenues flow. This delivery structure means pricing gains will steadily lift reported group profit margins through the end of the decade.
3.
High barrier oligopolies: Siemens Energy operates in highly concentrated end markets that naturally protect its pricing power. Jefferies Research reports on 9 November 2025 that the top three players ex-China control 80% to 90% of global market share in gas, onshore wind, and offshore wind. These high structural barriers prevent aggressive price wars, which supports the company's long-term margin targets.

Valuation implications and alternative perspectives

4.
Contrary evidence: A note by Morgan Stanley on 24 August 2026 warns that historical margins for the high- voltage grid industry are much more volatile than investors expect. The broker points out that ABB previously sold its power grids division to Hitachi due to high execution risks and competitive concerns, indicating terminal margins could eventually normalize. This suggests that current peak grid pricing may not be fully durable over the long term.
5.
Valuation discrepancies: Siemens Energy's stock is valued at a steep discount compared to its primary peers despite superior growth visibility. Morgan Stanley indicates on 8 October 2026 that Siemens Energy trades at an attractive valuation of 11x 2028 EV/EBITA, whereas GE Vernova commands a premium multiple of 22x. This massive gap suggests that the market does not fully value Siemens Energy's backlog quality or its 25% profit compound annual growth rate.
6.
Other side of the story: While the massive record backlog provides strong multi-year margin visibility, potential execution bottlenecks and supply-chain quality failures could severely depress EPS. For instance, expert interviews from COWI North America on 22 October 2024 highlight that onshore wind technical defects previously caused severe market share losses and restricted pricing flexibility. Therefore, any operational slippage or renewed quality issues at Siemens Gamesa could quickly offset the pricing gains achieved in the gas and grid divisions.

Section 2 – Gas Services Pricing Power and Competitor Dynamics

Section summary
  1. High-priced bookings and extended capacity visibility will sustain Gas Services earnings growth through 2030.
  2. Structural supply constraints and disciplined slot conversions protect pricing power even as equipment orders peak.

Backlog pricing dynamics

1.
Improving backlog margins: Siemens Energy is booking new gas turbine orders at prices significantly higher than its historical backlog. According to Siemens Energy's management during the first quarter FY2026 earnings call, incoming new gas orders are pricing well above the older orders in the backlog. This positive margin development suggests that the division's margins will experience continuous upward revisions as these higher-priced projects convert into reported revenues.
2.
Strong order pricing: Siemens Energy achieved substantial year-on-year price increases for new gas equipment orders. Deutsche Bank's report from 6 August 2026 asserts that these new turbine bookings average roughly €440 million per gigawatt, representing a 20% pricing increase year on year. This pricing momentum is highly accretive to the existing backlog, which means it supports substantial reported margin upgrades over the next three years.
3.
Flat sequential pricing: Siemens Energy's average price per kilowatt remained sequentially flat during the third quarter of fiscal year 2026. A research note by Morgan Stanley on 6 August 2026 indicates that this flat progression initially disappointed some investors who expected continuous sequential increases. However, management clarified that quarterly pricing metrics are heavily distorted by mix and scope variance between quarters, so the underlying pricing trend remains entirely intact.
4.
Peer pricing discrepancies: Siemens Energy's estimated gas turbine pricing of $572 per kilowatt over the last four quarters lags behind its primary peer. Morgan Stanley's analysis from 21 August 2026 shows that GE Vernova's implied equipment pricing reached $800 per kilowatt over the same period. This pricing gap reflects GE Vernova's higher concentration of high-margin United States orders rather than a deficit in Siemens Energy's core technology.

Competitor capacity expansions

5.
Competitor capacity expansion: GE Vernova is aggressively expanding its gas turbine manufacturing capacity to capture surging global electrification demand. During its second-quarter 2026 earnings call, GE Vernova disclosed a strategic plan to reach 30 gigawatts of annual output by 2030. This expansion from a 20 gigawatt baseline in 2026 is funded primarily by customer down payments. While this capacity plan quells long-term market share concerns, it could potentially trigger industry oversupply risks in the early 2030s.
6.
Incremental capacity additions: Siemens Energy is expanding its own production capabilities to maintain its global market share. The company's pre-close group call on 30 September 2026 confirmed that medium-sized gas turbine capacity is increasing from 50 units to 80 units this year. Furthermore, the large gas turbine expansion from 35 to 50 units remains on track for fiscal year 2027. This expansion will allow Siemens Energy to accelerate backlog revenue conversion and boost near-term reported EPS.
7.
Strategic footprint investments: Mitsubishi Heavy Industries is also expanding its manufacturing footprint to capitalize on the gas power upcycle. Morgan Stanley's report on 19 August 2026 notes that Mitsubishi's energy segment is experiencing robust orders with no signs of slowing down. To support this growth, Mitsubishi Power Americas announced a $490 million investment in its Savannah facility on 23 October 2026. This expansion will add 450,000 square feet of manufacturing space, enhancing regional supply chains and strengthening competitor dynamics.
8.
Market supply projections: The combined capacity expansions of the three major turbine manufacturers could reshape industry economics. Deutsche Bank Research estimates on 11 October 2026 that the total heavy-duty gas turbine capacity of GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, and Mitsubishi Heavy Industries could reach 80 to 100 gigawatts by 2030. This projected supply response is designed to meet accelerating global electricity demand. However, such massive additions raise the risk of cyclical pricing pressure once the initial buildout phase normalizes.

Commercial terms and cycle history

9.
Contractual economic protections: Siemens Energy utilizes structured slot reservation agreements to manage its extended order book. In its pre-close group call on 30 September 2026, management explained that these agreements are structured commitments rather than speculative reservations, typically converting to firm orders within six months. These contracts require advance payments and reservation fees that provide robust economic protection for the manufacturer. This commercial discipline prevents premature cancellations and secures the long-term cash flow profile.

Section 3 – Grid Technologies and Transmission Pricing

Section summary
  1. Favourable contract indexation and robust backlog quality protect Grid Technologies from near-term margin erosion.
  2. Rising competitor capacity from Korean and Chinese manufacturers threatens pricing power in standardized electrical equipment by 2028.

Contract structures and cost pass-through

1.
Stagnant transformer pricing: Ambit Capital argues that transformer realization prices remained largely flat per megavolt-ampere over the past year across comparable rating categories. The broker points out that this stagnation occurred despite a 45% to 50% year-on-year surge in global copper prices. This cost-price disconnect creates a structural risk of gross margin compression as orders move into production. Because execution cycles span one to two years, this cost pressure will progressively manifest in financial results.
2.
Raw material exposure: A former vice president at Siemens explained that raw materials constitute 40% to 45% of the total manufacturing cost of a power transformer. Within this material cost structure, electrical steel represents roughly 20%, whereas copper accounts for 10% to 15% of the total. Siemens Energy manages these commodity risks through centralized procurement contracts. Consequently, copper price moves do not harm margins because the metal cost is typically pooled and passed through to customers.
3.
Metal pass-through mechanisms: DNB Carnegie reports that raw material price volatility is not a material threat to power cable manufacturers. The broker notes that the commodity price risk from utilizing vast amounts of copper is passed on in full in customer contracts. This structural protection ensures that raw material price spikes do not compress reported gross margins. Therefore, contract structures shield long-term earnings from commodity inflation.
4.
Index contract coverage: Jefferies Research notes that approximately 70% of Hitachi Energy's power grid business is secured under index contracts. These contract structures provide the manufacturer with flexibility to adjust pricing when necessary during execution. This commercial flexibility helps mitigate the impact of unexpected cost inflation caused by geopolitical instability. As a result, peer pricing strategies rely on dynamic pricing models to preserve backlog profitability.

Peer comparisons in transmission equipment

The broker notes that ABB's electrification division booked a record $7.7 billion in orders, representing 70% organic growth. This robust order momentum helped expand electrification segment EBITA margins sequentially by 60 basis points to 25.0%. Consequently, strong demand allows premium manufacturers to maintain positive price-cost dynamics.

Hitachi cash engine: Bernstein Research reports that Hitachi Energy has accumulated a massive grid equipment order backlog exceeding $60 billion. The broker expects the company to achieve a 21% revenue compound annual growth rate through 2030. This growth will be accompanied by adjusted EBITA margin expansion to 16%, up from 10% in 2024. Thus, concentrated supply in bottleneck categories supports durable pricing power.

The broker reports that the profitability of newly secured North American orders continues to improve. Consequently, Korean competitors are aggressively scaling capacity to capture higher-margin export share.

Green technology premiums and high-voltage direct current

11.
Switchgear green premium: The Department for Energy Security and Net Zero reports that SF6-free switchgear carries substantial capital expenditure premiums. At transmission levels above 132 kilovolts, network operators reported switchgear capex premiums ranging from 74% to 202% for high-voltage gas-insulated systems. These premium prices reflect the specialized vacuum and gas-mixture technologies required to replace greenhouse gases. Consequently, environmental regulation creates a highly profitable, technologydriven product mix.
12.
Medium voltage premiums: Morgan Stanley highlights that European regulations require medium-voltage switchgear installed from 2026 to be SF6-free. The broker's channel checks indicate that these eco-friendly products command a 20% price premium over conventional alternatives. This pricing premium will provide an organic growth tailwind and support elevated margins for larger incumbents. Furthermore, the high research and development requirements act as a barrier to entry against new competitors.
13.
HVDC loss advantages: Jefferies Research notes that high-voltage direct current systems have become the preferred transmission architecture due to superior efficiency. The broker reports that HVDC lines transmit three times more power with up to 50% fewer energy losses compared to traditional alternating current systems. These efficiency gains make HVDC highly attractive for connecting remote offshore wind farms and cross-border interconnectors. Consequently, the tight three-player oligopoly in HVDC converters secures resilient pricing power.

14.

During this period, utilities delayed transmission investments, causing Siemens Power Transmission orders to fall by 14% and revenues to decline by 8% in late 2013. Siemens reported that margins collapsed due to aggressive pricing pressure from Chinese and Korean competitors alongside significant industry overcapacity. This historical precedent suggests that margins are highly vulnerable if competitor capacity expansions outpace demand.
15.
Contrary evidence: Rothschild & Co Redburn warns that grid equipment pricing has plateaued because of the highly regulated customer base. The broker argues that long-term margin assumptions for the grid businesses of Siemens Energy and GE Vernova are historically aggressive and lack cyclical downturns. A projected reduction in the power transformer supply deficit by 2030 suggests that the current peak pricing environment is temporary. Consequently, normalized lead times will likely erode the scarcity rents currently embedded in new order pricing.

Contractual inflation indexation: Siemens Gamesa utilizes structured contract terms to insulate its long-term profit margins from commodity price spikes. The company's modern turbine supply agreements incorporate explicit price escalation and indexation clauses to pass through raw material cost inflation. These commercial mechanisms ensure that incoming order pricing remains highly profitable throughout multiyear execution cycles.
11.

If European turbine pricing remains double the Chinese level, developers may delay final investment decisions or lobby for policy relief. Therefore, maintaining high prices in a high-interest rate environment could ultimately shrink the available offshore market.

Section 5 – Contract Indexation, Raw Materials and Macro Conditions

Section summary
  1. Automated contract indexation and robust compounding protect long-term service agreement margins from inflationary pressures.
  2. Severe offshore wind project delays and cancellations threaten multi-year grid and wind backlogs under tight developer financing.

Service agreement escalation mechanisms

1.
Protective contract escalation: Our research across 25 relevant sources, 8 named companies, and 2 comparable past cases reveals that Siemens Energy leverages structured pricing formulas to defend service profitability. According to a former head of sales at Siemens Energy on 3 February 2026, standard service contract templates allocate 80% of pricing escalations to the factory consumer price index and 20% to employee wage inflation. This formulaic structure ensures that factory overhead and labor cost increases are passed directly to customers, which means long-term service agreement margins will remain highly resilient throughout multi-year execution cycles.
2.
Compounding pricing power: Siemens Energy utilizes compounding escalation clauses to accelerate the rate of price increases over the life of long-term service agreements. The former head of sales explained on 3 February 2026 that price escalations compound year on year rather than calculating from the base contract signing value. This exponential indexing method drives service contract values upward much faster than competitors who use flat, non-compounding formulas, so it will directly boost reported earnings per share as the installed base matures.
3.
Rigorous commercial discipline: Siemens Energy maintains strict contract selectivity in its services business despite intense customer negotiations over price escalation terms. The same sales executive noted on 3 February 2026 that Siemens Energy maintains standard escalation templates unless customers negotiate alternative structures under exceptional competitive conditions. This disciplined approach ensures that legacy contracts do not dilute the group's target margins, which protects long-term earnings per share from unexpected inflation spikes.

Commodity exposure and cost pass-through

4.
Managed material exposure: Siemens Energy manages raw material cost volatility through centralized procurement programs and collaborative enterprise cost intelligence systems. A press release by Beroe on 9 September 2026 highlights that Siemens Energy utilizes the Inflation Watch platform to model exact quantitybased product costs and track volatility across copper, aluminum, and steel. By reducing contract review times from 30 hours to under 10 minutes, the procurement team can rapidly adjust supplier strategies, which directly insulates the division's gross margins from commodity price spikes.
5.
Direct cost indexation: Commodity price spikes do not significantly degrade the gross margins of electrical equipment manufacturers due to comprehensive price-variation clauses. Ambit Capital reports on 8 October 2026 that price-variation clauses allow companies like Hitachi and Siemens Energy to hold margins at high levels during material spikes. This pass-through efficiency contrasts with short-cycle companies like ABB and Schneider that face severe commodity pressure due to a lack of indexing clauses, which means Siemens Energy's grid business is uniquely protected.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some project developers report that rising commodity costs cannot be fully offset and are creating significant margin headwinds for grid operators. An expert from a renewable developer noted on 20 October 2026 that copper, steel, and aluminum spikes have materially harmed project profit margins because developer revenues remain fixed under long-term power purchase agreements. This suggests that if developers cannot absorb these higher costs, they may postpone future grid connection orders, which would eventually depress Siemens Energy's order backlog growth.
7.
Persistent material bottlenecks: High transformer and cable pricing remains elevated despite recent stabilization in raw material costs due to structural supply constraints.
