[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Nexans is a completed self-help case riding a structural grid tailwind, priced as fair and returning too little. Seven years of selling everything that was not electrification cable have taken the adjusted EBITDA margin from 6.1 percent in 2020 to 11.9 percent in 2025 and ROCE from 10.2 percent to 21.3 percent, while the sector has sold out high-voltage subsea capacity to the end of the decade; at €133.2 the shares pay the stock's own median multiple for that, and on our path they return about 13 percent a year against the 20 percent bar. The case becomes compelling below about €120, or if the company's own 2028 target proves right.

  • The external change is structural, sector-wide and dated beyond the horizon, and it has moved pricing power to the cable maker.
    • Grid, interconnector and data-centre demand is policy-backed and shows in order books, not only in forecasts, which is why we treat it as a demand force with two-plus years of visibility rather than a cycle.
    • The EU Grids Package targets €530bn to €540bn of grid investment from 2025 to 2031, about €77bn a year
      • TenneT plans €65bn to 2029
      • Germany's capacity act of 10 July 2026 adds 11 GW of dispatchable capacity.
    • High-voltage subsea capacity is booked to the end of the decade
      • Nexans's adjusted backlog was €7.7bn at the end of H1 2026, NKT's €13.5bn.
    • Average revenue per kilometre for 525 kV HVDC cable doubled to €1.44m for 2024 to 2026 awards from €0.62m in 2019 to 2020: the price, not the volume, is where the shortage shows.
    • The force does not reach all of Nexans: Connect, 42 percent of H1 2026 standard sales, is short-cycle, and its Nordic residential part is "deeply subdued with no near-term signs of recovery".
  • The internal change is broad, real and mostly executed; what is left is integration rather than transformation.
    • Seven of the twelve internal forces are at B or better, and the screen's decision rule is met twice over, so this is a genuine multi-dimensional change rather than a single event.
    • The perimeter is complete: AmerCable ($280m enterprise value, January 2025), Lynxeo (€525m, June 2025) and Autoelectric (€207m, 3 July 2026, about €708m of 2025 current sales) are sold
      • Republic Wire (€680m enterprise value, June 2026) and Electro Cables (December 2025) build North America to about €1bn of current sales.
    • A new operator chief executive since 13 October 2025, a new finance director from 19 January 2026 and a twelve-member executive committee from 15 January 2026
      • the board is 80 percent independent after Quiñenco's exit to 4.13 percent.
    • Capacity is coming on: the Electra vessel from June 2026, booked to end-2028
      • European Grid capacity plus about 40 percent by 2028, landing from late 2026.
    • Two of the screen's red flags are live and we carry them: the July 2026 guidance rise is "almost entirely" Republic Wire, and Republic Wire's 10.3x entry multiple is a 40 percent premium to Nexans's own.
  • The one overhang of 2026 has largely cleared, and the price has not moved on it.
    • The €1.2bn Great Sea Interconnector delay and the risk of an idle Halden plant took the shares down about 17 percent in the first half of 2026
    • both have been answered, so the price today is not a scare price.
    • A replacement Mediterranean contract at preferred-bidder stage loads the mass-impregnated line to mid-2028
      • signature is expected in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027.
    • Meridiam took 66 percent of the Great Sea Interconnector on 5 August 2026 and seabed surveys resumed under a tripartite agreement.
    • The shares are down 6.2 percent over three months and up 3.6 percent over twelve, while the four cable peers are up 38 to 106 percent: the good news has been absorbed, not rewarded.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up in margin and cash, not in revenue or earnings per share.
    • This is the finding that shapes the whole case: the forces are converted into price and margin by choice, so the pattern's payoff, an upgrade to the earnings trajectory, has not arrived.
    • Adjusted EBITDA margin on standard sales 9.1 percent in FY2022, 10.3 percent in FY2024, 11.9 percent in FY2025 and 11.9 percent again in H1 2026
      • free cash flow €344m in 2025 (47.3 percent of adjusted EBITDA) and €165.5m in H1 2026 against a consensus of €70m.
    • Organic growth of standard sales fell from +8.3 percent in 2025 to +1.5 percent in H1 2026, with Transmission at -0.1 percent
      • the 2024 to 2028 target is 3 to 5 percent a year.
    • Consensus earnings per share fall 11 percent in FY2026 (7.19 against the FY2025 actual of 8.08) on perimeter, depreciation and acquisition interest, then rise 28 percent in FY2027 and 22 percent in FY2028
      • the three-year compound rate from FY2025 is about 11 percent a year on consensus and about 10 percent on our path, below the 12 to 20 percent band.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain once, for the 2018 to 2025 phase; the 2026 to 2028 phase is at the input-plus-early-output stage.
    • The market is being asked to pay for the second phase on the record of the first, which is why the fit to the pattern is a C and not a B.
    • The first phase reached the estimates: the sell-side raised its long-term margin expectations 100 basis points above the company's initial targets.
    • The second phase (US integration, Grid capacity, Transmission margin to 17 percent) has one half-year of numbers: Transmission margin +195 basis points to 13.7 percent on flat sales
      • Connect margin -180 basis points to 11.8 percent
      • no synergy disclosure yet.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share on the brokers' basis of 8.08 in FY2025 falling to about 7.10 in FY2026, then 8.80 in FY2027 and 10.70 in FY2028, about 9.8 percent a year over three years
    • consensus is 7.19, 9.18 and 11.15.
    • We sit 1 to 4 percent below consensus in each year because we take adjusted EBITDA of about €810m, €920m and €1,010m against consensus €817m, €945m and €1,038m and the company's 2028 target of €1,150m plus or minus €75m
      • the differences are the Nordic drag on Connect, Grid capacity not landing before late 2026, and Transmission reaching about 16 percent rather than 17 percent.
    • The 24-month-forward figure the multiple is applied to grows from €10.23 today to €11.95 in March 2028 and €13.38 in September 2029 on our path.
  • What it costs: €133.2, or 12.4x consensus 24-month-forward earnings of €10.78, the 51st percentile of the stock's own ten-year range and the cheapest of the four cable makers by 1 to 5 turns (Hexatronic 13.3x, NKT 16.0x, Cenergy 16.4x, Prysmian 17.6x).
    • Against its own history there is little to re-rate: the ten-year median is 12.30x, the 75th percentile 13.80x, the 90th 14.80x
      • the discount to peers is the whole re-rating opportunity, and the peers are at the 26th to 91st percentiles of their own histories.
    • What the price assumes: consensus FY2028 earnings, the contract signing, Transmission at about 16 percent and a Connect recovery to 13 percent.
      • What it does not assume: the company's own €1,150m for 2028, or any closing of the peer discount.
  • The return does not clear the bar at this price, and the re-rating is the swing factor.
    • At a blended 13.2x on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, plus the dividends, the shares are worth €160.8 in March 2028 and €186.9 in September 2029: total returns of 20.7 percent and 40.3 percent, or 13.4 percent and 12.0 percent a year.
    • Earnings growth carries the return (about 17 and 31 percentage points of the 21 and 40)
      • the re-rating adds 1.5 percent on the blend
      • the cash return is about 2.3 percent over eighteen months.
    • At 14.8x, the branch where the contract signs and FY2027 earnings meet consensus, the eighteen-month rate is 22 percent a year
      • at 11.2x, where the stock traded in the GSI scare, it is 2 percent a year.
    • On the consensus path at the same 13.2x the return is about 23 percent a year, a B.
      • The whole gap between our C and consensus's B is our lower Connect margin and our lower FY2028 base.
  • The chain is thin below EBITDA, and that is where it breaks.
    • An ordinary adverse turn in depreciation, interest, tax and share count takes FY2028 earnings per share from 10.70 to about 9.5 and the eighteen-month return to about 5 percent a year, an F
    • a 10 percent fall in the Norwegian krone would take another 6 percent off.
    • The margin over the bar is thin enough that the fragility is itself a finding.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', not crisply.
    • The inputs fit (a completed multi-dimensional transformation with a chief-executive change and a guidance increase)
    • the output the pattern names, major upgrades to the earnings trajectory, has not arrived (FY2028 consensus +0.3 percent over twelve months).
    • 'Demand Inflection' is the secondary fit: the sector inflects, and the company by choice does not carry the volume.

  • The case is therefore a fair business at a fair price with a C return.

    • We park it at €133.2.
    • It becomes a B below about €122 on the eighteen-month path and about €108 on the three-year path, or if the full-year 2026 results in late February 2027 print Transmission at 15 percent or better with Connect stable and the Mediterranean contract signed.

  • Rating = C, because the change forces are strong (B) and have already travelled the whole chain once, but the financial outputs are improving in margin and cash rather than in revenue and earnings per share (C), and the 'Modal return' at €133.2 is about 13 percent a year over eighteen months and 12 percent over three years (C, with the three-year figure at the D anchor): the parts combine so that the return binds.

    • The forces are material
    • the price has already paid for the part of them that consensus believes, and the part it does not believe, the company's own 2028 target, is the part we also do not take as modal.
    • Not B, because a B needs 20 percent a year and we cannot reach it on our path with a multiple inside the stock's own ten-year range
    • not D, because the eighteen-month return sits at the C anchor and the earnings path is largely contracted.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: Nexans is a cyclical at a record margin dressed as a structural compounder.
    • The 11.9 percent margin of 2025 and the 13.7 percent Transmission margin of H1 2026 were earned while high-voltage capacity was sold out and buyers paid down-payments to secure slots.
    • Nexans, Prysmian, NKT and Asian entrants are all adding plant and vessels for 2027 to 2030
      • Hellenic Cables was certified for 525 kV subsea in 2026
      • LS Cable is building a €220m cable-laying vessel for H1 2028
      • a former Nexans Norway vice-president warns of structural overcapacity after 2030.
    • The uncontracted parts of the business are already normalising: Connect's margin fell 180 basis points in a year, a former Prysmian finance director expects low-voltage margins to fall from recent highs, and the July guidance raise was an acquisition, not organic.
    • Consensus FY2028 EBITDA of €1,038m already sits €112m below the company's target
      • Deutsche Bank's own FY2028 is €933m and Bernstein's stacked downside about €900m
      • the company itself says accelerated M&A is needed to bridge the gap.
    • The base rate is against the buyer: on Nexans's execution stumbles the bears have been right in the short term 80 percent of the time, with 30 to 50 percent drawdowns.
      • The architect of the turnaround left abruptly having sold shares, and the team that remains has never run this business through a downcycle.
    • The risks research's "false friend" profile is the winner of its own analysis of competing hypotheses: the top line will look fine (copper, Southern European volume, US consolidation) while the margin erodes underneath, and the stock at 12.4x is not cheap for that.
  • What we concede.
    • The Connect margin is the weakest line in the case and we do not model a Nordic recovery before 2027
    • our FY2027 and FY2028 EBITDA of €920m and €1,010m sit below consensus for that reason.
    • The margin cycle is the industry's, and its normalisation after 2028 is the largest risk beyond the horizon.
    • The 2028 target is not our modal case.
    • The below-EBITDA chain makes the case fragile.
    • And the price at the stock's own median multiple already assumes the contract signs and consensus is right, so the modal return from here is a C, which is the competing hypothesis's own conclusion reached by a different road.
  • Why we still hold a C rather than a D.
    • The business is not at a cyclical peak inside the horizon: the €7.7bn backlog is contracted through 2028, about two-thirds of Grid revenue is under multi-year frameworks, the replacement contract fills the one identified capacity hole, and the three European makers have shown the same margin-over-volume conduct for three years
    • a peak needs volume to fall or price to break, and neither is in the numbers before 2029.
    • The transformation is real and not optical: ROCE doubled while capex rose, the customer base was cut from 20,000 to 4,000 accounts, and the improvement survived a chief-executive change with a beat-and-raise first half.
    • The downside is a re-rating, not a collapse: leverage 1.4x on fixed-rate debt with no maturities before 2027, a trough multiple of 11.2x worth about -9 percent and a ten-year 10th percentile of 8.9x worth about -28 percent on unchanged earnings.
  • Where we do not hold our view: on the return.
    • The competing hypothesis says do not buy this at 12.4x, and on the modal path we agree, for the arithmetic reason (a C) rather than the structural one (a peak).
    • The case for a B rather than a C rests on the same facts read the other way, consensus rather than the company being right about 2028.
    • The disagreement is about C against D and C against B, not about the direction of the business.
  • Where the case against would win, and when.
    • The disagreement is dated.
    • If the full-year 2026 results in February 2027 show Transmission at 15 percent or better and Connect stable, the peak thesis loses its evidence and the case becomes a B at a price that may still be near this one
    • if Connect prints below 11 percent, the "false friend" thesis gains its evidence and the case is a D.

External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because grid, interconnector and data-centre demand is a structural tailwind with two-plus years of visibility (adjusted backlog €7.7bn at the end of H1 2026) and policy is underwriting it, offset by a live headwind in Nordic residential construction and by capacity additions that could normalise pricing from 2028.
  • Grid policy: a structural tailwind.
    • The EU Grids Package targets €530bn to €540bn of grid investment from 2025 to 2031, about €77bn a year
    • TenneT plans €65bn to 2029
    • Germany's capacity act of 10 July 2026 adds 11 GW of dispatchable capacity
    • the EU Electrification Action Plan of July 2026 targets a 46 percent electricity share by 2040.
  • Data-centre demand: a cyclical tailwind with visibility.
    • European hyperscale data-centre inquiries are moving from 50 MW to gigawatt scale
    • average revenue per kilometre for 525 kV HVDC doubled to €1.44m for 2024 to 2026 awards from €0.62m in 2019 to 2020.
  • Nordic residential: a cyclical headwind.
    • Nordic residential building is "deeply subdued with no near-term signs of recovery"
    • the Connect margin was 11.8 percent in H1 2026 against 13.6 percent in H1 2025.
  • Net direction: a tailwind for Transmission and Grid, about 45 percent of H1 2026 standard sales
    • a headwind for the Nordic part of Connect
    • neutral for Southern Europe.
  • Not A, because the company's own revenue does not show the force (organic +1.5 percent in H1 2026; a 2024 to 2028 organic target of 3 to 5 percent a year)
    • the force is real, but Nexans converts it into margin rather than growth.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B

Congruence: The congruence judgement: does the whole hang together across past, business, value chain and market environment; where ratings disagree, why (Part 4, E 3, BB 4)
  • The whole hangs together, and it hangs together as a C.
  • With the past: the expected margin expansion is the continuation of a trend the company has shown for seven years (6.1 percent to 11.9 percent; ROCE doubled; every 2021 capital-markets-day KPI met), so the change is plausible

  • With the business: the Transmission and Grid parts of the thesis rely on strengths the business demonstrably has (selectivity, integration from rod to vessel, frameworks, pass-through), and the Republic Wire part and the upper half of the 2028 target rely on strengths it has not shown (competing at scale in US low voltage; holding a peer margin).

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Capacity-constrained high-voltage oligopoly

    • Utilities have moved from two-year to six-and-seven-year frame agreements and pay down-payments to secure slots.

  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Loss of subsea mega-tenders after mid-2028 to capacity-expanding peers
    • Nexans, Prysmian, NKT and Asian entrants are all adding plant and vessels for 2027 to 2030, and the mass-impregnated line has no visible award beyond mid-2028.
