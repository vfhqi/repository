Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The coffee segment's financial trajectory determines if investors have one or two viable entry points into premium coffee.
  2. Evaluating segment scales and margins resolves whether Groupe SEB represents a mispriced early turnaround opportunity.

Investment Playbook and Ownership

1.
Strategic inquiry: "How large are the professional coffee machine businesses and the household fully automatic espresso businesses of Groupe SEB and De'Longhi, in sales, growth and operating margin, and what share of each group's profit do they earn? What is driving demand from cafés, hotels and offices for professional machines, and from households in Europe, North America and Asia for automatic machines, and how long do brokers and experts expect it to last? How quickly are competitors closing the reliability gap, and is the retail price ceiling of about EUR 1,000 for mass-market automatic machines holding? Why does Groupe SEB trade at about 6 times expected earnings, and how do brokers value its coffee businesses against the rest of the group?"
2.
Divergent pathways: Investors must determine if the premium coffee expansion offers two distinct entry points or just one pure-play vehicle. De'Longhi represents a highly visible coffee-centric operator where home coffee accounts for 56% of total revenue and professional coffee makes up another 13% of sales. Conversely, Groupe SEB operates as a diversified appliance giant where professional coffee is a highly profitable division but represents only a smaller slice of a much larger household product portfolio. Understanding segment sizes will reveal if both companies provide viable pathways to own the coffee theme.
3.
Strategic entry: Determining if Groupe SEB represents an early way into the premium coffee category is crucial for stock selection. The group is currently executing its new Rebound Plan launched at the end of 2025 to restore overall margins. If its coffee innovation and cheaper automatic products can successfully recruit a new generation of consumers, the stock could re-rate early in its recovery cycle. However, persistent equipment investment delays from large commercial clients in key markets could stall this turnaround and penalize early buyers.
4.
Valuation disconnect: Addressing the valuation gap between these European appliance giants highlights the market's mispricing. Groupe SEB trades at a depressed multiple of approximately 6 times expected earnings following historical downward revisions. In contrast, De'Longhi continues to command premium multiples due to its higher concentration of coffee pure-play assets. Uncovering whether brokers value SEB's coffee business as a separate high-growth entity represents a closely related valuation puzzle for investors.

Margin Longevity and Risks

5.
Margin durability: The sustainability of professional coffee margins is the central driver of long-term group profitability. Professional divisions historically generate operating margins exceeding 15% once fully operational, which is double the profit margin of typical consumer appliance segments. This superior profitability faces potential erosion if competitors close the reliability gap or if retail price ceilings on automatic machines start to fracture. Evaluating these competitive pressures will determine how long this highly lucrative margin advantage can persist before consolidating.
6.
Other side of the story: Skeptics argue that Groupe SEB's structural valuation disconnect is justified and cannot be easily unlocked by its coffee segment. The professional coffee business has continued to disappoint with organic sales dropping 9.1% in the third quarter of 2026 due to prolonged commercial renewal delays. Given that traditional kitchen cookware and small appliances still dominate the group's sales mix, the high-margin coffee division may remain too small to drive group re-rating independently.

Section 2 – Segment Size, Growth, and Profit Contribution

Section summary
  1. De'Longhi achieved substantial financial growth driven by the rapid expansion of its high-margin professional coffee segment in 2025 and 2026.
  2. Groupe SEB faced near-term cyclical headwinds in professional coffee that depressed margins despite stable household appliance sales.
  3. Structural margin advantages in professional coffee equipment continue to outpace traditional consumer segments across both major companies.

De'Longhi Segment Scale and Growth

1.
Robust revenue growth: De'Longhi achieved significant top-line expansion in its consolidated operations during fiscal year 2025. According to the De'Longhi annual report published on March 13, 2026, the group recorded total consolidated revenues of €3,801.5 million, representing a year-over-year increase of 8.7% at current exchange rates. This robust performance was heavily supported by the home coffee segment, which accounted for approximately 56% of total group revenues. This structural momentum indicates that high-end coffee remains a resilient growth engine for the group's earnings per share.
2.
Strong professional acceleration: De'Longhi experienced an extraordinary surge in its professional coffee segment during the 2025 fiscal year. The De'Longhi 2025 annual report released on March 13, 2026, showed that the professional coffee division generated 12.8% of the group's total turnover. This performance represents an impressive pro-forma growth of 32.0% compared to the prior year period. This acceleration should structurally enhance earnings per share as high-margin products expand.
3.
Accelerated interim expansion: De'Longhi continued its remarkable growth trajectory into the first half of fiscal year 2026. According to the De'Longhi interim financial report published on July 30, 2026, first-half revenues for the professional division grew by 36.3% to €302.8 million. This segment momentum was further confirmed during the company's interim operations report, which highlighted that constant-currency revenue growth in this professional division reached 40.0% during the same six-month period. Consistent execution in this high-margin segment is expected to drive substantial earnings per share growth over the next year.
4.
Steady household performance: De'Longhi recorded a moderate but highly stable growth profile in its household division during the first half of 2026. The De'Longhi interim financial report published on July 30, 2026, indicated that household division revenues reached €1,379.8 million, representing a modest increase of 1.2% at current exchange rates. This stabilization followed early-year channel destocking and significant currency headwinds, as noted in the group's quarterly earnings release. This household resilience supports a solid baseline for overall group earnings per share.

Section 5 – Valuation Disconnect and Sum-of-the-Parts Analysis

Section summary
  1. Groupe SEB trades at a severe valuation discount compared to historical averages and pure-play coffee peers like De'Longhi.
  2. The market applies a conglomerate discount that fails to reflect the high-margin profile of the professional coffee business.
  3. Executing the Rebound Plan and stabilizing commercial equipment demand remain critical to unlocking a comprehensive valuation re-rating.

Depressed Valuation Multiples and Derating

1.
Depressed trading: Groupe SEB trades at highly depressed valuation multiples that stand near historical troughs and below its closest consumer peers.
