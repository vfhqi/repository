EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report investigates how Hexatronic Group AB's share price and forward price-to-earnings multiple have moved relative to its optical and fibre cohort, European and Swedish small-cap indices, and Nordic hardware peers on a rolling six-month basis from 2022 to October 2026, while examining which company-specific and cohort events moved the shares idiosyncratically and how the stock's liquidity and thin coverage affect its sector co-movement.

The analysis reveals that Hexatronic experienced extreme multiple compression during this period, shifting from a premium valuation exceeding 25x forward earnings during the 2022 fiber boom to trading at a 35 percent discount to its peer group by late 2025, before recovering to approximately 9x forward EBITA for 2027 estimates by October 2026. Hexatronic's valuation premium during the early 2023 fiber-to-the-home expansion phase saw its enterprise value to EBIT multiple stand at 29.4x for 2022 before compressing to 18.5x in 2023, while by October 2025 the company traded at a 35 percent valuation discount to its peer group for 2025e and 2026e.

This valuation trajectory reflects both the company's exposure to cyclical European fiber-to-the-home rollouts and its strategic pivot toward higher-margin Data Center and Harsh Environment segments, which accounted for 40 percent of Q2 2026 net sales and almost 50 percent of adjusted EBITA. The compression was amplified by structural headwinds including elevated interest rates, regional construction slowdowns, and temporary fiber overcapacity in European markets.

Idiosyncratic catalysts created significant stock price volatility independent of broader sector trends. Hexatronic delivered record group sales of SEK 2,498m in Q3 2026, growing 33 percent year-over-year and surpassing consensus by 9 percent, while adjusted EBITA reached SEK 281m, exceeding forecasts by 16 percent. The company's September 2025 target rebasing refocused corporate goals toward segment-specific objectives, while the May 2026 directed share issue raised SEK 600m by issuing 15,789,474 shares at SEK 38 each, resulting in 7 percent dilution for existing shareholders.

The operational reversal at the Clinton plant, where Hexatronic reversed its decision to downsize following a strong rebound in US Fiber Solutions and transport networks demonstrated management's responsiveness to changing regional demand dynamics. These company-specific developments generated short-term price movements that temporarily decoupled Hexatronic from broader optical cohort trends.

Cohort spillover effects dominated relative performance during major sector events. Corning's second-quarter earnings on July 28, 2026, triggered a massive sell-off across optical networking stocks because its guidance offered no upside, with Corning shares plunging by nearly 20 percent despite beating near-term estimates. This market reaction immediately dragged down other photonics and fiber infrastructure providers, with shares of Lumentum, Marvell, and Coherent falling between 8 and 12 percent in sympathy.

The October 2026 "AI monetisation anxiety" sell-off reflected rising concerns over hyperscaler capital expenditure productivity, while Corning's carrier growth slowed sharply to 1 percent from 36 percent in the prior quarter, underscoring sequential revenue lumpiness risks. These sector-wide liquidations frequently overwhelmed idiosyncratic positive developments at Hexatronic, highlighting the stock's high beta to global optical sentiment.

Market microstructure dynamics exacerbated volatility and shaped relative performance patterns. Accendo Capital's ownership stake declined to 4.38 percent in October 2026 from 5.81 percent in October 2025, while Handelsbanken Fonder maintained a 5.81 percent ownership stake, down from 9.34 percent in late 2025. Hexatronic's 30-day average daily trading volume stood at 1.29 million shares, representing daily turnover of roughly USD 5 million at a share price of approximately SEK 39.

This restricted liquidity means large institutional orders trigger outsized price movements, while limited independent broker coverage often leads to retail-driven price inefficiency, and when global giants like Corning or Prysmian report earnings, Hexatronic's shares frequently experience exaggerated sympathy moves. The absence of a single controlling shareholder with a voting stake exceeding 20 percent reduces long-term stewardship and increases vulnerability to speculative trading during sector rotations.

Synthesis of evidence reveals that Hexatronic's relative valuation and share performance are governed by the interplay of three forces: strategic business mix transformation, global optical cohort sentiment, and Nordic small-cap liquidity constraints. Hexatronic's Q3 2026 interim report revealed that Data Center and Harsh Environment contributed 44 percent of sales, up from 35 percent in Q3 2025, meaning the group is rapidly decoupling from cyclical European FTTH rollouts.

However, the stock's thin coverage and moderate liquidity ensure that near-term price action remains tightly coupled to US optical peers during sector-wide events. The forward earnings trajectory depends critically on whether hyperscaler capital expenditure sustains its momentum, whether European transport network upgrades materialize with the expected two-year lag, and whether management can execute bolt-on acquisitions without excessive dilution.

Key information gaps include precise institutional lock-up tracking following the May 2026 placement, forward visibility on North American data center project pipelines beyond the rolling four-month order book, and the timing of European transport infrastructure upgrade cycles. If the optical infrastructure cycle diverges from consensus expectations through faster-than-anticipated Chinese preform capacity additions or hyperscaler purchasing pivots toward vertically integrated suppliers, Hexatronic's earnings volatility will intensify due to its rapid transition toward capitalintensive segments and structural reliance on debt-funded inorganic growth.
