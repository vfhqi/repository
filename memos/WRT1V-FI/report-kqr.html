Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The off-grid power paradigm shifts data centre capital allocation from utilities to reciprocating engine, heat exchanger, and pump manufacturers.

This trend accelerates 18-month to 3-year EPS growth for equipment suppliers and drives valuation multiple expansion on secular growth visibility.

The core investment question regarding whether data centres facing grid delays will purchase independent onsite power plants, heat exchangers, and water infrastructure is resolvable with high confidence across the reciprocating engine and thermal management dimensions, though water infrastructure remains partially resolvable due to municipal permitting opacity.

Surging off-grid data centre power demand drives unprecedented order backlogs for reciprocating engines as developers bypass lengthy utility interconnection queues, while equipment manufacturers are rapidly expanding manufacturing capacity to capture this secular prime power opportunity. On the on-site generation front, global data center power requirements are projected to reach 18 GW in 2026, representing a near forty percent annual increase, with reciprocating engines representing thirty-five gigawatts of engine capacity dedicated specifically to the data center sector.

Unprecedented demand has extended equipment lead times significantly, with large gas reciprocating engines now sold out through late 2028, while Wartsila has secured over two gigawatts of orders for data center power plants in the United States alone.

The structural pivot to high-density AI data centers accelerates heat exchanger content per megawatt while expanding thermal management opportunities across both white-space liquid cooling and BTM reciprocating engine prime power loops, with equipment vendors capturing expanding shipset values across the data center thermal chain. Regarding heat exchanger intensity, direct liquid cooling architectures raise total cooling equipment content to $1.3–$1.8M/MW compared with $1.1–$1.5M/MW in traditional air-cooled environments, while the data center liquid cooling market is projected to expand at a 30%+ compound annual growth rate toward a $40bn thermal management universe by 2030.

Alfa Laval holds a dominant 30–35% global market share in heat transfer, expanding its brazed heat exchanger capacity across Europe, the US, and China via a SEK 1bn investment program.

On-site water works and ultrapure treatment systems become critical licensing enablers as data centers shift from municipal dependencies to self-sufficient, closed-loop hydronic designs, with equipment shipsets expanding significantly for pump and polymer piping leaders.

For water infrastructure, reciprocating engines utilize closed-loop radiator cooling systems that require negligible process water consumption compared to highly water-intensive aeroderivative gas turbines, which require massive water injection and can consume two thousand times more water annually than closed-loop reciprocating engine setups.

High equipment backlogs and severe supply constraints award equipment manufacturers exceptional pricing power that is projected to sustain attractive margins through at least 2029, with thematic purity rankings placing Wartsila and 2G Energy at the top. However, material counter-risks persist that could disrupt the investment thesis.

Aggressive manufacturing capacity expansions by reciprocating engine OEMs create a medium-term risk of market oversupply that could erode pricing power, with global manufacturing capacity projected to double from approximately 85 GW in 2024 to one hundred and ninety-five gigawatts by 2030.

Meanwhile, rapid utility-scale battery deployments and long-term SMR commercialization represent viable technological pathways that could render continuous gas generation obsolete, though commercial SMR deployment is unlikely to achieve commercial scale before the mid to late twenty-thirties. The confidence classification reveals high plausibility for the core reciprocating engine backlog and thermal management dimensions, anchored by unprecedented equipment backlogs that provide high confidence in medium-term prime power equipment delivery visibility and rapid adoption of liquid cooling systems that bolsters secular growth visibility for heat exchanger and polymer piping vendors.

However, sceptical analysts warn that faster grid integration or sudden overcapacity could completely collapse off-grid generation premium multiples. Critical data gaps persist across permitting filings, engine order books, and water metrics.

Corporate ESG disclosures omit indirect Scope 2 water footprints, necessitating basin-level hydraulic flow modeling to calculate total lifecycle water consumption, though sceptical investors maintain that apparent data gaps reflect noise rather than structural asymmetry.

Second and third-order implications extend beyond direct equipment sales.

The off-grid data centre boom triggers localized gas pipeline capacity shortages and intense municipal environmental backlash, reshaping utility rate architectures across primary hubs.

Years of underinvestment have left severe constraints on the gas grid that limit behind-the-meter generation deployment, while municipalities, local policymakers, and advocacy groups increasingly challenge on-site gas emissions through moratoria and active permitting litigation.

The investment opportunity centers on European listed equipment manufacturers with concentrated exposure to reciprocating engines, thermal management systems, and specialized water infrastructure, where the combination of multi-year backlogs, structural pricing power, and recurring aftermarket service contracts creates a compelling 18-to-36-month EPS growth trajectory.

The window for premium multiple expansion remains open through at least 2028-2029, though investors must continuously monitor manufacturing capacity additions, regulatory friction at the municipal level, and the pace of grid interconnection improvements that could accelerate the transition back to utility-supplied power.
