Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"

The answer to this question is critical for investors because SCHOTT Pharma is transitioning from a period of high-growth COVID-19 demand and initial GLP-1 euphoria into a phase of significant capacity build-out and intensifying pricing competition. Between FY2020 and FY2024, the company benefited from the rapid adoption of High-Value Solutions (HVS), which expanded from 30% to 57% of sales, driving an EBITDA CAGR of 29% that outpaced revenue growth.

However, the next 18 months will be defined by the commissioning of major new facilities, such as the North Carolina syringe plant, and the renegotiation of multi-year contracts in a market where peers like Stevanato and Becton Dickinson have also aggressively added capacity. If drivers are concentrated in a single customer or specific pricing resets, the company's ability to meet its medium-term margin guidance of "low 30% range" could be at risk if utilization rates fall or prices erode faster than the mix shift can offset. 9 question involves assessing several closely related operational risks, including the "Lilly-effect" on customer concentration, the trajectory of incremental margins as fixed costs ramp, and the potential for a re-pricing of raw materials from its parent company.

For instance, Deutsche Bank estimates that Eli Lilly accounts for two-thirds of SCHOTT Pharma's GLP-1 exposure, a concentration that could lead to volatility if the customer shifts toward multi-dose pens over single-dose autoinjectors. Furthermore, the resolution of a dispute with a "large glass syringe customer" in FY2026, which included a EUR 15 mm one-off payment, creates a difficult year-over-year comparison for FY2027.

Investors must determine whether this "resolution" signals a stable recovery or a structural shift in bargaining power toward large pharmaceutical originators.

Section 2 – Incremental Margins and Capacity Utilization

Section summary
  1. Incremental EBITDA margins are under pressure as fixed costs from new plants in Hungary and the US offset the benefit of a higher HVS product mix. I am moderately confident that margins will remain in the 27% to 28% range through FY2027, as volume ramp-up in high-growth biologics is needed to absorb significant new depreciation and infrastructure costs. Incremental margin expansion at SCHOTT Pharma is currently a battle between a favorable product mix and the rising fixed costs associated with global capacity expansion. In 3Q26, the company reported a 26.8% EBITDA margin, which was a decline from 32.3% in the prior-year period, largely due to weakness in the Drug Delivery Systems (DDS) segment and higher costs for infrastructure and process optimization. While HVS sales reached 59% of the business in 3Q26, the margin was burdened by lower capacity utilization for polymer syringes and ramp-up costs for new glass-syringe capacities in Hungary.

Deutsche Bank notes that unlike some peers, SCHOTT Pharma does not adjust its EBITDA for capacity ramp-up costs, which results in higher earnings quality but also makes the reported margin more sensitive to the timing of new facility openings. Fixed cost absorption remains the primary driver of near-term margin volatility as the company moves through a heavy investment cycle. In the first half of FY2026, the DDS segment's EBITDA margin contracted by 250 basis points to 31.6% due to lower production utilization for polymer syringes and the aforementioned Hungary expansion costs.

Jefferies analysts point out that while the mid-term guidance targets a margin of approximately 30.5% by FY2028, near-term growth is running through plants that are still in the qualification or early ramp-up phases, limiting immediate operating leverage. This dynamic is mirrored in the broader sector; for example, Stevanato Group's gross margin only expanded by 10 basis points in 3Q26, as improvements at its new Fishers facility were offset by higher utility and depreciation costs.

This suggests that in the very near term, the company is finding enough operational efficiencies and mix benefits to offset the drag from its new facilities. Furthermore, West Pharmaceutical Services reported that its HVP upgrades generated over 200 basis points of organic revenue growth in 3Q26, demonstrating that the industrywide shift toward high-value formats can provide significant margin support even during capacity cycles. A comparable past case is Gerresheimer's 1Q26 margin collapse, where adjusted EBITDA plummeted to EUR 6 mm from EUR 32 mm due to "significant underutilization of assets" following a decision to stop inventory buildup. This event shows that for asset-heavy pharma packaging companies, a small shift in utilization can have a "high fall-through" effect on EBITDA, potentially wiping out several quarters of mix-driven gains. At SCHOTT Pharma, the incremental margin on extra revenue will remain highly dependent on the pace at which biologics and GLP-1 volumes can fill new lines in Hungary and North Carolina.

Section 3 – Pricing Dynamics and Contract Renewals

Section summary
  1. Pricing for ready-to-use vials and glass syringes is facing significant pressure from overcapacity, with some vial prices reportedly falling by 50% in 2026. I have medium confidence that 2027 and 2028 contract renewals will be skewed toward lower price points as pharmaceutical customers leverage excess market supply to renegotiate locked-price agreements.

Market pricing for primary packaging is entering a period of aggressive reset as the post-COVID capacity surge meets a more normalized demand environment. An executive at Gerresheimer noted on October 27, 2026, that ready-to-use (RTU) 6ml vial prices had fallen from EUR 0.75 per unit to a range of EUR 0.35 to EUR 0.40 per unit. This 50% decline reflects what experts describe as "significant overcapacity" in both molded and tubular glass, leading to more aggressive contract negotiations from pharmaceutical customers. products carry a 5x to 10x price premium over bulk components, the sheer volume of new supply entering the market is beginning to erode these premiums.

The cycle of locked-price contract renewals in 2027 and 2028 represents a major risk to SCHOTT Pharma's realized prices. Jefferies notes that weaker-than-expected syringe demand and a delayed recovery in bulk demand have already led to "leverage from customers due to surplus supply." Experts at SGD Pharma highlight that during the COVID-19 pandemic, main players greatly increased capacity, and the market is now facing a "huge, huge supply, overcapacity of tubular vials." As three-year contracts signed during the 2024-2025 period come up for renewal, the benchmark for pricing will likely be the significantly lower spot prices observed in late 2026.

1.
Contrary evidence: Some high-end product segments remain "sticky" and resistant to broad pricing erosion, particularly for complex biologics and GLP-1s where switching costs are high. An industry expert noted that once a customer has spec'd in a component for a large-molecule drug, they rarely switch, providing a degree of price protection for SCHOTT Pharma's Nexa and Alba syringe platforms. Furthermore, West Pharmaceutical Services reported that its pricing contributed over 400 basis points to growth in 3Q26, suggesting that market leaders can still push through increases for differentiated products. Historical dispersion in pharma packaging suggests that pricing forecasts resting on a single driver, such as utilization, often fail during capacity shifts. Becton Dickinson, for instance, has used its massive scale to keep basic syringe pricing flat in nominal terms for decades, effectively forcing real price declines on competitors and consolidating the market. For SCHOTT Pharma, the risk is that the "HVS mix shift" that supported margins from FY2020 to FY2026 may be insufficient to offset a 10% to 20% decline in like-for-like pricing across its core sterile portfolio.
2.
Other side of the story: The impact of price erosion may be mitigated by the regulatory shift toward Annex 1 compliance in Europe, which requires pharmaceutical companies to invest in sterile filling or source RTU components. If SCHOTT Pharma can position its RTU offering as a way for customers to avoid expensive cleanroom investments, it may maintain a higher value-added price even as the cost of the underlying glass container falls.

Section 4 – Customer Concentration and the GLP-1 Factor

Section summary
  1. Customer concentration, particularly in the GLP-1 category, is a high-impact driver that could lead to significant financial dispersion if demand patterns shift. I am highly confident that SCHOTT Pharma's exposure to Eli Lilly makes it vulnerable to the same idiosyncratic shocks recently experienced by its peer Stevanato. SCHOTT Pharma's GLP-1 business is heavily skewed toward a small number of major pharmaceutical originators, with Eli Lilly serving as the dominant partner. Deutsche Bank estimates that Eli Lilly accounts for approximately two-thirds of SCHOTT Pharma's total GLP-1 exposure. This concentration is underscored by the company's disclosure on May 13, 2026, that a single customer accounted for 10.3% of its first-half sales. While GLP-1 therapies currently represent a low-teens share of group sales, they are a primary driver of growth in the HVS segment, which is targeted to reach 60% of total revenue. The recent experience of Stevanato Group serves as a stark precedent for the risks of such concentration. In November 2026, Stevanato's stock plummeted after the company guided for only low-single-digit organic growth in 2027, a sharp decline from the previously expected 10%. This "temporary headwind" was attributed to lower GLP-1 demand from a single customer (likely Novo Nordisk) for Nexa syringes, exacerbated by a shift toward multi-dose pens that require only one cartridge rather than four single-dose syringes per month. 40 SCHOTT Pharma faces a similar risk; if Eli Lilly shifts its volume mix toward vials or pens, the revenue headwind for SCHOTT's high-margin syringe business could be substantial. Section 5 – Capital Spending and the North Carolina Expansion

Section summary
  1. Capital expenditure is reaching a strategic peak in FY2027, with the US$371 mm North Carolina facility representing a major commitment of capital and future fixed costs. I have high confidence that this investment will drive a significant increase in depreciation charges through FY2028, potentially tempering EPS growth despite revenue gains. SCHOTT Pharma is currently in the most capital-intensive phase of its recent history, driven by the construction of its US$371 mm North Carolina syringe plant. This facility is a core part of the company's strategy to expand its US manufacturing presence, a key requirement for winning large pharmaceutical tenders. In the first nine months of FY2026, the company continued to expand its capacities, particularly for HVS, which contributed to negative free cash flow in some quarters despite rising profits. The North Carolina plant is a significant commitment that will define the company's fixed-cost base for the next decade. financial impact of this spending is already visible in the company's rising depreciation and amortization (D&A) profile. In nine-month FY2026, D&A reached EUR 65.5 mm, an increase of 14% year-over-year. Deutsche Bank expects adjusted D&A as a percentage of revenue to increase from 5.3% in FY2022 to 7.7% by FY2027, reflecting the significant capacity expansion programs underway. This increase in non-cash charges will create a natural drag on net income and EPS, even if EBITDA margins remain stable.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. If the common view that GLP-1 demand will seamlessly backfill any capacity expansion is wrong, SCHOTT Pharma faces a significant EPS downside from fixed-cost deleverage and pricing resets. The most powerful actor capable of shifting this outcome is Eli Lilly, whose procurement decisions could trigger a margin collapse similar to the recent shock at Stevanato. The common view held by most brokers, including Deutsche Bank and Jefferies, is that SCHOTT Pharma's longterm growth is secured by the structural "injectable wave" and the company's successful transition to HVS. This view assumes that the company can maintain its EBITDA margins in the 27% to 28% range and eventually reach 30%+ as its new plants reach full capacity. However, if the industry-wide overcapacity in syringes (projected at 10% to 20% in 3 to 5 years) leads to a more severe pricing war than anticipated, the common view of "margin resilience" will be proven wrong. primary risk to the common view is the concentration of power in the hands of one or two global pharmaceutical giants. Eli Lilly, which accounts for two-thirds of SCHOTT Pharma's GLP-1 exposure, has both the reason and the power to push for lower pricing as competitors like Stevanato and Becton Dickinson aggressively bid for its business with their own newly built capacity. If Lilly successfully plays these suppliers against each other during the 2027 contract renewals, SCHOTT Pharma's realized prices could fall significantly below the EUR 0.35 to EUR 0.40 per unit levels currently seen in the market. An early sign that the outcome may differ from expectations is the recent "variable compensation" adjustments at Stevanato tied to lower GLP-1 volumes. Most sources have treated Stevanato's shock as "idiosyncratic," but it may actually be the first indicator of a broader shift in GLP-1 supply dynamics toward more price-sensitive formats like multi-dose pens or vials. If SCHOTT Pharma reports a similar "temporary moderation" in growth due to its own large customer renewals in 2027, the market's current EPS forecasts of EUR 1.05 to EUR 1.18 will likely be revised downward by double digits.
