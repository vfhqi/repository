Section 2 – Pro Forma Margins and Stranded Costs Treatment

Section summary
  1. Unilever's pro forma starting margin of 19% excludes €400 million to €500 million in expected stranded costs.
  2. Confidence in the restructuring timeline is medium because of historical demerger friction reported by brokers.
  3. Benchmark comparisons reveal a significant gross margin deficit versus highly profitable pure-play consumer peers.

Pro Forma Baseline and Stranded Costs

1.
Steady baseline: Unilever presented a pro forma 2025 home and personal care underlying operating margin of 19% during its Capital Markets Day on 4 November 2026. Management framed this 19% figure as the launchpad for a multi-year journey to achieve a 22% to 23% margin range by 2029. Achieving this target depends on growth-led operating leverage rather than aggressive, short-term cost-cutting that could impair brand health and long-term earnings per share.
2.
Residual overheads: Unilever constructed its pro forma margin baseline using 2025 reported financials for the remaining home and personal care business and retained India Foods. Consequently, this 19% pro forma starting margin excludes the anticipated stranded costs of €400 million to €500 million because these overheads only become a burden after the Foods business exits. Management must eliminate these costs through its new savings program to prevent near-term margin dilution.
3.
Unequal burden: Unilever faces gross stranded costs of €400 million to €500 million following the separation of its Foods division. These corporate costs will be distributed across the remaining Home Care, Personal Care, and Beauty & Wellbeing divisions based on their shared service usage. Management intends to utilize transitional service agreements with McCormick lasting two to three years to temporarily buffer this operational friction and protect near-term earnings.
4.
Phased reduction: Unilever intends to fully address these stranded costs over a three-year restructuring period from 2027 to 2029. The company has guided to a €500 million one-off restructuring spend specifically designed to offset these stranded corporate overheads completely. This timeline gives the company operational breathing room, though delays in integration could prolong the cost headwind.
5.
Organizational focus: Bernstein Research noted on 4 November 2026 that the new €1 billion productivity program is essential to mitigate the Foods stranded costs. The broker's analysis indicates that one-third of the program's savings will come from organizational simplification, while two-thirds rely on process and technology re-wiring by 2029. This highlights that margin expansion will require significant execution discipline across complex IT systems to support the earnings recovery.

Broker Models and Skepticism

6.
Modeled friction: Jefferies Research modeled on 11 August 2026 that Unilever will experience net stranded costs of €250 million in fiscal year 2027, representing a partial offset of the €500 million gross drag. The broker estimates that the total stranded costs from the Food exit represent around 4% of exited sales, creating immediate operating profit pressure. Jefferies project that these costs will initially depress the pro forma operating margin and weigh on near-term earnings per share.
7.
Heavy spending: Deutsche Bank Research reported on 31 March 2026 that the stranded costs from the Foods separation represent about 5% to 6% of the combined home and personal care business. The broker pointed out that Unilever's total restructuring commitment of €1.2 billion represents a substantial upfront cash investment to deliver these savings. Deutsche Bank expressed caution regarding the ultimate margin impact given the scale of these one-off expenses.
8.
Incremental reinvestment: Jefferies Research noted on 3 November 2026 that Unilever's €1 billion gross savings target will leave approximately €500 million of incremental savings after offsetting stranded costs. This remaining balance represents 1.1% of sales, which the broker expects will provide the necessary financial firepower to sustain advertising and promotional spending above 16% of sales. This investment is critical to maintain volume competitiveness once the high-margin Foods cash cow exits, defending long-term earnings per share.
9.
Contrary evidence: Rothschild & Co Redburn highlighted on 26 November 2025 that previous demergers of Unilever's Spreads and Tea businesses took far longer to execute and left the company saddled with multiple years of stranded costs. This historical precedent suggests that eliminating the €400 million to €500 million of Foods overhead could drag on well beyond management's guided 2029 timeline. If this friction persists, the projected home and personal care margin expansion will be delayed, depressing earnings growth.

Peer Benchmarks and Precedents

10.
Addressable gap: Unilever's Chief Executive Officer Fernando Fernandez acknowledged during the Capital Markets Day that approximately 40% of the company's gross margin gap versus peers is structural due to footprint differences. The remaining 60% of the 300 to 400 basis-point deficit is considered fully addressable through internal execution. Management expects to close this gap through premiumization, pricing discipline, and complexity reduction in formulations to improve earnings.
11.

Procter & Gamble reported an operating margin of 22.7% for its full fiscal year 2026, driven by high gross margins. This operating profit gap represents a 300 to 400 basis-point deficit that Unilever management aims to address through pricing discipline and complexity reduction.

Profitable rivals: Colgate-Palmolive serves as another high-margin benchmark, reporting an adjusted non-GAAP operating margin of 21.3% for full year 2025. Although Colgate's GAAP operating margin fell to 16.2% due to goodwill and intangible asset impairments, its core business remains highly profitable. Unilever's pro forma 19% margin underscores the operational catch-up required to match these pure-play consumer peers.
13.
Cautionary precedent: Colgate-Palmolive's North American division provides a warning, where operating margins contracted by more than ten percentage points between 2019 and 2023 due to heavy brand reinvestment. Redburn Atlantic noted on 10 April 2025 that while this investment succeeded in accelerating top-line growth, division margins have still not recovered. This historical case suggests that Unilever's plan to reinvest savings into brand equity could similarly restrict operating margin expansion and earnings recovery.
14.
Internal precedent: Unilever's demerger of its Ice Cream business as The Magnum Ice Cream Company in December 2025 provides a direct internal precedent for restructuring. To address the stranded costs from this separation, the board launched an €800 million productivity program that was accelerated to complete by yearend 2026. This past restructuring demonstrates that management can move rapidly to streamline overhead and cut personnel to defend operating profitability.
15.
Other side of the story: While critics focus on the risk of persistent stranded costs, Unilever's move to a pure-play home and personal care structure could unlock substantial valuation upside. By exiting lower-growth Foods and Ice Cream, the company's portfolio will be more focused on high-growth categories contributing 67% of turnover. If volume growth accelerates, the operating leverage generated will easily absorb any temporary stranded costs and drive long-term earnings per share.

Section 3 – McCormick TSA Accounting and Duration

Section summary
  1. Unilever transition agreements with McCormick are structured to last up to twenty-four months to ensure operational continuity during integration.
  2. Transition fees will be recorded inside underlying operating profit with net neutral margin impact due to offsetting service costs.
  3. Historical precedents indicate that separating integrated global food assets generates substantial transition friction and prolonged ERP migration timelines.

Scope and Guided Duration

1.
Guided duration: McCormick and Unilever have structured their transition services agreement to generally last up to twenty-four months following the anticipated transaction close in mid-2027. This timeline was formally communicated in a McCormick prospectus filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on 23 July 2026. This two-year duration is designed to give the combined entity's Integration Management Office sufficient headroom to execute a phased exit without disrupting day-to-day commercial operations.

A McCormick presentation at the Barclays Global Consumer Conference on 9 September 2026 confirmed that these agreements secure seventy percent of total processes. These services are crucial because Unilever's shared service centres are highly integrated and outsourced, whereas McCormick operates these functions internally.

A Morgan Stanley report on 31 March 2026 noted that while transition agreements are used to smooth stranded cost elimination, execution delays in past consumer product carve-outs have frequently led to costly extensions. If these agreements are prolonged, Unilever will remain saddled with administrative burdens that divert management focus from its core categories.

Financial and Accounting Recording

6.
Profit recording: Unilever will recognize transition service management fees received from McCormick within its reported operating profit figures. According to Unilever's official Q4 and full year 2025 results announcement published on 11 February 2026, the group recognizes these transitional fees directly inside operating profit. This established accounting treatment provides clear visibility into transition activities but does not alter the underlying performance of the core business.
7.
Neutral margin: Unilever's transition agreements are structured to be essentially margin neutral to the group because the service fees are directly offset by operating costs. The company's full year 2025 results presentation on 12 February 2026 stated that transition agreements are not expected to generate material profit as income is fully matched by the cost of providing the services. Consequently, these agreements do not provide any net margin cushion to offset the group's ongoing operating expenses.
8.
Expected dilution: Jefferies Research reported on 5 December 2025 that transition services income has no net impact on operating profit for the provider. The broker modeled that although these service fees flow through the income statement, they are fully absorbed by transition-related administrative expenses. This dynamic means that transition services cannot mitigate the gross margin dilution from lost scale after the Foods separation.
9.
Cost embedding: McCormick has already incorporated the expected expenses of these transition service agreements directly into its transaction economics and synergy targets. A McCormick prospectus filed on 10 September 2026 confirmed that transition costs are fully embedded in deal expectations, and the company expects some dual running costs as services are phased out. This upfront integration budgeting reduces the risk of unexpected earnings dilution for the acquirer during the two-year transition period.

Precedents and Transition Friction

10.
Complex separation: Unilever's historical divestment of its Spreads division to Upfield demonstrates the immense operational friction of asset separation in the consumer staples sector. A Deutsche Bank research note from 11 February 2020 detailed that the Upfield separation required a staggering six hundred transitional service agreements. This massive web of agreements required up to three years of preparatory work and an extensive IT migration before the final agreements could be exited.
11.
Sticky costs: Historical precedents show that large-scale consumer portfolio separations often leave the parent company saddled with multi-year stranded costs despite transition agreements. Rothschild & Co Redburn highlighted on 23 January 2024 that Unilever was left with two hundred and fifty million euros of annual stranded costs following its Spreads divestment. These residual overheads persisted for three years postdisposal, illustrating that transition agreements merely defer rather than eliminate corporate friction.
12.
System transition: McCormick faces substantial integration challenges because it must complete its North American ERP rollout prior to onboarding the Unilever Foods business. Deutsche Bank Securities reported on 30 April 2026 that the company must finish its SAP S/4HANA Phase 1 implementation before integrating the acquired operations. Any delays in this software rollout will prolong McCormick's reliance on Unilever's transition systems, inflating integration costs and delaying synergies.
13.
Other side of the story: The operational separation of the Foods division could proceed more rapidly and with less friction than historical transactions because of Unilever's extensive preparatory separation work. Unilever Chief Executive Officer Fernando Fernandez explained during a fireside chat on 9 April 2026 that eighty percent of the Foods business already operates via a completely standalone commercial organization. This high degree of existing operational independence suggests that McCormick can exit the transition agreements ahead of schedule, reducing transition overheads and accelerating EPS accretion.

Section 4 – Phasing and Allocation of the €1bn Savings Programme

Section summary
  1. Unilever targets one billion euros of gross savings by 2029 to offset Foods stranded costs and fund brand investments.
  2. Sell-side analysts project a gradual operating margin expansion of ten to twenty basis points annually.
  3. Comparative analysis shows Unilever savings target is smaller than peer initiatives from Nestlé and Haleon.

Phasing and Apportionment of Savings

1.
Ambitious productivity: Unilever is targeting one billion euros of gross productivity savings by fiscal year 2029 to reconstruct its operating model. Sourced from the company's Capital Markets Day presentation on 4 November 2026, the programme allocates one-third of savings to organizational simplification and two-thirds to process and technology re-wiring. This restructuring plan intends to fully neutralize the massive stranded corporate overheads from the Foods transaction to protect operating profit. Success in executing this ITled consolidation will determine whether Unilever can hit its medium-term earnings goals.
2.
Balanced reinvestment: Unilever plans to split the one billion euros program to support both competitiveness and financial returns. According to a Jefferies Research note dated 3 November 2026, five hundred million euros will eliminate stranded costs while the remaining five hundred million euros represents incremental savings to fund brand investment. This incremental capital provides critical firepower to sustain advertising above sixteen percent of sales. If successfully deployed, this marketing support will defend market share and drive long-term earnings per share.
3.
Gradual implementation: Unilever expects the underlying operating model changes to take effect progressively over a multi-year horizon. Management guided in their New York presentation on 4 November 2026 that the new regional structure will begin on 1 January 2027 and reach full operational status as the Foods transaction closes in mid-2027. The phased roll-out aligns with the anticipated run-rate of transition service fees from McCormick. Any delay in this transition will postpone the cost-out benefits and drag on intermediate earnings.
