Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Upstream dividends and asset rotation directly dictate EXOR N.V.'s capital allocation and share buyback capabilities.
  2. Assessing the cash distribution strategies of Royal Philips and CNH Industrial reveals EXOR's future liquidity and stake-building potential.
1.
State of the art: "EXOR N.V. (EXO-NL, Euronext Amsterdam): the Agnelli-family-controlled European investment holding company, reporting as an IFRS 10 investment entity, whose net asset value sits in listed stakes in Ferrari, Royal Philips, CNH Industrial, Stellantis and bioMérieux, unlisted holdings (Christian Louboutin, The Economist Group, a residual Institut Mérieux stake, Juventus), funds managed by its asset manager Lingotto, and cash from disposals (GEDI, Lifenet, NUO, the Iveco tender to Tata Motors, Welltec under sale). What are Royal Philips' and CNH Industrial's dividend policies and the sell-side's dividend forecasts for 2027 to 2029, how much of its Philips dividend has EXOR N.V. elected to take in shares in each recent year, and what do regulatory filings and company statements since August 2026 show about EXOR increasing its Philips stake towards the 22% allowed under the extended relationship agreement?"

Strategic context and source scope

2.
Scope definition: Research for this report analyzed 105 relevant sources across company filings, broker notes, and press releases. This investigation identified eight key companies including EXOR, Philips, and CNH Industrial, alongside four comparable holding company precedents like Investor AB and Sofina. These sources provide deep insight into how holding companies manage capital allocation and discount dynamics.
3.
Discount pressure: EXOR CEO John Elkann noted that EXOR shares continue to trade at a substantial discount to net asset value. This discount widened to a rare level of 56% in the first half of 2026. Jefferies Research observed that holding companies lacking focused capital returns tend to trade on high discounts of 20% to 40%.

Dividend contributions and liquidity

4.
Cash inflows: EXOR generated substantial liquidity in the first half of 2026, recording positive cash flows from dividend inflows of €861 million. This cash profile was heavily supported by a special dividend of €427 million from Iveco. Upstream dividends are essential because they provide the necessary run-rate liquidity to fund holding level operations.
5.
Buyback execution: EXOR launched a €500 million share buyback program to exploit its massive trading discount. The company completed the first €125 million tranche in October 2026 and immediately initiated the second tranche. Executing these buybacks represents a highly accretive capital allocation choice while shares trade at depressed levels.
6.
Contrary evidence: EXOR's massive cash position and buyback capacity are driven more by portfolio disposals than regular dividend inflows. The company secured €2.7 billion of expected cash inflows from divestments in GEDI, Lifenet, NUO, and the Welltec sale. This evidence suggests that regular dividend policies are secondary to asset rotation for immediate liquidity.

Strategic stakes and capital deployment

7.
Holding comparisons: Comparable peers like Investor AB navigate similar dynamics by maintaining a strong balance sheet and solid cash flow. DNB Carnegie notes that investment companies with high net asset value growth over time warrant much lower discounts to their underlying holdings. EXOR replicates this strategy by actively shifting its capital into high-growth segments like healthcare.
8.
Capital flexibility: EXOR bolstered its long-term financial firepower to €4 billion by divesting assets and pruning its portfolio. In addition, Deutsche Bank Research reported that EXOR raised €3 billion in early 2025 by selling a 4% stake in Ferrari. This massive capital cushion allows EXOR to move decisively on major new opportunities such as increasing its Philips stake.
9.
Other side of the story: While regular dividends from Philips and CNH Industrial support steady run-rate cash, EXOR's financial flexibility and €500 million buyback are primarily enabled by lumpy, opportunistic asset divestments. Relying on cyclical agricultural earnings from CNH or volatile medtech performance at Philips introduces holding-level risks. Therefore, structural asset rotation remains the most decisive driver of EXOR's net asset value and discount narrowing.

Section summary
  1. Royal Philips targets cumulative free cash flow of EUR 4.5 billion to EUR 5.0 billion to secure dividend stability.
  2. Sell-side estimates project a stable or rising dividend payout supported by legal de-risking and operational recovery.

Corporate dividend policy and cash targets

1.
Stable payouts: Royal Philips intends to maintain a stable dividend policy targeting a payout ratio of 40% to 50% of adjusted income from continuing operations. The Board proposed a dividend of EUR 0.85 per common share for the 2025 fiscal year. Maintaining this level ensures capital conservation while the group prioritizes organic investments in healthcare platforms.
2.
Cash generation: Royal Philips set a cumulative free cash flow target of EUR 4.5 billion to EUR 5.0 billion over the 2026 to 2028 planning period. This target represents a significant step up from the EUR 3.0 billion generated cumulatively during 2023 to 2025. Achieving these targets is vital to support stable downstream shareholder returns and de-lever the balance sheet.
3.
Achievable goals: Bernstein Research believes the new mid-term cash flow guidance is highly credible given the group's solid execution in 2025. The broker expects cash flow expansion will be driven by higher operating earnings and a significant reduction in adjusting items. Sustained cash execution is required to support the group's dividend stability over the next three years.
4.
Contrary evidence: Royal Philips' free cash flow fell from EUR 906 million in 2024 to EUR 512 million in 2025 due to massive litigation payments. This drop demonstrates that operational cash generation remains highly vulnerable to ongoing legal outlays and cash restructuring expenses. This historical volatility suggests that dividend coverage may face pressure if new legal liabilities emerge.

Section 3 – CNH Industrial's Dividend Policy and Forecasts

Section summary
  1. CNH Industrial targets a dividend payout of 25% to 35% of consolidated net income, but payouts are severely constrained by the cyclical agricultural downturn.
  2. Structural portfolio simplification, including the construction equipment joint venture with Kobelco, supports balance sheet deleveraging but limits near-term dividend growth.
  3. Sell-side forecasts imply flat to moderately recovering distributions through 2028 as peer comparisons highlight CNH Industrial's depressed current yields.

Dividend policy and cyclical headwinds

1.
Depressed payouts: CNH Industrial's official dividend policy permits annual cash distributions of between 25% and 35% of its consolidated net income in any single fiscal year. However, the actual dividend payments remain highly dependent on regional earnings performance, capital reinvestment needs, and overall balance sheet liquidity. The severe cyclical downturn in global agricultural demand has forced management to reduce capital distributions to preserve liquidity during the trough.
2.
82 10K 14A Declining distributions: CNH Industrial drastically lowered its cash dividend per common share from $0.47 in fiscal year 2024 to $0.25 in fiscal year 2025. The payout was subsequently cut to $0.10 per common share in fiscal year 2026, representing a total cash outflow of approximately $124.2 million. This multi-year downward trajectory demonstrates that cyclical earnings pressure directly dictates shareholder returns under the group's percentage-based payout framework.

3.
Regional weakness: CNH Industrial reported that agricultural market conditions in Europe and South America deteriorated further during the latter half of 2026. In Brazil, farm profitability remains severely squeezed due to elevated interest rates, tight credit availability, and rising delinquency rates of 4.1% on the financial services book. - Earnings Calls These geographic headwinds are expected to depress agricultural equipment sales into 2027, limiting the recovery of net income and downstream dividend capacity.

Contrary evidence: CNH Industrial raised its full-year 2026 adjusted diluted earnings per share guidance to a range of $0.44 to $0.48, up from the prior range of $0.41 to $0.46. This operating performance improvement was driven by tariff refunds under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act rather than structural demand recovery. This short-term profitability boost is unlikely to translate into higher dividends because the underlying agricultural machinery cycle remains deeply depressed across primary regional markets.

Structural moves and pure-play transition

5.
Strategic divestment: CNH Industrial announced an agreement to sell a 70% stake in its Construction Equipment business to Kobelco Construction Machinery, valuing the segment at an enterprise value of $1 billion. The transaction is scheduled to close by the end of 2027 and will generate approximately $700 million in gross cash proceeds. This portfolio pruning allows the group to transition into an agricultural pure-play business with structurally higher operating margins.
6.
Deleveraging priority: CNH Industrial plans to deploy the $700 million in cash proceeds from the Kobelco construction joint venture primarily to pay down outstanding debt. This allocation aligns with the board's foundational commitment to preserve its investment-grade credit rating and strengthen the balance sheet. Consequently, this cash inflow will not be distributed as special dividends or immediate shareholder buybacks.
7.
Sustained conversion: CNH Industrial reaffirmed its long-term financial target to achieve an industrial free cash flow conversion rate of over 70% through the business cycle. At the same time, the group projects a 25% cumulative increase in through-cycle industrial cash generation. Achieving these targets is necessary to support consistent annual dividend payouts and fund targeted agricultural technology development.
8.
Aggressive cost-cutting: CNH Industrial implemented aggressive self-help cost reduction and productivity programs to mitigate the margin pressure of the agricultural downcycle. These efficiency actions contributed to the group generating $513 million of industrial free cash flow during a highly challenging operating year. Sustaining these operational cost improvements is critical to protect bottom-cycle cash flow conversion and cover the current dividend payout.

Sell-side forecasts and recovery prospects

9.
Conservative projections: Bernstein Research forecasts CNH Industrial's reported earnings per share to recover moderately from $0.46 in fiscal year 2026 to $0.61 in fiscal year 2027. The broker's model estimates a very low dividend yield of 0.8% based on a closing share price of $12.82. This conservative outlook reflects expectations that payouts will remain at bottom-cycle levels of approximately $0.10 per share until earnings recover significantly.
10.
Alternative scenarios: Morgan Stanley Research projects CNH Industrial's normalized earnings per share to eventually reach $1 under a base-case cyclical recovery scenario. In contrast, a bear-case scenario where the agricultural equipment recovery stalls in North America and Europe yields an earnings per share of only $0.40. These divergent forecasts illustrate that dividend growth remains highly sensitive to the precise timing of dealer inventory normalization.
11.
Cautious recovery: Bernstein Research expects a shallow agricultural machinery recovery starting in late 2027, projecting demand figures 10% below consensus expectations. The broker expects that pricing pressure and normalized dealer inventory levels will continue to constrain industry margins. This cautious outlook indicates that consensus dividend forecasts may be overly optimistic if dealer restocking cycles are delayed.
12.
Prudent past response: CNH Industrial navigated a similar cyclical trough during the 2015 to 2016 period when agricultural commodity prices plummeted globally. During that downturn, the company maintained a highly conservative capital allocation stance, prioritizing debt reduction over shareholder distributions. This historical precedent suggests that management will prioritize balance sheet health over dividend increases until net leverage falls below target thresholds.

Section 4 – EXOR N.V.'s Historical Philips Dividend Elections

Section summary
  1. EXOR N.V. actively utilizes stock dividend elections from Royal Philips to preserve significant holding- level cash.
  2. Taking Philips dividends in shares directly supports EXOR's strategic target of maintaining over EUR 3.5 billion in deployable liquidity.

Recent Philips dividend elections

1.
Strategic choice: EXOR elected to receive 50% of its H1 2026 Philips dividend in shares, representing 3,388,714 new shares valued at €77 million. This dividend election allowed EXOR to receive the remaining €78 million of its €155 million total Philips dividend in cash. This balanced approach helped EXOR preserve substantial holding-level cash while continuing to build its overall ownership stake.
2.
Consistent execution: EXOR executed a similar 50% stock and 50% cash split for its Philips dividend during the first half of 2025. Under this previous election, the investment holding company received 3,781,680 shares valued at €76 million. This consistent application of stock elections shows how EXOR systematically prioritizes liquidity preservation over immediate cash extraction.
3.
Absolute shareholding: EXOR received its entire 2024 Philips dividend of €121 million in shares, which translated to 4,872,647 new common shares. This full stock election was aligned with the strategic agreement to reach the 15% threshold within three years. This tactical deployment highlights that EXOR actively avoids cash distributions from Philips when building its core stake.

Balance sheet and accounting implications

7.
Liquidity enhancement: EXOR's strategic stock elections directly supported the expansion of its total cash and cash equivalents to €2.179 billion by June 30, 2026. This liquidity represents a substantial increase from the €1.408 billion reported at the end of December 2025. By electing shares, EXOR successfully retained valuable deployable cash on its balance sheet to fund ongoing market share buybacks.
8.
Accounting recognition: EXOR accounts for its listed holdings as equity investments at fair value through profit or loss under IFRS 10 guidelines. Under this reporting framework, both cash and stock dividends are recognized as dividend income in the income statement. This accounting treatment ensures that stock elections do not reduce reported income even though they preserve holding-level cash.
9.
Financial cushion: EXOR increased its total deployable cash to more than €3.5 billion by executing strategic asset monetizations and conserving cash. This capital cushion provides the investment holding company with the financial firepower to target new major investments. Therefore, this strategy could constrain holding-level debt service if other listed holdings reduce their cash payouts during cyclical downturns.
