Fevertree (FEVR-GB): questions for the investor relations call with Ann Morris-Gibbons, Tuesday 24-Nov-2026 14:00 UK (version 3)

From the Investment Analyst, for Richard. Version 3, written 24-Nov-2026 at about 13:40 UK in the new form Richard signed off that day: fewer topics, each tied to the case, asked indirectly. Built from the Stock-specific Investment Case A&J Memo (written 3-Nov-2026 at 914.5p) and the two Key Question Research Reports of 20-Nov-2026, as version 2 read them; Richard's own notes of the investor relations call of 11-Jun-2026; and his plan for this call. Not read: his notes of 30-Jan-2026 and 26 and 30-Mar-2026. Fevertree is held: 2.37% of the fund, one probing tranche entered 8-Jun-2026, classified Bottoming / S1 Speculative Bet. Last close 930.5p (23-Nov-2026). Every analyst figure below is a real one from our files; none of our own numbers appears in a question. ★ marks the topics to lead with.

Terms. EBITDA: the company's adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. The floor: Molson Coors' undisclosed minimum royalty to Fevertree for 2026 to 2030. Rest of Group: everything outside the United States. Scanner data: Circana's tracked United States shop sales. The levy: the United Kingdom packaging levy on glass.

1. Attendees

Ann Morris-Gibbons, Director of Investor Relations. She took over in July 2026; the June answers quoted below came from her predecessor, so "we were told in June" is a fair, impersonal yardstick.

2. Objective and higher intent

  • Objective: decide whether this probing position has earned a second tranche, stays as it is, or goes.
  • Higher intent: the case is a United States turn that must reach Fevertree's profit before 2027 costs and a distracted partner get in the way. The call should show whether the company's confidence has risen or slipped since June on those three things.
  • What would move the position. Towards adding: United States sales through the shops holding above 15% with supply keeping up, Molson Coors' 2027 spend agreed, and 2027 consensus defended with reasons. Towards cutting: vague answers on Molson Coors' commitment, "timing" language on shipments, and the cost message softening from June.

3. Strategy

She will not forecast. So put an outside fact or an analyst's number on the table and ask her to react; use June's words as the yardstick; ask about shape and direction, not the confidential number.

4. Category

Sorting box teleological probing, not exploratory.

5. Banana skins and navigation plan

  • "We are comfortable with consensus." June's answer was "Absolutely fine" and "Very comfortable". Do not ask whether she is comfortable; give two analysts' numbers and ask which is nearer.
  • The floor is confidential. Ask what an investor should assume about its shape, never the figure.
  • "Progressing to plan" on Molson Coors. Ask for a single change since the summer.
  • "Pacific" is in the calendar title; who joins is not in our files.

6. What they said last time (11-Jun-2026, Richard's notes), and what has happened since

  • Demand: not seeing a tough environment, "even in UK"; the United Kingdom had "turned a positive corner".
  • Consensus: "Absolutely fine". About £50m of EBITDA for 2026, "mid-£60s" for 2027, "£80m+" for 2028. 2027 "could be an upgrade" if distributors reach a tipping point: "20% growth can be 30 or 40% conceptually".
  • Costs: glass and energy "fully hedged for FY 2026, largely for 2027, partly for FY 2028"; some inflation in 2027 "but it won't impact the profit expectations".
  • The risk they named: Molson Coors' execution; "a small brand that is part of a big distribution system".
  • Since then: guidance reaffirmed on 10-Sep-2026 (brand revenue £402.4m, EBITDA £49.9m), and the finance director said the guarantee underpins EBITDA growth for two years. Molson Coors cut its own guidance on 5-Nov-2026: volume (4.9)%, gross margin down 376 basis points. Glass makers are cutting capacity and gas prices are higher.

7. Topics (check-pick list)

T1 ★ Molson Coors under pressure. "Molson Coors cut its guidance on 5 November, with volumes down about 5% and gross margin down nearly four points. Some people say a partner under that pressure protects its own beer brands first. What has changed in how they resource Fevertree since the summer?"
- Pick from: "Is the 2027 marketing budget agreed, and is it above or below 2026?" / "Analysts assume marketing falls from about 20% of United States sales to about 11% by 2028. Does Molson Coors' plan look like that?" / "We were told in June the hard part was keeping a very big company pushing a small brand. Easier or harder today?" / "A distributor said in September that Molson Coors could not meet demand because of forecasting. Fixed?" / "Does their aluminium bill change who pays for cans in the United States?"
- Positive: a budget, named people, a specific fix. Negative: "progressing to plan" with nothing behind it; a budget "under review".

T2 ★ Does United States growth reach your profit? "Jefferies has the sell-side at 15% to 20% United States growth, and Deutsche Bank has the United States margin almost doubling next year, from about 6% to 11.5%. Is that step coming from the profit share or from the guarantee?"
- Pick from: "If United States sales came in 10% ahead next year, how much of that would show in your EBITDA?" / "On your planning, in which year does the profit share overtake the minimum?" / "What would you say to an investor who models the United States as a fixed step each year to 2028?"
- Positive: profit share close to overtaking; upside flows through. Negative: "the guarantee underpins it", meaning extra sales do not reach earnings yet; no shape at all.

T3 ★ Shipments against what is selling. "Scanner data had shop sales up about 16% over the summer, while first-half shipments grew 11%. Consensus needs about 20% in the second half. With five weeks left, are shipments running ahead of or behind what is selling through?"
- Pick from: "How do distributor stock levels compare with a year ago?" / "The scanner data misses bars and restaurants. Stronger or weaker than the shops?" / "The national campaign started in April. What happens to growth when you lap it?"
- Positive: shipments tracking sales, stock lean. Negative: "phasing" or "timing of shipments".

T4 ★ Glass, aluminium and 2027. "In June we were told glass and energy were 'largely' hedged for 2027 and that any inflation would not touch profit expectations. Since then glass makers have cut capacity and are pushing prices, and gas is higher. Is 'largely' still the right word?"
- Pick from: "When is the 2027 glass price fixed?" / "Some analysts need the margin outside the United States at 26% to 27% by 2028, the highest in six years. What makes that easier than it looks?" / "How much price are you taking in the United Kingdom and Europe next year?" / "Same question on aluminium."
- Positive: a hedge percentage, unchanged; price rises planned. Negative: "won't impact" becoming "manageable" or "we have mitigations".

T5 ★ Guidance conservatism and the 2026 landing. "Guidance has not been raised since 2022. Some analysts read that as a company that only guides what is already banked. Others think there is nothing spare this year, because tariff refunds and last year's levy charge are doing the work. Which is closer?"
- Pick from: "Net of tariffs, refunds and the levy, is 2026 better or worse than you expected in March?" / "How much of the second-half step is one-off?" / "What clean base should analysts grow 2027 from?"
- Positive: names the clean base; hints at a buffer. Negative: will not separate the one-offs.

T6 ★ 2027 against the analysts. "Consensus has about £64m of EBITDA for 2027. In June 'mid-£60s' was called absolutely fine, with talk of an upgrade if distributors hit a tipping point. Five months on, is that tipping point closer, and has anything made mid-£60s harder?"
- Pick from: "If you could nudge analysts on one line, which: the United States, the margin elsewhere, or central costs?" / "Will March bring a 2027 number, or only the cash target?" / "Who is at the top of the range, and what do they believe that the others do not?"
- Positive: "very comfortable" again, with evidence. Negative: retreat to "we don't comment on consensus" or to the cash target.

T7 Which analysts get it, and what sceptics say. "Which two or three analysts understand the royalty model best, and where do the others go wrong?"
- Pick from: "What do you hear most from investors who have sold or will not buy?" / "Only one house publishes line-by-line forecasts. Will you publish a company-compiled consensus?"
- Read it this way: if the analysts she praises carry the lower numbers, consensus is too high.

T8 The United Kingdom and Europe. "In June the message was a positive corner in the United Kingdom and no tough consumer. Pubs have had a hard autumn. Would you still use those words?"
- Pick from: "Is Germany still inflecting?" / "Are non-tonic mixers still carrying the growth?"
- Positive: same words, with examples. Negative: "resilient", "in line", or a switch to talking only about shops.

T9 The dollar. "Some people say the dollar matters less now the United States is a royalty. Is that right, or does a minimum set in dollars make it matter more?"
- Pick from: "Do you hedge it?" / "At today's rate, does consensus need to move?"

T10 The same deal elsewhere, and new products. "The United States deal swaps margin for reach. If it is working, why not do the same in Europe or Asia? And if you would not, what does that say about how you rate the trade?"
- Pick from: "Is any market under review for a partner?" / "In June new products were described as incremental. Is anything in the next twelve months bigger than that?"
- Positive: a considered reason either way. Negative: "nothing planned" with no view on why.

T11 Cash and the Molson Coors stake. "You have no debt, a buyback running and a cash target of at least £100m over two years, while Molson Coors holds 9.4% and cannot add until early 2028. Some people say the buyback is quietly moving them towards control. How does the board think about that?"
- Pick from: "Is the next buyback tranche the same size?" / "When is the levy ruling due?"

The final, difficult question. "What is the one term in the Molson Coors contract that would move the share price most if investors could read it, and in which direction?"
