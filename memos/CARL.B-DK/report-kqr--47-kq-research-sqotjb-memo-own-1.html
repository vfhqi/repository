Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence demonstrates that the perimeter and financing structures of Carlsberg's Sapporo joint venture and hybrid bonds directly influence reported earnings per share through complex minority interest charges and non-standardized adjusted EPS definitions. The Key Question can be substantially answered across most dimensions, though critical gaps remain in unlisted entity disclosures and consolidation mechanics under IFRS 18. The analysis establishes high confidence on the joint venture perimeter and hybrid coupon treatments by major brokers, but medium confidence on precise non-controlling interest projections due to regional earnings volatility and incomplete sell-side modeling. The Southeast Asia and Hong Kong joint venture encompasses six markets–Singapore, Hong Kong, Malaysia, Laos, Vietnam, and Cambodia–and explicitly incorporates Carlsberg's 51% ownership of Carlsberg Brewery Malaysia and its 61% stake in Lao Brewery. This broad perimeter allows Sapporo Breweries to acquire 25% of highly profitable regional assets for USD 643 million. Carlsberg valued the transaction at 21.3x 2025 EBIT and 17.0x 2025 EBITDA, while Sapporo disclosed an investment valuation of 11.8x 2026e EBITDA. This valuation discrepancy reflects differences in forward estimates and the treatment of distribution synergies, underscoring that the transaction is highly accretive on historical metrics but depends on operational execution to justify the pricing.

Carlsberg Brewery Malaysia reported resilient performance for 2025, with operating profit growing 8.1% to RM 449.7 million and net profit rising 11.4% to RM 375.6 million despite a 4.9% revenue decline, while existing noncontrolling interests stood at RM 1.62 million. This robust local profitability directly supports the joint venture's earnings baseline. In contrast, Chongqing Brewery reported net profit of RMB 1.12 billion for the first nine months of 2026, with high-end product sales declining 4.5% to RMB 7.37 billion due to severe weather-driven product destocking.

These divergent regional trends– strength in Malaysia offset by weather drag in China –create forecasting complexity for Carlsberg's consolidated non-controlling interests.

Carlsberg's non-controlling interests reached DKK 656 million in H1 2026 compared to DKK 658 million in H1 2025, reflecting stable performance across Chongqing Brewery and Carlsberg Malaysia. However, total noncontrolling interest fell to DKK 1,023 million in 2025 from DKK 1,147 million in 2024, driven by lower profits in China. This demonstrates that macro headwinds can rapidly suppress consolidated minority charges, introducing significant forecasting risk.

The analysis projects that full-year 2026 non-controlling interests will approach DKK 1.30 billion based on regional earnings momentum, but this baseline will face structural adjustments from 2027 onwards as the Sapporo partnership shifts profit distribution.

Major sell-side brokers exhibit divergent modeling approaches for the Sapporo minority interest charge and its earnings impact.
