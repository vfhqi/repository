This severe underutilization undermines expected capital investment returns across the European segment.

Section 4 – Subsidy Pull-Forward and Post-Step-Down Demand Dynamics

Section summary
  1. German heat-pump demand is highly sensitive to policy changes, with historical subsidy uncertainty creating severe demand spikes followed by protracted volume collapses.
  2. Bi-annual subsidy step-downs through 2030 will stretch German consumer payback periods, eroding the long-term economic incentive for discretionary retrofits.
  3. Near-term earnings are exposed to volatile pre-buying cycles as both Nibe and Ariston rely on Germany for about twenty percent of total revenues.

Historical Policy Volatility and Demand Gaps

1.
Massive pull-forward: German consumer demand surged dramatically during 2023 in anticipation of strict building regulations and generous federal subsidy support. Jefferies reported on March 3, 2025, that German heat-pump sales rose 59% in 2023 relative to 2022. This regulatory rush created an unsustainable volume spike that ultimately exhausted near-term organic demand, proving how highly sensitive European market volumes are to legislative timelines. Consequently, Nibe's near-term earnings are vulnerable to policydriven demand shifts.
2.
Sudden contraction: German heating installations experienced a severe demand cliff once policy debates subsided and gas prices stabilized. Jefferies reported on March 3, 2025, that German heat-pump volumes collapsed by 48% in 2024 as consumer confidence eroded. This rapid decline illustrates that subsidy changes can create multi-quarter demand gaps that severely depress capacity utilization for premium manufacturers. Slower volume recovery directly impairs Nibe's ability to generate historical operating leverage and limits near-term EPS growth.
3.
Unfavorable running economics: German consumer switching incentives are highly sensitive to the operating cost spread between electricity and natural gas. DNB Carnegie reported on March 13, 2026, that electricity prices around 0.31 euros per kilowatt-hour limit the running-cost benefits of heat pumps over gas boilers. Without a significant narrowing of this utility price gap, heat-pump adoption will remain depressed. This unfavourable ratio means that underlying organic demand remains weak once initial pull-forward effects subside.

Comparable pull-forward: Historical policy transitions in international markets confirm that subsidy step-downs consistently trigger volatile pre-buy behavior followed by demand voids.   1. Distributor inventory levels normalized throughout 2025, clearing the way for a recovery in primary manufacturer orders.

Destocking Resolution and the Crossover

1.
Rapid inventory normalization: Distributor stocks fell significantly during the 2024 and 2025 destocking phase as global channels normalized. Deutsche Bank reported on March 8, 2026, that HVAC distributor inventories posted only 0.1% year-on-year growth in late 2025, confirming that field destocking is substantially complete across major European and American channels. This inventory clearing means that future installer purchases will directly translate into primary sales for heat-pump manufacturers, preventing further backlog deterioration.
2.
Decreasing stock ratios: European distributor inventory-to-sales ratios decreased throughout 2025 due to careful stock management. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on March 18, 2026, that the average European distributor inventory-to-sales ratio was 25.0% in February 2026, down from 27.2% in January 2026 as destocking progressed. This sequential decline means that distributors are operating with lean stock levels relative to historical averages. Consequently, manufacturers like Nibe should experience more stable order inflows in the coming quarters.
3.
Improving book-to-bill ratios: Distributor orders have begun to exceed sales, establishing a positive crossover as destocking ends. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on March 18, 2026, that European distributor book-to-bill ratios reached 126% in February 2026, up from 122% in the previous month and indicating a strong order trajectory. This crossover reflects that sell-in is growing slightly ahead of sell-out as channels stabilize.
