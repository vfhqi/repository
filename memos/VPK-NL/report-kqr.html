Section 2 – Tank Storage Capacity Renewals and Contract Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Royal Vopak exhibits high earnings stability as long-term contracts protect over 70% of its gas and industrial storage revenues.
  2. However, chemical and oil storage renewals face weak demand and rollover risks over the next 18 months.

Capacity Rollovers and Contract Structure

1.
Predictable capacity rollovers: Vopak maintains high revenue predictability because approximately 25% of contracts roll over on a take-or-pay basis annually, limiting near-term volatility.
2.
Long-dated gas storage: The gas storage segment enjoys high defensive qualities since 60% of original contract durations exceed ten years, insulating this capacity from near-term rate renewals.
3.
Highly insulated industrial contracts: Industrial terminals feature the most secure cash flows with 91% of capacity committed under contracts exceeding ten years, leaving minimal renewal exposure in 2026 and 2027.
4.
Volatile chemical contracts: Chemical terminals present significant contract renewal risks because 52% of capacity matures in less than three years, exposing Vopak to persistent chemical market headwinds.
5.
Intermediate oil exposure: Oil storage contracts show moderate contract durations with 40% of capacity expiring in less than three years, creating regular repricing cycles during contract renewals.

Product Rate Trajectories and Demand Drivers

6.
Strong oil storage demand: Middle East supply chain disruptions and geopolitical conflicts support robust oil storage demand and positive repricing trends, particularly at major hubs like Fujairah and Singapore.
7.
Weak chemical rate outlook: In contrast to oil, chemical storage rates face downward renewal pressure as demand for chemical storage continues to be weak across key manufacturing regions.
8.
Resilient gas throughput: High-capacity utilization at gas and industrial terminals underpins stable volume throughput and cash flows, countering weaker renewals in the chemical business lines.
9.
Foreign exchange headwinds: Although operational repricing is favorable, adverse currency movements could impact FY 2026 EBITDA by approximately EUR 20 million, slightly masking underlying rate strength.

Short-term Storage and Earnings Sustainability

10.
Growth-driven earnings expansion: The upgraded FY26 proportionate EBITDA guidance of EUR 1,180 million to EUR 1,220 million is primarily driven by contributions from newly commissioned growth projects rather than short-term spot storage spikes.
11.
Sustainable growth contributions: Vopak expects growth project EBITDA to rise from EUR 20 million in FY25 to EUR 45 million in FY26E and EUR 100 million in FY27E, proving that mid-term strength is structurally sound.
12.
Stable underlying occupancy: Maintained occupancy rates of 91% across Vopak's subsidiary network demonstrate that earnings strength is backed by consistent storage utilization rather than temporary, volatile speculative bookings.
13.
Other side of the story: A backwardated crude oil forward curve could severely depress oil storage occupancy and rates at Vopak's hubs, making the current earnings strength vulnerable to a rapid correction if market structures shift.

Section 4 – ElecLink Regulatory Profit-Sharing and Regulator Settlement Timelines

Section summary
  1. Getlink faces lingering regulatory cash flow uncertainty as CRE and Ofgem have yet to finalize the exact cash payment rules for ElecLink profit sharing.
  2. Accounting provisions under IAS 37 reached €580 million in first half 2026 to reflect high cumulative cross-border interconnector profits.

Regulatory Exemption and Profit-Sharing Structure

1.
Extended regulatory concession: ElecLink operates under a 25-year exemption from standard European Union electricity market rules granted in 2014 under Article 17 of Regulation 714/2009.
2.
Dual grid distribution: Any profit generated above the return threshold must be split equally between ElecLink and national grid operators Réseau de Transport d'Électricité and National Grid. The regulatory framework ensures that windfall interconnector revenues partially flow back to national grid systems across both jurisdictions.
3.
Tiered return threshold: Economic value generated by the interconnector is retained by Getlink until cumulative profits exceed a defined 13% pre-tax internal rate of return threshold. Crossing this threshold triggers the mandatory 50% profit-sharing calculation across remaining exemption years through 2047.
4.
Harmonized access governance: The French regulator CRE and British regulator Ofgem approved updated ElecLink Access Rules in early 2026 to ensure transparent market allocation. Both authorities maintain joint oversight over capacity splitting rules to safeguard non-discriminatory cross-border electricity trading between France and Great Britain.

5.
Unclarified public formalization: While regulatory discussions in 2025 confirmed core profit-sharing parameters, the regulators have not yet finalized the global public formalization of the application rules. This ongoing procedural dialogue leaves final calculation mechanics partially open to regulatory interpretation and adjustment across future reporting periods.

Accounting Provisions and IAS 37 Methodology

6.
Escalating balance sheet liability: Getlink expanded its total non-current IAS 37 profit-sharing provision to €580 million by 30 June 2026 compared to €516 million at year-end 2025. This liability expansion reflects strong cumulative interconnector profits realized since commercial operations commenced in May 2022.
7.
Structured P&L charges: The first half 2026 provision increase comprised €45 million recognized within operating costs and €19 million in financial discount unwinding expenses. In full-year 2025, Getlink previously charged an €80 million profit-sharing provision against operating income.
8.
Expert modeling support: Management established the IAS 37 provision using external expert models incorporating discounted cash flows and sensitivity tests across long-term spread scenarios over the exemption life. External auditors included an emphasis of matter highlighting significant judgment required to estimate future revenues and allowable project costs for the return calculation.
9.
Life-of-asset allocation approach: Rather than matching cash outlays to annual earnings, Getlink calculates its provision as a proportional contribution to expected lifetime profit-sharing obligations. Strong early merchant performance accelerated the timeline for reaching the contractual 13% IRR threshold in absolute terms.

Settlement Timelines and Cash Outflow Mechanics

10.
Absent cash outflows: Getlink confirmed that zero cash outflows have occurred under the profit-sharing mechanism from inception through first half 2026 despite substantial balance sheet provision accruals. The absence of historical cash payments has protected near-term liquidity and corporate cash conversion.
11.
Potential year-end regulatory clarity: Bernstein Research notes that regulatory discussions regarding criteria for the profit-sharing threshold remain ongoing with potential clarity expected near year-end 2026 or early 2027.
12.
Delayed payment execution: Executive management indicated during regulatory dialogue that profit-sharing cash settlement is not designed as an immediate annual outflow and remains subject to finalized regulatory protocols. The exact calendar timing and structure of future cash installments remain unconfirmed by CRE and Ofgem in public disclosures.
13.
Other side of the story: If regulators demand immediate or accelerated cash settlement of accrued profitsharing liabilities, Getlink could face sudden multi-hundred million euro cash outflows that compress group free cash flow conversion.

Section 7 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. Non-consensus resolution of interconnector spreads and short-term storage renewals would trigger accelerated corporate capital reallocation toward higher-yielding gas infrastructure across European energy networks.

Second-Order Corporate and Market Reallocations

1.
Accelerated capital rotation: High merchant spreads at ElecLink give Getlink the means, motive, and opportunity to accelerate its growth investments into green hydrogen and rail capacity.
2.
Elevated terminal valuation: Extended contango or short-term storage demand spikes allow Vopak to execute its four billion euro growth program without increasing balance sheet leverage.
3.
Outlier: Regional grid decoupling: Persistent cross-channel price differentials signal a structural decoupling between British and French power grids that consensus models fail to capture.
4.
Means-motive-opportunity on storage: Industrial customers possess the means and motive to bypass third- party storage by building proprietary chemical import terminals during contract renewal windows.

Third-Order Structural and Policy Cascades

5.
Means-motive-opportunity on regulation: Energy regulators CRE and Ofgem hold the means, motive, and opportunity to enforce stricter cap-and-floor revenue regimes on future interconnector projects like ElecLink 2.

7.
Outlier: ARENH replacement arbitrage: The expiration of France's ARENH mechanism exposes cross-border transmission flows to unprecedented power price volatility across European trading desks.
8.
Shifted capital returns: Sustained high free cash flow from interconnector capacity sales enables Getlink to fund its one point seven billion cash return program while preserving debt ratios.
9.
Other side of the story: Rapid regulatory tightening and lower power spreads could permanently reduce interconnector terminal multiples and constrain long-term equity valuations.
