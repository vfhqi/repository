Section 2 – Allocation of Romanian Exit Tax Benefits and Spectrum Renewal

Section summary
  1. OTE is highly likely to retain its €170m Romanian tax benefits rather than distributing them in 2027. Net read: Retaining this cash windfall allows OTE to fund the 2027 spectrum renewals without drawing on organic free cash flow.
  2. The cash retention strategy secures OTE's high-yield base distribution and protects its exceptionally strong balance sheet.

Magnitude of Romanian tax windfalls

1.
Substantial windfall magnitude: Hellenic Telecom has projected a total reported free cash flow of approximately €750m for the financial year 2026. This headline figure includes substantial positive one-off tax items from Romania that inflate the cash generation beyond normal levels. The presence of this windfall creates a temporary cash buffer that management must decide how to allocate. This tax windfall could support a higher temporary payout unless retained.
2.
Disclosed underlying baseline: Management has clearly delineated that the underlying free cash flow without these tax benefits is estimated to be between €570m and €580m. This comparison indicates that the positive tax effect from the Romanian exit represents a one-off benefit of €170m to €180m (calculated). The board has emphasized that this underlying run-rate reflects the true operational strength of the Greek business. Sustaining this underlying level is critical for long-term dividends.
3.
Expected capex commitments: Management intends to maintain elevated capital expenditures of approximately €600m in 2026 to focus on fiber-to-the-home expansion. This target matches the capital budget of prior years and represents a significant reinvestment of organic cash into the Greek network. High network reinvestment leaves little room for extraordinary payouts without utilizing one-off proceeds. This capital intensity means OTE must manage its cash windfalls very carefully.
4.
Historical disposal distributions: The company completed the sale of Telekom Romania Mobile on October 1, 2025, which ended its presence in Romania. Following the closing, OTE decided to distribute the €40m of net proceeds as an extraordinary dividend of €0.10 per share to its investors. This historical precedent demonstrates that actual asset sale proceeds are directly distributed rather than retained. However, the tax benefits from the exit are being treated differently.

Spectrum liabilities and peer comparisons

5.
Upcoming spectrum liabilities: Hellenic Telecom faces major spectrum renewal auctions in 2027 for the 900MHz and 1800MHz frequency bands. Deutsche Bank Research includes €20m in perpetual annual spectrum costs in its OTE model starting from 2026 to reflect long-term spectrum license outlays conservatively. This ongoing liability reduces the bank's projected total cash returns to shareholders by €20m annually. These spectrum requirements prevent the company from paying out the entire 2026 free cash flow.
6.
Peer auction comparison: Historical Greek spectrum auctions show that operators routinely face multi-million euro outlays, such as the €117m paid by OTE in 2020. During that same 2020 auction, peer operator Vodafone Greece paid €124m while Nova paid €115m for their respective spectrum blocks. These past figures illustrate that Greek spectrum auctions are highly competitive and require substantial upfront cash reserves from all market participants. Retaining the tax benefits allows OTE to enter the 2027 auction in a superior financial position.
7.
Spectrum auction timing: Management has indicated that the exact timing and structure of the 2027 spectrum auction remain subject to final regulatory decisions. The Greek regulator EETT is expected to clarify the rules, pricing, and participant list toward the end of the year. This regulatory uncertainty means that retaining the Romanian exit tax savings serves as an essential financial safety cushion for Hellenic Telecom. A delayed auction could change the timing of when this cash is finally spent.

Strategic capital allocation choices

8.
Cash retention strategy: Morgan Stanley expects that instead of distributing the Romanian tax benefits to shareholders, Hellenic Telecom will use them to offset spectrum renewal costs in 2027. This assessment suggests that the company is prioritizing balance sheet protection over immediate cash distributions. By keeping these tax proceeds within the company, OTE can comfortably fund the upcoming spectrum auction without increasing leverage. This decision will keep the 2027 shareholder distribution based strictly on organic cash flows.
9.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley suggests that Hellenic Telecom could distribute a special dividend following the sale of the Romanian business rather than retaining all cash. This view contradicts the expectation that the entire Romanian exit tax windfall must be retained for the spectrum auction. If the company chooses to pay out a portion of these tax savings, shareholder distributions in 2027 would exceed organic cash flow expectations. This would provide a significant short-term boost to OTE's overall dividend yield.
10.
Parent company leverage: Majority owner Deutsche Telekom is highly focused on disciplined capital allocation and cash upstreaming from its European subsidiaries. However, Deutsche Telekom also prioritizes long-term value creation and network leadership across its footprint, including OTE. Therefore, the parent company is likely to support cash retention if it secures OTE's spectrum position without increasing debt. This strategic alignment suggests that the parent company will not push for an immediate distribution of the tax one-off.
11.
Operational cash generation: Hellenic Telecom is on track to deliver solid organic cash generation, with EBITDA expected to grow by approximately 3% in 2026. This growth is supported by resilient mobile service revenues and expanding fiber-to-the-home adoption in the Greek retail market. Steady operational performance ensures that the company can sustain high base distributions without relying on one-off windfalls. Strong underlying cash flows reduce the pressure on management to distribute the tax benefits.

Balance sheet strength and exit benefits

12.
Expected cash cushion: By retaining the €170m tax benefit, OTE's net debt is projected to remain exceptionally low at around €324m by the end of 2026. This leverage level is equivalent to a net debt to EBITDA ratio of under 0.4x, which is significantly below European peer averages of over 1.5x. Maintaining this conservative balance sheet gives Hellenic Telecom the flexibility to execute its fiber rollout and spectrum renewals simultaneously. This low leverage minimizes refinancing risks in any volatile interest rate environment.
13.
Strategic allocation consensus: Most sell-side analysts agree that the most prudent path is to smooth spectrum costs over time using the Romanian tax buffer. This capital allocation choice prevents any sharp drop in shareholder returns during the actual auction year. By using the tax savings to fund the spectrum renewal, OTE maintains a steady dividend growth profile through 2027. Any other path would introduce unnecessary volatility into the 2027 payout.
14.
Operational margin focus: Hellenic Telecom has successfully eliminated the EBITDA drag from Romania, where margins averaged 12% compared to over 40% in Greece. This operational exit significantly improves the group's overall cash conversion efficiency, allowing for more predictable future cash flows. The elimination of this drag means that OTE's core business is structurally healthier. Consequently, the company is less dependent on temporary tax benefits to fund its capital commitments.
15.
Other side of the story: While the consensus view holds that OTE will retain the tax benefits to fund the spectrum renewal, Deutsche Bank Research notes the company has no need to retain yearly free cash flow. With its exceptionally low net debt to EBITDA ratio, OTE could easily distribute the tax windfall directly to shareholders and fund any 2027 spectrum auctions through debt. This alternative strategy would maximize immediate dividend yields for investors. However, this path would reduce the company's conservative cash buffer.

Track record of capital returns

1.
Steady capital returns: OTE has demonstrated a strong track record of capital returns, distributing approximately 90% of its cumulative free cash flow over the three-year period leading to late 2025. This reliable payout strategy reflects the company's commitment to returning excess cash to its shareholders while maintaining a highly disciplined balance sheet. Investors have benefited from this predictable remuneration policy, which aligns the company's equity story with defensive sector leaders. The structural efficiency of these returns has helped narrow OTE's historical valuation discount relative to its European peers.
2.
Policy formulation changes: On February 25, 2026, the Board of Directors of OTE approved a new shareholder remuneration policy linked to actual prior-year free cash flow performance. This updated framework replaced the previous system of using projected cash flows, significantly enhancing financial transparency and corporate governance. The revised policy commits to returning 70% to 100% of the prior year's actual free cash flow to shareholders. This tax asset stems from the accounting loss OTE realized on the disposal of the Romanian operations. This tax break equates to €0.31 per OTE share, or about 2% of the company's total equity value. The substantial cash savings from this tax break will significantly bolster OTE's financial flexibility in upcoming years.
