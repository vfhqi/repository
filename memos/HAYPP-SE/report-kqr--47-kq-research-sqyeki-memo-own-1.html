Section 3 – Cost Classification and Gross Margin Evolution

Section summary
  1. The expansion of Haypp Group's gross margin from fifteen percent in 2024 to nineteen point two percent in mid-2025 was largely driven by a structural accounting shift.
  2. Automating the regional Texas warehouse relocated historical third-party logistics fees from cost of goods sold to depreciation below the operating profit line.

Significant Gross Margin Expansion

1.
Accelerated margin improvement: Haypp Group recorded a notable rise in its reported gross margin from 15.0% for the full year of 2024 to a peak of 19.2% in the second quarter of 2025. This expansion of over four percentage points sparked investor questions regarding whether underlying commercial pricing power or accounting changes drove the gain. Broker analysis confirmed that while commercial terms improved, structural adjustments played a primary role in this evolution. Management attributed the initial peak to a combination of favorable product mix and better supplier agreements.
2.
Favorable inventory valuation: Haypp Group maintained its core inventory valuation policy of measuring goods at the lower of cost or net realizable value using the first-in, first-out method across both fiscal years. The company clarified in its 2025 disclosures that merchandise costs are determined after accounting for all supplierfunded discounts. This explicit wording did not represent an active accounting policy alteration but rather a clarification of existing practice. Consequently, inventory accounting rules themselves did not explain the gross margin expansion during 2025.
3.
Contrary evidence: Continuous margin volatility impacted the latter half of the year as the group's reported gross margin contracted from 19.2% in the second quarter of 2025 down to 17.4% by the fourth quarter of 2025. This sequential decline occurred because of renewed growth investments and negative mix from expanding lower-margin segments. The margin contraction demonstrates that the high profitability levels recorded in early 2025 were not entirely permanent.
4.
Stable reporting categories: Haypp Group utilized the exact same principal operating expense categories in its statutory income statements for both 2024 and 2025. These recurring financial statement line items included goods for resale, other external costs, personnel expenses, and depreciation. Financial analysts observed that the company did not execute any broad, disclosed reallocation of standard operational costs. Thus, the group avoided any overt reclassification of operating overheads to artificially boost gross profitability.

Logistics Automation and Cost Shifts

5.
Structural logistics reallocation: Haypp Group experienced a major shift in its cost classification because of the automation of its US warehouse facility in Texas. Prior to this mid-December 2024 installation, third-party logistics warehouse service fees directly reduced the reported gross margin within cost of goods sold. After the automation was completed, these operating expenses transitioned to depreciation which is reported below the adjusted EBITDA line. This operational shift explains approximately half of the depreciation cost increase observed during the period.
6.
Escalating depreciation expenses: Haypp Group reported that its quarterly depreciation and amortization charges rose to SEK 20.3 million in the first quarter of 2025. This charge represented a sequential increase from the depreciation levels recorded in late 2024. Management confirmed that the automated Texas facility was the main catalyst for this asset-related cost expansion. By moving fulfillment overhead from variable logistics fees to fixed depreciation, the firm structurally elevated its gross margin while increasing its operating leverage.
7.
Favorable procurement benefits: Haypp Group benefited from strengthened bargaining power with major nicotine manufacturers during the first half of 2025. Nordic financial analysts noted that improved supplier agreements and favorable procurement terms helped support the gross margin alongside the warehouse cost reclassification. These enhanced commercial agreements allowed the firm to achieve sustained gross profit expansion despite temporary volume headwinds. Therefore, the gross margin rise reflected both commercial achievements and accounting presentation shifts.
8.
Expanded comparability metrics: Haypp Group introduced a broader definition of items affecting comparability in its 2025 annual report to include bonuses and depreciation. This adjustment allowed management to define and reconcile adjusted operating expenses as an alternative performance measure. While this revised disclosure did not alter the statutory operating profit, it gave investors more detailed transparency regarding overhead trends.

Because the pre-2025 financial periods include thirdparty logistics fees within cost of goods sold, they are not directly comparable to post-2025 periods. Consequently, failure to adjust for this operational shift leads to an overestimation of actual commercial margin expansion.
