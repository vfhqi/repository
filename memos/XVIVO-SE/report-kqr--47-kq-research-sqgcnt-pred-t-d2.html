Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The financial divergence of Xvivo depends on concentrated product launches and regulatory approvals.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Formulating the inquiry: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Clinical dominance: The core of Xvivo's commercial success remains heavily reliant on its Thoracic segment, which generated about 70 percent of sales in Q3 2026. This segment includes proprietary solutions and machine perfusion systems designed to optimize organ preservation. Resolving whether future growth is concentrated in heart commercialization or broader lung expansion is critical for forecasting future earnings per share over the next 18 months.

Financial and Commercial Drivers

3.
Thoracic expansion: The commercial rollout of the European heart technology and US FDA regulatory approval are pivotal near-term inflection points. Xvivo submitted its heart PMA application to the US FDA in Q2 2026, targeting a US launch by late 2027. If the FDA review is delayed, earnings projections could face significant downside because the heart opportunity represents the largest long-term growth catalyst.
4.
Segment recovery: The financial outcomes over the next four years are also tied to the recovery of abdominal gross margins and the Services unit. In Q3 2026, Services gross margin was negative 19 percent due to reduced organ recovery volumes. To meet consensus, Xvivo must return Services to profitability and push abdominal gross margins toward 70 percent through manufacturing scale and product launches.
5.
Valuation premium: Xvivo's historical valuation has been highly sensitive to execution and clinical milestones. RedEye notes that peer transactions, such as Getinge's acquisition of Paragonix at 11x EV/sales, support Xvivo's premium multiples. However, because quarterly growth has historically fluctuated by up to 50 percentage points, earnings volatility remains a key risk that could widen the range of plausible investment outcomes.

Regulatory and Macroeconomic Risks

6.
Customer concentration: Growth in the lung segment faces a transition risk as Xvivo's largest US customer, Lung Bioengineering, shifts to its own EVLP platform. DNB Carnegie estimates Xvivo's exposure to this outphasing is relatively limited at 5 to 6 percent of 2027 revenue. Stronger adoption of the hub-and-spoke model among other US Organ Procurement Organizations is expected to offset this revenue headwind and support overall earnings growth.
7.
Macroeconomic volatility: Fluctuations in the Swedish krona present a substantial unhedged currency risk for international earnings. Xvivo has a significant exposure, with approximately 55 percent of sales denominated in US dollars and 35 percent in euros. This high sensitivity means a 10 percent dollar move could swing EBITDA margins by two points, skewing the range of EPS outcomes significantly over the 18-month forecast horizon.
8.
Other side of the story: While bulls focus on rapid multi-organ expansion, Xvivo's future financial results could be highly volatile if launch timelines slip. Delays in product approvals or slower-than-expected European heart reimbursement present significant headwinds. RedEye cautions that US heart and liver commercial investments could put downward pressure on operating margins in H2 2027 before delivering material financial contributions in 2028.

Section 3 – Thoracic Lung Growth through OPO Hubs and Customer Concentration

Section summary
  1. Centralized OPO hub adoption will offset the impending step-down of Xvivo's largest US customer. Net read: Regulatory decertification of underperforming OPOs will accelerate the demand for machine perfusion technologies.

Risks

US Lung Segment Growth

1.
Accelerated organic growth: Xvivo delivered exceptional performance in its Thoracic segment with organic sales growth of 51 percent in the third quarter of 2026. This underlying growth was driven by robust momentum in the US lung market, where EVLP disposable sales surged by 67 percent during the quarter. Increased activity among major clinical transplant centers continues to solidify Xvivo's global market leadership in lung preservation. Near-term earnings will remain highly dependent on sustaining this strong clinical momentum across North American centers.
2.
Hub model traction: The company's centralized US Organ Procurement Organization hub strategy continues to gain significant commercial traction with three active OPOs and five more currently in the pipeline. Under this innovative model, Xvivo places its XPS hardware in centralized OPO facilities to streamline regional organ assessment. RedEye reports that these placements are now accounted for as finance leases, which recognizes the machine revenue upfront rather than over time. This accounting treatment provides an immediate boost to thoracic revenues as new hubs onboard.
3.
Historical market recovery: Xvivo's US lung business has historically recovered from temporary market contractions and customer destocking cycles. For example, during the first half of 2025, a softer EVLP market and customer destocking led to a 40 percent sales decline to its largest customer. However, Xvivo successfully returned to growth in late 2025 as underlying EVLP volumes rebounded strongly across other clinical centers. This historical resilience indicates that Xvivo's diversified clinical base can absorb periodic customer volatility.
4.
Contrary evidence: A former pulmonary and critical care physician expressed skepticism regarding the widespread viability of the OPO hub model. The expert noted that they have not personally seen harvested lungs being routinely transported to third-party centralized hubs for perfusion. High capital costs and slow clinical adoption are expected to limit rapid near-term market penetration of stationary EVLP systems. This indicates that the OPO expansion strategy may progress slower than management's ambitious projections.

Largest Customer Step-Down Risks

5.
Customer transition headwinds: Xvivo faces a major transition risk as its largest US customer, Lung Bioengineering, shifts to its own proprietary EVLP platform. Lung Bioengineering is a subsidiary of United Therapeutics, which received regulatory approval in June 2026 for its proprietary platform. Xvivo expects sales to this major customer to decline over time, though the exact timing remains highly uncertain. Any sudden reduction in purchases from this account could create a substantial near-term revenue drag for the lung business.
6.
Sizing customer exposure: DNB Carnegie calculated Xvivo's revenue exposure to the Lung Bioengineering outphasing at approximately 5 to 6 percent of 2027 projected sales. The broker notes that this exposure is relatively undramatic because the highest gross margin components will still be purchased from Xvivo. Furthermore, the ongoing expansion of the centralized OPO hub model is expected to successfully offset the shortfall in the coming years. Consequently, the long-term impact on consolidated earnings per share appears manageable unless other major accounts transition.
7.
Offsetting revenue losses: RedEye argues that Xvivo is well-positioned to mitigate the impending revenue loss from United Therapeutics' transition starting in 2027. The broker highlights that Xvivo will continue to supply certain proprietary disposables to LBE, allowing it to benefit from their volume growth. Additionally, increasing XPS machine adoption among other US transplant centers and active OPOs will partially absorb the transition impact. This diversified commercial base should protect thoracic segment profit margins from severe deterioration.
8.
Customer stocking distortion: The thoracic segment's Q3 2026 performance was temporarily inflated by 10 million SEK of inventory build-up by its largest US customer. Chief Financial Officer Kristoffer Nordström warned that this top-up could mean that Q4 sales to this specific customer will be slightly less than expected. This inventory distortion highlights the unpredictable volatility of major account purchases on a quarterly basis. Relying on consolidated quarterly run-rates can therefore lead to overly optimistic near-term EPS forecasts.

Regulatory OPO Decertification Trends

9.
Regulatory consolidation pressures: The US organ procurement system is undergoing a massive regulatory shake-up driven by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services. The CMS performance-based tiering rules, which evaluate donor conversion and organ transplantation rates, have put underperforming OPOs under immense pressure. Organizations that fail to meet these stringent federal metrics face decertification, which is expected to drive rapid technology adoption to maximize organ yields. This regulatory mandate serves as a major commercial tailwind for Xvivo's perfusion devices.
10.
Decertification timeline impacts: A former director at Xvivo noted that the federal OPO performance evaluation period represents a major industry turning point. For example, the organ procurement organization in Miami recently lost its certification due to poor performance outcomes. With another round of regulatory cuts scheduled for 2027, underperforming OPOs have only 12 months to improve outcomes or face closure. This tight timeline will likely force historically slow-moving organizations to rapidly integrate machine perfusion systems.
11.
Consolidation volume shifts: The decertification of underperforming OPOs is expected to trigger significant industry consolidation as high-performing organizations absorb failing territories. A director at Mid-America Transplant explained that high-performing Tier 1 OPOs will bid on contracts to take over these regional volumes immediately. Because larger, consolidated OPOs possess greater capital resources, they are far more likely to invest in advanced perfusion infrastructure. This geographic expansion of high-performing hubs will ultimately accelerate Xvivo's long-term disposable sales growth.
12.
Allocation policy scrutiny: Recent media and regulatory scrutiny regarding out-of-sequence organ allocations has added further operational complexity for US OPOs. In 2025, the Organ Procurement and Transplantation Network issued strict directives declaring out-of-sequence practices inconsistent with official transplantation policy. This heightened regulatory oversight caused kidney transplant volumes to fall by 7 percent at OPOs with large allocation adjustments. Similar regulatory scrutiny in the lung segment could temporarily constrain overall organ recovery volumes and impact consumable sales.
