Section 2 – Coal Spin-off and Ring-fencing Proposals

Section summary
  1. We have medium confidence that Polish utilities' coal assets will be ring-fenced because Fitch's September 2026 assessments show capacity revenues declining and conventional assets becoming unprofitable after 2028.
  2. The state asset restructuring negotiations remain highly complex because of divergent balance sheet strengths, with Enea carrying the highest exposure to coal mining.
  3. The European Commission has only granted short-term capacity payment derogations until 2027, leaving a major funding gap for conventional assets after 2028.

Government and Utility Negotiations

1.
Negotiating structural terms: The Polish Ministry of State Assets and domestic utilities are actively negotiating the restructuring of fossil-fuel generators to protect parent balance sheets. Fitch Ratings' press release of 28 September 2026 notes that PGE remains Poland's largest power utility with the lowest leverage, whereas peers like Enea carry much higher exposure to hard coal generation and mining. If these negotiations fail to transfer conventional generation debts to the state, the parents' ratings will remain under pressure, heavily impacting future earnings per share.
2.
Advancing nuclear strategy: Polish utilities are seeking alternative base-load options as they transition away from conventional generation. The BGZ BNP Paribas morning commentary of 14 October 2026 reported that the Ministry of Industry, PEJ, and PGE signed a key agreement to prepare for the development of Poland's second nuclear power plant. This strategic shift towards nuclear power will require massive long-term capital but will eventually replace lost coal capacity, providing sustained structural support for PGE's long-term earnings.
3.
Maintaining backup capacity: Utilities are forced to keep older fossil units running to bridge the gap before clean energy comes online. In a press release published on 11 September 2026, Tauron's CEO Grzegorz Lot explained that the company must maintain backup capacity until new gas-fired and renewable assets are operational. This operational strategy means that these conventional plants can remain competitive until 2028, which prevents immediate write-downs from impacting EPS.
4.
Divergent utility exposure: The financial risk of delaying the coal carve-out is not distributed evenly across the Polish utility sector. Fitch Ratings' press release of 28 September 2026 highlights that Enea's maximum leverage sensitivity for a BBB rating is 3.0 times, which is more restrictive than PGE's limit of 3.7 times. Because Enea has higher exposure to hard coal-fired generation and mining, its earnings per share are much more vulnerable to delayed spin-offs than PGE's.

Financial Terms and Loss Funding

5.
Declining capacity revenues: Under the current regulatory landscape, government capacity payments to coal plants are projected to fall significantly. Fitch Ratings' report on 28 September 2026 estimated that conventional generation capacity revenues will average PLN 914 million in 2026 and 2027, before falling to PLN 534 million over the 2028 to 2030 period. This sharp drop in capacity revenue will directly hurt profit margins, drag down net income, and pressure earnings per share unless state-backed compensation steps in.
6.
Bleak long-term profitability: Rising environmental costs and falling wholesale power prices threaten the financial viability of conventional coal plants. According to a Fitch Ratings press release of 28 September 2026, capacity payments are likely to be insufficient to keep coal-fired generation profitable after 2028. As a result, parents like Tauron and Enea must absorb these heavy operating losses, which will severely suppress their future EPS.
7.
Negative cash flows: Capital expenditures for the transition are straining the financial metrics of major Polish power operators. Fitch Ratings reported on 28 September 2026 that high capital expenditures will drive Tauron's free cash flow negative, lifting its leverage from 1.9 times in 2025 to an average of 3.9 times between 2026 and 2030. This debt accumulation restricts dividend capacity and lowers earnings per share unless coal liabilities are successfully carved out.
8.
Restructuring transition funds: Regional funding from European programs offers some financial relief for territories undergoing coal phase-outs. Bird and Bird's regulatory update of 23 September 2026 stated that Poland's allocation under the Just Transition Fund is approximately EUR 4.4 billion for five regions. These public subsidies help offset local modernization costs, meaning that utilities can preserve precious capital and support their consolidated EPS.

9.

Fitch Ratings' analysis of 28 September 2026 notes that Tauron secured capacity market revenues of PLN 543 million for 2026 and PLN 459 million for 2027 under European Commission temporary derogations. These payments ensure short-term profitability for older plants that would otherwise be loss-making, protecting EPS through 2027.
10.

Bird and Bird's legal advisory on 30 September 2026 detailed that the new ETS2 carbon allowance system is scheduled to start operations in 2028. This scheme will impose heavy compliance costs on fuel suppliers, driving coal operational costs higher and threatening parent utility earnings per share.

Rzeczpospolita reported on 9 October 2026 that Russia blocked a major rail route carrying Kazakh coal, which represents 62% of Polish coal imports, to Poland. This supply disruption forces utilities to buy expensive local alternatives, which drives up production costs and hurts near-term EPS.

Because conventional assets remain highly profitable in the short term, the urgency to agree on spin-off terms with the Ministry has temporarily diminished, which delayed structural re-rating benefits for EPS.

Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Failing to carve out coal assets will severely damage the earnings per share of Polish utilities due to negative cash flows and rising environmental liabilities.
  2. Regulators and the state ministry have the power to enforce a gradual transition that keeps fossil units on corporate balance sheets.

Earnings Impact of Failed Spin-offs

1.
Severe earnings deterioration: Failing to spin off fossil units will expose corporate balance sheets to escalating carbon compliance expenses. Fitch Ratings warned on 28 September 2026 that rising environmental costs will put clean dark spreads under pressure, making coal generation unprofitable after 2028. If PGE and its peers must absorb these losses on their own balance sheets, their consolidated net income will collapse. This structural drag would prevent any meaningful growth in consolidated earnings per share.
2.
Restricted dividend capacity: Unresolved conventional liabilities will prevent Polish power operators from returning cash to their shareholders. Fitch Ratings expected on 28 September 2026 that high capital expenditures will drive Tauron's free cash flow negative by an average of PLN 4.7 billion annually between 2026 and 2030. Without carving out these capital-intensive fossil operations, parent companies must pile on massive debts. Consequently, the resulting leverage expansion will severely limit future dividend payments and dilute earnings per share.
3.
Divergent credit risks: Enea faces a much tighter path to financial stability than its larger peer PGE if the spin-offs fail. Fitch Ratings' credit profile of 28 September 2026 showed that Enea has higher exposure to hard coal-fired generation and hard coal mining. Because Enea has a more restrictive maximum leverage sensitivity of 3.0 times, absorbing ongoing coal losses will trigger rating downgrades. This financial stress means that Enea's earnings per share are highly vulnerable to prolonged restructuring delays.

Systemic Pressure from Steering Entities

4.
Ministry dividend demands: The Ministry of State Assets has conflicting fiscal priorities that could divert utility cash flows away from clean investments. A news report from 3 September 2026 quoted Poland's state assets minister Wojciech Balczun stating that the ministry expected significantly higher dividend income from state-owned companies. If the state enforces aggressive payout policies to fund its budget, PGE and Tauron will lack the capital required for their grid transitions. This cash drain would impair clean growth and hurt future earnings per share.
5.
European regulatory constraints: Strict European Union climate mandates limit the scope of long-term state support for unprofitable fossil generators. An energy transition update from Uniper on 18 September 2026 noted that Poland plans to cut its hard-coal-fired power plant fleet from 18.5GW to 6-7GW by 2030. The European Commission restricts capacity payments past 2028 unless plants meet strict emission limits. Unless the ministry secures unprecedented derogations, these plants must be closed or operated at a severe loss.
6.
Compromised clean transitions: Diverting capital to maintain older coal blocks will slow down the growth of high-margin regulated networks. Fitch Ratings reported on 28 September 2026 that Tauron allocates about fifty-five percent of its capex to distribution networks and twenty-five percent to renewables. If the utility must fund ongoing conventional losses, it will be forced to defer these profitable green projects. Delaying these investments would permanently cap regulatory asset base expansion and drag down long-term EPS.

Early Indicators of Gradual Phase-out

This PLN 886 million investment represents a localized approach to phase out coal by 2028.
