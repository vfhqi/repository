Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. SMA Solar enters the Q1 2026 earnings with recovering market confidence following a highly volatile multi-year cyclical downturn in its residential solar inverter segment.
  2. Sell-side expectations have rebuilt rapidly with Jefferies and Deutsche Bank upgrading the stock to buy ahead of the May release, driven by unprecedented large-scale order backlogs.
  3. Persistent competitive pricing from Chinese manufacturers and high investment overheads remain significant profitability headwinds for the turnaround.
1.
Established industry provider: SMA Solar is a leading global supplier of solar inverters, battery storage systems, and energy management solutions. The company operates primarily through two segments, namely Large Scale & Project Solutions and Home & Business Solutions, which are increasingly structured around a shared solar inverters and energy management technological platform.
2.
Intense global competition: SMA Solar ranked as the fifth-largest manufacturer globally with a market share of approximately 6% in 2021. This global inverter market remains heavily dominated by Chinese competitors like Sungrow and Huawei.
3.
Disproportionate Chinese market share: Market intelligence indicates that Chinese solar equipment suppliers hold roughly 80% of the global inverter market. This massive concentration has forced SMA Solar to focus its competitive efforts on regional compliance and cybersecurity.
4.
High cyclical volatility: Financial results show severe multi-year financial cyclicality as revenue peaked at €1,904 million in fiscal year 2023. Revenue subsequently contracted by 19.6% in fiscal year 2024 to €1,530 million, before flatlining at €1,516 million in fiscal year 2025 due to a steep residential downturn.
5.
Severely compressed profitability: Operating EBITDA collapsed from a positive €313 million in fiscal year 2023 to negative €5.9 million in fiscal year 2024. This profit erosion led to basic earnings per share losses of negative €5.22 in fiscal year 2025.
6.
Severe asset write-downs: Earnings in fiscal year 2025 were heavily weighed down by a massive €261 million in provisions, and restructuring expenses. These extensive charges dragged operating EBIT down to negative €188 million.
7.
Strong share appreciation: The share price rebounded by approximately 40% year-to-date by late April 2026 as market sentiment pivoted toward optimism. This rally followed a prolonged downward cycle where the stock traded within a wide range of €12.17 to €38.76 in early March 2026, showing strong appreciation of approximately forty percent.

Rapid sell-side rerating: Deutsche Bank research analyst Pulvermacher maintained a hold rating with a target price of €17 in November 2025. Pulvermacher raised the target to €28 in March 2026 and then to €44 in April 2026, executing a valuation transition to updated near-term forecasts.

Aggressive target upgrades: Jefferies analyst Hesse upgraded SMA Solar from hold to buy in March 2026, raising the price target from €39 to €57. Hesse further lifted the price target to €71 in May 2026 ahead of the first-quarter results.

Section 16 – Conclusion

The first quarter 2026 earnings announcement on May 13, 2026, revealed that SMA Solar delivered group sales of €340.9 million, representing a modest 4% year-over-year expansion that fell slightly short of consensus expectations of €342 million. Operating EBITDA reached €26.1 million, missing published broker consensus of €28 million by approximately 7%, while EBIT of €13.3 million translated to a margin of 3.9% against sell-side expectations of 4.4%.

This mild operating miss against elevated pre-announcement expectations marked a critical inflection point in the company's multi-year turnaround narrative, as investors had priced in an aggressive recovery trajectory following the share price's approximately 40% year-to-date rally into the release. The most significant strategic shift revealed in the Q1 2026 results was the stark divergence in divisional performance, with the Home & Business Solutions segment recording sales of €61.4 million representing robust 27.4% year-over-year growth, while the Large Scale & Project Solutions segment stagnated at the prior year's level of €279.5 million.

Management capitalized on this residential demand pickup by refining its full-year 2026 guidance to the upper third of the range and highlighting the encouraging 27% sales growth in the struggling Home & Business Solutions division as proof of initial demand recovery. This strategic messaging pivot toward emphasizing residential stabilization represented a fundamental change from the prior consensus view that Large Scale utility projects would exclusively drive the group's profitability recovery.

The company's total order backlog increased to €1,414.5 million from €1,293.9 million in the prior year's quarter, reflecting strong project demand in Large Scale which provides high near-term revenue visibility. By June 2026, this massive project pipeline had expanded further to 1.8 billion euros, providing strong revenue visibility for the next several quarters. This unprecedented large-scale order accumulation fundamentally altered the investment thesis by demonstrating that utility-scale battery storage demand was not merely a transient phenomenon but rather a structural market shift that could sustain multi-year growth even as residential volumes remained volatile.

The bull case synthesized from post-earnings sell-side analysis rests on three primary pillars. Jefferies analysts Hesse and Comtesse raised their target price to €71 from €58 following the Q1 release, citing earnings estimate upgrades as the primary driver of their revised valuation. Jefferies analysts argued that the Q1 performance confirms that the worst of the residential downturn is over, signaling a sequential P&L turnaround is underway for the company.

Post-earnings commentary from Jefferies highlighted that utility-scale battery energy storage systems are no longer a temporary trend, with storage driving solid large-scale orders and accounting for more than 50% of Q1 bookings. The optimistic interpretation holds that the combination of accelerating cost reductions under the €250 million restructuring program, recovering residential demand driven by distributor restocking, and structural policy tailwinds from European and American trade protectionism will enable the company to reach sustainable profitability by fiscal year 2027.

The bear case presents a more sobering assessment of the company's structural challenges. Deutsche Bank research analysts noted that while structural self-help progress is visible, SMA Solar's long-term earnings trajectory is historically characterized by uneven performance and high volatility, prompting them to remain on the sidelines prior to Q2. Deutsche Bank analysts cautioned that any slower-than-expected recovery in German rooftop solar or persistent pricing pressure from China could delay the HBS division's goal of reaching breakeven by 2027.

Intense competition from low-cost Chinese manufacturers, who hold roughly eighty percent of the global market, has caused immense price pressure to persist in the residential and commercial sectors, forcing SMA Solar to focus its sales efforts on premium software. The skeptical interpretation emphasizes that the Q1 2026 residential sales recovery may have been artificially inflated by transient geopolitical demand rather than structural market healing, while the Large Scale segment faces margin compression from elevated service investments and aggressive Chinese pricing.
