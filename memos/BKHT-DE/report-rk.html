EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Section summary
  1. Brockhaus Technologies has not been targeted by published short-sellers, though its 2025 internal compliance crisis represents a severe accounting near-miss

Brockhaus Technologies has not been targeted by published short-sellers, and active short interest is estimated to be negligible and well below the regional mid-cap median, yet the company confronts a severe operational and structural inflection point following a compliance investigation at the IHSE subgroup that acted as the primary near-miss accounting risk catalyst. The central investment tension resides in whether management can successfully redeploy the substantial capital generated from the Bikeleasing divestment while simultaneously stabilizing the remaining operations that currently demonstrate negative profitability.

The disposal of Bikeleasing Group to Decathlon Pulse SAS represents both the investment case's greatest validation and its most acute vulnerability. The strategic transaction will generate €240 million in cash inflow for the holding company, realizing €240 million against a €102 million acquisition cost, demonstrating exceptional value-creation capability within the holding company model. However, this transaction fundamentally restructures the group's risk profile by eliminating its largest and most profitable asset.

Continuing operations recorded an adjusted EBITDA loss of €3.06 million in the 2025 fiscal year, while the reported GAAP EBITDA was negative €13.38 million for the same continuing period, exposing the fragility of the post-divestment operational foundation.

The bear case against Brockhaus Technologies rests on three interconnected pillars of operational, structural, and currency-driven risks that compound during stress scenarios. From an operational perspective, the sell-side pool drastically reduced forecasts after preliminary Q4 2024 earnings dropped sharply, with reported FY24 sales of €205 million missing consensus expectations of €230 million and adjusted EBITDA of €66 million missing the guided range of €80-90 million by over 17.50%.

The severity of this operational breakdown is underscored by group revenue declining by 33% YoY to €29.7 million in the final quarter of 2024, with the Financial Technologies segment falling 34% YoY to €21.9 million and Security Technologies declining 29% YoY to €7.8 million. Management's subsequent suspension of mid-term FY25 targets of €290-320 million in sales with a highly profitable 40% adjusted EBITDA margin eliminates critical visibility into the forward earnings trajectory.

The accounting and governance dimension presents material red flags that extend beyond standard roll-up execution friction. Brockhaus utilizes aggressive non-GAAP adjustments that structurally inflate presented profitability metrics, adjusting out €5.25 million in business combination and disposal costs, €3.48 million in special compliance costs, and €732 thousand of special insolvency costs during 2025. More critically, the contingent earn-out receivable from the 2022 Palas disposal was written down to zero in 2024, resulting in a noncash finance cost of €7.88 million, as Palas was highly unlikely to achieve its stipulated adjusted EBIT targets due to a significantly worse than planned development in core sales markets.

This total derecognition of initially valued contingent consideration reveals severe historical forecasting over-optimism. The governance structure compounds these concerns through recurring related-party transaction agreements with entities controlled by CEO Marco Brockhaus, including sublease payments to Brockhaus Private Equity GmbH that amounted to €200 thousand in 2023, €179 thousand in 2022, and €101 thousand during the first half of 2024.

The compliance crisis at IHSE represents the most visible accounting near-miss in the company's history. The investigation centered on one specific transaction at a foreign subsidiary of IHSE, involving approximately €2.2 million in disputed revenue, which is roughly 1.13% of total revenue. While management proactively excluded this transaction from preliminary group revenue, auditor KPMG significantly extended the audit scope across the entire Brockhaus group in March 2025, with the audit timeline largely determined by KPMG with limited management control over the speed, requiring substantial management capacity and extensive external advisor support. The subsequent substantial non-cash impairments in 2024 included IHSE goodwill impaired by €40 million, cutting carrying value in half, PPA assets from IHSE and kvm-tec impaired by an additional €8 million, and Bikeleasing shares impaired by €23 million to €47 million.

The Supervisory Board proposed electing Rödl Audit GmbH to replace KPMG for fiscal year 2025 after conducting a formal bidding process with seven prominent firms participating, with Rödl issuing an unqualified audit opinion on the 2025 consolidated statements on March 25, 2026, providing critical resolution to the audit uncertainty.

Broker coverage reflects deep skepticism regarding the sustainability of the business model following the operational collapse and asset disposal. Jefferies retains a Buy rating with a €34 price target despite the suspension of FY25 targets, with lead analyst Martin Comtesse CFA flagging severe near-term margin pressure following Q4 2024 results, maintaining this rating since initiating coverage in November 2021 with the target unchanged since early 2024.

In stark contrast, Montega successively cut its price target from €71 to €24 during 2024 and 2025, adjusting to €59 due to lower bike leasing volumes, further slashing to €25 following the €40 million IHSE goodwill write-down, and trimming to €24 after weaker Q3 2025 segment performance. Most significantly, Montega abruptly discontinued coverage of Brockhaus on February 2, 2026, amid persistent operational uncertainty, terminating with no recommendation or price target provided, signaling profound disillusionment with the investment case among local German research providers.

The downside valuation framework constructed by remaining sell-side analysts models severe contraction scenarios. Jefferies models a downside scenario of €15 per share, representing a 33.63% discount to current price, only valuing the existing portfolio without any future M&A accretion, implying 9x FY21 EV/EBITDA under extreme operational stress, and assuming 5% mid-term organic growth and 2% terminal growth.

An alternative, more severe scenario suggests even deeper drawdown potential: the bear case models a deep price target reduction to €8 per share under severe stress, assuming a prolonged period of subdued top-line growth and a sustainable EBT margin of only 10%. These valuation floors underscore the concentration risk inherent in the post-divestment structure, where the single largest risk is failing to reinvest cash proceeds at attractive multiples, as the holding model fails if management cannot deploy the €240 million Bikeleasing proceeds accretively.

The historical operational pattern demonstrates extreme volatility during stress periods, followed by strategic asset liquidations as the primary recovery mechanism. Preliminary FY24 revenues of €205 million missed the guided €220-240 million range, undershooting the €230 million sell-side consensus expectation, with preliminary adjusted EBITDA of €66 million failing to meet guided targets of €80-90 million, prompting management to suspend mid-term FY25 targets of €290-320 million sales and 40% adjusted EBITDA margin.

The stock response to these failures was catastrophic: the stock's collapse to €9.28 in 2025 reflects severe public de-rating amid delayed reporting, remaining depressed for much of 2025 due to audit delay uncertainty, with local broker Montega slashing its target price from €71 down to €24. Recovery materialized only through strategic divestment: the stock recovered immediately following the Bikeleasing stake sale announced on December 23, 2025, rallying by almost 50% from €10.50 to €15.55 on transaction news, validating asset values but simultaneously eliminating the group's primary earnings engine. The quality of bear evidence combines verifiable hard data with speculative structural concerns, creating a nuanced risk mosaic.

Hard evidence includes the €2.2 million IHSE revenue overstatement confirmed by regulatory filings that triggered the 2025 audit delay, with the local Managing Director of the affected foreign subsidiary relieved of duties with immediate effect, and IHSE segment revenue declining 4.7% YoY in FY25 to €30.29 million from €31.77 million in FY24, with EMEA revenues falling to €19.05 million as a result of subdued investing activity.

The documented opening of insolvency proceedings for kvm-tec on January 6, 2026, forcing full deconsolidation, with the group taking extraordinary impairment losses of €1.56 million on kvm-tec PPA assets in 2025 provides incontrovertible evidence of subsidiary-level distress. Conversely, speculative elements center on assertions that the holding company discount will persist without a clearer capital allocation framework, with fears that management may fail to acquire new pearls at highly attractive valuation multiples.

The foreign exchange exposure dimension introduces systematic volatility amplification that compounds operational risks during adverse currency movements. Continuing operations' 2025 revenues are estimated at 62.89% EUR, 27.10% USD, and 10.01% other Asian currencies, with EUR-denominated revenues shrinking relative to USD-denominated sales as a percentage of the continuing portfolio. This currency profile creates direct transaction sensitivities: a 10% appreciation of the Euro against the USD compresses earnings before tax by €113 thousand, while conversely a 10% depreciation of the Euro against the USD increases EBT by €197 thousand. The structural vulnerability is magnified by the complete absence of financial derivative hedges during the 2025 reporting period, maintained throughout fiscal years 2024 and 2023, with Brockhaus historically bypassing derivative instruments in 2022. This unhedged posture leaves the group exposed to severe translation and transaction shocks: a 30% USD depreciation would reduce translated Americas revenues by €2.46 million, with a simultaneous USD weakening of 30% and CNY weakening of 20% compounding translation drag, and foreign currency cash of €1,079 thousand experiencing immediate devaluation losses under dual-shock scenarios.

The currency risk integration with operational failure modes creates dangerous compounding dynamics. Unhedged FX headwinds interact directly with project postponement risks in the US and Europe, compound IHSE's high fixed operating expenses triggering rapid margin collapses, leave fewer cash flows to cover high corporate expenses at the holding level, and complicate the refinancing of the IHSE loan maturing December 31, 2026.

The macroeconomic consensus outlook provides limited comfort: Morgan Stanley forecasts EUR/USD declining from a peak of 1.23 in 3Q26 to 1.14 by 4Q27, driven by ECB rate cuts to 2% and French election risk premiums, suggesting near-term Euro weakness that could provide translation tailwinds. However, Deutsche Bank notes that escalation in Iran could push EUR/USD below the 1.15 support floor, with unhedged translation exposure at IHSE remaining highly vulnerable to prolonged Euro appreciation above 1.25, framing extreme geopolitical scenarios as existential risks to reported profitability.

The continuing operations guidance framework reveals the precarious margin profile of the post-divestment entity. Continuing operations are guided to a modest €30 million to €32 million revenue for the 2026 fiscal year, with continuing adjusted EBITDA forecasted at only €0 million to €2 million, highlighting thin post-divestment margins that provide minimal buffer against operational or currency shocks. This guidance implies that a 10% appreciation of the Euro against the USD directly compresses earnings before tax by €113 thousand, reducing estimated EPS by approximately 1.16 cents, with a simultaneous 10% EUR appreciation against the CNY further devaluing Asian earnings by €32 thousand, demonstrating how currency movements of moderate magnitude can swing the continuing entity from marginal profitability to material losses.

For concentrated, long-only investors, the Brockhaus Technologies investment case presents a binary outcome distribution centered entirely on management's capital allocation execution over the next eighteen to thirty-six months. The bull case rests on the holding company's demonstrated ability to monetize assets at substantial premiums to acquisition cost, validated by the Bikeleasing transaction's exceptional realized return, combined with a substantial dry powder position that enables transformative acquisitions.

The bear case emphasizes that the continuing operations demonstrate structural unprofitability, the compliance crisis exposed serious subsidiary-level control weaknesses, aggressive non-GAAP adjustments mask true economic cash generation, and complete currency hedge absence creates systematic earnings volatility that compounds operational execution risks.
