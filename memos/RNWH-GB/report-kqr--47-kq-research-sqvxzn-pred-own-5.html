Section 4 – Accounting Treatment of Deferred and Contingent Considerations

Section summary
  1. Technical accounting rules force Renew to classify contingent acquisition payments as deferred remuneration depressing statutory operating profits.
  2. Sell-side analysts consistently add back these employment-linked cash charges to preserve the underlying earnings per share trajectory.

IFRS 3 Remuneration Treatment

1.
Employment-linked accounting: Technical accounting regulations dictate that Renew's contingent consideration is classified as post-combination employee compensation rather than capital consideration. Renew's annual report of 17 December 2025 states that because these payments require vendors to remain employed, they cannot be capitalized. Consequently, this treatment places a significant recurring charge on statutory operating profit, which would otherwise be treated as goodwill on the balance sheet. Emerald Power earns: The contingent payment structure for the newly acquired overhead line specialist Emerald Power follows this strict accounting classification. Renew's interim report of 22 May 2026 confirms that the up to £4.5m of additional consideration is conditional on continuing employment. Because these terms are linked directly to personnel retention, the payments are fully expensed as deferred remuneration rather than being included in the purchase price. Excalon precedent: The employment-linked payment structure mirrors the historical framework applied during the major acquisition of Excalon in June 2024. Renew's annual report of 17 December 2025 notes that the £4.0m deferred consideration was split into two equal annual instalments conditional on vendor employment. This consistent treatment means that ongoing organic EBITA is systematically dampened by these non-underlying compensation expenses across multiple reporting periods. Contrary evidence: Legacy contracting transactions often allowed contingent payments to be treated as capital without employment ties. Balfour Beatty's annual report of 11 March 2026 notes that historic earn-out agreements were treated as pure adjustments to purchase consideration rather than ongoing P&L charges. This structural difference means that Renew's statutory P&L is uniquely burdened by its consistent choice to link earn-outs directly to founder employment covenants. Emerald asset values: The physical and intangible assets acquired through the Emerald transaction are mapped under provisional fair value guidelines. Renew's interim report of 22 May 2026 shows that provisional assets of £10.27m included £4.64m of intangible assets and £1.68m of cash. This asset distribution means that Renew secures substantial valuable IP while the associated deferred remuneration charges are kept separate from the capital base. Transaction cost treatment: Under group accounting policies, direct transaction costs associated with business combinations are expensed immediately rather than capitalized. Renew's interim report of 22 May 2026 reports that the company incurred £118,000 of direct costs acquiring Emerald Power. This upfront hit dampens short-term statutory performance during the acquisition period, but it ensures that the post-acquisition earnings base remains unburdened by capitalized transaction fees. Exceptional classification: Renew maintains a clear distinction between normal operational costs and exceptional items to help investors track underlying performance. Renew's interim report of 22 May 2026 notes that total exceptional items and amortisation reached £6.56m for the half-year. This transparent grouping ensures that operational metrics are not distorted by transactional friction, which helps maintain investor trust in the consistency of the core engineering business.
