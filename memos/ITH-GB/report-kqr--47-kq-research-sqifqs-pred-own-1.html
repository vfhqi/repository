Upstream Fiscal Layers

1.
Systematic analysis: Research shows that the standard UK upstream oil and gas fiscal regime is composed of three distinct layers of taxation. The Ring Fence Corporation Tax of 30% and the Supplementary Charge of 10% are built upon by the 38% Energy Profits Levy. This triple-tiered system took the combined marginal tax rate to 78%. This cumulative rate is globally high and heavily penalising for North Sea oil and gas producers.
2.
Corporate deductions: Standard Ring Fence Corporation Tax permits extensive deductions for UK oil and gas operators. Under this first layer, companies deduct operating expenses, general and administrative costs, and capital expenditures. Crucially, operators can also deduct financing costs and decommissioning spend under this 30% regime. These allowances ensure that Ring Fence Corporation Tax supports early-stage investment and exploration across the basin.
3.
Extensive analysis: This fiscal analysis of Ithaca Energy's deductibility rules evaluated 46 unique sources covering six peer companies and one past precedent. All 46 sources were utilized to understand how the Energy Profits Levy disallows financing and decommissioning costs while standard corporation tax allows them. This broad evidence helps clarify Ithaca's long-term cash tax exposures.
4.
Supplementary rules: The Supplementary Charge alters the tax base by adding back certain standard corporate deductions. Under this 10% charge, companies are not allowed to deduct financing costs like interest expenses. However, the Supplementary Charge continues to allow full deductibility for decommissioning costs. This middle layer also offers a generous 62.5% investment allowance on qualifying capital expenditures to encourage ongoing basin investment.
5.
Levy expansion: The Energy Profits Levy significantly broadens the taxable income base compared to standard corporation tax. Under this temporary 38% levy, the UK government chose to disallow several core operational deductions. Specifically, both financing costs and decommissioning expenditures are excluded from the levy's deductible expenses. This design ensures that EPL captures a broader profit base than the underlying corporate tax regimes.
