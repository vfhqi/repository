Counter-View The strongest counter-view is that rapid macroeconomic and geopolitical changes could trigger sudden, aggressive central bank interest rate cuts. If floating rates fall rapidly, poorly hedged companies (e.g., FastPartner) and those facing immediate interest rate resets (e.g., Castellum with SEK 21.8 billion resetting under 1 year)

would capture instant interest savings. Conversely, heavily hedged companies would remain locked into higher swap rates, reversing the sector's current winner-loser dynamic. Analytical Conclusion and Confidence Assessment The current financial evidence strongly supports the draft's thesis. Landlords currently replacing expensive legacy debt or executing strategic deleveraging (Diös, NP3, and CA Immo) are achieving flat or declining average costs of debt and stabilizing net financial items.

Conversely, landlords that had previously locked in ultra-cheap financing and are now losing those hedges or refinancing legacy low-coupon bonds (Aroundtown, Atrium Ljungberg, Fastighets Balder, and FastPartner) face clear, rising interest burdens. This upward repricing of debt is actively dragging down cash earnings. According to post-July 2026 broker adjustments, this transition is estimated to depress EPS or CEPS projections by 2% to 6% over the 2026–2028 horizon.
