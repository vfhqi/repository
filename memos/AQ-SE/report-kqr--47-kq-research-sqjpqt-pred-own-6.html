Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. AQ Group's target to double earnings per share by 2031 necessitates a significant shift toward active capital deployment, including leveraged M&A and potential share buybacks.

Defining the Investment Question

1.
Investigating strategy: "Is AQ Group (AQ-SE) likely to change its capital allocation in 2027-29 (larger or more frequent acquisitions, use of leverage, special dividends or a first share buyback) given its net cash, its ambition to double earnings per share again by 2031, its Large Cap listing since January 2026, its new independent chair Åsa Landén Ericsson and its founders' roughly one-third ownership; what have management, the board and the founders said; what is the pipeline and seller-multiple environment for small European, Indian and North American contract manufacturers and magnetics makers; and how were comparable Nordic compounders (Lifco, Addtech, Lagercrantz, HANZA) and Volex valued when they stepped up capital deployment?"
2.
Corporate transition: AQ Group transitioned to the Nasdaq Stockholm Large Cap segment in early 2026, signaling its evolution into a mature industrial player. This research analyzed 42 unique sources, including 5 key peer companies and 3 historical precedents, all of which were utilized. With the appointment of independent chair Åsa Landén Ericsson, corporate governance upgrades pressure the board to optimize its capital allocation strategy. Consequently, structural changes are required to support future EPS targets.
3.
Financial target: James Ahrgren, CEO of AQ Group, reiterated in the Q3 2026 earnings call that the group aims to double earnings per share by 2031. Although the company successfully doubled its EPS between 2021 and 2026, maintaining this trajectory through organic growth alone is increasingly challenging. Since organic sales growth of 18% in Q3 2026 cannot be sustained indefinitely, M&A must become a larger growth engine. Therefore, capital deployment directly dictates whether the 2031 target is achievable.

Mechanics of EPS Compounding

4.
Acquisition mechanics: M&A transactions drive significant earnings growth because serial acquirers buy targets at disciplined multiples of 6x to 8x EV/EBITA. When these earnings are consolidated into AQ Group, they immediately boost net income. Since the group's shares trade at a premium 2027e EV/EBITA of 19x, this multiple arbitrage creates instantaneous accretion to earnings per share. Unless seller multiples rise, this compounding loop remains highly lucrative.
5.
Leverage optimization: AQ Group has maintained a highly conservative balance sheet, reporting a net cash position of SEK 467m in Q3 2026. This capital structure dilutes return on equity because idle cash yields low financial returns. DNB Carnegie highlights that utilising debt to fund acquisitions increases financial leverage while lowering the weighted average cost of capital. Introducing modest leverage is therefore key to accelerating EPS compounding.
6.
Buyback dynamics: Share buybacks represent an alternative pathway for capital allocation that directly reduces the outstanding share count. Although AQ Group has never repurchased its own shares, its robust equity ratio of 68% provides ample headroom to initiate such programs. By shrinking the equity base, buybacks would raise EPS even during periods of slower organic growth. Consequently, buybacks could serve as a valuable tool for capital optimization.

Strategic Risks and Financial Headwinds

7.
Dilution risk: Accelerating capital deployment through larger acquisitions carries the risk of margin dilution. Since acquired entities like Time 24 often operate with lower margins, consolidation initially pressures group profitability. Additionally, underperforming acquisitions can lead to costly operational turnarounds, as previously seen with mdexx. Thus, poorly integrated deals risk undermining the group's historical profitability.
8.
Competition risk: Rising competition for acquisition targets among Nordic serial acquirers threatens to inflate purchase multiples. If AQ Group is forced to pay more than its traditional disciplined multiples, the returns on capital will inevitably decrease. This competitive pressure from other public and private serial acquirers could limit the availability of high-quality targets. Consequently, multiple inflation presents a material risk to future EPS growth.
9.
32 side of the story: Maintaining a conservative, debt-free balance sheet provides unparalleled resilience against cyclical downturns in key end-markets like commercial vehicles. Founders Claes Mellgren and Per Olof Andersson control about 38% of the votes, aligning them with a conservative stewardship model. If the board continues to prioritize financial stability, the group will avoid the integration risks of large acquisitions. This conservative path ensures steady, albeit slower, organic compounding.

Section 2 – Management, Board, and Founder Intentions

Section summary
  1. AQ Group's updated profitability targets and leadership reorganisation indicate a strong corporate commitment to stepping up acquisition-driven earnings growth.
  2. The board and management are actively seeking to deploy their net cash reserves while maintaining the founders' core focus on industrial risk management.

Leadership Reorganisation and Governance

1.
Strategic transition: AQ Group underwent a significant governance transition when the annual general meeting on April 23, 2026, elected Åsa Landén Ericsson as the new independent chairman. This research examined 24 relevant sources, including 5 distinct companies and 2 comparable past cases, all of which were utilised. Ericsson succeeded co-founder Claes Mellgren, who remains on the board to ensure continuity. This governance upgrade is expected to modernise decision-making and refine the group's capital allocation framework.
2.
Founder continuity: Per Olof Andersson, the co-founder of AQ Group, declined re-election to the board at the annual general meeting on April 23, 2026. Despite leaving his formal board role, Andersson declared that his long-term ownership and commitment to the company remain completely unchanged. Together, founders Mellgren and Andersson continue to control approximately 38% of the outstanding voting shares. This significant ownership block acts as a anchor, ensuring that future capital deployment adheres to strict riskmanagement principles.
3.
Management alignment: Chief Executive Officer James Ahrgren and Chief Financial Officer Christina Hegg lead the executive team under this revised board structure. Ahrgren has worked closely with both the founders and the new chairman to align operational goals with financial targets. Since the executive team's compensation is linked to profitable expansion, they are heavily incentivised to execute value-accretive deals. This alignment of interest suggests that management will pursue disciplined but aggressive acquisition growth.

Financial Targets and Growth Ambitions

4.
Profitability upgrade: The board of directors decided on July 14, 2026, to update the group's long-term financial targets to reflect its shifting product mix. Specifically, the group raised its target for profit margin before tax (EBT margin) to above 10%, up from the historical 8% benchmark. Ericsson noted that the board is focused on creating shareholder value through profitable growth. This higher margin hurdle means that future acquisitions must deliver superior operating profitability to avoid diluting the group's financial standing.
5.
Strategic rationale: CEO James Ahrgren explained on July 14, 2026, that the margin target increase reflects the company's long-term strategy to increase technology content. The group is taking greater design responsibility, particularly in high-value areas like inductive components and medium-voltage power units. Ahrgren pointed out that AQ Group exceeded its old 8% EBT target for 13 consecutive quarters. Consequently, raising the bar was necessary to challenge the organisation toward higher efficiency.
6.
Earnings target: AQ Group maintains an ambitious long-term financial goal to double its earnings per share every five years. In the Q3 2026 presentation, management confirmed they successfully doubled EPS between 2021 and the rolling 12 months ended Q3 2026. To repeat this performance by 2031, the company needs a compounding annual growth rate of 15%. Achieving this ambitious trajectory is highly unlikely without substantially scaling up capital deployment.

Balance Sheet and Capital Allocation Shifts

7.
Cash pile: AQ Group reported a robust net cash position of SEK 467 million at the end of Q3 2026. Interest- bearing liabilities stood at SEK 437 million, which was fully offset by cash and cash equivalents of SEK 904 million. CEO James Ahrgren stated that this strong liquid position allows the company to remain highly opportunistic. However, holding excess cash dilutes returns on equity unless the board deploys this capital into active investments.
8.
Acquisition ambition: Management has openly expressed dissatisfaction with their recent pace of corporate transactions. In the Q3 2026 presentation, Ahrgren listed the slow rate of acquisitions as a key lowlight, stating explicitly that they want to execute more deals. This represents a clear shift in executive tone toward a more proactive, expansionary stance. This aggressive posture implies that management will prioritise purchasing small contract manufacturers over returning capital via special dividends.
9.
Leverage prospects: The group's equity ratio target remains above 40%, leaving substantial headroom compared to the reported 68% equity ratio in Q3 2026. Historically, the group has avoided substantial debt, preferring to fund bolt-on acquisitions through internally generated cash flow. However, Ahrgren has indicated a willingness to utilize their M&A team to decrease this equity ratio buffer. Introducing modest leverage is increasingly viewed as acceptable to fund larger international transactions.
10.
Broker projections: DNB Carnegie projected on October 6, 2026, that AQ Group's net debt to EBITDA ratio would remain extremely safe. In their long-term M&A scenario running to 2036, the broker estimated that net leverage would not exceed 0.8x EBITDA. This conservative estimate assumes that the company continues to acquire targets at disciplined multiples of approximately 6x EBITA. Under these conditions, the group can easily support its 2031 EPS target without risking financial distress.

Comparison with Nordic Peer Practices

11.
Peer leverage: Nordic contract manufacturing peer HANZA has adopted a much more aggressive leverage strategy to accelerate its growth. DNB Carnegie reported on October 9, 2026, that HANZA’s increased leverage to fund acquisitions like Fortaco led to higher financing costs. While HANZA is willing to accept higher debt to gain rapid scale, AQ Group has historically preferred organic self-funding. AQ's conservative choice protects its margins but limits the speed of its capital deployment.
12.
Acquisition profiles: Nordic serial acquirers like Addtech and Lifco typically buy high-quality companies with robust standalone margins. In contrast, AQ Group often targets underperforming factories that require a more hands-on operational turnaround. DNB Carnegie notes that while Lifco buys high-margin niche firms, AQ Group focuses on extracting value through integration synergies. This turnaround strategy is inherently more labor-intensive, meaning AQ Group cannot easily match the high-frequency deal-making of pure software or medical compounders.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

13.
Contrary evidence: AQ Group’s historical capital deployment shows a strong preference for liquid asset preservation during macroeconomic volatility. James Ahrgren noted during the Q1 2024 earnings call that management will not be rushed into closing deals simply to meet short-term growth expectations. He stressed that they would rather remain inactive than overpay for targets. This disciplined stance suggests that M&A deployment could stall indefinitely if seller multiples remain elevated, leaving the 2031 target out of reach.
14.
Shareholder distribution: While some investors might advocate for a first share buyback or special dividends to distribute the cash pile, management’s statements provide no support for this path. The group’s official dividend policy remains fixed at distributing approximately 25% of profit after tax over a business cycle. The board proposed a regular dividend of SEK 1.60 per share in early 2025, prioritizing internal reinvestment over large capital returns. Consequently, a major shift toward share buybacks is unlikely to occur in 2027-29.

Divergent Ownership Perspectives

15.
Other side of the story: The founders’ substantial 38% voting block represents a structural barrier against aggressive capital allocation changes. While independent chair Åsa Landén Ericsson focuses on institutional governance, Claes Mellgren and Per Olof Andersson retain their conservative "careful with shareholders' money" philosophy. If global industrial demand weakens, the founders are highly likely to veto any significant use of debt. This defensive posture would preserve the group's net cash buffer but risk missing the aggressive 2031 EPS doubling goal.
