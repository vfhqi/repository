What the call should settle, and why

The Investment Analyst's case in one line: the repair is done and paid for, the growth has not started, and the next two results are more likely to bring a cut to FY2027 consensus than a raise. Hilton Food is a contract packer that earns about 18p of operating profit per kilogram on about 530,000 tonnes a year; revenue is mostly meat cost passed through to the grocer. The shares have risen about 60% from the January 2026 low as a new team sold three units, ring-fenced seafood and refinanced. Earnings per share are flat in FY2027 on our numbers (54.0p then 55.1p) and step up only in FY2028 to FY2030, mostly from the new Walmart Canada plant, Poland and the removal of seafood losses.

The call can test seven judgements. The first four decide the size and timing of changes to guidance and consensus over the next four quarters. The last three decide what the stock is worth holding for beyond that.

  • Judgement 1, where FY2027 guidance will be set against consensus. Consensus for FY2027 is profit before tax of GBP 74.0m and earnings per share of 57.6p (up 9.9%). Our numbers are about GBP 71m and 55.1p. The memo puts the odds that guidance in April 2027 is set below consensus at about 45%, in line at 40% and above at 15%, and expects a cut to FY2027 consensus of 3% to 6%, led by Deutsche Bank. Each GBP 1m of operating profit is about 0.8p of earnings per share.
  • Judgement 2, the finance charge in FY2027. The interim results showed GBP 1.6m of bank interest capitalised into the Brantford plant in the first half of FY2026. That stops when the plant starts in January 2027, so about GBP 3.2m a year of interest returns to the income statement. We carry net finance costs of GBP 30.0m in FY2027; consensus implies about GBP 25.6m. The gap of about GBP 4.4m is about 3.5p of FY2027 earnings per share, about 6% (our arithmetic). The memo says no broker models it.
  • Judgement 3, the tax rate and the FY2026 outcome. Our files hold company guidance of about 28% for FY2026 (27.7% in the first half); consensus earnings per share imply about 29.7%. With profit before tax in the upper half of the GBP 66m to GBP 71m range, that gives about 54p against consensus of 52.4p. This is the cleanest source of a small FY2026 beat, worth about 1.3p, and the memo judges it already paid for.
  • Judgement 4, Canada's first year. We carry GBP 0.5m of operating profit from Canada in FY2027, a loss in the first half and a profit in the second. Shore Capital carries GBP 1.5m. Management has said "a small low-single-digit profit contribution". The memo's bear case is a loss of GBP 3m. On the memo's arithmetic the bear case costs about 2.8p and Shore Capital's case adds about 0.8p.
  • Judgement 5, Canada's contract terms. We carry GBP 7.5m of operating profit from Canada by FY2030. If the Walmart fee covers the building lease and the capital cost, as two pieces of evidence suggest, the mature figure is nearer GBP 10m to GBP 15m. That adds 2p to 6p to FY2030 earnings per share, 3% to 8%, and it is the argument for a fair multiple of 15 times rather than 12 to 14 times. The memo calls it the largest swing in the case in both directions.
  • Judgement 6, the existing plants: the fee per kilogram and volume in the West region. Open-book resets return efficiency gains to the customer, which pins profit per kilogram at about 18.5p to 19.2p. Tesco moved its Scottish beef to other processors in the first half of FY2026. Australian cattle supply falls in 2027 and 2028. One penny per kilogram on the existing plants is about GBP 5.3m of operating profit, about 4p or 7% to 8% of earnings per share.
  • Judgement 7, Foppen, cash and what happens when leverage reaches the bottom of the target range. Foppen is the smoked salmon business whose Greek plant is shut out of the United States by the Food and Drug Administration. A resolution is worth about GBP 3m to GBP 4m of adjusted operating profit and GBP 10m to GBP 15m a year of cash. Separately, on our path net bank debt falls to 1.0 times EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) in FY2029, the bottom of management's range, which forces a choice between a return of capital and a new project. Neither is in consensus.

Order of play. Start with the mechanical questions on the calendar, consensus, tax, capitalised interest and Dalco (A1 to A7): they are factual, an Investor Relations Director can answer them fully, and they are where our numbers and the brokers' diverge most. Then Canada (A8, then B1.1 to B1.3), which is the largest swing. Then the open-book reset, Tesco and Australian cattle (B2.1, B2.2, B2.5). Then Foppen (B3.1, B3.2) and the return of capital (B6.1). Leave incentives, the shareholder register and disclosure requests to the end.

Expect two limits on the answers. On current trading and Christmas, the answer will be that the next statement is in January; where that happens, ask what the January statement will contain. On the Walmart contract, he may say the terms are confidential; where that happens, ask the question as an accounting question (what will the accounts show, and when), which he can answer.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the fourth quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2027)

The four events in this window are the January 2027 trading update, the FY2026 results with the first FY2027 guidance in early April 2027, the annual general meeting update in May 2027 and the interim results in September 2027.

A4 ★ The tax rate. "The first-half adjusted tax rate was 27.7% and we understand you guide about 28% for FY2026. Is that right, and what should we use for FY2027 and FY2028? Consensus earnings per share seem to assume nearer 30%."

- Tell: "2027 is a year of investment" or "growth is weighted to later years" points to a range with a midpoint below GBP 74m. "We are comfortable with where the market is for 2027" would be the memo's counter-case and would remove the main near-term risk to the share price.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. The Walmart Canada plant at Brantford: the largest swing in the case, in both directions

B1.1 ★ Whether January will be met. "Is Brantford on track to start in January 2027? Where is equipment testing, and what has to happen between now and the first deliveries? How many stores and which distribution centres does it serve at the start and at full ramp, and how long is the ramp?"
- Why: the launch has slipped once (from 2026) and the budget rose from GBP 60m to GBP 80m. The third set says every Hilton Food project of the last four years has been late, and our files put a 20% to 30% probability on a material slip or overrun. Our files hold about 400 Walmart stores in Canada, eastern Canada first, the distribution centres at Cornwall, Moncton and Mississauga, about 150 staff, and a ramp "over the first half of 2027".
