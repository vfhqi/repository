Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating standalone asset valuations and structural spin-offs will determine the mid-cycle earnings resilience and investment appeal of European steel leaders over the next three years.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Investigating structural transitions: "What value do brokers put on North American Stainless and Haynes as a standalone listed company against the Acerinox group, and has the company said more about a listing or its timing since? Is Haynes recovering after its weak first quarter of 2026, and how far have its synergies progressed? Has voestalpine said whether it would consider separating Railway Systems, and what do brokers think that business would be worth alone? How much of Aperam's earnings now come from aerospace and other alloys outside Stainless Europe?"
2.
Sector-wide valuation anomalies: This investigation highlights structural valuation mismatches across European steel and specialty alloy networks. Related industry questions focus on whether trade barriers like Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms can sustain European margins, or if producers must shift their primary listing locations to the United States to unlock value. Resolving these issues determines how global capital is allocated as regional trade protection reshapes basic resources.

Strategic Value and EPS Impact

3.
Accelerating earnings visibility: The strategic evaluation of Acerinox's American footprint dictates the company's long-term earnings per share trajectory. High-performance alloy integrations and resilient US stainless spreads have supported estimate upgrades, with North American Stainless and Haynes representing nearly 80% of corporate enterprise value. Capitalizing on this footprint through a New York listing would establish a clear price-discovery mechanism for investors. This structural change would materially re-rate the group's valuation multiple closer to premium specialty alloy peers.
4.
Unlocking defensive value: Separating the rail infrastructure division represents a major value creation option for voestalpine's management. While core carbon steelmaking remains highly cyclical, the Railway Systems business provides a recurring, defensive revenue profile backed by multi-year infrastructure commitments. Trading at a steep discount to rail infrastructure peers, a potential separation would highlight voestalpine's highly underappreciated downstream asset value. This restructuring could structurally lift mid-cycle earnings capability and decrease corporate cash flow volatility.
5.
Expanding specialty exposure: Aperam's financial performance increasingly relies on high-value non-stainless segments to navigate challenging European commodities cycles. The integration of Universal Stainless expands the company's US aerospace footprint and enhances specialty alloy pricing power. High-performance alloys generated over one-third of group earnings in 2025, offering a structural growth engine. Diversifying away from commodity steel is critical to achieving the company's normalised through-cycle EBITDA targets.

Portfolio Risks and Market Appeal

6.
Mitigating structural volatility: Transitioning toward downstream specialties reduces the cyclical cash flow risks inherent in upstream steelmaking. However, these strategic transformations require substantial capital expenditure, exposing producers to project execution delays and prolonged demand weakness. Rising energy costs in Europe also threaten the cost competitiveness of regional facilities. Investors must monitor these capital programs because they determine whether specialty alloy exposure provides a sustained hedge against macroeconomic downturns.
7.
Other side of the story: Slowing industrial demand and execution bottlenecks could prevent these specialty steel assets from achieving their targeted valuation premiums. Although management team presentations promote listing plans and specialty alloy transformations, actual transaction timelines remain highly uncertain and capital-intensive. If regional energy crises or supply chain constraints persist, these companies risk burning substantial free cash flow before delivering any visible earnings accretion. Investors may therefore experience protracted transition periods with limited upside over the next 18 months.

Section 2 – Standalone Valuation and Listing Prospect of Acerinox's US Business

Section summary
  1. Evaluating standalone asset valuations and listing prospects highlights a major valuation mismatch compared to regional steel peers.
  2. Potential US listing of North American Stainless and Haynes could unlock substantial equity value.

Concentrated US Enterprise Value

1.
Underestimated asset concentration: The vast majority of corporate value for Acerinox is concentrated in its highly resilient American operations. According to Morgan Stanley's report on 21 September 2026, North American Stainless (NAS) and Haynes International account for approximately 80% of the group's enterprise value at the midpoint. Specifically, NAS represents 60% of the enterprise value while Haynes represents 20%. This heavy concentration means the market currently fails to reflect the resilience of these US assets within the wider European entity.
2.
Discounted conglomerate footprint: European market valuations do not fully capture the strategic shift toward premium US-centric segments. Jefferies' report dated 24 August 2026 notes that North America will generate around 75% of Acerinox's 2027 estimated EBITDA. Analysts apply conservative SOTP discounts to reflect the diversified conglomerate structure. Therefore, investors receive a heavily discounted entry point into premium US high-performance alloys markets.
3.
Significant valuation arbitrage: Standalone US steel operations trade at much higher valuation multiples than their European parent company. Deutsche Bank's report on 2 October 2026 highlights that the US and specialty peer group trades materially higher than Acerinox's multiple of 7.2x EV/EBITDA. This operational and valuation gap has prompted management to consider structural options. A successful separation or US relisting would unlock substantial value for shareholders.

Peer Valuation Multiples

4.
Alloy multiple disparity: US specialty alloy competitors trade at premium multiples that far exceed commodity stainless steel producers. Morgan Stanley's report on 21 September 2026 notes that premium US alloys peers like Carpenter Technology and ATI trade near 20x EV/EBITDA. Conversely, the broker values Haynes at 9.0x EBITDA in its base case to reflect a lower-margin product mix. This discount highlights how Haynes' ongoing capital programs could narrow the alloy peer gap over the medium term.
5.
Specialty alloy benchmarks: Peer trading multiples reflect stronger structural margins and deeper exposure to high-specification aerospace end-markets. Jefferies' analyst note on 24 August 2026 confirms that US peers ATI and Carpenter Technology trade above 20x EV/EBITDA. By comparison, the broader Acerinox group trades at a major historical discount. This discrepancy means a US listing is the most viable path to achieve fair market multiples for Haynes.
6.
Stainless market dominance: Resilient domestic market structures allow US producers to maintain superior margins compared to European operations. Morgan Stanley's report on 21 September 2026 explains that NAS benefits from high industry concentration and robust trade protections. Consequently, NAS delivers a 19% through-cycle EBITDA margin, which is twice that of peer Outokumpu BA Americas. This superior performance means NAS serves as the absolute core earnings engine of the group.

Listing Update and Structure

7.
Uncertain IPO execution: Management has outlined a clear conceptual structure for a potential US listing but has avoided firm commitments. Morgan Stanley's report on 18 May 2026 reveals that the company favors an IPO of all or part of the American platform. Acerinox intends to retain majority ownership of the US business to maintain operational control. However, preparatory work is in the early stages and listing timing remains highly uncertain.
8.
Preparatory strategic phase: Corporate executives have formally confirmed that they are exploring listing alternatives without locking in a schedule. Deutsche Bank's report on 8 May 2026 indicates that management started initial preparation work on a possible US listing. While an IPO with a retained stake above 50% is the preferred corporate path, the timeline will be lengthy. This structural option is designed to address the persistent undervaluing of US steel assets.
9.
Delayed listing progress: Recent conference calls have provided no incremental updates, suggesting that immediate corporate action is unlikely. Morgan Stanley's feedback note on 24 July 2026 reports that no decision has been finalized regarding the potential IPO. The company continues to study market conditions and assess listing opportunities. This slow progress suggests the listing cannot be counted on nearterm to support EPS.
10.
Passive exploration mode: Strategic reassessments continue behind closed doors with no official dates announced to the public. Deutsche Bank's research note on 24 July 2026 confirms there is no new progress on the US listing plan. Operational focus has instead shifted to achieving a target net debt to EBITDA ratio of 2.2x by the end of 2026. This prioritization means capital allocation will remain conservative until a listing decision is taken.

SOTP Valuation Models

11.
SOTP upside potential: Sum-of-the-parts modeling shows that the parent group trades well below the intrinsic value of its individual units. Morgan Stanley's valuation note on 21 September 2026 details an illustrative peerbased SOTP range of €20.80 to €28.20 per share. This model applies peer multiples to 2029 normalised EBITDA assumptions for Acerinox's five businesses. It proves that the stock is fundamentally undervalued at current levels.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

13.
Contrary evidence: Softening global demand and operational execution risks could prevent the US business from achieving its target valuation. Section 3 – Recovery and Synergy Progress of Haynes International

Section summary
  1. High-performance alloy operations show a strong recovery path driven by record aerospace order backlogs in 2026.
  2. Integration synergies of Haynes International are tracking ahead of schedule toward the group's long-term targets.

Recovery in High-Performance Alloys

1.
Accelerating order backlogs: The high-performance alloys division is experiencing a robust recovery driven by unprecedented demand in key aerospace segments. According to Acerinox's Q1 2026 earnings transcript on 8 May 2026, Haynes International recorded its best order book entrance ever in history during April 2026. This historic volume is driven by both aerospace engine cases and gas turbines, which means earnings visibility remains highly secured for the next three years. This recovery will materially support mid-cycle earnings per share expansion starting in the second half of 2026.
2.
Improving segment profitability: Specialty alloy profitability is poised to rebound significantly as record backlogs begin converting into reported revenue. Deutsche Bank's report on 24 July 2026 confirms that Haynes is experiencing exceptionally strong order books in both aerospace and gas turbine end-markets. While VDM Metals continues to face industrial headwinds, the record Haynes backlog will underpin the segment's financial turnaround in H2 2026. This recovery would be threatened if broader commercial aviation supply-chain bottlenecks prevent the delivery of finished alloy products.
3.
Expanding secular demand: Strong secular tailwinds in aerospace and defense are driving the demand for advanced, high-purity specialty alloys. Morgan Stanley's report on 18 May 2026 highlights that the ongoing commercial aerospace recovery and stronger gas turbine demand are supporting Haynes. This positive trend will let Haynes migrate capacity-constrained aerospace production into VDM Metals' facilities. This strategic flexibility will optimize the group's global asset utilization and structurally enhance consolidated margins.

Progression of Synergy Targets

4.
Surpassing early milestones: The integration of Haynes within the wider group is delivering financial benefits ahead of original management timelines. This successful early execution is supported by close R&D collaboration and joint purchasing initiatives that secured better pricing for raw materials. This early success builds strong investor confidence in the group's long-term operational integration capabilities.
5.
Accelerating synergy extraction: The group continues to capture cost improvements at a rapid pace as the integration enters its second year. Acerinox's interim financial report on 24 July 2026 announced that achieved synergies have reached over $16 million to date. This rapid progress puts the company well on track to hit its FY2026 integration synergy target of $23 million. Meeting these targets is critical because synergies represent a major component of the group's through-cycle EBITDA goals.
