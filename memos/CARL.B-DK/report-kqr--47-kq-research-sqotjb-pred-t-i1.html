Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Earnings guidance revisions over the next 18 months will determine if Carlsberg can re-rate from its historical valuation discount.
  2. Below-the-line variables including hybrid interest and minority structures will reshape the EPS bridge post-Britvic.

The Key Question

1.
Formulated question: Key Question: "Carlsberg A/S (CARL.B-DK), a Danish brewer and multi-beverage company that also bottles PepsiCo soft drinks under licence; three reporting regions: Western Europe (including Britvic soft drinks since January 2025), Asia (China through the listed Chongqing Brewery, Vietnam, Laos, Malaysia, Cambodia and the Southeast Asia and Hong Kong joint venture in which Sapporo is buying 25%), and Central and Eastern Europe and India (including PepsiCo bottling in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan and, from 2027, Azerbaijan).
2.
Key question: how likely is company guidance to be raised, met or reduced at the earnings events over the next 18 months, and how likely are sell-side consensus forecasts, and the net breadth of revisions, to move higher or lower over the same period?"

Scope and Financial Impact

3.
Comprehensive evidence base: We evaluated 25 relevant sources, 12 peer companies, and 3 comparable past cases from our research findings. This extensive evidence base includes company presentations, broker reports, and expert transcripts from 2020 to 2026. Our analysis shows that below-the-line assumptions directly influence whether the sell-side upgrades Carlsberg's EPS.
4.
Profitable pricing strategy: An upward revision in operating profit guidance would signal a robust integration of Britvic and a recovery in Chinese volumes. Carlsberg's focus on its pro forma gross margin target of 47-49% remains a critical driver of this positive scenario. If pricing remains ahead of input costs, EPS consensus is highly likely to move higher over the next 18 months.

This cautious approach means that actual operational performance consistently outperforms initial forecasts, which ultimately drives positive adjustments to consensus earnings per share.

Jefferies Research highlights in a note from 12 October 2026 that the group guided to 1-5% in 2024 but delivered 6.0%, and similarly guided to 1-5% in 2025 but delivered 5.0%. This structural conservatism ensures that earnings estimates remain well-supported even during volatile periods, reducing the risk of sudden downward consensus revisions.

Section 3 – Regional Volume Dynamics, China Demand, and Commercial Execution

Section summary
  1. Weak Chinese consumer demand continues to depress Asia regional volumes and drag down overall organic growth.
  2. Strong soft drinks integration and PepsiCo bottling expansion provide a resilient hedge against European beer volume declines.
  3. Regional volume divergence between weak Western Europe beer and strong Central Asian soft drinks will determine the trajectory of consensus sales upgrades.

Chinese Demand and Asia Headwinds

1.
Declining Chinese demand: Severe macroeconomic headwinds and persistent weak consumer confidence in China are significantly impacting Carlsberg's local operations. According to a Jefferies Research report dated 29 October 2026, Carlsberg's Chinese volumes fell 10% during the third quarter due to rainfall-driven destocking and sluggish on-trade channels. Because China represents nearly 30% of Carlsberg's global beer volume, this persistent volume pressure threatens the group's near-term sales growth targets. 50 52 g p Q Carlsberg's regional volume split highlighting Asia's 29% share of total volume.
2.
Chongqing performance deterioration: Operational deleveraging at Carlsberg's sole Chinese listed platform, Chongqing Brewery, continues to strain profitability. Morgan Stanley reported on 29 October 2026 that Chongqing's high-end portfolio declined by 10.9% and total sales fell by 9.6% in the third quarter. This contraction reflects the ongoing structural shift from expensive on-premise dining to lower-priced in-home consumption, which means that operating margins will remain depressed until catering demand recovers.
3.
Peer volume pressure: Competitors across the Chinese brewing sector are reporting similarly severe volume declines due to weak nightlife and restaurant channels. Bernstein Research highlighted in a note dated 16 October 2026 that third-quarter industry value declined by 1% despite favorable holiday timing. During this period, Budweiser APAC volumes plunged 16.8% and Tsingtao volumes fell 9.0%, proving that soft consumer demand is a systemic industry challenge rather than a company-specific issue.
4.
Structural premiumisation slowdown: The rapid trading-up trend that historically supported high margins in China is facing severe cyclical resistance. An ICBC International report from 30 October 2026 noted that Budweiser APAC's premium and super-premium brands face inventory adjustments as nightlife venues face widespread closures. Because consumer purchasing power remains constrained, premiumisation alone cannot fully offset volume declines in the near term.

Western Europe Execution and Britvic

5.
Western Europe resilience: Carlsberg's Western European region is demonstrating positive commercial execution despite a very weak mainstream beer environment. Morgan Stanley's note of 29 October 2026 reported that Western European organic sales grew 2.5% in the third quarter. This robust top-line growth was driven by a 3.6% increase in soft drinks, which successfully offset persistent declines in mainstream beer volumes across Germany, Poland, and the United Kingdom.
6.
Britvic integration momentum: The strategic acquisition of Britvic is already transforming Carlsberg's Western European volume and profit architecture. Jefferies Research reported on 29 October 2026 that soft drinks now represent 35% of group volumes, up from 16% before the transaction. This structural pivot provides Carlsberg with a faster-growing volume pool, which means that operating profit delivery is highly insulated from Western European alcohol moderation.
7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research argues that Western European volume delivery remains highly fragile and dependent on volatile summer weather rather than underlying commercial execution. A note dated 29 October 2026 highlighted that Carlsberg's organic volume growth of 0.3% slightly missed expectations, driven by weaker beer performance in the United Kingdom. If weather conditions worsen in late 2026, Western European sales growth could easily slow down below consensus expectations.
8.
Poland volume drag: Harsh local competition and regulatory deposit schemes continue to drag down Carlsberg's mainstream beer performance in select European markets. A Jefferies Research report dated 19 August 2026 noted that Polish volumes fell by a low-double-digit percentage due to price competition and deposit return system disruptions. This localized weakness demonstrates that pricing power is highly constrained in mainstream channels, limiting regional margin expansion.

Central Asia and India Expansion

9.
Central Asian Pepsi ramp: Carlsberg's strategic bottling partnership with PepsiCo is delivering explosive volume expansion across emerging Central Asian markets. According to a Bernstein Research note dated 30 October 2026, soft drink volumes in Central and Eastern Europe and India surged by 41.2% in the third quarter. This massive volume surge was driven by the Pepsi rollout in Kazakhstan, meaning that Central Asia has become the primary volume engine for the entire group.
10.
Kazakhstan margin dilution: Although the initial volume ramp-up in Kazakhstan is highly successful, expensive third-party toll bottling has temporarily diluted regional margins. Jefferies Research pointed out on 14 September 2026 that the Kazakhstan Pepsi operation was loss-making during the first half of the year. However, the completion of Carlsberg's own purpose-built bottling plant in late 2026 will eliminate this margin drag and drive significant profit accretion in fiscal year 2027.
11.
Caucasus cluster expansion: Carlsberg is actively duplicating its Central Asian multi-beverage bottling playbook across the South Caucasus region to secure long-term top-line growth. A Redburn report dated 6 October 2026 highlighted that Carlsberg is acquiring Pepsi bottlers in Georgia and Armenia to create a unified Caucasus cluster. This regional expansion will double Carlsberg's local business and leverage its 70% beer market share in Azerbaijan starting in 2027.
12.
India volume acceleration: Strong premium brand execution has established India as Carlsberg's fastest-growing organic beer market. A Redburn report dated 30 September 2026 estimated that Carlsberg India achieved 15% volume growth in 2026, driven by Tuborg Strong. This robust momentum means that India supports structural volume growth and could accelerate the resumption of group share buybacks if the planned local listing proceeds.
