Plausible problems to monitor

The first material weakness concerns uncollected state subsidies totaling RON 2,544.2 million as of June 2026, representing a significant working capital drag following the plunge in subsidy revenues from RON 1,056.9 million in H1 2025 to only RON 25.7 million in H1 2026, as detailed in Section 1 – Business Model + Organisation and Section 9 – Operator + Foundation Quality. This massive receivables concentration with a single counterparty–the Romanian state–exposes the group to severe liquidity stress if reimbursement schedules slip further or external financing conditions tighten materially.

The second critical signal involves audit-qualified trade receivables, with auditors flagging RON 810,430 thousand of unconfirmed accrued receivables due to insufficient supporting documentation as of June 2025, embedded within gross trade receivables of RON 3,405,328 thousand, as documented in Section 5 – Customer Side Analysis. This audit qualification indicates material internal control weaknesses in billing and collection systems that could crystallize into write-offs or regulatory scrutiny, particularly following the historical billing system disruptions and cyberattack incidents referenced throughout the distribution and supply segments.

The third structural vulnerability centers on depressed supply segment profitability, where the competitive retail supply business generated only RON 181.3 million in H1 2026 EBITDA despite contributing 69.9% of consolidated revenues, contrasting sharply with distribution's 85.2% share of group EBITDA, as analyzed in supply segment leaves the group vulnerable to wholesale price volatility and competitive pressures from low-cost state-backed generators like Hidroelectrica, which achieve substantially higher margins through vertical integration. The fourth notable weakness involves accelerating volume attrition, with retail electricity volumes falling 9% to 3,375.5 GWh in H1 2026 from 3,710.4 GWh in H1 2025, while natural gas volumes contracted even more severely to 347.8 GWh from 442.6 GWh, as discussed in Section 5 – Customer Side Analysis and Section 7 – Competitive Landscape + Adjacent Stages + Vertical Integration Threats.

This market share erosion following price cap termination suggests that Electrica faces intensifying competitive churn risks as customers migrate to competitors offering more attractive tariffs backed by lower-cost generation assets.

Outliers worth noting

The financial and operational profile of Societatea Energetica Electrica S.A. reveals three material statistical outliers that position the company at the extreme edges of the regional utility peer universe and remain inadequately addressed in management commentary. First, the company exhibits an exceptionally low net working capital cycle relative to regional distribution and supply peers, driven by an unusual structural asymmetry in cash conversion dynamics.

Electrica maintains retail collection terms between 5 and 30 working days to accelerate cash receipts, while the Romanian state remains Electrica's largest working capital debtor with uncollected price-cap subsidies reaching RON 2,544.2 million as of June 30, 2026. This RON 2.5 billion state receivable represents approximately 23.8% of annual consolidated revenues and creates a bizarre liquidity profile where the company simultaneously extends minimal credit to retail customers while absorbing multi-year payment delays from sovereign counterparties.

Electrica's distribution subsidiary DEER utilizes sectoral procurement terms under Law 99/2016 to push standard payment terms to 120 days for contracts exceeding RON 20,000, gaining significant leverage over grid suppliers. Management has not provided a credible explanation for why this extreme bilateral working capital stress –short customer terms paired with massive state arrears–does not trigger more frequent liquidity crises or covenant breaches, particularly given the company's 79% capital gearing ratio as of March 2026. 268 / CSR reports Second, the company demonstrates a profoundly divergent segment profitability structure that places it in the bottom decile of integrated utilities for supply margin performance while simultaneously ranking in the top quintile for distribution returns. The group's consolidated revenues are 69.9% driven by supply but 85.2% of group

EBITDA is captured by distribution, with DEER generating a robust H1 2026 EBITDA margin reaching 38% based on RON 2,839.8 million revenues, while competitive retail supply continues to deliver low margins with H1 2026 EBITDA of only RON 181.3 million. This revenue-to-profit inversion implies that the supply business operates at an EBITDA margin below 3% while distribution achieves margins exceeding 38%, creating one of the widest intra-group margin spreads observed in the regional utility sector.

Upstream producers like Hidroelectrica capture high margins achieving 55% EBITDA margin on RON 3.1 billion revenue. Management's strategic rationale for maintaining an unprofitable supply franchise–particularly in the context of aggressive downstream expansion by vertically integrated competitors with structurally lower cost bases–remains insufficiently articulated beyond generic references to customer retention and balancing market participation.

Third, the company's capital intensity and return profile exhibit a striking statistical anomaly when benchmarked against regional peers undertaking comparable renewable generation buildouts. Strategy 2030 commits the group to installing 1,000 MW of renewable solar and wind capacity, developing 900 MWh of storage capacity by 2030, and directing investments of at least RON 3.7 billion into modernizing distribution infrastructure.

This represents a five-year capital commitment exceeding 34% of the current regulated asset base while simultaneously maintaining distribution operations under a 6.94% regulated return framework. The fifth regulatory period sets the Regulated Rate of Return at 6.94%, with own-source grid investments exceeding mandatory minimums receiving a 1% RRR incentive. The peer benchmarking table reveals that Ignitis Group invested heavily with 2025 capital expenditure of EUR 720.3 million while achieving a 7.5% adjusted ROCE, and CEZ AS increased capital capex causing a net debt of CZK 213.9 billion in 2025 while maintaining significantly higher scale and diversification.

Management has not adequately explained how Electrica's ambitious 1 GW generation target –representing a leap from negligible current capacity to a material generation footprint–can be financed and executed without either materially diluting equity returns or breaching the company's already-stressed debt covenants, particularly given that uncollected state subsidies remained elevated at RON 2,544.2 million in June 2026, with outstanding price-cap compensations restricting overall free cash flow generation.
