Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Elisa entered Q3 2026 as a high-quality Finnish connectivity incumbent whose historic premium had already narrowed sharply, with the market requiring renewed mobile growth and operating leverage before granting that premium again. Business profile, established: Elisa is a Finnish, internationally operating telecommunications and technology company serving more than 2.8 million consumer, corporate and public-administration customers in Finland and Estonia, while its software businesses sell globally across more than 100 countries.
1.
Segment structure, broadened: Since 2025, Elisa has reported Consumer Customers, Corporate Customers and International Software Services separately, replacing the former two-segment structure and making the software growth and margin profile more visible to investors.

Section summary
  1. Q3 2026 changed Elisa from a delayed-recovery story into a proof-of-execution story, because mobile deterioration and weaker cash conversion now threaten 2027 EPS before software restructuring and data-centre growth can contribute materially.
1.
Outlook, weakened: Elisa maintained 2026 guidance, but management said telecom-service growth would land at the lower end of the 0–2% range, while Q3 mobile-service revenue declined 2.3% and Finnish postpaid net adds turned negative, so the unchanged headline concealed a lower-quality earnings trajectory. Unchanged guidance now carries more risk.
2.
Consensus deviation, material: Group revenue of €561 million matched consensus, but telecom-service revenue was 0.9% below consensus and comparable EBITDA of €210 million missed by 1.5%, meaning the revenue headline overstated the quality of delivery and the earnings base entered Q4 weaker than expected. Revenue met, earnings missed.
3.
EPS surprise, negative: Comparable EPS was €0.59, approximately 7% below consensus, and adjusted profit before tax was €7 million short, making Q3 a more serious earnings event than the modest EBITDA miss because higher depreciation and financial costs magnified the impact below operating profit.EPS missed more sharply than EBITDA.
4.
Mobile evidence, deteriorating: Redburn had expected an improvement in average revenue per user, but it remained stable sequentially while churn rose to 18.7% and Finnish postpaid subscribers excluding machineto-machine and Internet of Things customers fell by 22,000, contradicting the expectation that Q4 contract renewals would quickly restore pricing power.The recovery mechanism failed in Q3.

Long-term growth, unsupported: Elisa still targets more than 4% annual revenue and EBITDA growth from 2024 through 2027, but Deutsche Bank forecasts only 2% EBITDA compound growth and said that shortfall was already reflected in consensus, leaving Q3 as confirmation that the formal target requires several unproven recoveries to occur together.The strategic target needs multiple successes.
