[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Alcon is a cause-for-concern-clearing case in an oversold, number-one ophthalmology franchise: core earnings per share are compounding at 12 to 15 percent again on a broad, dated product cycle, and at CHF 53.76 the shares sit at the lowest multiple of their own five-year range. The earnings alone pay us about 11 percent a year while we wait; the case clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months only if the 24-month-forward multiple recovers from 15.5x to about 18x, and half of that recovery is a call on a sector that is currently refusing to re-rate.

  • The change is internal, and it is a product cycle rather than a change of people or strategy.
    • The chief executive has been in post since July 2018 and the strategy is an evolution, so the force is what the company is launching: the Unity consoles into a 30,000-unit installed base, PanOptix Pro in premium lenses, Precision7 and TOTAL30 in contact lenses, Tryptyr in prescription dry eye, with a $100 million cost programme and a $1.5 billion buyback underneath.
    • The company calls the cycle the broadest in its history and the numbers agree.
    • Equipment sales grew 23 percent and 25 percent in constant currency in the first two quarters of 2026, the largest equipment beat in three years, with over 1,000 qualified leads after the Unity launch.
    • Guidance for FY2026 core EPS was raised in May and again on 10 August 2026, to $3.45 to $3.55 (+12 to +15 percent constant currency), after two cuts in 2025.
  • The external forces do not help, and one of them hurts.
    • End-market demand grows at low single digits and is not changing pace
    • United States procedure volumes are flat on surgeon capacity
    • And since late 2024 Johnson & Johnson and Bausch + Lomb have chosen to fight the highest-margin product line, United States presbyopia-correcting lenses, on price.
    • The tariff force reversed in 2026 with a $60 million refund, and currency turned from a drag to a small tailwind.
    • Taken together the external forces are neutral to mildly adverse, which is why 'Change Forces' is a B and not an A.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up and are inside the framework's target band on delivered numbers.
    • Constant-currency sales growth went from 3 percent in Q2 2025 to 7 percent in Q2 2026
    • Core operating income grew 16 percent in constant currency
    • Core EPS grew 9 percent in constant currency in the quarter and is guided to +12 to +15 percent for the year
    • The core gross margin reached 64.7 percent, up 250 basis points
    • Free cash flow was $1.70 billion in FY2025, above core net income.
    • The composition is the caveat: about six points of our 13 percent a year EPS path come from revenue, five from margin, one from the share count, and the margin is the component that reversed in 2025.
    • The highest-margin line, implantables, grew 1 percent in constant currency in each of the first two quarters of 2026.
      • It is the line the market watches and it is not yet growing.
  • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has travelled from the company's choices and actions through its key operating metrics and its financial statements, and since August it has begun to move the sell-side's estimates.
    • FY2026 consensus EPS rose 7.1 percent over the six months to 2026-09-11 and every horizon is being raised over one, three and six months
    • The dashboard's older revision series, dated 2026-07-16, still shows the post-Q1 cut, which is why the earnings-momentum pin is D on stale data.
    • The share price has not moved at all: the beat and the raise of 10 August were followed by a lower price.
  • What we think it earns: core EPS of $3.07 in FY2025 rising to about $4.45 in FY2028, 13.2 percent a year, with consensus at $4.48.
    • The parts reconcile: revenue +6.2 percent a year, core operating margin from 19.8 percent to 23.0 percent, a neutral ratio below operating income, and a share count falling about 1 percent a year on the buyback.
  • What it costs: 15.5x 24-month-forward earnings at USD 67.21, the 0th percentile of the stock's own five-year range and the 1st percentile of the last two years, against a five-year median of 22.9x.
    • Nothing of the 12 to 15 percent guided growth is in the price: at the dashboard peer median of 14.9x the shares are priced like a mature consumables company growing earnings at mid single digits.
    • But eleven of the seventeen peers in the comparison set also sit at or below the 5th percentile of their own ten-year multiple history, so about half of the gap to a fair multiple is a sector call, not a company call.
  • The return clears the bar over eighteen months and not over three years at the modal multiple.
    • On the framework's valuation convention, 18x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the modal return is 22.8 percent a year over eighteen months (a B on the framework's anchors) and 16.3 percent a year over three years (a C).
    • The earnings component carries about 11 percent a year in both horizons
    • The re-rating from 15.5x to 18x is the swing factor
    • The dividend adds half a point.
    • At the no-re-rating multiple the return is 11 percent a year, a D and not a loss.
    • The three-year bar is cleared only at 19.8x or above, or at a price about 9 percent lower.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', and it explains why the multiple has not moved.
    • An oversold, high-quality compounder whose cause for concern (a 2025 competitive scare in premium lenses and two guidance cuts within two quarters of a capital-markets day) has hard but incomplete evidence of clearing: share stabilised at 70 to 73 percent from 66, guidance raised twice, an 11 percent EPS beat in Q2 2026, but implantables still at 1 percent.
    • The pattern says the multiple recovers when the market sees the concern clear, and for this stock that means implantables growth, not equipment growth.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the product cycle to the operating metrics to the financial statements to the guidance to the early estimate revisions: every link is delivered, dated and measurable.
    • It breaks, or at least bends, at the last link, from earnings to the share price, which runs through a sector multiple the company does not control, and at the one operating line the market has chosen as its test.
    • The case therefore is: compelling on the earnings and on the entry multiple, less than compelling on the return, because half of the eighteen-month return is a re-rating the sector is currently refusing.

  • Rating = B, because the expected change is material: the input forces are strong on the internal side and delivered, the outputs are inside the 12 to 20 percent target band on reported half-year numbers, and from the lowest multiple in five years the return clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months with a multiple that is still cheap by every measure we have.

    • The parts combine as a chain that holds at each link over eighteen months
    • 'Change Forces' (B) and 'Financial Outputs' (B) carry it, and 'Modal return' (C) does not bind, because the three-year shortfall is a multiple assumption held deliberately below the stock's own 10th percentile (19.1x), not an earnings shortfall.
    • Not A, because there is no external tailwind, no leadership change and no acceleration in revenue growth beyond the three-year trend, the return rests on re-rating for half of its eighteen-month total, and the three-year modal return is 16 percent a year.
    • Not C, which was live, because the shortfall at three years is a deliberately conservative multiple, and the earnings path on its own is a D-to-C return with nothing in it that depends on the sector.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the Q2 2026 beat was low quality and the de-rating is right.
      • Equipment placements (+25 percent) are a one-off replacement spike on a ten-year cycle and fade in 2027 against +23 and +25 percent comparisons
      • The refund is timing
      • Implantables are trapped at 1 percent because PureSee, enVista Envy and the Light Adjustable Lens have permanently taken the growth of the category
      • United States volumes are capped by surgeon capacity and a proposed 12 percent Medicare cataract fee cut
      • Margins are capped near 20 to 21 percent by the Swiss franc cost mismatch, net tariffs of $40 to $90 million and R&D at 9 percent of sales
      • Earnings growth reverts to the 8 percent post-spin average once the launches annualise.
      • European medtech consumables as a group have de-rated to their own lows, and the market knows something about the group's growth that the bulls do not.
      • On that path core EPS grows 8 to 10 percent a year, the multiple stays at 15x and the return is 9 to 11 percent a year, an F to a D on the framework's anchors.
      • The evidence for it is real: two quarters of implantables at 1 percent, a 175 percent premium-lens surge at Bausch + Lomb in Q2 2026, a whole peer set at its lows, a guidance rise that was about half refund, and a beat that did not move the price.
    • What we concede.
      • The de-rating is partly sector-wide and beyond the company's control, so half the re-rating is not ours to earn.
      • Implantables at 1 percent is the weak link, and the odds we give the re-acceleration are about 42 percent, possible rather than plausible.
      • The August guidance rise was about half the $20 million refund drop-through, about $0.04 of the $0.07 rise at the midpoint.
      • If the sector's fair level is 14x, the return is 4 to 7 percent a year.
    • Why we hold our view: the earnings path does not depend on the sector or on implantables growing.
      • Consumables (about 24 percent of consensus revenue, growing 5 percent on the installed base) and contact lenses (record share of about 26 percent, +5 percent with four points of price) are the annuity.
      • Even if equipment growth falls to zero in 2027 and implantables stay flat, group revenue grows 4 to 5 percent and core EPS about 10 percent on the margin and the share count, which at an unchanged 15.5x is about a 10 percent annual return, a D and not a loss.
      • The share stabilisation at 70 to 73 percent with over 90 percent of PanOptix accounts converted to PanOptix Pro is delivered evidence against permanent share loss, and Bausch + Lomb's enVista Beyond trial missed its depth-of-focus target, which delays the next challenge to Vivity.
      • The sell-side's own FY2027 range, $3.80 to $4.26, already spans the bear and the bull, and the price sits at the bottom of it.
      • The three tests that would tell us the competing hypothesis is right are all measurable within two quarters: equipment growth below 15 percent constant currency for two consecutive quarters
        • Implantables growth below zero for two quarters or United States presbyopia-correcting share below 65 percent
        • Core margin expansion below 90 basis points in constant currency in H2 2026.
    • Where the case against would win.
      • If the sector keeps de-rating while Alcon's revisions stay positive, the multiple is not ours to earn at all, and we would move the modal multiple to the no-re-rating branch and 'Modal return' to D
      • The eighteen-month clearance goes with it.

  • It is the number one ophthalmology franchise in the world, growing revenue at 6 to 7 percent and core earnings per share at 12 to 15 percent in 2026, guided and delivered, after a 2025 in which two guidance cuts and a competitive scare in its highest-margin lens line took its forward multiple from about 28x to the lowest level of the last five years, 15.4x on 24-month-forward earnings.
