Peab (PEAB.B-SE) — what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results strengthen the existing case modestly where it was weakest, on delivery against the robust-base test, while leaving its fulcrum question about residential capital unanswered and opening two questions the case never asked: whether Civil Engineering is losing share, and whether a near-term beat has flattened the earnings-growth path the case needs.

What changed

Peab reported Q3 2026 on 28 October 2026. Segment net sales rose 4.50% to SEK 16,304m, 1.2% below DNB Carnegie's estimate. Segment operating profit rose to SEK 1,296m from SEK 1,148m, a 9.9% beat; the segment margin was 7.9% against 7.4%; segment EPS rose 21.93% to SEK 3.67. The rolling twelve-month segment operating margin reached 5.0% against 4.5% for FY25. The contracting divisions were in line with estimates, Construction at a 2.5% margin (2.2% a year earlier) and Civil Engineering at 4.2% (4.0%); the beat sat in Industry, where EBIT grew 14% on paving and aggregates and the margin reached 13.6% from 12.6%. Project Development capital employed stood at SEK 18,675m at a 1.5% return.

Orders fell 8.00% to SEK 9,405m, better than DNB Carnegie's SEK 8.3bn forecast, but lopsided: Construction rose to SEK 4,630m from SEK 3,994m, Project Development to SEK 670m from SEK 339m, Industry was flat at SEK 2,460m, and Civil Engineering fell to SEK 2,694m from SEK 4,384m. Backlog reached SEK 52,439m from SEK 48,279m, growth of 8.61% against 11% cited at Q2; the Civil Engineering backlog was flat at SEK 20,996m. Unbooked preliminary contracts stand at SEK 20bn.

Cash flow before financing fell 31.95% to SEK 1,900m, with capital tie-up in development properties the drag, although operating cash flow before working capital improved to SEK 1,548m. Net debt was SEK 7,212m (net debt/equity 0.4), down from SEK 7,991m a year earlier but up from SEK 6.4bn at December 2025.

The sell-side response is one broker. DNB Carnegie reiterated Buy, held its SEK 110 target (13x 2027 EPS; SOTP range SEK 88-125), raised 2026 adjusted EPS to SEK 8.20 from SEK 7.50, 2027 to SEK 8.30 from SEK 8.20 and 2028 to SEK 9.10 from SEK 8.90, and described the revisions as immediate operational improvement rather than structural change. SEB, Kepler Cheuvreux, ABG and Handelsbanken published nothing retrieved, so "consensus" here is a single-broker proxy. The share price on the reporting day was SEK 97.2.

Around the quarter: Civil Engineering and Swerock/Asphalt split from 1 January 2027; Johan Dagertun becomes CFO on 4 February 2027; a Gothenburg office sale adds SEK 60m to Q4 earnings; the Finnish competition investigation into Peab Industri Oy is ongoing. In the same quarter Skanska posted a 5.0% construction margin, SEK 68.0bn of orders and a record SEK 328.3bn backlog alongside SEK 0.9bn of development impairments; Veidekke a 7.7% pre-tax margin and a record NOK 52.6bn order book; NCC agreed to sell its Industry unit at an enterprise value of SEK 8.2bn. Swedish property transactions fell 43% year on year to SEK 22bn and the five-year swap rate rose 77bp in the quarter.

The challenge

The obvious bear reading, that this was a low-quality seasonal beat, is not a challenge to the case: it is what the covering broker itself said, and the case already treats Industry profitability as a named driver with bitumen cost as its named risk. The readings below are ones the case does not hold.

The beat flattened the growth path the case needs. The case's gate for the next stage is a 12-20% EPS growth test, and it cited a base-case path of SEK 6.90 for 2026 rising to SEK 7.90 for 2027, growth of 14.5%. After these results the only published path is SEK 8.20, SEK 8.30 and SEK 9.10 for 2026, 2027 and 2028: growth of about 1% (calculated) into 2027 and about 10% (calculated) into 2028. A 9% upgrade to the base year with a 1% upgrade to the next is a pull-forward, and on these numbers the case's own growth test fails at the stage it was meant to be run. For this to be right, outer-year estimates have to stay roughly where they are once other brokers publish, Industry's 2026 strength has to be a level shift rather than a run-rate, and residential has to stay flat. All three are plausible; the broker's "timing, not structural" language describes exactly that.

Civil Engineering is losing share, not waiting on award timing. The case's strongest leg is the infrastructure driver, rated HIGH plausibility and HIGH confidence. In the quarter Peab's Civil Engineering orders fell 38.55% and its backlog there did not grow, while Skanska booked SEK 68.0bn of orders to a record backlog, Veidekke took its order book to a record, and Sentia is named as a rising threat in public tenders. The broker's reading is that infrastructure award timing is lumpy and the public programme (a SEK 799bn transport plan; infrastructure and industrial spending projected to rise from SEK 94bn to SEK 130bn by 2027) is intact. The alternative is that the money is being spent and others are winning it. The case already carries a mechanism: its own flag that Peab bid on only 52% of Trafikverket road O&M tenders against Svevia's 87%. A selective-bidding posture that management frames as margin protection is also a posture that cedes volume in a market where peers are bidding hard. For this to be right, Civil Engineering orders have to stay down at the full-year report while Swedish peer intake holds, and the SEK 20bn of unbooked contracts has to convert slowly. Veidekke's own order-book commentary, that activity rises in Norway but potentially declines in Sweden, cuts this way.

The residential half is consuming more capital into a weakening market. The case names the SEK 15.0bn residential lock-up as its key risk and leaves open whether the own-balance-sheet housing strategy is flexibility or a trap. This quarter leaned toward trap, in a way the case did not anticipate: Peab started more tenant-owner housing (Project Development orders roughly doubled to SEK 670m), capital tie-up in development properties pulled cash flow before financing down 31.95%, net debt rose by roughly SEK 0.8bn across 2026 to date, and this happened in a quarter when Swedish property transactions fell 43%, the five-year swap rate rose 77bp and Skanska impaired SEK 0.9bn of development assets. The broker's 50% haircut to residential book values is a valuation cushion, not an operating one, and the review's own second pivotal question is whether the incoming CFO can sustain the SEK 0.5bn buyback on lower free cash flow. For this to be right, Swedish transaction volumes have to stay depressed through 2027 and the new starts have to sell slowly; a stuck swap rate is sufficient for both. What the case does not accommodate is a company adding to the trapped capital rather than releasing it.

The positive reading the case does not hold: Industry now has an external price. The case's valuation work is a SOTP range it had not yet tested. In the same quarter NCC agreed to sell its Industry unit for an enterprise value of SEK 8.2bn, and Peab is separating Swerock/Asphalt from Civil Engineering from 1 January 2027 with its own head on the executive team. Peab's Industry ran a 13.6% margin and grew EBIT 14% in Q3. We do not say management intends a sale; it has said the split is a platform for growth. We do say a live peer transaction gives the one segment that beat a visible marker the SOTP range did not have, and the split makes the segment separately readable for the first time. For this to matter, the Industry margin has to hold through a season with aggregates inflation at 8.3% and hot-rolled coil offers rising to EUR 840 per tonne; on one quarter that is plausible, not probable.

What it does to the case

The share price is SEK 98.15 (26 November 2026), inside the case's SEK 87-122 SOTP band and the broker's updated SEK 88-125, nearer the middle than either bound; the case's observation that the price sits near neither bound still holds. The case's SEK 50bn backlog tracking threshold is not crossed: the backlog is above it, though below the case's own record figure of SEK 53,757m and growing more slowly.

  • Element E1, Required input change forces [C]; RA2, Robust base? [D]. Less pressure. But the case's trigger for lifting RA2 is two consecutive quarters above 5.00% with the order miss reversing to a beat. The quarterly 7.9% is the paving season, which cleared 5% a year ago too; the rolling figure sits at the line, not above it; and the order "beat" is an 8.00% decline against a single broker who had forecast a steeper one. RA2's headwinds question stays failed: orders down, Civil Engineering orders down by more than a third, Swedish transactions down 43%.

What would settle it

The second Earnings Review, four working days on, carries whatever broker estimates and targets have landed since 28 October. On the evidence here that may be very little: only DNB Carnegie published, and if SEB, Kepler Cheuvreux, ABG and Handelsbanken still have not, the growth-path question stays a single-broker question and the second review should say so rather than call it consensus. It cannot carry Q4 orders, unbooked-contract conversion, residential sales or cash flow.

The full-year report on 3 February 2027 settles the Civil Engineering question one way or the other: a second quarter of orders down by a third alongside firm peer intake would make the share-loss reading probable; a rebound would make it noise. It also carries first progress on converting the SEK 20bn of unbooked contracts, Q4 cash flow, and the dividend proposal.

The Capital Markets Day on 18 February 2027 carries the 2027-2029 business plan and, on the review's reading, buyback guidance from the incoming CFO. The company has not committed to say anything about portfolio intent for Swerock/Asphalt after the split; if it says nothing, the positive reading stays a reading.

Sweden's national construction data in Q1 2027 carries 2027 building starts against the 7% growth the review treats as the recovery's test. The Trafikverket bid-participation rate, the case's one behavioural monitoring item, is in nothing scheduled and the review carries no update on it. The Finnish competition investigation has no timetable.
