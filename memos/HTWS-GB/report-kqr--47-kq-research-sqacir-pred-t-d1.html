Section 3 – Master Lease Agreements and Renewal Dynamics

Section summary

  3. Analytical confidence in Helios Towers' upcoming core lease renewals is medium, supported by regional density advantages but tempered by global operator integration precedents.

African MLA renewal precedents

1.
Label: Successful African extensions: Tower operators in Africa have demonstrated a track record of extending major master lease agreements ahead of expirations. IHS Holding successfully renewed its master lease agreement with MTN in Zambia in March 2024 for a 10-year term extending through 2034. This was followed by a similar 10-year renewal with MTN in Rwanda in June 2024. These renewals indicate that anchor tenants prefer long-term extensions to avoid network disruption.
2.
Label: Rebased contract terms: African renewals often introduce sophisticated multi-currency structures to protect both the operator and the tower company from volatility. IHS Holding's August 2024 renewal with MTN Nigeria through 2032 introduced rebased financial terms covering 13,500 tenancies. The rebased contract comprises a local Naira component linked to local CPI, a US dollar component with quarterly foreign exchange resets, and a diesel indexation component. This diesel component represents a direct hedge against fuel prices which did not exist in prior agreements.
3.
Label: Operational model shift: Tower operators can restructure existing leases to eliminate volatile operational cost exposures while securing longer durations. IHS Holding amended its South African agreement with MTN in the second quarter of 2024 to unwind its power Managed Services contract and extend the lease structure through 2034. The operational adjustment transferred power provision responsibilities back to the customer, removing gross diesel costs from the tower operator's income statement. This contract restructuring reduced gross revenues but left Adjusted EBITDA unaffected.
4.
Label: Durable lease structures: Helios Towers employs highly defensive long-term lease terms modeled after established international infrastructure frameworks. Management reports that standard lease agreements feature initial terms of 10 to 15 years with automatic multi-decade renewals. The average remaining life of the company's customer contracts stood at 6.5 years in the first half of 2026. These long-term arrangements provide strong revenue visibility of US$5.3 billion across the portfolio.

Indian and Indonesian contract restructurings

Label: Impending Indian renewals: Indian tower infrastructure operators face severe lease expiration concentration that could lead to near-term cash flow volatility. Jefferies reported on October 28, 2026, that slightly over 10% of Indus Towers' tenancies are coming up for renewal over the next 6 to 12 months. The broker warned that higher discontinuation or rental discounts during these cycles represent a major risk to future rental revenues. This near-term renewal volume means Indus Towers may face downward revision of its margins if negotiations turn unfavorable.
6.
Label: Severe client concentration: Contract renewal uncertainty from dominant wireless providers represents a structural overhang for operator-anchored tower networks. Emkay Research reported on October 28, 2026, that uncertainty regarding Reliance Jio's contract renewal remains a prominent risk. The broker estimates that this single contract renewal exposure impacts approximately 15 percent of total revenues for Indus Towers. This concentration highlights how dependency on major anchors can jeopardize cash flows unless alternative tenants are quickly secured. Label: Mutually beneficial concessions: Indonesian tower companies are mitigating merger-related site churn by trading rental discounts for significant contract extensions. Danareksa Securities reported on September 30, 2026, that Sarana Menara Nusantara negotiated an agreement with XL Axiata and Smartfren following their merger. The tower company accepted an 8% cut in lease rates in exchange for extending the contract tenor to 12 years with zero site churn. This restructuring is considered net value-accretive because it prevents destructive decommissioning of overlapping sites. Contrary evidence: Global master lease agreements are not always watertight because operators can unilaterally serve early termination notices to force pricing concessions. Morgan Stanley reported on March 25, 2026, that Swisscom's joint subsidiary Vodafone and Fastweb served notice to terminate its master service agreement with Inwit effective March 2028. Swisscom justified this action by claiming Inwit's pricing was above market rates and that management refused to engage in formal negotiations. This high-profile cancellation shows that operators can successfully disrupt longterm contracts to seek better commercial terms.
