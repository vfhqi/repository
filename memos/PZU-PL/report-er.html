EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. PZU entering this period held a dominant Polish market position but faced growing complexity and rate headwinds within its banking operations.

Powszechny Zakład Ubezpieczeń SA delivered a record FY 2025 financial performance that comfortably exceeded consensus expectations, generating net profit attributable to the parent company of PLN 6,699 million, representing a substantial 25.4% expansion compared to PLN 5,342 million in FY 2024. The group's consolidated net profit reached PLN 13,987 million, driven by exceptionally strong underwriting margins with a combined operating ratio of 86.2% in the non-life segment, robust investment results reaching PLN 2,784 million, and peak banking sector contributions as both Bank Pekao and Alior Bank continued to benefit from historically elevated interest rates in Poland.

The Solvency II ratio strengthened to 239% at year-end 2025, comfortably exceeding the regulatory target of 200%, enabling management to propose an attractive dividend of PLN 4.80 per share representing an 82% payout of standalone net profit. This operational outperformance was achieved despite persistent claims inflation pressures in motor insurance and a challenging macroeconomic environment marked by elevated nominal wage growth of 9.1% and consumer price inflation averaging 3.6% in 2025.

The Net Read for the entire investment case is that PZU has successfully demonstrated its capacity to generate high-conviction standalone returns exceeding strategic targets, with core return on equity reaching 21.0% versus a 2027 goal of 19%, while simultaneously defending underwriting profitability through disciplined pricing in retail motor segments that deliberately sacrificed volume growth to protect margins. However, the investment thesis remains fundamentally incomplete because the transformational Danish compromise merger between PZU and Bank Pekao, which was expected to unlock between PLN 15 to 20 billion in pro forma excess capital, has been structurally delayed by legislative and political gridlock that extended the targeted completion timeline from mid- 2026 to December 31, 2027.

This deferral represents the single most material deviation from prior sell-side expectations, as the Ministry of Finance suspended work on the critical draft bill in September 2025, introducing heightened political uncertainty that threatens the viability of the entire capital optimization framework. If the required statutory amendments to the Polish Banking Law and Insurance Activity Act are not published in the Journal of Laws by the end of December 2026, the entire transaction term sheet will expire, leaving PZU trapped in its complex state-controlled conglomerate structure without achieving any of the anticipated structural benefits.

The critical changes to the investment thesis center on three structural developments that fundamentally reshape the risk-return profile of the equity. First, the legislative suspension of the bancassurance reorganization has transformed the investment case from a near-term capital-unlocking event into a binary legislative outcome that will not resolve until late 2027, forcing investors to reassess whether PZU's standalone dividend capacity can sustain its highly attractive yield profile without the benefit of optimized group capital.

Second, the rapid monetary easing cycle initiated by the National Bank of Poland, which delivered 175 basis points of cumulative rate cuts in 2025 followed by an additional 25 basis points in March 2026, has triggered the early onset of net interest margin compression at both banking subsidiaries, with Alior Bank's margin contracting by 0.6 percentage points quarter-on-quarter to 4.5% in Q2 2026 while net interest income fell 12.9% year-on-year.

This deterioration confirms that the banking segment's record profitability was a cyclical peak rather than a sustainable earnings stream, threatening to offset the underwriting improvements that management has delivered through disciplined non-life pricing. Third, the implementation of revised Solvency II group rules effective January 2027 will increase overall capital requirements by incorporating combined credit institution buffer requirements at the insurance group level, creating an additional regulatory headwind that was not fully anticipated in prior sell-side forecasts and will compress the reported Solvency II ratio even if underlying capital generation remains robust.

Leadership changes during the reporting period restored execution credibility following a period of political turbulence, with Bogdan Benczak officially appointed as President of the Management Board for the 2027 to 2029 term on April 14, 2026, after initially serving as CEO from September 2025. The Supervisory Board simultaneously appointed a restructured Management Board including Maciej Fedyna to oversee risk management, Elżbieta Häuser-Schöneich, Tomasz Tarkowski, Jan Zimowicz, and Kazimierz Karolczak, implementing a professionalized governance framework designed to execute the company's ambitious 2027 strategic targets without further operational disruption.

Additionally, Piotr Matczuk joined the board effective September 1, 2026, bringing over 20 years of experience in Polish and international financial institutions to strengthen private equity and infrastructure expertise. The investor relations department maintained exceptional continuity, with Magdalena Komaracka continuing as Investor Relations Director, providing reassurance to institutional investors during the complex structural transition. These appointments represent a positive governance evolution away from politically motivated management overhauls, though the company remains exposed to state-control dynamics given the Polish State Treasury's 34.2% controlling stake. Strategic priorities and structural initiatives have evolved substantially as management adapts to the deferred merger timeline. PZU accelerated its IT Strategy for 2025-2027 in December 2025, targeting full leverage of cloud, AI, and Low Code solutions to drive business value, with specific goals to deliver 10% of new business needs using Low Code platforms and shorten time-to-market by 20%.

The group deployed more than 30 AI solutions with another 30 in the pipeline, including generative AI tools that reached 12,000 employees and generated 1.7 million prompts to improve administrative productivity. To mitigate the risk of legislative failure on the Danish compromise, management confirmed the development of an advanced internal risk model scheduled for implementation in the first half of 2027, which aims to optimize capital position under Solvency II rules and safeguard the current dividend policy even if the holding company reorganization fails to materialize.

The group also executed tactical consolidation moves, signing a formal merger plan with subsidiary LINK4 on May 29, 2026, to simplify the operating model and develop a highly efficient multibrand distribution system, while simultaneously pursuing geographic expansion through a conditional agreement to acquire 100% of MetLife Ukraine, the country's leading life insurer with a 50% market share and 900,000 active clients.

Demand dynamics and customer behavior demonstrated robust underlying commercial momentum despite PZU's deliberate decision to prioritize profitability over market share growth in retail motor segments. Mass motor gross written premiums fell 5.2% year-on-year as PZU implemented consistent premium price increases to counter high claims inflation, accepting a temporary loss of volume to aggressive competitors who actively underpriced policies.

This disciplined pricing strategy was partially offset by strong demand in non-motor mass segments, where premiums rose 13.6% year-on-year driven by the refreshed PZU Dom and PZU Firma packages that successfully attracted small and medium enterprise customers. The corporate non-life segment delivered exceptional growth with operating profit expanding 43.5% to PLN 1,075 million, supported by major contract wins in the energy and strategic industrial sectors and an 8.3% year-on-year increase in corporate motor registrations.

Life insurance performance remained structurally profitable with operating margins reaching 27.2%, driven by steady reductions in claims ratios and strong bancassurance channel distribution that generated PLN 110 million in additional individual protective life revenues through high sales of mortgage-linked products at Pekao and Alior Bank branches. Regulatory and political conditions represent the most significant near-term risk factor for the investment case, as the legislative suspension of the Danish compromise merger creates a binary outcome framework that will not resolve until late 2026 or early 2027.

The Polish Financial Supervision Authority intensified oversight of insurance undertakings throughout 2025 and 2026, focusing on solvency margins, product governance, and claims handling efficiency, while a landmark Supreme Court resolution in September 2025 confirmed that motor third-party liability compensation must be calculated based on hypothetical repair costs even if no repair is performed, restricting insurers' ability to apply parts and paint material discounts when calculating cash settlements and increasing average payout values.

The implementation of the Insurance Recovery and Resolution Directive effective January 30, 2027, introduces a harmonized recovery and resolution framework that will inevitably raise administrative and governance expenses as PZU aligns its risk management systems with national resolution authority guidelines. Political interventions continued to impact consolidated earnings, with the Office of Competition and Consumer Protection fining Bank Pekao PLN 119 million in December 2025 over mortgage holiday application restrictions, forcing the bank to maintain a full provision while appealing the decision.

Sell-side response to the FY 2025 earnings was broadly supportive of PZU's long-term dividend capacity but flagged emerging operational disappointments in underwriting trends relative to peers and continuing regulatory uncertainty surrounding the merger timeline. Erste Brokerage Poland maintained an Outperform rating with a target price of PLN 70, highlighting the company's highly supportive dividend yield of approximately 8% while cautioning that core operating trends do not appear as appealing when compared to key domestic competitor Warta.

Morgan Stanley maintained an Equal-weight rating on Bank Pekao with an updated price target of PLN 268, preferring rival PKO BP over Pekao due to transaction-related integration complexity and margin compression risks as interest rate cuts flow through the Polish banking sector. Millennium Dom Maklerski emphasized the severe margin squeeze at Alior Bank, where net interest income fell 12.9% year-on-year in Q2 2026 driven by a 0.6 percentage point quarter-on-quarter contraction in net interest margin to 4.5%, underscoring the accelerating headwinds faced by PZU's banking assets as monetary policy normalizes.

Financial forecasts for the 2027 strategic horizon remain highly ambitious but are increasingly vulnerable to banking margin compression and potential legislative failure on the capital optimization program. Management reaffirmed targets to achieve consolidated net profit over PLN 6.2 billion by 2027 with core return on equity over 19%, maintain a non-life combined ratio under 90%, and deliver an operating margin over 20% in life insurance.

The current standalone core ROE of 21.0% already exceeds the long-term target, demonstrating strong execution against strategic priorities, though this outperformance was heavily assisted by peak interest rates that are now reversing. Morgan Stanley's internal macroeconomic team expects the NBP base rate to fall from 5.75% in 2025 to 4% in 2026 and 3.75% in 2027, with Bank Pekao's net interest margin demonstrating an estimated sensitivity of 10 to 15 basis points contraction for every 100 basis points of central bank rate cuts.

If monetary easing accelerates beyond these projections or if the Danish compromise legislation fails to secure passage by December 2026, PZU's consolidated earnings could fall materially short of strategic targets despite continued strength in core underwriting operations, as the banking segment's steep margin compression would outpace insurance profitability improvements and potentially force downward revisions to the company's highly attractive dividend policy.
