Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Integrating Coca-Cola Beverages Africa represents a transformational expansion that will significantly accelerate Coca-Cola HBC's long-term organic growth profile.
  2. Stronger financial performance at the African business suggests the transaction will deliver more than five percent earnings per share accretion during the first consolidated year.
1.
The Key Question: "What are Coca-Cola Beverages Africa's most recent financial figures on a basis comparable with Coca-Cola HBC's (revenue, EBITDA, EBIT and their trends in 2025 and 2026 by country, seasonality by half-year, net debt by currency and interest rate, existing non-controlling interests in its subsidiaries, and capital spending as a share of revenue), what do The Coca-Cola Company's disclosures on the business show (Deutsche Bank, 27-Oct-26, reads them as dollar revenue up 36%), and what will Coca-Cola HBC's ownership of 75% and the remaining partners' stakes mean for minority interests in its earnings?"

Transforming Geographic Exposure

2.
Rapidly expanding footprint: Coca-Cola HBC is significantly altering its geographic profile through the acquisition of a seventy-five percent stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa. This transaction adds fourteen eastern and southern African markets to the portfolio, raising CCH-GB's total population served by thirty-eight percent to over one billion people. Consequently, the expanded footprint shifts CCH-GB's volume exposure from mature European territories toward dynamic developing regions with long-term volume runways.

11 Highly attractive demographics: Coca-Cola HBC benefits from the secular growth tailwinds of Africa, which is characterized by the youngest global demographic profile and low per-capita beverage consumption. Legacy

3.
African markets currently consume an average of twenty-eight liters per capita compared to fifty-four liters in CCH-GB's established markets. This structural upside supports the company's long-term organic growth algorithm of six to seven percent sales expansion.
4.
Stronger growth profile: Coca-Cola HBC's acquisition of the African bottling operations substantially enhances the entity's long-term growth and expansion potential. Deutsche Bank indicates that matching South Africa's level of beverage consumption across the acquired markets would double CCH-GB's total volume and revenue. This growth potential helps diversify CCH-GB's operations away from more volatile and restricted markets like Russia.
5.
Structured minority buyouts: Coca-Cola HBC designed the seventy-five percent acquisition with an option to purchase the remaining twenty-five percent stake after three to five years. This structural option provides CCH- GB with a clear path to full ownership of the African bottling business. However, the initial share issuance to partners slightly dilutes the existing shareholder base upon transaction completion.

Financial and Risk Analysis

6.
Materially accretive earnings: Coca-Cola HBC stands to experience stronger earnings accretion from consolidating the African business than management originally communicated. Recent financial disclosures by The Coca-Cola Company suggest that African revenues increased by approximately thirty-six percent between 2024 and 2026. Deutsche Bank expects this strong revenue trajectory to drive more than five percent earnings per share accretion for CCH-GB in the first full year.
7.
Increasing capital intensity: Coca-Cola HBC faces higher operational complexity and capital requirements as it integrates the lower-margin African business. The group consolidated net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio is expected to rise toward one point six times following completion. However, the company maintains its commitment to an investment grade credit rating by utilizing a structured mix of debt and newly issued equity.
8.
Elevated macroeconomic risks: Coca-Cola HBC must navigate severe currency devaluations and high inflation across several of its newly consolidated African territories. Bernstein highlights that increased exposure to volatile African markets shifts CCH-GB's risk profile, though the strategic fit remains compelling. Furthermore, investors remain concerned about the lack of clear management guidance regarding the timeline for CCBA margin improvement.

Section 2 – CCBA Financial Performance and Country Trends

Section summary
  1. Coca-Cola Beverages Africa demonstrates robust financial growth led by strong performance in South Africa.
  2. Operating margins face near-term dilution from competitive pressure and regional inflationary headwinds.
  3. Consolidating the African business will temporarily increase Coca-Cola Hellenic's leverage and capital intensity before delivering long-term cash flow benefits.
  4. High geographic exposure to the South African Rand and Kenyan Shilling introduces notable seasonal and foreign exchange volatility.
  5. Required capital spending of approximately eight percent of sales will exceed historical European baselines and peer averages.

Financial Performance Overview

1.
Significant revenue growth: Bernstein Research on 2 November 2026 reports that legacy CCBA markets account for 24% of pro forma group net sales and 17% of group EBIT. This represents a smaller proportion compared to its 27% pro forma volume share due to lower revenue per case. Consequently, the consolidation will dilute consolidated operating margins but boost absolute profit.
2.
Favorable historical performance: Rothschild & Co Redburn on 28 October 2025 reported that CCBA achieved 2024 net sales of $3,632 mm and operating income of $267 mm. This represented a 7.3% operating margin for the standalone business before the acquisition. This operating profit margin sits below the historical double-digit margins of Coca-Cola HBC's European divisions.
3.
Underlying organic expansion: Deutsche Bank Research on 27 October 2026 noted that disclosures from The Coca-Cola Company suggest dollar revenues for CCBA have increased by c. 36% since the original acquisition announcement. This suggests that recent financial performance is stronger than the historical baseline provided in the initial transaction disclosures. This high growth rate should translate into more than 5% earnings per share accretion for Coca-Cola HBC in its first full year.
4.
Increased profit contribution: Jefferies Research on 5 November 2026 calculated that the consolidation of CCBA will increase the African division's share of group profit to 20% of total EBIT. This substantial footprint helps dilute group profit exposure to more volatile markets such as Russia. The transaction is expected to close by the end of 2026 as regulatory approvals have been obtained in five of six jurisdictions.

In Ethiopia, the soft drinks market is highly attractive because it is led by bottled water which accounts for 47% of retail volumes. CCBA's extensive route-to-market execution in these emerging countries provides a strong runway for volume growth over the medium term.

13.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research on 26 June 2026 reported that prior to 2018, CCBA had been losing market share in South Africa. After a period of stability, the business suffered further retail volume share losses to Varun Beverages, which acquired the local Pepsi bottling license. This competitive pressure has led to stagnant retail volumes for CCBA in a growing market, proving that market share leadership is under threat from aggressive pricing and distribution expansion.

Slowing regional operations: Nuvama Institutional Equities on 30 October 2026 pointed out that while most African territories are performing well, Zambia has been slightly slower. Additionally, high inflationary pressures and local currency fluctuations across several African operations continue to impact gross profit margins. These factors create near-term earnings headwinds unless Coca-Cola HBC can rapidly achieve distribution cost efficiencies and supply-chain integration.

Other side of the story: Coca-Cola HBC AG in its third quarter trading update on 4 November 2026 highlighted that organic revenue grew 9.8% in emerging markets, driven by double-digit volume growth in Nigeria. This strong growth demonstrates that African consumer demand remains highly resilient despite localized macroeconomic challenges. This performance suggests that the CCBA consolidation could outperform cautious margin expectations and accelerate EPS growth faster than consensus estimates predict.

Balance Sheet and Debt Profile

16.
Elevated group leverage: Coca-Cola Hellenic will experience a material increase in consolidated balance sheet leverage upon completing the transaction. Jefferies Research on 29 June 2026 estimated that the group will absorb approximately EUR 1.5 bn of CCBA net debt at completion. This additional debt burden is projected to push the group pro-forma leverage to one point six times net debt to EBITDA. This consolidated leverage ratio sits comfortably within management's medium-term target range but represents a significant step up from historic standalone levels.
17.
Significant standalone debt: Standalone CCBA carried a substantial debt burden prior to the acquisition agreement with Coca-Cola Hellenic. Rothschild and Co Redburn on 28 October 2025 reported that CCBA's net debt as of 31 December 2024 was USD 1,063 mm. This debt excludes the Ghana Voltic water business which was sold earlier in 2025 to Equatorial Coca-Cola Bottling Company. This large baseline of debt highlights the capital intensity of emerging markets compared to mature European divisions.
18.
Increased funding costs: Coca-Cola Hellenic has already incurred higher financing expenses to fund the African acquisition prior to full consolidation. The company reported in its 2026 half-yearly financial report on 5 August 2026 that net finance costs rose by EUR 6.7 mm to EUR 8.0 mm. This increase reflects the higher interest expenses from issuing EUR 2.1 bn in Euro-denominated fixed-rate bonds in April 2026. These bonds were issued specifically to fund the agreed acquisition of CCBA and manage related bridge financing requirements.
19.
Significant currency risks: Coca-Cola Hellenic faces substantial currency exposure from local African denominations following the integration. Jefferies Research on 7 May 2026 estimated that South Africa represents 60% of CCBA's net revenue while Kenya accounts for 9%. This concentrated exposure to the South African Rand and the Kenyan Shilling subjects the combined entity to heightened foreign exchange volatility. This risk could impact translation into Euro reporting and dilute reported earnings per share.

Operational Seasonality and Weighting

20.
Pronounced seasonal weighting: Standalone CCBA experiences highly concentrated demand during the second half of the year due to weather patterns. Morgan Stanley on 30 July 2024 indicated that peak seasonal demand in African beverage markets typically occurs during the fourth quarter. This timing creates a strong second half volume weighting that will alter the seasonal earnings profile of Coca-Cola Hellenic. Consequently, the consolidated group's quarterly earnings will show greater dependence on southern hemisphere summer performance.
21.
Volatile regional volumes: Standalone African operations face significant volume and margin volatility during colder or wetter seasonal periods. Jefferies Research on 3 February 2026 noted that a weak season historically impacted volumes and margins across key African territories. Consequently, this seasonal variation requires careful working capital management to maintain consistent cash flows throughout the year. This volatility means that near-term quarterly performance may fluctuate depending on weather conditions.
22.
Contrary evidence: The first half of 2026 delivered an unusually strong volume performance that departed from typical seasonal trends. Coca-Cola Hellenic on 5 August 2026 reported that organic volume grew by 7.5% in the first half of the year. This expansion was aided by four additional selling days in the first quarter of 2026 which offset standard seasonal weakness. This temporary benefit shows that structural calendar changes can occasionally overshadow normal seasonal demand patterns.
23.
Complex working capital: Coca-Cola Hellenic must navigate more complex working capital cycles across its newly acquired African territories. Morgan Stanley on 3 February 2026 noted that peer Varun Beverages saw its working capital days rise to 46 days due to higher debtor days. Managing inventory and receivables in emerging markets requires disciplined cash collection protocols to prevent cash lock-ups. This complexity could drag on the consolidated group's short-term free cash flow generation.

Capital Spending Requirements

24.
Greater capital intensity: Standalone CCBA requires a higher level of reinvestment than Coca-Cola Hellenic's historical European business. Jefferies Research on 29 June 2026 modeled CCBA's capital expenditure at approximately 8% of sales for the first few years. This elevated intensity is necessary to upgrade older bottling infrastructure and fuel regional volume expansion across the continent. This elevated spending will dilute the consolidated group's free cash flow margin in the near term.
25.
Intensive legacy investments: Standalone CCBA previously underwent an intense phase of capital deployment under its previous majority owner. Deutsche Bank Research on 7 June 2022 highlighted that The Coca-Cola Company maintained an average capital intensity of approximately 10% over three years. This cumulative investment of USD 1.35 bn focused on improving supply chain capabilities and expanding cold drink equipment. This historical underinvestment in certain regions explains the ongoing need for elevated capital spending.
26.
Stable peer intensity: Peer beverage bottlers in developed European markets operate with a significantly lower and more stable capital spending profile. Coca-Cola Europacific Partners in its half-year results on 4 August 2026 reaffirmed its target of capital spending at approximately 5% of revenue. This lower capital demand allows the developed peer to generate higher free cash flow than emerging market operators. This benchmark demonstrates that CCBA operates with a structurally higher reinvestment requirement.

Aggressive peer expansion: Peer PepsiCo bottler Varun Beverages is executing a highly capital-intensive strategy to establish its own African footprint. This aggressive investment is aimed at expanding production lines in Africa to challenge CCBA's market leadership. This peer activity highlights the highly competitive capital environment in the region.

Leverage and Capital Allocation

28.
Shifted leverage targets: Coca-Cola Hellenic will experience a transition from a net cash position to a more leveraged capital structure. The company in its third quarter results presentation on 4 November 2026 reported a net debt to comparable EBITDA ratio of 0.7 times at the end of 2025. Consolidating CCBA will shift the group toward its medium-term leverage target range of 1.5 to 2.0 times. This leverage increase reduces the company's financial flexibility for further large-scale acquisitions.
29.
Disciplined capital priorities: Coca-Cola Hellenic plans to prioritize internal reinvestment over immediate shareholder returns following the transaction. Bernstein Research on 25 September 2026 reported that group capital expenditure will rise above 7.5% of sales following the acquisition. This prioritisation of capital expenditure represents a slower return on invested capital for shareholders in the near term. This capital allocation strategy shifts focus toward long-term organic growth over immediate cash distributions.
30.
Robust peer discipline: Peer Varun Beverages has demonstrated strong financial performance by maintaining disciplined working capital cycles alongside high capex. Morgan Stanley on 15 April 2024 reported that Varun consistently generated operating cash flows averaging 75% of EBITDA over five years. This disciplined execution has allowed the peer to deliver robust return ratios despite ongoing geographic acquisitions. This precedent suggests that high capital expenditure can coexist with strong shareholder value creation.
31.
Other side of the story: While elevated capital requirements and financing costs will depress near-term free cash flow, the long-term growth profile of the African markets remains highly lucrative. Coca-Cola Hellenic on 5 August 2026 reported that strong organic operating profit growth helped generate solid free cash flow of EUR 216 mm in the first half of 2026. This resilience suggests that the combined entity will quickly deleverage its balance sheet through robust organic cash generation and superior market execution.

CCEP Amatil and Philippines Integration

This acquisition highlights how larger consolidations can drive solid volume recovery and operating margin expansion. Consequently, the integrated group achieved a pro forma return on invested capital of eight percent in its first year, proving the immediate value creation of the transaction.

Such portfolio pruning mirrors the strategies utilized across other major Coca-Cola territories to streamline operations and enhance system efficiency. Ultimately, reorienting the product mix toward core sparkling brands supports higher profitability and drives faster earnings per share growth.
