Outliers worth noting 1 findings

A second structural anomaly appears in the Mercedes Vans division, which maintained an adjusted return on sales of 10.2% in Q2 2026, placing it in the top decile of global light commercial vehicle profitability. This margin level sits approximately 400-500 basis points above the typical European van segment median and demonstrates remarkable resilience despite broader automotive cyclicality and electrification transition costs.

The sustained double-digit performance in a traditionally mid-single-digit margin business represents a significant departure from sector norms. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 6, Section 7] The Hungarian manufacturing cost advantage constitutes a third extreme outlier, with factor costs in the Kecskemét facility running 70% lower than comparable German production facilities. This differential vastly exceeds the typical 30-40% Western-to-Eastern European labor arbitrage observed across diversified industrials and represents one of the widest intra-regional cost spreads in the European automotive manufacturing landscape.

The magnitude of this structural cost advantage underscores both the severity of German manufacturing uncompetitiveness and the scale of potential margin recovery if production successfully migrates eastward. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 9]
