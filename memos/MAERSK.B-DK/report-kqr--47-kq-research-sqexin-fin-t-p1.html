  2. Unit cost reductions consistently lag pricing drops during cyclical resets, exposing carriers to severe negative operating leverage and margin compression.

Section summary
  1. Heavy lease commitments and chartered fleet obligations lock in Maersk's ocean shipping cost base during market downturns.
  2. Asset-light logistics operators DSV and Kuehne + Nagel maintain superior cost flexibility and structural margin protection.

Rigid lease structures

1.
Heavy lease liabilities: Maersk reported total lease liabilities of USD 12,583 million in Q3 2026, down slightly from USD 12,797 million in Q3 2025. This high volume of capitalized leases represents a massive, nondiscretionary payment schedule that the group must service regardless of spot market rates. These structural lease outflows lock in high fixed network costs and severely restrict Maersk's cost-cutting room during cyclical shipping downturns.
2.
Locked lease profiles: Maersk faced total undiscounted lease commitments of USD 17,780 million at the end of fiscal year 2025. This obligation is divided into USD 3,697 million due within one year, USD 7,204 million due between one and five years, and USD 6,879 million due after five years. Because nearly USD 7.0 bn in lease cash flows are fixed into 2030 and beyond, the carrier's capital structure remains highly inflexible during the initial years of the projected shipping oversupply.

This capital-light approach yields high free cash conversion and superior pre-tax return on invested capital compared to asset-heavy carriers like Maersk.

Because Kuehne + Nagel passes through freight rates to its customers, it avoids direct pricing exposure and preserves steady earnings power when container rates collapse.

This strong cash performance is complemented by a net cash balance sheet and high dividend payout ratios. Consequently, the forwarder can maintain generous shareholder distributions during cyclical downturns when asset-heavy container liners are burning cash.

Flexible capacity procurement: Logistics experts observe that asset-light forwarders like DSV and Kuehne + Nagel act as neutral brokers that procure transport capacity dynamically. This operational agility contrasts with Maersk's integrated logistics model, which remains heavily burdened by its own fixed shipping assets. Because forwarders are not forced to fill expensive owned vessels, they consistently capture superior margins during periods of intense carrier price competition.

Opposing structural views

14.
Other side of the story: While Maersk's heavy capitalized leases and long-term charters limit its near-term cost flexibility, its integrated terminal assets and landside solutions provide a unique structural buffer.
