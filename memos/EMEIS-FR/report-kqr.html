Section 3 – Revenue Structures, Tariff Decisions, and Cost Inflation

Section summary
  1. Accommodation fees set by operators constitute the majority of care home revenues, providing crucial pricing power relative to rigid state care tariffs.
  2. Stagnant hospital tariffs in France have forced clinical operators to execute aggressive efficiency measures to offset cumulative labor cost inflation. Net read: Nursing operators have successfully mitigated rising payroll expenses through dynamic pricing adjustments on new resident admissions.
  3. Delayed pricing power and regulatory wage interventions remain key risks that could squeeze operating margins and delay earnings recovery.

Regulated Funding Models

1.
Regulated funding split: Revenue in French nursing homes is highly compartmentalized between state subsidies and resident payments. Emeis notes in its June 26, 2026 report that the accommodation component is paid by the resident, while care and medical allowances are covered by public authorities. In France, accommodation makes up approximately 70% of the total day rate, whereas care and dependency make up 20% and 10% respectively. This high accommodation share means operators must optimize occupancy pricing to restore their margins and grow future earnings.
2.
Domestic revenue distribution: Clariane operates under a similar dual-payment framework that distributes financial exposure between residents and public health insurance. Clariane disclosed in its February 27, 2026 report that the accommodation portion paid by residents accounts for 64% of its medico-social revenue in France. Conversely, the state-funded treatment rate represents about 26% of French revenue. This structure implies that accommodation pricing is the primary lever for offsetting public tariff rigidity and driving cash flow.
3.
Clinical revenue models: Ramsay Sante operates a vastly different clinical business model where state authorities dominate the funding mix. The company's September 17, 2026 presentation revealed that public care services generate 82% of its revenue, while non-regulated hospitality comprises just 6%. This is much lower than the 70% accommodation share seen at nursing peer Emeis. Consequently, Ramsay Sante is extremely vulnerable to public tariff decisions which will dictate its future profitability.
4.
Strict pricing limits: Annual accommodation price increases for existing residents are heavily restricted by French government ministries. Clariane's February 27, 2026 filing notes that while new contracts can be priced freely, existing.

French Tariff Impact

6.
Hospital tariff stagnation: French hospital tariff indexation has failed to match cumulative inflation, creating major headwind for clinical operators. Ramsay Sante reported on September 24, 2026, that the French base indexation was set at just 0.5% for 2025. This flatlined further to 0% for the 2026 tariff year, squeezing clinical margins. This regulatory cap means Ramsay Sante must execute efficiency programs to prevent structural operating profit deterioration.
