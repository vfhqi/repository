The one-line view. Bullish on the business, neutral on the shares at 1,272 pence, medium conviction: a founder-led services company is turning into a software-weighted one as public-sector demand recovers, but the price has paid for the revenue recovery and now depends on a margin recovery first tested on 9 November.

What the company does

Kainos is a Belfast-headquartered digital services and Workday software company listed in London. Digital Services (£241.7 million, 56% of FY2026 revenue) builds digital systems mostly for the United Kingdom government and health service; Workday Services (£107.6 million, 25%) implements Workday's human resources and finance software; Workday Products (£81.7 million, 19%) sells Kainos's own software inside the Workday platform at a 77.8% gross margin. FY2026 revenue grew 17.4% to £431.1 million, adjusted pre-tax profit was £67.1 million at a 15.6% margin, adjusted diluted earnings per share 41.1 pence, net cash £89.1 million, no debt. Market value is 1,620.7 million euros.

A plausible investment thesis

A self-help case riding a demand recovery. The Investment Analyst's memo of 2 October has the founder Brendan Mooney, chief executive again since December 2024, re-setting the company as "a service around the software business" just as United Kingdom public-sector digital procurement came out of a two-year, election-induced trough. The change has reached every layer: bookings up 32%, backlog up 18% to £433.9 million, recurring revenue up 23% to £89.0 million, second-half revenue up 27.6%, and consensus FY2027 earnings per share up 15.3% over twelve months with no broker cut.

The mechanism to earnings per share has three parts: contracted backlog converts to revenue; the 259 contractors (from 69) who cost £18.5 million in FY2026 are replaced by permanent staff, restoring the services margin from the second half of FY2027; and Workday Products scales toward £100 million of recurring revenue by calendar 2026. On the memo's path adjusted earnings per share rise from 41.1 pence to 53.5 pence in FY2027 and 70.0 pence in FY2029, about 19.4% a year compound against consensus 16.7%. The drivers to track are contractor headcount, utilisation against 70% and Products recurring revenue against £100 million.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Investment Analyst's memo of 2 October rated Case Riskiness C: simple, not low-risk at the price. Simple because three drivers and one swing factor, the services gross margin. Not low-risk because the price carries what the company does not: the memo measured a 19% to 25% downside on the multiple alone to levels traded within two years, and said B would be the rating near 1,100 pence. At 1,272 pence, above the memo's 1,250 pence do-not-chase line, the point stands more sharply.

Business quality

Good operator, bounded advantages. Foundations' Robustness B (Investment Analyst, 2 October): framework incumbency, integration switching costs, product intellectual property inside Workday, an exclusive resell channel, 86% of FY2026 revenue from existing customers, return on equity above 40%, 99% cash conversion. Against that: a 15.6% adjusted pre-tax margin below the framework's 20% threshold for solid, an adjusted-to-statutory gap of £9.0 million, and an unhedged currency book with 26% of revenue in dollars. Workday is both the moat and the largest dependency; every channel term is the vendor's to change.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Demand is contracted and policy-backed to 2030: a £150 million DEFRA award, NHS England and DVSA programmes, Healthcare up 55%. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 16 October is B, where the memo had D pinned for Kainos and all sixteen peers on 1 October; its Sell-side Momentum reading of 15 October is B against the memo's C; Technical Momentum is B on 16 October, the shares 3% below their 52-week high.

Cons. Displaced global integrators bid 5% to 10% below others on public frameworks and took Kainos's Digital Outcomes and Specialists share from 10% to 4.6%; United Kingdom active Workday partners rose from 60 to 114 and implementation pricing is down 20% to 25%; the planned disbandment of NHS England threatens 25% to 30% of next year's healthcare business. Kainos is the margin-prioritiser in a market that is not.

The strongest counter-case

The false friend: bookings and revenue surge while profit never follows. FY2026 is the evidence: revenue up 17%, adjusted pre-tax profit up 2%, gross margin down 1.8 points. Contractor dependence is structural because public contracts must be staffed in weeks while a permanent hire takes five months; integrators and a doubled Workday partner base set price; and artificial intelligence hands part of the delivery saving to clients. On that path earnings per share still compound at about 10% a year, so the case becomes mediocre at this price rather than wrong about the company, and a multiple at its five-year median while ten of sixteen peers sit below their fifteenth percentile has 19% to 25% to fall. 9 November decides.
