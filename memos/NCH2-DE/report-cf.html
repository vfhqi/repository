Section summary
  1. external value-chain forces are severely slowing down green hydrogen FIDs, though regulatory quotas and technology transitions present substantial mid-term optionality

Demand

1.
Muted deployed capacity: Deployed green hydrogen capacity in Europe stands below 1 GW, significantly lagging the initial 2025 target of 6 GW
2.
Customer FID reluctance: High development and electricity costs have led to widespread customer hesitation in reaching final investment decisions
3.
Backlog contraction signals: Industry-wide electrolyzer order intake has steadily declined, forcing manufacturers to work through existing backlogs
4.
Project cancellation wave: Industrial customers have began canceling major projects, exemplified by Nel losing a 1 GW capacity reservation

Competition

5.
Peer financing distress: Western peers are losing commercial momentum, with Plug Power facing significant execution and financing constraints
6.
Severe technology impairments: Peer Nel recorded NOK 799 million in technology impairments, representing roughly one-fifth of its market capitalization
7.
Low backlog coverage: Nel enters 2026 with merely 35% of consensus revenues covered by firm backlog execution
8.
Chinese pricing pressure: Asian manufacturers like LONGi are scale-producing low-cost alkaline electrolyzers, exerting 5% to 10% global pricing pressure

Disruption

9.
Pressurized alkaline advantages: Pressurized AWE technology reduces downstream gas compression costs, improving total cost of ownership
10.
High temperature SOEC: Solid oxide electrolysis cell technology can reduce energy consumption by up to 45% when using waste heat
11.
High SOEC hurdles: High-temperature SOEC systems continue to face material degradation and shorter operational durability
12.
Emerging AEM alternative: Anion exchange membrane technology is progressing through prototype stages, combining low-cost non-precious metals with PEM-like responsiveness

Regulation

13.
Binding RED III quotas: European RED III regulations set mandatory RFNBO quotas in transport, forcing industrial users to adopt green hydrogen
14.
Restrictive US guidelines: IRS Section 45V final rules impose stringent hourly-matching starting in 2030, severely damaging green hydrogen economics
15.
Slow funding disbursement: Less than 21% of green hydrogen projects awarded European IPCEI funding have successfully reached final investment decisions
16.
Funding reallocation risks: The Italian PNRR reallocated €640 million away from hard-to-abate hydrogen projects toward biomethane production

Supply

17.
Exclusive manufacturing alliance: Under the Toll Manufacturing Agreement, De Nora exclusively supplies active electrodes and cell assembly until November 2038
18.
Non-exclusive supply risk: De Nora is not contractually restricted from supplying competing electrolyzer developers with its electrode technology
19.
Noble metal dependencies: Electrode fabrication depends on noble metal catalysts sourced from concentrated and highly illiquid global markets
20.
Membrane supplier concentration: Electrolyzer membrane separators are sourced from third-party manufacturers, representing a critical supply chain dependency

Cycle

21.
Industry consolidation stage: The green hydrogen sector has entered a pronounced cyclical consolidation phase where unviable projects are cancelled
22.
Prolonged wait-and-see cycle: Major developers have adopted a wait-and-see posture, delaying equipment orders until regulatory clarity improves
23.
Overcapacity margin squeeze: Excessive expansion of global electrolyzer manufacturing capacity threatens to depress industry-wide pricing power and margins
24.
Legacy project cliff: Manufacturers are work-completing legacy backlogs, facing prolonged sales voids before new project FIDs materialize

Macro

25.
Capital expenditure inflation: Persistent high interest rates and inflation have materially inflated green hydrogen project capex, delaying FIDs
26.
Volatile electricity economics: levelized cost of green hydrogen remains highly sensitive to renewable electricity prices, which currently represent 70% of total production costs
27.
Geopolitical trade barriers: Escalating global trade tensions and potential tariffs threaten to disrupt localized component production and inflate supply chain costs
28.
Fossil fuel price gap: High cost gap between green hydrogen and cheap natural gas remains a primary barrier to voluntary offtaker adoption
29.
Other side of the story:
