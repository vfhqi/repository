EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Galenica operates the densest Swiss retail pharmacy network with over 380 locations, approximately four times larger than its closest competitor, while its Logistics & IT segment operates wholesale and pre-wholesale distribution platforms where one in two medication packs sold in Switzerland passes through its logistics network. The business model leverages high regulatory protection from Swiss domestic focus to shield against direct international e-commerce competition, while healthcare distribution and pharmacy sales remain noncyclical, contract-backed, and driven by clinical necessity.

Sustained high demand for GLP-1 weight loss medications drives strong volume growth but dilutes gross margin percentage, though these sales increase absolute EBIT contributions. The company faces structural margin pressure from specialty drug mix shifts despite maintaining exceptional downstream retail profitability and dominant logistics scale across the Swiss value chain.

Financial performance and capital structure

Galenica generated consolidated net sales of CHF 4,135.60 million in FY2025, representing solid growth of 5.5%, while net sales in H1 2026 grew by 7.1% year-on-year to CHF 2,136.50 million, with group sales growth of 4.0% excluding the Labor Team acquisition contributing 3.1%. The Products & Care segment gross margin fell from 46.50% to 46.20% due to low-margin GLP-1 weight-loss products, as high-priced prescription medication sales expand absolute volumes but dilute the consolidated gross profit percentage. Adjusted EBIT excludes IAS 19, IFRS 16 lease depreciation, and IFRS 3 acquisition amortisation, rising by 11.3% in FY2025 to CHF 234.80 million. Adjusted total assets reached CHF 3,061.12 million in FY2025 after removing lease right-of-use assets, while adjusted net debt stood at CHF 674.80 million in FY2025 after adjusting for lease liabilities, with gearing adjusted for lease liabilities rising from 27% in FY2024 to 44.70% in FY2025.

Operating cash flow conversion reached 79.20% in FY2025, exceeding the 72.80% achieved in FY2024, while free cash flow before M&A reached CHF 188 million in FY2025 against CHF 84.10 million in FY2024. The Board proposed a dividend of CHF 2.50 per share for FY2025, up 8.70%. Financial metrics demonstrate predictable organic revenue trajectory with mid-single digit growth, though specialty mix shifts pressure gross margins, while the balance sheet remains highly structured with manageable leverage and cash conversion sustainably strong near 80% supporting consistent dividend growth and targeted diagnostics M&A.

Industry structure and competitive positioning

The Swiss pharmaceutical market reached CHF 6.1bn at ex-factory prices in 2019, displaying high macroresilience, while the domestic pharmaceutical market expanded at a 2.9% CAGR from 2006 to 2016. The Swiss pharmaceutical wholesale sector is dominated by three primary operators, with Galenica holding a leading 44% wholesale market share, double the share of its next largest rival, while competitor Amedis-ue holds a 22% share and third-placed Voigt maintains 20% of the market. Galenica's subsidiary Alloga controls 60% of prewholesale volumes, outperforming Voigt's 24% share, while approximately 70% of Swiss pharmacies operate as independent businesses, with Galenica owning a 35% share of retail pharmacy sales. Swiss pharmacies achieve gross margins of c.20%, whereas pure-play European wholesalers operate at c.11%, with Galenica's integrated business model delivering blended gross margins in the mid-20s due to retail exposure. The industry exhibits high consolidation and defensive characteristics, growing slightly above GDP, though regulatory price cuts pressure wholesale margins and create asymmetric profit distribution favoring downstream retail.

Value chain integration and margin dynamics

Galenica controls the densest Swiss retail pharmacy network with over 380 owned locations, manages both prewholesale and wholesale distribution across Switzerland, with Alloga holding a leading 60% Swiss market share in pre-wholesale logistics. Profitability is highly asymmetric, with margins accruing downstream at the retail interface, as Products & Care gross margin reached 47.5% compared to only 9.0% in Logistics & IT, with Products & Care adjusted ROS at 9.6% versus 1.9% for Logistics & IT.

The diagnostics business delivers attractive operating margins of approximately 15% EBIT. Swiss drug pricing remains highly regulated, preventing independent commercial retail price adjustments, while FOPH reviews SL drug prices in three-year cycles to mandate price cuts. Galenica occupies a dominant and highly integrated position across the Swiss pharmaceutical value chain, capturing major logistics and retail market shares, though profit margins are heavily skewed toward downstream retail and diagnostic activities while regulated pricing and specialty drugs limit commercial pricing flexibility.

Customer base and switching barriers

The wholesale business supplies more than 8,500 healthcare providers across Switzerland, reducing singlecustomer concentration risks, while downstream retail pharmacy operations serve millions of Swiss consumers through the country's densest retail pharmacy network. The Coop Vitality joint venture agreement runs until at least 2030, locking in wholesale volume, automatically renewing for two-year terms unless terminated one year before expiration, with Coop Vitality pharmacies contractually obligated to source retail pharmaceutical inventories primarily from Galexis. Over 90% of Pharmapool medical practice customers are integrated into the proprietary DispoCura ordering platform, which connects directly to medical practices' internal IT, preventing customers from changing wholesale suppliers easily. The Documedis digital platform is integrated into more than 300 software systems across Swiss pharmacies and hospitals, with clinical decision support checks exceeding 553 million in 2025, cementing deep operational workflows. The company possesses a highly diversified domestic customer base secured by deep IT integration, though regulated pricing restricts retail-level pricing leverage.

Supplier dynamics and distribution model

Galenica coordinates procurement with over 1,000 suppliers to secure basic drug volumes throughout the domestic market, handling approximately 100,000 referenced products across pharma and medical devices. High-priced medications like GLP-1s are single-sourced from specific big pharma patent holders, with monopolistic patent structures restricting Galenica's ability to negotiate favourable purchase terms or discounts. Alloga acts as the primary gateway for over 100 international pharmaceutical partners entering Switzerland, controlling a leading 47% market share in the Swiss pre-wholesale logistics sector. Over 700 medical products remain structurally unavailable in Switzerland due to highly centralized global manufacturing, with Galenica collaborating with Sandoz on the Safety Stock Initiative to build generic reserves. Galexis maintains a leading 44% market share in the Swiss pharmaceutical wholesale channel, delivering daily to 80% of Swiss pharmacies and drugstores from its logistics centers. The business leverages dominant Swiss logistics scale to secure key supplier partnerships, although single-sourced specialty drugs and state-mandated pricing restrict variable margin flexibility.

Competitive landscape and vertical integration threats

Galexis commands a 44% market share in the domestic wholesale channel, with competitor Amedis-ue holding 22% and Voigt holding 20%, while Galenica commands the densest pharmacy network with over 380 direct locations and a leading 35% market share by pharmacy sales, with nearest retail competitor BENU holding only 5% market share by pharmacy count. Physical Swiss pharmacies focus on service expansion and generally avoid aggressive retail price wars, as Swiss drug pricing is heavily regulated by the state, reducing retaillevel price competition, though state price reviews generated cumulative healthcare savings of CHF 450 million.

Direct-to-consumer vertical integration by pharmaceutical manufacturers remains highly unviable in Switzerland, as no individual manufacturer can duplicate Galenica's end-to-end wholesale and pre-wholesale logistics network. The liberalisation of online OTC medicine sales poses a gradual long-term disintermediation risk, though management estimates that only 13% of pharmacy-related sales would be affected by OTC liberalisation, with public consultations beginning and implementation expected no earlier than 2029.

The Swiss pharmacy landscape remains a highly consolidated triopoly in distribution with Galenica holding unmatched downstream retail and wholesale scale, while competitive conduct focuses on infrastructure automation and targeted diagnostic M&A rather than aggressive margin-destructive retail price wars, and vertical integration threats from upstream manufacturers or downstream insurers carry low near-term credibility due to massive capital barriers.

Operator calibre and strategic execution

Marc Werner has directed Galenica's integrated network strategy since his appointment in 2020, with Julian Fiessinger successfully promoted to Chief Financial Officer in January 2025, while management decided in March 2026 to close the unprofitable pharmaceutical production division of Bichsel, with elimination of the deficit-ridden production improving adjusted EBIT by CHF 3 million annually starting in 2027. The CHF 238.4 million acquisition of Labor Team Group in September 2025 represents a major strategic milestone, with the diagnostics transaction progressing smoothly with strong cultural integration and immediate sales contributions.

Management raised its centenary 2027 adjusted EBIT target from CHF 250 million to CHF 270 million, with Products and Care return on sales targeted to exceed 10% by 2027. Galenica commands a leading 35% market share of Swiss retail pharmacy sales with 381 owned pharmacies and 167 partner locations, while Galexis secures a leading 44% market share in the domestic pharmaceutical wholesale segment, with pre-wholesale subsidiary Alloga controlling a dominant 60% market share.

Business operations are highly insulated from economic downturns due to non-cyclical healthcare demand, with operating cash flow rising to CHF 245.7 million in 2025 driven by improved supplier payment terms, while free cash flow reached CHF 188 million in 2025, easily financing targeted M&A. CEO Marc Werner has demonstrated exceptional capital discipline through high-return diagnostics M&A and swift rationalisation of unprofitable production assets, while Galenica's foundational quality is anchored by unmatched domestic scale and regulatory protection generating highly predictable through-cycle cash flows.

Long-term evolution and disruption forces

Capital-intensive logistics networks and strict safety regulations restrict direct competitive entry into Swiss pharmaceutical distribution, with pre-wholesale division Alloga commanding a dominant 60% market share creating a massive physical barrier. Demographic shifts and high-priced pharmaceutical innovations support market growth of 3% to 5% annually, with the domestic pharmaceutical market historically expanding above the average Swiss GDP growth rate. Swiss mail-order over-the-counter medicine liberalization is projected no earlier than 2029 or 2030 if approved, with management estimating that OTC liberalization would directly affect a maximum of approximately 13% of pharmacy sales. The acquisition of Labor Team Group establishes Galenica in Switzerland's CHF 3.1 billion laboratory market, with Labor Team representing the fourth-largest private laboratory commanding an estimated 8% Swiss market share, while collaboration with Galexis enables Galenica to offer integrated distribution and laboratory services to physicians. The Swiss pharmacy landscape faces orderly digital evolution while adjacent clinical diagnostics M&A and home care expansion secure defensive long-term growth, with high entry barriers and stable demographic-driven demand supporting predictable expansion trajectories. - Earnings Calls Foundation verdict: STRONG – Galenica's business foundation is strong due to its unassailable domestic scale monopoly across pre-wholesale (60%), wholesale (44%), and retail (35%) market shares, reinforced by deep digital integration barriers, defensive regulatory protections, and predictable through-cycle cash generation exceeding

79% conversion, which collectively create a structural moat that overwhelms the margin pressures from specialty drug mix shifts and regulatory pricing reviews.

Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Three statistical outliers and operational anomalies emerge from the comprehensive analysis of Galenica's operations, each representing significant deviations from peer norms or historical patterns that warrant close monitoring. The most pronounced outlier appears in the company's leverage trajectory, where gearing adjusted for lease liabilities surged from 27% in FY2024 to 44.70% in FY2025, representing a 65% year-over-year increase in a single fiscal period.

This dramatic shift occurred despite Galenica's otherwise conservative financial profile and management's stated commitment to maintaining approximately 2x net debt to EBITDA over the medium term. The spike coincides with adjusted net debt reaching CHF 674.80 million and net cash outflow from business combinations rising to CHF 275.40 million in FY2025, primarily driven by the Labor Team acquisition. While large M&A transactions naturally elevate leverage temporarily, the magnitude and velocity of this shift stand out as highly unusual for a defensive healthcare distributor operating in a stable domestic market.

The acceleration appears disconnected from underlying operational deterioration, suggesting either aggressive strategic repositioning or temporary balance sheet stress that management expects to normalize through organic deleveraging. This leverage anomaly carries neutral-to-positive sentiment given the strategic nature of the diagnostics acquisition, though it represents a clear departure from the company's historically conservative

A second operational outlier manifests in customer satisfaction metrics, where the rNPS target achievement rate collapsed from 95.5% in 2024 to 78.8% in 2025, representing a 17.5 percentage point decline in a single year. This deterioration is particularly striking given Galenica's otherwise strong operational execution across financial metrics and its dominant market position. Management responded with heavy investments in staff training in 2025 to reverse the decline, acknowledging the severity of the issue.

The sudden drop in customer satisfaction stands in sharp contrast to the company's expanding network density, growing digital user base, and rising service fee revenues, creating an unexplained disconnect between quantitative business growth and qualitative customer sentiment. No comparable peer experiences this magnitude of satisfaction erosion while simultaneously reporting strong volume growth and margin stability.

The outlier carries negative sentiment as sustained customer dissatisfaction typically serves as a leading indicator of future churn, pricing pressure, or competitive vulnerability, particularly in a market where switching costs depend heavily on service quality and convenience. The anomaly remains partially unexplained despite management's attribution to operational pressures, suggesting deeper cultural or execution challenges that may not yet be reflected in financial results.

The third outlier emerges in working capital dynamics, where DocMorris AG exhibits a negative EBITDA margin of -2.20%, placing it in the bottom ~2% of European pharmaceutical distributors and making it a clear statistical anomaly among Galenica's competitive peer set. This extreme underperformance stems from restructuring headwinds and Germany market expansion costs, but the magnitude of loss-making operations for a scaled digital pharmacy operator defies industry economics where even low-margin wholesale businesses typically generate positive mid-single-digit EBITDA margins at scale.

DocMorris represents a critical peer given its role as Galenica's primary digital competitor in the Swiss market, yet its financial profile suggests either fundamentally broken unit economics or an unsustainable growth-at-all-costs strategy that cannot persist. The outlier carries ambiguous sentiment for Galenica: on one hand, a weakened digital competitor reduces competitive intensity in the Swiss mail-order channel, but on the other hand, DocMorris's struggles may signal broader structural challenges in online pharmaceutical distribution that could affect Galenica's Redcare joint venture or future digital expansion plans.
