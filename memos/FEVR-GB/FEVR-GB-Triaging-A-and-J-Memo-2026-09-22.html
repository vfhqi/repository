[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Fevertree is an oversold, category-leading premium mixer brand whose self-made cause for concern is starting to clear: it licensed its largest region to Molson Coors in January 2025 while its margins sat at a trough, and the H1 2026 results of 10 September 2026 gave the first hard evidence that the new model grows. At 873p the shares pay 19.3x 24-month-forward earnings, the 7th percentile of the stock's own five-year range, for a business at its trough margin. On our modal path the shares return about 21 percent a year over eighteen months, and the case turns on whether US consumer takeaway keeps growing above 10 percent through 2027 under a partner the company does not control.

  • The change is internal, the largest in the company's history, and it is already executed.
    • Two decisions define it, and both are on the books rather than in a plan.
    • The US business was licensed to Molson Coors on 30 January 2025: the trading subsidiary was sold for £20.4m, Molson Coors took an 8.5 percent stake for £71m, guaranteed minimum US profits from 2026 to 2030, took US working capital and overheads onto its own books, and brought 400 distributors and 500,000 accounts.
    • Onshoring of US production follows, cans from autumn 2026 and glass in 2027, removing about £10.8m a year of transatlantic freight (8.2 percent of 2025 US sales) and £5m to £7m a year of tariffs from the joint operation's cost base.
    • The company has become a capital returner: £100m of buybacks in 2025 and £60m in 2026, about 16 percent of the market capitalisation at 873p over two years, with the share count falling about 7 percent since the start of 2025.
    • Five of the twelve internal forces are strong on the framework's screen, more than the three the rule asks for, and the red-flag check finds no caretaker chief executive, no debt-funded buyback and no transformation that exists only in presentations.
  • The external forces do not help; they net out close to neutral and lean against the core occasion.
    • The company's growth is share gain and distribution gain, not category pull.
    • UK gin is in structural decline, the UK on-trade is about 35 percent below 2019, and US spirits value fell 0.6 percent year on year in September 2026
      • 53 percent of sales are still tonic.
    • Against that, input costs have swung from a severe headwind to a tailwind (glass energy 100 percent hedged for 2026, 66 percent for 2027, 33 percent for 2028), and the company's own cycle troughed in 2023 and is turning up.
    • Regulation and the consumer are headwinds now: a £2.6m H1 2026 provision for the UK packaging levy (£5.4m cumulative), a Deposit Return Scheme in October 2027, and UK inflation back at 3.1 percent in August 2026.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up, on one half-year of evidence.
    • All four key outputs are improving against the last three years.
    • Brand revenue growth moved from +1.1 percent (FY2024) and +1.8 percent (FY2025) to +8 percent constant currency in H1 2026
      • the US grew 11 percent, and US off-trade scanner growth accelerated from 6 percent in Q1 to 11 percent in Q2 and 16 percent in July and August 2026.
    • The adjusted EBITDA margin troughed at 8.4 percent in 2023, recovered to 13.7 percent in 2024, dipped to 11.3 percent in 2025 on transition costs, and was 10.9 percent in H1 2026, up 20 basis points
      • consensus has 12.5 percent for FY2026, 15.0 percent for FY2027 and 16.6 percent for FY2028.
    • Working capital fell from 28.5 percent of revenue in 2023 to 16.7 percent in 2025, the group is debt-free, and consensus free cash flow is £38.8m for FY2026 and £60.1m for FY2027 against adjusted net income of £29.6m and £40.8m.
    • The sell-side has not moved: consensus earnings per share are +0.9 to +2.3 percent over three months, and those raises are the buyback, not the operating lines.
  • The change has travelled five of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and stopped at the sell-side.
    • It shows in the company's choices, its actions, its key operating metrics (US velocity, a beyond-tonic mix of 47 percent, working capital) and one half-year of financial statements
    • the estimates are flat.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of 24.1p in FY2025 rising to 28.5p in FY2026, 37p in FY2027 and 46p in FY2028 on our path, 24 percent a year compounded, against consensus of 28.8p, 39.4p and 49.8p.
    • The growth is of the coveted kind: revenue about 8 percent a year and the margin recovering from 11.3 percent toward 15.5 percent, with small help from tax and the share count
      • the parts multiply to the claim.
    • It is above the framework's 12 to 20 percent band, in the zone that is fine but riskier, because the recovery is front-loaded into FY2027, the onshoring and marketing-normalisation year.
  • What it costs on the valuation: 873p is 19.3x 24-month-forward consensus earnings of 45.2p, cheap against the stock's own history and dear against every peer set.
    • The 7th percentile of the five-year range (15.39x to 51.02x, median 25.70x), the 17th percentile of two years (median 22.15x), and below the ten-year 'crash' band of 20.5x.
    • A premium to the beer peers (10.1x to 15.1x), to global staples (about 17x) and to the spirits companies whose end market it shares (a 50 to 100 percent premium)
      • on a one-year-forward footing the same shares are 24x to 25x.
  • The return clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months at a blended multiple and falls to a C over three years.
    • At each horizon we value the shares at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it
    • the multiple is a 60/40 blend of a cleared branch at 25x and an uncleared branch at 16x, 21.4x.
    • Eighteen months: 21.4x on 53.3p is 1,141p, plus about 25p of dividends, 1,166p, +33.5 percent, 21.3 percent a year, the framework's B anchor.
    • Three years: 21.4x on 60.8p is 1,301p, plus about 57p, 1,358p, +55.5 percent, 15.9 percent a year, a C.
    • Earnings growth carries the return (+22 percent of the +33 percent over eighteen months)
      • the re-rating is the swing (+7 percent at the blend, +26 percent on the cleared branch, -20 percent on the uncleared)
      • the cash return is small and predictable.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (higher amortisation, a 28 percent tax rate, a paused buyback) takes FY2028 earnings to 41.1p and the eighteen-month return to 13.2 percent a year, a C, and this Building Block with it.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', with 'Major Corporate Change' as the mechanism of the clearing.
    • The concern is an investment phase in the US (a 6.2 percent US EBITDA margin in H1 2026 on doubled marketing) layered on a shrinking UK core
    • the clearing evidence is two months of scanner data on about half of one region.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the change to the operating metrics to the first financial statements and to a return that clears the bar at the nearer horizon.
    • It breaks if the 16 percent scanner rate is a campaign spike on the visible half of the US while the on-trade half is commoditised by beer representatives
    • the diagnostic reads the right way today, because consumer takeaway (16 percent) is running ahead of shipments (11 percent), the reverse of pipeline fill.

  • The case therefore is a recovery in an oversold quality brand that is compelling on price and on the direction of the evidence, and not yet compelling on the length of the evidence

    • a starter position is a bet on the Q4 2026 scanner print.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material on all three Elements and the parts combine as a chain that holds from the change to the outputs to the return: the internal forces are the largest in the company's history and executed (B), the outputs are improving on all four metrics with growth of the coveted kind at 24 percent a year (B), and the modal return over eighteen months clears the bar at a blended multiple (B), with the weakest link the three-year return of about 16 percent once the recovery is done

    • not A because the external forces do not help, the delivered output is one half-year, and the three-year return is a C
    • not C because every leg of the chain has dated evidence behind it.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the visible US growth is a false friend, and a 19x multiple is not cheap for what is left.
    • The 16 percent July and August scanner rate is pipeline fill and marketing-bought trial that coincides with the first national television campaign, on the half of the US business that can be seen.
    • The Molson Coors profit-share caps the group EBITDA margin near 17 percent permanently, so the 30 percent margins of history and the multiples paid for them are gone for good.
    • The UK gin decline erodes the profit pool that funds everything, whatever the weather does in a given half.
    • A 15 percent-margin, mid-single-digit grower with four company-specific concerns and a partner-controlled engine is not cheap at 19x when its beer peers are at 10x to 15x and staples at about 17x.
  • What we concede: the margin cap (our path stops at 15.5 percent, not the 30 percent of history), the UK (we model it flat at best), and the thinness of the evidence (one half-year).
  • Why we hold our view: the primary false-friend diagnostic reads the other way today.
    • The 16 percent is scanner data, consumer takeaway, not shipments
      • it exceeds the +11 percent of H1 shipments, so depletions run ahead of sell-in, the opposite of pipeline fill.
    • The guaranteed minimum profits make the near-term US contribution contractual, and the licence moved working capital and overhead risk to the partner, so the downside case is a stagnating royalty and not a broken balance sheet (net cash £68m, no debt).
    • At 19.3x the multiple already discounts a business at its trough margin
      • the market gives the cleared branch roughly one chance in three and we give it three in five, and that disagreement is the return.
  • Where the case against would win: a quarter in which US shipment growth exceeds scanner growth by more than 5 points, or scanner growth reverting below 10 percent by Q1 2027.
    • Either would turn this Building Block to C.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a recovery case in an oversold quality brand, with a self-help mechanism at its centre: the framework's 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' pattern, with 'Major Corporate Change' as the mechanism of the clearing.
  • Fevertree is the global number one premium mixer, seven times the size of its nearest premium rival, sold to 3.6 million UK households and now to 500,000 US accounts, debt-free, with capital expenditure at 1.5 percent of sales
    • its economics broke between 2022 and 2023 when glass, energy and freight inflation took the adjusted EBITDA margin from 29.6 percent to 8.4 percent and its share price from a 2018 peak of 3,956p to a 52-week low of 711p.
  • In January 2025 it licensed its US business to Molson Coors, which took an 8.5 percent stake for £71m, guaranteed minimum US profits from 2026 to 2030, took the US working capital and overhead onto its own books, and is onshoring production by 2027 to remove about £10.8m a year of transatlantic freight and £5m to £7m of tariffs
    • alongside, the company has returned £160m to shareholders over 2025 and 2026 and moved 47 percent of its sales beyond tonic.
  • The H1 2026 results of 10 September 2026 gave the first hard evidence that the change is working: brand revenue +8 percent at constant currency, the US +11 percent with off-trade scanner growth accelerating from 6 percent in Q1 to 16 percent in July and August, the UK back to +3 percent, adjusted EBITDA +9 percent.
  • The change has travelled through the company's choices and actions into its operating metrics and one half-year of financial statements, but not into the sell-side's estimates, which are flat.
  • The case is compelling on price and on the direction of the evidence, and not yet compelling on the length of the evidence: two months of scanner data covering half of one region, against a partner-run engine in a spirits market that is shrinking.
  • At 873p, 19.3x 24-month-forward earnings and the 7th percentile of the stock's own five-year range, it clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months on our modal path at a blended multiple (21.3 percent a year), and falls to 15.9 percent a year over three years once the recovery is done.
  • What we are betting on, in one line: that Molson Coors' 500,000 accounts and a doubled marketing budget keep US consumer takeaway growing above 10 percent through 2027 while onshoring lifts the group margin toward 15 percent, and that a market paying 19x for the trough pays 21x to 25x for the recovery.

Business
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The venue cash-margin mechanism
    • A bar earns more cash on a premium mixer than on a mainstream one, which is why the trade keeps the brand even under beer distribution.
    • It bears on the case being right.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The capital-light model
    • Production is outsourced and capital expenditure is a small share of sales, so recovered margin becomes cash and buybacks.

    • Costs excluded from adjusted figures have recurred for three years, and they widen the gap between the earnings the market prices and the earnings the company reports.
