Section 2 – Valuation Dynamics of Historical and Pipeline LOE Precedents

Section summary
  1. Large pharmaceutical companies facing blockbuster losses of exclusivity typically experience severe multiple contraction several years prior to the patent expiry.
  2. Valuations of historical precedents such as AbbVie and Pfizer contracted to single-digit forward price-to- earnings multiples before pipeline successes enabled subsequent re-ratings.
  3. Successor launches and strategic M&A acquisitions are the primary drivers of multiple expansion for biopharmaceutical peers.
  4. Mega-acquisitions can introduce significant financial leverage and integration risks that constrain multiples.
  5. Sanofi has avoided large defensive M&A in favor of disciplined bolt-on transactions and pipeline replenishment.
  6. Pipeline execution across immunology, vaccines, and rare diseases remains key to narrow Sanofi's peer discount.

Precedent Historical Valuation Troughs

1.
Premature valuation contraction: Large biopharmaceutical companies facing major patent cliffs historically experience valuation pressure long before the actual loss of exclusivity. Morgan Stanley reported on November 23, 2026, that next-twelve-month price-to-earnings multiples typically trough six to seven years ahead of major losses of exclusivity. This long lead time reflects the market discounting future revenue drops well before the product actually loses its market exclusivity. Consequently, companies must actively manage their portfolios early to avoid a prolonged period of depressed multiples ahead of key patent expiries.
2.
Concentrated portfolio risks: Pharmaceutical companies with extreme revenue concentration often see their forward price-to-earnings multiples fall into the single digits as major expiries approach. Jefferies noted on October 26, 2025, that companies within five years of a significant patent cliff historically trade at deep discounts to the sector average. This trend has been particularly visible for large-cap peers like Merck & Co, AbbVie, and GlaxoSmithKline, which have all traded in the single digits to low teens on a one-year-forward price-to-earnings basis. In total, this comprehensive analysis identified eight distinct historical precedents across eight major biopharmaceutical companies using one hundred and two relevant industry and company sources.
3.
Significant multiple contraction: AbbVie serves as a primary case study of severe multiple contraction followed by a strong pipeline-driven recovery. Jefferies reported on November 30, 2025, that AbbVie's forward multiple contracted significantly during the five-year window prior to the 2023 loss of exclusivity for Humira. The company's multiple dropped into the single digits as investors feared a massive revenue cliff from biosimilars. However, the launch of successor immunology products helped the company navigate this transition and eventually stabilize its long-term earnings growth profile.
4.
Successful product transition: AbbVie successfully offset the erosion of Humira by rapidly scaling its next- generation immunology assets, Skyrizi and Rinvoq. Morgan Stanley reported on July 31, 2026, that AbbVie's total immunology sales reached USD 8.8 bn in the second quarter of 2026, driven by these two successor drugs. In contrast, Humira sales declined by 38.6% to USD 688 mm in the first quarter of 2026. This massive commercial scale-up allowed AbbVie's price-to-earnings multiple to re-rate back to seventeen times on forward earnings estimates.

Valuation Trajectories of AbbVie and Pfizer

5.

Morgan Stanley reported on October 10, 2025, that Pfizer's forward price-toearnings multiple troughed in early 2005, which was six years ahead of the anticipated Lipitor patent cliff. Pfizer attempted to mitigate this impending decline through major strategic corporate moves, including its massive sixty-eight billion dollar acquisition of Wyeth in October 2009. This shows that mega-cap acquisitions are often used as defensive strategic shields to replace lost blockbuster revenues.
6.

Pfizer reported in its second-quarter 2012 earnings call that it suffered a USD 1.8 bn negative revenue impact primarily due to the loss of exclusivity of Lipitor in most major global markets. Management worked to maximize the inline portfolio by reorganizing its business units and utilizing post-LOE switches in emerging markets. This commercial effort, however, was unable to prevent significant pressure on operating margins immediately following the patent expiry.
7.
Sustained valuation pressure: Bristol Myers Squibb has faced intense valuation discounts due to concurrent losses of exclusivity on its legacy oncology and cardiovascular blockbusters. Bernstein Research reported on October 29, 2026, that Bristol Myers Squibb traded at an adjusted forward price-to-earnings multiple of 9.4 times for 2027. The company is absorbing significant generic erosion from Revlimid and is preparing for the imminent loss of Eliquis exclusivity. This shows how multi-product concentration depresses multiples below peer averages until new pipeline assets are commercially validated.
8.
Steep European erosion: Bristol Myers Squibb expects severe revenue step-downs as its blockbuster assets lose patent protection in international regions. Jefferies reported on October 29, 2026, that Eliquis sales are projected to experience a steep step-down of USD 1.5 bn to USD 2.0 bn in 2027. This expected decline is driven by the expiration of composition-of-matter patents in Europe in late 2026. Consequently, the company is highly dependent on newly launched assets to stabilize its long-term operating margins and support a future rerating.

Section 4 – Sanofi's Valuation Multiples and Broker Methodologies

Section summary
