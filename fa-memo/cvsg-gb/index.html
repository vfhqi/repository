The operating drivers behind the inputs

UK practices (about 80 percent of revenue). - Price 4.0, 3.5, 3.3, 3.2 and 3.1 percent. - Volume and mix −2.5, −1.5, −0.7, −0.2 and 0.0 percent. - LFL therefore 1.4, 2.0, 2.6, 3.0 and 3.1 percent. - Cost base (FY2026, derived): staff about £305m, drugs and consumables about £120m, other about £41m. Rent is below EBITDA under IFRS 16. - Staff inflation is 3.6, 3.7, 3.5, 3.4 and 3.3 percent. The April 2027 National Living Wage step is +3.9 percent to £13.21, and vet salary inflation is easing as vacancies fall to 5.8 percent. - Productivity is 0.6 percent of staff cost in FY2027, 0.5 percent in FY2028 and 0.3 percent thereafter. The rostering gain was largely banked in FY2026, when clinical staff fell to 34.3 percent of revenue. - Drug and other cost inflation is 3 percent. - Lost volume drops through at about 50 percent once rosters flex. - R&D credit £12.5m a year, against £15.7m in FY2026, which included a £6.6m release of prior-year provisions. - UK acquisitions: £15m in FY2027 (the two-site, nine-vet practice; completion assumed about mid-H1), then £12m a year, at about 8x EBITDA and 25 percent margins.

Australia. - Organic growth 2.5 percent. Undisclosed; Jefferies says it is not clearly stronger than the UK's. - Rollover of FY2026 deals about £13m of revenue. - New deals of about £33m initial cash in FY2027 (including the £8.9m completed or signed after the year end), then £30m a year. - Multiples 6.2x, 6.6x, 7.0x, 7.3x and 7.5x on total consideration, of which 20 percent is deferred. Margins 24 percent on new deals and about 25 percent for the segment. - The run-rate of about £100m annualised revenue reported for late 2026 is consistent with this.

Laboratories. Revenue +7 percent in FY2027 and +6 percent a year after (volumes +4 to 5 percent plus price), at an incremental margin of 45 percent.

Animed Direct. Revenue +5 percent a year. Margin 3.5 percent in FY2027, recovering to 4.5 percent by FY2029.

Central costs. +6, +5, +5, +4 and +4 percent: the change of chief executive, the Chief Client Officer and executive pay.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.

  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
