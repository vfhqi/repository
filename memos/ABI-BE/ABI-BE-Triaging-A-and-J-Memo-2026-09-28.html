[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. AB InBev is a deleveraging compounder in a mature category, not a recovery and not a demand inflection. At 68.3 EUR (76.89 USD on 24-Sep-26) the shares already pay the stock's own five-year median multiple for the mechanical part of that compounding, so the modal total return is about 11 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case is sound, middling and fairly priced.

  • The change is internal, mature and financial in origin.
    • The balance sheet has been repaired, and the cash that used to go to creditors now goes to shareholders.
    • Net debt to normalised EBITDA fell from 3.96x at FY21 to 2.87x at FY25 and 2.86x at 30-Jun-26 (company basis)
      • net debt fell 15.3 billion USD between FY21 and FY25.
    • A 6 billion USD programmatic buyback was approved on 29-Oct-25 to run over 24 months
      • 1.9 billion USD of it was done by 24-Jul-26.
      • The FY2025 dividend was raised 15 percent to 1.15 EUR and the policy is progressive.
    • The strategic pivot from mergers to organic category growth dates from 2021.
      • The buyback is the one internal force that is new in the last twelve months
      • guidance has not been raised in five years.
  • The external forces net to a headwind on volume, and that headwind binds.
    • It caps the revenue line the internal forces need.
    • Global beer volumes are 5 percent below 2019 and the company's own organic volumes 1 percent below.
      • China's volumes fell 9.7 percent in 2Q26 as consumers left the on-premise channel.
      • US mainstream brands are falling 8 to 13 percent a year.
    • The one demand tailwind is regional: Brazil beer volumes grew 5.0 percent in 2Q26 with revenue per hectolitre up 5.3 percent, and Mexico and Colombia set volume records.
      • Latin America is about 45 percent of group revenue and the majority of profit.
    • So the group's volume line is flat (+0.9 percent in 2Q26), and the growth is price and mix: revenue per hectolitre has risen 4 to 5 percent a year for three years.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving on every line the framework names.
    • Organic revenue growth stepped up from +2.0 percent in FY2025 to +5.7 percent in H1-26
    • the normalised EBITDA margin rose 101 basis points in FY2025 to 35.8 percent with a further step guided for H2-26 as transactional hedges turn
    • free cash flow was 11.3 billion USD in FY2025, 19 percent of revenue
    • leverage is falling while the buyback runs.
    • H1-26 underlying earnings per share grew about 22 percent and free cash flow rose 2.5 billion USD to 3.9 billion USD.
  • Only about half of that earnings growth comes from the lines investors extrapolate.
    • Revenue growth and margin expansion contribute about 6.2 points a year of the 12.9
    • falling net interest, a shrinking minority line, translation in FY2026 and the share count contribute the other 6.4 points.
    • The framework weights revenue and margin above the below-EBIT lines, because investors extrapolate the first and not the second.
      • On the coveted lines alone the earnings compound at about 6 percent a year, below the 12 to 20 percent band.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The choice was made in 2021
    • the actions (BEES, the megabrands, deleveraging, the buyback) are visible
    • the operating metrics and the financial statements show it
    • and the sell-side has revised FY2026 earnings per share up 9.9 percent in the twelve months to 18-Sep-26.
    • The layer where the case is weakest is not a missing layer but a missing driver: volume, which the chain does not carry outside Latin America.
  • What we think it earns: underlying earnings per share of 3.73 USD in FY2025 rising to 4.45 USD in FY2026, 4.88 USD in FY2027 and 5.35 USD in FY2028 on our path, which is 12.8 percent a year from FY2025 and 9.7 percent a year from FY2026.
    • Our path sits on the consensus median for FY2026 (4.46 USD) and 1 and 3 percent below it for FY2027 and FY2028 (4.94 and 5.54 USD), because we assume a 36.7 percent normalised EBITDA margin by FY2028 against the consensus 37.2 percent, on the China drag and the US reinvestment.
  • What it costs: 14.57x 24-month-forward earnings on 18-Sep-26, the 34th percentile of the stock's own five years (range 11.54x to 17.73x, median 15.07x), and a 25 percent premium to Heineken (11.8x) and Carlsberg (11.6x), the two peers that share its cohort, which sit at the 2nd and 8th percentiles of their own ten-year histories.
    • So the shares are a little cheap against their own history and dear against the only two brewers in the set.
      • The market is paying a median multiple for a mechanical compounding at a mid-single-digit operating rate.
  • The return does not clear the bar.
    • Valuing the shares at 15.0x (the five-year median) on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the total return is 17.4 percent over eighteen months (11.3 percent a year) and 34.7 percent over three years (10.4 percent a year): a D on the framework's anchors.
    • Earnings growth carries the return (about 8 to 8.5 percent a year on the 24-month-forward line).
      • The re-rating from 14.7x to 15.0x is worth about 2 percent in total.
      • The dividend adds about 2 percent a year.
    • At the arithmetic blend of the three multiple branches (15.4x) the return is 13.2 percent a year over eighteen months, the bottom of C, and 11.4 percent over three years.
      • At the ten-year 75th percentile of 17.3x it would be 22.2 percent a year over eighteen months, a B
      • we put the odds of that branch at about 30 percent.
  • The earnings path is exposed to the drop-through ratio in both directions.
    • A plausibly worse ratio from EBITDA to earnings per share (net interest flat at about 4,150 USDm, a 28 percent tax rate, minorities growing about 6 percent a year, the buyback halved after 2026) takes FY2028 earnings per share to about 4.84 USD, the eighteen-month return to 4.3 percent a year, the 'Modal return' to F and this Building Block to D.
    • The 2Q26 print, an 8.5 percent earnings-per-share beat on a 1.9 percent EBITDA beat, shows the ratio surprises the other way too.
      • The sensitivity is symmetric and large.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Compounder', a mechanically mis-modelled, proven upgrader of earnings per share, at a C.
    • The consensus model has under-estimated the lines below EBITDA and the timing of the hedge roll-off, and the company has met or beaten consensus in 15 of 17 quarters
    • but the sell-side has now caught up on interest and minorities, and the company holds rather than raises its guidance.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the 2021 choice through to the sell-side's estimates on the earnings leg, because every component (price, hedges, interest, minorities, share count) is evidenced and mostly arithmetic.
    • It breaks at the multiple, because a 20 percent return needs a re-rating to the ten-year 75th percentile that depends on the sector's appetite and on China stabilising, neither of which the company controls.

  • The case is therefore a sound, middling case at a fair price: real, mature, inside the earnings band, and not compelling at 68.3 EUR.

    • The price at which the modal path clears the 20 percent bar is about 68.7 USD on FactSet's basis, about 60 EUR at the 24-Sep-26 cross rate, roughly 11 percent below the price and just below the 200-day average.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and has travelled the whole chain (a balance-sheet repair that has become a capital-return programme, real price rises, a premium and non-beer mix shift, all visible in earnings per share growing inside the target band and in twelve months of upward revisions), but it is not material enough: the external forces net to a headwind on volume, the operating lines give about 6 percent a year of earnings growth with the rest from interest and the share count, and the modal return is about 11 percent a year against the 20 percent bar.

    • The parts combine as follows: 'Change Forces' at C and 'Financial Outputs' at C are the analysis
    • the 'Modal return' at D is where they bind, because the price already pays the stock's own median multiple for the mechanical part of the growth.
    • Not D, because the change is further along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain than most cases in the universe and the earnings line is inside the band
    • not B, because a B thesis needs a return near 20 percent and this one needs a re-rating it does not control to get there.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the earnings growth is a "false friend".
    • The margin expansion, the cash step-up and the beats are what a mature-market brewer produces when it prices above elasticity and under-invests in its brands.
    • Volumes sit 1 percent below 2019.
      • US mainstream brands fall 8 to 13 percent a year.
      • Bud China's value growth is the weakest of the national brewers (minus 4 percent in June 2026 against China Resources +6 percent and Yanjing +15 percent).
      • The multiple is already at par with European staples.
    • On this reading the growth is financial engineering on a shrinking volume base, and the 2Q26 print is the first evidence that the reinvestment bill has arrived: North America EBITDA +0.5 percent because top-line gains were fully reinvested, the EMEA margin down 49 basis points, APAC EBITDA down 10.9 percent.
  • What we concede: the volume base is not growing outside Latin America
    • half the earnings growth is below EBIT
    • the premium to Heineken and Carlsberg leaves no margin of safety against the sector.
  • Why we still hold a C rather than a D: the counter-hypothesis's own hard inconsistency is all-time-high Latin American volumes, and Latin America is about 45 percent of group revenue and the majority of profit.
    • Pricing is sticking at 4 to 5 percent a year with group volumes positive for two quarters.
      • The interest and buyback legs are arithmetic, not hope.
    • The case against does not break the earnings path.
      • It caps the multiple, and that is why the return is a D and the thesis a C.
      • The bull case, which is the one that would change the recommendation, is taken at the head of the 'Investment Case' Building Block.

  • External forces: no force outside the company is a tailwind of 'Demand Inflection' strength

    • the developed-market and China headwinds are structural.
  • Not B, because no external force is a tailwind of 'Demand Inflection' strength and no internal force is new in 2026 beyond the buyback and the chairman.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because end-market demand is changing structurally against the company in developed markets and in China, and for it only in Latin America.
  • Developed markets: US off-trade beer volumes down 5.3 percent over twelve weeks to June 2026
    • US domestic industry shipments down 2.5 percent over the three months to May 2026
    • US scanner volume decline stable at minus 6 percent in July 2026, with petrol prices capping any sequential improvement
    • alcohol penetration falling across all US income cohorts from 2024 to 2025, most in households under 40,000 USD.
  • China: volumes down 9.7 percent in 2Q26 and China EBITDA down 16.1 percent
    • consumers migrating from on-premise nightlife to in-home and online-to-offline channels, where online-to-offline is a mid-single-digit share of volume.
    • This is structural, not weather: the risks research named the channel shift as the single most critical risk before the 2Q print.
  • Latin America: Brazil beer volumes +5.0 percent in 2Q26 with revenue per hectolitre +5.3 percent
    • Colombia low-teens volume growth and high-teens revenue growth
    • Middle Americas revenue +9.8 percent on record volumes in Mexico and Colombia.
    • This is the one demand force that meets the 'Demand Inflection' evidence test, and it is regional.
  • Moderation and health: Gen Z drinking less, GLP-1 adoption, the World Health Organization's "no safe level" push
    • the no-alcohol beer market is growing at a 7 percent compound rate to 2028 and the company leads it (no-alcohol revenue +27 percent in 2Q26), the tailwind inside the headwind.
  • Not F, because the company's own volume line turned positive (+0.8 percent in 1Q26, +0.9 percent in 2Q26) and the Latin American swing has more than two years of visibility in the company's own share gains.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the regulatory direction is tightening on pricing (excise), marketing and packaging in most markets, and the one favourable change (US tariffs on Mexican imports) is proposed, not enacted.
  • Excise: Vietnam's Special Consumption Tax roadmap runs from 65 percent in 2026 to 90 or 100 percent by 2030
    • the company's Vietnam share is about 1 percent, so the effect is small for it and larger for Heineken at 43 percent.
  • Marketing and packaging: bans debated in Scotland and Spain on non-alcoholic branding at sports venues
    • Canadian celebrity-endorsement rules
    • EU packaging regulation banning several single-use plastics from 2030
    • US and Canadian extended producer responsibility.
  • Tax litigation: the Belgian excess-profit ruling awaiting the European Court of Justice
    • a Peru excise dispute with a worst case of 0.6 billion USD
    • Brazilian tax disputes unprovisioned with possible losses above 53 billion BRL.
    • These are read as shocks under 'Riskiness'
    • here they are a regulatory headwind on cash.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • AB InBev is a deleveraging compounder in a mature category: not a recovery, not a demand inflection and not a self-help in the usual sense.
  • The balance sheet has been repaired (net debt to normalised EBITDA 3.96x at FY21 to 2.86x at 30-Jun-26, company basis) and the cash that used to go to creditors now goes to a 6 billion USD buyback and a progressive dividend.
  • The operating business grows revenue at about 4 to 5 percent organically through price and mix, on a volume base that is flat at group level, growing in Latin America and shrinking in the US mainstream and in China.
  • Earnings per share therefore compound at about 12 to 13 percent a year from FY2025 to FY2028 on our path (3.73 to 5.35 USD), of which about 6 points come from revenue and margin and about 6 points from falling interest, a shrinking minority line, translation in FY2026 and the share count.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: the choice was made in 2021, the actions (BEES, the megabrands, deleveraging, the buyback) are visible, the operating metrics and the financial statements show it (H1-26 earnings per share +22 percent, free cash flow +2.5 billion USD), and the sell-side has revised FY2026 earnings per share up 9.9 percent in twelve months.
  • It fits the 'Compounder' pattern at a C: a named mis-modelled set of lines, now mostly in the models, in a business that beats and holds.
  • It is not compelling, because the price already pays the stock's own five-year median multiple for the mechanical part of the growth and a 25 percent premium to the two peers in its cohort, so that the modal total return is about 11 percent a year.
  • A 20 percent return requires a re-rating to the ten-year 75th percentile that depends on the sector and on China, neither of which the company controls.
  • The case is sound, middling and fairly priced, and we park it.

Congruence: The congruence judgement: does the whole hang together across past, business, value chain and market environment; where ratings disagree, why (Part 4, E 3, BB 4)

  • The whole hangs together on the earnings leg and comes apart on the re-rating leg, and every disagreement between the ratings traces to that one split.

  • With the past: congruent.

    • Earnings per share compounded at 6.7 percent a year from FY21 to FY25 through a boycott and adverse currency
    • leverage fell every year
    • pricing held at 4 to 5 percent.
    • The 2026 step is the arrival of the buyback and the interest line on top of the same engine, not a new engine.
    • The two years in which the company's own choices broke the trend (2018, 2023) are the reason the past is a B and not an A.
  • With the business: congruent, because the thesis relies only on strengths the business demonstrably has (scale, pricing, cash conversion, capital discipline) and not on the things it has not shown (volume growth, US mainstream recovery, China).
    • That is why 'Foundations' is B while 'Investment Thesis' is C: the foundations are stronger than the case built on them, because the case, to reach the bar, has to reach for the one thing the foundations do not supply.
  • With the value chain: against, in the sense that matters for the multiple.
    • The thesis sits with the industry structure (an oligopoly protecting the margin) and against the other actors (consumers drinking less beer, Chinese consumers leaving the on-premise channel, regulators tightening).
    • It is a thesis about the profit pool in a shrinking category, and a shrinking category is what the market refuses to pay a staple's multiple for.
  • With the market environment: against, and explained.
    • The market paid for the deleveraging for a year (+36.8 percent over twelve months) and then stopped (minus 6 percent over three months through a beat).
    • Technical momentum C, earnings momentum C and valuation C all agree with a middling thesis
    • the disagreement is only between the sell-side's median target (23 percent above the price) and the peers' multiples (11.6 to 11.8x at the bottom of their histories), and we side with the peers because the sector is de-rating and the company cannot re-rate far above its two cohort peers alone.
  • Where the ratings disagree with each other: 'Foundations' B against everything else C or D, explained above
    • 'Net debt' A and 'Sustainability' A against 'Modal return' D, because the strongest facts in the case (the balance sheet, the dividend cover) are the ones the price already pays for
    • 'Key metrics' B against 'Goldilocks growth' C, because the outputs are improving but the growth that results is half below EBIT
    • 'No broad mediocrity' D against 'No mediocre management' B, because the management is not the problem, the category and the price are.
    • None of these is an unexplained contradiction
    • each is the same fact seen from a different attribute.
