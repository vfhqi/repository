EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Business Model and Strategic Position

Sectra operates a highly predictable, non-cyclical business model transitioning to cloud subscription services that command premium pricing and deep institutional trust, with customer demand insulated from macroeconomic downturns due to demographic shifts and healthcare digitization priorities. Recurring revenue accounted for 69.2% of the total sales mix during the 2025/2026 fiscal year, while rolling 12-month cloud recurring revenues surpassed SEK 1 billion for the first time, marking a major financial milestone.

Sectra receives active equity coverage from only DNB Carnegie, employing three research analysts, with no other active financial analysts covering the stock, creating concentrated broker coverage that limits institutional visibility.

Financial Profile and Capital Returns

Sectra achieved net sales of SEK 3,541.7 mm during the 2025/2026 fiscal year, representing a 9.3% increase, while peer Pro Medicus recorded revenue of AUD 261.7 mm with a larger 22.9% expansion, reflecting Sectra's focus on steady organic geographic execution. Sectra recorded a consolidated operating margin of 20.1% during the 2025/2026 fiscal year, down from 22.3% in the prior year due to higher customer onboarding expenses and cloud migration investments, while Pro Medicus maintained a superior operating margin of 74.9%. Sectra generated a high return on capital employed of 34.8% during the 2025/2026 fiscal year, down from 40.5% in the prior period, with strong net profits and disciplined balance sheet management sustaining upper-quartile returns across changing economic cycles. Sectra generated operating cash flow before changes in working capital of SEK 742.2 mm during the 2025/2026 fiscal year, with total operating cash flow rising to SEK 1,097.3 mm, driven by efficient collections and reduced capital tied up in receivables. Sectra maintained cash and cash equivalents of SEK 1,810.3 mm as of April 30, 2026, representing a 34.9% increase from SEK 1,341.9 mm in the prior year, providing a strong buffer against macroeconomic disruptions.

Industry Structure and Competitive Landscape

The global enterprise imaging and clinical medical IT market was valued at approximately USD 4 bn in the 2025 fiscal year, with Grand View Research projecting double-digit compound annual growth rates into the 2030s as healthcare networks prioritize digital modernization. Sectra holds the leading global market share with a 15% share of the global PACS market in the 2025 fiscal year, while the top five vendors command a combined market share of 58%, and the next five competitors hold 37%.

Specialized competitor Pro Medicus maintains a 99% gross margin and a 75% EBITDA margin through a scalable single-code streaming architecture, while Sectra's Imaging IT Solutions division recorded a segment operating margin of 23.1% during the 2025/2026 fiscal year.

Value Chain Positioning and Disruption Risks

The medical imaging value chain spans from raw hardware components and physical scanning modalities to cloud infrastructure hosts and diagnostic software viewers, with Sectra operating as an integrated software provider connecting these disparate physical and digital layers. Specialized software streaming platforms command the highest operating margins with Pro Medicus maintaining a seventy-five percent EBITDA margin, while Sectra operates with intermediate profit margins and recorded a segment operating margin of 23.1% due to intensive direct deployment services.

Edge-heavy artificial intelligence workloads and scanner-embedded diagnostic algorithms present the most credible long-term disruption to Sectra's software margins, as original equipment manufacturers embed automated triaging algorithms directly on imaging hardware to bypass traditional PACS workflows.

Customer Base and Switching Costs

Sectra maintains low customer concentration with the five largest partners and customers jointly accounting for only 13.7% of consolidated sales, and no single customer exceeding 10% of total sales, protecting the group from sudden revenue drops. Typical contracts span five to seven years, while some extensive deals extend up to twelve years, including a major twelve-year software contract with MSSS Québec valued at SEK 3.1 billion. Sectra achieves industry-leading customer retention with a remarkably low recurring revenue churn rate of just 0.5% annually, ensuring that more than half of the company's annual sales comprise reliable, recurring software income. KLAS Research ranked Sectra as the number-one PACS provider in the US, Canada, and Europe in 2024, with survey data showing that 100% of interviewed users would buy Sectra PACS again and keep it in their long-term plans.

Supplier Dependencies and Distribution Model

Sectra relies heavily on Microsoft Corporation's Azure public cloud infrastructure to deliver its primary enterprise imaging platform as a fully managed Software-as-a-Service, with Microsoft Azure enabling high uptime and robust data redundancy, though hyperscaler hosting partnerships sustain cloud expansion but restrict Sectra's ability to negotiate base infrastructure pricing. Sectra balances its global commercial reach by deploying direct sales offices in 23 countries focusing on high-volume markets including Scandinavia, the United Kingdom, and the United States, while expanding coverage into approximately 30 additional countries through independent distribution partners.

Competitive Dynamics and Market Share Trends

Pro Medicus Limited delivers the primary software-based competitive threat through its flagship Visage 7 enterprise imaging platform favored by radiologists for rapid server-side rendering, forcing Sectra to accelerate cloud innovations to maintain its high-end clinical market standing. Sectra expanded its United States market share from 4% in 2024 to 6% in 2025 according to the Morgan Stanley AlphaWise hospital survey, while Pro Medicus increased its share from 7% to 8%, and legacy competitor GE HealthCare Technologies Inc remained flat at 75%.

Sectra invests 10% to 15% of its consolidated sales in research and development each year, contrasting sharply with Pro Medicus Limited which spent only 4% of revenue on capitalized R&D in fiscal year 2024.

Management Quality and Execution

Sectra President and Chief Executive Officer Torbjörn Kronander drives a long-term strategic direction focused on customer satisfaction and stable organic growth, targeting profitable niches in medical imaging and cybersecurity where trust is paramount, successfully transitioning to cloud-based services and expanding into digital pathology since 2012. Torbjörn Kronander personally interviews every candidate offered a permanent position to maintain high standards and cultural alignment, ensuring the company maintains a highly skilled workforce with more PhD graduates than typical competitors.

Sectra consistently delivers strong operating margins above its 15% long-term target across different economic cycles, outgrowing major hardware incumbents to capture leading clinical rankings, with organic sales expanding 27% during the first quarter of fiscal 2026/2027. Sectra clinical viewers benefit from exceptional customer lock-in due to immense software switching barriers, with hospitals avoiding platform replacement because of workflow disruptions, data migration risks, and staff retraining costs, ensuring a remarkably low recurring revenue churn rate of 0.5% annually.

Sectra distinguishes its product suite by unifying radiology, digital pathology, genomics, and cardiology onto a single cloud database, allowing consolidating hospital systems to reduce IT complexity by replacing several legacy vendors and blocking niche software competitors from encroaching on enterprise accounts.

Long-Term Growth Alignment and Investment Themes

Sectra aligns closely with long-term investment themes focused on digital healthcare modernization and rising cybersecurity requirements, with aging global demographics and expanding diagnostic data volumes sustaining structural demand for scalable enterprise imaging as public sectors prioritize software investments to mitigate clinician shortages. Sectra's secure communication solutions address critical state demands for postquantum cryptography and high-assurance communication security, with national authorities prioritizing cybersecurity investments to protect sensitive government networks from sophisticated digital threats, allowing the group to capture high-value public defense contracts.

Single Most Important Strength

Exceptional customer retention secured by massive switching costs creates a formidable competitive moat. Sectra secures exceptionally stable customer relationships due to massive software switching barriers, with PACS platforms deeply integrated into daily clinical workflows making replacement highly disruptive, while data migration risks and staff retraining costs prevent hospital systems from easily switching to cheaper software alternatives, guaranteeing high contract renewal rates and protecting premium recurring margins. This structural advantage manifests in a remarkably low recurring revenue churn rate of just 0.5% annually, ensuring that more than half of the company's annual sales comprise reliable, recurring software income that provides stable recurring cash flows during the transition to managed cloud subscriptions.

Single Most Important Weakness

High upfront cloud implementation costs temporarily depress operating leverage during the critical SaaS transition phase. Sectra's rapid subscription expansion causes near-term margin compression due to high direct customer onboarding expenses, with deploying massive hospital backlogs requiring substantial upfront personnel investments before recurring subscription billings generate profits, while in-house engineering implementations restrict initial operating leverage as external delivery costs expand.

This weakness is evident in the consolidated operating margin declining to 20.1% during the 2025/2026 fiscal year from 22.3% in the prior year, with higher customer onboarding expenses and cloud migration investments temporarily restricting operational profitability as subscription deployments scale.
