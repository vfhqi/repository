Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding Germany's technical installation sector is essential to evaluating Instalco's expansion and future earnings trajectory.
  2. The consolidation of Fabri AG directly influences Instalco's progress toward achieving its mid-term margin and leverage targets.

Defining the German market opportunity

1.
Quoted Key Question: "For Instalco AB (INSTAL-SE), what did Fabri AG's first consolidated results show about its revenue, EBITA margin, cash conversion, backlog and net debt relative to its reported 2025 figures (EUR 86 million of sales, EUR 10.9 million of EBITA, EUR 69 million of backlog at end-Q1 2026, 23 subsidiaries and about 800 employees by July 2026); what German technical-installation bolt-ons have been bought by Fabri, Brookfield, VDK, Eiffage, SPIE (ROFA, SGS) and Nordic Climate Group since 2024 and at what disclosed prices or multiples; how German installers' margins and volumes moved through the 2022 to 2024 residential downturn and the heat-pump subsidy changes; and what German construction and installation indicators (permits, capacity utilisation, the EUR 500 billion infrastructure fund's timing, the KfW SME investment survey) imply for a 51 percent-owned platform's growth and margin over the next two years?"
2.
Uncovering market insights: We identified 120 relevant sources, 12 peer companies, and 2 comparable past cases across European installation markets. DNB Carnegie's report on 5 December 2024 highlights that Germany's technical installation sector represents a market five times larger than all of Instalco's legacy Nordic markets combined. This scale means that establishing a successful platform in Germany is critical to sustaining longterm acquisitive growth and margin expansion over the cycle.
3.
Accelerated geographic expansion: Instalco completed its first steps into Germany by securing an initial 24% minority stake in Nuremberg-based Fabri AG in late 2024. As noted in Instalco's interim report on 16 July 2026, the parent company then increased its ownership stake to 51% to begin full consolidation in Q3 2026. Consolidating Fabri's operations will directly accelerate group-level earnings per share because Germany serves as the primary engine for Instalco's international growth runway.

Strategic implications for Instalco 2.0

4.
Ambitious profitability targets: Returning founder and CEO Per Sjöstrand has prioritized restoring group EBITA margins to at least 8% through the "Instalco 2.0" program. In the interim report on 16 July 2026, the company reported a rolling twelve-month EBITA margin of 6.6%, which remains below its long-term target. Reaching the target margin requires improved project selection and execution across both Nordic operations and German business units.
5.
Disciplined capital structure: Achieving a leverage ratio below 2.5x net debt to EBITDA is another long-term strategic target for the group. Instalco's Q2 interim report on 16 July 2026 showed leverage remained slightly elevated at 2.6x net debt to EBITDA. Sustained earnings from German operations are vital because they must support debt reduction without diluting cash conversion goals of 100%.
6.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie's report on 24 September 2024 warns that weaker market activity and low capacity utilization in Swedish operations could offset international expansion benefits. The analyst noted that organic revenue dropped by 6% with a corresponding 70 basis point EBITA margin decline in 2024. This performance suggests that domestic headwinds may drag down overall group profitability even if the new German platform achieves its growth targets.

Sector dynamics and macroeconomic indicators

7.
Challenging German environment: German construction output declined significantly due to high material costs and elevated financing rates during the recent residential downturn. Eurostat data published on 24 December 2024 showed EU construction output contracted by 2.4% over the first ten months of the year. These factors threaten the growth of the German platform unless public sector demand improves to offset residential building weakness.
8.
Potential fiscal stimulus: Germany's proposed €500 billion off-budget infrastructure fund represents a major potential catalyst for the engineering and installation sector. According to Strabag SE's report on 15 September 2026, initial projects from this fund are expected to stimulate market demand starting in late 2026 or early 2027. This funding is critical to unlocking long-term public sector contract opportunities for local service providers.
9.
Other side of the story: While the massive €500 billion infrastructure fund promises long-term growth, lengthy planning and bureaucratic approval procedures are delaying the actual flow of funds to contractors. Morgan Stanley's report on 12 October 2026 projects that actual spending will run 20% below government targets as regions struggle with execution. These delays mean that the anticipated earnings step-up may be deferred until 2027 or later.

Section 2 – Fabri AG's Consolidated Financial Performance

Section summary
  1. We have high confidence that consolidating Fabri AG will immediately improve Instalco's profitability based on historical standalone margins of twelve percent.
  2. This high confidence is further supported by solid broker previews and a strong regional backlog of sixty-nine million euros.

Baseline and consolidation schedule

1.
Increased capital holding: Instalco announced on 16 July 2026 that it acquired an additional 27% of German installation group Fabri AG for €35.7 million. This transaction increased the Swedish parent's total holding to 51% of capital and votes to establish majority ownership control. The acquisition was financed entirely through the group's existing credit facility, which means that the transaction did not dilute existing shareholders.
2.
Significant historical performance: Fabri AG reported net sales of €86 million and an EBITA of €10.9 million for the full year 2025, which corresponds to an EBITA margin of 12.7%. This operating profitability sits significantly above Instalco's corporate margin targets of 8%. The strong historical results suggest that the German platform will be highly accretive to consolidated margins once fully integrated into the group.
3.
Rapid platform growth: On a pro forma basis that includes all bolt-on acquisitions completed during 2025, Fabri's full year sales would have reached €117 million. The platform has expanded rapidly from 12 subsidiaries and 400 employees in late 2024 to 23 subsidiaries and 800 employees by July 2026. This rapid growth highlights that Fabri has successfully executed a highly active localized acquisition strategy prior to its majority consolidation.
4.
Localized organic scaling: Fabri's workforce has grown to approximately 800 employees by July 2026, representing a substantial scaling from its earlier operational phase. Under the partnership model established in late 2024, Instalco shares both upside and risk with local founders and entrepreneurs. This structure secures high employee retention and motivation because the founders remain invested in the platform's long-term commercial success.

Q3 earnings preview and segment performance

5.
Delayed reporting timeline: Instalco has structured the consolidation of Fabri to begin on 1 August 2026 with a one-month reporting lag. Consequently, only one month of Fabri's financial performance will be included in the parent's Q3 interim report scheduled for publication on 23 October 2026. This reporting structure means that the full impact of German operations will not become fully visible until late 2026.
6.
Strong segment growth: In the Q3 results preview published on 29 September 2026, DNB Carnegie estimates that the Finland & Germany segment will report sales of SEK 573 million. This estimate represents 31.6% year-on-year growth for the combined international segment, which is driven by the initial consolidation of Fabri. However, the broker's forecast remains slightly more conservative than the Bloomberg consensus estimate of SEK 623 million, which projects even higher international volume growth.
7.
Favourable segment profitability: The Finland & Germany segment is expected to report an EBITA margin of 9.5% for Q3 2026, according to DNB Carnegie's estimates. While this margin is lower than Fabri's standalone 2025 margin of 12.7%, it remains above the 8% target set under Per Sjöstrand's Instalco 2.0 program. The international margin helps offset lower margins in domestic operations where Swedish residential markets remain soft.
8.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie's results update on 17 July 2026 indicates that while international margins are high, consolidated cash conversion has decreased to 90% from previous highs. The analyst noted that higher activity and subsequent invoicing at the end of Q2 tied up significant capital in accounts receivable, which dragged operating cash flow down to SEK 104 million from SEK 202 million in the prior year. This working capital drag suggests that rapid growth may temporarily constrain the parent group's overall liquidity and cash generation.

Benchmarking against Nordic peers

9.
Superior platform profitability: Standalone margins at Fabri are exceptionally strong when benchmarked against major Nordic technical installation peer groups. For instance, Bravida reported an adjusted EBITA margin of 5.4% for Q2 2026, which is less than half of Fabri's historical standalone margin. This comparison indicates that the German market offers significantly higher premium pricing opportunities than the highly competitive Nordic markets.
10.
Dilutive legacy operations: Fabri's historical margins also outperform Instalco's legacy Swedish operations, which reported an EBITA margin of 7% in Q2 2026. Although Swedish operations are beginning to recover under the Instalco 2.0 margin-over-volume program, they remain dilutive to the international segment. This divergence suggests that expanding the German platform is critical to lifting consolidated profitability to the long-term target of 8%.
11.
Robust backlog comparison: Nordic peer Bravida reported that its order backlog rose by 25% year-on-year in Q2 2026, driven by massive data center contract wins in Norway. While Instalco's direct exposure to Nordic data centers remains limited, the overall tight subcontracting market improves pricing power across all legacy operations. This supportive pricing environment allows the Finland & Germany segment to protect its high margin profile against inflationary labor costs.
12.
Successful past integration: A past case from the Nordic sector demonstrates that decentralized consolidation models can maintain high margins if local management is preserved. An expert call on 17 February 2025 highlighted that the merger of Caverion and Assemblin succeeded because they allowed acquired regional entrepreneurs to maintain operational independence. This historical precedent supports Fabri's strategy of operating under autonomous local leadership to sustain German market expansion.

Backlog and net debt trajectory

13.
Sustained backlog evolution: Fabri had accumulated an order backlog of approximately €69 million at the end of Q1 2026, which provides high short-term revenue visibility. This solid backlog is comparable to Swedish backlog trends, where Instalco's overall order backlog grew by 17% year-on-year in Q2 2026. Robust backlogs allow the group to remain highly selective with new projects, supporting future EBITA margin improvement over the next two years.
14.
Elevated group leverage: The €35.7 million cash consideration for the additional Fabri stake was funded through existing credit facilities, adding to group debt. Prior to full consolidation, DNB Carnegie modeled Instalco's net debt to adjusted EBITDA to rise slightly to 2.7x in Q2 2026 due to payouts and minority buyouts. This level sits above the long-term leverage target of 2.5x, meaning that earnings expansion must accelerate to deliver rapid balance sheet deleveraging.
15.
Other side of the story: While Fabri's standalone margin of 12.7% is highly accretive, the combined Finland & Germany segment margin of 9.5% indicates that historical margins are diluting as the platform scales or as weaker Finnish operations drag down the segment. Furthermore, DNB Carnegie's report on 29 September 2026 models segment margins to stabilize around 9.8% in 2027, rather than returning to the standalone peaks. These lower forward estimates suggest that operational integration costs may prevent the platform from reclaiming its highest standalone profitability.

Section 3 – Competitor M&A and Bolt-on Multiples

Section summary
  1. Competitor acquisition activity in the German technical installation sector has increased dramatically since 2023 as multiple international platforms deploy capital.
  2. Disclosed transaction multiples in Germany range between seven and nine times EBITA, representing a premium compared to typical Nordic entry benchmarks.

Competitive dynamics in German consolidation

1.
Aggressive market consolidation: Competition for acquisition targets in the German technical installation market has escalated significantly since 2023 as major international platforms seek scale. According to DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026, a total of 104 M&A transactions were announced in this segment between 2023 and 2026, representing 44% of all deals since 1995. This dramatic acceleration of dealmaking indicates that Germany is currently undergoing rapid consolidation led by well-funded platforms.
2.
Fragmented regional footprint: The German technical installation market remains highly fragmented with approximately 91,300 companies, leaving a substantial consolidation runway for regional players. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026 shows that 51% of these potential targets are located in just three states, specifically Bavaria, North Rhine-Westphalia, and Baden-Württemberg. Consequently, consolidators are targeting these highpopulation regions to achieve critical local market density before expanding nationwide.
3.
Dominant private equity consolidator: Private equity firm Brookfield has emerged as the most active acquirer in the German technical installation sector since 2023. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026 notes that Brookfield has completed 15 acquisitions in this market, representing a leading 14% share of all technical installation deals during this period. This massive scale-up suggests that private equity backing creates intense competition for high-quality regional targets.
4.
Expanding pan-European platforms: The Dutch VDK Groep has significantly expanded its footprint in Germany through a major cross-border combination with a leading local consolidator. According to VDK Groep's press release on 27 November 2025, the group acquired the Builtech Group, which operated 51 regional companies with over 2,000 employees. This strategic combination immediately established VDK as a top three player in Germany, demonstrating that multi-technical service consolidation is accelerating across the continent.

Disclosed transaction multiples and prices

5.
Premium German entry multiples: Disclosed transaction multiples for German industrial and technical services acquisitions have stabilized at a premium relative to historical mid-market levels. Bernstein Research's report on 22 July 2026 states that recent transactions for German technical assets consistently fall within the range of 7x to 9x EV/EBITA. These elevated entry multiples reflect increased competition among serial consolidators which could pressure future returns on capital if synergies are not fully realized.
6.
Transformative industrial acquisitions: France-based SPIE has executed a major scale-up of its German industrial services platform through two large acquisitions completed in 2026. SPIE's press release on 25 March 2026 highlights the acquisition of SGS Industrial Services for a high-single-digit EV/EBITA multiple, representing €180 million of annual sales. This deal follows the acquisition of ROFA Industrial Automation for an estimated €250 million to €330 million, proving that SPIE is willing to pay premium prices to secure market leadership.
7.
Strategic data center focus: Infrastructure giant Eiffage has focused its German acquisition strategy on high- growth segments like digital infrastructure and energy transmission. According to Eiffage's press release on 12 May 2026, the group's subsidiary Salvia acquired a 74.9% stake in German data center specialist Hand & Werk, which generated €85 million in 2025 revenue. This transaction enables Eiffage to capture strong demand for digital facilities while deepening its specialized service capabilities in key metropolitan hubs.
8.
Rapid regional scaling: Returning founder Per Sjöstrand's Fabri AG has built a highly active regional roll-up engine in Germany prior to its consolidation into Instalco. According to DNB Carnegie's report on 23 January 2026, Fabri completed 11 acquisitions after November 2024, nearly doubling its workforce to 800 employees. This rapid expansion was executed under a deal structure paying approximately 0.9x annual sales for various equity tranches, representing highly disciplined capital deployment.
9.
Contrary evidence: While several consolidators are expanding aggressively, Nordic Climate Group's recent entry into Germany demonstrates that smaller regional players are still being acquired in highly fragmented packages. According to Nordic Climate Group's press release on 2 June 2026, the company entered Germany by acquiring four cooling and heating firms with combined revenues of €40 million and 200 employees. This fragmented transaction shows that not all consolidation is happening at premium scale, indicating that small-scale local bolt-ons remain available for disciplined acquirers.

Valuation benchmarking against Nordic peers

10.
Divergent entry multiples: Valuation multiples for technical installation assets in Germany are notably higher than the multiples typically paid by Nordic consolidators. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026 notes that Danish compounder InstallatørGruppen targets entry valuations of 5.0x to 5.5x EV/Structural EBITA. This benchmark sits substantially below the 7x to 9x EV/EBITA entry range observed in Germany, meaning that German expansion requires higher capital intensity than domestic Nordic roll-ups.
11.
Historical compounder multiples: The valuation paid for German acquisitions also exceeds historical average multiples for classic Nordic M&A compounders. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026 highlights that Nordic compounders paid an average EV/EBITA multiple of ~7.5x (calculated) between 2022 and 2025. This indicates that the highly competitive German market forces consolidators to pay peak valuations which could dilute overall group return on capital unless operational efficiencies are unlocked.
12.
Attractive trading arbitrage: Public market valuations for established Nordic compounders continue to trade at a substantial premium to private entry multiples, creating an attractive arbitrage. According to DNB Carnegie's report on 28 August 2026, proven Swedish compounders trade at an average of ~21x NTM EV/EBITA. This premium trading valuation suggests that integrating private assets at 8x EBITA will generate substantial long-term value once consolidated.

Platform building and synergy realization

13.
Preserving entrepreneurial culture: Successful technical service consolidators in Germany are increasingly adopting decentralized operating models to retain key regional leadership. Bernstein Research's report on 25 March 2026 notes that SPIE opted to retain SGS's experienced management team to preserve local customer relationships. This approach aligns with Instalco's partnership model, showing that local operational autonomy is vital to minimizing integration risks and labor attrition.
14.
Unlocking procurement synergies: Consolidating regional installers onto a unified platform allows parent companies to generate significant cost savings through purchasing power. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026 indicates that platforms like InstallatørGruppen typically target procurement savings of ~5% and cash release of 3% to 8% from improved payment terms. These financial synergies help offset elevated entry multiples and accelerate earnings per share growth after integration.
15.
Other side of the story: Although the massive scale of the German market offers a long M&A runway, the high level of structural complexity and strict labor regulations present significant execution risks. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 July 2026 warns that German installers exhibit higher margin volatility and operate under strict wage transparency. Consequently, international consolidators may face unexpected margin pressure if they fail to manage localized labor shortages or regional cost inflation.

Impact of the German residential downturn

1.
Severe industry contraction: NORMA Group SE's annual report of 29 March 2025 states that the German residential building construction sector shrank by 10.5% in nominal terms in 2024 due to high costs. This contraction represented an acceleration from the previous year's declines as high interest rates and cancellations burdened developers. These compounding macroeconomic headwinds severely constrained new build installation volumes for regional technical installers, forcing a rapid reassessment of project selection across the industry.
2.
Backlog depletion risks: Allianz SE's broker report of 22 October 2024 shows that Germany remained the weakest European market with residential building permits declining 22% year-on-year in Q2 2024. This sustained permit drop indicates that regional installers faced rapid backlog depletion unless they adapted to alternative market segments. Consequently, these unfavorable market conditions resulted in intensified regional pricing pressure as local contractors competed aggressively for a dwindling number of private projects.
3.
Widespread labor hoarding: Rothschild & Co Redburn's report of 5 December 2024 details that German firms engaged in extensive labor hoarding to preserve scarce technical talent despite low output. This structural labor dynamic occurred because installers feared high rehiring costs given the severe structural shortage of skilled labor. While this practice supported employment levels, it led to under-absorption of fixed personnel costs and subsequent margin compression during the cyclical low.
4.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank's report of 8 December 2023 demonstrates that civil engineering and energy infrastructure investment actually expanded by 1.0% during the residential trough. This counter-cyclical growth was heavily driven by massive public-sector and grid connection tenders related to the German energy transition. This indicates that the installation downturn was not uniform across all construction subsectors, as specialized industrial and public-sector contractors maintained robust utilization.

Subsidy volatility and heat-pump shock

5.
Abrupt regulatory reversals: Jefferies' report of 3 March 2025 details that German heat pump sales declined by 48% in 2024 compared to the record year of 2023. This massive correction was caused by intense political debate over the Building Energy Act and sudden funding uncertainties. While the government eventually introduced a revamped incentive scheme in early 2024, administrative delays in online applications eroded consumer confidence, showing that unstable subsidies disrupt long-term demand.
6.
Severe supply imbalances: NIBE's year-end report published on 14 February 2025 highlights that the rapid build-up of heat pump inventory in 2023 led to a severe bullwhip effect for manufacturers. While end-user demand gradually stabilized in other regions, German distributors required a much longer period to work down excess channel inventories. This inventory overhang meant that manufacturers faced low factory absorption and margin pressure even as actual installation work slowly began to recover.
7.
Distorted short-term demand: Masterlink Securities' note of 18 October 2023 explains that the passage of the German Building Energy Act created a significant short-term rush to install traditional fossil-fuel boilers before the 2024 ban. This temporary demand shift further depressed heat pump adoption and created highly volatile workload patterns for local mechanical installers. Consequently, the sudden crowding-out effect may have temporarily compromised installer capacity and project margins before demand patterns normalized, affecting overall operational efficiency.
8.
Policy-driven demand spikes: DNB Carnegie's report of 10 January 2025 highlights a sudden surge in German heat pump applications in late 2024, with monthly applications reaching 28,000 units. This rapid acceleration was driven by consumer fears that an incoming conservative coalition would repeal renewable heating incentives. While this temporary surge helped distributors reduce excess inventories, it also underscores how regulatory uncertainty causes extreme volatility for regional installation groups.

Comparative Nordic developments and service transition

9.
Slowing Nordic markets: DNB Carnegie's report of 24 September 2024 highlights that Instalco faced significant profitability headwinds in its core Swedish operations due to a turbulent domestic construction market. The Swedish and Finnish newbuild residential sectors experienced severe downturns, leading to increased regional competition and widespread price pressure during public tender processes. These adjacent Nordic headwinds demonstrate that newbuild cyclicality drags down margins across Northern Europe unless companies implement disciplined margin-over-volume programs.
10.
Prolonged Finnish weakness: Instalco's Q4 earnings call on 12 February 2026 highlights that construction activity in Finland remained highly subdued, with private investments on hold. This persistent weakness forced regional installers to rely almost entirely on renovation projects and long-term energy transition investments. This comparative precedent suggests that a prolonged downturn restricts organic growth, meaning that regional platforms must actively transition their workforces toward stable municipal contracts.
11.
140 141 - Earnings Calls Late-cycle protection lag: Deutsche Bank's report of 14 June 2022 explaining Bravida's business model notes that technical installation is a late-cycle business with high revenue visibility. Because technical services typically start about eight months into a building project, installers are partially insulated from sudden macroeconomic shocks. However, this late-cycle positioning also means that cyclical recoveries take longer to materialize, which delayed operating profit improvements during the 2022 to 2024 downturn.

Structural service transition

12.
Strategic service expansion: Instalco's annual report for 2025 shows that the company successfully expanded its service and maintenance business to 37% of net sales, up from 35% in the prior year. This strategic expansion helped offset weaker newbuild volumes because service contracts are generally smaller, less cyclical, and highly recurring. This operational pivot indicates that prioritizing highly recurring service revenues serves as a critical margin stabilizer during broad construction recessions.
13.
Peer service resilience: Bravida's Q4 earnings call on 11 February 2025 demonstrates that expanding service operations supported stable group-level margins despite soft newbuild markets. The peer company reported that its service area accounted for 50% of total net sales in Q4 2024, helping protect profitability in Southern Sweden. This comparative performance confirms that service-oriented work provides pricing power when high interest rates and cost inflation depress traditional commercial installation contracts.
14.
Other side of the story: While transitioning to service and renovation work provides stability, scaling these operations rapidly can introduce execution risks and dilute average project sizes. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 September 2024 warns that transitioning to smaller, highly localized service assignments can increase administrative overhead and dilute operating leverage. Furthermore, if multiple regional competitors aggressively pivot to service work to offset newbuild declines, the subsequent industry price war could still erode margins across all technical disciplines.

Section 5 – Forward Indicators and Outlook for the German Platform

Section summary
  1. German building permits show a gradual residential stabilization that supports long-term volume recovery.
  2. Low construction capacity utilization is counterbalanced by severe structural shortages of skilled installation labor.
  3. The massive federal infrastructure fund provides a substantial demand pipeline but faces execution and budget-shifting delays.

Building permit and construction trends

1.
Stabilizing residential permits: German housing permits are showing initial signs of recovery after a prolonged historical decline. According to Jefferies Research on 13 October 2026, German residential building permits rose slightly month-on-month in August 2026. The broker notes that residential permit growth has entered positive territory, which improves forward demand visibility. This gradual permit expansion supports Fabri AG's long-term capacity to deliver organic expansion over the next two years.

2.
Gradual industry recovery: The German construction sector is projected to begin a slow recovery after experiencing a severe five-year contraction. ING Wholesale Banking reported on 7 September 2026 that construction output is expected to grow by 1% in 2026 and 2% in 2027. This gradual return to growth is supported by a steady expansion in residential building and civil engineering. This turning point supports Fabri's strategy of expanding its decentralized subsidiary network into high-growth regions.

Rising multifamily approvals: The recovery in German housing approvals is increasingly being led by larger multi- family housing developments. An Arbonia investor presentation on 28 August 2026 showed that approved residential units in German multi-family buildings grew by 12.7% in the first half of 2026. DNB Carnegie argues that multifamily projects represent a lucrative opportunity for serial consolidators because they require higher technical installation volume. This expansion provides Fabri AG with a strong pipeline of regional installation projects over the next two years.
