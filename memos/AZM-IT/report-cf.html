EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 finding

Azimut Holding is undergoing a profound transformation driven by the historic domestic distribution spin-off valued at EUR 1.2 billion, the transformational acquisition of Yapı Kredi Portföy for EUR 305 million creating a EUR 29 billion Turkish platform, and aggressive international expansion across the United States, Brazil, and Australia. This multi-dimensional strategic repositioning marks the most material change in the company's history, fundamentally reshaping its geographic footprint, capital structure, and earnings trajectory through to 2030.

The materiality of this change is underscored by management's Elevate 2030 ambition to double global assets to EUR 95-110 billion and deliver a 10% net profit CAGR, while simultaneously executing EUR 250 million in share buybacks and targeting 25% of market capitalization returned to shareholders by 2027. However, regulatory governance remediations at ACM SGR have delayed the TNB spin-off closing to December 2026, introducing execution uncertainty around this cornerstone value-creation event.

Top five to eight key drivers of forward earnings expansion include: First, the Turkish acquisition creating a combined EUR 29 billion platform with exclusive 15-year distribution access to 18 million bank clients and 10% average EPS accretion through 2029. Second, the TNB spin-off transferring 928 advisors and EUR 26 billion in assets with a 12-year EUR 2.4 billion revenue guarantee and enabling 25% market cap returns.

Third, private market scaling reaching EUR 8.6 billion in assets with EUR 2 billion targeted 2026 inflows. Fourth, the UniCredit joint venture Nova ramping product distribution toward EUR 4 billion in assets. Fifth, US multiboutique expansion through North Square Investments contributing 5% of near-term net profit. Sixth, favorable interest rate normalization triggering client cash sorting into higher-margin managed solutions.

Seventh, integrated production and distribution models protecting management fee margins at 1.56% on AUM against peer compression. Eighth, elite capital returns through debt-free operations and robust operating cash generation.

Recent delivery has been exceptionally strong, with H1 2026 revenue reaching EUR 781 million representing 21% year-on-year growth and operating profit of EUR 354 million reflecting a 10% recurring EBIT increase. Organic net inflows reached EUR 10.2 billion YTD by August 2026, demonstrating sustained commercial momentum. Critically, Azimut delivered a 13% revenue beat and 5% operating profit beat in Q2 2026 versus consensus, triggering a major Deutsche Bank upgrade to Buy with a 31% price target increase to EUR 46.

Management operates as a classic sandbagger, with FY 2025 net profit of EUR 515 million beating the EUR 500 million target and net inflows of EUR 32.1 billion exceeding the EUR 28-31 billion range. Forward credibility remains high for financial targets but moderate for strategic milestones. Azimut has systematically beaten conservative profit targets across consecutive fiscal cycles, establishing a reliable delivery track record.

FY 2026 guidance of EUR 550 million ordinary profit is 44% achieved at mid-year, providing high visibility. However, regulatory delays pushed back the TNB digital bank transaction to late 2026, creating execution risk around the EUR 1.2 billion proceeds and capital distribution timeline. Earnings exhibit low sensitivity to public market cycles due to 1.56% management fee margins, 10% Turkish EPS accretion, and EUR 2 billion private alternatives cushion. - Earnings Calls Sell-side positioning has shifted materially positive following robust operating beats and strategic M&A announcements.

Deutsche Bank upgraded Azimut to Buy from Hold on September 3, 2026, raising the price target to EUR 46 from EUR 35, citing an attractive 9.3x adjusted 2027E P/E versus the domestic asset gatherer median of 15.8x. Sell-side analysts upgraded near-term earnings projections by 12% on average, with Deutsche Bank delivering a 16% EPS upgrade for 2027 and 15% for 2028. Consensus emphasizes the highmargin Turkish integration as a primary driver of forward expansion.

However, Bernstein Research maintains a Neutral rating, questioning TNB execution timelines and group-level cost inefficiencies. Fundamental peer positioning reveals elite operational efficiency but lagging valuation multiples. Azimut operates at a best-in-class 28% cost-to-income ratio versus Banca Mediolanum's 36.1% and delivers management fee margins of 1.38% exceeding Fineco's 68 basis points and Amundi's 16 basis points. Organic net new money is projected to inflect to +7% over 2026-2028 from -7% during 2023-2025, marking a structural reversal. Revenue growth of 21% in H1 2026 leads domestic peers, yet Azimut trades at 9.3x adjusted 2027E P/E compared to Fineco's 18.6x and Banca Mediolanum's 15.7x, reflecting a persistent complexity discount. Deutsche Bank upgraded near-term earnings projections by 16% for 2027, leading the domestic sector average of 7%.

Activism status is benign, with no active activist presence and internal managers controlling 21.4% via Timone Fiduciaria, while major institutional investors including JPMorgan Asset Management (3.0%), Helikon Investments (3.4%), and BNP Paribas Asset Management (5.8%) provide stable long-term backing. This aligned shareholder structure enables management to execute multi-year strategic plans unhindered.

Outliers worth noting

The Yapı Kredi Portföy acquisition creates a merged platform of EUR 29 billion in Turkey, securing Azimut's position as the nation's second-largest asset manager while locking in a 15-year exclusive distribution agreement providing privileged access to 18 million retail banking clients. This distribution partnership duration represents an extreme statistical outlier, exceeding the European asset management sector median of 5- 7 year exclusive distribution agreements by more than double, and positioning Azimut in the top 2% of longesttenured bank distribution partnerships globally.

However, hyperinflation within Turkish operations requires IAS 29 adjustments that distort EMEA segment reporting, creating unprecedented accounting complexity that obscures true underlying profitability and introduces severe foreign exchange translation risk absent from domestic Italian peer operations [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 6].
