Executive Summary 13 findings

Section summary
  1. OMV Petrom delivered operationally robust Q2 2026 results with a 27% increase in operating profit, yet the investment case remains bifurcated between near-term cash flow pressure from peak capital expenditure and an exceptional structural earnings inflection anticipated post-2027 driven by Neptun Deep's low-cost Black Sea gas production. OMV Petrom operates as the largest integrated energy producer in Southeastern Europe, managing the Petrobrazi refinery with a 4.5 million ton annual capacity and covering more than one-third of Romania's fuels and gas consumption. The company's consolidated net income fell from RON 4,190 million in 2024 to RON 3,058 million in 2025, representing a material 27% year-on-year decline driven by compressed refining cracks, higher operating costs, and punitive Romanian fiscal measures. However, the Q2 2026 Clean CCS Operating Result rose to RON 1.51 billion, beating unconstrained market estimates by 11.85% and surpassing the prior year by 27%.
  2. OMV Petrom stands on the cusp of a major earnings inflection driven by Neptun Deep, but faces

The most significant strategic development involves management's pragmatic capital reallocation, wherein OMV Petrom shifted approximately EUR 1 billion of capital from still-maturing low-carbon technologies back to traditional exploration and production opportunities, while reducing the investment weight of the Low Carbon Business segment to 25% of total gross investments from the previous target of 35%. This pivot enabled an upgraded 2030 total production target of approximately 170 kboe/d, representing a clear increase from the previous guidance of 160 kboe/d.

The Neptun Deep offshore gas project remains the cornerstone of the long-term investment thesis. The flagship project in the Black Sea is now over 85% complete, with first gas firmly on track for 1H27, and Morgan Stanley noted that the project is increasingly de-risked after the successful installation of the 16,500-ton production platform. Neptun Deep is expected to fundamentally transform OMV Petrom's upstream economics, driving overall exploration and production costs down from $18/boe today to $9/boe by 2030.

This structural cost transformation represents the primary value creation mechanism that underpins the multi-year earnings inflection.

However, substantial near-term headwinds constrain immediate cash generation and dividend capacity. The group is currently navigating the most capital-intensive period in its corporate history, having invested a record RON 7.84 billion (approximately EUR 1.6 billion) in 2025, with management intending to sustain this momentum by targeting a yearly average investment of EUR 1.10 billion through 2030. Due to this record-high capital expenditure cycle, management expects free cash flow before dividends to turn negative for the full year

Examining consensus deviations, the Q2 2026 results demonstrated mixed performance across segments. Exploration and Production reported a Clean Operating Result of RON 888 million, rising 35% year-on-year and beating consensus expectations of RON 810 million on higher oil and gas realizations. Refining and Marketing benefited from an indicator refining margin of USD 22.67/bbl (surpassing the USD 10.27/bbl in Q2/25), allowing the division to deliver ahead of conservative broker forecasts.

Conversely, Gas and Power Clean Operating Result turned into a RON 165 million loss (compared to a loss of RON 46 million in Q2/25), undershooting the sellside expectations of a minor profit due to the Brazi power plant's extended outage. Operating cash flow fell by 38% to RON 1.20 billion, which substantially lagged the consensus forecast of RON 1.70 billion due to unfavorable inventory valuation adjustments.

The investment thesis evolution reflects a shift from transition-led to hydrocarbon-focused value creation. The group modified its decarbonization pathway by slashing its 2030 carbon intensity reduction target to only 10% from the prior 20%, and management decided to entirely withdraw its previously communicated withdrawn absolute Scope 1-3 targets while retaining its focus on Scopes 1 and 2. This strategic repacing aligns with regional energy security priorities but introduces long-term regulatory risk if European Union decarbonization mandates accelerate faster than management currently anticipates.

From an earnings trajectory perspective, the 18-month to 3-year outlook presents a distinctive J-curve profile. The company faces a challenging FY2026 characterized by negative free cash flow and compressed net margins due to peak capital intensity and regulatory interventions. However, management reaffirmed its target to deliver a Clean CCS ROACE of approximately 15% by 2030, supported by Neptun Deep's low-cost production structure.

CEO Christina Verchere reconfirmed at the 2026 Capital Markets Day that the company intends to grow its profitability by targeting a remarkable 50% increase in EBIT by 2030 through its integrated portfolio transition. Assessing IRR potential, the current share price performance suggests the market is beginning to discount the Neptun Deep value inflection. During 2025, OMV Petrom's share price appreciated by 40%, significantly outperforming its regional peers (excluding Orlen and Neste) by an average of 11 percentage points, though the stock slightly underperformed the local Romanian BET index by 5.8 percentage points over the same period.

The pathway to 25%+ IRR depends critically on three interdependent factors: successful execution of the 1H27 Neptun Deep first gas timeline without operational delays, normalization of Romanian regulatory interventions to allow unconstrained capture of low-cost gas economics, and sustained European natural gas prices in the EUR 25-35/MWh range that management has embedded in its 2027-2030 planning assumptions.

The sell-side response to recent results has been constructive but cautious. Jefferies analyst Wilson reiterated a buy rating and €52 target on the parent company, reflecting the strong operational contribution of the Romanian unit, and subsequently raised the price target to €66, reflecting elevated refining margins. Morgan Stanley analyst Levy maintained an equal-weight rating on the parent firm with a price target of €65.50 following the Romanian site visit.

The key debate among analysts centers on whether the current valuation adequately reflects both the transformational upstream cost structure and the persistent regulatory discount that Romanian political volatility commands. Looking at competitive positioning, OMV Petrom's integrated structure provides defensive characteristics during the transition period. Regional peer MOL Hungarian Oil and Gas reported a 23% decline in Consumer Services EBITDA in Q2 2026 due to restrictive price controls, and this margin compression occurred despite resilient fuel volumes across Hungary and Slovakia.

Regional peer INA completed its EUR 700 million delayed coker unit in Rijeka to strengthen downstream product yields and regional supply security, while Russian-backed NIS AD Gazprom Neft is conducting a strategic review of its downstream and upstream operations in Romania and Bulgaria, explicitly considering a market exit due to complex local operating challenges.

The most important investment risk remains the tension between capital deployment timing and regulatory predictability. While management has de-risked project execution through early budget commitments, the persistent risk of unpredictable politically motivated fiscal changes and arbitrary extensions of domestic price caps could continue to suppress free cash flow, and these state interventions could jeopardize OMV Petrom's mid-term dividend capacity and dilute the highly anticipated economic returns of Neptun Deep if low-cost gas remains captive to artificially regulated local prices.

In summary, OMV Petrom presents a compelling asymmetric risk-reward profile for investors with a threeyear horizon willing to accept near-term cash flow volatility and regulatory uncertainty in exchange for structural upstream cost transformation and material EBIT growth from 2027 onwards, though achieving 25%+ IRR requires flawless operational execution, normalization of Romanian fiscal interventions, and sustained regional gas pricing at levels that justify the landmark offshore investment.

Section 16 – Conclusion

OMV Petrom's Q2 2026 earnings delivered a 27% increase in Clean CCS Operating Result to RON 1.51 billion, beating unconstrained market estimates by approximately 12% and demonstrating robust operational resilience despite a challenging regulatory environment. However, the 12% decline in Clean CCS Net Income to RON 1.10 billion missed buy-side whisper targets due to the temporary solidarity tax imposed under OUG 19/2026, revealing how Romanian fiscal interventions systematically cap the earnings upside that would otherwise flow from favorable commodity prices and strong refining margins.

The single most important change emerging from this earnings cycle is management's successful installation of the 16,500-ton Neptun Alpha platform and completion of six of ten planned wells, which significantly de-risks the 1H27 first gas timeline and positions the company to deliver a Clean CCS ROACE of approximately 15% by 2030. This landmark offshore gas project is expected to drive overall E&P production costs down from $18/boe today to $9/boe by 2030, fundamentally transforming upstream economics through low-cost Black Sea barrels that will offset legacy asset declines.

The strategic capital reallocation of approximately EUR 1 billion away from less mature low-carbon technologies back toward traditional exploration and production opportunities, reducing the low-carbon investment weight from 35% to 25%, reflects a pragmatic adaptation to realistic regional transition speeds and secures clearer near-term returns.

Yet the investment case faces major consensus deviations centered on cash flow timing and regulatory sustainability. Management's guidance for negative free cash flow before dividends in full-year 2026, driven by record capital expenditure of RON 9 billion and a drawdown of the net cash position toward zero or net debt by year-end 2025, represents a significant departure from historic cash accumulation patterns.

The Gas & Power segment's RON 165 million loss and a 38% decline in operating cash flow to RON 1.20 billion substantially lagged consensus expectations, underscoring how regulatory price caps and extended power plant outages can rapidly erode downstream contributions. The extension of the 0.5% turnover tax through end- 2026 and the progressive solidarity contribution that resulted in a RON 162 million windfall tax in Q2 2026 illustrate the persistent unpredictability of Romanian fiscal policy.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish post-2027 inflection narrative is that any delays in the 1H27 Neptun Deep drilling schedule could expose OMV Petrom to extended mature production declines without the safety net of high unconstrained gas prices, while unpredictable politically motivated fiscal changes and arbitrary extensions of domestic price caps could continue to suppress free cash flow, jeopardizing the company's mid-term dividend capacity and diluting the highly anticipated economic returns of Neptun Deep if lowcost gas remains captive to artificially regulated local prices. The specific evidence that would change this view includes sustained uncapped downstream margins following the expiration of OUG 19/2026 provisions, formal legislative closure of the 15-year license extension without further royalty or supplementary tax increases, and visible production ramp-up milestones at Neptun Deep that confirm the first gas timeline without operational setbacks. An outlier call-out deserving investor attention is the strategic importance of OMV Petrom to its parent group OMV AG, where Morgan Stanley values the 51% stake at EUR 7 billion, representing approximately 30% of the parent's total market capitalization and highlighting the subsidiary's critical role as a key engine of value and cash generation for the broader group.

This creates a structural floor valuation that may prevent severe share price dislocations even if near-term cash flow remains under regulatory pressure. Additionally, the forced exit of more than 12 gas suppliers from the Romanian market due to volatile pricing and regulatory caps has allowed vertically integrated utilities like OMV Petrom to capture significant market share, reinforcing the
