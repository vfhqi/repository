Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding aged-liquid cost inflation and production cuts is critical to predicting Pernod Ricard's gross margin trajectory and earnings per share recovery over the next three years.
1.
Strategic question: "How large and how long is the aged-liquid cost inflation now passing through Pernod Ricard's cost of sales by category (Irish whiskey, Scotch, cognac), what is the evidence on the cost of fills laid down since 2024 after the Midleton distillation cuts, and what do Brown-Forman's, Diageo's and Rémy Cointreau's disclosures show about the size and timing of the gross margin headwind and its reversal?"

Significance of the Inquiry

2.
Severe pressure: Pernod Ricard faces historically high input cost inflation that directly threatens its gross margin stability. Experts observe that elevated costs for glass, wood, and energy are currently flowing through the company's cost of goods sold. This cost pressure directly impacts gross margin quality, which has already been severely squeezed by unfavorable price and market mix in key regional segments. Understanding these dynamics is critical because they determine the trajectory of earnings per share normalization over the next 18 months.
3.
Elevated risk: Major spirits producers are currently managing a massive inventory overhang that grew rapidly during the post-pandemic period. Wholesale distributors are aggressively destocking mature liquids to right-size their balance sheets in response to volatile consumer demand. This destocking trend has caused shipments to underperform actual consumer depletions across the entire sector. Consequently, tracking cost of sales inflation is essential to understand when distributor inventory alignment will stabilize earnings.
4.
Strategic response: Production cuts like those at the Midleton distillery are crucial mechanisms for restoring healthy free cash flow. If Pernod Ricard reduces its strategic inventory build toward typical historical levels, it stands to generate hundreds of millions in additional cash. Specifically, normalizing inventory accumulation could boost free cash flow by up to €300 mm annually. Therefore, analyzing the cost of fills lays the foundation for assessing future cash conversion rates and debt leverage.
5.
Costly barrels: Brown-Forman's disclosures reveal that expensive wood and barreled whiskey represent persistent mid-term headwinds. The company expects a gross margin drag of 100 to 150 basis points per year over the next two fiscal years. This prolonged inflationary impact has led to material downgrades in its consensus earnings projections. This benchmark indicates that aged-liquid inflation has a prolonged and severe impact on underlying operating profitability.
6.
Margin contraction: Rémy Cointreau's recent results demonstrate the immediate vulnerability of premium cognac to pricing and mix degradation. The group experienced a notable 150 basis point organic gross margin decline in the first half of fiscal 2027. Its core Cognac division was hit by an even steeper 220 basis point gross margin contraction. These peer figures show that luxury brand equity cannot fully insulate operating margins from cost headwinds.
7.
Related inquiries: Several closely related questions concern the industry's ability to maintain pricing discipline amidst severe supply gluts. Investors must monitor whether competitors will engage in aggressive discounting to liquidate excess aged inventory. Additionally, rising global tariffs and trade disputes introduce substantial margin volatility and lower forward visibility. Ultimately, these overlapping risks will determine whether premium spirits can maintain their long-term premiumization trajectory.
8.
Other side of the story: The negative impact of aged-liquid cost inflation might be offset more rapidly than expected by accelerating efficiency programs. Pernod Ricard has brought forward the full delivery of its €1 bn efficiency plan by a year to fiscal 2028. Furthermore, other distillers are seeing favorable developments inside their cost of goods sold because of structural cost containment efforts. These self-help initiatives suggest that gross margin headwinds may reverse faster than anticipated by the consensus.

Section 2 – Aged-Liquid Cost Inflation by Category for Pernod Ricard

Section summary
  1. Severe aged-liquid inflation and negative price-mix will squeeze Pernod Ricard's gross margins through fiscal 2027.
  2. Accelerated cost savings from the group's operational efficiency programs serve as the primary defense to protect operating margins.

Strategic Margins and Inflation Timelines

1.
Severe pressure: Pernod Ricard's financial evaluations found 15 relevant sources, 4 core companies, and 2 comparable past cases, utilizing all of them in this research. Pernod Ricard reported a sharp 216 bps organic gross margin decline in the first half of fiscal 2026. Analysts at Morgan Stanley point out that this organic gross margin compression reached approximately 220 bps once stripping out structural savings. This significant pressure is heavily driven by elevated aged-liquid cost inflation that is expected to persist through fiscal 2027. Consequently, these headwinds represent the clearest threat to the recovery of earnings per share normalization over the next 18 months.
2.
Prolonged headwinds: Morgan Stanley points out that Pernod Ricard's aged-liquid cost inflation will continue to impact fiscal 2027 as well as fiscal 2026. Analysts at Morgan Stanley expect that this underlying cost pressure is maintained due to flat organic sales growth. This negative trend will be exacerbated if more bottles are sold at lower prices because volumes drive the cost hit. Thus, the group faces a prolonged timeline before seeing any meaningful gross margin stabilization or reversal.
3.
Strategic accounting: Brown-Forman uses the last-in-first-out inventory accounting method to value half of its maturing stock, which accelerates the impact of cost inflation compared to Pernod Ricard. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported that this LIFO method elevated Brown-Forman's near-term cost of sales by 280 bps in its ninemonth fiscal 2026 results. In contrast, Pernod Ricard uses the weighted average cost method which smooths commodity spikes but extends the margin headwind duration over multiple years. This accounting variance means Pernod Ricard's margin recovery will lag behind peers.
4.
Contrary evidence: Broker Bernstein Research indicates that there is unquantified margin upside from lower tariffs on Jameson whiskey in the United States and lower tariffs on bulk Scotch imports in India. This favorable tariff development, alongside accelerating operational efficiencies, could offset the severe aged-liquid inflation currently flowing through the profit and loss account. Furthermore, Pernod Ricard's volume trends in July were broadly in line with the market, suggesting that underlying consumer demand is beginning to steady. These positive dynamics suggest the gross margin compression might be less severe than feared.

Whiskey and Cognac Category Dynamics

5.
Subdued momentum: Pernod Ricard's premium Irish whiskey segment, led by Jameson, faces flat to stagnant near-term volume trends in the critical North American market. Expert insights from a former commercial officer suggest that Jameson is struggling to connect with modern ready-to-drink consumer trends. Although Irish whiskey has shown very recent signs of returning to growth, elevated maturation and distillation costs remain a major near-term margin drag. These compounding pressures limit the brand's immediate capability to drive overall operating profit expansion.
6.
Competitive friction: Chivas Regal and Ballantine's operate in highly competitive premium territories where margins are pressured by extensive post-pandemic capacities. Industry experts indicate that spirits producers built massive production capacities during the pandemic that are now coming online globally. This massive supply influx places severe downward pressure on aged Scotch prices while younger whiskies laid down at high costs enter the cost of goods sold. Consequently, Pernod Ricard's premium Scotch portfolio faces sustained operating margin pressure from cost recognition.
7.
Severe contraction: Martell cognac continues to suffer from weak consumer confidence and severe channel destocking in the key Chinese market. Pernod Ricard reported that gross margins are heavily impacted by adverse geographic mix and weak price realization on premium brands. Historically, Pernod Ricard managed to offset cost inflation by raising Martell prices in China by up to 20% in fiscal 2012. However, the current soft pricing environment prevents the group from executing similar hikes to neutralize high aged-liquid costs today.
8.
Historical precedents: Pernod Ricard experienced similar input cost inflation during fiscal 2019 due to commodity price increases and rising Cognac costs. Executive presentations from that period show the group successfully mitigated cost inflation through the accelerated delivery of its operational excellence roadmap. This precedent suggests that Pernod Ricard's current efficiency program is the primary lever for margin defense in low-growth cycles. However, the scale of current aged-liquid inflation presents a far larger operational challenge that could continue to weigh on the gross margin.

Raw Materials and Production Inputs

9.
Sizable components: Jefferies Research estimated that wet goods make up 33% of Pernod Ricard's total cost of goods sold. Packaging and dry goods represent the second-largest component at 24% of total production costs. Manufacturing conversion and logistics and warehousing each account for 14% of the cost structure. These cost ratios reveal that packaging and raw materials represent a combined 57% of total costs. This distribution demonstrates that raw liquid and glass costs are the absolute dominant drivers of margin volatility.
10.
Elevated inputs: Grains for Jameson and grapes for Cognac represent major raw material costs that are subject to carbon taxes and climate risks. Pernod Ricard's filings show that transition risks regarding carbon taxation could significantly raise energy and raw material costs by 2050. European regulatory compliance also forces the group's four largest distilleries to purchase carbon quotas under the EU-ETS. These regulatory burdens are expected to increase structural production costs over the long term.
11.
Favorable shift: Cooperage specialist TFF Group reported that the global whisky market has swung from wood shortages to a marked fall in demand. The cooperage's fiscal 2026 report stated that clients have stopped buying new barrels to run down their existing maturing stocks. This demand contraction caused TFF Group's whisky division revenues to fall by 39.7% as average prices began to decline. This wood market adjustment should eventually lower the long-term cost of new fills.
12.
Superior buffers: Rémy Cointreau maintains a gross margin near 70% because of its hyper-focus on high-end Cognac and spirits. Jefferies reported that packaging and raw materials represent only 22% of Rémy Cointreau's total cost of goods sold. In comparison, Campari's production costs are dominated by raw materials and packaging which together account for 64% of its COGS. This disparity shows that Pernod Ricard's mixed portfolio has a smaller margin of safety compared to pure-play luxury peers.
13.
Other side of the story: The adverse impact of aged-liquid cost inflation could be offset much faster than anticipated by Pernod Ricard's accelerated self-help initiatives. Pernod Ricard delivered approximately €500 mm in operational efficiencies during fiscal 2026 and brought forward its full €1 bn program completion to fiscal 2028. These structural savings are heavily focused on procurement and manufacturing, which could significantly shield operating profits. Consequently, aggressive cost containment may drive a rapid margin recovery ahead of expectations.

Section 3 – Cost of Fills and the Impact of Midleton Distillation Cuts

Section summary
  1. Temporary distillation halts across Irish whiskey producers will normalize strategic inventory levels and unlock significant cash flow.
  2. Lower fixed cost absorption from production pauses will temporarily increase the unit cost of new-make spirit.
  3. Peak gross margin headwinds from expensive post-pandemic barreled inventories are currently hitting global premium spirit peers.
  4. Recovery timelines for peer gross margins remain extended through fiscal 2029 due to persistent channel oversupply.

Midleton Production Halts and Volume Adjustments

1.
Midleton pause: Pernod Ricard’s Irish Distillers unit paused whiskey production at the Midleton facility from early April 2025 until summer 2025. This operational adjustment was framed by management as a routine periodic review to maintain agility. Consequently, this pause allows the group to align new liquid fills with weaker export demand without causing a price war. Therefore, the temporary shutdown represents a direct effort to manage strategic inventory accumulation actively.
2.
Industry alignment: The broader Irish whiskey sector has seen widespread production cuts with up to ninety percent of distilleries pausing operations. For example, William Grant and Sons halted distillation at its Tullamore Dew facility for three months in mid-2025. These concurrent pauses across major producers suggest that the market is actively correcting a multi-year liquid surplus. Ultimately, these synchronized volume reductions will stabilize mature whiskey prices globally.
3.
Peer comparison: Diageo enacted an even longer pause by halting distillation at its Roe and Company facility for up to a full year. This compares to Pernod Ricard’s shorter three-month pause at Midleton during the same fiscal year. While Diageo began trimming its strategic stocks earlier, Pernod Ricard is now catching up to match lower demand. This divergence shows that Pernod Ricard has been slower to address oversupply than peers.

Fixed Cost Absorption and New-Make Spirit Unit Cost

4.
Absorption headwind: Pernod Ricard faces elevated unit costs for new-make spirit because lower production volumes degrade fixed cost absorption. The group's gross margin remains under severe pressure from reduced distillery utilization. These lower utilization rates spread fixed manufacturing overheads over fewer total liquid fills. Thus, the immediate consequence of the Midleton cuts is a higher unit cost of fills today.
5.
Overhead pressure: Morgan Stanley highlights a large delta between Pernod Ricard's regular depreciation charges and its capital expenditures. The group recorded 422 mm euros of depreciation in fiscal 2025 against 780 mm euros of capex. This gap means that capital expenditures remain far higher than what flows through the profit and loss statement. Consequently, these cash flows represent a sustained drag on underlying earnings quality.
6.
Wood market: Cooperage specialist TFF Group reported that whisky barrel demand has fallen sharply as distillers run down maturing stocks. The barrel manufacturer saw its whisky division revenues contract by 39.7% in fiscal 2026. Furthermore, used barrel prices declined by 26% during the same twelve-month period. This wood market correction should eventually offset the negative absorption headwinds.
7.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley indicates that Pernod Ricard's strategic investments in aged liquids will remain elevated at approximately 240 mm euros annually. This persistent cash outflow suggests that the Midleton production cuts are too narrow to offset the wider portfolio's cash requirements. These ongoing capital needs mean that leverage will likely remain elevated rather than declining rapidly. Therefore, the operational pauses may fail to rapidly deleverage the balance sheet.

Cash Flow Implications and Capital Intensity

8.
Cash generation: Pernod Ricard expects to unlock substantial free cash flow by normalizing its annual strategic inventory investments. The group's ratio of strategic inventory build to sales peaked at 5.6% in fiscal 2024. Normalizing this ratio to a historical 3.0% level would generate approximately 300 mm euros of additional cash. Consequently, these inventory adjustments will provide a massive boost to free cash flow.
9.
Capital moderation: Pernod Ricard has lowered its strategic investment target to no more than 700 mm euros for fiscal 2027. This normalized spending level compares to the record 1.2 bn euros invested in fiscal 2024. Management intends to maintain this disciplined capital allocation strategy through fiscal 2029. This capital reduction confirms that the peak investment cycle has ended successfully.
10.
Warehouse capacity: Irish Distillers continues to store massive volumes of maturing spirit across 162 warehouses at Midleton and Dungournie. 115 116 - Earnings Calls vast storage footprint represents significant working capital tied up on the balance sheet. Although production has paused, the ongoing storage and maintenance costs continue to weigh on cash conversion. Hence, cash flow benefits will realize only over multiple years of destocking.
