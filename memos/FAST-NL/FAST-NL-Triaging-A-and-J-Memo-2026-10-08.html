[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Fastned is a demand-inflection case with a proven operating-leverage engine inside it: a doubling European battery-electric fleet is filling premium motorway charging sites whose operating cost per charger does not move with volume, so 38% volume growth became 110% operational EBITDA growth in H1 2026. The engine is proven, the balance sheet is thin, and the price is early: at EUR 44.05 the shares already pay 48x a consensus-mean 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 0.9113 for earnings that only begin in FY2028, so the three-year return clears the framework's bar and the eighteen-month return does not.

  • The external change is structural, sector-wide and policy-backed, and it is reaching the company faster than the fleet grows.
    • European battery-electric registrations rose 38% year-on-year in H1 2026 and 51.1% in July 2026, the fleet passed 11 million, and AFIR, the kept EU 2035 target, German purchase incentives and the March 2026 Düsseldorf ruling on open Autobahn tenders give visibility beyond two years.
    • Fastned's energy delivered rose 38% in H1 2026 and 44% in Q2 2026
      • Organic volume at existing stations rose 25.1% against weighted fleet growth of 3% on the company's measure, so the company is taking share of a growing pool, not only riding it.
    • Part of the Q2 2026 acceleration from 32% to 44% was the spring 2026 fuel-price spike of 15% to 20% at European pumps
      • That part is reversible, and ING and Deutsche Bank expect some normalisation.
      • The structural part is the fleet, which keeps charging whatever petrol costs.
  • The internal change is scale-up discipline, not corporate transformation, and it is secondary but real.
    • The founder chief executive has led since 2012
    • What changed in 2025 and 2026 is the strategy (Build-Grow-Optimize, selective tenders on return hurdles, decentralised country management), the standards (central procurement over about EUR 20m of indirect spend, zero-based budgeting, permits +89% year-on-year) and the funding (a EUR 200m green bank facility beside EUR 337m of retail bonds).
    • The one guidance raise of the company's recent history came on 13 August 2026: FY2026 operational EBITDA margin from 35% to 40% to about 45% on charging revenue, after a half at 50%.
      • It followed two years of station-target cuts, both grid-driven.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have moved decisively at the operating level and have not yet reached earnings per share or cash.
    • Revenue rose 45% in H1 2026 (total IFRS basis), operational EBITDA 110% and IFRS EBITDA fivefold, because network operating cost per charger stayed flat at about EUR 20.8k and SG&A fell from 71% to 66% of sales
    • The sell-side raised FY2026 EBITDA by 16% to 25% and targets at all three covering houses after the print.
    • The IFRS net loss was still EUR 13.0m in H1 2026 and free cash flow EUR -55m
      • On our path EPS is EUR -1.38 in FY2026 and EUR -0.56 in FY2027, and turns positive at about EUR 0.51 in FY2028.
      • Free cash flow turns about FY2030.
    • The change has therefore travelled through the company's choices, its actions, its key operating metrics, its financial statements and the sell-side's EBITDA estimates.
      • The last layer, EPS and cash, is where the leverage of the model makes the outcome large in both directions, which is why the brokers' FY2028 EPS runs from EUR -0.31 to EUR 4.67 across seven houses.
  • The framework's growth band cannot be met from a negative base, and from the first positive year the growth is far above it.
    • On our path EPS compounds at about 91% a year from FY2028 (EUR 0.51) to FY2031 (EUR 3.56)
    • The framework marks growth above 40% down hard for over-extrapolation, and in a 'Demand Inflection' case the growth is treated as cyclical until proven structural.
    • We rate the band test D and say plainly that the direction and magnitude are right and the base-rate risk is the framework's own.
  • What it costs is the whole question.
    • The shares trade at 48.3x consensus-mean 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 0.9113, about 183x on our own 24-month-forward figure of about EUR 0.24, and about 18.9x FY2027 consensus IFRS EBITDA on an enterprise value of about EUR 1,228m (EUR 859m of market capitalisation plus EUR 369m of net debt, our arithmetic).
    • The stock's own history is a range of multiples on near-zero earnings (five-year range 32.76x to 56,974x) and is unusable
      • The peer set (10.5x to 24.8x) is equipment makers on mature earnings and does not fit.
      • The multiple is company-driven, which means it is ours to earn and ours to lose.
    • The price has moved with the catalysts, not against them: the shares rose from a 52-week low of EUR 19.88 to EUR 44.05, +106.3% over twelve months, and the bank facility, the German ruling, the margin raise and the London joint venture were all public by 13 August 2026.
  • The modal return is about 10.3% a year over eighteen months and about 21.5% a year over three years, with no cash return.
    • On the framework's convention we value the shares at 28x our 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 1.82 in April 2028 (EUR 51.0) and at 24x EUR 3.29 in October 2029 (EUR 79.0)
    • The multiples are 50/50 blends of a cleared branch (35x and 30x) and an uncleared branch (21x and 18x), and the blend is the swing factor.
    • Earnings growth carries the return (a factor of 2.00 at eighteen months and 3.61 at three years on the 24-month-forward figure) and the re-rating takes most of it away (factors of 0.580 and 0.497).
      • At 21x the eighteen-month value is EUR 38.2, a loss
      • At 35x it is EUR 63.7, 27.9% a year.
    • The adverse drop-through (interest EUR 6m a year higher from FY2029, tax at 25% from FY2029, dilution of 25% rather than 15%) takes the eighteen-month return to a loss of 12.9% a year and the three-year to 15.4% a year
      • The 'Modal return' would be D and this Building Block would be C.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', fitting crisply enough for a B.
    • The mandatory demand condition passes at B, dominant position is B and fixed-cost leverage is A
    • The fit's limits are an industry that is not an oligopoly in behaviour (Electra at EUR 0.29/kWh in France against Fastned's EUR 0.59 to EUR 0.71/kWh) and a scalability that needs EUR 0.5m to EUR 0.6m of capex per site.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the fleet to the sessions to the kWh to operational EBITDA, every link measured quarterly in the company's own building blocks.
    • It breaks, or at least blurs, at the fourth link, operational EBITDA to EPS, which passes through network expansion costs of about EUR 45m a year, D&A rising to EUR 60.5m by FY2028 on consensus, net interest of EUR 24m to EUR 36m on our path, tax arriving from FY2029 and a share count that rises with a probable equity raise of about EUR 120m in 2027 or 2028.
    • The company guides none of these.

  • The case therefore is: compelling as a business change, conditionally compelling as an investment at EUR 44.05, and worth progressing with an entry discipline rather than buying here.

    • What we are betting on is that a proven operating engine turns a doubling fleet into EPS compounding above 40% a year from FY2028 while a thin, leveraged balance sheet gets through one equity raise and the market keeps paying a growth multiple as the first full years of profit arrive.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material in the framework's sense: the input forces are strong and evidenced ('Change Forces' B), they have reached the operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's EBITDA lines ('Financial Outputs' B), and what is left to travel is the last layer, EPS and cash, which the leverage of the model makes large

    • The 'Modal return' from EUR 44.05 is only a C because the price moved first.
    • Not C, which was live on the 'Modal return' alone, because this Building Block measures materiality
    • The price question is carried to the 'Investment Setup', the entry discipline and the 'Investment Case'.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest, is the "false friend" profile: a network scaling superficially while returns dilute, dressed up by two artefacts and a thin market.
      • The volume surge is a fuel-price artefact (pump prices +15% to 20% in spring 2026; growth from 32% to 44% in one quarter) and the margin surge an e-credit artefact (credit prices doubled in Germany, France and Belgium; Renewable Energy Units were EUR 13.2m of FY2025 revenue at near-100% margin; EUR 5.1m deferred in H1 2026).
      • The network's 13% average utilisation against 38% at the top five sites says every added site dilutes returns and the 1,000-station push adds the worst sites last.
      • The Dutch motorway share of over 60% cannot survive the 2028 to 2036 concession roll under rules that prohibit adjacent sites and cap local share at 50%.
      • Net debt of EUR 369m is 15.5x FY2026 forecast operational EBITDA, EUR 337m of retail bonds must roll, consensus equity falls to EUR 58.5m against EUR 640m of debt by FY2028, and a 7.5x debt-to-equity covenant has unquantified terms
        • There is no room for a miss.
      • The outside holder has no vote: the FAST foundation holds 100% of the votes and refuses proxies.
      • The shares have doubled on one half-year, in a stock trading EUR 2m to EUR 4m a day with a 24% free float, to 48x an EPS figure that six of seven brokers put below the mean.
        • On this reading the modal return is negative and the downside fair value is EUR 15, Jefferies' own downside case.
    • What we concede.
      • The e-credit dependence is real and is the single largest margin risk
      • The fuel-price component of Q2 2026 is real and reversible
      • The leverage is at the limit and an equity raise is in our path and not in the brokers'
      • The Dutch concession roll is a dated erosion of the best asset
      • The governance is an outlier we would not design
      • And the multiple will fall.
    • Why we still hold the view: the operating leverage depends on neither artefact.
      • Network operating cost per charger fell from EUR 22.6k to EUR 20.8k and SG&A from 71% to 66% of sales on volume alone
      • Gross profit per kWh of EUR 0.59 is about EUR 0.53 without the e-credit step on the FY2025 basis, which on our arithmetic is still about a 45% operational EBITDA margin at the H1 2026 cost base.
      • Organic growth of 25.1% was measured at existing sites in H1 2026 and exceeded the Q1 2026 figure of 21% that ING used for its share-loss argument.
      • The session-capture advantage (2.5x to 3x the sessions of forecourt add-ons at co-located sites; an 80% share where Fastned competes with legacy forecourts against a typical 30% to 50%) is a location fact that neither petrol prices nor e-credits create.
      • Oil-major retrenchment from charging capex and the German court ruling widened the moat in the same half the bear case says it narrowed
        • And five banks lent EUR 200m against the stations in January 2026, which is the market's own test of the asset quality the bear case doubts.
    • Where the case against would win.
      • If organic volume growth at existing stations falls below 10% with crude under EUR 70 a barrel for six months, or e-credit prices halve, the engine's inputs fail and the leverage runs in reverse on a balance sheet that cannot absorb it.
      • We do not hold the view at any price: at EUR 44.05 the three-year return clears the bar and the eighteen-month does not, and the adverse drop-through turns the eighteen-month into a loss.
      • The view is bullish on the business and neutral on the shares here.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the external demand force is among the strongest and best-evidenced in the universe (European battery-electric registrations +38% year-on-year in H1 2026; the fleet past 11 million) and the internal forces are several and real, but the internal change is evolutionary scale-up rather than a multi-dimensional corporate change, and one external force, grid access, runs against the case.
  • Direction of the forces: demand is a strong structural tailwind, regulation a tailwind, the company's own cycle is accelerating, the macro tailwind (fuel prices) is reversible, competition is mixed, and grid access is a headwind the company does not control.
  • Not A: there is no chief-executive change, no new strategy in kind, and a supply-side headwind outside the company's control.
  • The forces are strong enough to carry the thesis
    • How far they have propagated is the question 'Financial Outputs' answers next.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand is changing structurally at the sector level with regulatory visibility beyond two years, and the company's own volumes are growing faster than the fleet.
  • Registrations: European battery-electric registrations +38% year-on-year in H1 2026, the fleet past 11 million
    • France +62.9%, Germany +48.0%, Denmark +41.2% in H1 2026.
    • July 2026 registrations +51.1% year-on-year, penetration 25.4%.
  • Fleet penetration: Germany 24.8% in H1 2026 from 17.7% a year earlier
    • France 28.2% from 17.6%
    • The UK 25.0% from 21.6%
    • The Netherlands 36.5% from 35.0% despite registrations -34.3% on the company-car tax cliff at end-2025.
    • The fleet, not registrations, drives charging.
  • Company volume: energy delivered 112.4 GWh in H1 2026, +38%
    • Q2 2026 56.9 GWh, +44%
    • Organic volume at existing stations +25.1% in H1 2026 against weighted European fleet growth of 3% as the company measures it (13 August 2026).
    • The 'Demand Inflection' test (sector-level; structural, or cyclical with two-plus years' visibility; industry data and policy) is met.
  • Against: 70% to 80% of charging is at home
    • Public DC pricing is about three times domestic tariffs
    • And ING had organic growth lagging penetration growth in Q1 2026 (+21% against +24%).
    • The H1 2026 organic figure answers the Q1 point
    • The home-charging cap is structural and bounds the long-term share of fleet energy a highway network can take, without bounding the growth of the fleet.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because grid connection is the binding physical constraint on the roll-out and grid fees are rising, while hardware and power are not constraints.
  • Grid lead times: standard 30 to 40 weeks
    • Complex sites up to eight years
    • Spain over three years
    • Germany and Italy two to five years.
    • Grid fees are rising.
  • Hardware: diversified and non-exclusive (Alpitronic and ABB)
    • Transformer lead times normalised at about 40 weeks.
  • Power: 45% to 50% of Dutch volumes on fixed-price power purchase agreements

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because regulation is moving demand, site access and margin in the company's favour at EU level and in Germany, while the Dutch frame may take from 2028 what the German frame gives now.
  • AFIR: charging pools every 60 km on the TEN-T network, 400 kW per pool by 2025 and 600 kW by 2027
    • Mandatory ad-hoc card payment above 50 kW, which removes roaming fees.
  • EU and national policy: the 2035 target kept with minor flexibility (December 2025)
    • German purchase incentives of up to EUR 6,000 (2026)
    • The Düsseldorf Higher Regional Court ruling of March 2026 forcing open tenders for Autobahn charging.
  • Against, the Netherlands: the draft law on auction-based highway permits leaves residual-value and site criteria unfinalised
    • Rijkswaterstaat concessions expire 2028 to 2036
    • Adjacent-site ownership is prohibited and local share capped at 50%, so the 80% Dutch motorway share cannot be held as concessions roll.
  • E-credits: regulatory environmental credits doubled in price in Germany, France and Belgium and drove much of the gross-profit-per-kWh gain
    • Unpredictable credit-scheme changes are the single largest margin risk.
  • Regulation gives with one hand and may take with the other from 2028
    • The net is a tailwind over the horizon with a dated erosion inside it.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competition is tightening on price and in scale while it is easing at the prime-site tender table, and for a motorway operator the two pull in opposite directions with roughly equal weight.
  • Tightening on price: Electra at EUR 0.29/kWh in France against Fastned's EUR 0.59 to EUR 0.71/kWh.
  • Tightening in scale: Electra 760 parks and over EUR 1bn of funding, with a stated 2,200-station target for 2030 against Fastned's 1,000
    • TotalEnergies 1,600 points in France
    • EnBW over 9,000 points in Germany.
  • Easing on sites: Shell Recharge and BP Pulse are moderating charging capex and rationalising forecourts
    • Deutsche Bank reads this as reduced bidding intensity for prime concessions (13 August 2026)
    • European public DC installations fell 19% year-on-year in Q2 2026 as the industry's build pace slowed.
  • Fastned's answer: session capture at co-located sites of 2.5x to 3x the incumbents', and a Dutch electricity-sales share over 60% on 20% to 25% of sites.
  • Not B: the discounting and the scale targets are new and intensifying, and the warning that oversupply ends in a price war is live: the EV-per-fast-charger ratio fell from 122 to 56 over three years and the European average DC price from EUR 0.75 to EUR 0.64/kWh.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because two macro forces act on this company more than on the universe and both are tailwinds now, though both are reversible and a third is a headwind.
  • Fuel prices: the spring 2026 fuel-price spike (+15% to 20% in European pump prices, Middle East) pushed multi-car households onto their battery-electric cars
    • ING and Deutsche Bank expect some volume normalisation if crude falls.
  • Power prices: normalising wholesale power lifted the charging gross margin to 88% in H1 2026.
  • Interest rates: a headwind on EUR 369m of net debt at a 6% retail-bond coupon.
  • The fuel-price tailwind is the first thing the bear case points at and the first kill condition the research names
    • It is treated as cyclical throughout this record.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption forces that matter near term raise throughput per bay and favour public fast charging over slow AC, while the forces that could cut against the network are beyond the horizon.
  • Hardware and vehicles: 90% of 2,826 chargers at 300 kW or more by mid-2026
    • 1 MW trials at Aalscholver
    • 800-volt vehicles and five-minute cells
    • The Dutch average charge time down to 20 minutes at 28.5 kWh per session.
  • Grid bypass: battery energy storage at sites bypasses two-to-three-year grid queues
    • Sodium-ion lowers that capex over time.
  • Against: solid-state cells could reduce required public density
    • Heavy-duty hydrogen corridors are targeted for 2030.
    • Both are later and speculative.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a demand-inflection case with a demonstrated operating-leverage inflection inside it, in a capital-intensive, founder-controlled network builder whose share price has already doubled.
  • European battery-electric fleet growth of 25% to 40% a year, backed by binding regulation, is driving charging volume at Fastned's premium motorway sites faster than the fleet (organic growth +25.1% in H1 2026).
  • Because the network's operating cost per charger does not move with volume, 38% volume growth became 110% operational EBITDA growth in H1 2026 and the margin guide was raised from 35% to 40% to about 45%.
  • The change has travelled through the company's choices and actions, its operating metrics, its financial statements and the sell-side's EBITDA estimates
    • It has not yet reached EPS (negative to FY2027) or free cash flow (negative to about FY2030), and the leverage of the model makes that last layer large in both directions.
  • Over eighteen months we expect FY2027 to be the last loss-making year and FY2028 the first with positive EPS (about EUR 0.51 on our path), with IFRS EBITDA tripling from FY2026 to FY2028 and the 24-month-forward EPS the market looks at rising from about EUR 0.9 (consensus mean) to about EUR 1.8.
  • The cost on the valuation is that the shares already trade at 48x that forward figure and about 18x FY2027 EBITDA, so the multiple must roughly halve as the earnings arrive.
  • The modal return is therefore about 10.3% a year over eighteen months and about 21.5% a year over three years, with no cash return and the whole return in the earnings.
  • It is compelling as a business change and only conditionally compelling as an investment at EUR 44.05, because the price pays 48x a consensus mean skewed by two bullish houses, the company carries about 15x net debt to operational EBITDA and will probably raise equity once.
  • A reader could repeat it as: the engine is proven, the balance sheet is thin, the price is early.
