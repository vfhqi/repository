Section 1 – Brief company description and history

1.
Established SME acquirer: Storskogen was founded in 2012 as an international business group to acquire and operate well-managed small-and-medium enterprise companies. The group operates across three distinct business areas comprising Services, Trade, and Industry, which provides a diversified earnings stream.

Discounted peer valuation: Storskogen competes in the Nordic serial acquirer market against high-quality compounders like Addtech, Indutrade, and Lifco. However, the group historically trades at a steep valuation discount to peers due to its lower return on capital and higher cyclicality.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Sell-side focus prior to the Q1 2026 release was heavily concentrated on organic growth recovery and the capacity to safely resume acquisitions.
  2. Investors closely monitored whether Storskogen could sustain its strong cash conversion above the long-term floor.
  3. The market remained highly sensitive to construction and infrastructure headwinds within the key Services business area.
1.
Stabilizing organic revenue growth: DNB Carnegie analyst Redin anticipated that Storskogen would work toward its financial target of organic growth at gross domestic product plus 1% to 2%. Analysts prior to the release looked for signs of gradual stabilization in organic revenue, comparing the performance to the negative organic sales trend of 3% observed in the full year 2025.
2.
Comfortably positioned leverage ratio: Market participants focused heavily on Storskogen's leverage ratio, which ended 2025 at 2.3x net debt to adjusted EBITDA. DNB Carnegie's Redin noted that this leverage level was comfortably within the target of 2.0x to 3.0x, representing a major improvement from the elevated leverage levels of 2023 and 2024.
3.
High cash conversion focus: Storskogen's ability to maintain high cash generation was a core sell-side focus, as the company's full-year 2025 cash conversion of 74% exceeded the absolute floor of 70% established by the board. DNB Carnegie's Redin expected YTD cash flows to remain robust, although Q1 is historically a seasonally weaker quarter for cash generation.
4.
Anticipated M&A pipeline resumption: Prior to the earnings call, brokers identified the resumption of acquisitions as the main driver of earnings revisions over the next 12 months. DNB Carnegie's Redin highlighted that the de-leveraging process during 2024 and 2025 had finally restored the company's capacity to pursue selective, higher-margin platform acquisitions.
5.
Heavy construction sector exposure: Investors remained highly concerned about Storskogen's exposure to the construction and infrastructure sectors, which represented approximately 25% of group revenue. DNB Carnegie's Redin warned that this exposure was likely to face significant demand headwinds in Sweden due to the prolonged cyclical slowdown in the broader building and installation industry.
6.
Anticipated seasonal weather impact: Going into the Q1 2026 results, DNB Carnegie's Redin adjusted estimates downward to account for colder-than-normal winter weather across Sweden and the Nordics. This unfavorable weather was expected to have caused construction activities to slow down during the quarter, directly impacting the profitability of the Services business area.
7.
Projected margin expansion: The sell-side expected the Trade business area to show strong margin performance, driven by a favorable foreign exchange environment. DNB Carnegie's Redin projected that the stronger Swedish Krona would provide a tailwind from currency translation for Trade, in contrast to the transactional FX headwinds that negatively impacted margins in 2025.
8.
Underwhelming margin trajectory: Storskogen's operational profitability was under intense scrutiny as its 2025 adjusted EBITA margin of 9.4% lagged the long-term target of 10%. DNB Carnegie's Redin noted that recent divestments of low-margin businesses were designed to structurally push the group closer to this 10% threshold.
9.
Negative operational growth: Sell-side expectations were braced for weak short-term earnings growth, given that Storskogen's adjusted EBITA growth in 2025 was negative 3%. DNB Carnegie's Redin projected that the group's earnings growth would eventually accelerate toward a 15% EBITA compound annual growth rate for the 2025 to 2027 period, driven primarily by resumed M&A.
10.
High cyclical demand risk: Institutional investors were highly focused on the macro sensitivity of the portfolio, given that more than half of the group's business units operate in niches related to European industrial production. DNB Carnegie's Redin cautioned that swings in cyclical demand remained a major risk, even though early indicators suggested European industrial production volumes would gradually recover through 2026.
11.
Extreme peer valuation gap: The sell-side focused heavily on Storskogen's cheap valuation, with the stock trading at 8.4x adjusted 2026e EV/EBITA prior to the release. DNB Carnegie's Redin argued that this represented an unjustifiably deep valuation discount compared to high-quality Nordic serial acquirers like Addtech and Lifco, which traded at multiples well over 20x.
12.
Decreasing refinancing risks: Debt management remained a critical point of discussion for investors, despite the extensive refinancing completed during 2024 and 2025. DNB Carnegie's Redin noted that these refinancing efforts successfully pushed out the next significant debt maturity to 2027, which dramatically lowered the overall financial risk of the group.
13.
Expected shareholder returns: Investors looked for updates on capital allocation strategies, particularly after the board previously authorized a share buyback program of up to SEK 100 million. DNB Carnegie's Redin pointed out that stable leverage and improved cash flow allowed Storskogen to support the equity story through buybacks while simultaneously scaling up its M&A activities.
14.
Lingering execution skepticism: A central topic among institutional investors was the long-term track record of Storskogen's decentralized acquisition model. DNB Carnegie's Redin acknowledged that the aggressive acquisition pace of 2021 and 2022, followed by the operational downturn of 2023 and 2024, caused many investors to question the group's quality and complexity.
15.
Subdued return on equity: Investors closely monitored Storskogen's capital efficiency, as the company's adjusted return on equity stood at 9.4% in 2025. DNB Carnegie's Redin projected that return on equity would gradually recover to 10.1% in 2026, though it remained subdued compared to history due to the lack of near-term organic operating leverage.
16.
Other side of the story: Resuming acquisitions too quickly carries the risk of losing pricing discipline or experiencing failed integrations, which could result in lower earnings or renewed pressure on leverage ratios if debt levels begin to rise again.
