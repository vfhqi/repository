Working Capital and Revenue Policies

1.
Opaque revenue recognition: DiaSorin recognizes revenue from direct sales of reagents and diagnostic instruments as a single performance obligation at a single point in time. Distributor sales are separated into distinct performance obligations for kits and instruments recognized when control transfers to the distributor. The dual accounting models create potential earnings volatility if distributor channel mixes shift rapidly. Large distributor adjustments could compromise the visibility of underlying customer demand.
2.
Escalating credit risk: DiaSorin faces credit risk concentrations as shown by the company's application of the IFRS 9 provision matrix. The IFRS 9 provision matrix utilizes an expected loss rate of 38% for receivables past due 181 to 360 days. The expected loss rate rises to a massive 92% for receivables past due over 360 days which totaled €10.70m at the end of 2024. High past-due balances threaten near-term cash collections and operating margins if customer defaults accelerate.
3.
Strained cash generation: DiaSorin reported a sequential cash build-up of inventory that restricted operational cash flows during 2026. Working capital performance remained impacted by planned inventory accumulation to support the respiratory season and the LIAISON NES launch. The commercial roll-out represents significant execution risk because elevated inventory carries obsolescence exposure. Excess inventory write-downs would directly damage future earnings per share.
4.
Off-balance sheet factoring: DiaSorin utilizes trade receivable factoring to optimize the company's reported working capital position and cash flows. Factored receivables are completely derecognized from the balance sheet once the risks and rewards of ownership are transferred to third parties. Over-reliance on factoring programs could mask underlying cash collection issues and deteriorate the quality of reported receivables. Any reduction in factoring capacity would immediately compress reported operating cash flows.
