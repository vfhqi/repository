  2. Divergent treatment of heavy intangible amortisation and deferred tax recognition drives wide broker EPS estimates.

1.
Drag from acquired customer lists and brand assets: Zegona's purchase of Vodafone Spain created massive intangible assets that require aggressive annual amortisation charges. The company's annual report from 13 August 2026 shows customer-related intangibles of 824.19 million euros and brand assets of 358.48 million euros. This accounting drag lowers statutory EPS while underlying operational cash generation remains unaffected.

Refinancing fee write-offs: High upfront refinancing outlays also create temporary book losses that do not reflect ongoing operational run-rates. Deutsche Bank's daily note on 26 June 2026 reports that fees, expenses, and bond call premiums will total around 100 million euros. While these transaction costs are expensed immediately under certain GAAP definitions, they represent one-off transition expenses rather than structural cash drains.

Valuation multiple distortion: Standardizing cash flow yield metrics reveals that headline multiples are largely meaningless for evaluating Zegona. Section 4 – Competitive Repricing Dynamics and Macroeconomic Pressures in Spain

Section summary
  1. Spain's telco market exhibits deep bifurcation as rising inflation collides with aggressive discount strategies from challengers.
  2. The upcoming price rounds will test the limits of premium operators to pass through macroeconomic pressures.

Spanish macroeconomic inflation pressures

1.
Accelerating consumer inflation: Spain's macroeconomic environment became more challenging as headline annual inflation accelerated to 4.9% in September 2026 according to Deutsche Bank's report on 7 October 2026. This upward pressure was primarily driven by fuel and energy prices, which could soon threaten household purchasing power. Premium operators must balance these cost increases against the risk of driving customers to low-cost alternatives, which means that pricing power will be severely tested over the next 18 months.
2.
Elevated operating costs: Energy price volatility and wage increases have raised input costs across the Spanish TMT sector as noted by Deutsche Bank on 9 October 2026. While domestic real GDP growth remains robust at a projected 2.3% for 2026, persistent price growth limits margins. Unless operators can pass these expenses to consumers, rising overheads will compress EBITDA and ultimately reduce corporate earnings per share across the sector.

January price leader dynamics

3.
Premium price leadership: Telefónica continues to leverage its network quality to charge premium rates in the Spanish market as documented by Morgan Stanley on 17 September 2026. While Telefónica's Movistar brand charges 53 euros per month for its convergent package, challengers offer comparable services at steep discounts. This differential leaves Telefónica highly vulnerable to customer churn unless its premium brand equity remains resilient.
4.
Scheduled pricing adjustments: Spanish operators plan to implement standard tariff increases in early 2027 to offset inflation as historically seen in previous pricing rounds. Deutsche Bank reported on 5 January 2026 that Telefónica Spain raised prices by 4% while Orange Spain and Vodafone Spain increased theirs by 3.8% and 3.9% respectively. These coordinated repricing actions are crucial to support retail revenue growth unless low-cost competition forces promotional discounting.
5.
Stable tariff strategies: Telefónica has historically focused on upselling its customer base to convergence packages to protect its margins. Telefónica's presentation on 4 November 2025 indicated that its superior network quality allows it to maintain commercial momentum despite price cuts by competitors. This customer loyalty helps stabilize Spain's overall service revenues unless sustained low-end pricing triggers down-selling.

Digi Spain disruptive push

6.
Disruptive pricing freeze: Digi Spain continues to destabilize the market by committing to a frozen pricing strategy during competitive repricing cycles. Deutsche Bank reported on 5 January 2026 that Digi would keep its prices stable while major incumbents pushed through rate hikes of up to 4%. This widening price gap allows the challenger to capture the majority of market net additions and restrict ARPU expansion for rivals.
7.
Significant listing proceeds: Digi Spain successfully raised 287 million euros in gross proceeds through its initial public offering on 16 July 2026. According to Digi's presentation on 2 October 2026, these funds will be deployed to expand its SMART fiber network and build its mobile network. This fresh capital ensures that Digi's commercial expansion is fully funded without nearterm constraints.
8.
Significant subscriber gains: Digi Spain continues to capture substantial market share through its aggressive value-for-money commercial proposition. According to Digi's report on 14 August 2026, its Spanish broadband users increased by 28% to 2.9 million in the second quarter of 2026. This outstanding growth highlights that Digi remains the main growth engine of the group, which means market share gains will continue.
9.
Elevated capital requirements: Digi Spain operates a highly capital- intensive business model that requires massive upfront investments to deploy fiber networks. According to Digi's interim report on 30 September 2026, negative operating free cash flows require the company to rely heavily on bank financing. This heavy debt burden could limit strategic flexibility unless network monetization generates positive returns.
10.
Expanding product suite: Digi Spain is actively expanding into pay television services to improve customer convergence and brand stickiness. Deutsche Bank highlighted on 19 February 2025 that Digi's low-end TV proposition would directly target the premium segments of traditional operators. By adding video content to its cheap fiber and mobile offerings, Digi will likely further accelerate high-value subscriber migration.

Accelerated synergy delivery: MasOrange has surpassed its initial integration targets, delivering substantial operational savings to its parent company. Orange reported on 18 February 2026 that MasOrange achieved approximately 356 million euros in synergies, exceeding its 300 million target. This improved financial profile will provide Orange with the necessary capital to increase cash returns to shareholders.

Agile asset sales: Vodafone Group continues to pursue portfolio simplification and debt reduction through the monetization of its valuable tower and joint venture investments. This strategy suggests that non-consolidated associate assets hold substantial value, which provides a helpful benchmark for evaluating Zegona's infrastructure holdings.
7.
Portuguese competitive friction: Low-cost challenger competition has begun to weigh on adjacent markets within the wider Vodafone Group footprint. Morgan Stanley's report on 5 February 2026 notes that Vodafone Portugal's service revenues declined by 2.4% in the third quarter of fiscal year 2026 due to the aggressive entry of Digi. This operational headwind means that subscriber ARPU will continue to contract unless incumbents launch successful defensive multi-brand offerings.
8.
Preference share redemption: Upfront infrastructure transactions allow holding companies to settle major acquisition debts ahead of schedule. Deutsche Bank's report on 1 December 2025 notes that Zegona's combined fiber proceeds made the repayment of Vodafone Group's 900 million euro shareholder loan highly likely before the end of the year. This balance sheet deleveraging is positive because it improves Zegona's forward credit profile and reduces annual interest expenses.

Fixed wholesale network agreements

9.
Successful fiber monetization: Establishing joint venture networks allows mobile operators to secure long-term infrastructure access while unlocking massive equity value. Zegona's annual report of 16 June 2026 confirms that Vodafone Spain reduced its stake in the FiberPass joint venture with Telefónica to 5% for net proceeds of 380 million euros. This transaction means that Vodafone Spain has completed network monetization, securing guaranteed access to 3.7 million premises under highly attractive economic terms.
10.
Enormous scale advantages: Partnering with major competitors creates highly efficient wholesale entities that reduce redundant fiber overbuild across the domestic market. Zegona's press release on 4 December 2025 announces the completion of the PremiumFiber joint venture, which is 58% owned by MasOrange, 17% by Vodafone Spain, and 25% by GIC. This transaction is highly favorable because it delivers 1.4 billion euros of proceeds while covering a network of over 12 million premises.
11.
High leverage capacity: Joint venture fiber companies can support significant debt recaps due to their highly stable, captive customer bases and fully built footprints. Deutsche Bank's report on 14 January 2026 estimates that PremiumFiber has raised investment-grade debt at attractive terms of around 250 basis points, resulting in leverage of nine to ten times. This high leverage is sustainable because upgrade capex has already been spent to cover nearly 5 million anchor customers.
12.
Wholesale rent outgoings: Although joint venture creation generates immediate cash windfalls, the subsequent rent payments represent a substantial headwind to operational margins.
