Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group's updated cash conversion definition from average working capital to point-to-point movements impacts cash transparency and affects its valuation, share buybacks, and investor trust.
1.
Key Question: "How will Kerry Group plc's (KRZ-IE) change in its cash conversion definition, from average working capital to point-to-point balance-sheet movements for the 2027-2030 planning cycle (Investor Day, October 2026), change its reported free cash flow and cash conversion?"

Strategic Importance of Cash Metrics

2.
Accounting shift: Kerry Group announced during its October 2026 Investor Day that it is transitioning its cash conversion methodology for the 2027-2030 cycle from an average working capital movement basis to a point-topoint balance sheet basis. Under the prior system, the company calculated free cash flow using average working capital across twelve months, whereas the new definition calculates the movement from opening to closing balances. This change directly impacts reported free cash flow and changes how analysts evaluate the quality of Kerry's earnings.
3.
Valuation implications: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that Kerry Group trades at 16x 2027 estimated price- to-earnings, representing a substantial discount to peers. Because valuation multiples are heavily tied to cash conversion consistency, changing the definition could obscure underlying cash flow volatility and alter peer valuation comparisons. This could ultimately impact how the market values the stock relative to its food manufacturing competitors.
4.
Buyback execution: Rothschild & Co Redburn noted that Kerry Group announced a new €300m share buyback program to commence after the current one ends on 31 December 2026. This program represents a key pillar of capital allocation alongside selective bolt-on mergers and acquisitions. However, executing these capital returns relies on the company successfully delivering on its newly upgraded cash conversion targets over the 2027-2030 cycle.

Investor Concerns and Related Questions

5.
Related metrics: The definition change is closely linked to other financial targets, such as the company's goal to achieve a 12-13% return on average capital employed by 2030. Investors are questioning how much the working capital rise to 12.9% of revenue in H1 2026 was driven by Middle East inventory buffers and whether this will reverse. This is critical because working capital management directly dictates free cash flow generation and cash conversion ratios.
6.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley Research indicates that the definition change has not impacted their long-term cash generation models, which still project a 10% earnings per share compound annual growth rate through 2030. This suggests that the metric definition does not alter the fundamental cash-generating power or investment appeal of the business.
7.
Restructuring cash drag: The definition change also connects to how the company handles non-trading restructuring cash costs, which have historically been a significant drag on reported free cash flow. With the Accelerate 2.0 program ending in 2028, analysts are closely monitoring whether these cash outlays will structurally decline. This directly affects the sustainability of cash conversion targets in the outer years of the planning cycle.
8.
- Analyst/Investor Other side of the story: While critics argue that the point-to-point methodology introduces balance sheet windowdressing risks, management's step-up of the cash conversion target to 85%+ indicates a heightened commitment to cash discipline. Standardizing cash conversion to point-to-point movements aligns Kerry Group with global ingredients peers, reducing complexity for international investors.

This increase in working capital highlights how strategic operational pivots can temporarily constrain reported cash generation before normalization.

The company recorded an average working capital outflow of €81 million in H1 2026, which was partly driven by geopolitical de-risking. These defensive actions mitigated local supply chain disruptions effectively across key markets. Management prioritized regional operational resilience over short-term working capital optimization to protect market share and customer relationships.

Section 4 – Evaluation of Supplier Finance Unwinding Risks

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group's €138.2 million supplier financing facility carries low immediate termination risk but presents structural cash flow sensitivities.
  2. Unwinding the program would compress trade payable days from 180 days down to standard terms, triggering a direct working capital outflow.
  3. Comparative benchmarking reveals that Kerry's extended payment terms of 180 days are substantially longer than those of its sector peers.

Mechanics of Kerry's Supplier Finance

1.
Steady reduction: Kerry Group managed a carrying liability of €138.2 million in its supplier financing facility at the end of fiscal year 2025. This balance reflects a decrease from the €169.4 million reported at the end of fiscal year 2024. The reduction indicates that Kerry is gradually lowering its reliance on reverse factoring arrangements to support trade payables.

Standard accounting: Kerry Group classifies these liabilities within trade and other payables because the arrangement does not significantly extend payment terms. The payments to the bank are included within operating cash flows as they continue to be part of the normal operating cycle. This classification helps preserve reported operating cash flows without presenting the program as a formal bank borrowing.
3.
Extended terms: Kerry Group offers payment terms of 90 to 180 days for suppliers participating in the finance arrangement. In comparison, standard trade payables that are not part of the arrangement carry payment terms of 60 to 180 days. The program effectively allows participating suppliers to discount their receivable positions ahead of the standard due dates.

This figure represents a slight decrease from the €113.3 million early payments recorded at the end of 2024. This trend suggests that fewer suppliers are discounting their invoices through the third-party bank facility.
5.
Persistent obligations: Kerry Group retains the original liabilities on its balance sheet because entering into the financing program does not provide a legal release. Furthermore, the original commercial terms are not substantially modified under the reverse factoring program. This accounting treatment ensures that Kerry does not derecognise trade payables or artificially lower its reported net debt figures.

Risks of Program Unwind

6.
Low termination: Kerry Group manages liquidity risk by ensuring that only a small portion of total financial liabilities is included in supplier financing. Because the program is highly decentralized, it does not result in a high concentration of liquidity risk for the group. Thus, the immediate risk that banks unilaterally terminate the facility remains low under current credit market conditions.
7.
Substantial outflow: Kerry Group would face a direct cash outflow of up to €138.2 million if the supplier financing program were to unwind completely. This transition would force Kerry to settle invoices directly with suppliers under shorter standard payment terms. Such an unwind would severely compress payable days and temporarily inflate the company's working capital requirements.
8.
Contrary evidence: Contrary evidence shows that Bernstein Research estimates Kerry's free cash flow will increase to approximately €700 million in 2026 despite working capital headwinds. This implies that even if the supplier finance arrangement were to unwind, the strong cash generation of the core business would easily absorb the funding gap without compromising the group's long-term liquidity.
9.
Limited credit: Kerry Group maintains a strong credit profile, supported by its €1.5 billion revolving credit facility maturing in June 2030. This robust funding structure ensures that any sudden termination of supplier credit lines would be covered. However, relying on backstop facilities to pay suppliers would increase interest expenses and drag down earnings per share.

Section 6 – Comparative Analysis of Peer Cash Conversion Metrics

Section summary
  1. Benchmarking global ingredients peers reveals that Givaudan, Symrise, and DSM-Firmenich utilize distinct cash definitions and targets to measure conversion.
  2. Kerry Group's shift to a point-to-point working capital methodology aligns with direct industry competitors who calculate cash flows on period-end balances.
  3. Peer performance shows that structural working capital normalization and capex discipline are essential to meeting long-term cash conversion targets.

Peer Cash Definitions

1.
Distinct methodology: Givaudan formally defines its key cash generation metric as Adjusted Free Cash Flow which is calculated as operating cash flow after net investments, interest, lease payments, and transactions in own equity. This definition is being updated to align with upcoming IFRS 18 regulations by incorporating nonrecurring significant items and cash movements on foreign exchange gains and losses. The company evaluates its long-term performance based on this cumulative free cash flow margin as a percentage of sales rather than net income conversion. This indicates Givaudan prioritizes sales-to-cash efficiency over earningsto-cash metrics.
2.
Operating control: Symrise utilizes a unique performance metric called Business Free Cash Flow which acts as the primary internal control variable to assess its cash generation capability. This alternative metric is calculated by taking earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization and adjusting for capital expenditures and working capital changes. By focusing on EBITDA rather than net income, Symrise isolates its operating performance from capital structure, tax, and interest variations. This operating-centric calculation yields a structurally higher cash conversion figure than traditional net income-based metrics.
3.
Gross metric: DSM-Firmenich utilizes an operating cash metric defined as Adjusted Gross Operating Free Cash Flow to evaluate its annual financial performance. Under the company's own definition, this metric represents adjusted EBITDA less cash flows for capital expenditures and working capital movements. This focus on operating-level cash flows helps the company manage its post-merger integration progress more effectively than net income metrics. Consequently, this definition bypasses non-operating restructuring cash outflows that typically depress statutory cash flows.
4.
Methodology update: Kerry Group historically calculated free cash flow using average monthly working capital balances over the year to smooth out timing and seasonal distortions. However, for its 2027-2030 planning cycle, the company is transitioning to a point-to-point balance sheet movement calculation. This shift means that Kerry will calculate working capital changes solely between the opening and closing dates of the reporting period. This realignment removes the smoothing effect of the average methodology and increases comparison directness with peers.

5.

This target continues Givaudan's previous financial framework which successfully delivered an average free cash flow of 12.5% of sales from 2021 to 2025. Meeting this target relies on keeping net working capital between 22% and 24% of sales and capital expenditures at 4% to 5% of sales.
