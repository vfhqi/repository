[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Clariant is a cause-for-concern-clearing case with a self-help engine underneath it, and at CHF 10.67 the market has already paid for the half of the clearing that has happened. The concern was a EUR 9 billion-plus set of follow-on ethylene-cartel claims that capped the valuation for four years; the first court test, in Amsterdam on 29 July 2026, went the company's way, and the shares have risen about 50 percent in three months. What remains to be earned is an earnings recovery that the two-year-forward figure already contains, a 3.9 percent yield, and a re-rating that a German court decides on a date nobody can name; on our modal path that is about 10 percent a year over eighteen months against the 20 percent bar.

  • The largest change force is external, legal and half-cleared.
    • The Amsterdam District Court dismissed Shell's EUR 1 billion damages claim and the Stichting vehicle's claim on 29 July 2026, rejecting Shell's damages model and finding no evidence that the buyers' information exchanges moved the monthly ethylene contract price.
    • About EUR 6.4 billion of claims remain before German courts in Munich and Dortmund, unprovisioned and carried as contingent liabilities
      • Dow Europe's EUR 1.1 billion Dortmund claim was filed on 16 July 2026, inside the window in which the concern is supposed to be clearing.
    • The other external forces are, on balance, headwinds: European gas at 3.3 times the United States price, Chinese producers moving up from commodities into specialty additives, the Swiss franc against a cost base that is 10 percent franc and a revenue base that is less than 1 percent franc, and a Middle East refill deferral that took 14.2 percent off Catalysts volumes in Q2 2026.
      • We rate the external forces D as change forces because the tailwinds are the reversal of headwinds, not new demand.
  • The internal change is real, measured and led by a strong operator, but it is the continuation of a programme, not a new one.
    • The chief executive is unchanged since January 2021
    • the change is in the people beneath him (a new chief financial officer from August 2025, a new chairman from April 2025, a board cut from eleven to eight in April 2026), in cost and cash discipline, and in a savings target raised by CHF 20m to CHF 100m on 31 July 2026 with CHF 69m banked by June 2026.
    • Two of the framework's twelve internal forces are strong and five are moderate, so the screen's rule of three strong forces, or chief-executive change plus a guidance increase, is not met
      • 'Change Forces' is a C.
  • The 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on the two lines the company controls and not on the line investors extrapolate.
    • The EBITDA margin before exceptional items rose from 14.6 percent in 2023 to 17.8 percent in FY2025 and 18.2 percent in Q2 2026
    • free cash flow conversion rose from 32 percent in 2024 to 52 percent on a last-twelve-month basis at June 2026.
    • Revenue has fallen every year since FY2022 (CHF 5,198m to CHF 3,915m in FY2025), and consensus has it falling 2.6 percent again in FY2026 before growing 3.7 percent in FY2027 and 3.1 percent in FY2028, below the company's own 4 to 6 percent target.
    • Consensus EPS on the brokers' basis recovers from CHF 0.573 in FY2025 to CHF 0.708 in FY2026, CHF 0.868 in FY2027 and CHF 0.920 in FY2028: about 23 percent a year for two years, then 6 percent.
      • Our modal path sits at consensus on EBITDA for those years.
      • 'Financial Outputs' is a C because the eighteen-month growth is recovery, not compounding.
  • The change has travelled the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain as far as the profit line and the cash line of the financial statements and, for ten weeks, into the sell-side's estimates.
    • Consensus FY2027 EPS is up 6.3 percent in three months and the mean broker rating is Overweight
    • the revenue line is the layer the change has not reached, and it is the layer that decides whether the 2027 targets are met.
  • What it costs on the valuation is the setup's price condition, which has lapsed.
    • At CHF 10.67 the shares trade at 11.9x 24-month-forward earnings of CHF 0.90 on the dashboard (11.49x on FactSet's history), which is the median of the stock's own five-year range, the 94th percentile of the last two years (median 8.87x), and about 15 percent below the peer median of 14.0x for reasons the record can name: a return on invested capital of 6.9 percent below a 7.8 percent cost of capital, four years of falling revenue, and an unprovisioned legal tail.
    • The re-rating that was ours to earn, from the two-year median of 8.9x to today's 11.9x, has already happened
      • the shares rose 16 percent on 30 July 2026 alone.
  • The return on our modal path is a D, and the chain therefore breaks at its last link.
    • At each horizon we value the shares at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it.
    • Eighteen months out the 24-month-forward EPS on our path is CHF 0.99 (from CHF 0.90 now)
    • at 12.0x, the uncleared-and-delivering branch, plus a CHF 0.42 dividend, the total is CHF 12.30, +15.3 percent, about 10.0 percent a year.
    • Three years out it is CHF 1.08 at a blended 12.2x plus CHF 1.33 of dividends, CHF 14.51, +36.0 percent, about 10.8 percent a year.
    • The one-year-forward footing gives the same answer: 12.9x on CHF 0.83 now, CHF 12.46 in eighteen months, 10.9 percent a year.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (tax and minorities at 35 percent of pre-tax profit rather than 32.8 percent, interest CHF 10m higher) takes the eighteen-month return to 6.0 percent a year, an F on the 'Modal return' and a D on this Building Block
      • the modal path is the D.
    • The price below which the modal path clears the 20 percent bar is about CHF 9.35 over eighteen months.
      • The crisp entry was at CHF 7 to 8 in June and July 2026, and the park has moved against us by about 40 percent.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major CfC Clearing', with 'Major Corporate Change' as the mechanism beneath it, and the fit is a C rather than a crisp one because the trough-multiple condition no longer holds.
    • Four of the setup's five conditions carry dated evidence of having passed by July 2026
    • the fifth, the price, was an A in July and is a D in September.

  • The case is therefore a fair one at CHF 10.67 and a good one at about CHF 9.35.

    • The self-help keeps earnings rising about 10 percent over eighteen months on the two-year-forward figure while a German court decides whether those earnings are worth 12 or 13.5 times
    • the wait is paid at about 10 percent a year at this price and at about 20 percent a year at CHF 9.35.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the outputs it touches (margin up 320 basis points since 2023, cash conversion up 20 points, a 23 percent-a-year earnings recovery for two years, a legal overhang half-cleared), and is measurable and dated, but the chain breaks at the last link: at CHF 10.67 the modal return is about 10 to 11 percent a year, because the market has paid for the clearing already and the growth that remains after FY2027 is about 6 percent a year with 3 to 4 percent of it from revenue.

    • The parts combine as a decisive weakness at the return binding a moderately strong set of forces and outputs: 'Change Forces' C, 'Financial Outputs' C, 'Modal return' D.
    • Not D, because the change is genuinely material to the business and a price about 12 percent lower brings the same path to the framework's B anchor
    • the thesis is fairly priced, not weak.
    • Not B, because no leg of the return reaches the bar on its own, and the force that would (a German ruling) is not the company's to deliver.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against our C is the bull case in its strongest form, which is Deutsche Bank's (Buy, CHF 12.60, 23 September 2026): the market still discounts twice the litigation risk that is justified after Amsterdam
    • the German courts are likely to follow a precedent built on the same economic record
    • Clariant's EBITDA has been the most stable in its European peer group through 2022 to 2025
    • the 2027 targets (a 19 to 21 percent margin, 4 to 6 percent growth) are substantial upside to consensus
    • and a business with 52 percent cash conversion and an 8.1 percent 2027 free cash flow yield re-rates to its historical 26 percent premium to peers once the overhang is gone, which puts the multiple above 14x and the return above 25 percent a year.
  • We concede the quality of the cost and cash delivery, the direction of the legal precedent, and that our uncleared multiple of 12.0x sits below the ten-year median of 13.4x.
  • We hold our view because the bull case needs two things inside eighteen months that we rate unlikely together: a German first-instance ruling (Bernstein expects the German proceedings to take considerable time, and the Dow claim was only served on 17 July 2026), and a return to 4 to 6 percent organic growth against a record of flat volumes, energy at 3.3 times United States prices and Chinese competitors moving up the chain.
  • Without both, the bull case is our 'cleared' branch, which at 13.5x gives about 18.6 percent a year over eighteen months and carries about a one-in-four chance on our odds.
  • The case is a good one at CHF 9.35
    • at CHF 10.67 it is a fair one.
