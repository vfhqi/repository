Section 2 – German and French January 2027 Reimbursement Reforms

Section summary
  1. Stricter European pricing controls from January 2027 in Germany and France will squeeze MedTech manufacturer margins.

German Healthcare Reform Dynamics

1.
Squeezing revenues: German statutory health insurance providers face deep structural changes designed to consolidate public healthcare funds. The Statutory Health Insurance Contribution Rate Stabilization Act implements a comprehensive €16.3 bn relief package starting in January 2027. This policy targets hospital operating budgets and forces manufacturers of chronic care products to absorb significant localized price reductions. Consequently, public insurers are empowered to enforce stricter procurement policies that limit manufacturer pricing power.
2.
Restricting price: Hospital remuneration in Germany faces a strict cap under the newly approved statutory legislation. The government will limit annual price increases by tying the State-specific Base Rate directly to the GKV basic pay rate. Furthermore, legislators approved an additional one percentage point reduction to this base pay rate for the years 2027 through 2029. This measure restricts price adjustments and forces medical device suppliers to absorb negative price variances.
3.
Limiting margins: German pricing moratoriums have been extended to enforce strict limits on healthcare spending. Legislative changes extend the domestic price moratorium through December 31, 2030, to curb cost inflation. This extension prevents Coloplast from raising prices on chronic consumables to offset rising raw material expenses. Therefore, margins on intimate healthcare products will likely remain depressed over the medium term.
4.
Straining hospitals: German hospital operators face intense operational pressure that directly affects their procurement budgets under the new GKV Stabilization Act. Savings measures targeting clinical providers are projected to generate a headwind of 2% to 4% to hospital sector earnings. Consequently, hospitals will likely intensify their cost-containment efforts by squeezing medical consumable prices. This cost pressure means that suppliers like Coloplast must prepare for more aggressive purchasing negotiations in Germany.

French Social Security Measures

5.
Targeting consumables: France proposes a new social security tax that directly penalizes reimbursed medical device spending. The draft 2027 Social Security budget introduces a targeted 3.6% tax on medical device operators. This levy applies directly to items listed on the Liste des Produits et Prestations, which covers standard chronic care products. As a result, manufacturers commercializing devices in France must directly penalizes reimbursed medical device spending to the state.
6.
Reducing tax: French fiscal authorities structured the proposed MedTech levy to moderate the absolute financial impact on operators. The tax applies to seventy percent of eligible LPP reimbursements rather than total gross revenue. This structure reduces the effective tax rate to approximately 2.5% of the total reimbursed amount. Furthermore, the fiscal base is capped at ex-VAT revenues to protect lower-margin manufacturers from excessive liabilities.
7.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research reported on October 20, 2026, that France's proposed 3.6% MedTech tax represents only a manageable half percent impact on pre-tax profits for covered companies. The measure faces a highly fragmented French parliament, making complete legislative passage in its original form highly unlikely. Consequently, the financial headwind to European profit margins may be far smaller than initial market panic suggested.
8.
Shifting policy: French budgetary caps create a highly unpredictable environment for pharmaceutical and medical technology manufacturers. The French social security system uses a safeguard clause to reclaim spending that exceeds annual ONDAM budgetary limits. Under this mechanism, companies can be forced to pay up to ten percent of their reimbursed revenues as a collective tax. This framework shows that France is increasingly willing to use targeted levies to enforce strict healthcare spending caps.

Section 5 – Italian Payback Liabilities and European Court of Justice Referral

Section summary
  1. Italy's medical device payback system imposes retrospective financial liabilities on medical technology suppliers to offset public healthcare spending overruns.
  2. Regulatory provisions are being actively adjusted following the Italian Constitutional Court's July 2024 decisions and subsequent government discount decrees.
  3. MedTech companies maintain distinct accounting strategies with major operators recording multi-million dollar sales reductions to cover outstanding liabilities.

Italian Payback System Context

1.
Retroactive pressure: Coloplast faces significant financial exposure to Italy's medical device payback system which forces suppliers to pay back a portion of public spending overruns. According to Morgan Stanley in February 2023, the national expenditure ceiling was set at 4.4% of the national health fund, equating to approximately two billion euros in overruns for the 2015-2018 period. Consequently, medical device companies must refund regional authorities once spending caps are exceeded.
2.
Decreasing liabilities: Coloplast benefited from a gross margin benefit after adjusting its outstanding financial provisions following a series of Italian government decrees. The Italian government published a Legislative Decree on June 30, 2025, which established a seventy-five percent settlement discount on requested amounts. According to Rekeep in June 2025, this discount allowed its subsidiary to partially reduce the provision it set aside by €0.70 mm.
3.
Positive margins: Coloplast realized a significant tailwind in its gross margins due to the release of over-provisions related to the Italian payback system. The company recorded a baseline gross margin benefit of approximately 40 basis points during fiscal year 2023/24. Management reported in August 2024 that this gross margin benefit directly resulted from the one-off Italian payback reform provision accounted for during fiscal year 2022/23.

Constitutional Challenges and Legal Status

4.
Sustained legality: Medacta confirmed that the Italian Constitutional Court upheld the validity of the controversial medical device payback system. The Constitutional Court published ruling number 140 on July 22, 2024, confirming the constitutional legitimacy of the rules governing these regional payback schemes. Additionally, judgment number 139 declared that the government's payment reductions must be recognized to all operators to ensure market equity.
