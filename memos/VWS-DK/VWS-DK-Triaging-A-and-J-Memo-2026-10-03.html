[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Vestas is a self-help margin recovery in a consolidated Western wind industry, and most of it has already happened. At 200.20 DKK (26.78 EUR) the shares already pay for the 10 percent EBIT margin of 2028 before it is delivered, so the modal return from here is about 9 to 10 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case is compelling as a business and not compelling as an investment at this price.

  • The change is internal, broad and already in the reported numbers.
    • A chief executive in place since August 2019 has run the value-over-volume strategy set at the 2021 Capital Markets Day through the Operating Model Reset of 2025 and 2026 and a Service recovery plan to end-2026, and the measurable standards have followed.
    • Warranty costs: net warranty provisions fell from 6.4 percent of revenue (2022) to 3.2 percent (2025) and 3.0 percent (Q2 2026)
      • In Q2 2026 consumption of 218 EURm exceeded new provisions of 141 EURm.
    • Pricing: order ASP was 1.02 EURm per MW in Q2 2026 against Nordex's 0.91
      • Renegotiated Service contracts were repriced 20 to 30 percent and parts prices up to 15 percent.
    • Guidance: the FY2026 EBIT margin range was raised on 12 August 2026 to 7 to 9 percent from 6 to 8 percent, about 210 EURm of EBIT at the midpoint on a 20 to 22 bn EUR revenue base.
    • Shareholder returns: buybacks stepped up four times in twelve months, to 400 EURm running to 16 December 2026, and the payout floor was raised to at least 40 percent of net profit.
    • Breadth: five of the twelve internal screen forces are strong and three more are moderate
      • The one force wholly absent is a chief-executive change, which weakens the pattern, not the company.
  • The external forces help on structure and hurt on cost and in the United States, so they are mixed rather than strong.
    • GE Vernova's halt to new offshore orders in November 2024 left a Vestas and Siemens Energy duopoly in European offshore, the Western price wars have ceased, and the top four Western OEMs are projected at about 94 percent of the ex-China onshore market by 2028
    • Against that, Section 232 moved in April 2026 to a 50 percent tariff on imported components with about 50 percent of potential US onshore orders on hold, steel is up 32 percent and copper 18 percent year-to-date, and the European utility risk-free rate is 5.0 percent, up 86 basis points year-to-date.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have followed in the order the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain predicts, all the way to the sell-side's estimates.
    • The group EBIT margin before special items went from minus 8 percent (2022) to 5.7 percent (2025) and 9.4 percent in the quarter to June 2026
    • Reported EPS from 0.08 EUR (2023) to 0.78 EUR (2025)
    • FY2027 consensus EPS rose 17.3 percent in the six months to 2 October 2026.
    • Resources, choices, actions, key operating metrics, financial statements and estimates have all moved, so the chain has been travelled in full and paid for.
  • What the price already assumes is the finished recovery.
    • The 24-month-forward multiple of 14.97 times (2 October 2026) applied to 26.78 EUR implies 24-month-forward earnings of about 1.79 EUR, the consensus blend of FY2027 (1.64 EUR mean) and FY2028 (1.84 EUR mean), which is a 10 percent EBIT margin in 2028.
    • That multiple is the 60th percentile of the stock's two-year range, the 25th percentile of five years and the 30th of ten
    • The shares rose 67.1 percent in twelve months as the margin recovery was proven.
  • The modal return therefore falls well short of the bar, because the earnings growth that is left to pay for is the high-single-digit growth that follows a completed recovery.
    • On our path (EPS 1.33 EUR in FY2026, 1.61 EUR in FY2027, 1.81 EUR in FY2028, 1.99 EUR in FY2029), with the multiple held at 15.0 times and the consensus dividend, the return is 9.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.2 percent a year over three years.
    • The dividend yield is under 1 percent a year
    • The re-rating component is zero in the modal case.
  • Where the chain holds is the step already taken; where it could break is the remaining step to a 10 percent margin.
    • That step turns on offshore break-even in 2027, which we put at probable (about 60 percent), and on a Service margin that has not moved off 16 to 17 percent for three years, which we put at plausible (about 45 percent).
    • The announced Q3 2026 order intake of 2,754 MW against a compiled consensus of 4,554 MW is the first sign that the volume side may wobble while that step is walked.
  • A re-rating is possible, not probable, and would only reach the bar's neighbourhood.
    • A move to the ten-year median of 16.6 times needs both the offshore and the Service conditions to land, which we put at about three-in-ten odds
    • At 16.6 times the return is 17.2 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.9 percent over three years.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', crisp on every condition and late in timing.
    • The internal forces are the ones the screen names, the outputs followed in the chain's order, and the sell-side reacted as the pattern says it should
    • The pattern pays best at input plus early output, and this case is at full output.

  • The case is therefore material in its forces and outputs and immaterial in its return at this price: a park, with re-engagement below about 175 DKK or on reported evidence of a Service margin inflection.

  • Rating = C, because the thesis is material in its forces (B) and its outputs (B) but not in its return (F): the chain has been travelled to the sell-side layer and paid for, and what is left on the modal path is 9 to 10 percent a year against a 20 percent bar.

    • The parts bind: a thesis whose change is real but whose return at the price is insufficient is not a material thesis for capital deployment.
    • Not B, because the return is not marginally short but half the bar
    • Not D, because the forces and outputs are among the stronger in the universe and a credible re-rating path exists at odds we can state.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull says consensus is too low.
    • Redburn forecasts FY2027 EBIT of 2,138 EURm and FY2028 EBIT of 2,442 EURm, 12 and 15 percent above consensus (22 May 2026), on four mechanisms: the Service book rotating onto post-reset pricing (about 45 percent of serviced capacity by 2030), offshore break-even in 2027, buybacks of 5 percent a year and a closing of the 20 percent discount to capital-goods multiples.
    • On that path FY2028 EPS is about 2.15 EUR and a multiple of 18 to 18.5 times gives 25 percent-plus a year over eighteen months.
    • We concede that the Service repricing mechanism is real, under-modelled and the one named line where consensus could be materially wrong.
    • We hold our view because the reported Service margin has not moved (16.3 percent in Q1 2026, 16.6 percent in Q2 2026, down 60 basis points year on year), because consensus already carries 19.2 percent for 2028, because the Q3 2026 order shortfall and the US hold cap the volume side that the bull's revenue needs, and because the capital-goods multiple belongs to businesses with 15 to 20 percent margins, not 10.
    • The bull case is possible, at about one in four, and it is why we park rather than reject.

  • Components: main-shaft bearings dominated by SKF, Schaeffler and Timken
