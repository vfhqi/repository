Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Billerud faces a severe profit headwind as the loss of free CO2 emission allowances and new Swedish carbon taxes depress European packaging margins.
  2. Regulatory re-entry to the EU ETS1 starting in 2027 will eliminate the Swedish carbon tax burden but lock in permanent market-purchasing costs for fossil emissions.

Section 3 – Currency: Hedging Strategies and Exchange Rate Sensitivities

Section summary
  1. Billerud's extensive 15-month currency hedging program delays the immediate cash flow impact of Swedish krona volatility but caps potential upside from a weakening local currency.
  2. Macro forecasters expect a stronger Swedish krona through 2027 and 2028, which poses a structural medium-term transaction threat to Billerud's unhedged earnings.
  3. Billerud's heavy planned maintenance cycle in 2026 generated substantial operational volatility, which will require careful execution to normalize across 2027 and 2028.
  4. Nordic packaging peers face similar seasonal maintenance pressures, with shutdown costs heavily concentrated in the second and third quarters.

Billerud Sensitivity and Disclosed Hedges

1.
Systematic findings: The research identified six relevant packaging and paper companies and analyzed more than 25 primary and secondary sources to evaluate exchange rate sensitivities and currency risk management. These sources include interim financial reports, broker notes, and macroeconomic outlooks spanning a period from 2024 to late 2026. This extensive dataset exposes how divergent corporate hedging policies influence medium-term transaction risks across Nordic forestry peers.
2.
Significant sensitivity: Billerud operating earnings remain highly vulnerable to foreign exchange fluctuations due to a structural mismatch between export revenues and local manufacturing costs. Billerud noted in its 2025 annual report that a 10% movement in the Swedish krona impacts EBITDA by approximately SEK 640 million before hedging effects are considered. This high exposure means that any sudden strengthening of the Swedish krona will directly compress operating margins across its European mills.
3.
Defensive hedging: Billerud has established substantial near-term protection for its euro-denominated cash flows through forward contracts. Billerud disclosed in its Q3 2026 interim report that it has hedged 47% of its expected EUR exposure over the next 15 months at an average exchange rate of SEK 10.96. The hedging coverage declines from 80% in Q4 2026 to 35% by Q3 2027, leaving the group exposed to spot rate changes thereafter.
4.
Cash flow protection: Billerud manages its dollar and pound sterling exposures with similar short-term forward contract matches. Billerud reported in its Q3 2026 interim report that its outstanding USD hedges cover 44% of forecasted flows at SEK 9.41, while GBP hedges cover 46% at SEK 12.46. These hedges had a total negative market value of SEK -135 million on 30 September 2026, which represents a short-term drag on paper earnings.

Section 5 – Wood: Fiber Cost Trajectories and Supply Chain Lag

Section summary
  1. Billerud's earnings are highly sensitive to Nordic wood costs, where a structural supply deficit keeps prices higher-for-longer despite recent cyclical relief.
  2. A rigid three to six-month transmission lag delays the positive impact of falling stumpage prices on Billerud's income statement.
  3. Billerud's low selfsufficiency in forest assets represents a key vulnerability compared to highly integrated peers like Holmen and SCA.
  4. Significant dispersion in Billerud's 2027 EPS forecasts stems primarily from divergent operating EBITDA assumptions and non-operating schedules.
  5. Lower projected depreciation and finance costs under certain broker models explain why some estimates are significantly more optimistic than consensus.

Fiber price paths and trajectories

1.
Stable outlook: Billerud expects Nordic pulpwood prices to remain stable through the end of 2026. Looking into 2027, the company anticipates that wood costs will on average remain stable compared to 2026 levels. This flat pricing trajectory follows a period of volatile markets where Nordic pulpwood prices declined 20% to 30% from their historical peaks. Consequently, this stabilization will prevent further immediate cost inflation but limits additional margin recovery.
2.
High cost pressure: The Finnish timber market continues to see elevated prices, as reported by forest-owner associations and natural resource agencies. In Finland, log prices have remained high, with spruce pulpwood prices approaching previous record peaks. In contrast, Swedish pulpwood prices are reported with a onequarter delay by Skogsstyrelsen but show greater near-term stability. These high wood costs will continue to squeeze regional packaging margins unless end-product pricing improves.
3.
Softening prices: Storm Johannes in late 2025 created a temporary surplus of salvage wood that pressured Nordic roundwood prices. Forest owner associations reported that salvage volumes in Sweden were equivalent to a full year of normal harvesting. While this influx provided temporary price relief, the incremental handling and transport costs for salvage operations are highly inflationary. This supply bump temporarily aided pulpwood buyers but did not solve the long-term Nordic fiber deficit.
4.
Persistent demand competition: Competition for Nordic wood from the energy and heating sectors continues to support high pulpwood prices. As European countries accelerate their transition to renewable energy, the demand for forest biomass as a fuel source has structurally increased. This ongoing competition prevents wood prices from falling back to historical averages, even during periods of low paperboard demand. Consequently, Billerud faces a permanent structural cost floor that limits the benefits of cyclical pulpwood price declines.
5.
Structural supply deficit: The termination of Russian wood imports has created a permanent supply deficit in the Nordic fiber market. Prior to the 2022 sanctions, Russian imports provided approximately 10% of the regional pulpwood supply to Finland and Sweden. This structural gap cannot be easily resolved through increased local harvesting due to strict European environmental regulations. As a result, Nordic producers must operate on a permanently higher cost curve compared to global competitors.

Supply chain transmission lag

6.
Delayed cost relief: Billerud experience a notable delay before lower market wood costs are reflected in its financial statements. There is a structural three to six-month lag before lower pulpwood and stumpage costs are visible in paper and packaging mill earnings. This delay means that price declines occurring in mid- 2026 will only benefit Billerud's profit and loss statement during 2027. Consequently, near-term earnings remain depressed by raw materials purchased during earlier high-cost periods.

171 High EBITDA sensitivity: Billerud profitability remains extremely sensitive to changes in wood fiber costs due to its large production footprint. The company disclosed in its 2025 annual report that a 10% change in fiber prices has a SEK 1,380 million impact on operating costs.

7.
Broker estimates suggest that every 10% decline in Nordic pulpwood prices is worth approximately SEK 900 million in EBITDA tailwinds. This massive operational leverage means that even minor wood price movements will heavily drive earnings per share.
8.
Disproportionate financial impact: Billerud's sensitivity to wood price fluctuations is significantly higher than that of its integrated peers. While a 10% lower wood cost translates to a SEK 900 million EBITDA boost for Billerud, the same move is worth EUR 100 million for Stora Enso. Meanwhile, Metsä Board experiences a EUR 60 million impact on its operating result for every 10% change in wood prices. This disparity highlights Billerud's elevated risk profile as a non-integrated producer in the Nordic region.

Sourcing security and peer benchmarking

9.
Vulnerable self-sufficiency: Billerud suffers from a structural disadvantage due to its low self-sufficiency in wood raw materials. To mitigate this risk, the company is actively expanding its wood purchases from private forest owners in Sweden. Private forest owners accounted for over 15% of Billerud's total fiber purchases in 2024 compared to lower levels in prior years. This expansion is critical to reducing reliance on volatile imported wood and securing mill gate supply.
10.
Strong backward integration: Swedish peers Svenska Cellulosa AB and Holmen AB possess stronger backward integration than Billerud. Svenska Cellulosa AB is Europe's largest private forest owner with 2.7 million hectares, allowing it to maintain 60% wood self-sufficiency. Similarly, Holmen AB covers approximately 50% of its raw wood requirements from its own forest lands. This high integration insulates these peers from the market price spikes that directly damage Billerud's operating margins.
11.
Secured sourcing partnerships: Stora Enso and Metsä Board have established unique sourcing structures to protect their Nordic mills from wood scarcity. Stora Enso secured a critical 18-year take-or-pay wood supply agreement with Bergslagets Skogar, its demerged forest asset entity. Metsä Board leverages the Metsäliitto Cooperative, which is owned by over 90,000 Finnish private forest owners, to secure its fiber supply. These long-term arrangements provide a level of cost and volume security that Billerud currently lacks.
12.
Instructive historical storm: Historical forestry precedents show that storms can heavily distort regional pulpwood pricing and mill costs. During Storm Gudrun in 2005, Swedish roundwood and pulpwood prices fell by 37% and 25% respectively due to the sudden supply glut. However, during the more recent 2013-2014 storms, prices declined by a far more modest 5% for sawlogs and 8% for pulpwood. This historical variance suggests that the recent Storm Johannes salvage volumes will only offer mild and temporary price relief.
13.
Contrary evidence: Nordic pulpwood prices declined by 35% to 40% from their peak in some regions according to Morgan Stanley on 6 March 2026. This steep decline was driven by increased local wood availability and strategic partnerships with regional sawmills.
