Section 2 – Hydro Volatility, Drought Risks, and Flexibility Earnings

Section summary
  1. Severe Alpine drought and water shortages threaten Verbund's generation volumes and expose the utility to costly hedge buyback mechanisms.
  2. Peer utilities across Europe experience similar hydrological deficits that depress hydro earnings and force strategic transitions toward client-centric or data-center supply models.
  3. Rapid utility-scale battery deployment poses a long-term risk of cannibalizing intra-day spreads and eroding the flexibility premium of pumped-storage assets.
  4. Confidence in this analysis is high because findings are supported by detailed operational data from Verbund, BKW, Hidroelectrica, and EDP.

Verbund Hydrological Volatility

1.
Depressed water inflows: Verbund reported a low hydro coefficient of 0.61 for the first nine months of 2026, including a particularly dry third quarter at 0.49. Deutsche Bank Research notes that the company expects the full-year coefficient to recover to between 0.62 and 0.65. If water levels remain below normal, Verbund risks missing its revised net profit guidance of 1.05 billion euros. This operational headwind highlights the substantial earnings volatility that is inherent in run-of-river Alpine assets.
2.
Severe overhedging penalties: Verbund suffered substantial losses from its net power buyback obligations, which reached 120 million euros in the first nine months of 2026. This total included a massive 90 million euros of buyback losses in the dry third quarter alone. These buybacks are required when generation falls short of pre-hedged volumes. Such cash outflows significantly reduce the net margin on generation and pressure short-term earnings.
3.
Elevated overhedging risks: Morgan Stanley analysts warn that persistent hydrological deficits leave Verbund highly exposed to overhedging. Under these conditions, the utility is forced to buy back power to fulfill its contracts. This buyback mechanism turns higher wholesale prices into a penalty rather than an opportunity. These dynamics make Verbund's earnings sensitivity highly asymmetric to the downside during droughts.

Peer Drought Narratives

4.
Challenging Swiss hydrology: BKW reported that its water production in the first half of 2026 was once again characterized by challenging hydrological conditions. Low water levels resulted in lower generation than in the previous year, which weighed down its Energy Solutions segment. BKW now expects its full-year operating profit to land at the lower end of its target range. This demonstrates that even flexible Alpine portfolios cannot fully escape prolonged water deficits in Central Europe.
5.
Severe Danube shortages: Morgan Stanley reports that July and August Danube inflows for Hidroelectrica were just 39.7% and 36.1% of their longterm averages. To manage these commitments, the Romanian utility was forced to make substantial external power purchases of 1001 GWh in the third quarter. These high procurement costs severely limit the earnings protection typically offered by its retail supply expansion. Analysts maintain an Underweight rating because the stock's valuation already assumes a full hydrological recovery that remains highly uncertain.
6.
Strategic supply shifts: Hidroelectrica is actively transitioning from an asset-centric model to a client-centric structure to mitigate hydrology risks. While the asset-centric model offered high margins, it left the company highly vulnerable to seasonal droughts. Increasing retail supply and integrating solar assets helps diversify this structural exposure. However, purchasing power on the spot market to serve these clients exposes Hidroelectrica to margin compression when hydro generation is weak.
7.
Low Nordic reservoirs: DNB Carnegie reports that Nordic hydro reservoirs fell 13 TWh below normal levels during the third quarter of 2026. This hydrological deficit has pushed Nordic forward prices higher, supporting Fortum's seasonally strong quarters ahead. Fortum also signed a 22-year nuclear PPA with Google, which should start to support its hedge book in 2028. However, the broker notes that extreme weather remains a key risk to baseline generation volumes.
8.
Disappointing hedge updates: Morgan Stanley reports that Fortum's disclosed 2028 hedging of 45% of its output at 42 euros per megawatt-hour was lower than expected. This low hedge price reflects older contracts in the portfolio and includes only small volumes from the Google PPA. Compared to estimates for realized spot prices, this update implies a 5% to 10% downside to consensus earnings. This highlights that financial hedges can lock in lower-than-market realization when spot prices spike.

Iberian Hydro and Flexibility

9.
Strong Iberian recovery: Deutsche Bank reports that Portuguese hydro generation was 21% above normal levels in the first nine months of 2026. This robust hydro performance, especially in pumped storage, allowed EDP to upgrade its full-year earnings guidance. High reservoir levels in Portugal contrast sharply with the severe deficits observed in Italy and Austria. These favorable conditions provide EDP with a strong near-term earnings tailwind relative to its European peers. 60 61 Q p Hydro reserves in Portugal during 2026 compared with 2025 and the long-term average.
10.
Expanding flexibility margins: Bernstein reports that high solar penetration in Iberia is deepening the duck curve and widening peak-to-off-peak spreads. EDP's pumped-storage assets are capturing premium pricing by charging during low-priced midday hours and discharging during peak demand. The premium achieved by EDP's conventional hydro is expected to rise above 20% over 2026 to 2028, while the pumping premium could reach 65%. Consequently, structural growth in flexibility margins is supporting resilient earnings despite price normalization in Europe.
11.
Sizable data load: Bernstein reports that rapid data center expansion in Portugal is creating massive demand for new generation capacity. EDP estimates that each additional gigawatt of data center demand will require four gigawatts of new renewable capacity paired with storage. This creates a massive growth opportunity for EDP's 5.5 gigawatt Iberian wind, solar, and battery pipeline. Thus, the rise of digital infrastructure underpins long-term volume and margin growth for Iberian utilities.

Battery Storage and Cannibalization

12.
Flattening price curves: Jefferies reports that massive battery storage deployment in Australia's National Electricity Market has begun to flatten the intraday price curve. Batteries set prices during 48% of dispatch intervals in the third quarter of 2026, up from 18% in the prior year. This increased participation has displaced higher-priced gas and hydro generation, driving average wholesale prices down by 27%. This real-world precedent warns that excessive battery capacity can rapidly erode energy arbitrage spreads for flexible hydro assets.
13.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein analysts suggest that the risk of battery storage eroding hydro earnings in Iberia is very low because BESS projects remain uneconomic without high spreads. A battery storage project in Spain or Portugal currently requires a high spread of about 70 euros per megawatt-hour over ten years to achieve a double-digit return. Consequently, the installation of utility-scale batteries will take significant time, allowing hydro reservoir and pumping spreads to remain highly profitable for years to come.
14.
Hydrological risk premium: Operational data shows that reservoir flexibility and external purchases cannot fully protect generation earnings during severe droughts. Morgan Stanley analysts point out that lower hydro volumes typically lead to procurement exposure and overhedging losses. Consequently, weather-driven volume deficits present a persistent downside risk to utility EPS forecasts. Therefore, investors must apply a higher risk premium to these earnings until sustained inflows provide better visibility on long-term volumes and cash flows.
15.
Other side of the story: Favorable hydrological trends can rapidly reverse recent water-shortage downgrades and drive significant EPS upgrades for Alpine utilities. Deutsche Bank Research notes that European hydro conditions could be turning, which would allow generators to capture high spot power prices with normalized volumes. A sudden return of normal precipitation would completely eliminate overhedging buyback risks and restore generation margins to peak levels across Central Europe.

Section 3 – Regulated Grid Growth, Transmission Tariffs, and Valuations

Section summary
  1. Austrian Power Grid is undergoing a massive capital expenditure cycle that will expand its asset base but requires substantial external funding.
  2. Peer networks like Iberdrola and RWE's Amprion command premium valuations that contrast sharply with Verbund's conglomerate discount and volatile merchant hydro earnings.
  3. Rising bond yields present a valuation headwind for long-duration regulated utilities despite robust inflation-indexation and cost-pass-through protections.

Austrian Power Grid Expansion

1.
Regulated asset expansion: Austrian Power Grid expects its regulated asset base to grow from 3.2 billion euros in fiscal 2025 to approximately 9.3 billion euros by fiscal 2033. This projected expansion is driven by a massive 9 billion euro investment programme aimed at modernizing and reinforcing the national transmission system. Consequently, the grid segment's contribution to Verbund's total EBITDA is projected to rise from 14% to 22% by fiscal 2030.
2.
Substantial capex deployment: Verbund intends to allocate 2.24 billion euros to grid investments via Austrian Power Grid between fiscal 2026 and 2028. This compares with 1.38 billion euros allocated to hydropower maintenance and 2.12 billion euros to new renewables over the same three-year period. Analysts note that this substantial grid capital expenditure program is heavily back-end loaded through 2034.
3.
Special regulatory release: Verbund anticipates that the Austrian regulator will reduce a special regulatory account of 230 million to 240 million euros in fiscal 2026. Unlike the ordinary regulatory account of approximately 600 million euros, this special release will not lower reported IFRS EBITDA because of existing provisions. This unique mechanism provides sizable near-term earnings insulation that helps offset severe hydrological deficits.

Regulatory Tariffs and Returns

4.
Decreasing regulatory uncertainty: Verbund operates under a supportive regulatory framework where the weighted average cost of capital for legacy assets is fixed through the current 2023 to 2028 regulatory period. The allowed pre-tax nominal WACC for legacy assets is set at 4.16% while returns for new investments in 2026 are higher at 6.11%. Looking ahead, the upcoming 2029 regulatory period represents a pivotal reset for allowed returns that will determine Verbund's long-term grid profitability.
5.
Competitive national returns: Austria's nominal allowed return on equity of 9.18% for new 2026 investments compares favorably with Spain's rate of 8.12%. However, it lags behind the post-tax nominal return of up to 10% expected for high-performing UK transmission operators under RIIO-T3. These structural differences highlight how national frameworks influence utility capital allocation choices across continental Europe.

Peer Valuation and Re-rating Narratives

6.
Strategic grid acquisition: RWE entered an agreement in September 2026 to acquire an additional 3% stake in M31 Beteiligungsgesellschaft to increase its total pro rata share in Amprion to approximately 58%. The German utility financed the transaction via a 4 billion euro equity measure to maintain financial discipline while expanding its regulated footprint. This transaction helps RWE establish a growing share of regulated revenue to offset merchant generation volatility.
7.
Regulated valuation premium: Morgan Stanley analysts value RWE's stake in Amprion using a business-specific WACC of 4.1% which implies a 41% premium to its 2027 regulated asset base. In a bullish scenario, this premium could rise to 60% as the market increasingly values stable transmission infrastructure. Consequently, RWE represents a prime example of a networks-led rerating story where regulated grid growth drives valuation multiples.

Iberdrola and Global Networks Supercycle

8.
Massive network scale: Iberdrola expects its global regulated asset base to grow in all countries and exceed 90 billion euros by 2030. This massive expansion is supported by allocating nearly 75% of its 10.1 billion euro investment program to stable grids in the United Kingdom, United States, and Brazil. Management's strategic pivot toward networks is designed to reinforce earnings defense while capturing double-digit growth.
9.
Premium networks multiple: Morgan Stanley analysts value Iberdrola at 20 times forward price-to-earnings, representing a 37% premium versus the utilities sector. This networks-led premium is supported by the company's ability to pivot capital across stable regulatory frameworks. Consequently, the broker argues that Iberdrola's high-quality compound growth justifies a permanently higher valuation multiple than peers.
10.
Network-driven target upgrade: Alantra raised its target price for Iberdrola by 27% to 27.40 euros per share while maintaining a buy recommendation. The firm expects networks to increase their share of the utility's EBITDA from 52% in 2026 to 57% by 2030. This strategic shift reinforces Iberdrola's role as a primary winner of the global investment cycle in networks today.
11.
Strategic geographic expansion: Iberdrola completed the acquisition of Finland's largest electricity distribution company, Caruna, for an enterprise value of approximately 5 billion euros in mid-2026. Caruna expects its earnings to grow by around 7% annually supported by annual investments of up to 300 million euros to digitalize and reinforce its electricity network. This strategic acquisition is fully aligned with Iberdrola's goal of strengthening its high-quality networks in stable, AA-plus rated countries.

Macro Yields and Transmission Precedents

12.
Rising rate pressures: Historically, European utility stocks tend to underperform the broader market when sovereign bond yields experience significant upward moves. Regulated network utilities and pure-play renewable developers are often hit hardest because their long-duration cash flows are highly sensitive to discount rate adjustments. However, Morgan Stanley analysts emphasize that higher yields represent a valuation headwind rather than earnings risk due to robust cost-pass-through mechanisms.
13.
Effective inflation hedging: Regulated utility networks in inflation-indexed jurisdictions like the United Kingdom and Italy benefit from automatic regulatory adjustments to their asset bases. For instance, National Grid and Terna enjoy high earnings protection because rising inflation translates directly into higher allowed revenues and regulatory asset values. This structural indexation helps insulate regulated utility cash flows from the dilutive effects of rising interest rates.
14.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein analysts reduced their valuation of Austrian Power Grid by 4.5% due to higher weighted average cost of capital assumptions and revised capital expenditure forecasts. This valuation downgrade implies that rising yields are not fully offset by regulatory adjustments in the short term. Consequently, Verbund's regulated grid expansion may deliver lower value creation spreads than historical averages if financing costs remain elevated.
15.
Instructive Spanish precedent: Spain's transmission operator Redeia experienced a 7% decline in its regulated asset base in fiscal 2024 after assets built before 1998 stopped being remunerated under historical regulatory provisions. This regulatory cliff illustrates how legacy asset retirements can suddenly disrupt a network utility's growth trajectory. The Spanish precedent warns that Verbund's long-term grid valuation depends heavily on maintaining a supportive regulatory framework beyond current price controls.
16.
Other side of the story: Accelerating grid capital expenditure and supportive regulatory updates could drive significant valuation upgrades and multiple expansion for Verbund. Regulators have a strong incentive to remain lenient and provide attractive returns to ensure that vital transmission investments are successfully executed. Therefore, Austrian Power Grid's projected asset growth could unlock substantial low-risk value that remains underappreciated by the sell-side's cautious consensus.

Section 4 – Windfall Levies, Litigation, and Power Price Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. The Austrian Constitutional Court's ongoing review of the EKB-S levy represents a pivotal regulatory catalyst that could eliminate Verbund's windfall tax burden.
  2. Verbund's effective price cap of approximately €103 to €105/MWh limits its net sensitivity to power price rallies compared to historical periods of unconstrained gearing.
  3. Czech peer CEZ enjoys a clear fiscal tailwind from the confirmed expiry of its windfall tax in 2025, highlighting the disadvantage of Verbund's extended Austrian levy.

EKB-S Levy Litigation and Verbund Impact

1.
Highly anticipated litigation: The Austrian Constitutional Court is currently reviewing the legal validity of the energy crisis contribution levy applied to power generators. In its report dated October 29, 2026, Bernstein Research notes that the court is evaluating whether the exceptional economic conditions originally used to justify the levy continue to exist from fiscal year 2024 onwards. This review remains a critical legal overhang for Verbund because a rejection of the levy would dramatically alter the company's long-term earnings trajectory.
2.
Favorable legal developments: Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 5, 2026, that the Austrian Constitutional Court is likely to reject the generator windfall levy in its upcoming decision. According to the broker's call feedback, this anticipated legal victory could eliminate the persistent regulatory drag on Verbund's hydro earnings. If the court rules against the levy, it would remove a major valuation discount currently applied to Verbund's merchant generation business.
3.
Severe legislative risk: Bernstein Research highlighted on November 6, 2026, that the Austrian Constitutional Court questioned the underlying federal legislation during July 2026 hearings. However, the broker notes that Verbund's management warned that the Austrian government could seek to amend the regulation to preserve its revenues if the current framework is invalidated. This potential legislative response prevents analysts from fully pricing in the positive legal upside until a final, unamendable ruling is established.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research calculated on November 6, 2026, that if the Austrian energy crisis contribution levy is declared unconstitutional and charges drop to zero, it would represent a substantial direct upside to Verbund's earnings. The broker estimates that a complete elimination of the levy would boost Verbund's valuation by approximately €0.9 per share. This calculation demonstrates that the windfall tax risk overhang may be entirely cleared by legal challenges, rendering the bearish regulatory narrative obsolete.

Power Price Sensitivity and Effective Thresholds

5.
High effective thresholds: Bernstein Research explained on November 6, 2026, that while the statutory threshold for the windfall tax is €90/MWh, the effective realized threshold for Verbund next year will be approximately €103 to €105/MWh due to renewable investment offsets. Consequently, the broker models the windfall tax using a realized cap threshold of €105/MWh through March 2030. This higher threshold implies that Verbund keeps a larger share of power price upside than a bare reading of the €90/MWh cap suggests.
6.
Highly asymmetric risk: Morgan Stanley reported on November 5, 2026, that Verbund faces an asymmetric risk profile because the effective Austrian price cap of approximately €103/MWh limits earnings upside while leaving the downside fully exposed. The broker maintains an Underweight rating because any drop in gas and power prices could quickly wipe out recent consensus earnings-per-share upgrades. This asymmetry makes the utility highly vulnerable to merchant price corrections regardless of short-term forward price rallies.
7.

This extended levy imposes a 95% tax rate on revenues exceeding €90/MWh for older assets and €100/MWh for new plants built after April 1, 2025. This policy extension ensures that the Austrian state will continue to capture excess generator profits, limiting Verbund's long-term organic cash generation.
8.
High historical gearing: ING Wholesale Banking reported in its sectoral review dated June 25, 2012, that Verbund is structurally the most power leveraged name in the European utility sector. The broker explained that this extreme sensitivity is driven by a high share of fixed-cost hydro generation and historically low short-term hedging levels compared to international peers. However, the introduction of modern regulatory caps and windfall levies has permanently weakened this direct relationship by limiting earnings upside.

Peer Windfall Tax Narratives

9.
Rising peer exposures: Bernstein Research highlighted on May 27, 2025, that EVN AG reiterated its fiscal year 2024/2025 group net result guidance of €400 to €440 million despite falling market prices. While EVN also operates under the Austrian EKB-S levy, its larger retail supply segment provides some earnings insulation. This structural setup differs from Verbund's high merchant exposure, which remains far more sensitive to the regulatory cap thresholds.
10.
Decisive regulatory expiry: CEZ AS confirmed in its annual financial report published on April 29, 2025, that the Czech windfall tax surcharge of 60% is set to terminate on December 31, 2025. The company expects its effective income tax rate to fall to 21% from fiscal year 2026 onwards, down from a high of 75% in fiscal year 2024. This clear expiry date stands in sharp contrast to Verbund's extended Austrian levy, which restricts the Austrian company's profitability until 2030.
11.
Strong post-levy recovery: Morgan Stanley reported on March 12, 2026, that CEZ's fiscal year 2026 adjusted net income guidance of CZK 27 to 31 billion was supported by the removal of the Czech windfall tax. The broker notes that this tax removal helps offset several operational headwinds, including lower realized power prices as older hedges roll off and scheduled nuclear maintenance. Consequently, the elimination of the windfall tax provides CEZ with a clear fiscal tailwind that Verbund cannot match due to its ongoing Austrian levies.
12.
Severe Czech tax impacts: CEZ AS experienced substantial earnings compression during the peak of the energy crisis due to the Czech government's 60% corporate tax surcharge on windfall profits. In its interim report published on August 31, 2023, the utility estimated that this windfall tax would reduce its fiscal year 2023 net income by approximately CZK 22 to 30 billion. This extreme regulatory intervention highlights how European governments successfully captured utility profits, a historical precedent that continues to inform Verbund's regulatory risk premium.
13.
Complex sensitivity modeling: Bernstein Research reported on November 6, 2026, that Verbund's year-to-date power buyback costs through September reached €470 million gross and €120 million net. Analysts must distinguish between gross sensitivity and net sensitivity because dry-year water deficits force Verbund to buy back pre-hedged volumes at high spot prices. This buyback mechanism severely dampens Verbund's net sensitivity to rising power prices, preventing the company from fully capturing market rallies.

Section 8 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

The common view in the research is cautious. Regulation and windfall tax dynamics create asymmetric downside risk for Verbund because the effective price cap restricts upside earnings potential while lower market prices fully pass through to EPS. The sections above show that this view rests on four assumptions: the EKB-S levy persists in a form that caps price upside, hydrology stays a drag, net debt rises with capex, and regulated grid growth is valued conservatively. If any of these proves wrong, the outcomes diverge sharply. The upside cases are driven by external decisions, and the downside cases by weather and cost.

1.
Upside case 1: levy abolishment. This is the largest single swing factor. Deutsche Bank reported that the Constitutional Court is likely to reject the generator windfall levy. Bernstein estimates that a complete elimination would lift Verbund's valuation by approximately €0.9 per share. The knock-on effects go beyond that figure. Bernstein puts the effective threshold at about €103 to €105/MWh and models a realized cap of €105/MWh through March 2030. Removing the levy would remove that cap, so higher forward prices would flow into earnings. This is the mechanism Morgan Stanley cites for staying cautious: the cap limits earnings upside while leaving the downside fully exposed. Deutsche Bank argues that legal resolution would allow analysts to re-rate the stock's long-term valuation. The upside is not guaranteed, however. Management warned that the government could amend the regulation to preserve revenues, and this prevents analysts from fully pricing in the legal upside until a final ruling. A rejection would force the government to design a different tax method, which would take significant time to resolve legally. A court win could therefore produce a replacement levy rather than a clean exit. Upside case 2: hydrology normalises. Verbund reported a hydro coefficient of 0.61 for the first nine months of 2026, including 0.49 in the dry third quarter. Net power buyback losses reached 120 million euros, of which 90 million euros fell in the third quarter. A reversal would be self-reinforcing. Deutsche Bank notes that a return of normal precipitation would eliminate overhedging buyback risks and restore generation margins. It sees up to 20% earnings upside if hydro conditions normalize alongside high power prices. Strong operating cash flow could then deleverage the balance sheet ahead of sell-side expectations. A record hydro year combined with the levy cap would be less valuable than the same year without the cap. The two upside cases therefore compound each other.
2.
Downside case 1: persistent drought. Verbund is forced to buy back power to fulfill its contracts, which turns higher wholesale prices into a penalty rather than an opportunity. If water levels remain below normal, Verbund risks missing its revised net profit guidance of 1.05 billion euros. The gross-to-net distinction matters here. Year-to-date buyback costs reached €470 million gross and €120 million net. A prolonged shortfall could stop that cushion from working. Peers show the pattern is regional. BKW expects full-year operating profit at the lower end of its range. Hidroelectrica made external power purchases of 1001 GWh in the third quarter. A related risk is price mean-reversion. Bernstein projects the achieved price falling from 130.5 euros/MWh in fiscal 2027 to 113.0 euros/MWh in fiscal 2028. Because the downside is not capped, a combined drought and price correction would hit earnings fully.

Downside case 2: grid capex inflation and the 2029 reset.
