Section 3 – Asset-Mix Shifts, Deposits, and Leverage-Ratio Loops

Section summary
  1. Customer asset-mix shifts create a powerful balancing loop that stabilizes Avanza's earnings across differing market cycles.
  2. Stricter leverage ratio rules constrain balance sheet growth, forcing capital optimization through AT1 debt issuance.

The Asset Mix Feedback Loop

1.
Shifting asset mix: When retail customers shift cash into equities and funds during bull markets, Avanza's liquid balance sheet deposits contract. Deutsche Bank's report on 30 September 2026 notes that this rotation improves the regulatory leverage ratio because deposit liabilities decrease. However, this shift reduces the high-margin cash balances that generate net interest income, meaning that near-term interest earnings soften. Therefore, rising risk appetite creates a balancing feedback loop that caps total interest income growth during equity market expansions. Counter-cyclical deposit accumulation: Conversely, a market downturn triggers de-risking behavior, pushing customer assets back into cash deposits. DNB Carnegie's report on 6 October 2026 highlights that clients historically accumulate cash on investment accounts during periods of high equity volatility. This cash sorting provides automatic stabilizers because the expanding cash base fuels net interest income even as brokerage revenues decline. Consequently, this counter-cyclical flow represents a natural hedging mechanism that protects platform earnings during corrections.
2.
Historical velocity comparison: The speed and scale of these asset-mix shifts differed markedly between the post-pandemic cycle and the current expansion. Deutsche Bank's report on 30 September 2026 shows that customer cash balances normalized rapidly as risk appetite recovered in 2024 to 2026. In contrast, during the 2020 to 2022 cycle, the rapid rate hikes by the Riksbank drove NII from SEK 321 million to SEK 1.6 billion. This indicates that the current loop is more volume-driven, whereas the earlier cycle was propelled by unprecedented rate velocity. Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie's report on 19 October 2026 shows that despite a sequential drop in deposits during the third quarter of 2026, net interest income was supported by lending growth. The broker indicates that interest-bearing assets remained highly resilient because customers increased their margin borrowing. This suggests that risk-on shifts do not inevitably damage interest income because leverage demand acts as an offset, stabilizing the platform's net revenues.

Leverage Capital Constraints

3.
Tightening leverage constraints: Rapid deposit growth acts as a headwind under capital adequacy regulations by consuming valuable leverage ratio headroom. Deutsche Bank's report on 30 September 2026 points out that customer deposits increase total balance sheet exposure regardless of asset risk weights. Because the leverage ratio serves as the binding capital constraint, massive cash inflows jeopardize excess capital reserves. This regulatory pressure means that unchecked platform deposits will constrain long-term shareholder distribution capacity. Sparkonto phase-out consequences: The termination of the Sparkonto+ program accelerated balance sheet growth by internalizing third-party deposits back onto the core platform. Avanza's interim report on 15 October 2026 confirms that this wind-down added nearly SEK 40 billion in deposits over one year. Although this internalization provided abundant liquidity, it pushed the leverage ratio down to 4.2% in early 2026. Therefore, the termination created a structural capital burden that forced strategic balance sheet remediation.
4.
Dilutive capital issuance: To restore leverage headroom without restricting growth, Avanza engaged in dilutive Tier 1 capital market activities. Avanza's press release on 25 March 2026 announced the issuance of SEK 500 million in Additional Tier 1 notes. This followed an earlier SEK 800 million AT1 issuance in 2025 to absorb the deposit inflows from Sparkonto+. Section summary

  2. Capitalizing software development costs defers immediate expenses, while underlying pricing pressure has historically forced costly platform fee concessions.
  3. Advisory-led custody growth can function as a false friend that expands assets without driving proportional revenue gains.

Social Network and Engagement Loops

1.
Interactive engagement network: Nordnet actively operates its proprietary social network Shareville to drive customer interaction and create an engagement loop that competitor Avanza currently lacks. This platform brings together approximately 300,000 active users who can view, analyze, and directly copy the real-money portfolios of other traders. Consequently, this social infrastructure creates a powerful reinforcing loop that sustains high retail trading volumes during periods of elevated volatility. Regional network consolidation: Nordnet has historically utilized acquisitions to expand its customer footprint and integrate diverse regional user groups into its social ecosystem. The acquisition of Norwegian broker Netfonds in 2019 allowed Nordnet to launch a consolidated, pan-Nordic platform that serves over 1 million customers. This consolidation amplified regional network effects because it unified active Norwegian and Swedish retail traders under a single digital hub.
2.
Low-cost customer acquisition: Shareville functions as an organic customer acquisition machine that dramatically lowers marketing costs across Nordnet's pan-Nordic operations. This social network engages users and enhances brand loyalty, which allows Nordnet to maintain very low acquisition costs relative to customer lifetime value. This means the network reinforces regional brand equity without requiring expensive paid marketing campaigns. Threat of social competition: Emerging platforms threaten to disrupt Nordnet's social engagement advantage by launching heavily funded social trading applications across Europe. Social broker Shares raised $90 million to expand its European footprint and target retail investors with integrated chat and sharing features. This expanding competitive threat means Nordnet must continuously upgrade Shareville's functionality or risk losing younger, highly active cohorts to specialized neo-brokers. Mainstream platform constraints: Avanza lacks a native social networking ecosystem, which forces the platform to rely on traditional content delivery and educational resources. The company utilizes public savings economists and investment coaches on social media to build trust with younger clients. However, this structure cannot easily replicate the organic engagement loop of a peer-to-peer sharing application, which means Avanza remains more vulnerable to cyclical retail trading drops.

Platform Accounting and Capitalization Loops

3.
Capitalized technology investments: Nordnet utilizes IAS 38 accounting rules to capitalize a substantial portion of its software development costs. The carrying amount of its major capitalized projects reached SEK 381.8 million in late 2024, focusing primarily on websites and customer journeys. This capitalization policy defers immediate expense recognition because development costs are amortized over three to seven years instead of being expensed immediately.
4.
Geographic capitalization variations: Accounting differences also extend to how the two platforms capitalize or expense their respective geographic expansion initiatives. Avanza plans to capitalize approximately 20% of its estimated SEK 120 million to SEK 150 million Denmark launch costs on the balance sheet. However, the remaining 80% is expensed immediately, whereas Nordnet's heavy German launch investments are subject to gradual capitalization and amortization which softens the near-term earnings drag.
5.
Conservative expensing philosophy: Avanza follows a highly conservative accounting philosophy by recognizing nearly all of its technology and product development costs directly in the income statement. The platform's management explicitly states they wish to keep the balance sheet as simple as possible and do not want to postpone costs. Therefore, Avanza's operating expenses reflect real-time technology investments which creates a cleaner balance sheet than Nordnet's capitalized model.
6.
Amortization path consequences: Capitalized technology investments eventually translate into rising depreciation and amortization charges that depress mid-term operating profits. Nordnet's continued platform investments have steadily expanded its stock of amortizable intangible assets, resulting in a rising depreciation charge. This means that while capitalization inflates current-period operating margins, it creates a predictable, multiyear drag on future earnings per share unless revenue growth outpaces the amortization ramp. Distorted profitability comparisons: These divergent accounting practices heavily distort reported operating margins and cost metrics of the two platforms. Nordnet reported a sector-leading operating margin of 76% in fiscal year 2025, whereas Avanza's margin was lower at 62%. However, this gap is partially artificial because Nordnet capitalizes balanced personnel expenses of SEK 178.3 million annually while Avanza expenses personnel fully.

Precedents of Balancing Price Loops

7.
Precedent margin compression: Hargreaves Lansdown provides a prominent industry precedent of how a premium platform fee structure can become unsustainable when customer inflows decelerate. The UK market leader historically maintained a 45 basis point platform fee, which was up to three times higher than cheaper platform competitors. This structural premium triggered chronic customer market share losses because younger clients preferred low-cost alternatives.
8.
Forced fee reductions: Facing persistent client outflows, Hargreaves Lansdown was eventually forced to introduce significant fee cuts starting on 1 March 2026. The platform reduced its headline platform charge from 45 basis points to 35 basis points for a majority of its workplace pension investors. This repricing immediately impacted back-book fee margins because pricing concessions applied to existing assets under administration. topic-specific reports Comparative platform flywheels: United Kingdom competitor AJ Bell provides a contrasting precedent where scale economies are proactively reinvested into lower customer charges to drive inflows. AJ Bell's direct-toconsumer platform fee of 25 basis points remains significantly cheaper than Hargreaves Lansdown's historical fee structure. This aggressive pricing reinvestment drives a highly effective flywheel because it attracts younger retail investor demographics that fuel long-term asset compounding.
9.
False friend inflows: Italian platform FinecoBank provides a comparable past case showing how custody inflows can act as a false friend during rapid interest rate hikes. FinecoBank experienced massive customer cashsorting as clients rotated cash out of deposits and into sovereign bonds like BTP Valore. This shift grew assets under custody but decelerated high-margin deposit inflows to zero, proving that unmonetized custody assets do not expand platform profitability.

Strategic Adjustments and Contrarian Views

10.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank's initiation report on 30 September 2026 argues that Avanza's conservative expensing policy is widely understood by investors and actually justifies a valuation premium over Nordnet on a price-to-earnings basis. Because Avanza maintains a simpler balance sheet without deferred amortizable assets, the market perceives its earnings as higher quality. This implies that conservative expensing does not penalize Avanza's valuation, which means its reported operating margin remains highly credible. Other side of the story: Although Nordnet's software capitalization policy flatters its current operating margin, it creates a persistent balancing loop by storing up future amortization charges that drag on mid-term earnings. In contrast, Avanza's decision to expense technology costs immediately means its income statement is fully insulated from future write-down risks and unexpected depreciation. Therefore, Avanza's clean accounting structure preserves dividend capacity over the next eighteen months because it avoids deferred balance sheet amortization.
