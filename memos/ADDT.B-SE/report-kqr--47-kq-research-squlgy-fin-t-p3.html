Section 3 – The Arithmetic Loop and M&A Valuation Economics

Section summary
  1. The arithmetic loop shows that reinvesting strong cash flows into low-multiple acquisitions generates substantial self-funded growth.
  2. Passing the SEK 25bn sales threshold increases the pressure to acquire larger targets, which risks diluting entry returns.

Mechanics of the Compounding Loop

1.
Efficient compounding: The core arithmetic compounding loop operates by systematically reinvesting strong subsidiary cash flows back into new acquisitions at low multiples. Addtech converts approximately 73% of its EBITA into free cash flow, which is then deployed into buying niche businesses at a median multiple of 6.8x EV/EBITA. This self-funded reinvestment loop generates an impressive 17% inorganic growth potential without requiring any outside equity.
2.
Stable leverage: Programmatic acquirers maintain a constant leverage ratio because each acquired target contributes fresh EBITDA to the consolidated group. Assuming that new EBITA can be leveraged by 2.5x, Addtech can fund its transaction pipeline with a mix of cash and debt while keeping its net debt leverage unchanged. This multiplier effect allows the group to expand its absolute debt capacity in lockstep with earnings growth.
3.
Highly profitable: The inverse of the entry multiple paid for a target represents the steady-state return on capital of that investment. By consistently acquiring private niche companies at 6.8x EV/EBITA, Addtech achieves an immediate implied return of approximately 14.7% on its deployed capital. This high return comfortably exceeds the weighted average cost of capital even before factoring in post-acquisition operational synergies.
4.
Balancing pressures: Maintaining a double-digit acquired growth rate becomes mathematically more difficult as consolidated group sales pass the SEK 25bn threshold. To move the needle at this size, the group must either increase its transaction frequency or pursue larger target acquisitions. If Addtech shifts toward larger deals, it faces higher entry multiples that diminish its reinvestment returns.

Section 4 – The Earn-Out Signal and Purchase Plan Performance

Section summary
  1. Recurring earn-out revaluation gains represent a non-cash accounting adjustment that mechanically elevates Addtech's reported profitability.
  2. Contrasting peer frameworks show that companies like Lifco and Lagercrantz use equity-settled structures to insulate their operating earnings.

Mechanics of Revaluation Accounting

1.
Systematic revaluations: Contingent considerations are measured at fair value on the acquisition date based on the projected earnings of the target company. Under IFRS 3 guidelines, if an acquired subsidiary underperforms its purchase plan, the estimated liability is written down. This downward adjustment is recognized as a non-cash gain in other operating income, which mechanically raises operating profit despite weaker business operations. Consequently, these adjustments can temporarily distort true underlying earnings.
2.
Distorted earnings: Recurring revaluation gains flow directly through the profit and loss statement to boost reported EBITA. Because these paper gains increase net profit without requiring any actual cash inflows, they artificially inflate the diluted earnings per share metrics presented to investors. This financial presentation makes the acquisition flywheel appear highly efficient even when individual subsidiaries are struggling to meet their initial financial targets. Ultimately, this dynamic creates a mismatch between reported profitability and operational cash conversion.
3.
Obscured growth: Organic performance trends can be masked when companies utilize frequent non-cash adjustments to meet quarterly market consensus. Analysts often strip out these contingent consideration adjustments to assess the clean operating margins of serial acquirers. - Earnings Calls For example, DNB Carnegie highlighted that adjusting for these revaluations was necessary to reveal the persistent weakness in organic volume trends during late 2025. Without these clean adjustments, investors risk overestimating the organic health of the compounding model.
