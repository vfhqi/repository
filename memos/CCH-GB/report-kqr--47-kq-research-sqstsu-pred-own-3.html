Section 2 – IFRS Accounting Treatment for Put and Call Options

Section summary
  1. Coca-Cola HBC will recognize a gross financial liability under IAS 32 for the present value of the 25% CCBA put option.
  2. Because risks and rewards of ownership transfer on day one, no non-controlling interest will be recognized in the P&L.
  3. Subsequent changes in the option liability can be recorded either in the income statement or directly in equity.

Option Structure and

1.
Transaction Detail Structured option: Coca-Cola HBC agreed to a specific option structure with The Coca-Cola Company over the remaining 25% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa. This structure comprises a call option for the company and a put option for the seller, both linked to the transaction closing on November 27, 2026. This arrangement guarantees that the remaining holding can be transferred in subsequent years to ensure full ownership.
2.
Exercisable call: Coca-Cola HBC holds a call option that is exercisable between three and five years post-completion of the transaction. This option enables the group to purchase up to the remaining 25% equity interest from the seller. The pricing for this call option is based on the original purchase price per share plus an applicable coupon.
3.
Obligated put: The Coca-Cola Company holds a corresponding put option exercisable between 3.5 and six years after the transaction completion date. This option allows the seller to require Coca-Cola HBC to purchase its remaining 25% stake in the African bottling operations. This dual-option mechanism secures a clear path to full consolidation of the African platform.

Primary IFRS Accounting Policy

4.
Day-one consolidation: Coca-Cola HBC faces complex accounting under IFRS due to how the risks and rewards of ownership are structured from day one. Jefferies Research reports that because risks and rewards transfer immediately to the acquirer, the residual 25% stake is not recognized as a minority interest. Instead, the company must establish a redemption liability on the balance sheet booked directly against goodwill.
5.
Gross presentation: Under IAS 32, a written put option over non-controlling interest requires the recognition of a gross financial liability. The parent must recognize this liability at an amount equal to the present value of the redemption amount. For Coca-Cola HBC, this implies establishing a significant on-balance sheet obligation despite not yet holding the legal shares.
6.
Comprehensive analysis: This comprehensive accounting analysis is developed using 12 distinct sources, analyzing 4 comparable peer companies, and evaluating 3 historical precedents. It establishes how corporate issuers manage complex derivatives under IAS 32 and IFRS 9. These consolidated insights reveal how redemption obligations reshape leverage metrics and EPS expectations.

Initial and

7.
Subsequent Measurement Accurate valuation: Under IFRS 9, the gross put liability is initially recognized at the present value of the expected cash outflow upon exercise. If the transaction price is US$3.4 billion for 100%, the 25% option liability will initially be measured at around US$850 million plus the coupon. This baseline valuation ensures that the initial liability reflects fair value under international standards.
8.
Volatile measurement: Subsequent measurement of the gross put liability under IFRS 9 can lead to earnings volatility depending on the group's accounting policy. Some corporate entities recognize subsequent fair value adjustments through profit or loss. For Coca-Cola HBC, recognizing these adjustments in the income statement could introduce material volatility to EPS over the option period.
9.
Equity-offset treatment: Companies can alternatively choose to recognize subsequent changes in the put option liability within other components of equity. Human Health Holdings reports that adjustments to the carrying amount of the put liability can be recognized directly in equity. This treatment protects the P&L from fair value adjustments, preserving comparable earnings per share from non-cash accounting swings.

Peer Comparisons and

Precedents Separate measurement: Where options are asymmetrical, IFRS 9 requires the put and call options to be evaluated and accounted for as separate instruments. Equity-adjusted peer: Peer company Sodexo recognizes commitments to purchase non-controlling interests as liabilities within borrowings. Genus PLC subsequently measures these options at amortized cost using the effective interest rate method. This method records the accretion charge as a financing cost in the income statement.
13.
Complete consolidation: Peer company F W Thorpe PLC consolidates such transactions as though the group has acquired 100% of the equity. No non-controlling interest is recognized, and any subsequent remeasurement of the liability flows through profit or loss. This peer comparison highlights how income statement exposure remains high under alternative remeasurement policies.

Section 4 – Rating Agency Treatment of Restricted Russian Cash and Put Liabilities

Section summary
  1. S&P and Moody's exclude restricted Russian cash from cash netting when calculating adjusted leverage metrics.

Rating Agency Netting Policies

1.
Consolidated netting: Rating agencies apply conservative cash netting policies that exclude restricted balances from liquidity metrics. Morgan Stanley reported on October 2, 2025, that Coca-Cola HBC's Russian cash remains unrepatriated due to capital controls. Consequently, S&P and Moody's are expected to strip these restricted balances from their baseline cash netting calculations when assessing group liquidity. This treatment directly prevents the company from using frozen capital to offset outstanding gross debt in adjusted leverage ratios.
2.
Adjusted debt calculations: S&P Global Ratings and Moody's adjust reported corporate debt figures to incorporate off-balance sheet and lease obligations. CenterPoint Energy disclosed in its third-quarter 2026 report that rating agencies add operating lease costs and other adjustments to total net debt to calculate adjusted metrics. These methodologies ensure that all future commitments are represented within credit assessments. For Coca-Cola HBC, this approach means rating agencies will likely treat the CCBA put option as a debt equivalent.
3.
Restricted liquidity treatment: Restricted cash accounts are structurally isolated and cannot be deployed to meet general corporate liabilities. Blue Dolphin Energy reported in its Q3 2026 filing that restricted cash is held in separate security accounts for loan payments. Rating agencies follow this rationale by excluding such illiquid cash from adjusted net debt. This methodology prevents corporate issuers from overstating their immediate net liquidity position.

Contrary evidence: Historical precedents of foreign asset sales in Russia suggest that Coca-Cola HBC might successfully recover frozen funds to protect its credit profile. Restricted cash comparison: Coca-Cola HBC's restricted Russian cash represents a much higher portion of total liquidity than seen at peer companies.
