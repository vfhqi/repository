Section 2 – Germany's Infrastructure Fund Allocations and Tender Pipeline

Section summary
  1. Germany's special infrastructure fund allocates €500bn over twelve years to modernise transportation networks and energy grids.
  2. Severe bureaucratic delays and sluggish regional drawdowns defer the materials volume lift until at least 2027.
  3. The massive funding influx will gradually stimulate construction demand once administrative bottlenecks are resolved.

Structural funding allocation

1.
Ambitious structural packages: The German federal government has established a massive off-budget fund to address severe underinvestment in critical transportation and climaterelated projects. According to Deutsche Bank Research's update on 8 September 2026, the Special Fund for Infrastructure and Climate Neutrality allocates €500bn over a twelve-year horizon. This massive program is intended to lift Germany's long-term potential growth but the administrative execution remains highly complex. 31 32 g y y, q gy p p Projected annual spending path and funding sources of Germany's infrastructure fund through 2036 Therefore, these capital injections will underpin a multi-year construction cycle that supports regional aggregates demand.
2.
Segmented transport priority: The deployment of the special fund relies on heavy allocations to transport networks over the near term. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 3 July 2026 that €33bn is earmarked for roads and railways in 2026, with an additional €94bn planned between 2027 and 2029. This combined funding dwarfs past national allocations and targets critical freight corridors. 37, the substantial scale of transport spending should drive robust medium-term sales growth for aggregates suppliers like Buzzi.
3.
Concentrated rail investments: Official budget plans indicate that the majority of transport-related funding is directed specifically toward revitalising Germany's dilapidated railway network. Morgan Stanley's strategy note on 12 October 2026 states that transport represents the largest special fund spending group in 2027 at €17.7bn, with nearly 80% dedicated specifically to railways. This concentration is designed to modernise key routes, but the direct civil engineering content is lower than simple physical upgrades. & Co Redburn This means the earnings benefit of these railway upgrades is skewed toward specialised construction suppliers rather than pure heavyside volume producers.

Regional drawdown and delays

4.
Sluggish regional drawdowns: Although the central government has secured massive borrowing permissions, regional authorities are drawing down funding at an extremely slow pace. Morgan Stanley reported on 22 October 2026 that regional states have drawn down only €1.3bn of SVIK funds in the first nine months of 2026. This performance is exceptionally weak compared to the government's full-year earmarked regional target of €8.3bn. These delays mean that the positive volume impulse for cement plants is deferred until regional administration bottlenecks are cleared.
5.
sluggish municipal spending: Severe budgetary constraints at the local level have created an unprecedented backlog in municipal infrastructure investments despite available national guarantees. Deutsche Bank Research's note on 1 October 2026 highlighted that German municipalities registered a record deficit of over €20bn in the first half of 2026. This financial distress prevents local authorities from co-funding new civil engineering works despite the massive backlog in roads. Unless local financing is restructured, the municipal demand for building materials will remain deeply depressed.
6.
Contrary evidence: The ongoing public investments in infrastructure have actually declined in real terms despite the highly publicised launch of the special fund. CaixaBank Research's economic monitor on 22 October 2026 revealed that German public investment in infrastructure has fallen by over 5% year-on-year up to August 2026. This contraction contrasts sharply with the federal government's target growth rate of nearly 29% for the full year. 53 54 suggests that the actual volume lift in 2027 may fall far short of optimistic consensus expectations.

Projected volumes and recovery

7.
Gradual construction expansion: Despite near-term administrative bottlenecks, industry experts expect public spending to eventually translate into visible construction volume growth. ING Wholesale Banking projected on 7 September 2026 that German construction will grow by 1% in 2026 and accelerate by 2% in 2027. This recovery represents a critical turning point following five consecutive years of sector contraction. Consequently, this turning point will provide a highly supportive demand backdrop that protects European cement pricing power.
8.
Significant demand stimulation: The long-delayed flow of infrastructure funds is expected to provide a substantial boost to physical materials consumption. Jefferies reported on 14 July 2026 that the transport-focused portion of the special fund alone could stimulate 3% to 6% growth in the broader German construction sector. This expected volume increase represents the most significant stimulus in a generation, but contractors are not expected to call off materials in earnest until 2027. 61 62 Thus, the material profit contribution to Heidelberg Materials will only register during the 2027 fiscal year.
9.
Refurbishment demand growth: Refurbishment of deteriorating structures represents a major driver of specialized materials consumption that benefits high-margin suppliers. Rothschild & Co Redburn argued on 15 September 2025 that over 40% of German bridges require replacement or significant repair, driving a massive wave of refurbishment. This structural backlog should support a 2% annual increase in German construction output. This trend will underpin long-term volume stability for specialized additives and high-performance concrete mixtures.

Past baseline comparison

10.
Depressed historical volumes: The planned infrastructure recovery must be evaluated against historically low volumes of German civil engineering and transport construction. Morgan Stanley reported on 3 September 2026 that German civil engineering order volumes remain well below postpandemic peaks and historical 1990 levels. Furthermore, investment in German road construction has declined to near 35-year lows in physical volume terms. This massive historical deficit means the planned stimulus represents a necessary catch-up phase rather than an inflationary demand shock.
11.
Comparing past cycles: Historically, major infrastructure rollouts require significant lead times before they stimulate physical materials consumption. Strabag SE noted on 15 September 2026 that off-budget funds will begin to stimulate physical building demand from late 2026 or early 2027 at the earliest. This timeline mirrors the slow multi-year deployment of the US Bipartisan Infrastructure Law which faced similar planning backlogs. 72 & Co Redburn Therefore, investors must expect a delayed but highly durable earnings opportunity rather than an immediate profit spike.
12.
Other side of the story: While the €500bn headline figure suggests an unprecedented boom, a significant portion of this fund is being used to substitute existing core budget lines. Morgan Stanley estimated on 12 October 2026 that only 30% to 40% of the special fund represents genuine, additional infrastructure investment. This translates to approximately €15bn to €20bn of incremental annual spend, because the core budget has been simultaneously reduced. Consequently, the total net volume impact on cement and aggregates will be materially lower than what the headline fiscal package suggests.

Section 3 – Post-Q2 2026 Cement Volumes and 2027 Corporate Growth Outlooks

Section summary
  1. Germany and Italy cement volumes since Q2 2026 remain depressed due to weak residential construction, deferring the anticipated volume inflection to 2027.
  2. Major European cement producers assume a gradual volume recovery in 2027, driven by infrastructure offtake and stabilizing housing starts. Net read: Diversification provides peers like Holcim and Cementir with more resilient mid-term volume projections than regional pure-plays face.

Post-Q2 2026 German volume trends

1.
Depressed German demand: High mortgage rates and financial distress continue to choke the German residential sector. Morgan Stanley reported on 12 October 2026 that German building permits continue to weaken and remain near cyclical lows. This persistent residential decline means that heavyside producers are operating at severely depressed utilisation levels, limiting near-term volume recovery. Consequently, any significant volume lift is deferred until the housing market stabilises in late 2027.
2.
Stabilising infrastructure demand: While the residential market remains in contraction, public civil engineering works are starting to register some interest. Jefferies reported on 30 September 2026 that Buzzi has received initial project enquiries in Germany related to the federal infrastructure program. However, physical offtake is unlikely to become visible until early 2027 due to construction cycle lags. Therefore, the immediate earnings contribution from German infrastructure remains limited for the rest of fiscal 2026. 83 84 87 Management quotes on the deployment timeline and volume impact of German infrastructure funding.

Divergent regional expectations: Investment delays are causing short-term volume contractions that overshadow positive pricing actions in Central Europe. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 29 September 2026 that Buzzi faces a mid-single-digit cement volume decline in Germany for the third quarter of 2026. This decline is only partially offset by low-single-digit price increases. This negative volume trend implies that Central Europe will act as an organic drag on group profitability in the near term.

Post-Q2 2026 Italian volume trends

4.
Sluggish Italian demand: Italian cement consumption has suffered a notable slowdown since the middle of fiscal 2026. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 29 September 2026 that Buzzi's Italian volumes are projected to contract by approximately 6% in the third quarter of 2026. This downward pressure persists despite a projected 3% price increase supported by the incoming carbon border adjustment mechanism. This volume decline indicates that domestic demand remains highly challenging across the peninsula.
5.
Italian incentive phase-out: The expiration of state-backed renovation incentives has removed a key pillar of Italian construction activity. Morgan Stanley noted on 5 August 2026 that the gradual phase-out of the superbonus tax scheme continues to weigh heavily on Italian residential construction. Furthermore, public works are slowing down as projects linked to the National Recovery and Resilience Plan begin to wind down. This regulatory transition means that Italian cement volumes will contract unless new public civil works accelerate.
6.
Temporary volume stabilization: Despite the broader structural slowdown, certain regional segments have experienced brief demand improvements. Expert interviews by AlphaSense on 7 August 2026 revealed that Italian cement purchasing volumes actually increased by 5% to 6% in the second quarter of 2026. However, this recovery was highly localized and driven primarily by immediate infrastructure project awards. This suggests that the Italian cement volume recovery is too fragmented to support a sustained national volume inflection.

Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A delayed infrastructure recovery would severely compress earnings for under-capacity cement producers.
  2. Structural overcapacity and aggressive local pricing by independent players represent substantial downside risks.

Severe operational downside risks

1.
Unabsorbed fixed overheads: Persistent volume stagnation into fiscal year 2027 would trigger severe margin contraction across regional building material plants. Morgan Stanley reported on 12 October 2026 that German and Dutch cement margins are already squeezed by cost inflation and low utilization. If expected infrastructure projects fail to materialize in 2027, Buzzi and Heidelberg Materials will face unprecedented fixedcost absorption penalties at their underutilized facilities. Consequently, this operational drag would depress Central European segment profitability below current consensus estimates.
2.
Destructive pricing wars: The failure of demand to turn would force regional producers into aggressive price- cutting to protect their kiln utilization levels. An expert from Oldcastle Infrastructure explained on 16 September 2026 that cement kilns must operate continuously like chemical plants to remain viable. If a supplier attempts to capture downstream assets to fill its capacity, it deprives competing kilns of volume, prompting rivals to aggressively slash local delivered prices. Therefore, a delayed recovery would spark destructive price competition that erodes industry-wide pricing power.
3.
Systemic overcapacity exposure: European cement markets remain structurally vulnerable to demand shocks due to a massive overhang of underutilized plants. Morgan Stanley reported on 17 February 2026 that the average utilization rate across Europe's five largest cement markets is only 50%. Without a major volume inflection in 2027, maintaining this idle capacity will become economically unviable, forcing manufacturers to accelerate expensive plant closures. Thus, a prolonged downturn would likely trigger severe asset writedowns and heavily dilute group-level earnings.
