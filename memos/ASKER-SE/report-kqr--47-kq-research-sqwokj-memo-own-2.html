Section 2 – AddLife AB: Post-2023 Leverage Peak Analysis

Section summary
  1. AddLife AB successfully deleveraged from its Q3 2023 leverage peak of 3.9x to 2.2x by year-end 2025.
  2. The valuation multiple began its recovery approximately nine quarters after the leverage peak, coinciding with the Q4 2025 earnings release.
  3. Operating cash conversion and a return to smaller bolt-on acquisitions driven by new management re-established investor confidence in the decentralized compounding model.

Deleveraging and the leverage peak

1.
Elevated leverage peak: AddLife AB experienced a severe balance sheet stretch when its net debt to EBITDA ratio peaked at 3.9x in the third quarter of 2023. This peak was driven by a combination of major acquisitions completed during 2021 and 2022 under previous management and a post-pandemic normalization of Covidrelated sales. The company was forced to deprioritize acquisitions in order to focus on debt reduction through self-generated cash flows. This strategic pivot successfully reduced leverage to 2.2x by the end of 2025.
2.
Disciplined balance sheet recovery: AddLife AB maintained operational momentum throughout 2024 and 2025 despite the temporary M&A freeze. The company focused on organic optimization and restructuring unprofitable units like AddVision and Camanio. These internal measures enabled the group to report 15% adjusted EBITA growth in 2024, demonstrating that organic improvements could offset the lack of acquisitions. Consequently, the company entered 2026 with a healthy balance sheet that was finally ready to support a renewed M&A program.
3.
Contrary evidence: AddLife AB struggled with a slower-than-expected recovery in its Labtech segment during late 2024. DNB Carnegie reported on October 23, 2024, that persistent destocking and public funding constraints delayed the anticipated margin rebound. This operational weakness suggests that the deleveraging path was not entirely smooth because underlying market demand fluctuated.

Valuation multiple contraction and recovery

4.
Severe valuation contraction: AddLife AB suffered a significant de-rating as its valuation multiple fell during the leverage crisis. RedEye noted on September 18, 2026, that the company traded at a discount of 25% on P/E relative to its five-year historical median multiple of 44.4x. Investors feared a highly dilutive equity issue because net leverage had risen well above the company's internal target. This valuation discount highlights how elevated gearing harms investor sentiment and multiple expansion.
5.
Catalyst for re-rating: AddLife AB experienced its first major valuation re-rating nine quarters after its leverage peak. DNB Carnegie upgraded the stock to BUY on February 4, 2026, following the release of the Q4 2025 annual report. This corporate disclosure confirmed that net leverage had dropped to 2.2x, which was comfortably below the target threshold. Demonstrating that debt reduction was fully achievable through organic cash flow unlocked immediate multiple expansion for the company.
6.
Incomplete multiple recovery: AddLife AB saw its forward EV/EBITA multiple recover toward historical averages, but it remained below its pandemic-era highs. The company traded at 17.6x forward EV/EBITA in April 2026, compared to over 22x in late 2025 before the de-rating. DNB Carnegie downgraded the stock to Hold on October 22, 2026, arguing that the valuation now fully reflects the near-term recovery. This shows that while multiples can recover, they may stabilize at a lower, more sustainable level.

Cash conversion and acquisition volumes

7.
Strong operating cash conversion: AddLife AB relied on excellent working capital management and operating cash flows to fund its deleveraging. The company's Q3 2023 report showed operating cash flow strengthened to SEK 138m from just SEK 20m in the previous year. DNB Carnegie noted that the group's closure of unprofitable subsidiaries like Camanio generated substantial cash flow tailwinds. This robust cash conversion is essential for organic debt reduction because self-generated cash flow preserves liquidity without diluting equity.
8.
Muted M&A activity: AddLife AB completed 5 acquisitions in 2022 before leverage constraints forced a near-total halt to its M&A program. The company completed very few transactions over 2023 and 2024 because of elevated leverage. DNB Carnegie notes that this drastic reduction in deal volume slowed adjusted EBITA growth and muted investor enthusiasm. This slowdown demonstrates that high debt halts M&A execution, restricting the primary driver of compounding growth.
9.
Reactivating the M&A engine: AddLife AB accelerated its M&A program in 2026 once leverage fell well below its internal target of 3.0x. The company completed 4 acquisitions by October 2026, which DNB Carnegie notes is a step toward its long-term target. Management intends to scale this to 6 to 8 annual deals as the restored balance sheet supports growth again. Proving that the company can execute accretive deals is critical for pushing earnings per share growth above 15%.

Peer comparisons and historical precedents

10.
Peer valuation comparison: AddLife AB has historically traded at a discount to its peer Asker Healthcare Group AB. DNB Carnegie reported in April 2026 that AddLife traded at an 8% discount to Asker on a 2026e EV/EBITA basis. This discount closed by late 2026 after Asker faced its own earnings-quality critique and de-rated. This convergence shows that accounting quality and leverage profiles are the primary determinants of relative valuations.
11.
Stable peer benchmarking: AddLife AB's valuation is frequently benchmarked against premium Swedish compounders like Addtech, Lifco, and Indutrade. DNB Carnegie argues that AddLife should trade at a 10% to 15% discount to these proven peers due to its historical operational struggles. While premium peers maintained leverage below 2.0x, AddLife's elevated debt justified its persistent valuation discount. This benchmark indicates that closing the peer valuation gap requires sustained operational consistency.
12.
Disappointing historical precedent: AddLife AB's acquisition of MBA Incorporado in January 2022 serves as a clear historical warning. This large transaction added SEK 670m in sales but ultimately contributed to the balance sheet stretch and subsequent operational underperformance. DNB Carnegie argues that the previous management team's shift toward larger, complex deals ended up disappointing. This case suggests that larger acquisitions increase operational risk, reinforcing the need for smaller, local bolt-ons.
13.
Other side of the story: Some quality-focused investors remain skeptical of AddLife AB's long-term capital efficiency despite its recent deleveraging. RedEye noted on September 18, 2026, that accumulated goodwill from historical transactions has kept the group-level return on invested capital at a low 6% to 8%. If organic growth slows or public healthcare budgets tighten across Europe, the company may struggle to generate high returns. This risk could prevent full multiple expansion toward premium serial acquirers, limiting the stock's long-term valuation potential.

The leverage crisis and operational peak

This balance sheet stretch was exacerbated by previous rapid M&A and supply chain disruptions. Consequently, the group was forced to shift focus toward debt reduction to defend its capital structure and liquidity.
2.
Cyclical margin pressures: The group's profitability faced headwinds from inflation and a weak Swedish krona during 2022. Storskogen reported a full-year adjusted EBITA margin of 9.2% for 2022, down from previous years. This margin compression occurred as cost increases were passed on to customers with a significant lag. Thus, cyclical exposures in the industry segment impaired the company's operating leverage.
3.
Persistent debt burden: Storskogen's absolute debt levels remained elevated through early 2024 despite initial mitigation efforts. DNB Carnegie reported that end-Q1 2024 net debt to EBITDA reached a high of 3.31x on an all-in basis. This persistent gearing de-rated the equity multiple because rising interest rates increased the cost of debt capital.

Balance sheet restructuring and catalysts

4.
Strategic leadership transition: Storskogen initiated its operational turnaround by appointing Christer Hansson as permanent chief executive officer on July 1, 2024. Under his leadership, the group launched several major restructuring initiatives to restore investor confidence. This leadership change served as the primary catalyst for re-rating.
5.
Aggressive asset divestments: Storskogen streamlined its decentralized model by executing the divestment of nine loss-making business units to M Industrial Invest AB in late 2024. This critical transaction removed underperforming assets and immediately boosted group-level margins. Consequently, the remaining portfolio of 116 business units was structurally stronger and less volatile.
6.
Debt maturity extension: Storskogen resolved its near-term refinancing risks by issuing SEK 1.25bn in senior unsecured bonds in May 2025. These new bonds carry an interest rate of Stibor plus 290 basis points and mature in 2029. Extending the debt runway alleviated immediate bankruptcy fears.
7.
Refinancing cost savings: Storskogen reduced its interest expenses by executing a series of bond redemptions and new issuances during 2025. The group replaced expensive 2025 debt with senior unsecured bonds maturing in 2030 at Stibor plus 265 basis points. This debt optimization lowered annual interest costs by SEK 75m.
8.
Upgraded credit outlook: Storskogen received validation of its deleveraging progress when S&P upgraded its rating outlook to stable. S&P affirmed the group's BB credit rating following the refinancing of its 2025 bonds. This rating stability reduced the group's default risk premium.

Valuation multiple de-rating and recovery

9.
Severe multiple de-rating: Storskogen suffered a massive valuation de-rating when its multiple fell to 8x forward EV/EBITA during the leverage crisis. This depressed valuation reflected extreme investor skepticism regarding the group's decentralised acquisition strategy. This multiple compression severely impaired the company's ability to use equity as currency.
10.
Valuation re-rating inflection: Storskogen's forward multiple began to recover approximately eight quarters after its late 2022 leverage peak. The first meaningful re-rating coincided with the Q4 2024 earnings release when leverage fell to 2.3x. This milestone confirmed the group comfortably reached its target leverage.
11.
Gradual multiple recovery: Storskogen's forward valuation recovered from its 8x low to trade at 10x to 12x EV/EBITA by mid-2026. Despite this improvement, the group's multiple remained below its historical listed peak. Investors continue to apply a 30% discount relative to premium serial acquirers because of Storskogen's shorter operating track record.

Cash conversion and acquisition volumes

Rising cash conversion: Storskogen achieved superior cash generation by prioritizing working capital management and inventory reduction. The rolling 12-month adjusted cash conversion rate improved from 59% in 2022 to 104% in 2023. This cash generation fully funded the group's organic degearing.

Drastic M&A slowdown: Storskogen halted its aggressive acquisition engine to preserve liquidity during the leverage crisis. The company completed only 12 acquisitions in 2023, down from over 50 deals annually during 2021. This transaction freeze stifled the group's inorganic growth and slowed annual earnings per share expansion.

Gradual M&A resumption: Storskogen began to carefully ramp up its deal-making activity once balance sheet health was restored. The group completed 9 acquisitions in 2025 and 10 deals in the first nine months of 2026. Resuming disciplined acquisitions is critical for pushing long-term EBITA growth toward 15%.

In August 2026, proven compounders traded at 20x 2027e EV/EBITA compared to Storskogen's multiple of 14x.
