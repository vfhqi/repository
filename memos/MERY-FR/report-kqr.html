Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The French commercial rent indexation recovery drives significant earnings-per-share growth for retail property companies, yet recent share price declines of approximately 20% at Mercialys and 12% at Carmila through October 2026 reflect a fundamental mispricing where macroeconomic hedging flows have overwhelmed operational resilience, creating a structural divergence between robust tenant performance and equity market valuations. The French commercial rent index bottomed out and turned positive at +0.3% year on year in the second quarter of 2026, with analyst forecasts projecting a rise to approximately 2% by the end of 2026, representing a critical inflection point for recurring earnings growth across the sector. Broker models forecast indexation contributions will rise to between +0.9% and +1.3% in 2027, accelerating to over +2.5% in 2028, although structured publication lags mean the impact of recent indexation inflections will mostly materialize in 2027 and 2028 rather than benefiting current period results. Each percentage point increase in indexation adds approximately €1.64 million to Mercialys' minimum guaranteed rents, translating to an estimated €0.018 per share or approximately 1.38% to the mid-point of 2026 guidance, demonstrating the high operational leverage embedded in these business models. Similarly, Carmila exhibits earnings sensitivity where a one percentage point rise in indexation adds a calculated 0.72% to total rents, translating to about €2.20 million in incremental net rental income or approximately €0.016 per share, although this sensitivity is constrained by French indexation lags affecting 72% of its rental base.

The share price declines were driven by macroeconomic headwinds as the 10-year OAT sovereign yield surged past 5% for the first time since 2002, creating severe valuation pressure despite resilient asset-level performance. Mercialys' €300 million bond placement required a high annual coupon of 5.25% with a spread of 220 basis points, representing wider corporate borrowing premiums compared to its historical 180 basis points average, directly illustrating how rising financing costs offset potential indexation benefits. Operational metrics show no signs of distress implied by the equity sell-off, with Carmila reporting third-quarter like-for-like rental growth of 2.1% that outpaced current lease indexation by 180 basis points, demonstrating that the stock price declines were driven by macro hedging flows rather than underlying tenant failures.

Mercialys maintains outstanding portfolio health with financial occupancy reaching 98.2% following a 30 basis point sequential improvement in the third quarter of 2026, while Carmila's financial occupancy rose by 70 basis points year on year to 96.0%, validating cash flow reliability across dominant retail assets. 6 Klépierre exhibits strong operational safety margins as its tenant cost ratio remains highly manageable at 12.5% across its prime shopping centre portfolio, comparing favorably to historical high-stress periods and suggesting that operators can sustain high occupancy even if indexation increases.

Mercialys tenant sales rose 3.0% likefor-like through September 2026, which is 2.8% ahead of the national French retail index, protecting landlord earnings by preventing occupancy cost ratios from rising to dangerous levels. Consensus broker models and management guidance assume a gradual indexation recovery will translate mechanically into recurring earnings growth, yet this transmission mechanism faces two critical contradictions that equity markets appear to be pricing.

Rising financing costs and debt maturities could compress corporate margins, as evidenced by Klépierre generating strong cash flows in the first half of 2026 while facing higher overall financing costs and taxes, potentially offsetting any recurring earnings gains from rent indexation. Tenant default risks tend to rise when indexation outpaces retail sales growth, and if rent indexation increases while consumer spending slows down, high occupancy cost ratios could lead to tenant failures and higher vacancies, meaning the indexation recovery is not a risk-free driver of earnings.

Western Europe is experiencing a notable surge in major corporate insolvencies with major retail insolvencies reaching 69 cases globally year-to-date, representing a significant increase of 21 cases compared to the prior year period, justifying investor concerns regarding potential tenant defaults in weaker locations as rates remain elevated. If bond yields continue to drift higher, real estate operators will face much higher coupon rates on upcoming refinancing rounds, and this interest expense pressure would quickly wipe out any organic cash flow gains from positive indexation adjustments, representing the core structural risk that markets are discounting.

The fundamental contradiction between operational resilience and market pricing stems from upgraded corporate guidance at Carmila, which raised full-year recurring earnings per share targets to €1.87 representing annual growth of 3.30%, supported by accretive acquisitions, juxtaposed against equity valuations that imply structural distress. The operational outperformance of dominant, grocery-anchored retail property portfolios suggests the bearish market outlook is overly pessimistic, with tenant sales and occupancy metrics remaining exceptionally robust, and if low vacancy rates and positive rental reversions persist, these landlords will comfortably absorb higher financing costs through strong organic rent growth, indicating the current steep valuation discount likely reflects political hedging flows rather than fundamental decay in retail property earnings potential.

Critical information gaps remain that could resolve this valuation paradox, including Carmila's upcoming Capital Markets Day on November 19, 2026, which will present strategic plan details through 2030 and explicit mediumterm earnings growth targets, alongside full Q4 2026 sales and collection data to evaluate whether rising national insolvencies will eventually impact mid-tier retail tenants, determining if the market has correctly anticipated a structural deterioration or fundamentally mispriced the timing of the retail real estate cycle recovery.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The recovery of French commercial rent indexation drives significant earnings-per-share growth for retail property companies, meaning the recent share price sell-offs represent a mispriced opportunity.

Direct transmission mechanisms

1.
Critical inquiry: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector N.3. Real estate - retail property, with Mercialys (MERY-FR) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: The French commercial rent index rose 0.26% year on year in the second quarter of 2026 after three quarters of falls. What contribution from indexation to like-for-like rental income do brokers and managements now assume for 2027 and 2028 at Mercialys, Carmila, Klepierre and the French centres of Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield, and how much recurring earnings per share does each percentage point of indexation add at each company? What reasons do brokers and investors give for the falls of about 20% at Mercialys and 12% at Carmila in the three months to 22 October 2026, while both companies raised 2026 guidance: higher bond yields, leverage and acquisition funding, fears of an equity raise, tenant failures, or lower indexation forecasts?."
2.
Direct transmission: The indexation of commercial leases in France represents a highly predictable driver of like-for-like rental growth because it automatically adjusts passing rents on an annual basis. Since shopping centre operators face fixed cost structures, any incremental rental growth from indexation flows directly to the bottom line. This mechanism makes the timing of indexation inflections critical for predicting changes in recurring earnings per share.
3.
Lagged recovery: The impact of French commercial rent indexation is heavily delayed because of calculation and publication lags in the official indices. Gecina notes that the recent pick-up in inflation is not expected to benefit rents before late 2027 or early 2028. This delayed transmission mechanism means that immediate operational results may not reflect upcoming indexation benefits.
4.
Fundamentally mispriced: The recent share price declines at major retail operators could indicate that equity markets are ignoring the imminent cash flow acceleration. While companies like Mercialys upgraded their full-year guidance, their share prices fell because investors focused on short-term macro worries. Closely related questions include how rising bond yields affect refinancing costs and whether acquisition funding costs will pressure balance sheets.

5.
Contrary evidence: The benefits of a rebounding commercial rent index could be undermined if rising financing costs and debt maturities compress corporate margins. For example, Klépierre generated strong cash flows in the first half of 2026, but faced higher overall financing costs and taxes. Section 2 – Indexation Contributions and Forecasts for 2027 and 2028

Section summary
  1. Landlords and brokers assume a gradual recovery in the French commercial rent index will drive stronger indexation contributions of around 1% in 2027 and over 2% in 2028.
  2. Management projections for Mercialys and Carmila remain conservative for 2027 due to indexing lags, while larger peers like Klepierre and Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield rely on stronger organic drivers.
  3. Historical lease indexation shifts and regulatory caps demonstrate how contract structures and policy interventions can alter the transmission of inflation to recurring earnings.

Rebounding indexation trends

1.
Strong recovery: Jefferies Research on October 02, 2026, reports that the French commercial rent index has bottomed out and turned positive at +0.3% year on year in the second quarter of 2026. This report incorporates findings from more than ten relevant sources, covering four major listed real estate companies and two comparable past cases. The analyst forecasts that the index will rise to approximately 2% by the end of 2026. This projected recovery is expected to strengthen like-for-like rental growth for retail property owners.
2.
Gradual acceleration: Jefferies Research on June 26, 2026, suggests that the trajectory of French indices points to a flat contribution to like-for-like rental growth in 2026. However, the indexation contribution is modeled to rise to between +0.9% and +1.3% in 2027, and accelerate to over +2.5% in 2028. This gradual acceleration means that retail earnings will experience lags before the full impact of inflation is captured.
3.
Delayed transition: Bernstein Research on May 06, 2026, explains that shopping centre leases in France are typically indexed to the commercial rent index on the first of January. Due to a two-quarter publication lag from the statistical agency INSEE, the impact of recent indexation inflections will mostly materialize in 2027 and 2028. This structured timing means that landlords face delayed rent adjustments compared to instant spot inflation measures.
4.
Structural composition: Selectirente reported on May 28, 2026, that the formula for the French commercial rent index was revised in 2022 to remove the volatile retail revenue index. The index is now composed of 75% consumer price index and 25% construction cost index. This structural shift ensures that indexation tracks core inflation metrics more closely than historical figures.
5.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research on March 16, 2026, notes that although inflation expectations have shifted upward since the onset of geopolitical tensions, euro area inflation has remained below 2%. This stable lowinflation environment could lead to subdued rental indexation in 2027 if macro pressures ease. This indicates that the projected earnings recovery might be smaller or more delayed than currently modeled by more optimistic brokers.

Target company forecasts

6.
Management expectations: Mercialys SA announced on October 15, 2026, that the commercial rent index turned positive in the second quarter of 2026, which will gradually roll out on lease anniversary dates. Management expects this trend to make indexation an additional growth driver in 2027 and 2028. This recovery follows a broadly neutral impact from indexation during 2026 as previously guided.
7.
Broker models: Jefferies Research on October 15, 2026, notes that Mercialys is positioned to benefit from a rebounding index in the second half of 2026. The broker forecasts that the commercial rent index will contribute approximately +0.6% to Mercialys' like-for-like rental growth in 2027. This contribution should support stable recurring earnings growth for the developer.
8.
Subdued contributions: Carmila SA reported on July 30, 2026, that its like-for-like net rental income grew by 1.4% in the first half of 2026. Out of this total growth, the contribution from indexation was only +0.4% due to subdued conditions in France. Management expects its upcoming strategic four-year plan to outline how it will consistently grow rents above indexation.
9.
French constraints: Jefferies Research on May 26, 2026, notes that indexation-driven growth for Carmila will remain subdued in 2026 and 2027. French lag effects, which impact about 72% of Carmila's rental base, are expected to keep near-term rental growth low before realigning with inflation. This means that Carmila must rely on acquisitions to drive meaningful earnings acceleration in the intermediate term.

Large cap comparison

10.
Flagship performance: Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield reported on October 23, 2026, that its shopping centre portfolio delivered like-for-like gross rental growth of 3.8% in the first half of 2026. However, the actual contribution from indexation was low at only 0.6% during this period. To manage tenant relationships, the company considers a modest 1% uplift in its internal business planning.
11.
Conservative targets: Jefferies Research on February 12, 2025, noted that Klépierre initiated conservative guidance for 2025 like-for-like rental growth of 3%. The model assumed indexation would contribute 1.5%, which the broker criticized as being too low given the commercial rent index was already at 3%. This shows that managements often understate indexation assumptions to ensure easy guidance beats.
12.
Outperforming peers: Eurocommercial Properties NV reported on October 29, 2026, that its like-for-like rental growth reached 2.1% for the twelve months ended September 30, 2026. Jefferies Research on September 02, 2026, notes that Eurocommercial's like-for-like growth will be supported by a recovery in indexation to 1.7% in 2027. This projected peer recovery is higher than the French averages due to Eurocommercial's geographical exposure in Italy and Belgium.

Historical indexation shifts

13.
Volatile indices: ING Wholesale Banking reported on February 23, 2012, that the introduction of the French LME law in 2008 allowed retail landlords to transition lease agreements from the volatile construction cost index to the commercial rent index. During this period, Mercialys proactively shifted 90% of its shopping centre leases to the new index. This historical case suggests that proactive lease management mitigates volatility during economic shifts.
14.
Regulatory interventions: Frey SA reported on March 05, 2026, that the French government implemented a temporary 3.5% indexation cap on the commercial rent index for small and medium enterprises between 2022 and 2024. Selectirente reported on September 30, 2025, that this law was designed to protect small tenants from extreme inflation. This past intervention shows that regulatory caps can limit upside for landlords during periods of high indexation.
15.
Other side of the story: Investors may face downside risks if high indexation is not matched by tenant retail sales growth, as rising occupancy cost ratios can trigger defaults. Deutsche Bank Research on May 08, 2026, notes that while French leases benefit from uncapped inflation-linked reviews, a weak retail environment poses significant collection risks. Therefore, a rapid recovery in the commercial rent index could strain retail tenant balance sheets rather than purely boosting landlord cash flows.

Section 3 – Sensitivity of Recurring EPS to Indexation

Section summary
  1. Each percentage point of indexation adds a calculated one to two percent to recurring earnings across the peer group due to high operational flow-through.
  2. High operational leverage and stable occupancy cost ratios amplify the bottom-line sensitivity to indexation, although substantial publication lags defer the financial benefits.

Operational leverage and indexation flow-through

1.
Significant impact: Mercialys exhibits high sensitivity to indexation because 96% of its lease structure is directly linked to the French commercial rent index (ILC). Each percentage point increase in ILC indexation adds a calculated €1.64 million to minimum guaranteed rents based on its €164.4 million ILC-linked baseline. This incremental rental revenue flows directly to bottom-line recurring earnings, adding an estimated €0.018 per share or approximately 1.38% (calculated) to the mid-point of 2026 guidance.

2.
Subdued exposure: Carmila's earnings sensitivity to indexation is governed by its retail focus where French indexation lags affect 72% of its rental base. A one percentage point rise in indexation applied to this French base adds a calculated 0.72% to total rents, translating to about €2.20 million in incremental net rental income from its nine-month base. This net rental income flow-through adds a calculated €0.016 per share, representing 0.86% (calculated) of Carmila's confirmed 2026 recurring earnings per share target.
3.
Contrary evidence: Rebounding commercial rent indexation may fail to translate into equivalent recurring earnings growth if offset by rising corporate financing expenses. At Mercialys, the potential earnings benefits from indexation were completely offset in 2026 by zero indexation and the refinancing of its €300 million bond from a coupon of 1.8% to a rate of 4.0%. This demonstrates that macro balance sheet dynamics can easily overpower positive impacts of lease indexation on recurring earnings per share.
4.
Balanced leverage: Klepierre demonstrates solid sensitivity to indexation because its large-scale retail portfolio benefits from strong occupancy and stable tenant cost ratios of 12.5%. Each percentage point of indexation adds a calculated €12.0 million to net rental income, boosting net current cash flow per share by 1.10% (calculated) against the upgraded 2026 guidance of €2.83. This leverage is supported by a solid balance sheet and accretive acquisitions that amplify organic cash flows over time.

Indexing lags and lease structures

5.
Massive scale: Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield has a lower direct sensitivity to French indexation because its portfolio is highly diversified across Europe and the United States. A one percentage point indexation increase adds 1.9% in Continental Europe, adding a calculated €0.07 per share to adjusted recurring earnings. This massive scale means that organic factors like retail media and leasing reversion play a more dominant role in earnings than indexation.
6.
Deferred benefits: The transmission of indexation to recurring earnings is delayed by structured publication lags, which prevent immediate reflection of spot inflation. In France, indices applied to leases for any given year typically reflect the inflation of the previous year, with most retail leases adjusted on the first of January. This timing gap means that bottom-line benefits of indexation recoveries will mostly materialize in 2027 and 2028 rather than current periods.
7.
Organic outperformance: Operational drivers such as lease reversion and active asset management allow these retail landlords to consistently outperform baseline indexation rates. During the first nine months of 2026, Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield achieved shopping centre growth of 3.8% which was 320 basis points above indexation, while Carmila outpaced indexation by 180 basis points. This outperformance suggests that recurring earnings are highly resilient even during periods of low or negative indexation.
8.
Other side of the story: Extreme indexation can deteriorate tenant credit quality and increase vacancy rates, ultimately destroying landlord earnings instead of supporting them. If indexation outpaces retail sales, occupancy cost ratios rise above historical norms, which forces tenant defaults and vacancy increases. Therefore, the sensitivity of recurring earnings to indexation is non-linear and becomes highly negative if indexation exceeds tenant affordability thresholds.

Section 4 – Drivers of Share Price Declines at Mercialys and Carmila

Section summary
  1. Sharp share price declines at Mercialys and Carmila were driven by rising sovereign yields and funding costs rather than operational deterioration.
  2. While both operators upgraded operational performance, a volatile interest rate environment and widening credit spreads amplified market concerns about refinancing.

Sovereign risk and widening spreads

1.
Rapid yield expansion: Listed French property developers faced intense valuation pressure as macroeconomic headwinds dominated sentiment during the autumn of 2026. The broader French interest rate environment became highly volatile as the 10-year OAT sovereign yield surged past 5% for the first time since 2002. This macroeconomic pressure created a severe headwind that depressed property company equity valuations despite resilient asset-level performance.
2.
Widening credit spreads: Mercialys experienced the direct consequences of this volatile interest rate environment during its latest capital market transaction. The company successfully placed a €300 million bond maturing in 2033, but had to accept a high annual coupon of 5.25%. This transaction pricing reflected a spread of 220 basis points, which represents wider corporate borrowing premiums compared to its historical 180 basis points average.
3.
Contrary evidence: Operational metrics at both firms show no signs of the distress implied by the steep equity market sell-off. Carmila reported a significant operational acceleration with third-quarter like-for-like rental growth rising by 2.1%, which outpaced current lease indexation by 180 basis points. This resilient performance suggests the stock price declines were driven by macro hedging flows rather than underlying tenant failures.

Balance sheet metrics and funding

4.
Disciplined capital allocation: Carmila managed its balance sheet risk by maintaining a highly conservative funding and hedging strategy through the rate cycle. The group reported a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 7.30x and successfully kept its property portfolio loan-to-value ratio at 39.30%. Management mitigated interest rate volatility because its net debt is fully hedged through 2027, keeping its average cost of debt stable.
5.
Staggered debt maturities: Mercialys actively insulated its corporate balance sheet from immediate refinancing stress through proactive liability management. The developer had an average debt maturity of 3.80 years at mid-year and faces no major bond repayments before November 2027. By engaging in €30 million of asset disposals, the firm sustained solid credit ratings and avoided any dilutive equity capital raises.

Operating strength versus market pressure

6.
Upgraded earnings targets: Carmila demonstrated robust bottom-line resilience by upgrading its corporate guidance amid the broader market downturn. Management raised its full-year recurring earnings per share target to €1.87, representing solid annual growth of 3.30%. This operational momentum was supported by the accretive Grand Quetigny acquisition, proving that the business could expand despite high interest rates.
7.
Comparative financial profiles: Financial and leverage metrics demonstrate that both retail property operators maintain robust credit structures relative to their sector peers. A side-by-side comparison highlights the varying degrees of interest rate hedging, leverage, and portfolio occupancy across the two companies. These metrics confirm that fundamental balance sheet strength remains intact despite the recent sharp equity sell-offs.

Strong leasing activity means that Mercialys faces minimal direct threat of immediate rental income loss from unoccupied units.
