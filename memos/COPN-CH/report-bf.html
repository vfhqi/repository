Appendix

Outliers worth noting 3 findings

1.
Extreme milestone revenue volatility: Cosmo experienced a dramatic 94% EBITDA collapse to EUR 9.5 million in 2025 from EUR 161.2 million in 2024, driven by the non-recurrence of EUR 185 million in Medtronic milestone payments and a EUR 18.6 million shift from capitalized to expensed Phase III development costs, representing an exceptionally severe year-over-year profitability swing far exceeding typical specialty pharmaceutical sector milestone lumpiness and placing this performance volatility in the bottom ~5% of peer stability metrics [Cross-ref: Section 2]. While Cosmo demonstrates superior capital-light asset intensity compared to industrial CDMO peer PolyPeptide, its ROIC remains materially depressed relative to integrated specialty peer Rovi's 11.0% return, creating an unusual performance disconnect where balance sheet efficiency fails to translate into through-cycle returns
2.
Unprecedented partnership duration: The Medtronic collaboration spans 50 years, representing an extraordinarily long-term commercial alliance that sits in the top ~5% of pharmaceutical licensing contract durations globally and signals exceptional strategic lock-in and reciprocal dependency rarely observed outside major vaccine or insulin franchises [Cross-ref: Section 5]. This durability establishes structural revenue visibility but simultaneously creates concentrated single-partner exposure where any execution deterioration carries disproportionate impact
3.
Elevated working capital absorption: Trade receivables reached EUR 29.72 million against H1 2026 revenue of EUR 50.24 million, representing an elevated collection period of approximately 107 days, substantially exceeding peer Almirall's 85-day NWC cycle and placing Cosmo in the bottom ~5% of specialty pharmaceutical cash conversion efficiency [Cross-ref: Section 8]. This unexplained working capital expansion suggests downstream commercial partners are exerting payment leverage during launch scaling phases, creating potential liquidity friction despite the company's fortress cash position
