  2. Key competitive threats from recombinants and refinancing pressures are critical debates that will shape Grifols' mid-term earnings per share.
  3. Grifols is pursuing gross-margin recovery by reducing plasma collection costs and rebalancing its global sourcing network.
  4. Weakness in Chinese albumin pricing and elevated channel inventories compel Grifols to voluntarily limit immunoglobulin volume growth to protect margins.
  5. Operational initiatives like the Egyptian joint venture and the rollout of individualized nomograms are expected to improve future profit margins.

Gross Margin Recovery and Cost per Liter

The company credits this expansion to optimization of plasma sourcing costs and disciplined operational execution. These structural improvements support the company's objective of gradual deleveraging.
9.
Lagged cost benefits: Grifols has stabilized its cost per liter of plasma at approximately 25% below the peak reached in July 2022. Because of the nine-month inventory accounting lag in the plasma industry, these lower collection costs take three quarters to hit the income statement. Deutsche Bank noted that cost cutting implemented in 2023 only became fully visible in the 2024 results. This delayed accounting effect means current sourcing efficiencies will underpin margin expansion into next year.
10.
Operational footprint restructuring: Grifols optimized its collection network by closing 18 underperforming donor centers in the fourth quarter of 2022. Morgan Stanley reported that the company consolidated more sites in the first half of 2023 to eliminate regional cannibalization. Management expects productivity gains and higher average center maturity to offset the lower center count. These structural adjustments are designed to improve labor and collection efficiencies over the long term.
11.
Sourcing yield optimization: Grifols is deploying automated plasmapheresis equipment incorporating individualized nomogram technology across its US fleet. The company expects to achieve 100% US adoption of this technology by the end of 2026. Management projects that the nomogram will improve plasma volume yields in the mid-to-high single digits. This technology will directly lower future cost per liter and support gross profit expansion.

Last Liter Economics and Co-Product Constraints

16.
Marginal return constraints: Grifols operates under last liter economics because fractionation processes yield a fixed, consistent ratio of co-products. Sourcing and manufacturing are dictated by immunoglobulin demand, meaning albumin is a secondary last-litre product. If market demand is not balanced for all extracted proteins, incremental plasma processed becomes less profitable. This dynamic limits gross margin expansion unless the company can sell every purified protein.
17.
Peer margin dilution: CSL demonstrates last-litre dilution because the incremental plasma liter is effectively sold as immunoglobulin and albumin only. Jefferies calculated that CSL Behring's full-cost last-litre gross margin is approximately 38%. This marginal margin sits well below CSL's average gross margin of 50.5% because higher-margin specialty products are excluded. Volume growth is dilutive unless fractionation costs are entirely fixed and absorbed.
18.
Margin-driven growth slowdown: Grifols is prioritizing margin expansion over top-line growth by voluntarily limiting its immunoglobulin volume expansion. Morgan Stanley reported that the company is reallocating supply to higher-priced geographies. This balanced last-litre approach aims to protect margins from unbalanced protein growth caused by weak albumin demand. Management expects this strategic pivot to optimize EBITDA growth over the next few years.

China Pricing and Channel Dynamics

19.
Regional pricing pressure: Grifols experienced a 14.2% constant-currency decline in first-half 2026 albumin revenues. This drop is due to price controls and cost-containment measures imposed by the Chinese government. Jefferies reported that hospital tender prices in China declined by roughly 10% mid-year. These pricing headwinds compress profitability since China is historically a premium market for global fractionators.
20.
Channel inventory overhang: Chinese plasma manufacturers currently hold approximately six to nine months of albumin inventory. Distributors are holding an additional three months of product, making them reluctant to make new purchases. Jefferies estimates that this supply-driven overstocking will depress prices and import volumes through fiscal year 2028. This persistent market drag reduces near-term revenue visibility for major global importers.
21.
Competitive margin comparison: CSL Behring reported a gross margin of 49.6% for fiscal year 2026. In comparison, Takeda reported a half-year gross profit margin of 66.8% for its plasma portfolio. Grifols has historically lagged behind these peers because lower average center volumes create fixed-cost absorption headwinds. Closing underperforming centers and deploying nomograms is critical for Grifols to narrow this profitability gap.
22.
Contrary evidence: Grifols faces persistent skepticism from analysts who argue that operational savings cannot fully offset weak top-line growth. Deutsche Bank research on May 11, 2026, highlighted that intensifying competition and pricing pressure could restrict margin expansion through the end of the year. Furthermore, high collection center costs and underutilized capacity remain key drags on baseline gross margins. These persistent headwinds suggest that the recovery pathway will be highly non-linear.
23.
Other side of the story: Grifols could see its margins expand faster than anticipated if the group simplifies its minority shareholding structure. Bernstein Research reported on November 11, 2026, that the buyout of Haema and BPC minorities represents a strong structural catalyst. This simplified corporate setup will be highly accretive to future earnings per share next year. Additionally, a potential US listing for the Biopharma unit represents an underappreciated value unlock.

Section 4 – Immunoglobulin Net Pricing, Recombinant Threat, and Clinical Disruption

Section summary
  1. Grifols faces mounting net pricing pressure in the US immunoglobulin market despite list-price increases, lagging behind the pricing premiums maintained by competitors CSL and Takeda.
  2. The competitive threat to Grifols' alpha-1 antitrypsin franchise is temporarily deferred by Sanofi's pipeline delays but remains a major long-term risk.
  3. Rapid clinical adoption of FcRn inhibitors and complement therapies in CIDP represents a disruptive threat to Grifols' long-term immunoglobulin volumes.

Immunoglobulin Net Pricing

1.
Pricing pressure: Grifols experienced net pricing pressure for its flagship product Gamunex, which saw a 1.40% year-on-year reimbursement decline in the fourth quarter of 2025 according to Jefferies on October 30, 2026. This decline occurred despite list-price increases, highlighting a growing disconnect between gross list prices and realized net pricing within the US market. By contrast, Jefferies reported that peer CSL saw its subcutaneous Hizentra reimbursement rise by 6.60% over the same period. Consequently, Grifols must improve its commercial contracting to prevent further erosion of its immunoglobulin margins.
2.
Competitor premiums: CSL Behring maintained a significant net pricing premium over its rivals during 2026 according to Jefferies on October 30, 2026. Jefferies data showed that CSL's Privigen was reimbursed at 101.47 dollars per gram, which was higher than Gamunex at 97.93 dollars per gram. Takeda also maintained Gammagard reimbursement at 90.63 dollars per gram, representing a 1.40% annual increase. These competitive dynamics indicate that Grifols faces stiffer retail-level price competition than its peers.
3.
Sourcing precedents: Grifols is attempting to bypass US pricing pressure by using cheaper ex-US plasma collected through its joint venture in Egypt. A former CSL executive noted on May 10, 2023, that historical efforts to diversify sourcing have yielded massive cost advantages. Grifols obtained EMA approval to use Egyptian plasma in Europe, which directly mirrors how CSL historically scaled European centers. Utilizing this ex-US supply will allow Grifols to improve its global margin profile from 2027.
4.
Specialty subcutaneous: Takeda is driving its plasma-derived therapies business by expanding its subcutaneous immunoglobulin products HyQvia and Cuvitru. Jefferies reported on October 29, 2026, that Takeda's subcutaneous portfolio grew double digits on strong global demand. This strategic focus allows Takeda to avoid aggressive price discounting in standard intravenous immunoglobulins. This margin-preservation strategy shows that subcutaneous formulations command superior pricing.

Recombinant Alpha-1 Threat

5.
Pipeline threats: Sanofi represents a major competitive threat to Grifols' high-margin alpha-1 antitrypsin franchise with its recombinant candidate efdoralprin alfa. Morgan Stanley noted on October 30, 2026, that Sanofi's US regulatory filing has been pushed back to 2027. This pipeline delay provides Grifols with a crucial window to defend its Prolastin brand. If Sanofi eventually secures accelerated approval, it could jeopardize Grifols' specialty protein revenues.
6.
Clinical defense: Grifols expects to counter the recombinant threat using the upcoming readout of its Phase 3 SPARTA trial around year-end 2026. Deutsche Bank reported on November 6, 2026, that SPARTA is evaluating Prolastin-C in slowing emphysema progression over three years. A positive SPARTA readout could help Grifols insulate its clinical franchise. This trial represents the primary catalyst to mitigate emerging recombinant competition.
7.
Contrary evidence: Grifols' Prolastin franchise remains highly vulnerable because Sanofi's recombinant therapy demonstrated massive superiority over plasma-derived options in Phase 2 trials. Deutsche Bank reported on June 12, 2026, that efdoralprin alfa supported three-week and four-week dosing intervals, whereas plasma-derived therapies require weekly infusions. This dosing convenience means Sanofi could rapidly erode Grifols' market share regardless of positive SPARTA data. Consequently, Grifols faces an inevitable long-term earnings decline in its specialty segment.

Clinical Disruption in CIDP

8.
Market erosion: Grifols faces a severe threat of clinical substitution in its chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy business from argenx's Vyvgart. Jefferies reported on October 30, 2026, that Vyvgart is steadily capturing market share in CIDP. Vyvgart's weekly subcutaneous administration offers a massive convenience advantage over prolonged intravenous immunoglobulin infusions. This shift could significantly dampen Grifols' long-term immunoglobulin volume.
9.
Changing paradigms: Neurologists are increasingly willing to transition stable patients from standard immunoglobulin therapies to targeted subcutaneous alternatives. HC Wainwright reported on October 1, 2026, that a Phase 4 study showed 87% of patients successfully transitioned to efgartigimod within one week of their last immunoglobulin dose. This rapid clinical adoption highlights the vulnerability of Grifols' standard of care. Consequently, Grifols must actively defend its installed base of chronic patients.
10.
Pipeline intensification: Complement inhibitors represent the next wave of clinical disruption targeting the CIDP market. Jefferies reported on October 22, 2026, that argenx is advancing its anti-C2 complement asset empasiprubart in registrational Phase 3 trials. This novel therapeutic mechanism represents a direct challenge to standard immunoglobulin therapies. If these complement therapies prove successful, they will further accelerate standard therapy substitution.

Complement Therapeutics and Pipeline

11.
Emerging competitors: Dianthus Therapeutics is actively positioning its selective C1s inhibitor claseprubart as a best-in-disease option for CIDP. Dianthus reported an impressive 75% response rate among forty patients in its CAPTIVATE trial. Morgan Stanley noted on September 11, 2026, that claseprubart is designed for convenient subcutaneous self-administration every two weeks. This competitive profile represents another highly disruptive threat to Grifols' immunoglobulin business.
12.
Clinical setbacks: The competitive threat from complement inhibitors is not entirely uniform, as shown by recent clinical trial failures. Morgan Stanley reported on September 11, 2026, that Sanofi terminated its Phase 3 MOBILIZE study of riliprubart for futility. This clinical failure demonstrates that targeting complement activation in CIDP carries substantial scientific risk. These setbacks show that immunoglobulins will remain the clinical reference standard longer than expected.
13.
Indication expansion: Grifols is actively expanding its immunoglobulin portfolio into secondary immunodeficiencies to secure long-term demand. The company is conducting the Phase 3 SIGMA and EXCELL trials to evaluate intravenous and subcutaneous immunoglobulins in patients with blood cancers. These pipeline expansions are critical to offset any future volume losses in CIDP. Successful trial readouts will unlock new high-margin patient pools for the company.
14.
Other side of the story: Grifols' immunoglobulin franchise exhibits robust durability because more than half of the global immunoglobulin market is driven by immunodeficiencies that targeted biologics cannot treat. Jefferies reported on November 13, 2026, that primary and secondary immunodeficiencies have zero exposure to FcRn or complement therapies. Furthermore, payers continue to enforce strict step-therapy guidelines that mandate cheap immunoglobulins before allowing expensive biologics. Therefore, Grifols' core business will maintain highly stable volume growth.
