Section 2 – The Cooler and Route-Density Loop

Section summary
  1. Rapid cooler placement and route-to-market optimization represent a powerful reinforcing feedback loop that directly accelerates organic revenue and margin growth.
  2. Evidence from Coca-Cola HBC's turnaround in Egypt alongside peer programs confirms that cold-chain investments consistently drive market share gains and operational scale.
  3. Structural margin expansion is achieved as higher outlet velocity supports a more profitable single-serve and returnable packaging mix.

Egyptian Fleet Acceleration

1.
Accelerated deployment: Coca-Cola HBC has aggressively expanded its cold-chain infrastructure in Egypt since acquiring the business in 2022. The bottler successfully tripled its total cooler fleet compared to 2021 levels to expand immediate consumption availability. This massive placement of energy-efficient and connected coolers has established an 87% penetration rate across high-potential retail outlets. Achieving this density has transformed local market dynamics by unlocking impulse purchases.
2.
Strong improvement: The dramatic expansion of the cooler fleet in Egypt has directly compressed the market share lead of its primary competitor. Since Coca-Cola HBC acquired the asset, the NARTD value share gap against Pepsi has shrunk from 13 percentage points to just 5 percentage points. This increased execution capability fueled a 350 basis point gross margin expansion between 2022 and 2025. Consequently, the business model has successfully converted a historically challenged territory into a key group growth engine.
3.
Elevated velocity: Target activations across traditional trade outlets have unlocked substantial incremental revenue. Coca-Cola HBC has activated over 15,000 traditional retail points of sale to maximize customer engagement. These data-driven activations generated a 36% increase in net sales revenue per activated outlet compared to non-activated peers. This operational velocity has allowed the company to comfortably navigate intense geopolitical and macroeconomic headwinds.
4.
Smarter execution: Advanced digital tracking tools have significantly boosted the profitability of Coca-Cola HBC's physical assets in Egypt. The integration of in-store image recognition has driven cooler occupancy levels up to 81%, representing an 11 percentage point expansion compared to 2024. This technology provides sales developers with automated, outlet-specific suggested orders to reduce product stock-outs. Sustained route density is thus reinforced as digital intelligence optimizes delivery schedules.

This commercial strategy provides the primary framework for driving sustained volume and transactions.

Section 4 – The Energy-Scale Loop

Section summary
  1. Scaling in-house production of Monster Energy allows Coca-Cola HBC to capture manufacturing efficiencies and improve margins.
  2. Deploying a tiered portfolio with affordable brands like Predator and Fury enables consumer recruitment across diverse price points in volatile emerging markets.
  3. Peer strategies demonstrate that while affordable energy drives massive volume scale, managing packaging cost inflation requires tactical price-pack architecture interventions.

In-House Production Economics

1.
Scale advantages: Coca-Cola HBC has transformed its energy category economics by shifting from third-party co- packing to in-house manufacturing. Jefferies reported on November 4, 2026, that the group now produces approximately 85% of its Monster Energy volume in-house across local bottling lines. This localization protects the group against supply chain disruptions while capturing manufacturing cost recoveries as volumes expand. Consequently, localized production helps CCH leverage fixed assets to improve comparable EBIT.
2.
Category momentum: Energy drinks have emerged as a highly reliable driver of volume growth and category mix enhancement for the bottler. Jefferies reported on August 5, 2026, that Coca-Cola HBC's energy segment volume sustained a strong 26.1% growth rate in the first half of the year. This expansion is supported by continuous flavor innovations and marketing activations linked to music and sports. Rapid volume gains are expected to propel energy to a double-digit share of group revenues in the near term.

Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research argued on September 25, 2026, that the rapid expansion of Coca-Cola HBC's energy category does not yield a major margin benefit.
