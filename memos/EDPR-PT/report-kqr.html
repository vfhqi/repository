  1. Large valuation disconnects between public share prices and private transaction multiples prove that public markets systematically undervalue renewable asset portfolios.

Persistent market discount: Public equity markets consistently apply a severe valuation discount to the portfolios of European renewable developers. This disconnect is illustrated by Orsted, which trades at a public EV/Capacity of 2.40 million euros per megawatt compared to the higher asset valuations achieved in private transactions. Such deep public discounts suggest that private infrastructure funds are willing to pay a premium for operational assets, which means that developers can unlock significant unrecognized equity value through farm-downs.

Section 4 – The Influence of Rising Bond Yields on Asset Pricing

Section summary
  1. The rise in sovereign bond yields since mid-2026 depresses asset valuations by inflating discount rates and financing costs.
  2. Despite yield headwinds, private infrastructure demand remains resilient, allowing developers to crystallize premium valuations compared to public markets.

Impact on Multiples and

1.
Valuations Yield-driven valuation compression: The rise in sovereign bond yields since mid-2026 has triggered significant valuation impairments across the renewable energy sector because higher discount rates compress asset values. Specifically, Morgan Stanley's report of 9 October 2026 notes that Orsted is facing a DKK 3.75 billion yield-driven impairment in its third-quarter results. This charge follows a previous DKK 1.20 billion interest rate impairment recorded during the second quarter, as reported by Morgan Stanley on 13 August 2026. These adjustments show how rising interest rates depress the present value of operational assets.
2.
Multiple contraction pressures: Rising yields have forced analysts to compress exit multiples for key asset rotation programs because higher rates increase buyer return hurdles. Specifically, Bernstein Research's report of 29 September 2026 adjusted its assumed exit multiple for Grenergy from 1.50x to 1.40x enterprise value to invested capital. This multiple contraction directly reflects how higher investor return requirements reduce the prices that infrastructure funds are willing to pay. Consequently, developers face lower capital gains unless they achieve stronger power prices.
3.
Sovereign spread headwinds: Widening sovereign yield spreads, particularly the French borrowing premium over Germany that reached 141 basis points as reported by Rothschild & Co Redburn on 15 October 2026, have elevated borrowing costs across Europe. This sovereign market volatility has spilled over to Spain and Italy, as noted by Rothschild & Co Redburn on 15 October 2026, which means that developers face higher project hurdle rates. These broader macro tensions increase capital constraints, so developers must adapt to tighter financial conditions. Ultimately, these widening spreads threaten to erode project development margins.
4.
Peer valuation metrics: While public market multiples have contracted under yield pressures, utilities continue to trade at premium levels compared to historical averages. Bernstein Research's note of 12 October 2026 indicates that European utilities trade at an 11.40% premium to their historical average forward price-to-earnings multiple. However, renewable pure-plays like Solaria have de-rated to 7.30x 2030e EV/EBITDA, as reported by Bernstein Research on 29 September 2026, because of conservative development assumptions. This de-rating shows that the public market is heavily discounting future pipelines under a higher bond yield environment.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some completed transactions have managed to insulate themselves from yield pressures by leveraging robust inflation-linked revenues. Deutsche Numis argues in a report of 12 October 2026 that alternative infrastructure funds maintain inflation-linked cash flows that help protect real returns as yields rise. Furthermore, Deutsche Bank's report of 22 September 2026 notes that Finnish and German projects can offset lower wind yields through high power prices. This suggests that the impact of rising yields is not uniform, so some projects can maintain premium valuation metrics.

The Valuation Transmission Mechanism

6.
WACC sensitivity impacts: The primary mechanism translating higher yields into lower asset values is the upward adjustment of weighted average cost of capital assumptions. For example, Jefferies' note of 1 September 2026 rolled forward its valuation of EDP Renewables and raised its WACC to reflect higher interest rates. Specifically, the US ten-year Treasury yield rose 25 basis points above previous assumptions, directly offsetting fundamental improvements. Thus, higher discount rates neutralize the positive earnings impact of increased renewable capacity additions.
7.
Project NPV sensitivities: Large-scale renewable projects are highly sensitive to discount rate assumptions because of their long-duration cash flows. Bernstein Research's report of 29 September 2026 highlights that a 50 basis points change in the WACC for Orsted's US offshore assets carries a DKK 2.20 billion NPV impact. Similarly, Grenergy's target price declines by approximately 7.0% for every 50 basis points increase in bond yields. This high sensitivity means that sustained rate increases will materially depress project valuations.
8.
Debt financing hurdles: Higher sovereign yields directly elevate the cost of project-level debt financing, which compresses developer equity returns. Alerion CleanPower's report of 30 July 2026 notes that its net financial indebtedness has risen to 744.90 million euros to fund Romanian and Italian investments. This growing debt burden, combined with rising interest rates, significantly increases net financial expenses. Consequently, developers must achieve higher operational efficiencies to offset rising interest expenses.
9.
Higher hurdle rates: Private infrastructure buyers have responded to higher financing costs by enforcing stricter investment discipline. DNB Carnegie's report of 12 October 2026 notes that Scatec requires a project equity IRR threshold of at least 1.20x the cost of equity. This strict discipline means that buyers will submit lower bids for operating assets if debt costs rise. As a result, the bid-ask spread is likely to widen, limiting the volume of transactions.

Resiliency of Private Infrastructure Demand

10.
Sustained premium demand: Despite rising yields, private infrastructure funds still demonstrate a robust appetite for operational renewable assets. Morgan Stanley's report of 7 October 2024 notes that private takeovers of listed developers have achieved an average 40.0% EV/EBITDA premium over public market valuations. This substantial premium is driven by the stable, contracted cash flows that emerging market renewable projects offer. This persistent demand suggests that the private transaction window remains fundamentally open for high-quality operating renewable portfolios.
11.
Strong farm-down realizations: Developers continue to execute asset rotations at multiples that comfortably exceed their conservative business plan assumptions. For instance, EDP Renewables completed recent asset rotations in Italy and the US at a 40.0% net present value to invested capital multiple. This represents a massive premium compared to the company's long-term guidance, as highlighted by Deutsche Bank's report of 30 July 2026. These strong realizations confirm that private buyers are still willing to pay premium multiples for assets.
12.
Balance sheet protection: Successful asset rotations are critical to protecting developers' credit ratings and maintaining investment-grade status. Acciona Energia expects its asset rotation program to bring net debt down to 3.70 billion euros by the end of 2026. This deleveraging is vital to maintain credit ratings, such as the BBB- stable rating affirmed by Fitch in December 2024. Therefore, executing asset sales preserves balance sheet flexibility even as corporate borrowing costs increase.
13.
Other side of the story: While private market demand remains resilient, a prolonged period of elevated yields will eventually force a downward repricing of operating assets. Bernstein Research's report of 29 September 2026 notes that infrastructure funds cannot indefinitely absorb rising financing costs without reducing bid multiples. If buyers compress exit multiples, developers like Grenergy and EDP Renewables will face smaller capital gains. Consequently, a sustained high-interest-rate environment risks closing the premium private valuation window.
