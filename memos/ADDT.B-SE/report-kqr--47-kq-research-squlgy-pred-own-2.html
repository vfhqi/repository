Historical Pricing and Return Dynamics

1.
Disciplined pricing: Addtech has historically maintained a conservative acquisition pricing strategy that aligns with proven serial compounding peers. According to DNB Carnegie research from October 2026, proven Swedish compounders recorded a median EV/EBITA acquisition multiple of 6.8x over the 2020-2024 period. This multiple represents a highly consistent pricing anchor that has enabled these companies to generate steady returns over multiple economic cycles. Reinvesting cash flows at these levels supports sustainable earnings per share compounding over the long term.
2.
Attractive returns: Entry multiples directly dictate the return on incremental capital employed for programmatic buyers. DNB Carnegie notes that paying between 6.0x and 8.0x EV/EBITA translates to an attractive steady-state return on capital of approximately 13% to 20%. This return is mathematically derived as the inverse of the entry multiple paid for the target assets. Consequently, maintaining strict pricing discipline is essential to protecting the group's overall return profile.
3.
Conservative forecasting: Broker models for Addtech now incorporate slightly higher pricing assumptions to reflect broader market dynamics. In its October 2026 valuation model, DNB Carnegie assumed that Addtech will pay an average of 7.5x EBITA for future bolt-on acquisitions. This estimate is slightly above the historical median but remains well below the public market trading multiples of listed serial acquirers. This gap ensures that Addtech continues to benefit from substantial pricing arbitrage on new investments.

Peer Comparisons and Multiple Arbitrage

4.
Moderate valuations: Several niche acquirers in the Nordic region have recently achieved even lower entry multiples by targeting smaller operations. For instance, Tangen paid an average EV/EBITA multiple of 4.8x for its acquisitions conducted between 2023 and 2025. Similarly, Salix Group reported a weighted-average multiple of 6.7x EV/EBITA for its transactions completed over the same period. These peer benchmarks indicate that smaller, focused buyers can still secure highly accretive transaction terms in fragmented markets.
5.
Valuation divergence: Programmatic acquirers leverage the wide valuation gap between private targets and listed parent companies. RedEye research from November 2022 emphasizes that serial acquirers typically purchase private niche businesses at far lower multiples than their own shares trade at. This pricing mismatch creates immediate multiple arbitrage when acquired earnings are rerated to the parent's public trading multiple. This rerating mechanism provides a powerful driver for near term earnings per share expansion.
6.
Elevated precedents: Some newer acquirers have historically paid higher entry multiples before implementing stricter capital discipline. DNB Carnegie highlights that Sdiptech paid higher historical multiples than most of its peers. However, Sdiptech's recent transactions closed at approximately 6.0x EV/EBITA. Sdiptech now targets an internal rate of return of 20% over a five-year earn-out period, indicating that post-acquisition integration must drive the effective multiple down over time.

DNB Carnegie highlights that targeting owner-led niche companies through direct relationships yields lower entry pricing than intermediated auctions. This localized, relationship-driven procurement of targets is particularly vital in highly fragmented markets. By avoiding broad auction processes, serial acquirers can maintain strict discipline on multiples paid.

RedEye reports that intense competition among serial acquirers has the potential to make acquisitions more expensive over time. If transaction multiples expand, the returns generated from the group's capital allocation strategy will inevitably face downward pressure. This indicates that the historic 6.8x median may be difficult to replicate in crowded sectors.

Other side of the story: While Addtech's disciplined relationship-sourcing model has successfully defended against entry multiple expansion, the increasing institutionalization of private markets poses a long-term risk. Morgan Stanley highlights that private transaction multiples in Europe have consistently outpaced public valuations. If more niche founders demand institutional premiums or turn to competitive auctions, Addtech may be forced to pay higher entry prices. Consequently, relying on historical medians could underestimate future capital costs and dilute EPS growth.
