Section 6 – Acquisition-Driven Forecast Dispersion and EQT Precedents

Section summary
  1. Accelerated transactional activity under institutional ownership creates substantial near-term forecast dispersion as traditional consensus models exclude unannounced transactions.
  2. Historical precedents show private equity ownership structures successfully expand acquisition pipelines but introduce elevated governance and cultural integration risks.

Forecast Dispersion and Unannounced M&A

1.
Systematic growth drop-off: Traditional consensus financial models for serial acquirers frequently experience a sharp artificial drop-off in expected growth because analysts typically exclude unannounced future transactions. This restrictive modeling approach means that consensus expectations often project a steep deceleration in earnings growth one to two years out as already announced transactions scale. By contrast, incorporating normalized transaction pipelines into financial models reveals a much steeper drop in forward valuation multiples because future acquisitions drive stronger actual earnings growth.
2.
Underestimated medium-term earnings: Financial analysts who evaluate active niche compounders often struggle to capture the true earnings potential of highly liquid companies without modeling forward deal pipelines. Traditional modeling methods fail to reflect how future acquisitions drive faster multiple contractions for premium niche acquirers. Consequently, omitting future deal flow from core estimates means that expected mediumterm earnings per share are frequently understated by consensus.
3.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that including unannounced future transactions in core earnings models introduces unacceptable forecasting risks because the timing and valuation of individual deals are highly unpredictable. For instance, predicting the specific timing and margins of upcoming acquisitions is practically impossible for complex distributors operating across multiple regional segments. Therefore, modeling only confirmed transactions remains the most reliable approach to protect near-term earnings forecasts from speculative overestimation.

The Beijer Ref Precedent

4.
Institutional ownership acceleration: The EQT-era precedent at Beijer Ref demonstrates how institutional private equity ownership can rapidly accelerate a distributor's transactional engine. Following EQT's entry in late 2020, Beijer Ref completed more than 20 acquisitions within a three-year period, representing more than half of its total transactions over the preceding decade. This aggressive transactional push established the company as a global leader in HVAC distribution, showing that institutional backing can unlock massive scale benefits.
5.
Proactive pipeline management: The structural transformation at Beijer Ref under EQT ownership focused on building an institutionalized, decentralized M&A framework. Rather than relying on central private equity sourcing, the company established a dedicated five-person internal M&A team working directly with local divisional presidents. This organizational setup allowed the acquisition pipeline to remain highly active and independent of EQT's direct participation.
6.
Transition and governance risk: The final exit of private equity ownership often triggers executive transitions that create near-term uncertainty for serial acquirers. Melker Schörling AB became the largest shareholder with 14.1% of votes following the acquisition of EQT's series A shares. Shortly thereafter, EQT-era Chief Executive Officer Christopher Norbye stepped down, indicating that the transition could disrupt short-term execution stability.

Comparative M&A Models and Multiples

7.
Disciplined valuation hurdles: OEM International has historically demonstrated superior capital discipline by securing acquisitions at valuation multiples well below its peers. Between 2020 and 2024, the company paid an average multiple of approximately 6.5x EV/EBITA for its targets, compared to a peer group average of 7.5x. This disciplined pricing structure ensures that OEM preserves exceptionally high returns on invested capital as its portfolio expands.
8.
Strategic arbitrage hurdles: Indutrade maintains strict valuation parameters to ensure that newly acquired businesses remain accretive to group capital return metrics. The group targets transaction multiples of five to eight times EBITA, requiring each business to reach a 20% return on capital within three to five years. This disciplined pricing framework prevents Indutrade from overpaying during market peaks and supports sustainable compounding.
