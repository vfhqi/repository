Plausible problems to monitor

The comprehensive risk analysis across all three parts of the Helios Towers investment memo reveals three material cross-cutting plausible problem to monitor that warrant heightened monitoring across accounting quality, operational execution, and currency resilience dimensions. These structural vulnerabilities represent areas where the platform meaningfully underperforms peer benchmarks or exhibits potential decay that could compound under stress scenarios.

The first and most severe plausible problem to monitor centers on extreme customer concentration dynamics, where the top four mobile network operators generate 72.3% of total group revenue while unrecognised potential contract revenue ballooned 149% year-on-year to forty-seven million dollars in FY25. This dual deterioration pattern reflects both structural exposure to single-operator credit events and emerging liquidity issues with distressed customer operators, as payment disputes and collectability challenges prevent immediate revenue recognition under IFRS 15 criteria.

The risk materiality is validated by peer experience, as comparable African tower operator IHS Holding faced severe customer cash collection disputes in Nigeria, demonstrating that this concentration vulnerability is not merely theoretical but represents a proven failure mode within the sector. The compounding danger lies in the fact that five key operators represent eighty percent of total group turnover, meaning any single client consolidation, default, or aggressive renegotiation could structurally impair the entire cash flow trajectory.

The second material plausible problem to monitor manifests as power-driven margin dilution, where contractual fuel escalators successfully protect absolute Adjusted EBITDA dollars but simultaneously compress relative profitability margins through elevated pass-through revenues. This false-friend dynamic became particularly acute in FY25, when high fuel revenues flattered top-line metrics while underlying organic lease-up rates faced pressure, creating a deceptive appearance of operational strength that masks potential structural tenancy decay.

The vulnerability is amplified relative to peer IHS Holding, which suffers from severe currency mismatch in Nigeria where Naira revenues fail to cover dollar-linked operating expenses, yet Helios exhibits its own variant through margin compression rather than absolute EBITDA exposure. The structural decay risk intensifies under scenarios where sudden local currency collapses squeeze local operating expense margins if inflation escalators lag behind realworld devaluations, creating timing mismatches that temporarily impair cash conversion.

The third cross-cutting plausible problem to monitor emerges from aggressive accounting policy changes, where management extended tower useful lives from fifteen to thirty years, reducing annual depreciation by fifty-two point eight million dollars and directly enabling the Group to report its first positive net income. When combined with acquisitions of under-commercialized portfolios that dilute the group tenancy ratio from 2.53x in established markets to only 1.58x in new markets, this accounting optically flatters statutory profitability while potentially obscuring underlying return on capital deterioration.

The accounting quality concern compounds with foreign exchange transaction exposure, as the Group holds three hundred and ninety-six point seven million dollars in foreign currency monetary liabilities against only two hundred point eight million dollars in assets, while simultaneously purchasing the vast majority of high-value tower equipment in US Dollars, exposing capital expenditure to transactional currency risks during local devaluations.

These three plausible problem to monitor exhibit dangerous correlation patterns, as extreme customer concentration increases the probability of payment delays that worsen during currency stress episodes, while margin dilution from power pass-throughs obscures the early warning signs of tenancy decay, and aggressive depreciation extensions artificially suppress the visible financial impact of integration failures on under-commercialized acquisitions.

The aggregate risk profile remains medium plausibility rather than high severity because ninety-three percent of Adjusted EBITDA expansion is driven by organic tenancy additions that have consistently exceeded expectations, demonstrating that operational execution has historically overcome structural vulnerabilities.
