Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Replacing high-cost contractors with permanent employees represents the primary driver of margin recovery and EPS expansion for Kainos and its digital service peers following peak public sector demand cycles.

The Central Investigation

1.
Central question: "Kainos Group plc (KNOS-GB), a Belfast-headquartered, London-listed digital services and software company with a 31 March year end and three divisions: Digital Services (digital transformation for UK central government, healthcare and defence, plus Canadian public-sector work through Davis Pier), Workday Services (Workday implementation and optimisation consulting) and Workday Products (its own software sold to Workday customers: Smart Test, Smart Audit, Smart Shield, Employee Document Management and the Pay Transparency Analyzer, which Workday resells exclusively). How did UK and European IT services and digital consultancies comparable to Kainos Group plc (KNOS-GB), such as Made Tech, TPXimpact, Netcompany's UK business, CGI UK, Adesso and Reply, manage contractor and subcontractor use after a demand surge: how quickly did contractor share fall, how much gross margin and operating margin was recovered, how much was lost to bench, training and holiday costs when contractors were replaced with employees, and how did HMRC's scrutiny of contingent labour supply chains and the IR35 rules affect the cost and availability of contractors in UK public-sector delivery in 2025 and 2026?"

The group operates two other divisions, with Workday Services growing 9% to £58.3 million and Workday Products climbing 22% to £48.0 million. Across this entire research project, we identified 105 relevant sources, 7 comparable peer companies, and 3 historical precedents to analyze these staffing dynamics.

According to the interim results presentation on 9 November 2026, contractor and partner usage in Digital Services more than doubled from 139 to 316 capacity and strategic partners. This staffing shift compressed the overall Digital Services gross margin by 390 basis points to 31.9%, which directly muted near-term operational gearing and limited EPS growth.

Strategic transition: Efficient operations depend on the company's ability to systematically replace flexible contractors with permanent recruits once delivery growth rates moderate below peak levels. Management noted on 9 November 2026 that replacing high-cost contractors is predicated on revenue growth slowing below 20% to permit permanent staff onboarding. A failure to manage this transition would lock in low partner margins of 9.6% and contractor margins of 24.9%, permanently impairing the consensus adjusted EPS target.

Section 2 – Contractor De-allocation and Employee Substitution Timelines

Section summary
  1. Consultancies achieve medium confidence contractor de-allocation within six to eighteen months following peak demand.
  2. Our confidence rests on multi-year reporting from Made Tech and Netcompany.

Peer Staffing Timelines

1.
Rapid substitution: Made Tech managed its post-peak staffing mix by reducing contractor share from 19% in early fiscal 2026 to 14% of the billable workforce by November 2025. This shift occurred as the company expanded its permanent team by 16% to 433 employees during the same six-month period. This rapid contractor deallocation allowed the group to improve its delivery margins. This reduction is expected to restore long-term operating margin quality as reliance on high-cost independent contractors falls.
2.
Gradual replacement: Netcompany managed its UK contractor de-allocation by reducing its independent contractor share from 55% in the third quarter of 2018 to 42% in the third quarter of 2019. This transition was initiated ahead of major regulatory changes in the UK public sector regarding freelance tax rules. However, the business warned that shifting the UK delivery mix toward permanent staff is a protracted three-to-five-year structural process. This slow timeline limits how quickly the company can expand margins.
3.
Stable ratio: TPXimpact maintained a permanent-to-contractor ratio of approximately 70:30 during its operational restructuring in 2025. The company decreased its contractor headcount by 17% from 177 to 147 in the half year ended 30 September 2025. This de-allocation occurred alongside a 6% reduction in permanent staff to 405 people. This coordinated scale-down preserved margin resilience and delivery flexibility without creating excessive bench overheads.

Staffing Ratio Comparisons

4.
Elevated utilization: Adesso experienced rising capacity utilization in 2023 which allowed the group to decrease its external service provider expenditure by 1% to 38.0 million euros in the third quarter. However, the company still required substantial external resources to support double-digit sales growth across its core public sector markets. The group reported deploying 1,534 non-employees, including 1,445 self-employed freelancers, at the end of 2025. This high external count indicates a slower transition to fully insourced delivery teams compared to UK peers.
5.
Asymmetric hiring: Kainos Group reported that hiring a temporary contractor takes only four weeks to deploy onto public-sector projects. In contrast, hiring a permanent staff member requires five months to onboard. This disparity creates operational friction when substituting contractors with employees during rapid growth cycles. This slow permanent recruitment pace restricts near-term operational margin gearing when demand normalizes.
6.
Contrary evidence: Made Tech delayed its contractor de-allocation during fiscal 2025 when it increased its contractor ratio to 19% from 9% in fiscal 2024 to hedge against election volatility. This intentional delay in contractor replacement occurred despite the company targeting a permanent contractor ratio of 10% by the end of fiscal 2026. This high contractor share compressed profit margins due to increased subcontractor delivery costs throughout the year.

Precedents and Flexibilities

7.
Successful transition: Netcompany demonstrated a rapid contractor substitution precedent by transitioning a major UK public-sector project from independent contractors to permanent staff in 2019. This project transition was completed over a period of just six months, shifting the project status to highly profitable. This precedent suggests that consultancies can swiftly replace expensive contractors when project scopes stabilize. Such rapid changes can help recover gross margins during procurement pauses.

  2. Our confidence in these financial dynamics is supported by detailed reporting from peer consultancies in the 2025 and 2026 fiscal years.

This improvement was achieved as the company managed overhead growth and successfully reduced its reliance on high-cost contractors. According to the annual report published on 29 September 2026, the company successfully expanded its permanent workforce while lowering its subcontractor dependencies to restore margin quality.
2.
Strong leverage: TPXimpact recorded an accelerated margin improvement as its adjusted EBITDA margin expanded from 7.3% in fiscal 2025 to 11.0% in fiscal 2026. CFO Noel Douglas stated in the annual report on 7 August 2025 that gross margins climbed to 28.6% due to better resource planning and streamlined operations. By 30 September 2026, the company reported that its half-year adjusted EBITDA margin surged further to approximately 13% on a lean cost base.
3.
Improved utilization: Adesso benefited from rising utilization of internal staff to lift its EBITDA margin from 7.3% in the first nine months of 2025 to 8.1% in the same period of 2026. The corporate release on 11 November 2026 detailed that third-quarter EBITDA margins reached 12.4% as employee hiring slowed down. Jefferies research on 11 November 2026 verified that this profitability was supported by stronger public sector digitalization contracts in Germany.

Resilient operations: Reply achieved superior profitability by reducing contractor spending from 460 million euros in 2023 to 400 million euros in 2024. A Deutsche Bank note on 14 March 2025 observed that this resource optimization drove full-year adjusted EBITDA margins up to 17.9% in 2024. Company presentations on 30 September 2025 confirmed that EBITDA margins had expanded to 18.4% for the first nine months of the year.

Section 4 – Bench, Training, and Holiday Friction Costs of Employee Replacement

Section summary
  1. Transitioning from contractors to permanent employees introduces substantial hidden friction costs including training outlays, holiday pay, and underutilized bench capacity.
  2. Our medium confidence is based on historical margin compression at Made Tech and TPXimpact during periods of low employee utilization.

Employee Overhead Friction Costs

1.
Inflationary overhead: TPXimpact experienced severe pressure on gross margins during its fiscal 2023 restructuring because of expanded employee benefits. The company introduced an enhanced package in April 2022 that increased holiday entitlements, pensions, and other benefits for permanent staff. These permanent employee overheads added significant structural costs to the delivery mix. This financial drag demonstrates the ongoing margin risks of expanding permanent headcounts.
2.
Eroded utilisation: Made Tech struggled to maintain profitability during headcount transitions as billable utilization fell to 70% in fiscal 2023. The group attempted to right-size its workforce by reducing contractor share to 8% while absorbing permanent employee overheads. This lower utilization rate directly undermined operating efficiency across the business. These capacity challenges illustrate the high cost of carrying underutilized permanent bench staff during soft market conditions.
3.
Comparative overhead: Kainos reported that its own permanent delivery staff carry high overheads that contractors do not require. The company noted that contractors have no associated expenses for training, holidays, and unutilised time. This lack of benefits allows consultancies to scale down contractors instantly without incurring trailing costs. This pure hourly-rate structure contrasts with the expensive long-term commitments required by permanent employees.

Historical Utilisation Precedents

4.
Severe compression: Made Tech demonstrated a costly historical precedent in H1 23 when its gross margin declined to 32.9% from 39.1% in the prior year. The business suffered because client delays coincided with average contractor usage of 12% as they tried to replace contractors with permanent staff. As a direct consequence, employee utilization fell to a low of 68% from 79% in H1 22. This transition mismatch shows how sudden bench capacity growth can severely damage operating profit.
5.
Rising metrics: Adesso reported that its material cost ratio rose to 17% in late 2026 due to the use of external project staff. The company expanded its full-time equivalent staff by only 4% to 11,578 to protect utilization and manage overheads. This controlled hiring strategy focused on increasing capacity utilization and leveraging smartshore delivery locations to control personnel costs. This approach highlights how peers limit domestic permanent recruitment to avoid carrying expensive bench liabilities.
6.
Contrary evidence: Kainos benefited from its high contractor mix in H1 27 because contractors kept direct delivery expenses low. The interim report from 9 November 2026 showed that Digital Services direct expenses grew by only 23% compared to massive revenue growth of 57%. This operational efficiency was achieved because the 316 contractors deployed carried no training, holiday, or bench costs. This outcome proves that contractor reliance can sometimes protect operating margins during rapid demand surges.
7.
Other side of the story: Retaining a flexible contractor buffer represents a vital defense against sudden market downturns that would otherwise result in massive underutilization penalties. Shifting completely to a permanent workforce exposes digital consultancies to costly permanent overhead liabilities if public sector contract awards are delayed or cancelled. Thus, maintaining a balanced staffing model protects baseline operating profit throughout volatile procurement cycles.

Section 5 – HMRC IR35 Compliance and Contingent Labour Scrutiny in 2025/2026

Section summary
  1. Tightened HMRC enforcement of IR35 rules and new umbrella liability shifts in 2026 significantly raise compliance risks and contractor pricing for UK digital consultancies.
  2. Peer consultancies are expanding permanent workforces and centralizing supply chain compliance to protect public sector margins from rising contingent labour tax burdens.

Regulatory Scrutiny and Rules

1.
Rising regulatory pressure: HMRC intensified its scrutiny of off-payroll working arrangements in 2025 and 2026 to eliminate disguised employment across public-sector supply chains. Staffing firms face severe financial exposure because incorrect status determinations can trigger backdated PAYE liabilities and HMRC penalties up to 100% of unpaid taxes. This enforcement environment forces digital consultancies to implement extensive, audited compliance programs to protect their future earnings from retrospective tax assessments.
2.
Structural policy changes: Government modifications to the small company threshold from 6 April 2026 raised the turnover limit from £10.2 million to £15 million to redefine who carries IR35 compliance duties. This change exempts smaller agencies but leaves larger consultancies entirely responsible for issuing Status Determination Statements to all contingent workers. Consequently, mid-sized public-sector delivery partners must absorb substantial administrative overheads to maintain compliant, auditable contractor records.
3.
Increased tax liability: HMRC introduced new Pay As You Earn compliance regulations for umbrella company supply chains on 6 April 2026. These rules make the top agency or end user jointly and severally liable if an intermediary fails to operate tax payrolls correctly. This major regulatory shift increases the level of due diligence required by IT consultancies, which directly inflates supply-chain monitoring costs and depresses operating profit.

Impact on Peer Consultancies

4.
Severe margin headwind: Made Tech experienced escalating project delivery difficulties in 2025 as public sector clients imposed increasingly tight restrictions on contractor usage due to IR35 compliance concerns. This restriction forced the consultancy to accelerate its transition toward permanent employees to protect its public service contract pipeline. Although the transition mitigates compliance risks, it exposes Made Tech's near-term operating margins to elevated recruitment, academy training, and onboarding costs.
5.
Operational risk mitigation: TPXimpact formalized its compliance protocols in 2025 to manage the legal and regulatory risks associated with large public sector contracts. The group matured its central operations model to handle complex modern requirements including rigorous IR35 supply chain oversight. This centralized administrative support helps protect TPXimpact from contract breaches but increases structural overhead costs relative to historical periods.
6.
Strategic contractor reduction: Netcompany mitigated its IR35 tax risks in the UK business by systematically reducing its contractor share of total headcount to approximately 42%. This strategic shift helped the company insulate its delivery model from tax compliance audits, representing a major catalyst for transforming its UK business. By replacing expensive independent contractors with permanent output-driven teams, the group achieved more stable long-term pricing and improved utilization rates.

Niche Skills and Sourcing Bottlenecks

7.
Rigorous defense security: Atomic Weapons Establishment implemented comprehensive IR35 status checks before advertising any contingent worker roles in fiscal 2026 to ensure full tax compliance. The defense employer issued status determination statements to all workers to ensure absolute compliance with HMRC. However, these rigid vetting and tax determination procedures slow down recruitment pipelines, creating severe delivery bottlenecks for defense projects that require highly specialized technical expertise.
8.
Severe healthcare shortages: CAMHS Professionals reported that agency price caps and evolving IR35 tax rules hindered the recruitment of specialized mental health clinicians in 2026. This regulatory friction has depressed the supply of qualified nurses and psychiatrists when clinical vacancies remain exceptionally high. Consequently, healthcare IT consultancies face extreme contractor rate inflation and resource constraints, which limits their ability to deliver complex digital healthcare programs on budget.
9.
Unequal hiring environments: Public sector delivery partners faced deep recruitment disadvantages in 2025 because public employers prioritize specific formal qualifications over work experience. Private sector competitors are far more flexible, offering higher salaries and remote options that state-backed entities cannot easily match. This compensation gap, combined with rigorous IR35 assessments, makes it difficult for public sector consultancies to source specialized data science, cybersecurity, and artificial intelligence professionals.
