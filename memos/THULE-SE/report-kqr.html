Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Channel inventory levels across European consumer product manufacturers remain uneven as some segments stabilize while others face ongoing destocking.
  2. The timing of restocking cycles directly influences operating leverage and projected earnings trajectories for these major consumer brands.

The Pivotal Question

1.
Key Question: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector H.17. Consumer products, with Thule (THULE-SE) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: Since July 2026, have retailers and dealers in Europe and North America stopped cutting their stocks of bike and car carriers, leisurevehicle and boat equipment, garden tools and robotic mowers, pool equipment and enterprise headsets? For Thule, Dometic, Fluidra, Einhell, Husqvarna and GN Store Nord, what did third-quarter 2026 results, calls and channel checks show about orders against sales to end customers, retailer stock levels and order books, and what do brokers and dealers expect for spring 2027 orders? How long did earlier restocking phases last, for example in bikes in 2024, and how much did the makers' sales and margins rise during them? How are higher interest rates and dealers' stock-financing costs affecting their willingness to restock?."
2.
Uncertain demand: The resolution of dealer stock cutting represents a critical binary pivot for the earnings trajectories and valuation multiples of listed European outdoor and consumer brands. DNB Carnegie notes that retail caution persists due to high interest rates, making near-term volume growth highly dependent on the pace of destocking. While some segments show signs of normalisation, prolonged caution among channel partners could delay any meaningful earnings recovery into late 2027.
3.
Delayed recovery: For brands with high discretionary exposure like Dometic, prolonged dealer caution directly dampens the near-term outlook. DNB Carnegie downgraded Dometic because weak cash generation and limited visibility on the end-market recovery in 2027 have elevated financial risks. Slower destocking in RV and marine segments threatens to push EBITA margins far below management's long-term targets.

Impact on Trajectories and Multiples

4.
Structural resilience: Outdoor equipment manufacturers such as Thule rely on positive mix shifts and structural efficiencies to offset weak volumes. Jefferies highlights that Thule's margin resilience is driven by pricing power and cost-saving programs, even when organic growth remains soft. The timing of retail restocking remains a key driver for reaching their mid-term EBIT margin ambition of 20%.
5.
Resilient aftermarket: In contrast to high-ticket discretionary products, aftermarket-driven segments like pool equipment show superior resilience during cyclical downturns. Jefferies reports that Fluidra has maintained steady growth because non-discretionary maintenance demand offsets the steep decline in new pool construction. A stabilization of dealer inventories would act as an upside-only trigger for its North American volumes.
6.
Severe weakness: Severe end-market weakness and pricing pressures continue to cloud the recovery outlook for garden tools and robotic mowers. DNB Carnegie cuts its projections for Husqvarna, noting that adverse weather and competitive pressures in Europe are delaying the benefits of its cost-saving program. Elevated channel partner inventories continue to constrain demand, meaning that any near-term earnings acceleration is highly uncertain.

Restocking Pressures and Financial Constraints

7.
Structural headwinds: Headset manufacturers are facing structural market headwinds alongside temporary channel adjustments and pricing pressure. Bernstein Research notes that GN Store Nord's Enterprise headset business faces a transitioning replacement market and pricing pressure from price-sensitive customer segments. Meeting full-year targets requires a significant acceleration in H2, which remains highly debated among brokers.
8.
34 side of the story: While many brokers maintain a cautious outlook on dealer replenishment, some segments are demonstrating an early shift toward healthy inventory levels. DNB Carnegie reports that retail inventories for Thule are already viewed as broadly healthy, suggesting sell-in and sell-out should align in late 2026. However, the structural improvement in retailers' inventory management means that the next upcycle may not deliver the massive restocking boost seen in previous cycles.

Section 5 – Capital Costs and Dealer Willingness to Restock

Section summary
  1. High interest rates and rising floor-planning costs have created significant financial friction across retail distribution channels in Europe and North America.
  2. Retailers are actively optimizing their working capital by shifting from large pre-season bookings to smaller and more frequent in-season replenishment orders.
  3. Distributor financing constraints are highly polarized, leaving highly leveraged manufacturers vulnerable while financially stable peers remain largely unbottlenecked.

Impact of Interest Rates on Inventory Carrying Costs

1.
Elevated interest: Retail partners face a persistent headwind from rising interest rates as the Federal Reserve raised rates by 25 basis points in September 2026. Morgan Stanley expects two additional 25 basis point hikes by March 2027, further escalating dealer stock-financing costs. Consequently, these rising borrowing costs directly squeeze retail margins and dampen dealer willingness to hold excess buffer stock.
2.
Increased funding: Consumer product retailers are prioritizing inventory turns over absolute sales growth because high interest rates have dramatically increased capital costs. Jefferies Research reports that the rising return on cash has increased the hurdle rate that physical inventory must earn to justify remaining on balance sheets. Therefore, distributors are actively paring back safety-stock levels and shortening their overall procurement visibility.
3.
Persistent caution: Thule Group's retail partners are displaying extreme caution because of expensive stock- carrying costs in a high rate environment. DNB Carnegie notes that retail inventories of premium outdoor brands are already low compared to pre-pandemic baselines. However, dealers refuse to rebuild these safety buffers due to the high opportunity cost of carrying inventory today.
4.
Escalating expenses: Einhell Germany AG is feeling the indirect effects of elevated global financing rates through its distribution partners and foreign operations. The manufacturer reported that its financial costs reached EUR 9.20 million in the first half of 2026 due to refinancing challenges. This cost surge reflects the unfavorable refinancing conditions in countries with high interest rates, which limits the capital available for pre-season orders.

Shift in Ordering Patterns and Pre-Season Bookings

5.
Flexible replenishment: Global retailers are aggressively restructuring their procurement habits by shifting from massive pre-season commitments to frequent in-season orders. Jefferies Research highlights that buyers are ordering less per transaction but increasing frequency to four times annually instead of twice. This strategic pivot allows distributors to optimize cash flows and hedge against volatile consumer demand.
6.
Constrained booking: Off-price retail buyers are intentionally avoiding long-lead pre-season bookings to maintain liquidity in a volatile macroeconomic climate. Jefferies Research observations show that firms are asking suppliers to hold physical inventory rather than taking early delivery. This shift forces manufacturers to bear higher warehousing risks unless they negotiate flexible regional distribution agreements.
7.
Decreased visibility: Outdoor equipment dealers are refusing to lock in early bookings because high borrowing costs make stockpiling extremely risky. DNB Carnegie warns that the investment case for seasonal brands remains vulnerable if retailers continue to delay their order placements. Consequently, manufacturers face compressed visibility and must operate with highly volatile order books leading into spring 2027.
8.
Delayed commitment: Garden tool distributors are delaying their seasonal inventory commitments because high financing costs make carrying slow-moving winter stock unviable. DNB Carnegie highlights that European gardening channels are highly sensitive to weather-driven demand fluctuations under high rate regimes. Therefore, retailers are pushing ordering decisions closer to the actual spring 2027 selling season, restricting near-term factory utilization rates.

Dometic Group operates with an elevated leverage ratio of 3.4x net debt to EBITDA, limiting its financial flexibility. Conversely, Fluidra SA boasts a stable leverage ratio of 2.2x, allowing it to sustain robust commercial excellence initiatives and support dealer networks.
