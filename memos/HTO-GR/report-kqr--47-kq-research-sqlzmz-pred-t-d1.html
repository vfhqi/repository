Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The expected transition of Hellenic Telecommunications Organization hinges on leveraging its premium network infrastructure to sustain profitable growth.
  2. Sustaining this trajectory is critical to supporting future dividend capacity and protecting valuation multiples amid escalating competition and macroeconomic shifts.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Critical inquiry: The core focus of this investment analysis is "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Significant earnings impact: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization faces a critical juncture where its ability to maintain pricing power will directly dictate its mid-term earnings per share. According to Morgan Stanley forecasts from October 30, 2026, the company is projected to grow its earnings per share from €1.23 in 2025 to €1.83 by 2028. If price indexation fails to offset competitive pressures, this projected earnings expansion could be severely compromised because the high-margin wholesale revenues are simultaneously eroding.
3.
Robust dividend capacity: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization relies heavily on free cash flow generation to sustain its generous shareholder return policy. Morgan Stanley reported on October 30, 2026, that OTE's free cash flow growth is expected to propel shareholder returns to 98% of free cash flow by 2030. However, any failure to achieve low single-digit EBITDA after leases growth will directly restrict this dividend capacity unless cost efficiency measures compensate.
4.
Elevating valuation multiples: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization currently enjoys premium multiples relative to its European peers due to its strong financial position and market leadership. According to Morgan Stanley's report on April 21, 2026, OTE traded at a 2027 estimated unlevered free cash flow yield of 8.5%, compared to the European sector average of 6%. Resolving the key question negatively would trigger multiple de-rating, whereas proving operational resilience would solidify its premium valuation over the 2027 to 2030 period.

Section 2 – Plausibility of Consecutive Inflation-Linked Price Rises

Section summary
  1. European incumbents are successfully implementing contractual inflation-linked indexation mechanisms to sustain retail revenue growth amidst elevated inflation.
  2. While customer churn has remained remarkably low across peers, regulatory risks in high-inflation environments present a potential mid-term hurdle.

Domestic Indexation and Regulatory Dynamics

1.
Sustained pricing: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization implemented a 2.6% tariff increase in December 2025 and established contract terms in July 2026 to permit annual price indexation starting in January. This contractual shift helps shield retail revenues from severe inflationary pressures. If executed without significant customer churn, this pricing power will underpin projected mid-term EBITDAaL expansion over the next three years.
2.
Favorable environment: Greek mobile competitors Vodafone Greece and Nova recently raised their service prices in line with consumer inflation for the second consecutive year. This coordinated repricing provides essential air cover for the incumbent operator to implement its contract modifications. These market dynamics help OTE defend its dominant retail revenue share without triggering destructive discount wars.
3.
Regulatory templates: Greece's consumer price inflation reached 5.0% in September 2026, raising concerns about potential cost-of-living political interventions. Other international regulators have already introduced restrictive measures to protect consumers from aggressive inflation-linked pricing practices. For example, communication authorities in Turkey and the United Kingdom recently established strict transparency rules for indexation clauses.
