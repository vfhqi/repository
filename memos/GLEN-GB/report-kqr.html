This central question is critical for global investors as the mining sector undergoes a structural shift away from bulk commodities. Restated in one sentence, the primary driver is the strategic divestment of non-core, volatile, or carbon-intensive assets by diversified miners to concentrate their capital and operations exclusively on high-growth copper assets. The financial transmission mechanism of this simplification driver operates through three distinct, step-by-step pathways: First, on revenues, divesting low-performing or unrelated bulk commodities eliminates revenue dilution, ensuring that corporate top-line results are directly geared to tight physical copper supply and secular electrification demand.

Second, on operating margins, shedding high-cost, complex non-core assets (such as underperforming coal, nickel, or diamond operations) directly streamlines the cost structure, allowing high-margin copper assets to expand the consolidated operating margin.

Third, on the valuation multiple, the transition eliminates the historical conglomerate discount applied to diversified miners–who typically trade at 5x to 6x EBITDA multiples–re-rating them toward the 10x+ multiples commanded by pure-play copper equities.
