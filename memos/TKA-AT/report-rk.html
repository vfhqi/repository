Section 10 – Pre-Mortem: Executive Summary 1 finding

The pre-mortem framework applied to Telekom Austria reveals that the FALSE FRIEND profile emerges as the winning hypothesis because it carries the absolute fewest hard inconsistencies across all structural operational parameters. This analytical lens proves most robust in explaining how superficially positive metrics–such as strong international EBITDA growth, zero net leverage, and expanding CEE market positions –can simultaneously coexist with severe underlying structural vulnerabilities that threaten long-term shareholder value creation.

The Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix identified two hard inconsistencies under the FALSE FRIEND profile: persisting Belarus dividend bans that trap reported international earnings, and the potential failure of

Dubai-based holding company restructurings that would leave capital repatriation completely blocked for concentrated public minority equity investors. In contrast, the YES (bull thesis holds) profile accumulated eight hard inconsistencies across competitive positioning, domestic execution, and geopolitical exposures, while the NO (outright failure) profile demonstrated five hard inconsistencies related to the company's demonstrable operational resilience and cash generation capacity.

1.
The total number of disconfirming evidence points concentrated in ten distinct failure narratives spanning revenue collapse modes, margin erosion scenarios, and execution risks. These included critical FALSE FRIEND scenarios where strong reported international EBITDA masks severe cash repatriation barriers and achieving zero net leverage masks complete capital allocation paralysis. The joint failure probability across correlated risk clusters is assessed as moderately high, driven by the tight linkage between geopolitical capital controls and pricing restrictions in Belarus, compounded by domestic Austrian margin pressures from infrastructure lease escalators and intensifying retail competition. The highest-plausibility failure mode identified is geopolitical decoupling in Belarus, where sustained international trade sanctions act as the trigger and transmit to earnings per share via written-down Belarusian Ruble cash reserves. The second critical high-plausibility scenario involves capital-heavy fiber rollouts in Austria failing to generate adequate investment returns, triggered by installation costs of nearly EUR 3,000 per household that delay deployment and compress internal rates of return. The least-monitored failure mode emerges as the progressive erosion of premium network leadership to rivals, where competitive victories in independent network quality tests gradually trigger high-value postpaid customer churn before manifesting in reported subscriber metrics. The key false-friend indicator centers on strong reported international EBITDA that superficially signals robust operational health while masking severe underlying cash repatriation barriers. A second critical misleading metric is achieving zero net leverage, which appears to demonstrate pristine balance sheet strength but actually reflects complete capital allocation paralysis and structural minority shareholder marginalization under the concentrated ownership structure dominated by América Móvil's 61% stake. These false-friend signals create a dangerous divergence between reported accounting performance and the actual economic reality facing public equity investors, who lack both the ability to influence capital deployment decisions and access to trapped cash reserves that constitute approximately one-quarter of consolidated group liquidity. Other side of the story: successful Belarus cash repatriation and disciplined regional asset integration would rapidly narrow the valuation discount currently embedded in the share price relative to European telecommunications peers
