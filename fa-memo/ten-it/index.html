The reasoning behind the inputs

The operating drivers behind the inputs

  1. US price (30-43% of sales reprice off PipeLogix, with a lag of one to two quarters). The seamless index rose from a trough of about $2,110/t (August 2024) to about $2,574/t (mid-2026), and is up 16% year to date (September 2026). Realised prices sit about 34% above the index. I assume about $2,850/t by end-2026, held through 2027. Realised US prices therefore rise about 8-10% in FY2027. They plateau in FY2028 as distributor restocking ends and imports and new US capacity respond, and fall about 5-6% in FY2029. This driver supplies about 60% of the FY2027 EBITDA increase.
  2. International prices. Formula contracts with national oil companies have a lag of two to three quarters and are held flat in nominal terms. Saudi and Argentine line-pipe share is under pressure.
  3. Volume. Offshore deepwater (about 20% of revenue) grows about 8% in 2027 and 6% in 2028; Saipem research has offshore capex up about 30% in 2027. Suriname and Guyana use Rig Direct. Vaca Muerta OCTG grows. US rigs are about 600 at end-2026 and flat in 2027. Artrom (200kt of seamless capacity) is consolidated from FY2027 at low margin.
  4. Strait of Hormuz. The base case has it closed through 2026, as guidance assumes, and reopening in mid-2027. About $130m a quarter of Gulf revenue returns from Q3 2027 with partial catch-up, at about 55-65% drop-through including about $30m a quarter of freight savings. This is the largest timing judgement in the inputs. If the Strait stays closed through 2027, FY2027 revenue is about $300m lower and EBITDA about $180m lower.
  5. Section 232 cost. The gross cost is $140m-150m a quarter. Net mitigation is +$80m in FY2027, +$150m in FY2028 and +$200m from FY2029 (Nucor Memphis bars, the Koppel upgrade in the second half of 2027, Sault Ste. Marie). Domestic bars carry a protected-market price, so only about a third of the gross cost is saved.
  6. Cost base. Fixed cash costs are $2.0bn-3.0bn, so the incremental margin on volume is about 45%. Fixed-cost inflation and North American regionalisation add about $60m-80m a year. Hot-rolled coil and scrap inflation reach cost of goods sold with a lag of about two quarters (inventory of about 140 days). I assume no new cost programme; in the FY2029 downturn there are about $50m a year of cuts.
  7. Working capital. It is 39.9% of trailing revenue at June 2026 (143 days). It ends FY2026 at about 40% and falls to 38.5% in FY2027 as Gulf stock ships. A return to 128-130 days, about $0.4bn-0.5bn of extra cash, is not in the base.
  8. Capex. First half of 2026 $236m. FY2027-28 is higher than historical levels because of the $90m Pennsylvania programme, the CAD 306m Sault Ste. Marie modernisation (net of CAD 148m of federal and provincial money) and Artrom.

  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
