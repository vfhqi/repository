EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 findings

Skanska delivered a mixed Q2 2026 financial print characterized by strong Construction operating income beating consensus by 10% while group revenue missed expectations by 9%, exposing the persistent tension between robust US civil contracting momentum and frozen commercial real estate capital. The Construction segment achieved an impressive 4.3% operating margin led by the USA Construction unit at 4.6%, yet Commercial Development suffered operating income of SEK -170M including SEK 464M in US property impairments and Residential Development margins collapsed to 1.5% burdened by SEK 70M in Nordic restructuring provisions.

The CFO transition from Jonas Rickberg to Pontus Winqvist after only ten months introduced short-term governance friction, though Winqvist's 28 years of internal experience minimizes systemic disruption. The group raised its long-term Construction operating margin target to at least 4% from 3.5% previously, reflecting management confidence in selective bidding discipline, while divesting the entire 50% ownership of Florida's I-4 Ultimate highway for approximately USD 75M signals geographic rebalancing toward higher-return US civil and tech infrastructure.

The materiality of change at Skanska centers on a deliberate strategic pivot from capital-intensive Nordic property development toward margin-accretive US civil construction and specialized technology builds. CEO Anders Danielsson has maintained consistent leadership since 2018, anchoring this transformation through three distinct phases: first, improving construction risk screening to halve large project failures compared to the 2018 write-down baseline; second, accelerating data center contract bookings to over SEK 15 billion during 2025; and third, divesting non-strategic Nordic land and restructuring the residential portfolio to focus on three key Swedish cities. Construction backlog reached an all-time high of SEK 297.5 billion in Q2 2026, providing unprecedented forward earnings visibility, while Commercial return on capital employed fell to a weak 1.3% underscoring the asymmetric performance of the two core business streams.

Segment revenues expanded steadily from SEK 34,878 million in Q1 2023 to SEK 44,600 million in Q2 2026, yet Construction margins improved from 3.0% in Q1 2026 to 4.3% in Q2 2026 demonstrating operational leverage as project selectivity filters into execution. The magnitude of change is substantial but geographically concentrated, with US Construction representing over 50% of Construction earnings serving as the primary driver of group profitability, while Nordic residential starts remain structurally depressed and completed unsold US commercial projects total SEK 18.5 billion tying up critical capital.

The top five to eight key drivers of forward earnings per share ranked by estimated impact through 2028 are: US Civils margin expansion estimated at +15% EPS impact with high plausibility, driven by the US Civil Works segment delivering roughly three times the margin of traditional Buildings construction and US Construction orders growing 55% in Q2 2026 skewing heavily toward high-margin Civils projects; US Commercial property divestments estimated at +12% EPS impact with low plausibility, constrained by elevated long-term interest rates forcing SEK 464M in US asset impairments during Q2 2026 and selling US office projects remaining tricky, limiting near-term capital recycling velocity; Advanced Technology and data centers estimated at +10% EPS impact with high plausibility, supported by data center bookings reaching nearly SEK 10 billion in a single quarter supporting future margin expansion and tech builds converting to revenue in 12 to 18 months accelerating nearterm cash conversion; Nordic Residential recovery estimated at +8% EPS impact with medium plausibility, dependent on gradual interest rate cuts potentially restoring consumer confidence and residential starts yet hindered by Q2 housing starts of 225 units remaining well below prior year levels of 420 units; and cost restructuring estimated at +5% EPS impact with high plausibility, achieved through integrating the BoKlok lowprice housing concept directly into Skanska Sweden after exiting unprofitable regions and management significantly reducing Residential selling and administrative overheads positioning the segment for operating leverage.

Sell-side positioning remains constructive on Construction margins yet deeply divided on Commercial Development recovery timelines.

The bull narrative highlights structural tech demand and US civil backlog margin expansion, whereas the cautious neutral narrative argues that high interest rates trap commercial capital and delay US real estate transaction recovery. Fundamental peer positioning reveals Skanska maintains a strong peer-relative momentum profile in Construction yet lags on capital efficiency metrics. Skanska holds a leading position in Construction profitability with a 4.3% margin outperforming the traditional peer average of 2% to 3%, and Jefferies highlights that Skanska's Q2 order bookings grew 20% year on year driven by US civil and data center contracts.

However, DNB Carnegie remains cautious on Skanska's high capital intensity with SEK 63 billion tied up in low-yielding development segments versus competitor NCC reporting net debt of SEK 2.5 billion in Q1 2026 and peer Peab operating with net debt of SEK 4.6 billion in mid-2025, highlighting Skanska's superior liquidity cushion yet structurally lower return on equity. Norwegian peer Veidekke commands a premium valuation multiple compared to Swedish players reflecting stronger historical residential market stability, while peer Strabag posted a record EBIT margin of 6.7% for FY2025 driven by major German transport projects demonstrating that European infrastructure leaders can achieve sustainable mid-single-digit margins.

Skanska's free working capital reached negative 18.9% of sales in Q2 boosted by substantial US mobilization cash inflows, establishing a structural competitive advantage in cash conversion that top-tier contractors are increasingly leveraging by acting as technical integrators improving structural cash conversion in AI infrastructure. Activist presence is notably absent from Skanska's shareholder register. No active activist presence appears in the AS library for Skanska, suggesting the company operates without external pressure for accelerated capital reallocation or portfolio restructuring. This absence reflects either market confidence in management's ongoing strategic pivot toward higher-return US civil and tech builds, or alternatively, a perception that the deeply embedded Nordic property portfolio represents an intractable structural challenge requiring patient multi-year resolution rather than activist intervention. The lack of activist engagement reduces near-term execution risk from forced asset sales yet simultaneously removes a potential catalyst for accelerating Commercial Development capital recycling.

The two to three most material risks confronting forward earnings are: first, prolonged commercial real estate illiquidity whereby a prolonged high interest rate environment could freeze real estate transactions indefinitely forcing material commercial asset write-downs and if long-term interest rates stay elevated Skanska will face deeper US asset impairments that completely erase Construction's margin strength, with Commercial Property Development holding SEK 40.4 billion in capital largely tied up in the US creating asymmetric downside if yield expansion persists; second, US civil funding contraction whereby if US public funding contracts the core Construction margin buffer would rapidly dissolve and excessive reliance on upfront US customer prepayments masks weak underlying cash conversion leaving Skanska highly vulnerable to rapid cash drains when public project starts decelerate, threatening the foundational earnings driver that currently compensates for property development weakness; and third, Nordic residential demand collapse whereby Nordic housing markets remain heavily depressed by macroeconomic uncertainty and historically low consumer confidence while Q2 housing starts of 225 units remained well below prior year levels of 420 units and Nordic results included SEK 70M in provisions and warranty costs, risking permanent capital impairment if demographic and monetary policy tailwinds fail to materialize over the medium term.

Plausible problems to monitor

Outliers worth noting

Skanska's Construction backlog reached an all-time high of SEK 297.5 billion in Q2 2026, representing a record accumulation of future revenue potential, yet the group's Q2 2026 revenue of SEK 44,564 million missed consensus expectations by 9%, revealing a significant divergence between order intake velocity and execution ramp-up speed that suggests either deliberate strategic phasing of project mobilizations or structural constraints in converting backlog to recognized revenue. This disconnect becomes particularly pronounced when considering US Construction orders grew 55% in Q2 2026 while segment revenue missed consensus targets due to slow ramp-up in US and Europe, creating an unexplained operational oddity where accelerating demand fails to translate proportionally into near-term revenue recognition despite the company's strong execution track record. The group achieved an exceptionally strong negative working capital position of 18.9% of sales in Q2 2026 and delivered outstanding operating cash flow of SEK 7.1 billion compared to SEK 1.3 billion year-on-year, representing a statistical outlier in cash generation intensity that appears disconnected from the underlying operational revenue trajectory.

This abnormally high cash conversion rate primarily stems from mobilization and milestone prepayments on newly started Construction projects, creating a temporal mismatch where upfront customer payments significantly exceed proportional project completion, raising questions about the sustainability of this cash profile once the current wave of project starts matures and prepayment benefits normalize. Commercial Development return on capital employed collapsed to 1.3% in Q2 2026 while simultaneously completed unsold commercial projects totaled SEK 18.5 billion, creating an unexplained capital deployment anomaly where management maintains massive SEK 40.4 billion in Commercial Development capital employed despite returns falling more than 87% below the stated 10% target.

This represents a severe operational oddity in capital allocation discipline, particularly given Skanska develops properties with equity, avoiding forced liquidations during market downturns, suggesting the group is deliberately warehousing lowyielding assets rather than executing capital recycling strategies that would align with stated return on equity targets of 18%.

- Earnings Calls Construction operating margins reached 4.3% overall with USA Construction delivering 4.6%, representing a statistically significant outlier when benchmarked against the typical historical European building contractor baseline of 2% to 3%, yet rolling 12-month return on equity stood at 10.8%, missing the 18% long-term target by a substantial margin.

This creates a fundamental paradox where best-in-class Construction profitability fails to translate into competitive group-level returns, revealing that the SEK 63.4 billion property-heavy capital structure effectively dilutes what would otherwise be sector-leading financial performance and highlighting an unexplained strategic persistence with capital-intensive development models that structurally depress consolidated returns.
