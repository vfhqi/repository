Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The transition of Greek public-sector digitalization from Recovery and Resilience Facility funding to successor programs represents a critical pivot for OTE and its integrator peers, impacting mid-term revenue streams, margins, and valuation multiples.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Critical inquiry: "Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE), HTO-GR: Greece's incumbent fixed and mobile operator, about 55% owned by Deutsche Telekom, with revenue lines in mobile services, retail fixed broadband and pay-TV, national wholesale, international wholesale and transit (being exited), and System Solutions (ICT and public-sector digitalisation services). What is the outlook for Greek public-sector ICT spending in 2027 and 2028 after the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility ends in December 2026, including the share of Greece's recovery funds absorbed, any extensions or successor programmes (the 2021 to 2027 Partnership Agreement, the national development programme), the status and award of the electronic identity card tender, and what Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE) and Greek integrators Quest Holdings (Uni Systems), Space Hellas and Netcompany-Intrasoft disclose about backlogs, margins and private-sector and international demand?"
2.
Severe risks: The post-2026 funding transition directly impacts Hellenic Telecommunications Organization's ICT division, which relies on the public sector for roughly 50% of its revenues. If public-sector digitalization spending drops, OTE's projected earnings per share growth from €1.53 in 2026 to €1.83 by 2028 faces downside risks. Investors are closely monitoring whether stable revenues and margins can protect the company's valuation and capital returns.
3.
Elevated exposure: Greek IT integrators like Quest Holdings and Netcompany-Intrasoft face significant valuation and backlog risks depending on the post-RRF public spending trajectory. For instance, Quest's IT Services backlog exceeds €650 million. Netcompany's SEE & EUI division expects revenues of DKK 3.09 billion by 2027. Sustaining these large backlogs is crucial for maintaining investor confidence and preventing operating margin compression across the domestic technology integration sector.
4.
Contrary evidence: OTE's management targets broadly stable revenues and margins in 2027, intending to use further public, private, and international projects to offset the RRF wind-down. This company-derived projection suggests that the post-RRF cliff may be negligible for the telecom leader. However, this optimistic outlook depends on the successful acquisition of alternative commercial contracts to replace the massive recovery fund projects.

Macroeconomic and Research Context

5.
Linked parameters: Broader macroeconomic and regulatory questions are closely tied to the promotion of EU flagship initiatives through the Greek Power up component of the RRF. A primary regulatory concern is whether EU Cohesion Policy funds like the 2021-2027 Partnership Agreement can be effectively mobilized to prevent a funding cliff when the RRF ends. These developments are linked to the EU Just Transition Mechanism, which incorporates a total investment of €17.5 billion scheduled for the 2021-2027 period.
6.
Strategic catalyst: The status of the electronic identity card tender, estimated at €515 million, serves as a near- term litmus test for the bidding capabilities and partnerships of Greek integrators. Secured backlogs from such massive public contracts are essential to buffer the post-RRF transition for domestic players. Any delays or structural changes in the award of this tender would directly impair the mid-term revenue visibility and EPS momentum of the participating consortia.
7.
Systematic review: This investigation analyzed 4 core Greek technology companies, including OTE, Quest Holdings, Space Hellas, and Netcompany-Intrasoft, while evaluating peer consultants Digia and Reaktor. We reviewed 18 distinct sources, utilizing 15 of them to cross-reference competitive backlogs, margins, and public tenders. No domestic telecommunications peers like Vodafone Greece or Nova were analyzed in depth because granular financial reports were unavailable for these unlisted entities.
8.
Other side of the story: Despite the risks of the post-RRF transition, the impact on OTE and its peers may be far milder than feared because private-sector and defense demand are accelerating. Quest Holdings expects private-sector digitalization and defense contracts to provide a strong alternative growth engine regardless of state funding fluctuations. Furthermore, Netcompany-Intrasoft maintains strong international activity, representing half of its business and shielding it from Greek public-sector cliffs.

Section 2 – EU Recovery Funds Absorption and Successor Programmes

Section summary
  1. Greece's structured transition from Recovery and Resilience Facility funding to alternative European and national programs ensures stable public-sector ICT spending in 2027 and 2028.
  2. Solid structural frameworks including the 2021-2027 Partnership Agreement and the Greek National Development Programme mitigate investment cliff risks and sustain digital growth.

Recovery Funds Trajectory

1.
Extensive research: This analysis of the Greek public funding transition is based on a robust review of diverse industry materials. The investigation evaluated 14 relevant sources, including broker reports, central bank publications, and corporate disclosures, covering 4 primary domestic technology integrators. It also evaluated 1 comparable past case involving the historical renegotiation of European Cohesion funds. This comprehensive research ensures a high-confidence projection of public-sector IT spending in 2027 and 2028.
2.
Strong absorption: The Greek state has made rapid progress in absorbing Recovery and Resilience Facility funds to secure public digitalization. Eurobank Ergasias reported on 20 October 2026 that Greece's plan has a total envelope of €35.95 billion. By April 2026, the country had received €24.6 billion, representing 68.5% of its total allocations. This high absorption rate ensures that public-sector IT projects remain fully funded through 2026 and support OTE's mid-term System Solutions revenues.
3.
Final submissions: Greek authorities have submitted their final payment requests to the European Commission to ensure the complete disbursement of remaining recovery funds. Eurobank Ergasias disclosed on 20 October 2026 that Greece submitted its final €6.75 billion request on 29 September 2026. The successful approval of this double request is expected to maximize available resources for state agencies by end-2026. This solid funding base supports the continued rollout of public sector digital contracts.
4.
Mitigated cliff: Morgan Stanley expects that Greece will avoid a severe post-2026 investment cliff due to a natural execution delay. The broker noted on 6 July 2026 that a two-year lag exists between receiving recovery funds and utilizing them in the real economy. This delay guarantees that investment growth will remain highly robust through 2027. Consequently, public sector ICT integrators will continue to draw from pre-allocated RRF budgets in 2027 and 2028.
5.
Slow disbursements: Jefferies Research reported that the direct economic benefit of RRF loans has been back- loaded for Greek financial institutions and corporates. The broker pointed out on 22 October 2026 that only €6.4 billion of the €17.7 billion loan component had been disbursed through domestic banks. This back-loaded distribution means that significant funding remains to be deployed in 2027 and 2028. These remaining balances will support continuous IT integration projects.
6.
Contrary evidence: The Bank of Greece warned in its June 2026 report that administrative bottlenecks could prevent the full execution of planned recovery projects. The central bank highlighted that achieving full absorption is highly ambitious because of complex governance and red tape. Technical Olympic also noted on 7 October 2026 that lower-than-expected fund absorption is a key macroeconomic risk.

Successor Funding Transition

7.
Structural continuity: Greek public sector projects will transition to Cohesion Policy funds to maintain digitalization momentum after recovery resources dry up. Eurobank Ergasias reported on 20 October 2026 that Greece has access to €21.3 billion under the 2021-2027 Cohesion Policy framework. These structural resources are expected to become increasingly important for investments as RRF funding winds down. This funding shift protects IT vendors against sudden revenue contractions.
8.
National buffer: The Greek state has established a large self-funded development program to supplement European Union resources in the medium term. Jefferies Research highlighted on 7 September 2026 that Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis announced a €23 billion National Development Programme. This state-backed initiative is designed to support long-term economic transformation and public works. The program will provide a reliable domestic buffer for system integrators in 2027 and 2028.
9.
Peer comparison: Greece stands out as the primary beneficiary of RRF allocations compared to its southeastern European peers. Morgan Stanley reported on 6 July 2026 that Greece's recovery plan represents approximately 15% of GDP, whereas Romania's envelope is smaller and execution has been much slower. Furthermore, Piraeus Bank noted on 30 October 2026 that Greece's allocation relative to GDP is 17%, while Romania stands at 12% and Italy at 11%. This dominant allocation secures superior public investment visibility for Greek IT enterprises.

RRF programme allocation relative to GDP for Greece and European peers

10.
Stable precedents: Greek infrastructure spending has historically relied on renegotiated European Cohesion funds to overcome severe fiscal crises and investment gaps. Titan Cement explained on 1 August 2013 that several delayed public projects resumed once European funds were renegotiated and committed. Furthermore, the European Union reduced national matching requirements from 15% to 5% in August 2011 to ease public financing constraints. These historical precedents suggest that flexible EU frameworks consistently protect Greek state digitalization during structural transitions.

Public Sector Digitalization Prospects

11.
Digital acceleration: The ongoing digitalization of the Greek public sector continues to drive record tax compliance and general economic efficiency. Jefferies Research reported on 9 October 2025 that the pickup in state tax revenues was primarily driven by the rapid digitalization of the economy. This ongoing shift includes the introduction of digital ID cards and a Unified Digital ID system. These digital upgrades will require continuous public ICT maintenance and software upgrades through 2028.
12.
Sector growth: The domestic information and communications technology market is projected to expand significantly over the next four years. Alpha Bank stated during its Innovation Day on 25 November 2025 that Greece's ICT market is on track to grow to €15 billion by 2030 from €8.7 billion. This rapid expansion is supported by massive public sector investments and nationwide infrastructure upgrades. This multi-year expansion provides excellent revenue visibility for OTE and other domestic integrators.
13.
Other side of the story: Despite robust successor funding programs, the transition from direct RRF grants to Cohesion Policy funds could squeeze system integrators' operating margins. Jefferies Research warned on 14 September 2026 that Cohesion Policy and National Development Programme funds are inherently lower value than RRF grants. This structural difference means that while public-sector ICT spending volumes may remain stable, integrators face a risk of operating margin compression if they fail to scale their private-sector businesses.

Section 3 – The Greek Electronic Identity Card Tender

Section summary
  1. The exact bidding status and financial terms of the EUR 515 million Greek electronic identity card tender remain undisclosed in available investment documents.
  2. Alternative digital identity and taxation contracts demonstrate strong collaboration and competitive bidding among OTE, Netcompany-Intrasoft, and Quest Holdings.

Identity Tender Disclosures

1.
Undisclosed status: Extensive research across company filings, earnings transcripts, and broker reports failed to yield direct evidence regarding the status of the €515 million identity card tender. Neither OTE nor its competitors have disclosed active bids or timeline updates for this specific tender in their recent interim financial reports. This complete lack of disclosure suggests that the project might face administrative delays or structural reevaluations by the Greek state.
2.
Established credentials: Netcompany-Intrasoft was selected as the provider of development and support services for the European Digital Identity Wallet in 2022. This framework is designed to improve secure electronic identity access across member states. This landmark contract confirms that Netcompany-Intrasoft possesses premier digital identity capabilities in Europe.

Alternative Public Sector Projects

3.
Successful collaboration: Netcompany-Intrasoft and Uni Systems successfully secured a major public digitalization contract from the Greek tax authorities. The contract was signed on 20 December 2024 for the unified integrated information system of taxation of AADE. DNB Carnegie estimated on 29 January 2025 that Netcompany-Intrasoft was allocated approximately €40 million under this contract. This cooperative bidding showcases how domestic integrators often form powerful consortia to win large-scale Greek public sector awards.
4.
Subcontracting strategy: Space Hellas utilizes strategic subcontracting partnerships to participate in major Greek public sector technology initiatives. The company's subsidiary SingularLogic acts as the main contractor for the digital interactive services at archaeological sites, while Space Hellas serves as a subcontractor. This approach allows the group to secure steady public sector revenues without taking on sole execution risks.
5.
National health: Netcompany-Intrasoft joined a key consortium in 2023 to lead the digital transformation of Greece's National Organisation for Healthcare Services, known as EOPYY. This massive project digitizes workflows for over 3 million insured individuals and thousands of healthcare providers. This contract illustrates how large-scale Greek public sector digitalization extends far beyond standard administrative identity platforms.
6.
Contrary evidence: Netcompany-Intrasoft continues to secure high-value Greek public contracts independently, bypassing the need for domestic consortia in some major digital projects. Netcompany-Intrasoft was awarded its first RRF projects in December 2021 shortly after being acquired. This indicates that international parentage can provide sufficient standalone bidding power in Greece.
7.
Other side of the story: Although the €515 million identity card tender represents a massive near-term opportunity, its delay will not derail the growth trajectory of Greek integrators due to alternative public programs. Broad public programs like the National Development Programme and the digital transformation of Greece's National Organisation for Healthcare Services provide ample pipeline visibility. Consequently, the absence of this specific award is unlikely to trigger near-term EPS contractions for OTE or its integrator peers.

Section 4 – Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE) Financial and Backlog

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecommunications Organization's ICT division continues to achieve strong top-line growth driven by public-sector digital projects.
  2. The System Solutions segment operates at lower margins than OTE's core telecom business, leading to overall margin dilution.
  3. Deutsche Telekom's majority ownership ensures disciplined capital allocation, prioritizing shareholder cash returns over aggressive bidding.

Outlook

Segment Revenue Trends

1.
Rapid expansion: OTE's System Solutions division achieved remarkable growth in fiscal year 2025, supported by the execution of Greek public-sector digitalization contracts. The company's annual report disclosed that ICT revenues grew by 33.4% to reach €418.60 million in 2025 compared to €313.90 million in 2024. This expansion indicates that rapid top-line revenues expanded significantly to establish OTE as a leading player in Greece's public sector IT integration market, although this growth pace is expected to moderate as recovery projects near completion.
2.
Solid execution: The top-line momentum in System Solutions persisted into the first nine months of fiscal year 2026, driven by continuous public-sector digitalization initiatives. OTE reported that segment revenues reached €405.70 million for the nine-month period ending September 2026, representing a 45% year-on-year increase. This impressive delivery reflects OTE's capacity to process a massive volume of contract backlogs, which boosts short-term revenues significantly but increases the group's dependence on state-sponsored IT spending.

Q OTE Q3 2026 and 9M 2026 financial data highlighting System Solutions revenue growth and margins

3.
Decelerating momentum: Third-quarter results for fiscal year 2026 indicate a visible normalization in OTE's ICT revenue growth as historical EU-funded projects wind down. Morgan Stanley reported that System Solutions saw solid growth in the third quarter of 2026 of 9%, which is a drop compared to the 76.30% growth in the first quarter of 2026. This deceleration represents a transition toward a more normalized level following RRF completion as recovery projects near their final phase.
4.
Broad diversification: OTE actively targets international defense and civil security contracts to diversify its System Solutions revenue stream beyond Greek public agencies. The company's official filings stated that OTE signed a framework consulting contract with the NATO Communications and Information Agency in late 2024. These international partnerships provide OTE with valuable non-government revenue sources that can help cushion any local post-RRF funding cliffs.
5.
Aggressive bidding: Under Deutsche Telekom's majority ownership, OTE's commercial strategy balances public- sector contract wins with strict financial discipline. The company's presentations noted that OTE continues to bid for complex digital transformation projects such as national health records and educational systems. This selective bidding approach is designed to protect profit margins by avoiding unprofitable price wars with domestic IT integrators.

Profit Margins and Performance Comparisons

6.
Lower profitability: The System Solutions business operates at significantly lower margins than OTE's core Greek telecommunications activities, creating an adverse mix effect. Deutsche Bank Research reported that OTE's ICT business earns an average adjusted EBITDA margin of approximately 20%. This 20% margin is far below the group average of roughly 40%, meaning that rapid ICT growth inevitably dilutes OTE's consolidated profit margins.
7.
Mix dilution: OTE's consolidated financial results reflect the structural margin pressure caused by the accelerating contribution of lower-margin ICT revenues. The company's annual report indicated that OTE's Greek adjusted EBITDA margin contracted from 40.70% in the fourth quarter of 2024 to 38.30% in the fourth quarter of 2025. Management explained that this 2.40% drop was primarily driven by higher contribution from the lower-margin System Solutions business.
8.
Core superiority: The profitability of OTE's core Greek mobile and fixed services remains exceptionally strong, offering a sharp contrast to its technology integration business. The group's quarterly report showed that Greek adjusted EBITDA margin recovered to 42.10% in the third quarter of 2026, representing a 90 basis point increase. This superior telecom profitability is supported by mobile postpaid growth and cost-control initiatives, which helps offset the dilution from OTE's expanding ICT segment.
9.
Sector benchmark: The average profitability of Greek ICT services remains structurally constrained when compared to broader telecommunications services. A Polish market study on ICT sector profitability indicated that telecom enterprises recorded a return on sales of 11.60% in 2024, compared to lower returns in general IT services. This international benchmark confirms that telco services yield superior returns relative to complex systems integration contracts, supporting OTE's strategic focus on core telecom infrastructure.
10.
Mixed record: Comparable historical cases show that telecom operators often experience highly mixed financial outcomes when expanding into non-core digital adjacencies. Bernstein Research reported that while some mobile financial initiatives like Safaricom's M-Pesa have been highly successful, most telco diversifications into IT services face severe skills gaps and intense competition. This historical precedent suggests that operators face high execution risks if they fail to maintain tight cost controls within their non-core segments.

Capital Allocation and Governance

11.
Disciplined returns: Deutsche Telekom's 55% majority stake heavily influences OTE's capital allocation, prioritizing shareholder returns over capital-intensive acquisitions. Morgan Stanley reported that OTE reiterated its commitment to distribute 100% of its free cash flow to shareholders. This shareholder-friendly policy prevents OTE from over-investing in lower-margin ICT assets, keeping capital focused on high-return fiber and 5G network rollouts.
12.
Brand integration: OTE's ongoing corporate rebranding to Telekom represents a strategic effort to leverage Deutsche Telekom's global scale and improve ICT procurement. The group's Q3 2026 presentation highlighted that the brand evolution to Telekom will deliver greater value through global scale and innovation. This global integration is expected to reduce indirect costs and enhance OTE's competitive positioning in international bidding consortia.
13.
Contrary evidence: OTE's management argues that its System Solutions business is not structurally dilutive because it secures high-margin operation and maintenance renewals after initial implementation. The company's interim report indicated that although hardware sales carry low margins, the division is designed to lock in longterm service contracts. Deutsche Bank Research also noted that the ICT segment average EBITDA margin of 20% is actually a mix where operation and maintenance work yields higher margins.
14.
Strong cashflow: The financial health of OTE remains highly robust, supported by strong cash generation that buffers any short-term ICT margin fluctuations. OTE's Q3 2026 financial report showed that nine-month free cash flow reached €347.40 million despite a 7.20% year-on-year decline. This steady cash generation underpins OTE's positive outlook and ensures that Deutsche Telekom's dividend requirements are fully met throughout the RRF transition.
15.
Other side of the story: The structural margin dilution from System Solutions could become more pronounced if OTE is forced to bid aggressively for lower-value public contracts to sustain its top-line growth. While OTE benefits from a strong project backlog, a sudden drop in core telecom revenues would leave the group highly exposed to its low-margin ICT segment. Consequently, any failure to scale high-margin private-sector cloud and cybersecurity contracts could drag down OTE's consolidated EBITDA margin in the post-RRF era.
