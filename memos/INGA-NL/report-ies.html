ING Groep (INGA-NL): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results modestly strengthen the earnings half of our case and leave the price half, which is the half that parks it, exactly where it was.

1. What changed

The case set one test for this print: a liability margin at or above the case's 1.05 percent, and confirmation of the German deposit defence. The margin limb passed with room. The German limb is only half evidenced.

  • Liability margin 113 basis points in Q3 2026, up six basis points in the quarter, with 2026 guidance lifted to about 110 basis points from 100 to 110. The case's Alarm is 1.05 percent and its Invalidate 1.00 percent.
  • Replicating portfolio revenue guidance raised to €9.9 billion for 2026 and €13.6 billion for 2027 (from €11.9 billion), and to €15.9 billion for 2028 (from €13.3 billion).
  • Commercial net interest income €4,335 million, up 13.39 percent year on year and 3.86 percent on Q2 2026. Fee income €1,273 million, up 9.27 percent year on year and flat on the quarter.
  • The bottom line was softer than the top. Total income €6,227 million, down 0.91 percent on Q2. Net profit €1,850 million, down 4.98 percent on Q2 and about 6 percent short of expectations on one broker's count. Return on tangible equity 16.3 percent against 17.0 percent in Q2. All other income fell 32.02 percent year on year to €619 million. Loan loss provisions were €403 million, including a €111 million overlay ahead of a model update in Wholesale project finance. A €175 million restructuring provision follows in Q4 2026.
  • Capital beat. CET1 13.3 percent against 13.1 percent in Q2 and a 13.1 percent consensus; risk-weighted assets down €4.3 billion to €337.7 billion, €3.0 billion of it from two risk-transfer transactions. The distribution is €1.9 billion (€1.65 billion buyback, €250 million dividend) against about €1.5 billion expected.
  • Deposits barely grew. Retail net core deposits rose €2.7 billion against €16.7 billion in Q2; management attributes it to summer spending. No German deposit balance is given. Since the quarter closed ING Germany has run a 3.75 percent campaign rising to 4 percent with a current account, a 25 basis point increase in the promotional rate, and Rabobank has raised its core Dutch savings rate to 1.6 percent.
  • Sell-side response is small and split. One house expects 2027 consensus earnings per share to rise 1 to 2 percent; one modestly raised 2027 and 2028; one cut its earnings per share 2 percent on other income and restructuring. Targets held at €38, €37, €35 and €31. The broker titles are "noisy miss", "messy quarter", "soft quarter" and "mixed quarter versus high expectations".

Two cautions on the evidence. The 2027 targets presented as upgrades, return on tangible equity above 16 percent and income above €26 billion, are ones the case already carried from Q2. And a €1.7 billion increase in 2027 replicating revenue producing only a 1 to 2 percent rise in consensus earnings says most of it was already modelled or is being given back in deposit costs; we cannot tell which.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the margin was bought with volume, and the bill arrives in Q4. The case reads a rising margin as a franchise defending deposits below top-of-market rates. The same numbers admit the opposite: ING held its rates, the margin widened to 113 basis points, and retail inflows fell by more than four-fifths. The response came after the quarter end, in a higher German promotional rate. On this reading 113 basis points is the peak, not the run-rate. It also exposes a gap in the case's own trigger: the case watches the standard German savings rate, but the fight is being conducted in promotional rates and current-account bundles, so funding cost can rise without the trigger ever firing. For this to be right, German deposits were flat or down in Q3, the campaign balances are material, and the Q4 margin prints below 113 basis points. One house already assumes deposit pass-through rising to 46 percent from 40 percent in 2027. We cannot knock this down: the seasonal explanation is plausible, but the one number that would separate the two, German deposits, is absent.

Reading two: the tailwind is larger and later than the case assumes, so the fade the case leans on is mis-dated. The case parks because the hedge roll plateaus in 2028 and the multiple should therefore fall to the case's modal 8.6x. A 2028 replicating guide raised by €2.6 billion, still stepping up €2.3 billion on 2027, pushes the plateau out and lifts the level it plateaus at. If that holds, the case's FY2028 earnings of about 3.35 EUR and its deceleration to about 9 percent in FY2029 are both low, and the case's 40 percent odds on the held 9.4x branch are too mean. For this to be right, swap rates must stay near current levels and deposit costs must not absorb the gain. The muted consensus response is the evidence against it. It is plausible, not probable.

Reading three: capital is no longer bounded by earnings. The case, and its counter-case more sharply, treat CET1 headroom of 10 basis points as a binding limit on the buyback. Risk transfers released capital faster than lending consumed it, and one house notes ING has taken 28 basis points of such relief against peer averages near 100. If repeatable, the case's 3.3 to 3.6 billion EUR a year buyback is conservative and the share-count leg is larger. Against it: a rule change in January 2027 is expected to cost about 20 basis points of CET1.

A reading we considered and do not put up: that the miss shows the tailwind failing to reach earnings. The overlay, the other-income swing and the restructuring are dated and one-off in character, and the case's drop-through sensitivity already holds higher risk cost and tax. One quarter does not make it a pattern.

3. What it does to the case

  • 'Change Forces' and 'Financial Outputs' : less pressure. The external force is bigger and the margin is above the case's own modal. Upgrades remain "likely but small", as the case said.
  • 'Modal return' and Building Block 1 : the same. A point or two on FY2028 earnings moves the return by tenths, not grades.
  • Foundations, Building Block 2 : pulled both ways. The margin evidence eases it; the deposit stall and the higher German promotional rate press on exactly the fragility 'Value chain, industry' names. Net, the same, with the first reading unresolved.
  • Case Riskiness : slightly less pressure on capital headroom; the rate-and-multiple exposure is untouched.
  • Sell-side Momentum , Technical Momentum , valuation : the pinned letters are unchanged. The store's TSR/Valuation reads F, which is the re-basing the case expected and not an effect of these results.
  • The price triggers: not met. At 30.125 EUR (13 November 2026) the shares sit below their 50-day average and above every level the case states: about 28 EUR for a D return, 25 to 27 EUR to progress, about 25 EUR to clear the bar. The drop trigger has not fired either.

The recommendation's logic is intact: a good company at a price the case declines.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It carries the broker estimate and target changes that have landed since, so it settles whether consensus FY2027 and FY2028 earnings rose by the 1 to 2 percent one house expects or whether the cuts offset it. That is the test of reading two. It carries no new company disclosure, so it cannot settle reading one.
  • Full-year results, which the case dates to late January 2027. The Q4 liability margin after the German campaign, Q4 retail deposit flows and the first 2027 guidance under the new finance director. This is the first real test of reading one.
  • Capital steps: the Dutch mortgage risk-weight floor expiry, which the case dates to 30 November 2026, and the January 2027 trading-book rule. Together they show whether reading three survives.
  • The Capital Markets Day on 14 June 2027 for targets beyond 2027.
  • What nothing is committed to answer: German deposit balances and deposit pass-through by country. ING has not undertaken to disclose them. Until it does, the German half of the case's test rests on inference from the group margin and from published savings rates.

VERDICT

  • Direction: positive
  • Grade: minor
  • Moves: liability-margin condition of the case's progress trigger met (113 basis points against the case's 1.05 percent); no price threshold crossed at 30.125 EUR; no rating moved
  • Reason: The earnings leg is firmer on a higher margin and a larger hedge-roll guide, but the case is parked on price and return, neither of which moved, so no rating would change a full grade and the net is a small positive with an unresolved margin-for-volume question.
