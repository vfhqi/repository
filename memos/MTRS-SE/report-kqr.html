Severe constraints: Massive utility interconnection delays represent a major hurdle that could defer projected data center deliveries into future periods. Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that data center backlash and power limitations have blocked or delayed 74 projects representing USD 130bn in combined investment. Easing high-end compute constraints is essential to support the next leg of capacity growth. If utility interconnection delays persist, equipment suppliers will face deferred shipment schedules.
6.
Rising delays: Stranded data center capacity metrics provide an early warning sign that hyperscale buildouts are hitting a physical delivery ceiling. Bernstein Research disclosed in November 2026 that global stranded data center capacity has risen to a record 61 GW. This stranded capacity represents cancelled, delayed, or unapproved projects that cannot access local power grids. These localized utility bottlenecks threaten to stall the unprecedented capital expenditure expansion of major operators.
7.
Extended lead times: Sourcing electrical equipment remains a critical pacing challenge for hyperscale operators trying to commission new facilities. An AWS senior engagement manager explained in March 2026 that lead times for transformers stretch to four years. Sourcing switchgear and generators takes up to 18 months, while specialty chillers require 24 months. These component shortages prevent operators from converting massive backlogs into immediate cooling sales.

Volatile inputs: Rising raw material prices and tariff compliance costs threaten to squeeze margins across the entire thermal management sector. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that copper prices have risen 10.5% year-to-date to spot levels of Rmb 109,820 per ton. Volatile metal costs have a significant impact on industrial margin profiles due to several-month price-transmission delays. Unfavorable commodity moves will suppress margins unless manufacturers successfully implement aggressive price pass-through mechanisms.
