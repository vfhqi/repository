  2. Active capital reallocation has shifted the geographic mix toward faster-growing southern European regions
  3. High-indexed lease structures ensure predictable rental income while protecting against macro volatility
  4. Non-rental ancillary revenue streams provide growing high-margin income and offset softer indexation backdrops
  5. Disciplined capital recycling strategies refocus the portfolio on high-performing prime assets Net read: Leadership transitions in early 2026 maintain strategic continuity and a highly conservative balance sheet

Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. European retail real estate is experiencing structural supply-side tightening due to strict zoning laws and construction cost inflation
  2. Market concentration remains highly polarized around prime
  3. Competitors have pivoted from greenfield expansion to asset transformation to protect marginal development returns

Retail market scale and growth

1.
Modest consumption recovery: European real retail sales are forecast to expand by 1.3% in 2026, led by CEE and the Nordics.
2.
Improving growth outlook: Projections indicate retail sales will accelerate to 1.7% in 2027, supporting broad recovery across softer markets.
3.
Resilient prime rents: Prime European shopping centre rents increased at a 2% CAGR over the past three years driven by intense retailer competition.
4.
Low retail density: CEE shopping centre density remains highly supportive of growth at 139 sqm in Romania versus 355 sqm EU average.
5.
Average French density: Shopping centre density in France stands at 290 sqm per 1,000 inhabitants, matching the broader European average of 260 sqm.
6.
Strong Scandinavian demand: Climate-driven consumer habits sustain shopping centres at over 50% of total retail sales across all major Nordic markets.

Market concentration and competitor tiers

7.
Consolidated leader tier: Unibail-Rodamco-Westfield holds the largest listed position with a €49 billion commercial real estate portfolio concentrated in prime urban flagships.
8.
Dominant pure player: Klepierre commands leading continental scale with a €20.6 billion retail portfolio, targeted primarily toward high sales density assets.
9.
Focused grocer-anchored peer: Carmila operates a €6.8 billion portfolio of shopping centres, leveraging strong co-ownership linkages with Carrefour.
10.
Regional niche specialist: NEPI Rockcastle dominates Eastern Europe with an €8.4 billion retail portfolio, capturing rapidly rising middle-class consumer spending.
11.
Scale-driven efficiency gains: Elite shopping centre platforms leverage operational scale to drive down overhead cost ratios, leaving smaller standalone properties severely disadvantaged.

Regulatory barriers and development conduct

12.
Strict development limits: France's Climate and Resilience Act curbs new out-of-town retail space to protect existing catchment centers.
13.
Stringent environmental barriers: Belgium's Flanders and Wallonia divisions enforce strict new environmental permits, severely restricting out-of-town greenfield projects.
14.
Severe Central European blocks: Hungary's tight Plaza Stop regulations impede new retail constructions, forcing developers to focus entirely on refurbishment.
15.
Restrained supply addition: Active shopping centre development is highly constrained, with pipeline projects expanding total European floorspace by a mere 0.6%
16.
Softened indexation backdrops: French shopping centre rent indexation has moderated, with ILC index turning barely positive at 0.3% in second-quarter 2026.
17.
Other side of the story: Persistent e-commerce growth threatens long-term physical tenant sales; a structural decline in footfall would trigger defaults and compress prime shopping centre valuations.
