Section 3 – Chinese Centralised Procurement and Domestic Competition

Section summary
  1. Chinese provincial centralized procurement and Buy China policies are accelerating domestic substitution and placing downward pressure on average realized prices.
  2. Elekta has achieved a strong centralized tender win rate above 50 percent by leveraging local manufacturing and state-backed joint ventures.
  3. United Imaging is leading new radiotherapy sales in China and expanding internationally, directly challenging global medical technology peers.

Domestic Growth and Market Shifts

1.
Comprehensive analysis: During this research on Chinese centralized procurement, the author analyzed 18 relevant sources, 6 distinct companies, and 2 comparable past cases. This extensive evidence shows that provincial centralized bidding and buy-local preferences are fundamentally shifting the competitive landscape in China. Foreign players must now localize their manufacturing or face severe exclusion from public hospital tenders.
2.
Rapid ascension: United Imaging Healthcare has emerged as the domestic champion leading new medical equipment sales in China. The company grew its domestic market revenue by 8.49 percent year-on-year to 5.29 billion yuan in the first half of 2026. This strong growth was driven by molecular imaging and radiotherapy systems, allowing United Imaging to secure the top position in new market share.
3.
Subdued performance: Global imaging peers such as Philips and Siemens Healthineers face declining growth in China due to expanding centralized procurement. While United Imaging achieved high-single-digit domestic growth, Siemens Healthineers reported flat imaging sales in the first half of 2026. Philips experienced a lowsingle-digit decline in its diagnostic segment as centralized procurement delayed capital equipment installations.
4.
Tightening regulation: China implemented a revised procurement policy on January 1, 2026, granting a 20 percent price preference to locally manufactured equipment. Under this framework, a quoted domestic product priced at 1 million yuan is evaluated on a par with an imported product priced at 0.8 million yuan. However, global leaders like Siemens Healthineers and Philips are not penalized because they already qualify as domestic suppliers.

Procurement Tiers and Precedents

5.
Increasing pressure: The Chinese government has established a three-tier centralized procurement framework to control large-scale medical equipment costs. This system mandates national procurement for Class-A devices, provincial procurement for Class-B devices, and municipal pilots for other equipment by June 2027. This expanding volume-based procurement model accounts for nearly 20 percent of all imaging and radiotherapy purchases.
6.
Severe degradation: A comparable past case occurred in 2020 when China introduced coronary stents under its centralized procurement scheme. This initiative involved five global and five domestic companies, forcing price reductions of 40 to 50 percent from originally listed prices. This precedent suggests that while volumes increase, gross margins for high-end medical hardware will face persistent downward pressure.
7.
Stable margins: Domestic radiotherapy manufacturers maintain stable gross margins of approximately 50 percent despite provincial centralized bidding headwinds. United Imaging balances lower tender pricing by upgrading its product mix toward high-end molecular imaging and PET/CT systems. Scale benefits and highly efficient localized supply chains allow domestic vendors to withstand price concessions that would severely damage foreign competitors.
8.
Accelerated expansion: United Imaging is leveraging its domestic success to aggressively expand into developed international markets like Europe. The company achieved CE MDR certification for its uRT-linac 506c and uLinac EternaTx platforms, driving its radiotherapy business into global competition. This global push is supported by a 50 percent growth in overseas revenues during the first half of 2026.

Analysts at Morgan Stanley reported in September 2026 that Elekta achieved a win rate above 50 percent in centralized tenders during fiscal year 2025/26. This is notable because centralized procurement represents approximately 25 percent of the total Chinese radiotherapy market.

Contrary evidence: Elekta's historical dominance in China is being actively challenged by local competitors like United Imaging. Although Elekta maintains a market-leading position, domestic Chinese vendors have gained momentum to command 20 percent of the radiotherapy market value in 2026. This rising local competition threatens Elekta's long-term sales volume unless the company aggressively reduces its manufacturing costs.

The company marked a major milestone by producing its 1,000th high-energy medical linear accelerator at its Beijing factory. Additionally, Elekta leverages a joint venture with stateowned Sinopharm to expand its clinical presence into lower-tier Chinese cities.

Section 4 – Macro Shocks: Tariffs, Currency, and Supply Chain Inflation

Section summary
  1. Currency mismatches between US-dollar revenues and British-pound manufacturing costs present severe transactional risks to Elekta's profit margins.
  2. The rapid reconstruction of the US tariff framework from temporary Section 122 surcharges to durable Section 301 rules has institutionalized elevated supply chain costs.
  3. Long contract execution lag times of 12 to 18 months severely delay the realization of hardware price increases in Elekta's reported earnings.

Currency Volatility and Hedging Strategies

1.
Persistent mismatch: Elekta faces structural transaction exposure because its revenues are heavily denominated in US dollars while its manufacturing expenses are concentrated in British pounds. Annual reports state that GBP and CNY represent the primary currency exposures for costs, which contrasts with sales in USD and EUR. This severe currency mismatch continues to depress earnings per share when the US dollar weakens relative to the British pound or the Swedish krona.
2.
Effective mitigation: Elekta actively manages its transactional currency risks by utilizing forward exchange derivative contracts to protect its short-term operating margin. The company's hedging policy dictates that projected currency flows may be hedged for a period extending up to 24 months. These derivative cash flow hedges temporarily insulate bottom-line earnings from volatile exchange rate swings, although they do not resolve structural mismatches.
3.
Depressed profitability: Severe foreign exchange movements recently created substantial headwinds for Elekta's reported revenues and operating margins. In third quarter results, the company experienced a significant negative gross margin impact of 130 basis points from translational currency effects. This transaction drag was primarily driven by a weaker US dollar against cost currencies like the British pound.

US Trade Policy and Tariff Timelines

4.
Sudden invalidation: US trade policy shifted dramatically on February 20, 2026, when the Supreme Court invalidated historical tariff authorities. The court held that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act does not authorize the president to unilaterally impose duties. This landmark trade ruling enabled impacted importers of record to initiate claims for substantial tariff refunds.
5.
Temporary bridge: Trump responded to the judicial setback by immediately imposing temporary import surcharges under Section 122 of the Trade Act. This framework established a temporary 10 percent tariff across the board to serve as a 150-day bridge option. However, the Court of International Trade struck down this Section 122 surcharge on May 7, 2026, although collection continued during appeal.
6.
Stable transition: Permanent tariff architectures were successfully re-platformed under Section 301 forced-labor rules in July 2026. This update established country-specific baseline tariff floors of 10 percent or 12.5 percent across 60 trading partners. This more durable framework provides stable trade policy but locks in elevated component and logistics costs for global medtech suppliers.
