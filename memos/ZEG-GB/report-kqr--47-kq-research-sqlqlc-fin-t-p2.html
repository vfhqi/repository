EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. Evidence suggests Vodafone Spain's pricing power is constrained by Digi's aggressive growth, making the January 2027 repricing round a high-risk event for blended ARPU. Market share leader Digi Spain continues to weigh on ARPU trends for larger operators as it focuses on gaining share through its 2Gbps broadband offering at a stable €20 per month price point. Incumbent operators like Telefónica are reacting to low-end pressure by launching €5 per month mobile-only offers under sub-brands like O2 Spain to compete directly with Digi and Lowi. In the German market, Deutsche Telekom successfully implemented a €2 per month broadband price rise in April 2026 with significantly lower churn than anticipated, providing a positive precedent for inflationary adjustments. Vodafone Germany has similarly improved its ARPU trajectory by reducing upfront sign-on discounts and promotions, achieving stable service revenue growth of 1.2% in Q2 2026.

Zegona management remains focused on EFCF growth potential, with a projected 15% CAGR from 2027 to 2030 supported by mobile network savings and a potential cash returns policy. Digital services are a bright spot for B2B pricing, with Telefónica Spain reporting that over half of its domestic B2B revenues now stem from double-digit growth in digital segments. The Spanish regulator CNMC has maintained a focus on Cellnex's significant market power, though the tower operator has proposed five years of price stability with inflation-only adjustments. High energy costs remain a structural threat to margins, as European large-consumer electricity prices in 2H25 were significantly higher than US peers, impacting the cost base for network operators. Despite competitive pressure, the Spanish upper-market segment (broadband and convergence) remains relatively solid, while the low-end tiers experience the most intense price tension. Zegona's valuation remains attractive to some analysts at 9.0x 2027E EV/OpFCF, representing a discount to the sector average of 11.2x.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. This question determines if Zegona can transform Vodafone Spain into a high-yield asset or if it will be trapped in a deflationary price war.
1.
Key Question: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: high and re-accelerating consumer inflation going into the January 2027 repricing round; an expanding low-cost challenger with its own fibre and frozen prices; a squeeze on household real incomes with slowing growth in 2027; legal or regulatory limits on unilateral mid-contract price rises; a consolidated four-operator market with a rational top tier; the spread of AI tools that compare tariffs and automate switching?"

Pricing outcomes directly impact Zegona's ability to deliver its targeted 15% EFCF CAGR between 2027 and 2030 as projected by Deutsche Bank. The 4.9% inflation rate in September 2026 increases the urgency for indexation-linked rises to protect margins against rising operational costs. The success of the January 2027 repricing will signal whether the Spanish "four-operator" market is truly rational or if Digi's share-gain strategy forces further back-book cannibalization.

Failure to hold a price premium over Digi at comparable speeds could lead to a permanent de-rating of the Vodafone main brand's value proposition. Regulatory scrutiny on unilateral rises, similar to the European Court of Justice's ruling in Germany, poses a legal risk to the standard Spanish contract structure.

2.
Other side of the story: If the Spanish market follows the recent German trend of "more encouraging signs" where competition subsided and price rises stuck, Zegona could see significant EBITDA upside without the feared churn spike.

Section 2 – Market Structure and Competitor Pricing

Section summary
  1. The Spanish market is bifurcated between a solid upper-end convergence segment and a highly aggressive low-end mobile tier.

Challenger Impact on Incumbents

Digi Spain's recent performance shows Q2 2026 revenues grew 15.1% YoY, supported by heavy FCF investment in its FTTH rollout. The challenger continues to drag down sector averages, with its own mobile ARPU declining 9.8% YoY in Q2 2026 compared to an 11.8% decline in Q1. Deutsche Bank expects Digi's low prices to continue impacting incumbent ARPUs in the near term, though it anticipates increased rationality as Digi eventually seeks to deliver recurring FCF. In August 2026, Digi upstaged incumbents by replacing its 1Gbps offer with a 2Gbps service at the same €20 price point.

1.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Telekom reported in August 2026 that its own German price rises "stuck better than expected," suggesting that incumbent brands can maintain pricing power if they offer superior value or bundles.

Incumbent Counter-Strategies

Telefónica has launched a disruptive €5 mobile-only offer with 15GB data to defend the low end against Digi and Zegona's Lowi brand. Incumbent Orange has maintained that the French market exhibits limited top-line growth but has seen ARPO growth in fixed broadband due to low churn across products. Vodafone Germany has successfully pivoted to reducing upfront promotions to enhance ARPU, a strategy Zegona may look to replicate in Spain.

2.
The "four-operator" market structure is being tested as operators shift focus from volume to value-based bundles, with some targeting minimum price points of €30 per month. Other side of the story: While incumbents focus on value, the sheer magnitude of Digi's net adds (+141k broadband and +254k mobile in Q2 2026) suggests that pricing power remains severely compromised for the premium brands.

Section 3 – Customer Segmentation and Churn Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Churn risks are being mitigated through higher convergence ratios, but back-book migration to value brands remains a persistent threat.

Convergence and Loyalty Trends

Iliad's performance in France suggests that an increasing convergence ratio supports lower churn even in maturing or competitive markets. Vodafone Spain's main brand has previously seen ARPU declines, and analysts are closely watching if the rate of decline has moderated after past price cuts. Zegona's Lowi brand competes at the €5 price point for 25GB, directly matching the lowest offers from Digi and Telefónica's O2. The spread of AI tools for automated switching may be offset by loyalty programs, such as Sunrise's rewards system which achieved 60% recognition in 3 months.

1.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank notes that Dutch consumers who stay with providers for years pay "hundreds of euros more" than new subscribers, highlighting the potential for massive churn if AI tools effectively automate this comparison.

ARPU and Service Revenue Performance

Vodafone Group's European service revenue growth was 0.9% in Q1, with "Other Europe" (including Portugal) seeing ARPU pressure resulting in a 2.7% service revenue decline. Vodafone Business has shown better resilience, with service revenue growth accelerating to 5.0% in Q1 compared to 3.2% in the prior quarter. Telefónica Spain managed 2.9% service revenue growth in Q2 2026, though analysts estimate EBITDAaL would have declined without significant headcount reductions. Fixed broadband ARPU for Digi Spain fell 6.9% YoY in Q2 2026, illustrating the deflationary environment at the bottom of the market.

2.
Other side of the story: The "back-book price rises" from main competitors in markets like Germany have occasionally benefited Vodafone by allowing them to grow ARPU on new customers by 30% YoY.

Section 4 – Regulation and Operational Conditions

Section summary
  1. Regulatory limits on unilateral rises and rising infrastructure costs are squeezing the "rational top tier" of the market.

Regulatory and Legal Constraints

The European Court of Justice's ruling against Vodafone Germany's unilateral price rise has set a dangerous precedent for mid-contract adjustments across Europe. In Spain, Cellnex is under a regulatory framework where it must provide price stability with inflation adjustments for wholesale terrestrial TV broadcasting. The "network operator agreement" discussions for challengers like 1&1 in Germany have introduced financial uncertainty for incumbents hosting these rivals. Regulatory scrutiny in the Czech Republic recently led to a €102 million payout by Telefónica to minority shareholders, highlighting latent legal risks in European subsidiaries.

1.
Contrary evidence: Spain introduced a new regulatory framework in July 2026 that allows for faster recovery of grid rights from dormant permit holders, potentially easing infrastructure bottlenecks.

Inflation and Cost Pass-Through

2.
Spanish inflation of 4.9% in September 2026 is significantly higher than the 2% to 3% indexation-based rises seen in other European markets like Proximus. Rising energy demand from data centers could impact 2030 power prices by up to €4 per MWh in Spain, adding long-term pressure to telco opex. Incumbents are increasingly relying on AI-driven cost control to offset the inability to fully pass through inflation to the consumer base. The cost of live entertainment and other "bundle additions" like Disney+ or Netflix is rising, forcing operators to decide between margin dilution or risky price hikes. Other side of the story: If Zegona successfully optimizes capex and mobile network savings, it may be able to maintain its EFCF growth targets even if nominal pricing power remains weak.

Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A shift toward rationality by Digi or a favorable legal ruling on indexation could trigger a major re-rating of Zegona's earnings. If Digi Spain pivots to FCF generation over market share sooner than expected, the entire Spanish sector could see a rapid recovery in blended ARPU. A legal clarification in Spain that explicitly protects indexation clauses would remove the "unilateral rise" risk and allow for consistent 4%+ annual price increases.
1.
Should Zegona achieve higher-than-expected synergies from mobile network sharing, its EFCF could exceed the 15% CAGR even in a flat pricing environment. An early sign to watch is Telefónica Spain's EBITDAaL trajectory; if it turns positive without further headcount cuts, it suggests pricing power is returning to the premium tier. Conversely, if Digi uses its listing proceeds to further accelerate its own fibre build, the "frozen price" environment could persist well into 2028. Other side of the story: Most sources assume a rational "four-operator" market, but a price-war relapse by MasOrange to protect its new scale could invalidate the consolidation bull case.
