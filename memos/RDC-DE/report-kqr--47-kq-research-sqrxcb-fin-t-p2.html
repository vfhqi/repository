Mechanics of the Fixum Fee Adjustments

1.
Phased fee adjustments: The German Federal Cabinet officially approved a phased increase in the fixed prescription fee known as the Fixum. This regulation increases the dispensing fee per prescription pack from €8.35 to €9 starting on 1 July 2026. A subsequent legislative step is scheduled to raise this fixed fee to €9.50 on 1 January 2027. This sequential fee progression provides a highly predictable margin tailwind for Redcare Pharmacy over the coming years.
2.
Incremental earnings benefits: These regulatory fee adjustments are estimated to provide substantial cash flow boosts to Redcare Pharmacy as adoption of electronic prescriptions accelerates. Deutsche Bank estimates the phased fee adjustments will boost Redcare's adjusted EBITDA by €2 million to €4 million in 2026. This positive impact is projected to scale to €16 million in 2027 and €26 million by 2028. This progression demonstrates how regulatory fee adjustments drive earnings expansion for major online platforms.
3.
Policy framework stability: The implementation of the pharmacy reform via separate ordinances represents a structural relief for the online channel. The German Ministry of Economic Affairs will govern future pharmacy fee adjustments through ministerial ordinances. This legislative process bypassed the Bundesrat, ensuring speedy execution of regulatory benefits for mail-order operators. This shift creates a much more stable framework for online pharmacies.

Refining channel investments: Financial discipline is driving a strategic shift from broad awareness campaigns to high-efficiency marketing channels. Legal Frameworks and Judicial Precedents

4.
Confirming bonus legality: The German Federal Court of Justice handed down a landmark ruling on 17 July 2025 regarding cross-border pharmacy promotions. The court rejected an appeal by domestic pharmacy groups and affirmed the legality of Rx bonuses for foreign online operators. Redcare Pharmacy management highlighted that the ruling provides long-term legal clarity and protects European market access. This decision formally establishes that European Union pharmacies are not bound by German fixed-price regimes.

Striking down promotions: Despite the general legality of prescription bonuses, courts continue to tightly regulate the specific forms of advertising. The Karlsruhe Higher Regional Court ruled on 9 December 2025 to ban a specific promotion. This legal decision barred DocMorris from offering a twenty-five euro voucher promotion for first-time e-prescription app redemptions. This demonstrates that regulatory bodies will continue to restrict aggressive cash-equivalent vouchers if they are deemed to distort consumer choices.
6.
Restricting assortment cross-selling: Legal precedents also prevent online pharmacies from offering prescription bonuses that can be spent on over-the-counter products. A prior European Court of Justice ruling from February 2025 clarified that Member States can prohibit Rx vouchers redeemable for general pharmacy assortments. Deutsche Bank highlights that this restriction prevents players from directly linking prescription orders to over-the-counter purchases via promotional coupons. This pushes platforms to focus on direct cash rebates and separate beauty-only vouchers to drive customer lifetime value.
