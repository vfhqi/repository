EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

MATERIALITY OF CHANGE Endesa SA has undergone a comprehensive executive overhaul with CEO Gianni Vittorio Armani assuming leadership in April 2026, replacing the long-standing José Bogas Gálvez who had helmed the company since 2014. The new leadership inherits solid financial momentum but faces untested operational alignment in the

Spanish market, as the complete management transition introduces short-term execution friction while reinforcing parent Enel's operational control. The strategic priority has shifted decisively toward regulated electricity grids, with over 50% of the €9.6 billion net capex plan allocated to networks under the 2026-2028 strategic roadmap, while renewable development capex was reduced by 20% versus the previous plan to avoid low solar capture prices.

Spain's updated Royal Decree lifted the distribution investment cap to €10.2 billion for the 2027-2030 period, unlocking unprecedented grid investment visibility and creating substantial regulated asset base expansion opportunities. The company demonstrates a clear conservative forecasting pattern, consistently beating initial guidance ranges over the past three fiscal periods, with FY25 EBITDA exceeding the upper bound by €160 million and Net Ordinary Income outperforming by €351 million. The Spanish power market faces structural demand acceleration from industrial recovery and data centers, with electricity demand projected

1.
to reach 5% of total Spanish consumption by 2030 if datacenter capacity reaches 3.0GW as projected.

DRIVERS (RANKED)

2.
Driver 1: Regulatory Networks Capex Expansion. Spain's Royal Decree lifted the distribution investment cap to €10.2 billion for 2027-2030, enabling Endesa's annual capex ceiling to potentially rise to €1.5 billion in 2027e and €2.15 billion in 2028e given its 44% distribution market share. Endesa's 2026-2028 plan outlines net capex of €9.6 billion (12% increase vs prior plan), with networks receiving €4.5 billion or 47% of total allocation. This driver carries HIGH plausibility as regulatory approval is secured and expanded investment headroom directly drives regulated asset base growth with inflation-linked returns that insulate earnings from wholesale market deflation.
3.
Driver 2: Nuclear Operating Life Extensions. The Spanish Nuclear Safety Council issued a highly favorable technical opinion to extend Almaraz's operating license, with Bernstein modeling a 10-year extension adding €5.1 billion cumulative EBITDA over 2029-2045e and €1.9 billion of net equity value. Operating life extensions of nuclear plants are increasingly probable following Almaraz's favorable safety technical opinion. This driver carries HIGH plausibility given the formal ministerial order extending operations until June 2030, though longterm extensions beyond 2030 depend on government licensing approvals amid evolving energy policy priorities.
4.
Driver 3: Integrated Retail Margin Protection. Endesa grew its 1H26 gross margin to €4,253 million from €3,812 million in 1H25, with network EBITDA surging to €1,200 million in 1H26, representing 24% year-on-year growth. Retail plans target 6.7 million deregulated customer contracts by 2028 to act as a natural hedge against spot market volatility. This driver carries MEDIUM plausibility as high customer churn above 25% and intense retail competition from over 300 active players threaten margin sustainability despite stricter commercial discipline and pricing power recovery among major competitors.
5.
Driver 4: Gas Wholesale Sourcing Spreads. Endesa's diversified sourcing benefits from approximately 3.0bcm of Henry Hub contracts, capturing lucrative global gas spreads during market volatility. Capturing high margins on only 25% of expected wholesale volumes could add over €100 million EBITDA, though this driver carries MEDIUM plausibility with LOW confidence as sourcing contract benefits will gradually decline as historical favorable contracts roll off over the medium-term and projected 2028 forward curves imply material spread compression.
6.
Driver 5: Renewable Asset Hybridisation. Endesa enhanced its generation mix by acquiring 626MW of hydro capacity and completed the sale of 49.99% of its solar assets to Masdar for €817 million. The strategic plan prioritizes co-located battery storage systems deployment alongside distressed solar acquisitions to mitigate power price cannibalization. This driver carries HIGH plausibility with MEDIUM confidence as grid connection bottlenecks and solar capture rates approaching 50% levels make standalone execution difficult, though integrated storage models effectively address merchant margin pressures.

RECENT DELIVERY PERFORMANCE

7.
Endesa sits second in peer earnings momentum following the strong 1H26 beat and raise results, with adjusted EPS projected to grow at a 7.4% CAGR over the 2025-2028 strategic period. The company comfortably exceeded its FY25 ordinary net income target of €1.9-2.0 billion by delivering €2.35 billion, with strong 1H26 results triggering a net income guidance upgrade to more than €2.4 billion, representing over 60% of full-year guidance. However, earnings quality is heavily impacted by large non-recurring items including €100 million in extraordinary network settlements in Q1 2026, €104 million in positive impacts from the suspended 7% generation tax, and €76 million in island fuel recovery. Stripping out all one-offs reduces 1H26 attributable net income to approximately €1,260 million, revealing underlying performance more moderate than headline figures suggest and raising questions about earnings sustainability once transient regulatory tailwinds normalize.

Management exhibits a clear tendency to sandbag initial guidance, consistently beating targets, earning Endesa a MEDIUM-to-HIGH credibility rating based on historical target outperformance. The company behaves as an honest tracker to conservative sandbagger, delivering progressive guidance upgrades as visibility improves throughout fiscal periods.

However, the complete overhaul of executive leadership introduces short-term execution friction, with new CEO Gianni Vittorio Armani assuming his role in April 2026 following unanimous board approval, inheriting solid financial momentum but facing untested operational alignment. The new management team comprises infrastructure specialists from parent Enel's group functions, signaling

SELL-SIDE POSITIONING

The consensus rating mix as of July 2026 stands at 10% Overweight, 57% Equal-weight, and 33% Underweight, with current price targets spanning from a low of €24 to a high of €41.50 and a consensus mean target price of approximately €37 per share. Bernstein raised its target to €41 per share on positive nuclear life extensions, while Morgan Stanley maintains its Underweight rating with a €37 price target and Jefferies reiterates its Hold rating with an upgraded €35 target reflecting premium multiples.

Morgan Stanley strategist Robert Pulleyn expects only a 1% EPS CAGR for Endesa out to 2030 and warns that unprecedented 2028 supply margin guidance justifies a peer valuation discount. The sell-side debate centers on bulls highlighting datacenter demand acceleration and higher distribution grid capex ceilings versus bears warning of

Endesa's 1H26 Net Debt/EBITDA stood at 1.6x versus 1.8x at FY25, comparing favorably to Iberdrola's expected 3.9x by 2030e, Naturgy's 2.2x in June 2026, and EDP's elevated 3.5x at the end of 1H26. However, Morgan Stanley forecasts only a 1% EPS CAGR for Endesa out to 2030 due to supply margin normalization, suggesting the retail and supply margin outperformance is unsustainable and earnings momentum will rank bottom-quartile if wholesale power prices collapse further. Endesa maintains exceptionally robust leverage metrics versus

ACTIVIST PRESENCE

11.
No active activist presence exists in the AS library for Endesa because parent Enel holds 71.2% of the total share capital, effectively precluding meaningful activist engagement or minority shareholder pressure campaigns. The concentrated ownership structure eliminates any potential for activist-driven strategic interventions or capital allocation challenges, though it also raises governance concerns about minority shareholder alignment given parent Enel's ability to dictate board composition, management appointments, and strategic direction without independent oversight mechanisms.

MATERIAL RISKS

12.
Standalone solar projects face severe pricing cannibalization as zero-price and curtailment hours increase across the Spanish mainland, forcing a structural pivot toward an integrated energy management model combining renewables, batteries, and customer solutions. Proposed draft datacenter rules require datacenters to source 80% of power from new renewables, creating significant execution hurdles, with industry lobby SpainDC warning that rigid rules could jeopardize up to €60 billion of prospective digital investments. Network nodes suffer 94% saturation limits, highlighting why management must prioritize distribution grid upgrades, though restrictive datacenter rules and grid saturation may trigger severe demand delays that stall regulated asset base growth. The most critical risk is that unexpected political intervention in Spain's energy market or retail price wars could severely compress margins, with Morgan Stanley flagging that if customer churn spikes, integrated supply earnings will collapse before network capex materializes, while the systematic reliance on non-recurring regulatory settlements masks a flat organic growth profile that cannot sustain premium valuations if legal tailwinds reverse.
