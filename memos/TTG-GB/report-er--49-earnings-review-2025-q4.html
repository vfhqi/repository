Section 1 – Brief company description and history

1.
Established market position: TT Electronics operates as a global manufacturer and provider of design-led, highly engineered electronics for performance-critical applications. The company's primary operations span across three distinct geographic regions, which are Europe, North America, and Asia. These operations serve structurally high-growth markets, including healthcare, aerospace and defence, and automation and electrification. Through these segments, the group focuses on designing sensors, power management systems, and connectivity devices where operational reliability is mission critical. The business is categorized within the technology sector and the capital goods industry.

2.
Declining historical revenue: The company's three-year fundamental performance showed a downward trajectory in headline revenues prior to the FY2025 results. Specifically, group revenue declined from £613.9 million in FY2023 to £521.1 million in FY2024, representing a statutory decrease of 15 percent. This downward trend continued into FY2025, where the group reported a further revenue contraction to headline sales of £481.4 million due to persistent destocking. This performance was heavily contrasted against peer discoverIE, which conversely reported a 5 percent revenue increase to £443.3 million in its comparable annual period.
3.
Volatile operating profitability: Under the regional structure, the company's adjusted operating profits experienced significant volatility over the three-year period. Adjusted operating profit fell from £47.1 million in FY2023 to £37.1 million in FY2024 due to severe operational execution issues in North America. For FY2025, adjusted operating profit stabilized slightly at £37.2 million, which was above the market consensus expectation of £34.5 million. However, statutory operating profit collapsed to a loss of £28.2 million in FY2025 compared to a loss of £23.5 million in FY2024.
4.
Improving leverage profile: The group successfully managed its balance sheet and progressively reduced leverage despite the underlying profit pressures. Bank covenant leverage, which excludes the impact of IFRS 16 lease liabilities, stood at 1.9 times in HY2025 but materially improved to 1.1 times by the end of FY2025. This represents a solid improvement from the 1.8 times leverage recorded at the end of FY2024. This deleveraging effort was significantly ahead of peer Synthomer, which struggled to deleverage due to a slow recovery in its EBITDA.
5.
Outstanding cash conversion: Cash conversion emerged as a key fundamental strength for the group over the three-year historical period. Cash conversion improved dramatically from 117 percent in FY2024 to an exceptional 150 percent in FY2025. This cash generation was driven by a £14.8 million inventory reduction under the group's working capital initiatives. This far exceeded the cash conversion of peer Kitron, which saw its order backlog develop more slowly under mixed market conditions.
6.
Underperforming return metrics: The group's pre-tax return on invested capital ( ROIC ) exhibited a recovery in FY2025 after previously deteriorating. Pre-tax ROIC fell from 12 percent in FY2023 to 10 percent in FY2024 as a direct consequence of lower North American operating profits. However, due to turnaround actions, ROIC recovered to 13.3 percent in FY2025, representing a 330 basis point increase. This recovery trailed the performance of peer discoverIE, which maintained high-single-digit organic growth and superior margin stability.
7.
Weak relative share performance: The company's trailing share price return metrics showed severe long-term underperformance leading into the FY2025 reporting cycle. Prior to the FY2025 results, the group's share price had experienced a severe 12-month absolute decline of 27.2 percent as of December 2024. This underperformance was starkly contrasted against the broader benchmark FTSE All Share Index, which conversely rose by 10.6 percent over the same 12-month period. Over the shorter trailing 3-month and 6-month windows leading up to June 2025, the share price recovered slightly by 33.3 percent and 20.2 percent absolute, respectively.
8.
Pre-earnings consensus degradation: Market expectations for TT Electronics ' future profitability were heavily downgraded in the six months prior to the FY2025 earnings announcement. Following operational challenges at two North American plants and a subsequent chief executive departure, analysts aggressively cut their near-term forecasts. Sell-side consensus was forced to lower its FY25F earnings expectations, with multiple brokers pricing in further downside risk to earnings due to North American issues. This degradation contrasted with peer discoverIE, which delivered highly resilient updates and kept its consensus estimates stable.
9.
Pre-results rating downgrades: Broker ratings shifted from highly optimistic to cautious as structural issues in the components business became clear. In September 2025, Deutsche Bank analyst Paige decided to discontinue coverage of the stock entirely, having previously maintained a Buy rating and a price target of 130p. This coverage suspension followed a series of price target cuts from 260p in May 2024 down to 130p in June 2025. The broker noted that its previous ratings and price targets should no longer be relied upon by investors.
10.
Maintained hold stance: Jefferies analyst Jeffriess consistently maintained a Hold rating on the stock in the six months leading up to the FY2025 earnings release. The broker held its price target at 95p as of November 2025 due to persistent operational challenges at the Cleveland site. This target was previously cut to 77p in May 2025 before being adjusted to reflect turnaround progress. The broker noted that while the valuation was highly supportive, the lack of positive near-term catalysts prevented a rating upgrade.
11.
Divergent peer ratings: While TT Electronics was plagued by hold ratings and coverage drops, its industry peers enjoyed far more positive sell-side momentum. Deutsche Bank noted that peer companies like discoverIE and Volex delivered far more resilient updates during the period of North American disruption. The broker highlighted that TT Electronics ' operational setbacks were largely company-specific rather than industry-wide.
12.
Dividend suspension disappointment: Unlike several of its main competitors, TT Electronics was forced to suspend its shareholder returns during its restructuring phase. The board decided to pause the dividend payment in FY2024 as a prudent measure to preserve liquidity during the North American operational turnaround. This pause was maintained through HY2025 due to elevated macro uncertainty and localized business risks. This compared negatively with peer discoverIE, which consistently paid dividends and grew its payouts.
13.
Other side of the story: Although the historical three-year period was defined by severe operational disruptions in North America and significant revenue declines, the underlying European business remained a powerful structural growth engine for the group. Europe delivered 7.4 percent organic revenue growth to £144.4 million in FY2025, driven by robust demand in aerospace and defence markets. This localized strength suggests that the company's core engineering capabilities remain intact despite the severe execution errors that damaged the group's headline profitability.

11.

Shareholder returns and forward expectations

This capital preservation stance compared unfavourably with peer Luceco, which increased its proposed full-year dividend by 20 percent to 6 pence.

This outlook reflects a period of commercial consolidation rather than immediate high-volume recovery.

This downgrade was driven by persistent destocking expectations across global Electronic Manufacturing Services channels.
15.
Other side of the story: While the massive 150 percent cash conversion and £50.3 million net debt figures represent excellent cash management, this performance was heavily assisted by a non-recurring inventory reduction of £14.8 million that cannot easily be repeated. If the working capital drawdown reverses in FY2026 to support new contract wins, cash generation will fall significantly, as shown by management's FY2026 cash conversion guidance of only 70 to 80 percent.
