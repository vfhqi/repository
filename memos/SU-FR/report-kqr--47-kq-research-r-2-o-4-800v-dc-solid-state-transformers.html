Section 2 – Technical disruption and hardware migration

Section summary
  1. The transition to 800V DC architectures cannibalizes legacy low-voltage portfolios while driving a massive value migration toward medium-voltage grid conversion, high-voltage busways, and wide-bandgap silicon carbide semiconductors.

Grid-to-chip substation conversion topologies

1.
Collapsing conversion stages: Upstream substation designs are transitioning toward medium-voltage direct current systems where solid-state transformers convert 10 to 34.5 kV alternating current directly into a regulated 800V direct current bus. This advanced topology eliminates multiple intermediate transformers, rectifiers, and low-voltage switchgear, effectively shrinking the traditional electrical grey-space footprint from 12 meters to under 3 meters. This structural change allows operators to maximize whitespace for compute racks.
2.
Pioneering solid-state transformers: Equipment suppliers are industrializing modular solid-state transformers capable of connecting directly to utility grids up to 36 kV. Siemens and Reinhausen have codeveloped a modular solid-state transformer platform designed to respond to extreme load steps in milliseconds, mitigating training workload fluctuations. This power electronics-based design replaces traditional passive iron-core windings with semiconductor switches to precisely control voltage and current. These software-controlled units enable bi-directional energy flows and raise technical barriers to entry. This transition establishes a critical foothold for smart grid integration.
3.
Certifying medium-voltage platforms: Commercial validation of high-voltage solid-state transformer platforms is accelerating through joint venture roadmaps. Eaton has collaborated with Infineon to deploy its MVSST 2.0 platform, utilizing advanced silicon carbide technology to streamline grid-to-data-center interfaces. This platform is one of the first medium-voltage solid-state systems to achieve formal IEC certification, demonstrating a robust pathway to commercial industrialization. Eaton's CTO Michael Regelski notes that deep organic investments in power electronics have positioned the company to run active pilots in the APAC region. This commercial progress represents a significant lead over standard transformers.

Disintermediation of legacy low-voltage hardware

4.
Squeezing out low-voltage: The shift toward 800V direct current architectures eliminates multiple traditional low-voltage hardware layers between the grid substation and the server rack. Under a native DCdistribution model, traditional step-down low-frequency transformers, AC switchgear, uninterruptible power supplies, and remote power panels face complete disintermediation. Base case modeling shows that native 800V adoption directly displaces legacy low-voltage equipment and will experience a major decline in content value per megawatt. This structural transition directly threatens legacy capital goods portfolios.
5.
Removing rack-level converters: Traditional server-facing alternating-current power distribution units and intermediate AC-to-DC power shelves are being phased out of high-density compute cabinets. Redburn highlights that the core function of the ORv3 AC-to-DC power shelf is moving upstream into dedicated sidecar conversion modules. This disaggregated design relocates power electronics outside the computing frame to release rack space. These structural space savings allow operators to pack more graphics processing units.
6.
Upstreaming power backup: Uninterruptible power supply infrastructure is undergoing a complete paradigm shift from centralized legacy AC systems to distributed DC battery backup units. High-voltage direct current architectures eliminate bulky online UPS rooms and instead distribute modular battery backup units directly close to the computing workload. These localized battery shelves provide rapid millisecond-scale energy discharge to shave volatile GPU power spikes. This integration of energy storage directly on the DC bus unlocks massive efficiency gains. This transition successfully reclaims white-space deployment area.

Next-generation whitespace and rack silicon

7.
Mandating silicon carbide: Delivering 800V direct current distribution through the white space to the rack level makes the adoption of wide-bandgap silicon carbide semiconductors mandatory. Silicon-based power devices are physically unable to match the low switching losses and high breakdown voltages of silicon carbide at 800V levels. Traditional silicon MOSFETs waste excessive power as heat, whereas 1,200V-rated silicon carbide devices maintain optimal end-to-end efficiency. This material shift drives an unprecedented power semiconductor demand cycle.
8.
Expanding silicon shipset value: Power semiconductor content per megawatt scales non-linearly as AI data centers migrate from standard 48V to 800V direct grid architectures. Microchip Technology has launched 3.3 kV mSiC power modules specifically designed to enable solid-state transformers for high-voltage direct current environments. Infineon is partnering with DG Matrix to supply latest-generation CoolSiC MOSFETs for multi-port solid-state transformer architectures. This semiconductor content expansion is expected to drive the global SST semiconductor market toward one billion dollars by 2030. This trend will multiply semiconductor dollar-content per rack.
9.
Pioneering downstream gallium nitride: Downstream intermediate bus conversion stages are adopting advanced gallium nitride devices to step down high voltages near the processor core. Renesas has begun supplying samples of 650V GaN devices for 800V high-voltage direct-current architectures to major AI data center players. These wide-bandgap devices are qualified for step-down intermediate bus converter stages to maximize rack power density. This technology represents a complete disruption of silicon power.
10.
Other side of the story: Conservative procurement directors caution that high upfront hardware costs and unproven long-term reliability of solid-state transformers could severely delay native DC adoption. Standard line-frequency steel transformers and legacy AC UPS systems remain highly reliable, whereas advanced medium-voltage solid-state systems currently cost up to 10x more than traditional equipment. If hyperscalers prioritize low near-term capital expenditure over long-term operating efficiency, then the 800V DC transition may remain confined to niche high-density training deployments.
