Section 16 – Conclusion 1 finding

Mediobanca's H1 2026 earnings delivery on August 6, 2026, demonstrated record consolidated revenues of €1,948.2m, up 6.1% year-on-year, supported by strong momentum in corporate and investment banking and consumer finance. However, the fundamental investment question has shifted decisively away from standalone trajectory analysis toward integrated execution risk within the parent BMPS structure. The finalized merger terms approved in March 2026 feature an exchange ratio of 2.45 Mediobanca shares for each MPS share, representing a 3% premium, with high-value CIB and private banking assets carved out into a new unlisted entity. This structural reorganization has permanently altered the 18-month to 3-year earnings trajectory by replacing standalone capital-light expansion with retail integration synergies. The single most important change from this earnings cycle is the demonstrated resilience of the corporate advisory fee engine amid severe transitional friction in wealth management.

CIB revenues reached €492.6m in H1 2026, up 12.9% year-on-year and 22.2% quarter-on-quarter, driven by €220.2m in investment banking fees, with Messier & Associés contributing over €80.6m in advisory fees to fully offset the expected reduction from Arma Partners. This rebalancing validates management's diversification strategy and confirms that Mediobanca can sustain mid-teens consolidated earnings growth even as the wealth franchise undergoes structural compression.

The consumer credit exposure provides a robust defense against declining rates, keeping net interest income highly resilient, while the 100% dividend payout policy supports up-streaming of dividends to parent BMPS and underpins combined group distributions. This defensive positioning materially de-risks the 25%+ IRR potential by anchoring downside protection through retail profitability and reducing sensitivity to wealth management outflows.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to this constructive view centers on the permanent structural damage to the high-margin wealth franchise through ongoing talent attrition and client churn. Morgan Stanley documented a sequential loss of 17 wealth advisors and 9 private bankers, indicating that structural talent attrition is already diluting the standalone franchise, suggesting the market may be too optimistic about smooth integration execution, and warning that if domestic banks continue to raid the private banking network, the unraveling of highmargin wealth fees could severely impair the group's long-term EPS trajectory.

While the CIB division drove record consolidated numbers, underlying wealth management fees and net interest income remain depressed year-onyear, with stabilization of assets only achieved through margin-dilutive use of commercial exemptions and significant one-off retention expenses. If competitor raiding intensifies or if €0.7 billion integration synergies fail to materialize due to product distribution friction across the MPS retail network, the combined entity could deliver a return profile substantially below management's 12% ROTE target by 2028.

Specific indicators that would change the analyst's view and validate the bearish counter-hypothesis include: First, sustained quarterly net outflows exceeding €1 billion in Wealth Management for three consecutive quarters beyond Q2 2026, indicating permanent franchise erosion rather than temporary transition noise. Second, private banking salesforce headcount falling below 100 advisors from the current base of approximately 120, which would represent a critical threshold triggering irreversible damage to high-net-worth client relationships.

Third, wealth management net interest margin compression exceeding 50 basis points year-on-year by Q4 2026, driven by persistent use of commercial exemptions and promotional deposit rates. Fourth, failure to deliver at least 20% of the €0.7 billion standalone synergy target during 2026, as management has committed to achieving 30% in the integration's first year. Fifth, regulatory disapproval or material delay beyond Q4 2026 for the merger by incorporation clearances from the European Central Bank or Bank of Italy, which would undermine execution credibility and synergy timelines.

Two notable outliers merit close examination. First, the Markets Division within CIB posted revenues surging 83.5% year-on-year to €73.6m, with management noting that trading desks successfully profited from periods of high volatility and geopolitical tensions, particularly in equity trading which climbed 76% year-on-year driven by bespoke activity and structured certificates. This exceptional performance likely represents unsustainable cyclical positioning rather than structural earnings power, suggesting material downside risk to CIB revenues if market volatility normalizes. Second, group cost of risk normalized to 54 basis points in H1 2026, up 21 basis points year-on-year, due to a mix shift toward personal loans and the use of €29m in macroeconomic overlays, while consumer finance cost of risk remained stable quarter-on-quarter at 187 basis points.

This elevated provisioning trajectory in consumer finance warrants scrutiny, as any further deterioration beyond the 190 basis points full-year threshold could pressure bottom-line profitability and dividend sustainability.
