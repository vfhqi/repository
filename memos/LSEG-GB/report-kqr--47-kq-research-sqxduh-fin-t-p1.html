Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be answered at medium confidence. LSEG carries real fixed-cost operating leverage, concentrated in people and technology. How much of that reaches EPS over the next 18 months to four years depends on three open items: the Microsoft cloud schedule, the underlying margin expansion once SwapClear has lapped, and the normalization of rate and volume tailwinds.
1.
Cost base: Adjusted staff costs of £1.21bn and third-party services of £138m in H1 2026 together made up 72% of total adjusted operating expenses. This is the main source of structural leverage, and it makes workforce and insourcing programmes the key lever to monitor.
2.
Peer margins: LSEG's 52.7% adjusted EBITDA margin sits between desktop and toll-collector peers, above FactSet at 33.0% but below MSCI at 62.2%, Euronext at 66.5% and CME at 69.4%. The remaining margin gap is the scope for operating leverage, and the gap to the highest-margin peers is unlikely to close fully.
3.
SwapClear: The partner-bank share was cut from 30% to 15% in 2025 and to 10% from 2026, driving a 38% fall in Markets cost of sales to £87m in H1 2026. It added 140bps of H1 margin expansion, but this step-up will largely reverse in Q4 2026, so the SwapClear step-up is largely one-off. Jefferies attributes about 30bps of the FY2026 guided expansion to SwapClear, leaving roughly 70bps of normalized underlying expansion.
4.
Rates and volumes: NTI compressed in 2025 on a 16.4% fall in average cash collateral, then recovered by 21.3% by Q3 2026. A 1-percentage-point fall in short-term rates would cut profit after tax by only £5m, so rate sensitivity looks modest but real. Roughly half of LCH clearing revenues are flat-fee, which cushions volume downturns relative to CME's transaction-heavy model.
5.
Integration precedent: LSEG delivered £562m against its upgraded £400m target of Refinitiv run-rate cost synergies by end-2024, but at a total integration cost of £1.4bn. Dual-running costs also compressed margins against the original synergy timelines. Euronext delivered €121m of synergies against an initial €60m target, while still booking €46.9m of integration and double-run costs in 2024. Synergy delivery is feasible, but it arrives with friction.
6.
Cloud timing: LSEG has committed to a minimum of $2.8bn of Microsoft cloud spend, strongly weighted towards the second half of the term. One expert says almost none of this has been executed yet, and the exact annual commitment steps are undisclosed. The cloud commitment is back-end weighted and unscheduled, so the timing of legacy cost elimination cannot yet be pinned down. Annual filings that detail the steps are the key disclosure to watch.
7.
In-housing: The insourced engineering mix rose to 60% in FY2025 from 49%, against an 80% target, and engineering headcount fell from 15,101 to 14,244. Cloud and AI-specialist wage inflation and token costs are expected to be only partly offset by insourcing. Another source puts the internal share at 77%, and the net savings after wage inflation remain unclear, so the insourcing savings versus specialist wage inflation balance is unproven.
8.
Capex: Management guides capex down from 10.2% of revenue in FY2025 to approximately 8% by FY2029. This boosts cash flow but risks slowing platform upgrades as competitors accelerate AI spending. With capex falling toward eight percent by 2029, product-delivery quality should be tracked alongside the cash benefit.
9.
AI savings: FactSet cut headcount 3% and secured $30m of gross savings in FY2026. S&P Global has delivered 60% of its $100m Enterprise Data Office target. Yet FactSet's Q4 gross margin fell 305bps as compensation and compute costs erased short-term gains, and LSEG has disclosed no verified dollar savings. AI savings proven at peers only is the fair reading until LSEG quantifies its own.
10.
Broker consensus: Deutsche Bank assumes significant margin expansion through 2028 and around 70bps annually. The 150bps cumulative guidance for 2027 to 2029 itself assumes a stable macroeconomic environment with minimal transaction-related volatility. Jefferies sees underlying expansion of about 70bps, above the 50bps annual run-rate the target implies, and Rothschild & Co Redburn assumes solid double-digit EPS compounding. In short, consensus assumes benign macro backdrop conditions and on-time cost delivery.
11.
Capital return: LSEG returned £2.6bn via buybacks in 2026, upgraded equity free cash flow to at least £2.8bn, and plans up to £1.5bn of buybacks in 2027. Because this cash generation helps insulate EPS from transaction-linked cyclicality, buybacks cushion per-share earnings volatility even if margin delivery lags.
12.
Downside paths: Deutsche Bank's alternative view is that if volumes normalize and rates fall, margin expansion flattens and the 150bps target is missed. Further threats include BGC's FMX platform challenging incumbent rate-futures pricing, possible financial transaction taxes or higher capital requirements, and AI disintermediation of the Workflow desktop layer. The speed of that last transition is unknown, so competition, regulation and AI disintermediation threaten leverage and should be tracked against the clearing-share and desktop-pricing disclosures.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. LSEG possesses a highly scalable business model whose operational leverage will directly dictate future earnings per share volatility and capital return capacity over the next four years.

Core Question

1.
Crucial inquiry: "London Stock Exchange Group plc (LSEG-GB), a global financial markets infrastructure and data group with four segments: Data & Analytics (Workflows desktops including Workspace, Data & Feeds, Analytics), Markets (capital markets including a majority stake in the listed Tradeweb, and post-trade clearing through LCH including SwapClear), FTSE Russell (indices and benchmarks) and Risk Intelligence (screening and KYC data).
2.
Key question: how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"

Financial Leverage Mechanisms

3.
Accelerating margin: LSEG continues to experience strong operational momentum because subscription revenues have reached a growth high of 6.6% in 3Q26. This expansion is critical because fewer distortions from mergers and acquisitions will support significant margin expansion through 2028. These efficiencies are expected to accelerate earnings per share growth even as capital markets activities normalize.
4.
Latent profitability: LSEG has contractually committed to a minimum cloud spend of 2.8 billion dollars with Microsoft over its ten-year partnership. An expert notes that LSEG has executed almost none of this committed cloud spend with Microsoft yet, presenting a major strategic transition. Resolving this spend phasing will determine the timing of legacy data-centre cost elimination and its ultimate impact on future earnings per share.
5.
Enhanced capital: LSEG has returned 2.6 billion pounds via buybacks in 2026 and upgraded its outlook for equity free cash flow to at least 2.8 billion pounds. Management plans to sustain this capital return with up to 1.5 billion pounds in buybacks in 2027. This robust cash generation helps insulate earnings per share volatility from underlying transaction-linked revenue cyclicality.

Investment Risks and Volatility

6.
Moderating workflow: LSEG faces investor concerns that artificial intelligence could disrupt its core Workflow desktop revenues. This risk is concentrated in the desktop layer where interface disintermediation represents a structural threat. Addressing these competitive dynamics is essential for investors because Workflows remains the slowest-growing sub-segment within Data and Analytics.
7.
Cyclical exposure: LSEG remains highly exposed to interest rate cycles through its majority stake in clearing houses and Tradeweb. Rising collateral balances and elevated interest rates have boosted net treasury income, but this exposure presents downside risk to future earnings if macro conditions normalize. Investors must monitor these capital market volumes to assess the durability of operating leverage over the next four years.
8.
Contrary evidence: LSEG experienced a 5% share price decline following the announcement of its 3.5 billion dollar Octus acquisition. This market reaction suggests that investors remain highly skeptical of the immediate earnings accretion from large acquisitions given execution risks. Such structural integration challenges could offset expected operating leverage and pressure near-term margins.
9.
Other side of the story: LSEG is positioned to mitigate transaction volatility through its diversified portfolio and high share of recurring revenues, which reached 73% in late 2026. This structural buffer suggests that even if capital market volumes normalize, the robust subscription engine will prevent significant earnings per share downside. Thus, operating leverage might prove more resilient than skeptics expect, provided the Microsoft cloud integration delivers on time.

Section 2 – Cost Structures and Structural Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. LSEG carries a highly fixed-cost business model where structural operating leverage is concentrated within its centralized personnel and technology platforms.
  2. Peer margin benchmarks indicate that tollcollector index and ratings businesses achieve structurally superior profitability compared to labor-heavy desktop providers.

LSEG Operational Cost Lines

1.
High concentration: LSEG operating expenses remain heavily dominated by personnel expenses, which represent the primary driver of structural leverage. In the first half of 2026, LSEG reported adjusted staff costs of 1.21 billion pounds and third-party services of 138 million pounds, together representing 72% of total adjusted operating expenses. This high employee concentration makes the group highly dependent on workforce insourcing programs to drive margin expansion over the next three years.

The company reported IT costs of 343 million pounds in the first half of 2026, reflecting a 5.9% increase driven by cloud expenditure. Because these cloud-based infrastructure costs are largely fixed, this expenditure increases the structural operating leverage of the data segment as subscription revenues scale.
3.
Variable dependencies: LSEG limits its short-term margin flexibility because a significant portion of its cost of sales is tied to external data providers. The group recorded Data and Analytics cost of sales of 426 million pounds in the first half of 2026, reflecting the cost of purchased content, royalties, and exchange fees. This contract alteration drove a 38% decline in Markets cost of sales to 87 million pounds in the first half of 2026, providing immediate margin expansion.

Contractual headwind: LSEG remains bound by a long-term news supply agreement that acts as a substantial fixed operating cost. Under this partnership, which extends through October 2048, LSEG pays a contractual minimum payment to Reuters News. In the first nine months of 2026, Reuters recorded revenues of 311 million dollars under this agreement, compared to 298 million dollars in the prior-year period.

Section 3 – Markets Division Margins and Interest Rate Cycle Dynamics

Section summary
  1. LSEG's Markets division exhibits substantial cyclical leverage driven by interest rate volatility and cash collateral balances that dictate net treasury income fluctuations.
  2. The renegotiation of the SwapClear revenue share with partner banks has permanently restructured the post-trade division's margin profile. Net read: Benchmarking highlights that CME Group maintains structurally superior transaction-driven operating leverage compared to LSEG's mixed clearing model.

Interest Rate Cycles and Treasury Income

1.
Cyclical collateral: LSEG's post-trade clearing business generates highly cyclical net treasury income through interest earned on cash collateral deposited by clearing members as part of the risk management process. During the extreme volatility of early 2020, first-half net treasury income surged 47% to 176 million pounds as cash margin balances peaked. However, this income proved highly rate-sensitive as subsequent rate cuts and collateral optimization led to a decline to 207 million pounds in fiscal 2021.
2.
Rate-driven recovery: LSEG saw a rapid cyclical reversal in net treasury income during the 2022 to 2023 interest- rate up-cycle as central banks hiked rates globally. The group reported 289 million pounds of net treasury income in fiscal 2023, representing 25% of total post-trade income. This rapid recovery demonstrates how steeper short-term yield curves expand interest margins earned on overnight money market deposits.
3.
g p LSEG post-trade revenue split in 2023 highlighting the contribution of Net Treasury Income Collateral normalization: LSEG experienced NTI compression in 2025 due to a 16.4% decline in average cash collateral balances. This decline reflected collateral optimization by customers and the loss of clearing balances following the Euronext migration. By third-quarter 2026, NTI recovered by 21.3% as LCH implemented its new minimum cash framework to support liquidity resilience.

Volume Cyclicality across Markets Division

4.
Transactional expansion: LSEG's Capital Markets division captures significant operating leverage through its 51% majority stake in the electronic trading platform Tradeweb. Tradeweb's average daily volumes increased by 22% year-on-year to 3.2 trillion dollars in third-quarter 2026. This volume surge was driven by a 34% increase in rates volumes as investors dynamically hedged shifting central bank monetary policy expectations.
5.
Mixed derivatives: LSEG's equities clearing and OTC derivatives segments experience contrasting cyclicality during market volume spikes. OTC derivatives cleared through SwapClear surged by 16.7% in third-quarter 2026, benefiting from elevated swap activity. Conversely, cash equity trading remains a smaller driver of operational leverage, representing only about 2% of LSEG group revenues in late 2026.
6.
Clearing margins: LSEG's SwapClear platform cleared a record 1,941 trillion dollars in interest rate swap notional during fiscal 2025. This high-volume clearing generates substantial incremental margins because platform running costs are largely fixed. This clearing volume represents a highly durable franchise because SwapClear clears over 90% of the global interest rate swap market.

SwapClear Revenue Share Restructuring

7.
Financial restructuring: LSEG permanently restructured its post-trade economics by purchasing an increased share of SwapClear's revenue surplus from partner banks. Under this contract amendment, the revenue share paid to founding banks was reduced from 30% to 15% in 2025. The bank share was further reduced to 10% from fiscal 2026 onward to maximize LSEG's economic capture.
8.
Margin enhancement: LSEG's revised SwapClear agreement immediately lowered group cost of sales by 38% to 87 million pounds in first-half 2026. This retroactive agreement contributed an additional 140 basis points of adjusted EBITDA margin expansion during the same period. This structural margin step-up will largely reverse in fourth-quarter 2026 due to the timing of historical recognition.
9.
Contrary evidence: LSEG experienced a major negative working capital impact of 400 million pounds on February 26, 2026, showing that contract modifications carry near-term cash frictions. This outflow was driven by a 90 million pound reduction in the pay accrual for SwapClear partner banks. This working capital drag suggests that the structural margin gains from the partner-bank buyout were offset by transitional cash outflows.

Of the guided margin expansion, the SwapClear contract contribution accounted for 30 basis points in fiscal 2026. Peer Benchmarking and CME Group

11.
Structural benchmarking: CME Group exhibits a highly transaction-focused clearing model that delivers structurally superior operating leverage compared to LSEG. The US-based derivatives marketplace operates with exceptionally high incremental margins of 90% or more as contract volumes expand. This allows volume surges to drop directly to the operating line during volatile trading cycles.
12.
Operating margins: CME Group achieved an adjusted operating margin of 69.4% in third-quarter 2026, driven by persistent interest rate volatility. The exchange's clearing and transaction fees fluctuate directly with contract volumes, which hit a record 71.5 million contracts in late 2026. This transaction-heavy structure makes CME Group highly levered to macroeconomic uncertainty and geopolitical rate debates.
13.
Margin durability: CME Group leverages massive capital efficiencies and over 95 billion dollars of daily margin offsets to secure its clearing moat. This deep liquidity pool enables CME Group to maintain an EBITDA margin of 72.2% under its base-case projections. This asset-light model contrasts with LSEG's capitalintensive desktop business, which demands constant technology reinvestment.
14.
Defensive hedges: CME Group is positioned to sustain trading volumes even as global central banks shift toward rate-easing cycles. This volume resilience is primarily driven by interest rate volatility and curve flattening that spur portfolio repositioning. Consequently, hedging demand remains highly robust despite tough volume comparisons against prior peak interest rate cycles.
15.
Other side of the story: LSEG's Markets division possesses a highly resilient profile because only a portion of clearing revenues is directly linked to transaction volumes. Approximately half of LCH's clearing revenues are structured as flat-fee subscriptions, creating a natural buffer during volume downturns. This high share of recurring fees means LSEG's operating leverage is less volatile than CME's transaction-heavy model during cyclical normalization.

Section 4 – Integration Precedents and Large Scale M&A Cost Synergies

Section summary
  1. Large scale market infrastructure integrations repeatedly demonstrate that corporate and technology consolidations drive substantial operational efficiencies.
  2. Peer integration histories reveal that post acquisition margin trajectories depend heavily on aggressive technology migration and legacy platform decommissioning.

Refinitiv Integration Outcomes

1.
Significant outperformance: LSEG originally targeted 350 million pounds of run-rate cost synergies by 2025, but later upgraded the goal to at least 400 million pounds. Ultimately, the group delivered 562 million pounds of run-rate cost synergies by the end of 2024, representing an outperformance of the upgraded target two years ahead of schedule. This accelerated delivery supported near-term EBITDA margin expansion during the critical transition phase.
2.
Successful rationalization: LSEG achieved its savings by consolidating its corporate property footprint, closing redundant data centres, and renegotiating contract terms with strategic vendors. Jefferies reported that corporate functions represented 50% of targeted savings, while technology and property represented 30% and 20% respectively. However, these initiatives required total integration costs of 1.4 billion pounds to be fully realized through 2025.
3.
Elevated complexity: Refinitiv historically exhibited lower operating margins than standard industry benchmarks due to legacy systems and operational duplication. Former LSEG strategy executives noted that while backoffice integration was deep, certain product lines required full enterprise technology replatforming before achieving efficiency. Consequently, dual-running costs during the initial cloud migration phase temporarily restricted immediate realization of structural operating leverage.
4.
Strategic transition: LSEG is transitioning towards a product-led operating model that targets an 80% internal engineering resource mix. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported that LSEG exited 2,200 external contractors in 2024 while adding 800 internal staff to upgrade capabilities. This resourcing transition is expected to generate significant ongoing cost savings by reducing reliance on expensive third-party software developers.

S&P Global and IHS Markit

5.
Substantial delivery: S&P Global completed the 43.5 billion dollar merger on February 28, 2022, initially targeting 480 million dollars in cost synergies. Management subsequently upgraded the target to 600 million dollars and delivered 619 million dollars in run-rate savings by the end of 2023. This represents successful execution of above-target merger run-rate synergies ahead of schedule.
6.
Disciplined management: S&P Global reported that integration efforts helped expand group operating margins by approximately 125 basis points annually since 2022. Cost savings were primarily driven by office consolidations in two dozen locations, procurement rationalization, and combining duplicated IT infrastructure. Consequently, the group successfully mitigated elevated inflationary headwinds by pulling forward key merger and integration targets.
7.
Uneven progress: S&P Global faced integration challenges within its Market Intelligence division, which experienced slower subscription revenue growth in high-growth solutions. Former directors noted that while backoffice consolidation was rapid, combining disparate datasets across the Capital IQ platform required extensive product rationalization. These transitional frictions suggest that achieving full revenue synergies from IHS Markit takes longer than back-office consolidation.

Intercontinental Exchange Mortgage Integrations

8.
Accelerated consolidation: ICE acquired Ellie Mae for 11 billion dollars on September 4, 2020, delivering run-rate cost synergies of 50 to 65 million dollars by year three. Subsequently, ICE completed its 11.8 billion dollar acquisition of Black Knight on September 5, 2023. Management exited 2025 with 230 million dollars in annualized synergies, exceeding the updated target of 200 million dollars.
9.
Rapid optimization: ICE rapidly consolidated back-office operations and migrated acquired entities onto its proprietary technical infrastructure. Former executives indicated that acquired mortgage platforms achieved immediate cost reductions by eliminating duplicative corporate functions. This disciplined approach allows ICE to target 50 to 60 percent margins in its mortgage technology business.
10.
Cyclical volatility: ICE experienced a severe cyclical downturn in US mortgage origination volumes that temporarily diluted near-term transaction growth. Morgan Stanley reported that pro-forma integration risks and elevated interest rates created near-term EPS dilution during the initial transition. These cyclical headwinds show that execution risks post large M&A can obscure structural cost synergy benefits during macro downturns.

Deutsche Börse and SimCorp Integration

11.
Strategic expansion: Deutsche Börse completed the acquisition of SimCorp on September 29, 2023, for a total cash consideration of 3.9 billion euros. Management established a new Investment Management Solutions segment, targeting 90 million euros in run-rate EBITDA synergies. This target is split between 55 million euros in pre-tax run-rate cost synergies and 35 million euros in revenue synergies.
12.
High cost: Deutsche Börse incurred 46 million euros in synergy realization costs during 2024 to support the integration of SimCorp. Jefferies reported that SimCorp was acquired at a premium valuation of 28 times EBITDA, creating near-term EPS dilution before synergies materialized. Consequently, the group experienced higher interest expenses on new bond issues that affected the financial result of the transaction.
13.
Complementary development: Deutsche Börse achieved strong recurring revenue momentum by combining SimCorp and Axioma into a unified software solutions offering. Management reported that this integration drove the highest ARR growth for Axioma since its acquisition. This commercial success suggests that combining complementary data assets can accelerate top-line growth and support future valuation rerating.

Euronext Borsa Italiana Case Study

14.
Transformational acquisition: Euronext completed the transformational acquisition of Borsa Italiana Group in April 2021 for a cash consideration of 4.3 billion euros. Management initially targeted 60 million euros in runrate EBITDA synergies, which was subsequently upgraded to 115 million euros. Ultimately, Euronext delivered 121 million euros in synergies by Q3 2024, representing twice the amount targeted at closing.
15.
Euronext integration synergy targets and achievements including Borsa Italiana Internalized clearing: Euronext migrated Borsa Italiana cash and derivatives markets to its proprietary Optiq trading platform within three years of acquisition. Management reported that the expansion of Euronext Clearing to all listed derivatives in September 2024 marked the final integration milestone. This single technology stack migration allowed Euronext to decommission third-party systems and maintain high operating margins.
16.
Contrary evidence: Euronext experienced significant integration-related salaries and double-run costs that temporarily weighed on operating profitability during the multi-year transition. The group recorded total integration and double-run costs of 26 million euros in 2025 and 46.9 million euros in 2024. These ongoing transitional expenses show that large-scale infrastructure migrations carry elevated dual-running costs before structural synergies are fully captured.
17.
Systematic execution: Euronext completed its Borsa Italiana integration while spending 110.8 million euros in implementation costs, significantly below its initial 160 million euro guidance. This disciplined cost control helped Euronext expand its adjusted EBITDA margin to a record 66.5% in late 2026. These precedents demonstrate that rigorous integration execution is highly feasible and directly translates into enhanced earnings power.
18.
Other side of the story: Large-scale data and exchange acquisitions can introduce significant integration complexity and financial friction that offset near-term cost synergy benefits. S&P Global, ICE, and Deutsche Börse all incurred substantial transaction and restructuring costs that initially diluted adjusted earnings. Furthermore, expert insights highlight that cultural clashes and leadership transitions during integrations can lead to a loss of key institutional knowledge. Therefore, investors must closely monitor integration timelines because execution delays or cyclical downturns can significantly impair expected operating leverage.

Section 5 – Cloud Migration, In-Housing, and Capex Trajectories

Section summary
  1. LSEG targets structural margin expansion by driving its engineering in-housing share toward eighty percent to offset persistent technology labor cost inflation.
  2. The ten-year partnership with Microsoft involves a minimum cloud spend of two point eight billion dollars that will build as legacy systems migrate. Net read: Declining capital expenditure from ten point two percent of revenues to eight percent aligns with peer history but introduces product delivery execution risks.

Engineering In-Housing Efficiency

1.
Efficient resourcing: LSEG is systematically restructuring its software engineering workforce to enhance internal technical capabilities and reduce third-party developer dependency. The group successfully increased its insourced engineering resource mix to 60% in fiscal 2025 from 49% in 2024. Management continues to target an ultimate eighty percent internal engineering resource mix to drive structural productivity. This personnel transition should significantly lower overall resource costs as a share of revenue.
2.
Successful workforce optimization: LSEG has executed substantial headcount rationalization by replacing expensive technology contractors with permanent staff members. The group reduced its total engineering headcount from 15,101 to 14,244 during 2025. This workforce transformation delivered a net reduction of one thousand engineers by exiting contractors. Such structural changes directly support operating leverage as the company simplifies its product development processes.
3.
Rising labor inflation: LSEG faces notable cost pressures from wage inflation for cloud-native and artificial intelligence specialists. Management acknowledges that cloud and token usage-based costs will rise. However, these technical expenses are expected to be partly offset by engineering insourcing efficiencies and automation. Net personnel savings will ultimately depend on local talent retention dynamics and general compensation movements.
4.
Constrained desktop margins: FactSet's corporate strategy historically experienced lower gross margins compared to peer groups like LSEG. The firm's operating efficiency is highly sensitive to research and development investments. Unlike index-led peers, desktop software providers require continuous capital reallocation to software development to maintain competitiveness. This dynamic makes the desktop model structurally more labor-intensive than scale indices.

Microsoft Cloud Spend Mechanics

5.
Contractual cloud commitment: LSEG entered into a landmark ten-year strategic partnership with Microsoft starting in late 2022. The group contractually committed to a minimum cloud spend of two point eight billion dollars over the term of the agreement. This massive technological investment is designed to architect LSEG's unified data infrastructure. Such a structural shift represents a foundational change in the firm's operational delivery model.
6.
Strategic spending phasing: LSEG's massive Microsoft cloud expenditure is not distributed evenly across the ten- year partnership timeline. Management disclosed that the minimum commitment is strongly weighted towards the second half of the term as operational consumption builds. This deliberate phasing is already incorporated into the company's long-term expense plans. Consequently, the near-term cash impact remains highly manageable as systems migrate.
7.
Accelerated system migration: LSEG is systematically transferring its core technological assets onto Microsoft Azure to eliminate legacy running costs. This cloud-native architecture allows the firm to reduce operational complexity by consolidating legacy technologies. The successful migration will eventually enable the decommissioning of multiple siloed databases. This technical integration is expected to deliver significant cost synergies over time.
8.
