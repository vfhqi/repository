Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The multi-year replacement cycle from 2026 to 2028 offers substantial pricing power and EPS growth for leaders like Siemens Healthineers.
  2. Revenue growth remains highly sensitive to hospital solvency pressures and technological maturity across European and American healthcare systems.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Central key question: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector B.6. Medtech, capital goods, with Siemens Healthineers (SHL-DE) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: How large and how long is the hospital replacement cycle for computed tomography scanners and radiotherapy machines in the United States and Europe from 2026 to 2028? What do hospital capital budget surveys, tender data and expert calls since June 2026 say about the age of the installed base, the share of orders going to photon-counting computed tomography and to adaptive radiotherapy machines (Varian Accela and Elekta Evo), the price premium of the new machines, and the reimbursement limits on hospitals buying them? Is Philips losing computed tomography market share because it has no photoncounting scanner, and is Elekta losing or regaining radiotherapy market share since the Accela launch?"
2.
Accelerating upgrade driver: The clinical demand for high-end diagnostic imaging and advanced cancer treatment systems underpins a major capital upgrade cycle. Morgan Stanley's industry view from September 2026 indicates that order books are expected to remain healthy, driven by elevated procedure volumes, AI-powered innovation, and the ongoing upgrade cycle. For investors, understanding this cycle is critical because it dictates whether manufacturers can sustain price increases and improve their structural margins. Consequently, the speed of order book expansion will serve as the primary catalyst for earnings per share growth over the next three years.
3.
Delayed revenue conversion: A key mechanism of this cycle is the substantial three-year operational lag required to convert equipment orders into recognized revenue. An expert oncologist noted in January 2026 that hospitals face at least a 12-month lag time between initiating a linear accelerator procurement and completing the actual physical installation. According to Jefferies in July 2026, Koninklijke Philips is also experiencing conversion delays across modalities as larger deals and limited installation capacities stretch revenue realization timelines. Thus, orders secured in 2026 will primarily drive earnings per share developments through 2028.

Capital Allocation and Pricing Power

4.
Premium pricing power: Pricing power represents the primary differentiator of manufacturer margins during this upgrade window. Morgan Stanley notes that the rapid rollout of photon-counting computed tomography systems by Siemens Healthineers could significantly boost high-margin sales. This allows premium innovators to command steep price increases over legacy equipment, unless hospital purchasing budgets restrict high-end procurement. This technological gap determines whether Siemens Healthineers or competitors like Philips capture the highest-margin capital allocation.
5.
Expanding earnings potential: This replacement cycle directly determines the mid-term earnings trajectory and market valuation of European medtech manufacturers. Jefferies notes that Elekta is relying heavily on order backlog conversion to meet its medium-term sales targets, raising questions about backlog quality and execution. Similarly, Morgan Stanley forecasts that Siemens Healthineers will grow its adjusted EPS from €2.37 in fiscal 2026 to €2.66 by fiscal 2028, driven by the expansion of its high-margin imaging and precision therapy segments. Therefore, the commercial success of next-generation platforms like Varian Accela is central to investor returns.

Solvency and Technological Maturity

6.
Restricted hospital budgets: The financial health of healthcare providers acts as a critical constraint on the size of the replacement opportunity. Morgan Stanley MUFG notes that Japanese hospitals face a continued harsh business environment and serious cash flow challenges, driving demand for medical management support. In the United States, Deutsche Bank highlights that upcoming regulatory changes like 340B could act as a negative catalyst, prompting hospitals to contract their capital budgets in 2027 or 2028. Consequently, deteriorated hospital solvency could prematurely choke off the replacement cycle.
7.
Contrary evidence: In contrast to the optimistic view of a massive upgrade cycle, Accuray's SEC filing in October 2026 reports that reduced budgets and lower capital deployment priority for radiotherapy equipment in the United States have negatively impacted net revenue since fiscal 2024. The company explicitly expects these macroeconomic headwinds and extended customer installation timelines to persist through at least fiscal 2027. This demonstrates that high interest rates and regulatory uncertainty are already negatively impacted net revenue since delaying advanced cancer therapy placements.
8.
Other side of the story: While clinical needs suggest a robust and highly predictable upgrade cycle, the reimbursement landscape and structural cost inflation could severely limit actual hospital adoption rates. Deutsche Bank's interview with a Central Florida hospital executive in October 2026 reveals that while hospitals cannot easily delay urgent MRI and CT replacements, they are actively extending the useful life of other routine capital assets to cope with a 20% surge in construction costs. If inflation remains high, even advanced technologies like photon-counting CT and adaptive radiotherapy may fail to justify their high premium costs, which means manufacturers would struggle to raise prices and actual EPS growth would fall short of current consensus estimates.

Section 2 – Hospital Capital Budgets and the Replacement Cycle

Section summary
  1. The aging installed base of CT scanners and linear accelerators is driving a robust replacement cycle from 2026 to 2028.
  2. US hospitals are prioritizing imaging and oncology upgrades while extending the life of routine assets.
  3. European public procurement rules are shifting toward quality-weighted tenders and regional preferences.

Age of the Installed Base

1.
Aging diagnostic systems: Capital constraints have forced many hospitals to operate diagnostic hardware past its traditional lifecycle. A Deutsche Bank expert call with a Florida hospital CFO on October 8 2026 revealed that their system has very little operational flexibility around MRI and CT equipment because multiple systems are already materially beyond their normal replacement age. This urgent need to replace older units prevents administrators from delaying these advanced acquisitions, despite escalating construction costs. Consequently, this aging installed base underpins a mandatory demand runway that directly supports high-end equipment manufacturer volumes through 2028.
2.
Extended linear lifecycles: The operational lifespan of cancer treatment systems has reached a critical juncture that necessitates prophylactic replacements. An expert radiation oncologist noted on January 1 2026 that the generally accepted lifespan of a linear accelerator is between 10 and 15 years. Many active clinical systems have already reached the 10-year mark, meaning departments must begin replacement discussions immediately to avoid catastrophic system failures. This necessity will drive steady equipment replacement activity over the next three years, supporting long-term backlog execution.
3.
Deferred routine maintenance: Hospitals are selectively deferring non-clinical assets to preserve capital for critical clinical technology. According to a Deutsche Bank research report of October 8 2026, UF Health plans to extend the life of routine capital spend on beds and computers to fund high-priority clinical expansions. This strategic reallocation highlights that while secondary capital assets face budget cuts, core diagnostic imaging and radiation oncology remains heavily protected. This spending resilience insulates diagnostic equipment manufacturers from broader hospital capital conservation measures.

US Capital Spending Priorities

Protected strategic spending: US healthcare systems are aggressively defending strategic revenue-generating projects while cutting operational overhead. A Deutsche Bank report of October 8 2026 indicates that UF Health expects to reallocate funding from routine maintenance toward projects that directly support revenue growth. These priority projects include newly planned imaging centers, additional catheterization labs, and freestanding emergency departments. This rising expenditure trend ensures a highly supportive capital environment for advanced medical technology vendors.
9.
Ambulatory care shift: US hospital systems are shifting capital away from traditional inpatient settings to lower-cost ambulatory centers. Morgan Stanley reported on September 16 2026 that Siemens Healthineers is experiencing a clear trend toward ambulatory care in the United States. This structural transition strongly benefits technologies like helium-free MRI systems, which lower the total cost of ownership by eliminating expensive helium infrastructure. This means that manufacturers with strong outpatient and ambulatory-focused portfolios will enjoy accelerated market share gains.

European Public Tender Frameworks

10.
Harmonizing public tenders: The European Union is executing a massive legislative overhaul to streamline and standardize public healthcare purchasing. On September 9 2026, the European Commission proposed the Public Procurement Act, which will replace three existing procurement directives with a single, directly applicable regulation. This major reform eliminates the administrative friction of divergent national transposition rules across 27 member states. For global medical equipment manufacturers, this unified framework will dramatically reduce compliance costs and improve bid predictability.
11.
Quality weighted scoring: Evolving European procurement rules are actively de-emphasizing low-cost bids in favor of clinical and technological value. A Clifford Chance briefing on September 15 2026 noted that the proposed European regulation reduces the proportion of contracts awarded on price alone. Under the proposed Public Procurement Act, quality, sustainability, and security must account for at least 30% of the contract award evaluation weighting. This structural shift allows high-end innovators like Siemens Healthineers to command premium pricing in public tenders.
12.
Fostering regional supply: European regulators are introducing protectionist bidding rules designed to strengthen regional manufacturing resilience. A Paul Weiss alert on September 17 2026 explained that the proposed Public Procurement Act introduces a 50% European content threshold in strategic sectors. Under these rules, public buyers are empowered to reject bids where more than half of the value originates from non-covered third countries. This protectionist shift creates substantial supply chain hurdles for manufacturers relying heavily on Chinese components.

A regulatory report from October 19 2026 noted that under the International Procurement Instrument, the EU excludes bidders of Chinese origin from public contracts valued at €5 million or more. This measure also enforces a strict 50% concentration cap on Chinese-sourced components for any successful bid. This regulatory barrier insulates European incumbents like Philips and Siemens Healthineers from low-cost Chinese competition in their domestic markets.
14.
Stricter origin screening: European courts are enforcing rigorous compliance checks to prevent non-EU companies from bypassing procurement restrictions. Regulatory news from July 27 2026 reported that the Lyon Administrative Court ruled that contracting authorities must conduct genuine diligence into the commercial substance of bidders registered in the EU. This precedent blocks Chinese entities with minimal local operations from using European shell subsidiaries to bid on major tenders. Consequently, this legal tightening further secures the European market for established domestic medtech manufacturers.

Section 3 – Computed Tomography Dynamics and Photon-Counting Adoption

Section summary
  1. Siemens Healthineers dominates the computed tomography market through its established photon-counting scanner portfolio.
  2. GE HealthCare poses a credible threat after securing dual regulatory approvals for its competing deep-silicon system.
  3. Koninklijke Philips is losing market share due to its lack of a proprietary photon-counting scanner.

Photon Counting Market Adoption

1.
Established technology leadership: Siemens Healthineers has maintained a strong first-mover advantage in next-generation diagnostic imaging by establishing multiple commercial pricing tiers. Morgan Stanley's report of September 16 2026 notes that the manufacturer launched its photon-counting computed tomography platform five years ago and has since introduced two additional price points to broaden market access. This multi-tier strategy ensures that the company can capture a wider demographic of hospital purchasing departments.
2.
Accelerating order books: The commercial success of these high-end imaging systems is increasingly reflected in the manufacturer's diagnostic backlog. Deutsche Bank's research note of August 18 2026 reports that photon-counting systems now account for approximately 30% of total CT equipment order volume. This robust demand supports the company's long-term revenue targets because these advanced orders will convert into recognized sales over the next three years.
3.
US market share gains: Strategic product differentiation has successfully closed the competitive gap between the leading diagnostic equipment vendors in North America. Deutsche Bank's report of August 18 2026 highlights that clinical differentiation from photoncounting scanners has boosted the company's CT market share to levels on par with its historically dominant magnetic resonance imaging position. Consequently, this commercial momentum directly supports elevated earnings per share growth through fiscal 2028 unless macroeconomic barriers restrict capital allocations.

GE HealthCare Competitive Threat

4.
Gaining regulatory clearance: GE HealthCare is executing a rapid commercial rollout of its competing diagnostic hardware to capture market share in premium academic medical centers. GE HealthCare's press release on August 31 2026 announced that its Photonova Spectra system achieved CE Mark in Europe, expanding international access to its proprietary Deep Silicon detector technology. This milestone follows its Japanese regulatory approval and United States FDA 510(k) clearance in March 2026. This dual-region regulatory success will catalyze competitive bids against established market incumbents.
5.
Backlog conversion potential: High-end clinical accounts are expected to transition their purchasing commitments to the newly approved deep-silicon platform. An expert call with a former GE HealthCare Strategic Development Leader on June 23 2026 predicted significant backlog conversions to Photonova Spectra in the premium market segment. The expert noted that GE HealthCare historically lost share because Siemens Healthineers had its scanner available, but the new platform will help GE HealthCare regain market share in high-end academia. Consequently, this competitive re-entry will dilute the monopoly pricing power previously enjoyed by the market leader.
6.
Incremental revenue contribution: The commercialization of next-generation imaging platforms represents a direct driver of mid-term organic growth. GE HealthCare's press release on August 31 2026 reported that Photonova Spectra is the result of more than $5 billion in innovation investment. The manufacturer explicitly expects this wave of transformational products to drive one to two percent revenue growth. This incremental contribution will directly support margin expansion as these premium systems carry higher gross profitability.

Philips Competitive Share Erosion

7.
Losing core imaging share: Koninklijke Philips is facing severe competitive headwinds in its core diagnostic divisions due to a delayed high-end technology roadmap. Jefferies' equity research report of September 29 2026 reiterates a Hold rating on Philips, pointing directly to persistent share loss in its Imaging segment as a key operational challenge. This competitive displacement is exacerbated by lingering quality issues and a slow recovery in key regional markets. Thus, the lack of a proprietary photon-counting scanner threatens to permanently depress the company's mid-term earnings potential.
8.
Persistent margin pressure: Elevated competitive intensity is compounding inflationary headwinds to depress the profitability of traditional capital equipment portfolios. Rothschild and Co Redburn's report of July 30 2026 notes that they remain cautious on Philips' core Imaging business where the company faces intense competitive pressure in CT and magnetic resonance imaging. This competitive threat, alongside rising freight and component costs, continues to challenge the division's margins. Consequently, without a highmargin premium CT product, Philips will struggle to meet its consensus margin targets.
9.
Regional market share decline: The competitive pressure on legacy diagnostic portfolios is particularly acute in mature European healthcare networks. An expert interview with a CT Modality Manager at GE HealthCare on August 21 2026 revealed that Philips faces severe challenges in CT innovation and is actively losing CT market share in the DACH region. Because public and private providers are prioritizing advanced imaging capabilities, Philips' reliance on traditional hardware limits its participation in high-value replacement tenders.
10.
Differentiated defense strategy: In response to competitive erosion, Philips is executing a defensive strategy focused on selective participation in high-end tenders. Jefferies' report of July 28 2026 reports that Philips is adopting a highly targeted go-to-market approach focusing exclusively on differentiated technologies like its new Spectral CT Verida and helium-free magnetic resonance systems. By avoiding the highly commoditized low-end segments, the company hopes to preserve its remaining market share and defend its division margins. However, this selective approach restricts its total addressable market size.

Clinical Value Propositions

11.
First line cardiac imaging: The clinical demand for high-end CT systems is supported by evolving international diagnostic guidelines. An expert cardiologist at an academic health system noted on October 9 2026 that clinical guidelines have shifted toward CCTA as a first-line test for patients with intermediate risk chest pain. This clinical protocol, combined with the faster temporal resolution and lower radiation of photon-counting systems, makes comprehensive cardiac imaging highly feasible in a broader patient population. This guideline transition ensures a long-term volume driver for premium scanners.
12.
Superior diagnostic accuracy: Technical improvements in image acquisition are directly translating into better clinical outcomes and reduced hospital costs. A clinical trial registration on September 21 2026 notes that photon-counting technology provides higher spatial resolution and reduced coronary calcification blooming artifacts compared to conventional energy-integrating scanners. This technical precision reduces false-positive findings and subsequently decreases the need for costly downstream invasive coronary angiographies. Consequently, these clinical efficiencies help hospitals justify the high procurement costs of premium imaging hardware.
13.
Multidisciplinary clinical utility: Premium scanners are expanding their clinical utility beyond traditional cardiology to pediatric and oncology care. Jefferies' research note of October 6 2026 reports that because of their fast throughput and low radiation, photon-counting scanners can provide near-PET-like diagnostic images for pediatric and cancer patients. This multispecialty utility allows hospitals to maximize system utilization rates across multiple clinical departments.

Cost and Adoption Constraints

14.
Prohibitive price premium: Despite compelling clinical benefits, the high price premium of next-generation hardware remains a critical barrier to widespread market adoption. Jefferies' report of October 6 2026 notes that the acquisition cost of a photon-counting scanner is approximately ten times a traditional computed tomography scanner. The broker argues that a lower cost of about two times the price of a traditional system is necessary to trigger broader clinical adoption. Therefore, these capital constraints will restrict initial sales to well-funded academic medical centers.
15.
Contrary evidence: In contrast to the view that photon-counting systems will immediately dominate high-end capital budgets, hospital purchasing surveys show that price remains the primary determinant of procurement decisions. Morgan Stanley's Japanese hospital survey published on October 1 2026 reports that 70% of hospital administrators identify equipment price as the most important factor when purchasing a photon-counting system. This economic prioritization suggests that high-end clinical adoption will be significantly slower than manufacturers anticipate, especially if hospital cash flows deteriorate.
16.
Other side of the story: While Siemens Healthineers and GE HealthCare compete fiercely at the premium end of the computed tomography market, broader market demand may remain concentrated in mid-range systems due to extreme cost constraints. Morgan Stanley's hospital survey of October 1 2026 highlights that sixtyfour slice CT scanners currently account for 36% of the installed base, while photon-counting scanners represent a mere 1% of active units. If high cost premiums continue to restrict advanced purchases, low-cost and mid-range systems will continue to dominate hospital capital budgets, which means premium manufacturers will struggle to sustain their high gross margins.

Section 4 – Radiotherapy Dynamics and Adaptive Systems

Section summary
  1. Siemens Healthineers holds a dominant lead in the radiotherapy market as Varian's Accela capitalizes on superior software integration.
  2. Elekta continues to lose market share because of delayed US regulatory clearances for its Evo adaptive platform.
  3. Financial hurdles and lack of dedicated reimbursement severely restrict hospital adoption of high-cost systems like MR-Linacs.

Commercial Battle in Adaptive Platforms

1.
Growing competitive disparity: The commercial battle in adaptive radiotherapy shows Siemens Healthineers outperforming its primary European rival. Siemens Healthineers reported that Varian achieved 9.3% comparable revenue growth in its third quarter ending June 30 2026, as noted in its financial release on July 31 2026. This contrast is sharp compared to Elekta, which reported a 2% decline in constant-currency net sales for its first quarter ending July 31 2026, as detailed in its interim report on August 27 2026. This widening gap suggests that Varian is capturing a larger share of active replacement budgets.
2.
Persistent share erosion: Elekta struggles to keep pace with broader market demand due to localized growth execution challenges. During Elekta's earnings call on August 27 2026, the chief executive officer admitted that the company is still not growing with the global radiotherapy market, which is expanding at approximately 6%. Because Varian maintains more than 60% radiotherapy market share as cited by Morgan Stanley on September 21 2026, Elekta's slower growth confirms it continues to lose share to the market leader. This means Elekta's mid-term earnings per share expansion is highly dependent on a rapid sales turnaround.
3.
Regulatory execution delays: Delayed regulatory approvals in the United States hinder Elekta's ability to effectively compete with Varian's established platforms. An expert interview with a former Vice President at Elekta on September 1 2026 revealed that while the Elekta Evo linear accelerator is cleared, its online adaptive software component remains uncleared by the Food and Drug Administration. This regulatory bottleneck prevents Elekta from shipping fully functional adaptive systems to US customers, whereas Varian has continuous clearances for its Ethos and Accela systems. Unless these clearances materialize quickly, Elekta will struggle to regain premium US market share.
4.
Superior clinical positioning: Evolving clinical protocols in radiation oncology are driving rapid demand for highly precise adaptive systems. A Morgan Stanley research report on September 28 2026 noted that online adaptive radiotherapy dramatically improves the standard of care by allowing real-time replanning during treatment. This clinical shift toward hypofractionation is accelerating because it delivers higher radiation doses in fewer sessions. Consequently, manufacturers that can provide seamless online adaptive workflows will capture a disproportionate share of the multi-year hospital replacement cycle.

This ecosystem lock-in makes it extremely difficult for Elekta to displace Varian at legacy accounts.

This margin deficit highlights Elekta's weaker operating leverage compared to Varian's larger, highly integrated global service base.
7.
Growing clinical optimism: Some oncology professionals believe Elekta's newly introduced platform possesses technical advantages that could reverse its historical market share losses. A California professor of radiation oncology remarked on September 22 2026 that Elekta Evo is well-positioned to compete against Varian's Halcyon because of superior cone-beam computed tomography imaging quality. This suggests that Elekta has technical advantages that could reverse its market share losses if it executes successfully on US clinical education.

Contrary evidence: Despite clinical enthusiasm for next-generation systems, hospital purchasing heads remain highly cautious about adopting adaptive workflows due to severe operational bottlenecks. If these staffing constraints persist, hospitals will continue to prefer conventional C-arm accelerators that offer higher raw patient throughput.

This prohibitive cost forces hospitals to undergo extensive financial deliberations before purchasing, which slows sales cycles. This capital friction means that MR-Linacs will remain restricted to well-funded academic institutions rather than entering the mainstream clinical market.

Although some clinics repurpose existing billing codes to offset planning costs, this regulatory gap discourages risk-averse community hospitals from making large capital commitments. This structural headwind will continue to suppress Elekta's high-margin product revenues unless payers establish standardized payment pathways.
11.
Severe operational downtime: Mechanical complexity and frequent maintenance requirements limit the clinical throughput and economic viability of advanced magnetic resonance systems. A former business operations manager at Varian noted on August 28 2026 that MR-guided systems suffer from significant operational downtime and slower throughput compared to conventional accelerators. The expert explained that hospitals cannot justify long installation periods and constant technical issues when trying to manage high patient volumes. This operational vulnerability means that conventional C-arm machines will continue to dominate the global capital replacement cycle.
12.
Cautionary past case: Historical failures in the high-cost radiotherapy sector demonstrate the extreme financial risks of commercializing advanced technology without sufficient scale. Bernstein Research noted on June 19 2026 that the premium manufacturer ViewRay suffered severe financial strain and ultimately filed for bankruptcy in 2023 because of limited scale and cost disadvantages, despite possessing highly competitive technology. This past case suggests that smaller specialty players face immense default risks if they over-allocate development resources to niche hardware platforms.

side of the story: While Varian currently dominates the US market through its parent Siemens Healthineers, Elekta's aggressive cost-restructuring and strategic diagnostic partnerships could successfully insulate its margins and restore organic growth. Section 5 – Price Premiums and Reimbursement Limits

Section summary
  1. High equipment pricing premiums face intense hospital procurement scrutiny as capital budgets tighten.
  2. The complete lack of dedicated reimbursement codes severely restricts widespread clinical adoption of adaptive radiotherapy.
  3. Existing Medicare SBRT payment structures incentivize complex therapy adoption and support manufacturer margin expansion.

Technology Price Premiums

1.
Prohibitive pricing hurdles: High equipment acquisition costs for next-generation photon-counting CT systems represent a severe barrier for hospital purchasing departments. An expert from University Radiology on September 16 2026 noted that premium photon-counting scanners command a steep price of $1.20 million or more, representing a substantial premium compared to a standard $400,000 computed tomography scanner. Consequently, this premium pricing restricts initial placement activity to wealthy academic centers, which limits the immediate volume contribution to Siemens Healthineers and GE HealthCare imaging revenues.
2.
Elevating capital demands: The premium pricing of advanced linear accelerators further tests the capital budgets of oncology departments. A DNB Carnegie broker note on October 2 2026 reported that Varian's newly launched Accela platform is expected to cost between $4 million and $5 million, which represents a massive price premium over the legacy TrueBeam system. Because Elekta Evo is positioned as a lower-cost alternative, this pricing pressure creates a distinct competitive split. Consequently, Varian's high prices will restrict near-term sales unless hospitals can prove clear return on investment.
3.
Prohibitive specialty costs: Advanced magnetic resonance guided systems suffer from even steeper pricing premiums that limit their mainstream clinical adoption. An expert interview on September 1 2026 with a former marketing executive at Elekta revealed that the Elekta Unity MR-Linac is three to four times more expensive than a conventional standard linear accelerator. Because hospitals face strict return-on-investment requirements, these multi-million-dollar systems remain largely confined to highly funded research institutions. Thus, Elekta cannot rely on Unity to drive near-term earnings per share expansion without major price concessions.
4.
Contrary evidence: Despite the heavy financial premium of next-generation hardware, some well-funded oncology providers are showing a high willingness to absorb these upfront costs due to rapid throughput gains. Jefferies reported on October 15 2026 that Mass General Brigham approved the purchase of the Varian Accela platform because its significantly shorter beam-on times translate into rapid payback periods. This evidence suggests that C-suite capital approvals remain protected for premium platforms that offer clear clinical and operational capacity benefits.
