EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Steve Hare has delivered strong shareholder returns during his tenure as chief executive, transforming Sage from a legacy software vendor into a high-performing subscription business with 115% total shareholder return since 2018 compared to 45% for the FTSE 100. Sage recorded underlying total revenue of £2,332 mm in FY24 with operating margins recovering to approximately 24% by FY25 as strategic cloud investments reaccelerated highquality recurring revenue, representing substantial expansion from organic revenue growth under 8% at the start of his tenure.

Hare executed a critical strategic pivot by accelerating the cloud-connected migration strategy to move legacy customers seamlessly to modern solutions, shifting corporate focus to high-quality subscription and recurring revenues.

Hare maintains a clear and non-evasive communication style on earnings calls, addressing both performance goals and operational trade-offs directly by dynamically reallocating spend during the year to maximize the tradeoff between top-line growth and margin expansion. The chief executive established high guidance credibility by consistently meeting or exceeding public organic revenue growth targets over three years, projecting 9% growth for FY25 but actually delivering 10% organic revenue growth to reach £2,513 mm.

Sage attracts wide research coverage from ten named analysts across three major institutional brokers including Morgan Stanley, Jefferies, and Bernstein, with active consultation ensuring at least forty percent of analysts shape the consensus assessment.

Executive remuneration structures balance market competitiveness with performance-based incentives, as Steve Hare received a base salary of £925,000 in FY24 following a 9.9% above-inflationary salary adjustment, representing the first noninflationary adjustment since his November 2018 appointment. Sage enforces strict share deferral rules requiring one-third of any annual bonus to be deferred into Sage shares, with compulsory deferral dropping to 15% once a director meets enhanced corporate shareholding guidelines to prevent short-term decision-making.

Non-executive directors executed substantial open-market purchases to signal confidence, as Lori Mitchell-Keller bought 10,000 ordinary shares on 17 February 2026 at an average price of £8.3937 each, establishing her initial stake and signaling positive sentiment about the undervalued software business. However, Chief Technology Officer Aaron Harris reduced his personal investment by selling 75,000 ordinary shares on 28 May 2026 at £8.5074 per share, realizing approximately £638,055 and reducing his total personal holding by 27.54%, suggesting insiders see limited valuation upside over the near term.

Overall insider transactions show a relatively even distribution with non-executive directors buying 55,000 shares on the market while executive officers sold 111,000 shares, suggesting no unified insider expectation regarding near-term share price movements.

Former employees highlight significant internal operational challenges as Sage faces operational bloat with internal structure overly reliant on external agencies, where managers spend excessive time passing files and messages up and down the hierarchy instead of focusing on direct execution, potentially limiting long-term research and development efficiency. Experts report that while customers showed high interest in registering for new AI tools, the actual software enrollment process felt notably clunky during early launch phases, highlighting that initial software integrations suffered from engineering hurdles despite strong strategic partnerships.

Industry experts and competitors confirm that Sage possesses robust pricing power through strong brand equity, raising software prices by approximately 5.5% annually in September or October every year without experiencing major client attrition, as annual churn rates remain highly stable during these price increases. Hare established his professional career foundation by qualifying as a chartered accountant in 1985 with Ernst & Whinney after completing a Bachelor of Commerce degree from Liverpool University, providing rigorous technical training that defines his corporate trajectory compared to peers who climbed through sales or engineering. The single largest risk to management credibility involves a significant gap between management's optimistic agentic AI narrative and current technical reality, as Sage describes its platform as an always-on agentic finance ecosystem while Bernstein analysts argue that current capabilities remain assistive rather than broadly autonomous across enterprise workflows. Intense mid-market competitive pressure from larger software giants poses ongoing execution risks, as aggressive price promotions from global competitors may force Sage to maintain permanently high marketing spend that would limit expected operating margin expansion over the next three years.
