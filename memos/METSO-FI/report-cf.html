Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Metso has experienced successive C-suite departures concentrated in growth-critical leadership positions, with Chief People Officer Carita Himberg resigning in November 2025 and Chief Growth Officer Claudia Genin resigning in April 2026, both replaced by long-term insiders who may lack the external perspective required to challenge strategic orthodoxy during a pivotal industry transition. This rapid executive churn masks deep integration fatigue and a culture that prioritizes internal consensus over necessary, aggressive restructuring, raising concerns about the organization's capacity to execute on ambitious margin expansion targets while simultaneously integrating multiple bolt-on acquisitions.

The pattern suggests execution bandwidth constraints at precisely the moment when Metso needs to deliver on its upgraded 18% adjusted EBITA margin target by 2028.

The ERP system rollout created significant margin headwinds, including EUR 10 million in direct costs during Q2 2025, and past execution delays and integration friction surrounding the ERP system rollout have historically weighed on cash conversion. Although management completed the implementation in early 2026, the multiyear distraction coincided with critical market share battles in high-pressure grinding rolls and slurry pumps, potentially allowing competitors to solidify customer relationships during Metso's operational hiatus.

Ambitious margin and M&A targets risk execution failure if intense competition in high-margin niches like pumps prevents Metso from scaling its newly acquired businesses, particularly as the company attempts to digest Swiss Tower Mills, Saimu, and MRA Automation simultaneously while stabilizing core operations. Weir Group maintains over 50% market share in slurry pumps while Metso aims to capture market share in pumps and high-pressure grinding rolls, yet the company enters these battles from a structurally weaker position with lower installed base captivity. Unauthorized Chinese non-OEM parts increasingly contest the highmargin aftermarket, threatening the 57% services revenue mix that underpins Metso's margin resilience and differentiation thesis. Unauthorized third-party parts providers increasingly challenge high-margin OEM wear parts market share in mining, and aggressive non-OEM parts penetration and peer-leading capacity expansions at Sandvik could erode Metso's high-margin comminution services market share.

The convergence of these competitive pressures creates a scenario where Metso must simultaneously defend its existing aftermarket base while attacking entrenched rivals in adjacent categories, a two-front war that historically compresses margins when execution stumbles. Metso recorded a net debt-to-EBITDA ratio of 1.3x in Q2 2026, up from 1.2x at end-2025, with net interest-bearing liabilities increasing to EUR 1,214 million, consuming valuable balance sheet capacity at a moment when the company's growth strategy explicitly relies on 1% to 2% annual sales contribution from bolt-on M&A.

Leverage remains comfortably below the group's maximum target ceiling of 1.5x to maintain its Baa2 rating, but the narrowing headroom limits management's ability to pursue transformative acquisitions or weather unexpected working capital shocks. Working capital totaled EUR 1,070 million in Q2 2026, reflecting higher inventory requirements from a 1.10 book-to-bill, suggesting that Metso is building inventory in anticipation of future equipment deliveries that remain vulnerable to project postponements.

Cash flow from operations missed consensus expectations by 17% in the most recent quarter, raising questions about whether the company's vaunted 98% cash conversion rate can withstand simultaneous pressures from aggressive growth investments, working capital builds, and integration-related payables.

Equipment order growth is historically back-end loaded due to board-level decision delays at mining companies, creating a structural mismatch between Metso's near-term capacity investments and the uncertain timing of customer final investment decisions. Complex environmental permitting limits near-term construction impulses for major copper projects, while mining capital expenditures typically take up to two years to react to major metal price changes, and copper mining capex relative to operating cash flow sits at historically low levels.

This delayed conversion dynamic means that even if copper prices remain elevated, Metso's equipment backlog may not translate into revenue and margin delivery until late 2027 or 2028, precisely when consensus expects the company to hit its upgraded profitability targets. Equipment sales growth will materialize primarily in 2027–2028 rather than 2026, creating a dangerous window where the company must sustain elevated operating costs and integration expenses without the corresponding top-line support.

European aggregates markets are recovering more slowly from distributor destocking, while Aggregates revenues exhibit strong seasonality, dropping significantly in Q3 due to weather and holidays. The Aggregates segment contributed meaningful volume during previous cycles but now faces structural headwinds from elevated energy costs and slower infrastructure spending in key European markets. Infrastructure demand is solid in North America but remains somewhat softer in Europe due to fuel costs, creating geographic imbalances that complicate production planning and capacity utilization.

Aggregates order intake grew just 1% to EUR 333 million, missing expectations by 2%, though Aggregates adjusted EBITA margin reached 16.3%, suggesting that management is managing profitability through pricing discipline rather than volume growth. This volumestarved margin defense is unsustainable if European demand continues to languish, as fixed cost absorption will

Metso historically traded at a 30% discount to its engineering peers despite superior organic growth, with the multiple expanding in late 2025 as the market reacted favorably to the updated CMD objectives. This valuation re-rating embeds optimistic assumptions about execution that may prove difficult to justify if any combination of the plausible problem to monitor identified above materializes. Morgan Stanley remains cautious due to limited scope for sequential order beats in late 2026, while Jefferies remains cautious, arguing that competitive M&A landscapes will make Metso's 20% Minerals margin target ambitious.

The consensus positioning has shifted from skeptical to constructive, creating asymmetric downside risk if delivery stumbles prompt a re-evaluation of the company's structural competitive position. A prolonged freeze in mining corporate capex budgets would invalidate the bull case and trigger sharp consensus earnings cuts, while persistent equipment delays and margin compression could occur if localized non-OEM aftermarket competition successfully targets the high-margin comminution install base.

The combination of elevated expectations, execution complexity, and external dependencies creates a fragile setup where relatively modest disappointments could trigger disproportionate multiple compression.

Outliers worth noting

Metso's elite employee engagement score of 62 eNPS in Q2 2026 positions the company in the top 5% of global industrial peers, representing a statistically significant outlier that materially exceeds the typical engineering sector median of 35-45 eNPS and raises questions about whether this cultural strength can be sustained through accelerating M&A integration cycles and the recent wave of C-suite departures spanning CFO, Chief People Officer, and Chief Growth Officer roles.

The company's 98% 12-month rolling cash conversion rate stands as another exceptional outlier when anchored against peer medians, particularly given that Weir Group recorded just 41% free operating cash conversion in H1 2026 and FLSmidth posted negative operating cash flow of DKK 83 million during comparable periods, suggesting Metso has achieved working capital discipline that rivals best-inclass industrials despite managing a 1.10 book-to-bill ratio and strategic inventory builds to support backlog conversion.

Metso's 50% year-on-year surge in Minerals equipment orders during Q2 2026 substantially outpaced the 13% average organic order growth recorded by major equipment peers during the same period, creating a statistically significant positive outlier that either signals genuine market-share capture in grinding and crushing solutions or reflects lumpy project-booking patterns that may normalize in subsequent quarters as large copper and gold mine expansions move through their approval cycles.

Metso's valuation multiple of 17x adjusted 2027 P/E trades in line with the broader engineering peer median despite the company targeting margin expansion to over 18% and delivering superior organic growth, representing an unexplained valuation anomaly when competitors like Sandvik command premium multiples on similar or inferior fundamental trajectories, suggesting either persistent sell-side skepticism about execution credibility or structural market concerns about late-cyclical equipment exposure that have not yet been resolved through the company's strategic pivot toward aftermarket captivity and self-help cost-out programs.
