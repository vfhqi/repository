8.
Price erosion: Premium spirits companies are struggling to maintain pricing power in the United States as consumer trade-down and oversupply pressure retail execution. Deutsche Bank reported on September 3, 2026, that 72% of top spirits SKUs experienced year-on-year price decreases in July. This widespread discounting suggests that organic pricing gains are no longer sufficient to offset volume declines across developed markets. Suppliers are forced to choose between defending price points and maintaining market share as industry competition intensifies.
9.
Operational underperformance: The strategy of lowering retail prices has largely failed to stimulate positive volume trends for the most heavily exposed cognac and whiskey brands. Deutsche Bank reported on September 3, 2026, that 57% of Rémy Cointreau's SKUs experienced the dual headwind of falling prices and falling volumes. This operational underperformance demonstrates that price cuts alone cannot resolve structural consumer moderation trends. Consequently, brand equity is diluted without delivering the volume growth required to support future earnings per share.
10.
Brand investment: Divergent marketing spend strategies are highly likely to extend the performance gap between top-performing distillers and struggling peers. Deutsche Bank reported on October 7, 2026, that Campari increased its brand investment while Diageo and Pernod reduced A&P relative to sales. This spending divergence suggests that Campari is better positioned to capture market share and defend its premium brand equity. Distillers that cut marketing to protect near-term margins risk long-term volume erosion and permanent brand dilution.

Historical De-rating Precedents

11.
Severe de-ratings: Historical industry cycles show that premium spirits valuations can compress aggressively when external regulatory shocks disrupt key growth markets. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted on January 19, 2024, that Diageo's profitability was severely impacted during the Chinese consumption crackdown in 2013- 14 due to its high exposure to premium banquet dining. This precedent suggests that current regulatory threats, such as European tariffs on cognac, could trigger a similar multi-year de-rating. Without geographic diversification, corporate earnings remain highly vulnerable to sudden regulatory shifts.
12.
Multiple compression: Broader macroeconomic recessions historically force distributors to aggressively liquidate inventories, compounding the downward pressure on supplier shipment volumes. Jefferies reported on October 28, 2026, that during the 2014 anti-extravagance period in China, Rémy Cointreau's valuation fell to just 3x its inventory book value. This historical precedent demonstrates that premium spirits multiples are highly vulnerable to sudden distributor inventory corrections during economic downturns. Consequently, relying on inventory value to support stock prices can prove risky when retail demand deteriorates.
13.
Other side of the story: The current valuation discount of the spirits majors may represent a highly attractive entry point rather than a permanent structural decline. Bernstein Research reported on October 12, 2026, that spirits valuations trade near twenty-year lows relative to the broader market. If distributor destocking concludes and underlying consumption normalizes, these depressed multiples could support a powerful re-rating and rapid earnings per share recovery. Therefore, long-term investors who prioritize fundamental value over near-term technical headwinds stand to capture significant upside.
