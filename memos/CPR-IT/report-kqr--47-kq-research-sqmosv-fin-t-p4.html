Campari Operating and Financial Adjustments

1.
Normalized operating definition: Campari defines Adjusted EBIT as the operating result before other operating income and expenses. These excluded operating adjustments comprise items like asset write-downs, restructuring programs, brand impairments, and legal settlements. The group believes these adjustments allow for a more comparable evaluation of underlying operational performance across fiscal periods. This definition helps isolate recurring profitability from transitory shocks.
2.
Adjusted earnings definition: Campari defines Adjusted EPS by dividing Adjusted Group Net Profit by the weighted average number of outstanding shares. The adjusted net profit excludes other operating adjustments, financial adjustments, and put-option or earn-out revaluations. Additionally, the diluted Adjusted EPS figure is calculated to incorporate the dilutive impact of outstanding convertible debt instruments. This metric provides a normalized benchmark for equity investors tracking core profit trends.
3.
Restructuring program tracking: Campari recorded restructuring and reorganisation expenses of €102.6 mm in fiscal year 2024 to fund a three-year cost containment plan. This extensive operational program was designed to optimize the group's organizational structure and support operating margins. In the first half of 2026, restructuring adjustments amounted to €4 mm alongside €7 mm in related cash outflows. The group continues to exclude these organizational rebalancing costs from its adjusted profitability metrics.

Key Specific Non-Recurring Items

4.
Severe asset write-downs: Campari experienced a major operating adjustment of €109 mm in the first half of 2026. This charge was primarily driven by €82 mm in asset impairment losses to align non-strategic disposal groups with expected realisable values. These portfolio streamlining efforts involved write-downs for brands including Trois Rivières, Bisquit&Dubouché, and Cabo Wabo. Normalizing these charges prevents a distortion of the core operating performance reported to the financial market.
5.
Earn-out valuation benefit: Campari benefited from a €49.6 mm positive adjustment to its non-operating expenses in fiscal year 2025. This non-cash credit arose from a downward remeasurement of the contingent earn-out liability related to the Courvoisier acquisition. The valuation reduction reflected updated expectations for the acquired cognac brand's contractual business performance targets. Campari adjusted this remeasurement out of its normalized EPS to avoid inflating core earnings with non-cash accounting adjustments.
6.
Tariff refund treatment: Campari successfully collected a €15 mm refund in the third quarter of 2026 for past US tariffs. The company chose to re-invest these funds while utilizing a portion to offset COGS pressure in the second half of 2026. Rather than treating the refund as a non-recurring adjustment, the cash benefit flows directly through recurring cost lines in the income statement. This treatment helps the group mitigate logistical cost headwinds within its adjusted operating results.
