Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The direct-let rental weakness at Unite has intensified the investment focus on high-tariff university locations.
  2. Stretched leverage and soft regional markets pose severe downside risks to the earnings per share of student housing operators.
  3. The outlook for rent growth and occupancy in the 2027/28 academic year is bifurcated with medium confidence based on robust top-tier UK demand and acute continental shortages.
  4. Soft regional demand in the United Kingdom and elevated balance sheet leverage in Europe represent the primary downside risks to sector earnings per share.

Section summary

  2. Market weakness remains concentrated in secondary regional cities with high supply, while prime high- tariff university locations demonstrate resilient occupancy.

1.

This weakness was driven by a 0.3% decline in revenue per occupied room as the company pivoted to undergraduate bookings. Consequently, management had to implement selective discounting in oversupplied regional cities to secure their occupancy targets. This shift suggests that broader direct-let pricing power is eroding across secondary UK markets.

Depressed valuations: Morgan Stanley reduced its share price target for Unite Group to 560p from 600p following the disappointing trading update. The broker highlighted that the 8.5% year-to-date valuation decline was driven by persistent yield expansion and negative rental growth. Furthermore, there is a clear risk that rising financial expenses will continue to weigh on the group's earnings per share. This downward revision confirms that valuation headwinds are offsetting operational gains across the portfolio.
3.
Trimmed projections: Deutsche Bank adjusted its earnings estimates downward for fiscal years 2027 and 2028 by 4% and 8% respectively. These adjustments reflect the combined impact of accelerated non-core asset disposals and higher marginal interest expenses. However, the broker maintained its buy rating on the company with a revised price target of 570p. This stance indicates that earnings expectations are undergoing a transition rather than a structural collapse.
4.
Subdued recycling: Jefferies maintained its hold rating and a conservative price target of 450p for Unite Group. The analyst pointed out that the company completed £200 million of disposals at an average discount of 6% to book value. To restore investor confidence, the company needs to execute more meaningful sales of purpose-built student accommodation in soft regional locations. These transaction dynamics reveal that asset disposal values remain under pressure from higher interest rates.

Attractive risk-reward: Morgan Stanley maintained an Equal-weight rating on Unite Group because the shares screen cheap relative to historic valuations. The stock trades on a wide 43% discount to its estimated net asset value, which provides a strong buffer against downside risks. Consequently, the current investment thesis remains a medium-term recovery story with limited catalysts over the next twelve months.
