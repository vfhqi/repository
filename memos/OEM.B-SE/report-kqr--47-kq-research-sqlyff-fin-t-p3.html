Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The resolution of OEM International's feedback loops will determine whether its accelerated acquisition pace under EQT ownership can sustainably drive earnings expansion or if working capital constraints and cultural dilution will break the model.

Core Analytical Question

1.
Key Question: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"

Strategic Relevance to EPS

2.
Significant constraints: OEM International faces a critical balance between its working capital requirements and its ambitious acquisition flywheel. The company reported that working capital tied up SEK 308m in Q1-Q3 2026, which severely depressed operating cash flow. If this working capital absorption continues during periods of high organic growth, it will reduce internal funding for acquisitions and force reliance on debt. This dynamic directly threatens the company's historical compounding model and near-term earnings growth potential.
3.
Accelerating growth: EQT's entry as a major owner is expected to shift OEM's strategic focus toward aggressive capital allocation and deal execution. DNB Carnegie argues that underleveraged serial acquirers can substantially accelerate capital deployment through acquisitions to drive multiple expansion. However, entering a highly competitive M&A market risks overpaying for targets or experiencing integration challenges. Consequently, the success of this valuation loop will dictate whether earnings can grow by over fifteen percent annually.

Leadership and Culture Risks

4.
Critical transition: OEM International faces heightened organizational risk due to its upcoming chief executive transition on 31 December 2026. The departure of Jörgen Zahlin, who has led the company since 2000, could disrupt the highly decentralized culture. Henrik Forsberg Schoultz was appointed Deputy CEO in early 2026 to help preserve this entrepreneurial structure. Any cultural dilution or subsidiary manager departures would weaken organic growth and pressure margins over the 2027 to 2029 period.

Macroeconomic Feedbacks

5.
Cyclical drivers: OEM International also remains highly sensitive to macroeconomic restocking and destocking cycles within European industrial markets. Organic sales growth reached fourteen percent in Q3 2026, driven by a record order backlog and strong short-term demand. If this restocking phase reverses into destocking over the 2027 to 2029 period, organic growth will slow, placing more pressure on acquisitions to sustain EPS expansion.
6.
Contrary evidence: OEM International maintains a highly liquid balance sheet with SEK 941m in total liquidity and a net cash position of SEK 178m as of Q3 2026. RedEye notes that this robust financial position suggests the acquisition flywheel remains fully funded despite recent working capital build-up. Therefore, the working capital loop may act as a temporary drag rather than a structural barrier to growth.
7.
Other side of the story: While the transition to EQT ownership and a new CEO introduces cultural and operational risks, it also unlocks a major growth engine. OEM's historically low leverage means that even a minor increase in debt capacity could unleash significant dry powder for accretive acquisitions. If the incoming leadership preserves the decentralized model, the combination of organic strength and accelerated M&A could drive substantial EPS outperformance relative to historical trends.

Section 2 – The Balance Between Working Capital and the Reinvestment Flywheel

Section summary
  1. Rapid organic growth at OEM International currently exerts a balancing force on its cash generation by tying up significant working capital in receivables and inventories.
  2. While this capital absorption temporarily lowers operating cash flow, OEM's net cash position and substantial total liquidity prevent any structural stalling of its acquisition flywheel.
  3. High structural working capital intensity at Bufab contrasts with the asset-light compounding models of Addtech, Lifco, and Indutrade, which maintain superior cash conversion.

OEM Working Capital Dynamics

1.
Weaker cash: OEM International reported that its operating cash flow in the third quarter of 2026 dropped to SEK 88m from SEK 179m in the prior year. This cash reduction occurred despite net sales growing 24% to a record SEK 1,582m, driven by 14% organic growth. Fast organic expansion created a temporary imbalance as growth tied up cash, showing that rapid growth balances near-term cash generation before translating into sustainable earnings per share.
2.
Tied capital: OEM International experienced a working capital outflow of SEK 155m during the third quarter of 2026, according to its interim report. RedEye reported on 30 October 2026 that this tie-up was driven by a SEK 126m increase in receivables across larger units and SEK 55m in inventories. If organic growth continues at this pace, the resulting working capital absorption will reduce internal funding for bolt-on acquisitions and slow the corporate reinvestment pace.

Comparative Peer Intensity

3.

DNB Carnegie reported on 26 October 2023 that Bufab's inventory turnover days historically exceeded 180 days, leading to uneven cash conversion cycles. This extreme inventory load acts as a severe balancing loop, because high capital intensity limits available cash for serial acquisitions unless the company increases debt.

DNB Carnegie noted on 10 October 2023 that high leverage put a short-term halt to Bufab's M&A agenda. RedEye noted on 29 October 2026 that Lifco's low maintenance capital expenditure represents only 1.5% to 2% of sales, which ensures robust free cash flows. This asset-light setup allows the company to fund steady niche acquisitions comfortably while maintaining manageable debt levels.

Section 4 – Market Position and the Supplier-Mandate Feedback Loop

Section summary
  1. OEM International leverages its extensive customer base to act as the preferred outsourced sales channel for over 400 manufacturers.
  2. The supplier-mandate loop reinforces OEM's market position by driving product portfolio expansion and customer lock-in.
  3. While direct disintermediation remains a theoretical risk, exclusive mandates and deep customer customization protect the company's operating margins.

Preferred Channel Dynamics

1.
Robust expansion: OEM International acts as a highly efficient local sales representative for more than 400 specialized industrial component manufacturers. By providing these global suppliers with direct access to 30,000 active industrial clients, the distributor becomes the preferred outsourcing partner in northern Europe. This vast market reach and deep local presence allow the group to strengthen its outsourced sales channel and secure a leading market position over the long term.
2.
Superior integration: OEM International strengthens its customer relationships by offering custom-made component solutions alongside its standard product range. This technical support encourages clients to consolidate their purchases and buy more items from the distributor's extensive portfolio. These overlapping relationships create strong network effects that drive long-term customer lock-in and improve organic growth rates across European markets.
3.
Stable distribution: OEM International manages its operational risk by maintaining a highly diversified supplier structure across its decentralized units. No single supplier represented more than 3% of total sales in 2025, which protects the company against sudden mandate cancellations. This low concentration ensures that the group can maintain stable operating margins and protect earnings per share compounding over the medium term.
4.
Moderate exposure: OEM International maintains a highly resilient trading model when compared to sector peers with different sourcing structures. The distributor avoids carrying competing product lines to ensure absolute loyalty to its primary manufacturers. In comparison, Bufab AB handles over 165,000 individual C-parts from 2,500 suppliers, which results in higher working capital intensity and lower return on capital over the cycle.
5.
Growing network: OEM International leverages its strong local warehouse and logistics infrastructure to make entry into new geographic markets easier. New manufacturers are attracted to the group's established sales network, which acts as a better alternative to launching greenfield operations. This continuous addition of product lines lets the group expand its technical product portfolio and increase market share continuously without increasing overhead costs.

Expanded reach: OEM International increases its market presence by following its clients abroad when they relocate production. The distributor established subsidiaries in China and the Baltic States to serve Nordic customers who moved their manufacturing facilities. This proactive geographic expansion allows the company to protect its supplier relationships and maintain its role as an essential distribution partner.
