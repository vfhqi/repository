  1. Understanding Hexpol's underlying earnings per share requires a meticulous analysis of currency, debt costs, tax rates, and purchase price allocations.

Core Investment Question

Financial net and cost of debt: what are Hexpol's facility terms, interest rates and maturities, and how will the financial net move as net debt rises toward the 2.5x net debt to EBITDA ceiling set in November 2025, including the funding of Vipa Group and Symplast? Translation and Leverage Dynamics

This strategic pivot will transmit rising interest expenses through the financial net as the company funds the acquisitions of Vipa Group and Symplast. Consequently, these borrowing costs will act as a direct drag on reported net income, offsetting some of the operating profit growth from these newly consolidated businesses.

The integration of Kabkom and the pending acquisition of Symplast in Turkey introduce local tax volatility and cash repatriation challenges. Section 2 – Currency Translation and Forex Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. Hexpol faces substantial translation volatility from its unhedged currency structure as a majority of profits originate abroad.
  2. Quantitative analysis shows operating profit is highly sensitive to the US dollar and European euro swings against the Swedish krona.

Translation Exposure Mechanics

1.
Localized operations: Hexpol operates a localized business model because most sales and purchases are executed in local currencies. This operational design naturally limits transaction risks but leaves the group heavily exposed to unhedged translation FX effects. Consequently, the primary currency impact on reported earnings per share stems from translating foreign subsidiaries' accounts into Swedish kronor.
2.
Significant sensitivity: Hexpol's annual sensitivity analysis reveals that a 10% change in the Swedish krona against all major currencies impacts sales by SEK 1,811 million. This same currency swing alters operating profit by SEK 251 million and shareholders' equity by SEK 1,880 million. The sensitivity is heavily concentrated in the US dollar, which accounts for SEK 164 million of the operating profit exposure. This high foreign currency exposure directly influences the volatility of the group's consolidated net income.
3.
Euro translation: Hexpol's European business introduces secondary translation risks that are primarily tied to the euro. Within the group's currency sensitivity framework, a 10% shift in the EUR/SEK exchange rate affects operating profit by SEK 103 million. This exposure is lower than the US dollar risk of SEK 164 million but remains a critical driver of European compounding results. Any persistent euro weakness relative to the Swedish krona will therefore depress consolidated operating profit.
4.
Historical drag: Hexpol reported sales of SEK 19,324 million in fiscal year 2025, which included negative currency translation effects of SEK 1,051 million. Over the same annual period, exchange rate fluctuations dragged operating profit down by SEK 160 million. This compares to a minor negative currency effect of only SEK 12 million on operating profit in fiscal year 2024. These historical swings demonstrate how quickly Swedish krona volatility can erode reported operating performance.
5.
Sequential headwinds: Hexpol faced continued currency headwinds during the first nine months of fiscal year 2026 as translation effects reduced adjusted operating profit by SEK 83 million. In the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 alone, translation effects negatively impacted operating profit by SEK 74 million. Although negative currency effects moderated to SEK 21 million in the second quarter of fiscal year 2026, they continued to dilute earnings per share. These persistent headwinds illustrate why the group's earnings path remains highly dependent on external currency trends.
6.
Quarterly volume variance: Hexpol's third quarter of fiscal year 2026 experienced a mild positive currency shift that added 2% or SEK 12 million to operating profit. This stands in sharp contrast to the massive SEK 74 million negative translation impact recorded in the first quarter of the same year. These sharp quarterly variations illustrate that translation mechanics can quickly swing from a heavy headwind to a modest tailwind within a single fiscal year. Investors must therefore monitor sequential exchange rate volatility closely when projecting quarterly earnings per share.

Macro Forecasts for Key Crosses

7.
Nordea projections: Nordea FX strategists reported spot rates of 10.03 for USD/SEK and 11.10 for EUR/SEK in mid-November 2026. The bank forecasts the USD/SEK rate to reach 10.32 by mid-2027 before depreciating to 9.29 by the end of 2028. Meanwhile, the EUR/SEK rate is projected to remain flat at 11.15 from mid- 2027 through the end of 2028. This outlook implies that USD-driven translation tailwinds will peak in mid-2027 before reversing into a major drag.

Peer Translation Hedging Models

This choice reflects the fact that Trelleborg's earnings are largely generated outside Sweden, exposing it to significant home-currency translation volatility. A sensitivity analysis shows that translating Trelleborg's 2024 earnings to 2025 average rates would have reduced EBITA by SEK 312 million. This operational vulnerability mirrors Hexpol's own sensitivity of SEK 251 million to a 10% currency shift.
11.
Swiss translation: EMS-Chemie Holding AG operates with an unhedged home-currency translation model that faces severe Swiss franc headwinds. The Swiss franc has remained exceptionally strong, which pressures EMS-Chemie's international sales when translated back into Swiss francs. This model outperforms only when global currencies strengthen against the home currency, a situation that has rarely occurred for Swissbased operations. Unlike Swedish peers who benefit from a weak krona, Swiss exporters face structural translation penalties.
12.
Peer performance: Trelleborg's unhedged translation structure resulted in a negative exchange rate impact of SEK 329 million on EBITA in fiscal year 2025. This negative FX movement reduced earnings per share by SEK 0.38 in the fourth quarter of 2025 alone. Similarly, Hexpol reported a currency translation drag of SEK 160 million on its operating profit during the same fiscal year. These parallel impacts show that both Swedish polymer compounding peers suffer synchronized translation headwinds when the krona strengthens.
13.
Comparative sensitivities: Trelleborg's translation risk is heavily weighted toward the US dollar, which accounted for SEK 770 million of its total SEK 1,870 million sales exposure in 2025. This closely aligns with Hexpol's exposure profile where the US dollar accounts for SEK 971 million of sales exposure out of a SEK 1,811 million total. Both companies exhibit almost identical translation vulnerabilities, with approximately half of their currency sensitivities tied to North American operations. This operational alignment means both peers will experience nearly identical translation tailwinds during periods of US dollar strength.
14.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie revised its adjusted earnings per share forecasts for Hexpol downward by 3% in early 2026. This downward revision was primarily driven by FX assumptions that incorporated a stronger Swedish krona against the US dollar. This forecast adjustment suggests that sell-side consensus may be overestimating currency translation tailwinds if the krona recovers faster than anticipated.
15.
Other side of the story: Hexpol's unhedged translation model could deliver exceptional earnings per share accretion if the Swedish krona depreciates further against the US dollar. While DNB Carnegie warns that a stronger krona will drag earnings downward, Nordea's flat EUR/SEK projection of 11.15 through 2028 indicates a stable buffer for European sales. If US interest rates remain elevated, the resulting USD/SEK strength will easily override local volume weakness. Therefore, Hexpol's translation exposure represents a highly levered play on a weaker home currency rather than operational underperformance.

Variable financing: Hexpol utilizes repayment-free bilateral loans that carry variable interest rates adjusted for one-month and three-month periods. The group also manages a commercial paper program where outstanding volumes reached 5,149 MSEK in September 2026 compared to 4,082 MSEK in September 2025. This debt mix keeps borrowing costs low during rate-cutting cycles but exposes the group to immediate interest rate volatility during inflationary spikes.

Management reported that a one percentage point increase in market interest rates on closing liabilities for 2025 would reduce pre-tax earnings by 43 MSEK. Section 4 – Amortisation, Depreciation and M&A Asset Valuation

Section summary
  1. Non-cash purchase price allocation amortization creates a significant divergence between Hexpol's reported GAAP earnings and underlying cash-generating profitability.
  2. Benchmarking against peer Arkema reveals that extensive acquisition strategies commit substantial parts of operating income to ongoing intangible asset write-downs.

Non-Cash Purchase Price Allocations

1.
Significant adjustments: Hexpol separates its operating earnings into distinct metrics to isolate the non-cash effects of purchase price allocations. The group reported a third-quarter fiscal year 2026 EBIT of 651 MSEK alongside a higher EBITA of 700 MSEK. This variance reflects a non-cash amortization charge of 49 MSEK during the quarter. This drag represents a persistent gap between reported GAAP net income and cash-generating capacity.
2.
Accumulated write-downs: Hexpol experiences a growing amortization headwind as it continues to execute its leveraged acquisition strategy. During the first nine months of fiscal year 2026, the group recorded an EBIT of 2,080 MSEK against an EBITA of 2,210 MSEK. The resulting 130 MSEK in PPA amortization directly reduces reported earnings per share. These regular non-cash charges will expand further as recent transactions are fully integrated into consolidated reporting.
3.
Accounting policies: Hexpol applies a straight-line depreciation and amortization policy based on the estimated useful lives of its assets. The group amortizes acquired patents and trademarks over 20 years and other acquired intangibles over 3 to 15 years. This accounting framework means that large customer relationship valuations are amortized over a decade or more. Consequently, the non-cash drag on GAAP EPS will persist long after transactions close.

4.

The final valuation increased technology and customer relationships by 273 MSEK while deferred tax liabilities rose by 63 MSEK. This revaluation reduced final goodwill by 202 MSEK to 825 MSEK. This allocation shift directly increases the annual non-cash amortization charges flowing through the thermoplastic compounding segment.
5.
Kabkom allocation: Hexpol reported detailed balance sheet adjustments for its Kabkom Kimya acquisition during fiscal year 2025. The preliminary purchase price allocation recognized a 248 MSEK increase in acquired intangible assets representing technology and customer relationships. This fair value step-up was offset by a 62 MSEK deferred tax liability. This revaluation creates a predictable annual amortization charge that dilutes GAAP earnings.

Section 5 – Tax Rate Drivers and Structural Geographic Changes

Section summary
  1. Hexpol experienced a structural rise in its effective tax rate from twenty-two percent to nearly twenty- seven percent due to geographic profit shifts.
  2. Recent acquisitions in Italy and Turkey expand the company's footprint in high-tax jurisdictions and introduce complex local inflation accounting.
  3. Benchmarking against polymer peers shows Hexpol's tax rate has converged with the industry average of twentyfive percent.

Rising Historical Tax Trajectory

1.
Steady upward: Hexpol experienced a persistent increase in its consolidated effective tax rate over the first half of the decade. The group reported a tax rate of 22.2% in fiscal year 2021, which climbed sequentially to 23.5% in 2022. The rate continued its upward path to reach 24.6% in 2023. This steady upward tax trajectory reduced the overall conversion of operating profit to reported earnings.
2.
Geographic profit: Hexpol's tax rate escalation was primarily driven by a changing regional mix of operating earnings. Management stated during multiple earnings calls that higher profits in countries with elevated corporate tax rates, particularly the United States, inflated the group's tax liability. This structural shift meant that profit growth in high-tax jurisdictions had a disproportionate tax drag on EPS compared to previous years. Unless the company shifts production back to lower-tax regions, this geographic mix will remain a critical variable in projecting future net income.
3.
Non-recurring tax: Hexpol encountered further upward tax pressure during fiscal year 2024 as the reported rate reached 26.0%. This spike was largely driven by non-recurring restructuring charges in the United States that carried unfavorable tax treatments. These unique events temporarily inflated the consolidated tax rate and restricted net income growth. The restructuring expenses of 75 MSEK ultimately distorted the underlying tax profile of the polymer compounding operations.
4.
Elevated parent: Hexpol recorded its highest historical effective tax rate of 26.7% during fiscal year 2025. This rate increase was driven by a combination of higher earnings in high-tax countries and one-off tax expenses in the parent company. Consequently, these cumulative tax headwinds compressed the final net income reaching shareholders to 1,943 MSEK. This represents a significant decline from the 2,220 MSEK in net profit reported in the prior fiscal year.

Geographic Expansion Tax Effects

5.
Increased Italian: Hexpol expanded its European compounding presence through the 100% acquisition of Italy- based Vipa Group in October 2026. The acquisition of this major thermoplastic developer adds substantial operational assets in Italy, where corporate tax rates are traditionally higher than in Sweden. This geographical expansion is highly likely to sustain the elevated tax rate of the thermoplastic compounding segment. Consequently, the consolidation of Vipa's 76.4 MEUR turnover will shift the group's profit mix further toward high-tax European jurisdictions.
6.
High-profit Turkish: Hexpol entered the Turkish market by acquiring an 80% stake in Kabkom Kimya during May 2025. Kabkom operates as a leading independent cable compounder in Turkey and possesses profitability that exceeds the Hexpol group average. This strong profitability means that Kabkom's earnings will contribute more taxable income at Turkey's local statutory corporate tax rates. Therefore, the addition of Kabkom's 30 MEUR in sales will enhance operating margins but also increase the group's consolidated tax provisions.
7.
Expanded Turkish: Hexpol further reinforced its regional presence by signing an agreement to acquire 80% of Symplast in October 2026. This transaction will integrate a leading thermoplastic elastomer compounder with 34 MEUR in annual sales into the group's Thermoplastic Compounding segment. The inclusion of these Turkish operations will alter the group's tax profile by exposing more earnings to local emerging market tax frameworks. Fahri Tolga Sarıkahya and Ali Rıza Kır will continue to manage the subsidiary, ensuring local continuity under the new tax structure.
8.
Contrary evidence: Hexpol experienced a temporary reduction in tax rate during 2021 to 22.2% due to certain tax- exempt insurance payments. This unique tax credit temporarily lowered the tax burden in that fiscal year. However, management cautioned that this low rate was not representative of the group's long-term tax trajectory. Investors should therefore treat the 2021 results as an anomaly rather than a baseline for future earnings calculations.

Peer Benchmarks and Pillar Two

9.
Favorable comparison: Trelleborg AB managed a consolidated effective tax rate of 25% in both fiscal years 2024 and 2025. This tax level was slightly lower than Hexpol's 26.0% rate in 2024. The peer's stable performance shows that polymer compounders can maintain competitive tax rates despite global supply chain reconfigurations. This indicates that Hexpol's recent rate escalation is somewhat high relative to its immediate Swedish peer group.
10.
Volatile historical: Avient Corporation experienced highly volatile tax rates, reporting a tax benefit of 30.2% in 2022 before returning to a 25.2% tax rate in 2025. This volatility was driven by significant international affiliate tax restructurings and capital loss deductions. This peer's experience demonstrates that international compounding acquisitions can introduce severe tax rate volatility to consolidated earnings statements. Unless Hexpol actively hedges its foreign tax assets, similar geographic restructurings could inject volatility into its reported EPS.
11.
Standardized tax: Indutrade AB maintained a consolidated tax rate of 23% in 2025, which was slightly lower than Hexpol's rate. RedEye analysts apply a standardized tax rate of 25% for Indutrade due to its extensive international sales outside of Sweden. This analytical assumption implies that serial acquirers typically face higher tax rates as they expand their geographic footprints. Consequently, both Hexpol and Indutrade face a similar upward tax trajectory as their international sales mix expands.
12.
Immaterial global: Hexpol evaluated its exposure to the OECD Pillar Two model rules and concluded that all group subsidiaries have effective tax rates exceeding 16%.
