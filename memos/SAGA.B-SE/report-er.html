Section 16 – Conclusion

AB Sagax's Q2 2026 earnings revealed a fundamental shift in the investment case as the company delivered mixed financial results where rental revenue of SEK 2,719M represented modest 4% growth, while like-for-like rental growth turned negative at -0.5% during H1 2026 and diluted profit from property management per share contracted by 2% to SEK 5.89. The single most important change was management's strategic recalibration of long-term targets, resetting the return on equity target to at least 12% per year for 2026-2030 from the historical 15% established in 2013, while simultaneously lowering the annual growth target in profit from property management per share to a range of 5-10% from the previous minimum target of 15% growth.

This transition reflects the company's acknowledgment that with a matured scale exceeding SEK 72 billion in assets, reproducing historical growth rates has become structurally more difficult and requires a more conservative capital structure going forward.

The sell-side response to the earnings diverged sharply, with DNB Carnegie maintaining a BUY rating and SEK 200 target price while highlighting the 80 basis point quarter-on-quarter occupancy improvement that nearly erased the Q1 wobble and defending the company's high trading multiples as warranted by its superior cash return on net asset value and best long-term total return track record among Nordic real estate coverage.

In stark contrast, Jefferies reiterated an Underperform rating while cutting the price target from SEK 150 to SEK 145, raising alarms over organic leasing performance with negative like-for-like rental growth of -0.5% compared to positive 2.4% growth achieved in 2025, cutting their 2026 FFO per share estimate by 5% to SEK 9.54 which sits 20% below consensus of SEK 11.88, and warning that the headline vacancy rate of 4.5% is misleading as the adjusted vacancy rate reaches 7.1% when accounting for outstanding tenant termination notices.

The material deviation from expectations centered on management's raised 2026 adjusted property management guidance to SEK 4.60bn from SEK 4.50bn, supported by accelerating acquisition momentum with net property investments reaching SEK 5.2 billion year-to-date, including a massive SEK 2.0 billion post-Q2 acquisition of 48 public transport bus depots in France at an estimated net initial yield of 6.7% with 94% of rental income indexed to ILAT, alongside SEK 710 million in acquisitions across France, Benelux, Denmark, and Germany and an additional SEK 310M deployed to acquire two fully occupied assets in Finland.

However, this growth strategy relied on highly dilutive capital raising through the June 2026 issuance of 30 million Class D shares that raised SEK 1.005bn at a 5.2% discount, which critics warned represents six percent economic dilution that may fail to offset economic dilution for ordinary shareholders if organic leasing continues to soften. The macroeconomic backdrop revealed steady pressure from rising debt costs as the average interest rate on interest-bearing liabilities increased to 2.7% on June 30, 2026, up from 2.5% at the end of 2025, with the company issuing SEK 3.0 billion of unsecured bonds in September 2026 at a 3.4% average initial interest rate comprising tranches with credit spreads ranging from 0.50% for 2-year bonds to 0.95% for 5-year bonds. Despite these refinancing headwinds, property revaluations had a positive impact of SEK 831M during the first half of 2026, representing a substantial reversal compared to the negative SEK 388M revaluation recorded in the same period of 2025, while the run-rate yield of the property portfolio stabilized at 6.3% as of June 30, 2026, compared to 6.6% in June 2025, suggesting that capital capitalization rates have found a floor.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bearish organic growth concerns emerges from the competitive dynamics and balance sheet positioning thesis. If competitor balance sheet constraints accelerate across European logistics markets, where weighted European prime logistics yield remained stable at 4.95% for six consecutive quarters through Q2 2026 while peers like CTP saw LTV expand to 47%, WDP faces an expected 85bps increase in average cost of debt by 2030, Argan experiences elevated leverage with 8.8x net debt to EBITDA, Logicor faces Net Debt to EBITDA at 12.6x, and Balder maintains tight financial flexibility with LTV at 54.4%, then AB Sagax's conservative balance sheet with net debt to EBITDA of 6.1x positions it to acquire highquality assets from capital-constrained peers at the recent 6.7% net initial yield achieved in France.

Under this scenario, the rapid consolidation of the European warehouse market through highly accretive acquisitions would prove the bearish Jefferies view overly cautious as new cash flows easily absorb the dilutive effects of Class D shares, allowing Sagax to comfortably achieve its new 12% return target and justifying its premium valuation relative to continental peers.
