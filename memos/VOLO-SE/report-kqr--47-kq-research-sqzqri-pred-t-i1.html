Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The outlook for Volati ordinary shares depends on end-market recoveries and leverage reduction. Net read: Structural preference share dividends restrict the capital available for acquisitions and growth.

Shifting corporate structure

1.
Crucial inquiry: "how likely is company guidance to be raised, met or reduced at the earnings events over the next 18 months, and how likely are sell-side consensus forecasts, and the net breadth of revisions, to move higher or lower over the same period?"
2.
Structural transformation: Following the distribution of Salix Group on 15 June 2026, Volati operates as a more focused but smaller industrial acquirer. This transaction changes how equity investors value the ordinary shares, as Salix previously drove a massive portion of the group's historical EBITA growth. A DNB Carnegie note on 26 October 2026 explains that elevated leverage post-separation hampers near-term acquisition capacity, directly impacting future EPS development.
3.
Heavy obligations: The preference shares remain entirely with Volati post-spin-off, requiring SEK 64 million in annual dividends. This fixed dividend represents a significant financial drain on continuing operations, which reported a LTM net loss of SEK 8 million for ordinary shareholders in Q3 2026. DNB Carnegie argues on 20 October 2026 that the preference share burden restricts M&A because leverage has reached a stretched level.

Capital allocation and leverage

4.
Capital allocation: Volati's dividend policy targets distributing 10 to 30 percent of the group's net profit to ordinary shareholders. This ordinary payout is highly subject to net debt levels and future acquisition opportunities, which are currently constrained by high leverage. Since the group paid an ordinary dividend of SEK 2 for 2025, future distributions could be pressured unless profitability and cash flow recover significantly.
5.
Elevated leverage: Net debt stood at SEK 1,331 million on 30 September 2026, which yields a leverage ratio of 3.1x Net Debt/EBITDA. This outcome exceeds the company's long-term financial target of 2.0x to 3.0x Net Debt/EBITDA. A DNB Carnegie report on 20 October 2026 notes that including preference shares increases leverage to 4.5x, which significantly restricts near-term acquisition capacity and limits EPS growth.
6.
Contrary evidence: Volati reported that underlying EBITA before items affecting comparability rose by 15 percent in Q3 2026. This operational improvement suggests that underlying platform performance is stronger than the reported net loss indicates, which could support near-term EPS recovery if end-market demand stabilizes. Consequently, brokers might be too pessimistic about the pace of organic recovery.
7.
Delayed recovery: End-market challenges continue to impact Volati's primary industrial segments, particularly Tornum and S:t Eriks. A DNB Carnegie note on 26 October 2026 emphasizes that uncertain end-market recovery makes current trading weaker than peers. If these struggles persist over the next 18 months, consensus forecasts will likely trend lower.
8.
Consensus expectations: Sell-side expectations remain highly sensitive to Volati's ability to deleverage and resume M&A. If the company fails to meet its leverage targets, brokers may lower their target prices and ratings. Thus, monitoring revision breadth over the next 18 months is crucial for assessing ordinary share valuation risks relative to preference shares.
9.
Other side of the story: Volati's long-term decentralized model has historically demonstrated a strong record of building and listing successful platforms like Salix Group. This historical success indicates that the decentralized acquisition strategy remains viable, potentially allowing the group to deleverage rapidly once cash flow improves. Thus, long-term investors may look past near-term EPS volatility to focus on structural value creation.
