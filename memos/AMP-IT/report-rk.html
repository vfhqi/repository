Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive analysis of Amplifon's bear case, pre-mortem scenarios, and currency exposures reveals three statistically extreme outliers that position the company as a structural anomaly within its peer group. These outliers represent material deviations from industry base rates and warrant heightened investor scrutiny given their potential to amplify downside scenarios. The first and most extreme outlier is the astronomical 12,121 days-to-cover ratio in the US OTC short interest market, driven by highly illiquid average daily trading volumes despite 3,636,400 shares held short in late December 2025.

This metric represents an orders-of-magnitude deviation from typical institutional short positioning dynamics and suggests either severe structural liquidity constraints or extremely concentrated short positioning that cannot be unwound through normal market mechanisms. By comparison, the broader European medical devices sector maintains a relatively balanced hedge fund long-to-short ratio of 2.6x, highlighting that Amplifon's OTC positioning exists in a fundamentally different liquidity regime.

This extreme illiquidity profile creates asymmetric squeeze risk where forced covering could generate violent price dislocations disconnected from fundamental value, while simultaneously signaling that sophisticated investors maintaining these positions expect structural deterioration that justifies accepting such extreme illiquidity costs. The second critical outlier is the governance concentration anomaly where majority owner Susan Carol Holland controls 70.09% of voting rights through Ampliter S.r.l. while holding only 38.68% of share capital as of June 30, 2026, maintained through enhanced loyalty voting mechanisms.

This governance structure represents a material outlier relative to peer foundations, where Demant's William Demant Foundation maintains stable majority ownership of 55% to 60% with proportional voting rights, and Fielmann's family control of 72.91% is achieved through transparent foundation structures. The 2.8x vote-to-equity leverage ratio at Amplifon creates severe principal-agent misalignment risks, as minority shareholders bear 62% of the economic risk while exercising only 30% of voting control.
