Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Structural division restructuring and the high-priced Atmo acquisition highlight Vaisala's strategic shift toward high-margin digital software.

  3. Persistent wind energy cyclical lows and foreign exchange headwinds present ongoing risks to overall weather segment profitability.

Industrial Measurements recovery

1.
Accelerating recovery: Vaisala entered the pre-earnings period with expectations that its Industrial Measurements business would continue its gradual demand recovery. Analysts expected the division to grow faster than the lower-margin Weather and Environment business. Industrial Measurement recovery was highly critical to investor sentiment because the division generated approximately 80% of group operating profits. Investors hoped a cyclical upturn would drive overall group margins higher over the next 18 months.
2.
Surging infrastructure: Sell-side focus was intensely directed at Vaisala's data center exposure due to the massive global artificial intelligence buildout. Analysts noted that Vaisala's sensors tracked cooling equipment growth rather than graphics processing unit shipments. The company planned to provide explicit details on this exposure because these growth segments represented 30% of industrial sales. Data center growth was expected to provide substantial upside compared to historical industrial measurement growth trends.
3.
Enhanced pricing: Analysts focused heavily on Vaisala's pricing power and its ability to defend gross margins against inflationary pressures. The market expected annual price increases implemented in early 2026 to successfully offset rising component costs. Management's new pricing excellence program was highly anticipated as it targeted granular case-by-case pricing to maximize order margins. Investors believed these actions would safeguard the company's superior industrial gross margins across different geographic regions.
4.
Resilient demand: The sell-side anticipated strong order momentum in the power and life science segments before the Q3 earnings release. Vaisala's dissolved gas analysis equipment was expected to see robust demand driven by grid modernization projects. Although the power segment had experienced year-over-year declines in late 2025, a rapid recovery was anticipated. Analysts believed that strong secular growth in life sciences would complement this industrial recovery.
5.
Elevated premiums: Prior to the earnings release, Vaisala's valuation was heavily debated by analysts at Inderes. The sell-side noted that the company's valuation was trading at a premium compared to traditional hardware peers. Inderes expected this premium would be supported by the exceptional data center investment cycle in 2027. However, analysts warned that these optimistic growth expectations were already fully priced into the stock.

Weather business cyclicality

6.
Lumpy execution: Investors approached the Weather, Energy, and Environment business with caution due to the inherent quarterly lumpiness of project deliveries. The sell-side focused on the progress of two major EUR 25 million tenders won in Spain and Indonesia. Major weather projects were expected to temporarily boost group net sales by 3% to 4% in 2026. However, delays in finalizing the Indonesian financing agreement had kept it out of the official backlog.
7.
Severe decline: The market expected a sharp drop in Vaisala's renewable energy business due to weak wind energy investments. Analysts projected a revenue decline of EUR 20 million in this high-margin segment during 2025. Postponed investments in offshore wind and slow grid connections in Germany continued to drag on the sales mix heading into Q3. A renewables decline was highly negative for overall group profitability due to the high margins of wind LiDAR systems.
8.
Soft spending: Sell-side analysts expressed concern over the stability of public sector demand for meteorological infrastructure. There was a clear risk of US public sector orders not recovering quickly after restructuring. The market watched closely to see if weather spending as national security would support long-term budget stability. Despite near-term challenges, Vaisala's massive order backlog was expected to cushion any immediate revenue decline.
9.
Decisive control: Vaisala's cost-saving initiatives were a key focus for analysts hoping to see margin stabilization. The company implemented efficiency actions in its weather business to lower its fixed cost base. Analysts expected these measures to be fully visible and protect margins against lower sales volumes. Vaisala's aggressive cost-control steps were viewed as critical for maintaining operating leverage during a cyclical downturn.
10.
Expensive acquisitions: Vaisala's acquisition of AI weather startup Atmo was viewed by the sell-side as a high- priced but necessary defensive move. Analysts calculated that the potential USD 130 million purchase price represented an extremely high multiple. The market questioned why Vaisala was willing to pay such a premium to secure Atmo's technology. Brokers concluded that Vaisala wanted to prevent competitors from acquiring the startup and disrupting the weather market.

Recurring software and macro risks

11.

Xweather possessed the highest gross margin in the group at 77.5% in fiscal year 2025. Analysts expected recurring subscription sales to scale margins very quickly as top-line growth continued. The market expected the business to achieve a positive EBITA margin as fixed cost growth was capped.
12.
Resilient defenses: Analysts debated whether emerging generative artificial intelligence tools would commoditize weather software. Vaisala argued that its proprietary and curated observation data protected Xweather from generic competition. Brokers agreed that Vaisala held a relatively safe competitive position against unproven AI models. A strong proprietary data moat gave investors confidence that double-digit recurring software growth could be sustained.

Volatile rates: Significant currency fluctuations were a major headwind for Vaisala's reported margins before Q3. Key export currencies including the US dollar, Chinese yuan, and Japanese yen experienced severe volatility. Analysts noted that these negative exchange rate movements would dent first-half reported results. The market anticipated that these currency headwinds would ease significantly in the second half of the year.
14.
Persistent friction: Sell-side analysts remained cautious about potential disruptions in global trade flows and trade policy changes. Ongoing US tariff risks were expected to pressure Vaisala's gross margins and complicate supply chain planning. Analysts kept their estimates conservative to account for potential trade disruptions and geopolitical surprises. Investors closely watched for any resolution to past tariff overpayments that could provide a temporary cash boost.
15.
High expectations: The market set a very high bar for Vaisala's upcoming Q3 financial performance. Analysts at DNB Carnegie raised their price targets and earnings estimates over the preceding months. DNB Carnegie expected that a full-year guidance hike was highly probable. However, analysts remained cautious in their forecasts to account for potential negative geopolitical surprises.
16.
Other side of the story: Vaisala's pre-earnings optimism could be undermined by a persistent global economic slowdown that dampens industrial demand. Analysts at DNB Carnegie warned that the weather business profit margins could fail to improve over time. If the company's Xweather data business fails to continue growing at double-digit rates, group margins would contract. Furthermore, ongoing US tariff risks could heavily pressure gross margins if supply chain adjustments are delayed.
