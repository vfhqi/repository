Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  2. Understanding the operating leverage of IT value-added resellers is critical for forecasting earnings per share and capital allocation.

Primary Inquiry

1.
Primary inquiry: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Significant leverage: Operating leverage directly determines how changes in business volume translate into pre-tax profit and earnings per share. Because Bechtle is burdened by investments in its internal IT, resolving this question will clarify when these margin drags will ease and allow earnings to recover. Investors require this transparency to evaluate whether Bechtle can meet its target of a pre-tax margin by 2030.
3.
Strategic spending: Capital allocation decisions between organic expansion, shareholder returns, and acquisitions depend heavily on the cash conversion cycle and margin stability. For instance, peer CDW targets returning 50% to 75% of free cash flow through dividends and buybacks while maintaining a net leverage ratio of 2 to 3 times. A clearer understanding of cost structures helps investors assess whether Bechtle can sustain its dividend policy while funding its extensive European acquisition strategy.
4.
Inherent volatility: Financial performance is highly sensitive to macroeconomic shifts and IT investment caution within the German public and commercial sectors. For example, CANCOM has experienced prolonged customer decision-making and project delays in its workplace segment. If cost structures are highly fixed, even minor volume declines can trigger severe margin contraction, elevating operational risks across the entire reseller peer group.

Spend Cycles and

5.
Client Mix Cyclical demand: Industry demand is closely tied to product refresh cycles, particularly the transition to Windows 11 and AI-ready infrastructure. Resellers such as Softcat act as critical channels for these structural spending waves, yet face volatility when clients defer non-essential hardware upgrades. Knowing the flexibility of the cost base helps model earnings downside during a Windows 11 migration and digitalisation pause.
6.
Diverse clienteles: Profitability variations are heavily driven by the balance between public sector contracts and corporate accounts. Peer Atea exhibits over 60% public-sector exposure, which provides resilient volume but subjects the company to political and budget spending cycles. The cost flexibility of each player must be matched against its client mix to evaluate how margins react to structural shifts.
7.
Complex ecosystems: Value-added resellers operate in a complex dynamic where profitability depends on manufacturer incentives and back-end rebates. For instance, Bechtle has faced margin headwinds from reduced manufacturing partner bonus payments and changes in partner fee structures. This reliance on external incentive programs highlights why understanding operating leverage is essential for predicting earnings volatility.

Section 2 – Cost Structure and Historical Operating Leverage of Bechtle

Section summary
  1. Bechtle carries a highly rigid opex structure dominated by personnel costs that limit short-term flexibility during volume slowdowns.
  2. Historical cycles demonstrate that Bechtle's pre-tax margin contracts during volume declines due to fixed-cost under-absorption but recovers rapidly as demand normalizes.

Fixed Personnel Cost Structures

1.
Inflexible cost base: Bechtle operates with a highly rigid operational expense structure where personnel costs represent the largest single expense item. The company reported in its 2025 Annual Report that personnel costs constitute approximately 80 per cent of its distribution costs and more than 60 per cent of its administrative expenses. This personnel intensity prevents management from restructuring costs rapidly when demand weakens because maintaining a skilled workforce is prioritized over short-term savings.
2.
Verified personnel ratios: Bechtle verified the high concentration of staff costs in its 2025 audited financial statements. The group recorded 376,828 thousand euros in distribution personnel expenses out of 483,796 thousand euros in total distribution costs, representing 77.89 per cent of the total. Administrative personnel expenses reached 262,704 thousand euros out of 422,735 thousand euros in total administrative costs, translating to 62.14 per cent.
3.
p Bechtle cost structure breakdown showing personnel expenses across functional areas in 2024 and 2025 Opex ratio targets: Bechtle targets aggressive operational expense ratios to mitigate its rigid personnel cost structures as business volume expands. The company stated in its 2025 Annual Report that it is endeavoring to keep the distribution cost ratio below 7.5 per cent and the administrative expense ratio below 6.5 per cent of revenue. These efficiency targets are supported by the growing deployment of internal automation tools that enhance sales productivity.
4.
Compensation variables: Bechtle implements compensation structures with a higher stake of variable components to cushion margin pressures during volume contractions. Deutsche Bank Research noted that this flexible compensation approach distinguishes the firm from less adaptable competitors and naturally shields operating profitability during downturns. This structure lowers the break-even threshold and partially offsets the fixed-cost nature of its consulting-intensive personnel base.

Historical Downturns and Pandemic Resilience

5.
Severe financial crisis: Bechtle navigated the 2009 global financial crisis with resilient margins despite experiencing a revenue decline of 4.0 per cent. While the company did not suffer pre-tax losses during this severe contraction, the fixed cost base restricted margin expansion because opex could not be trimmed in parallel. This historical precedent demonstrates that Bechtle carries substantial operating leverage that exposes profits during sudden volume declines.
6.
Resilient pandemic margins: Bechtle maintained stable operating profitability during the 2020 pandemic slowdown despite widespread lock-downs and project postponements. The company reported that fiscal year 2020 revenue rose by 8.1 per cent to 5,819.2 million euros while pre-tax earnings climbed 14.6 per cent to 270.7 million euros. This resilient performance was supported by a pandemic-induced surge in demand for homeoffice hardware and virtual collaboration solutions.
7.
Contrary evidence: Bechtle demonstrated unexpected cost flexibility during recovery phases by executing headcount reductions on an organic basis to protect operating margins. The group reported that its organic headcount actually decreased by 1.4 per cent year on year in June 2026 and by 0.6 per cent in September 2026, showing that its human capital cost base is not entirely fixed. This organic contraction allowed the company to raise its EBT growth outlook as productivity improved.
8.
Lockdown dynamics: Bechtle experienced localized regional resilience during the 2020 economic slowdown due to strong public sector demand. Jefferies reported that Austria and Switzerland grew by 33.1 per cent in the second quarter of 2020 thanks to demand from public authorities. This regional boost offset severe lockdown headwinds in other European countries, showcasing how customer diversification mitigates fixed-cost under-absorption.

Recovery Cycles and Operating Leverage

9.
Robust recovery margins: Bechtle exhibited strong operating leverage during the 2021 post-pandemic recovery cycle as supply chains normalized. Preliminary figures showed that fiscal year 2021 pre-tax earnings surged by 18.0 per cent to approximately 320 million euros on revenue growth of over 7 per cent. This margin expansion reflected elevated trade margins, inventory stockpiling advantages, and operational efficiency gains.
10.
Restated agent revenues: Bechtle restated its historical revenue figures to reflect the high proportion of software agency commissions that distort gross margin comparisons. Jefferies reported that fiscal year 2021 revenue was reduced by 930 million euros to account for proper revenue recognition where Bechtle qualifies as an agent versus principal. This accounting adjustment did not impact absolute EBT but significantly inflated the reported pre-tax margin.
11.
Elevated inflation drag: Bechtle faced rising operating expenses during the 2022 macroeconomic slowdown despite delivering strong top-line growth. The company reported that business volume grew 16.6 per cent to 7,285.7 million euros but its EBT margin declined from 6.0 per cent to 5.8 per cent. Profitability was burdened by inflation-driven building expenses, increased travel costs, and an 8 million euro provision for employee inflation bonuses.
12.
Significant margin compression: Bechtle suffered severe margin contraction during the 2024 economic slowdown due to fixed personnel cost under-absorption and a reluctance to invest among small and medium-sized enterprises. The group's pre-tax margin for Q3 2024 fell from 6.3 per cent to 5.2 per cent of revenue as EBT declined by 16.9 per cent. This drop was amplified by rising personnel costs from acquisitions and a lack of supplier rebates.
13.
Accelerated volume expansion: Bechtle achieved double-digit profitability growth during the 2026 recovery phase as public sector and enterprise demand rebounded strongly. The company reported that its third-quarter 2026 business volume rose 19.0 per cent to 2,438.0 million euros while EBT rose 13.9 per cent to 91.7 million euros. This recovery confirmed that substantial operating leverage remains intact to drive EPS when volumes accelerate.
14.
Price-driven expansion: Bechtle benefited from substantial pricing effects during the 2026 recovery that amplified its operational margins. Deutsche Bank Research reported that more than half of the recent revenue growth in 2026 was driven by rising hardware prices. This positive pricing dynamic allowed Bechtle to successfully pass through input cost increases to clients and sustain stable pre-tax margins.
15.
Other side of the story: While Bechtle's high personnel cost base is structurally rigid, variable compensation structures and scaling efficiencies significantly mitigate downside margin risks during volume contractions. Deutsche Bank reported that Bechtle utilizes compensation packages with higher stakes of variable components than competitors, which naturally cushions opex pressure during market downturns. Furthermore, the ongoing deployment of internal AI tools and process harmonization represents a self-help lever that reduces structural personnel intensity over time.

Section 4 – Gross Profit Conversion, Supplier Rebates and Margin Drivers

Section summary

  2. The pre-tax margin contraction to 5.2 per cent in late 2024 reflects under-absorbed personnel opex and shortfalls in high-margin supplier rebate thresholds.
  3. Strategic renegotiations of vendor agreements are transitioning back-end thresholds from volume to value bases to stabilize future operating leverage.

This drop in back-end margin indicates that missing volume thresholds has an immediate and direct impact on absolute net profit. Shifting business mix: Bechtle's product mix shifted toward software and services in 2024, which protected the gross margin but did not prevent EBIT decline. Section 5 – Cost Elimination Feasibility, Headcount Controls and ERP Migrations

Section summary
  1. Bechtle's internal ERP migrations to SAP S/4HANA represent a persistent margin headwind but are vital for future scaling.
  2. Headcount controls and restructuring programs at peers demonstrate that personnel costs can be successfully managed through natural attrition or targeted capacity actions.

Enterprise system harmonization

1.
Ambitious migration: Bechtle continues to invest heavily in its group- wide internal IT landscape to prepare for future growth. Deutsche Bank Research reported in November 2026 that Bechtle faces an EBT headwind of about three per cent, or 15 basis points of margin, from these systems. The company continues to roll out SAP S/4HANA across 90 to 100 subsidiaries, which will require several years of sustained capital allocation. This central IT spend represents a major short-term margin drag but is considered essential.
2.
Stable implementation: Bechtle management maintains that these centralized IT investments are progressing without operational complications. The company reported during its Q1 2026 earnings call that central IT costs will remain stable throughout the fiscal year. CEO Olemotz confirmed that Bechtle has not experienced any further hiccups with the rollout of its cloud platform or the SAP S/4HANA system. While these investments currently weigh on profitability, they are critical to standardizing administrative processes across its decentralized house structure.
3.
Comparable complications: Peer IT integrators have historically faced severe operational disruptions when consolidating legacy systems. Computacenter reported that migrating its French business to the group-wide ERP system in 2013 led to severe operational problems and non-current asset impairments of 12.20 million pounds. This past integration failure suggests that large-scale system migrations carry substantial operational execution risk. To mitigate such risks, Computacenter is currently executing a multi-year cloud-based ERP design phase.
4.
Incremental consolidation: Cancom has similarly prioritized the harmonization of its internal IT landscape to streamline administrative workflows. Jefferies Research reported in November 2022 that Cancom's migration to a new ERP system had a negative one-off impact on depreciation and amortization. The transition temporarily depressed operating margins to 4.20 per cent of revenue. Cancom subsequently completed the shutdown of its legacy ERP platforms in late 2025 to capture digital efficiencies.

Personnel flexibility and attrition

5.
Disciplined hiring: Bechtle chose to implement strict headcount controls rather than executing costly mass redundancies in response to the 2024 demand slowdown. The company reported in its 2024 annual report that organic headcount grew only 1.7 per cent in fiscal year 2024. Total employee numbers grew 4.20 per cent to 15,801, but acquisitions accounted for 381 of those additions. This conservative hiring stance helped protect operating margins without damaging Bechtle's longterm consulting capabilities.
6.
Natural attrition: Bechtle accelerated its headcount reduction strategies in 2025 by freezing vacant positions and leveraging natural employee turnover. The company reported in its Q3 2025 earnings call that organic headcount decreased 1.6 per cent year-on-year. This organic contraction demonstrates that Bechtle's human capital cost base is not entirely fixed. The company continues to rely on natural attrition to lower its distribution opex as sales volume recovers.
7.
Contrary evidence: Bechtle has sometimes been unable to prevent absolute personnel costs from expanding due to wage inflation and newly acquired subsidiaries. The company reported in March 2026 that personnel expenses increased by 5.8 per cent in fiscal year 2025, outstripping revenue growth. This inflation-driven expansion pushed Bechtle's personnel opex ratio up to 19.40 per cent of revenue. Consequently, absolute staff costs remains a highly rigid overhead line.
8.
Rigid obligations: Sesa maintains a decentralized organization that centralizes core administrative functions to keep operational expenses variable. The firm reported in July 2026 that its corporate segment manages governance, information systems, and personnel administration across wholly owned subsidiaries. This centralized model allows Sesa to integrate acquired entities quickly while maintaining flexible overheads. However, Sesa is still exposed to baseline personnel inflation across its expanding Italian consulting network.

Peer restructuring and cost cutting

9.
Rigorous reduction: Cancom executed a material cost savings program in 2025 to reverse a challenging three-year profitability decline. Jefferies Research reported in August 2025 that Cancom's management initiated annualized personnel savings of twelve million euros. The program targeted redundant structural layers and administrative overheads to enhance operating leverage. By aggressively cutting personnel expenses, Cancom sought to restore its historical mid-market profitability.
10.
Successful delivery: Cancom's restructuring program delivered significant opex reductions ahead of its medium-term strategy. The company reported in November 2026 that total headcount was reduced by 4.9 per cent to 5,137 employees. This headcount reduction, combined with tighter cost discipline, cut Cancom's nine-month operational cost base by 8.30 million euros. These actions successfully supported a 14.90 per cent increase in nine-month EBITDA despite lower absolute revenues.
11.
Strategic capacity: Computacenter implemented substantial efficiency programs in its core German division to realign its operational resources. The company reported during its H1 2026 earnings call that it recognized a five million euro early retirement provision in Germany. These capacity adjustments were designed to eliminate redundant headcount following the exit of non-core data center contracts. Computacenter's German operating profit remained flat to slightly up once these capacity charges were excluded.
12.
Active management: Computacenter has also focused on eliminating legacy costs within its underperforming French operations. The group reported in March 2026 that reducing legacy costs from BT Services remains a key operational priority. French underperformance previously triggered non-cash impairment charges of 8.30 million pounds on non-current assets and 11.90 million pounds on goodwill in fiscal year 2025. Computacenter successfully narrowed its French losses in H1 2026 through aggressive cost-containment measures.
13.
Comparative headcount: Bechtle's conservative headcount approach contrasted sharply with the aggressive personnel cuts executed at peer system houses. While Bechtle limited its organic headcount growth to 1.7 per cent in 2024, Cancom executed an absolute headcount reduction of 4.9 per cent by September 2026. Similarly, Computacenter took early action by taking early leaver scheme provisions in Germany to align resources. These different strategies reveal that Bechtle prioritizes long-term personnel retention over the immediate cost cuts favored by its rivals.
14.
