Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Bavarian Nordic's long-term earnings per share trajectory depends heavily on transitioning from outbreak-driven stockpiling windfalls to stable commercial vaccine growth.
  2. Plausibility of the company's growth path hinges on realizing manufacturing cost savings and successfully integrating commercial acquisitions.
  3. Travel Health growth faces post outbreak demand normalization as temporary rabies and tick-borne encephalitis awareness surges in the United States and Europe begin to fade.
  4. Historic precedents from travel vaccine launches suggest that developed market traveler penetration remains highly limited, prompting peers to pivot toward endemic public health markets.

Post Surge Demand Dynamics and Historical Precedents

14.
Historic analogues: Sanofi experienced a long-term compound annual growth rate of only three percent in its travel vaccine business after initial post-pandemic demand spikes subsided. This slow growth occurred despite Sanofi holding a dominant fifty-eight percent share of an underpenetrated one billion dollar global travel vaccine market in 2019. This historical precedent suggests that travel vaccine demand typically reverts to low single digits once initial public awareness campaigns lose their immediate momentum.
15.
Outbreak normalization: Historical cases in the wider vaccine sector show that demand surges driven by local outbreaks typically contract by double digits within two years. For example, single-use fluid path sales for biopharma manufacturing fell nineteen percent in 2023 after pandemic-related vaccine production subsided. This demonstrates that temporary demand surges are unsustainable over a three-year horizon.
16.
Underpenetrated markets: Broker research indicates that under two percent of eligible Western travelers currently receive recommended vaccinations before visiting endemic regions. Bavarian Nordic has attempted to address this gap through intensive consumer outreach programs in Germany and the United States. However, history indicates that shifting consumer behavior requires years of sustained marketing investment.
17.
Waning interest: Global travel trends often show rapid normalization following short-term pandemic disruptions as pent-up passenger demand stabilizes. United Nations World Tourism Organization data showed international arrivals recovered to sixty-three percent of pre-pandemic levels by late 2022. This indicates that downstream vaccine demand is directly tied to broader international passenger travel recovery.
18.
Slowing volume: Historical data from the travel sector shows that passenger bookings tend to cluster closer to actual flight dates over time, creating highly unpredictable revenue patterns. Combined daily visits to travel websites remained flat against three-year averages in late 2026, indicating a plateau in travel interest. This suggests that travel vaccine portfolios face a naturally capped addressable customer base.
