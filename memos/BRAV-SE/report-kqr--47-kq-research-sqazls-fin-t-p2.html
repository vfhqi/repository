Section 2 – Contract Structures and Pass-Through Mechanics

Section summary
  1. Bravida and its peers navigate inflation risks through a diverse mix of fixed-price, partnering, and cost- plus contracts that determine their ability to pass through rising operational costs.
  2. Standard Nordic contract frameworks like Swedish AB 04 and Danish AB 18 establish distinct rules for indexation and cost sharing that dictate regional margin lags.

Bravida Contract Distribution

1.
Stable duration: Bravida maintains an average project lifecycle of nine months across its technical installation portfolio, which allows rapid backlog turnover. This short project duration enables Bravida to refresh its order backlog with higher-priced contracts within two to seven months as legacy work concludes. Consequently, the group can limit its exposure to prolonged fixed-price projects and protect operating profit margins during inflationary cycles.
2.
Regional variation: Bravida utilizes different contract structures across the Nordic region, relying on indexation in Norway while facing fixed-price risks in Sweden. The group actively promotes the adoption of index-linked contracts in Sweden and Denmark to mitigate raw material volatility. Management prioritizes these contract structures because index-linked solutions reduce contract write-downs when material prices escalate unexpectedly.
3.
Lagged recovery: Bravida Denmark and Norway demonstrate distinct pricing mechanisms, with Norway showing high utilization of partnering and index-linked project models. In Denmark, the turnaround in EBITA margin to 7.5% in Q3 2026 highlights the success of phasing out legacy, low-margin contracts. These structural improvements suggest that selective bidding supports pricing power even when organic sales growth remains low.

Standard Nordic Legal Frameworks

4.
Fixed exposure: Swedish installation contracts under standard AB 04 and ABT 06 frameworks often lack automatic indexation, placing inflation risks on the contractor. Under these rules, installers must absorb wage and material increases unless specific indexation clauses are negotiated upfront. This exposure can significantly dilute technical installation margins on fixed-price projects during high-inflation periods.
5.
Structured indexation: Danish contracts governed by the AB 18 framework provide automatic price indexation for projects with durations exceeding twelve months. For shorter Danish contracts under twelve months, installers must price anticipated wage and material inflation directly into their initial bids. This legal framework ensures that long-term projects enjoy inflation protection unless contractors explicitly choose to bypass indexation.
6.
Faster pass-through: Standard Danish AB 18 contracts offer more predictable cost-sharing rules compared to Swedish AB 04 agreements which require proactive indexation. Under AB 18, client responsibility for design risks reduces execution uncertainty for installers. This regulatory distinction means that Danish installers face lower margin volatility on complex commercial developments.

Peer Contract Frameworks

7.
Flexible remuneration: Instalco AB balances its portfolio by conducting 57% of its contracts on a cost-plus or variable basis. The remaining 43% of Instalco's projects are executed under fixed-price agreements, exposing the firm to potential cost overruns. This contract mix allows Instalco to stabilize earnings across business cycles by passing through volatile material costs.
8.
Dynamic indexing: InstallatørGruppen utilizes the Danish AB 18 framework to secure automatic indexation on projects exceeding twelve months. For projects shorter than twelve months, the group relies on pricing anticipated wage inflation upfront into its tender bids. This contract discipline helps the company defend profitability on large installations where execution spans multiple quarters.
9.
Short lifecycles: Fasadgruppen Group AB operates primarily on a fixed-price basis, utilizing a cost-based decentralized pricing model. The group mitigates inflation risk because its renovation projects have relatively short lifecycles. This high turnover of projects allows Fasadgruppen to adjust pricing rapidly to material inflation without being locked into unfavorable contracts.
10.
Contrary evidence: Instalco AB reports that fixed-price projects can offer attractive margins despite higher execution risk, challenging the view that variable contracts are always superior. In its October 2026 presentation, management noted that profitability depends more on customer characteristics and execution quality than the contract type. This indicates that fixed-price work can yield high returns under stable operating conditions.

Benchmark Pass-Through Tools

11.
Daily monitoring: SPIE SA operates as the sector benchmark for inflation pass-through by utilizing daily pricing and procurement monitoring tools. The group secures its margins through indexation clauses, short validity windows for tender offers, and a highly flexible cost structure. This disciplined pricing mechanism allows SPIE to pass through cost inflation efficiently to its European customer base.
12.
High visibility: SPIE SA derives over 75% of its consolidated production from recurring maintenance and multi-year framework contracts. These agreements typically run for three to five years, providing exceptional revenue visibility. This high share of recurring service work shields the group from cyclical downturns in newbuild construction markets.
13.
Local procurement: Burkhalter Holding AG manages material cost volatility by sourcing 90% of its critical materials from Swiss suppliers. While Swiss public contracts are indexed, private contracts are typically fixedprice, requiring careful price adjustments. This localized sourcing strategy enables Burkhalter to offset sharp material cost fluctuations in a labor-scarce construction market.
