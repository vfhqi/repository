EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Overall verdict on the chief executive. The evidence on Lars Fruergaard Jørgensen is mixed, with strong early delivery and a weak late tenure. His career was primarily in specialist corporate staff functions rather than operating divisions with direct profit and loss responsibility, unlike the commercial or geographic backgrounds typical of peers. His guidance record flipped over time.

In 2023 he raised full-year sales and operating profit guidance multiple times, lifting initial growth targets from 13-19% to a final 36%. In 2025, by contrast, three consecutive profit warnings cut initial sales growth expectations of 16-24% to a realized 10%. He then stepped down unexpectedly in August 2025 by mutual agreement with the board, and the departure was heavily driven by the Novo Nordisk Foundation losing confidence after a mid-2024 share price decline. The report also credits him with some of the pipeline's success, but notes that predecessor decisions established the clinical foundation that he commercialized.

Performance against the leadership qualities in section 16. The six traits show a split record under the current leadership of Mike Doustdar. On bold decisions and drive, management implemented a 9,000-position global workforce reduction in late 2025. On strategic adaptability, the record is weak, with a failure to implement a preemptive commercial plan to blunt Eli Lilly's Zepbound launch.

Capital allocation is mixed: over USD 10 billion of US manufacturing capital expenditure was committed, and analysts note capex-to-sales ratios well above historical corporate levels. Communication transparency is a concern, because management refused to provide concrete performance parameters for fiscal year 2027 and the shift to adjusted non-IFRS metrics masks underlying pricing concessions. Peer-outperforming execution is not demonstrated: Eli Lilly captured 61.9% of the weekly US GLP-1 market against Novo Nordisk's 38.1%.

The strongest counter-evidence is the oral launch, with cumulative US Wegovy pill prescriptions surpassing 5 million within 30 weeks.

Delivery record. The tenure is best read as a strong first phase followed by a reset. Early on, once-weekly Ozempic was successfully rolled out in 2018. Later, the company issued unprecedented fiscal 2026 guidance on adjusted metrics, with sales and operating profit declines of -5% to -13% at constant exchange rates.

The September 2026 Capital Markets Day triggered a significant stock sell-off, and management conceded that planning models assume steady annual price erosion of about 10% for the GLP-1 portfolio. Governance is structurally insulated but behaviourally volatile.

The Foundation's vehicle owns 28.1% of share capital but commands 77.3% of votes, and the board has no activist investor representation. In October 2025, Chair Helge Lund and six other directors resigned following a boardroom rift. The returning chair, Lars Rebien Sørensen, is intended to be temporary. The reconstituted board has five independent directors out of eight shareholder-elected representatives, and independent director Stephan Engels chairs the Audit Committee.

Against this, several major institutional shareholders abstained at the November 2025 Extraordinary General Meeting over perceived conflicts of interest. Pay disclosure is also opaque, as opaque target policies restrict real-time investor scrutiny, although a new 2026 long-term incentive programme links awards to relative total shareholder return and adjusted operating profit. Legal exposure includes antitrust suits in the US and China, but management expects no material financial impact from its active GLP-1 product liability cases, securities class actions, or the 2025 FDA warning letter.

Insider share dealing signals. The signal is weak and ambiguous. Activity was very light, with one open-market purchase by independent director Stephan Engels of 6,450 B shares in November 2025 and a sale by Martin Holst Lange of 12,000 B shares in November 2026. By value, transactions showed a net bias toward selling, though the sale may reflect a liquidity event rather than a systemic loss of confidence in the pipeline. Directors and officers hold only about 0.03% of shares outstanding, and concentrated Foundation control reduces the necessity for high executive equity ownership. Insider dealing therefore neither confirms nor contradicts the other findings.

Gaps identified from expert transcripts. Experts describe a wider distance between narrative and operations than management presentations suggest. On manufacturing, integrating the three Catalent fill-finish sites is a gradual, several-year transition, and existing contracts prevent an overnight shift to semaglutide. Device suppliers report that glass syringes and high-quality spring wire are the primary supply bottlenecks.

On pricing, an insurer executive reported that negotiated rebates reduced net realized drug prices by 10% to 15% year-on-year. On culture, global employee turnover rose to 18.4% in 2025 from 5.5% in 2024, and a former strategy leader said the restructuring completely destroyed employee trust. On pipeline, experts see CagriSema as less differentiated than Eli Lilly's retatrutide. These accounts should be weighed with care, since views from former employees could be skewed by personal grievance following the 9,000 redundancies.
