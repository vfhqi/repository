C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

C-542nd of 30

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: input-cost inflation is a net tailwind to IMCD's gross profit in euros in 2026 (the spread model passes it through), a headwind to cash and to gross margin percent, and a known reversal risk for 2027; supplier concentration is low and stable.

  • Mechanism. IMCD earns a spread, so higher supplier prices raise gross profit in euros at a roughly constant percentage margin. The Hormuz closure (from March 2026) brought supplier increases from single digits to 30-40% on semi-specialties, container freight two to three times normal, Brent above USD 100 (USD 111 in late October), and base-chemical tightness (methanol). IMCD passed these through without demand destruction; management restrained specialty increases to low single digits at principals' request.
  • Cost of the tailwind. Working capital rises with prices: net working capital was 24.5% of last-twelve-month revenue at H1 2026 against 21.6% at end-2025 (FactSet basis). Azelis' Q3 free cash flow fell 52.5% on exactly this. Gross margin percent drifts down when pass-through is in euros. Logistics costs (outsourced but paid by IMCD) inflate with diesel and driver shortages.
  • Reversal. European ethylene and propylene fell 12.9% and 14.2% in July 2026 and petrochemical spreads retraced 27% from mid-April (BASF file), yet oil re-tightened into October. When freight and feedstock normalise, prices fall, gross profit in euros falls with them, and the semi-specialty tail faces renewed Chinese supply.
  • Supplier concentration. Over 3,000 principals; the top ten are about 5% of cost of goods; exclusivity in more than 90% of contracts; top-ten relationships over ten years. Shrinking European capacity (9% of capacity announced for closure) pushes outsourcing to distributors (positive) but erodes some European-made exclusive lines (slowly negative).
  • For the model. Treat price as a separate, explicitly reversing driver of organic gross profit; link working capital to price, not only to volume.
