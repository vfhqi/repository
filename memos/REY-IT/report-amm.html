APM Mini-Memo: Reply

The one-line view. Reply sells technology projects at a fixed price, so automation can become Reply's margin, and the case depends on clients not taking that saving back when contracts renew.

What the company does

Reply is an Italian technology consultancy built as a federation of about 300 small specialist firms, each with its own profit and loss account. The specialist firms design and build digital, cloud, data and artificial intelligence systems for large clients, mostly with staff based close to the client. Revenue was €2,484 million in 2025 at a 15.8% operating margin, and Italy supplied 49% of that revenue.

About 70% of Reply's revenue is fixed-price work, where Reply quotes for a finished result and not for hours. No client exceeds 10% of sales, and about 80% of growth comes from existing clients buying more.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is that artificial intelligence raises Reply's earnings while investors still fear that the same technology shrinks them. A fixed price lets Reply keep the saving when its engineers work faster, whereas a consultancy billing by the hour hands the saving to the client. That fear took the shares from €168 in March 2025 to €90 in March 2026, and the shares stood at €135 on 13 November 2026.

The evidence so far supports the growth half of the story. Organic growth rose to 8.0% in the second quarter of 2026 from 6.6% in the first, against 3.5% at EPAM and a small decline at Globant. Deutsche Bank then raised its profit forecasts for Reply by 3% for 2026, 8% for 2027 and 9% for 2028.

Three drivers carry the story to earnings per share. The first is organic growth of 7% to 8% a year, and the second is the operating margin, which fell to 14.5% from 15.4% in the first half of 2026 because Reply launched 30 to 40 start-up units, nearly all in artificial intelligence. The third is the share buyback: shareholders authorised up to €550 million in April 2026, although one of our research reports expects a pace of only €80 million to €100 million a year.

The next twelve months can improve all three. The start-up units begin to earn their costs, German growth has already recovered to 5.5% from 0.8%, and FedTec, the American government contractor bought in July 2026, adds about €50 million of revenue at group margins in 2027.

The Target Setup

No Investment Analyst memo exists for Reply, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and the Target Setup read is my own. The Target Setup is 'CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal'. The cause for concern is artificial intelligence deflating consultancy fees, and accelerating growth is the evidence of that concern clearing. The fit holds only if Reply's earnings multiple really is low, which the valuation section questions.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple to state, harder to verify. One thing needs to go right: Reply's contract prices must hold when fixed-price contracts come up for renewal. The main risk is that clients, seeing engineers work about 30% faster, demand the saving, and our research models contracts renewing at half the billable hours. The research's downside scenario puts earnings per share at €5.50 by 2028 and fair value at €61, with a probability of about 25%.

Three signals show which way the risk is going. Average contract size falling below €1 million would show deflation arriving, and organic growth below 4% for two quarters would break the case. The German operating margin before depreciation, 12.9% in the second quarter of 2026, needs to move towards 15%.

The first check is the third quarter. One research report in our files states that organic revenue fell 1.2% in the third quarter of 2026, yet the report is dated 11 November, the day before Reply's results. A share price 4% below its 52-week high on 13 November does not look like a miss, in my reading, so the real figure needs confirming before anything else.

Business quality

High. Free cash flow has compounded at 19% a year over the last decade, capital expenditure is under 2% of revenue, and net cash was €402 million at June 2026. Reply reports profit with no adjustments, while research in our files finds peers flattering theirs by 30% to 60%. The founding family holds 69% of the votes, which brings stability and leaves minority shareholders little say.

Plausible returns and valuation

Attractive on our numbers, but the key input needs checking. The weekly routine's TSR/Valuation rating of 13 November is A, a valuation reading only, and the same routine computes a plausible return averaging 40.5% a year. That return assumes consensus earnings growth of 8.1% a year and a price-to-earnings multiple rising from 13.1 to 21.6. Against your bar, 25% or more a year is A.

Nearly all of that return is re-rating. Earnings growth, a dividend yield of about 1% and the buyback alone give a return near the 12% that marks the bottom of your scale. The 13.1 multiple also sits oddly with the rest of our files: the shares are 50% above the March low, brokers' targets run from €121 to €140, and the historic average multiple is 23. Confirming the earnings figure behind the 13.1 multiple decides whether Reply is cheap.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The pros are how Reply charges and what forces its clients to spend. Our Sector work finds that fixed-price sellers keep the automation gain, and European rules on cyber security, banking resilience and artificial intelligence make much of the demand compulsory. The weekly routine rates Sell-side Momentum B as of 12 November and Technical Momentum B as of 13 November.

The cons are a weak sector backdrop and Italy's public spending. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 13 November is C, based on the sector environment alone, with faster price falls as the sector's main listed threat. Italy's recovery-plan funding peaks in 2026, and the market studies in our files point to moderate growth afterwards, not a cliff.

In short

  • Interesting because of how Reply charges: 70% of revenue is fixed-price, so faster engineers raise Reply's margin instead of cutting its bill
  • Improving now: organic growth reached 8.0% in the second quarter of 2026, Germany recovered and brokers raised forecasts
  • One dependency: contract prices must hold at renewal, so track average contract size against €1 million and the German margin against 15%
  • Two checks before more work: the true third-quarter organic growth figure, and the earnings behind the 13.1 multiple that produces the A valuation reading
  • Next step in our process: an Investment Analyst memo, because Reply has no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings today
