EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Clariant entered the Q2 2026 print under severe operational and litigation-driven pressure, yet delivered a resounding beat across all operating divisions, with Q2 2026 net sales of CHF 941.3 million beating Vara consensus by 2.9% and adjusted EBITDA of CHF 171.0 million surpassing consensus by 12.6%. The critical deviation from pre-print expectations centered on management's ability to defend profitability through aggressive value-based pricing and accelerated cost initiatives, even as the Catalysts division suffered a severe 14.2% yearon-year volume contraction due to Middle East geopolitical disruptions.

Care Chemicals delivered the strongest outperformance, with sales reaching CHF 504.0 million and beating consensus by 3.5%, while the segment's adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to a premium 20.7%, beating consensus by 11.8%. The Catalysts division, despite experiencing a 19.2% sales decline in Swiss Francs to CHF 176.0 million, still beat the depressed consensus whisper of CHF 160.0 million by 10%, and segment EBITDA of CHF 33.0 million exceeded pessimistic expectations by 85%.

The single most transformative event was the Amsterdam District Court's complete dismissal of Shell's EUR

1 billion ethylene damages claim on July 29, 2026, just two days before the earnings release, which fundamentally de-risked the company's worst-case litigation scenario and triggered a 16% single-day share price rally. The court rejected all allegations of market harm, confirming that Shell failed to prove any quantifiable financial damage from the 2020 ethylene purchasing coordination, and validated Clariant's defense that historical buyer information exchanges had zero actual impact on market price dynamics.

This legal victory prompted immediate sell-side rating upgrades, with Deutsche Bank's Tristan Lamotte raising his target price by 68% from CHF 7.50 to CHF 12.60 and upgrading to Buy, while Jefferies' Marcus Dunford-Castro upgraded from Underperform to Hold with a target price increase from CHF 6.00 to CHF 10.20, primarily by removing a punitive 30% litigation discount. However, the litigation overhang remains material, as German courts continue to process approximately CHF 5.9 billion in outstanding damages claims, including a newly filed EUR 1.1 billion lawsuit from Dow Europe in Dortmund on July 16, 2026. Dow Europe's massive new civil lawsuit alleges antitrust damages totaling approximately EUR 1.1 billion, and German judges remain entirely independent of Dutch legal findings, free to reach divergent conclusions regarding antitrust causation. the operational front, management upgraded its performance improvement program by CHF 20 million to target CHF 100 million in total run-rate savings by 2027, with CHF 90 million expected by the end of 2026, representing the primary lever to offset geopolitical and macroeconomic headwinds. The company rapidly accelerated implementation, having already achieved CHF 69 million in savings by the end of June 2026, driven by corporate headcount reductions.

The upgraded efficiency measures triggered one-time restructuring costs of CHF 29 million, of which CHF 24 million was recognized during the second quarter, with CFO Oliver Rittgen confirming that the majority of program expenses are now behind the company. This aggressive self-help strategy successfully defended the Group's 18.2% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q2 2026, which expanded 80 basis points year-on-year despite severe volume headwinds. The critical question for the 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory centers on whether the upgraded cost-savings program can fully offset persistent Catalyst volume weakness and whether German litigation outcomes will follow the favorable Dutch precedent or instead trigger multi-billion franc cash drains.

Employee engagement metrics continue to improve, with the employee Net Promoter Score rising from +34 in 2025 to +37 in 2026, supported by an overall engagement score of 87%, positioning the company in the top quartile of global specialty chemical peers.

From a financial delivery standpoint, Clariant's H1 2026 operating cash flow surged to CHF 168.7 million from CHF 115.9 million in H1 2025, driving last-twelve-months free cash flow conversion to 52%, comfortably beating the annual target of well over 40%, though this cash generation was partially offset by higher net debt of CHF 1,493.1 million following the CHF 0.42 per share capital distribution and EUR 500 million Eurobond placement in June 2026. The oversubscribed EUR 500 million Eurobond placement, carrying a fixed coupon of 4.125% and maturing in January 2032, highlights strong institutional liquidity and credit confidence, with CFO Oliver Rittgen emphasizing that this refinancing further diversifies funding and secures the long-term capital structure. The company maintained a manageable net leverage ratio of 2.2x net debt to EBITDA, preventing capital allocation constraints despite the elevated litigation uncertainty.

Looking forward, the investment case pivots on three critical verification points: first, whether German courts in Dortmund and Munich follow the Amsterdam precedent and dismiss the remaining CHF 5.9 billion in ethylene claims; second, whether Middle East Catalyst volumes normalize in H2 2026 or remain structurally impaired into 2027; and third, whether European energy costs remain elevated, testing the sustainability of Clariant's 3.0% value-based pricing against high single-digit input inflation. European natural gas prices averaged 3.3x those in the United States during January-April 2026, placing European specialty chemical producers at an extreme structural disadvantage, while Rothschild & Co Redburn forecasted a 20% energy cost increase in Europe for 2026 at spot prices, marking a sharp reversal after three consecutive years of energy cost declines.

The strongest counter-hypothesis is that German courts processing CHF 5.9 billion in active claims are not bound by the Dutch ruling and may find against Clariant, or that H2 2026 Catalyst sales fail to recover, proving that Middle Eastern volume degearing is structural. sell-side consensus has shifted materially post-results, with Jefferies upgrading FY2026 EBITDA estimates by 4.8% to CHF 677 million and Deutsche Bank raising FY2026 EPS forecasts by 21.7% to CHF 0.76, reflecting both the operational beat and litigation de-risking. However, the underlying quality of the earnings beat remains controversial, as Deutsche Bank's Lamotte cautioned that the Q2 Care Chemicals outperformance was heavily supported by a temporary CHF 8 million inventory revaluation, suggesting that the organic upgrade is less pronounced than headline figures imply. Deutsche Bank pointed out that the Q2 2026 EBITDA beat was heavily supported by a temporary CHF 8 million inventory revaluation in Care Chemicals, without which the implied consensus upgrades would be largely flat, and if this inventory effect reverses in the second half of the year, Clariant's progress toward its target margins could stall.

This inventory distortion, combined with the persistent execution risks in Catalysts and the unresolved German litigation exposure, means that the 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory remains highly path-dependent on external factors largely outside management's control. In summary, Clariant's Q2 2026 results mark a definitive inflection point, transitioning the investment narrative from catastrophic litigation fears and Catalyst volume collapse to self-help margin expansion, specialty cash generation, and legal de-risking, though the ultimate valuation outcome hinges critically on German court decisions and the speed of Middle East volume normalization over the next twelve to eighteen months.
