EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Tenaris has undergone a material leadership transition with the appointment of Gabriel Podskubka as CEO in May 2026, succeeding Paolo Rocca who retained the Chairman role, though this dual-power structure may constrain operational autonomy if legacy leadership resists structural change. Simultaneously, the company executed a strategic pivot to an all-dividend capital allocation policy, doubling its interim dividend to USD 0.59 per share while pausing share buybacks, a move that has disappointed equity investors accustomed to aggressive capital returns. The Rig Direct service model now accounts for 70% of global sales and 90% of US sales, establishing a powerful competitive moat through integrated supply-chain coordination from mill to wellsite.

The forward financial profile reflects accelerating momentum, with revenue projected to reach USD 14.2 billion by FY27 from USD 12.4 billion in FY26, while EBITDA margins are expected to expand to 25.6% from 22.9%, supported by free cash flow generation of USD 2.2 billion on the back of recovering pricing and structural volume growth. This trajectory is underpinned by an elite balance sheet carrying USD 4.0 billion in cash and USD 3.6 billion in performance guarantees on long-term customer contracts, providing substantial operational flexibility during cyclical downturns.

Recent Q2 2026 earnings delivery demonstrated resilient fundamental performance despite transitory headwinds, with revenues falling only 4% sequentially to USD 2.97 billion while maintaining EBITDA of USD 649 million in line with consensus expectations, and net income of USD 477 million beating consensus by 9% on volumes of 946 thousand tons that exceeded forecasts. However, the Strait of Hormuz closure deferred USD 130 million in high-margin Middle Eastern premium tube shipments, though management characterizes these volumes as postponed rather than permanently lost and expects to recover them within late 2026 once maritime lanes normalize.

Peer-relative earnings momentum positions Tenaris as the second-ranked operator in a five-company peer group, delivering an actual EBITDA margin of 21.9% versus a sector median of 12.9%, narrowly trailing only Vallourec at 21.4%, while maintaining stable three-month EPS revisions at 0% change compared to negative peer trends. This outperformance is supported by robust international offshore project demand, US OCTG pricing rising on imports that are 34% lower year-on-year, and a structural global seamless supply deficit creating favorable pricing dynamics for premium products.

The top five to eight ranked key drivers that will determine forward EPS trajectory include PipeLogix Index Recovery with an estimated 15% EBITDA impact driven by US steel scrap dynamics and import volume containment creating tight domestic pricing conditions, US Onshore Rig Count Recovery contributing an estimated 10% EBITDA impact supported by lateral drilling intensity growing at 3% to 4% annually per rig, Strait of Hormuz Reopening representing an 8% EBITDA impact with high plausibility once the USD 130 million in deferred Middle Eastern volumes ship within 70 to 90 days, Premium Deepwater Volume Expansion driving a 12% EBITDA impact through offshore developments in Brazil, Guyana, and West Africa requiring specialized high-margin connections, and Vaca Muerta Frac Fleet Deployment adding a 5% EBITDA impact as Argentine shale production grows by 60,000 barrels in 2026 boosting local oilfield service demand.

Forward earnings remain highly sensitive to US PipeLogix pricing adjustments which drive immediate contract price changes across 43% of global sales, though these adjustments require one to two quarters to fully impact the P&L statement, while Tenaris maintains a variable contribution margin of approximately 49% of average selling price per ton. & Co Redburn Management guidance credibility is exceptionally strong, with Tenaris beating consensus EPS expectations in 18 of the past 21 quarters through consistent delivery above conservative forecasts, robust cost-control initiatives, and high-value product mix execution that regularly triggers post-earnings upward revisions in full-year EBITDA estimates, while current 2H 2026 guidance conservatively excludes any potential Strait of Hormuz resolution providing substantial upside optionality. - Earnings Calls & Co Redburn Sell-side positioning reflects increasing constructive sentiment, with the consensus rating distribution showing 58% Overweight, 33% Equal-weight, and 8% Underweight across fifteen active brokers as of August 2026, while the mean consensus target price of USD 60 per share implies 13% upside potential from current levels.

Key analysts include Jamie Franklin at Jefferies maintaining a Buy rating with a EUR 32 target highlighting superior Middle East and Argentina exposure, Sebastian Erskine at Redburn maintaining Neutral at EUR 26 expecting margins to exceed 25% by FY27 but noting valuation already discounts this expansion, Guillaume Delaby at Bernstein reiterating Outperform at EUR 29 emphasizing the USD 3.9 billion net cash position supporting an 11% shareholder distribution yield, and Daniel Kutz at Morgan Stanley maintaining Equal-weight at USD 60 after upgrading from Underweight on domestic pricing rising 10% year-to-date from falling imports and tariff-driven foreign steel costs. External headwinds remain material, as global seamless pipe markets face accelerating competitive intensity from restructured western peers like Vallourec that trades at a 30% EV/EBITDA discount despite 100% seamless product mix, while Chinese and Indian welded mills command 40% market share each in commoditized segments and Chinese seamless producers increasingly export sophisticated products to the Middle East threatening premium market share through blatant intellectual property replication.

Regulatory protectionism provides a double-edged sword, with US Section 232 tariffs raised to 50% in June 2025 and the EU New Trade Tool reducing duty-free imports by 47% while imposing out-of-quota tariffs of 50% and the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism enforcing punitive default carbon costs up to EUR 600 per ton, simultaneously shielding domestic margins but increasing raw material import costs by approximately USD 140 million per quarter and disrupting global supply flows.

The primary risk scenario centers on a prolonged maritime blockade in the Middle East combined with a collapse in US steel protectionism, which would compress margins below 25% if domestic operators aggressively substitute seamless connections with low-cost Asian welded imports flooding the market, while the Rig Direct model's elevated working capital intensity of 143 days locks up substantial cash in inventory that would face write-downs if US drilling activity contracts sharply.

Conversely, the joint probability of simultaneous US pricing recovery and offshore volume expansion is assessed as highly likely to drive outsized EPS growth, supported by safe-side model assumptions that exclude optimistic pricing scenarios and provide substantial valuation buffer if individual drivers fail to materialize.
