This structural shift would displace traditional truck brokers by enabling shippers to coordinate directly with autonomous carriers, bypassing asset-light intermediaries entirely.
7.
Heightened competition: Amazon Supply Chain Services poses a growing threat of direct disintermediation as it expands its asset-heavy logistics capabilities to non-Amazon marketplace sellers. Morgan Stanley notes that Amazon has spent years refining its domestic parcel, warehousing, and air cargo networks for its own operations. By opening these services to all shippers, Amazon could compress industry contract logistics pricing and erode the competitive advantage of diversified forwarders like DHL.
8.
Integrated capabilities: Direct customer booking initiatives by vertically integrated ocean carriers threaten to bypass traditional ocean freight consolidation models. Bernstein Research observes that Maersk has openly prioritized its own Logistics & Services shipments over those of competing forwarders during periods of tight capacity. This aggressive integrator strategy risks alienating major freight forwarding customers and forcing them to divert their container volumes to non-integrated carriers.

Structural Rewiring and Macro Squeezes

9.
Lower complexity: The structural regionalisation of manufacturing and supply chains represents a profound headwind for deep-sea logistics volumes over the next four years. Morgan Stanley analysis indicates that as companies shift toward nearshoring and domestic sourcing, supply chains will naturally become shorter and far less complex. This transition would reduce the need for freight forwarders who primarily earn their margins by smoothing out international shipping friction and border-crossing inefficiencies.
10.
Stricter regulations: Rising regulatory enforcement and landmark judicial rulings in North America are dramatically increasing the compliance costs and legal liabilities of transport brokers. Jefferies Research highlights that the Supreme Court's unanimous Montgomery decision strips logistics intermediaries of their historic federal preemption safety shield. This ruling will likely force freight brokers to exclude smaller independent owneroperators entirely, severely tightening available trucking capacity and escalating administrative overhead.
11.
Slower growth: Shippers are responding to persistent macroeconomic and geopolitical uncertainty by transitioning away from long-term contract arrangements toward short-term mini-bids. An expert from an energy procurement background revealed that companies are actively splitting their shipping volumes across multiple forwarders to build supply chain resilience. This highly transactional behavior limits the pricing leverage of forwarders, making it difficult to sustain high conversion margins when rates decline.
12.
Adverse translation: Freight forwarders face persistent foreign exchange exposure when their reporting currency appreciates against major global trade currencies. Jefferies Research notes that logistics conglomerates like DHL earn a massive share of revenues in foreign currencies, introducing material translation volatility. For Kuehne + Nagel, a strengthening Swiss franc would depress reported operating profits even if local-currency conversion margins remain stable.
