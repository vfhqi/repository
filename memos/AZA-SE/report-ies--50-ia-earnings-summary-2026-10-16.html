Avanza (AZA-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

16 October 2026

These results, as far as the research in front of us carries them, strengthen the case modestly: the substance stands where it was, the price leg has moved toward the level at which the case says the thesis clears its bar, and the one thing the quarter adds that the case did not count is that the Swedish duopolist has started using price.

One caveat first, because it governs everything below. The research we have was written before the print. It carries no reported Q3 figure: it sets the company-collected consensus as the bar, carries the September monthly statistics, and records the broker stances taken into the results, not after them. So what we can test is the run-in to the quarter and the market's positioning. The print itself, and the sell-side's reaction to it, fall to the second review four working days from now.

What changed

The recurring base ran ahead of the case's path. Customers reached 2,391,100 at 30 September 2026, up 8 percent year on year from 2,204,000, with 19,200 net new in September. Net inflow was SEK 7.18 billion in September against SEK 4.82 billion in September 2025, SEK 58.5 billion for the year to date and SEK 65 billion rolling twelve months. Savings capital reached SEK 1.28 trillion, up 21 percent year on year, and Avanza's share of the Swedish savings market reached 8.7 percent at 30 September 2026. Set against the case: its confirm levels were about SEK 4 billion a month of inflow, about 15,000 customers a month and a share of 8.4 percent and rising, and its path needs savings capital compounding about 11 percent a year on a 7 percent market plus 4 to 5 percent net inflow. Every series passes. The case also said consensus FY2027 revenue implied average 2027 savings capital of only about SEK 1,250 billion; capital already stood above that at the end of Q3.

The transactional leg normalised, and for the first time in this data it fell year on year. DNB Carnegie puts Q3 trading volume down 16 percent sequentially and daily trades per user down 25 percent, partly offset by average trade sizes up 9 percent. Brokerage-generating notes averaged 151,200 a day in September 2026, down 11 percent from 169,000 in September 2025, and Deutsche Bank's tracker has executed trades down 10 percent year on year in September. Cross-border trading was 31 percent of turnover in Q2 2026 against 23 percent a year earlier, and gross brokerage income held at 11 basis points of turnover in Q2. The case's alarm levels, monthly turnover down 30 percent and a foreign share below 22 percent, are not touched.

The bar for the print. The Modular Finance consensus of 18 analysts has Q3 net profit at SEK 801 million (SEK 795 million in Q2), operating income SEK 1,310 million (SEK 1,334 million), operating profit SEK 935 million (SEK 928 million), net interest income SEK 473 million (SEK 486 million), net brokerage SEK 323 million, fund commissions SEK 252 million and operating expenses SEK 373 million (SEK 406 million). Deutsche Bank sits 17 percent above on net profit at SEK 931 million, with net interest income of SEK 524 million and brokerage of SEK 385 million; DNB Carnegie models total income down 1 percent sequentially with net interest income down 4 percent and brokerage down 6 percent. DNB Carnegie notes consensus estimates were trimmed into the results, on the guided Danish pre-launch costs.

The sell-side went into the print more bullish than the case assumed, and more split. Deutsche Bank initiated on 30 September 2026 at Buy with a SEK 440 target, net profit estimates for 2026 to 2028 9 percent ahead of consensus and a 14 percent EPS compound rate 2025 to 2028. DNB Carnegie kept Buy and raised its target to SEK 430, with 2026 EPS at SEK 19.04 and 2027 at SEK 19.34 after a 3 percent cut for Denmark, and names a potential 15 percent upside to 2027 EPS if trading returns to H1 levels. Jefferies kept Underperform at SEK 335 on 2027 EPS of SEK 18.65 against a compiled consensus of SEK 19.60, about 21 times 2027 earnings and a PEG of about 2 against Nordnet's 1.5.

The rate path turned. Nordea expects the Riksbank to hold at 1.75 percent, then hike 25 basis points in November and again in February 2027 to 2.25 percent, which it calls the cycle's peak. DNB Carnegie expects three-month STIBOR to stabilise at about 2.25 percent and calls a parallel 20 basis point upward shift a direct net-interest-income catalyst. Against that, DNB Carnegie also notes household deposit betas reached 80 percent in the cutting cycle with deposit rates near zero, so the room to buffer any further cut is gone. The case's modal path had the rate at or near 1.75 percent through 2027.

Competition became an event. Nordnet launched a two-part campaign in Sweden in September 2026 for its 30th anniversary: existing Swedish users trade commission-free on buys one week a month on 30 selected stocks, and new clients trade commission-free on Nordic exchanges up to SEK 10 million of turnover through June 2027. DNB Carnegie calls it a direct competitive threat to Avanza, at a gross cost to Nordnet of SEK 26 million of foregone commission, and expects the dilution to be minor. Interactive Brokers has launched Swedish ISK accounts. Deutsche Bank's tracker has Nordnet outperforming on customer and deposit growth in late September. Avanza's stated response is that brand, simplicity and user experience are now more differentiating than price; it is not matching.

Denmark moved from recruiting to staffed. Christian Sillesen, from Lunar and Saxo Bank, is Country Manager from 12 October 2026. The envelope is unchanged: SEK 120 million to 150 million pre-launch, 20 percent capitalised, SEK 50 million in the 2026 cost base of which SEK 3 million was expensed by Q2, up to SEK 60 million a year of marketing for three years, launch in H2 2027, profitability five years after. DNB Carnegie counts SEK 80 million a year of running cost as about 2 percent annual dilution and excludes all Danish income until launch. Jefferies counts SEK 340 million to 380 million over three years and expects the group operating margin to fall to 60 percent. Swedish cost guidance of 9 percent for 2026 was confirmed, with H1 cost inflation at 6.6 percent.

The price fell into the print while estimates rose. The shares closed at SEK 376.3 on 15 October 2026 against SEK 390 at the memo, below the 50-day average of SEK 391.0 and 4.5 percent above the 200-day of SEK 360.1; three months minus 2.7 percent, twelve months plus 4.8 percent; relative strength at the 47th percentile of the universe. Consensus EPS 24 months forward is SEK 22.52 against the SEK 22.13 the case priced on, so the multiple fell from 17.6 times to 16.7 times, the 15th percentile of the stock's ten-year range from the 18th. Revisions are up 2, 3 and 6 percent over one, three and six months, the buy share is 48 percent from 44, the consensus target SEK 420.96. The dashboard's pinned letters now read technical momentum B, sell-side earnings momentum A and valuation A, and the re-based TSR/Valuation reading is B; the case carried C, absent, C and C.

The challenge

First reading: the Swedish duopoly's margin-first posture, which the Foundations rest on, has broken. The case rated 'Competition' C on the ground that intensity was tightening at the margin but "no price war has started", rated 'Competitor attitude' B as "margin-first, neither has used price as a weapon", and placed the one plausible shark attack in Denmark, not Sweden. Nordnet is now buying Swedish customers with nine months of commission-free trading, in the same month Interactive Brokers arrived with a Swedish ISK, and Nordnet out-added Avanza on customers and deposits in late September. In its strongest form this reading says the growth gap the case conceded (8 against 13 percent customer growth) is about to be bought wider, that Avanza's refusal to match is the right policy for margin and the wrong one for the growth narrative the market in this sector pays for, and that the case's 'Industry structure' B and 'Competitor attitude' B are rated on a posture that no longer holds. For it to be right, Avanza's monthly net new customers would have to fall below the case's 15,000 a month confirm level over October to December while Nordnet's rise, or Avanza's brokerage yield would have to fall below the Q2 level if it does respond. What stands against it is in the data: 19,200 net new customers in September, share up to 8.7 percent, and DNB Carnegie's reading that a SEK 26 million campaign dilutes little. The case's own defence, that its path needs the Swedish franchise and not the growth race, holds unless the Swedish franchise itself slows. Plausible, not yet probable, and the case does not hold it at all.

Second reading: the rate lever has turned from a cleared concern into a driver the case did not model. The case carried the policy rate as a 'Case/SP Key Risk', neutral in direction, with the sensitivity asymmetric in the company's favour (a point of cuts SEK 420 million of net interest income, a point of rises SEK 1,170 million) and the modal path flat at 1.75 percent through 2027. If Nordea is right, two 25 basis point hikes by February 2027 add, on the case's own sensitivity, about SEK 585 million of annualised net interest income, which is about 16 percent of the consensus FY2026 pre-tax profit the case carried and several times the 3.5 percent gap between the case's FY2027 path and consensus. In its strongest form this reading says the next upgrade round is net-interest-income-led rather than flow-led, that an upgrade on the exact line the old concern compressed is the clearing evidence the pattern wants, and that the thesis's C at the price is wrong in the other direction, since on this path the return would clear the bar at SEK 376 without a re-rating. For it to be right, the Riksbank would have to hike in November and February, and Avanza would have to keep most of the rise rather than pass it to depositors, which the first reading bears on: a nine-month Nordnet campaign and a new foreign entrant are the conditions under which deposit pricing competes too. Against it, the macro evidence in the research is not unanimous, and the case's own note that Swedish inflation near zero argues for cuts has not been withdrawn. Plausible; the case holds the asymmetry but not the path.

The obvious bear reading is one the case already holds, and we do not put it up as new. That H1 2026 was the top of the trading cycle and Q3 is the first quarter of the down-leg is held in the case at about 25 percent odds, beside normalisation at about 60 percent, and the case names "a seasonal normalisation read as a miss" as a share-price risk. What the quarter adds is the first year-on-year decline in daily notes and a price that fell 3.5 percent on rising estimates, which is that risk happening. The run-in raises the pressure on the 'Transactional velocity and cross-border mix' Key Driver; it does not move the reading into probable, because one seasonally weak month with trade sizes up 9 percent and the foreign share at 31 percent is not a cycle turn, and the case's alarm levels are untouched.

What would settle it

The print of 16 October 2026 and the second review four working days on carry most of it. That review will carry Q3 net interest income against the SEK 473 million consensus, the first full quarter of the internalised deposits and the case's own near step; brokerage against SEK 323 million and fund commissions against SEK 252 million, which is the trading-cycle reading in money; operating expenses against SEK 373 million with Danish spend to date against the SEK 50 million for the year; net profit against SEK 801 million against Deutsche Bank's SEK 931 million; deposit volumes and the interest-bearing share; and the broker revisions after the print, which is where we learn whether Deutsche Bank's 9 percent above consensus is chased and whether DNB Carnegie's conditional 15 percent is kept or dropped.

It will not carry the answer to either new reading. The Riksbank's November decision falls after it. The October monthly statistics, the first full month under Nordnet's campaign, land in early November on the company's pattern, and the campaign runs to June 2027, so the full answer to the first reading is a 2027 answer, read monthly against the case's 15,000 customers and SEK 4 billion of inflow a month and quarterly against the Q2 brokerage yield. The second reading is read daily in three-month STIBOR against the case's 1.25 percent refute level and DNB Carnegie's 2.25 percent stabilisation, and settled by the Riksbank in November and February.

Two things nothing scheduled will answer. Avanza Sigma's assets under management beyond the SEK 100 million of 30 June 2026 and whether its 0.55 to 0.85 percent yield holds as it scales: the company has not committed to a quarterly figure, and the research itself names this as the open disclosure. And the Danish cost against its envelope: the first marketing year starts at launch in H2 2027, so Jefferies' SEK 340 million to 380 million against the company's SEK 120 million to 150 million cannot be adjudicated before 2028.
