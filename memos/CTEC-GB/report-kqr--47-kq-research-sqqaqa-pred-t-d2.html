Section 3 – US Medicare DMEPOS Bidding and Distributor Implications

Section summary
  1. The planned US Medicare competitive bidding round starting in 2028 introduces major pricing risks that are partially mitigated by high volume gains from market consolidation.
  2. Legislative efforts to delay the program for five years provide a significant near-term catalyst that could completely eliminate expected earnings headwinds.

Bidding Timeline and Delay Bill

1.
Threatening timeline: The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services established a clear framework for the upcoming competitive bidding round that begins on 1 January 2028. This program opens bidder registration on 1 December 2026 and sets the formal bidding window from 5 January to 10 March 2027. Winning suppliers and pricing rates will be announced in late summer of 2027 before implementation. These regulatory milestones mean that Convatec faces a highly concentrated timeline of potential pricing resets over the next eighteen months.
2.
Potential relief: The bipartisan Access to Medical Supplies Act introduced in Congress on 9 October 2026 proposes a five-year delay for ostomy and continence bidding. DNB Carnegie reported on 22 October 2026 that the bill is at an early stage and its passage remains highly uncertain. Consequently, baseline estimates must incorporate competitive bidding starting in January 2028. Analysts should continue to retain baseline implementation in their models until legislative approval is secured.
3.
Quantified headwind: Convatec has guided to a manageable one to two percent reduction in group sales during the initial implementation year. Morgan Stanley calculated on 13 October 2026 that this guidance assumes a thirty percent reimbursement cut on the company's direct Medicare exposure. This drag equates to an estimated thirty to sixty million dollars in lost sales and a three to eight percent headwind to consensus group earnings. Consequently, a successful delay of the program would directly protect Convatec's near-term profitability and accelerate margin expansion.
4.
Relative insulation: Coloplast faces a smaller top-line threat from the planned Medicare competitive bidding program due to its lower direct retail distribution exposure. Bernstein Research reported on 28 January 2026 that Coloplast's Comfort Medical retail business represents only one percent of total group sales. This creates a lower top-line headwind compared to Convatec's substantial retail presence in the United States. Thus, Coloplast is relatively insulated from the immediate margin compression at the retail level during the initial rollout.

Historical Precedents and Rate Pressure

5.
Massive compression: Historical Medicare bidding rounds across other durable medical equipment categories have consistently triggered steep pricing cuts. Deutsche Bank reported on 9 December 2019 that prices for seven key items plunged by thirty-four point seven percent compared to baseline fee schedules. These deep adjustments broke traditional medical equipment business models and forced providers to alter their servicing structures. This precedent shows that competitive bidding represents a highly disruptive tool for government cost containment that structurally lowers fee schedules.
6.
Forced consolidation: Previous competitive bidding rounds have aggressively consolidated the medical supply industry by eliminating smaller regional providers. Deutsche Bank reported on 9 December 2019 that the number of providers collapsed by about forty percent after the program was implemented. This severe rationalization occurred because smaller players lacked the operational efficiency and scale to survive on lower reimbursements. Larger providers with integrated technology and automated processes are generally better positioned to gain market share during these regulatory resets.
7.
Shifting profitability: Prior bidding rounds transformed the durable medical equipment market by decimating high- margin equipment rentals. An expert call on 5 January 2024 revealed that pricing pressure on rentals forced a structural shift toward recurring consumable supplies. While base devices are now sold close to cost, distributors generate their margins on recurring resupply orders. This structural shift highlights why the recurring revenue of ostomy and continence supplies remains a highly attractive but heavily targeted sector.
8.

Bernstein Research explained on 28 January 2026 that compressed retail margins force distributors to demand steep wholesale price cuts. While retailers bear the initial shock, they lean on manufacturers to protect their own operating profits. Consequently, even manufacturers with no direct retail channels face inevitable pressure on wholesale pricing schedules once bidding concludes.

Section 4 – European Regulatory Pressures and Price Cut Precedents

Section summary
  1. European regulatory pricing measures planned for 2027 present direct margin pressures that are highly dependent on geographic exposure.
  2. Historic price cuts, such as the 2019 French reform, demonstrate that medical device manufacturers can successfully mitigate up to half of the pricing impact.
  3. Coloplast remains structurally more vulnerable to European pricing reforms than Convatec due to its much larger regional revenue footprint.
  4. Convatec possesses a highly structured self-help margin bridge that relies on automated packaging lines and G&A consolidation to offset persistent polymer and logistics inflation.
  5. Cash generation remains strong with a default capital allocation policy that channels surplus cash into post-2026 share buybacks while maintaining a stable leverage target of 2.0 times EBITDA.
