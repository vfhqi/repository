EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 5 findings

Renew Holdings plc has executed a deliberate strategic transformation into a pure-play engineering services provider through the disposal of its Specialist Building segment (Walter Lilly) in October 2024, while simultaneously deploying capital into high-margin acquisitions across onshore wind (Full Circle), electricity transmission and distribution (Emerald Power, EDES, PWR-X), and marine water services (Edwards Diving Services).

The group is navigating a planned leadership transition with Pete Raby–former CEO of Morgan Advanced Materials who achieved peak operating margins of 13.6%–joining as Chair designate in October 2026 before formally succeeding David Brown in January 2027. Recent operational momentum shows material near-term deceleration, with organic revenue growth slowing to 2.2% in H1 2026 versus a five-year historical average of 9%, though the group delivered record absolute revenues of £589m and maintained operating margins of 5.7% within management's guided 6%-7% full-year corridor.

Forward earnings visibility remains underpinned by unprecedented regulatory commitments, with 90% of infrastructure spending driven by non-discretionary cycles including Ofwat's £104bn AMP8 water programme representing a circa 75% spend increase, Network Rail's £45.4bn CP7 allocation, and RIIO-ED2/T3 grid upgrades totaling over £50bn. Sell-side positioning is universally positive with 100% Buy recommendations and zero Hold or Sell ratings across the consolidated coverage pool, though structural risks persist if organic growth stagnation continues and management fails to execute the

French wind subsidiary restructuring or resolve ongoing Sellafield industrial action impacts.

1.
Change Materiality: The Walter Lilly disposal on 4 October 2024 for nominal cash consideration eliminated historical latent defect risk and is estimated to enhance group operating margins by 40 bps, establishing Renew as a structurally higher-margin business. The £49.3m acquisition of onshore wind specialist Full Circle at approximately 10x EBITDA unlocks a technology-enabled platform generating sustainable operating margins of approximately 14% under long-term service agreements. The sequential acquisitions of Emerald Power (£7.8m initial consideration at 4.1x EV/EBITDA) and subsequent bolt-ons create a turnkey overhead and underground transmission and distribution offering that immediately upgraded group EPS estimates. Operationally, the group created an Executive Board in 2024 to manage increased complexity following rapid M&A deployment, while the employee survey response rate declined to 70% in 2025 from 78% in 2024

Ofwat's £104bn AMP8 determination more than doubles prior infrastructure investment, with Renew operating across 10 of 12 major waste and water regions, and Deutsche Bank analysts noting water activity is tracking ahead of expectations as AMP8 year two approaches. Network Rail's prioritisation of reactive maintenance weather-related works fully offsets subdued renewals spend, with management anticipating smoother CP7 renewals activity moving into year two of the cycle, while Renew's mid-single digit rail market share leaves substantial room for organic growth.

The M&A integration pathway includes Excalon's £26m entry into underground extra-high voltage cabling, Emerald Power's £7.8m addition of overhead line expertise, EDES design capabilities, PWR-X cable jointing, and Full Circle's expansion to 747 active turbine service agreements.

Plausible problems to monitor

Renew Holdings faces material organic growth deceleration, with H1 2026 organic revenue expansion slowing sharply to 2.2%, falling well below the five-year historical average of 9%. This slowdown represents a significant structural shift in the group's volume momentum and raises questions about whether the defensive infrastructure thesis can sustain historical compounding rates without aggressive M&A deployment. The organic growth rate of 2.2% lags substantially behind peer Keller Group's double-digit expansion, suggesting that Renew's positioning in purely UK-regulated markets may be constraining near-term top-line acceleration relative to internationally diversified competitors with exposure to higher-growth geographies. - Earnings Calls The H1 2026 adjusted operating margin of 5.7% remained below the full-year guided range of 6% to 7%, indicating persistent operational drag during the first half despite management's assertion that work mix improvements would drive margin expansion.

While this margin outperforms the peer median of 3.3% led by Balfour Beatty, the failure to reach the lower bound of guidance during a period characterized as delivering "record revenue" signals execution challenges or mix headwinds not fully communicated to the market. Adjusted operating profit dipped slightly to £32.0m in H1 FY25 due to Rail renewals delays, establishing a pattern of temporary margin compression during regulatory transition periods that management has struggled to fully offset through reactive maintenance volume.

Network Rail renewals remained subdued during H1 2026, compelling a strategic shift toward reactive maintenance, which inherently carries lower visibility and less predictable revenue phasing than large planned renewals programs. Rail transition headwinds caused adjusted operating profit to dip in H1 FY25, demonstrating that the group's rail division exhibits recurring vulnerability to regulatory cycle handovers despite management's assurances of transition smoothing.

The group experiences temporary volume deferrals whenever new regulatory control periods commence in rail, establishing a clear pattern that the CP7 transition weakness is structural rather than isolated, and future cycle handovers (CP8 onward) will likely repeat this dislocation absent proactive contract restructuring. Ongoing site-wide industrial action at Sellafield continued to impact civil nuclear performance, while Full Circle's short-term performance was impacted by subsidiary underperformance in France, indicating that two material operating units are simultaneously experiencing execution headwinds.

Full Circle's operations require restructuring of wind operations in France, suggesting that the £49.3m acquisition completed in October 2024 is delivering below initial underwriting assumptions within its first year of ownership. The French exposure, representing 22% of Full Circle revenue based on the stated 78% UK weighting, introduces geographic diversification risk that Renew has historically avoided, and the need for immediate restructuring questions the quality of pre-acquisition due diligence.

Net debt reached £21.6 million when including IFRS 16 lease obligations, though leverage remains well below peer limits and the group's maximum target of 2x EBITDA. While leverage metrics appear conservative in absolute terms, the shift from a pre-IFRS 16 net cash position of £10.6m to post-IFRS 16 net debt of £21.6m reveals meaningful operating lease commitments that could constrain financial flexibility if cash conversion deteriorates.

Peer Keller's geotechnical model remained highly capital-intensive, requiring substantial working capital of £87.5 million, and while Renew's asset-light model avoids this burden, any migration toward more capital-intensive service lines (such as design-build rather than pure maintenance) could rapidly erode the group's structural ROCE advantage.

The Full Circle acquisition was completed for £49.3m, representing entry at approximately 10x EBITDA compared to cheaper sector transactions, suggesting Renew paid a valuation premium for strategic positioning rather than securing a distressed or opportunistic entry price. Management anticipates Full Circle will generate a sustainable operating margin of approximately 14%, yet the immediate need for French restructuring indicates this margin target may be at risk in the near term, potentially requiring further integration costs or revenue write-downs that were not contemplated in the original acquisition model.

the true underlying run-rate profitability may sit below reported figures. H1 FY26 saw working capital inflow of £0.2m compared to an outflow of £3.3m in H1 FY25, indicating favorable year-over-year comparison, but the absolute inflow of only £0.2m on a revenue base of £589m suggests working capital management remains a drag on cash generation relative to the group's historical conversion rates. The retained liabilities from disposed builder Allenbuild present potential latent defect claim outflows, creating an unknown tail risk that could crystallize into material cash outflows if legacy construction defects emerge years after project completion.

The Walter Lilly disposal was executed for a nominal cash consideration, eliminating historical latent defect risk, yet the need to dispose of the business for nominal consideration rather than positive proceeds signals that the Specialist Building division had become a value-destroying drag on group returns, and management effectively paid to exit the segment.
