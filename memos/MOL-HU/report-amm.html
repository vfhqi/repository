APM Mini-Memo: MOL Hungarian Oil & Gas

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 1 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 10 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: the list of 1 October; price and FactSet data of 30 September; script ratings of 29 and 30 September; research digests of 29 July and 23 September; no Investment Analyst memo.

The one-line view. Bearish, working hypothesis: MOL Hungarian Oil & Gas has strong momentum and a 9.2 times headline multiple, but both rest on peak-cycle refining margins that management itself expects to normalise, so earnings per share over 18 months to 3 years look more likely to step down than up.

What the company does

MOL is Central Europe's integrated energy group: exploration and production in eight countries, three landlocked refineries and two petrochemical plants, over 2,300 service stations, Hungary's sole high-pressure gas transmission network of nearly 6,000 km, and a waste and recycling arm built on a 35-year Hungarian state concession. It holds 45% of Hungarian retail fuel, 46% in Slovakia and 60% in Croatia through INA. A holding structure took effect through a demerger on 1 April 2026. At 4,974 forints on 30 September the market value is about 12.0 billion euros.

A plausible investment thesis

Cyclical, not structural. The bull thesis is that disrupted European diesel supply keeps refining margins high for longer, and MOL's landlocked, diesel-heavy system turns that into cash. The mechanism is direct: each dollar a barrel on the complex refining margin adds 117 million dollars a year to clean operating profit (MOL's clean current-cost-of-supply earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation), and each 10 dollars on Brent adds a net 102 million dollars. The second quarter of 2026 showed the gearing: clean operating profit of 1,297 million dollars, up 89% and 38% above consensus, on a complex margin of 21.7 dollars a barrel against 6.0 a year earlier, with net profit of 786 million dollars against 103 million.

The self-help leg is Tomorrow Downstream, targeting 500 million dollars of annual savings by 2027 to hold Downstream above 1.4 billion dollars. The earnings review's own call (23 September) runs the other way: consensus earnings per share for 2027 and 2028 cut by 15 to 20% as margins normalise. Drivers to track: the complex refining margin, Druzhba pipeline flows, the unsigned JANAF contract for the Adria pipeline, Hungary's 95% windfall tax on the Brent-Urals spread (extended to end-2026), and petrochemical margins, already back to 119 euros a tonne in July from 548 in the second quarter.

The Target Setup

There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and this read is mine. No setup fits cleanly. The closest is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU', resting on Tomorrow Downstream, the new holding structure and the Aphrodite gas acquisition, but none of those lifts earnings per share inside our window, and Aphrodite's first gas is expected in 2031. The live driver is a margin peak, the mirror image of 'CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough, leading to reversion to normal': here reversion to normal points down.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Not simple. There are six drivers and they are correlated: falling Brent and refining margins together could cut cash generation by over 300 million dollars. Governments (price caps in 10 of 12 markets, Slovak levies, the windfall tax) and a transit monopoly (JANAF at more than three times Transalpine pipeline rates) capture much of the rent. Add the July 2026 legal dispute over 126 billion forints of carbon dioxide quota taxes, which our files describe both as a refund claim and as a disputed liability of about 395 million dollars, against 18 million recognised in the quarter. Project HIVE doubled staff turnover to 13% in the first half of 2026, just as the savings programme needs delivering. The July pre-mortem directed a firm Park.

Business quality

Good franchise, poor rent capture. Landlocked isolation, feedstock integration and oligopolistic retail (the top five distributors hold over 80% of regional volumes) are real advantages, and non-fuel reached a record 41.5% of Consumer Services margin. But the earnings record is erratic: a 13.9% miss in the second quarter of 2025, a refinery fire that October, and a 10-week Druzhba stoppage that took the first quarter of 2026 down to 626 million dollars. Full-year 2026 guidance is 3 billion dollars. Net debt was 0.49 times operating profit at 30 June, but reached 1.0 times in the first quarter after a 1.4 billion dollar working capital build. Minorities have little leverage: a 30.49% foundation stake, a voting cap and a state veto share.

Plausible returns and valuation

The script's TSR/Valuation reading of 30 September is F, a valuation reading only with no Modal return. FactSet puts the multiple at 9.2 times 24-month forward earnings per share of 541 forints and ranks it at the 98th percentile. My arithmetic against 4,974 forints: the August consensus target of 5,350 is 8% up; the bull case of 6,307, which assumes tight refining into 2028, is 27% up; the bear case of 3,690 is 26% down. The 2025 dividend of about 300 forints a share, including a 120-forint special, yields about 6%. The earnings review puts three-year returns at mid-single digits a year, below the D anchor of about 12%.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros: the script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is B, on the energy environment only and provisional, and the list-maker cites the Energy Overweight. Central and Eastern European fuel demand grows steadily, energy security favours integrated regional supply, and Aphrodite adds 104 billion cubic metres of contingent gas.

Cons: European naphtha-based petrochemicals face structural decline against gas-fed rivals, and the May explosion at Tiszaújváros cut petrochemical volumes 33%. The internal model assumes European Union carbon prices of 100 to 140 euros a tonne by 2030, each 10 euros costing 26 million dollars. Hungary's deficit, expected at 7% of gross domestic product in 2026, invites more taxation of MOL.

The strongest counter-case

Momentum is the counter-case, and it is strong. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of 29 September is A and its Technical Momentum reading of 30 September is B; twelve-month relative strength is 97 and the price is 6% below its 52-week high. Overweight ratings rose from 11% in May to 33% in August as the consensus target moved from 4,504 to 5,350. Management budgets on 65-dollar Brent against 92.3 dollars realised in the first half, the savings programme does not depend on the cycle, and a signed JANAF contract or the end of the windfall tax would lift earnings structurally. If refining stays tight into 2028, 9.2 times is cheap and the bear case is simply early.
