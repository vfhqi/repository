[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Cenergy is a 'Demand Inflection' case in its fourth year: a good and improving high-voltage cable maker whose change has already travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and into the share price. At EUR 23.10 the shares pay for the growth we can prove, so the modal return is about 10 percent a year over eighteen months against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case is compelling as a business and not compelling as an investment at this price.

  • The change is external, sector-wide and policy-mandated, and it is as strong as any in the universe.
    • European and US grid operators are contracting high-voltage cable for years ahead at prices that have doubled, because regulated transmission capex is rising faster than qualified capacity.
    • The whole tier's order books say so, not one company's: NKT's transmission backlog EUR 13.5bn, Nexans' EUR 7.9bn, Prysmian's EUR 17bn
      • Morgan Stanley reads required high-voltage cable demand as equal to or above industry capacity through 2028
    • Cenergy's own backlog rose from EUR 1.0bn in 2021 to a record EUR 3.9bn at 30 June 2026, 1.9 times FY2025 revenue of EUR 2,061m, with IPTO Lot A production contracted from 2027 through 2031
    • Policy pushes the same way in every jurisdiction where the company has just invested: EU steel safeguards from 1 July 2026, US Section 232 tariffs of 50 percent, Build America Buy American, UK local-content rules
  • The company met the change with its own actions, and those actions are already in the profit-and-loss account.
    • It doubled subsea and land capacity between 2023 and early 2025, chose value over volume, bought Hartlepool for GBP 10m, is building Maryland for USD 200m, and qualified for 525 kV HVDC in July/August 2026.
    • Cables adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 16.3 percent in H1 2025 to 19.5 percent in H1 2026 on pass-through metal, so the gain is price in real terms
    • Guidance was raised four years running, most recently on 4 August 2026 to EUR 390-420m of FY2026 adjusted EBITDA from EUR 370-400m, pulling the 2027 ambition forward a full year
    • What is absent is leadership or perimeter change
      • the internal force is the extension of a five-year path, not a new direction
  • 'Financial Outputs' have moved on every layer, and the sell-side has caught up.
    • Revenue grew from EUR 1.05bn in FY2021 to EUR 2.06bn in FY2025, EPS from EUR 0.116 to EUR 0.912, and H1 2026 EPS rose 45 percent.
    • Consensus FY2027 EPS is up 21.8 percent over twelve months with no cut anywhere in the series, and all five named brokers are at Buy or Overweight
    • But consensus FY2026 adjusted EBITDA of EUR 417m already sits above the guidance midpoint of EUR 405m, and the one-month revision is nil, so the upgrade leg behind us is larger than the one ahead
    • Cash lags by the design of the capex cycle: free cash flow was EUR -34.4m in FY2025 and consensus has EUR -54m for FY2026, then EUR 268m in FY2027 as capex falls from EUR 285m to EUR 132m
  • What we think it earns: EPS of EUR 1.21 in FY2026 rising to EUR 1.79 in FY2029, 14.2 percent a year, of the coveted kind.
    • Revenue grows 11.7 percent a year to EUR 3,250m, the adjusted EBITDA margin holds at about 18 percent, net finance costs fall from EUR 44m to EUR 24m, tax holds at 22.5 percent, and the share count stays at 212.4m.
    • That is a little above consensus, which compounds at 12.3 percent a year from EUR 1.20 to EUR 1.70, because consensus drifts the margin to 17.1 percent by FY2028 and we hold it at 18 percent
    • The growth is revenue-led at an unchanged high margin rather than a below-EBIT effect, which is what investors extrapolate
  • What it costs: EUR 23.10, or 15.7 times consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 1.4695, at the 84th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history.
    • Against the cable majors the shares are modestly cheap (Prysmian 18.5x, NKT 17.8x, Nexans 13.4x, an average of 16.6x)
    • against their own past they are dear.
    • The share price is +105.8 percent over twelve months and -3.0 percent over three: the 4 August results, the guidance raise and the EUR 1.15bn IPTO award did not move it further, so the contracted catalysts are in the price
    • The multiple is industry-driven, having tracked the grid theme from a five-year median of 10.2x to 15.7x, so the swing in it is not ours to earn
      • the residual discount to NKT and Prysmian is company-specific (Viohalco control at 66.7 percent, a 33 percent free float, no vessels) and does not change in the horizon
    • We concede the ten-year median of 11.0x is the wrong floor because the mix has changed (adjusted EBITDA margin 8.8 percent in FY2019 to 16.4 percent in FY2025; cables 61 percent of revenue in FY2019 to 71 percent in FY2025), which moves our floor to the ten-year 75th percentile of 13.3x
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the multiple is what decides it.
    • On the framework's convention, valuing the shares at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond the horizon, the modal case gives 10.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 7.3 percent a year over three.
    • Eighteen months: 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 1.83 at March 2028 times a blended 14.4x gives EUR 26.35, plus EUR 0.34 of dividend, EUR 26.69, +15.5 percent
    • Three years: EUR 2.08 times 13.1x gives EUR 27.25, plus EUR 1.26 of dividends, EUR 28.51, +23.4 percent
    • The blend is 25 percent a re-rating to 17.0x, 45 percent a hold near 15.0x and 30 percent a de-rating to 11.3x
      • earnings growth is worth +24.5 percent over eighteen months, the modal re-rating -8.3 percent, cash +1.5 percent
    • On the one-year-forward footing beside it (17.9x now, de-rated in proportion to 16.4x) the same path gives 12.5 percent a year
      • the convention gives the lower figure because it credits less of the near-term growth to the exit value
    • Even the branch in which the multiple holds at 15.7x gives 16.6 percent a year over eighteen months, a C
      • a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (net finance costs held at EUR 50m, tax 25 percent, D&A EUR 5m a year higher) cuts FY2029 EPS to EUR 1.62 and the modal return to 3.3 percent a year, an F
  • The chain holds from the forces to the sell-side's estimates and breaks at the price.
    • Below about EUR 20.30 the eighteen-month path clears the 20 percent bar on the modal multiple
    • at EUR 23.10 it does not.
    • The case would also clear the bar at today's price if the EPS path lifted toward 20 percent a year, which needs a first 525 kV HVDC interconnector award and a Maryland order book within eighteen months
      • neither is contracted

  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', at B, in the middle of the pattern rather than at its start.

    • The case is therefore a strong business bought at a price that already reflects the inflection: neutral on the shares, park, and re-open at about EUR 20.

  • Rating = C, because the rating measures materiality and the Building Block's question has three links: the change forces are as strong as any in the universe (A), the outputs are improving in the coveted way (B), but at EUR 23.10 the return they drive in the modal case is a D, and the parts bind, because a change the market has already paid for is not material to the return, whatever it is to the company.

    • Not B, because the third link fails on our arithmetic
    • not D, because the first two links are strong and the case clears the bar below about EUR 20.30.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against, in its strongest form: Cenergy is a tier-two subcontractor riding a tier-one cycle.
    • Its record margins are the product of a temporary capacity shortage that the leaders are spending to close by 2028, just as European offshore-wind auctions falter.
    • NKT is investing over EUR 1bn at Karlskrona and Cologne
      • Prysmian and Nexans are adding capacity and integrating installation
      • LS Cable and Sumitomo are building in the UK and US
      • BNEF has cut its long-term global offshore wind installations by 19 percent
    • Without vessels Cenergy cannot bid the largest turnkey jobs
      • 68 percent of its backlog is one Greek state operator
      • cash conversion has been negative for two years and its year-end working capital rests on EUR 331.67m of supplier finance
    • Its parent controls 66.7 percent, complies with fewer governance principles than peers, and sold stock at EUR 24.20 in June 2026
      • the market has re-rated the shares from 10x to 15.7x on a growth rate (FY2026 EPS +32 percent) that halves next year
    • The evidence for this is in the research itself: the business report's own "temporary structural arbitrage" warning, the experts' tier-two classification, the risks report's bear case at EUR 6.80 a share, and consensus' own margin drift
  • What we concede.
    • The margin peak is cyclical in part, and our path holds rather than raises it
    • the multiple risk is real and the largest risk in the case
    • the concentrations are real
    • the parent's sale is information.
    • We make no bounded-downside claim that any division covers the enterprise value, because none does on like-for-like multiples: the whole group at the five-year median of 10.16x is worth EUR 15.85 a share on our 24-month-forward EPS
  • Why we hold our view of the business, and not of the shares at this price.
    • The backlog to 2031 is contracted, not forecast
    • TSO capex is regulated and rate-insulated in a way developer capex is not
    • the qualification barriers and the localisation rules are strengthening, not weakening
    • the 525 kV ticket puts Cenergy in tenders it could not bid before
    • and the free-cash-flow inflection is arithmetic in capex, not hope.
    • The competing hypothesis is right that the shares are not cheap
      • it is wrong that the business is a cyclical subcontractor, which is why the recommendation is neutral on the shares and the 'Foundations' are rated B
  • The competing hypothesis in the other direction: that we are underrating the earnings path.
    • With 525 kV HVDC awards, a Maryland ramp into a 38 GW US grid-access shortfall and the free-cash-flow inflection, EPS could compound at 20 percent a year through FY2029 (EUR 1.21 to EUR 2.09), which at a held 15.7x gives 21 percent a year over three years and clears the bar at today's price.
    • Our response: that path needs both the first HVDC interconnector award and a full Maryland order book within eighteen months, and neither is contracted
      • the tier-one capacity wave of 2027-2028 and consensus' own margin drift argue against it
      • and a held multiple at an 84th-percentile reading is a 45 percent branch, not a base case.
      • We hold C

  • Not A, because Cenergy is the mid-single-digit-share challenger, not the price-setter, and the tier-one capacity wave lands in 2027-2028, the window in which our three-year return is earned.
