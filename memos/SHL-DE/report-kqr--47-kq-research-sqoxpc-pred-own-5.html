Section summary
  1. The US trade investigations and tariff replacements directly threaten Siemens Healthineers' operating margins and earnings path during its major 2027 spin-off and 2028 diagnostics carve-out.

Defining The Key Issues

What is the status and likely outcome of the US Section 232 investigation into imports of medical equipment (opened in September 2025), what replaced or will replace the 15% Section 122 tariff after its 150-day statutory limit, and how exposed are Siemens Healthineers' imaging and radiotherapy systems made in Germany, China and Mexico, compared with GE HealthCare, Philips and Canon Medical, including what each company has said about mitigation and pricing?"

Section 2 – The US Section 232 Investigation on Medical Equipment Imports

Section summary
  1. The Section 232 medical equipment investigation remains an open regulatory overhang that could result in rapid tariff implementation by late 2026.
  2. Historical precedents in metals, chips, and pharmaceuticals demonstrate that the administration utilizes tariff threats to force multi-billion-dollar domestic manufacturing commitments.

Current Status And Timeline

1.
Delayed resolution: The Department of Commerce has extended its review of medical imports well past its typical statutory boundaries. The Bureau of Industry and Security opened the investigation on September 2, 2025, covering personal protective equipment, consumables, and devices. Under the statute, the agency had 270 days to report its findings, which expired in late May 2026. Because the final report has not been officially published, the medtech industry remains in limbo regarding potential tariff rates.
2.
Impending action: President Trump has signaled that the long-delayed medical device tariffs are set for rapid implementation. In October 2026, the administration announced on Truth Social that medical equipment tariffs will begin by the end of the year. This announcement ended months of speculation that the administration would prioritize other trade sectors. Consequently, suppliers must prepare for sudden import barriers that could disrupt fiscal 2027 operations.

Precedents From Metals And Chips

4.
Severe metals penalties: The historical Section 232 actions on steel and aluminum show how the administration can escalate tariff calculations. In June 2025, the administration raised metals tariffs to 50% and added 407 derivative products. In April 2026, a new proclamation altered the duty basis from metal content to the full customs value of the good. This methodology serves as a warning that any medical equipment tariffs might apply to complete system values rather than individual components.
5.
Rapid semiconductor implementation: The January 2026 semiconductor action proves that the administration can move swiftly from investigation to enforcement. Following its April 2025 probe, the administration imposed an immediate 25% tariff on advanced chips. This action bypassed traditional transition periods, forcing fabless companies to immediately restructure their import logistics. This precedent indicates that the medical device probe could result in immediate tariffs once the final proclamation is signed.
6.
Evolving target scopes: The administration's historical Section 232 revisions demonstrate that product exclusions remain highly unpredictable. In April 2026, President Trump issued a proclamation modifying steel and aluminum tariffs that removed fitness equipment from scope. This change provided immediate relief of 50% for affected consumer fitness importers, though other industrial metal products remained heavily taxed. Such arbitrary scope adjustments illustrate that medtech companies must actively lobby the Department of Commerce to secure crucial product-level carve-outs.

Precedents From Pharma And Solar

7.
Structured pharma agreements: The pharmaceutical Section 232 framework provides a clear template for trading price cuts and investment for tariff relief. In April 2026, the administration announced a 100% tariff on patented drugs but allowed onshoring exemptions for domestic spend. Major pharmaceutical firms can avoid these tariffs by signing domestic manufacturing agreements through contract manufacturers. This structure shows that the administration prefers using tariff threats to force capital relocation rather than simple revenue collection.
8.
Minimum import prices: The August 2026 polysilicon action introduced a complex price-floor mechanism that could be applied to medical devices. Effective December 4, 2026, the administration established minimum import prices and stacked a 15% tariff on downstream solar derivatives. This dual-barrier system enforces domestic pricing power by penalizing any imports priced below the floor. Such structured protectionism could easily be replicated for high-volume medical consumables like syringes or gloves.

In October 2026, Becton Dickinson announced an agreement with the U.S. government to shift $19 bn of spend to the domestic market. This deal includes $3 bn for direct manufacturing expansion in exchange for total relief from future Section 232 device tariffs. This agreement will increase the company's domestically supplied consumables to 80%, securing a massive moat against tariffexposed peers.

Replicated onshoring commitments: Other medical technology giants are following this roadmap to eliminate future regulatory trade risks. In June 2026, Johnson & Johnson unveiled $1 bn vision investment in Florida to secure similar tariff relief. These multi-billion-dollar deals show that the administration's tariff strategy is successfully forcing capital back into the United States. However, smaller suppliers who cannot afford such massive capital expenditure will remain highly exposed to the upcoming tariff regime.
11.
Uneven corporate exposure: The eventual Section 232 medical device action will impact suppliers in a highly unequal manner. European medtech companies with domestic manufacturing, such as Siemens Healthineers, face different levels of margin risk depending on their supply chain structures. While simple consumables might face standard tariffs, high-value imaging equipment with complex supply chains is harder to relocate. Consequently, companies must rely on product-specific exemptions or risk losing substantial U.S. market share to localized competitors.
12.
Severe margin headwinds: Medtech companies are already forecasting significant earnings drag from the impending tariff regime. Major suppliers such as Medtronic have reported that existing tariff actions have already cost 50 basis points of operating margin in fiscal 2026. These margin pressures will directly dilute adjusted earnings per share unless suppliers can successfully execute onshoring agreements.

Section 3 – Section 122 Tariff Expiration and Successor Tariff Regimes

Section summary
  1. The expiration of temporary Section 122 tariffs in July 2026 marked a seamless handoff to a durable Section 301 forced labor tariff framework.
  2. Active Court of International Trade litigation and Customs and Border Protection refund channels represent significant near-term margin recovery options for affected suppliers.

Transition From Section 122

1.
Permanent shift: Varex Imaging has transitioned its import cost modeling from temporary balance-of-payments duties to a durable structural trade regime. The temporary 10% global surcharge under Section 122 expired on July 24, 2026, after reaching its statutory 150-day limit. Because Section 122 required congressional approval for any extension, the administration utilized alternative authorities to preserve baseline tariff economics without legislative consensus. This regulatory handoff ensures that tariff pressures remain an ongoing headwind for medical technology cost structures.
2.
Stable baselines: Morgan Stanley economists expect the new trade framework to maintain the statutory effective tariff rate near 10% through the end of 2026. The transition from Section 122 to Sections 301 and 232 has placed the U.S. tariff regime on a more durable statutory footing than the previous emergency declarations. While actual collections may vary due to company-specific onshoring deals, the broad country-level floor is expected to persist. Consequently, medical equipment manufacturers must adapt to these institutionalized trade barriers rather than anticipating a near-term tariff cliff.
3.
Mixed relief: Scotiabank macro strategists noted that Section 122 was a temporary bridge that allowed the administration to rebuild its tariff programs. The expiration of the 15% Section 122 rate on July 24, 2026, is estimated to reduce the weighted average effective tariff rate by approximately 0.25% for affected importers. However, this marginal relief is offset by parallel trade escalations across other strategic commodity corridors. This complex regulatory whiplash makes long-term supply chain planning difficult for multi-national corporations.
4.
Moderate pressure: Deutsche Bank analysts observed that the replacement Section 301 tariffs on 60 economies have a somewhat muted immediate impact. The 10% to 12.5% successor rates are lower than the unilateral reciprocal tariffs that exceeded 15% in late 2025. Despite the lower baseline rates, the prospect of ongoing capacity investigations continues to inject severe policy volatility into corporate budgets. Consequently, the broader trade environment remains a persistent downside risk for industrial and medical equipment sectors.

The Section 301 Forced Labor Framework

5.
Broad enforcement: Hour Loop reported that the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative implemented new Section 301 tariffs ranging from 10% to 12.5% on July 24, 2026. This forced labor tariff regime targets imports from 60 economies that failed to enforce robust import prohibitions on forced labor. Unlike the expired Section 122 duties, these Section 301 tariffs carry no statutory expiration dates. Therefore, medical device companies must treat these duties as permanent operational expenses.
6.
Tiered structures: Jefferies apparel analysts noted that countries deemed to be making greater efforts to address forced labor face a lower 10% tariff rate. Other investigated trading partners are subject to a higher 12.5% tariff rate, forcing companies to constantly re-evaluate their geographic sourcing mixes. While some consumer brands had prepared for higher rates, the finalized Section 301 framework still adds sequential cost pressure. These persistent duties compress operating margins unless manufacturers can successfully execute pricing adjustments.
7.
Geographic caps: Morgan Stanley researchers highlighted that the final Section 301 action capped the combined rate for the European Union at 10%. Conversely, imports from Japan, South Korea, and Switzerland are capped at 12.5% under the forced labor determination. These regional differences create uneven cost burdens for medical device components imported from European and Asian supply hubs. This complex country-of-origin framework requires rigorous customs compliance diligence to avoid overpayment of duties.

Legal Challenges And Refunds

11.
Judicial pushback: Mistras Group reported that the U.S. Court of International Trade ruled Section 122 tariffs unlawful in May 2026. Although this ruling was stayed pending administrative appeal, it establishes a critical precedent for ongoing challenges. Importers are actively litigating both the expired Section 122 duties and the new Section 301 forced labor framework. If these appeals succeed, medical device companies could become eligible for substantial retroactive duty recoveries.
12.
Retroactive windfalls: Dollar Tree achieved a massive financial recovery by submitting IEEPA refund claims totaling $379 mm in April 2026. Through the third quarter of fiscal 2026, the company received refunds of $372.4 mm plus $14.4 mm of interest. This recovery represents approximately 98% of the total duties paid under the invalidated emergency trade regime. This substantial cash injection directly offset cost of sales and provided a significant, one-time benefit to earnings.
13.
Corporate recoveries: Mattel successfully recognized approximately $88 mm of retroactive IEEPA tariff refunds during the third quarter of 2026. These refunds were processed through the Customs and Border Protection phased CAPE administrative portal. This windfall directly expanded the company's gross margin to 54.8% from 50% in the prior year's quarter. These cash recoveries illustrate the material EPS upside available to tariffexposed companies that aggressively pursue judicial refund pathways.
14.
Peer comparisons: ICU Medical recognized $29.2 mm of IEEPA refunds in cost of goods sold during the first nine months of fiscal 2026. This recovery is substantially higher than Tennant Company, which received $6.1 mm in approved refund claims. Both outcomes were accounted for under gain contingency models, meaning the benefits were not recognized until cash realization was fully assured. These uneven cash windfalls highlight why analysts must normalize quarterly earnings to assess true underlying operational profitability.
15.
Contrary evidence: Some medical equipment is entirely exempt from Section 301 and Section 122 duties under international humanitarian frameworks. Under the UNESCO Nairobi Protocol, the United States exempts products for disabled persons from all import duties. For example, Fisher and Paykel estimates that nearly 70% of its respiratory imports are approved as compliant with these protocol exemptions. Consequently, these specialized medical devices escape the margin dilution and inflationary cost pressures that impact standard industrial components.
16.
Other side of the story: The transition to Section 301 tariffs may fail to provide longterm regulatory stability because persistent litigation and capacity reviews invite constant adjustments. Importers have already challenged the forced labor framework at the Court of International Trade, and a pending USTR capacity investigation could trigger additional duties by March 2027. Furthermore, retaliatory measures from China and the European Union could escalate overall import costs. Therefore, instead of stabilizing supply chain planning, the successor regimes will likely prolong margin compression and EPS volatility for several fiscal periods.
