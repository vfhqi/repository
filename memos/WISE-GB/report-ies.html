Wise (WISE-GB) — what the Q2 FY27 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case, moderately and not marginally: the revenue beat and the guidance raise are real, but the beat was delivered by interest income and cards rather than by the cross-border flywheel the case is built on, the personal volume engine slowed again with the take rate holding better than expected, and the US regulatory position has deteriorated since the memo in a way the case does not hold at all.

What changed

Reported 15-Oct-26 for the quarter to 30-Sep-26 (Q2 FY27). Company figures unless stated.

  • Net revenue $739.1m, up 21% year on year at constant currency, 1.6% above the $727.8m consensus. Transaction revenue $561.3m, up 22%, in line. Card and other revenue $206.1m, up 34%, 3% above the $200.2m consensus. Net interest income $177.8m, 3% above consensus, on a 10bp gross-yield beat and customer balances of $43.5bn, up 28% and $1.0bn ahead of expectations. Non-cross-border revenue is now 52% of net revenue.
  • Cross-border volume $71.7bn, up 22% reported, 2% below consensus. Constant-currency volume growth 21%, down from 24% in Q1 FY27 and 31% for FY26. Personal volume $49.5bn, up 18%, 2% below consensus; business volume $22.3bn, up 32%, in line. Volume per customer fell 2% quarter on quarter to $4.2k. Take rate 0.49%, down from 0.52% a year earlier but 1bp above the 48bp consensus; management plans a further 1bp cut in each of the next two quarters. Cross-border revenue $355.2m against $305.0m in Q2 FY26, growth of roughly 16% on volume growth of 22%.
  • Active customers 12.5m in the quarter, up 20% (a quarterly measure; the 19m figure for FY26 is an annual one). Business customers 633k after 26k net additions against a 21k seasonal average. Card spend $14.3bn, up 30%, against 37% growth in FY26. Wise Platform 6% of cross-border volume against 5% a year earlier; OakNorth, Monzo and Standard Chartered named as partners.
  • Guidance: FY27 constant-currency net revenue growth raised to 17.5% to 20%, midpoint 18.75% from 17.5%. H1 FY27 profit-before-tax margin about 23%, including two percentage points of one-off cost, $15m of it dual-listing. FY27 margin still guided to the top of the 20% to 25% range; consensus 24.8%.
  • Regulatory, all since the memo: the OCC denied the US national trust bank charter in July 2026, citing AML deficiencies and the open multistate consent order; a May 2026 Fed policy change paused payment-system access for uninsured trust banks; Wise intends to re-apply under the GENIUS Act as a payment stablecoin issuer. US consent-order remediation is complete but closure awaits regulator feedback. No update on the Belgian investigation. One-third of ongoing investment now goes to compliance and financial crime. Barak Eilam succeeds David Wells as Chair in April 2027. A ninth direct rail (Malaysia) went live.
  • Capital: buyback 39% complete at mid-October, 17m shares for $209m, on course for about 4% of the share count.
  • Sell-side response: muted. Morgan Stanley Equal-weight, $13.30 target and EPS unchanged ("revenues better but volumes slightly disappointing; no update on outstanding issues"); its sterling target had already been cut from 1,220p to 1,010p in October. Rothschild & Co Redburn Buy, $18.91, FY profit-before-tax estimates up 1%, calls the guidance conservative. Jefferies Buy, 1,239p, 0% to 2% estimate revision, expects a muted share price reaction. The stock went into the print at 19x 12-month forward earnings against a 35x historical average (Jefferies).

Against the case: the memo assumed a flywheel in which price cuts buy volume, expected the take rate to drift lower from 51bp, held net interest income as the largest quantified risk (about a quarter of income, roughly 5% of EPS per 25bp, the case's figures), held the Belgian investigation as the second, and contained no US charter denial.

The challenge

Four readings of this quarter that the case does not hold. None is easy to knock down.

The flywheel is slowing for reasons price cannot fix. The case's bear case is take-rate compression accelerating faster than volume can offset. This quarter shows the opposite mechanism: the take rate held 1bp better than consensus, and volume still missed. Constant-currency volume growth has stepped down from 31% (FY26) to 24% (Q1) to 21% (Q2); volume per customer fell again; personal growth is 18%. Management blames dollar dynamics on large US transfers, but the two brokers who repeat the explanation give it opposite signs (Morgan Stanley: dollar strength; Redburn: a weaker dollar), which tells us nobody outside the company can check it. A former Wise expansion manager says mature European corridors have reached saturation. The strongest form: the personal corridors that built the case are maturing, so each further 1bp cut buys less volume than the last, and the cross-border revenue line (about 16% growth on 22% volume) is where this already shows. For this to be right: volume per customer falls for a third quarter, personal constant-currency growth stays below 20% after the currency effect has had a quarter to wash out, and the two planned 1bp cuts produce no visible volume step. This presses on the case's demand-side read (robust), not on its competitive read.

The beat is the risk wearing a tailwind's clothes. Transaction revenue was merely in line. The beat came from net interest income and cards, and net interest income is the line the case names as its largest downside. Worse for the case, the margin is held up by a regulatory accident: Wise returned only 45% of its targeted interest to customers in FY26, two-thirds of the shortfall because UK deposit rules block it. The memo reads the margin-beat record as "sandbagger" management discipline and anchors two ratings on it. The alternative reading is that part of that record is an unreturned windfall rather than conservatism, and it unwinds either when rates fall (a 1% shock takes £110.8m off net interest income, the company's own sensitivity) or when the company obtains the licences it is seeking in order to pay interest out, at which point Morgan Stanley puts the margin at 17% in FY28 to FY29 against 26.4% in FY26. Card growth, the other offset, is already decelerating (30% from 37%). For this to be right: policy rates fall over the next 18 months as the macro sources expect, the UK deposit restriction eases or a licence lands, and card growth keeps slowing. If all three happen the case loses its margin cushion and its "conservative guidance" evidence at the same time, with the take-rate cuts still running.

The case is simpler than the memo found, and the fulcrum is business, not personal. The positive reading the case does not hold. Business volume grew 32% and matched consensus; business net additions beat the seasonal norm; Platform is 6% of volume from 5%; three bank partners are named. The memo failed the simplicity guardrail on six drivers. This quarter suggests two: a maturing personal cash generator and a business-plus-platform growth engine. If that is the shape of the business, the memo's heaviest negative finding weakens and its third key question (does Platform scale) is being answered yes. For this to be right: business volume growth stays above the 25% line the review sets as its reversal trigger, Platform share keeps rising toward the 10% medium-term target, and OakNorth shows in second-half volume. The obstacle is the fourth reading.

Regulation has become an operating constraint on the moat, not a tail-risk fine. The case prices the Belgian matter as a possible £1bn to £2bn hit and lists it for monitoring. Since the memo, the OCC has refused the US trust charter, the Fed has closed the route the application used, and the consent order stays open pending regulator feedback. Expert evidence puts US clearing through partner banks at 15bp to 20bp per transfer, and Morgan Stanley has the denial locking that in for 18 to 24 months. The case's structural moat is direct rails bypassing intermediaries; the US is the biggest single market opportunity; and Platform depends on bank partners whose procurement lengthens while consent orders are open. The strongest form: the regulatory position costs no fine but caps the two drivers the case most needs, direct-rail cost advantage in the US and Platform conversion, for two years, while consuming a third of the investment budget. For this to be right: the GENIUS Act re-application takes more than 18 months or fails, Platform partner announcements slow, and US volume growth lags the group.

What does not survive the evidence is a reading that competition got worse this quarter. Business volume matched, the take rate held better than expected, and no number in the print contradicts management's cost-plus framing. The case's competitive read (aggressive) stands as it was, no worse.

What it does to the case

In the memo's own terms:

  • E11 RA1 Demand side : more pressure. The grade rests on volume and customer growth; volume growth has slowed three periods running and volume per customer is falling. This is the Element most likely to move a full grade.
  • E8 RA8 False-friend guardrail : more pressure. The memo's test was unit revenue net of take-rate compression against volume; this quarter cross-border revenue grew about 16% on 22% volume and the beat came from elsewhere. The case's named central tension moved the wrong way.
  • E8 RA2 Big demand change and E8 RA4 Compounder-through-change : more pressure; the compounder read needs the flywheel.
  • E1 RA2 CQ3 Delivery congruency and E7 RA5 Conservative investor relations : on the surface less pressure (guidance raised, margin on track); under the second reading, more. Net the same, but the sandbagger anchor is now contestable where it was not.
  • E1 RA2 CQ1 Absence of headwinds , E7 RA3 Zero headwinds , E7 RA4 Change-from-current items : more pressure. The listing has settled, one item closed, but the charter denial, the GENIUS re-application and a chair transition are added and the Belgian matter is no nearer closure.
  • E7 RA1 Focused bet : less pressure under the third reading; the same under the others.
  • E11 RA4 Political and regulatory and E11 RA5 Supplier side : more pressure. A ninth direct rail is live, but the US intermediary dependency is now locked in for a stated period.
  • E5 RA1 Downturn suitability : slightly more pressure; the rate sensitivity is now quantified by the company and rate cycles are turning.
  • E11 RA2 Competitive : the same.
  • E1 RA2 CQ5 Low trading multiple : less pressure. The dashboard has the P/E at 14.8x, in the 2nd percentile of its own ten-year history; Jefferies has 19x forward against 35x historical. The memo's unresolved multiple discrepancy has been overtaken by compression on every basis.
  • MR1 Technical momentum and MR2 Sell-side earnings momentum : both pinned to the dashboard, which now reads F and A (15-Oct and 14-Oct snapshots). These moved before the print and independently of it; we note them and do not grade them.
  • Share price against the case's thresholds: the dashboard close is 902.6p on 15-Oct-26, the eve of the print, so the reaction is not in our data. The case's price was 917p with the 200-day at 921p; the price now sits 1.4% below a 200-day of 915.1p. No threshold the case states has been crossed. The case's 1,220p consensus median target is not comparable with the dashboard's $12.47 now that the primary listing is in dollars. The memo's conviction-lowering trigger, two consecutive quarters of take-rate decline without volume offset, is met on the Q1 and Q2 prints if "offset" means revenue growth keeping pace with volume growth, and not met if it means revenue still growing; the memo should say which it meant.

We do not re-rate. The pressure runs net negative, concentrated on the demand side and the false-friend guardrail, with a genuine positive on simplicity and valuation.

What would settle it

  • Saturation: Q3 FY27 volume per customer and personal constant-currency growth, with the next quarterly print. A split of volume per customer by customer vintage would settle it faster; the company has not committed to disclosing one.
  • Windfall margin: the FY27 interest-return percentage (FY26 was 45%), any UK deposit-licence development, and the next ECB and Fed decisions. No date for a UK licence has been given.
  • Business fulcrum: second-half Platform share against the 10% target and whether OakNorth shows in volume; the next half-year report, which Jefferies dates to 12-Nov-26.
  • Regulatory constraint: three things, none dated by the company or by regulators: feedback closing the US consent order, a Belgian prosecutor decision, and the GENIUS Act outcome. "No update on outstanding issues" is the honest state; this is a question nothing scheduled will answer.

The second Earnings Review, four working days on, will carry the settled broker estimates and targets after the print, the share price reaction we do not yet have, and any revisions to FY27 volume and margin forecasts. It will not carry Q3 volumes, the interest-return figure, a Belgian decision, consent-order closure or the GENIUS outcome. It can settle whether the sell-side has moved on the first two readings (cuts to personal volume forecasts would say so); it cannot settle the third or fourth, which wait on the company and on regulators.
