[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Theon is a contracted demand-inflection case in European soldier optronics, compelling at the operating line and only adequate at the shareholder's line at 30.96 EUR. The company has turned a policy-backed rearmament cycle into a 1.46 EURbn backlog and a 26.2% adjusted EBIT margin, but it has financed its expansion with 8.6m new shares and about 550 EURm of peak net debt, so EBITDA growing at about 30% a year reaches earnings per share at about 20%, and the modal return is about 17% a year over eighteen months and 15% over three years against the framework's 20% bar. At about 30 EUR, 3% below the price, the modal path clears the bar.

  • The change is external, structural and already contracted.
    • European soldier modernisation is a sector-level demand swing with visibility to 2029 and beyond, and Theon holds the largest single piece of it.
    • European defence order intake across Morgan Stanley's coverage rose 25% year on year to over 85 EURbn and the aggregate European backlog reached a record 438 EURbn (September 2026)
      • NATO agreed in June 2025 a 5% of GDP target by 2035 with at least 3.5% for core military equipment.
    • Theon signed the 1 EURbn OCCAR framework for 100,000 devices for Germany and 4,000 for Belgium in December 2025, the largest night-vision procurement in NATO history
      • H1 2026 order intake rose 38.5% to 232.5 EURm
      • the soft backlog stood at 1.46 EURbn at 30 June 2026 plus 902 EURm of contractual options, 2.4x cover of the c. 600 EURm FY2026 revenue guidance, with about 500 EURm already scheduled for FY2027.
    • The headwinds in the value chain are on the supply side, not on demand: image intensifier tubes are 60% to 65% of manufacturing cost and come overwhelmingly from Exosens, which sits on a Chinese gallium chokepoint and now sells finished goggles itself.
  • The company's own change is broad, and it is bought with leverage.
    • Six of the twelve internal forces are at B or better, which passes the framework's screen, but the balance sheet has gone from 126.9 EURm of net cash at end-2025 to 238 EURm of net debt at 30 June 2026 and a pro-forma 3.0x to 3.1x EBITDA in early 2027 against a 3.5x covenant.
    • Guidance was raised twice in 2026 and the 1 EURbn revenue target was pulled forward to 2029
      • the pivot from night vision (93% of FY2025 revenue) to a 50/50 mix by 2029 is resourced with a 325 EURm term loan, the c. 300 EURm HGH deal, Merio, the Safran gimbal venture and Twin Prime.
    • The shareholder-return force runs backwards (payout cut to 20% to 30%, a rights issue at a 30% discount), and the September 2026 reversal on the Exosens board seat shows the capital-allocation path can bend inside a year.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on the two lines the framework weights most, and not yet on the line that pays the shareholder.
    • Revenue grew 35.4% in H1 2026 and the adjusted EBIT margin held at 26.2% with guidance lifted to above 26%
    • clean earnings per share were roughly flat in the same half, because 12% more shares and a new interest bill absorbed the growth.
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS is up 11.9% over twelve months and margin guidance now sits above consensus, so EBIT upgrades are probable
      • EPS upgrades are less likely, because Deutsche Bank has already cut EPS 5% for interest and the out-year revisions are slightly negative.
    • Our modal clean EPS path is 1.16 EUR (FY2025) to 1.26 EUR (FY2026) to 1.60 EUR (FY2027) to 1.98 EUR (FY2028), 19.5% a year over three years, at the top of the framework's 12% to 20% band.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The external force, the founder's choices and actions, the operating metrics (orders +38.5%, gross margin 35.1%), the financial statements (revenue +35.4%, adjusted EBIT +37.5%) and the sell-side's estimates all show it
    • what the sell-side is now arguing about is the interest line, which is where the chain weakens.
  • What it costs: 30.96 EUR is 16.4x our 24-month-forward EPS of 1.89 EUR, the 50th percentile of the stock's own 2.6-year listed life (median 15.98x) and about two-thirds of the multiples of Hensoldt (26.8x) and Exosens (24.6x), the only two peers whose economics resemble Theon's.
    • The contracted catalysts are priced in the earnings, not in the multiple: consensus revenue of 600, 749 and 875 EURm for FY2026 to FY2028 is our path, and the stock pays a median multiple for it.
  • What it earns on the framework's convention: valuing the shares at 16.0x the earnings two years beyond each horizon gives 17.1% a year over eighteen months and 15.1% a year over three years, a C
    • a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS takes the eighteen-month figure to 11.9% a year, a D
    • the re-rating branch alone (17.9x) gives 26.1% a year.
    • The cash return is about 1% a year
      • earnings growth carries the return
      • the re-rating is the swing factor, and it is not ours to earn while leverage is at 3.0x and the sector multiple is contracting (land-defence EV/EBIT from 21.6x to 17.2x by September 2026).
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', at B for crispness.
    • The mandatory demand-explosion condition passes at A and the dominant position at A
    • one-to-many scalability and fixed-cost leverage fail, on the framework's own example, so the demand explosion reaches EPS through a labour-variable model with a below-EBIT leak rather than through operating leverage.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the external force through the backlog to the margin, and it is the strongest such chain in the comparison set.
    • It breaks at the last step: the price is fair for the earnings that are contracted, and the leverage and the shares issued to fund the change take a third of the EBITDA growth before it reaches the shareholder.

  • The case therefore is a material, contracted change at a fair price: neutral at 30.96 EUR and bullish at or below 30 EUR, where the modal path clears 20% a year over eighteen months.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material in the way the framework asks: the external force is sector-wide, policy-backed and contracted ('Change Forces' at A), it has already reached the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates ('Financial Outputs' at B), and the modal earnings path grows at about 20% a year

    • the chain weakens as it travels, because the change has been bought with debt and shares and the price is at the stock's median multiple, so the return that reaches the shareholder is a C ('Modal return' at C).
    • Not C, although C was live because the framework's question ends in a 20%-plus return and our modal case gives 15% to 17% a year: the shortfall is a price and balance-sheet effect on a material change, not a doubt about the change, and an entry price of about 30 EUR closes it, which is why the recommendation carries that condition.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The hypothesis at its strongest: the change is a cyclical peak dressed as a structural inflection, and it has been financed at the top.
    • Theon's own former managers date the demand peak at 24 to 36 months from April 2026 and expect margins of 15% to 18% as it passes
      • agency procurement of the OCCAR kind is expected to reach 40% of European buying by 2030 and is priced competitively.
    • The sector has already de-rated 20% to 38% over twelve months
      • Deutsche Bank's own free-cash-flow-yield value is 18.00 EUR
      • free cash flow on FactSet's definition was 16.9% of net income in FY2025.
    • Leverage goes to 3.0x to 3.1x in early 2027 against a 3.5x covenant, in the very quarter a ceasefire could delay the tranches.
  • Our response: the demand evidence is contracted, not forecast: 1.46 EURbn of backlog with about 500 EURm scheduled for FY2027, 2.4x cover of this year's guidance, and a policy floor that runs to 2035.
    • Gross margin rose to 35.1% in H1 2026 during the OCCAR deliveries that were meant to dilute it, and guidance was lifted above 26% on the strength of that.
    • When the sector fell about 30% in May to June 2026 on ceasefire speculation, Theon fell 4.8%.
  • What we concede: the timing of the leverage is poor and the free-cash-flow record is weak on any definition but the company's
    • that is why the return is a C and not a B, and why the recommendation carries a price.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because European soldier-modernisation demand is a structural, sector-level swing with visibility to 2029 and beyond, evidenced by the sector's own order books and by policy, and Theon's own intake and backlog confirm it.
  • Sector orders: European defence order intake across Morgan Stanley's coverage rose 25% year on year to over 85 EURbn and the aggregate European backlog reached a record 438 EURbn (September 2026)
    • Indra's H1 2026 group orders rose 60% to 2.15 EURbn
    • Hensoldt's Q1 2026 book-to-bill was 3.0x with Optronics the standout.
  • Policy: NATO agreed in June 2025 a 5% of GDP target by 2035 with at least 3.5% for core military equipment
    • Germany targets 180 EURbn of spending by 2030 and 3.1% of GDP by 2027
    • the SAFE instrument offers 150 EURbn of low-interest loans.
    • Militaries are moving to equipping soldiers one-to-one with night vision, a structural volume multiplier.
  • Company intake: the 1 EURbn OCCAR framework for 100,000 devices for Germany and 4,000 for Belgium, signed December 2025, is the largest night-vision procurement in NATO history
    • H1 2026 order intake was 232.5 EURm, up 38.5%, with Q2 2026 the strongest non-Q4 order quarter in the group's history
    • the soft backlog was 1.46 EURbn at 30 June 2026 plus 902 EURm of contractual options, 2.4x cover of the c. 600 EURm FY2026 revenue guidance, with about 500 EURm already scheduled for FY2027.
  • The evidence against: former Theon managers expect analog night-vision demand could peak by 2027 and the procurement upcycle within 24 to 36 months of April 2026
    • Q1 2026 order intake fell 40.6% to 70 EURm on seasonality
    • framework contracts "often materialise at much lower actual spend" (the Frankenburg chief executive)
    • budget execution lags plans by two to three years (Jefferies, September 2026).
  • Not B, because the framework's definition of a sufficiently strong demand force is met on every clause and the contracted backlog removes the two-year visibility doubt.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the single most important input, the image intensifier tube at 60% to 65% of manufacturing cost (Deutsche Bank; 30% to 65% of material cost in the prospectus), comes overwhelmingly from one supplier with which Theon is co-dependent, sits on a Chinese raw-material chokepoint, and is scarce
    • this is a headwind, intensifying on raw materials and labour, and only partly hedged.
  • Concentration: Exosens supplied about 75% of Theon's tube spend in FY2024 and is the only at-scale ITAR-free tube maker outside the US, with about 80% of the international market
    • Theon is 40% of Exosens' revenue and is to take about 52% of its tube volume to 2030.
    • Tube lead times reached 18 months.
  • Raw materials and labour: gallium refining is about 95% in China, and without gallium tube manufacturing halts
    • indium gallium arsenide is squeezed by AI and defence demand
    • skilled optical assembly technicians are scarce in Germany and Greece.
  • Mitigants: the Exosens long-term agreement was extended in December 2025 to end-2030 for more than 400,000 tubes with priority production
    • the Harder Digital stake was raised to 65.22% on 24 July 2026, its capacity more than doubled to 15,000 tubes a year and targeted at 20,000 by 2027, Figure of Merit above 2,000, internal absorption raised from 50% to about 66%
    • the designs are tube-agnostic, with relationships at Elbit America and L3Harris
    • supplier pricing is passed through back-to-back.
  • Not C, because a single-source input at 60% to 65% of cost on a Chinese raw-material chokepoint is a bottom-quartile supply position in this universe
    • not F, because it is contractually and structurally hedged to 2030.
  • For the case: this is the one external force that can stop the backlog converting, and it is the fact that sits behind the Key Risk on tube supply.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because intensity in the core European man-portable market is easing (the US primes are ITAR-blocked, the European primes hold 12% to 13% each and avoid Theon's markets), while it is tightening at the two edges Theon is moving into, platforms and the upstream tube layer.
  • Core market: Theon holds over 50% (the company's figure) to about 60% (the expert calls) of the European man-portable market
    • Safran and Thales hold 12% to 13% each
    • competitors "actively avoid markets where Theon is strong"
    • Theon prices 15% to 20% below Western European peers.
    • L3Harris and Elbit are blocked in Europe by ITAR and are absorbed by the 1.27 USDbn US BiNOD award.
  • The edges: Hensoldt is expanding optronics capacity at Oberkochen and booked 759 EURm of Optronics orders in Q1 2026
    • Thales signed a German land-forces framework for several hundred vision units a month
    • NextVision is scaling aerial gimbals
    • Photonis Defense is to assemble BiNOD systems in Pennsylvania as a prime.
    • Upstream, Exosens bought NVLS in July 2025 and won 17,000 Spanish monoculars and a US Army BiNOD share, so the supplier has become a competitor.
  • Direction: mild tailwind in the core, headwind at the platform edge.
    • Not A, because the platform and digital segments, where the strategy is taking the company, are more contested and lower-margin than the core.
