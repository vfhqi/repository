Section 2 – Mine Electrification Economics and Adoption Friction

Section summary
  1. Underwhelming infrastructure readiness has slowed the adoption of underground battery-electric fleets despite proven opex advantages.
  2. High capital premiums are partially mitigated by dramatic reductions in ventilation and maintenance expenses over equipment lifecycles.

Total cost of ownership dynamics

1.
Compelling opex advantages: Calculations by Sandvik show that its battery-powered Z50 truck achieves a 12% lower total cost of ownership than equivalent diesel-powered units.
2.
Drastic maintenance savings: Maintenance expenses for these battery-electric trucks are estimated to run 50% to 55% lower than standard internal combustion engines.
3.
Substantial ventilation savings: Ventilation represents up to 40% of mine opex, and replacing diesel with battery-electric fleets reduces ventilation energy demands significantly.
4.
Prohibitive capital premium: The transition faces headwinds as battery-powered mining machines command upfront prices 50% to 100% higher than traditional diesel equivalents.
5.
Uneven loader economics: For electric loaders, the total cost of ownership is actually 1% higher than diesel equivalents, showing uneven economics across different machine categories.
6.
Small-tonnage economic sweet-spot: Analysis by Huatai Research indicates that 70-tonne electric trucks yield RMB 1.24 million in lifecycle opex savings over comparable fuel-powered alternatives.
7.
Alternative rental structures: Contract miner Darma Henwa mitigates high upfront capital expenditures by purchasing electric truck bodies while renting battery packs to limit depreciation.

Fleet adoption rates and repeat orders

8.
Negligible industry penetration: Despite positive marketing by OEMs, fully electric equipment currently represents less than 1% of the global active mining fleet.
9.
Adoption momentum pause: Recent order data from both Sandvik and Epiroc reveals a notable lull in battery-electric purchases as miners evaluate initial pilot fleets.
10.
Customer deployment hesitation: Apart from early adopters, the broader mining community is maintaining a cautious wait-and-see approach before committing to full fleet overhauls.
11.
Encouraging repeat purchases: Demonstrating site-level viability, Epiroc has deployed BEVs across 40 sites, with more than 40% placing repeat orders.
12.
Strong commercial validation: Highlighting long-term commitment, Eldorado Gold placed a SEK 160 million order to expand its Sandvik BEV fleet from two units up to twelve.
13.
Underground segment outperformance: Equipment orders for underground operations show materially faster electrification adoption than surface mining, where large battery trucks remain virtually unadopted.

Operational and physical mine bottlenecks

14.
Severe runtime mismatch: At the Lalor Mine, operators face a challenge with a four-hour battery runtime attempting to meet ten-hour shift requirements.
15.
Critical grid bottlenecks: Ground research from Epiroc reveals that the availability of electricity at mine sites is the primary bottleneck for brownfield electrification.
16.
Heavy grid infrastructure demand: Fully electric mines require massive support infrastructure, as variable- frequency drives require active harmonic filters to prevent severe power disruptions.
