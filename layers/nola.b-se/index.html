C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

C4515th of 44

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: competitive intensity is rising modestly in medical devices, at new tenders and renewals rather than in the installed base, and is high and stable in commodity industrial components. In drug delivery, competitors are adding capacity aimed at the same GLP-1 demand: Gerresheimer is spending EUR 166m to expand its Peachtree City autoinjector plant and, after selling Centor and Primary Packaging Plastics for about EUR 1.5bn, becomes a smaller, more focused medical-systems rival (Bernstein argues it will redeploy capital where Nolato competes); Phillips-Medisize is winning wearable insulin pump work and expanding inhalation; Ypsomed and SHL lead autoinjectors. An expert reports "hungry new entrants" in device contract manufacturing pricing for 15% operating margins against incumbents' 25%. Pharma buyers, under drug-price pressure, benchmark contract manufacturers' costs and demand cuts when drug prices fall. In glass syringes (not Nolato's field) the industry expects 10-20% overcapacity within three to five years, and Stevanato now has freed capacity; this sets the mood of procurement teams even where it does not touch Nolato's products directly.

What protects Nolato. Installed programmes are protected by 9-18 months of customer-funded revalidation and 10-20 year product lives, so share moves at new awards and through dual sourcing, not by churn. Gerresheimer's accounting investigation and leverage (about 4.7-5x) also make it, for some buyers, a less trusted partner, which can push programmes towards reliable mid-sized suppliers such as Nolato. In Engineered Solutions, Chinese molders press on non-regulated components; Materials shows no sign of intensifying competition.

Direction for the model: neutral for volume over 18 months, mildly negative for like-for-like price on renewals in 2027-2030; the risk is a gradual erosion of unit prices, not a loss of programmes.

C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

A584th of 44

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: input costs were a clear headwind from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, and the mechanism is now turning neutral to briefly favourable; supplier concentration and availability are not an issue. Raw materials are about 36% of sales and about 60% of them are linked to oil, so roughly SEK 1.6-1.7bn of the cost base moves with petrochemical prices. Contracts pass costs on with a lag of three to four months (three to six in practice), which produced a drag of SEK 25-30m in Q4 2025 (silver in EMC silicones), about SEK 20m in Q2 2026 and SEK 10m in Q3 2026 (polymers), with management and both brokers expecting none in Q4 2026. Morgan Stanley reports European petrochemical and polymer prices up about 30-36% year to date in late 2026, but oil fell from about USD 115 (late October) to below USD 100 (5 November).

The insight for the model: the lag is symmetric. If oil and resin prices fall in late 2026 and early 2027, customer prices are indexed down after costs have already fallen, which gives a temporary margin gain in H1 2027, the mirror image of the 2026 drag. This is a small, unmodelled upside (perhaps SEK 10-20m over two quarters if prices ease as they rose; my estimate). Materials has shortened its pricing windows for silver-bearing products, which narrows that lag.

Other inputs: electricity is 97% of an energy bill of SEK 279m (2025) and is hedged 20-80% up to 12 quarters ahead; labour inflation in Central Europe and the US is offset by automation, though US staff turnover of 22-24% raises training costs during validations. Supply is multi-sourced (721 key suppliers, top suppliers about 80% of purchasing); some specialised polymers and electronic components are customer-directed, which reduces flexibility but transfers qualification risk to the customer.

C-03

To what extent is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved, and in which direction does it act on the company?

C+5810th of 44

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: over the next 18 months the disruption forces act net positively on Nolato (electronics-intensive medical devices and artificial-intelligence thermal management), while the one force that could hurt badly, oral GLP-1 drugs, is neither proven nor disproved and is a three-to-five-year risk.

  • Oral GLP-1 (negative, long-dated, being partly disproved for now). Injections were 89.9% of US prescriptions for the new obesity and diabetes drugs in September 2026; Rothschild & Co Redburn finds no evidence of the Wegovy pill cannibalising pens, and about 80% of patients starting Lilly's pill were new to the class. Against that, Bernstein expects pills to reach 50% of the market by 2028 and Stevanato 30%. A shift from weekly to monthly dosing, flagged in the SCHOTT file as a more immediate volume risk than pills, would cut device units per patient wherever single-dose devices are used; this has not been discussed for Nolato and should be tested.
  • Electronics-intensive and connected medical devices (positive, strengthening). Healthcare is moving towards software-augmented devices; Nolato answered with Unitron and M&M Qualtech, moving from component molder to system supplier. This is a disruption Nolato is riding, not suffering, provided integration works.
  • Artificial-intelligence data-centre cooling (positive, strengthening). Liquid-cooling thermal interface materials and EMC shielding drove Materials to 27% growth in Q3 2026. A move to standardised cold-plate architectures that bypass specialised interface materials is the counter-risk.
  • Additive manufacturing (disproved for volume). It cannot match injection-molding economics at millions of units; it is a prototyping aid.
  • Format shifts after GLP-1 price deflation (two-sided). Cheaper formats (vials, multi-dose pens) gain as generics arrive; multi-dose pens could favour a pen-component maker, vials would not.
C-04

To what extent is political or regulatory change under way, and how does it bear on the company?

C-5419th of 44

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: substantial regulatory change is under way, and on balance it raises Nolato's barriers more than its costs; the one political channel that hurts is drug-price policy, which transmits to suppliers as price pressure.

  • Medical regulation (cost up, barrier up): the FDA's Quality Management System Regulation took effect on 2 February 2026, aligning US rules with ISO 13485; the EU MDR and IVDR lengthen customers' development cycles and raise documentation and surveillance costs. Nolato already holds the certifications, so the net effect is a higher wall around incumbents and modestly higher quality-assurance overhead (visible in the Hungarian validation headcount).
  • Trade and regionalisation (positive): local-for-local production in Europe, the US and Mexico (from 2025), with Penang in Malaysia as a "China plus one" site, insulates Nolato from tariffs and attracts customers who want regional redundancy. The BIOSECURE Act list (due 18 December 2026) and Western pharma's avoidance of Chinese device components for US supply favour Western-footprint suppliers; about 30% of Nolato's workforce is in China, but that footprint serves mainly Asian consumer electronics, so this is a watch point rather than a threat.
  • Drug pricing (negative): Novo Nordisk's lower US prices from January 2027, US pharmacy-benefit-manager pressure and generic semaglutide abroad push pharma to demand device price cuts.
  • Environment: SBTi-validated targets, net zero by 2045 and 99.9% renewable electricity in 2025 make Nolato a preferred partner on sustainability scoring; PFAS restrictions and medical-grade recycled-resin shortages cause revalidation friction and some cost.

The position is fragile only through the drug-price channel; the regulatory moat itself is stable.

2. Three lines in the model summary should not be quoted as they stand.

  • "Earnings per share against consensus, FY2026: +2.8%." This compares model-basis EPS (2.79) with a consensus that is mostly on a reported basis. Like for like, the forecast of record is about 3-4% below consensus for 2026: reported EPS 2.60 against 2.71, and reported net income of about 702 against consensus net income of 721.
  • "Earnings per share growth, FY2026: +3.3%." The FY2025 base in the model (2.70) is the standing Viewforth definition built from FactSet's EBIT of 976, which sits about SEK 90m below the company's EBITA; the 2026 figure is built from adjusted EBITA. Like for like, 2026 is a down year: reported EPS falls about 10% (2.88 to 2.60) and model-basis EPS falls about 6% (my calculation: 2025 on the model basis is about 2.96, from adjusted EBITA of about 1,065, net financial items of -54 and tax of 21%). The like-for-like path is therefore about -6% (2026), +16.5% (2027), +12% (2028).
  • "Net cash (debt), year end: -668 (2026) to -321 (2030)." The model starts from FactSet's net debt of 165 at Q3 2026, which the first set showed to be wrong (the company reports net financial debt of about 1,537 including leases and pensions, 1.0x adjusted EBITDA). Use the second set's leverage path (about 0.85x at the end of 2026, 0.7x in 2027, 0.6x in 2028), not the model's net cash line.

3. The gap the model summary flags: FY2026 free cash flow, the second set's 490 against the model's 345.6. The explanation is the one the second set gave in advance, and it does show an input that is wrong for this model. The model computes free cash flow from the change in the year-end working-capital balance. The 2026 working-capital input (25.5% of revenue) includes about SEK 150m of working capital that arrived with Unitron and M&M Qualtech, so the model charges it as a cash outflow in free cash flow (a build of about 230 in the year, against about 80 of organic absorption in the second set's cross-check). The same working capital was paid for inside the purchase price, which the model charges again through "Acquisitions less disposals" (-440). The year's cash flow therefore counts about SEK 150m twice. The arithmetic confirms it. In cash terms the second set's own build for 2026 is free cash flow of about 490, less the dividend paid in 2026 of about 458, less acquisitions of about 440: about -408. The model's is 346, less 431, less 440: about -525. The difference is the double count of about 145, partly offset because the model deducts the dividend forecast for 2026 (SEK 1.60 a share, 431) in the year, where the cash paid in 2026 was the SEK 1.70 for 2025 (458). I change "Acquisitions less disposals" for FY2026 from -440 to -290 (see inputs-changed.md); the working-capital input stays, because the year-end balance will indeed contain the acquired working capital. After the change the model's 2026 free cash flow is still about 346, which is correct on the model's definition (after the working capital that came with the acquired businesses); the Consumer should describe underlying 2026 free cash flow as about 490 before that item. The cross-check for 2027-2030 is within about 2% of the model and needs no change. The EPS differences (2.77 against 2.79; 3.23 against 3.25) come from rounding in the growth and margin inputs and are immaterial.
