Section 2 – Vodafone's Capital-Allocation Framework and Buyback Funding

Section summary
  1. Vodafone has suspended its share buybacks through March 2028 to prioritize organic cash flow and deleveraging.
  2. Proceeds from completed asset sales, including the sale of VodafoneZiggo, are being directed toward reducing net debt rather than returns.

Verification of Buybacks and Guidance

1.
Broad evidence gathered: Vodafone's strategic evaluation in this section is based on 32 relevant sources, covering 8 companies and 3 historical precedents. These documents, ranging from company reports in November 2026 to broker research from Deutsche Bank and Morgan Stanley, show that the buyback remains paused for fiscal year 2027 due to the UK minority acquisition. This capital allocation priority is verified across multiple filings.
2.
Completed capital returns: Vodafone returned a total of €9 bn to its shareholders over three years, including €4 bn via buybacks since May 2024. The company completed the second of its €2 bn buyback programmes on May 11, 2026, which returned substantial cash to equity holders following European asset sales. This return strategy was supported by €12.10 bn of cash proceeds from Spain and Italy. Consequently, the share count fell by 8.8% during this multi-year execution window.
3.
Halted share repurchases: Vodafone suspended further buyback programmes for the year to March 2028 to fund the acquisition of domestic assets. The group is deploying £4.30 bn, equivalent to approximately €5 bn, in the second half of fiscal 2027 to buy the remaining 49% of VodafoneThree UK. This strategic decision redirects cash that could have otherwise funded a third buyback programme. Thus, near-term share consolidation is halted while the group integrates its domestic operations.
4.
Upgraded cash forecasts: Vodafone upgraded its adjusted free cash flow outlook during the November 10, 2026 half-year results call. Chief Executive Margherita Della Valle noted that the upgraded guidance implies accelerating core cash generation in the second half of the fiscal year. However, the pre-recorded remarks did not specify that this incremental free cash flow would be allocated to reactivating share buybacks. This cash will instead support balance sheet deleveraging.
5.
Progressive dividend policy: Vodafone declared an interim dividend of 2.31 eurocents on November 10, 2026, representing a 2.5% increase year-on-year. The company remains committed to a predictable and progressive dividend framework starting from fiscal year 2026 onwards. This dividend commitment takes priority over share buybacks in the capital-allocation hierarchy. Therefore, cash returns will focus primarily on stable payouts rather than repurchases.

Disposal Proceeds and Strategic Cash Inflows

6.
Successful asset sales: Vodafone completed the divestment of its 50% interest in VodafoneZiggo to Liberty Global on July 31, 2026. The transaction delivered cash proceeds of €1 bn and a 10% equity stake in Ziggo Group to the parent company. This inflow helps mitigate the net debt increase from buying out UK minorities. Consequently, the group successfully simplified its European portfolio while securing cash.
7.
Cash inflow allocation: Vodafone recorded a net cash inflow of €973 mm from the VodafoneZiggo transaction in its November 10, 2026 interim report. The group utilized €908 mm of these proceeds to settle its outstanding loan receivable from VodafoneZiggo. Management explicitly confirmed that the remaining proceeds are earmarked to reduce overall net debt rather than fund buybacks. This allocation preserves liquidity during a period of high capital intensity.
8.
Projected tower monetisation: VodafoneZiggo agreed to sell its mobile tower assets to DigitalBridge for €669 mm on September 8, 2026. The transaction is designed to assist Ziggo Group with its 4.5x leverage target by fiscal year 2028 ahead of a planned spin-off. This divestment forms part of a wider €1.20 bn to €1.40 bn non-core asset program scheduled before the Amsterdam listing in mid-2027. Thus, the valuation of Vodafone's remaining 10% stake could appreciate significantly.
9.
Substantial future liquidity: Vodafone owns additional non-consolidated assets that could be monetized to fund future capital returns. For instance, its 50% stake in Oak Holdings is valued in excess of €6 bn based on recent transactions. The group also holds a 13% stake in Vodafone Idea that has become highly divestible. If these holdings are sold, Vodafone can unlock massive cash reserves to restart buybacks.

Balance Sheet Pressures and Cash Generation

10.
Rising net debt: Vodafone reported that its net debt increased by €8,062 mm to €33,473 mm on September 30, 2026. This expansion was driven by acquisitions and disposals, specifically the buyout of VodafoneThree and Safaricom transactions costing €6,957 mm. Elevated debt levels have pushed leverage higher in the near term. This rise in leverage prevents management from resuming share buybacks until the balance sheet is repaired.

This represents a 24.8% increase compared to €5,092 mm generated during the same period in 2025. However, this cash must be prioritized for debt reduction rather than capital returns.

Section 3 – Leverage, Credit-Rating Thresholds, and Rating-Agency Constraints

Section summary
  1. Vodafone's credit ratings remain stable at Baa2/BBB, but its leverage has risen to the midpoint of its target range.
  2. Maintaining these rating thresholds restricts immediate share buyback execution until leverage declines below target limits.

Credit Ratings and Peer Benchmarks

1.
Stable outlook: Moody's Ratings affirmed Vodafone's senior unsecured debt rating of Baa2 with a stable outlook on October 13, 2026. Fitch Ratings also affirmed Vodafone's long-term issuer default rating at BBB with a stable outlook on September 11, 2026. These stable ratings provide essential financial flexibility for future buybacks. Maintaining these ratings prevents the group from facing elevated financing costs in the future.
2.
Investment grade: Standard and Poor's maintains a BBB credit rating with a stable outlook on Vodafone's senior unsecured debt. Maintaining this credit rating is highly crucial because any downgrade would immediately increase refinancing costs across debt portfolios. This stable positioning ensures that liquidity remains preserved for strategic goals. Consequently, rating agency constraints directly limit the capital available for share buybacks.
3.
Positive refinancing: Moody's Ratings upgraded three of Vodafone's hybrid debt instruments to Baa3 from Ba1 on November 17, 2026. This upgrade ensures that all of Vodafone's hybrid securities are now rated one notch below its senior debt. The rating action reflects revised terms where these instruments are no longer treated as preferred equivalents. This development lowers interest expenses and improves the group's financial stability.
4.
Peer comparison: European telecom peers also manage heavy debt loads and tightly monitor rating agency leverage constraints. For example, Telecom Italia operates with a leveraged profile where its senior debt is rated Baa3 by Moody's and BBB- by Fitch. Unlike its peers, Vodafone enjoys a stronger investment-grade buffer at Baa2. This relative balance sheet strength helps insulate Vodafone from sudden rating downgrades during capital-intensive integrations.

Leverage and Buyback Constraints

5.
Elevated leverage: Vodafone's leverage ratio increased to 2.5x net debt to adjusted EBITDAaL in the half-year results ending September 30, 2026. This leverage level marks a notable increase from the 2.2x net debt to adjusted EBITDAaL reported at the end of fiscal year 2026. Management targets the lower half of its 2.25x to 2.75x target range over the medium term. Operating at the higher end of this band limits immediate capacity to execute further share buybacks.
6.
Strategic transactions: Vodafone's net debt expanded significantly due to key acquisitions and consolidations executed during the first half of fiscal year 2027. The company deployed substantial cash to complete the buyout of the remaining 49% of VodafoneThree and to purchase a 15% stake in Safaricom. These cash outflows pushed the group leverage up by approximately 0.4x in the near term. As a result, buybacks have been temporarily suspended to prioritize balance sheet deleveraging.
7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research expects Vodafone's group leverage to move below the bottom of its target range in fiscal year 2028. This rapid balance sheet repair would unlock prospects for higher shareholder returns and potential buyback reactivations. Therefore, current leverage pressures may only act as a short-term constraint rather than a long-term barrier.
