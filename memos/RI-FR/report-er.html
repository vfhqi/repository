Executive Summary

1.
FY26 headline delivery: Pernod Ricard reported FY26 net sales of €9,404 million on 27 August 2026, an organic decline of 3.9% and a reported contraction of 14.2%. This marginally missed the consensus expectation of a 3.7% organic decline. Double-digit declines in the United States and China made it the second weakest growth year since 1975.
2.
Profit beat against a weak top line: Profit from recurring operations was €2,423 million, an organic fall of 5.2%, ahead of the 5.9% decline in Vuma consensus. Reported profit fell 17.9% on foreign exchange and perimeter effects. This is the one clear positive deviation from expectations in the annual print.
3.
Margin defended, but with low-quality support: Organic operating margin contracted only 35 basis points to 25.8%. That delivery leaned on a 109 basis point contribution from lower advertising and promotion, which offset a 221 basis point gross margin drag. The cut in brand support is a trade-off, because Morgan Stanley warned that reducing brand support could weaken relative competitiveness if sustained over a multi-year downturn.
4.
Self-help is the core of the EPS defense: Fit for Future delivered €500 million of savings in FY26, and organic structure costs fell 8.0%. Delivery of the full €1 billion was pulled forward from FY29 to FY28.
5.
Cash conversion exceeded guidance: Free cash flow was €1,197 million, and cash conversion rose 17 percentage points to 91%. This exceeded the historical guidance of about 80%.
6.
Q1 FY27 beat on the headline: Q1 net sales were €2,234 million, reported on 15 October 2026, with organic sales down 0.9%. That beat the consensus forecast of a 1.7% decline. Management presented it as a sequential improvement on FY26's 3.9% decline. Analysts focused on whether it marked a cyclical bottom.
7.
United States deviation: Americas was a negative surprise. Organic sales fell 9.0% against a consensus decline of 7.0%. US organic sales fell 10% in Q1 against a four percent contraction in the full-strength spirits market. Wholesaler destocking explained about four points of the decline. The sell-out gap-to-market narrowed to about minus two percentage points. The gap to the market is closing, but reported sales still lag.
8.
China deviation: Reported China sales fell 25% organically in Q1, driven almost entirely by destocking. Underlying depletions returned to positive growth ahead of the Mid-Autumn Festival. Morgan Stanley cautioned this was flattered by easy comparisons. China net sales peaked at €1.4 billion in FY23 and have halved to €0.7 billion. The structural slide in China is still unresolved, even though shipments were in line with management's expectations.
9.
India deviation: India grew 19% organically in Q1, beating Morgan Stanley's regional forecast of 14%. Bernstein noted that a pipeline fill from IPO preparation flattered this, against an underlying high-single-digit trend. The upside surprise is therefore partly timing.
10.
Regional mix and Europe: Europe fell 6.0% against a consensus decline of 1.9%, while Asia-Rest of World grew 9.0% against 2.0%. High-margin US and China declines were offset by lower-margin India growth, leaving a near-term margin headwind. Morgan Stanley projected a 54 basis point organic margin contraction in FY27. The top-line beat is lower quality than it looks.
11.

After Q1, Bernstein trimmed them again by 0.5% and 0.7%. Jefferies left its organic operating profit forecast unchanged at a 1.3% decline. Redburn left its estimates largely unchanged. Morgan Stanley raised near-term EPS by 4%, driven entirely by foreign exchange and lower scrip dilution, with no change to fundamental value.
12.

Morgan Stanley held a cautious Underweight stance, arguing the beat was helped by temporary phasing benefits in Asia and Rest of World. Deutsche Bank kept a neutral view, noting that although organic sales beat, reported sales fell 1.2% below consensus. The FY27 EPS debate is about the quality of the beat rather than its size.
13.
EPS outlook: Bernstein expects diluted adjusted EPS to be broadly flat in FY27 and a 6% EPS CAGR over FY27 to FY30. Redburn projects 0.0% organic sales growth in FY27 and 3.1% in FY28. The shape is a flat transition year followed by a recovery that rests on destocking easing and cost delivery.

Jefferies noted the framework relies on self-help supporting margins rather than an immediate macro-led recovery.
