Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding distributor and supplier signals is critical for forecasting adidas's 2027 earnings per share and identifying early shifts in brand momentum.
1.
Key strategic question: "What do adidas AG's (ADS-DE) largest wholesale customers and distributors (JD Sports, DICK'S Sporting Goods including Foot Locker, Zalando, Frasers, Intersport, Topsports, Pou Sheng) and suppliers (Yue Yuen, Huali, Lai Yih, Pou Chen) say in their latest results, calls and monthly sales about adidas's spring/summer and autumn/winter 2027 order books, full-price commitments, markdown support, shelfspace allocation against Nike, and order timing; and how well have such customer and supplier signals predicted adidas's next-year sales in past cycles (2017 to 2019, 2022 to 2023)?"

Significance of the strategic inquiry

2.
Strategic relevance: Evaluating wholesale order books and supplier manufacturing schedules provides crucial visibility into adidas's forward sales pipelines. Because wholesale represents approximately 57% of adidas's total revenue, shifts in distributor sentiment can immediately derail or accelerate top-line growth. Consequently, tracking these external channel check metrics allows investors to cross-reference company guidance against real-world retail activity and factory commitments.
3.
Financial implications: Shifting dynamics in full-price commitments and markdown support directly determine adidas's gross margins and ultimate earnings per share. For example, when retail inventory becomes saturated, margins are heavily impacted by promotional support and write-offs. Therefore, understanding whether distributors are securing clean full-price order books helps size next-year EPS growth or contraction.
4.
Competitive pressure: Retail shelf-space allocation trends are critical as adidas fights to capture market share from Nike. Industry checks indicate that distributors are increasingly turning to adidas to replace lost Nike volume during the latter's channel transition. This redistribution of retail space could provide a multi-year tailwind for adidas's performance categories, especially in underpenetrated markets.
5.
Strategic alignment: Related inquiries concern how macroeconomic factors like global tariffs and localized product customization influence the regional supply chains of major sportswear brands. These external factors directly impact sourcing strategies, prompting suppliers to relocate capacity to lower-cost countries in Southeast Asia. Thus, monitoring localized design and agile production setups in key regions like China is essential to assess adidas's margin resilience.
6.
Contrary evidence: none found, and search included company filings, expert calls, and broker research from May 2025 to November 2026.

Supply chain risks and predictive history

7.
Supplier alignment: Sourcing signals from major manufacturers like Yue Yuen and Huali reflect the physical production timelines for upcoming seasons. Since footwear production represents nearly 60% of adidas's business, factory utilization and order timing serve as leading indicators of future wholesale deliveries. Thus, supplier constraints or cost pressures can directly crimp gross margins before retail sales are even registered.
8.
Predictive power: Analyzing past supply chain cycles from 2017 to 2019 and 2022 to 2023 demonstrates how accurately channel metrics forecast financial inflections. Historical inventory de-stocking and capacity adjustments often anticipated actual reported sales turns by multiple quarters. Therefore, these lead signals are vital for validating adidas's mid-term target of 10% operating margin by 2027.
9.
Other side of the story: While distributor and supplier indicators are highly valuable, their predictive power can be disrupted by unpredictable direct-to-consumer sales trends. Because direct-to-consumer channels reflect realtime demand and are highly volatile, wholesale-biased checks might overstate or understate adidas's total consolidated revenue trajectory. Consequently, relying solely on wholesale partner networks ignores the rapid growth of adidas's own retail and digital channels.
