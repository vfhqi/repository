Consequently, the rapid cash recovery underscores how working capital serves as a self-correcting liquidity buffer when market prices normalize.

This severe tax rate significantly restricts cash generation and reduces the capital available for share repurchases.
2.

DNB Carnegie notes on 16 September 2026 that the OGRL will apply a revenue-based tax rate of 35% on exceptional revenues. The tax only activates when prices exceed $90 per barrel for oil and 90 pence per therm for gas. This transition provides some long-term visibility but structurally alters the economics of future projects.
3.
Depressed upstream investment: Prolonged fiscal uncertainty on the United Kingdom Continental Shelf has severely deterred capital deployment. Drilling Tools International reported on 22 October 2026 that high tax rates directly contributed to reduced drilling activity and project deferrals in a region facing unprecedented capital flight.
