Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Instalco operates a decentralized technical installation model across the Nordics and Germany that has delivered mixed margin performance.
  2. Balance sheet deleveraging and Swedish margin recovery have driven a significant stock rerating and positive broker revisions.
  3. Historical leverage concerns have begun to ease as organic growth returned and the consolidation of Germany's Fabri group expanded M&A.

Decentralised business model and history

1.
Decentralised operational structure: Instalco AB provides technical installation services across electrical, plumbing, ventilation, and industrial specialties through a decentralized network of over 175 independent subsidiaries. The company focuses primarily on small to medium-sized projects to minimize concentration risks. High operational freedom allows local units to adapt quickly to regional demand variations. This decentralized approach supports resilient service and installation volumes across changing Nordic economic cycles.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Section summary
  1. The evidence from the Q3 2026 review supports an 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory driven mainly by margin recovery, not by balance-sheet-funded acquisitions. That trajectory is credible but exposed to one unresolved risk, the conversion of profit into cash. The quarter strengthened the operating case and weakened the financing case, and the two pull the EPS outlook in different directions. The single most important thing that changed is that Swedish operating leverage moved from forecast to reported fact. Group EBITA reached SEK 254 million against SEK 180 million a year earlier, and the EBITA margin was 7.2% against 5.6%. The group beat the SEK 225 million consensus by 12.9%. The source of the beat was Sweden, where EBITA of SEK 179 million exceeded consensus of SEK 141 million by 27%, reflecting a 200 basis point year-on-year margin step-up. This is the first hard evidence that the Instalco 2.0 self-help case can reach the group income statement. It is what makes the path toward the 8% EBITA target look more attainable than it did before the report.

The headline EPS beat overstates the improvement. Diluted EPS was SEK 0.69 against SEK 0.24 and beat the SEK 0.40 consensus by 72.5%. However, the result was boosted by a SEK 102 million non-cash Fabri AG revaluation, which inflated reported EPS by approximately SEK 0.38. On simple arithmetic, underlying EPS was roughly SEK 0.31, below the consensus figure.

The same gain flatters financing lines: net financial items were positive SEK 28 million against negative SEK 55 million. Excluding the SEK 102 million gain, net financial items would have been about negative SEK 74 million, so financing costs weighed more heavily than a year earlier. Operating profit therefore drove the quarter's improvement, and below-the-line items did not help.

The conflict between Swedish margins and weak cash collection is real. Operating cash flow was SEK -66 million against SEK 133 million, and rolling cash conversion fell to 74% from 112%. Leverage stayed at 3.3x, above the 2.5x ceiling. That compares with a broker model of 2.3x for full-year 2026, so the deleveraging path the market had assumed has not yet materialised.

On balance, the evidence favors reading the Swedish margin gain as genuine and the cash shortfall as primarily timing and mix. Management attributes the shortfall to late invoicing and a higher mix of industrial projects, which typically demand longer payment terms. It also states that overdue receivables had not increased. DNB Carnegie's Söderblom likewise points to seasonal cash collection in the fourth quarter as the test.

This verdict is provisional. The overdue-receivables assurance is management's own, and a single quarter cannot separate timing from structure. Leverage, not margin, is the binding constraint on EPS compounding. Management says capital allocation will remain cautious in upcoming quarters to focus on cash generation and deleveraging.

Near-term EPS growth must therefore come from margin, volume and lower financing drag, not from a faster bolt-on pace. This helps explain why sell-side estimate changes were small: the 2027e adjusted EPS estimate was cut by 1.7%, balancing higher Swedish margin assumptions against lower Norwegian ones. Consensus sees the improvement as largely offset rather than additive. Volume visibility is strong, but it carries a working-capital cost.

Order backlog rose to SEK 11,746 million, up 30.1% year-on-year. Organic growth contributed 14.2% and acquisitions 12.7%, and the backlog-to-sales ratio reached 1.25x. A backlog this large supports 2027 revenue and labor utilization. My inference is that converting a larger, more industrial backlog will absorb more working capital, which makes the cash question more important than the revenue question.

Norway is the main drag on the path to 8%. Norwegian EBITA fell to SEK 20 million from SEK 28 million and missed the SEK 33 million forecast by 39.4%. Management does not expect significant Norwegian profitability improvement until delayed projects start in early 2027. Meanwhile Norwegian housing sales fell to the secondweakest level recorded since 1999.

As DNB Carnegie noted, group-wide margin recovery remains uneven and highly dependent on one domestic market. The strongest case against this conclusion is that the cash weakness is a reversal rather than a timing blip. Cash conversion reached 108% in fiscal 2025, but that was driven by intense working capital reduction programs, which are one-off in nature. If 2025 was flattered by a release of working capital, 74% may be closer to the normal level for a growing, industrial-heavy book.

The Swedish comparison is also soft: a low prior-year baseline helped amplify the 41% operating profit growth. Norwegian credit losses also sit uneasily beside the assurance on receivables. On this view, reported margin is running ahead of cash-backed earnings, and EPS growth would be capped by interest costs and a stalled deleveraging path.
