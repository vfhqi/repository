External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external environment is the best it has been for a Norwegian harsh-environment driller in a decade, and that is the problem for incremental upside: demand is high and stable, rates are high and flat, and the repricing that scarcity enabled has already been captured in this company's contracts. Over the last six months and the next six months, the forces have stayed favourable but have not improved: leading-edge rates are unchanged, the only new fixture for this company (Vår at about USD 473,000 a day) is at or slightly below the current level, and competitors are spending heavily to enter Norway at a discount. The forces that would change the picture are external and largely outside the company's control: oil falling below USD 85 (sentiment) or below USD 65 to 70 sustained (operator budgets), redeployed supply into Norway, and the post-2028 step-down in development drilling. For the operating model, the external inputs support full utilisation and current rates to 2027 and a flat-rate base case for 2028 to 2030, with a downside case of about 10% to 15% lower rates on the rigs that recontract in 2027 to 2029.

To what extent is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically, and with how much visibility? (A cyclical swing counts when it has two-plus years of visibility.)

D646th of 11

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: demand for Norwegian harsh-environment semi-submersible capacity is high and stable, not accelerating; it is cyclically strong with about two years of hard visibility (to end-2027 for this company, longer for the market), and the structural question for 2029 and beyond is unresolved rather than negative.

  • Level is high. Marketed utilisation of high-specification harsh semis on the Norwegian Continental Shelf is about 90% (company) and harsh-environment floater utilisation under contract 94% (DNB Carnegie, 9 October 2026). Only about 22 semi-submersibles can operate on the Norwegian shelf and most are committed. Statistics Norway raised its 2026 oil and gas investment estimate by NOK 11 billion to NOK 266 billion. Exploration activity in 2025 almost doubled from 2023, and Equinor, Aker BP and Vår Energi run a joint programme of about five high-impact wells a year.
  • The demand driver is maintenance of production, which is sticky. Operators drill infrastructure-led exploration, infill wells and subsea tie-backs to arrest natural decline; that needs more wells per barrel and supports rig days even when exploration budgets are flat (global exploration and production spending growth about 0% in 2026; offshore spending rose 2% in 2025 despite a 15% fall in the oil price).
  • The change is in duration, not volume. Operators are lengthening lead times and contract terms to secure scarce rigs (the Nordkapp extension was confirmed ahead of the option date; Vår Energi committed in September 2026 to a rig for early 2028 to Q1 2031). This raises visibility without raising the price.
  • Visibility. The company's firm backlog of USD 2.3 billion (plus about USD 0.5 billion from the Vår award) covers about 96% of 2027 revenue (DNB Carnegie). The market itself has visibility through 2027 to 2028 on sanctioned developments.
  • Structural question. Norwegian oil production is expected to start a structural decline by about 2035 (Wood Mackenzie and Rystad, cited by Morgan Stanley), and the wave of developments sanctioned in 2022 under the temporary tax package (background fact, not in the files: it pulled forward many development plans, such as Yggdrasil, whose drilling peaks in the middle of this decade) will pass its drilling peak around 2028 to 2030. This is the demand risk inside the four-year modelling horizon; it is a plausible flattening, not an evidenced decline.

For the model: treat rig-day demand as flat at full utilisation of the five-rig fleet through 2027; treat 2028 to 2030 as a market in which recontracting happens at roughly today's rates in a base case, with a meaningful probability (about one in three on my judgement) of softer rates if the post-2028 development drilling step-down coincides with redeployed supply.

Layer 1 · Internal change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The company changed a great deal in 2024 to 2025 and has changed little since. A stable, operations-led leadership team executed a refinancing, a well-priced acquisition and a doubling of the dividend, and has kept utilisation at the top of the sector. The internal forces now at work are those of a mature harvest: falling capital expenditure until late 2028, deleveraging, and a progressive dividend that consensus expects to rise to USD 1.20 a share in 2027. Negatives are moderate (recent unplanned events, cost inflation, a minimum-tax step, thin governance).
