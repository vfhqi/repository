[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Instalco is a founder-led margin recovery whose first and most certain part the market has already paid for. Per Sjöstrand's return as chief executive on 1 August 2025 with a margin-over-volume directive has moved the Swedish margin, the backlog and the sell-side's estimates, and the change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain; but at SEK 42.88, up 69.8 percent in twelve months and at the stock's own median multiple of 13.0x consensus earnings 24 months forward, the modal total return we compute is about 9 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case to own the shares existed at SEK 21 to SEK 30 in early 2026; the case now is to hold them if owned and to buy them below about SEK 37.

  • The change inside the company is broad, resourced and already visible in the outputs.
    • Five of the twelve internal forces are strong at once, where the framework's screen asks for three, and the first margin proof is in the largest segment.
    • The founder who built the group from SEK 570m of sales to SEK 8,890m in 2021 at an 8.2 percent adjusted EBITA margin replaced Robin Boheman on 1 August 2025, was made permanent on 19 March 2026, holds 8.56 percent of the company and bought 42,000 shares in Q4 2025.
    • The strategy changed in substance: "margin over volume", selective bidding, a baseline of minimum execution standards, thirteen business areas collapsed into three countries with operating officers, and acquisitions made conditional on margin, debt reduction and cash flow.
    • Sweden, 71 percent of sales, printed a 7.0 percent EBITA margin in Q2 2026 against 6.6 percent a year earlier
      • The group's adjusted EBITA margin was 7.1 percent in the quarter against 5.9 percent reported for FY2025.
    • Germany is a real new market entered through Fabri AG: 24 percent for EUR 15m in March 2025 and 51 percent from 1 August 2026 for a further EUR 35.7m, a business with EUR 86m of 2025 sales at a 12.7 percent EBITA margin.
  • The external forces help, but they are a cyclical turn, not a demand inflection.
    • Swedish construction bottomed in late 2025 and the backlog is up 17.0 percent to SEK 10,937m, 14.8 percent organically
    • But contract visibility is 12 to 18 months, new residential is "extremely sluggish", and tenders still attract fifteen bidders against a historical five, so competition and wage inflation still push the other way.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on three of the four lines the framework tests; cash conversion is the fourth and it has gone the other way.
    • Organic growth ran −2.8, −3.6, +5.0, +4.9 and +8.4 percent from Q2 2025 to Q2 2026, ending six quarters of contraction
    • The EBITA margin rose from 6.0 percent in Q3 2025 to 7.1 percent in Q2 2026
    • Net debt to EBITDA fell from 3.3x to 2.6x.
    • Rolling cash conversion fell from 107 to 90 percent over the same year and Q2 2026 operating cash flow halved to SEK 104m, which is where the case against lives.
  • The chain is complete to its sixth layer: the sell-side has moved, and has moved more on price than on earnings.
    • The consensus target is up 34 percent in six months to SEK 49.67 and the buy share up 14 points to 86 percent, while consensus earnings per share are up only 6 percent over six months and the near year, FY2026, was cut 5.7 percent.
    • That is what a re-rating by the sell-side looks like after a beat, not an estimate cycle with much left in it.
  • What we think it earns is a recovery to 2028 and a 7 to 8 percent-a-year grower after it.
    • On our path adjusted earnings per share rise from SEK 1.76 in FY2025 to SEK 2.79 in FY2027 and SEK 3.14 in FY2028, about 21 percent a year, with the EBITA margin reaching 7.6 percent in FY2027, 7.9 percent in FY2028 and 8.0 percent in FY2029
    • Beyond FY2028 the rate falls to about 7 to 8 percent a year.
    • Consensus sits above us at SEK 3.01 for FY2027 and SEK 3.40 for FY2028, and the gap is almost all margin.
  • What it costs is the stock's own median multiple on earnings that already embed the recovery.
    • At SEK 42.88 the shares trade at 13.0x consensus earnings 24 months forward, the 43rd percentile of the stock's own ten-year history, and at 14.06x our own 24-month-forward figure of SEK 3.05
    • On a one-year-forward footing the multiple is 16.2x.
    • The shares have risen 69.8 percent in twelve months and 21.1 percent in three, and the 52-week low was SEK 21.364.
    • The contracted catalysts are in the price: this is the price-moved-against-the-park pattern of the framework's Bravida documents, and it is the central fact of this record.
  • The return does not clear the bar on the modal path, and only one branch clears it at all.
    • Valuing the shares at 14.0x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the eighteen-month value including SEK 0.70 of dividends is SEK 49.00, 9.3 percent a year, and the three-year value including SEK 2.50 is SEK 55.84, 9.2 percent a year: a D at the boundary of F against the framework's anchors.
    • The 14.0x is a blend of one third at 16x, the branch in which the market treats Instalco as Bravida, and two thirds at 13x, the stock's own median rounded down for leverage above target.
    • The 16x branch returns 19.4 percent a year over eighteen months
    • Its odds are about one in three because it needs the margin, the cash and then the market's response all to go right.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITA to earnings per share takes the return to 3.8 percent a year and the 'Modal return' rating from D to F
    • The recommendation does not change, because it does not depend on the difference.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', crisply on the inputs and early on the outputs.
    • The pattern it fitted six months ago, 'Major CfC Clearing', has completed: the multiple left the bottom quartile and the capitulation reversed, and that pattern's return has been collected by whoever bought at SEK 21 to SEK 30.

  • The case therefore is a compelling business turnaround and an uncompelling investment at this price.

    • The chain holds from the founder to the sell-side's estimates and breaks at its last link, the return against the bar, because the market moved before we did.
    • We park, and the price below which the modal path clears 20 percent a year over eighteen months is about SEK 37.

  • Rating = C, because the Building Block measures materiality along the whole chain and the chain breaks at its last link: 'Change Forces' (E 1, BB 1) is B and 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1) is B, but the 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1) is D at SEK 42.88, and a return that does not clear the bar binds the judgement about whether the expected change is material to a shareholder.

    • Not B, because no modal path we can build from these files reaches 13 percent a year at this price without a multiple above the stock's own median.
    • Not D, because the forces and the outputs are real, dated and travelling, and the same arithmetic on consensus earnings reaches 14.4 percent a year.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against our park, at its strongest, is that the compounder re-rating is probable, not possible.
      • The market has already decided that Swedish installers with a founder at the helm and a restored margin deserve Bravida's multiple: Bravida trades at 16.6x on a 5.4 percent adjusted margin, Instalco at 13.0x on 7.1 percent.
      • Instalco's organic growth of 8.4 percent is top-quartile in the sector, its backlog per employee is the highest since Q1 2023, Fabri gives it a market five times the size of the Nordics to roll up at 12.7 percent margins, and the sell-side's three-year earnings compound rate of 16.5 percent at 10.9x 2027e EV/EBITA is the cheapest growth in the peer group.
      • On consensus earnings and a 16x multiple the eighteen-month return is about 25 percent a year, an A.
    • We concede that Bravida's multiple is a live anchor, that Instalco's margin is higher than Bravida's, and that the sell-side's targets of SEK 47 to SEK 49.67 sit above the price.
    • We still hold our view because the hypothesis needs three things to be true at once, and the third is the market's.
      • The margin must reach 7.5 percent or more (probable, about three in five)
      • Leverage must fall below 2.5x with the acquisition engine restarted (probable, about two in three, and correlated with the first)
      • And the market must then pay 16x rather than 13x (plausible, a little over one in two).
      • Their product is about one in three, and a one-in-three branch does not set the modal path.
      • The sell-side's own targets imply a 16 to 18 percent total return over twelve months, below the bar.
    • Where the case against would win: a Swedish margin of 7.5 percent or more and operating cash flow recovering toward SEK 200m in the Q3 2026 report on 23 October 2026 would move our earnings path to consensus and the eighteen-month return at this price to a C, not yet a B
      • The odds of the 16x branch would then rise toward one in two if bolt-on acquisitions resume in Germany in H1 2027 with leverage below 2.5x.
