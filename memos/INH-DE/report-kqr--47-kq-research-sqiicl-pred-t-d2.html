2.
Range of Plausible Outcomes: The range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months is extraordinarily wide, spanning from FY2026 earnings per share of 5.51 euros under sustained tungsten pricing to a potential collapse toward 3.63 euros in FY2027 as raw material normalization compresses margins, with inventory valuation allowances on 495 million euros of stock representing a material downside catalyst that could further suppress reported profitability below consensus expectations.
3.
Working Capital Volatility: Working capital peaked at 627 million euros in June 2026 due to strategic tungsten procurement, but management expects significant cash release in H2 2026 based on seasonal patterns, which would restore liquidity for the temporarily paused programmatic M&A strategy that spent only 16.60 million euros in H1 2026 despite targets to complete further acquisitions before year-end.

Engineering Margin Path: The Engineering segment's record order backlog of 430.40 million euros and expansive book-to-bill ratio of 1.22x provide multi-year revenue visibility, yet profitability remains depressed at a 5.6% H1 2026 adjusted EBITA margin due to underutilization and unfavorable mix, requiring backlog conversion and capacity recovery to return toward the 8.5% to 10.5% target band amid persistent German capital goods weakness.

Infrastructure Structural Resilience: The Infrastructure segment demonstrated exceptional resilience with H1 2026 adjusted EBITA margins of 11.2% expanding to 12.6% in Q2 2026, driven by high-margin digital network product mix that successfully offset weak residential construction, though this performance remains vulnerable to telecommunications capital expenditure slowdowns and delayed federal infrastructure fund deployment.
6.
Broker Consensus Assumption: Cited broker consensus from NuWays AG and mwb research AG already assumes that Materials Solutions margins will normalize gradually from the exceptional FY2026 levels toward historical baselines by FY2027-2028, with earnings per share declining from the peak 5.51 euros to approximately 3.63 euros as tungsten pricing reverts and no permanent structural upgrade to BETEK's baseline profitability is embedded in forecasts.
7.
Financial Structure Constraints: The EBITA-to-EPS bridge faces significant headwinds from volatile tax rates ranging from 28.7% to 43.1% due to 5.98 million euros of stranded trade-tax losses, elevated net interest expenses of 25.3 million euros on 665.8 million euros of net financial debt, and rising PPA amortization of 20.8 million euros annually, though the flat share count of 24.9 million stabilizes per-share metrics.

Peer Discipline Comparison: Nordic serial acquirers like Lifco AB and Indutrade AB demonstrate superior cash conversion rates of 73% to 82% and lower leverage of 0.5x to 0.8x Net Debt/EBITDA compared to INDUS's temporarily elevated working capital profile, highlighting the operational gap that prevents INDUS from commanding premium valuation multiples despite its conservative capital allocation and disciplined approach.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The financial trajectory of INDUS Holding AG hinges on whether its current tungsten carbide windfall can transition into sustainable growth across its engineering and infrastructure segments.

Significance of the Key Question

1.
Core research focus: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Extreme margin skew: The nearterm earnings profile of INDUS is heavily distorted by the Materials Solutions segment where BETEK has achieved massive windfall profits due to tungsten supply shortages. This special situation drove the segment's adjusted EBITA margin to an unsustainable 22.4% in the first half of 2026, compared to just 8.1% in the prior year period. Unless the company successfully redeploys this cash into accretive M&A, the EPS path is highly skewed toward a sharp decline once raw material prices normalize.

Section 2 – Materials Solutions Margin Path and BETEK Tungsten Cycle

Section summary
  1. BETEK's exceptional Q2 2026 margins of 26.90% are peaking as tungsten prices face correction from non-Chinese supply additions.
  2. Peers show a high vulnerability to raw material timing lags, risking significant valuation allowances when pricing peaks reverse.

Extraordinary Windfall at BETEK

1.
Hiked guidance: INDUS Holding AG significantly raised its FY2026 adjusted EBITA margin guidance for the Materials Solutions segment to a range of 15% to 17% following stellar H1 results. The segment's adjusted EBITA margin reached 22.40% in H1 2026, driven primarily by the exceptional price-driven contribution of its subsidiary BETEK. However, management explicitly warns in the Q2 2026 interim report that this pricing momentum does not represent a new normal level. NuWays AG on 8 October 2026 confirmed that this windfall drove H1 segment adjusted EBITA to 84.50 million euros, up from 23.10 million euros.
2.
Key financial metrics and margin development for the Materials Solutions segment in H1 and Q2 2026 Concentration risks: BETEK's dominant position creates a massive corporate profit dependency, as the subsidiary represents approximately 51% of total Materials Solutions sales. The parent company's strategic decision to build up tungsten inventories was crucial to maintaining delivery capacity during shortages. This inventory build-up tied up significant liquidity, raising group working capital to 627 million euros as of June 2026. We expect that any rapid normalization in tungsten prices will directly compress BETEK's gross margin as high-cost inventory is worked through.
3.
Contrary evidence: Structural defense demand and military stockpiling are sustaining elevated Western tungsten prices far longer than standard cyclical indicators predict. Morgan Stanley's report on 19 October 2026 states that the US Defense Logistics Agency awarded up to a 2 billion dollar contract to rebuild stockpiles. This massive institutional procurement ensures that Western pricing premiums are likely to remain structurally robust into 2027. This high-price environment would prevent the immediate collapse of BETEK's profit margins even as new mining projects commence.

Peer Lags and Inventory Vulnerabilities

4.
Timing windfalls: Kennametal Inc reported an extraordinary adjusted operating margin of 58.40% in its Infrastructure segment for the quarter ended 30 June 2026. According to Kennametal's annual report of 9 September 2026, this margin surge was driven by 198 million dollars of favorable raw material timing benefits. This temporary timing benefit occurs because toolmakers pass pricing adjustments to customers much faster than raw material costs flow through the balance sheet. This correlation suggests BETEK's peak 26.90% Q2 margin was similarly inflated by transient raw material price-cost spreads.
5.
Lagged cost flow: Sandvik AB manages raw material price fluctuations through scrap indexing and structured pricing lags within its machining business. Morgan Stanley notes on 19 October 2026 that Sandvik's powder selling prices adjust with a 1-month lag while cost of goods sold lags by up to 3 months. Sandvik's Q3 2026 interim report reported a temporary timing benefit of 104 million Swedish kronor from tungsten. This operational lag means both Sandvik and BETEK face margin compression if spot prices fall quickly.
6.
Recycling advantages: Mitsubishi Materials Corporation has insulated its cutting-tool operations from China's export controls by significantly expanding its tungsten recycling capacity. The company's presentation of 6 August 2026 revealed a positive ordinary profit impact of 7.60 billion yen from releasing low-cost tungsten inventory. Mitsubishi Materials is targeting a 100% tungsten recycling rate across its non-Chinese manufacturing sites by the fiscal year ending March 2031. This recycling capability provides a massive competitive advantage over smaller, unhedged competitors that rely solely on expensive spot markets.

Global Tungsten Supply and Price Bifurcation

7.
Restricted exports: China's dominant role in controlling approximately 79% of global tungsten supply continues to restrict concentrate availability. Diamond Equity Research reported on 20 October 2026 that Chinese export controls introduced in February 2025 significantly reduced product availability outside China. This supply restriction has heightened global supply chain uncertainty, directly driving international prices higher. In response, Western defense procurement policies are accelerating re-shoring efforts to secure non-Chinese primary tungsten.
8.
South Korean supply: South Korea's Sangdong Mine has officially transitioned to commercial tungsten concentrate production to supply Western markets. Almonty Industries announced on 21 September 2026 that its processing plant received final operational certification, allowing commercial sales. The company has secured an amended 21-year offtake agreement covering more than 90% of its Phase I production. This long-term contract with Global Tungsten & Powders guarantees a secure revenue stream that significantly derisks the mine's financial outlook.
9.
Competitive landscapes: Competitors like Ceratizit and Element Six are adopting distinct raw material strategies to mitigate the tungsten squeeze. Morgan Stanley argues on 19 October 2026 that Ceratizit's parent company Plansee has secured most of Sangdong's concentrate through long-term agreements. In contrast, Element Six focuses heavily on synthetic diamond adoption for industrial tooling as a highly durable alternative. This structural divergence means competitors with direct mining alliances can sustain high production utilization while unhedged toolmakers face margin erosion.

Precedents of Historical Pricing Dispersion

10.
Price crash dispersion: Historical analysis of previous tungsten price peaks reveals a wide dispersion of financial outcomes for specialized toolmakers. Diamond Equity Research noted on 21 October 2026 that ammonium paratungstate prices peaked in mid-2011 and mid-2013 near 350 dollars per metric ton unit. Following these peaks, unhedged toolmakers suffered severe margin compression within two quarters due to rapid inventory devaluations as high-cost supply flowed through the P&L. This precedent suggests BETEK's currently elevated gross margins are highly vulnerable to similar post-peak pricing corrections once Rotterdam benchmarks decline.
11.
Fanya market collapse: The collapse of the Fanya Metal Exchange in 2015 triggered a massive pricing downturn that heavily penalized toolmakers carrying high-cost inventory. Diamond Equity Research reported on 21 October 2026 that the Fanya collapse abruptly disrupted the robust pricing environment for critical materials. Kennametal responded by implementing restructuring actions to reduce footprint and cost structures in early 2015. Consequently, Kennametal's Infrastructure segment posted an operating loss of 4 million dollars for the quarter ended 31 December 2015.
12.
Post-peak margin drag: The rapid decline in tungsten prices during late 2019 illustrates the severe lag effect on toolmaker margins. Kennametal's management noted on 5 November 2019 that average tungsten prices dropped from 270 dollars to 200 dollars in a single quarter. This price drop created a 360 basis point drag on Kennametal's quarterly EBITDA margin. This lag occurred because the company was forced to work through higher-priced raw materials while passing price cuts to customers immediately.
13.
Valuation risk warning: INDUS management issued an explicit warning in its Q1 2026 report regarding elevated inventory levels. NuWays AG reported on 8 October 2026 that group inventory turnover remained flat at 1.80 times, reflecting slower utilization. The company's total inventories stood at 495 million euros at 31 March 2026, posing significant valuation allowance risks. If tungsten spot prices fall sharply, INDUS will face material write-downs that will directly impact its reported earnings per share.
14.
Other side of the story: BETEK's margins might remain permanently higher than historical baselines due to structural shifts in global sourcing. NuWays AG argues on 30 July 2026 that Chinese export controls are unlikely to normalize in the short term, sustaining elevated pricing. Furthermore, BETEK's proactive inventory building has allowed it to gain permanent market share as smaller competitors exit. This increased market consolidation could allow INDUS to self-finance its programmatic acquisition strategy and support elevated EPS targets.

Section 3 – Working Capital Release and Acquisition Program Dynamics

Section summary
  1. INDUS AG expects a swift working capital release in late 2026 as seasonal and raw material pressures begin to subside.
  2. Strategic focus is returning to acquisitions as inventory requirements at BETEK normalize, positioning the company for growth.

Peak Working Capital Dynamics

1.
Working capital expansion: INDUS Holding AG experienced a massive capital drain as working capital peaked at 627 million euros in June 2026 from 461.50 million euros at the end of 2025. The company's interim report of 12 August 2026 reveals this increase was primarily driven by strategic tungsten carbide procurement and seasonal impacts. This massive inventory build-up temporarily suppressed operational cash flows but was deemed necessary to protect market share. Consequently, near-term earnings per share will remain depressed unless these inventories are rapidly liquidated to release cash.

Strategic asset disposal: INDUS Holding AG finalized the sale of its subsidiary MEWESTA Hydraulik to Lange Group on 5 October 2026 as part of its active portfolio management. The company's press release indicates that this divestment is aligned with its EMPOWERING MITTELSTAND strategy. Although MEWESTA generated modest annual sales of approximately 5 million euros, the disposal allows INDUS to focus resources on higher-margin technology clusters. This streamlined portfolio focus should enhance long-term operating margins, defending the group's consolidated earnings per share.

Contrary evidence: NuWays AG reported on 8 October 2026 that INDUS's deal flow is set to accelerate because the tungsten inventory crisis is now fully resolved. This suggests that the M&A slowdown was much shorter than the market feared, meaning inorganic growth will resume sooner to support consolidated earnings per share.

Engineering Segment Recovery and Backlog Conversion

1.
Promising order backlog: The Engineering segment has built a robust cushion against macroeconomic downturns through massive order accumulation. Challenging sector outlook: German engineering as a whole is facing intense pricing pressure, which threatens INDUS's ability to easily pass on elevated wage costs. NuWays AG reported on 28 July 2026 that the VDMA industry association lowered its real machinery production forecast for 2026 from 1% growth to a flat 0%. This sector stagnation has forced INDUS's subsidiaries to accept lower prices to secure volume. Therefore, we expect that pricing pressure will remain a headwind for the engineering segment unless global industrial capital expenditure experiences a rapid cyclical recovery.

Infrastructure Resilience and Digital Network Mix

10.
Stellar infrastructure margins: INDUS's Infrastructure segment has delivered remarkable profitability, serving as a critical operational pillar for the group while other divisions face cyclical pressures. The company's H1 2026 interim report revealed that Infrastructure adjusted EBITA surged by 30.2% to 34.50 million euros. This drove the segment's H1 adjusted EBITA margin to 11.2%, which then expanded to a stellar 12.6% in Q2 2026. This exceptional performance was propelled by favorable pricing power and high operational efficiency across its portfolio.

Digital network mix: High-margin digital infrastructure products have successfully offset persistent weakness in residential construction. According to the company's H1 2026 report, incoming orders in the Infrastructure segment grew by a highly impressive 23.3% to 351.80 million euros. This surge was driven primarily by high demand for digital network and cable laying technologies. This positive product mix means that margins can remain structurally high because telecommunications investments command much better pricing than standard residential components.
12.
Weather installation benefits: Seasonal weather patterns provided a temporary but significant top-line boost to the segment's environmental portfolio in the second quarter. NuWays AG reported on 13 August 2026 that above-average summer temperatures drove a rapid increase in demand for building envelope sealing and air-conditioning units. German residential permits dropped as high interest rates and elevated building costs continue to deter developers. Consequently, INDUS's standard building technology subsidiaries will continue to face volume pressure until domestic mortgage rates decline significantly.
