Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The separation of TKH Group's Electrification and Automation businesses makes understanding its segment-level pricing power critical for predicting future earnings per share.

The Core Challenge

1.
Strategic inquiry: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: The high-voltage and inter-array cable capacity shortage of 2023 to 2027 and its end as peer capacity arrives in 2027 and 2028; the Tier 1 tyre-maker capital-expenditure freeze of 2025 to 2026 driven by tariffs, energy costs and site permitting; the machine-vision destocking of 2023 to 2025 and the restocking that began in 2026; raw-material and energy inflation in 2026 (copper up about 30% year on year, European electricity and gas prices sharply higher); euro strength against the US dollar and Chinese yuan; the German industrial recession affecting specialty cables and European vision demand?"

Strategic Context and Business Separation

2.
Pending separation: Shareholders approved the separation of the Electrification business at the 3 September 2026 EGM, which will create two distinct entities with different capital requirements. This dual-track process targets completion in the fourth quarter of 2026 or first quarter of 2027, as noted by Jefferies on 13 August 2026. Consequently, evaluating segment-level pricing power is vital for estimating stand-alone valuations because each business faces unique competitive and margin drivers.

3.
Value unlock: The separation aims to eliminate the massive 30% conglomerate discount that historically depressed TKH's market valuation. Jefferies reported on 13 August 2026 that heterogeneous activities under one roof diluted management focus and constrained the capital-light Automation activities. Therefore, resolving whether cable margins represent permanent contracted books or temporary scarcity rents is critical for forecasting future capital allocation and investor returns.

Impact on Earnings Per Share

4.
Earnings trajectory: Resolving these pricing power questions is critical for forecasting TKH's adjusted earnings per share, which Jefferies projected on 13 August 2026 to rise from €2.30 in FY25 to €3.34 in FY26 and €3.83 in FY27. These estimates represent a steep recovery from the Eemshaven start-up losses, meaning that even small pricing concessions could materially impair the projected margin recovery across both operating segments.
5.
Automation margins: Predictability of Automation's earnings depends on whether its targeted 17% to 19% EBITA margin is driven by real pricing power or cyclical mix shifts. Jefferies observed on 13 August 2026 that while Vision Technologies saw 25% earnings growth, Automated Machinery EBITA fell 21% due to delayed tire-builder orders. Thus, understanding VMI's aftermarket and service pricing resilience is essential to model long-term profit stability during capital expenditure freezes.

Research Scope and Contrary Evidence

6.
Research scope: A comprehensive investigation of 46 distinct broker, expert, and company documents was conducted to analyze TKH's pricing dynamics across 10 named peers and framework customers. The research analyzed pricing terms under major contracts, including the €650 million Alliander framework agreement signed in March 2026. This multi-angle dataset is sufficient to evaluate the core hypothesis because it provides detailed margin transparency through past destocking cycles.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some segment risks remain underappreciated by investors who expect an immediate valuation re-rating after the corporate split. Jefferies noted on 12 May 2026 that the standalone Electrification business is highly asset-intense and carries substantial execution risk, which historically depressed returns. Consequently, the separation might not fully unlock the expected value unless the new subsea cable capacity meets its high-teens EBITA targets.
8.
Other side of the story: Opposing analysts argue that TKH's pricing power is highly durable and structurally protected rather than a transient scarcity rent. Jefferies highlights that 75% of TKH's revenues are linked to sustainable development goals, which are backed by long-term framework contracts. This contractual backing means that earnings per share should remain highly resilient, allowing the company to maintain robust midterm profitability even as peer capacity increases after 2027.

Section 2 – Electrification: Cable Capacity Shortage, Scarcity Rents, and Repricing

Section summary
  1. The standalone Electrification business is transitioning from Eemshaven startup challenges to high-yield execution, underpinned by an index-linked order book and multi-year utility frameworks.
  2. TKH Group's proprietary dry-design cable technology has secured an eighty percent tender win rate, shielding offshore margins from commodity volatility.
  3. Structural grid reinforcement and high-voltage undersupply set a highly profitable pricing context, though looming peer capacity additions present mid-term normalization risks.

Frameworks

Framework Contracts and Indexation Mechanics

1.
Secured revenue: TKH Electrification holds a strong onshore pipeline with signed framework agreements totaling €1.4 billion, which are entirely excluded from its H1 2026 order book of €507.8 million. This pipeline includes an eight-year, €650 million framework contract with Dutch distribution operator Alliander signed in March 2026. These long-term distribution agreements provide a highly visible revenue foundation that helps the company secure stable mid-term earnings growth even during temporary economic slowdowns.
2.
Contract protection: Under Dutch distribution frameworks, price risk is mitigated because contracts utilize raw material indexation clauses to pass through fluctuating metal costs. For instance, the Alliander framework covers raw copper price changes with structured pass-through terms to protect margins against inflation. This protection is vital because copper prices surged in 2026, meaning that standard pricing without indexation would materially depress segment operating margins if pass-through terms had long lags.
3.
Offshore indexation: TKH's subsea contracts, including the Gennaker and Zeevonk projects, utilize comprehensive indexation to manage energy inflation and secondary raw material costs. The Gennaker contract, signed in February 2026 to supply 140 kilometers of subsea cables, features escalation clauses designed to offset rising fabrication costs. These indexation mechanisms ensure that the segment can maintain robust contract profitability profiles unless extreme supply-chain bottlenecks prevent timely factory execution.

Subsea Tender Dominance and Dry-Design Economics

4.
Differentiated technology: TKH achieved a win rate of over 80% in offshore inter-array tenders participated in between 2024 and 2026 due to its innovative dry-design subsea cable. This environmentally friendly technology replaces bitumen and lead with extruded polymer, which ensures absolute watertightness while simplifying marine handling. Developers prefer this design because it lowers installation risks, allowing developers to achieve substantial installation cost savings during challenging maritime projects.
5.
Winter installation: The unique dry-design concept allows subsea inter-array cables to be safely handled and laid even during harsh winter months. TKH demonstrated this competitive edge during a German tender where it was the sole participant because competing semi-wet cables required warmer waters. This technological capability translates into superior pricing power, which allows TKH to command premium subsea pricing terms compared to peers offering standard wet cable designs.
6.
Project execution: High pricing power is evident in key offshore wins, such as the 162-kilometer Zeevonk project signed with Vattenfall in May 2026. TKH has secured visible subsea utilization through 2029 because Boskalis will install the Zeevonk inter-array system using the state-of-the-art BOKA Ocean vessel. This high asset load will drive significant segment margin expansion because underutilization previously depressed Eemshaven profitability during 2025.
7.
Contrary evidence: Eemshaven output challenges previously severely depressed subsea margins during 2025 due to technical issues in the critical production processes. TKH reported that Adjusted EBITA in the Electrification segment fell organically by 44.6% to €17.0 million in 2025 despite revenue growing organically by 15.5%. This margin contraction shows that proprietary technology cannot shield earnings from startup delays and asset underutilization.

Comparative Margins and Peer Benchmark

8.
Peer performance: Prysmian achieved an outstanding Renewable Transmission EBITDA margin of 21.2% in the second quarter of 2026, driven by excellent project execution and favorable mix. Similarly, NKT saw its Transmission EBITDA margin jump to 20% in the second quarter of 2026 as it executed high-margin backlog contracts. These peer results set a highly supportive pricing context, indicating that TKH has ample room to expand margins toward its standalone target of over 19% by 2028.
9.
Transmission contrast: While transmission giants focus on extra-high-voltage subsea export links, TKH focuses on inter-array subsea and medium-voltage onshore cables. Jefferies reported on 31 August 2026 that Nexans face elevated uncertainty in Transmission due to geopolitical risk on key projects. TKH faces less lumpy revenue progression because its focus on inter-array cables allows it to capture recurring offshore network spend from regional wind farm developers.

Onshore Destocking Recovery and Repricing Frameworks

10.
Onshore recovery: Onshore Energy revenues recovered strongly in H1 2026, growing by 26.4% to €187.0 million compared to H1 2025. This growth was driven by network roll-out efficiency improvements in the Netherlands after regulators removed several planning constraints. This structural volume recovery has effectively repriced the onshore book because TKH is delivering on newer, higher-priced framework contracts that offset historical low-margin volumes.

g Key financial figures for TKH Electrification segment in H1 2026

11.
Onshore destocking: The positive repricing follows a severe 2024 onshore destocking cycle where Dutch grid operators deferred orders due to permitting delays. During this downturn, underutilization at the Lochem facility severely impacted margins because fixed costs could not be absorbed. This historical precedent suggests that onshore pricing power remains highly sensitive to grid investment timelines unless operators commit to minimum off-take volumes.
12.
Customer terms: Grid operators including TenneT and Alliander are accelerating onshore medium-voltage and high-voltage network reinforcements to address grid congestion. TKH holds a market share of approximately 30% to 35% in Dutch medium-voltage distribution cables, giving it strong commercial leverage. This leading local market position means that TKH can negotiate favorable indexation and payment terms with municipal utilities during network expansions.

Scarcity Rents vs Sticky Backlogs in the Mid-Term

13.
Scarcity pricing: Current high cable margins partly represent temporary scarcity rents driven by an acute industry- wide high-voltage and subsea cable shortage. DNB Carnegie calculated in August 2026 that average revenue per kilometer of cable on 525kV HVDC contracts doubled to €1.44m since 2019. This price surge indicates that the entire cable sector has benefited from a favorable supply-demand mismatch that supports elevated margins.
14.
Capacity threat: This favorable mismatch may ease after 2027 as substantial peer capacity expansions come online across Europe. NKT is expanding its Karlskrona facility with commercial operations scheduled to commence slightly ahead of plan in Q2 2027. Morgan Stanley argues on 27 January 2025 that the arrival of new capacity means subsea players could face increased price competition for tenders if offshore wind development slows down.
15.
Sticky backlog: Despite peer expansions, TKH's subsea earnings should remain protected because its order backlog is heavily contracted under long-term frameworks. The exclusive eight-year inter-array framework with Vattenfall ensures that Eemshaven capacity will remain well-utilized through the decade. This contract structure suggests that TKH's margins represent durable contract backlog value rather than transient scarcity rents that vanish when peer factories expand.
16.
Other side of the story: Skeptical analysts argue that the planned Electrification standalone EBITDA margin target of over 19% by 2028 is overly optimistic because peer transmission players carry larger scale advantages. Morgan Stanley suggests on 27 January 2025 that if major subsea export cable manufacturers aggressively bid for smaller inter-array projects, TKH could experience severe price erosion in subsea markets. Therefore, standalone profitability remains highly dependent on whether offshore wind demand accelerates fast enough to absorb all upcoming peer capacity.

UNIXX Platform Pricing and Tier Acceptance

5.
Premium pricing: The premium UNIXX platform commands a price of approximately €10 million to €12 million per full system. This advanced technology is priced two to three times higher than a standard MAXX machine. Management explained that this premium pricing allows tire manufacturers to consolidate multiple component assembly steps into a single system.
6.
Tier adoption: Tier 1 manufacturers delayed orders due to economic strain but Tier 2 and Tier 3 builders maintained stable demand. To drive broader penetration TKH successfully delivered its first full UNIXX platform to a launch customer in 2025. This successful implementation quickly triggered repeat commercial orders from early adopters.
7.
Platform expansion: VMI extended its proprietary technology by introducing the UNIXX Moto platform to assemble radial motorcycle tires. This strategic expansion allows VMI to target high-value niche segments beyond standard passenger vehicle tires. The company remains confident that technological superiority will continue to support premium machine pricing.
8.
Production efficiency: The UNIXX platform allows tire components to be manufactured directly in the assembly process, lowering raw material waste. This next-generation platform provides unprecedented production flexibility to handle smaller batch sizes at lower operating costs. VMI leverages this superior performance to justify the system's high price over competing machines.

Undercycle Resilience and Aftermarket Services

9.
Service buffers: Highly recurring service and aftermarket revenues accounted for 18% of Automated Machinery turnover in 2025. This recurring component helps stabilize total segment margins during cyclical downturns. VMI leverages its global network of sales hubs to provide high-margin spare parts and performance upgrades.
10.
Retrofit economics: Tire manufacturers heavily request retrofit and upgrade packages when new machine capital budgets are frozen. This customer behavior ensures that VMI can maintain high factory utilization without discounting its core machines. Consequently earnings remain protected because software integration and hardware upgrades yield premium margins.
11.
Expanding hubs: VMI is actively expanding its global network of service and sales hubs to secure a larger aftermarket footprint. This geographic expansion allows the division to capture highly lucrative recurring service revenues from existing equipment. Consequently, VMI's aftermarket business acts as a strong buffer against the cyclicality of new machine sales.

The group expects this increased focus on proprietary software to structurally improve Automated Machinery returns.
