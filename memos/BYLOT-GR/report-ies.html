Ballys Intralot (BYLOT-GR): what the nine-month results do to the case

These results weaken the existing case modestly: they confirm its demand side, close off its one route to an early entry, and press on its standalone earnings base and its leverage assumptions, without touching the Evoke judgement on which most of the value rests.

1. What changed

  • Timing. Preliminary nine-month results were published on 10 November 2026. The case had dated the event at 27 November. A fuller third-quarter release is referenced with a date of 26 November, so the print is in hand and the guidance conversation may still be ahead.
  • Third-quarter profit. Group revenue EUR 286.1m and adjusted EBITDA EUR 88.9m, a 31.1% margin (36.5% a year earlier on the old perimeter). Nine months: revenue EUR 830.3m, adjusted EBITDA EUR 273.6m, margin 33.0%. The case's markers for a print that would pull the entry forward were a quarter above EUR 97m, UK growth above 12% and a BII margin near 37%.
  • UK demand. UK constant-currency online revenue grew 12.2% in the third quarter, from 11.6% in the second, with record net gaming revenue on lower marketing. That is above the case's 12% marker. One caution: this figure is attributed to the later-dated release, and a second figure of 11.4% for UK online growth appears without a clear period. Spain online grew 18.9% to EUR 13.3m.
  • BII margin. BII contributed EUR 579.2m of revenue and EUR 195.6m of adjusted EBITDA over nine months, a 33.8% margin. Set against a first-half figure of EUR 377.6m at 35.2%, our arithmetic puts third-quarter BII revenue near EUR 202m and its margin near 31%. That is derived, and rests on the first-half figure describing the same segment. The case assumed 36-37% for the second half.
  • Duty mitigation. Gross duty impact EUR 68.0m over nine months, of which 48.5% was offset. Management had described about two-thirds mitigated in the first duty quarter. Either the third-quarter rate was well below that, or the measure has been redefined again.
  • Legacy. Adjusted EBITDA EUR 78.1m over nine months, down 13.4%, margin 31.1%. That is consistent with the case's legacy outlook.
  • Balance sheet. Adjusted net debt EUR 1.63bn at 30 September 2026 (EUR 1.62bn at June, EUR 1.49bn at end-2025), including the EUR 85.0m Victoria licence. Pro forma adjusted net leverage 4.33x on twelve-month pro forma adjusted EBITDA of EUR 376.7m. Liquidity EUR 472.4m, of which cash EUR 312.4m. The case was written on 4.05x.
  • Guidance. Full-year guidance of about EUR 422m now needs EUR 148.4m in the fourth quarter, 67% above the third. We have no evidence of what the company said about guidance on 10 November; the reaffirmations on record date from earlier in the year.
  • Evoke. Still expected to complete by the first quarter of 2027, subject to the Gibraltar court sanction and gaming approvals. Synergy target unchanged at GBP 180m.
  • Sell-side. The only response so far is NBG Securities, which keeps the company not rated with no target price. No estimate or target has moved: consensus target EUR 1.405, one-month EPS revision zero.
  • Price. EUR 1.118 on 10 November, against EUR 1.117 when the case was written. It did not react.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the margin is the new level, not a mitigation lag. The case holds that the duty takes the group margin to 35-36% and that volume is the open question. This quarter says the reverse may be true. Volume is intact at 12.2% growth, yet the group margin moved only from the case's 30.6% in the second quarter to 31.1% in the third, with a full quarter of mitigation running. If growth is being held by giving margin back, the flow-through on incremental BII revenue is below the case's 40-45%, and the case's standalone EBITDA of EUR 385m, EUR 400m and EUR 408m for FY2026 to FY2028 is too high even if the volume test is passed. The case's EUR 385m needs about EUR 111m in the fourth quarter, 25% above the third; the bottom of its EUR 375-398m range needs about EUR 101m. On the case's own gearing, each EUR 10m of EBITDA is about 4% of the share price. For this reading to be right, three things have to hold: the third quarter contains no one-off cost, the second-half generosity cuts are already in it, and the fall in the mitigation rate is real and not definitional. The case weighs a volume loss with margin held. It does not weigh a margin shortfall with volume held.

Reading two: leverage rises through the denominator, so the covenant question is on the central path, not in the tail. Net debt was flat in the quarter and leverage still rose from 4.05x to 4.33x, because twelve-month EBITDA fell as a pre-duty quarter dropped out. Two more pre-duty quarters have still to drop out. It is probable that leverage on this basis rises again at December and March with no change in debt, before Evoke, which Deutsche Bank puts at 4.50x pro forma. The case's base is covenants passed and no issuance, with an amendment or an equity raise as a tail that timing can avoid. Against the facilities test the case cites at 4.75x, headroom is now about 0.4x and narrowing mechanically. If the tested definition is close to the reported one, a negotiation with lenders is plausible in the same months as the Evoke completion, which is the window the case names for entry. The strongest objection is the cash: EUR 312.4m covers the small Greek retail bond the case flags, so that instrument is manageable. The facilities test is the one that matters, and its definition is unknown.

Reading three, more tentative: the standalone rebase is not a catalyst at all. A print that makes EUR 422m arithmetically remote left the price unchanged. It is possible that nobody holding the shares believes the guidance, and that price formation is entirely about Evoke and flows. If so, the case's first entry condition, rebased standalone numbers that can be beaten, describes an event the market has already discounted, and waiting for it buys no protection. For this to be right, the eventual cut has to pass without a price reaction. The case's own flow argument supports the premise but draws the opposite conclusion from it.

3. What it does to the case

  • Case Riskiness (13) and topic 9 (15): more pressure. Leverage of 4.33x is further from every threshold the case cites. Both already sit at F.
  • Topic 12, total shareholder return (C, 52): more pressure, and the closest to moving. The case's probability-weighted return of about +13% sits three points above the floor of its band. A standalone base EUR 5-15m lower and year-end net debt above the case's EUR 1,550m would take most of that.
  • Topic 6, forecast financials (B, 75): more pressure on the 35-36% margin path. The growth rate itself is not tested.
  • Topic 4, guidance (F, 20): more pressure, same grade. The gap between guidance and arithmetic widened. The case's 65% probability of a cut or reframe is unresolved, not disproved.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (52): slightly more pressure. Topic 1 (D, 30) is the same: demand confirmed, cost confirmed.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (50): slightly more pressure. The pull-forward counter-case met one marker of three. The entry stays dated in the first half of 2027.
  • Foundations' Robustness (26), topics 7, 8, 10 and 11: the same. The technical momentum letter now reads D against the C the case quoted; relative strength is at the 37th percentile.
  • The case's lean that FY2026 skews slightly up: under pressure.
  • Price thresholds: none crossed. EUR 1.118 sits between the case's standalone value of EUR 0.90 and its half-synergy value of EUR 1.27, where it was.

4. What would settle it

  • The full third-quarter release and call, expected 26-27 November. This should carry the guidance language, BII margin by quarter, mitigation by lever and UK actives. It is the test of reading one. A divisional reframing counts as a cut on the case's terms.
  • The second review, four working days on, around 16-17 November. It carries whatever brokers publish after the preliminary print: estimate cuts, target changes, any view on the fourth-quarter requirement. It probably does not carry the full release or the call, which fall later, and it cannot carry fourth-quarter trading. If only NBG has written by then, it adds little.
  • FY2026 results, March-April 2027. The fourth-quarter actual, year-end leverage, and whether the FY2027 target is shown standalone.
  • The covenant definitions. The company has not committed to disclosing which EBITDA and which perimeter are tested. The case expects them in the FY2026 annual report. Until then reading two cannot be closed either way.
  • The first-quarter 2027 trading update. UK volume against record comparatives. Unchanged from the case.
  • Reading three settles only on the day guidance is cut, by what the price does.
