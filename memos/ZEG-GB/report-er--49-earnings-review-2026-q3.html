Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Zegona's proven turnaround strategy transformed Vodafone Spain, driving operational recovery and massive cash flow growth that re-rated the stock ahead of FY26 results.
1.
Strategic TMT vehicle: Zegona Communications is a London Stock Exchange-listed investment company founded in 2015 by former Virgin Media executives Eamonn O'Hare and Robert Samuelson. The company operates a disciplined "buy-fix-sell" strategy focused on underperforming European telecommunications, media, and technology assets.
2.
Proven Spanish track record: Zegona's initial investment cycle in Spain involved acquiring regional cable operator Telecable for €640m in 2015, selling it to Euskaltel in 2017, and subsequently taking a 21% controlling stake in Euskaltel. Zegona engineered Euskaltel's operational turnaround and national expansion before selling the business to MásMóvil in July 2021 for €3.5bn, delivering an 87% return on net invested capital.

4.
Market position in Spain: Vodafone Spain represents Zegona's sole operating asset and positions the group as the third-largest integrated telecommunications operator in Spain. The business provides fixed broadband, mobile, pay-TV, and B2B services to consumer and corporate clients across Spain.
5.
Multi-brand commercial strategy: The company operates a dual-brand retail model, utilizing the premium Vodafone brand for high-ARPU convergent offerings and Lowi for the fast-growing value segment. This structure allows Zegona to address the entire customer spectrum while defending market share against low-cost challengers.
6.
Operational turnaround trajectory: Following the acquisition, management implemented over 700 transformation initiatives, including a 28% headcount reduction, IT system consolidation, and contract renegotiations. Operating cash flow expanded from ~€400m at acquisition to €625m in FY25 and reached €763m for fiscal year 2026.
7.
Fundamental financial delivery: Over the three-year comparative period, reported revenues stabilized at €3,628m in FY26 compared to €3,629m in FY25, halting years of top-line decline. EBITDAaL expanded 7.4% year-over-year to €1,341m in FY26, lifting EBITDAaL margins to nearly 35% from 34% in FY25.

Absolute share price performance: Zegona's share price staged an exceptional multi-year rally, rising over 164% in the 12 months to June 2025 as transaction execution progressed. 10.
Short-term price volatility: Despite strong long-term gains, Zegona's stock experienced temporary pullback pressure in spring 2026. Sector valuation discount: Prior to results, Zegona traded at an attractive valuation relative to European peers. Morgan Stanley's Harrison Williams highlighted that Zegona traded at 5.8x EV/EBITDAaL versus the European sector average of 7.0x–11.0x, offering a sector-leading FCFE yield of nearly 12%.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

  1. FibreCo monetization and aggressive debt refinancing unlocked massive capital returns while derisking Zegona's balance sheet.
  2. Operating cash flow expanded dramatically toward mid-term guidance as cost transformation initiatives took effect.
  3. Disruptive competition from Digi Spain and tower infrastructure renewal renegotiations remain critical variables for medium-term earnings growth.

Board governance expansion: Tim Pennington was appointed as a non-executive director in March 2026 to transition into the Audit and Risk Committee Chair role. A dedicated Executive Risk Committee was also established to enhance cross-functional oversight of enterprise risks.

Transformational fibre monetization: Zegona completed two major FibreCo deals delivering €1.8bn in combined upfront proceeds. These transactions comprised the PremiumFiber joint venture with MasOrange and GIC yielding €1.4bn, and the FiberPass joint venture with Telefonica and AXA generating €0.4bn.

Strategic M&A speculation: Press reports indicated preliminary interest from Telefonica regarding a potential acquisition of Vodafone Spain. Deutsche Bank's Keval Khiroya noted that while M&A activity faces high regulatory barriers, potential network-sharing deals represent a natural catalyst.

Margin expansion delivery: Group EBITDAaL increased 7.4% year-over-year to €1,341m in FY26. Operating efficiencies lifted EBITDAaL margins to nearly 35%, up three percentage points over two years.
6.
Accelerating cash generation: Operating cash flow surged 22% year-over-year to €763m in FY26. Cash conversion nearly doubled from approximately €400m at the time of transaction closing in 2024.
7.
Substantial interest savings: In June 2026, Zegona successfully refinanced its €3.7bn senior debt facilities, extending maturities beyond five years. The refinancing reduced annual debt servicing costs by approximately €60m to a run-rate of €170m.

Massive initial capital return: Zegona returned €1.6bn to shareholders during FY26. Distributions included a €1.4bn special dividend that funded the redemption of Vodafone Group's preference shares and a €200m share repurchase program.

Sustainable capital allocation framework: Management announced a formal capital allocation framework in September 2026 delivering an additional €400m shareholder payout. The policy includes a €200m ordinary dividend and a €200m share buyback program.

Deleveraging milestone achieved: Consolidated net debt to EBITDAaL declined from 3.1x at acquisition to 2.4x by March 2026. Leverage further improved to 2.33x by June 2026, aligning with management's target range of 2.25x to 2.75x.

Revenue stabilization confirmed: Consolidated revenue stabilized at €3,628m in FY26 compared to €3,629m in FY25. Top-line growth turned positive in the second half of FY26, rising 1.1% in Q3 and 2.0% in Q4.

This expanded footprint provides Zegona with the alternative physical sites necessary to execute its migration strategy away from Vantage.

Zegona and MasOrange have resumed negotiations to combine their radio access network assets into a dedicated, co-owned network company.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Zegona Communications delivered a pivotal operational turnaround in fiscal year 2026, transforming Vodafone Spain from a structurally declining asset into a highly cash-generative platform that reached €763 million in operating cash flow while stabilizing revenues at €3,628 million and expanding EBITDAaL margins to nearly 35%. The company's execution of its disciplined buy-fix-sell strategy has fundamentally reshaped both its financial profile and strategic positioning within the Spanish telecommunications market, setting the stage for sustained shareholder value creation over the next 18 to 36 months.

The most important change driving Zegona's medium-term earnings outlook has been the successful monetization of €1.8 billion in FibreCo assets through the PremiumFiber and FiberPass joint ventures, which unlocked massive capital returns while simultaneously reducing annual debt servicing costs by approximately €60 million to a run-rate of €170 million following the comprehensive refinancing. This structural transformation lifted EBITDAaL margins by three percentage points to nearly 35% and positioned management to target medium-term margins approaching 40%.

The combination of aggressive cost restructuring, balance sheet deleveraging, and formal capital allocation frameworks has created a powerful earnings trajectory that should support double-digit equity free cash flow growth through fiscal year 2029, underpinning the analyst community's conviction that Zegona trades at a compelling valuation discount to European peers despite offering sector-leading free cash flow yields.

The strongest counter-case to Zegona's bullish medium-term outlook centers on the relentless competitive threat posed by Digi Spain, whose aggressive fiber-to-the-home rollout and low price points threaten average revenue per user across the Spanish telecommunications market. Skeptics argue that Zegona's historical share price outperformance reflects one-off financial engineering and cost-cutting that cannot mask underlying structural market share losses to Digi in Spanish fixed broadband.

The removal of aggressive promotional tariffs such as Lowi's Fibra Fit discount coincided with Zegona's total reliance on third-party network access, meaning it now incurs wholesale FTTH fees across its entire retail customer base. The dramatic margin expansion has been heavily reliant on one-off headcount reductions and network lease renegotiations, meaning that underlying customer churn on legacy networks could quickly reverse profitability gains if competitor Digi continues its aggressive pricing.

If Digi's successful July 2026 initial public offering provides the low-cost challenger with cheap funding to accelerate its proprietary fiber expansion beyond its current 14% fixed broadband market share, sustained subscriber migration could undermine Zegona's ambitious target of generating over €1 billion in medium-term operating cash flow. The key factor that would change this investment view involves clarity on Zegona's ability to sustain positive net subscriber additions without sacrificing pricing discipline over the next six to eight quarters.

While Zegona reversed years of historical customer losses by delivering 28,000 broadband net additions and 128,000 contract mobile net additions in fiscal year 2026, expanding the base to 2,591,000 broadband lines and 10,185,000 contract mobile lines the quality and stickiness of these additions remain critical variables. Recent subscriber gains have been driven by lower-quality prepaid and eSIM volume rather than high-ARPU convergent accounts, meaning management's optimistic narrative obscured ongoing core market share losses in high-value fixed broadband segments to low-cost rivals.

If quarterly subscriber momentum reverses or if churn rates accelerate among core convergent customers as promotional pricing rolls off, the entire narrative surrounding

Zegona's operational turnaround would be called into question. Conversely, if Zegona demonstrates consistent broadband net additions while maintaining or expanding blended ARPU through tactical repricing initiatives, confidence in the medium-term cash flow trajectory would strengthen materially. A non-obvious reading of the current strategic landscape reveals that Zegona's aggressive renegotiation of tower infrastructure costs represents a highly material yet underappreciated earnings lever, as management actively challenged its annual €150 million lease payments to Vantage Towers, demanding a 50% discount and indicating a willingness to migrate sites to alternative tower providers when the contract expires in 2028.

Sell-side analysts estimate that successfully forcing a renegotiation or executing a migration away from Vantage Towers could unlock approximately €60 million in annual savings, representing roughly 10% of Zegona's equity free cash flow, though upfront transition costs and physical complexity of moving tower infrastructure remain important variables. Beyond tower savings, the creation of a mobile network sharing joint venture with MasOrange covering urban radio access network assets could deliver Zegona's share of synergies reaching €100 million to €150 million annually, with Deutsche Bank calculating that executing this mobile network sharing agreement could unlock up to 70% valuation upside.

These supply-side restructuring opportunities are receiving insufficient attention relative to headline subscriber metrics, yet they represent transformational margin expansion pathways that could fundamentally re-rate Zegona's equity valuation if executed successfully over the next 24 to 36 months. The two most critical upcoming disclosures that will shape Zegona's earnings trajectory over the next 18 months involve first, the outcome of ongoing Radio Access Network Company negotiations with MasOrange, which would clarify the magnitude and timing of potential mobile infrastructure synergies, and second, the resolution of tower lease disputes with Vantage Towers ahead of the November 2028 contract expiry.

Management's aggressive behavioral focus was highlighted in expert interviews revealing that Zegona was demanding a 40% discount from Vantage Towers and threatening to move antennas to alternative providers if lease prices were not renegotiated. Skeptical experts caution that public threats to leave Vantage Towers are primarily a negotiating tactic, as reverting to a secondary tower network would be highly complex, slow, and capital-intensive, with Cellnex believing that migrating off Vantage's grid would reveal a physical site shortfall of approximately 3,000 towers and face massive switching costs of €50,000 per point of presence to dismantle and relocate active radio equipment.

Clarity on tower economics will determine whether Zegona can achieve its medium-term operating cash flow targets, while progress on mobile network sharing will dictate whether the company can sustain margin expansion beyond the initial restructuring phase. These infrastructure negotiations represent binary outcomes that could either validate management's ambitious medium-term guidance or expose structural limitations in Zegona's ability to compete effectively against both premium incumbent Telefonica and disruptive low-cost challenger Digi Spain within an increasingly bifurcated Spanish telecommunications landscape.
