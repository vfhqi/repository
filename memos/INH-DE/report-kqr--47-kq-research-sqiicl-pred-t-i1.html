2.
Severe EPS sensitivity: Fluctuations in tungsten pricing and macro demand directly drive INDUS's consolidated profitability. Because subsidiary BETEK enjoyed a temporary special situation with high-margin windfall profits in early 2026, any price drop could expose the company to margin risk. Therefore, tracking whether guidance is met is critical to evaluating if normalized earnings per share will drop back to historical baselines.
3.
Guidance revision patterns: INDUS's historical guidance record shows frequent adjustments that can significantly impact investor sentiment and market expectations. Section 2 – FY2026 Guidance and the Implied H2 Run-Rate

Section summary
  1. INDUS Holding AG's raised FY2026 guidance appears highly achievable but implies a significant profitability slowdown in the second half of the year.
  2. Strategic inventory investments in tungsten carbide have temporarily weighed on cash generation despite record-breaking segment margins in H1 2026.
  3. Historical precedent reveals a highly cyclical forecast pattern where initial targets are set conservatively and subsequently revised upward during periods of operational delivery.

Implied H2 Performance Fade Strong

1.
H1 delivery: INDUS delivered robust results in the first half of 2026, recording revenue of EUR 965.2m and adjusted EBITA of EUR 123.5m. This performance represents a significant increase compared to H1 2025, which saw revenue of EUR 836.6m and adjusted EBITA of EUR 56.1m. Consequently, these actual figures provide a solid foundation for the year, demonstrating excellent momentum.
2.
Implied H2 deceleration: Consolidated guidance of EUR 1.90 to 2.10bn in revenue and EUR 220 to 260m in adjusted EBITA implies an abrupt slowdown in the second half. This guidance translates to an implied H2 2026 adjusted EBITA range of EUR 96.5m to 136.5m (calculated), which is notably lower than the H1 run-rate of EUR 123.5m. Although this guided range remains above the H2 2025 adjusted EBITA of approximately EUR 91.7m (calculated), it indicates a clear normalization of extraordinary earnings over the coming quarters.
3.
Upper range probability: Strong incoming orders support the high probability of INDUS landing in the upper half of its consolidated guidance. Group incoming orders surged 22.5% to EUR 1,104.0m in the first half of 2026, leading to a healthy book-to-bill ratio of 1.14. Because of this sustained order momentum, the company appears well-positioned to hit peak targets unless industrial demand collapses.

Solutions normalisation: Materials Solutions drove the bulk of H1 outperformance but faces an imminent margin contraction. The segment achieved a record adjusted EBITA margin of 22.4% in H1 2026, yet full-year segment guidance is capped at 15.0% to 17.0%. This target implies that margins will drop significantly in H2 2026 because rising raw material procurement costs will dilute BETEK's temporary windfall profits.
5.
Peer machining benchmark: Swedish peer Sandvik AB reported its Q3 2026 results on 22 October 2026, highlighting similar margin dynamics. Sandvik observed that machining margins and tungsten timing effects are starting to stabilize as European spot prices level off. This read-across confirms that INDUS's exceptionally high material margins are unsustainable, aligning with management's guided H2 performance fade.
6.
German industrial comparison: Holding peer Gesco SE's 2026 results reflect the challenging baseline of the German domestic economy. Gesco struggled with persistent customer reluctance and volume declines in its capital-goods business, which aligns with INDUS's Engineering performance. The comparative weakness in Gesco's portfolio highlights that external macroeconomic headwinds remain severe, proving that INDUS's core engineering business cannot easily offset Materials Solutions' normalisation.

These revisions reveal that management is highly sensitive to macro shifts, making frequent guidance adjustments a regular feature of its corporate reporting cycle.

Profit targets were first increased on 30 April 2026 and then raised again on 29 July 2026, lifting consolidated adjusted EBITA targets to EUR 220 to 260m. This rapid series of upgrades confirms management's habit of setting initial ranges defensively before raising them upon solid delivery.

Working capital rose to EUR 627.0m in H1 2026 from EUR 461.5m at year-end 2025, driven by seasonal patterns and high tungsten prices. Management remains confident that the group will achieve its target of at least break-even free cash flow by the end of the year.

Contrary evidence: Although management guides for an H2 margin fade, some evidence suggests that demand remains exceptionally resilient. Consolidated order backlog reached record highs in multiple segments, and engineering book-to-bill stood at 1.22x in H1 2026. These metrics suggest that the guided performance slowdown may not materialize as severely as management predicts.

Section 3 – Materials Solutions Segment and the Tungsten Pricing Dynamics

Section summary
  1. The Materials Solutions segment's record H1 profitability faces normalisation as raw material costs catch up with prior price increases.
  2. Global tungsten markets are structurally bifurcating due to tightening Western defense procurement rules and Chinese export controls.
  3. Direct peer comparisons confirm that vertically integrated producers can maintain superior margin baselines amid price volatility.

Segment Margins and BETEK Focus

1.
Targeted segment profitability: INDUS Holding's management raised its adjusted EBITA margin guidance for Materials Solutions to 15.0% to 17.0% in its ad-hoc announcement of 29 July 2026. This updated range exceeds the previous guidance of 8.0% to 10.0% due to the segment's record-breaking performance in the first half of the year. We believe achieving this target depends heavily on whether BETEK can sustain highmargin orders through the end of the year, which is supported by the same July 2026 release.
2.
Inventory investment strategy: In its interim report of 12 August 2026, INDUS Holding noted that BETEK made a strategic decision to build up tungsten inventories. This proactive raw material sourcing tied up substantial liquidity, which drove negative free cash flow to EUR -36.9 million in the first half. However, we analyze that this move successfully gained market share for BETEK as smaller, unbacked competitors withdrew, as noted in the interim report.
3.
Materials segment revenue: According to INDUS Holding's H1 report of 12 August 2026, segment revenue surged 32.4% year-on-year to EUR 376.6 million. This massive increase was driven predominantly by severe price hikes for tungsten carbide feedstock rather than volume expansion. We assess that these windfall profits will subside unless extreme pricing pressures persist in H2, in line with management's cautious outlook.
4.
Contrary evidence: In its update of 30 July 2026, NuWays argued that the segment's extraordinary performance is highly unsustainable. The broker calculated that approximately EUR 50 million to EUR 60 million of Q2 revenue represented positive one-off effects. This means the segment's underlying profitability might fall below the guided range once raw material pricing normalizes, according to NuWays' analysis.

Global Tungsten Pricing Dislocation

5.
Chinese pricing relaxation: Shanghai Metals Market reported in October 2026 that Chinese domestic tungsten prices have started to soften. This relaxation contrasts sharply with the massive rally in early 2026, when Chinese export controls first disrupted global markets. We believe this domestic easing indicates that Chinese supply pressures are moderating locally despite tight Western conditions, which is supported by SooChow Securities' report of 18 October 2026.
6.
Western price premium: An Agence France-Presse report of October 2026 confirmed that Western tungsten prices remain highly elevated. Chinese export licensing introduced in mid-2025 has successfully restricted global supply, forcing international manufacturers to seek alternative origins. We analyze that this persistent dislocation penalizes non-integrated European fabricators who must search for alternative sources, as described by the AFP news agency.
7.
Futures market introduction: To address the severe pricing opacity, CME Group announced on 19 October 2026 that it will launch a dedicated tungsten futures market. Trading is set to begin by early Q1 2027, with settlement based on Fastmarkets assessments. We judge that this new financial instrument will enhance price transparency for buyers like BETEK once active trading commences.

Geopolitical Regulations and Sangdong Mine

8.
Defense procurement restrictions: A press release from Western Star Resources on 7 October 2026 highlighted that U.S. defense procurement rules will restrict Chinese tungsten. Effective 1 January 2027, the DFARS clause 252.225-7052 prohibits the acquisition of Chinese-origin material. We analyze that this regulation accelerates the reshoring of supply to certified Western mines, which is supported by the same press release.
9.
Provenance verification needs: Guardian Metal Resources announced on 10 September 2026 a collaboration with Oritain to build a forensic database. This initiative aims to verify the absolute origin of tungsten from the mine to the finished product. We judge that traceable and certified raw materials will command significant pricing premiums, as highlighted by Guardian Metal Resources.
10.
Sangdong mine restart: Almonty Industries announced on 14 September 2026 that its flagship Sangdong mine in South Korea has officially restarted. This world-class asset is projected to supply up to 8% of global tungsten demand once Phase II is complete. We analyze that Sangdong represents a critical alternative for Western buyers seeking to bypass Chinese export licensing, as supported by Almonty's filing.
11.
Supply offtake details: Diamond Equity Research noted on 21 October 2026 that Almonty secured a 21-year offtake agreement with Global Tungsten & Powders. This contract guarantees a floor price of US$ 183 per metric tonne unit, significantly de-risking the mine's operations. We assess that this long-term deal locks in vital non-China feedstocks for premium European system partners, according to Diamond Equity Research's note.

Peer Benchmarks and Read-Across

12.
Sandvik margin resilience: Swedish peer Sandvik reported its Q3 2026 results on 22 October 2026, achieving a strong 25% underlying machining margin. Morgan Stanley's note on the same day highlighted that Sandvik's vertical integration insulated its operations from spot pricing shocks. This suggests that fully integrated manufacturers can preserve margins even if tungsten feedstock prices roll over, as Morgan Stanley argues.
13.
Kennametal pricing power: In its earnings call of 5 August 2026, Kennametal reported a record adjusted EBITDA margin of 26.9% for fiscal 2026. Jefferies Research argued on 12 October 2026 that Kennametal's fiscal 2027 guidance assumes stable tungsten and a mid-teens EBITDA margin exit. We analyze that this baseline proves toolmakers can successfully pass costs to their end customers, as supported by Kennametal's call.
14.
110 - Earnings Calls Ceratizit pricing intentions: Morgan Stanley reported on 19 October 2026 that Ceratizit has achieved a remarkable 91% tungsten recycling rate. Ceratizit's parent company holds a strategic alliance with Almonty's Sangdong mine to secure primary raw materials. We judge that these combined recycling and mining initiatives underpin stable pricing intentions across the European tool market, as Morgan Stanley argues.
15.
Other side of the story: While segment guidance seems secure, there is a risk that high tungsten prices will eventually trigger severe customer resistance. An expert from a leading metal-cutting tools manufacturer noted on 27 July 2026 that automotive clients are actively fighting price hikes. Therefore, if macro demand weakens, BETEK might fail to sustain premium pricing without sacrificing sales volumes, as the expert suggests.

In the second quarter alone, the adjusted EBITA margin expanded to 12.6%, up from 10.6% in the prior year. 16.

Consequently, the current industrial trough may prove to be a temporary pause before a major investment cycle.

Strategic acquisition criteria: INDUS Holding AG targets highly profitable medium-sized Mittelstand companies with annual revenues of EUR 20 million to EUR 100 million. According to company presentations, targeted companies must operate in attractive niche markets and demonstrate double-digit operating EBIT margins of at least 12%. Management focuses on identifying sustainable family successions to expand its technology fields. This disciplined selection process ensures that the consolidated group preserves its long-term operating margin stability through economic cycles.

Capital allocation framework: The long-term EMPOWERING MITTELSTAND corporate strategy outlines a clear roadmap to significantly expand the holding portfolio. Management announced plans to invest around EUR 500 million in acquisitions by 2030 to generate EUR 600 million in annual sales. This growth initiative is designed to be fully funded through operating cash flow and debt without requiring dilutive capital increases. Consequently, maximizing cash generation remains the key driver of shareholder dividends and strategic flexibility.
3.
Deal flow deceleration: NuWays reported that INDUS temporarily slowed its acquisition pace due to substantial capital requirements at BETEK. High tungsten inventory investments tied up consolidated cash reserves, forcing a short-term deceleration in transaction activity. However, the broker notes that with raw material pricing under control, management is refocusing on capital deployment. This strategic pivot is expected to accelerate inorganic earnings contributions heading into late 2026.
4.
Active portfolio management: INDUS completed the strategic divestment of hydraulic block manufacturer MEWESTA Hydraulik to the Lange Group on 5 October 2026. The subsidiary had been part of the Materials Solutions segment since 1997 and generated annual sales of approximately EUR 5 million. Management emphasized that selling non-core or slower-growing assets represents an active component of their corporate strategy. We judge that recycling capital from mature businesses will underpin funding for larger acquisitions in high-growth engineering fields.
