Post-Spin Capital Reset and Financing

1.
Stable capitalization: Hexagon emerged from the Octave spin-off with a structurally lighter balance sheet and a significantly reduced asset footprint. The company's total assets decreased to 10,468.0 MEUR primarily due to the separation of the software unit. Consequently, shareholders' equity declined to 6,031.7 MEUR but maintained a healthy 57.6% equity ratio. This reset provides Hexagon with substantial capacity for future acquisitions without overleveraging its balance sheet.
2.
Reduced leverage: Hexagon reported a substantial reduction in net debt to 488.7 MEUR after completing the distribution of Octave. This debt reduction resulted in an extremely low net debt to EBITDA ratio of 0.3 times. However, pro forma leverage is expected to increase to 1.4 times net debt to EBITDA following the consolidation of Waygate Technologies and other recent transactions. This increased leverage is projected to dilute near-term earnings per share through higher interest payments unless offset by operational synergies.
3.
Structured refinancing: Hexagon updated its medium-term note financing framework by applying for a prospectus approval on 4 September 2026. This Swedish MTN programme has a total limit of SEK 20bn or its euro equivalent and allows note maturities of up to 6 years. Skandinaviska Enskilda Banken acts as the lead bank for the note issuances under this framework. Normalizing debt maturities allows Hexagon to reduce its immediate refinancing risk during capital market fluctuations.
4.
Ample liquidity: Hexagon maintains high financial flexibility through its large cash balances and committed credit limits. The group reported cash and unutilised credit limits totaling 3,329.7 MEUR as of 30 September 2026. This liquidity pool is anchored by a multicurrency revolving credit facility of 1,500 MEUR with a tenor of 5+1+1 years. This strong liquidity position ensures that short-term refinancing needs are easily covered during market disruptions.

Debt Maturities, Taxation, and Incentives

5.
Disciplined maturity: Hexagon maintains strict internal governance rules to prevent liquidity shortages from short- term debt maturities. The group policy dictates that no more than 20% of its gross debt is allowed to mature within any succeeding 12-month period. Furthermore, Hexagon supports its capital structure with a 1,500 MEUR multicurrency revolving credit facility that runs until December 2028. This structured maturity framework stabilizes the group's interest costs as interest rates fluctuate.
6.
Rising tax: Hexagon's tax expenses are projected to move higher due to European tax reforms. The group's finance director guided for an increased tax rate of 19% to 21% going forward. This increase from the historical average of 18% is driven by the harmonization of Switzerland's minimum tax rate under the OECD Pillar 2 rules. This rising tax burden is expected to moderately compress the net income available for distribution.
7.
Stable equity: Hexagon's share capital is divided into 2,684,377,888 outstanding shares, comprising Series A and Series B shares. The company retains 21,100,000 Series B shares in treasury to cover future share-based incentive commitments. Management has not initiated active share repurchases under recent authorizations, using treasury shares solely as an incentive hedge. This static treasury balance means share buybacks will not contribute to EPS accretion in the near term.
8.
Dilutive compensation: Hexagon implemented the performance-based Share Programme 2026/2029 to align management incentives with long-term capital growth. This programme comprises a maximum of 4,442,657 Series B shares, representing approximately 0.2% of the company's total outstanding shares. The total cost of the programme is capped at an estimated 45 MEUR over the vesting period. If fully allocated, the new shares will slightly dilute basic earnings per share for existing equity holders.
