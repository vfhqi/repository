The company includes corporate cash interest within operating revenue, lifting the FY25 operating margin to 30% from a true underlying level of 23%, a practice that deviates from standard industry peer accounting presentation.

This accounting anomaly creates a misleading comparison framework against competitors such as AJ Bell, IntegraFin, and St. James's Place, who report adjusted operating profit metrics excluding such shareholder cash yields. The distortion is further amplified when considering that Quilter's adjusted EPS of 11.0 pence would be approximately 8% lower if gross corporate debt interest expenses were included above-the-line in a manner consistent with peer methodology.

This practice exposes the company to severe downside earnings sensitivity from central bank interest rate cuts, transforming what appears to be robust operating leverage into a structural vulnerability. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 10]

The fifth genuine anomaly relates to the explosive growth trajectory of Quilter's WealthSelect managed portfolio solutions, which surged 15% to £29.3 billion in H1 2026 while simultaneously capturing a 13% market share of the entire UK industry MPS asset base. This market share concentration represents a statistically significant outlier relative to the fragmented competitive landscape, where no other single provider commands comparable scale in the retail advised MPS segment.

The growth rate substantially exceeds both Quilter's overall platform AuMA expansion of 21% and peer platform competitors such as AJ Bell, whose net inflows of £4.2 billion were achieved through a more diversified product mix without comparable reliance on proprietary managed solutions. The strategic risk embedded in this outlier is the concentration of organic growth into a single lower-margin product architecture, creating a structural trade-off between flow leadership and revenue yield sustainability.

The WealthSelect dominance pattern suggests either a genuine competitive moat in multi-asset portfolio construction and distribution, or alternatively, an unsustainable advisory incentive structure that may attract regulatory scrutiny around product bias and client suitability.
