Section 2 – Grid Volume, Fixed-Cost Absorption, and Turnkey Delivery

Section summary
  1. Grid operator volume growth faces severe labor and permitting bottlenecks that constrain Alfen's near- term substation installation pace.
  2. Turnkey distribution solutions create a powerful reinforcing loop of deeper utility dependence and market share protection.
  3. Peers experience similar capacity constraints and long-term utility service loops that dictate margin profiles.
  4. Regulatory interventions represent critical catalysts to resolve execution backlogs and accelerate grid equipment rollout.

Grid Expansion and Downstream Bottlenecks

1.
Accelerated planning: Dutch grid operators Alliander, Enexis, and Stedin plan about 13% more substation installations annually to 2028 according to Jefferies on May 21, 2026. This aggressive expansion plan is designed to reinforce the distribution network and relieve acute grid congestion. This sustained volume growth is expected to provide Alfen with a strong baseline of future grid demand and support earnings.
2.
Delayed execution: Alfen reported in its February 2026 annual report that two of the three main Dutch grid operators scaled down their order quantities due to persistent labor shortages. Despite high underlying demand, the mismatch between planning and field execution severely hampers Alfen's volume growth. This bottleneck directly restricts Alfen's ability to achieve optimal fixed-cost absorption in its new production facility.
3.
Severe shortages: Netbeheer Nederland reported in its first-quarter 2025 progress update that grid operators require thousands of extra technical personnel to meet grid reinforcement targets. Alfen's grid operator clients face structural hurdles in expanding their installation crews in the near term. These labor shortages act as a powerful balancing loop on equipment sales by capping annual rollout speeds.
4.
Stricter regulation: Alfen noted in its May 2025 trading update that stricter government interpretations of nitrogen emission rules have prolonged project permit lead times. These prolonged timelines prevent grid operators from executing scheduled substation installations on time. The resulting backlogs mean Alfen's standard substation orders are subject to significant delivery delays that defer revenue recognition.

Regulatory Catalysts and Labor Dynamics

5.
Ambitious expansion: The Scaling Plan 2030 launched in November 2025 by Dutch grid operators and the government aims to add 5,000 grid technicians and accelerate grid infrastructure deployment. This collaborative plan is intended to resolve downstream execution bottlenecks and restore installation velocity. Success in this recruitment initiative would allow Alfen to rapidly convert its production capacity into realized revenues.
6.
Streamlined permitting: Alfen noted in its first-quarter 2026 report that Dutch regulatory support has been elevated with plans for a Crisis Act on grid congestion and standard rights of entry. These legislative reforms are designed to shorten appeal procedures and simplify permitting for stations. This regulatory acceleration is expected to catalyze substation orders after summer 2027 as project sites open up.
7.
Contrary evidence: Alfen's former sales engineer observed in April 2025 that even with streamlined permitting and extra technicians, the physical availability of land to place substations remains an unresolved bottleneck. Grid operators must negotiate individual site agreements with municipal authorities, which often takes several years. This spatial constraint means that regulatory permit relief may fail to accelerate grid rollout as fast as most financial models assume.
8.
Diminished absorption: Deutsche Bank reported on January 8, 2026, that lower substation volumes are likely to result in diminished fixed-cost absorption for Alfen. Although Alfen opened a new production facility in February 2024 to support higher grid volumes, actual utilization has been constrained by downstream delays. Operating with high overhead costs and low utilization pressures Alfen's adjusted EBITDA margin in the near term.

Turnkey Delivery and Operator Lock-In

9.
Accelerated execution: Alfen has pioneered a standardized turnkey transport distribution station approach that reduces installation times from more than a year to only four months, according to its annual report of February 12, 2025. Operating as the end-to-end main contractor, Alfen coordinates the entire process from groundwork to technical installation. This standardized method combines prefabricated concrete with pre-assembled switchgear to drastically simplify the grid expansion process for operators.
10.
Deepening partnership: Enexis partnered with Alfen to scale its transport distribution stations from 10 to approximately 120 per year using the new modular method, as announced on January 16, 2025. By taking on the main contractor role, Alfen embeds itself directly into Enexis' long-term planning. This deep service integration creates a reinforcing loop of operator dependence that secures Alfen's long-term market share.
11.
Growing contribution: Alfen reported during its third-quarter 2025 call on November 5, 2025, that turnkey transport distribution stations accounted for about 5% of its overall Smart Grid Solutions revenue. Management expects this high-voltage product-market combination to grow toward a high-single-digit share in 2026 as grid operator rollouts accelerate. This growing turnkey segment represents a vital driver of project revenue that offsets weaker private domain sales.
12.
Margin dilution: Alfen's third-quarter 2026 report published on November 4, 2026, showed that its Smart Grid Solutions gross margin decreased from 25.9% in Q3 2025 to 23.7% in Q3 2026. This decrease was primarily driven by the higher share of lower-margin grid operator products in the revenue mix. Standardized turnkey projects carry structurally lower gross margins than private projects because they involve extensive civil work and sub-contractor costs.

  2. Standardized product transitions and business charger mix shifts provide near-term stabilization to average selling prices and gross margins.
  3. Nordic peers Zaptec and Wallbox showcase resilient scale dynamics, while DC charging specialists suffer from capital preservation policies of operators.

This contraction was heavily driven by the phase-out of legacy products and continued competitive pressure in the residential segment. This top-line decline strains overall corporate fixed-cost absorption and limits near-term EPS expansion.

The company launched its new Eve Single Plus and Eve Double Plus chargers featuring vehicle-to-grid ready capabilities and smart load balancing. These innovative products are designed to recapture market share as the European network expands.
8.
Unified platform: Alfen expects to see the full financial benefits of its new digital platform and home charger models in fiscal year 2027. Management plans to migrate all chargers to a single unified software platform to lower production complexity. This operational transition is projected to improve segment profitability and restore positive top-line growth.

1.
Frequent executive turnover: Alfen has experienced significant management instability with four distinct finance heads leading the company since early 2024. The supervisory board mutually withdrew Boudewijn Tans' CFO nomination in April 2024 after a brief handover period due to a lack of cultural fit. Subsequently, Onno Krap served as interim and then permanent CFO before stepping down in July 2026. This persistent executive instability has severely disrupted corporate financial leadership during a critical operational transformation.
2.
Repeated guidance adjustments: Alfen has repeatedly lowered its financial forecasts between fiscal years 2023 and 2025 due to challenging market conditions. In May 2025, the company adjusted its full-year revenue outlook downward from its initial range of €445 mm to €505 mm to a lower bracket of €430 mm to €480 mm. This revision forced management to reduce its adjusted EBITDA margin targets to a range of 5% to 8%. This historical pattern of misses has eroded investor confidence in management's forecasting capabilities.
3.
Continued outlook narrowing: Alfen further tightened its guidance during 2026, reflecting persistent margin pressures in its core segments. Deutsche Bank reported in November 2026 that Alfen narrowed its full-year guidance to expect revenues in the upper half of its €435 mm to €475 mm range. However, the broker noted that this adjustment implies a softer fourth-quarter performance than previously anticipated by the market. Elevated personnel expenses: Alfen's ongoing organizational transformation has generated substantial temporary personnel costs that weigh heavily on near-term margins. This pressure demonstrates how restructuring initiatives can delay the recovery of positive earnings per share.

The company announced on November 4, 2026, that it renewed its revolving credit facility for three years until October 2029. As of June 30, 2026, Alfen had utilized €52.20 mm of this facility to provide critical performance bonds to utility clients. Any restriction in accessing these guarantees would immediately halt its project execution pipeline.
