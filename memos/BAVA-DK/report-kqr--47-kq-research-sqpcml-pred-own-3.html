OECD Pillar II Impact on Bavarian Nordic

1.
Initial exposure: Bavarian Nordic officially entered the scope of the global minimum tax framework at the start of its 2025 fiscal period. This regulatory transition followed the Danish Parliament's adoption of the Minimum Tax Act on December 7, 2023, which codifies the OECD Pillar Two model rules into local law. Management does not expect any additional tax costs for the group under its current operating structure. Therefore, shortterm earnings per share will not face immediate dilution from new minimum tax levies.
2.
Favorable assessment: Bavarian Nordic successfully avoided any top-up tax expenses during the initial year of Pillar Two implementation. The group confirmed that no Pillar Two top-up tax was recognized in its 2025 financial statements. This favorable outcome was achieved because the group utilized Transitional Safe Harbour provisions and specific valuation allowance rules. Consequently, these safe harbors continue to protect the group's net income from global tax increases.
3.
Escalating burdens: Bavarian Nordic faces a significant compliance workload to meet global tax reporting obligations. Management warned that meeting these standards represents a not insignificant compliance task for the group. These administrative costs will increase overhead expenses if the company fails to qualify for transitional relief in future periods. Thus, long-term administrative efficiency is highly dependent on preserving safe harbor status.

Standard disclosure: Bavarian Nordic maintains restricted disclosure regarding the potential future balance sheet impacts of global minimum tax rules. Local taxation: Bavarian Nordic's headquarters in Denmark are subject to a corporate tax rate of 22.0% which exceeds the global minimum tax threshold. This high statutory Danish tax rate ensures that domestic operations naturally meet the OECD's 15% effective tax rate requirement. Therefore, parent company earnings remain insulated from direct Danish top-up tax charges.

Switzerland introduced a Qualifying Domestic Top-up Tax on January 1, 2024, followed by the Income Inclusion Rule on January 1, 2025. These local Swiss updates mean that Bavarian Nordic Berna GmbH is subject to local minimum rates of 15%. Therefore, the Swiss site will face higher local cash taxes unless it maintains qualifying tax exemptions.

Section 5 – Tax Deductibility of Acquired Product Rights Amortisation

Danish Asset Acquisition Rules

1.
Favorable tax: Bavarian Nordic acquired the product rights for Rabipur and Encepur as an asset transaction which typically allows for tax amortization. Under Danish tax law, asset purchases permit the buyer to depreciate acquired intangible assets over a seven-year period. This tax amortization provides a powerful shield against corporate income taxes by reducing the parent company's taxable profits in Denmark. Consequently, this tax shield substantially increases the group's net earnings per share.
2.
Reliable precedent: Netcompany Group structured its acquisition of SDC in 2025 as a taxable merger to achieve full tax deductibility on acquired assets. The peer company reported in its 2025 annual report that the acquired net assets are eligible for tax depreciation because the transaction was structured as an asset deal rather than a share purchase. This specific corporate structure allowed Netcompany to write down the entire purchase price of DKK 1 billion. Therefore, peer comparison confirms that structuring acquisitions as asset deals is critical for securing tax deductions.
3.
Accelerated write-down: Netcompany Group successfully depreciated both goodwill and customer relationships over a short period under Danish regulations. The technology firm confirmed in an investor call that both assets would be depreciated over a seven-year period. In contrast, Bavarian Nordic amortizes its core Travel Health product rights over a much longer ten-to-twenty-year period. This slower amortization pace means that Bavarian Nordic's annual non-cash tax benefits are spread out over a longer horizon.

Swiss Intangible Asset Treatment

4.
Beneficial step-up: Swiss tax jurisdictions allow companies to step up the tax basis of intangible assets during restructuring or transfers. Dynatrace completed an intra-entity asset transfer of intellectual property to a Swiss subsidiary in fiscal year 2025. The company reported that this transaction resulted in a step-up of the tax-deductible basis of these assets in Switzerland. This step-up created a substantial deferred tax asset of USD 320.90 million for the firm.
5.
Statutory deduction: Logitech International leveraged Swiss tax reform to optimize its deferred tax assets and goodwill valuations. The consumer electronics peer disclosed in its 2026 proxy statement that it utilized discrete tax benefits from Swiss tax deductions. Specifically, Logitech remeasured the tax basis of its goodwill under Swiss TRAF rules to lower its effective income tax rate. This precedent demonstrates that Swiss operations can actively reduce their tax burden through localized corporate restructuring.

Share Versus Asset Transactions

6.
Direct contrast: Share transactions do not typically allow the buyer to step up or amortize acquired intangible assets for tax purposes. Under standard Danish tax guidelines, a simple share purchase leaves the historical tax basis of the target's assets unchanged, preventing any new amortization benefits. In contrast, Netcompany's asset-structured taxable merger created immediate tax-deductible customer relationships and goodwill. Consequently, Bavarian Nordic's tax benefits depend heavily on whether its historic vaccine acquisitions were executed as asset purchases rather than share deals.
7.
Complex structuring: Bavarian Nordic concluded its rabies and tick-borne encephalitis vaccine acquisition from GlaxoSmithKline as an asset purchase. The specialty vaccine manufacturer paid GlaxoSmithKline a total consideration of EUR 955 million, including milestone payments, for the physical assets and product rights. Because this transaction was structured as an asset purchase, the acquired rights became eligible for tax depreciation. Thus, this deal structure ensured that the amortization of product rights is deductible under local Danish tax rules.

Amortisation Deductibility of Travel Assets

8.
Significant amortization: Bavarian Nordic recorded massive amortization expenses on its core Travel Health product rights in fiscal year 2025. The company amortized its Rabipur and Encepur product rights by DKK 285 million in 2025. Additionally, the company recorded amortization for its Vivotif, Vaxchora, and Vimkunya product rights during the same period. These continuous amortization charges help minimize the group's overall taxable income across its jurisdictions.
