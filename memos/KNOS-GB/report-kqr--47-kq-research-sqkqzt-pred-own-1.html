Section 2 – The July 2024 Built on Workday Agreement

Section summary
  1. The July 2024 strategic agreement secures an eight-year partnership with a critical three-year break option to mitigate downside risks.
  2. Replacing the standard 20% revenue commission with a fixed annual fee of US$10 million allows Kainos to capture strong operating leverage as products scale.

Core Partnership Provisions

1.
Expanded partnership: Kainos announced an enhanced eight-year strategic partnership with Workday Inc. in July 2024 to accelerate software development and distribution. Deutsche Bank Research confirmed on 17 September 2024 that the agreement establishes an eight-year term extending until 2032. This contract structures the core foundation for Workday Products to scale globally.
2.
Strategic flexibility: The agreement includes a three-year break clause that Kainos can call on if the partnership does not develop as anticipated. Because the partnership officially commenced in August 2024, the first option becomes exercisable in August 2027 at the third anniversary. This structural exit window limits long-term financial downside if product sales underperform.
3.
Improved economics: Under standard Built on Workday terms, partners must pay a hefty 20% commission on sales directly to Workday. To maximize margins, Kainos replaced this variable commission with a flat US$10 million annual fee to Workday. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 17 September 2024 that this translates to approximately £7.6 million annually. This fixed cost structure should expand long-term divisional operating leverage.

Section 3 – Flex Credits and API Consumption Monetization

Section summary
  1. Workday's transition to a tiered consumption model under Flex Credits ends the historical free access for third-party vendors.
  2. Mixed customer sentiment and pricing confusion surrounding real-time api metering continue to elongate enterprise procurement cycles.

Strategic Pricing Shift

1.
Significant platform monetization: Workday is ending the historical free access model for external integrations to capture more ecosystem value. Morgan Stanley reported on 25 February 2026 that management plans to eliminate the "free ride" third-party vendors previously enjoyed on the platform. Consequently, Workday is shifting all programmatic and API access to a consumption-based billing model powered by Flex Credits. This shift introduces new recurring transaction expenses that could affect independent software vendors.
2.
Structured API pricing: Workday's new framework implements a tiered pricing structure that charges clients based on programmatic consumption. Workday executive Gerrit Kazmaier explained on 24 February 2026 that API calls are the foundational unit monetized via Flex Credits. Furthermore, the company charges premium prices for agent APIs because they complete highly structured, high-value workflows. This tiered structure forces third-party vendors to re-evaluate their integration economics.
3.
Ratable revenue recognition: Workday's credit model features an annual entitlement structure rather than purely variable, real-time consumption billing. Bernstein Research reported on 25 February 2026 that Flex Credits are recognized ratably over the contract term due to this annual structure. Customers also retain the option to purchase additional credit packs if their actual usage exceeds their annual allocation. This predictable accounting treatment helps stabilize near-term software vendor margins.

Impact on Independent Software Vendors

4.
Adaptive partner response: Kainos is positioning itself as a fast follower to align its proprietary software products with Workday's pricing evolution. Kainos management stated on 22 October 2025 that their billing models will follow whatever Workday does in the consumption space. However, Kainos executive Damien Taylor noted that Workday was not yet ready to fully disclose how partners should structure their custom agent pricing. This coordination delay complicates Kainos's medium-term product revenue forecasting.
5.
Rising development commitments: Kainos faces escalating internal costs as it integrates complex agentic workflows into its software suite. Kainos management reported on 11 November 2024 that product development and marketing costs would increase in the second half of the fiscal year. These escalating outlays are required to support the Built on Workday integration and prepare products for the Flex Credits framework. Consequently, these up-front development expenses could temporarily weigh on division operating margins.

Customer Perception and Market Friction

6.
Divided market sentiment: Workday's partner network reports highly polarized customer feedback regarding the commercial utility of Flex Credits. Jefferies reported on 23 August 2026 that 35% of surveyed partners saw Flex Credits improving flexibility and driving evaluation of AI offerings, while an equal 35% noted that customers believe pricing remains confusing. This split sentiment shows that market education is still in its early stages, presenting near-term hurdles to widespread product monetization.
7.
Pronounced deal friction: Workday's shift to incremental credit pricing is slowing enterprise procurement cycles due to intense financial scrutiny. Morgan Stanley reported on 23 February 2026 that moving from "AI included" to Flex Credits has created confusion and additional scrutiny from Chief Information Officers. Customers consistently voice concerns regarding the predictability of running real-time meters for software consumption. Consequently, these budget debates are elongating average software transaction closing times.
8.
Contrary evidence: Rapid consumption of complimentary credit allocations can trigger unexpected budgetary overruns and customer pushback during initial deployment. Morgan Stanley reported on 20 May 2026 that one customer burned through complimentary credits in just two weeks because agents pulled much broader datasets than expected. This massive consumption variance creates severe friction for enterprise customers trying to transition from sandbox testing to production-scale workflows.
9.
Elevated deployment hurdles: Workday's lack of historical precedent in consumption pricing introduces substantial execution risks for the entire ecosystem. Workday acknowledged in its 5 May 2026 annual filing that it has limited experience with optimal pricing for Flex Credits-based contracts. The company warned that customer resistance to these new billing models could result in lower-than-expected adoption rates of its AI solutions. Therefore, pricing missteps could directly degrade partner-led software distribution revenues.
10.
Other side of the story: Proactive account management and customer education are steadily resolving early confusion around consumption pricing. An IT services director noted on 22 October 2026 that clients are becoming more educated on how the credits function as account executives drive migrations to the Universal Main Subscription Agreement. This educational push helps transition clients from regular contracts to flexible pool agreements. Consequently, clearer budgeting metrics should eventually dismantle transaction blocks and stabilize long-term channel revenues.

Potential Economic Disruptions

Adverse pricing adjustments: Workday's aggressive mid-market strategy has led to significant price reductions of 5% to 10% to secure new contracts. Promotional discounting practices include offering highly favorable terms such as six to nine months of no invoicing on five-year commitments. If these deep discounts become standard across the enterprise segment, Kainos's co-selling margins will contract below consensus expectations. Consequently, this pricing pressure would impair long-term earnings per share expansion because partner software revenues would yield lower incremental returns.
2.
Severe seat contraction: Traditional headcount-based subscription models are facing direct pressure as customers adopt autonomous agentic workflows. Enterprise buyers are increasingly negotiating harder to reduce user seats as artificial intelligence automates transactional business processes. Although Wall Street assume seat counts will remain flat, widespread client layoffs would directly degrade core subscription revenues for Workday. Under this alternative scenario, Kainos's products division would experience slower contract expansions as the underlying user base shrinks.
3.
Rising integration friction: Evolving consumption-based models under Flex Credits are creating pricing confusion that slows down customer decision-making cycles. Enterprise clients frequently request extensive sales meetings to clarify how real-time consumption meters will affect their long-term IT budgets. Procurement budgeting uncertainty has caused late-stage deals to slip, elongating procurement pipelines by several months across the partner channel. If this procurement friction persists, Kainos will face delayed software revenue recognition.

Unexpected delivery pressure: Workday's own professional services team occasionally competes directly with external implementation partners for high-value enterprise deployments. Partner channel conflict reduces the volume of partner-led implementations and pressures consulting services margins.
