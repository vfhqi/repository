[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Zegona is a private-equity turnaround of Vodafone Spain in a listed wrapper, and the case is a cash case, not a business case. The first-order change (28% of the workforce out, the fixed network sold for EUR 1.8bn, 69% of the shares cancelled, EUR 3.7bn of debt refinanced) has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and is in the price at 1806.0p; the return from here rests on a payout policy of about 70% of equity free cash flow, due in September 2026, persuading the market to value the shares on the cash they pay (9.1x 24-month-forward equity free cash flow, an 11% yield) rather than on the earnings that amortisation hides (a 24-month-forward price-to-earnings ratio of 14.28x, above the peer median).

  • The change is internal, executed and unusually broad.
    • Eight of the framework's twelve internal change forces are present and already acted on, 26 months after the acquisition of Vodafone Spain for EUR 5.0bn at 3.9x FY23 EBITDAaL on 31 May 2024.
    • 898 roles cut, 28% of the workforce
      • about 700 efficiency initiatives
      • the fixed network sold into two FibreCos for EUR 1.8bn at 12.5x to above 14x EV/EBITDA against the 3.9x paid
      • EUR 975m used to redeem Vodafone's preference shares and cancel 523m shares
      • a EUR 440m special dividend
      • a EUR 200m buyback
      • EUR 3.7bn refinanced on 14 July 2026, cutting run-rate interest from EUR 230m to EUR 170m.
    • The one false-positive flag that fires is 'selling crown jewels': the assets sold were the network, and the case against is built on that sale.
  • The external forces net to a mild headwind, so the input picture is strong but not clean.
    • Digi took about 80% of Spanish fixed broadband net additions in early 2026 and prices fibre at EUR 10 a month on its own footprint.
    • Against that, Spain's economy grows at 2.7% to 2.8% in 2026 against 0.8% to 1.0% for the euro area, and the top tier (Telefónica, MasOrange and the company) has repriced together three times in 2026.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have moved on every line but revenue.
    • EBITDAaL margin 34% (FY25) to 36.5% (FY26), with 40% in Q4 FY26
    • operating cash flow EUR 400m at acquisition to EUR 763m in FY26
    • leverage 3.1x at closing to 2.33x on 15 July 2026
    • operating profit from a EUR 86m loss to EUR 220m
    • consensus earnings per share from a loss to EUR 0.61 in FY27 and EUR 1.44 in FY29.
    • Revenue has gone from declines of 4.4% before the acquisition to +2.3% in Q1 FY27, and consensus carries +1.2% a year to FY30.
      • That is stabilisation, not growth, and it is the line that keeps the outputs at B.
  • The first-order change has travelled all six layers of the chain; the second-order change stands at the input layer.
    • The sell-side re-based its out-year earnings by about half over twelve months (+48.1% on the line we read as FY29) and then paused (-8.2% over three months on the line we read as FY28).
    • The payout policy, a further EUR 300m to 400m of cost, a Vantage Towers discount worth about EUR 60m a year from 2028 and a RANCo option worth EUR 100m to 150m a year are none of them yet in the accounts or in consensus.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Equity free cash flow of EUR 371m in FY27 (consensus) rising to EUR 620m in FY30 on our path (consensus EUR 557m): EUR 1.65 to EUR 2.75 a share on 225.5m shares, 18.7% a year.
    • Sixteen and a half of those eighteen points come from below EBITDA: the contracted interest saving, refinancing fees and working capital washing out, and zero cash tax on losses above EUR 5.0bn.
      • Revenue contributes about one point a year and margin about half a point.
    • The quantity of growth is a B.
      • Its quality, on the framework's weighting of revenue and margin above the lines the market does not extrapolate, is a C.
  • What it costs.
    • EUR 21.10 (1806.0p), or 9.13x the 24-month-forward equity free cash flow of EUR 521m (EUR 2.31 a share), an 11.0% yield.
    • On the earnings lens the same price is 14.28x 24-month-forward earnings and a dashboard P/E of 15.0x against a sector median of about 11.9x.
      • On the cash lens it is EV/OpFCF of 9.0x against European peers at 11.2x, EV/EBITDA of 4.9x against 6.4x, and an unlevered free cash flow yield of 7.1% against 6.7%.
    • The gap between the two lenses is EUR 1,459m of depreciation and amortisation in FY2026, most of it non-cash.
      • That gap is the case.
  • The return clears the bar over eighteen months only with a re-rating, and misses it over three years.
    • At 10.5x equity free cash flow two years beyond the horizon (a blend of 11.2x if the payout policy lands and 9.1x if it does not, at odds of 65 to 35), less the management carry of EUR 600m, plus consensus dividends, the modal return is 20.2% a year over eighteen months (EUR 27.81 against EUR 21.10) and 15.4% a year over three years (EUR 32.43).
    • The cash return carries about 5 points a year and is the predictable leg
      • earnings growth carries about 12 points a year to March 2028
      • the re-rating about 10 points
      • the carry takes about 6.
    • At the uncleared 9.1x the eighteen-month return is 8.8% a year
      • at the cleared 11.2x it is 25.7%.
      • The re-rating is the swing factor, and it turns on one dated event.
    • On the one-year footing (10.5x FY29 equity free cash flow of EUR 560m, at March 2028) the return is +18.5%, about 12% a year
      • the two-year look-through adds about 8 points a year by crediting the FY30 step.
  • Where the chain breaks: the drop-through from EBITDAaL to equity cash.
    • A 3% revenue decline flowing through at 70% takes about EUR 80m off FY30 EBITDAaL, and a 100 basis point rise in Euribor takes about EUR 26m off the floating book.
    • FY30 equity free cash flow falls from EUR 620m to EUR 514m, below consensus
    • a miss does not re-rate
    • at 9.1x the eighteen-month return is -5.2% a year.
    • Under that ratio the 'Modal return' Element would be F, this Building Block C and the 'Investment Case' D.
    • The case does not survive a Digi-led ARPU decline arriving with higher rates.
  • The pattern.
    • 'Major Corporate Change', crisp on inputs and good on outputs, with the crispness reduced because the upgrades already made are in the price: the shares are +49.5% over twelve months and near their 52-week high of 2025.0p.

  • What the case therefore is.

    • A material change, most of it delivered and paid for, with a dated second step whose outcome decides whether the return is a B or a D.
    • It is compelling as a cash proposition and not as a business, and the reason for the discount to peers on cash is real: a single deflationary market, a challenger taking four fifths of net additions, suppliers with inflation-linked contracts, 2.33x of leverage, a 15% management carry, and a profit-and-loss account whose earnings are mostly amortisation.

  • Rating = B, because the expected change is material on every test the framework sets except one: the input forces are exceptionally broad and executed (E 1, B), the outputs have moved on margin, cash and leverage and the sell-side has followed (E 2, B), and the modal return is about 20% a year over eighteen months and about 15% over three (E 3, C).

    • Not A because the return clears the bar only with a re-rating the market has to be persuaded into, and revenue growth is 1% to 2% a year
    • not C because the cash yield alone approaches the framework's D anchor and the delivered outputs are unusual in size and speed.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the case is a false friend.
    • The EUR 1.8bn of FibreCo proceeds and the EUR 60m of interest savings produced a headline transformation that masks a retail business in structural decline.
    • Revenue growth of 0.4% a year in the bear models against 1.2% to 1.8% for peers
      • 49% of EUR 504m of gross receivables beyond 180 days at March 2026
      • a EUR 1,514m bridge from EBITDAaL of EUR 1,325m to a statutory net loss of EUR 189m in FY26.
    • A network sold to landlords whose fees escalate (EUR 110m a year by 2027 in the bear case) while the retail price is set by a challenger at EUR 5 to EUR 10 a month
      • a tower threat the experts say the company cannot execute
      • a carry that gives two executives 15% of the upside
      • leverage that at 2.73x on a 3% revenue decline leaves the equity return "highly vulnerable".
    • Once the cost and financial levers are spent, and the deceleration in EBITDAaL growth from +16.3% in Q4 FY26 to +2.5% in Q1 FY27 says they nearly are, the equity is a levered claim on a shrinking margin.
  • What we concede.
    • The revenue line is the weakest in the sector set.
    • Earnings quality is poor and the cash bridge below operating free cash flow is not disclosed.
    • The network sale capped the long-run margin and handed leverage to suppliers.
    • The carry is expensive and the governance is below standard.
    • The deceleration is real.
    • On the framework's default price-to-earnings footing the shares show no return, which is why the case rests on a metric choice we have had to argue.
  • Why we still hold the view.
    • The cash is delivered and distributed, not promised: EUR 1.6bn returned within eighteen months of closing, EUR 763m of operating free cash flow in FY26, four beats
    • the next EUR 60m of interest saving is contractual.
    • The payout policy is dated, and the mechanism from a paid yield to a re-rating does not require growth.
    • The exposure to Digi is bounded by management at about 10% of revenue, and Digi's own funding has failed a public-market test (its EUR 150m to 200m IPO suspended in mid-2026; fixed ARPU -6.9% and mobile ARPU -9.8% in Q2 2026; H1 2026 operating free cash flow EUR -101m).
    • The top tier has repriced three times in 2026.
    • The downside is bounded by a cash yield that at 8x would be 8.7% on the dividend alone.
    • Our modal path sits at consensus for two years and only EUR 63m above it in the third.
  • Where the case against wins.
    • If the policy disappoints or the market keeps the earnings lens, the return is 5% to 9% a year, not a loss.
    • If Digi also breaks the ARPU line, it is the adverse drop-through above and a loss.
    • We hold the view with medium conviction on an eighteen-month horizon
    • on a three-year horizon the two hypotheses converge on a C.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a 'Major Corporate Change' case of the private-equity kind: a proven owner-operator bought an under-managed national telecom asset cheaply (Vodafone Spain, EUR 5.0bn, 3.9x FY23 EBITDAaL, 31 May 2024) and has, in 26 months, cut 28% of its workforce, run about 700 efficiency initiatives, sold the fixed network into two FibreCos for EUR 1.8bn at 12.5x to 14x, used the cash to cancel 69% of the shares and pay a special dividend, and refinanced EUR 3.7bn of debt to cut run-rate interest from EUR 230m to EUR 170m.
  • The outputs have followed on every line but revenue: EBITDAaL margin from 34% to 36.5% with 40% in Q4 FY26, operating cash flow from EUR 400m to EUR 763m, leverage from 3.1x to 2.33x, and consensus earnings from a loss to EUR 0.61 a share in FY27 and EUR 1.44 in FY29, on revenue that has gone from -4.4% to +2.3%.

  • The second-order programme (a payout policy of about 70% of equity free cash flow due in September 2026, a further EUR 300m to 400m of cost, a Vantage Towers discount worth about EUR 60m from 2028, and a RANCo option worth EUR 100m to 150m) is at the input layer.
