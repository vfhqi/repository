Section 2 – Historical Consensus Beats and Guidance Conservatism

Section summary
  1. AQ Group has demonstrated a consistent track record of beating sell-side expectations due to conservative target setting.
  2. The historical earnings show that while operational leverage drives major EBIT beats during demand surges, quality issues present downside risks.

Recent Quarterly Performance Trends

1.
Excellent execution: Swedish manufacturer AQ Group delivered an exceptional performance in the third quarter of 2026. Net sales rose by 21% year-on-year to SEK 2,566 million, while operating profit increased by 36% to SEK 259 million. This means that the company continues to benefit from strong operating leverage as high-growth niches scale rapidly ahead of expectations.
2.
Consensus outperformance: The reported operating profit of SEK 259 million in the third quarter of 2026 beat analyst expectations significantly. DNB Carnegie's note on 15 October 2026 explains that this EBIT figure exceeded their forecast of SEK 222 million by 16.7%. This outperformance shows that consensus estimates often lag behind the company's true organic growth momentum in data centres and defence.
3.
Consistent beats: The previous quarter also illustrated AQ Group's tendency to exceed sell-side projections. DNB Carnegie's report on 14 July 2026 shows that second-quarter adjusted EBIT was 6% above their estimate, reaching SEK 255 million. This highlights how the company's decentralized structure allows it to capitalize on sudden volume increases without experiencing margin dilution.
4.
Peer comparison: While AQ Group achieved a high EBT margin of 10.0% in the third quarter of 2026, some Nordic peers have struggled with margin volatility. For example, HANZA's integration of BMK faced lower volumes similar to historical integrations, keeping its multiples lower. This suggests that AQ Group's operational consistency justifies its premium contract manufacturing valuation relative to peers.

The board raised its pre-tax margin target to above 10% on 14 July 2026 after 13 consecutive quarters of outperformance. This target increase demonstrates that the company's actual run-rate consistently outpaces its historical EBT target of 8%.
6.
Integration milestones: The integration of acquired businesses like mdexx Magnetronics has historically progressed sequential quarter-by-quarter. DNB Carnegie's report on 31 March 2025 indicated that mdexx diluted group margins initially but improved to a 7% EBT margin by early 2026. This reveals that management's structured approach helps rehabilitate lower-margin acquired assets to meet group profitability standards.

Growth challenges: Despite high profitability, the company has struggled to meet its annual sales growth target of 15%. Press releases from 2024 and 2025 show that annual sales growth was below target due to general industrial slowdowns. This mismatch indicates that while margins are highly protected, top-line goals rely heavily on M&A acceleration to succeed.

Contrary evidence: AQ Group's organic sales growth turned negative at 5% during the first quarter of 2025, which was worse than expected. DNB Carnegie's review on 23 April 2025 noted that this decline stemmed from low demand in commercial vehicles and agriculture. Section 5 – Macro Conditions, Pillar II Tax, and Currency Transmission

Section summary
  1. Currency translation and raw material lags represent key operational swing factors for AQ Group's near- term margins.
  2. Structured tax changes under Pillar II and regional pricing lags will continue to test the group's decentralized pricing agility.

Foreign Currency Transmission Dynamics

1.
Unhedged translation exposure: Swedish contract manufacturer AQ Group faces substantial translation exposure because 73% of its annual sales are generated in currencies other than the Swedish Krona. According to AQ Group's 2025 annual report, a 10% central shift in exchange rates impacts consolidated annual sales by approximately SEK 664 million. This unhedged exposure means currency swings directly affect reported earnings unless management implements tactical translation hedges during periods of high volatility.
2.
Peer currency comparisons: Peer component manufacturer Trelleborg AB similarly generates the vast majority of its sales outside of Sweden. In its 2025 annual report, Trelleborg estimated that a 10% strengthening of the Swedish Krona against all currencies would negatively impact its total net cash flows by SEK 95 million. This unhedged stance aligns with AQ Group's policy, exposing both Swedish manufacturers to volatile translation headwind pressures when the Swedish Krona strengthens seasonally.
3.
Active hedging strategy: Swiss peer SFS Group AG employs a far more active hedging strategy to manage its foreign currency translation risk. According to SFS Group's half-year report of 2026, the company utilizes cross-currency swaps and Euro-denominated loans to hedge net investments in foreign subsidiaries. This active hedging limits balance sheet translation volatility, presenting superior earnings predictability compared to AQ Group's unhedged centralized policy.

4.

The company's 2025 annual report indicates that its primary material inputs include aluminum, steel, electric steel, and copper. This specific procurement model exposes the firm to secondary processing markup costs, meaning that raw material price fluctuations still influence overall component profitability.

OECD Pillar II and Cash Tax Modeling

7.
Tax legislation adoption: Global minimum tax regulations under OECD Pillar II are already impacting AQ Group's legal structure. The company's interim report of 22 April 2025 confirmed that Sweden adopted Pillar II legislation with effect from 1 January 2024. This early local adoption means that AQ Group's cash tax payments are structurally adjusting sooner than peers based in jurisdictions with delayed enforcement.
8.
Peer safe harbor: Swiss competitor SFS Group AG has modeled its Pillar II exposures and anticipates no near-term top-up tax liabilities. SFS Group's annual report of 5 March 2026 stated that the group expects to meet the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour tests in all key operating jurisdictions. This safe harbor compliance ensures that SFS Group maintains its expected local tax rate of 14.3% without sudden cash tax cash outflows that would depress net earnings per share.
9.
Effective tax comparison: Modeling AQ Group's long-term tax trajectory requires comparing its historical tax baseline against larger diversified peers. Trelleborg AB reported a stable tax rate of 25% for the third quarter of 2026 and expects this rate to hold on a full-year basis. If AQ Group's tax rate rises toward this 25% peer level, its consolidated net margins will contract significantly unless offset by substantial operational cost synergies.
10.
Contrary evidence: Broad macroeconomic relief in the form of falling European energy costs and declining transport rates could quickly offset material price headwinds. In its third-quarter report of 15 October 2026, AQ Group indicated that its decentralized model has allowed it to shift production fluidly to avoid regional cost spikes. This production agility means that underlying margins can actually expand during periods of high raw material volatility if freight and operating costs fall simultaneously.
11.
Other side of the story: Prolonged cost escalation across the secondary metal processing supply chain represents a severe threat to AQ Group's newly raised EBT margin target. While price clauses protect core profits, a prolonged lag in passing through high-value copper and steel contracts will continue to dilutively impact cash generation in the near term. Consequently, if geopolitical conflicts trigger a secondary raw material spike, AQ Group's earnings per share could fall below consensus expectations unless acquisition integrations accelerate margin expansion.
