Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. OTE's EPS trajectory hinges on the transition of its fixed-line and mobile feedback loops, where retail fibre gains could offset wholesale overbuild risks.
1.
Crucial Key Question: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"

Defining the Key Question and EPS Drivers

2.
Favourable EPS growth: Hellenic Telecom's long-term earnings per share expansion is directly tied to the successful monetization of advanced networks and fiber infrastructure because these investments drive higher average revenue per user. Morgan Stanley's note of 16 September 2026 forecasts OTE's EPS to rise from €1.23 in 2025 to €1.51 in 2026 and €1.68 in 2027. This indicates that resolving whether OTE can maintain its network superiority will determine if EPS can sustain a mid-single-digit growth trajectory.
3.
Accelerating fiber migration: The fixed-line transition to fiber-to-the-home networks represents a critical reinforcing feedback loop that directly enhances customer experience and reduces retail churn. OTE reported in its Q2 2026 results that its FTTH utilization rate rose significantly from 31.3% to 41.7% in a single year. This momentum means that as customers switch to fiber, higher in-home switching costs will lock in subscribers, thereby supporting long-term retail margins and EPS stability.
4.
Threatened wholesale revenues: The proposed joint venture between Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation represents a balancing loop that could disrupt OTE's domestic wholesale business. Morgan Stanley's note of 16 September 2026 warns that competitors building their own infrastructure could cause a midterm decline in OTE's wholesale growth. This indicates that the entry of a utility-backed overbuilder could accelerate wholesale revenue loss, pressuring OTE's high-margin infrastructure returns unless retail fiber lock-in offsets this decline.

Strategic and Regulatory Matters

5.
Strong convergence momentum: The integration of fixed and mobile services under a single bundle creates a powerful reinforcing loop that lowers subscriber churn. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 29 May 2025 that OTE's rebranding of its converged offerings will help the operator defend its market leadership under MagentaONE. This means that high convergent customer lock-in shields OTE from aggressive competitor pricing, ensuring that mobile and fixed service revenues continue to support EPS expansion.
6.
Favourable regulatory frameworks: The implementation of the regulatory stop-sell of fiber-to-the-cabinet services serves as a balancing loop to copper infrastructure but reinforces fiber adoption. OTE's Q4 2025 financial report highlights that the stop-selling of FTTC in FTTH connected buildings will further support subscriber migration. This regulatory push ensures that FTTH uptake will accelerate rapidly as copper is phased out, driving capital efficiency and higher retail ARPU.
7.
Sustainable capital returns: The reinforcing loop of returning 100% of prior-year free cash flow to shareholders via dividends and buybacks reduces the outstanding share count. Morgan Stanley reported on 26 February 2026 that OTE returned all free cash flow via a dividend of €0.87 per share and a €177 million share buyback. This continuous share reduction drives EPS growth directly even if net income growth remains modest, ensuring attractive long-term shareholder value.
8.
Other side of the story: While OTE's retail fiber migration is expected to create a highly profitable and defensive customer base, aggressive overbuild and wholesale migration by competitors could turn the business dynamics negative. Deutsche Bank Research warned on 17 November 2025 that if OTE loses a material share of its wholesale FTTH business to the Public Power Corporation, its investment returns would be compromised. This means that wholesale customer losses to rival networks might outweigh the benefits of retail lockin, leading to a down-rating of the stock and lower EPS.

Section 2 – The Fibre Migration and Convergence Loops

Section summary
  1. OTE's reinforcing fiber migration and convergence loops drive high customer retention, offsetting competitive wholesale overbuild risks.

OTE Fibre Migration Dynamics

1.
Accelerating fibre migration: Hellenic Telecom's fixed-line business is experiencing a highly favorable structural shift as subscribers rapidly transition from legacy copper to advanced fiber networks. OTE reported in its Q2 2026 earnings release that its FTTH utilization rate rose significantly to 41.7% in Q2 2026, representing a substantial increase from the 31.3% utilization rate reported in the prior-year period. This rapid operational transition improves retail service revenues and supports long-term EPS expansion through lower customer churn.
2.
Regulatory copper phaseout: The Greek telecom regulator EETT has established a decisive operational framework to transition users by stopping copper-based cabinet sales in buildings connected to fiber. OTE's Q2 2026 earnings release highlights that this newly adopted regulatory framework for the stop-selling of FTTC services accelerates fiber adoption. This regulatory intervention eliminates double-network maintenance costs, boosts mid-term operating margins and enhances customer lifetime value.
3.
Strategic in-home lock-in: OTE is leveraging high switching costs and advanced in-home hardware installations to secure its growing retail fiber subscriber base. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 20 October 2025 that physical connection disruption and modem changes make switching difficult for active customers once connected. OTE reinforces this barrier by rolling out advanced WiFi Mesh repeaters and Fiber to the Room services to enhance premium customer retention.
4.
Network quality advantages: OTE's heavy network investment has driven significant improvements in domestic fixed broadband speeds, solidifying its market leadership and service quality. OTE reported in Q2 2026 that Greece advanced 14 positions in fixed broadband speed rankings according to the Ookla Speedtest Global Index. This superior speed performance allows OTE to justify its premium retail pricing and shields its highmargin retail offerings from competitors.
5.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research warned on 24 April 2026 that government subsidy programs aimed at quickening fiber take-up were completely exhausted in February 2026. This means that future retail migration must rely entirely on organic demand and unsubsidized consumer spending power without government coupon support. The loss of these state-backed coupons could slow customer upgrades and dampen near-term fixed revenue growth if macroeconomic headwinds intensify.

OTE Convergence and Retail Churn

6.
Highly converged base: Hellenic Telecom defends its market leadership by bundling fixed broadband with mobile postpaid plans to form tightly integrated accounts. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 4 August 2025 that combining the COSMOTE brand with Deutsche Telekom strengthens customer loyalty and ARPU across convergence bundles. With c.70% of broadband users taking convergent services, OTE drives steady mobile data monetization and decreases long-term retail churn.
7.
Sustained postpaid migration: OTE actively transitions its prepaid mobile base to higher-value postpaid plans to secure consistent monthly cash flows. OTE's Chief Financial Officer Charalampos Mazarakis stated on 29 July 2026 that this pre-to-post migration increases mobile ARPU by €5 to €6 per month. This shift expands long-term EBITDAaL margins and drives EPS growth, since less than 50% of OTE's base is currently on postpaid.
8.
Defensive convergence pricing: Competitors Nova and Vodafone Greece struggle with weak cash generation, which limits their ability to match OTE's converged offers. Morgan Stanley reported on 16 September 2026 that OTE's premium network quality justifies higher pricing compared to budget rivals. This enables OTE to implement inflation-linked tariff adjustments without triggering significant subscriber churn to lower-tier brands, supporting steady EPS expansion.
9.
Enhanced television cross-selling: OTE leverages premium TV content bundling to accelerate broadband customer acquisition and lower subscription churn. OTE's TV subscriber base rose by 7.9% year-on-year to 793,000 as of June 2026, driven by content sharing partnerships. This expanding TV base strengthens the core convergence loop by encouraging subscribers to take triple-play bundles, reducing household churn.

This explosive top-line momentum means that public sector demand continues to accelerate ahead of the upcoming RRF phase-out, supporting near-term EPS growth.
2.
Impending program end: The Recovery and Resilience Facility program is scheduled to conclude at the end of 2026, creating structural revenue concerns for OTE's public sector projects. OTE noted in its Q2 2026 half-year report that RRF-funded projects are expected to wind down in the second half of 2026. This sunset clause means that OTE must rely on private-sector contracts to replace this public funding, unless the European Commission extends the RRF disbursement deadlines beyond 2026.
3.
Defensive post-RRF support: Hellenic Telecom expects to defend its B2B revenues after the RRF ends by providing ongoing maintenance and expanding its international operations. Deutsche Bank Research argued on 24 April 2026 that OTE will continue to earn lucrative ICT support revenues from maintaining newly completed RRF infrastructure. This contract continuity ensures that recurring services will cushion the transition, meaning that B2B operating margins should remain resilient even after public investment flows decline.

This temporary cash drag means that working capital has been heavily consumed by the rapid acceleration of RRF projects, which temporarily pressures OTE's operating cash flows.
6.
Expected cash release: The wind-down of the Recovery and Resilience Facility program in late 2026 is expected to unlock substantial working capital for Hellenic Telecom. Morgan Stanley's mid-year outlook of 22 May 2026 notes that there is a one-to-two year lag between receiving RRF funds and actually absorbing them. This absorption lag indicates that receivables will convert to cash as public projects finish, which means that OTE's free cash flow will experience a powerful positive reversal in 2027.

Subsidized funding options: OTE is leveraging targeted, low-cost RRF financial structures to fund its long-term fiber rollout without placing stress on its corporate balance sheet. OTE's 2025 financial report shows the company entered a common secured bond loan of up to €405.1 million utilizing funds from the Recovery and Resilience Facility. This non-dilutive, cheap funding ensures that capital intensity remains highly manageable, meaning that interest expense will remain low while fiber coverage expands rapidly.
8.
Contrary evidence: Eurobank's offering circular published on 20 October 2026 warns that the Bank of Greece has identified lower-than-expected absorption and utilization of RRF funds as a material downside risk to the medium-term economic outlook. This lower absorption rate means that public sector clients may delay project payments to OTE if milestones are missed, which would prolong working capital pressure and prevent the anticipated cash release.

Cash flow smoothing: Hellenic Telecom incorporates flexibility into its payout calculations to protect its dividend growth trajectory from temporary operational volatility. This smoothing mechanism ensures that dividends can maintain a positive trajectory unless underlying structural trends deterioriate, reducing the impact of short-term cash flow swings on EPS.

Greek Challenger Cash Flow Fragility

1.
Greek rival cash constraints: Vodafone Greece and Nova suffer from persistently weak cash generation compared to Hellenic Telecom's massive cash flow generation. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 12 June 2025 that operating free cash flow for OTE was €784 million in FY24, while Vodafone Greece generated only €41 million and Nova recorded negative €33 million. This extreme capital disparity means that rivals cannot afford to initiate price wars because they lack the balance sheet depth to sustain prolonged cash drains, which secures OTE's high-margin premium retail position.
2.
Compelled pricing discipline: Cash flow limitations force Greek challengers to adopt rational pricing strategies rather than aggressive customer acquisition campaigns. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 4 August 2025 that Vodafone Greece and Nova cannot afford to start a price war, meaning they must protect their existing margins. This forced financial discipline has led to consecutive annual CPI-linked price hikes by competitors, allowing Hellenic Telecom to raise its own premium retail tariffs without experiencing significant customer churn.

Ownership Consolidation and Sale Processes

3.
Potential ownership transition: Prolonged cash underperformance and high leverage have driven major structural changes, including the potential sale of United Group's Greek assets. Morgan Stanley reported on 3 July 2024 that United Group B.V. invited bids for its southeastern European telecom assets after ending FY23 with high leverage of 4.2 times net debt to EBITDA. This strategic sale process means that Nova could undergo an ownership change to a well-capitalized buyer, potentially revising its long-term network build and commercial plans in Greece.
4.
Acquisition interest diversity: Strategic global operators and private equity firms are actively evaluating the purchase of United Group's regional telecom infrastructure. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 20 September 2024 that global operators including Saudi Telecom and e&, along with private equity players like Warburg Pincus, are conducting due diligence on Nova. This broad interest indicates that international capital continues to value the consolidated Greek three-player market structure, reducing the risk of a destructive price competitor entering the region.
5.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley's note of 16 September 2026 warns that the entry of well-capitalized buyers like Saudi Telecom or e& into the Greek market could suddenly re-energize Nova's commercial aggressiveness. This strategic transition means that a buyer with a longer investment horizon might be willing to endure near-term cash losses to build market share. This aggressive backing could disrupt the current pricing harmony in Greece, putting downward pressure on OTE's retail broadband margins.

Portuguese New Entrant Comparative Dynamics

6.
Portuguese challenger entry: The Portuguese telecommunications landscape represents a key comparable case where Digi's aggressive low-cost entry in November 2024 tested incumbent resilience. Bernstein Research reported on 23 January 2025 that Digi introduced mobile tariffs up to 70% cheaper than the market average to gain quick scale. This aggressive pricing means that incumbents faced potential margin compression, although the challenger's lack of premium television content and extensive fixed coverage initially limited its overall retail penetration.
7.
Incumbent market resilience: Established Portuguese operators successfully defended their premium customer bases through strong opex discipline and targeted sub-brands rather than cutting main brand prices. Morgan Stanley's report on 11 June 2026 revealed that NOS actually increased its mobile market share by 1.0% over the past two years despite Digi's entry. This operational resilience suggests that incumbent customer retention remains highly defensive because of convergent bundling, which protects core operating cash flows from low-cost attacks.
8.
Opex offset success: Strong efficiency programs can completely offset top-line pressure caused by a new entrant's aggressive pricing.
