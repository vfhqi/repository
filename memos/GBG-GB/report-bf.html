EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Section summary
  1. divisional reorganisation successfully separates high-performing core capabilities from localized non- core assets
  2. revenue model transition increases predictability through higher contracted subscription commitments
  3. platform transition drives immediate customer adoption with substantial multi-solution cross-sell momentum
  4. operating leverage improves by retiring legacy software platforms and centralising on unified infrastructure
  5. strategic leadership transition establishes a simpler global operating model with unified accountability
  6. financial turnaround yields H2 acceleration driven by successful turnaround of the Americas Identity division

GB Group plc is a global digital identity and location verification platform operator that processes over 65 billion transactions annually through two highly complementary core offerings: Identity (61% of revenue) and Location (31% of revenue). The company serves more than 20,000 customers globally across financial services, ecommerce, gaming, and technology verticals. Management launched the flagship GBG Go unified orchestration platform in April 2025, which has surpassed adoption expectations with over 100 customer contracts secured and more than 225 qualified prospects in the pipeline.

The business model generates highly predictable cash flows through a deliberate commercial transition from volatile consumption-based contracts to structured multi-year subscription commitments. Repeatable revenue now represents 94.8% of group revenues, with subscriptions contributing 56% of total revenue. Sales execution delivered a 20% increase in renewals with minimum commitments, and new business annual contract value is over 35% pre-committed. Customer retention metrics remain strong, with Identity and Location net revenue retention stabilized at 100.0% in FY26.

From a financial perspective, GB Group delivered revenue of £285.0 million in FY26 with an adjusted operating profit margin of 23.7%, aligning with historical target levels of 23-24%. However, management has guided that adjusted operating margins will compress to 21-22% in FY27 to absorb a strategic £6.0 million one-off investment in the GBG Go technology roadmap, before recovering to 23-24% in FY28 and exceeding 24% thereafter as legacy platforms retire. Capital allocation has been disciplined, with the board returning £45 million to shareholders through share buybacks that repurchased and cancelled approximately 8% of issued equity. Net debt to adjusted EBITDA leverage stood at 1.15x at the end of FY26, providing ample financial flexibility relative to peers.

topic-specific reports The company operates within a $50 billion global digital identity and fraud prevention market that is expanding rapidly due to AI-driven fraud threats and tightening regulatory mandates. Synthetic identity fraud losses are projected to exceed $23 billion by 2030, while deepfake-related fraud losses are expected to reach $40 billion by 2027. GB Group occupies the high-margin platform orchestration stage of the value chain, sitting between upstream credit bureau data suppliers and downstream enterprise customers. The platform orchestrates verification workflows by integrating over 200 global suppliers to deliver real-time identity checks embedded directly inside critical customer onboarding systems.

The competitive landscape exhibits a dual structure of intense fragmentation among specialized point-solution providers and high concentration among credit bureau oligopolists like Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion. Value chain dynamics operate under a complex "coopetition" framework where credit bureaus act as critical data suppliers while simultaneously competing with their own downstream fraud prevention suites. GB Group's multi-source triangulation model delivers superior match rates over single credit bureaus, with former executives noting 10% to 15% higher verification accuracy. Under the leadership of CEO Dev Dhiman, who joined from Experian, the company has executed a focused global turnaround of the historically underperforming Americas Identity division. The segment returned to growth in Q4 FY26 following strengthened leadership and disciplined customer segmentation. Operational improvements compressed customer time-to-revenue by over 50%, accelerating commercial conversion cycles. Management is transitioning from fragmented regional brands to a unified global functional operating model to reduce operational complexity and improve cross-selling efficiency.

From a foundation quality perspective, GB Group benefits from substantial structural customer moats driven by deep API and SDK integrations that create high switching costs. Unified platform orchestration via GBG Go embeds modules directly inside critical business onboarding workflows, creating substantial structural barriers to switching competitors. Proprietary data networks spanning over 200 external data suppliers and 470 datasets across 200 countries protect against direct competitive duplication by local point-solution providers. Entry is restricted by the extreme data intensity and data licensing requirements needed to achieve high verification match rates, requiring massive capital investments to establish comprehensive global registries.

However, the business faces persistent structural risks from upstream credit bureau verticalization threats. Operating as a pure-play data intelligence aggregator exposes GB Group to structural upstream pricing power, and upstream credit bureaus could aggressively verticalize by restricting core registries or undercutting on bundled software pricing, rendering pure-play platform models structurally unviable. Equifax represents the largest individual data provider in GBG's cost structure, historically accounting for 12% of total data costs. Financial performance faces risk if a major supplier stops providing data, representing a persistent operational threat to software delivery.

Looking forward, the company is well-positioned to capitalize on long-term structural demand drivers including regulatory compliance mandates, AI-driven fraud escalation, and the emergence of agentic commerce. GBG launched "GBG for Agents" to deliver capabilities designed to establish trust in autonomous agent-to-agent interactions, and released "Reach" as the world's first dedicated agent decisioning layer for location intelligence.

Leveraging generative-AI models allows engineers to launch upgrades 3.5x faster to market, creating compounding mechanical advantages over fragmented local peers. The global digital identity and fraud prevention market represents an estimated $50 billion addressable opportunity with high-single-digit underlying expansion supported by regulatory compliance, customer experience optimization, and online transaction growth. - Earnings Calls The primary investment consideration centers on whether management can sustain organic growth acceleration beyond the FY27 transition year while defending against upstream supplier verticalization threats and startup pricing pressure.

The commercial model transition to contracted minimum commitments significantly reduces transactional volume volatility and improves forward revenue visibility, but execution risk remains elevated given the historical underperformance in the Americas division and the competitive intensity from larger, bettercapitalized credit bureau peers. The £6 million incremental investment in GBG Go represents a critical test of management's capital allocation discipline, with the platform's adoption trajectory and cross-sell momentum serving as key forward-looking indicators of foundation quality and operator skill.

Outliers worth noting

GB Group plc exhibits three genuine statistical outliers when benchmarked against its peer group of global credit bureaus and identity verification providers. These outliers reveal both structural advantages and inherent vulnerabilities in the company's business model positioning within the digital trust value chain. The first outlier is GB Group's exceptionally high repeatable revenue composition of 94.8%, which places it at the extreme upper end of software business model quality metrics.

This aggregate comprises 56% subscription agreements and 39% consumption-based contracts, creating forward visibility that rivals pure SaaS operators despite the company's data orchestration positioning. When compared to Experian's $8.4 billion revenue scale or RELX's £9.6 billion, GB Group's £285 million revenue represents less than 3.5% of these peers' size, yet the repeatable revenue percentage exceeds typical industry norms for mid-tier data intelligence aggregators.

This metric positions GB Group in approximately the top 5% of repeatability among comparable B2B software infrastructure providers, though the underlying consumption exposure creates latent volatility risk masked by the headline figure. Cross-reference to Section 1 and Section 2 reveals that management is actively transitioning consumption customers to minimum commitment subscriptions, which should further strengthen this already exceptional metric.
