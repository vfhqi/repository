EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 finding

This divestment leaves a less capital-intensive business with improved cashflow dynamics, while new mediumterm targets mandate double-digit Group margins versus 7.3% in FY24 excluding the Aerostructures business.

The transaction fundamentally reshapes Senior's competitive positioning and earnings quality, establishing a focused industrial platform with structural margin expansion potential and reduced cyclical volatility compared to the historical diversified aerospace portfolio. The forward earnings trajectory is underpinned by five to eight ranked key drivers with differentiated plausibility profiles.

Civil aerospace build rate recovery remains the single largest contributor to the estimated 15% EPS impact, supported by global revenue passenger kilometers increasing by 5% year-on-year in 2025 and Airbus holding a backlog of 9,222 aircraft while Boeing carries 6,202, representing a decade of production.

Spencer Aerospace high-pressure fittings drive a robust 8% EPS upside potential through-the-cycle, reflecting successful integration and product portfolio expansion following the November 2022 acquisition.

Divesting structural operations delivers a 12% margin accretion on group-wide continuing operations, removing the loss-making drag that historically suppressed consolidated profitability metrics.

Negotiated long-term commercial agreements secure a predictable 6% gain to offset inflationary costs, demonstrating management's disciplined approach to protecting operating leverage in the face of persistent input cost pressures.

Structural Flexonics cost actions contribute 4% to forward earnings efficiency improvements, while Flexonics land vehicle normalization creates an estimated 5% headwind, reflecting cyclical US heavy truck weakness.

Group continuing revenue rose 7% on constant currency to £390.8m in H1 2026, while continuing adjusted operating margin reached double digits at 10.0%, expanding 170 bps on constant currency, meeting the minimum medium-term target substantially earlier than the market anticipated.

Aerospace adjusted operating margin expanded 270 bps to 13.1%, driven by pricing and volume recovery, reflecting the division's superior operational gearing to commercial build rate inflection.

Flexonics adjusted margin reached 11.4% excluding the JV, helped by favorable pricing and product mix, demonstrating resilient profitability despite softer North American land vehicle end-markets.

Adjusted earnings per share expanded 27% to 6.46p compared to 5.07p in H1 2025, substantially outperforming the broader UK capital goods peer group and validating the strategic rationale for portfolio concentration.

1.
Other side of the story:

Adjusted EBIT of £63.6m in FY25 beat the initial conservative consensus expectation of £59.0m, while FY24 actual EBIT met the revised expectations at £53.0m despite Boeing labor strike disruptions, demonstrating management's ability to navigate significant external headwinds without material guidance resets.

Deutsche Bank maintained its buy rating with a price target of 255p in January 2026, while Jefferies raised its price target to 230p following the positive trading and divestment completion, reflecting broad consensus around the strategic transformation's value creation potential.

However, shareholders approved the Zeus cash acquisition with 99.7% of votes cast in favour, with management expecting completion by end-2026 having secured 10 of 12 required regulatory approvals, effectively capping near-term share price upside and rendering traditional sell-side price targets largely academic until the transaction closes or regulatory obstacles emerge. Fundamental peer positioning analysis reveals mixed relative momentum across the aerospace and industrial engineering landscape.

Melrose's Airframes margin collapsed to 6.3% in H1 2026, heavily weighed down by supply-chain bottlenecks, positioning Senior favorably against this restructuring-focused peer but underscoring the sector-wide operational challenges stemming from commercial aerospace supply disruptions.

Senior benefited from two consecutive consensus upgrades, reflecting the completed Aerostructures disposal, contrasting sharply with Melrose's FY26 EPS estimates being cut by 9% to 34.5p, following the disruptive Garden Grove incident.

Senior's 0.9x net debt to EBITDA leverage compares favorably with the 1.1x capital goods peer average, while the 14.5% ROCE in H1 2026 approaches but does not yet exceed the 12.3% sector average, suggesting incremental rather than transformational relative value creation to date. Two to three material risks warrant continuous monitoring and could derail the forward earnings trajectory if realized.

The pure-play FCTM pivot concentrates Senior's macro-cyclical risks in land vehicle and depressed commercial aviation supply chains, eliminating the historical diversification benefit that partially insulated the group from single-platform or single-end-market shocks.

A synchronized global economic slowdown could stall commercial build rates while triggering severe commodity supply shocks, creating a scenario where both revenue growth and margin expansion reverse simultaneously, compressing earnings substantially below the base case forward trajectory.

Extreme platform dependencies and recurring customer delivery delays could derail the projected EPS expansion if commercial aerospace disruptions return, particularly given Senior's concentrated exposure to Airbus and Boeing narrowbody programs which collectively represent the majority of civil aerospace revenues and remain vulnerable to engine supply shortages, labor disputes, and regulatory quality interventions.

These risks are cross-referenced in Section 3 External Change, Section 4 Financial Profile Change, and Section 10 Forward Guidance Credibility and Sensitivity, where detailed scenario modeling quantifies the potential downside impact under adverse external conditions.
