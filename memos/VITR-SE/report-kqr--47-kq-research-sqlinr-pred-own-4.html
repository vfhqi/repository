Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Vitrolife's reported earnings are driven largely by the krona, and the evidence is firmest on transaction exposure and realised effects and weakest on the cost base by site. Domestic revenues are less than 1% of total sales, while a large share of operating expenses is settled in Swedish krona. Vitrolife used forward contracts historically but has abandoned them. The 2025 krona strength therefore flowed straight into reported sales, margins and consensus estimates. The 2027-2028 bank outlooks do not agree on direction, so the next FX tailwind or headwind is open to question.

Currency exposure. Euro net transaction exposure is the largest single transaction exposure in the group, at EUR 467 million. The US dollar is second, with a net cash flow surplus of USD 267 million. The cost side leaves the group structurally short in Nordic currencies.

Vitrolife reported Danish revenues of SEK 15 million against localized Danish costs of SEK 144 million. A ten percent move in the krona changes income before tax by +/- SEK 114 million. Translation effects work differently. Exchange differences on foreign subsidiaries' net assets go through equity via other comprehensive income.

Revaluation of trade receivables and payables does reach the income statement, under other operating income or expenses.

Cost base composition. The footprint maps onto three currency clusters. Consumables is produced in Gothenburg, Denver and San Diego. Technologies is developed and assembled in Gothenburg and Viby. These European sites incur costs mainly in Swedish and Danish krone, the currencies in which the company is short. The genetics laboratories pay personnel and service costs in euros, US dollars and local currencies. Raw materials add a further dollar element, as Redeye noted that higher material costs are driven heavily by USDdenominated purchases. US production also brings a tariff benefit, as DNB Carnegie reported that San Diego and Denver shield Vitrolife from tariffs. This is the least certain part of the analysis. Vitrolife reports regional market contribution but explicitly excludes localized segment cost bases from its standard reports. The site-level currency split is therefore inferred from footprint disclosures, not reported.

Realised effects since 2024. The currency drag was mild in 2024 apart from a larger hit in one quarter. Q1 2024 sales were SEK 841 million, down 2% in krona on a negative currency impact of SEK 13 million. Q3 2024 carried a five percent negative currency drag.

The drag intensified through 2025: Q2 2025 had a negative currency impact of eight percent. Q3 2025 had seven percent. Q4 2025 had ten percent. Q1 2026 had nine percent.

The cost showed up in profitability. Full-year gross margin fell from 59.3% in 2024 to 58.1% in 2025, primarily due to exchange rate headwinds. Management said FX dragged the Q4 2025 EBITDA margin down by about 2.5 percentage points. The pressure then eased: Q2 2026 carried only a one percent currency drag.

Q3 2026 showed a two percent positive tailwind. The underlying business was healthier than the reported numbers suggested. Q1 2026 had five percent organic growth in local currencies, obscured by a nine percent currency drag.

Estimate revisions. Brokers moved with the krona. DNB Carnegie lowered its adjusted EPS estimates for 2025 through 2027 by two percent. Redeye lowered full-year EBITDA expectations by eight percent.

After the krona weakened, the direction reversed. DNB Carnegie raised adjusted EPS estimates by two to four percent. Redeye raised its 2027 to 2028 estimates by four percent. Bank and broker FX outlooks for 2027-2028.

The outlooks diverge. Nordea projects EUR/SEK flat at 11.15 through 2027 and 2028. Nordea sees USD/SEK falling from 10.14 in late 2026 to 9.29 by the end of 2028. Morgan Stanley projects EUR/SEK falling to 10.40 by Q4 2027 and rising to 10.70 by end-2028.

Morgan Stanley projects USD/SEK of 9.63 at end-2027 and 9.82 at end-2028. ING expects EUR/SEK to glide down to 10.80 by Q4 2027. SEB and Handelsbanken did not publish specific 2027-2028 calendar-year forecasts in the compiled materials. Morgan Stanley expects the Riksbank to raise its policy rate to 2.25% in February 2027.

ING expects only one hike in early 2027. Most projections imply a stronger krona against the dollar, which would work against the tailwind that Redeye expects in 2027. The Nordea EUR/SEK path would leave euro translation broadly neutral.

Precedents and countermeasures. Swedish exporters felt the 2025 krona strength in sales and margins. Getinge faced a headwind of over one billion kronor from exchange rates and tariffs combined. Elekta's first-quarter gross margin was burdened by a one hundred basis point currency headwind and ninety basis points of tariff costs.

Arjo reported a four percent sales decline despite five percent organic growth. Peers responded with pricing and cost action. Surgical Science used price increases to hold a 68% gross margin despite a 1.1-point currency headwind. Elekta cut its workforce by about ten percent.

Brokers cut estimates for peers in the same way, for example lowering Getinge adjusted EPS estimates by two to four percent. Sectra was insulated by its subscription software model. Vitrolife's hardware and consumables mix is more transaction-exposed than that.

Forward view. If the krona weakens beyond consensus, the same unhedged structure that depressed 2025 would amplify reported growth. A weaker krona would act as a powerful earnings tailwind, with DNB Carnegie noting EPS estimate upgrades of two to four percent. If the krona strengthens beyond consensus, a stronger krona would immediately dilute international sales and suppress consolidated net profit margins. The main countermeasure would be pricing, but such interventions risk losing market share if global IVF cycle volumes slow. Prolonged krona strength would also offset the tariff advantage of the US sites by inflating Swedish manufacturing costs.

Limitations. Overall confidence is medium. Confidence rests on robust public disclosures on transactional sensitivities despite persistent gaps in localized segment cost bases. Confidence is low on the site-level cost currency split, high on transaction exposure and realised quarterly effects, and medium on the FX forecasts. The materials also contain an inconsistency in the stated krona sensitivity: SEK 114 million for a ten percent move in one place and SEK 141 million in another. Resolving these gaps requires Vitrolife's next annual report, quarterly calls and a more complete set of bank forecasts. SEB and Handelsbanken are the missing outlooks.

Translation Exposure Dynamics

5.
High translation risk: Vitrolife translation exposure primarily arises when converting the net assets of its foreign subsidiaries from local currencies to Swedish krona. These exchange rate differences between reporting periods are recognized directly in equity through other comprehensive income in the balance sheet. Because these non-cash conversion adjustments bypass the consolidated income statement, they do not directly pressure reported operating income of the group.
6.
Large asset concentration: Translation exposure remains particularly significant for the group in relation to the Euro where the largest net assets are located. This balance sheet mismatch occurs because the largest portion of Vitrolife net assets, including intangible assets and loans, is denominated in Euros. This structural allocation means that translation fluctuations significantly alter the total equity reported on the balance sheet.
7.
Significant working capital: Operating translation effects also impact the income statement through the conversion of short-term balance sheet items. Exchange rate shifts alter the value of trade receivables and trade payables denominated in foreign currencies. These short-term fluctuations are recognized under other operating income or expenses, leading to direct volatility in monthly operating profit.

Diminishing revenue hurdles: DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that currency headwinds have finally been weathered after multiple quarters of pressure. The broker noted that Q3 2026 marked the first quarter without severe FX headwinds on sales. Consequently, this easing currency pressure supports a welcome return to reported year-on-year revenue and earnings growth.
