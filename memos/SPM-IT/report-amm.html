APM Mini-Memo: Saipem

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 9 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 15 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: the Mini-Memo card of 10 October 2026, drawing on the Investment Analyst's memo of 4 October, the ratings store pins of 9 October and the research digest.

The one-line view. Neutral on the shares with medium conviction and bullish on the business: Saipem owns scarce deepwater installation assets in a tightening oligopoly in the strongest sanctioning cycle since 2013, and at EUR 4.57 the shares already pay for the cycle, the margin repair and the merger.

What the company does

Saipem is the Italian offshore energy services contractor, the largest by revenue in its peer set, on revenue of EUR 15,497 million in 2025 and a backlog of EUR 29.9 billion. It installs subsea pipelines and production systems, drills offshore with its own rigs and builds onshore liquefied natural gas plants, reporting as Asset Based Services at about 58 percent of revenue, Offshore Drilling and Energy Carriers. Eni holds 21.19 percent, described as non-core, and with CDP Equity holds 25 percent jointly under a pact running to January 2028.

A plausible investment thesis

The mechanism to earnings is margin and drop-through, not volume. Offshore sanctioning capital spending is forecast to rise 30 percent to 149 billion US dollars in 2027; twenty high-end rigid pipelay vessels exist, none is being built, and Saipem and Subsea 7 hold eight. The fleet is booked into 2028 and 2029, so the cycle arrives as price and mix, with contract rates up 3 to 5 percent and day rates up 5 to 7 percent.

The memo of 4 October has earnings per share on the brokers' basis rising from EUR 0.154 in 2025 to about EUR 0.26 in 2027 and EUR 0.31 in 2028, roughly 26 percent a year, of which revenue contributes about 1 point and margin about 6, while about 18 come from below the cash profit line as depreciation, interest and tax fall with the lease roll-off. Track the Asset Based Services margin against its high-teens target, recovery of the EUR 140 million of Middle East transit costs, and the February 2027 guidance.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

This is where the case binds. The Investment Analyst's memo of 4 October rated Case Riskiness D, where A is simple and low-risk. A fall from 12.93 times 24-month-forward earnings to the two-year median of 7.98 times is a 38 to 48 percent fall on unchanged earnings, the Thai Oil arbitration claims 3.8 billion US dollars unprovisioned with no verdict before 2028, and 38 percent of backlog sits behind the Strait of Hormuz. The thesis is four lines and trackable monthly, but thirteen brokers cannot agree on 2027 earnings within a factor of two.

Business quality

Advantaged assets, below-average conversion. The memo of 4 October rated Foundations' Robustness C, over a value chain and industry at B and the business itself at C. The moats are verifiable: eight of twenty rigid pipelay vessels and four of five J-lay units in the combined fleet. The outputs are not: a margin on earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation of 11.1 percent in 2025 against Subsea 7's 24 percent guidance and TechnipFMC's 23.2 percent. Cash reads better, with free cash flow after leases of EUR 792 million in 2025 and net cash of EUR 1,078 million before leases.

Plausible returns and valuation

At EUR 4.57 on 9 October the market value is EUR 8,295.8 million on a price-to-earnings ratio of 12.6 times. Our two readings disagree, and the disagreement is the decision. The script's TSR/Valuation reading of 9 October is B, a valuation reading only, carrying a plausible return of 35 percent a year, 37.8 percent over eighteen months and 32.1 percent over three years, on the multiple moving from 12.6 towards 15.2 times. The memo of 4 October, at EUR 4.322, put the modal return at minus 6.4 percent a year over eighteen months and minus 0.7 percent over three years on exit multiples of 11 and 10 times. The gap is almost entirely the exit multiple.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The thematic read is the strongest thing on the card. Thematic Fit/Momentum in the ratings store on 9 October is A, on an environment score of 70.2 with a strengthening energy-prices tailwind; the memo of 4 October had it B. The deepwater upcycle and European energy security carry about 85 percent of backlog by segment. Against that, Petrobras is piloting direct charters and has appealed the merger clearance, TechnipFMC takes share with an integrated model Saipem lacks, shallow-water work is 42 percent of backlog, and the cycle is named as plateauing after 2028.

The strongest counter-case

The lens is wrong. On enterprise value to cash profit Saipem trades at 3.8 times 2027 before leases, a 38 percent discount to its own upcycle average, against about 5.8 times for Subsea 7 and 12.9 times for TechnipFMC, because lease depreciation overstates economic depreciation. Consensus is too low, with the drilling recovery and EUR 300 million of merger synergies outside it, and a cleared Saipem7 holding 60 to 65 percent of global subsea construction with no new supply to 2028 would be worth EUR 5.50 to 6.00 on Subsea 7's multiple.
