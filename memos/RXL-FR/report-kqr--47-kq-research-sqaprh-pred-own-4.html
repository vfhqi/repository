The 2011-12 Copper Price Correction

1.
Declining organic momentum: Rexel experienced a significant top-line deceleration in the latter half of 2012 as global macroeconomic conditions weakened. Over the full year of 2011, the distributor achieved robust organic same-day sales growth of 6.2%, which was supported by a 1.7 percentage point tailwind from rising copper-based cable prices. However, as copper prices retrenched in 2012, organic same-day sales collapsed to a negative decline of 1.8%. This transition demonstrates how rapidly commodity price shifts can erode the group's nominal revenue baseline.
2.
Resilient cost controls: EBITA performance during the 2011 to 2012 correction was protected primarily through structural cost-saving initiatives and labor right-sizing. In 2011, adjusted EBITA margin improved by 70 basis points to reach 5.7% because of strict control over distribution and administrative expenses. By 2012, the group maintained its adjusted EBITA margin stable at 5.7% despite the organic sales contraction. Consequently, disciplined cost management remains the primary mechanism to insulate operating margins from price deflation.

Vulnerable asset values: Severe commodity downturns also exposed the group to localized asset impairments across underperforming European and Asia-Pacific markets. These non-cash charges primarily affected sub-scale operations in Spain, Slovenia, New Zealand, and the Netherlands. This trend shows that prolonged commodity declines can trigger material balance sheet write-downs.
