APM Mini-Memo: VERBUND

The one-line view. VERBUND is an exceptional hydropower fleet priced low in its own valuation range after the driest year in a century, and the story depends on Austria's rivers returning to normal flow.

What the company does

VERBUND is Austria's dominant electricity producer and the owner of the national high-voltage grid. Its 136 hydropower plants on Austrian and German rivers, 9.1 gigawatts in all, earned 2,024 million euros of operating profit before depreciation in 2025, 74% of the group's 2,738 million. The wholly owned grid earned 440 million euros, and wind, solar and retail supply earned the rest. The Republic of Austria holds 51% and less than 20% of the shares trade freely.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is a return to normal water. River flow ran at 63% of its long-run average in the year to late August 2026, the lowest in 100 years of records, and first-half operating profit before depreciation fell 25% to 1,062 million euros. Droughts have historically reverted to the average, and VERBUND's profit follows the water quickly because the plants burn no fuel.

Volume reaches earnings per share first, then price. Each 1% of hydropower output moves the group result by about 9.6 million euros, so a 37% shortfall is worth roughly 350 million euros on my own arithmetic, against a guided 2026 group result of 1,000 to 1,150 million. On price, 86% of 2026 hydropower output was sold forward at 87 euros per megawatt-hour, while the 2027 output sold so far fetched about 96 to 97.5 euros. The Limberg III pumped-storage plant returns in full in December 2026, restoring 40 to 60 million euros a year.

Four drivers tell you how the story is going. They are the monthly river-flow readings, the monthly cost of buying power to honour forward sales, the price of 2027 forward sales, and the Austrian budget's treatment of the profit levy. Third-quarter results are not in our files; our research runs from July to September 2026.

The Target Setup

My own read, because no Investment Analyst memo exists. There are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings. The Target Setup is 'CfC in quality cyclical at trough-on-trough, leading to reversion to normal': the drought is the cause for concern, the hydropower fleet is the quality, and drought-year earnings sit on a multiple at the 15th percentile of its ten-year history.

The earnings trough is the unproven half. Consensus has earnings falling 2.3% a year, because forward-sale prices dropped from 117 euros per megawatt-hour in the first half of 2025 to 86 euros a year later. An Investment Analyst memo should establish whether 2026 is the low point.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple. The case turns on one variable, and that variable is weather rather than management execution. Full-year guidance of 2,100 to 2,400 million euros of operating profit before depreciation assumes normal river flow for the rest of 2026. VERBUND beat its initial guidance every year from 2013 to 2024, by 7% on average, before the drought broke the run in 2025.

The main risk is an uneven pay-off. A water shortfall has no floor, while Austria's levy takes 95% of generation revenue above 90 euros per megawatt-hour until March 2030, or above about 105 to 110 euros after investment deductions. Forward sales turn a volume shortfall into a cash cost: VERBUND spent 196 million euros buying power in the first half of 2026. Dry soil also absorbs rain, so river flow recovers later than rainfall.

Two things need to go right. River flow must hold near normal through the winter, and the levy thresholds must stay where they are. The warning signs are power purchases above 50 million euros a month and any cut to the 90 euro cap. Deutsche Bank's dry-weather case puts earnings at 761 million euros, 29% below consensus.

Business quality

High at the asset level. Hydropower costs VERBUND about 20 euros per megawatt-hour in full and 5 euros at the margin, which produced a 75% hydropower margin in 2025 against a peer average near 22.5%. Water concessions run for 90 years, so no competitor can build a rival fleet.

The grid adds slow, regulated growth. The grid's regulated asset base of 3.2 billion euros is projected to reach about 9.3 billion by 2033, growth of 14% a year. Group net debt was 1.0 times operating profit before depreciation at the end of 2025, against a covenant limit of 3.0 times.

Capital allocation is the weaker point. VERBUND wrote off about 480 million euros on Spanish renewables in 2022 and 2023, and 1.1 gigawatts of Spanish solar is being built without price contracts.

Plausible returns and valuation

The valuation is low and the return rests on the multiple. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 13 November is A, a valuation reading only: 17.2 times earnings at a price of 73.7 euros. The weekly routine's plausible return of 12 November averages 19.1% a year, a B against your bar, made of 25.1% a year over eighteen months and 13.0% over three years. That return assumes the multiple rises to 23.3 times while consensus earnings fall, so rising earnings would be the upgrade to the case.

Two checks belong in a memo. Morgan Stanley's 2026 estimate of 3.04 euros a share would put the shares nearer 24 times, so the earnings base needs reconciling. In September brokers stood 71% underweight with none overweight and targets near 55 euros, so the share price has run ahead of the analysts.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros: VERBUND owns the scarce parts of the chain. Generation earns about 80% of group profit, flexible storage plants earned 274 million euros balancing the grid in 2025, and the regulator raised the allowed return on new grid assets to 6.11% for 2026. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of 13 November is A, with the shares 2% below their twelve-month high. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 12 November and the weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 13 November are both B, the second resting on the sector environment alone. The book holds no utility, a gap this stock is large enough to fill.

Cons: the government sits in the chain. Our Sector Digest of 18 October says to pick the regulator and the government before the power price, and it judged the drought a smaller theme because normal rain lifts earnings once. Batteries are the slower threat, with German capacity heading for 15 gigawatts by 2028 and narrowing the price spreads that storage plants earn. The weekly Lists judgement also recorded that three months of rotation into utilities may already have paid for the asset.

In short

  • Interesting, because an exceptional asset is priced low: hydropower at 20 euros per megawatt-hour of full cost, on 17.2 times earnings, the 15th percentile of ten years
  • One variable drives the case: river flow at 63% of normal, with each 1% of output worth about 9.6 million euros of group result
  • The return needs earnings to rise: the plausible 19.1% a year rests on the multiple moving to 23.3 times while consensus earnings fall 2.3% a year
  • Track the uneven pay-off monthly: river flow, power purchases above 50 million euros a month, and any cut to the 90 euro levy cap
  • A memo is the next step: the earnings trough and the earnings base behind 17.2 times are both unconfirmed
