EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Consensus Earnings Outlook

The only quantified broker consensus EPS in the report is 5.03 euros, and a 10% shortfall in Stellantis' adjusted operating income would push forward earnings below it; no year-specific split is given. Bloomberg consensus lists only a small handful of regional brokers with a neutral stance as of November 2026, while Morgan Stanley's screen shows a 16.0% positive earnings revision for EXOR. Drivers pointing above consensus are Ferrari and bioMérieux, since upward revisions reflect their robust performance, which offsets weaker industrial trends; Stellantis cash burn and CNH's cycle point below.

Pace of Change at EXOR

1.
Portfolio change is fast: EXOR has moved at a rapid pace to streamline investments, closing the sales of GEDI, Lifenet and Nuo and accumulating approximately 4 billion euros of deployable cash. Leadership change is gradual and planned: COO Suzanne Heywood leaves in January 2027, Benoît Ribadeau-Dumas became Deputy CEO on 1 July 2026, and John Elkann was reappointed through 2029.

Rank 1, Ferrari luxury pricing power, has high plausibility: management views it as exceeding 80% probability, and a multi-year order backlog extends into fiscal year 2028. Rank 2, bioMérieux diagnostics expansion, has high plausibility: EXOR is lifting its direct stake from 3.87% to 8.53%, and management cites strong structural public health demand. Rank 3, Royal Philips, has medium plausibility: EXOR estimates a 50% to 80% likelihood of success against a plan targeting mid-single-digit sales growth and mid-teens margins over 2026 to 2028.

Ranked Earnings Drivers, Lower Plausibility

Rank 4, CNH Industrial agricultural recovery, has medium plausibility: EXOR cites prolonged cyclical troughs, even as a Kobelco joint venture buys 70% of the Construction segment. Rank 5, the Stellantis turnaround, has low plausibility: EXOR monitors it as a low plausibility driver despite a Value Creation Program targeting 2.4 billion euros of savings by 2027. The joint probability that all five drivers succeed is only about 6% (calculated), which the report says is partly mitigated by the 4 billion euro cash pile.
