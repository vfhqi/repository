Section 2 – Operating Leverage, Capacity Utilisation, and the Savings Programme

Section summary
  1. Croda's operating leverage is starting to deliver margins as volume returns, but the recovery remains hindered by negative business mix and substantial inflationary cost pressures.
  2. Market expectations often conflate indexed shared-site volumes with absolute capacity utilization levels, ignoring the lower base utilization rates of pre-pandemic operations.

Volume Recovery and Capacity Utilisation

1.
Delayed recovery: Croda is seeing a steady volume rebuild at its eleven shared multi-purpose manufacturing facilities as destocking trends ease. According to Rothschild & Co Redburn on 9 November 2026, shared-site volumes are currently tracking approximately 7% below pre-pandemic 2019 levels. This volume return is expected to serve as the primary driver of margin expansion over the next three years unless demand patterns in agrichemicals deteriorate further.

2.
Utilisation conflation: The investment community frequently conflates Croda's reported shared-site volume indices with absolute capacity utilization rates. Rothschild & Co Redburn clarified on 9 November 2026 that while indexed volume is 7% below 2019 levels, absolute capacity utilization in 2019 was only 80% to 85%. This mismatch means that absolute manufacturing utilisation remains lower than what a simple 2019 volume comparison would suggest to investors.
3.
Shared site complexity: Croda's multi-purpose manufacturing network operates under unique constraints that differ from dedicated chemical plants. Rothschild & Co Redburn explained on 9 November 2026 that Croda's shared sites require high-volume loading to absorb a substantial fixed-cost base. Consequently, capacity utilisation remains a critical driver of profitability because the business acts as a price-taker in competitive surfactant markets.
4.
Profitability drag: Positive operational leverage at Croda has initially failed to lift the group EBIT margin significantly due to unfavorable pricing and product mix. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 30 July 2025 that despite strong volume improvements of 11% in the first half of 2025, margins remained stubbornly low. This suggests that operating leverage is being diluted by a deliberate management decision to fill plants with lower-value products during the early stage of recovery.

Cost Savings Program Execution

5.
Accelerated savings: Croda is executing a comprehensive business transformation programme to restructure its cost base and streamline enabling functions. Croda announced in its H1 2025 results presentation on 29 July 2025 that it has more than doubled its annualised cost savings target to £100m by the end of 2027. These savings represent around 8% of the company's total 2024 cost base.
6.

Croda's management indicated during the Q2 2025 earnings call on 29 July 2025 that the annualised cost savings will not entirely drop through to the bottom line on a net basis. Specifically, the company expects annual wage and overhead inflation of approximately £15m to consistently offset these self-help gains.
7.
Organizational simplification: Croda's efficiency gains are heavily focused on streamlining internal structures and corporate overhead. Croda's annual report on 16 March 2026 detailed that the company is reducing layers of management to professionalize enabling functions. This organizational simplification is designed to permanently reduce indirect costs and improve operational agility across regions.

Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on 9 November 2026 that Croda achieved £28m in savings in FY25 compared to a budget of £25m. This program is projected to contribute an incremental £25m to FY27 EBIT and £15m to FY28 EBIT to help restore operating margins.

Section 4 – Competitive Threats, Biosolutions, and Fermentation Ceramides

Section summary
  1. Competitive threats to Croda's biotechnology and active beauty segments are intensifying as major European peers and Chinese synthetic biology firms rapidly scale up production.
  2. The commercialization of fermentation-derived squalane serves as a historical precedent for how biotechnology can disrupt specialty ingredients markets and reshape profit pools.

Peer Chemical Giants and Active Beauty Threats

1.
Evonik ceramide: Evonik has launched its biotechnology-derived ceramide SPHINOX Vively at the PCHi 2026 trade show. SPHINOX Vively represents a major competitive threat because it stimulates elastin and hyaluronic acid production to deliver retinol-like anti-aging benefits with improved skin tolerance. This biosolution represents a direct challenge to Croda's Beauty Actives dominance by targeting the fast-growing Chinese skinrejuvenation market. Consequently, Evonik's rapid biotechnology expansion could erode Croda's active ingredient pricing power over the next three years.

Evonik biosurfactants: Evonik is also scaling its industrial biotechnology footprint through its pioneering rhamnolipids biosurfactants. BASF bioactives: BASF is also expanding its presence in the active beauty sector with its biotechnologically produced skin solution Plantigenix Bisabolol. 5.
Chinese collagen: Chinese biotechnology firms are rapidly scaling recombinant collagen to capture global skincare market share. Bernstein Research reports that China's recombinant collagen market is projected to expand at a 33% CAGR from 2024 to 2029 to reach RMB 72 bn. The rapid commercialization of recombinant collagen in China could cap Croda's growth in vital markets like Asia-Pacific.

Giant Biogene utilizes high-density fermentation and advanced purification to manufacture highly bio-compatible recombinant collagen products. Consequently, global giants have historically chosen to partner with Chinese collagen innovators rather than developing competing in-house solutions.
7.
LivingPHOENIX breakthrough: Chinese start-up LivingPHOENIX has achieved a major milestone by securing China's first medical device registration for biomimetic collagen. LivingPHOENIX's technology utilizes microbial fermentation of biomass substrates like corn starch to extract proline and hydroxyproline. This green biomanufacturing process overcomes traditional bottlenecks by enabling triple-helix structures to withstand terminal heat sterilization without damage. This industrialization breakthrough accelerates entry into highvalue medical dressings and implants.

Bloomage is leveraging one of the world's largest synthetic biology platforms to scale up new bioactive ingredients. This diversification creates a direct overlap with Croda's premium active cosmetic ingredients portfolio. Bloomage is also expanding its global reach by backing Los Angeles-based skincare brands to commercialize its new bio-actives in the West.
