Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The mid-term EPS trajectory of European defence suppliers is highly resolvable due to unprecedented order backlogs and capacity expansions, which insulate these niche players from broader macroeconomic volatility and political budget debates.

The European defence electronics, optronics, communications, weapons, and aerostructures investment landscape presents a compelling opportunity set driven by structural demand acceleration, sovereign technology mandates, and aggressive industrial capacity expansions.

The key question focuses on how the 18-month to 3- year EPS trajectory of European defence suppliers will respond to rising sensor, night vision, and ammunition demand.

Based on comprehensive analysis across six sub-sectors, this trajectory is highly resolvable for mid-cap specialists operating in capacity-constrained niches, partially resolvable for platform integrators facing export licensing friction, and indeterminate only for commodity aerostructure suppliers vulnerable to commercial aerospace disruptions.

The group's massive order backlogs, such as Kongsberg's expected 2.0x book-to-bill in Q3 2026, provide exceptional mid-term earnings visibility for investors.

This structural shift from demand risk to execution risk fundamentally changes the investment framework, as earnings growth becomes predictable and insulated from traditional defence budget volatility.

Mid-cap specialists like Hensoldt are insulated from budget reallocation pressures affecting larger primes.

The most exciting sub-sector growth story centers on software-defined tactical communications and ruggedized IT systems, where MilDef continues to scale operations by expanding its Rosersberg integration facilities by 7,600 square meters to meet surging NATO demand and German subsidiary roda computer secured a seven-year framework extension with the Bundeswehr worth up to EUR 527 million for tactical IT deliveries through 2032.

This segment benefits from high switching costs, long-term framework contracts, and margin-accretive software licensing models that support valuation re-ratings toward fifteen times EV/EBITA multiples. Autonomous undersea warfare represents the most defensible high-margin niche, where Exail holds a dominant European position in maritime robotics and proprietary fibre-optic gyroscope navigation systems, which are critical for operations in GPS-denied environments.

This consolidation validates the structural moat around ITAR-free sovereign navigation technology and positions Exail for exceptional EBIT margin expansion to 22.8% by 2030. Sovereign mobile satellite communications infrastructure is transforming specialist operators into high-growth defence pure-plays.

Swedish operator Ovzon's proprietary Ovzon-3 satellite commenced commercial service in July 2024, providing unique geostationary onboard processing capabilities for dismounted military forces.

The platform carries highly accretive 90% gross margins on proprietary capacity, supporting extraordinary EPS expansion to SEK 2.14 by 2027e. Optronics capacity dominance represents a very early and massive opportunity, as European defense optronics and electronics are experiencing unprecedented demand with backlogs stretching past 2030, driven by rapid military modernization, structural shortages of critical components, and aggressive manufacturing capacity expansions.

Hensoldt doubled its order intake to EUR 2,812 million in the first half of 2026, lifting its total backlog past the EUR 10 billion milestone, while Hensoldt's Optronics segment recorded a strong increase in order intake to EUR 971 million in the first half of 2026, primarily driven by major contracts for equipping Puma and Schakal vehicles.

Critical component makers like Exosens hold a dominant 70% market share in European image intensifier tubes, enabling superior pricing power as Exosens upgraded its full-year guidance to EUR 558-570 million, driven by a faster-than-expected deployment of additional European image-intensifier tube capacity. in 2029.

Differentiated manufacturing depth and sole-source positions on 19 major engine families ensure highly favorable aftermarket pricing power with inflation-indexed cost pass-throughs.

Near-term catalysts include accelerating tactical IT contract call-offs from the German Bundeswehr framework, the deployment of additional Amplification capacity in Europe for Exosens, increased commercial satellite capacity utilization for Ovzon following the landmark US government order, and the successful manufacturing restart at Melrose's Garden Grove facility in California following the August 2026 chemical leak disruption.

Export authorization delays merely defer short-term revenue recognition rather than destroying underlying contract value, while the European Commission clarified that the Sustainable Finance Disclosure Regulation does not mandate defense sector exclusions, lowering systemic barriers for asset managers and unlocking passive capital inflows through revised ESG index methodologies.

Material data gaps remain concentrated around undisclosed subcontractor component volumes, real-time export license queues, and exact margins on dual-use commercial shipments.

While these information deficits create near-term earnings volatility, analysts can partially resolve them through detailed supplier surveys and monitoring dual-use shipping data.

However, if investors closely monitor publicly available national budget allocations and company backlog disclosures, these data gaps do not prevent accurate mid-term EPS modeling, as the structural multi-year growth trajectory remains highly visible despite localized execution delays. Second-order implications point to a profound reshaping of European defence industrial economics.

Government policies to nationalize critical supply chains and enforce data sovereignty create robust regional monopolies for localized defense tech suppliers, while European defense planners are increasingly dependent on Ukrainian expertise in emerging drone technologies, cementing a long-term defense-industrial partnership.

An outlier development sees defense platform owners actively buying up bottleneck nodes like explosives and thermal batteries rather than end-product capacity to secure fragile supply chains, representing a fundamental shift in vertical integration strategies.

Cross-sector spillovers from military autonomous innovation are generating valuable commercial transport applications, broadening the addressable market for specialized defense technology. Other side of the story: If geopolitical tensions normalize after 2030, short-cycle ammunition and vehicle demand will likely contract, causing margins to mean-revert to historic single-digit levels, while if national regulators refuse to harmonize cross-border transfer procedures, the fragmented European defense market will continue to suffer from localized margin pressure and execution delays.

Section 9 – Second and third-order implications
