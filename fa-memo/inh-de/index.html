The operating drivers behind the inputs

Revenue. FY26 +15.5%: H1 actual +15.4%, H2 about +16% on Materials price still elevated in Q3 and fading in Q4, Engineering backlog conversion with its normal Q4 peak, Infrastructure digital-network demand, and about 2 points inorganic (PRO VIDEO, AMIRA, prior-year add-ons). FY27 -2.0%: Materials Solutions -13% as BETEK gives back about half of its 2026 price gain and about half of the volume taken from retreating competitors, with the hardest comparison in Q2 2027 against the Q2 2026 peak; Engineering +4% on the EUR 430m backlog (US plant projects delivering in 2027 and 2028); Infrastructure +3.5% as grid and fibre demand offsets residential weakness and the EUR 500bn German fund converts to revenue from H2 2027; about 1 point inorganic from deals done in Q4 2026 and H1 2027. FY28 to FY30 +5 to 5.5%: organic volume 2 to 3% (German capacity utilisation and construction recovering from 2028, price 1 to 2% in Infrastructure and the non-tungsten Materials units, flat in Engineering) plus 2 to 3% acquired revenue from EUR 75 to 95m a year of acquisitions at 5.5 to 7x EV/EBIT.

Margin. The FY26 to FY27 move is price leverage in reverse at BETEK (feedstock bought at USD 2,000 to 3,100 per MTU in H1 2026 processed through H2 2026 and H1 2027 under a nine-month industry cost lag, while selling prices adjusted at short intervals fall with Western APT as Chinese domestic prices have already collapsed and Sangdong supply arrives), plus about EUR 17 to 20m of German wage inflation on a semi-fixed labour base while revenue falls, only half offset by productivity. From FY28 the margin drifts up 20 to 30 basis points a year on Engineering utilisation, acquired mix (targets with EBIT margins above 10%), lean and ERP programmes and disposals of underperformers; the 2030 target of 11% is the ceiling, not the base.

Depreciation (excluding PPA): EUR 74m in FY25, rising with the Grand Rapids and Serbian facilities, acquisitions and right-of-use assets. PPA amortisation, which the model's D&A excludes, is EUR 21.5m in FY26, 22m in FY27, 24m, 25m and 26m thereafter; the brokers deduct it, so the model's EPS runs EUR 0.58 to 0.74 above the brokers' basis.

Net financial items: net interest of about EUR 25m in FY25 on average net debt of about EUR 580m at an all-in cost of about 4.3 to 4.5% (promissory notes, swapped bank debt at 2.49% on EUR 185m, lease interest), rising to about EUR 32m in FY26 on the peak working-capital funding and falling to EUR 27 to 28m as net debt falls and then re-levers with acquisitions; includes put-option revaluations on EUR 37.8m of minority commitments and other financial items.

Tax: structural rate of 33 to 34% for a holding of limited partnerships whose trade-tax losses at loss-making subsidiaries cannot be offset (EUR 5.98m of FY25 tax expense); H1 2026 ran at about 36%; the FY25 rate of 28.7% included an EUR 8.36m one-off credit. From FY28 the German corporate income tax rate falls one point a year, taking about a point a year off the group rate.

Dividend: 40 to 50% payout policy; FY26 at the low end of policy on windfall earnings with a stretched balance sheet (EUR 1.90 on EPS of about EUR 4.85 after PPA), FY27 EUR 1.50 as earnings fall, then rising with earnings.

Share count: 24.9m shares outstanding (25.8m issued less about 3.5% treasury); no change other than buybacks; no buybacks in FY26 to FY28 while working capital and acquisitions absorb cash and the share trades at the top of its own multiple range; a token EUR 10m a year in FY29 and FY30.

Capital spending (cash, fixed assets and intangibles): EUR 48m in FY25 on FactSet's cash basis against the company's EUR 60.6m "investment in property and equipment" (which I read as including right-of-use additions) and consensus of EUR 74 to 88m (which appears to be on that broader basis); 2.5 to 2.8% of sales on the cash basis.

Working capital (FactSet definition: receivables plus inventories less payables, as a percentage of trailing revenue; the company's own narrower definition was EUR 627m at June 2026 against FactSet's EUR 707m): 37.9% at June 2026, 34% at end-2026 as some inventory releases in H2, 30.5% at end-2027 as tungsten inventory values and receivables deflate (a release of about EUR 80m in FY27), then a slow structural improvement from 203 days of cash conversion cycle toward 180 days. The pre-shock level was 30 to 33%.

Leases: about EUR 22m a year on EUR 88m of lease liabilities, rising with the US facility and acquisitions; outside both the company's and FactSet's free cash flow definitions.

Acquisitions less disposals: H1 2026 EUR 16.6m plus one or two bolt-ons in Q4 2026 (the company said on 5 October 2026 it expects further acquisitions before year-end) and minority buy-outs, less MEWESTA proceeds; EUR 55 to 60m in FY27 as working capital releases and the policy thresholds (net debt to EBITDA below 2.5x, equity ratio toward 40%) are met; EUR 75m, 85m and 95m in FY28 to FY30, below the 2030 plan's EUR 100m-plus a year; disposals of 0.3 to 1% of sales a year under the 2025 policy.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
