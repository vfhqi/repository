German Succession Market Dynamics

Aging SME ownership: German small and medium enterprise succession demand is accelerating rapidly due to demographic shifts. A study by KfW Research cited in a DNB Carnegie report on 11 November 2024 showed that 30% of German SME owners are aged 60 or older. This massive wave of business handovers provides a fertile sourcing ground that will support INDUS Holding's capital deployment pace and long-term earnings per share expansion.
2.

According to KfW Research details published in a DNB Carnegie broker note on 11 November 2024, nearly 46% of SME owners in Schleswig-Holstein are aged 55 or older. Challenging operational hurdles: Small and medium enterprises in Germany face increasing operational obstacles that encourage founders to seek exits. A survey by Coface on 02 October 2026 revealed that 31% of German SME participants reported escalating obstacles in their business operations. These mounting difficulties are likely to lower pricing expectations for succession candidates, which will favor INDUS's acquisition multiples and protect its long-term profit margins.

Differentiated investment model: INDUS Holding historically distinguished itself from private equity firms by offering a unique "buy-hold-develop" strategy. Under this positioning, a NuWays AG report on August 13, 2025, highlighted that the holding committed to a strict non-exit promise for its subsidiaries. This long-term commitment was designed to preserve the brand identity and maintain regional operational structures indefinitely. Consequently, a NuWays AG note on November 13, 2025, argued that the promise served as a core competitive advantage when sourcing family-owned companies.
2.
Established seller trust: The non-exit promise built substantial goodwill among German Mittelstand founders facing succession challenges. According to broker updates from NuWays AG on July 28, 2026, INDUS is widely perceived as a trusted partner because it rejects standard exit-oriented private equity strategies. This reputation was supported by the fact that around 75% of its portfolio companies remained with the group for over 10 years. For many founders, safeguarding local employment outweighed maximizing the absolute transaction price.
3.
Preserving operational autonomy: The holding's partnership model relies on granting substantial operational freedom to its acquired subsidiaries. A research report by NuWays AG on February 25, 2026, explained that INDUS grants operational autonomy while adding value via strategic guidance and shared services. This structured independence ensures that the original owner's entrepreneurial spirit and identity are preserved alongside growth. Favorable entry pricing: The strength of INDUS's reputational positioning directly translated into financial advantages during target negotiation phases. This pricing discipline has historically been a critical driver of the holding's return on capital.

MEWESTA Divestment and Strategy Shift

5.
Historic portfolio exit: INDUS disrupted its long-standing permanent ownership paradigm by divesting a key subsidiary in late 2026. On October 5, 2026, a company press release announced the sale of its 100% stake in MEWESTA Hydraulik to the Lange Group. MEWESTA, a hydraulic control block manufacturer, had been a stable part of the Materials Solutions portfolio since 1997. This transaction marked the end of nearly 30 years of continuous ownership under INDUS.
6.
Active portfolio management: The MEWESTA disposal represents a structural shift toward more active asset rotation under the EMPOWERING MITTELSTAND strategy.
