Single-Source Vulnerability and the Borealis Relationship

1.
High concentration risk: Nexans' PWR-Transmission segment relies solely on Borealis for its standard cross- linked polyethylene (XLPE) compounds used in subsea and land high-voltage cable systems. While Borealis is a premier supplier of ultra-super-clean insulation compounds, this exclusive single-source relationship exposes Nexans to sudden upstream supply shocks. Any operational or logistics failure at Borealis directly threatens manufacturing schedules at Halden and Charleroi because qualifying alternative compound suppliers typically takes several months.
2.
Technical qualification barriers: The high-voltage cable industry requires rigorous, system-specific qualification processes that prevent rapid supplier switching. For example, Terna Group initiated extensive experimental protocols with the University of Bologna specifically to validate AC cables utilizing XLPE insulation produced by Borealis' reactors. Because accessories and cable compounds are qualified together as an integrated system, Nexans cannot easily substitute Borealis XLPE without triggering years of expensive re-testing.
3.
Downstream project execution risks: Any disruption in XLPE supply translates directly to project delays and financial penalties in the PWR-Transmission business. Cable manufacturers face severe downside risks from weak execution and delays in high-voltage project delivery. Consequently, Nexans' single-source bottleneck could lead to substantial liquidated damages from utility customers if compound shortages halt the Halden or Charleston extrusion lines.

Hormuz Disruption and Petrochemical Cost Pressures

4.
Upstream feedstock shock: The Strait of Hormuz military confrontation beginning in late February 2026 has severely disrupted global petrochemical feedstocks. Crude oil refining yields naphtha, which is then cracked to produce ethylene and propylene, the essential building blocks for polyethylene. Because the Gulf region exports massive volumes of naphtha and downstream polymers, the Hormuz closure triggered a severe petrochemical input shock that is driving up polymer manufacturing costs globally. Persistent price volatility: Although a brief ceasefire in June 2026 temporarily eased energy and chemical markets, renewed hostilities have extended the disruption. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz continues to restrict normal commercial shipping, keeping petrochemical risk premiums elevated heading into 2027. The Economist Intelligence Unit projects that traffic through the Strait of Hormuz will not normalize until mid- 2027, ensuring persistent volatility in polymer contract prices in the meantime.

Morgan Stanley Research shows that European average contract polyethylene prices remained highly elevated throughout 2026, hovering near multi-year highs. Alternative Sourcing and Mitigation Strategies

6.
Peer diversification strategies: Nexans' competitors have actively structured their supply chains to avoid single- source dependencies. Prysmian has implemented a robust qualification system to collaborate with multiple selected suppliers of critical raw materials. Safety stock buffers: To mitigate short-term supply chain disruptions, cable manufacturers are forced to hold larger inventories. Furthermore, because high-voltage cable demand exceeds available manufacturing capacity globally, Nexans can successfully pass through raw material cost increases directly into its utility framework agreements. Ultimately, the tight market for high-voltage cable slots means grid operators are willing to absorb polymer surcharges to secure their delivery timelines.
