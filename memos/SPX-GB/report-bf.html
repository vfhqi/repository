EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business model resilience and organizational momentum

1.
Mission-critical portfolio positioning: Spirax operates a differentiated three-segment portfolio delivering highly resilient mission-critical process and thermal solutions globally, with Steam Thermal Solutions generating GBP 853 million, Electric Thermal Solutions expanding process heating capabilities, and Watson- Marlow commanding high-precision fluid technology margins of 26.2%.
2.
Defensively insulated revenue model: approximately 85% of group sales are funded from customer operating budgets with recurring maintenance orders comprising 45% of total revenues, protecting the business from capital expenditure cycles and delivering small average invoice sizes of circa GBP 3,000.
3.
Scaled direct engineering footprint: the consultative sales model leverages 2,900 technical sales engineers globally complemented by twenty-two US co-generation distribution partnerships that delivered 6% firsthalf 2026 growth, successfully expanding addressable markets without triggering channel conflict.
4.
Restructuring operating leverage unlocked: STS EMEA consolidation reduced operating companies from nineteen to ten and delivered 20% more customer-facing time alongside GBP 40 million in annualized savings, while establishing Heat Trace as a standalone division drove double-digit growth in 2025.
5.
Experienced leadership transition: former CFO Nimesh Patel assumed Group CEO in January 2024 with Louisa Burdett commencing as CFO in July 2024, establishing focused execution discipline to restore historical compounding growth trajectories.

Financial profile and peer benchmarking performance

6.
Muted near-term organic growth: group organic revenue grew by only 5% in fiscal year 2025 with Steam Thermal Solutions achieving just 1% growth due to biopharma destocking and deferred Chinese capital projects, representing a significant deceleration from historical peaks despite outperforming global industrial production growth of 2.1%.
7.
Segmented growth divergence patterns: Watson-Marlow division achieved 6% organic sales growth in 2025 with second-half biopharma recovery accelerating above 10%, while Electric Thermal Solutions faced margin pressure at 16.2% despite manufacturing throughput improvements.
8.
Stable but depressed profitability: group adjusted operating margin reached 20.0% in fiscal year 2025 with 30 basis points of organic expansion, though statutory operating profit declined to GBP 265 million on restructuring costs and amortisation of acquired intangibles remained flat at GBP 35 million.
9.
Rebounding cash conversion trajectory: adjusted cash conversion recovered to 89% in 2025 from 87% in 2024, though first-half 2026 conversion fell to 54% on defensive inventory builds to mitigate Middle East shipping disruptions.
10.
Superior capital returns maintained: return on capital employed improved to 36.0% in 2025 from 35.5% in 2024, though return on invested capital remained sub-target at 13.1% due to asset amortisation impacts, while net capital expenditure declined to a disciplined 3.8% of sales.
11.
Conservative balance sheet discipline: net debt settled at 1.5x net debt to EBITDA meeting the target ceiling, while the company extended its fifty-eight year record of progressive dividend increases with a 3% boost and deployed GBP 11 million for strategic bolt-on acquisitions.
12.
Peer benchmarking lag: Spirax's three-year revenue CAGR of 1.8% significantly trails Halma's 14.9% and peer ROIC of 13.1% underperforms Halma's 16.2%, though EBITDA margin of 23.7% remains competitive while net working capital days of 80 exceed Halma's superior 65 days.

13.
Exceptional customer diversification: sales are spread across over 100,000 direct buying customers with no single account exceeding 1% of total revenue, while defensive sectors represent 60% of revenues with food and beverage comprising 20% and pharmaceutical applications contributing 17%.

Sustained premium pricing power: local technical expertise justifies a 20-30% price premium in key European markets through value-based consultative audits that identify unrecognized customer savings, while localized manufacturing footprints and pricing surcharge programs successfully mitigate tariff exposures without triggering volume loss.
16.
Disciplined capital allocation framework: capital deployment prioritizes high-return organic investments over large-scale transactions with historical ROCE reaching 36%, maintaining target leverage of 1.0x to 1.5x net debt to EBITDA, and protecting through-cycle profitability via 85% OPEX-funded revenue streams despite unprecedented biopharma and semiconductor destocking cycles in 2023-2024.
17.
Structural moat depth validation: the specialized network of 2,900 technical sales engineers cannot be easily replicated by regional distributor-based competitors and requires three years of specialized training per engineer, while the massive catalog of specialized engineering products blocks competitors from addressing 30% of the critical steam systems market and secures specification rates up to 90% in European projects.
18.
Medium-term growth enablement: strategic targets seek mid-single-digit organic revenue growth outperforming industrial production with adjusted operating margins of 22-23% and ROIC improvement above 15%, while volume recovery in biopharma will drive margin drop-through up to 200 basis points and adjusted cash conversion targets consistently exceed 80% over medium-term horizons.

Operator confidence assessment: business quality confidence is HIGH given unparalleled customer diversification and mission-critical applications, though the largest quality risk remains failure to upskill technical sales teams against rising competitor knowledge with deterioration in adjusted cash conversion below 80% flagging rising structural capital intensity as the key early-warning signal.

Disruption forces and structural evolution outlook

20.
Exceptional entry barrier height: demanding regulatory validation and ISO 13485 certification requirements prevent low-cost competitors from entering biopharma fluid path markets, while the specialized 2,900- engineer direct sales network cannot be replicated and a massive specialized product catalog blocks competitors from addressing 30% of critical steam applications.
21.
Manageable electrification transition: decarbonisation of steam systems presents a multi-decade conversion opportunity across 500,000 industrial boilers globally with customers actively adopting proprietary electric SteamVolt boilers and TargetZero solutions, while peristaltic pumps continue displacing traditional displacement technologies due to contamination-free transfers.
22.

23.
Secular adjacency expansion: ETS developed heating products critical to liquid-cooled datacenter load banks with accelerating low and medium-voltage sales into the expanding global nuclear sector, while revenue has consistently outperformed industrial production by 2.1x to 2.4x during expansion cycles and local Devens manufacturing mitigates direct tariff exposure of roughly 6% of group revenue.
24.
Foundation verdict: ADEQUATE – the business maintains strong through-cycle resilience via exceptional customer diversification and regulatory switching costs, but sub-peer organic growth momentum, elevated working capital days, and slow digital platform integration prevent a "strong" rating until consultative sales upskilling closes the narrowing competitive knowledge gap.
