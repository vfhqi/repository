APM Mini-Memo: Subsea 7

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 1 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 8 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: Mini-Memo card of 1 October 2026 (price and script ratings as at 29 and 30 September), change-forces report of 21 September, earnings-review report of 22 September and business-foundations report of 23 September 2026; no Investment Analyst memo.

The one-line view. Neutral, working hypothesis: a best-in-class offshore contractor delivering record margins on unusually full order books, but the shares you would own from late 2026 are a nil-premium merger with lower-margin Saipem, near what may be the top of the offshore margin cycle, so earnings per share beyond 2026 are less predictable than the standalone numbers suggest.

What the company does

Subsea 7 designs, builds and installs the seabed pipework that links offshore oil and gas wells to production facilities, using its own fleet of 35 vessels. Subsea and Conventional, mostly deepwater subsea umbilicals, risers and flowlines, is 82% of revenue; Renewables, branded Seaway7, installs offshore wind farms and is about 16%. Most revenue comes from lump-sum engineering, procurement, construction and installation contracts, where Subsea 7 carries cost overruns, and Brazil is 24% of revenue and over 30% of backlog. 2025 revenue was $7.1 billion; market value is 8,643.5 million euros at 325 kroner on 30 September.

A plausible investment thesis

Bullish on the standalone mechanism, unproven on the merged one. Contracts won at higher prices since 2024 are replacing legacy low-margin work, in a market with only twenty high-specification rigid pipelay vessels across nine operators and none being built. Adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (cash profit from here) rose 37% to $1.49 billion in 2025, a 21% margin against 16% in 2024. Management has raised 2026 margin guidance twice, from 22% to about 24%, and beat its initial guidance by 12% in 2024 and 11% in 2025.

The mechanism to earnings per share is margin, not volume. Revenue guidance is flat at $7.0 to $7.4 billion against $7.11 billion in 2025, so on my arithmetic a 24% margin at the midpoint takes cash profit to roughly $1.73 billion, about 16% up. Beyond 2026 the merger with Saipem (50-50 ownership, €300 million of annual synergies targeted by 2029) replaces the standalone earnings stream; earnings per share forecasts are not in our files.

Drivers to track:
- The European Commission Phase 2 decision, statutory deadline 26 November 2026, and the Australian review
- Fleet utilisation (87% in the second quarter) and pricing on new awards
- 2027 and 2028 backlog coverage, now 72% and 38% against historical averages of 56% and 30%
- The second-half working capital outflow, guided at $100 to $200 million

The Target Setup

There is no Investment Analyst memo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and this read is mine. The Target Setup is 'Demand-driven EPSU': the offshore spending upcycle and tight vessel supply lift pricing, and the better-priced backlog lifts margins as it executes. Once the merger closes, 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU' could combine with it through synergies, but before clearance that leg is a risk more than a driver. It is not a reversion-to-normal setup: there is no cause for concern to clear, margins are at records and the shares stand about 9% below their 52-week high.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

No memo rating exists; my read is that near-term delivery is low-risk but the case is not simple. Backlog covers 97% of 2026 revenue against a historical 92%, and the change-forces report calls management a conservative sandbagger. Against that, lump-sum projects book most profit in the final 20% of execution, so delays defer earnings, and the merger faces Phase 2 reviews with cancellation rights if required divestments exceed €500 million. Our files also conflict on Brazil: one report says approval was unconditional in June, another that appeals including Petrobras's pushed the final decision to November.

Business quality

Strong for a cyclical, but cyclical. The business-foundations report of 23 September calls foundation quality strong. Barriers are real: a high-specification vessel costs up to $900 million with a 50% cash deposit, and deepwater installation is a three-player market. 2025 free cash flow more than doubled to $1.2 billion, and net debt of $602 million became net cash of $21 million including leases. But return on capital employed was 9% in 2025, below the cost of capital, and net operating income was $105 million at the 2023 low.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C, on the environment only and provisional; your interest level in the sector is none. Pros: offshore oil and gas spending is in a multi-year expansion, with final investment decision spending projected to peak around 2027 at $149 billion; no new pipelay vessels are coming; the OneSubsea alliance with SLB targets over $9 billion of bookings in 24 months. Cons: that 2027 peak is close; offshore wind outlooks have deteriorated outside the United Kingdom; and Brazil's weight makes Petrobras both the key customer and an objector to the merger. The script's Sell-side Momentum reading of 29 September is B and its Technical Momentum reading of 30 September is B, though twelve-month relative strength of 86 trails the sector's 90.

The strongest counter-case

You would be buying a cyclical at peak margins just before it turns into a different company. DNB Carnegie warns margins may peak by 2027 or 2028, offshore spending decisions peak around 2027, and the second quarter's cash came partly from a seasonal working capital release that reverses this half. A price to earnings ratio of 12.3 looks moderate, but cyclicals look cheapest at the top. The merger then blends Subsea 7's 21% margin with Saipem's 11.1%, at nil premium, with synergies not complete until 2029 and regulators able to force disposals. For an investor who wants predictable earnings per share over 18 months to three years, the predictable part is mostly 2026.
