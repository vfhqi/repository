The CMS Performance Tier Framework

1.
Impending regulatory deadline: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services is finalizing its performance-based tiering system for organ procurement organizations in late 2026. This final rule ties an OPO's certification directly to the performance of its designated service area with current agreements expiring in January 2027. These strict outcome measures will force underperforming organizations to compete to retain their territories or face immediate decertification. This regulatory pressure will directly dictate long-term perfusion equipment sales and consumable volumes.
2.
Upheld legal validity: A federal district court in Florida recently granted summary judgment to the government, upholding the legality of the 2020 OPO final rule. The landmark decision on September 28, 2026, confirmed that the competitive ranking system is valid despite industry challenges. This ruling establishes a clear legal path for CMS to proceed with full implementation and decertify underperforming OPOs. Resolving this litigation reduces regulatory delays, meaning that transplant stakeholders must prepare for rapid system consolidation.
3.
Tiered performance benchmarks: Under the new regulatory framework, OPOs are evaluated based on donation and transplantation rates. Tier 1 OPOs represent the top 25% of performers and receive automatic four-year recertification. OPOs falling into Tier 2 must compete for their territories, while Tier 3 OPOs face automatic decertification pending administrative appeals. This high-stakes environment means OPOs must quickly adopt advanced preservation and logistics solutions to survive.
4.
Redefined area accountability: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services has shifted accountability by assigning performance tiers at the designated service area level rather than the OPO itself. This structural shift allows a single high-performing OPO to oversee multiple separate service areas and choose whether to merge them.
