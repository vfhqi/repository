9 The sell-side response to the H1 results was marked by skepticism regarding the sustainability of the hybrid develop-and-sell model. Morgan Stanley's Arthur Sitbon noted that while the hybrid platform helps defend the investment-grade credit rating, it introduces higher earnings volatility, with asset sales diluting the highly predictable core operational EBITDA of the business, and the broker expects underlying EBITDA to trend downwards without capital gains.

Bernstein's Jorge Alonso Suils observed that the pivot to a capital recycling model supports near-term cash requirements, but warned that the fragmented nature of the remaining pipeline could depress long-term capital returns, with incremental capital deployed remaining value dilutive across diverse geographies. ANE has abandoned its rigid, volume-based 20 GW capacity target in favor of a flexible, value-driven target of approximately 0.7 GW net additions per year, compatible with balance sheet restrictions and assuming gross annual additions of 1.0-1.3 GW offset by 0.4-0.5 GW of selective asset rotations.

Management has initiated an organizational optimization program targeting €40 million in annualized savings by 2027, with a €21 million H1 2026 restructuring provision booked to accelerate workforce and overhead reductions. This strategic recalibration prioritizes balance sheet preservation over unconstrained growth, but raises fundamental questions about whether the company can maintain attractive returns while systematically monetizing its highest-quality mature assets.

The operational performance deterioration in Spain presents a structural challenge that extends beyond cyclical power price weakness. Spanish baseload prices fell 20.9% to €49.4/MWh in the first half of 2026, while peakload prices collapsed 39.7% to €30.0/MWh, driven by solar generation reaching record levels and pushing spot prices to near-zero during peak daylight hours. The transition to the new 2026-2028 regulatory period in Spain introduced a rise in regulated revenues to €10.7/MWh compared to €3.7/MWh in H1 2025, but this was heavily offset by a sharp reduction in the regulatory banding mechanism, which swung to a net negative contribution of - €2.0/MWh from the positive +€10.5/MWh recorded in H1 2025. These structural headwinds are compounded by grid congestion, with more than 500 hours of zero or negative pricing recorded during the 2025 calendar year, driving down solar capture rates and forcing unhedged developers to curtail output, with grid saturation compressing realized solar load factors to between 15% and 17%.

Management is deploying tactical responses to mitigate cannibalization risks, including battery hybridization and data center colocation strategies. ANE has approved and is launching construction of two new battery energy storage systems in Palencia and Cuenca, Spain, totaling 33 MW with late 2027 commissioning scheduled, representing a tactical push to protect margins from daytime price cannibalization in its home market.

The company is negotiating behind-the-meter PPAs with data center operators to provide a stable floor price while retaining electricity market upside, representing an innovative channel to monetize underutilized merchant capacity and bypass grid saturation. However, the company temporarily halted construction on two major Texas storage projects totaling 400 MW due to tariff policy headwinds, with this precautionary delay implemented in 2025 to contain near-term capital expenditure requirements during a period of rising financial leverage.

The investment case over an 18-month to 3-year horizon hinges on whether the asset rotation program can deliver consistent 25%+ IRRs while maintaining operational earnings stability. The strongest bull case rests on three pillars: first, if Spanish power prices recover in H2 above the €100/MWh forward curves and the company successfully collects the €498 million of proceeds from the Galp and White Summit deals, the full-year targets will be easily achieved; second, better than expected project IRRs, supported by pricing power, remain a major upside risk, and if the company successfully recontracted its PPAs at higher levels, the underlying earnings rebound would materialize much faster than consensus models; and third, parent company Acciona SA, which owns 91% of ANE, can buy shares off the open market without public communication, providing a valuation floor through buyout optionality, though it remains highly dependent on parent cash constraints.

The core bear case centers on structural earnings dilution and balance sheet strain. While management focused on locked-in H2 capital gains to reiterate their €1.2 billion EBITDA target, Morgan Stanley's Arthur Sitbon warned that the underlying operational EBITDA remains under severe pressure, and if transaction gains are stripped out, the EBITDA from Operations could drop to the €800-900 million range, indicating that the asset rotations are merely masking weak core generation cashflows.

While the shift to a flexible, IRR-driven capital model protects the balance sheet, it risks structurally depleting the company's long-term asset base, and if management is forced to continually sell down its highest-yielding, mature assets to fund lower-return pipeline projects, the overall return profile of the business could deteriorate, with this strategy making the earnings trajectory highly dependent on lumpy, volatile capital gains rather than predictable, recurring generation cash flows, ultimately undermining the stock's premium valuation relative to pure-play utility peers.

The most critical unanswered question is whether ANE can execute its hybrid develop-and-sell platform without systematically eroding its operational earnings base. Management has demonstrated pricing power in select international markets and secured attractive private market valuations for mature assets, but the velocity and scale of required divestments to protect the credit rating may force the company to monetize premium assets at an unsustainable pace.

The structural challenge is amplified by the fact that new capacity additions in a lowpower-price environment generate lower operational EBITDA per megawatt than the mature, high-quality assets being sold, creating a negative compounding effect on the quality of the remaining portfolio. If this dynamic persists, the company's transition from a pure growth story to a capital recycling platform will fail to deliver the risk-adjusted returns that justify its current valuation premium versus regulated utility peers, particularly when adjusted for the elevated earnings volatility introduced by lumpy transaction gains.

Section 16 – Conclusion 1 finding

The H1 2026 earnings event fundamentally reframes the Acciona Energías Renovables investment case as a transitional value trap rather than a compelling growth opportunity over the 18-month to 3-year horizon. The reported EBITDA of €388 million, down 57.3% year-on-year and missing consensus by 6%, exposes the core weakness of a business model now critically dependent on lumpy capital gains to mask deteriorating operational generation economics.

The severe captured price deflation in Spain, where prices collapsed 26.7% to €59.3/MWh, driven by structural solar cannibalization and zero-price hours, represents not a cyclical dip but a fundamental recalibration of renewable power economics in saturated markets.

The central question of whether the asset rotation model can deliver predictable 25%+ IRRs must be answered with high conviction in the negative when evaluated on a risk-adjusted, sustained basis. While the July 2026 Galp Spanish wind sale secured an impressive €255 million capital gain on €432 million of equity value, this hybrid model introduces higher volatility into EBITDA, as operational generation margins are diluted by asset deconsolidations while near-term profits depend on volatile transaction closings.

Morgan Stanley's Arthur Sitbon warned that if transaction gains are stripped out, the EBITDA from Operations could drop to the €800-900 million range, indicating that the asset rotations are merely masking weak core generation cashflows. The mathematics are unforgiving: the group added only 245 MW of new capacity while net financial debt increased

6.8% to €4,442 million during H1, reflecting €324 million in net investment cash outflow. This negative free cash flow dynamic, even before considering the back-ended H2 disposal proceeds, demonstrates that the underlying business is consuming rather than generating economic value at current power price levels.

The strongest bull case counter-hypothesis rests on three interconnected catalysts that could materially alter the investment thesis. First, if Spanish wholesale prices rapidly recover above €100/MWh supported by better-thanexpected project IRRs and successful PPA recontracting at higher levels, the underlying earnings rebound would materialize much faster than consensus models. Second, if regional energy demand grows by 20% to 25% annually driven by AI data center expansion as projected by industry experts, the current power surplus would be absorbed much faster than consensus expects, instantly driving PPA pricing higher and restoring highmargin cash flows for ANE's domestic fleet.

Third, if battery technology costs continue to fall rapidly and wider midday-to-evening price spreads strengthen the investment case for batteries in Spain, developers can capture high-value evening peak prices, lifting project IRRs well above conservative baseline assumptions. These three forces operating in concert could transform ANE from a defensive deleveraging story into a genuine re-rating opportunity, particularly given the parent company Acciona SA's 91% stake and the ability to buy shares off the open market without public communication, which provides a valuation floor and potential minority squeeze-out scenarios.

However, the base case remains structurally challenged by irreconcilable tensions between credit preservation and organic growth. The Board's radical dividend cut from €0.44 to €0.03 per share, totaling just €10 million and significantly below the consensus expectation of €0.53, signals that management prioritizes BBB- rating defense over shareholder distributions. The abandonment of the rigid 20 GW capacity target in favor of a flexible, value-driven strategy with only approximately 0.7 GW net additions per year confirms that management has permanently downshifted growth ambitions to align with balance sheet constraints.

This strategy risks structurally depleting the company's long-term asset base, as management may be forced to continually sell down its highest-yielding mature assets to fund lower-return pipeline projects, making the earnings trajectory highly dependent on lumpy, volatile capital gains rather than predictable, recurring generation cash flows, which could ultimately undermine the stock's premium valuation relative to pure-play utility peers.

What would change this assessment requires monitoring specific, quantifiable indicators over the next 12 to 18 months. First, Spanish captured prices must stabilize above €65/MWh on a rolling four-quarter basis without requiring aggressive hedging drawdowns that sacrifice long-term contracted revenue visibility. Second, operational EBITDA excluding asset rotations must exceed €950 million annually by year-end 2027, demonstrating that the underlying generation business can self-fund its maintenance capex and modest growth without structural capital depletion.

Third, net financial debt must fall below €3.0 billion by Q4 2026 as management committed, proving that the back-ended H2 disposal program is executable without requiring firesale pricing. Fourth, the delayed MacIntyre wind farm in Australia must reach 70-80% capacity operational by yearend with full commissioning in Q1 2027, validating that the group can still execute large-scale greenfield projects without material cost overruns.

Fifth, the €40 million annualized efficiency program targeting 2027 delivery must materialize with verifiable structural cost reductions, proving management can defend margins operationally rather than solely through financial engineering.
