Outliers worth noting

Sixt's decade-long average EBT margin of 11.4% between 2015 and 2025 represents a statistical outlier in the global car rental industry, particularly when benchmarked against the negative 1.5% and negative 9.9% EBT margins posted by Avis and Hertz respectively in 2025. This profitability gap justifies Sixt's historical trading premium of approximately 100% on P/E multiples relative to public peers, driven by sustainably higher margins and conservative depreciation and amortization reporting.

While management attributes this performance to disciplined capacity management and premium fleet positioning, the magnitude of outperformance–spanning multiple economic cycles and persisting through the COVID-19 pandemic recovery–suggests structural advantages that competitors have been unable or unwilling to replicate. The operator commands a 70% revenue per day premium over European competitor Europcar and runs approximately 40% above standard US competitors, indicating pricing power that materially exceeds what would be expected from brand positioning alone in a commoditized short-term rental market.

Sixt's tripling of US market share from 1.2% in 2020 to 3.7% in 2025 constitutes an exceptional outlier in penetration velocity within a mature, highly consolidated oligopoly.
