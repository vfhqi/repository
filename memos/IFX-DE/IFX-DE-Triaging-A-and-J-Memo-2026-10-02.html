[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Infineon is a 'Demand Inflection' case whose operations are compelling and fully evidenced down the chain, at a price that already pays for them. A structural, contractually evidenced surge in AI data-centre power demand is landing on the number one power-semiconductor manufacturer while its fabs carry €650m of idle cost, its cost base has been cut by Step Up and the automotive and industrial cycle turns up beneath it. At €59.42 (2026-10-01) the shares stand at 15.6x consensus earnings two years out, the stock's own ten-year median, and on our modal path the 'Modal return' is 1.7% a year over eighteen months and 4.5% a year over three, against the framework's 20% bar.

  • The external change is a demand explosion of a strength rarely seen, and it is written into contracts.
    • Dedicated AI data-centre power revenue was €250m in FY2024 and over €700m in FY2025
    • It is guided at over €1.6bn for FY2026 (raised from €1.5bn on 5-Aug-26), and the FY2027 target of €2.5bn is described by management as an indicative floor, to be raised materially on 10-Nov-26.
    • Capacity agreements: more than ten hyper-scalers and AI chipmakers have signed capacity reservation agreements covering high-single-digit billions of euros cumulatively, with prepayments and volume-shortfall penalties, so the visibility exceeds two years and part of the demand risk has moved to the customer.
    • The cycle beneath it: the group order backlog reached €30bn at end-June 2026, up €5bn in the quarter, about two-thirds of it for delivery in FY2027
      • Q3 FY2026 revenue was a record €4,172m, +12.6% year on year, the sixth consecutive quarterly rise from the trough of €3,424m in Q1 FY2025
      • Lead times are extending and cancellations falling.
  • The internal change is a second engine, real but secondary to the demand.
    • Spare 300mm silicon IGBT lines in Dresden and Villach were reallocated to AI server MOSFETs, which cut FY2026 idle costs from €800m to €650m
    • Step Up delivers high-triple-digit-million euro annual savings, two-thirds in FY2026 and the full run-rate in H1 FY2027
    • Commoditised high-voltage automotive silicon is being pruned from over 10% to about 7% of automotive sales
    • Non-automotive silicon MOSFET prices rose by double digits in April and July 2026
    • The €5bn Dresden fab opened three months early.
    • Leadership: unchanged, which is the choice rather than the change
      • The chief executive's contract was extended early, on 19-Feb-26, to March 2032.
      • The forces are cost and mix, not renewal.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned, and the sell-side has followed.
    • Revenue growth goes from -2.0% in FY2025 to +11.5% guided for FY2026 and +22% in consensus for FY2027
    • Segment Result Margin from 17.5% in FY2025 to about 20% guided for FY2026 and towards the 25% target
    • Brokers'-basis EPS from €1.39 (FY2025) to €1.75 (FY2026 consensus), €2.84 (FY2027) and €3.81 (FY2028 mean).
    • Printed evidence: adjusted gross margin 42.8% in Q3 FY2026, up 180 basis points in a quarter
      • Power Systems margin 24.9%, up 450 basis points
      • Q4 FY2026 guided at about €4.7bn of revenue and about 23% Segment Result Margin.
    • Estimates: consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 25.5% in six months and sits at the top of its twelve-month range (€2.19 to €2.84)
      • Net EPS revisions are +26% over six months, the buy share is 71% and management has pre-announced a raise.
  • The change has travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • From the capacity pivot and Step Up (resources, choices and actions), through backlog, AI revenue and segment margins (key operating metrics), into the Q3 FY2026 financial statements and into the sell-side's estimates.
    • Nothing in the operations is left for the market to discover
    • The question is what the price assumes.
  • Our modal path earns less than consensus, because we hold the margin at the company's own target and grow at the market's rate after FY2028.
    • We take revenue to €22,500m in FY2028 (+15.3% a year from FY2025), Segment Result Margin to 24.0% in FY2027 and 25.5% in FY2028, and EPS to €2.71 (FY2027, 5% below consensus) and €3.28 (FY2028, 12% below the €3.81 mean).
    • After FY2028 we grow at 8% to 10% a year, because management's own 2030 framework (an €8bn to €12bn AI power addressable market at a 30% to 40% share, so €2.4bn to €4.8bn of revenue) places the AI line in FY2028 at the level the company expects for 2030, after which the line grows with its market.
    • The gap to consensus: the sell-side has the margin near 27% in FY2028 and 12% to 15% growth a year beyond
      • The whole 7-point gap between our 33% and consensus's 40% EPS compound rate from FY2025 to FY2028 is the FY2028 margin (about €0.27 of EPS) and revenue (about €0.09), our arithmetic.
  • At €59.42 the shares already capitalise consensus FY2028 earnings at the stock's own median multiple. 15.6x consensus EPS 24 months forward is the 45th percentile of the ten-year range and the 67th of five years
    • 20.9x consensus FY2027 on a one-year-forward footing
    • 18.1x our own FY2028.
    • The shares rose 74.9% in twelve months and fell 23.0% in three, a third from the late-July peak of €88.00, while estimates rose
    • The research explains only the 6% fall after the 5-Aug-26 results, and the remainder is not explained by any file we hold.
    • Peers: against the automotive-chip cohort (STMicroelectronics 14.9x, Melexis 15.5x, Elmos 16.2x) the multiple is at par
      • Against the sector median of about 24x it is a discount that reflects the cohort, not the company.
  • The return does not clear the bar on our path, so the chain holds through the operations and breaks at the price.
    • Valued at 16.3x the earnings two years beyond each horizon (the ten-year median multiple, which is also the odds-weighted blend of our three multiple branches), the shares are worth €60.95 including cash returns at eighteen months (2028-04-01) and €67.76 at three years (2029-10-01): 1.7% and 4.5% a year.
    • The consensus path at the same multiple gives about 20% a year, which is what the price assumes.
    • The swing factor is the multiple, worth about 12 points of annual return across its 13.96x to 19.2x range, and behind it the growth rate after FY2028, which is where our path and the price diverge.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', crisply on three of five conditions, with 'Major Corporate Change' as the secondary.
    • The framework's own caution applies: the inflection is cyclical until proven structural, and at this price the proof has to be structural.

  • The case is therefore the right company, fully evidenced, at a price that already pays for consensus delivery and a modest re-rating.

    • A B in its forces and its foundations and an F in its return from here.
    • We park for valuation, with re-entry below about €46 (about €42 on the adverse drop-through) or on evidence at the November results that growth beyond FY2028 is above our 10% a year.

  • Rating = C, because the input forces (B) and the financial outputs (B) are strong and far advanced, but the 'Modal return' on our path is an F at today's price, and the return is the Element that answers this Building Block's question

    • The parts bind, so the weaker sets the level.
    • Not D, because the error band on the return is wide: the consensus path at the same ten-year-median multiple gives about 20% a year, and consensus has been right more than wrong in this upcycle (FY2027 EPS up 25.5% in six months).
    • Not B, because no reading of our own path reaches the bar from €59.42.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

    • The strongest case against our C is the sell-side's: that this is a structural breakout, not a cycle, and that we are wrong to cap growth after FY2028.

    • The case against, at its strongest: capacity reservation agreements with more than ten hyper-scalers and AI chipmakers covering high-single-digit billions of euros, with prepayments and shortfall penalties

      • A Dresden fab that adds over €2bn of revenue capacity in two to three years and up to €5bn at maturity
      • A CPU-inference second engine (the CPU power addressable market from $1.2bn in 2025 to $6.0bn by 2030, on Bernstein's sizing)
      • Bernstein's FY2028 AI revenue of €4.5bn and a Segment Result Margin near 30%
      • A 42% EPS compound growth rate through FY2028
      • Price targets of €96 to €124
      • A stock at the third-lowest multiple of its sector with the sector's best momentum.
    • What we concede: the AI line could exceed our FY2028 figure by about €1bn, worth roughly €0.25 of EPS at a 40% segment margin and 22% tax on 1,308m shares, our arithmetic
      • And a peak-cycle Segment Result Margin of 27% is possible.
      • Together the two take our FY2028 EPS to about €3.80, the consensus mean, and the eighteen-month return at 16.3x to about 10% a year: from F to D, not to B.
    • Why we hold our view: the price's assumption is not FY2028 but the compounding after it.
      • Management's own framework puts the 2030 AI power addressable market at €8bn to €12bn with a 30% to 40% share target, which is €2.4bn to €4.8bn of revenue in 2030, a level the sell-side has the company reaching in FY2027 to FY2028
      • So the inflection is front-loaded and then the line grows at the market, and the rolling 24-month-forward earnings that the valuation convention capitalises decelerate sharply from April 2028.
      • The stock's own history says the multiple falls with the sector regardless of delivery.
      • The operations are a B
      • The price is not.
    • Where the case against would win: a raised 2030 framework in November, or Dresden utilisation disclosure implying more than €2bn of revenue by FY2028.
      • Either moves our growth after FY2028 and with it the return
      • That is the re-entry trigger we name.

    • The company spends a sixth of sales on capacity and converts well under its earnings into free cash.
    • It is a share-price risk, not a case risk, unless the Dresden capital fails to earn a return.
