EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Elia Group stands at an inflection point driven by a €31.6 billion capital expansion program through 2028, marking the most material strategic transformation in the company's history as it transitions from a traditional transmission system operator to a pan-European offshore grid infrastructure platform. Leadership continuity has been preserved through the appointment of Bernard Gustin as CEO in January 2025 and Marco Nix as CFO in April 2025, though the voluntary resignation of 25-year veteran Catherine Vandenborre represents a significant loss of institutional knowledge during this critical expansion phase. The absence of active activist investors in the AlphaSense library confirms that Elia's reference shareholder structure remains stable, with NextGrid Holding controlling 44.8% of shares and providing long-term capital backing for the unprecedented grid build-out.

Recent delivery demonstrates accelerating financial momentum, with annual revenue reaching EUR 4,273 million in FY25 and projected to surge to EUR 7,763 million by FY28, driven by the rapid deployment of regulatory capital. The group has established a consistent pattern of exceeding conservative guidance, delivering adjusted net profit beats in both FY24 and FY25 through a combination of internal procurement efficiencies and external regulatory tax rate adjustments in Germany. However, international diversification efforts have encountered severe headwinds, with Elia recognizing a EUR 99 million impairment on its US portfolio in FY25 and establishing a EUR 28.3 million provision for onerous remaining commitments, forcing a strategic pivot back to core European markets.

The top eight forward earnings drivers rank as follows, reflecting a balanced mix of regulatory catalysts, funding execution, and operational delivery risks. First, the German Regulatory Framework RP5 transition represents the most critical catalyst, as BNetzA shifts transmission system operators to an annual cost-plus WACC-based model targeting a post-tax equity return above 6% and an all-in regulated return of approximately 8% commencing January 2029.

Second, the Belgian Regulatory Framework for 2028-2031 has been finalized by CREG, delivering high return visibility with an all-in realized ROE of approximately 8.2% and an expanded incentive package that theoretically supports up to 3.75% of additional returns. Third, hybrid debt financing execution has successfully de-risked the near-term capital structure, with approximately 60% of the EUR 4 billion to EUR 4.5 billion equity requirement secured through the EUR 2.2 billion capital increase and a EUR 900 million hybrid bond issuance carrying 50% equity credit from S&P.

Fourth, sovereign bond yield escalation creates a dynamic earnings tailwind, as the Belgian 10-year OLO directly links to fair remuneration with a protective floor of 1.0%, supporting a baseline allowed ROE of 6.0% at current yield levels. Fifth, the German Grid Development Plan capex trajectory outlines total TSO investment requirements up to EUR 392 billion through 2045, though a subsidiary study suggests that slower electricity demand growth could trim onshore expansion requirements by 20% to 23%, enabling Elia to maintain a stable EUR 7.5 billion annual spending level.

Sixth, supply chain constraints present material execution risks, as unprecedented global demand pressure and severe HVDC technology cost inflation forced Elia to postpone critical offshore procurement decisions in early 2025. Seventh, demand trajectory divergence shows Belgium preparing for a 50% increase in electricity consumption by 2032, while German consumption projections face downward revisions that could reduce grid expansion requirements.

Eighth, unregulated US asset de-risking remains a persistent drag, with the EUR 99.1 million impairment on energyRe Giga, the rejection of Priority Transmission Project status for Clean Path NY, and the three-year postponement of SOO Green development timelines collectively eliminating near-term earnings contribution from international ventures.

Forward guidance credibility assessment classifies management as a classic sandbagger, establishing conservative baseline ranges that consistently underestimate operational and regulatory outperformance, with the group delivering actual FY24 net profit of EUR 421 million against an initial mid-year revised range of EUR 355-395 million and setting FY26 guidance of EUR 690-740 million that stood 11% ahead of initial consensus estimates.

Financial sensitivity remains highly favorable, with every 10 bps movement in the Belgian OLO rate altering net profit by EUR 1 million and a 10 bps shift in the German regulatory base rate impacting earnings by EUR 2 million, while automatic interest rate pass-through mechanisms convert macro rate increases into near-term earnings support. Multi-scenario EPS projections model a bull case FY26 EPS of EUR 6.91 assuming 100% Belgian incentive capture and German cost-plus WACC set at 8%, a base case of EUR 6.69 reflecting 60% incentive capture, and a bear case of EUR 6.10 if German RP5 returns are restricted to gas draft levels, with the company's projected 13% EPS CAGR through 2029 significantly outpacing European regulated peers like Redeia at 5%.

Sell-side positioning reveals overwhelmingly bullish sentiment, with Elia delivering a leading 13% EPS CAGR from 2026 to 2029 compared to Redeia at 5% and Terna at 5%, though the company's dividend yield of 1.6% represents the lowest in the sector, far below Redeia's 5.3% and Terna's 4.1%, while Jefferies notes Elia trades at the lowest PEG ratio in the sector due to its double-digit growth profile. Peer comparison highlights significant balance sheet leverage divergence, with Elia's Net Debt to EBITDA projected to reach 8.1x in 2027, representing the highest among compared European regulated networks, and FFO to Net Debt of 10% marking the lowest among peers, though Net Debt to RAB averaging 67% over 2026-2029 sits comfortably below E.ON's peak of 81%.

Earnings quality analysis reveals that Elia's RAB premium relative to RAB growth stands at a discounted 1.1x compared to the peer median of 1.7x, while the company's return on regulatory equity of 6.0% represents the lowest among listed network peers, partially driven by non-cash capitalized interest reaching EUR 90 million in Germany that distorts underlying profitability metrics. The three most material risks threatening the investment thesis center on regulatory execution, supply chain inflation, and balance sheet sustainability.

First, severe regulatory friction could compress the ROE-COE spread, as German and Belgian regulators may exclude capital overruns from the allowed asset base if cost inflation exceeds permissible pass-through thresholds, causing Elia's premium valuation to collapse. Second, unprecedented supply chain bottlenecks and severe cost inflation could render key projects unaffordable, particularly if regulators restrict the pass-through of cost overruns to protect consumer bills, forcing a decline in Elia's regulated asset base and earnings growth.

Third, high capital intensity creates downgrade risks, as capex overruns exceeding hybrid capacity would force Elia's regulated returns to fail to cover debt costs, potentially triggering rating agency downgrades that increase funding costs and compress equity returns. plausible problem to monitor aggregate to reveal peak leverage multiples with Net Debt to EBITDA projected at 8.1x versus a peer average of 6.1x, FFO to Net Debt depressed at 10% versus a peer average of 14%, and a Group Return on Regulated Equity of 6.6% lagging the peer average of 10%, collectively highlighting structural pressure on balance sheet sustainability and regulatory return adequacy.

The net assessment positions Elia Group as a high-growth, capital-intensive regulated utility executing the largest grid expansion program in European transmission history, benefiting from constructive regulatory frameworks that provide full debt cost pass-through and predictable returns, but facing elevated execution risks from supply chain inflation, bottom-tier peer balance sheet metrics, and unresolved German regulatory parameters that could materially compress outer-year earnings if BNetzA implements restrictive return frameworks similar to recent gas utility proposals, making the investment thesis heavily dependent on successful regulatory outcomes in the German RP5 framework expected by year-end 2026 and continued access to hybrid debt markets to avoid dilutive equity issuances beyond the current funding package.
