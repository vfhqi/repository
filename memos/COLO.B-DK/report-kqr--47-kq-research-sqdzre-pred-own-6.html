Section 2 – Gross Margin Movements and Inflationary Headwinds

Section summary
  1. Coloplast A/S experienced a substantial gross margin contraction from 68.4% in Q4 2024/25 to 65.5% in Q4 2025/26 due to escalating raw material and freight costs.
  2. Rising input cost inflation and pricing constraints from public healthcare systems restrict near-term margin recovery and operating leverage.

Quarterly Trajectory and

1.
Compression Weakening progression: Decreasing gross margins reflect a clear step-down in operational profitability over the last year. Bernstein Research reports that Coloplast's gross margin fell from 68.4% in Q4 2024/25 to 65.5% in Q4 2025/26, representing a severe drop of 290 basis points. This decline was driven by increased investments, cost inflation, and margin-dilutive acquisitions. Consequently, this downward pressure continues to weigh on the company's near-term earnings potential.
2.
Severe disappointment: Unfavorable cost dynamics significantly depressed the fourth-quarter performance relative to consensus expectations. Morgan Stanley indicates that Coloplast's gross margin of 65.5% missed broker estimates by 210 basis points in Q4 2025/26. The company stated that raw material costs rose 2-3% during the quarter, while freight rates experienced double-digit increases. This cost structure expansion directly contributed to a flat constant-currency operating profit growth in the period.
3.
Progressive deterioration: Sequential margin erosion throughout fiscal year 2025/26 underscores persistent operational challenges. Bernstein Research reports that Coloplast's gross margin was 66.7% in Q3 2025/26 and fell to 65.5% in Q4 2025/26. While the company maintained its full-year gross margin at 68.0% in FY2024/25, the exit rate in late 2026 highlights increasingly difficult macroeconomic conditions. These challenges restrict management's ability to achieve its historical EBIT margin targets.

Key Input Cost Drivers

4.
Escalating headwind: Rising raw material expenses represent the most significant pressure for Coloplast's high-volume manufacturing model. Deutsche Bank Research reports that raw materials and freight costs are expected to increase by 3-4% during fiscal year 2026/27. These input categories are particularly critical because they accounted for approximately 55% of the total cost of goods sold in the previous year. This persistent inflation offsets operational efficiency gains achieved through automation.
5.
Persistent pressure: Elevated freight rates and logistical complexities continue to compound the pressure on global operating margins. Deutsche Bank Research notes that the company faces inflationary pressure from raw materials, freight, and wages. Coloplast plans to limit air freight by shifting to ground and sea transport to reduce escalating Scope 3 emissions and costs. However, the ongoing Middle East conflict could drive reactionary shipping rate increases that threaten near-term profit recovery.
6.
Contrary evidence: Easing cost pressures in previous quarters suggest that some inflationary headwinds can subside rapidly. Coloplast reported that gross margin was partly offset by favorable impact from lower inflation on raw materials, freight, and utilities. Additionally, the company benefited from favorable development in input costs during the prior fiscal year. This history demonstrates that input cost volatility can be successfully managed through proactive sourcing strategies.

Pricing Pressures and

7.
Revenue Mix Constrained pricing: Limited pricing power in key reimbursement- dominated markets prevents Coloplast from passing input cost increases to customers. Bernstein Research indicates that Coloplast's revenue is heavily exposed to fixed public healthcare budgets and payers. Because pricing in these channels is generally regulated rather than commercially negotiated, high inflation directly compresses margins. Consequently, the company has historically experienced flat-to-negative pricing contributions.
8.
Incoming headwinds: Pricing reforms in major European countries are poised to introduce additional margin headwinds. Deutsche Bank Research reports that healthcare pricing reforms in Germany and France will create low-single-digit pricing pressure starting in January 2027. Across the full strategic period, management assumes a neutral-to-negative pricing contribution due to these reforms and US competitive bidding. These pricing constraints will require higher organic volume growth to sustain flat earnings per share.
9.
Other side of the story: Strategic resource reallocation and mix shifts toward high-margin segments could facilitate a gross margin recovery. Bernstein Research suggests that gross margin should benefit from Biologics and Voice and Respiratory Care growth. Furthermore, the newly launched Funding the Journey program aims to reallocate 500 million Danish krone in operating expenses to support these initiatives. If these operational shifts are executed successfully, they could fully offset near-term inflation and restore historical profitability levels.

Section 3 – Hungarian Manufacturing Wage and Currency Pressures

Section summary
  1. Coloplast's extreme manufacturing concentration in Hungary exposes its gross margins to high regional labor costs and volatile exchange rate fluctuations.
  2. Hungarian wage growth is projected to remain elevated at high single-digit levels from January 2027, posing a persistent headwind to operating profitability. Net read: Operating profit sensitivities show that a stronger Hungarian forint exerts substantial margin pressure, requiring geographic diversification to protect long-term earnings.

Concentration of Manufacturing Footprint

1.
High manufacturing concentration: Coloplast maintains a heavily centralized global production network with its primary high-volume facilities located in Hungary. Bernstein Research indicates that the company's factories in Tatabánya and Nyírbátor account for approximately 74% of total production volume. This extreme concentration means that the vast majority of intimate healthcare consumables are subject to local operational changes. Consequently, localized cost shocks in these two cities directly threaten group margins.
2.
Persistent labor dependency: Coloplast employs a substantial workforce in its Hungarian subsidiaries to support its scaled manufacturing processes. The company reported that its Hungarian headcount stood at 4,640 employees in fiscal year 2025/26. This headcount makes Hungary the single largest employment hub for the group, far exceeding Denmark's 1,543 employees. Such labor density ensures that any shift in local labor relations impacts overall output.
3.
Contrary evidence: Easing concentration risks may emerge as Coloplast actively expands its production footprint in lower-inflation regions. Management expects its long-term manufacturing volume in Hungary to decline to 60-65% by 2030. This strategic reduction is designed to diversify geographic risks as Portugal and Costa Rica facilities scale up. However, Hungary will remain the dominant production source for the next several years.
4.
Critical margin vulnerability: Coloplast's heavy reliance on its Eastern European manufacturing hub concentrates the company's cost risks. Because sales in the local Hungarian market are highly limited, there is no natural currency hedge for production costs. This leaves the cost of goods sold fully exposed to localized inflation. Therefore, any domestic economic instability in Hungary directly translates into volatile earnings per share.

Consequently, rising compensation costs will continue to weigh on the company's global cost structure.

Section 4 – International Site Economics: Portugal and Costa Rica

Section summary
  1. Manufacturing diversification into Costa Rica and Portugal reduces footprint concentration in Hungary while offering lower localized wage inflation.
  2. Immediate start-up costs and underutilization across the new facilities act as a substantial margin drag before volume efficiencies scale.
  3. Strategic disclosures show significant capitalized assets in Portugal though exact scaled unit-cost benefits and local depreciation schedules remain unquantified.
  4. Medicare reimbursement revisions disrupted the US outpatient biologics segment, prompting Coloplast to phase out its high-cost Shield product.
  5. Pivoting toward the resilient inpatient hospital setting allows Kerecis to leverage stable reimbursement and recover operating margins.
  6. The Funding the Journey cost program helps reallocate resources to protect group margins during the biologics transition period.

Manufacturing Footprint Diversification

1.
Progressive diversification: Coloplast is actively diversifying its production base to mitigate its high concentration of manufacturing in Hungary. Bernstein Research reports that the company's new facilities in Cartago and Felgueiras are designed to reduce Hungarian volume concentration from 74% to 60-65% by 2030. This geographical shift is expected to enhance overall supply chain resilience as global demand continues to rise.
2.
Growing capacity: The Cartago facility in Costa Rica represents a mature step in Coloplast's long-term international manufacturing strategy. Bernstein Research notes that Cartago produces ostomy and continence products and currently accounts for approximately 13% of total production volume. Bernstein Research estimates that the facility's production volume will eventually scale to represent 20% to 25% of total volumes by the 2029/30 fiscal year.
3.
Rapid inauguration: Coloplast recently completed construction on its largest manufacturing facility to date in Felgueiras, Portugal. Coloplast announced that this massive two-floor, 50,000 square meter site became operational in the fourth quarter of the 2025/26 fiscal year. The facility will focus primarily on meeting the growing global demand for continence care products.
4.
Favorable timeline: The combined capacity from these two international sites will fulfill Coloplast's expansion needs for several years. Coloplast management stated in its fourth-quarter earnings call that the expansion in Costa Rica and Portugal removes the need for additional factories until the 2029/30 fiscal year. Consequently, capital expenditures are projected to decline from 5% of sales to 4% as the construction phase concludes.

5.
Persistent drag: The initial operational phase of these international manufacturing sites has created persistent headwinds for the group's profit margins. Jefferies Research reports that the transfer of production and initial underutilization at the new sites have acted as a direct drag on gross margins throughout the expansion period. This margin pressure remains a primary challenge as the company transitions its high-volume lines.
6.
Substantial headwind: Broker models indicate that the immediate financial impact of starting up these facilities is substantial. DNB Carnegie estimates that the staffing, training, and initial underutilization of the Portugal and Costa Rica sites will create a gross margin headwind of 50 million Danish krone in its base case scenario. This drag sits within a projected range of 25 million to 75 million Danish krone before accounting for volume offsets.
7.
Extended pressure: The negative margin impact of these facilities is expected to peak during the immediate start- up phase of the Portuguese site. Deutsche Bank Research expects that the continued ramp-up of the Felgueiras facility in Portugal will act as a persistent gross margin headwind during the 2026/27 fiscal year. Deutsche Bank Research notes that these ongoing start-up dynamics will continue to weigh on short-term operating efficiencies as utilization gradually builds.
8.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie suggests that rapid automation, procurement savings, and better overall capacity utilization under the Global Operations Plan 6 will quickly offset the ramp-up costs in Portugal and Costa Rica. Consequently, these efficiency gains could support a swifter EBIT margin recovery toward the company's long-term targets. This rapid recovery would allow the company to achieve operating leverage much sooner than most conservative broker models currently project.
9.
Aggressive mitigation: Management has established a comprehensive savings initiative to cushion the negative margin impact of its international factory investments. Coloplast launched its Funding the Journey program to free up around 500 million Danish krone, with 75% of the impact expected to materialize in the 2026/27 fiscal year. These savings will be partially reinvested to support the commercialization of new products and offset the ramp-up headwinds.

The company started phasing out the high-priced Shield brand because its manufacturing and commercial pricing exceeded the new cap. This transition is designed to restore long-term outpatient competitiveness once the product mix stabilizes.

Coloplast reported that while MariGen is competitively positioned, its lower price point initially compressed average selling prices and gross margin contributions. 21.

The inpatient segment represents around 80% of biologics sales and relies on stable diagnosis-related group payments. This institutional setting remains largely insulated from outpatient Medicare pricing caps. Consequently, focusing the sales force on hospitals provides a stable environment to rebuild commercial momentum and protect earnings.

DNB Carnegie reports that hospital surgical wounds typically require superior graft strength and thickness to support complex tissue regeneration. This clinical differentiation protects Kerecis from low-cost entrants that struggle to replicate its robust clinical trial evidence. Therefore, hospital-based demand should remain the primary volume driver for the wound care segment.

Morgan Stanley reports that outpatient-exposed suppliers are targeting hospitals with aggressive discounts to replace their lost outpatient revenues. This development demonstrates that the inpatient segment is not entirely immune to broader industry reimbursement disruptions.

This precedent supports management's confidence that integrating high-margin acquisitions can successfully offset base manufacturing cost inflation.
