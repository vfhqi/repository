Section 2 – Cost Structures of GLP-1 and Peptide Production

Section summary
  1. Manufacturing GLP-1 peptides involves extremely high asset intensity and solvent consumption, with commercial scale-up drastically reducing the cost per gram of active ingredients.
  2. Oral peptide formulations require up to 73 times more active pharmaceutical ingredient than subcutaneous injections, creating massive volume demands that offset their distribution cost advantages.
  3. Amgen is building massive capacity to support its candidate MariTide, while Novo Nordisk's Catalent acquisition secures crucial fill-finish capacity but introduces significant near-term amortization drag.

Capacity Economics and Asset Intensity

1.
High material intensity: Peptide manufacturing requires an exceptionally high volume of chemical inputs because of repeated washing and coupling cycles. Standard solid-phase peptide synthesis processes require an average process mass intensity of 13,000 kilograms of input per kilogram of product. This chemical intensity is significantly higher than traditional small-molecule processes that only require 170 to 310 kilograms of input per kilogram.
2.
Rapid cost reduction: Moving peptide manufacturing from clinical to commercial scale significantly lowers the cost per gram of drug substance. Commercial GLP-1 manufacturing at a mega-scale of 150 to 200 kilograms per batch reduces costs to 200 dollars to 500 dollars per gram. In contrast, clinical-scale production of the same peptides ranges from 2,000 dollars to 5,000 dollars per gram because of smaller batch sizes.
3.
24 S1 25 Elevated input expenses: Raw materials make up the largest component of peptide production costs, with solid support resins representing a key driver. According to industry experts, raw materials account for approximately 40 percent of total cost of peptide manufacturing. Solid-support resins used during the chemical assembly and purification of peptides represent 10 percent to 20 percent of these total raw material costs.
4.
Evolving synthesis methods: Peptides can be manufactured either through microbial recombinant technology or synthetic chemical processes. Synthetic manufacturing via solid-phase peptide synthesis has become the industry standard because it allows the incorporation of non-natural amino acids to improve molecular performance. While recombinant fermentation is cheaper at scale, synthetic chemistry avoids complex cellular purification steps that lead to high batch failure rates.

Subcutaneous and Oral Formulation Economics

5.
Massive volume demands: Oral peptide formulations require a significantly larger volume of active pharmaceutical ingredients than weekly subcutaneous injections. At a 25 milligram daily maintenance dose, oral Wegovy requires approximately 73 times as much active ingredient per patient-year as the standard 2.4 milligram weekly injection. This massive difference escalates active ingredient requirements and limits the scalability of oral pills compared to injectable alternatives.
6.
Poor systemic absorption: The primary challenge for oral peptides is their exceptionally low bioavailability within the human digestive system. Oral GLP-1 peptide formulations typically exhibit only 5 percent bioavailability because of rapid degradation in the stomach. This low absorption rate necessitates massive chemical synthesis runs to produce enough active pharmaceutical ingredient to achieve therapeutic weight loss.
7.
Favorable distribution margins: Orals avoid the complex cold-chain storage and specialized refrigerated transport required for injectable semaglutide. Bypassing temperature-controlled warehousing and refrigerated freight lowers the overall cost-to-serve per unit. Even when priced below subcutaneous alternatives, the reduced distribution and handling burden creates wider net margins for suppliers on private-pay channels.
8.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley analysts estimated on October 13, 2026, that despite pricing approximately 20 percent to 30 percent below injectables, oral peptide EBIT margins will be only marginally above injectables because of the high volumes of active ingredient required. This suggests that the distribution savings from avoiding cold-chain logistics are mostly offset by the high production costs of low-bioavailability oral active pharmaceutical ingredients.

Contract Fill-Finish Economics and Catalent

9.
Securing sterile capacity: Novo Holdings completed its 16.5 billion dollar acquisition of Catalent in December 2024 to address severe fill-finish bottlenecks. This transaction gave Novo Nordisk direct ownership of three key sterile manufacturing sites in Belgium, Italy, and the United States for 11.7 billion dollars in cash. Securing this in-house capacity is expected to significantly accelerate Novo's GLP-1 supply expansion from 2026 onwards.
10.
Enormous scale advantages: The three acquired Catalent sites provide massive syringe-filling capacity to support Novo's long-term commercial goals. The Brussels facility alone provides over 200 million units of annual syringe-filling capacity. This extra fill-finish capacity provides enough volume to serve between 19 million and 77 million patients depending on single-dose or multi-dose pen configurations.
11.
Slow integration timelines: Novo Nordisk's head of corporate development noted in December 2024 that 2025 will serve as a transition and tech transfer year. Tech transfer and validation processes for sterile fill-finish operations require significant lead times before commercial manufacturing starts. Consequently, these three newly acquired Catalent sites will only begin supporting active growth in GLP-1 treatments from 2026.
12.
Significant margin headwinds: The acquisition of Catalent's manufacturing assets introduces severe amortization expenses that weigh directly on gross margins. Analysts expect the transaction to have a 3 percent negative impact on COGS because of the amortization of acquired intangibles. This high asset intensity will compress near-term operating margins and require higher volumes of product sales to offset.

The company broke ground on its state-of-the-art biomanufacturing facility in Holly Springs, North Carolina, which uses portable and smaller equipment to provide manufacturing flexibility. This flexible design allows Amgen to respond to changing market demands with increased scale and agility.

The majority of this capital allocation is designated for scaling up manufacturing capacity in preparation for the commercial launch of MariTide. Amgen expects this massive biomanufacturing build-out to support future volume growth without requiring any reduction in its established biosimilar franchise.

Section 3 – Precedents in Down-Cycles and Price Pressure

Section summary
  1. Strategic restructurings and aggressive cost-containment programs allowed global pharmaceutical leaders to successfully preserve core operating margins during severe historical pricing down-cycles.
  2. Structural pricing pressure on United States insulin portfolios forced major manufacturers to implement extensive headcount reductions and lower their long-term operational targets before volume-driven recoveries materialized.
  3. Operational discipline and lean manufacturing initiatives enabled Roche to navigate simultaneous biosimilar and patent expiries while maintaining stable operating profits through pipeline transitions.

US Insulin Pricing Down-Cycle Impact

1.
Severe margin pressure: Industry researchers analyzed 12 relevant sources across four companies to understand historical price down-cycles. Novo Nordisk faced intense United States insulin pricing pressure from 2016 to 2019, causing global insulin revenues to decline by 3% in 2019. To defend profitability, Novo Nordisk executed a 2% global workforce reduction in November 2016, laying off approximately 1,000 employees. This headcount reduction represents a significant operational precedent for metabolic peers.
2.
Lowered financial targets: Novo Nordisk's management acknowledged in October 2016 that a challenging pricing environment would compress operating margins. Consequently, the company lowered its long-term annual operating profit growth target from 10% to 5% in 2016. This target adjustment followed a previous reduction from 15% to 10% earlier in 2016. This sequence demonstrates how pricing headwinds can directly force companies to reset mid-term performance expectations.
3.
Aggressive cost containment: Eli Lilly navigated similar pricing pressure on its U.S. insulin portfolio by executing structural cost-reduction programs. Eli Lilly's research and development and marketing expenses benefited from actions to lower its cost structure in 2018. Although net prices declined, Eli Lilly's gross margin as a percent of revenue remained relatively stable at 78.2% in 2018. The gross margin subsequently improved to 78.8% in 2019 because of favorable currency impacts and lower amortization.
4.
Delayed financial recovery: Eli Lilly's gross margin eventually experienced a severe downturn in subsequent years as competitive dynamics intensified. In 2021, Eli Lilly's gross margin declined to 74.2%, representing a decrease of 3.5 percentage points compared with 2020. This margin erosion was driven by unfavorable product mix and higher sales of lower-margin COVID-19 antibodies. This precedent indicates that recovery from pricing pressure can be prolonged and disrupted by changing product mixes.

Section 5 – Commercial spend and R&D Flexibility

Section summary
  1. Shifts in commercial spend toward direct-to-patient digital networks and telehealth platforms offer substantial opportunities to reduce traditional selling and distribution costs.

Digital and Direct Commercial Shifts

1.
Accelerated commercial transition: The pharmaceutical commercialization model is rapidly shifting toward direct- to-consumer digital platforms that bypass traditional intermediaries. Morgan Stanley analysts reported on September 10, 2026, that this parallel transaction rail allows patients to see prices, obtain digital prescriptions, and receive home deliveries. By avoiding pharmacy benefit managers and traditional retail pharmacy counters, manufacturers can retain more product economics and reduce selling and distribution expenses. This disintermediation represents a powerful lever to sustain high margins as net prices decline.
2.
Frictionless telehealth adoption: Telehealth platforms like TelyRx are expanding access to cash-pay medications and streamlining patient acquisition. In its third-quarter 2026 financial report, TelyRx announced that its platform grew revenue to 23.1 million dollars by offering more than 500 everyday medications across 49 states. This digital platform connects patients with state-licensed independent providers for synchronous medical consultations and direct-to-door shipping. Bypassing traditional commercial structures allows players to scale efficiently and capture greater share of the private-pay market.
3.
Rapidly growing self-pay: Self-pay options for GLP-1 medicines have gained massive scale because of high commercial access friction and patient-led demand. Morgan Stanley reported on September 11, 2026, that the monthly cash spend on the LillyDirect card panel grew from 220,000 dollars in October 2024 to 6 million dollars in July 2026. In this channel, Eli Lilly offers Zepbound vials and KwikPens for 299 dollars to 449 dollars per month. This self-pay rail materially lowers the traditional commercial infrastructure requirements because demand is organic.
4.
Contrary evidence: Direct-to-consumer marketing campaigns can potentially be extremely costly and face escalating regulatory risks. For example, LENZ Therapeutics reported that its selling, general, and administrative expenses increased by 30% to 35.8 million dollars in the third quarter of 2026 due to launching its direct advertising campaign. Furthermore, the federal government's recent strategy guidelines indicate that agencies plan to increase enforcement on direct-to-consumer pharmaceutical advertising. This indicates that shifting commercial spend to direct platforms does not guarantee immediate, low-cost margin expansion.
