Materiality of Change

1.
The geographic revenue mix has undergone a structural shift, with US revenue surging to £927 million (+24% constant currency) in FY26, exceeding 51% of total Group revenue versus 48% in FY25, fundamentally repositioning the business as a North American-anchored luxury retail platform with accelerating top-line momentum while simultaneously reducing exposure to the structurally mature UK domestic market

The margin profile has rebased downward through Adjusted EBIT margin compression of 60 basis points yearover-year to 8.5% in FY26, reflecting structural headwinds from US import tariff impacts, record gold price inflation compressing precious metal gross margins, and brand partner margin term adjustments that partially offset fixed cost operating leverage from accelerating revenue scale

The balance sheet has deleveraged materially, with pre-IFRS 16 net debt reducing by £39 million to £57 million (0.3x net debt to Adjusted EBITDA) while operating cash flow reached £176 million and free cash flow expanded to £162 million at an 80% conversion rate, demonstrating robust cash generation capacity that fully self-funds

Plausible problems to monitor 4 findings

1.
EBIT margin structural discount: Adjusted EBIT margin of 8.5% in FY26 sits materially below brand-owner peer medians, specifically trailing Pandora at 25.0% and Richemont at 21.4%, reflecting the inherent retailer distribution model versus vertically integrated luxury conglomerates . Cross-ref: Section 7. This discount stems from the fundamental economics of multi-brand specialty retail distribution, where dealers operate under tightly controlled wholesale margin bands imposed by Swiss watch manufacturers, typically restricting gross margins to the 20-40% range and limiting structural upside potential compared to branded goods manufacturers who capture both wholesale and retail economics
2.
UK market maturity headwinds: UK revenue growth of +4% constant currency in FY26 significantly underperformed the US region's +24% constant currency expansion and fell below the hard luxury peer median growth of approximately +6%, reflecting structural market saturation . Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 4. The UK market now serves predominantly domestic buyers representing 95% of sales versus 67% historically, with the permanent absence of tourist VAT refunds eliminating a critical high-margin customer segment that historically drove 33% of revenues, creating a materially constrained growth ceiling relative to international luxury retail peers operating in markets with intact tax-free shopping frameworks
3.
Elevated working capital intensity: Net working capital days reached 115 days, exceeding the specialty retail peer median of 85 days by 30 days, driven by inventory depth requirements for supply-constrained luxury watch allocations . Cross-ref: Section 7. This elevated working capital position reflects the structural necessity of maintaining comprehensive display inventory across multi-brand showrooms to service customer preference discovery, particularly for supply-constrained brands like Rolex and Patek Philippe, creating a capital intensity disadvantage versus mono-brand boutiques or vertically integrated retailers with superior inventory turn velocity
4.
Wholesale credit risk exposure: US wholesale EBIT margin contracted 210 basis points to 19.7% in FY26, materially below the Roberto Coin standalone wholesale margin of 20%+, driven by a £3.5 million bad debt writeoff for Saks Fifth Avenue that exceeded specialty wholesale peer median credit loss rates . Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 6. This operational misstep highlights concentration risks inherent in luxury department store wholesale distribution, where credit exposure to financially stressed retail partners can trigger sudden profit deterioration, particularly as the broader department store channel faces structural headwinds from direct-to-consumer channel shifts by luxury brands seeking to control brand presentation and customer data. Return on Capital Employed moderation: ROCE decreased 100 basis points year-over-year to 18.0% in FY26, falling toward the specialty retail peer median of approximately 17-19% despite maintaining premium EBIT margins, as capital employed expanded 8.8% to fund US showroom growth . Cross-ref: Section 4. The ROCE compression reflects the natural tension between aggressive geographic expansion requiring upfront capital deployment for showroom fit-outs, inventory stocking, and acquisition consideration, versus the typical 2-3 year maturation period before newly opened luxury retail locations achieve steady-state productivity, temporarily diluting returns during the high-velocity US network buildout phase that absorbed £66 million in expansionary capital expenditure during FY26 alone
