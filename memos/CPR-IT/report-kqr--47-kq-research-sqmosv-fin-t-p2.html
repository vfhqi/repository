Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Campari's pricing power is real but conditional. It is strongest in Aperol and Campari, where the group has accepted costly delistings to hold price, and in Espolòn volume. It is weakest in cognac, in US tequila pricemix, and in any 2027 scenario with a tariff rate above 25% or an energy spike. Overall confidence in what the evidence shows on Campari's pricing power is Medium because of regional discrepancies. The Key Question can be answered directionally but not quantitatively: the evidence supports "pricing power holds in aperitifs, is contested in agave and cognac, and is untested for 2027 cost shocks."
1.
Retailer standoffs: Campari has repeatedly chosen price integrity over shelf space. In Q2 2012, Aperol and Campari were delisted by a major German retailer, contributing to a 9.7% German sales decline in H1 2012. In Q3 2024 the group walked away from an Italian customer demanding price cuts, at a cost of about €10 million in lost peak-season sales. In 2025 a German delisting had an €11 million negative impact (€5 million in H1, €6 million in H2). Germany and Switzerland saw further disruption in early 2026, and Morgan Stanley said Q1 execution was hampered as the company maintained strict pricing discipline.
2.
Aperitif growth path: Price led growth in the inflation years, and volume and convenience formats now carry the recovery. Organic sales rose 16.4% in 2022 and 10.5% in 2023, the latter on high-single digit price increases. The House of Aperitifs then grew 6% in 2024, slowed to 2.3% in 2025 and rebounded to 6.7% in the first nine months of 2026. Convenience formats drove roughly one-third of that growth. They carry a lower gross margin than 700ml bottles, but one broadly in line with the group average. The evidence does not give a clean price/mix versus volume split for every year, which limits precision.
3.
Aperitif competitors: Direct peer threats are real but uneven. Pernod Ricard's Lillet declined in fiscal 2026, driven by weakness in Germany. Bacardi's St-Germain volumes nearly tripled between 2019 and 2025, and Bacardi is launching into major French grocers. Morgan Stanley found Campari's European off-trade spirits sales fell only 1.2% over four weeks, against a 4.7% market decline, 4.9% for Diageo and 3.3% for Pernod Ricard.
4.
US discounting backdrop: The industry is cutting price without winning volume. Deutsche Bank's control-state data showed 72% of the top 20 SKUs with year-on-year price cuts in July 2026, yet only 31% of discounted brands grew volume. 57% of Rémy Cointreau's and 38% of Diageo's top SKUs fell in both price and volume, versus just 14% of Campari's. Aperol's US price-mix fell 1.4% in late spring 2026, while its volume grew 1%.
5.
Espolòn versus Diageo: The agave price gap is narrowing, but volume has held. Diageo cut Casamigos retail prices by 10% to 15%. Casamigos Blanco averages $31.42 against $28.40 for Espolòn Blanco. Morgan Stanley reported that Espolòn's volume share stayed resilient despite further Casamigos cuts. A deeper 35% Diageo cut would force Espolòn to reposition down a price tier. Agave spot prices fell from MXN 29/kg in 2022 to MXN 10/kg in early 2026, and Campari's third-party sourcing captures this faster than integrated peers such as Becle. The six-to-eight-year growing cycle makes a quick reversal unlikely.
6.
Cognac: This is the weakest pricing-power link. Hennessy cut VS pricing by about 10% during 2023-24, and Courvoisier followed with a lag to prevent share erosion. Rémy Cointreau's fiscal 2026 cognac organic growth was -0.5%, with a 7.8% volume gain fully offset by an 8.3% negative price-mix. The pricemix drag was still 5.7% in H1 of fiscal 2027. Courvoisier still grew 4.6% organically in H1 2026. Grand Marnier fell 8.1% in 2025 as the group prioritized price discipline over volume.
7.
Whiskey and bourbon: Discipline is visible at both Campari and Brown-Forman, but Campari's volume is soft. Jack Daniel's Black Label takes modest off-premise price increases of about 0.3%. Woodford Reserve net sales grew 4% in fiscal 2026. Bernstein said bourbon pricing has stayed rational. Campari raised Russell's Reserve prices by 5 to 10 points in late 2024. Its Whiskey and Rum House still fell 6% organically in H1 2026 on demand-led supply constraints.
8.
Input-cost inflation: Contract structure delays cost shocks rather than removing them. Campari secures about 90% of its glass through long-term contracts, and glass is roughly 16% of cost of goods sold. Priceadjustment clauses historically require consecutive months of elevated energy prices before resets take effect. The triggers had not been activated as of the May 2026 report. Suppliers are proposing 3% to 5% increases but cannot fully realize them. The exact contract formulas are undisclosed.
9.
Tariffs and packaging regulation: The 2026 cushion does not carry into 2027. Campari guided to a €20 million full-year US tariff headwind. A €15 million refund of 2025 tariffs offsets part of it but is non-recurring and will not protect 2027. Morgan Stanley warns that tariffs above 25% or a European energy surge would cause severe margin compression. Brown-Forman's 60.2% first-quarter gross margin gives it more room to absorb trade shocks. Its Canadian sales fell 60% in trade disputes. The UK deposit return scheme starts on 1 October 2027 with a 20p deposit.
10.
What brokers and consensus assume for 2027: The cited figures assume pricing power sufficient to cover costs, not a specific price number. Consolidated projections assume Campari maintains organic operating accretion through fiscal 2027, with a forecast operating margin baseline of about 20%. Morgan Stanley frames EBIT margins as able to expand toward 22% by fiscal 2028 if pricing holds. Jefferies noted in March 2025 that the company expected to leverage price to mitigate cost pressures. The cited material gives no explicit 2027 price/mix percentage and does not itemize the announced 2027 price increases by country. Morgan Stanley also cautions that Campari cannot easily raise prices across its consolidated European retail network. A prolonged price war would restrict mid-term EBIT margin expansion and lead to downward revisions of consensus.
11.
Reconciling conflicting figures: Most conflicts reflect differences in horizon, scope or timing rather than true contradiction. (1) The margin figures differ by horizon: about 20% is the 2027 baseline and 22% is a fiscal 2028 aspiration, so they describe one trajectory. (2) Aperol's US pricing signals differ by scope: Jefferies' +2.5% price-mix is for the parent group, alongside 12.6% US off-trade volume growth for Aperol while Bernstein's -1.4% is Aperol-specific in late spring 2026. The best reading is flat-to-slightly-negative Aperol US price with strong volume, not a contradiction. (3) The tariff rate differs by date: Bernstein cited a stable 15% on EU imports in January 2026, while the Key Question cites a 10% rate after the 20 Feb 2026 Supreme Court ruling, so the 10% figure is the later regime. (4) Section 3 and Section 5 are rated High confidence, yet their own "other side" findings warn of a 35% Diageo cut forcing Espolòn down a tier and highly uncertain 2027 costs. The High ratings apply to the evidence on current conditions, not to 2027 outcomes.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Davide Campari-Milano N.V. faces a critical test of its pricing power across key regions in 2027. Net read: High retailer concentration in Europe and discounting in the United States directly threaten the group's margin and EPS trajectory.

Analytical Framework and Transmission

1.
Core investigation: "Davide Campari-Milano N.V. (CPR-IT), a Dutch-incorporated, Milan-headquartered premium spirits company controlled by the Garavoglia family through Lagfin; four Houses of Brands (Aperitifs: Aperol, Campari, Sarti Rosa, Crodino; Agave: Espolòn; Whiskey and Rum: Wild Turkey, Russell's Reserve, Jamaican rums; Cognac and Champagne: Courvoisier, Grand Marnier) plus local brands, reported from 2026 in four regions (Europe, North America, Developing Markets, Asia-Pacific and Global Travel Retail). Key question: how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: an industry in which most US spirits SKUs are cutting prices; European retailer negotiations and delistings (Germany 2012, Italy Q3 2024, Germany 2025, Germany and Switzerland Q1 2026); input-cost inflation from European energy, glass and freight in 2026-27; agave price deflation and its possible reversal; US import tariffs; consumer down-trading and pack-size shifts; currency devaluation in Brazil and Argentina; excise and "sin tax" changes (Brazil); deposit return schemes?"
2.
Crucial transmission: Davide Campari-Milano's ability to raise prices directly dictates its gross margin stability and downstream earnings per share expansion. If pricing power holds, top-line leverage can expand EBIT margins toward 22% by fiscal 2028, significantly boosting investor returns. Conversely, any pricing weakness would compress multiples and delay progress on deleveraging.
3.
Vulnerable multiples: Davide Campari-Milano's relative valuation remains sensitive to growth expectations for flagship brands like Aperol and Espolòn. Bernstein Research reported on 16 March 2026 that Aperol still has plenty of runway. Slower expansion in the key United States market would hurt investor sentiment and contract the stock's forward price-to-earnings multiple.
4.
Elevated pressure: Davide Campari-Milano faced severe gross margin contraction of 200 basis points in fiscal 2022 due to commodity and supply chain inflation. Jefferies reported on 7 March 2025 that the company expected to leverage price to mitigate cost pressures. Failing to execute this pricing discipline in 2027 would directly impair downstream earnings per share.

Category Debates and Market Concentration

5.
Debated strength: Davide Campari-Milano's aperitifs are positioned within a category that behaves somewhat discretionary but exhibits solid pricing rationality. Bernstein Research noted on 30 October 2026 that pricing in the off-premise has not been overly aggressive, leaving room for brand stability. However, affordability pressures from high gas prices continue to squeeze consumer volumes.
6.
Intense concentration: Davide Campari-Milano must navigate a consolidating European retail environment where multinational buying groups dominate negotiations. Morgan Stanley highlighted on 12 November 2026 that alliances like Concordis have consolidated purchasing scale against branded suppliers. These disputes present severe delisting risks that can disrupt regional volume gains.

Section 2 – Pricing Power in Aperitifs and European Retailer Negotiations

Section summary
  1. Davide Campari-Milano maintains strict pricing discipline despite high retailer concentration and frequent delistings across European grocery networks.
  2. Standoffs with retailers in Germany and Italy have caused temporary volume drops but successfully protected the long-term gross margins of the group. Net read: Flagship bitters organic growth rebounded strongly in fiscal 2026 as pre-mixed convenience formats unlocked incremental consumption occasions.
  3. Competitive pressures remain intense as Bacardi aggressively expands St-Germain in Europe, while Pernod Ricard faces declining Lillet sales.

European Retailer Price Standoffs

1.
Historical friction: Davide Campari-Milano experienced severe off-trade challenges in Germany during 2012 due to major commercial negotiations. During Q2 2012, management confirmed that flagship brands Aperol and Campari were completely delisted by a major retail customer after the group refused to concede on price. This delisting contributed to a 9.7% sales decline in Germany during the first half of 2012. Although an interim agreement was eventually reached, this dispute proved that retailer standoffs can severely disrupt localized volume momentum.
2.
Costly discipline: Davide Campari-Milano sustained a short-term financial hit in Italy in late 2024 to preserve its pricing integrity. During Q3 2024, the group walked away from a prominent retail customer who demanded price cuts, resulting in a temporary loss of retail shelf space. This standoff cost the group approximately 10 million euros in lost sales in Italy during the critical peak season. Management defended this decision because conceding to retail pressure makes subsequent price negotiations much more difficult.
3.
Prolonged pressure: Davide Campari-Milano faced another round of retail standoffs in Germany during fiscal 2025 as it defended its gross margins. The group experienced a negative sales impact of 11 million euros in Germany in 2025 due to a retailer delisting over its refusal to lower prices. This delisting dented first-half sales by 5 million euros, with an additional 6 million euros impact in the second half of 2025. Despite a challenging 3% organic decline in Germany, holding the line protected the group's premium brand equity.
4.
Blocked execution: Davide Campari-Milano experienced temporary commercial disruption across Germany and Switzerland during early 2026 over ongoing price negotiations. Morgan Stanley reported on June 9, 2026 that Q1 sales execution was hampered as the company maintained strict pricing discipline. This standoff was likely exacerbated by aggressive early promotional pricing actions taken by Diageo in Europe during March. While holding the line protects long-term brand health, these disputes led to near-term share losses in Europe.

Flagship Aperitifs Growth Trajectory

5.
Mixed growth: Davide Campari-Milano delivered strong topline growth in 2022 and 2023 driven by robust pricing across its core aperitifs portfolio. The group achieved a 16.4% organic sales increase in 2022 as price increases successfully mitigated elevated cost inflation. In 2023, organic sales expanded by 10.5%, supported by high-single digit price increases that offset a poor summer season. However, these pricing actions led to extremely poor sales mix as low-margin volumes grew faster than premium bitters.
6.
Resilient volume: Davide Campari-Milano navigated severe weather and consumer spending pressures in 2024 through off-season demand. The House of Aperitifs grew organically by 6% in 2024, supported by doubledigit expansion in the fourth quarter. Flagship brand Aperol recorded solid 5% growth as international expansion offset subdued summer trends in Italy. Meanwhile, Campari grew organically by 9%, proving that the core bittersweet portfolio can hold consumer loyalty despite macroeconomic challenges.
7.
Subdued momentum: Davide Campari-Milano reported a sharp deceleration in its House of Aperitifs organic growth during fiscal 2025. The division grew just 2.3% organically in 2025 to 1,337.7 million euros as consumer spending pressures mounted. Aperol sales rose by a modest 1.4% to 785.3 million euros, dragged down by retail disputes and a 2.8% contraction in Italy. Furthermore, Campari sales fell by 1.5% to 323.0 million euros, showing that even flagship brands are vulnerable to retail disruptions.
8.
Recovering trajectory: Davide Campari-Milano achieved a solid top-line rebound in its aperitifs portfolio during the first nine months of 2026. The House of Aperitifs grew organically by 6.7% to 1,116 million euros, accelerating from 4.0% growth in the first half. Aperol organic sales rose by 3.3% in the first half of 2026, showing the benefits of strong summer execution and marketing campaigns. This performance shows that the group's core brands can regain positive momentum as retail disputes resolve.

Innovation Formats and Serve Economics

9.
Strategic innovation: Davide Campari-Milano expanded its consumption occasions by rolling out new convenience formats across Europe in 2026. The group launched pre-mixed formats, including Aperol To Go cans, ready-to-serve bottles, and Aperol on Tap, to recruit younger legal-drinking-age consumers. Cans achieved strong traction in the UK, capturing the top ready-to-drink spot at Sainsbury's with sales velocity running 2x to 3x management's initial forecasts. These formats successfully unlock incremental consumption occasions, particularly in high-velocity venues and outdoor settings.
10.
Dilutive margins: Davide Campari-Milano accepted slightly lower profitability per litre on pre-mixed formats to maximize volumes. Morgan Stanley reported on October 29, 2026 that convenience formats carry a lower gross margin than standard 700ml bottles. However, these pre-mixed innovations still generate a gross margin that is broadly in-line with the group's overall average. The convenience portfolio remains highly additive because it drives over 60 million incremental serves and limits cannibalization of bottles in offtrade channels.
11.
Significant contribution: Davide Campari-Milano relied heavily on convenience innovations to prop up its European organic growth in 2026. Convenience formats drove approximately one-third of the total organic growth in the House of Aperitifs during the first nine months of 2026. This contribution represents a significant expansion opportunity as these formats have only been launched in a handful of initial pilot markets. This innovation pipeline helps buffer the group against the retail pressure affecting standard bottles.

Competitive Landscape and Counter-Views

12.
Peer underperformance: Pernod Ricard struggled to sustain momentum for its daytime spritz alternative, Lillet, in key European off-trade channels. Lillet reported a decline in sales during fiscal 2026, driven primarily by weakness in its core German market. This decline came despite Pernod Ricard investing heavily in brand awareness campaigns to capture daytime occasions. Lillet's struggle suggests that Davide Campari-Milano's bitters face limited direct threat from major international peer brands in Europe.
13.
Threatening rival: Bacardi's St-Germain elderflower liqueur has emerged as a rapid competitor by capitalizing on the booming Hugo spritz trend. St-Germain global volumes nearly tripled between 2019 and 2025 as consumers increasingly adopted lighter, lower-alcohol drink options. Bacardi reported that search volumes for the Hugo Spritz rose 172% in 2024, showcasing strong consumer interest in France, the UK, and the US. To capture more off-trade sales, Bacardi is launching St-Germain into major French grocery retailers to drive at-home mixology.
14.
(UK) & Markets Contrary evidence: Davide Campari-Milano's flagship aperitif portfolio continued to grow and gain off-trade share in Europe despite intense retailer pushback. Morgan Stanley reported on October 30, 2026 that Campari's European off-trade spirits sales fell by only 1.2% over a 4-week period, outperforming the wider spirits market decline of 4.7%. While Diageo fell 4.9% and Pernod Ricard dropped 3.3%, Campari's resilient sell-out trends proved its superior category pricing power.
15.
Sweet expansion: Davide Campari-Milano leveraged Sarti Rosa as a fruity spritz option to capture consumers who dislike traditional bitters. Sarti Rosa expanded rapidly to account for 11% of the group's net sales in Germany during 2025, becoming its second-largest brand behind Aperol. This non-bitter option expanded into other European markets in 2026, offering a sweet-and-sour tropical profile of passion fruit and mango. Launching Sarti Rosa allows the group to protect its overall market share while expanding the total spritz category.
16.
side of the story: Davide Campari-Milano remains highly exposed to severe off-trade volume pressure if retailer standoffs become prolonged. German retailer delistings in 2025 had an 11 million euros negative impact, showing how disputes disrupt sales. If buying groups like Concordis continue to consolidate power, the group will face depressed off-trade volumes across Central Europe. This risk could offset the incremental gains from convenience formats and restrict mid-term EBIT margin expansion.

Section 3 – US Spirits Pricing Dynamics and Agave Category Realignments

Section summary
  1. Intense pricing pressure and promotional discounting are reshaping the United States tequila landscape as major distillers fight for market share.
  2. Davide Campari-Milano N.V. continues to record resilient volume growth for Espolòn despite aggressive price cuts from market leader Diageo.
  3. Agave agricultural price deflation has dramatically lowered input costs for tequila producers but also lowered the price barriers for mid-market competitors.

Severe Discounting and Control States

1.
Severe discounting: Control-state data compiled by Deutsche Bank on 3 September 2026 reveals that spirits pricing remains a major challenge in the United States. Across the top 20 industry SKUs, 72% experienced year-on-year price cuts during July 2026. This promotional environment failed to stimulate demand; only 31% of discounted brands achieved volume growth.
2.
Elevated pressure: Distillers show varying levels of exposure to the promotional "double down" trend where both prices and volumes fall. Deutsche Bank reported that 57% of Rémy Cointreau's top SKUs and 38% of Diageo's top SKUs suffered this dual decline. Conversely, Davide Campari-Milano N.V. limited its double-down exposure to just 14% of its leading SKUs. This divergence shows that Campari's brands maintain superior relative volume resilience over peers.
3.
Worsening mix: National retail scanner data shows that pricing across the tequila category has entered a structural deceleration phase. Jefferies reported on 14 October 2026 that tequila price-mix fell by 2.6% in September. This deflationary trend worsened to negative 3.7% in October as competitive intensity increased. This contraction indicates that premiumization alone can no longer carry category volume growth.

Tactical Repositioning and Price Gaps

4.
Strategic repositioning: Diageo aggressively adjusted its tequila price ladder in 2026 to address severe volume declines. The group implemented a 10% to 15% retail price reduction on Casamigos to restore brand competitiveness. This tactical price cut successfully revived Casamigos Blanco volumes to 4% growth in late 2026. However, this volume recovery came at the expense of extremely negative price-mix progression for the brand.
5.
Narrowing gap: Retail price comparisons show that Casamigos has moved much closer to the middle tier of the market. Jefferies reported on 11 November 2026 that Casamigos Blanco is priced at an average of $31.42 per unit. Campari's Espolòn Blanco continues to offer an accessible premium alternative at $28.40 per unit. This narrowing price gap represents an direct competitive threat to Espolòn's volume market share.
6.
Resilient volume: Espolòn maintained excellent volume momentum despite the aggressive pricing moves from Casamigos. Morgan Stanley reported on 14 September 2026 that Espolòn's volume share remained exceptionally resilient. This outperformance persisted despite Casamigos cutting its prices further. This strong volume execution demonstrates that Espolòn retains highly robust consumer pull in the premium tier.

Agave spot prices collapsed from MXN 29 per kilogram in 2022 to MXN 10 per kilogram in early 2026. Morgan Stanley projects that spot quotes could drop to MXN 3 per kilogram. This steep decline provides tequila producers with a substantial gross margin cushion to absorb promotional investments.

Organic Growth and Category Dynamics

10.
Accelerating growth: Tequila has consistently outperformed Campari's wider portfolio, recording a 30% organic sales compound annual growth rate over four years. Espolòn represents approximately 8% of group sales and remains a key growth driver. While the overall tequila category has been subject to downward pricing pressure, Espolòn continues to gain volume share. This strong volume performance shows that Espolòn dominates the premium tier effectively.
11.
Temporary slowdown: Davide Campari-Milano N.V. experienced localized pricing volatility in its core aperitifs portfolio during early 2026. Bernstein Research reported that Aperol's United States price-mix fell by 1.4% in late spring 2026. This modest contraction was driven by targeted distributor promotions and inventory optimization. However, Aperol's underlying volume momentum remained positive at 1% growth during the same trailing period.
12.
Historical precedent: The US spirits industry has previously experienced severe pricing disruptions when premium brands over-extended their pricing umbrellas. During the Great Financial Crisis, the value segment gained 2% of total volume share. This expansion came entirely at the expense of premium and super-premium brands. This historical case suggests that maintaining rigid premium pricing can lead to structural market share loss during consumer downturns.
13.
Contrary evidence: Davide Campari-Milano's premium tequila business continues to capture substantial market share without resorting to margin-diluting retail price cuts. Morgan Stanley reported on 14 September 2026 that Espolòn's volume share remained exceptionally resilient. This outperformance persisted despite Casamigos cutting its prices further. This resilience suggests that Espolòn possesses genuine brand equity that is insulated from peer promotional tactics.

Strategic Outlook and Vulnerabilities

14.
Resilient execution: Davide Campari-Milano's Agave House is highly likely to maintain its market outperformance in 2027. Favorable third-party agave sourcing allows the group to protect its operating profitability. The narrowing price gap with Casamigos represents a manageable threat. Therefore, Campari's category strategy is well-positioned to sustain earnings expansion over the medium term.
15.
Other side of the story: Davide Campari-Milano's volume momentum could face severe pressure if competitive pricing cuts become even more aggressive. Morgan Stanley warned that a larger 35% price cut by Diageo would force Espolòn to reposition down an entire price tier. Such a defensive move would cause severe margin and brand-equity trade-offs. Therefore, Campari remains highly vulnerable to a prolonged price war in the United States tequila category.

Section 4 – Cognac and Whiskey Pricing Discipline and Competitive Responses

Section summary
  1. Davide Campari-Milano N.V. maintains robust pricing discipline in premium whiskey and rum while carefully repositioning its newly acquired cognac portfolio.
  2. Competitive price cuts by LVMH's Hennessy VS have put significant pressure on Courvoisier, forcing tactical promotional adjustments.
  3. Brown- Forman and Campari continue to defend premium bourbon pricing, although supply constraints and volume-price trade-offs persist in the United States.
  4. Rémy Cointreau has experienced severe post-pandemic cognac price-mix erosion as it prioritizes volume recovery and affordability.
  5. Davide Campari-Milano faces rising packaging and logistics cost headwinds that threaten its operating margins in fiscal 2027.
  6. Price adjustment clauses in long-term glass contracts protect the group from sudden energy spikes but delay exposure to sustained inflation.
  7. Import tariffs present a direct risk to US spirits earnings, though historical refunds and high margins help top peers absorb the blow.
  8. The upcoming UK deposit return scheme will introduce transitional retail price increases that could temporarily disrupt local sales volumes.

Cognac Competitive Realignment and Pricing Battles

1.
Premium acquisition: Davide Campari-Milano N.V. completed its acquisition of Courvoisier in early 2024 to enter the premium cognac segment. Management believes the brand was historically priced too low under prior ownership and sees a substantial gross margin opportunity. Over time, the group aims to reduce exposure to entry-level VS expressions and pivot toward premium VSOP plus options. This premiumization path mimics the successful strategy Campari applied to its Grand Marnier franchise.
2.
183 - Earnings Calls Aggressive competition: LVMH Moët Hennessy has severely disrupted the United States cognac pricing landscape by increasing promotional intensity for Hennessy VS. Hennessy reversed its post-pandemic price increases, cutting VS pricing by approximately 10% during 2023 and 2024. Courvoisier followed these price cuts with a lag to prevent severe market share erosion in the US. This aggressive environment has made it extremely difficult for Campari to raise prices on its newly acquired brand without sacrificing volume.
3.
Severe erosion: Rémy Cointreau has suffered a sharp correction in its cognac segment as post-pandemic demand normalized. The group posted a negative price-mix of 8.3% in fiscal 2026, equally split between price cuts and adverse mix. To revitalize volumes, the new leadership is forced to balance premiumization with absolute affordability. This pricing pressure continued into late 2026, as cognac volumes grew 16% while price-mix fell 5.7%.
4.
Declining momentum: Grand Marnier has faced significant organic sales volatility as Campari prioritizes pricing discipline over short-term volume gains. The brand recorded an organic decline of 16.5% in 2023, primarily driven by negative shipments as the US market underwent destocking. Although Grand Marnier returned to positive growth of 5% in the first half of 2026 off an easy comparison base, the underlying pricing remains highly promotional. This volatility demonstrates that pricing power remains highly fragile for premium liqueurs.
5.
Contrary evidence: Courvoisier recorded solid organic sales growth of 4.6% in the first half of 2026 despite severe category pressure. This positive momentum was driven by solid expansion in developing markets and the Asia-Pacific region, while United States sales remained remarkably stable. This performance suggests that Courvoisier's brand equity remains resilient outside its core American market.

Whiskey and Bourbon Pricing Discipline

6.
