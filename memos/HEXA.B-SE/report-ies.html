Hexagon (HEXA.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to our case

These results strengthen the operating half of our case by more than a little and leave its verdict where it was: the business is delivering ahead of what we assumed, the one risk we named on mix has tripped its alarm, and the price has moved further from every level at which the case progresses.

What changed

Hexagon reported the three months to 30 September 2026 on 23 October 2026.

  • Growth beat the case, not only consensus. Organic growth was 13 percent, against a consensus of 8.8 percent, and 16 percent excluding a large prior-year defence contract. Autonomous Solutions grew 23 percent, Manufacturing Intelligence 16 percent, Infrastructure and Geospatial 4 percent. Europe, Middle East and Africa grew 17 percent, China 15 percent, the rest of Asia 14 percent, the Americas 6 percent. Order intake exceeded sales. The case's test was a print below 8 percent with a lower backlog; neither happened.
  • Profit was what the case expected. EBITAC was 265.3 MEUR, up 11 percent, a 25.3 percent margin against a 23.1 percent consensus. The case's modal outcome was a 10 to 15 percent rise. The surprise was the sell-side's, not ours.
  • Gross margin fell again. Adjusted gross margin was 61.6 percent against 63.7 percent a year earlier, on hardware and sensor mix. Gross earnings rose 4 percent on net sales up 7 percent to 1,049.1 MEUR. EBITDA was flat at 369.9 MEUR and its margin fell from 37.9 to 35.3 percent.
  • The rest. Adjusted earnings per share 8.2 euro cents, up 19 percent, against a 6.9 euro cent consensus. Cash conversion 82 percent against 85 percent. Net debt 489 MEUR, 0.3 times EBITDA, 1.4 times pro forma. Cost savings 18.5 MEUR in the quarter, the programme complete. Robotics investment 13 MEUR in the quarter, with 70 to 80 MEUR indicated for 2027. Waygate is expected to close in Q4 2026 and to dilute the group margin by 150 basis points initially.
  • Sell-side. DNB Carnegie raised its target to SEK 118 from SEK 110 and kept Buy, adding SEK 4 a share for Robotics. Deutsche Bank, at Hold and SEK 95 before the results, had not published. One broker's response is all the review carries.

Three cautions on the evidence. The review calls net sales of 1,049.1 MEUR a beat of a 1,050 MEUR consensus; it is in line. It gives Q3 2025 EBITAC as 238.4 MEUR and organic growth as 5 percent, where the case carried comparatives of 232.8 MEUR and 4 percent. And it describes the 25.3 percent margin once as excluding Robotics, without saying so elsewhere.

The challenge

Reading one: the growth and the gross margin are the same fact, and the case treats them as two. The case's cleared branch asks for organic growth at or above 6 percent and gross margin above 63 percent together, as if they were independent. This quarter suggests they pull against each other. The fastest-growing lines are defence positioning hardware, electronics metrology and new instruments, and the review attributes the gross margin fall to exactly that mix. On this reading the faster Hexagon grows, the further it moves from the second condition, and the branch at 22 times cannot be reached by the route the case describes.

The profit bridge supports it. EBITAC rose 26.9 MEUR year on year. Cost savings were 18.5 MEUR of that. Positive currency was worth roughly 50 to 70 basis points of the margin beat, and tariff refunds helped. The case's own rule is that one point of organic growth is worth about 11.7 MEUR of EBITAC a year; on that rule thirteen points should have been worth about 40 MEUR in the quarter on their own. What is left after the cost programme is under 9 MEUR before currency and refunds. Thirteen percent growth produced almost no operating leverage of its own, and flat EBITDA says the same.

If that is right, 25.3 percent is plausibly the high mark rather than the new base. The cost programme's year-on-year benefit runs out in 2027, Waygate arrives, Robotics spend rises, and DNB Carnegie itself projects the EBIT margin falling from 24.6 percent in 2026 to 23.1 percent in 2027. For this reading to hold, the mix effect has to be structural rather than a product-launch phase, and currency has to have flattered the quarter as much as the review implies. It fails if gross margin recovers above 62 percent while growth stays in double digits.

Reading two: the case's earnings path is too low, and the multiple argument is aimed at the wrong number. The case conceded the bull's multiple and showed the return failing anyway, on an earnings path of about 10 percent a year and organic growth of 5.5 percent from 2027. It did not consider that path being materially wrong. Three consecutive quarters at 8, 12 and 13 percent, a backlog that grew, Europe and China turning together, and earnings per share up 19 percent are evidence that growth is broader than "small in the mix", and that the earnings layer is compounding faster than the case's band. Management says there are no signs of weakening in aerospace, defence and electronics, and says the targets do not need automotive or construction to recover.

In its strongest form: on the case's own arithmetic, with today's multiple held, the return clears the bar at about 98 SEK, so earnings running roughly a tenth above the case's path would clear it at today's price. That is not a large miss to have made on a company growing at twice the rate assumed. For this to be right, organic growth has to hold near 8 percent through 2027 against 12 and 13 percent comparatives, and the profit has to come through despite reading one. The two readings cannot both be fully right, which is why the gross margin series decides between them.

A reading we considered and do not put up. That the quarter is a cyclical peak. The case already holds it: a short turn, cyclical end-markets, no visibility. Nothing here adds to it except the size of a single prior-year defence order moving group growth by three points, which shows how lumpy that line is in both directions.

What it does to the case

  • 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1), B: upward pressure. Revenue growth and sell-side upgrades are both stronger; 'Goldilocks growth' at C is under upward pressure if 19 percent is more than one quarter. The margin reading is pulled both ways.
  • 'Change Forces' (E 1, BB 1), B: modest upward pressure on the external half, from regional breadth. Internal forces unchanged; the cost programme completed as dated.
  • 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1), F: same letter, more pressure. The price is 107.2 SEK (9 November 2026), 26.2 times, the 99th percentile of ten years against the 82nd when the case was written. That is about where the case put its eighteen-month value on the cleared branch, 108.0 SEK. The market has paid for the cleared branch before either condition is shown.
  • BB3, 'Major Corporate Change': upward pressure on output evidence and maturity. The case asked whether consensus earnings would rise further after Q3; one-month earnings revisions are +6 percent.
  • BB2, 'Business': downward pressure on the financial-outputs deduction. The case described gross margin as 62 to 63 percent and falling; it is now below that range.
  • BB4, 'Riskiness' C: more pressure. Mark-to-market risk, already F, is larger at 26.2 times, and the 'Cockroach' flag has a second consecutive gross-margin fall behind it.
  • Key Drivers and Key Risks: organic growth and the EBITAC margin are under less pressure. Gross margin and mix has crossed its alarm of 62 percent; the refutation level, below 62 percent for two quarters, is one print away. The cleared branch's odds are pulled both ways, one condition running well ahead and the other failing.
  • Pinned letters: technical momentum D from C; thematic C from D; sell-side momentum C and valuation F unchanged.
  • Thresholds: none crossed. The case progresses at about 75 SEK, or about 82 SEK on the cleared branch.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on. It should carry the remaining brokers' estimates and targets, Deutsche Bank's first among them. That tells us whether the upgrade to FY2027 is larger than the mid-single-digit the case called modest, and whether brokers model gross margin recovering or falling. It is unlikely to carry anything new from the company: no gross margin bridge, no backlog figure, no split of the margin beat.

The Q4 2026 print, in early 2027. A second gross margin below 62 percent meets the case's own refutation level and favours reading one. A recovery with growth held favours reading two. The case's comparatives are soft on growth (3 percent) and hard on margin (25.2 percent), with Waygate consolidating part-way through.

What nothing is committed to answer. The company has not committed to quantifying the backlog, to bridging gross margin between mix, tariffs and currency, or to stating how much of the margin was currency and refunds. Reading one can therefore only be inferred from the series over two quarters, not read off a disclosure.
