Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Greatland Resources delivered a transformational FY26 financial print that fundamentally validated its strategic pivot from junior explorer to debt-free mid-tier producer, accumulating A$1,289 million in net cash while beating production and cost guidance across 328,987 ounces of gold output at an All-In Sustaining Cost of A$2,179 per ounce. The operational delivery completely de-risks Havieron's A$1,065 million underground development without equity dilution, positioning the company for substantial re-rating relative to Australian mining peers trading at 1.1x to 1.2x Net Asset Value compared to Greatland's deeply discounted 0.39x NAV multiple. The most striking financial outcome from the FY26 results was the velocity of operational cash generation from the recently acquired Telfer mine. Full-year operating cash flows reached A$714 million, with the June quarter alone generating A$302 million in operational cash, enabling Greatland to fully recoup its upfront cash acquisition price within just two quarters of ownership. This exceptional cash harvest was driven by record spot gold prices

averaging A$6,468 per ounce in the final quarter alongside robust copper by-product credits of A$16,107 per tonne, which together delivered 64% all-in sustaining margins and quarterly revenue of A$545 million. The combination of favourable commodity pricing and operational efficiency improvements, particularly 88.0% metallurgical gold recoveries, enabled Greatland to significantly outperform market expectations on both production volume and unit cost metrics.

The FY26 production beat was material, with gold output reaching 328,987 ounces against upper guidance of 310,000 ounces, representing a 6% outperformance driven by superior gold recoveries averaging 88.0% and strong copper by-product realizations. Even more impressive was the cost performance, where Greatland delivered an All-In Sustaining Cost of A$2,179 per ounce, comfortably undercutting the guided range of A$2,400 to A$2,800 per ounce.

This dual beat on production volume and unit costs stands in stark contrast to the broader Western Australian gold sector, where peers have struggled with inflationary pressures and contractor rate increases. The outperformance validates management's assertion that the Telfer acquisition provides an immediately accretive cash generation platform capable of funding organic growth without balance sheet strain. The consensus deviation narrative centres on management's transparent framing of FY27 as a transitional saddle year.

Management explicitly defined FY27 as a transitional saddle year characterized by lower head grades and elevated capital expenditure, arguing this reinvestment phase directly builds the foundation for long-term production growth. Guidance for FY27 calls for gold output of 260,000 to 300,000 ounces at an elevated AISC of A$2,900 to A$3,330 per ounce, with higher unit costs reflecting processing lower-grade stockpiles while reinvesting over A$700 million in growth capital across Telfer life extensions and Havieron decline construction.

Sell-side analysts, led by SCP Equity Research's Justin Chan, characterized this guidance as a temporary divisor effect rather than structural operational decay, emphasizing that elevated unit costs stem from lower gold ounces produced acting as the divisor while fixed mine costs remain relatively stable. The market's initial negative reaction to this guidance adjustment, which drove a 24% single-day share price drawdown on 29 July 2025, was subsequently reversed as investors recognized the strategic rationale for reinvesting cash flows into multi-decade asset expansion rather than maximizing near-term margins. 2 - Earnings Calls From an operational delivery perspective, both Havieron and Telfer exceeded critical development and production milestones.

Greatland achieved total de-risking of Havieron's development funding and permitting by receiving primary State and Federal environmental permits, enabling Board Final Investment Decision approval in June 2026, while cash reserves eliminate financial execution risk. The December 2025 Feasibility Study expanded Havieron's steady-state underground mining target to 3.9 million tonnes per annum through a dual-decline layout, targeting 266,000 ounces of annual gold production at a lowest-quartile AISC of A$1,610 per ounce over a 17- plus year mine life.

On the Telfer side, Group Ore Reserves expanded 62% to 5.0 million ounces of gold, supported by a 150% surge in Telfer reserves to 1.8 million ounces, with a baseload of 90.6 million tonnes of open pit ore securing continuous mill feed through the transition period. This reserve expansion, coupled with aggressive exploration success at West Dome Underground and the Pinnacles extension, provides management with multiple pathways to extend Telfer's operational mine life well beyond the current two-year production outlook of 280,000 to 320,000 ounces per annum.

The Internal Rate of Return implications from the Feasibility Study are substantial and highly sensitive to prevailing commodity prices. The December 2025 Havieron Feasibility Study adopted conservative long-term base case metal price assumptions of A$4,500 per ounce for gold and A$15,747 per tonne for copper, yet spot gold prices exceeding A$6,250 per ounce generate post-tax IRR expansion to 31.5% and NPV5% expansion to A$5.39 billion for Havieron. Given that Greatland maintains 100% upside exposure to spot gold prices with zero mandatory hedging covenants in its corporate credit facilities, the company is positioned to capture the full margin expansion from sustained elevated gold prices. The zero-cost put option structure covering 150,000 ounces at strike floors up to A$5,200 per ounce through June 2027 provides cash flow downside protection during the peak capital deployment phase without capping spot participation.

This unhedged commodity strategy, combined with a debt-free balance sheet holding A$1,289 million in net cash, creates an asymmetric risk-reward profile where upside IRR sensitivity to gold prices is preserved while downside funding risk is eliminated. The structural transformation achieved through consolidating 100% ownership of both Telfer and Havieron represents the single most important change to Greatland's investment thesis.

Greatland completed its core strategic transformation from a junior explorer with a 30% non-managing joint venture interest into a 100% owner-operator of both the Telfer mine and Havieron project, eliminating joint venture decision friction and consolidating full operational control over the Paterson Province asset platform. Management re-anchored group corporate strategy around a Paterson regional hub-and-spoke model using Telfer's 20 million tonnes per annum dual-train processing plant, with securing captive regional mill capacity eliminating capital-intensive processing construction risk for Havieron and satellite discoveries.

This infrastructure moat is difficult for competitors to replicate, as regional discoveries across the Paterson Province cannot justify standalone plant capital outlays at current interest rates, effectively positioning Greatland as the sole commercial processing hub for all future Paterson Province discoveries.

The sell-side response to the earnings update was overwhelmingly constructive despite near-term guidance adjustments. SCP Equity Research maintained its BUY rating and reaffirmed its price target at A$9.60 per share, derived from a 1.0x NAV5% framework, noting that Greatland's valuation remains heavily discounted at 0.39x NAV compared to Australian mid-tier gold peers. Analyst Justin Chan emphasized that Telfer's quarterly operating cash flow of A$175 million confirmed the asset's rapid capital return, with the upfront cash purchase price fully recouped through operational cash flow within two quarters.

Analysts further emphasized that Greatland's A$1,289 million cash balance and operational cash flow eliminate the need for secondary equity raises, with existing liquidity and A$500 million in debt facilities completely securing the A$1,065 million Havieron development build. The progressive price target increases from 13 pence in December 2024 to 17 pence, 21 pence, 22 pence, and ultimately A$9.60 per share following ASX listing reflect expanding mine life and operational de-risking at Telfer, with no major covering broker publishing a rating downgrade following recent operating updates.

Management's strategic narrative evolved from exploratory asset positioning to disciplined cash harvest and hub expansion during the reporting period. Managing Director Shaun Day transformed management's tone from junior explorer marketing to tier-one operational execution following the 100% consolidation of Telfer and Havieron, framing Greatland as a self-funding mid-tier producer capable of operating Western Australia's third-largest goldcopper processing infrastructure.

Management explicitly set a medium-term production target of 400,000 to 500,000 ounces of gold per annum from the combined Telfer-Havieron complex, building on FY26 delivered production of 328,987 ounces which beat market guidance of 260,000 to 310,000 ounces. Capital allocation priorities remained firmly focused on organic reinvestment rather than shareholder distributions, with management confirming that cash flows will be strictly directed toward organic development and mine life extension rather than early dividend distributions, viewing organic reinvestment in Telfer and Havieron as delivering superior risk-adjusted returns.

The macroeconomic backdrop presents a mixed but generally supportive environment for Greatland's execution. Greatland achieved an average realized gold price of A$6,468 per ounce in 4Q26 and A$6,223 per ounce across full-year FY26, with record gold prices providing substantial top-line revenue strength driving full-year operating cash flows from Telfer to A$714 million. However, inflationary pressures across the Western Australian resources sector driven by competitive labor markets and contractor rate increases impact Greatland's forward cost structure, with management explicitly acknowledging that broad economy-wide cost inflation contributes to higher FY27 AISC guidance of A$2,900 to A$3,330 per ounce.

The company has partially insulated itself from input cost volatility through long-term infrastructure arrangements, including natural gas power generation via the Pilbara Pipeline System, life-of-mine open pit contracts with Macmahon Holdings under cost-plus structures, and a 23 million tonne surface stockpile buffer representing over 12 months of continuous mill feed. The competitive positioning analysis reveals that Greatland trades at a substantial valuation discount to regional peers despite comparable or superior operational metrics.

Western Australian gold producers trade at an average NAV multiple of 1.1x to 1.2x and US$638 million per 100,000 ounces per annum, whereas Greatland trades at 0.39x to 0.50x NAV, with closing this valuation gap offering compelling equity re-rating upside as Havieron reaches production. Recent transaction metrics in Western Australia reflect an average valuation of US$659 per reserve ounce and US$209 per resource ounce for derisked pre-production assets, underscoring strong corporate appetite among cash-rich producers seeking accretive reserve replacement.

Greatland's ownership of Telfer's 20 million tonnes per annum dual-train mill creates an insurmountable processing moat in the Paterson Province, where regional exploration discoveries lack standalone infrastructure and cannot justify independent plant capital outlays at current interest rates. From a regulatory and political perspective, Greatland has systematically de-risked its permitting pathway through proactive engagement with traditional owners and environmental regulators.

Greatland achieved a major permitting milestone by securing both primary environmental approvals for Havieron from Commonwealth and Western Australian regulators in April and May 2026, receiving Federal clearance under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act 1999 on 24 April 2026 and WA Ministerial Statement 1273 on 25 May 2026, enabling the Board to formally approve the Final Investment Decision for Havieron in June 2026.

Operations are governed under the Telfer-Havieron Indigenous Land Use Agreement registered under the Native Title Act 1993 in February 2016, with Greatland formally executing a Deed of Assumption on 4 December 2024 to assume all rights and obligations under the Martu ILUA. The consolidated environmental framework being developed by the WA Department of Water and Environmental Regulation streamlines long-term statutory compliance overheads by unifying historic Ministerial Statements into a single regulatory structure.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish investment case centers on capital concentration risk and execution exposure during the critical FY27 transition year. The strongest counter-hypothesis is that simultaneous multi-asset capital deployment drains net cash reserves, with allocating A$315-335 million to Telfer growth capital alongside A$365-435 million to Havieron pre-production in FY27 leaving cash generation vulnerable to cost inflation, where localized labor or contractor inflation in Western Australia could compress operating margins during peak capital build years.

While WA mining labor and contractor costs remain elevated, the core risk to the investment case is the concentration of capital expenditure into the A$1.065 billion Havieron underground build, where potential underground development delays or inflation could squeeze net cash reserves. Any unexpected cost overruns or underground construction delays would become observable in quarterly physical decline advance rates, potentially forcing debt drawdowns against the A$500 million revolving credit facility and compressing projected Internal Rates of Return. The evidence that would fundamentally change the bullish investment thesis would be a material deviation from Havieron's underground development schedule or capital budget.

The primary evidence that would overturn the bullish thesis is an unexpected cost overrun or underground delay at Havieron, which would become observable in the December 2026 quarterly update if physical decline advance rates fall below study schedules, with any capital cost expansion beyond the A$1.065 billion budget or secondary permitting delay compressing net cash and forcing debt drawdowns. Secondary trigger points include failure to convert West Dome Underground resources into maiden reserves during the H2 FY27 feasibility study, unexpected delays in receiving secondary environmental clearing permits and water licenses in 1QFY27, or a sustained collapse in spot gold prices below the A$4,650 per ounce put option floor combined with a strengthening Australian dollar that compresses realized revenue margins.

Looking forward, the two most critical items for investors to monitor in upcoming reporting periods are the receipt of secondary environmental permits and the publication of the West Dome Underground feasibility study. The first item to check next is the official grant of secondary State environmental clearing permits and water licenses in 1QFY27, which allow site clearing and main boxcut construction to commence at Havieron, confirming the transition from study phase to physical ground development.

The second item to check next is the publication of the West Dome Underground feasibility study in H2 FY27, with maiden underground reserves at West Dome potentially supplying high-grade feed into the Telfer mill by FY28, providing low-capital high-grade ore that displaces lower-grade stockpiles prior to Havieron startup. These two milestones will provide clear observable evidence of whether management can execute on its ambitious parallel development strategy while maintaining cost discipline and schedule adherence.

An overlooked aspect of Greatland's competitive positioning is the strategic value of its regional processing monopoly in the Paterson Province. Telfer's 20 million tonnes per annum dual-train mill acts as an insurmountable regional processing moat, with regional discoveries across the Paterson Province unable to justify standalone plant capital outlays at current interest rates, positioning Greatland as the sole commercial hub for all Paterson Province discoveries.

This infrastructure dominance creates optionality for future third-party tolltreating arrangements or strategic acquisitions of nearby exploration discoveries, potentially allowing Greatland to supplement its own mine feed with external ore sources without incremental processing capital expenditure. The hub-and-spoke model establishes a durable competitive advantage that becomes increasingly valuable as junior explorers make new discoveries in the region but lack pathways to commercial production without access to Greatland's processing infrastructure.

In conclusion, Greatland Resources has successfully executed the most critical phase of its transformation from explorer to producer, validating the Telfer acquisition thesis through exceptional operational and financial delivery in FY26. The company now stands at an inflection point where it must balance near-term margin compression from lower-grade stockpile processing against the long-term strategic imperative of funding Havieron's underground development and extending Telfer's mine life.

The debt-free balance sheet holding A$1,289 million in net cash, combined with 100% unhedged exposure to record gold prices and zero equity dilution risk, creates a compelling risk-reward profile for long-term investors. However, the concentration of over A$700 million in annual growth capital deployment during FY27 leaves limited room for execution error, making quarterly operational updates on underground advance rates, cost control, and permitting timelines critical inputs for validating management's multi-decade production ambitions. The deep valuation discount to peers suggests substantial re-rating potential exists as Havieron progresses toward first production in FY29, though investors should remain alert to signs of capital budget expansion or schedule slippage that could trigger debt drawdowns and compress projected returns.
