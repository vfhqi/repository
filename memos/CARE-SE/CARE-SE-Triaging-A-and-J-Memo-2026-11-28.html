Careium AB (CARE-SE): a recovering telecare operator with 73% recurring revenue, on 9.2x earnings, offering about +35% at the guideline multiple but held back by a 2027 UK hardware cliff and thin liquidity

Triaging A&J Memo, 27 November 2026

Price SEK 29.40; market capitalisation SEK 710m; enterprise value SEK 895m; 24.3m shares; two-year-forward P/E 9.2x. All money is in SEK.

#0 The investment case

The five conclusions that matter for the decision

  1. FY2026 EPS should beat consensus by 10-15% on 11 February 2027. Viewforth expects SEK 2.40 against the brokers' 2.08, because consensus implies a Q4 well below Q3's run-rate in what is normally the strongest quarter.
  2. FY2027 earnings rest on changes already made. Norway's onboarding is finished, the Doro hybrid loan is settled and licence savings are under way, which take the EBITDA margin to about 17% and EPS to about SEK 3.0.
  3. The return is about +35% in 12-18 months at the 11.0x guideline multiple (+23% at Viewforth's own 10.0x), and about +20% when weighted by probability.
  4. The main risk has a date. UK hardware demand from the analogue switch-off ends after January 2027, while segment consensus still has UK product sales growing 20% in 2027.
  5. The FY2027 EBITDA margin is the single largest judgement. If the margin stalls at FY2026's 15.8%, the return falls to roughly nothing, and the stock's illiquidity means position size must be set by the ability to exit.

The one idea. Careium earns 73% of its revenue from recurring, inflation-indexed municipal contracts, yet its earnings swing like a cyclical's, because its fixed-cost alarm centres turn small changes in volume and one-off costs into large changes in profit.

Careium runs the whole chain of home alarms for the elderly in Sweden, Norway, the UK and the Netherlands. It designs the devices (made in Asia and paid for in dollars), owns the software platform (i-Care Center) and staffs the 24/7 alarm centres that answer 344,000 connected users. The recurring service income comes from four-to-five-year municipal contracts with inflation indexation; the other 27% of revenue is hardware, sold or leased when a customer upgrades.

Each alarm centre is a fixed cost that needs about 5,000 connections to break even, and the software has near-zero marginal cost. Careium's profit is therefore extremely sensitive to volume and to one-off onboarding and installation costs: a 50bp move in EBIT margin moves EPS by about 8% (ABG). That sensitivity explains why a business with entirely non-cyclical demand has an adjusted EBIT margin history of 6.4% in 2019, minus 7.4% in 2022, 9.2% in 2024 and 6.0% in 2025. The main risk follows from the same fixed cost base: when customers delay purchases (a UK switch-off slips, a Swedish framework is late) or a new contract starts (Norway), the costs stay and the margin collapses.

The change: four forces, three of them already visible in reported results.

  1. The UK analogue-to-digital switch-off (January 2027) is forcing the non-discretionary replacement of over 1.6m analogue alarm units. Careium's UK product sales rose 33% in Q2-26, and group revenue grew 24% in Q2 and 22% in Q3, against a sector growing 4%.
  2. The two temporary margin drags of H1-26 have cleared. The Norway municipal onboarding (a 12-year contract worth SEK 24m a year) completed its technical integration in Q2, and the Swedish financial-lease accounting that depressed reported sales through 2025 has rolled off. Q3 gross margin was therefore 45.0% and EBITDA margin 18.1%, the best since the 2021 listing.
  3. The balance sheet and the leadership were repaired in one quarter. The SEK 64m Doro hybrid loan was settled for SEK 35m, which removes SEK 3-4m of annual interest and the block on dividends. Tove Christiansson, who ran MedCap's Assistive Tech division at 27% EBITA margins, became permanent CEO on 1 June 2026 after ten months of interim leadership.
  4. Sweden's national ADDA purchasing framework was signed in May 2026, reopening a home market that municipal buyers had frozen for a year.

The headwinds are real but smaller. ADDA admits more competitors; UK tender pricing is aggressive; UK minimum-wage inflation raises the cost of labour-heavy alarm centres; the dollar cost base is unhedged; and the UK hardware surge has a visible expiry date.

How the change reaches the financials. Viewforth expects FY2026 EPS of about SEK 2.40, roughly 50% above FY2025's SEK 1.57 (brokers' basis). Revenue grows about 16% in FY2026 (nine months: +17.3%), and the EBITDA margin recovers from 14.1% to about 15.8%, helped further by lower interest. The two brokers sit at SEK 2.08 because their implied Q4 steps back from Q3's run-rate, so the 11-Feb-2027 report is the first opportunity for upgrades.

In FY2027, revenue growth slows to about 7% as UK hardware normalises. Three effects nonetheless take the EBITDA margin to about 17% and EPS to about SEK 3.0 (consensus 2.75): a full year without onboarding costs, about SEK 10m of licence savings from moving Swedish users onto i-Care Center, and slower opex growth. Most of the gap between Viewforth's and consensus FY2027 EPS lies in interest and tax, which are mechanical. What re-rates the shares is a change in the market's description of Careium, from "margin relief" to "a recurring business with a 17-18% margin, 7% growth and the capacity to pay dividends, on 9x earnings".

The foundation: what is sound

  • 73% recurring, indexed revenue
  • 40% market share in Sweden and Norway
  • No customer above 10% of sales
  • A proprietary platform with switching costs
  • Positive free cash flow every year since 2023 (FactSet FCF SEK 83m in FY2025, on SEK 32m of net income)

The foundation: what is weak

  • 6% EBIT margin and 6.4% ROIC, against 20-27% at the best peers
  • Gross margins structurally below Tunstall's
  • Three CEOs in a year, a CTO who lasted 14 months and a vacant CCO role
  • Blockholders (Cidro 17.9%, Accendo 12.1%) who removed the chair in 2025
  • All development cost capitalised (SEK 146m), none expensed; no FX hedging
  • Daily traded value of about USD 0.1m

The foundation is of medium quality at best, and management has shown it can execute a recovery but not yet that it can hold a margin for two years. The delivery record is mixed: FY2024 targets were met at the top of the range, FY2025 profit missed by 36% and guidance was withdrawn, and the four quarters since have beaten expectations on organic growth (and the last two on margin).

The setup fits only moderately. Careium is closest to a Demand-driven EPS Upgrader (the switch-off, Norway and ADDA lifting volume through a fixed cost base), with a Big Corporate Change overlay (new CEO, platform unification, hybrid cleared). The Cause-for-Concern-Clearing element (the FY2025 profit warning and CEO churn, available at 7.2x in March 2026) has largely been paid for, since the multiple has risen from the 10th to the 61st percentile of its own range.

  • Present: dated, non-discretionary demand; onboarding costs behind; estimates turning up; a clean balance sheet.
  • Missing: a durable margin record; a credible second growth leg once UK hardware fades; medium-term targets from the new CEO; and broader sell-side coverage (two brokers, one of them sponsored).
The reward and the skew: positive, but only moderately. If the case plays out, FY2027 EPS of about SEK 3.0 and a move from 9.2x to the 11.0x guideline multiple give a share price near SEK 39-40 in 12-18 months, a return of about 35%, and about 68% over three years. If the case fails (UK hardware falls 30% after the switch-off, ADDA pricing holds Nordic gross margin below 40%, and FY2027 EPS is SEK 2.4 on 7.6x), the shares fall to about SEK 20, a loss of 30%. Weighted by probability, the 18-month return is about +20%.

Why the skew is positive: the 2026 earnings beat is already in the bank, the balance-sheet and leadership fixes cannot be reversed, and the stock is still on a single-digit multiple with an 8% free-cash-flow yield on FY2027.
Why it is not strongly positive: the biggest driver expires in January 2027, and the stock is too illiquid to sell quickly after a disappointment.

What has to be true, and when it will be tested.

  1. Q4 2026 report (11-Feb-2027): EBITDA margin at or above 17%, group gross margin at or above 44%, Nordic gross margin back above 40%, FY2026 EPS at or above SEK 2.30, and UK product sales still growing. Confirmation would be a FY2026 EPS beat of 10% or more, consensus FY2027 EPS moving toward SEK 3.0 and, ideally, a first dividend or medium-term targets from the new CEO.
  2. Q1 and Q2 2027 reports (late April and mid July 2027): revenue still growing against the +24% Q2-26 comparative, UK product sales holding above about SEK 35m a quarter after the switch-off, and EBITDA margin at or above 15.5% in Q1 and 16.5% in Q2. Confirmation would be proof that the H1-26 margins, not the Q3-26 margin, were the anomaly.
  3. Through 2027: Nordic product sales recovering under ADDA (consensus SEK 18m rising to 30m) with Nordic gross margin holding at or above 40%. Confirmation would show that ADDA's additional competitors are not eroding prices.

The judgements that move the value most.

  1. The FY2027 EBITDA margin (17.0% assumed) matters most. Each 1pp of margin is SEK 10.6m of EBITDA, about SEK 8.3m of net income and SEK 0.34 of EPS (11%). If margins stall at FY2026's 15.8%, EPS is about SEK 2.55 and, at 11x, the shares are worth SEK 28 against SEK 33 in the base case. The margin alone is therefore the difference between a 35% return and none.
  2. UK hardware after January 2027 is the most exposed assumption. FactSet segment consensus has UK product sales rising from SEK 150m in 2026 to 180m in 2027. If UK product sales instead fall 30% (SEK 45m) at a 35% gross margin, and half of the lost gross profit reaches EBITDA after opex adjusts, EBITDA falls about SEK 12m and EPS about SEK 0.38 (13%). At an unchanged multiple, that fall is worth about SEK 4 a share.
  3. Revenue growth (7% in FY2027) matters much less than margin. Each 1pp of growth is SEK 10.6m of revenue and, at a 30% drop-through, SEK 3m of EBITDA and SEK 0.10 of EPS (3%).
  4. The multiple supplies a fifth of the return. Each turn of P/E on about SEK 3.6 of two-year-forward EPS is worth SEK 3.6 a share (12% of the price), and the move from today's 9.2x to the 11.0x guideline adds 20%. If the stock stays at 9.2x, the 12-month return is only the 13% growth in forward EPS.
  5. Currency cuts both ways. The Risks report puts the cost of a 10% rise in the dollar against the krona at SEK 17m of extra cost of goods and SEK 0.55 of EPS, since the exposure is unhedged; a 10% fall in sterling costs about SEK 5m of EBITDA through translation. The consensus macro view (a strengthening krona) would therefore reduce reported growth through translation but lower dollar costs.

#1 Operating environment: dated tailwinds on volume, headwinds on price

Evidence Demand for telecare is structural and non-cyclical. Europe's over-65 population is heading for 200m by 2050, over 90% of seniors prefer to stay at home, and public telecare budgets are shielded from the business cycle. The European telecare market is projected to grow 11-13% a year, with next-generation users growing 21% and the traditional base 3%.

Three dated infrastructure events force spending. The UK PSTN (analogue telephone network) switch-off comes in January 2027; over 60% of the UK installed base was still analogue in 2024, and copper fault rates rose 45% that year. UK and Swedish 3G shutdowns were completed by late 2025, and Swedish and French 2G networks close from 2025 to 2027. Separately, Sweden's ADDA framework (signed 12-May-2026 for four years, and used by about 80% of municipalities) ends a year-long purchasing freeze, and Norway, the most advanced market, awards 12-year contracts.

Currency adds to reported growth for now, but may reverse. The sell-side's updated assumptions add about 1% a year to reported sales for 2026-2028. Morgan Stanley and Nordea, however, expect the krona to strengthen into 2027 (EUR/SEK 10.2-10.5), which would reverse that gain on the 56% of sales earned in sterling and euros.

Headwinds The headwinds fall mainly on price and margin. English councils face a GBP 6.2bn two-year funding gap. UK minimum-wage rises raise alarm-centre labour costs, which municipal contracts index only annually. UK tender pricing is aggressive ahead of the switch-off, with competitors bidding below Careium's floor. ADDA admits more competitors than before. UK telecoms operators (Virgin Media O2, with Telecare Cardiff) are testing direct migrations that could bypass standalone providers, and Legrand is buying software companies (Enovation, Performation, Axel Health) to become a platform competitor. Hardware is 85% sourced in Asia, and 35% of cost of goods is in dollars, unhedged.

Tailwinds act on volume, and are dated

  • UK PSTN switch-off, January 2027
  • 3G shutdowns done; 2G closures 2025-2027
  • ADDA ends Sweden's purchasing freeze
  • Ageing population; next-generation users growing fastest

Headwinds act on price and margin

  • Aggressive UK tender pricing; council funding gap
  • UK minimum-wage rises, indexed only annually
  • More competitors under ADDA; telecoms and Legrand moving in
  • Unhedged dollar cost of goods

Inference and so-what The tailwinds are unusually dated and non-discretionary for a healthcare company, which makes the growth surge credible, but the UK tailwind ends in a cliff in 2027. The market's narrative ("the pressure valve opening") captures the tailwinds. In my view the market underweights the question of UK hardware demand after the deadline, since segment consensus still has UK product sales growing 20% in 2027.

Counter-case Deadlines can slip, and a slip freezes orders. The UK switch-off has already moved from 2025 to 2027, and a further grace period would freeze orders as the last delay did in late 2024, when product sales fell 33%. What would change my view: Ofcom or BT announcing further analogue extensions, or Nordic gross margin below 39% for two quarters after ADDA.

Conclusion: a strong, dated demand tailwind, with price-side headwinds that are real but secondary over the next 12 months. Bearing on #0: the tailwind supports "The change", and its January 2027 expiry is the main fragility in "What has to be true".
Score: 66, set by the Rater. #2 Internal change forces: the 2027 repairs are done; the 2028 changes are unproven

Evidence Leadership. Tove Christiansson became permanent CEO on 1-Jun-2026, after interim CEO Peter Heuman's nine months of stabilisation. She spent 11 years at MedCap, grew its Assistive Tech division to SEK 1.0bn of sales at 27-28% EBITA margins, and chairs the Swedish Medtech Association. CFO David Granath (in post since August 2024) is the anchor of continuity and has lifted free cash flow every year.

Strategy. Careium is moving from a buy-and-build collection of fragmented regional businesses to one European platform. Three UK alarm centres have been merged into Blackburn, unprofitable UK contracts exited and UK finance systems unified. Sixty percent of Swedish users have been migrated to i-Care Center, with SEK 10m of annual external licence savings as the prize. Acquisitions are paused until targets can be integrated within nine months.

Capital structure and governance. The Doro hybrid loan (SEK 64m at 5.5%) was settled for SEK 35m in July 2026; SEK 29m was written back to equity, and the dividend restriction is gone. Kai Tavakka (Accendo) left the board in April 2026 and Sophie Reinius (former CFO of Arelion) joined; the board started a share programme and added clawback and measurable one-year bonus targets.

Problems The management bench is thin and governance is weak. Careium has had three CEOs in a year. CTO Jubner left after 14 months (Golubev is interim CTO), and CCO Puumalainen left in March 2026, with sales leadership still unfilled at the last report. There is no disclosed succession plan. Blockholders control 30% of the shares and removed the chair in June 2025. The board acts as its own audit and remuneration committee and has opted out of the Swedish Corporate Governance Code. The CEO's options vest early (February 2029) with a SEK 36.84 price barrier, and the CFO holds only 15,000 shares.

Done: the changes that matter for 2027 earnings

  • Norway integration finished
  • Blackburn consolidation done; unprofitable UK contracts exited
  • Licence savings from the i-Care Center migration
  • Hybrid settled: interest saved, dividend block gone

Open: the changes that matter for 2028 and beyond

  • One platform across four countries, and one culture
  • Interim CTO; CCO role unfilled
  • No medium-term targets from the new CEO
  • Blockholder-driven board without separate committees

Inference and so-what The changes that drive 2027 earnings are executed or mechanical, not promised. The changes that matter for 2028 and beyond (one platform across four countries, one culture) depend on a new CEO whose record is in a higher-margin niche and who has inherited a thin bench. The research does not say whether she has set medium-term targets, or when she might; such targets are the single most useful thing the February report could add.

Counter-case The organisation, not the strategy, may be the constraint. Former executives say unifying systems was easier than unifying "us-versus-them" local cultures, and UK acquisitions took six quarters to stabilise. Another senior departure within 12 months of the CEO's start would point to that constraint. What would change my view: a CCO appointment and a stable team through 2027 (positive), or a second interim appointment (negative).

Conclusion: the near-term internal changes are delivered and strong; the long-term changes are credible but unproven under a new CEO. Bearing on #0: supports "The change" and "How the change reaches the financials" for FY2027, and leaves "The foundation" open until the management team shows stability.

#3 Industry structure, business model, financial profile, management: a medium-quality business with a high-quality revenue profile

Evidence The industry is concentrated in equipment and protected by scale. Traditional telecare equipment is a duopoly of Tunstall and Legrand (70% share), while next-generation services are fragmented. The barriers to entry are accreditation (ISO 27001, TSA, Chainmark), tender history, the SCAIP alarm protocol that Careium's engineers invented and, above all, scale: an alarm centre with fewer than 5,000 connections loses money.

Careium holds strong local positions on long contracts. It has a 40% share in Sweden and Norway, 14% in the UK, and 10% (a top-three position) in the Netherlands; Tunstall was excluded from Swedish tenders on product quality. Contracts run four to five years (Norway's 12) with annual indexation, and the largest client is below 10% of sales.

Value in telecare accrues to software and private-pay customers, not to labour or hardware.

Careium's financial profile is below its peers on margins and returns. In FY2025 the EBITDA margin was 14.1%, the adjusted EBIT margin 6.0% and ROIC 6.4%; FactSet free cash flow was SEK 83m on SEK 854m of sales, and net debt including leases was SEK 191m (1.4x EBITDA). By comparison, MedCap Assistive Tech earns a 27% EBITDA margin, Vitec a 19.6% EBIT margin and Doro a 50% gross margin.

Inference The recurring, indexed and diversified contract base deserves a steady multiple, but the labour-heavy delivery model and sub-scale hardware purchasing cap Careium's margins well below those of the software peers management compares itself with. The i-Care Center migration is the one lever that shifts the mix toward the 70%-margin software activity, and it is the single most important margin driver. Progress on the migration (60% of Swedish users moved; the first external SaaS customer, MD Medicus, with 60,000 users) therefore matters more than any quarter's hardware sales.

Management The CEO's record is excellent, but in a niche, and numeric targets are absent. The board is engaged, perhaps too engaged. Culture indicators are mixed: 82% participation in the staff survey and 56% women among managers, but friction in recruiting minimum-wage staff for UK centres. Management states its goals qualitatively and has set no stretching numeric targets.

Counter-case The platform may be a cost saver rather than a moat. Careium's former UK finance director calls competitive advantage in telecare "relatively replicable" and describes tenders as a matter of "price and scale". If that is right, the fair multiple is the 8-9x EV/EBITA paid for Tunstall-like peers, not a software multiple. What would change my view: Netherlands-like gross margins appearing in another region, or external SaaS wins beyond MD Medicus.

Conclusion: a favourable, non-cyclical industry structure; a defensible but not advantaged business model; and a financial profile that is below average on margins and returns but above average on recurring revenue and cash conversion. #4 Guidance conservatism, sell-side expectations, delivery record: a short, improving record and brokers anchored low

Evidence Careium has no numeric guidance. It withdrew its 2026 guidance with the December 2025 profit warning and now gives only a qualitative outlook. Management told the market that H1-26 margins would be squeezed and would then normalise; both halves happened as described, which the Change Forces report labels an "honest tracker".

The delivery record swings year by year.

  • FY2024, met at the top of the range: organic growth 5.6% (range 5-10%) and EBIT margin 9.2% (range 7.5-10%).
  • FY2025, a 36% miss on profit: EBIT SEK 54m against a floor of SEK 84m, because of integration costs and write-offs; free cash flow, however, beat at SEK 41m against more than SEK 36.9m guided.

Sell-side Two brokers cover the stock, and their FY2026 estimates imply a weak Q4. ABG Sundal Collier (sponsored research, not rated, fair value SEK 20-34) forecasts FY2026 EPS of 2.25, and SEB forecasts 1.92. Consensus FY2027 EPS has risen 4% over three months but is still 12% below its level a year ago. #5 Operating leverage, pricing power, financial leverage, cost-out: one strong gear

Evidence Operating leverage is high and has been proven in both directions. Fixed-cost alarm centres and near-zero-marginal-cost software give a high drop-through of revenue to profit. ABG's sensitivities: a 1pp change in revenue moves EPS 6.8%, a 2pp change in connection growth moves it 9.7%, and 50bp of EBIT margin moves it 7.8%. In Q4-25, revenue rose 0.9% and EBIT fell 68%; in Q3-26, revenue rose 21.6% and EBIT rose 91%.

Pricing power is partial. Public contracts carry annual CPI indexation: the Netherlands fully recovered labour inflation with 2024 price rises, and Oslo renewed at higher prices. But municipal caps limit pass-through, UK legacy rates were fixed at GBP 0.12-0.20 per user per month, and Careium walks away from low-price tenders rather than winning them. The real pricing power lies in private-pay and B2B2C customers, such as the 20,000 private customers in the Netherlands.

Cost-out is about slowing opex growth, not cutting. The identified savings are SEK 10m of licence costs and SEK 4-5m of interest, and the alarm-centre consolidation is already done. Opex grew 18% in Q2-26 and 23% in Q4-25, so the remaining lever is slower opex growth.

Financial leverage is modest (1.4x net debt to EBITDA) and falling. Feedback loops exist but are slow: more connections per centre lift the margin, which funds platform investment, which lowers the cost to serve.

Inference Careium's business model converts revenue growth into profit growth at roughly 2-3 times, which is the whole case for 2026-2027 and the whole risk if growth stalls. With a 17% EBITDA margin and 7% revenue growth, EBITDA grows about 15% and EPS 20% or more; with 2% revenue growth and flat opex, EPS can fall.

Counter-case Cost growth could break the recovery. ABG's own warning is that opex growth above 15% "breaks the EBIT recovery", and wage inflation in UK alarm centres is the one cost line that indexation does not fully cover. What would change my view: opex growth above 10% in a quarter in which revenue grows less than 10%.

Conclusion: high operating leverage, partial pricing power, little financial leverage and modest cost-out scope. The operating leverage is both the engine of the case and its main hazard. Bearing on #0: explains how the change reaches EPS, and why the margin judgement dominates the growth judgement.

#9 Riskiness and simplicity of the case: trackable, but with fast-acting fragilities

Simplicity Three of the case's four moving parts are easy to track each quarter; the fourth, the UK hardware path after the deadline, is not. The trackable parts are group and Nordic gross margin, EBITDA margin, and product versus service sales by region. Careium reports quarterly with regional gross margins and connection counts, and the warning thresholds are explicit: Nordic gross margin below 39%, UK gross margin below 35%, opex growth above 15%, and connection growth below 4%.

Fragilities and assumptions Seven fragilities, two of which can strike in a single report.

  1. The UK hardware surge is time-limited, and segment consensus assumes it continues.
  2. 35% of cost of goods is in dollars and none of it is hedged: a 10% dollar move is SEK 0.55 of EPS, about 18% of FY2027 EPS, and municipal pricing resets only annually.
  3. ADDA admits more competitors into a framework covering 80% of the Swedish market.
  4. UK wage inflation runs against annual indexation.
  5. Capitalised development totals SEK 146m with no research expensed. Reported margins overstate cash earnings by roughly SEK 30m a year of capitalised cost, and a product stall would bring a write-down (the sector digest flags the same pattern at Chemometec and Elekta).
  6. Execution: three CEOs in a year, an interim CTO, a CCO vacancy and a blockholder-driven board.
  7. Liquidity: about 16,000 shares (USD 0.1m) trade a day, and the top ten holders own 68%, so an exit after a disappointment would take weeks and move the price.

A surprise has cost this stock 35-45% before. The shares fell 35% from February to March 2026 on the profit warning, and 45% in 2022 on the margin collapse after the spin-off.

Sector and cyclicality precedents Demand is non-cyclical, but Careium's earnings have behaved cyclically because of execution and the timing of infrastructure changes. EBIT margins were 10% in 2019, 1% in 2021, (7)% in 2022, 9% in 2024 and 6% in 2025. European microcap carve-outs have a 65% base rate of margin-led failure (Risks report). The closest precedent for today's setup is the 2024 UK delay: when the switch-off slipped, product sales fell 33% in a quarter and the shares fell from SEK 35 to 23.

The market environment works against small, complex stocks. The Fitness-for-Fighting digest sets Fragility at Double Underweight and Momentum at Underweight. The case does not need lower interest rates, but Careium is a small, complex, illiquid stock in a market paying for clean defensive stocks, so it fights the environment on size and simplicity rather than on its own fundamentals.

Where I differ The Risks report's "false friend" verdict is half out of date. The report's analysis of competing hypotheses concludes that Careium is a "false friend" (growth masking margin decay) and recommends parking the stock. It was written in June on Q1-26 data; Q2 and Q3 then delivered EBITDA margins of 13.8% and 18.1% with positive free cash flow, which refutes the margin-decay part for 2026. The capitalisation and currency parts remain valid and unaddressed.

Conclusion: a moderately complex case with good trackability, two fast-acting fragilities (the dollar and capitalised development) and one structural fragility (illiquidity). Bearing on #0: these fragilities are why the skew is only moderately positive, and they define the watch list in "What has to be true".
Score: 36, set by the Rater. The memo's FY2027 EPS cone of SEK 2.4-3.3 around 2.97 runs from about (19)% to +11%, amplified by the fixed-cost structure. The case rests on a dated UK hardware driver whose post-January 2027 path cannot be tracked in advance, and it carries fast-acting fragilities: an unhedged dollar cost base (10% is about 18% of EPS), SEK 146m of capitalised development that could bring a write-down, and churn among senior staff arriving by press release. #13 Summary conclusion

A good business recovery in a mediocre-quality, hard-to-trade vehicle.

Careium is a non-cyclical municipal telecare operator, with 73% recurring revenue, whose earnings swing like a cyclical's because fixed alarm-centre and onboarding costs meet lumpy, deadline-driven hardware demand.

In 2026 every swing factor turned positive at once. The UK switch-off pulled hardware demand forward, Norway's onboarding costs ended, the Swedish lease accounting rolled off, ADDA reopened Sweden, the Doro hybrid was settled and a credible permanent CEO arrived. The result was a Q3 EBITDA margin of 18.1% and consensus upgrades that have so far recovered only a third of the 2025 cuts.

The case: FY2026 EPS beats consensus by 10-15% in February; FY2027 delivers a 17% margin on 7% growth, for EPS of about SEK 3; and a stock on 9.2x re-rates toward the 11x it carried in its last delivery phase, for a return of about a third in a year.

What weakens the case: a dated hardware driver with a 2027 cliff that segment consensus ignores, an unhedged dollar cost base, aggressive capitalisation of development costs, a thin and recently churned management bench, and a free float that trades USD 0.1m a day.

What to watch, in order:

  1. The Q4 report, 11-Feb-2027: EBITDA margin at or above 17%, Nordic gross margin above 40%, FY2026 EPS above SEK 2.30, UK product growth, and a dividend or targets from the new CEO.
  2. The Q1 report, late April 2027: UK product sales above SEK 35m and group EBITDA margin above 15.5%, against the first comparative after the deadline.
  3. The Q2 report, mid July 2027: growth still positive against the +24% comparative, and a margin above 16.5%.
  4. In any quarter, warning signs: Nordic gross margin below 39%, opex growth above 15%, a senior departure, capitalised development additions rising faster than sales, or a 10% move in the dollar.
Thesis's Change Forces' Strength: 72, set by the Rater. Positive external forces (66) and executed internal repair (64) are already reaching profits: Q3 EBITDA margin was 18.1%, FY2026 EPS is up about 60% and EPS compounds 15% a year to FY2030 (A floor). That puts it in the 70s, but below 75 because the strongest force expires in January 2027, the profit step-up is a recovery to just above the FY2024 peak margin rather than a new level, and the second growth leg is unproven.
Foundations' Robustness: 45, set by the Rater. Non-discretionary, 73% recurring, indexed municipal revenue with local scale and strong cash conversion would argue for stability, but unlike Richard's CVS case the margins are not stable: they swing like a cyclical's (from (7)% to 10% EBIT) on a labour-heavy, sub-scale model with ROIC below the cost of capital.
