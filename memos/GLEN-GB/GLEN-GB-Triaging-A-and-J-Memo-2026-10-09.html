[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Glencore is a late-cycle cash-return case on a diversified trader-miner, with a self-help overlay, that the market has already paid for. The change is real: a reset of marketing earnings power, a funded recovery in own copper volumes, dated disposals, a capital-return framework under a stated net debt cap and a new listing. At 568.7p those things are in the price, the earnings path beyond FY2026 is flat, and the modal return is about 6 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar; the same case clears the bar at about 471p.

  • The change is internal, dated and funded, and it is additive to a cyclical earnings base rather than transformative of it.
    • On 2-Oct-26 the company raised FY26 Marketing Adjusted EBIT guidance to exceed $5.0bn, against $3.2bn in 2024 and $3.5bn in 2023, and replaced its fixed long-term marketing guidance with a matrix whose midpoint of about $3.5bn is 20 percent above the prior $2.9bn.
    • It confirmed own copper volumes recovering from 840kt in 2026 to 1.0Mt by 2028, a disposal programme of up to $7.5bn, a $10bn net debt cap with every surplus returned, and an ASX secondary listing from 14-Oct-26.
    • Four of the twelve internal forces are strong: guidance raised, perimeter reduction, shareholder returns and balance-sheet discipline.
      • That meets the screen's rule of three.
    • No chief-executive change and no new strategy sit behind them.
      • Gary Nagle has held the role since 1-Jul-21 and the update confirmed continuity.
      • The forces are capital-allocation choices by an unchanged team.
  • The largest single driver of the 2026 step is external and transient.
    • H1 2026 marketing EBIT of $3.3bn, against $2.2bn expected, came from energy and freight volatility and from realised copper prices up 39 percent and zinc up 22 percent.
    • The company itself guides H2 2026 marketing EBIT to $1.4 to 1.7bn.
    • The external picture nets to mixed.
      • Copper demand and concentrate tightness are a structural tailwind
      • Thermal coal pricing, DRC regulation, battery-chemistry substitution, Chinese smelting integration and input-cost inflation are headwinds.
      • Copper cash-cost guidance was raised from 185 to 225 cents a pound in a single half-year.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have stepped up once and then flatten.
    • H1 2026 adjusted EBITDA was $10.1bn, up 86 percent
    • H1 adjusted earnings per share of 31.0 cents beat consensus of 27.3 cents
    • FY2027 consensus earnings per share rose 30 percent in six months and 54 percent in twelve.
    • Then the path is flat: consensus earnings per share of 0.594 USD for FY2026, 0.630 for FY2027 and 0.629 for FY2028
    • Our own modal path 0.61, 0.60, 0.62 and 0.65 USD for FY2026 to FY2029, because marketing normalises to its midpoint as the copper volumes arrive.
    • Sell-side upgrades over the next six to twelve months are likely, because the sell-side's long-term marketing models sit below the new midpoint.
      • They are upgrades to a flat path, not to a rising one.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for 2026 and part of the way beyond it.
    • It shows in the choices (coal retained, the framework reset), the actions (disposals, the listing, land access at Katanga, Argentina's RIGI approval), the operating metrics (marketing delivery; copper volumes up 15 percent in H1 2026), the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates, where pinned sell-side momentum is B.
    • The layer that decides the three-year case, own copper volume at 1.0Mt, is scheduled for 2028 and not yet in the statements. 2026 is guided flat at 840kt.
  • What it costs. 568.7p, after a rise of 59.9 percent in twelve months
    • A 24-month-forward multiple of 11.76x at the 78th percentile of the stock's own five-year range, against a five-year median of 10.19x.
    • That is in line with Rio Tinto on price-to-earnings, which for Glencore is a high point and not a discount, while on enterprise value the stock is the cheapest major at 4.3x 2027E spot EBITDA.
    • The two readings disagree because the same things that keep the enterprise-value multiple low (coal, the DRC, the trading opacity, the litigation file) have always kept the price-to-earnings range low.
  • What it earns.
    • A modal return of 5.8 percent a year over eighteen months and 6.5 percent a year over three years: a cash return of about 3.5 percent a year in base dividends, earnings growth on the 24-month-forward footing of about 3.5 percent a year, and a re-rating slightly below zero at a blended multiple of 11.8x.
    • The cash return carries the return
    • The multiple is the swing factor.
    • The bull branch to 13x reaches about 12.5 percent a year over eighteen months.
      • The bear branch to the five-year median of 10.2x is negative.
      • Neither is modal.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change' at C, not crisply, because the internal forces are a contributor to the upgrades rather than their cause.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the inputs through the outputs into the estimates.
    • It breaks at the last link, the return, because an input change the market has already paid for is not material to us at this price.
    • The case is therefore a park at 568.7p and a progression at or below about 471p.

  • Rating = D, because the thesis is material in its inputs ('Change Forces' B) and in the one-year step up of its outputs ('Financial Outputs' C), but the modal return from this price ('Modal return' F) is about 6 percent a year, the change is in the price and the earnings path beyond FY2026 is flat

    • The parts bind, because the return is what the thesis is for.
    • The forces and the outputs describe what has happened and is well understood
    • The return describes what is left
    • D says the thesis's materiality from here is below the median of the set.
    • Not C, because a thesis whose modal return is under 7 percent a year cannot be rated at the median for materiality whatever the quality of its inputs
    • The swing to C rested on the multiple holding and marketing staying above $4bn into 2027, and neither is modal.
    • Not F, because the inputs are real, dated and funded, the balance sheet and the cash return are strong, and a cheaper entry at about 471p would make the same thesis clear the bar.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The hypothesis, at its strongest: record marketing profits and elevated coal cash are an illusion of resilience that masks structural industrial decay.
      • This is the false-friend profile of the risks research, which found zero hard inconsistencies against nine for the profile in which the case is right, and which puts the joint probability of that pathway at 40 to 45 percent.
      • Copper cash costs were raised to 225 cents a pound and sit in the bottom quartile of cost competitiveness among major producers.
      • H1 2026 industrial EBITDA missed consensus, 6,471 against 6,665 USDm.
      • 75 percent of industrial operating costs are unhedged beyond three months
        • A 20 percent synchronised producer-currency move cuts EBITDA 10.4 percent and earnings per share 21.2 percent.
      • Spot thermal coal fell from $150 to below $110 a tonne.
      • The DRC subcontractor regulator ordered KCC and Mutanda to terminate contracts covering over $5bn of business, and tax authorities sealed KCC's offices in July 2026.
      • The earnings review's own reversal trigger, readily marketable inventories below $25bn with SOFR below 2 percent, would shrink the marketing midpoint to about $2.8bn.
    • What we concede.
      • The 2026 earnings step is volatility- and price-led.
      • The industrial cost position is weak and the DRC is the largest risk.
      • Marketing will normalise, which our modal path already carries at the $3.5bn midpoint from FY2028.
      • The multiple is the risk, not the business.
    • Why we hold our view, which is close to the hypothesis in its conclusion though not in its mechanism.
      • The balance sheet (net debt to adjusted EBITDA 0.56x at H1 2026, $14.0bn of committed liquidity, up to $7.5bn of disposals) and the capital-return framework make the industrial weakness survivable and the cash return durable.
      • The copper volume recovery is funded brownfield growth with RIGI protection in Argentina.
      • The dynamic framework makes the marketing floor observable each half-year.
      • The hypothesis does not change our rating, because our rating already rests on the flat earnings path and the full multiple.
        • It changes the skew: it makes D rather than C the safer reading.

    • Cobalt is oversupplied from Chinese-owned DRC mines.

  • Not A, because Glencore's own copper volumes are guided flat at 840kt in 2026 and the demand inflection reaches its earnings only from 2027 to 2028

    • The turn that matters for the thesis is the industrial volume turn, which is ahead, not behind.
