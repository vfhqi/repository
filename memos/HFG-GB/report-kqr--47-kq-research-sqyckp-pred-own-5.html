Section 5 – IFRS 18 Implementation and Presentation Changes

Section summary
  1. Implementing IFRS 18 will standardize Hilton's income statement by introducing mandatory operating profit subtotals and standardizing categories, while shifting adjusting items into functional expenses.
  2. Mandatory disclosure of management-defined performance measures in a single footnote will significantly increase the transparency and regulatory scrutiny of Hilton's custom adjusted metrics.

Standardized Presentation and Subtotals

1.
Standardized presentation: The introduction of IFRS 18 starting 1 January 2027 represents a major shift in how companies organize their profit and loss statements. This new standard replaces the legacy IAS 1 framework to improve financial presentation consistency. Under IFRS 18, entities must classify income and expenses into five distinct categories: operating, investing, financing, income taxes, and discontinued operations. Consequently, Hilton will present a newly-defined operating profit subtotal that is standardized across the industry.

MPM Disclosure and Auditor Scrutiny

5.
Increased metric transparency: The creation of a mandatory footnote for Management-defined Performance Measures will alter how Hilton communicates adjusted results. Under IFRS 18, any management-defined metric that is not directly defined by IFRS must be disclosed in a single note in the financial statements. NH Investment & Securities noted that this mandatory footnote requires a full reconciliation between the custom adjusted measures and the closest IFRS-defined profit subtotal. This structured disclosure will prevent management from presenting custom adjustments without formal audit oversight.
6.
Enhanced expense disaggregation: The standard's requirements for aggregation and disaggregation will provide much deeper visibility into specific adjusting costs. Companies must provide enhanced disclosures on how information is grouped in the financial statements, particularly for items previously labeled as "other" or "exceptional". For Hilton, this means the specific cash costs of the One Hilton transformation and Foppen integration will be subject to rigorous disclosure rules within the notes. This level of detail will allow investors to track the exact nature of these costs over time.
7.
Rigorous auditor oversight: Moving alternative performance measures into the audited notes will subject adjusting items to higher audit assurance. Under previous guidelines, alternative performance measures were often presented in non-audited sections of the annual report, such as the strategic review or highlights page. By forcing these measures into the notes, IFRS 18 subjects custom adjusted profit figures to formal audit procedures. This regulatory scrutiny will reduce management's flexibility in defining what constitutes an exceptional item.
