[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Tryg is a quality yield at a sector trough, not a change case. It is Scandinavia's largest non-life insurer, a disciplined underwriter in a four-player oligopoly, whose shares have de-rated to the cheapest multiple in their own ten-year history because earnings per share grow 4 to 6 percent a year, half what Sampo and Gjensidige deliver, and because elevated Nordic rates have taken the scarcity premium off bond-like earnings. At DKK 143.5 the modal return is a 7 percent cash yield plus mid-single-digit growth plus a re-rating at even odds, about 11 to 13 percent a year; it does not reach the 20 percent bar, and it does not reach it on the bull's own anchor either.

  • The change under way is internal, well executed and of ordinary size.
    • The 'United Towards 27' programme is worth DKK 1.0bn by 2027 on a normalised insurance service result of DKK 7.5bn, and it is the continuation of a disciplined operator's self-help rather than a multi-dimensional change
    • The chief executive and finance director have been in place since 2023
      • There is no acquisition
      • The 2027 targets were reaffirmed, not raised, on 10 July 2026, and the 2026 revenue guide was cut to about 3 percent from 3.5 percent on the same day
    • The Norway repricing that drove the last margin step has delivered (a Norway combined ratio of 77.3 percent in Q2 2026, a ten-year low) and is now slowing by management's own account
    • The corporate book has been pruned, real estate is being exited, and DKK 17-18bn of capital is being returned over 2025-2027
      • These are refinements that lower the growth rate rather than lift it
  • The external forces net to a mild headwind on revenue and a mild tailwind on margin.
    • Claims inflation is easing, which helps the claims ratio, but the premium indexation that funds revenue growth fades with it
    • The two forces that are new are both headwinds: the Danish Supreme Court ruling of 28 April 2026 cost DKK 1.2bn pre-tax, and the Danish Competition and Consumer Authority's investigation into automatic indexation, open since June 2025, bears on the pricing mechanism of the home market
    • Nordic rates cut both ways: they lift the reported combined ratio through discounting and they are the sell-side's stated reason for the de-rating
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain; what is missing is magnitude, not propagation.
    • The 2023 leadership refresh and the December 2024 strategy show in the pricing actions, in retention and the underlying claims ratio, in the statements (a combined ratio of 80.3 percent in 2025) and in the sell-side's estimates, which already carry the 2027 targets
    • Because the change has already reached the estimates, there is nothing left in it to surprise the sell-side with
      • Consensus models a 2027 insurance service result of about DKK 8.5bn against the company's DKK 8.0-8.4bn range
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving at a rate the framework calls low, and the direction of the revenue line is down.
    • Insurance revenue growth in local currencies has gone from 4.0 percent (Q2 2025) to 3.3 percent (Q2 2026) and is guided to about 3 percent for 2026
    • The underlying claims ratio improves 30 to 50 basis points a year on a combined ratio that is already at its about 81 percent 2027 target
    • Earnings per share grow about 6 percent a year from a weather-normalised 2025 base of about DKK 8.3, and about 4 percent a year from the reported, benign-weather base of DKK 8.83
      • The sell-side's 3.7 to 5.5 percent is a base effect as much as a growth rate
    • After 2027 the margin leg drops out, because the 2027 combined ratio target is the level already reached in 2025, leaving revenue growth and buybacks at about 4.5 percent a year
  • What it costs is cheap against the stock's own history and in line with a cohort that has de-rated together. 14.6x 24-month-forward earnings of DKK 9.82 at DKK 143.5 (2026-10-07), the 0th percentile of five years against a median of 17.35x, and below the dashboard's ten-year 'crash' band of 14.50x
    • Gjensidige sits at the 3rd percentile of its own ten-year history, Sampo the 17th and Alm.
      • Brand the 20th
      • The de-rating has a stated cause, a growth gap and elevated rates, which Tryg's own actions do not change over the horizon
    • So the re-rating is industry-driven, roughly two-thirds of it on our reading, and the industry part is not ours to earn
  • The return on offer is a D against the bar.
    • The cash return of about 7.2 percent a year (dividend 6.0 percent, buybacks 1.2 percent) carries it
    • Earnings growth adds 7 to 13 percent cumulatively
    • The re-rating at even odds adds 3 to 4 percent
    • About 12.8 percent a year over eighteen months to 7 April 2028 and 11.1 percent a year over three years to 7 October 2029
      • The three-year figure is the binding one
    • Full reversion to the ten-year median of 17.40x gives 15.5 percent a year, a C
      • Nothing short of the 'mania' band of 20.80x clears 20 percent
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from the insurance service result to earnings per share, an ordinary macro move of 6 percent on the earnings line, takes the three-year rate to 9.1 percent, an F
      • Nothing inside the company offsets it
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', and the fit is half-crisp.
    • The quality and the oversold halves are crisp
    • The concern the market is pricing, the growth deficit, is neither transient nor clearing on the hard evidence, and the framework's instruction for this setup is to assume persistence until the evidence says otherwise
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the inputs to the statements and the estimates: the self-help delivers, the margin holds, the cash is paid.
    • It breaks at the step from outputs to return, because the outputs are too small to lift earnings growth toward the 12 to 20 percent band and the leg that could substitute for growth, the multiple, depends on the Nordic cohort re-rating for reasons outside the company

  • The case is therefore a 7 percent cash yield from an unbroken underwriting record, held until the Nordic non-life multiple mean-reverts; at even odds and 11 to 13 percent a year it is not material enough to deploy capital at a 20 percent bar.

    • The business is better than the case

  • Rating = D, because the three Elements of the chain are each D: the change forces are self-help of ordinary size with no chief-executive change, a guidance cut and fading price rises

    • The outputs are mid-single-digit growth with consensus already above guidance
    • And the 'Modal return' is 11 to 13 percent a year.
    • Not C, because no Element reaches C
    • Not F, because the cash return is real, large and sustainable and the direction of every operating metric is still up.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

    • The strongest case against this verdict is the bull's, not the bear's: that we are under-rating a quality compounder at its cheapest, and that the 0th-percentile multiple is itself the case.

    • The case against, at its strongest.

      • A total capital return yield of about 7 percent (6 percent dividend plus buybacks) from a 95 percent covered-bond balance sheet and a 196 percent solvency ratio gives strong downside protection
      • The Norway turnaround (a combined ratio of 77.3 percent, a ten-year low) proves the technical execution
      • The underlying claims ratio is improving at an accelerating pace (30, 40 and 50 basis points across FY2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026)
      • The stock trades 21 percent below its five-year average on DNB Carnegie's discounted cash flow
      • A premium to Nordic peers is justified by scale, record and a 6 percent yield
      • And the solvency glide path to about 170 percent unlocks DKK 1.6bn for buybacks, lifting the total yield toward 8 to 9 percent
      • Add the market's own history: a stock at the 0th percentile of a five-year range whose median is 17.35x has, by arithmetic, more upside than downside in the multiple, and every Nordic property and casualty peer reads A on the valuation pin, so the next move in Nordic rates could re-rate the group together
      • On that reading the 'Modal return' is a C or a B, the setup is a crisp 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', and the recommendation is to progress
    • What we concede.
      • The yield, its sustainability and the balance sheet, which we rate A.
      • The quality of the operator and the business, B.
      • The cheapness against the stock's own history.
      • The accelerating underlying claims ratio through H1 2026.
      • That the downside is bounded: at a 13x trough on DKK 9.82 the price is DKK 128, 11 percent down, and the dividend covers about half of that in a year.
      • That the sector-wide cause could reverse quickly if Nordic rates fall
    • Why we hold our view: the arithmetic first.
      • We ran the bull's own anchor and it does not reach the bar: full reversion to the ten-year median of 17.40x at three years gives 15.5 percent a year
      • The 'defendable' band of 19.30x gives about 19 percent
      • Only the 'mania' band clears 20 percent.
      • A case that needs a top-decile multiple on a bottom-decile growth rate is not a modal case
    • Second, the cause.
      • The de-rating has a stated reason, a 5 percent growth rate against peers above 10 percent and elevated Nordic rates, which the company's own 10 July 2026 guidance confirmed rather than contradicted
      • The bull disputes the multiple, not the growth
    • Third, the growth leg is thin on the bull's side.
      • Private lines at 5 percent are the engine, but Commercial is flat by design and the new partnerships are months old
      • The earnings review's own first test, Q3 2026 local-currency growth above 4 percent, sits above guidance
    • Fourth, the framework's warning on this setup applies with force.
      • Assume the concern is more persistent than it looks and require hard evidence of clearing
      • Here the market's concern is structural and the hard evidence (revenue guided down; the company declining a growth target) says persistence
    • Fifth, the earnings base is not a floor to extrapolate from.
      • The bear's reminder, which we do not adopt as a verdict, is that prior-year run-off gains have averaged fifty-three percent of pre-tax profit over five years against a Nordic base rate of twenty to thirty percent, and the risk-adjustment confidence level was cut from 75 to 65 percent in 2025
      • So the margin leg is more likely to be flat than to accelerate once the Norway effect fades
    • Where the case against would win.
      • A Nordic rate cut cycle beginning, the Danish regulator closing its indexation investigation benignly by mid-2027, or two consecutive quarters of local-currency growth above 4 percent
      • Any of these moves the odds on the re-rating leg, and the first two could move the whole cohort at once
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the external forces net to a mild headwind on revenue and a mild tailwind on margin, and the internal forces are a well-executed self-help programme of ordinary size (DKK 1.0bn on a DKK 7.5bn normalised insurance service result over three years), with no chief-executive change, no guidance increase and fading price rises
    • Nothing here is strong enough to drive a 20 percent return.
  • The decision rule: the framework's screen asks for at least three internal forces strong, or a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase
    • At most two internal forces are strong here (standards improvement; shareholder returns), the chief executive has been in post since June 2023, and the one guidance change of 2026 was a cut.
  • The strongest external force is already in the numbers: easing claims inflation has been lifting the underlying claims ratio since 2025, and the premium indexation that it funded is fading with it.
  • Not C, because the decision rule is not met on any reading and the best internal forces are a continuation, not a change.
