EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report examines the commercial terms, economics, and durability of Solid State plc's subsidiary Steatite's exclusive UK partnership with Persistent Systems for MPU5 radios and Wave Relay networks, the post-fielding revenue streams following major UK deployments under Project EVE and Project CAIN, and the risks of original equipment manufacturer bypassing based on international channel partner precedents and comparable tactical radio business models.

Steatite's communications deployments through the Systems division deliver high-value equipment sales and support that are critical for Solid State's strategic margin expansion targets toward its 2030 adjusted EPS ambition. The analysis reveals that Steatite operates as the exclusive UK partner for Persistent Systems' Wave Relay networks, with major modernization programs like Project CAIN awarded through Steatite, creating structural barriers that prevent direct commercial disintermediation by the original equipment manufacturer in the UK and insulate the division's long-term earnings per share trajectory.

High-volume program-level sales often yield channel margins of around 10%, while customized value-added deals involving technical integration can achieve margins of 20% to 25% or more. However, when deals scale up to massive MoD orders like Project CAIN, manufacturers and distributors often agree to split or reduce margins to secure the contract, meaning lower percentage margins than smaller orders and pressure on operating profitability.

Post-fielding revenue economics demonstrate significant long-term value creation potential. The Royal Marines completed fielding of over 2,000 MPU5 tactical radio systems by October 2025 under Project EVE, running on Wave Relay mobile ad hoc networks. Steatite secured an initial contract valued at $10.8 million under Project CAIN to supply Wave Relay communications technology to the British Army, with equipment delivered in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026/2027.

The initial sale of MPU5 hardware serves as the anchor for subsequent high-margin software licenses and through-life support, with broader adoption of the Persistent Systems platform driving increased opportunities for higher-quality recurring revenue streams, where proprietary software licenses and spiral upgrades carry virtually zero incremental cost.

International precedents provide valuable benchmarks for understanding channel economics and durability. The U.S. Air Force's February 2026 contract to upgrade its WaRTAK network for $7 million, following an earlier $14.6 million upgrade in 2020, demonstrates how initial military networks yield significant follow-on value through multiyear modernization cycles. However, the long-term EPS sustainability remains contingent on factors largely outside management's control, including Persistent Systems' strategic decisions regarding direct ministry engagement, the pace of MoD procurement cycles, and the successful conversion of hardware deployments into predictable high-margin software and support contracts.

Without greater transparency on partnership renewal terms and software revenue splits, investors face elevated uncertainty regarding the true durability and margin profile of what has become Solid State's most important growth driver, representing approximately 47% of total group revenue through the defense and security sector.
