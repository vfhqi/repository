Following shareholder approval reaching 75.8% to elect Förtsch to the Supervisory Board in June 2024, public criticism ceased entirely after the appointment of Chief Executive Officer Oliver Behrens in summer 2024, establishing a stable governance environment. Shareholders overwhelmingly approved all joint board proposals at the June 2026 meeting with 74.07% of capital represented, with individual discharge resolutions for both Management Board members and Supervisory Board member Bernd Förtsch passing with near-unanimous voting support and management compensation policy securing 93.46% voting approval.

Management overhauled capital allocation in February 2026 by establishing a formal dividend payout policy targeting 20% of net income, with the initial distribution reaching €0.30 per share for the 2025 financial year, representing a sevenfold increase from the prior €0.04 per share distribution. The Federal Financial Supervisory Authority terminated the special commissioner mandate on September 30, 2024 following full remediation of special audit findings, with regulators reducing bank-specific Supervisory Review and Evaluation Process capital requirements by 25 basis points to 2.50% in January 2026.

Operational leverage enabled management to raise 2026 net income guidance to €200-230 million, delivering its 2027 strategic financial targets a full year early. Bernd Förtsch's multi-layered holding architecture maintains a controlled 18.35% anchor stake that provides an effective blocking minority at typical general meeting attendance without triggering mandatory BaFin banking regulatory filings or takeover thresholds.

With Annual General Meeting capital representation averaging 74.07%, Förtsch's 18.35% nominal stake translates into 24.8% active voting power, yielding effective veto capability, though structural governance vulnerability remains unresolved because any future share cancellation under buyback programs naturally inflates Förtsch's voting weight closer to regulatory ownership control thresholds, potentially triggering renewed BaFin oversight friction.

Applying the means-motive-opportunity framework reveals that while anchor voting power creates systemic veto capability, Chief Executive Officer Oliver Behrens commands strong operational credibility following upgraded 2026 net profit guidance of €200-230 million, raising the barrier against shareholder intervention. Second and third-order implications under non-consensus friction scenarios would trigger strategic paralysis pitting aggressive M&A consolidation demands against conservative organic platform investment, drive sudden senior executive departures undermining CEO Oliver Behrens' early mandate extension through March 2029, and risk triggering fresh BaFin supervisory intervention threatening to reverse recent SREP capital surcharge reductions.

The analysis remains limited by unobservable private board discussions and informal shareholder alignment channels that public disclosures may only surface after formal operational or governance ruptures occur, requiring continuous monitoring of directors dealings disclosures, AGM agenda filings, and expert channel checks with former executives to detect early signaling of re-emerging friction.
