EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

MATERIALITY OF CHANGE

AT&S has executed a dramatic leadership restructuring, appointing Dr. Michael Mertin as CEO on May 1, 2025, and Gerrit Steen as CFO on February 1, 2026, while streamlining the Executive Board to three members from a previous five-member structure. The company achieved a strong revenue inflection, with consolidated revenue rising to €1.8 billion (21% currency-adjusted growth) and Q1 FY2026/27 revenue reaching €548.7 million (37.5% year-on-year growth), matching previous record sales levels.

Balance sheet leverage improved materially, with net debt to EBITDA declining from 3.2x to 1.9x in the first quarter following a 28.4% reduction in net debt to €954.0 million and the successful placement of a €400 million hybrid convertible bond classified within shareholders' equity. Profitability margins have recovered from historical lows below 20% to exceed 30%, with Q1 FY2026/27 EBITDA margin reaching 30.1% (up 12.4 percentage points year-on-year) and the company achieving a positive net profit of €14 million in Q4 FY2025/26, reversing a multi-quarter loss trend.

TOP RANKED KEY DRIVERS WITH PLAUSIBILITY

The Kulim expansion represents the highest-impact driver, with €1.5 to 2.0 billion of planned investment fully backed by customer commitments from AMD (ramping production lines in Plant 1), Intel (ramping the K2 shell facility), and Marvell Technology (joining as second anchor customer). Chongqing capacity expansion is fully customer-financed in the double-digit millions and is projected to yield high double-digit million EBIT gains in FY2026/27, representing a high-plausibility driver with immediate earnings visibility.

Advanced AI infrastructure demand is accelerating rapidly, with AT&S and Marvell expanding cooperation to support next-generation AI infrastructure, while agentic AI workloads are reviving traditional server CPU demand and initiating a critical volume inflection point. AT&S's Aurora cost-saving programmes delivered €170 million in sustainable savings in FY2025/26 (exceeding initial €130 million targets), with an additional €110 million targeted for the current financial year, representing a high-plausibility, high-confidence driver with proven execution history.

Leoben R&D and embedded power delivery capabilities represent a medium-plausibility, high-value driver, with the new facility commissioned in summer 2025 focusing on embedded component packaging technology for power delivery and developing breakthrough glass core substrate prototypes.

RECENT DELIVERY TRACK RECORD

Historical quarterly revenues showed clear trough-to-recovery dynamics, with Q1 FY2025/26 sales of €398.9 million representing a low-capacity utilization period before new facilities began contributing, followed by Q3 FY2025/26 revenue reaching €467.7 million (18% year-on-year growth) as Kulim and Leoben ramps contributed. The company experienced a severe guidance downgrade cycle, delaying its mid-term €3.5 billion target by one full fiscal year to FY26/27 and cutting FY24 revenue to approximately €1.6 billion, while scaling back net CAPEX from highs of €996 million to restore balance sheet flexibility during the downturn.

Strategic turnaround execution accelerated through the sale of the Ansan medical plant for €405 million in cash, newly negotiated AMD and Intel agreements fully financing €1.5 to 2.0 billion of required Malaysian plant capex, and operating profitability expanding sharply to reach a 30.1% EBITDA margin in Q1 FY27. Recent delivery reflects mixed signals, with disciplined cost reductions defending EBITDA margins despite cyclical pricing headwinds, but upgraded FY27 guidance bolstered by €300 to 350 million of non-operational customer payments, and the company remaining vulnerable because two main customers generate over 70% of historical high-end substrate revenues.

FORWARD GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

Management's revised FY26/27 guidance anticipates constant-currency revenue growth of 45% to 55% (upwardly revised from previous projections) with EBITDA margin raised to 32% to 37%, supported by stronger pricing tailwinds and favorable mix. Projected net CAPEX is set at €1.0 to 1.2 billion, resuming previously postponed Malaysian expansion projects, while management expects net debt to EBITDA to remain clearly below three times, strengthening overall balance sheet stability targets.

Guidance sensitivity is elevated, with substrate production carrying substantial operational leverage (historical drop-through rates of 40% to 50% on incremental revenue), revenues heavily denominated in USD while a large part of the cost base is denominated in Chinese Yuan, shortages of specialty T-Glass from Nittobo threatening to constrain volume growth, and high customer concentration meaning losing allocation would severely impact operating margins and underutilize expensive plant assets.

SELL-SIDE POSITIONING

The sell-side pool remains highly concentrated with limited coverage, as only two primary global brokers (Deutsche Bank led by George Brown and Robert Sanders, and Jefferies led by Alexander Thiel and Janardan Menon) maintain active detailed coverage, though consensus rating mix has upgraded from a consensual Sell rating as of early 2026. Deutsche Bank values AT&S using an EV/EBITDA multiple benchmarked against Asian substrate peers, applying a 25% historical peer discount to reflect lower immediate AI exposure, and raised the price target to €320 in August 2026 following Q1 results.

The dominant bull case argues that surging server CPU demand will absorb capacity at high prices and fully customer-financed investments de-risk the complex Kulim campus ramp, while the contrarian bear case argues that high balance sheet leverage and customer concentration threaten cash flow durability.

FUNDAMENTAL PEER POSITION

AT&S ranks third globally in total packaging substrate share, with Unimicron leading at 23% share followed by Ibiden at 19%, while AT&S commands a solid 12% total market share in 2025 ahead of SEMCO, with Shinko and Nan Ya PCB trailing at 8% share each and Kinsus representing only 7% of total revenues. Peer revenue growth is accelerating across the sector, with AT&S reporting 37.5% growth in Q1 approaching the upper end of long-term guidance ranges, Ibiden's first-quarter revenue growing 26.4% year-on-year (beating consensus estimates by 6%), and Nan Ya PCB recording 42% year-on-year expansion driven by mass production of switch substrates.

Substrate players are exercising substantial pricing power, with several large peers implementing price hikes of 3% to 7% quarter-on-quarter in early 2026, successfully passing on 30% price increases for copper-clad laminates and copper foil from Resonac, and Unimicron's gross margin recovering to 24.8% supported by improving price bargaining power in tight conditions.

ACTIVIST PRESENCE

The shareholder register remains highly stable with no active activist presence, as the Androsch Private Foundation holds a stable 18.2% stake in ordinary shares and the Dörflinger Private Foundation maintains a longterm 17.6% ownership position, with institutional and public free float comprising the remaining 64.3% of capital.

Appendix

Outliers worth noting

The AT&S financial profile exhibits several extreme statistical deviations that distinguish it from conventional substrate manufacturing peers, particularly in capital structure engineering, customer financing arrangements, and operational leverage characteristics that fall outside typical industry parameters. The company executed a EUR 400 million deeply subordinated perpetual hybrid convertible bond with a 30% conversion premium to EUR 254.2514, classified directly within shareholders' equity rather than debt.

This financing structure represents an extreme outlier in the substrate manufacturing sector, where peers typically rely on conventional senior unsecured debt or equity raises to fund capacity expansions. The hybrid instrument's equity classification mechanically reduces reported leverage ratios while maintaining significant embedded refinancing risk, as the EUR 350 million January 2022 hybrid faces an interest step-up of 9.942 percentage points to approximately 15% beginning in January 2027.

No comparable substrate peer–including Ibiden, Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, or Shinko–has employed such complex subordinated hybrid structures to manage balance sheet optics during capacity expansion cycles. [Cross-ref: Section 4] The Kulim expansion involves a planned investment of EUR 1.5 to 2.0 billion that is fully backed by customer commitments, representing an unprecedented customer co-investment model in the advanced packaging substrate industry.

Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that the upgraded FY26/27 guidance relates directly to non-operational customer financing, adding positive cash flow. This degree of customer-funded capacity expansion–where anchor customers essentially pre-finance multi-billion euro greenfield facilities–stands in stark contrast to the capital-intensive self-funded expansion models employed by Asian substrate leaders, who typically absorb the full balance sheet risk of new capacity additions.

The arrangement creates an extreme asymmetry in capital risk allocation that has no clear precedent in the sector's history. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 9]

Consolidated revenue rose to EUR 1.8 billion representing 21% currency-adjusted growth, while first-quarter revenue reached EUR 548.7 million, growing 37.5% year-on-year. This sequential acceleration from 21% full-year growth to 37.5% quarterly growth represents an extreme velocity inflection that far exceeds typical substrate market recovery patterns. Peer Ibiden's first-quarter revenue grew 26.4% year-on-year, while Nan Ya PCB recorded 42% year-on-year revenue expansion, placing AT&S's growth trajectory within the top decile of substrate manufacturers globally during the current semiconductor upcycle.

However, upgraded FY27 guidance is bolstered by EUR 300 to 350 million of non-operational customer payments, suggesting that reported growth rates embed substantial non-recurring revenue components that distort underlying operational performance comparisons. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 6, Section 7]

First-quarter FY2026/27 EBITDA margin recovered strongly to reach 30.1%, up 12.4 percentage points year-onyear, while Microelectronics EBITDA margin reached 42.3%, driven by volume leverage. The 42.3% segment-level EBITDA margin in Microelectronics represents an extreme positive outlier relative to peer base rates, as Unimicron's gross margin recovered to 24.8%, supported by improving price bargaining power in tight conditions.

AT&S's Microelectronics segment is achieving EBITDA margins approximately 75% higher than Unimicron's gross margin levels, suggesting either exceptional product mix advantages, temporary pricing windfalls from customer prepayments, or accounting treatment differences that amplify reported profitability during the capacity ramp phase. The sustainability of such extreme margin levels remains questionable given historical substrate industry economics. [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 7]
