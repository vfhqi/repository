Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The structural shift in Scandinavian Tobacco Group's manufacturing footprint represents a critical determinant of its future earnings trajectory.
  2. Consolidating production at Holstebro while managing the temporary Japan Tobacco contract directly influences operating leverage and capital allocation efficiency.

Financial and Strategic Realignment

1.
Critical inquiry: Key Question: "For Scandinavian Tobacco Group (STG-DK), what are the terms and economics of the contract-manufacturing agreement with Japan Tobacco for the BREAK and Moro fine-cut brands sold on 1 October 2026 (up to three years with six months' notice, per the company): expected volumes and revenue, margin, which plant (Holstebro) carries the volume, how it interacts with the move of Mac Baren fine-cut production to Holstebro by summer 2027, and what fixed costs would be stranded at the end of the agreement; and how have other tobacco makers (for example STG itself after its Russian contract with British American Tobacco ended in 2015, or Imperial Brands with private-label volumes before closing Langenhagen) handled factory capacity after contract volumes ended?" Resolving the contract manufacturing agreement's margins is essential to understand how dilutive pressures will weigh on EPS over the next three years.

Operational synergy: Scandinavian Tobacco Group planned to consolidate its fine cut tobacco manufacturing into an expanded single facility in Holstebro, Denmark. This site must integrate both the temporary Japan Tobacco contract volumes and the newly acquired Mac Baren production by summer 2027. Successful consolidation is designed to deliver DKK 150 mm in annualized operational cost synergies by the end of 2027.
4.
Strategic realignment: The divestment of the BREAK and Moro brands to Japan Tobacco represents a clear step forward in Scandinavian Tobacco Group's Focus2030 strategy. This strategy concentrates on core brands with strong growth and long-term value creation opportunities. The resulting contract manufacturing agreement offers a temporary cushion while the company optimizes its manufacturing network.

Section 2 – Terms and Economics of the Japan Tobacco Agreement

Section summary
  1. The divestment of BREAK and Moro fine-cut brands to Japan Tobacco shifts high-margin brand sales into lower-margin contract manufacturing.
  2. Transitional contract manufacturing provides up to three years of volume cushion but carries a highly dilutive margin profile.
  3. Proceeds of DKK 1.0 billion post-tax support capital return but reduce Scandinavian Tobacco Group's core Europe Branded revenue by four percent.

Divestment Value and Agreement Terms

1.
Substantial cash inflow: Scandinavian Tobacco Group completed the sale of its fine-cut tobacco brands BREAK and Moro to Japan Tobacco on 1 October 2026. This asset purchase agreement was valued at EUR 176 million, translating to post-tax proceeds of DKK 1.0 billion. The cash influx is designed to lower leverage to 2 times, which will support adjusted earnings per share by enabling future share buybacks.
2.
Temporary manufacturing buffer: The contract-manufacturing agreement with Japan Tobacco spans up to three years with a six-month termination notice. This transitional period allows Scandinavian Tobacco Group to continue producing the fine-cut brands at its Holstebro factory. The temporary volumes prevent immediate stranded costs, although earnings per share will eventually face headwinds when manufacturing stops.
3.
Significant volume contribution: The divested BREAK and Moro brands historically represented a substantial portion of Scandinavian Tobacco Group's performance. These two brands accounted for approximately 4% of group net sales and 6% of adjusted EBITDA during the 2025 fiscal year. Removing these high-margin branded sales immediately dilutes net margins and puts pressure on near-term adjusted earnings per share.
4.
Elevated cash flow guidance: Scandinavian Tobacco Group raised its full-year 2026 free cash flow expectations following the divestment's completion. The company upgraded its guidance to a range of DKK 1.2 billion to DKK 1.4 billion. This upward revision includes proceeds from transferring BREAK and Moro inventories to Japan Tobacco, which will provide immediate capital allocation flexibility.

Divisional Economics and Margin Gap

5.
Low-margin contract structure: The transition from branded ownership to contract manufacturing structurally alters the profitability of the BREAK and Moro volumes. Scandinavian Tobacco Group registers contract-manufacturing revenue under its "Other" category, which runs at a lower gross margin. This lower-margin structure is dilutive to the group's overall operating profitability and will depress adjusted earnings per share.
6.
High Europe Branded benchmarks: Europe Branded operates as a highly profitable commercial division for Scandinavian Tobacco Group. The division reported net sales of DKK 3,270 million and an EBITDA margin of 19.8% during the 2025 fiscal year. Replacing proprietary brand margins with simple toll manufacturing fees will significantly drag down Europe Branded's divisional profitability.
7.
Divergent division sizes: Europe Branded represents the largest commercial division by sales volume for Scandinavian Tobacco Group. The division generated 36% of group net sales in 2025, which was slightly larger than North America Branded at 33%. Shrinking Europe Branded's proprietary sales through this divestment increases the group's reliance on US premium handmade cigars to drive growth.
8.
Extreme margin divergence: The contract-manufacturing activities exhibit a stark profitability gap compared to core tobacco operations. Core machine-rolled cigars and smoking tobacco generated a gross margin before special items of 53.8% in the first nine months of 2026. In contrast, the "Other" category containing contractmanufacturing activities recorded a much lower gross margin of 16.9% during the same period.

9.
Diluted divisional contribution: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has historically relied on high-margin fine-cut tobacco to support its cash flow generation. Moving BREAK and Moro to a contract-manufacturing cost-plus model removes the premium pricing power that supported historical divisional performance. This shift will permanently lower the organic growth potential of Europe Branded, dragging down adjusted earnings per share.
10.
Moderate global peer comparison: Peer tobacco manufacturers typically target much higher gross margins on proprietary products than contract agreements. Philip Morris International and other global players rely on premium brand equity to sustain operating margins over 40%. Accepting low-margin contract volumes represents a defensive consolidation strategy that protects volume at the expense of consolidated profitability.
11.
Heavy regional concentration: The BREAK and Moro fine-cut brands are primarily sold in Germany and other European markets.
12.
Morgan Stanley reported that Germany will face front-loaded retail price increases of 12% to 14% due to higherthan-expected excise increases starting in 2027. Passing these brands to Japan Tobacco insulates Scandinavian Tobacco Group from Germany's structural combustible volume declines. This strategic exit protects adjusted earnings per share from volatile European regulatory headwinds.
13.
Temporary shipment inflation: Sales of the BREAK brand rose during the third quarter of 2026 immediately before the divestment completed. Deutsche Bank reported that Europe Branded's revenue growth was supported by timing effects and increased shipments. These temporary volume spikes will reverse in the fourth quarter of 2026, which means quarterly earnings per share will contract as branded BREAK sales disappear.

Broker Outlook and Shareholder Returns

14.
Improved outer-year estimates: Deutsche Bank upgraded its financial outlook for Scandinavian Tobacco Group despite the dilutive manufacturing agreement. The broker increased its outer-year EBITDA forecasts by 2% to 3% to reflect improving core brand profitability. These upgrades, coupled with lower group interest costs, are expected to drive 6% to 7% increases in adjusted earnings per share.

Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank noted that the lower-margin BREAK and Moro contract manufacturing agreement will partially offset core business improvements. The broker cautioned that shipment timing benefits and duty refunds inflated the third-quarter 2026 upgrade, meaning underlying profitability could weaken into 2027. This suggests that the dilutive impact of the Japan Tobacco deal may drag down earnings per share more than current consensus expects.

Minor financial impact: Japan Tobacco expects the acquisition of the BREAK and Moro fine-cut brands to complement its Western European portfolio. The buyer confirmed that the transaction will have a minor financial impact on its current consolidated balance sheet. This asymmetric impact highlights that while the deal is minor for Japan Tobacco, it creates a material structural headwind for Scandinavian Tobacco Group.
17.

Deutsche Bank noted that the company's valuation remains highly undemanding, trading at 7.5 times price-to-earnings for 2027. Retiring shares with DKK 1.0 billion in post-tax proceeds would easily offset the 6% EBITDA dilution and drive positive adjusted earnings per share growth.

Section 3 – Production Integration at Holstebro and the Mac Baren Transition

Section summary
  1. Consolidating production at the Holstebro factory allows Scandinavian Tobacco Group to buffer capacity before integrating Mac Baren volumes.
  2. The transitional contract manufacturing of BREAK and Moro provides temporary factory utilization but at structurally lower margins.
  3. Underutilized capacity and potential stranded costs at Holstebro will emerge if Japan Tobacco terminates the agreement early.

Factory Consolidation and

1.
Synergies Gradual physical consolidation: Scandinavian Tobacco Group plans to consolidate its entire European fine-cut tobacco production at the expanded Holstebro factory. This integration incorporates the newly acquired Mac Baren fine-cut production of approximately 600 tonnes by the summer of 2027. Moving these volumes onto a single site is designed to establish a more efficient operating asset base and drive long-term profitability.
2.
Substantial cost synergies: The centralization of manufacturing at the Holstebro site acts as the key driver for Scandinavian Tobacco Group's cost efficiency goals. Management expects to realize DKK 150 million in annualized cost synergies from the Mac Baren acquisition by the end of 2027. This integration timeline means the full positive impact on adjusted earnings per share will begin materializing in the 2027 fiscal year.
3.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that the Mac Baren business comprises a very large number of small-volume product stock keeping units. Managing this highly fragmented product mix during the relocation to Holstebro could create complex operational bottlenecks. This complexity might delay the execution of the transfer and prevent achieving targeted cost synergies on time.
4.
Strategic volume buffer: The transitional contract manufacturing agreement with Japan Tobacco provides Scandinavian Tobacco Group with vital operational flexibility. Producing BREAK and Moro for up to three years allows the company to maintain high factory utilization rates at Holstebro during the physical relocation of Mac Baren. This buffer prevents sudden capacity underutilization while the domestic manufacturing network is optimized.

Capacity Economics and Stranded Costs

5.
Stark margin dilution: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces near-term profitability headwinds at Holstebro as proprietary volumes convert to contract manufacturing. While JTI volumes sustain factory utilization, the lower-margin contract profile will drag down consolidated gross profit and EBITDA from 2027 onwards. This transition from high-margin branded sales to low-margin toll manufacturing will compress operating leverage and earnings per share.
6.
Significant stranded costs: The divestment of BREAK and Moro leaves Scandinavian Tobacco Group with substantial unabsorbed overheads once contract volumes cease. The transaction terms dictate that related operating expenses, representing about 6% of adjusted EBITDA, will not transfer to Japan Tobacco. Consequently, these stranded fixed costs will burden the Holstebro facility unless management aggressively rationalizes plant footprint.
7.
Defensive capacity planning: The three-year window under the Japan Tobacco contract gives Scandinavian Tobacco Group essential breathing room. Management is using this period to evaluate how best to optimize long-term factory efficiency and capacity across its Danish factories. This planning is crucial because any sudden termination of the contract with a six-month notice would immediately expose Holstebro to severe operating deleverage.
8.
Other side of the story: The physical integration of Mac Baren's 600 tonnes could completely absorb the capacity freed up when the Japan Tobacco contract terminates. If Scandinavian Tobacco Group successfully expands Holstebro's fill-rate, the consolidated site will achieve structural economies of scale that offset any stranded fixed costs. Therefore, the eventual loss of the low-margin contract manufacturing volume could actually optimize long-term margins and boost adjusted earnings.

Section 4 – Tobacco Industry Precedents on Ending Contract Volumes

Section summary
  1. Historical precedents show that ending contract manufacturing or private-label volumes can trigger severe near-term revenue declines.
  2. Tobacco manufacturers manage the transition of excess capacity through structured reciprocal agreements or aggressive factory closures.
  3. Realizing substantial cost savings and disposing of physical assets are the primary methods utilized to protect adjusted earnings per share.

Scandinavian Tobacco Group and BAT Russia

1.
Severe organic decline: Scandinavian Tobacco Group suffered a significant top-line impact when its contract-manufacturing agreement ended in the Russian market. Deutsche Bank Research reported that the group's other net sales experienced a 9% organic decline during the 2015 fiscal year after British American Tobacco terminated its Captain Black contract. While the loss of these volumes depressed segment revenue, it allowed the company to pivot its operational focus. This shift ultimately protected consolidated earnings per share by removing low-margin business.
2.
Moderate margin dilution: Contract manufacturing has historically served as a defensive tool for Scandinavian Tobacco Group to utilize excess factory capacity. Deutsche Bank Research noted that the company's "Other" segment, which includes contract manufacturing, generated 14% of net sales in the 2015 fiscal year. The broker modeled segment net sales to decline by 3% annually with gross margins falling by 10 basis points per year. This steady margin compression illustrates how contract volumes carry weak profitability that ultimately drags down group adjusted earnings.
3.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research argued that reduced contract-manufacturing revenues can actually represent a net positive for Scandinavian Tobacco Group. The broker noted that when the Captain Black contract with British American Tobacco ended in Russia, the group took direct ownership of the brand. This transition shifted the volume from low-margin contract manufacturing into the higher-margin machine made cigar division in Russia. Consequently, the termination of the contract did not depress adjusted earnings per share but instead enhanced overall product mix.
4.
Strategic brand ownership: Scandinavian Tobacco Group mitigated the termination of its Russian contract by acquiring the brand outright. Deutsche Bank Research confirmed that taking ownership of Captain Black in Russia allowed the group to capture full brand equity. This tactical move replaced low-margin tolling fees with retail sales. Integrating the brand into its proprietary portfolio helped protect the group's operating profitability from severe capacity deleverage.

Imperial Brands and German Private Label

5.
Planned capacity exit: Imperial Brands chose to shut down its Reemtsma factory in Langenhagen after experiencing structural volume declines across Europe. The company announced its intention to cease production at the site on 1 October 2025 following a detailed review of its global manufacturing network. After failing to find a third-party buyer, the manufacturer confirmed in March 2026 that production would finally end in July 2027. This plant shutdown highlights how major tobacco players must consolidate operations when contract volumes shrink.
6.
Substantial restructuring charges: Imperial Brands recognized enormous financial penalties to execute the consolidation of its manufacturing network. The company reported that the total cost of the Langenhagen closure is forecast to reach £220 million, which is in addition to a prior-year asset impairment charge of £101 million. These costs include £77 million of people-related provisions, a £69 million retirement benefit liability, and £18 million of site decommissioning costs. Such massive upfront cash and non-cash charges severely depress reported earnings per share during the transition years.
7.
Inefficient volume economics: Imperial Brands utilized German private-label contract manufacturing for years despite its poor financial contribution. Jefferies Research estimated that these private-label arrangements, which accounted for approximately 30% of the company's German volume, returned unattractive margins of cost plus 10%. The broker noted that while these volumes supported high factory utilization rates, they severely diluted the group's average net pricing. This dilution demonstrates why relying on low-margin contract manufacturing is an unsustainable long-term strategy for protecting earnings.
8.
Strategic margin optimization: Imperial Brands chose to deliberately phase out its German private-label manufacturing to improve its regional profitability. Morgan Stanley reported that the company's withdrawal from low-margin private label volumes would pressure reported shipment numbers but have limited operating profit impact. The company confirmed that excluding private-label volumes, its European tobacco net revenue grew by 0.4% at constant currency during the 2026 fiscal year. This outcome proves that shedding low-margin contract volumes can improve structural operating margins and EPS.

Greensboro and Reciprocal Manufacturing

9.
Complex asset acquisition: Imperial Brands established a major manufacturing presence in the United States through its acquisition of assets from Reynolds and Lorillard. Under the terms of the 2014 agreement, the company's subsidiary, ITG Brands, purchased Lorillard's manufacturing facilities in Greensboro, North Carolina. This acquisition formed part of a cash transaction valued at USD 7.06 billion. The addition of this plant gave ITG Brands the necessary infrastructure to compete in the US market, but also introduced complex reciprocal manufacturing requirements.
10.
Strategic transitional tolling: Imperial Brands managed the Greensboro plant transition through a reciprocal manufacturing agreement with Reynolds American. Under this agreement, ITG Brands manufactured the Newport brand for Reynolds at Greensboro while Reynolds manufactured Winston, Kool, and Salem for ITG Brands at Tobaccoville. This temporary arrangement ensured continued supply to retail customers while both companies prepared to relocate production machinery. The transition prevented sudden capacity shocks at Greensboro, showing how structured tolling contracts protect near-term operational stability.
11.
Successful volume migration: Reynolds American successfully completed the migration of its acquired Newport production from Greensboro to Tobaccoville in 2016. The company noted that its third-quarter 2016 net pricing realization of 3.9% included a negative impact from the completed reciprocal manufacturing agreement. Once the agreement terminated, equipment was transferred to Tobaccoville and the contract volume ceased. This exit allowed Reynolds to capture higher margins on Newport while ITG Brands was forced to adapt Greensboro's capacity to its proprietary brand volumes.

British American Tobacco Malaysia

12.
Radical structural transition: British American Tobacco Malaysia restructured its business model by shutting down its local manufacturing facility after contract volumes declined. The company announced in March 2016 that it would close its Virginia Park factory and source tobacco products from regional factories. Production at the site was completely transferred by June 2017 to state-of-the-art facilities in Indonesia, Singapore, and South Korea. Transitioning to a full importation model eliminated domestic factory overheads.
13.
Lucrative asset disposal: British American Tobacco Malaysia cushioned its heavy restructuring costs through the sale of its real estate assets. The company completed the sale of its factory land and buildings in November 2016, generating one-off disposal proceeds of RM 218 million. These proceeds offset restructuring charges of RM 86 million in 2016 and RM 9 million in 2017. This capital gain allowed the company to pay a special dividend and protected its balance sheet during the factory wind-down.
14.
Severe export contraction: British American Tobacco Malaysia experienced a dramatic drop in export revenues when its contract-manufacturing operations ceased. The company reported that contract manufacturing revenue declined by 82% or RM 214 million during the 2017 fiscal year due to the cessation of contract manufacturing for export markets. This lost volume caused a severe contraction in total shipments and profit. The loss of export scale highlights how ending contract manufacturing leaves domestic operations highly exposed to local volume declines.
15.
Other side of the story: Tobacco manufacturers can successfully defend their earnings after contract manufacturing volumes end by aggressively shrinking their fixedcost footprints. Imperial Brands' closure of its Langenhagen plant and the sale of its Taiwan factory are expected to deliver £100 million in annualised savings when fully implemented. Likewise, British American Tobacco Malaysia's transition to an import-only model ultimately lowered operating expenses by 4.7% in 2017. These historical precedents prove that while losing contract volumes hurts near-term revenue, aggressive footprint rationalisation protects long-term adjusted earnings per share.

Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

  1. Early contract termination by Japan Tobacco represents the most severe threat to Scandinavian Tobacco Group's projected earnings trajectory.
  2. Unabsorbed plant overheads and delayed integration synergies would disrupt the consensus adjusted earnings recovery over the next three years.

This early contract termination would bypass the expected three-year buffer and immediately hit Scandinavian Tobacco Group's factory utilization. Consequently, instead of a gradual transition, the group's adjusted earnings per share would face sudden and unmitigated downside pressure as stranded fixed costs spike.

Heightened shareholder influence: Chr. Augustinus Fabrikker AS holds a dominant 28.4% stake in Scandinavian Tobacco Group, giving it substantial power to influence corporate decisions. Alongside C.W. These controlling entities would likely push management to accelerate factory closures to protect cash flows and dividend payouts. If these shareholders force rapid cost actions, they could help defend adjusted earnings per share against severe capacity underutilization.
3.
Volatile tax headwinds: Deutsche Bank Research reported that German combustible tax hikes are expected to prompt steep retail price increases of 12% to 14%. If German consumer demand for BREAK and Moro collapses under these excise burdens, Japan Tobacco is highly likely to cancel the agreement. This sharp demand contraction would leave Scandinavian Tobacco Group with unabsorbed manufacturing overheads. The sudden drop in contract revenues would severely dilute Europe Branded profitability and drag down earnings.

Supply Chain and Peer Impacts

8.
Volatile leaf supply: Universal Corporation's leaf supply dynamics indicate that the global tobacco market is highly cyclical and sensitive to farmers switching crops. If Scandinavian Tobacco Group experiences sudden contract volume terminations, its demand for raw leaf tobacco will drop abruptly. This sudden demand shock would force global leaf merchant partners to absorb excess inventories or cut farmer pre-financing. Ultimately, severe disruptions in the raw leaf supply chain would inflate procurement costs for core products and damage adjusted earnings.
9.
Shifting peer landscape: Japan Tobacco is reportedly exploring a potential takeover of British competitor Imperial Brands in partnership with Altria Group. This major consolidation attempt indicates that global peers are aggressively seeking scale to fund reduced-risk products. If Japan Tobacco focuses its capital and management attention on a massive Imperial Brands acquisition, it may deprioritize smaller transitional agreements. This strategic pivot would increase the likelihood of Japan Tobacco terminating the contract manufacturing deal early to streamline its supply chain.
10.
Squeezed commercial margins: Tobacco manufacturers are continuously seeking to eliminate the long tail of low-volume products to protect their supply chain margins. A sudden early termination of the BREAK and Moro brands would severely impact regional packaging and logistics partners. These packaging suppliers would face immediate capacity underutilization and lost tolling revenues. Consequently, the loss of manufacturing scale would weigh on consolidated commercial margins across the European tobacco supply chain.
11.
Other side of the story: Scandinavian Tobacco Group could achieve significantly higher long-term profitability if the low-margin Japan Tobacco contract ends ahead of schedule. Converting Holstebro's capacity from low-margin contract manufacturing to higher-margin proprietary Mac Baren brands would dramatically improve product mix. If the group successfully redeems the DKK 1.0 billion in post-tax proceeds to aggressively retire outstanding shares, the reduction in share count will easily offset any short-term EBITDA dilution. Consequently, an early exit from the agreement would ultimately boost adjusted earnings per share rather than impairing long-term valuation.
