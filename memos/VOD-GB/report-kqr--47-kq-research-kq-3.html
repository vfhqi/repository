Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Swisscom-INWIT Master Service Agreement termination dispute remains partially resolvable through judicial intervention, with the Court of Milan procedural rulings confirming Swisscom's termination notice stays active while the fundamental question of contract validity transitions into a protracted ordinary trial expected to conclude by 2029.

Swisscom has not withdrawn its formal termination notice served on March 25, 2026, which specifies contract expiration for March 2028, establishing the foundational legal status of the dispute.

Swisscom explicitly declined INWIT's November 2025 proposal for out-of-court arbitration, electing instead to file a writ of summons before the Court of Milan, signaling the operator's preference for judicial resolution over negotiated settlement.

In July 2026, the Court of Milan rejected INWIT's Article 700 emergency injunction application, ruling that the dispute lacks urgency and must be decided in ordinary court, representing a critical procedural defeat for the tower operator's attempt to freeze the termination notice during litigation. In April 2026, INWIT filed an application for urgent interim measures under Article 700 of the Italian Code of Civil Procedure to freeze Fastweb's termination notice, but on July 13 and 14, 2026, the Court of Milan rejected INWIT's Article 700 applications against Fastweb and Telecom Italia, ruling that the filings lacked procedural urgency.

INWIT launched two separate legal appeals on July 27 and 28, 2026, challenging the Court of Milan's rejection of emergency interim relief, demonstrating the tower operator's commitment to exhausting all available procedural remedies.

The legal validity of the March 2028 expiry versus INWIT's 2038 claim will be adjudicated in an ordinary trial expected to conclude by 2029, establishing a multi-year timeline before definitive contractual clarity emerges.

December 2020 constituted a Change of Control event that prevented any automatic 16-year contract extension, while INWIT maintains that joint control between Telecom Italia and Vodafone terminated in August 2022, which legally triggered a binding 16-year renewal extending the contract to August 2038.

This interpretive dispute creates fundamental uncertainty regarding whether the Master Service Agreement expires in March 2028 or remains binding until August 2038, with profound implications for revenue recognition, capital allocation, and strategic positioning across both entities. - Guidance Calls Telecom Italia served a parallel termination notice on March 29, 2026, targeting an August 2030 exit or March 2028 if prior change of control dates apply, amplifying contagion effects across INWIT's revenue base.

Combined termination notices from Fastweb and Telecom Italia expose greater than 82% of INWIT's group revenues to potential fee reductions of 15%, creating existential pressure on the tower operator's cash flow sustainability and credit profile.

Terminating the legacy Master Service Agreement addresses an annual Italian lease expense of approximately €1 billion, supporting a 25% plus IRR potential for the acquisition, establishing Swisscom's core financial motivation to pursue aggressive lease optimization.

Swisscom's operational flexibility extends beyond litigation strategy into concrete network alternatives.

Swisscom and Telecom Italia formed a non-binding 50/50 joint venture in March 2026 to construct up to 6,000 new mobile sites outside INWIT's grid, providing tangible infrastructure substitution capacity that strengthens the operator's negotiating leverage.

Fastweb and Vodafone plans to commence its multi-vendor network migration in April 2028, aligning operational execution timelines with the contested contractual expiration date and demonstrating preparedness to physically exit INWIT's tower portfolio absent favorable commercial repricing. The rejection of interim injunctions fundamentally alters the risk allocation between parties during the pendency of litigation.

The rejection of interim injunctions allows operators to prepare network migration plans while awaiting full judicial review on contract validity, enabling Swisscom to advance technical preparations, vendor selection, and site construction without immediate judicial constraint.

This procedural posture creates asymmetric execution risk where INWIT bears revenue uncertainty while Swisscom retains operational optionality, potentially forcing the tower operator toward out-of-court commercial concessions prior to final judicial resolution. The financial and valuation ramifications extend across multiple stakeholder groups and market segments.

INWIT equity valuation de-rated by 35% over twelve months, trading at 14x EV/EBITDAaL compared to its historical average multiple of 18x, reflecting market pricing of elevated contractual risk and reduced cash flow visibility.

Broker modeling indicates a 10% fee haircut across both anchor tenants would reduce INWIT EBITDAaL by 10% and free cash flow by 18%, quantifying the severe operating leverage embedded within the tower business model when exposed to anchor tenant repricing.

Morgan Stanley analysis indicates that ongoing anchor tenant litigation keeps European tower equities trading at an enduring 20% to 30% valuation discount relative to US peer multiples, suggesting the dispute carries systemic implications for infrastructure asset pricing beyond the immediate parties. Looking forward, the resolution pathway remains multi-dimensional with significant uncertainty regarding ultimate outcomes.

The ordinary trial proceeding will determine contractual validity through first-instance judgment by 2029, but subsequent appellate pathways could extend final legal certainty into the early 2030s.

Meanwhile, commercial incentives for out-of-court settlement persist given mutual exposure to execution risk, protracted legal costs, and operational disruption.

The convergence of alternative infrastructure capacity, regulatory emission limit expansions enabling multi-tenant colocation, and cross-border European tower lease repricing creates structural pressure for negotiated commercial restructuring regardless of judicial outcomes.

However, the absence of interim injunctive relief tilts bargaining power decisively toward mobile operators who retain unimpeded capacity to advance physical network migration during litigation, potentially forcing INWIT to accept substantial fee concessions to preserve anchor tenant relationships before irreversible site departures materialize.
