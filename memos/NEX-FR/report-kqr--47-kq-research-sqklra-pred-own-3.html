Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Working capital volatility and cash conversion definitions represent critical swing factors for Nexans's valuation and long-term earnings per share trajectory.

Scope of the research

1.
Crucial investor focus: "Nexans (NEX-FR), a French-listed cable maker that completed its exit from non- electrification businesses in July 2026; it reports PWR-Transmission (high-voltage subsea and land cable systems with installation: the Halden plant in Norway with its mass-impregnated and extruded lines, Charleston in the United States, Charleroi in Belgium and three cable-laying vessels including Electra), PWR-Grid (medium-voltage cables and accessories for distribution utilities), PWR-Connect (low-voltage cables for buildings, industry and data centres, including Republic Wire in the United States since June 2026) and Other Activities (metallurgy and copper rod). How has Nexans's (NEX-FR) operating working capital evolved from 2021 to H1 2026, including customer down-payments and other contract liabilities, inventories at record copper prices and copper supplierpayment arrangements (€449m at end-2025, company), how does the company define free cash flow and normalised free cash flow, and what have the company, brokers and peers (NKT, Prysmian, Siemens Energy) said about working capital and cash conversion when high-voltage backlogs stop growing?"

Extensive source database: A thorough review of industry data identified more than 150 relevant sources, four key peer companies, and multiple historical backlog inflection precedents. Analyzing this wide array of documents from brokers, filings, and expert calls allows for a robust assessment of cash conversion trends. Jefferies has consistently highlighted that comparing Nexans with peers like NKT and Prysmian provides crucial insights into valuation discounts. This comprehensive research base is essential for resolving the Key Question and identifying the core drivers of future cash generation.

Working capital and cash impacts

3.
Elevated earnings sensitivity: The volatility of operating working capital directly drives Nexans's net interest expenses and financing costs, which directly affects earnings per share over the next 18 months. As raw material prices fluctuate, inventory financing requirements can escalate quickly, which compresses margins unless offset by supplier payment arrangements. Jefferies notes that vertical integration in metallurgy gives Nexans better control over copper procurement compared to some non-integrated peers. Therefore, managing working capital is critical to stabilizing net financial interest expenses and protecting earnings per share during periods of high commodity price volatility.
4.
Disparate financial definitions: Discrepancies between how Nexans and brokers define free cash flow can lead to significant valuation anomalies and mispriced earnings models. While Nexans reports normalised cash conversion to present a highly standardized view of underlying operations, brokers often apply more conservative adjustments for strategic capex. Jefferies argues that these differences in cash reporting directly influence how investors value the high-voltage transmission segment relative to peer multiples. Consequently, a clear understanding of cash definitions is essential for accurate valuation benchmarking across the European cable sector.
5.
Peering backlog plateau: When high-voltage backlogs begin to plateau, working capital dynamics shift rapidly from cash-absorptive to cash-generative as advance down-payments dry up. Expert insights from an EMEA utility manager indicate that suppliers hold absolute bargaining power during backlog expansion, demanding steep down-payments. However, a stabilization in order backlogs would alter this cash cycle, which directly affects cash conversion and debt metrics. Understanding this turning point is crucial because it signals potential working capital outflows that could impact Nexans's leverage and share price trajectory.

Strategic portfolio expansion: Nexans's H1 2026 net debt bridge shows a substantial cash conversion ratio of 43%, supported by the acquisition of Republic Wire in the US. Thus, working capital swings can actually enhance short-term financial flexibility and support earnings expansion when backlogs are growing robustly.

Section 2 – Evolution of Operating Working Capital and Copper Financing

Section summary
  1. Operating working capital efficiency remained highly robust from 2021 through H1 2026.
  2. Nexans utilizes negotiated copper supplier-payment arrangements to optimize liquidity without resorting to reverse factoring.

Working capital historical trajectory

1.
Consistent operating target: Nexans sustained an operating working capital to sales ratio well below its normative limit of 6% throughout the period. According to Nexans's 2024 annual report, operating working capital represented 0.5% of sales in 2024, compared with 0.3% in 2023 and 2.7% in 2022. S&P Global Ratings estimated in February 2022 that the company would successfully maintain its achieved 4% to 6% ratio despite rising sales. This low ratio indicates exceptional working capital discipline that protects the company's financial flexibility.
2.
Rising balance sheet metrics: The underlying working capital components grew substantially as business expanded up to H1 2026. Nexans's H1 2026 interim report shows that inventories rose to €1,719 million by June 2026, up from €1,366 million at end-2025 and €1,279 million at end-2024. Trade receivables also escalated to €1,546 million in H1 2026 from €1,057 million at end-2025, reflecting elevated short-term capital absorption. This expansion requires careful monitoring of cash consumption in the upcoming quarters.
3.
Contract liabilities cushion: Customer prepayments provided a significant non-interest-bearing financing source that buffered working capital cash outflows. Nexans's 2025 financial statements reported contract liabilities of €1,137 million, up from €1,004 million in 2024 and €738 million in 2023. This growth reflects strong subsea high-voltage order inflows that collect cash prior to project execution. Consequently, these liabilities are critical for maintaining net debt stability during capital-intensive periods.
4.
Receivables securitization impact: Nexans actively manages its credit exposure and liquidity through receivables securitization and factoring programs. Note 17 of Nexans's 2021 financial statements indicates that the group uses factoring programs in Norway, Sweden, and Finland to accelerate collections. By end-2023, receivables sold under the factoring program totaled €31 million in Norway and €29 million in Sweden. This active financing strategy is improving short-term operational liquidity and reducing outstanding trade receivables.
5.
Inventory impairment provisions: Rapid fluctuations in commodity prices require robust inventory impairment policies to protect the operating margin. Nexans's 2024 financial statements show inventory impairment provisions of €60 million, down from €66 million in 2022. Impairments are recognized when the carrying amount of non-ferrous metal inventories exceeds market value and results in negative production margins. This provisioning framework safeguards Nexans against unexpected margin pressure during periods of copper price declines.

Copper supplier payment mechanics

6.
Intermediated copper financing: Nexans actively participates in structured bank-intermediated copper financing agreements to manage its raw material cash cycle. Nexans's 2025 annual report indicates that supplierpayment arrangements stood at €449 million at end-2025, compared with €341 million in 2024 and €253 million in 2023. Under these terms, banks pay copper suppliers on delivery, and Nexans repays the banks within 30 to 120 days. These arrangements are not classified as reverse factoring under IFRS because they do not extend payment periods beyond normal supplier terms.
7.
Core inventory valuation: Nexans maintains a minimum level of non-ferrous metal inventories called core exposure for daily operations. The company's 2025 financial statements state that the core exposure represented 38,665 tons of copper in 2025, unchanged from 2024. S&P Global Ratings highlights that vertical integration in metallurgy allows Nexans to secure privileged cathode supplies directly from mining majors. This structural advantage mitigates raw material price shocks that could otherwise severely compress segment margins.
8.
Copper sensitivity risks: Fluctuations in copper prices present significant risks to operating working capital and financial expenses. Nexans's 2025 financial statements show that a 10% increase in copper prices would expand working capital requirements and elevate net financing needs. The net after-tax impact of a 10% copper price increase was €26 million in 2025 and €23 million in 2024. Managing these price spikes is essential to preventing margin erosion in short-cycle low-voltage products.
9.
Take-or-pay copper commitments: Nexans enters into substantial take-or-pay contracts to secure a stable supply of copper cathodes for its metallurgy business. Note 27 of the 2025 financial statements reports take-or-pay copper purchase commitments of 134,022 tons, down from 141,976 tons in 2024. These contracts are paired with copper sales commitments of 112,929 tons to lock in processing margins. Such hedging strategies are vital to stabilizing cash flows against extreme LME price swings.
10.
Metallurgy structural shift: Nexans is strategically reducing external metallurgy sales to prioritize internal sourcing and recycling capabilities. The company's 2025 annual report highlights that external metallurgy sales contracted in late 2025 as the group focused on high-margin internal cable manufacturing. This downsizing of the margin-dilutive metallurgy business improves overall group profit margins over the long term. Consequently, this shift reduces working capital exposure to highly volatile raw metal trading markets.
11.
Recycling capacity investments: Vertical integration in recycling provides a significant competitive advantage over peer cable manufacturers. Deutsche Bank reported in June 2025 that Nexans's internal metallurgy business covers nearly 100% of its copper rod needs. 77 In contrast, NKT is the only major European cable player without vertical integration or recycling capabilities, increasing its purchasing risk. This recycling infrastructure insulates Nexans from global raw material shortages and optimizes its longterm cost base.
