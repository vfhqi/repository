Teqnion (TEQ-SE): a self-funded compounder of 15% to 17% a year in earnings per share, priced at the bottom of its range, with a negative February headline due before dated proofs that could re-rate it

8 November 2026. Share price 177.2 SEK (23 October 2026); 17.17 million shares; market value about 3,040 MSEK. Money is in SEK millions (MSEK) and per-share figures are in SEK. "Adjusted" figures exclude earn-out revaluations, sterling revaluation and amortisation of acquired intangibles. No broker publishes estimates and the company gives no guidance, so the Financial Analyst's model is the only numerical forecast in existence. Bases and definitions are in the end notes.

#0Summary: key insights, implications and conclusions

The investment case in brief. Teqnion is a mechanical earnings compounder that has just finished a two-year repair, and its shares are priced as if the repair might not hold. At a held multiple the shares return about +28% over 18 months and +58% over three years, all from earnings growth; a re-rating of one to three turns is a free option on top, and the events that could trigger it are dated.

What Teqnion is. Teqnion is a Swedish perpetual owner of about 45 small niche product and service companies, listed on First North and run by its founder from a central team of six to nine people. Sweden provides about 55% of sales and the UK about 30%, a share that is rising. Teqnion buys founder-owned businesses with 30 to 80 MSEK of revenue and bottom-line margins of 15% or more, at about 4x to 5x EBITA on the latest purchase-price note (management describes the price as 5x to 6x post-tax profit). It leaves the acquired businesses to run themselves, pays no dividend, and reinvests every krona, plus modest debt, into the next acquisition. From 2026 Teqnion reports two business areas: Nord (Sweden and the Nordics, about 1,400 MSEK of sales at an underlying EBITA margin of 11% to 12%) and Väst (the UK and Ireland, about 600 MSEK of annualised sales at an EBITA margin of 20% to 25%).

How the earnings engine works. Teqnion's earnings growth is mechanical and self-funded, in four linked steps:

  1. Free cash flow after leases of about 150 to 180 MSEK a year, plus roughly 100 MSEK of new debt, buys 250 to 290 MSEK of annual revenue at margins of about 22%.
  2. The acquired EBITDA keeps leverage flat at about 1.8x to 1.9x EBITDA while the group grows 12% to 13% a year.
  3. The rising UK share of sales adds about 0.3 points of group margin a year.
  4. As a result, adjusted EPS compounds at 15% to 17% a year in the model: 9.55 SEK in 2026, 10.71 in 2027, 12.58 in 2028 and 17.13 in 2030.

What the price implies. The shares trade at about 15x two-year-forward adjusted EPS and about 13x FY2027 EV/EBITA with every liability counted. That is the level of the "newer compounder" peers (Idun, Beijer Alma) and the bottom of Teqnion's own range. If the 15x multiple holds, the shareholder's return equals the earnings growth, with nothing from dividends.

The six insights that matter most.

  1. Teqnion's reported numbers will understate the business at the next two reports, and the market will read them without an analyst to interpret them. Reported rolling EPS falls from 9.45 to about 8.7 SEK at the 13 February 2027 report, for two reasons: the Q4 2025 quarter that leaves the rolling window carried a non-operating gain, and 2026 carries about 30 MSEK of sterling revaluation losses and amortisation that adjusted EPS excludes. Q1 2027 reported EBITA will look roughly flat on Q1 2026, whose 68.0 MSEK included 7 MSEK of earn-out gains, despite revenue growth of 13% or more. The company's only target, doubling EPS every five years, will read as "missed" at every report until early 2029, because its comparison years (2022 and 2023) were peaks: the FY2027 marker of 13.68 SEK compares with adjusted EPS of about 10.7. From FY2029 the marker drops to 11.14 SEK and the target reads as beaten by 25% to 30%, with no change in the business. With 93% of the share's price variance specific to the company, no analyst in between and about USD 0.5m traded a day, the share price reacts to headlines. The practical conclusion is that the gap between Teqnion's reported figures and its underlying business is at its widest now, probably widens on 13 February 2027, and then closes through 2027.
  2. Teqnion's acquisition engine is cheaper and faster than the model assumes. Note 5 of the Q3 2026 report gives the nine-month 2026 acquisitions a total consideration of 303.7 MSEK, acquired cash of 63.3 MSEK and nine-month pro-forma EBITA of 45.4 MSEK. Annualised, that is an enterprise value of about 240 MSEK for about 60 MSEK of EBITA, roughly 4x; the 2025 acquisitions cost 430.7 MSEK for 93.6 MSEK of EBITA, or 4.6x. The model prices acquisitions at 5.5x, rising to 6.25x. On pace, Teqnion had closed about 275 MSEK of acquired annual revenue by September 2026, and on 29 October it closed Odlings Group (UK memorials; average revenue above GBP 10m, about 125 MSEK), which is in no forecast. Adding Odlings to the model, and trimming 2027 new-deal volume to 200 MSEK to respect absorption capacity, lifts FY2027 adjusted EPS to about 11.4 SEK, 6% above the model's 10.71, and moves Q1 2027 reported EBITA growth from flat to about +9%.
  3. Teqnion's margin repair is real and already in the base, and the true margin is about 1.2 points below the headline. The company's EBITA margin of 14.4% for nine months of 2026 includes 18.8 MSEK of earn-out write-backs booked as income; the underlying margin is 13.2%, against about 8.9% in 2025. Nord's margin fell 80 basis points year on year in Q3 2026. From here the group margin rises only through the growing UK share, from 12.4% adjusted in 2026 to 13.8% in 2030. Nobody outside the company has published the underlying figure.
  4. Three things are not yet demonstrated, and they are exactly what the multiple is waiting for. The first is positive organic growth: Teqnion has reported seven negative quarters, the weakest record in its peer group, and about 3 points of the decline come from deliberate exits that lap by Q2 2027. The second is control depth: after a fraud at a subsidiary in 2025, Teqnion still has no internal audit function and the auditor does not review the quarterly reports. The third is a clean fourth quarter from the restructured portfolio: the fourth quarter broke in 2024 and 2025 at named units that have since been exited. Organic growth and the fourth quarter are tested at dated reports, on 13 February 2027 and in late April 2027.
  5. The risk/reward skew is negative for six months and balanced thereafter, so the downside reaches the share price before the upside. The range for FY2027 adjusted EPS runs from about 8.0 to 11.8 SEK around 10.7, with a probability of about 70% of landing between 10.0 and 11.5. Three forces tilt the next six months down. Operating leverage is asymmetric in time: a fall in volume hits profit within a quarter, while a recovery lifts profit with a lag. Interest rates have moved against the forecast since October. The April 2027 UK wage increase lands before any price reset. From Q2 2027 the balance improves: the exits have lapped, Odlings and the September acquisitions are fully consolidated, and the housing and purchasing-manager indicators for Nord begin to feed through.
  6. Teqnion is a serial acquirer whose EPS compounds mechanically, but it lacks, for now, the channel through which upgrades usually reach the share price. Under the sector rule that "consensus models only businesses already owned", a first set of analyst estimates would start low, on reported figures for the businesses already owned, and would be upgraded with every acquisition. No estimate set exists, however, and I put the probability of one existing within 18 months at only about 40%. Three catalysts would convert the fundamentals into market recognition: organic growth at or above zero in the Q1 or Q2 2027 report; a first estimate set, probably sponsored by the company; and management presenting an underlying EPS line beside its doubling marker (35% to 45% likely by the FY2027 report).

#1Operating environment: tailwinds, headwinds and risks

Headwinds come first and tailwinds second. Over the next 18 months financing costs, UK wages and the krona weigh on Teqnion, while the improving leading indicators reach Nord's organic growth only from mid-2027 to 2028.

Evidence: demand. Demand in the businesses Teqnion is keeping is roughly flat. Reported organic growth of −3% in Q2 and Q3 2026 includes about 3 points of deliberate exits, and Q2 organic growth excluding discontinued activities was +0.3%. Nord's construction and contract-manufacturing units are at the trough, with no coincident sign of a turn: order books showed "no clear trend shift" in October 2026. The leading indicators, by contrast, have moved up (panel below). The Swedish manufacturing purchasing managers' index (PMI) has historically preceded organic acceleration at the general-industrial peers by two to five quarters. Väst's niches are steadier: decontamination validation for the UK National Health Service (over 80% recurring revenue), multi-year vending contracts, replacement-market composite doors and compressed-air service.

Evidence: costs, rates and competition for deals. The headwinds in the panel below arrive first. The one that bears on the acquisition engine itself is competition: Nordic buyers are converging on the UK deal market, where Bergman & Beving made six of its eight deals, Beijer Alma made its first deal and Röko has its largest market.

Inference and so-what. The environment does not drive the case. It sets three things: the cost of debt, the multiple (both currently adverse) and the timing of the organic turn. The one structural tailwind, founder succession supplying thousands of small targets at low multiples, is intact and is the core of the case. The demand tailwind is real but lagged. Building products are late-cycle, so Nord's housing-linked units will feel the recovery in late 2027 to 2028, and the PMI lead reaches the industrial units from mid-2027. Over the next 18 months, therefore, the headwinds (rates, UK wages, the krona) come first and the tailwinds second.

Strongest counter-case. The strongest counter-case is that the UK is becoming a crowded hunting ground, so that Teqnion's low entry multiples erode over 18 to 36 months. The evidence of crowding is real (Nordic private deals at 8x to 10x EBITDA; European private-equity business-services deals near 12x), but it applies to deals larger than Teqnion's bracket of 30 to 80 MSEK of revenue, which experts describe as "seldom subject to bidding wars".

Where I differ from the market. The sector narrative groups Teqnion with Idun and Storskogen as construction-exposed and organically shrinking. That grouping is right on the coincident data and too pessimistic on the leading data, which now point to Nord improving from 2028. I agree with the market that interest rates are the dominant macroeconomic force in the near term.

What would change my view. Two developments would turn the environment from a headwind to the multiple into a headwind to the acquisition engine itself: a disclosed entry multiple above about 7x EBITA, or two more rate rises by the Riksbank and the Bank of England beyond February 2027.

Conclusion: external forces are a mild net headwind through financing cost, currency and the multiple, and roughly neutral over 18 months; the demand tailwind for Nord is real but arrives late in that period.

Score: 48, set by the Rater. Retained demand is about flat (reported organic -3% includes about 3 points of deliberate exits); rates, the April 2027 UK wage step, metals and freight inflation and a stronger krona come first, and the leading indicators (Swedish PMI 56-58, housing starts +12%, Byggmaterialindex +6.5%) only reach Nord's organic growth from mid-2027 to 2028. Inference: what is weak. Teqnion's internal weaknesses are structural rather than directional. So-what. For the next 18 months the internal change force is mechanical (acquisitions plus mix) rather than further restructuring, and it supports adjusted EBITA growth of 15% to 17% a year. The quarter's one new internal development, the Odlings acquisition, fits a pattern of thematic mini-groups across the two business areas (Edurus, a Swedish headstone business bought in 2025, was the first). Odlings also shows that the engine can absorb a group of four entities and 110 people.

Strongest counter-case. The strongest counter-case is that the same thin centre that missed the problems at Reward Catering must now absorb eight acquisitions a year across two countries, and that the write-back pattern is the early signature of the next underwriting miss. This is the right worry. It is why the model carries a margin haircut of 0.2 to 0.4 points and why a unit failure (J4) is a key judgement.

Where I differ from the market. The narrative credits the turnaround and worries about organic growth. I would redirect the worry. Whether the turnaround can be repeated is no longer the question, because the turnaround is done; whether the control layer is adequate is the question, and the control layer is untested at Teqnion's new scale.

What would change my view. An earn-out write-back above 10 MSEK in a single quarter, or any unit failing its impairment test in the March 2027 annual report, would tell me that the problems of the 2018 to 2022 acquisitions are not finished. A hire into internal audit or a group-controller layer would tell me that the control scaffolding is being built before it is needed.

Conclusion: the corporate change is real, broad and mostly in the base; the live force from here is a cheap, accelerating acquisition engine run by a centre that is thin for its task.

#3Value chain, business model, financial profile and management

Teqnion is a capable compounder, not yet a proven one. A favourable value chain and a genuine sourcing moat produce returns on new capital at the top of the peer group, but the older acquisitions hold blended returns mid-table, and the centre is small for the group's new scale.

Evidence: industry structure. The industry structure helps where it matters. The supply of targets is fragmented into thousands of businesses, and the largest buyers hold well under 30% of transactions. Product niches of 200 to 2,000 MSEK are too small to attract global rivals. 94% of Teqnion's goods are sold directly to business customers and public bodies, no customer exceeds 10% of group sales, and capital expenditure is about 1% of sales. The sector's own rule is that groups that bought at 6x to 7x trade at about twice the multiple of groups that paid 9x to 10x (Storskogen, Sdiptech), and that the multiple tracks organic growth, because organic growth is the evidence that acquired businesses keep compounding.

Evidence: the moat. Teqnion's moat lies in sourcing and ownership. Founders accept a lower price in exchange for permanence, autonomy and minimal disruption; a former CFO reports deals won against higher bids. Database-driven direct outreach screens 100 to 150 candidates a year, at about nine deals per M&A employee. The subsidiaries' own moats rest on specification, certification and relationships: Unique Door Panels works with installers of up to 20 years' standing, T.E.S.T.'s work for the National Health Service is mandatory, and Edurus, Norband and MITAB lead their niches. These product moats are improving as the legacy businesses leave the group.

Evidence: financial profile. New capital earns top-of-peer-group returns, but the 2018 to 2022 acquisitions, and their goodwill, hold down the blended figures (chart below). Blended post-tax return on invested capital is about 11%, against 15% to 18% at Lagercrantz, Addtech and Lifco, while pre-tax returns on new capital at the evidenced entry price are about 20% to 25%. The adjusted EBITA margin of 12.4% in 2026 is mid-table. Working capital is heavy at about 24% to 25% of sales (Lagercrantz about 13%), because every acquisition loads a year of working capital before its income statement catches up. Cash conversion is about 0.9x to 1.0x of adjusted net income after leases.

Inference. Teqnion has the winners' ingredients: a low entry price, low capital expenditure, appeal to founders and an aligned owner-operator. Of the four conditions under which members of the peer group outperform, Teqnion meets two (low entry multiples, leverage inside limits) and lacks two (positive organic growth, clean governance). Its ambition is high and expressed as a compounding rule, which suits the model. The strategy is simple and has been made more robust by its own mistakes. The culture is consistent with the strategy, except for the gap between "eternal" ownership and the closures, a gap that is pragmatic.

So-what. Teqnion's advantage shows up mechanically, in acquired EBITA and mix, rather than in organic growth. The forecasts should therefore rest on the acquisition engine and treat organic growth as near zero with upside, which is what the model does. The gap in return on invested capital to the leaders is the legacy of the old acquisitions. The gap closes slowly as the new acquisitions' share of capital grows, and the model does not assume that it closes.

Strongest counter-case. The strongest counter-case is scale. Teqnion is a tenth to a fifteenth the size of the leading compounders, with a higher cost of debt, thinner controls per subsidiary, less diversification (one unit's failure moves group profit), a larger discount on its equity and no analyst coverage. The Judges Scientific precedent (revenue −21% and adjusted pre-tax profit −75% in H1 2026) shows what a volume shock does to a small acquirer with heavy fixed costs.

Where I differ from the market. The narrative treats an entry price of 5x to 6x "earnings" as the source of Teqnion's advantage. On an enterprise-value basis the price is about 4x EBITA, which puts Teqnion's acquisition economics at the very top of the peer group (chart below). I agree with the market that the centre is thin.

What would change my view. Evidence that the 2025 and 2026 acquisitions' combined organic growth of 6% is holding a year later would move me from "capable" to "proven" on the engine. A second control failure would move me the other way on governance.

Conclusion: a favourable value chain and a genuine sourcing moat, applied by an aligned and candid founder through a centre too small for the group's new scale, produce top-of-cohort returns on new capital and mid-table returns on the whole.

Score: 57, set by the Rater. Qualitatively a favourable chain (thousands of fragmented founder targets, sub-scale niches, 94% direct B2B, no customer above 10%, capex about 1% of sales) and a real sourcing-and-ownership moat with uneven product moats, run by an aligned, candid founder through a centre too thin for about 45 companies after a fraud; incremental pre-tax returns on new capital of about 20-25% at 4x entry are top of the cohort while blended ROIC is about 11% (figures: ROIC 7.2%, 53rd percentile; EBITDA margin 28th, distorted by the old cohort's goodwill and a reporting perimeter still being pruned), and predictability is only moderate (Q4 breaks in 2024 and 2025, seven negative organic quarters). Coherent with the groups (54, 49, 53, 57) and lifted slightly for the return on new capital; above Sdiptech-style niche groups at 9x entry, below Indutrade's proven scale and Helios Towers' unit economics.

#4Guidance, sell-side expectations and the record of delivery

Teqnion keeps the promises it controls, and its one EPS yardstick will read as missed for two years for arithmetical reasons. There is no guidance to beat and no consensus to lead, so the doubling target is the only published expectation, and it understates the business until 2029.

Evidence: targets and expectations. Teqnion gives no annual guidance and declines to, "so as not to force deals". It has three standing targets: EPS doubled every five years, net debt below 2.5x EBITDA, and an EBITA margin above 9%. No broker contributes estimates to FactSet. Redeye, the company's Certified Adviser, publishes no estimates, and the review of results coverage found no research published after any report.

Evidence: the record. Teqnion has met its margin, leverage, savings and deal-pace promises and has missed on earnings (ledger below). The only near-term number management has ever offered, that rolling EPS "might" reach about 10.13 SEK once the impairment rolled off, came in at 9.45. EPS fell 26% in 2024 in conditions like today's: high interest rates and weak Swedish construction.

Inference. There is nothing conservative or aggressive to assess in the usual sense. There is instead a yardstick that, by arithmetic, overstates what the business should deliver for two years and understates it afterwards. The EPS-doubling marker will be missed at every report through early 2029 with near certainty: FY2027 adjusted EPS would need to exceed 13.68 SEK, which is 28% above the base case and beyond the bull case. The margin and leverage targets, by contrast, will very probably be met at every one of the next six reports. Management under-promises in tone ("we are not satisfied" after a record quarter) and has never revised a target, so a formal upgrade is unlikely; a reframing to an underlying EPS line is more plausible (odds below).

So-what. No upgrade cycle exists to drive the shares, and the one published yardstick will read badly for two years, which depresses the expectations of anyone who reads it literally. In substance the gap is favourable, because the yardstick understates the business; in optics the gap is unfavourable. Teqnion's de facto expectation-setting is therefore the inverse of a "beat-and-raise" peer's.

Strongest counter-case. The strongest counter-case is that the absence of guidance signals a management that will not be held to numbers, and that the 10.13 SEK miss shows optimism. The point is fair, but most of the miss was currency revaluation, and the refusal to set targets has protected acquisition discipline: Teqnion has not forced volume, unlike Storskogen with its 54 deals in 2022.

Where I differ from the market. The narrative will report the February 2027 "miss" against the doubling target as a miss. I read the doubling arithmetic as a two-year base effect that flips to a comfortable beat in 2029, and I would watch for management to say so.

What would change my view. A capital markets day or a restated target framework would create a real expectations anchor for the first time. I put the probability at about 30% once revenue passes 3 billion SEK, prompted by the largest holder's representative on the board.

Conclusion: there is no guidance to beat and no consensus to lead; the company's only yardstick will read as missed for two years for arithmetical reasons, which is a narrative headwind now and a tailwind from 2029.

Score: 46, set by the Rater. No formal guidance (not itself negative), and on what it does target it delivers: the 9% margin floor beaten by four to five points, leverage inside 2.5x, restructuring and sourcing savings met on time, deal pace met or beaten; but EPS fell 26% in 2024, three-year EPS growth is -5.7% (30th percentile), the only near-term number offered (10.13 rolling EPS) came in at 9.45, the CEO graded 2024 F and 2025 D-, 52 MSEK of earn-out write-backs show acquired units missing plans, and the doubling target will read as missed until 2029. A mixed record weighed by the 2024-25 misses, between the groups' 34 and 55; just below Yara's no-guidance over-delivery on controllables, above TKH's weak two-year record.

#13Summary conclusion

Teqnion's two-year repair is finished, and its acquisition engine is now running faster and cheaper than the only forecast in existence assumes. Teqnion is a small, founder-run, perpetual owner of niche businesses. The case does not need organic growth, pricing power or a kinder environment to deliver 15% to 17% compounding of adjusted EPS. The return is the earnings growth, and the re-rating is a dated option. The fundamental outcome is skewed to the positive from mid-2027, and the probability of upgrades is skewed to the positive whenever a channel for them exists.
