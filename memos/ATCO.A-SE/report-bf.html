EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Atlas Copco operates as a best-in-class industrial equipment manufacturer with dominant global positions across compressor, vacuum, industrial tool, and portable power segments, serving approximately 180 countries through a highly decentralized organizational model of 24 autonomous divisions. The group's business foundation rests on exceptional operational execution, driven by an asset-light manufacturing strategy that outsources roughly 75% of production costs while retaining proprietary core component design and assembly capabilities.

This structural configuration delivers elite financial returns, including a 10-year average ROIC of 25.4% that more than doubles the European capital goods sector median, supported by resilient aftermarket services representing 38% of total revenues with gross margins exceeding 32%. The company benefits from high structural barriers to entry, including prohibitive customer qualification cycles, deep physical integration into production lines, and massive scale advantages estimated at three to four times larger than nearest competitors, which collectively protect robust pricing power and sustain group EBITDA margins above 26% through industrial cycles.

Core competitive moats include an unreplicable global direct service network spanning more than 70 countries, proprietary AI-driven diagnostic platforms like SMARTLINK that embed customers into multi-year service contracts, and technology-agnostic positioning in semiconductor vacuum equipment that captures rising equipment intensity per wafer regardless of node architecture. Leadership continuity remains exceptionally strong, with CEO Vagner Rego preserving the group's action-led compounding culture after ascending through three decades of internal operational roles, while financial flexibility is underscored by conservative net debt to EBITDA of 0.2x that funds continuous organic innovation at approximately 4% of sales alongside serial bolt-on acquisitions totaling 126 deals from 2021 to 2025. However, the business faces concentrated cyclical risks in Vacuum Technique, where electronics customers represent 60% of divisional orders and exhibit highly lumpy capital expenditure patterns, while aggressive Chinese capacity expansions and subdued automotive manufacturing create persistent headwinds for lower-margin segments.

Business model resilience and market leadership

1.
Diversified divisional structure: Four autonomous business areas operate across compressor, vacuum, industrial tools, and portable power, each maintaining market-leading positions with minimal cross-segment cannibalization and delivering specialized execution through decentralized accountability structures that track performance across more than 5,000 business line P&Ls globally.
2.
Predictable aftermarket anchor: Service and spare parts generate 38% of Group revenues with gross margins of 32-35%, acting as the single most critical margin stabilizer during industrial downturns while creating high switching costs through multi-year maintenance contracts, cloud-connected diagnostics, and proprietary software integration that mechanically widens competitive advantages as the installed equipment base expands over time.
3.
Asset-light capital efficiency: Outsourcing approximately 75% of equipment manufacturing costs to globally distributed suppliers while restricting internal production to critical core components limits net capex intensity to 3.0% of sales, substantially below the 3.2% capital goods sector average, enabling exceptional operating leverage of 60% during demand recoveries and rapid variable cost flexing to protect profitability through cyclical contractions.
4.
Geographic diversification strength: Balanced regional footprint across Asia/Oceania at 36%, Europe at 28%, and North America at 26% of revenues mitigates single-market exposure risks, while strong secular US demand posted 17% local-currency growth in Q1 2026 and resilient European orders grew 8% in local currency despite broader macroeconomic uncertainty, offsetting subdued Chinese industrial and battery manufacturing headwinds.
