EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Mediobanca has undergone a complete leadership and governance overhaul following the successful BMPS public tender offer in December 2025, with Alberto Nagel and Francesco Vinci departing and Alessandro Melzi d'Eril appointed as Chief Executive Officer. The entire twelve-member board was appointed from the BMPS list under Chairman Vittorio Umberto Grilli, with governance transition costs reaching Euro 49 million and Deutsche Bank noting that governance turmoil caused transitional noise that initially slowed business performance. The boards have approved merging Mediobanca into BMPS at an exchange ratio of 2.45 shares, while high-end investment banking will be transferred to a new unlisted Mediobanca S.p.A. entity. Post-merger, Delfin will control a 17% stake and Caltagirone retains 13%, both holding potential vetoes over consolidation and extraordinary dividend distributions, introducing significant medium-term strategic friction despite management executing over fifty integration projects targeting full consolidation by year-end 2026. The financial profile shows resilient momentum with net interest income down only 1% year-on-year to EUR 982 million in 1H26, fee income growing 3% to EUR 479 million, and operating expenses falling 2% to EUR 775 million, delivering a cost-to-income ratio improvement to 40%. CET1 ratio reached 15.85% with an 80 bps year-on-year expansion, supporting the newly upgraded 100% ordinary dividend payout policy. However, unhedged corporate credit expansion could erode the 15.85% CET1 ratio, compromising the elevated dividend payout targets. Historical earnings trajectory demonstrates resilient fee income expansion with consolidated total income reaching EUR 1,009.6 million in 2Q26, up 8% quarter-on-quarter, and net profit rising 15% to EUR 388.5 million. CIB fees reached EUR 216 million, up 17% year-on-year, though restructuring costs totalled EUR 49 million, affecting near-term operating profitability.

Most recent earnings delivery showed group revenues growing to EUR 1,948.2 million in H1 2026, up 6.1% yearon-year, with net profit reaching EUR 711.2 million. While Corporate & Investment Banking revenue surged 12.9% to EUR 492.6 million and CIB net profit grew 28.7% to EUR 171.6 million, Wealth Management revenues declined 4.6% to EUR 447.0 million and net profit dropped 32.9% to EUR 81.1 million, impacted by network retention costs of EUR 18.2 million and EUR 1.4 billion net outflows concentrated in domestic private banking.

Morgan Stanley highlighted a 4% core earnings beat driven by exceptional CIB fees, with Q2 revenue exceeding consensus by 1.98%, EBITDA surpassing consensus by 3.47%, and reported Q2 EPS exceeding consensus by 9.52%.

Peer-relative positioning places Mediobanca in the top-quartile for capital return and fee momentum, with fee generation growing 3% year-on-year driven by a record 17% CIB advisory surge and CET1 ratio of 15.85% exceeding Banco BPM's 14.40% and BPER's 15%. The 100% ordinary cash payout ratio implies a 30% threeyear yield, dramatically outperforming the 7.10% European peer average yield. However, underperforming wealth management flows could restrict commission momentum, compromising the 100% cash payout targets if private banker exits accelerate during the MPS integration phase.

Sell-side consensus has shifted decisively from defensive neutral to buy ratings as the BMPS corporate merger replaced stand-alone valuation models, with Deutsche Bank raising Mediobanca to Buy with a target of EUR 29.40 in July 2026 applying the 2.45 swap ratio to parent BMPS valuation. Morgan Stanley terminated active equity coverage in October 2025 with a final Equal-weight rating at EUR 24. Core analytical debates centered on Morgan Stanley modeling only 51% of announced post-merger cost synergies due to minimal operational overlap and warning that the private franchise risks banker exits and deposit outflows, while Deutsche Bank highlighted the positive signal of transitioning to a 100% ordinary dividend payout ratio.

Key forward drivers synthesis identifies five primary operational pillars: Wealth Management Inflows (15% impact, MEDIUM plausibility) facing wealth revenues contracted by 4.6% with retention measures costing EUR 18 million; Consumer Credit Normalization (25% impact, HIGH plausibility) with consumer cost of risk normalized to 185 bps; Structural Cost Efficiency (10% impact, HIGH plausibility); CIB Fee Recovery (20% impact, MEDIUM plausibility); and BMPS Integration Synergies (30% impact, LOW plausibility) targeting Euro 0.8 billion synergies but facing Morgan Stanley noting merger integration risks talent exits and modeling only 40% of synergies reflecting limited operational overlap.

Guidance credibility remains highly credible and insulated by structural net interest margin hedges, though previous standalone strategic targets are no longer formally applicable following the parent-level corporate reorganisation. The updated 2026-2030 plan unifies strategic guidelines with BMPS targeting mid-to-high single- digit revenue growth supported by geographical and product diversification. Consumer credit represents over 50% of net interest income, heavily insulated by fixed-rate client contracts, with group net interest income sensitivity standing at EUR 50 million for a 100 bps downward shift. Credit provisions are guided at 55 to 60 bps with planned partial use of credit overlays. Material risks include macroeconomic stress scenarios projecting Italian GDP at 0.70% for 2026 baseline, with the severe scenario modeling Italian GDP at negative 0.62% during 2027 and triggering a 2.8% ECL increase across the loan book. Operational risks center on wealth management net new money experiencing EUR 1.1 billion of outflows, missing standalone targets. Integration execution risks remain elevated, with the potential for severe advisor talent attrition if the BMPS integration disrupts culture could permanently depress wealth inflows, and accelerated integration into BMPS risks worsening the private banking talent departures and client outflows already visible during the transition phase.
