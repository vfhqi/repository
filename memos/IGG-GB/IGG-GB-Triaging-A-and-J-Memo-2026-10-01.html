[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. IG Group is a chief-executive-led corporate change case in a high-quality, cyclical, regulated business, onto which a $1.3bn US acquisition has been bolted; it is compelling in its parts and not yet in its whole. At 1,305p and 8.4x earnings two years forward the shares pay nothing for the acquisition and return about 12.7 percent a year on the organic business alone, below the 20 percent bar. The case clears the bar only if the multiple re-rates, and that re-rating depends on a transaction closing and a US regulator's decision rather than on anything the organic business can deliver by itself.

  • The change is internal, chief-executive-led, and has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for the organic business.
    • In the thirty months since Breon Corcoran's appointment on 29-Jan-2024 the change has moved from resources (a new chief executive, finance director, chair, technology head and three divisional chief executives) through choices (product velocity, a marketing step-up, portfolio pruning, two guidance upgrades in 2026) into actions and the key operating metrics, and from there into the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates.
    • Organic active customers +13 percent to 314.1k and organic first trades +74 percent to 88.4k in H1 2026
      • Over-the-counter customer income retention in the mid-80s percent against under 80 percent in 2024.
    • Net trading revenue +21 percent to £588.8m and adjusted EPS +21 percent to 68.9p in H1 2026
      • Consensus FY2026 EPS +10.3 percent over the six months to 25-Sep-2026.
    • The framework's screen rule for a corporate change case, a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase, passes twice over: organic revenue guidance was raised in May 2026 (to 10 to 15 percent for CY26) and again in July 2026 (to at least 10 percent a year beyond 2026).
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up where the market extrapolates and down where it also looks.
    • Revenue growth stepped from -3.6 percent in FY24 to +7 percent in calendar 2025 to +18 percent reported in H1 2026, so the top line is improving
    • The EBITDA margin fell from 47.3 percent in calendar 2025 to 44 percent in H1 2026 by choice, because marketing rose 51 percent to £75.4m, and net interest income fell 10 percent to £54.0m as rates fell.
    • The honest measure of organic profit momentum is EBITDA +4 percent in H1 2026, against adjusted EPS +21 percent
      • The gap is share count, lower depreciation and adjustments, and the files do not fully decompose it.
    • Consensus EPS growth straddles the framework's 12 to 20 percent band rather than sitting in it: +14.0 percent for FY2026, +7.9 percent for FY2027, +12.3 percent for FY2028.
  • The sell-side upgraded for six months and has paused since the results.
    • FactSet's mean FY2026 EPS is +10.3 percent over six months and -0.8 percent over one month at 25-Sep-2026
    • Both named brokers trimmed standalone EPS by 2 to 5 percent after 30-Jul-2026.
    • The upgrade cycle has stalled, not reversed.
  • On 30-Jul-2026 the case changed shape, and the market read the change as negative.
    • The proposed acquisition of Underdog (US daily fantasy sports and prediction markets) for up to $1.3bn, 60 percent in shares, and the pause of the £125m buyback after £33m, took the shares down 11 percent on the day and 28.2 percent over three months to 1,305p on 25-Sep-2026.
    • The market took roughly the upfront consideration off the market value.
  • What the organic case earns.
    • Adjusted EPS of 115.3p in calendar 2025 rising to about 130p in FY2026, 140p in FY2027 and 156p in FY2028 on our path, 10.7 percent a year over three years
    • Consensus is close at 131p, 141p and 159p.
    • The 24-month-forward EPS on our path is about 178p at March 2028, against 154.85p now.
  • What it costs. 1,305p is 8.43x the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of 154.85p: the 60th percentile of the stock's own five-year range (median 8.14x) and the 26th percentile of its ten-year history (median 10.60x), and below every named peer (Plus500 about 9x, Swissquote 13.7x, CMC Markets 14.6x, Robinhood about 31x).
    • The price pays for the organic engine at about the consensus rate and nothing for a re-rating, for Underdog's accretion or for buybacks resuming.
  • The return against the bar does not clear it on the modal path.
    • We value the shares at each horizon at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond that horizon.
    • At 8.5x held on 178p, plus 49p of dividends, the eighteen-month return is +19.7 percent, about 12.7 percent a year, a D on the framework's anchors
    • Over three years about 12.2 percent a year.
    • The carrying leg is earnings growth (+15.0 percent on the 24-month-forward figure over eighteen months)
      • The cash leg is 3.8 percent a year and predictable
      • The re-rating leg is +0.8 percent at 8.5x.
    • The one-year-forward footing gives the same answer: about 9.5x on one-year-forward EPS of about 160.5p at March 2028 is 1,525p, within 1 percent of the convention's 1,513p, because the growth is steady.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (net interest income at £110m then £100m, marketing at 13 percent of revenue, a 25 percent tax rate, no buyback in 2027) gives a 24-month-forward EPS of about 159p at March 2028 and a return of about 4.8 percent a year, an F
      • The earnings leg is not robust to two ordinary adverse developments at once.
  • The re-rating that would carry the case over the bar is not ours to earn.
    • At the 9.0x blend of our two multiple branches the return is about 17.0 percent a year (C)
    • On the re-rating branch alone, 10.6x, about 30.1 percent a year (A).
    • The branch needs three things: Underdog completes on the announced terms by H1 2027 (probable, about three chances in four)
    • The CFTC leaves sports event contracts standing without material restriction (plausible, about 55 percent)
    • Underdog's first two disclosed quarters track toward the sell-side's threshold scenario (plausible given the first two, about 60 percent).
    • The product is about one chance in four, the framework's "possible".
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', a B fit.
    • The input breadth is at the top of the framework's scale, the output evidence is partial (top line and customer metrics translated; margin not; sell-side momentum positive then stalled), and the maturity is full output for the organic change and input-only for the Underdog change.
    • The secondary fit is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' at C: a high-quality business 33 percent off its high on a nameable concern, without clearing evidence.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the new chief executive through choices and actions into customers, retention, revenue and raised guidance.
    • It breaks in three places: at the margin, which is being spent on marketing by choice
    • At net interest income, which is set by central banks on £4.8bn of client cash
    • And at the return, where a good business at its own five-year median multiple delivers an ordinary return unless an event that is possible, not probable, delivers a re-rating.

  • The case therefore is a materially changed business whose organic case is proven and priced, and whose route to a 20 percent return runs through a bet the market has already marked down.

    • We park it on the return, not on the quality of the business, and name the three things that would re-open it.

  • Rating = C, because the three Elements combine so that the weakest binds: the change forces are strong (B) and the outputs are improving but mixed (C), and together they deliver a modal return that falls short of the bar (D) unless a re-rating that is not in our modal path arrives.

    • The 'Investment Thesis' measures materiality
    • The change is material in the business and not yet material enough in the return.
    • Not B, because the framework's question is whether the change drives a 20 percent-plus return in the modal case and it does not
    • Not D, because the case is one event (a favourable CFTC outcome and a clean Underdog completion) away from an A-band return, and the organic case alone delivers a C-band return at the blended multiple.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The competing hypothesis, at its strongest: IG is a volatility-geared, rate-geared broker at the top of both cycles, whose chief executive has reinvested a windfall into marketing and a $1.3bn bet on an unregulated US market.
      • When volatility normalises and rates fall further, the organic active-customer base (314.1k, growing 13 percent) is shown to have been bought at a rising cost, the margin falls below the mid-40s, and the shares re-rate to Plus500's 9x on a lower earnings base, or lower if the CFTC restricts sports contracts.
      • Net interest income fell 16 percent in calendar 2025 and 10 percent in H1 2026.
      • Marketing rose 51 percent for EBITDA growth of 4 percent in H1 2026.
      • European retail activity per customer fell 9 percent year-on-year in July 2026.
      • Independent Reserve's goodwill was impaired within a year of purchase.
      • The risks research names this false-friend profile, volatility masking customer decay, as its "highly credible runner-up".
      • The market's own verdict is -28.2 percent over three months.
    • What we concede.
      • H1 2026 EBITDA growth of 4 percent is the honest measure of the organic case's profit momentum, and the margin is unlikely to return to 47 percent while marketing runs at about £150m a year.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • The operating metrics that would show decay are showing the opposite: organic active customers up for six consecutive quarters, retention at a record, multi-product adoption +67 percent, blended payback under six months.
      • The rate sensitivity is bounded and partly in guidance.
      • At 8.4x the market is not paying for the re-rating, so the competing hypothesis is largely in the price already, which is why our modal return is D rather than F.
    • Where it would win.
      • A Q3 2026 trading update (October 2026) showing organic active customers falling toward the 270,000 alarm level, or a CFTC ruling that restricts sports event contracts.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • IG Group is a chief-executive-led corporate change case in a high-quality, cyclical, regulated business, onto which a transformational acquisition has been bolted, and it is compelling in its parts and not in its whole.
  • Since January 2024 Breon Corcoran has rebuilt the leadership, pruned the perimeter, raised the product cadence fivefold, lifted over-the-counter customer income retention from under 80 percent to the mid-80s and turned a flat organic customer base into one growing 13 percent, and the outputs have followed: revenue +18 percent and adjusted EPS +21 percent in H1 2026, and organic revenue guidance raised twice in 2026 to at least 10 percent a year.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for the organic business, from resources through choices and actions into the key operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus EPS +10.3 percent over the six months to 25-Sep-2026).

  • On 30-Jul-2026 the same chief executive announced a $1.3bn acquisition of a US daily fantasy sports and prediction-market operator, 60 percent in shares, and paused the buyback
    • The shares fell 11 percent that day and 28 percent over three months to 1,305p, 8.4x 24-month-forward earnings.
  • At that multiple the organic case, on our modal path, returns about 12.7 percent a year over eighteen months, against a 20 percent bar.
  • The case clears the bar only with a re-rating that depends on the acquisition closing, the US regulator leaving sports event contracts standing and Underdog's early numbers holding, a combination we rate possible, at about one chance in four.
  • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS takes the modal return to about 4.8 percent a year, so the earnings leg is not robust to two ordinary adverse developments at once.
  • The case is therefore parked on the return, not on the quality of the business, with three dated things that would re-open it.

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Regulatory and capital barrier
    • Nearly all of IG's leveraged revenue is earned in jurisdictions with active marketing and leverage restrictions, which IG's compliance standards and capital turn into a barrier against entrants.
    • It keeps the profit pool with a few large regulated providers, of which IG is the largest by over-the-counter revenue.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Client concentration
    • A very small share of clients produces about half of core over-the-counter revenue, so the demand signal is the behaviour of a few tens of thousands of people.
    • It is a share-price risk and a case risk in a downturn; the probability is moderate, the magnitude large, the trend stable.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Regulatory narrowing of the core product
    • The regulators who cut the industry's European and Australian revenue before are now scrutinising interest retained on client cash, consumer duty and leverage.
    • It is a case risk of low probability in any one year, high magnitude, and a trend that has moved against the industry twice before.
Business
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Unit economics
    • Marketing is spent to a stated lifetime-value ratio with payback measured in months, into a product set that is wider each quarter.
    • It is the mechanism that turns marketing spend into the organic customer growth the revenue guide needs, and it is not volatility-dependent even if revenue per customer is.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Cash conversion and capital
    • Own funds generated from operations exceed operating profit and regulatory capital stands well above the requirement.
    • It funds the dividend, the deal's cash element and the resumed buyback without strain.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Acquisition record
    • Two of the three acquisitions before Underdog have disappointed or been impaired, and the third is guided not to be accretive for two years.
    • It is a case risk on the Underdog leg: the probability of a repeat is moderate, the magnitude large, the trend worsening with each larger deal.
Leadership and management
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Chief executive's delivery record
    • Thirty months of beat-and-raise on revenue, cost and capital return, and a top-decile record before IG.
    • It is why the organic guide is credible and why the market will give the deal a hearing it would not give a weaker operator.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Culture
    • The employee net promoter score is far below the financial-services benchmark and engineering hiring is behind plan.
    • It is a case risk of moderate probability and moderate magnitude; the trend is a deliberate high-performance transition whose outcome is not yet evidenced.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Capital allocation conflict
    • The chief executive holds a small personal stake in the target, disclosed and recused, and the buyback was paused to fund the purchase.
    • It is a share-price risk through the reaction of income-oriented holders more than a case risk; the probability is moderate, the magnitude small, the trend stable.
External
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Market volatility and retail demand
    • Elevated volatility lifted net trading revenue strongly in the first half of 2026 across every region.
    • It is the external force behind the top-line step-up, and it is cyclical, which is why it does not carry the case on its own.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: US options deregulation
    • The removal of the US pattern day trading rule in June 2026 released options demand at tastytrade.
    • It is a tailwind to the US leg that predates Underdog and does not depend on it.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Adverse CFTC outcome
    • The US regulator's treatment of sports event contracts is unresolved and undated, and the Underdog investment depends on it.
    • It decides whether the re-rating branch exists at all, so it threatens the case and the share price together; the benign outcome is plausible, a little better than even, the magnitude is the whole investment, and the trend is unmoved.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Volatility lull stalling organic customers
    • European retail activity per customer fell in July 2026 and US retail sentiment cooled, after a first half of exceptional conditions.
    • It threatens the case and the share price together, because the organic engine is the case; the probability is plausible, the magnitude large if customers fall rather than revenue per customer, and the trend is cooling into the third quarter.
Internal
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: The Underdog transaction
    • A transformational acquisition of a US daily fantasy sports and prediction-market operator, part-paid in shares, signed in July 2026 and due to complete in the first half of 2027.
    • It decides whether the case is right about the re-rating and whether the shares re-rate, and it has three conditions inside it: completion, the regulator, and early delivery.
    • The market has priced it as a destroyer of value, so its first disclosures can move the shares either way.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Product velocity and marketing step-up
    • Product releases run at several times the 2024 cadence and marketing has been stepped up to a stated payback.
    • It is the internal choice that drives multi-product adoption and organic customer growth.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Jersey redomicile and buyback resumption
    • The holding-company scheme due in late 2026 is designed to free capital, and the buyback is guided to resume in 2027 subject to it.
    • It is a driver of the share count and of the cash leg from 2027, and it carries a lender-consent risk.
Key operating and financial metrics
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Organic active customers and retention
    • Organic active customers have grown for six consecutive quarters, first trades have surged and over-the-counter customer income retention is at a record.
    • It decides whether the case is right and whether the shares re-rate, because the revenue guide is customers times revenue per customer and the competing hypothesis is that the customers were bought at a rising cost.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Multi-product adoption and cost-to-serve
    • Customers trading a second product are rising fast and the fixed cost to serve each customer has fallen sharply over two years.
    • It is the reason the margin can hold in the mid-40s while marketing runs high.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Margin below the mid-40s
    • The EBITDA margin has been taken down by choice to fund marketing, and operating costs grew faster than revenue in the first half of 2026.
    • It is a share-price risk first and becomes a case risk if the margin keeps falling; the probability that it holds is plausible, the magnitude on EPS is material, and the trend is down by choice with consensus expecting it to stabilise.
Key financial statement items
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Net trading revenue growth
    • Net trading revenue grew strongly in the first half of 2026 and organic growth is guided at a double-digit floor beyond 2026.
    • It is the line that supplies more than the whole of the EPS growth on our path.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Net interest income below guide
    • Interest earned on client cash is falling as rates fall, even as client cash rises, and the level is set outside the company.
    • It is a share-price risk, not a case risk; the probability that it stays inside guide is better than even, the magnitude per percentage point of rates is known, and the trend is down.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: EPS-to-EBITDA drop-through
    • Adjusted EPS grew far faster than EBITDA in the first half of 2026 and the gap is not decomposed, and a plausibly worse drop-through takes the modal return from D to F.
    • It is a share-price risk of moderate probability and material magnitude; the trend is unknown until the next results decompose it.
Sell-side estimates
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Consensus below the guided floor
    • Consensus revenue growth for 2027 sits below the company's guided organic floor, partly because the models do not yet carry the deal uniformly.
    • It is the scope for upgrades once the models are restated after completion.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Post-deal trims and models in flux
    • The two named brokers trimmed standalone estimates after the results and the last month's consensus changes are negative on every year.
    • It is a share-price risk of moderate probability and small magnitude; the trend is a stall, not a reversal.
Cash return, earnings growth, re-rating
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: The valuation multiple
    • The shares trade at their own five-year median multiple and below every named peer, and the case clears the bar only if the multiple moves toward its ten-year median.
    • It decides whether the shares re-rate, which is the test the return needs; the first two Key Drivers drive it and it is not ours to earn.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Dividend yield
    • A progressive dividend set by formula, covered about two and a half times by earnings and by cash conversion above operating profit.
    • It is the predictable leg of the return, about a third of the bar at most.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Earnings growth
    • Adjusted EPS compounds at about eleven percent a year on our path from a revenue-led mix, with the margin subtracting and the share count adding a little.
    • It is the carrying leg of the return and it does not reach the bar on its own.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Mark-to-market and vendor overhang
    • The stock has traded at a multiple a third lower within five years, and the deal issues about a tenth of the share count to vendors whose lock-ups are not stated.
    • It is a share-price risk, not a case risk; the probability is moderate, the magnitude about a third of the value, and the trend follows the sector's multiple as much as the company's.
