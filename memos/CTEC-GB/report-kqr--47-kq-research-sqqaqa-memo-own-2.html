Section 3 – Detailed Case Study of FY2022 and FY2023 Margins

Section summary
  1. Convatec achieved significant margin expansion in FY2022 and FY2023 through mix improvement, pricing, and G&A cost reductions.
  2. Foreign exchange acted as an 80 basis points tailwind in FY2022 but reversed to a 60 basis points headwind in FY2023.

Analysis of FY2022 Margin Guidance and Outturn

1.
Positive projections: Convatec set its initial FY2022 financial targets by guiding for an adjusted operating profit margin of at least 18% on a constant currency basis. This initial expectation was established in March 2022 alongside the Group's full-year results for the prior period. During the ten-month trading update in November 2022, management upgraded organic sales growth targets but maintained the constant currency margin floor of 18% despite intensifying supply challenges.
2.
Strong outturn: Convatec achieved an adjusted operating profit margin of 19.5% on a reported basis for the full FY2022 year. This delivered profitability stood 150 basis points higher than the company's minimum constant currency guidance floor. When evaluated at constant currency rates, Convatec delivered a constant currency margin of 18.7% which successfully exceeded the initial target of 18%.
3.
Positive movements: Foreign exchange movements acted as an important tailwind for Convatec during the FY2022 financial period. The Group benefited from an 80 basis points foreign exchange tailwind that directly inflated the reported adjusted operating margin from its underlying constant currency level. This exchange rate movement explains why the reported operating margin of 19.5% was higher than the underlying constant currency performance.
4.
Core operational: Convatec delivered 180 basis points of total reported margin expansion from the 17.7% reported in FY2021. Dissecting this expansion reveals that 80 basis points of the rise was entirely due to currency movements. The remaining 100 basis points of core operational expansion came from improvements in pricing, mix, and efficiency efforts.
5.
Significant savings: Jefferies reported that Convatec's positive profitability surprise was heavily supported by lower general and administrative expenses. The broker highlighted that G&A costs fell by 70.4 million USD as a direct result of simplifying group structures and improving corporate productivity. These structural savings successfully offset a 40 basis points gross profit headwind from elevated cost inflation and investment spending.
6.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley research in November 2022 pointed out that sequential margin declines over the prior three years had made the mid-20s margin target seem highly aspirational to investors. The broker noted that raw materials and freight inflation had been more volatile than management anticipated, which threatened the linear trajectory of margin improvements. This volatility suggests that structural improvements remained heavily dependent on external pricing pressures.

Section summary
  1. Convatec displays a pronounced half-year margin seasonality driven by sequential volume progression, productivity phasing, and lag effects in inflation pass-through.
  2. The company successfully navigated the intense cost inflation of 2022 and 2023 through its Pricing Centre of Excellence and systematic automation of manufacturing facilities.

Seasonal Profitability Shifts

1.
Favorable progression: Convatec historically exhibits a clear profit skew where second-half margins exceed first-half performance due to business volume weighting. During the pandemic-disrupted year of FY2020, the first-half adjusted gross margin reached 60% before temporary operating spending reductions began to reverse. Management proactively deferred several commercial transformation projects to protect short-term returns during this highly volatile global period. Consequently, sequential profitability fluctuated as deferred investments were gradually reintroduced during subsequent trading periods.
2.
Sudden contraction: Convatec faced substantial margin pressure during the second half of FY2021 as macroeconomic headwinds accelerated. The first-half adjusted operating margin of 20.30% was flattered by delayed commercial investments and pandemic-related spending pauses. However, rising logistics costs and targeted commercial expansions in emerging markets drove a steep second-half margin reduction. This compression ultimately dragged the full-year adjusted operating margin to its turnaround trough of 17.70%.
3.
Expected acceleration: Morgan Stanley noted that the corporate turnaround would require substantial upfront reinvestments, creating near-term operational headwinds. The broker highlighted that while G&A savings were clearly visible, rising commercial spending in Advanced Wound Care and Infusion Care would cap immediate profit growth. Consequently, sequential improvements remained heavily back-half weighted as new product sales gained commercial traction. This sequential variation continues to define the group's financial trajectory.

Section 6 – Capital Allocation, Share Buybacks, and Leverage

Section summary
  1. Convatec prioritizes organic growth and progressive dividends while returning surplus capital through structured share buybacks and maintaining a target leverage of 2.0x.
  2. Pronounced working capital and cash flow seasonality causes leverage ratios to peak in the first half of the year before improving sequentially in the second half.

Share Buyback Timelines and Execution

1.
Structured capital returns: Convatec has increasingly utilized share buybacks as a primary mechanism to distribute surplus capital to its shareholders while operating within its disciplined capital allocation framework. The group's capital allocation priorities systematically prioritize organic investments in research and development and capital expenditure first, followed by a progressive annual dividend targeting a payout ratio of 35% to 45% of adjusted net profit, and accretive bolt-on mergers and acquisitions. Any remaining surplus capital is then made available for return to shareholders through share buybacks when no active M&A opportunities are executed.
2.
The 2025 share buyback: Convatec announced a share buyback programme of up to $300 mm of surplus capital on August 20, 2025, to enhance shareholder returns. The programme commenced immediately upon announcement on August 20, 2025, and was funded entirely from the company's available cash reserves. Management reported that as of October 31, 2025, the group had successfully repurchased $202 mm of the targeted amount. The buyback programme was completed in December 2025, specifically concluding on December 3, 2025. Through this programme, Convatec repurchased 94 937 530 ordinary shares at a volume weighted average price of £2.38 per ordinary share, representing approximately 4.63% of the issued share capital, for a total consideration of £226 mm, or $301 mm inclusive of transaction costs.
3.
The 2026 share buyback: Convatec announced a subsequent share buyback programme of up to $200 mm of surplus capital on August 4, 2026, alongside its interim financial results. This programme commenced immediately on August 4, 2026, and entered a non-discretionary agreement with Morgan Stanley to purchase ordinary shares on behalf of the company within pre-set parameters. Up to 195 485 202 ordinary shares could be acquired under this mandate to reduce capital or satisfy employee share options. During its tenmonth trading update on November 18, 2026, the company confirmed that the $200 mm share buyback was on track to complete by December 31, 2026. Individual transactions executed through the programme included the purchase of 447 225 shares on September 1, 2026, and 650 447 shares on November 10, 2026, with all purchased shares being held in treasury.
4.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley research on November 18, 2026, noted that while the $200 mm buyback is on track to complete in FY2026, the overall margin guidance did not receive a proportionate raise despite upgraded revenue guidance. The broker pointed out that higher foreign exchange and InnovaMatrix headwinds offset the margin benefits of a lower share count and sequential operating progress.

Leverage Ratio Profile and Trends

5.
Consistent leverage tracking: Convatec monitors its net debt to adjusted EBITDA leverage ratio as a key performance indicator to manage financial risk and ensure credit worthiness. The company calculates this ratio by dividing net debt, which excludes lease liabilities, by the rolling twelve-month adjusted EBITDA. Throughout the period from 2023 to June 2026, management maintained a long-term leverage target of approximately 2.0x over time, while allowing for temporary fluctuations above or below this baseline to fund strategic M&A or capital investments. At June 30, 2023, Convatec's net debt was $1 298 mm, resulting in a leverage ratio of 2.5x adjusted EBITDA. This ratio expanded from 2.1x at the end of 2022 due to heavy cash outflows from strategic investments, including $57 mm for the Starlight nitric oxide platform, a $95 mm earn-out payment for the Triad Life Sciences acquisition, and elevated inventory holdings. By December 31, 2023, the leverage ratio improved sequentially to 2.1x adjusted EBITDA on net debt of $1 129 mm, driven by strong second-half operating cash conversion of 84%.

Seasonality and Capital Return Decisions

7.
Working capital seasonality: Convatec's financial profile is subject to a distinct half-year seasonality where cash flows are heavily back-half weighted. The first half of the financial year typically experiences a net working capital outflow, driven by inventory accumulation to support product launches and sequential volume weighting. Additionally, cash outflows in the first half are historically elevated due to the concentration of annual employee bonus payments, cash tax payments, and the timing of historic acquisition earn-outs.
8.
Cash conversion and recovery: During the second half of each year, this seasonal outflow systematically reverses, generating substantial working capital inflows. In 2025, working capital increased by approximately $40 mm overall, driven by an exceptionally strong fourth quarter, which is seasonally the group's highest sales quarter. For the full year of 2026, management projected that overall working capital growth would remain lower than revenue growth as these seasonal positions unwind. This cash generation model is expected to deliver a robust full-year equity cash conversion of approximately 100% in FY2026.
9.
Capital return decision-making: Convatec's Board of Directors typically reviews its capital structure and decides on capital returns, such as dividends and share buybacks, during key reporting milestones. Ordinary dividends are decided bi-annually, with an interim dividend declared in August and a final dividend recommended in February alongside the full-year results. Decisions to execute additional capital returns, such as the major buyback programs in 2025 and 2026, are also finalized in August alongside the half-year results, once the Board has established visibility over the seasonal working capital recovery in the second half and confirmed that leverage will remain within the 2.0x target.

Section summary

Reimbursement Scenario Divergence

1.
Financial trajectory: A structural deviation in Medicare's Competitive Bidding Program from current expectations would significantly impact Convatec's long-term earnings trajectory. DNB Carnegie research on October 22, 2026, calculated that a standard CBP price cut would impose a 1.0% group revenue headwind. However, if the proposed Access to Medical Supplies Act passes, this headwind would be eliminated entirely, elevating longterm EPS growth above consensus forecasts.

Strategic exposure: Coloplast is comparatively less exposed to direct US distribution headwinds but would face severe margin pressure if wholesale price concessions are forced upstream. Bernstein Research on January 28, 2026, estimated that US Chronic Care represents 12% of Coloplast's sales, with half tied to Medicare. If wholesale prices are cut by 15%, Coloplast would experience a one percent top-line drag, impacting its valuation premium.
