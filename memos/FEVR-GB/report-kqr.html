Section 2 – The US Royalty Structure and the Molson Coors Partnership 15 findings

Section summary
  1. The exclusive licensing agreement with Molson Coors provides Fevertree with guaranteed profit elements and high visibility over US earnings from 2026 to 2030.
  2. The profit-sharing model transitionally compresses immediate margins but de-risks operational logistics and working capital as the local production shift progresses.

Partnership Structure and Profit Mechanics

1.
Transformational licensing structure: Fevertree transitioned its US business in February 2025 by entering an exclusive license agreement with Molson Coors. Under this structural pivot, Molson Coors acquired the Fevertree USA subsidiary to consolidate commercial operations. This shift moves the US profit and loss statement entirely onto Molson Coors' balance sheet, which alters Fevertree's statutory revenue recognition. This asset-light approach ensures that Fevertree receives royalties without bearing direct operational risks
2.
Balanced profit sharing: Under the terms of the contract, the partners share US earnings through an agreed formula where both parties receive a broadly equal share of profits. Molson Coors recognizes 100 percent of US revenue and records production and operating costs, then pays Fevertree its share via a royalty fee. This royalty payment is accounted for as cost of goods sold by Molson Coors. Consequently, this partnership model converts US profits to highly efficient cash flows for Fevertree
3.
Relieved working capital: The agreement significantly improves Fevertree's balance sheet by transferring US working capital requirements directly to Molson Coors. Fevertree's working capital fell to 16.7 percent of adjusted revenue in 2025, down from 20.3 percent in 2024. Molson Coors now funds US customer receivables and local inventories, which unlocks cash for Fevertree to deploy globally. This operational relief allows Fevertree to focus its capital on brand stewardship rather than logistical funding
4.
Contrary evidence: Transition inefficiencies and high marketing investments compressed Fevertree's US adjusted EBITDA margin to 6.3 percent in 2025, compared to 14.4 percent in 2024. This near-term margin compression reflects the deliberate sharing of profits with Molson Coors before local production benefits are captured. Unless these scale efficiencies are realized rapidly, the structural shift may dilute short-term returns
