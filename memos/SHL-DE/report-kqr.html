Section 3 – Free Cash Flow Definitions, Debt Trajectory, and Buyback Triggers

Section summary
  1. Strict capital definitions and looming separation expenses will delay Siemens Healthineers from executing strategic share buybacks until late fiscal year 2028.
  2. Debt reduction is heavily constrained by over one billion euros of annual dividend payouts, separation costs, and brand licensing fees.
  3. While peer companies leverage cleaner capital structures to fund immediate shareholder returns, the group remains focused on organic deleveraging.

Defining Free Cash Flow

1.
Stringent cash definition: Siemens Healthineers defines its free cash flow as cash flows from operating activities minus additions to intangible assets and property, plant, and equipment. This supplemental liquidity measure totaled EUR 2,446 mm in fiscal year 2026, which represents a decline from EUR 2,714 mm in the prior fiscal year. This cash flow definition excludes income tax and interest paid, which are reconciled separately at the consolidated level. Consequently, the actual cash available for debt service is lower than the reported segment-level metrics.

2.
Heavy financing cashflows: Interest paid by the company is officially classified under financing cash flows rather than operating cash flows. Interest payments on loans from the Siemens Group alone amounted to EUR 316 mm in fiscal year 2024, alongside EUR 25 mm for lease liabilities. This financing cost treatment means that free cash flow overstates disposable cash available to shareholders. Analysts must adjust for these financing cash outflows when modeling the actual pace of balance sheet deleveraging.
3.
Robust conversion metrics: Siemens Healthineers achieved an excellent cash conversion rate of 0.9 in the first quarter of fiscal year 2025 due to working capital improvements. The group maintains a historical cash conversion rate of 0.8 to 0.9, which underpins its ability to generate consistent liquid funds. This cash collection efficiency supports organic debt reduction targets over the medium term. However, near-term cash conversion remains highly sensitive to temporary working capital builds and regional procurement cycles.
4.
Skeptical analyst projections: Deutsche Bank Research remains highly cautious about the company's near-term cash generation because of escalating operational headwinds. Their analysts highlight that tariff impacts and weak Diagnostics margins in China will continue to diluting cash conversion. These headwinds mean that free cash flow will remain depressed during the transition years. Consequently, the speed of organic deleveraging is unlikely to meet optimistic market expectations.

Mapping the Debt Trajectory

5.
Elevated leverage position: Siemens Healthineers reported a high net debt level of EUR 11.9 bn, or EUR 12.3 bn including pension obligations, in late fiscal year 2026. Deleveraging this balance sheet has become a critical step in strengthening the balance sheet before approaching public debt markets. Management aims to reduce net debt toward the target leverage range before initiating other capital returns. This robust cash flow enabled the company to reduce net debt by EUR 800 mm quarter-over-quarter.
6.
Costly structural separation: The trajectory of net debt reduction is restricted by EUR 600 mm of separation and carve-out expenses in fiscal year 2027. Additionally, the company agreed to pay Siemens AG a fixed brand fee of EUR 300 mm over a 36-month transition phase. This brand licensing agreement requires annual cash payments of 100 mm euros starting from the deconsolidation date. These substantial outlays will directly drain cash reserves that would otherwise be used to pay down outstanding debt.
7.
Massive dividend commitments: Siemens Healthineers faces a significant annual cash outflow due to its proposed dividend increase to EUR 1 per share. This dividend payment represents an aggregate cash outflow of over EUR 1 bn, which is paid during the second quarter of each fiscal year. This substantial dividend payout limits the pace of debt reduction because it consumes a large portion of annual free cash flow. Thus, the group must rely on incremental operational improvements to drive deleveraging.
8.
Rising interest expenses: The company faces rising interest expenses as it refinances its historical low-interest parent loans in the public markets. A large portion of the outstanding debt must be refinanced under higher prevailing interest rates. This refinancing activity will drive annual interest expenses to elevated levels of EUR 380 mm to EUR 420 mm post-separation. These higher financing costs will restrict the group's net income and further slow the trajectory of debt reduction.

Leverage Triggers and Buyback Timing

9.

The company utilizes adjusted EBITDA as the denominator for its target leverage calculations. This leverage ratio reached 2.8 times EBITDA in late fiscal year 2026, marking an improvement from earlier periods. This specific metric is used by lenders to monitor covenant compliance and by management to guide strategic actions.
10.
Strict leverage trigger: Management has committed to a leverage target zone of 2.5 times net debt over EBITDA. Jefferies Research indicates that strategic share buybacks for cancellation will only become an option once this leverage target is reached. Their analysts calculate that a share buyback is highly unlikely in fiscal year 2027 because of separation outlays. Therefore, buybacks will be delayed until the group successfully deleverages its balance sheet.
11.
Improving leverage forecast: Morgan Stanley projects that the group's net debt will fall to EUR 11.1 bn in fiscal year 2027 and further to EUR 9.6 bn in fiscal year 2028. This trajectory would bring the net debt to EBITDA ratio down to 2.1 times in fiscal year 2027 and 1.7 times in fiscal year 2028. These consensus projections suggest that the leverage trigger will be met by late fiscal year 2027. Consequently, share buyback programs could realistically be announced ahead of fiscal year 2028.

Section 4 – Olyrian Diagnostics Separation and Post-Carve-Out EPS Modeling

Section summary
  1. Structural separation of Olyrian Diagnostics will remove a persistent margin drag and focus capital on the high-growth core business.

  3. Sell-side consensus remains divided on the exact timing and route of the separation, with many models still consolidating Diagnostics through fiscal year 2029.

Strategic Rationale and Timeline

1.
Definite strategic pivot: Siemens Healthineers officially initiated the carve-out process for its in-vitro diagnostics business, preparing the division to operate under the new name Olyrian Diagnostics. The formal separation process is scheduled for completion by mid-calendar year 2028, establishing a standalone diagnostics entity. Chief Financial Officer Jochen Schmitz has taken executive responsibility to guide the business through this transition period. This major structural shift is expected to unlock long-term valuation multiples by highlighting core imaging growth.
2.
Gradual operating execution: Siemens Healthineers has planned the transition through structured legal separations and distinct brand roll-outs. The execution phase will run through fiscal years 2027 and 2028, focusing on independent IT setups and dedicated commercial models. Sharon Bracken continues to emphasize clinical workflow efficiencies to support customers during this transitional phase. Consequently, the business will remain fully consolidated in group results until the formal separation occurs.
3.
Prudent margin targets: Deutsche Bank Research reported that Siemens Healthineers has reiterated its mid-term ambition to achieve mid-teens adjusted EBIT margins for Olyrian Diagnostics. Achieving these targets is critical because the segment has historically underperformed the market in both growth and profitability. Management expects the Atellica platform transition to serve as the key driver for this financial improvement. Thus, stabilizing operations will support a higher valuation multiplier over the long term.

Therefore, timing mismatches could weigh heavily on group profitability in fiscal year 2028.

Management intends to use this program to improve margins toward their mid-term target. This efficiency push should significantly boost standalone segment margins if fully implemented.

Removing this dilution is projected to accelerate organic sales growth by approximately 150 basis points.
