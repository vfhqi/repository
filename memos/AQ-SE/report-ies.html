AQ Group (AQ-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results strengthen the business half of the case by a full step and leave the recommendation where it was, because the park is on price and the price rose: the growth condition the case set for a higher multiple is now probable, a consensus line the case judged correctly modelled has been shown wrong by a wide margin, and the one measure the case paired with its margin test, delivery precision, went the other way.

The challenge

Three readings press on the case, and the case holds none of them in the form the quarter gives them.

First: the mis-modelled variable is earnings, not the multiple. The case's park rests on two legs: the multiple at the 97th percentile is the risk, and our earnings path sits 3 to 5 percent below consensus. The quarter says the earnings line is the wrong one. Organic growth of 17.9 percent doubled the broker's forecast; EBIT beat by 16.67 percent; the beat came with deliveries delayed, not pulled forward, so the quarter's demand was larger than its revenue; Virginia is full, Norlake doubles it, and the group wins every US quote. Against that, the consensus FY2027 revenue growth of 10.3 percent that the case carried has been lifted by one broker by 3 percent, and the case's own path, EPS SEK 8.20, 9.17 and 10.07 for FY2026 to FY2028, sits below a consensus that is itself being shown too low. In this reading the 24-month-forward EPS of SEK 10.13 that the case divides the price by is the wrong denominator, so 24.5 times is a lower multiple than it looks, the growth condition's odds of about 60 percent and the joint odds of 40 percent are too low, and the case's statement that no combination of the inputs' figures reaches the bar is true only of inputs' figures that are themselves wrong. What has to be true: hyperscale capital spending holds through 2027 and 2028; the data-centre backlog is demand rather than incapacity; Norlake fills on the timetable; the one customer that is about half the data-centre segment stays and keeps its design. The case's own arithmetic bounds the reading: consensus earnings at 24.5 times held gives about 12.2 percent a year over eighteen months, a C, and the cleared branch alone gives 5.0 and 7.1 percent a year on our path. To reach the 20 percent bar, earnings have to run well above even the raised broker, so this is plausible for the ratings and possible for the recommendation.

Second: the margin was made by shipping late. The case's margin condition pairs two consecutive quarters at or above 10 percent EBT with delivery precision above 95 percent, because the framework's judgement was that a margin earned while deliveries slip is not a margin the customers have agreed to. The quarter delivered exactly the combination the case said does not count: 10.0 percent with precision at 91.1 percent, every data-centre plant at full throttle, turns at 2.9, inventory building for shipments not yet made, and the margin carried by US and Chinese utilisation through the European shutdown. The case's "false friend" counter-case looked for strain in cash and margin and did not find it; this reading says the strain is showing in the one place the case's own test looks, on-time delivery, and that the customers who dual-source and contract for convenience, the case's value-chain finding, are the ones being shipped late to, with the price of their patience not yet set. Time 24, India, Mexico and Finland are the same pattern at smaller sites. What has to be true: Q4 does not recover the backlog; precision stays below 95 percent into 2027; a late-delivery cost appears as a lost nomination, a price concession or a quality provision like the one of late 2025; and the "every quote won" record is a snapshot taken before any penalty. The reading does not need the margin to fall to be right; it needs the margin's cost to show up somewhere the profit and loss has not yet charged it.

Third: the setup has changed under the case. The case fits 'Compounder' at C and rates 'Demand Inflection' D because the demand explosion reaches about 12 percent of sales and there is no dominant position. A 17.9 percent organic print is not low-teens compounding, and every US quote won with the only two US plants full is a dominant position in the niche, if a small one. If the right setup is now 'Demand Inflection', the case's own counter-case applies: a late-cycle inflection bought at the top, where the right pattern is a clearing after a de-rating, and the re-visit level is a question of where in the inflection we are rather than where the multiple reverts to. This reading agrees with the park and disagrees with the reason for it, which matters because it changes what a de-rating "without a fundamental break" would look like: in an inflection the break and the de-rating arrive together. What has to be true: the data-centre line keeps outgrowing the group for several more quarters, so the structural slice passes the case's modal "toward 10 percent of sales by FY2028" well ahead of time.

The reading that fails on the evidence: that the beat is a timing artefact. Q3 2025 had the same European shutdowns, so the comparison is like for like; deliveries were deferred into Q4, not advanced into Q3; and the sequential step from 9 percent to 17.9 percent organic is too large to be the phasing of one quarter.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on, will carry what the dashboard does not yet hold: the post-results revisions from the other visible brokers (the case named Restricted, Nordea and Pareto beside DNB Carnegie), the consensus FY2027 revenue and EPS lines, the consensus target and the buy share, and with them the store's sell-side momentum letter. That settles whether the consensus FY2027 revenue line moves from about 10 percent growth toward something that carries 17.9 percent organic growth and a doubled US footprint, which is the first reading's test, and whether Master Rating 5 returns to A. It will not carry a Q3 data-centre share of sales, which was not disclosed; segment margins, which the company does not disclose; the Norlake volume and utilisation path, which was asked for and not committed; Q4 delivery precision, cash flow or turns; or anything on the data-centre customer's contract terms, which no scheduled disclosure answers.

The second reading is settled by the Q4 2026 report in February 2027: a second consecutive quarter at or above 10 percent EBT with delivery precision above 95 percent moves the margin condition to probable on the case's own rule; precision still below 92 percent with the backlog unrecovered keeps the alarm live; below 90 percent invalidates. Management has committed to recovering the data-centre backlog and the Finnish defence deliveries before year end, so Q4 is a committed test rather than a hope. Operating cash flow and turns in the same report test whether the receivables and inventory build was shipments in waiting, as stated; inventory write-downs above SEK 50m in a quarter invalidate.

The third reading is settled slowly: several more quarters of the data-centre line outgrowing the group, the defence nominations expected in late 2026, and whether 2027 capacity additions arrive with customers attached. Perception risk is dated by the hyperscalers' capital-spending guidance and by the Texas permitting pause, which the review expects to lift after the November 2026 midterms. The 800V DC and solid-state transformer transition is 2027 to 2028 and undated within it.

The price thresholds are unchanged and the price moved away from them. The case re-looks below about SEK 185 and enters at about SEK 165, or sooner on a de-rating without a fundamental break; the shares are at SEK 251.6.
