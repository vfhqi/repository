Section 2 – The Prepayment Loop

Section summary
  1. Customer prepayments and slot reservation fees have generated an unprecedented cash buffer for Siemens Energy.
  2. The prepayment loop faces substantial reversal risks if the book-to-bill ratio falls below parity by fiscal year 2028.
  3. The historical precedent of GE Power illustrates how rapidly working capital benefits can unwind during an industry downturn.

Mechanics of prepayments and contract liabilities

1.
Favorable advance payments: Siemens Energy has successfully capitalized on robust demand to secure substantial upfront customer payments. According to the company's Q3 fiscal year 2026 financial report, free cash flow pre-tax benefited significantly from customer advance payments and reservation fees. This inflow of cash directly enhances liquidity and allows the group to comfortably execute its operational targets. Consequently, these prepayments allow the group to capitalize on robust demand and drive near-term cash generation before actual project costs are incurred.
2.
Expanding contract liabilities: The accumulation of contract liabilities serves as the primary engine for Siemens Energy's working capital funding. The group's balance sheet has experienced a massive surge in these liabilities due to record-breaking order intakes across Gas Services and Grid Technologies. Specifically, the cash flows from operating activities reached €7.76 billion for the first nine months of fiscal year 2026. This growth indicates that contract liabilities provide vital liquidity that shields the company's balance sheet from immediate debt requirements.
3.
Vulnerable booking momentum: The prepayment loop remains highly dependent on maintaining a book-to-bill ratio comfortably above parity. Siemens Energy reported a strong book-to-bill ratio of 1.57 in Q3 fiscal year 2026, which continues to expand its backlog. However, if booking momentum slows and the book-to-bill ratio falls below 1, progress collections will inevitably decline. This contraction means that cash outflows will exceed cash inflows because progress collections will inevitably decline as existing orders are executed without being replaced.

This mismatch led to billions in working capital cash outflows because reversals of prepayment loops severely strained General Electric's consolidated liquidity.
