4. How the issues link

  1. Volume drives like-for-like growth, and like-for-like growth drives the multiple, not this year's earnings. United Kingdom practices grew under 1% with price up low to mid single digits, so volume fell. About half of revenue is wages rising 4% to 5%. Below about 4% like-for-like growth the margin cannot expand by itself.
  2. Earnings are held up by other things: price rises, about £50m a year of acquisitions at about 6 times EBITDA, cost savings (locums, the central Careline, a bonus release last year) and the research and development credit. This is your point from the call: cost control is what is holding guidance.
  3. How fragile that is depends on the date. For the four months just ended, cost savings and acquisitions make "in line" likely. The fragility bites in the second half, when consensus needs volume to accelerate. So your reading raises the risk of a soft tone tomorrow and of a cut in January or March 2027, more than the risk of a cut tomorrow.
  4. The price rises feed back on volume. A further rise went through this summer, beside published price lists and a squeezed household.
  5. The buyback and acquisitions lift leverage and interest. More debt explains the £20m interest steer. The extended buyback is a support under the shares on a fall.
  6. The chief executive is both the trigger and a risk. The search is near its end. A credible name could lift the shares; the same person may rebase the 4% to 8% growth framework.
  7. Pets at Home reports at the same hour. After the 1.9% of its first quarter, a soft number there is expected and confirms what is known. A strong number would undercut "the weakness is market-wide".
  8. The sell-side is already positive. 8 of 11 ratings are Buy, no target price was raised in the last 100 days and three were cut. There are few buyers left to convert on an in-line statement.

6. The balanced view

  • A cut is unlikely. A finance chief who knew the four-month numbers said "average" when you asked which part of consensus he was comfortable with.
  • The number is likely to meet the bar. August and September were about 2.7%, and Deutsche Bank had already written that August improved.
  • The downside has supports. The buyback is running to 28 January 2027. The memo of 3 November 2026 puts the fair trough at about 1,150p, (17)% below the close. A private buyer at 9 times EBITDA would pay about 1,770p, and he called the company vulnerable to a bid.
  • But the upside tomorrow is thin. The company has never raised guidance, and the shares have had the relief rally. The one real positive surprise is a chief executive.
  • What it implies on your yardstick: an average move of about (2)% with little upside. Because you would buy back after a reassuring statement, one tranche sold today is cheap insurance. Holding both is defensible only as a choice to ignore a fall of about (6)%.

7. The sceptical, risk-centred view

  • Your reading of the call. Asked an open question, he chose a major negative. That is the problem they are living with, it will be the main thrust of what they tell investors, and so it is likely to set the tone of the statement.
  • Guidance rests on cost control. Savings can be repeated only so many times; consensus needs growth of about 3.3% to 3.5% for the rest of the year from initiatives he described as early.
  • He cited August and September, not October, although he would have had October's sales by 19 November 2026.
  • The last two statements are the template: in line on profit, soft on like-for-like growth, (6.9)% and (6.2)%, both from lower prices than today's.
  • Nothing fresh supports the price. No broker has written since 1 November 2026, and the shares have slipped (2.4)% from the 13 November 2026 high to sit on the 20-day line.
  • Your stop-loss lines act late. On the 50-day line (1,296p) the exit is confirmed only on a close at or below 1,218p, (12)% below Monday's close, and sold the next session. Your 12 November 2026 intention (one tranche on the 20-day line, 1,373p) would confirm at 1,291p, (6)% below. Either way the sale comes after the fall.
  • What it implies on your yardstick: sell the larger tranche at least, arguably both, and buy back on a reassuring statement.

8. Where the two views differ, and the strongest case against selling

  • They agree that a cut is unlikely, that the skew is negative, and that the earnings are sturdier than the share price.
  • They differ on October, on how the market reads about 2.3%, and on whether to keep any exposure through the statement.
  • The strongest case against selling a tranche:
    • On the balanced view, 6 times in 10 the statement is steady or better, and the sale achieves nothing.
    • Buying back is not automatic. Under section 9 of the Position Entry and Exit Rules a sold tranche needs an Early Stage Indicator, a Late Stage Indicator and a trigger day, and it returns on the tightest of the 5-day and 10-day lines, not the 50-day line the held tranches keep.
    • A bid, or the first move on a well-received chief executive, cannot be bought back.
    • The whole difference is about 0.1% to 0.3% of the fund.
  • What would have to be true for holding both to be right: October was at least as good as September, the statement leads with the recovery since July and not with volume, and Pets at Home reports nothing worse than expected.
