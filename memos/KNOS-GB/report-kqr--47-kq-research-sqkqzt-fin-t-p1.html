Section 2 – Kainos's Cost Structure and Operating Leverage Mechanics

Section summary
  1. Kainos exhibits a high proportion of short-term fixed costs in permanent staff and property, which is balanced by highly flexible contractor outlays.
  2. Historical margin cycles demonstrate that operating leverage is highly sensitive to utilization levels, contractor mixes, and restructuring actions.
  3. The Workday Products division offers high long-term positive operational leverage, whereas near-term services operating gearing remains constrained by delivery partner reliance.

Classification of Operating Cost Lines

1.
High fixed payroll: Kainos Group plc maintains a substantial permanent payroll that acts as a fixed expense over a twelve-month horizon. According to company event transcripts from 11 November 2024, management mitigated this pressure by reducing average salaries by 2% through shifting recruitment toward entry-level roles. This rigid cost base restricts near-term operational efficiency unless staff can be reallocated to higher-growth areas. Therefore, permanent staff remains predominantly fixed in the short term.
2.
Highly variable capacity: Contractor and third-party delivery costs represent highly flexible operational expense lines within the company's services business. Interim report disclosures from 9 November 2026 showed that contractor and partner costs rose to support rapid revenue growth of 57% in Digital Services. Since these contracts can be scaled down quickly during growth slowdowns, contractor costs are highly variable within twelve months. This flexible resource pool serves as the primary tool for margin protection.
3.
Fixed partnership commitments: The Built on Workday agreement signed in July 2024 introduced a major fixed licensing commitment to the Workday Products cost structure. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 9 January 2025 that this agreement requires a flat payment of US$10m per year over eight years. This payment replaced a variable 20% commission on software sales to Workday. Because this obligation does not scale with volume, it represents a fully fixed operating expense line that dampens near-term products profitability.
4.
Rigid expensed research: Product development and research expenses are treated as fixed, front-loaded outlays because the group fully expenses all software investments. Company interim reports from 9 November 2026 revealed that research and development expenditure increased by 33% to £11.4 mm. Since management prioritizes its long-term product roadmap over short-term margin goals, fully expensed product R&D behaves as a non-discretionary fixed cost. This continuous spending prevents operational leverage from expanding until products mature.

Overheads and Belfast Headquarters Commitments

5.
Sticky marketing overheads: Sales, marketing, and central administration costs have historically shown low short- term elasticity during demand fluctuations. In the H1 FY27 interim results, product sales and marketing expenses increased by 11% to £11.1 mm, alongside a 15% increase in central overheads. This expansion occurred despite ongoing efforts to manage central overheads, which rose due to reduced interest income from cash reserves. Consequently, discretionary marketing and central overheads remain relatively sticky over a twelve-month period.
6.
Heavy property commitments: Capital commitments for the new Belfast headquarters represent a large, unavoidable fixed cash outflow. Company earnings calls from 10 November 2025 confirmed that property spending for the One Bankmore office will escalate to £20 mm for the full year. This construction cost is a non-discretionary capital allocation that cannot be paused without significant penalty. Therefore, the new Belfast headquarters projects an inflexible fixed-cost burden on the group's capital structure.
7.
Inelastic administrative costs: Analysis of Kainos's operating expenses reveals that administrative and overhead cost lines do not adjust dynamically to revenue trends over short periods. Management's interim presentation on 9 November 2026 showed that direct expenses in Digital Services increased 23%, helping to cushion the impact of a lower gross margin. However, central overheads and depreciation rose 15% due to non-discretionary investments, indicating that operating expenses remain mostly fixed within the year. This rigidity restricts the company's ability to protect operating profit margins when top-line growth slows.

Margins and Cost Flex in Past Cycles

8.
Significant pandemic leverage: During the FY2021 period, the group demonstrated exceptionally high operational leverage when gross margins surged under unique circumstances. Earnings briefings on 16 November 2020 revealed that Digital Services utilization rose to 87%, while travel and training costs fell by £3.0 mm. This combination of high client demand and depressed discretionary expenses allowed Kainos to deliver a peak EBIT margin of 20.8% on a FactSet basis. This cyclical peak shows how high operational gearing can be when services utilization is optimized.
9.
Prompt restructuring actions: In the FY2025 slowdown, the group took decisive restructuring actions to protect its profit margins in a softer services market. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 20 May 2025 that revenue fell 4% and adjusted pre-tax profits declined to £65.6 mm. To manage this downturn, the group implemented a 7% workforce reduction that cut 190 roles and incurred an exceptional charge of £8.4 mm. This restructuring demonstrates that permanent payroll can be cut under severe spending pauses, though it requires significant upfront severance cash.
10.
Diluted volume growth: The FY2026 financial results highlighted how rapid volume growth can fail to generate operational gearing due to rising delivery expenses. Deutsche Bank Research on 18 May 2026 reported that although revenue rose 17%, adjusted pre-tax profit grew by only 2%. This margin compression occurred because the group added 21% more workers, bringing total headcount to 3,475 including 259 contractors. Therefore, increased contractor and bonus costs offset any operational leverage that the revenue acceleration should have delivered.

Operational Leverage and Margin Gearing Paths

11.
Delayed services gearing: Operating leverage within the services division is highly dependent on headcount optimization rather than pure revenue volume. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 9 November 2026 that services margins declined because the company relied on high-cost capacity contractors to start new projects. If revenue growth stays above 20%, management will continue to use expensive external workers, preventing margins from expanding. Thus, near-term services operating leverage remains limited until contractors are replaced by permanent employees.
12.
Expanding product gearing: In contrast to the services division, the Workday Products software division carries highly favorable operational leverage characteristics. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 17 September 2024 that the products business operates with high gross margins exceeding 75% and very low customer churn. This high-margin recurring revenue stream means that once the fixed Built on Workday fees are covered, incremental sales flow directly to divisional profits. This software-driven operating model remains the primary source of long-term group margin expansion.
13.
Contrary evidence: The company was unable to maintain stable operating margins during the rapid revenue expansion of FY2026, which shows that cost flexibility is lower than expected during demand surges. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 18 May 2026 that the group's adjusted pre-tax profit margin fell from 17.9% to 15.6% despite double-digit sales growth. This margin drop was driven by unplanned contractor and partner costs of 1.1 percentage points, proving that the group's cost structure lacks immediate positive operating leverage when major public contracts must be mobilized quickly.
14.
Other side of the story: While the group's permanent headcount and property commitments present significant fixed costs in the short term, the high variability of contractor payroll and discretionary R&D outlays provides management with ample capacity to absorb severe spending pauses. According to interim presentations on 9 November 2026, contractor and partner usage accounted for 30% of Digital Services revenue, providing a massive variable buffer that can be cut within weeks. Consequently, the business model is highly resilient in down-cycles, as management can rapidly shrink direct costs to protect group profitability and cash conversion.
