Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The recovery of Chinese medtech procurement remains a critical determinant of multi-year earnings per share visibility for European medical technology suppliers.

Framing the core investment debate

1.
Urgent inquiry: "Is Chinese hospital buying of imaging, radiotherapy, eye surgery and laboratory equipment recovering after the 20% rise year on year in August 2026, and who is winning the tenders? What do monthly procurement data, tender awards and expert calls since June 2026 show for September 2026 onwards; how much of the volume goes to European makers that research and manufacture in China (Siemens Healthineers, Elekta, Carl Zeiss Meditec) against United Imaging and Mindray; what does the 15th Five-Year Plan change in practice for those makers; and how deep are the price cuts in the latest volume-based procurement rounds for radiotherapy machines and intraocular lenses?"
2.
Significant exposure: China represents a highly material portion of total revenues for leading European medical equipment manufacturers. For instance, Carl Zeiss Meditec derives approximately 25% of its group sales from China, whereas Elekta and Siemens Healthineers have sales exposures of 12% and 10% respectively. Consequently, any structural shift or recovery in hospital procurement directly drives the multi-year trajectory of their earnings per share.
3.
Delayed conversion: The lengthy transition of equipment backlogs into realized revenue creates a substantial lag in financial visibility. Complex capital equipment orders typically take twelve to twenty-four months to fully convert to sales, meaning that procurement recoveries in late 2026 will primarily impact fiscal year 2027 and 2028 corporate earnings. Because of this timeline, near-term order intake remains the most reliable leading indicator for long-term earnings per share growth.
4.
Divergent performance: Individual company execution and product positioning create highly uneven earnings exposure across the medtech sector. While Carl Zeiss Meditec faces severe ophthalmic headwinds from regional price erosion, Elekta defends its market leadership through strong radiotherapy order growth. These variations indicate that a blanket recovery assumption is incorrect because individual portfolio dynamics dictate EPS outcomes.

Regulatory and volume-based procurement challenges

5.
Increasing competitive pressure: Local medtech champions are rapidly gaining market share through aggressive pricing and domestic mandates. Chinese companies like United Imaging and Mindray are securing major wins in municipal tenders, which threatens the historical dominance of Western incumbents. This intense competitive rivalry forces multinational players to rapidly expand their local manufacturing footprint to sustain their sales volume.
6.
Tightening regulations: China's forthcoming 15th Five-Year Plan introduces stringent guidelines requiring local research and domestic software integration. These policies demand that European makers achieve full local value creation to qualify for public hospital tenders. This regulatory evolution creates a high entry barrier that will redefine the competitive landscape for capital equipment suppliers.
7.
Other side of the story: While national policies favor localization, premium European medical platforms can protect their positions through superior innovation and brand equity. Siemens Healthineers and Carl Zeiss Meditec can leverage highly differentiated technologies to offset pricing cuts. Thus, the procurement shifts may ultimately expand market access long term for those players that possess localized manufacturing capabilities.
