Switching Barriers and Requalification

1.
High validation: Medical device manufacturers face significant operational barriers when attempting to transition to alternative contract development partners. An expert from Medtronic noted in March 2025 that switching suppliers for higher-risk Class III devices is a lengthy and costly process. This transition typically requires 12 to 24 months of extensive regulatory and clinical validation work before production can shift. Consequently, these regulatory hurdles prevent customers from quickly replacing incumbent molders at contract renewal.
2.
Initial qualification: The upfront capital expenditure required for supplier transition acts as a powerful financial deterrent against contract reallocations. An expert from Catalent explained in January 2026 that the initial fee for qualification, validation, and customization represents a massive upfront hurdle. In the first year alone, these qualification costs can constitute forty to fifty percent of total provider expenses. For this reason, medical original equipment manufacturers actively avoid switching suppliers unless severe quality issues arise.
3.
Incumbent loyalty: Custom tooling requirements form a significant physical barrier that binds medical device customers to their existing manufacturing partners. An expert from Peak Toolworks noted in July 2026 that supplier relationships remain exceptionally sticky due to trust and the complexity of custom tooling designs.
