Section 6 – The German Policy Shift and Overhead-Line Priority

Section summary
  1. Germany's July 2026 legislative reversal of underground cable priority significantly reduces the long- term addressable market for Friedrich Vorwerk Group SE.
  2. The contractor lacks any internal overheadline capabilities, requiring a strategic reallocation of its internalized workforce back to natural gas and adjacent segments.

Legislative Timelines and Grandfathering

1.
Sudden reversal: Germany enacted a sweeping policy amendment in July 2026 that officially removes the decade- long priority for laying underground high-voltage transmission lines. The policy shift represents an effort by the German Economy Ministry to significantly accelerate power grid construction while reducing infrastructure expenses. This sudden regulatory transition effectively ends the Erdkabelvorrang principle, which had previously served as a major profit driver for specialized contractors. Under the new guidelines, future electricity corridors will be built primarily as cheaper overhead transmission lines instead of subsurface cables.
2.
Gradual legislative progression: The German cabinet initially approved the legislative amendment on April 29, 2026, before it received its first reading in the Bundestag on June 11, 2026. The German parliament subsequently reviewed the bill within the Economic Affairs Committee to finalize the legislative text. In July 2026, the Bundestag passed the landmark legislation, officially ending the long-standing preference for underground high-voltage cables. This rapid legislative progress confirms that Germany is prioritising grid expansion cost efficiency to bolster industrial competitiveness.
3.
Protected backlog insulation: The legislative shift does not affect any ongoing megaprojects that have already completed or started formal planning. Major high-voltage direct-current corridors such as A-Nord and SuedLink are completely grandfathered under the old rules. This structural insulation ensures that Friedrich Vorwerk Group SE can execute its current order backlog without facing immediate contract cancellations. Consequently, the company's near-term revenues are shielded from the legislative change until these massive projects conclude.
4.
Drastic market reduction: The regulatory amendment directly threatens approximately 4,100 kilometers of planned power lines that are in early stages of planning. These early-stage projects have not yet commenced formal approval processes and will be converted to overhead tenders. Because these future routes will bypass underground cable laying, the total domestic addressable market for trenchless contractors will contract sharply. This structural shift represents a severe long-term headwind for electricity segment growth.

Market Impact and Exposure

5.
Concentrated backlog risk: Friedrich Vorwerk Group SE has substantial exposure to this policy shift because electricity projects constitute 65% of its total backlog. Broker estimates indicate that roughly half of this electricity order backlog is directly tied to underground cabling works. This high concentration means that approximately 30% of the total corporate backlog remains vulnerable to the transition. If the company fails to capture new awards, the loss of these underground activities will threaten its long-term profit margins after 2027.
