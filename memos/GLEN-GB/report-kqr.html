Section 3 – Aluminium, specialty alloys, and metal recycling 15 findings

Section summary

  2. Stricter trade defenses and regional scrap export restrictions are retaining high-value feedstocks within European boundaries.

Structural shift to secondary aluminium

1.
Tighter import barriers: The European Parliament backed a broader Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism regime to close the scrap loophole by bringing pre-consumer aluminium scrap into precursor calculations from January 2028. This regulatory revision lowers import thresholds from fifty to five tonnes to ensure smaller unwrought aluminium shipments face symmetric carbon border costs. The tougher stance narrows the carbon-cost arbitrage between domestic low-carbon metal and unverified imports
2.
57 58 Restricting material leakage: The European Commission is pursuing a delegated act under the Waste Shipment Regulation to ban aluminium scrap exports to non-OECD countries. Mature European economies are net exporters of scrap, with exports annualising at a record high of one point three three million tonnes in 2026. Retaining this scrap within Europe is expected to widen regional scrap spreads and support domestic recycling utilization
3.
60 61 Substantial energy savings: Producing aluminium from recycled feedstock bypasses energy-heavy primary smelting processes and requires ninety-five percent less energy. This recycling model functions as an energy strategy that reduces exposure to high industrial carbon costs. Secondary markets require mature sorting and processing infrastructure rather than completely new smelting capacity to upgrade scrap

Section 4 – Carbon steel, stainless steel, and trade dynamics 16 findings

Section summary
  1. The European steel sector is undergoing a regulatory-driven structural pricing reset as import safeguards and the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism restrict low-cost foreign supply.
  2. Rapid decarbonization via electric arc furnace transitions remains highly capital-intensive, creating execution risks and near-term free cash flow pressure for domestic producers.

Tightening trade defense and CBAM mechanics

1.
Restricting import volumes: Jefferies Research reports that the revised European Union steel safeguard quotas effective July 2026 have halved tariff-free import volumes to eighteen point three million tonnes. This regulatory tightening has driven domestic hot-rolled coil prices up to peak levels of seven hundred and forty-five euros per tonne
2.
Implementing carbon border costs: The Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism entered its definitive levy phase on January 1, 2026, forcing European Union importers of iron and steel to declare embedded emissions and surrender carbon certificates. According to Morgan Stanley, this mechanism raises import parity by sixty euros per tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent, directly strengthening domestic pricing power
3.
Closing downstream circumvention loopholes: The European Commission proposed extending the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism to approximately one hundred and eighty steel-intensive downstream goods by January 2028
4.
Tightening origin tracking rules: New melt-and-pour documentation requirements became mandatory for European steel imports on October 1, 2026, to prevent origin circumvention. Morgan Stanley notes that Mill Test Certificates will become the core mandatory evidence of origin by October 1, 2027
