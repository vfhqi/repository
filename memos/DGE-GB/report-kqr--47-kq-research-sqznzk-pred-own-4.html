Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. US monthly retail and distributor data are vital leading indicators for Diageo's highly profitable North American segment.
  2. Disparities between shipments and consumer depletions directly impact operating margins and overall corporate earnings per share.

Defining the critical research inquiry

1.
Critical inquiry: "Diageo plc (DGE-GB), the world's largest international spirits company and the owner of Guinness (Johnnie Walker, Smirnoff, Don Julio, Casamigos, Crown Royal, Baileys, Tanqueray; 56% of United Spirits in India; 63% of Shui Jing Fang in China; a 34% stake in Moët Hennessy); from fiscal 2027 it reports five regions (North America; Europe including Africa; Asia Pacific; Latin America and Caribbean; India) plus a Corporate segment; fiscal year ends 30 June; reports in US dollars. What do monthly US data (Nielsen and Circana scanner data, NABCA control-state data, distributor depletion and inventory reports) show for Diageo plc (DGE-GB) by brand and category from July 2026 to the latest month, in particular Casamigos (Blanco and Reposado), Don Julio, Crown Royal, Johnnie Walker, Smirnoff and Diageo's ready-to-drink cocktails: volume, price/mix and market share against Campari, Pernod Ricard, Bacardi, Brown-Forman, Becle and Sazerac; have competitors matched Diageo's price cuts; and what do these trends imply for Diageo's North America organic sales and margin in the second half of FY2027?"
2.
Broad baseline: The analyst found 27 distinct documents covering 8 peer companies and 2 historical cases to evaluate Diageo's North American market dynamics. This comprehensive analysis incorporates company filings, expert interviews, and broker reports to establish a rigorous baseline of industry performance. Understanding these sources helps clarify the structural challenges facing international spirits manufacturers as they navigate post-pandemic normalization.
3.
Strategic importance: Retail scanner data and control-state metrics serve as critical leading indicators because they track real-time consumer behavior before changes manifest in wholesale shipments. NielsenIQ scanner data covers approximately 90% of US off-trade retail channels, though it understates on-trade sales. Meanwhile, National Alcohol Beverage Control Association data represents about 25% of the US population, providing crucial insights into regional pricing structures and depletions.
4.
Significant disparity: Distributor depletion data represents what consumers actually purchase from retail shelves, which often diverges sharply from supplier shipments. When depletions lag shipments, wholesale inventories build up and trigger severe destocking cycles that compress manufacturer revenue. Managing this balance is critical because global spirits leaders are currently holding billions in undrunk inventory across global distribution networks.

Translating retail indicators to financial outcomes

5.
Extreme profitability: The North American market represents Diageo's largest and most profitable segment, historically contributing 40% of group operating profit. This region possesses some of the highest margins globally, meaning that any disruption in US sales directly threatens corporate profitability. Consequently, retail volume declines in key brands like Casamigos directly drag down overall corporate earnings per share and margins. 35 g Q p y Diageo regional financial performance and projections highlighting North America's margin contribution.
6.
Persistent risk: Wholesale distributors are aggressively destocking inventories to manage high interest rates, which lowers supplier shipments regardless of underlying consumer demand. Morgan Stanley noted that Diageo's North American segment continues to suffer from an unwind of the retail inventory load. This destocking trend forces lower volume leverage, squeezing operating margins and earnings visibility for the foreseeable future.
7.
Unusual elasticity: High promotional activity in the US spirits market has pressured premium brands as consumer affordability constraints persist. Jefferies observed ongoing de-premiumization trends where consumer pressure on disposable income directly affects purchasing tiers. This pricing pressure makes volume recovery highly dependent on promotional execution, limiting the organic sales recovery curve for premium spirits.
8.
Contrary evidence: Diageo's retail performance shows that certain flagship brands are actually showing resilience, with Johnnie Walker delivering positive organic growth despite broader segment weakness. Bernstein Research reported that Johnnie Walker and Smirnoff Ice continue to grow by double digits in specific regional channels. This select brand momentum suggests that Diageo's portfolio diversification can partially offset premium tequila declines during the downturn.
9.
Other side of the story: Although US monthly retail data indicates persistent category weakness, Diageo's management argues that distributor stock levels have finally aligned with historical baselines. This stabilization could signal that the worst of the inventory destocking cycle is over, allowing shipments to match consumption in H2 FY2027. However, unless consumer demand rebounds, promotional pricing will continue to compress corporate gross margins regardless of stock levels.

Section 2 – Tequila Trends: Casamigos and Don Julio Performance

Section summary
  1. Diageo’s premium tequila portfolio continues to face intense competitive pressure and demand softness in the United States market.
  2. Heavy price repositioning of Casamigos has stabilized Blanco volumes but continues to dilute overall brand pricing power.
  3. Sequential volume growth for Don Julio has turned positive as retail sales begin to stabilize across core expressions.
  4. Competitors like Campari with Espolon continue to gain market share while high-end rivals suffer double-digit declines.

Volume recovery across core tequila expressions

1.
Sequentially improving: Retail scanner data shows Diageo’s flagship tequila brands are beginning to stabilize in the off-premise channels. Under the latest NABCA data for the three months ending September 2026, Casamigos overall depletions declined by a modest 1.5%. This performance represents a significant improvement from the prior fiscal year when Casamigos net sales plummeted by 27.7%. These retail scanner trends suggest that the brand's volume performance is slowly stabilizing following extensive promotional intervention.
2.
Divergent performance: Retail trends indicate the recovery within the Casamigos family remains highly uneven across individual variants. While the Blanco expression returned to a positive 4% volume growth, the premium Reposado variant remained weak with a 6% decline. This persistent weakness in high-end variants highlights that consumers are prioritizing affordability over super-premium options during the current economic downturn. This divergence will limit the overall brand mix recovery in the near term.
3.
Moderate growth: Retailers report that Tequila Don Julio has experienced a sequential turnaround in retail channels. NABCA tracking data indicates that Don Julio volumes returned to a positive 0.5% growth rate. This minor expansion is a favorable reversal from the 19.2% net sales decline reported in Diageo’s preliminary annual results. This stabilization across Blanco and Reposado expressions suggests improved consumer pull at retail shelves compared to previous inventorydriven declines.
4.
Persistent drag: Retail tracking confirms high-end expressions in Diageo’s portfolio continue to suffer from weak consumer demand. The ultra-premium Don Julio 1942 and Anejo variants remain in negative volume territory. This continuing downtrend confirms that the luxury spirits segment remains under pressure as affluent consumers pull back on discretionary spending. Consequently, the weak performance of high-margin expressions will drag down Diageo's segment profitability.

Pricing adjustments and brand equity impact

5.
Sharp repositioning: Pricing tracking shows Diageo has executed aggressive price cuts on Casamigos to restore competitive positioning in the United States. Nielsen scanner data shows that price repositioning efforts accelerated sharply since late March 2026 across all major bottle sizes. This price adjustment lowered Casamigos' average retail selling price by approximately 10%. This deliberate discounting represents a major shift as management pivots to prioritize volume over premiumization to protect market share.
6.
Negative price/mix: Sales value tracker shows retail price cuts have significantly impacted the brand's financial contribution. Jefferies scanner data for September 2026 revealed that Casamigos sales value fell 6.0% with a negative 6.0% price/mix impact. The negative trend continued into October 2026 with price/mix remaining depressed at negative 4.7%. This negative pricing pressure confirms that volume stabilization has come at a cost to overall brand sales value.
7.
Overlapping segments: Internal portfolio analysis shows Diageo faces internal brand friction due to similar pricing structures between its two primary tequila houses. Jefferies noted that Casamigos core expressions are priced closely to comparable Don Julio variants. For instance, both Casamigos Reposado and Don Julio Reposado carry an identical average unit price of $49. This tight price index creates significant overlap and potential cannibalization within Diageo’s own premium tequila portfolio.
8.
Contrary evidence: Pricing trackers show that high promotional spending by competitors has minimized the market share benefits of Diageo's pricing strategy. Jefferies reported that competitive brands like 818 Tequila are aggressively buying growth with a negative 24.4% price/mix. This intense discounting by rivals prevents Casamigos from capturing the volume share gains management expected. Consequently, Diageo may be forced to sustain higher promotional spending levels indefinitely to defend its retail positions.

9.

Campari’s Espolon tequila recorded robust double-digit volume growth of 16% in late 2026. This growth was driven by strong performance in its Reposado expression which expanded by 36%. This performance indicates that Espolon is capturing pricesensitive premium consumers who are trading down from Diageo's higher-priced offerings.
10.
Weakening peers: Competitor tracking indicates other major tequila producers are also facing severe volume contractions. Brown-Forman’s premium Herradura brand experienced a steep 16% volume decline in NABCA channels. This decline is worse than Don Julio's modest growth of 0.5% during the same period. This benchmark comparison suggests that Don Julio is showing superior relative resilience compared to some of its close high-end competitors.
11.
Mainstream pressure: Category analysis shows the mainstream tequila segment is exhibiting similar demand challenges. Morgan Stanley noted that Jose Cuervo's total tequila portfolio sales fell 1% with flat volumes in October 2026. 89 This performance is marginally better than Casamigos' flat volume print in October. This wider category trend suggests that the entire tequila market has slowed from its historical double-digit growth highs.
12.
Historical precedent: Industry transactions show that high-priced acquisitions in the tequila category often face structural growth challenges during post-boom normalization. Diageo acquired Casamigos in 2017 for an upfront payment of $700 mm, representing an estimated valuation of 19 times revenue. Similarly, Brown- Forman purchased Herradura and El Jimador in 2006 for $876 mm. These historical cases demonstrate that extreme transaction valuations create severe margin pressure when category growth inevitably moderates.

Section 3 – Whiskey and Core Spirits: Crown Royal, Johnnie Walker, and Smirnoff

Section summary
  1. Retail scanner and control-state data indicate that Crown Royal, Johnnie Walker, and Smirnoff face persistent competitive volumes and flavor cycling headwinds in the United States.

Crown Royal Blackberry cycling and flavor dynamics

1.
Severe sales decline: Diageo's Crown Royal brand is experiencing persistent downward pressure as it cycles the massive launch of its Blackberry variant. According to Jefferies research from November 11, 2026, Crown Royal's retail sales declined by 11.1% in the four weeks ending October 31, 2026. This trend reflects the challenging comps from the previous year's highly successful rollout of Crown Royal Blackberry, which saw sales of that specific flavor drop by 18.4% in the same period. Consequently, these flavor headwinds continue to depress Diageo's North American whiskey growth and near-term organic sales.
2.
Accelerating flavor contraction: Bernstein Research reported on October 9, 2026, that the broader flavored whiskey category has reached a clear structural inflection point and is now in secular decline. Crown Royal had achieved immense short-term success with its Blackberry launch, selling 760,000 cases in its first year, but is now facing severe double-digit volume contractions. The launch of Crown Royal Chocolate in August 2025 has also failed to offset the steep declines of core variants. This means Diageo will struggle to find new flavor growth to stabilize whiskey segment margins.
3.
Demographic spend headwinds: Morgan Stanley analysis from November 10, 2026, suggests that Diageo's core spirits brands skew heavily toward middle-income and high-income demographics in the United States. This contradicts the common narrative that Diageo's volume declines are driven solely by lower-income consumers trading down to private label options. Since middle-income households contribute 44% of Diageo's spend, persistent macroeconomic pressures on this cohort directly restrict premium whiskey depletions. This demographic skew limits the pace of volume recovery for Crown Royal.

In late 2026, Johnnie Walker continued to outperform the broader full-strength spirits category, which remains down by 1.5% in value.
