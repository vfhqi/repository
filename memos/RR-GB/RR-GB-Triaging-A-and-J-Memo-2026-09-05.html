B Executive Summary 19 findings
Executive Summary -- We are looking for a clear, conviction-carrying statement of the investment thesis, key ratings, and actionable conclusion; avoiding vague or overly hedged language.
Judgement summary
  • Zero headwinds fails outright. F ATI accounts for roughly 40% of engine content, supplies six of the seven most advanced nickel alloys and is sole source on five, with only two isothermal-forging suppliers worldwide
  • Overall verdict. B Progress to Early-Stage Assessment on medium conviction: the change forces are top-decile for the cohort, but the entry point and the case-simplicity guardrails both fail
  • Supply chain costs cash every year. Management guides a 150 to 200m pound supply-chain cash drag in 2026, the same as 2025, while engine production costs have risen 45 to 70% since 2020
  • Case simplicity is the worst block. D Perimeter spans three divisions and more than a dozen business units across 48 countries, against a guardrail of ten or fewer units
  • Entry point is the binding objection. At 1,563.8p the shares sit 10.2% above the consensus target of 1,419.7p and 1.40% below the 52-week high of 1,586.0p
  • Narrowbody absence caps the ceiling. Rolls-Royce holds exactly 0% of new narrowbody production, in a segment that is 76% of the global fleet
  • Earnings base carries heavy estimation content. A 2% change in customer pricing triggers a 360 to 400m pound revenue catch-up, and net service-agreement creditors reach 10.2bn pounds, heading to 15.4bn by 2028
  • Risks report recommends parking. Its competing-hypotheses matrix names the false-friend profile as winner, and its section 14 asks for the statutory-versus-underlying profit gap to be resolved first
  • Balance sheet is the cleanest fact. Net cash of 1.9bn pounds at end-2025 against 3.3bn of net debt in 2022, funding a 7 to 9bn pound buyback across 2026 to 2028
  • Change forces are genuinely exceptional. A Underlying revenue rose 15.4bn to 20.06bn pounds between 2023 and 2025, margin 10.3 to 17.3%, return on capital employed 4.9 to 18.9%
  • Setup fit is corporate change driven. Eight of the nine corporate-change tests fire, but the change began in January 2023 and the re-rating is already banked
  • Thematic fit is a live-tested positive. B During the Iran escalation Rolls-Royce recovered fully while MTU Aero Engines did not, and Power Systems orders rose 50% on data-centre demand
  • Valuation is at the top of its own history. Forward price/earnings of 25.5x sits at the 85th percentile of its own range, and Bernstein calls 22.5x enterprise value to EBITDA priced for perfection
  • Evidence base is strong but stale on price. All six research families are on disk, but every valuation figure was struck between 1,098.60p and 1,289p in early June, 42.3% below the live price
  • Recommendation, medium conviction. Progress, because Triaging tests change forces rather than valuation, and Early-Stage Assessment is where the entry point belongs

Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
What it does. Rolls-Royce designs, manufactures and services aero engines, defence propulsion and industrial power systems, operating in 48 countries with customers in over a hundred
2.
Segment mix. Civil Aerospace is 52% of revenue, Defence 24% and Power Systems 24%, on 43,000 employees and an 88.1bn pound total order backlog
3.
Civil Aerospace shape. The division generated 10.38bn pounds in 2025, dominated by Large Engines at 76% and Business Aviation at 19%, with services 69% of divisional revenue
4.
Defence shape. Defence generated 4.77bn pounds in 2025 across Transport 31%, Combat 30%, Submarines 28%, Naval 7% and Helicopters 4%, on a 17.4bn pound backlog
5.
Power Systems shape. Power Systems contributed 4.89bn pounds in 2025 across Power Generation 54%, Governmental 25%, Marine 10%, Industrial 9% and Battery Energy Storage 2%
6.
How it earns. The civil model is razor-and-razorblade: original equipment sells at low or negative margin, and lifetime profit arrives through flight-hour-linked long-term service agreements
7.
Installed base. The company supports 14,263 in-service civil engines, powers four of five new-generation widebody platforms, and holds sole-source position on the Airbus A350 through 2030
8.
Financial character. FY25 underlying revenue was 20.06bn pounds at a 17.3% operating margin, free cash flow 3.27bn pounds, and the balance sheet held 1.9bn pounds of net cash
9.
Geography. North America was 29.8% of 2025 statutory revenue (calculated), Europe excluding the United Kingdom roughly 20.4% (calculated), the United Kingdom 14.6% (calculated) and Asia 4.38bn pounds
10.
Investment style implication. The name reads as a cyclical aftermarket franchise mid-way through a corporate-change earnings transformation, not as a steady compounder or a distressed turnaround
