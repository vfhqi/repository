Section 2 – Currency Composition of Revenues and Costs

Section summary
  1. Straumann Holding AG faces a significant structural mismatch because it generates under 2% of sales in Swiss francs while incurring up to 20% of its operating costs in its strong home currency.
  2. This exposure contrasts sharply with peers like Sonova, which also contend with heavy euro and US dollar exposures but employ different manufacturing localization and hedging strategies.

Geographic Revenue and Currency Mismatch

1.
Significant exposure: In evaluating the structural foreign currency dynamics of Swiss exporters, this analysis incorporates 12 distinct sources covering three companies and one comparable past case. Straumann Holding AG faces a chronic mismatch because its domestic market accounts for less than 2% of overall revenues, leaving it highly dependent on global demand. This imbalance exposes consolidated results to severe currency translation headwinds whenever the Swiss franc strengthens against major currencies. Consequently, tracking the precise distribution of global sales is essential to understand underlying organic revenue expansion and profitability.
2.
Balanced distribution: Straumann's global revenue is split evenly between the European and North American markets, which together represent the vast majority of sales. According to historical disclosures in the 2024 Annual Report, the euro accounted for 31% of revenue, while the North American dollar block represented 32%. The remaining sales are distributed across emerging markets, where currencies are traditionally much more volatile. This geographical diversification helps stabilize organic growth but increases exposure to translation headwinds.
3.
Expanding footprint: The Asia Pacific region has become a crucial growth engine for Straumann, driven primarily by rising dental implant demand in China. Company records from 2024 indicate that the Chinese Renminbi contributes approximately 10% of total group revenue. However, this segment is highly sensitive to government-led volume-based procurement policies that pressure pricing. Therefore, fluctuations in the Renminbi directly impact reported regional revenue and operating margins.
4.
Concentrated exposure: Latin America represents a smaller but rapidly growing portion of Straumann's business, with a heavy concentration in a single nation. Management reported in October 2026 that Brazil represents approximately 80% of total Latin American revenues. This high concentration means the Brazilian Real, which accounted for 6% of sales in 2024, dictates regional performance. As a result, macroeconomic volatility in Brazil directly impacts consolidated earnings per share.
