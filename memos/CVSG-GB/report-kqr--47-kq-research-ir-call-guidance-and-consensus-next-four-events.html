Context and Core Strategic Mandate

Section 2 – CVS Group Corporate Strategy, Formal Guidance, and Management

Commentary

Medium-Term Financial Targets

1.
Organic growth: CVS Group plc has re-committed to its medium-term financial targets first established at its November 2022 Capital Markets Day. The group aims to deliver organic revenue growth of between 4.0% and 8.0% per annum across its operations. This target is expected to support long-term earnings per share growth as the cohort of pets acquired during the pandemic ages and requires more advanced treatments.
2.
Consistent performance: CVS Group plc continues to target adjusted EBITDA margins of between 19.0% and 23.0% over the medium term. Chief Financial Officer Robin Alfonso noted that the group maintained a stable margin of 19.9% in the year ended 30 June 2026 despite wage pressures. This margin compares favorably with the typical 18.0% to 22.0% EBITDA margins reported across the Australian veterinary sector.
3.
Robust liquidity: CVS Group plc remains committed to maintaining its annual operating cash conversion above 70.0% to ensure robust liquidity. The company reported actual operating cash conversion of 70.6% for the year ended 30 June 2026, compared to 76.9% in the prior year. This cash generation provides the financial flexibility necessary to fund future earnings-accretive investments.
4.
Contrary evidence: CVS Group plc reported a like-for-like sales growth of only 2.1% for the financial year 2026. This performance represents a significant drop from historical pre-pandemic averages of 5.0% to 6.0% like-for-like growth. Such sluggish organic expansion threatens to drag down near-term adjusted earnings per share unless M&A activities can compensate.

Capital Allocation and Leverage Guidelines

5.
Disciplined investment: CVS Group plc has reduced its annual capital expenditure guidance to no more than £30.0m per annum going forward. This represents a downward revision from the previously committed Capital Markets Day framework of £30.0m to £50.0m. Chief Financial Officer Robin Alfonso explained that this reduction in capex will free up extra cash to support other strategic corporate initiatives.
6.
Manageable debt: CVS Group plc maintains a strict self-imposed leverage ceiling of 2.0x net debt to bank- test EBITDA. Net bank borrowings increased to £199.6m as of 30 June 2026, which pushed leverage up from 1.18x to 1.63x. This leverage headroom ensures that the company can execute its acquisition strategy without risking balance sheet stability.
7.
Strategic flexibility: CVS Group plc management has expressed willingness to temporarily exceed the 2.0x leverage ceiling if attractive acquisition opportunities present themselves. Chief Executive Officer Richard Fairman emphasized that this would only occur if there is a clear runway to quickly return leverage below the 2.0x threshold. This flexibility allows the company to pursue larger, high-quality acquisitions that could accelerate earnings per share growth.
