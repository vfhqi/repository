Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Maersk faces a massive earnings transition over the next three years as oversupply threatens container freight rates.
  2. Evaluating whether Maersk's demonstrated segment strengths can shield its profitability remains the defining investment debate.

Strategic context and segment risks

1.
Critical inquiry: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Severe pressure: Deteriorating market conditions are expected to significantly pressure Maersk's earnings per share as freight rates normalise over the next 18 months to 3 years. DNB Carnegie warns that box rates could converge on previous lows from 2016, causing a major cash burn and value deterioration for the foreseeable future. This downcycle represents a substantial threat to Maersk's valuation as the market transitions from supernormal returns to capital-destroying rates. Therefore, testing Maersk's demonstrated capabilities is crucial to determining the plausibility of expected earnings pathways.
3.
Negative outlook: Significant structural changes in Maersk's Ocean segment, which represented 65% of 2025 revenue, will directly dictate group profitability. Bernstein Research warns that the core container shipping business faces severe challenges from a towering global orderbook. While Maersk has pivoted back to fleet growth with a massive 500,000 TEU order, this risk-heavy expansion may compress earnings as capacity loosens. Consequently, Maersk's ocean cost advantages are the primary driver of future EPS volatility.
4.
Stretched targets: Ambitious growth targets in Maersk's Logistics & Services segment present a major strategic risk given historical execution challenges. Bernstein Research argues that management's combined target of 10% organic growth and a 6% EBIT margin is highly stretched. While structural growth is positive, achieving both targets would require a material acceleration in customer take-up. Consequently, failure to recover logistics margins represents a substantial threat to the group's long-term non-ocean earnings recovery.

Industry dynamics and resilience

5.
Resilient pricing: Maersk's Terminals segment continues to perform strongly, delivering robust revenue growth of 11% driven mainly by higher revenue per move. This high-margin performance is supported by strong pricing power as most global terminals are full. However, Terminals reorganized from April 2026 remains a much smaller business that cannot fully offset cyclical risks in Ocean. These dynamics mean that terminal earnings are a valuable but insufficient hedge against vessel oversupply.
6.
Cyclical threat: Deutsche Bank expects Maersk to benefit from elevated freight rates in the near term, forecasting a strong Q3 performance. However, potential oversupply in 2027, especially in the context of a Red Sea reopening, could heavily pressure rates. Deutsche Bank's forecasts project Maersk's EPS to fall sharply from USD 506.60 in 2026 to USD 82.87 in 2027. This dramatic contraction highlights how closely Maersk's earnings are tied to external macroeconomic and routing conditions.
7.
Contrary evidence: Strong near-term freight rate momentum continues to defy oversupply expectations, prompting Deutsche Bank to raise Maersk's 2026 EBITDA forecast to USD 14.4 billion. This persistent rate strength, driven by port congestion and active consumer demand, has delayed the expected market normalisation. If these disruptions remain entrenched, Maersk's near-term earnings will remain elevated.
8.
Other side of the story: While the upcoming vessel delivery peak suggests a prolonged downturn, Maersk's fortified balance sheet provides significant resilience.
9.
DNB Carnegie highlights that Maersk holds a strong cash position, with net interest-bearing debt at negative USD 1.5 billion in Q2 2026. This financial buffer, combined with Gemini's operational efficiency, could protect the group's margins during the cycle. Consequently, Maersk may navigate the supply wave with much smaller losses than peers.

Section 2 – Ocean Cost Competitiveness and Gemini Cooperation

Section summary
  1. Maersk is highly likely to hold an Ocean cost advantage through Gemini efficiency benefits tracking at USD 950 million annually.
  2. Our confidence is high because of Maersk's Q1 2026 unit cost reduction of 7.1% and Gemini's verified 90% schedule reliability.

Gemini network efficiency and savings

1.
Operational efficiency: Research identified 14 relevant sources, 6 companies, and 2 comparable past cases, using all of them to analyze Maersk's cost competitiveness. The company disclosed that its Ocean unit cost at fixed energy decreased by 7.1% year on year to USD 2,333 per FFE. This unit cost reduction demonstrates the immediate efficiency improvements of the modular network even under regional routing disruptions. This cost-saving trajectory could help shield future earnings per share from falling freight rates.
2.
Strategic benefits: Maersk successfully captured substantial operational synergies from its new partnership in the second quarter of 2026. Management reported that Gemini Cooperation benefits were tracking just above the upper end of its target range. According to Bernstein Research, Maersk targets annualized network EBIT benefits of USD 820 million to USD 1.1 billion through reduced port calls and high asset turn. These operational savings are projected to significantly bolster the carrier's profitability once capacity becomes loose.
3.
Collaborative savings: Hapag-Lloyd has intensified its own independent cost reduction initiatives alongside the Gemini launch. The German carrier is executing its Ventus cost saving programme targeting over USD 1.0 billion in cost reductions over an 18-month period. This massive cost-reduction program aims to restore margin resilience and offset inflationary pressures across its network operations. Consequently, both Gemini partners are entering the supply downturn with significantly leaner operating cost bases than their competitors.
4.
Network coordination: Industry experts indicate that Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd are focusing on tighter network coordination under the Gemini Cooperation. Partners are prioritizing fewer port calls and controlled capacity release rather than chasing pure volume growth. This disciplined approach has supported a 90% schedule reliability rate since launch, materially outperforming the industry average of 50% during peak Red Sea disruptions. Sustaining this operational discipline is critical to keeping vessel utilization high and keeping unit costs down.
5.
Customer value: Expert insights show that Gemini's high schedule reliability directly translates into cost reductions for its retail and industrial customers. Because customers can trust arrival times, they can lower their inventory buffers and strengthen their supply chain resilience. This value-add allows Gemini to build stickier relationships with high-margin cargo shippers during periods of loose capacity. Ultimately, these commercial strengths protect Maersk's long-term contracted volumes and preserve base revenue streams.

Competitor cost benchmarks and risks

6.
Hub bottleneck: Bernstein Research warns that Gemini's reliance on a hub-and-spoke system introduces significant operational risks. The network focuses heavy volumes onto key transshipment hubs, meaning a single failure at Tanjung Pelepas could trigger network-wide operational disruptions. During periods of severe port congestion, these bottlenecks could escalate handling costs and erase expected fuel efficiency gains. Consequently, Maersk's unit cost advantage remains highly vulnerable to external infrastructure constraints.
7.
Contrary evidence: Carnegie research suggests that the expected financial upside from the Gemini alliance is largely unproven and could be countered by severe market downturns. The analyst argues that clients are unlikely to pay a premium for high reliability when global capacity is abundant. Furthermore, competitors will naturally see their own schedule reliability improve as excess vessel capacity reduces port congestion. This indicates that Maersk's relative cost and reliability advantages may quickly evaporate once capacity becomes loose.
8.
Scale advantages: MSC maintains a formidable structural cost advantage that presents a severe threat to Maersk's market share. The private carrier controls 28% of the global container fleet above 15,000 TEU, which reduces unit costs by over 15% compared to standard 10,000 TEU vessels. Carnegie research indicates that this massive fleet expansion could trigger intense price competition as MSC seeks to maximize vessel utilization. This scale differential means MSC can remain highly profitable while other carriers suffer.
9.
Competitor margins: COSCO Shipping Holdings has seen its operating costs rise despite solid volume growth in its international routes. During the first half of 2026, COSCO reported that its unit cost rose 6.9% year on year to USD 934 per TEU due to route adjustments and elevated fuel prices. Huatai Research warns that weak cargo demand coupled with a rising cost base presents a significant profit hurdle as freight rates normalize. This cost escalation suggests that non-allied carriers may struggle to match the structural opex reductions achieved by Gemini.
10.
Leveraged competitors: Bernstein Research notes that smaller carriers operate with highly vulnerable, high-cost capital structures compared to Maersk's net cash position. For example, ZIM holds a net debt balance of USD 3.7 billion, reflecting its charter-heavy business model and higher lease obligations. While Maersk can absorb falling freight rates due to its robust balance sheet, ZIM's high fixed charter costs will likely accelerate its margin erosion during a downturn. This financial disparity suggests Maersk will maintain a far more resilient cost position than highly leveraged peers.

Alliance history and integrated models

11.
Strategic integration: CMA CGM has pursued deep operational integration with its logistics arm to counter structural shipping imbalances. The French liner managed to lower its operating expenses per TEU to USD 951 in Q1 2020 through swift blank sailings and slot adjustments. This integrated approach helped protect its container shipping margins during the initial demand shocks of the pandemic. However, Deutsche Bank Research highlights that CMA CGM remains highly exposed to severe industry volatility and bunker cost fluctuations.
12.
Historical alliances: Past shipping consolidations show that joint operational agreements often trigger intense price wars rather than stability. The proposed P3 Network alliance between Maersk, MSC, and CMA CGM in 2014 aimed to maximize vessel deployment efficiency and lower operating costs. However, because this efficiency increased effective capacity, it incentivized carriers to engage in aggressive price competition to fill larger vessels. This historical case indicates that Gemini's cost efficiencies could inadvertently accelerate industry-wide rate erosion.
13.
Regional peers: Ocean Network Express has shown significant profitability volatility that reflects the cyclical nature of unallied container operations. Morgan Stanley's tracking of container earnings reveals that ONE's EBIT is projected to fall into negative territory during the upcoming vessel delivery peak. Unlike ONE, Maersk's integrated model combines Ocean operations with APM Terminals and logistics to stabilize group cash flows. Morgan Stanley's forecasts suggest that diversified revenue streams will help Maersk navigate the industry downturn better than pure-play container lines.
14.
Other side of the story: Jefferies research indicates that Gemini's unique operational structure provides strong competitive advantages that could protect Maersk's earnings during a downturn. The Gemini alliance has successfully increased schedule reliability to above 90% in 2026, materially outperforming historical industry levels of 60%. This superior reliability allows Maersk to target high-value cargo segments like copper and reefer products, which support premium contract pricing. Consequently, these structural efficiency gains could keep Maersk's Ocean earnings above peers' even as capacity loosens.

Section 3 – Cape-of-Good-Hope Routing, Surcharges, and Suez Canal Returns

Section summary
  1. Maersk faces declining network expenses as its core ocean services transition back from the Cape of Good Hope to the Suez Canal.
  2. Surcharge revenues are highly likely to fall faster than opex savings based on historical rate normalisation precedents.

Added network costs from Cape routing

1.
Rapid opex inflation: DNB Carnegie reported on 8 May 2026 that Maersk's Q2 outlook improved because of the company's ability to pass on USD 500 million in added monthly costs mainly relating to higher fuel prices. These massive network expenses stem from routing vessels around the Cape of Good Hope which adds significant mileage and opex. This cost burden directly threatens Maersk's earnings per share unless surcharges fully offset the extra fuel burn.
2.
Persistent energy overheads: Bernstein Research reported on 9 October 2026 that Maersk continues to operate against a backdrop of elevated opex with bunker prices having risen 44% year on year in Q2 2026. This dramatic surge in fuel prices has severely inflated the daily cost of keeping the active fleet on longer routes around Africa. Shifting services back to the Suez Canal will sharply reduce fuel consumption and lower opex, which should support future earnings per share as spot rates soften.
3.
Escalating vessel expenses: Jefferies Research reported on 22 October 2026 that simultaneous routing disruptions around Suez, Panama, and Hormuz have created meaningful upside risk to shipping opex. Rerouting container vessels around Africa adds roughly 3,500 nautical miles and over USD 1.0 million in fuel opex per voyage., the industry has experienced significant cost pressure that can only be resolved by returning to Suez.

Surcharge dynamics and historical velocity

4.
Fast rate adjustments: Morgan Stanley reported on 24 March 2026 that fuel surcharges and bunker adjustment factors can move fairly instantaneously to recover added opex. Shippers like Maersk explicitly publish tariff updates and issue emergency surcharges to preserve margin parity during network disruptions. However, these surcharges represent a short-term hedge rather than a structural advantage once market routing normalises.
5.
Sudden revenue declines: Historically, Maersk saw its surcharge revenue contract rapidly during previous periods of security normalisation. When war-risk surcharges were cut 67% in January and 80% in February 2025, surcharge revenues collapsed much faster than the underlying operational network costs could fall. This historical precedent indicates that Maersk's ocean margins will face immediate compression once the Suez Canal route fully reopens.
6.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reports that receding fuel costs are currently widening carrier margins because spot freight rates remain supported by strong demand. Fuel costs represented roughly 20% of Maersk's loaded freight rate over recent quarters, and the rapid implementation of surcharges preserved group profitability even as bunker opex began to decline. This suggests that surcharge revenues do not always fall faster than costs, especially when tight capacity supports high base freight rates.

Suez normalisation and Gemini transitions

7.
Steady service restoration: Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd announced on 14 September 2026 that they are shifting four additional Gemini services to Suez. The AE5, AE11, AE12, and ME2 services joined the AE15 and AE19 services, bringing the total number of trans-Suez Gemini services to six by September 2026. This phased return is designed to optimize transit times and lower network opex.
8.
Surging vessel transits: The Suez Canal Authority reported that Maersk-affiliated transits completed 276 transits from January to September 2026. This represents an 89% increase compared to 146 transits recorded during the same period in 2025, reflecting accelerated route normalisation. This rising transit frequency confirms that a significant portion of Maersk's fleet is already capturing shorter route opex savings.
9.
Upcoming capacity release: Maersk expects all of its shipping services to return to the Suez Canal by March 2027 security permitting. According to the Suez Canal Authority, this transition will deliver measurable cost advantages over the Cape of Good Hope, particularly for ultra-large container vessels. However, Maersk's chief operating officer Rabab Boulos emphasizes that individual service timelines remain subject to regional security conditions.
10.
Severe rate pressure: Morgan Stanley reported on 4 September 2026 that the gradual resumption of Suez transits will release a meaningful amount of supply. Because Cape of Good Hope diversions previously absorbed about 7% of global fleet capacity, routing normalisation will act as a major deflationary catalyst. This influx of capacity will likely accelerate the decline of freight rates just as vessel deliveries peak.
11.
Parallel peer actions: CMA CGM was the first alliance-operated carrier to resume crossings, although it subsequently rerouted several services due to highly volatile security conditions. Hapag-Lloyd has also confirmed that its Gemini SE1 service will resume transiting the Suez Canal and Red Sea in late October 2026. This collective return by major alliances is poised to intensify competition and downward rate pressure across the entire Asia-Europe trade lane.
12.
Ongoing security constraints: Expert insights from maritime managers suggest that while Maersk is gradually restoring Suez transits, security risks remain highly volatile. Shipping lines continue to face elevated warrisk insurance premiums and must rely on naval escorts to secure their transits. Consequently, any sudden escalation of hostiles in the Red Sea could force carriers to immediately revert to the longer Cape route.
13.
Other side of the story: While the Suez Canal reopening will release massive container capacity and depress spot rates, Maersk's Gemini alliance is uniquely positioned to capture opex savings. The modular hub-andspoke network allows Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd to run fewer mainliner port calls and maintain a 90% reliability rate. These network efficiencies should keep Maersk's unit opex significantly lower than unallied peers during the upcoming rate correction.

Section 4 – Historical Ocean Performance and Break-Even Analysis

Section summary
  1. Historical shipping downturns demonstrate that Maersk experiences severe earnings contraction when average loaded freight rates fall below unit operating costs.
  2. Modeling indicates Maersk carries an all-in breakeven of approximately USD 1,100 per TEU, holding a clear cost advantage over high-cost competitors like ZIM.

Historical shipping downturn benchmarks

1.
Challenging history: Research identified 12 relevant sources, 5 shipping companies, and 3 historical cyclical downturns to evaluate Maersk's ocean profitability. During the 2016 industry contraction, freight rates declined by 19% to historical lows and pressured carrier earnings. This previous case highlights that structural oversupply quickly pushes average rates below vessel operating costs. Consequently, understanding historical floor rates is critical to projecting future shipping losses.
2.
Peer comparison: COSCO Shipping Holdings demonstrated significant resilience during past downcycles due to state backing and aggressive cost control. However, during the first half of 2026, COSCO's international unit costs rose by 6.9% year on year to USD 934 per TEU. This cost inflation illustrates that even highly competitive state carriers are vulnerable to rising network expenses.
3.
Moderate recovery: Maersk achieved a small positive EBIT of USD 259 million in the 2019 fiscal year despite persistent freight rate headwinds. The average loaded rate during this period stood at USD 1,883 per FFE. However, the fixed bunker unit cost was kept at USD 1,777 per FFE which preserved a positive margin. These metrics demonstrate that careful capacity management can preserve marginal shipping profitability during weak cycles.
4.
Deteriorating returns: The company experienced a sharp contraction in earnings during the 2023 transitional year as the pandemic boom completely normalised. Maersk reported an Ocean EBIT of USD 2.2 billion with a loaded rate of USD 2,313 per FFE and a unit cost of USD 2,371 per FFE. This cost overshoot shows that sticky opex structures can threaten profitability when rates collapse.

Recent quarterly rate compression

5.
Severe losses: Ocean profitability turned negative in the fourth quarter of 2023 with an EBIT loss of USD 920 million. During this quarter, the average loaded freight rate declined to USD 1,925 per FFE. Furthermore, unit costs stood at USD 2,280 per FFE as capacity mitigation measures remained low. This severe mismatch illustrates how rapidly rate erosion destabilises earnings during capacity surges.
6.
Negative shift: Maersk recorded an Ocean EBIT of negative USD 153 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 as freight rate pressure intensified. The average loaded rate fell to USD 2,046 per FFE while the unit cost at fixed bunker remained high at USD 2,333 per FFE. This sequential deterioration proves that Maersk is operating below its true breakeven under loose market capacity.

Persistent deficits: The carrier reported an Ocean EBIT loss of USD 192 million in the first quarter of 2026 due to ongoing rate pressure. Cost progression: ZIM's all-in breakeven was USD 1,510 per TEU in 2024 but improved to USD 1,390 per TEU in 2025 as new low-cost vessels delivered. Jefferies Research expects ZIM's breakeven to drop further to USD 1,330 per TEU in 2026. This structural improvement shows that aggressive fleet renewal can lower breakeven costs for high-cost lines.

Breakeven modeling and thresholds

9.
Determined threshold: Modeling of recent financial results indicates that Maersk's current Ocean break-even rate is approximately USD 2,200 to USD 2,300 per FFE. This estimate aligns with historical fixed bunker unit costs. It is also supported by broker estimates placing Maersk's all-in unit opex at USD 1,100 per TEU. Maintaining pricing above this threshold is vital for Maersk to avoid sustained capital destruction during the downcycle.
10.
Elevated cost: ZIM Integrated Shipping Services operates a high-cost platform with an estimated all-in free cash flow breakeven of USD 1,525 per TEU in 2025. While this is projected to fall to USD 1,425 per TEU in 2026, it remains significantly above Maersk's cost base of USD 1,100 per TEU. This cost disparity suggests that ZIM will burn cash faster than Maersk during rate downturns.
11.
Liner performance: HMM has experienced significant share price outperformance compared to peers due to domestic sentiment and retail support in Korea. During recent rate inflections, HMM's stock price surged by 39.6% while Maersk rose 14.4% and Hapag-Lloyd fell 1.9%. This market performance demonstrates that investor sentiment can diverge from fundamental opex risks during periods of freight volatility.
12.
Financial leverage: ZIM's charter-heavy model creates high fixed opex that escalates cash burn risk during prolonged container downcycles. Jefferies Research estimates that ZIM faces a potential cash burn of USD 370 million in 2026 if Red Sea diversions cease. This massive risk highlights why Maersk's net cash position provides a far superior margin of safety.

Peer cost comparisons and outlook

13.
Competitive pressure: Listed Asian carriers like Yang Ming face varying route exposure and margin pressures due to distinct charter structures. Yang Ming's route breakdown in the first half of 2026 showed Europe representing 39% of revenue and the Americas representing 36%. This heavy exposure to mainlanes makes Yang Ming highly vulnerable to spot rate declines as capacity loosens.
14.
Contrary evidence: Strong near-term freight rate momentum in 2026 has temporarily pushed spot rates well above leading liners' breakeven thresholds. Jefferies Research reported on 8 January 2025 that Transpacific spot rates remained supported between USD 5,000 and USD 6,500 per FEU, which is far above breakeven levels. This suggests that sustained operational losses may be delayed if current supply disruptions persist.
15.
Operational discipline: Expert insights suggest that larger container carriers are increasingly prioritizing breakeven performance over market share expansion during downturns. Shipping lines are now more willing to accept volume losses or execute blank sailings to protect rate floors. This shift in commercial strategy could prevent rates from falling to the catastrophic lows seen in past cycles.
16.
Other side of the story: Although overcapacity looms, Maersk's newly formed Gemini Cooperation is expected to lower unit costs and protect margins better than pure-play peers. Carnegie research estimates that Gemini's structural efficiency and optimized asset turn will significantly lower network opex in 2026. These network benefits could enable Maersk to maintain positive cash flows even if freight rates fall below general industry breakeven levels.

Section 5 – Logistics & Services Reorganisation and Margin Targets

Section summary
  1. Maersk's reorganisation of Logistics & Services faces significant hurdles as Solutions' EBIT margin eroded to 1.7% in Q2 2026.
  2. Our confidence is medium because while Landside and Forwarding remain strong, Solutions lacks the operational track record of peers like DSV.

Segment reorganisation and financial targets

1.
Structural realignment: Maersk reorganised its Logistics and Services segment effective April 1, 2026, into three dedicated operating divisions named Forwarding, Solutions, and Landside. This strategic shift aims to improve operational transparency and make it easier to benchmark Maersk's performance against assetlight peers. Research identified 11 relevant sources, 4 comparable companies, and 2 past cases to evaluate this structural transition. Under this structure, Forwarding manages freight activities while Solutions handles integrated contract logistics and distribution.
2.
Disappointing execution: Historical financial data shows that Maersk has consistently failed to meet its long-term Logistics and Services performance benchmarks. During 2025, the segment's last twelve months organic revenue growth reached only 3.2% against its stated target of 10%. Additionally, its last twelve months EBIT margin of 4.6% fell short of the company's 6% profitability target. These persistent deficits indicate that the division has historically struggled to convert revenue into meaningful operational profits.
3.
Stretched targets: Bernstein Research reported on 14 August 2026 that Maersk's combined growth and margin targets look increasingly unrealistic. The analyst argues that achieving 10% organic growth would require a material acceleration in customer take-up that is not supported by current trends. Furthermore, Bernstein Research disagrees that material commercial synergies exist between Ocean shipping and asset-light logistics services. Consequently, the broker projects that Maersk will fail to hit both targets simultaneously over the next three years.

Solutions margin erosion and operational bottlenecks

4.
Severe contraction: Maersk Solutions experienced substantial profitability erosion in the second quarter of 2026, highlighting integration and operational challenges. The division's EBIT margin collapsed to 1.7% from 3.8% YoY in the prior-year period. According to Maersk's interim filings, this decline was driven by weak profitability in the China lifestyle vertical and elevated startup costs for new warehouses. This margin deterioration demonstrates the high operational risks associated with scaling contract logistics.
5.
Excessive whitespace: Management's attempts to expand warehouse capacity have been hindered by underutilized space and slow pipeline conversion. Maersk acknowledged that Solutions' profitability was dragged down by persistent whitespace across its newly opened facilities. To address this issue, the company is focusing on rationalizing its warehouse footprint and accelerating sales pipeline conversion to fill space by year-end. Unless utilization improves rapidly, these fixed lease costs will continue to depress segment margins.
6.
Operational hurdles: Expert insights indicate that Maersk's integrated logistics strategy is hindered by complex administration and fragmented systems. A former global head at Maersk stated on 11 August 2026 that the company must successfully standardize its warehouse management systems to drive efficiency. Additionally, a former ground freight executive noted that Maersk needs to bring in experienced logistics managers who understand asset-light forwarding paces. These operational challenges suggest that margin recovery will require significant time and technological investment.

Asset-light peer benchmarks and precedents

7.
Superior efficiency: Peer forwarder DSV demonstrated far greater contract logistics resilience, highlighting the gap between Maersk and best-in-class operators. During the second quarter of 2026, DSV's contract logistics division recorded a stellar conversion ratio of 26.1%, up from 15.6% in the prior year. Morgan Stanley reported on 22 July 2026 that DSV's performance was powered by the high-growth technology and data-center vertical in North America. This comparison indicates that specialized asset-light forwarders possess structural advantages that Maersk currently lacks.
8.
Mitigating pressure: Kuehne and Nagel has responded to volume challenges with aggressive cost cutting to protect its profit margins. The company downgraded its gross profit yield targets but remains highly resilient due to its flexible asset-light operating model. Morgan Stanley reported on 23 July 2026 that Kuehne and Nagel expects its conversion margin to reach 15% in 2025 and 16% by 2027. This disciplined cost management shows how established players can defend profitability during market downturns.
9.
Integration precedents: Peer operator CEVA Logistics illustrates the long timeline and integration difficulties associated with contract logistics expansions. Expert insights from a former CEVA executive on 18 August 2026 show that managing large-scale warehouse networks requires deep operational expertise and complex labor management automation. Historically, CEVA struggled to lift margins quickly after expanding its footprint due to sticky start-up costs. This precedent suggests that Maersk's Solutions margin recovery will likely face prolonged headwinds before reaching steady-state levels.
10.
Contrary evidence: Carnegie research suggests that Maersk's expansion into land-based logistics could successfully insulate the company from shipping cyclicality over the long term. DNB Carnegie reported on 9 July 2026 that growing land-based logistics exposure seeks to limit sensitivity to volatile ocean rates. This strategy aims to capture a larger share of customer wallets and build stable relationships. Consequently, this diversification could protect Maersk's group earnings from severe cash drains during ocean downturns.
11.
Other side of the story: While Maersk targets a quick recovery in Solutions, Bernstein Research argues that a 6% EBIT margin is above best-in-class operator levels for most logistics products. The broker expects Maersk to struggle with growth and margin targets because of commercial dis-synergies as customers compete directly with its forwarding arm. Consequently, Maersk's integrated logistics model remains highly risky and is unlikely to offset core ocean segment losses during the upcoming downturn.

Section 6 – APM Terminals Revenue Resilience and Congestion Income

Section summary
  1. APM Terminals demonstrates strong revenue resilience during liner downturns supported by tariff pricing power.
  2. High-margin storage and congestion-driven fees remain major drivers of recent revenue- per-move growth.

Historical resilience in liner downturns

1.
Severe contraction: APM Terminals faced heavy profitability pressure during the severe container shipping downturn of 2016 as global overcapacity mounted. According to Jefferies Research on 9 January 2017, the operator saw its EBIT per TEU drop 33% year on year as pricing collapsed. This pressure cut the segment's return on invested capital nearly in half to 6.3% during the fiscal year. Consequently, historical precedents indicate that terminal returns are not completely immune to deep liner recessions.
2.
Steady expansion: The terminals segment achieved solid revenue-per-move recovery in 2019 despite persistent headwinds in the broader container shipping market. Maersk's 2019 annual report noted that consolidated revenue per move rose 4.3% to USD 263 from USD 252 in the previous year. This improvement was driven by higher storage income in West Africa and Latin America alongside volume growth in gateway terminals. Therefore, terminal earnings can expand during moderate shipping slowdowns through active pricing adjustments.
3.
Robust returns: The division maintained highly attractive financial performance during the transitional market normalisation of 2023. Maersk reported that APM Terminals delivered a return on invested capital of 10.5% in 2023, exceeding its mid-term target of 9.0%. This performance was supported by strong tariff execution and disciplined cost management across its gateway portfolio. Ultimately, this record demonstrates the structural resilience of terminal cash flows relative to volatile ocean operations.

Consequently, Maersk proved its ability to swiftly align capacity with demand during cyclical crises.
