Section 3 – New Renewables Execution and Project Commissioning Track Record

Section summary
  1. Verbund faces severe resource and meteorological constraints that challenge its ambitious 2030 non- hydro renewable expansion targets.
  2. Technical delays at Alpine pumped-storage assets and permitting slippage in Spain underscore the operational execution risks.

Renewable Target Feasibility

1.
Targeted capacity expansion: Verbund aims to expand its wind and solar generation capacity to reach 4.8 GW by 2030, striving for 25% of its total generation from these sources. However, this expansion requires accelerating commissioning schedules across multiple European markets to pivot away from pure hydro dependence. Achieving this goal is critical because non-hydro assets are expected to diversify Verbund's volatile weather risk and stabilize long-term EPS.
2.
Depressed wind resource: Verbund reported a disappointing new renewables coefficient of 0.82 for the 2025 financial year, which fell 18 percentage points below its planned baseline. Low wind speeds and weak solar radiation across its regional assets caused total wind and solar generation to decline by 111 GWh year-on-year to 2,153 GWh. These persistent resource deficits mean that even with capacity additions, lower-than-planned generation volumes drag on earnings performance.
3.
Deteriorating weather yields: Verbund experienced continued meteorological headwinds in its wind and solar operations throughout the first nine months of 2026. The new renewables coefficient declined further to 0.80 for the 9M 2026 period, representing a deficit of 20 percentage points against the company's planned value. Unless regional weather patterns dramatically shift, these poor resource yields will continue to suppress the segment's expected cash contributions and delay financial payback.
4.
Contrary evidence: Verbund successfully increased its operational wind and solar capacity by 19 MW during the third quarter of 2026. This marginal capacity growth was driven by completing and commissioning new solar installations in Germany and Italy. This progress demonstrates that Verbund can execute smaller-scale international projects despite the broader supply chain and regulatory challenges facing larger portfolios.

Section 5 – Regulatory Surcharges, Windfall Levies, and Capital Retention

Section summary
  1. High confidence exists that windfall levies will drag on Verbund's earnings through 2030 based on legislative extensions, while peer precedents demonstrate immediate post-levy earnings recovery.

Austrian Windfall Levy Mechanics

1.
Extended fiscal burden: Verbund faces a prolonged regulatory overhang following the Austrian coalition's decision in March 2025 to extend the energy crisis contribution windfall tax (EKB-S) on electricity generation until 2030. This extension aims to raise €200 mm annually from the domestic energy sector to address national budget deficits, setting a base revenue cap of €90/MWh for existing power plants. These regulatory interventions are projected to depress Verbund's net income recovery over the multi-year period by capping its generation upside.
2.
Elevated threshold offsets: Verbund can partially mitigate the EKB-S tax impact through an offset mechanism that rewards investments in renewable energy and energy efficiency. Historically under the initial December 2022 framework, which capped inframarginal revenues first at €140/MWh and then €120/MWh, generators could deduct up to €36/MWh for such qualified investments. These provisions allow Verbund to effectively raise its implied tax-free revenue threshold to a range of €103 to €110/MWh.
3.
Increasing levy guidance: Verbund reported a substantial windfall tax liability of €135.90 mm for the 2025 financial year, which exceeded its initial expectations. For 2026, the company initially guided to an energy contribution of €200 mm, but later faced announcements that the Austrian government intended to double the sector-wide levy target to €400 mm. Assuming Verbund maintains its historical 40% share of this sector tax, its direct levy contribution would escalate to €150 mm annually, squeezing group cash flows.
4.
Contrary evidence: Potential legal victories in the Austrian Constitutional Court could entirely eliminate Verbund's future windfall tax liabilities. If the court rules the retroactive tax extensions unconstitutional, Verbund's cumulative earnings could see an immediate upward revision. This legal outcome would swiftly remove the fiscal overhang and restore full earnings capacity to the company's run-of-river generation assets.

State Demands and Corporate Precedents

5.
Sovereign cash extraction: The Republic of Austria, as a 51% majority owner, increasingly relies on state- controlled companies like Verbund to repair its strained federal budget. This persistent fiscal pressure suggests Verbund could face a windfall tax of approximately €100 mm per year out to 2030 at the EBITDA level. Although partly tax-deductible, this recurring levy is expected to create a steady cash drag on Verbund's corporate earnings.
6.
Peer regulatory insulation: Austrian peer EVN AG experienced a much smaller direct impact from the national windfall tax than Verbund due to its different generation mix. EVN AG reported that the national EKB-S levy resulted in expenses of only EUR 9.80 mm for the 2023/24 financial year, down from EUR 31.20 mm in 2022/23. This minimal impact occurs because the Austrian tax is highly generation-related, leaving retail-heavy utilities largely insulated from windfall surcharges compared to hydro-heavy operators.
7.
Divided state ownership: EVN AG operates under a complex state-ownership structure, with Lower Austria and the city of Vienna holding major stakes through a joint venture. Despite these state ties, EVN AG’s 2025/26 financial results benefited from a higher Verbund dividend of EUR 138 mm, up from EUR 123 mm in the prior year. This dependency highlights how regional state entities indirectly capture Verbund's cash flows through shareholding chains.

Section 8 – What Is Known and What Is Not

The confidence mapping below follows each part of the Key Question: how plausible the expected change is, given Verbund's record of delivery and the strengths it demonstrably has. Overall, the question can be answered partly. The historical record is well documented, but the three variables that decide the outcome are still open.

1.
Hydrology and earnings recovery: high confidence on the damage, low confidence on timing. The damage is clear: the run-of-river coefficient was 0.61 for the first nine months of 2026, the lowest level recorded in approximately 100 years, and EBITDA fell 29% year-on-year. The recovery path is much less certain. Soil moisture and snowpack remain depleted, and the 3Q22 drought needed five consecutive quarters to normalize. Against this, one source cites rainfall at 260% of the seasonal average sparking rapid reservoir replenishment, but that comes from related catchments, so its transferability to Verbund is unproven.
2.
New renewables execution: moderate-to-high confidence that the record is weak. The 2025 coefficient was 0.82 and 0.80 for 9M 2026. Limberg III and Limberg I together cost €40 million to €60 million, and €200 mm to €220 mm of solar capex slipped from 2025 into 2026. What remains unknown is whether the Spanish 1.1 GW pipeline will clear permitting on schedule.
3.
Regulated grid expansion: moderate confidence on the target, low on deliverability. The 14% annual RAB growth target is a Bernstein figure from June 2026. Meanwhile, extra-high-voltage units ordered today are scheduled for delivery only in 2030 to 2031, although a Siemens Energy project manager said lead times are less severe than a year ago. & Securities Levies and capital retention: high confidence on mechanics, low on outcome. The EKB-S has been extended to 2030, with an implied tax-free threshold of €103 to €110/MWh. The Constitutional Court challenge is a major swing factor.
4.
Funding and hedging: moderate confidence. Net debt is around €4 bn against a 3.0x ceiling. The funding levers are plausible but politically uncertain: the CEO has no immediate plan to sell the grid, though up to 49% of APG could theoretically be sold. On hedging, buyback losses reached €470 mm gross year-to-date by late 2026, but the final full-year cost is not yet visible. Three gaps matter most. The first is actual soil moisture and groundwater replenishment. Low soil moisture means normal precipitation will not translate into normal river flows, so rainfall totals alone cannot settle the question. Observed Alpine catchment soil moisture and river discharge through the 2026/27 autumn-to-spring season, tracked against run-of-river output, would resolve it. A first-half 2027 coefficient near normal would validate the optimistic case. The second gap is the final Constitutional Court ruling on the windfall levy. A favorable ruling could be worth about €0.9 per share, but no judgment is evidenced in the research. Only a published judgment, including whether it covers the 2030 extension and any retroactive elements, would resolve it. Its effect would also depend on whether the sector-wide levy target is doubled to €400 mm. The third gap is vendor transformer delivery agreements. The evidence is sector-wide lead times, not Verbund's or APG's contracts, and the claim that large utilities lock in manufacturer capacity through long-term procurement agreements is asserted rather than documented for APG. Disclosure of delivery slots, framework agreements, or APG's commissioning schedule would resolve it. Smaller gaps also remain. One is whether the Austrian owner would accept a lower payout, given nine-month capex outflows of EUR 1,121.30 mm against dividends of EUR 1,295.70 mm. Another is the full-year hedging buyback cost. Broker evidence is also uneven: Deutsche Bank projects a 15% downside to consensus 2026 earnings, while other views assume faster normalization. The conclusion is therefore conditional. Verbund's strengths, its hydro fleet and regulated grid, are real, but the change expected of it relies on external variables that this evidence base cannot yet settle.
