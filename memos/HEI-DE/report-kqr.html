Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The rising carbon costs will force uneconomic European cement plant closures, driving industry consolidation that supports elevated pricing power and boosts earnings per share for market leaders.

The Key Question

1.
Investigating consolidation: "Which European cement plants, especially those of unlisted independent producers in Germany, Italy, Spain and Central Europe, do brokers and experts expect to close or be sold by 2030 because their carbon costs make them uneconomic, and how much capacity is that in each country? In local markets where a competitor has closed or been bought since 2023, how have cement and aggregates prices and the leaders' margins changed? What purchases are the listed groups (Heidelberg Materials, Vicat, Cementos Molins, SigmaRoc, Breedon, Buzzi, Cementir) expected to make next, at what multiples of operating earnings, and how often have competition authorities forced sales or blocked deals?"

Defining Industry Significance

2.
Elevated carbon costs: The phase-out of free ETS allowances will heavily penalize high-emission independent plants, which means the competitive landscape will shift toward decarbonized leaders. According to Bernstein Research's report on 16 July 2026, the cost curve implies that by 2030, the most carbon-efficient plants will enjoy up to 30% lower costs than the most carbon-intensive installations, which compares to a 60% advantage by 2039. Consequently, this massive divergence will drive significant European capacity rationalization and raise long-term earnings per share.
3.
Structural pricing power: Industry consolidation is shifting from localized bolt-on deals to a structural, region-wide reduction in supply that supports long-term pricing discipline. Bernstein Research argues on 26 August 2026 that pricing power in cement is determined by industry concentration rather than demand-supply balances, which means Holcim's exit from fragmented markets has left it with best-in-class pricing. Therefore, as carbon regulations tighten through 2030, further capacity rationalization will enhance structural pricing power and solidify margins.

Thus, this pricing momentum will protect the earnings per share of leaders like Heidelberg Materials as free allocations decline.

Consolidation capacity: Fragmented European markets present significant consolidation opportunities for highly cash-generative listed cement majors with ample balance sheet capacity. This implies that significant financial optionality exists for companies like Buzzi, which holds close to 1 billion euros in net cash to fund accretive acquisitions.

Section 2 – Uneconomic Cement Plants and Carbon Costs by 2030

Section summary
  1. Rising carbon costs under the EU ETS will force substantial capacity rationalization by 2030 as unlisted independent cement plants face escalating compliance liabilities.
  2. Unlisted operators in fragmented markets like Germany and Italy are highly vulnerable because they lack the capital to invest in carbon capture.
  3. Major listed producers maintain a strong competitive advantage through superior carbon efficiency and extensive alternative fuel substitution rates.

Cost Curve Pressures and Capacity Rationalization

1.
Escalating carbon liabilities: The progressive withdrawal of free carbon allocations will heavily penalize high-emission installations across Europe. Bernstein Research argues on 16 July 2026 that the carbon cost curve will create a massive 30% cost divergence between the most and least efficient plants by 2030. Consequently, this cost escalation will make marginal facilities highly unprofitable, which means that significant capacity rationalization is inevitable across the continent. Therefore, these cost pressures will structurally alter industry supply and ultimately support the pricing power of surviving players.
2.
Clinker capacity decommissioning: Regulatory tightening is expected to drive a massive wave of plant closures or conversions before the end of the decade. Bernstein Research projected on 13 January 2026 that approximately 30% of total European clinker capacity will be decommissioned or converted to grinding stations by 2030. This structural supply reduction compares to historical overcapacity where average utilization rates hovered around 60% to 65% in recent years. Thus, the exit of inefficient kilns will substantially improve plant utilization rates and boost mid-term earnings per share for consolidated majors.
3.
EBIT margin compression: Marginal cement producers face severe financial distress as they reach a critical threshold on the cost curve. Bernstein Research expects that the worst three deciles of European cement plants will enter negative operating margin territory by 2030. This profitability collapse occurs because smaller, unhedged players cannot pass through more than 10% price increases to their customer base. Consequently, these uneconomic operations will be forced to shut down unless carbon costs are miraculously mitigated.

Unlisted Independent Producers and Country Exposure

4.
German market fragmentation: The highly fragmented German cement market faces disproportionate pressure due to its high concentration of unlisted independent operators. Jefferies Research reported on 14 July 2026 that Germany remains highly fragmented with 16 competitors operating 47 integrated plants. This fragmented structure means that unlisted independent players, who lack the financial scale of listed majors, control about 30% of local supply. Therefore, escalating ETS costs will likely drive independent capacity out of the German market, which will significantly consolidate local market shares.
5.
Italian production vulnerability: Unlisted independent producers in Italy face immediate economic threats as their carbon deficits widen. The Italian producer Colacem reported a national production of 4.3 million tonnes of cement in 2025 across its six integrated plants. While Colacem has invested heavily in environmental monitoring, unlisted operators generally lack the capital to implement industrial-scale decarbonization. This lack of investment capacity means that independent Italian capacity remains at risk of closure by 2030.
6.
Spanish consolidation dynamics: The Spanish cement market is undergoing rapid consolidation as independent operators seek shelter under larger groups. The Spanish listed group Cementos Molins accelerated its European diversification by fully consolidating the operations of Secil from the second quarter of 2026. This acquisition follows its purchase of a 90% stake in the precast concrete specialist Zenet in January 2026. Consequently, these transactions show that independent assets are being absorbed by capitalized players to hedge against rising environmental compliance costs.

Relative Carbon Efficiency of Listed Players

7.
Decarbonization frontrunner status: Heidelberg Materials is aggressively restructuring its European production network to optimize its carbon footprint. In its half-year results on 14 August 2026, Heidelberg Materials disclosed that its Wave 1 strategy successfully cut 13% of its clinker capacity between 2022 and 2025. The company is now executing its Wave 2 masterplan, which aims to permanently close five additional plants by 2030. This network optimization will insulate Heidelberg's earnings per share by concentrating production in its most carbon-efficient installations.
8.
Alternative fuel leadership: High thermal substitution rates allow major listed groups to insulate themselves from volatile fossil fuel and carbon costs. Holcim announced on 31 July 2026 that its alternative fuel substitution rate in Europe reached 70% in the first half of 2026. This compares to its global thermal substitution target of 50% by 2030. 38 & Co Redburn Thus, Holcim's extensive alternative fuel utilization significantly lowers its Scope 1 intensity and protects its operating margins from carbon inflation.
9.
Buzzi efficiency lag: While some peers have clear decarbonization pathways, other listed players are taking a more conservative approach. Jefferies Research notes that Buzzi Unicem's direct carbon emissions of 551 kilograms per tonne of cement in 2025 sits in the middle of its peer group. Furthermore, Buzzi's 2030 emissions target of under 500 kilograms per tonne remains less ambitious than the sub-400 kilogram targets of its closest peers. Consequently, Buzzi's relatively slower decarbonization pace means that its European margins could face pressure as free allocations decline.

Regulatory Changes and Carbon Capture Feasibility

10.
Contrary evidence: The expected wave of independent cement plant closures may not materialize because recent regulatory adjustments have significantly delayed the free allowance phase-out timeline. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 19 October 2026 that the European Commission's July 2026 ETS reform proposal seeks to extend the final phase-out of free allowances from 2034 to 2038. This proposed reform would retain 59% of free allocations in 2030, compared to the much steeper cuts originally scheduled. This means that marginal independent plants receive a temporary reprieve, which allows them to delay costly final investment decisions.
11.
Carbon capture infrastructure bottlenecks: The viability of carbon capture as a survival pathway for inland plants is severely threatened by immature infrastructure. Heidelberg Materials announced on 22 October 2026 that it has paused its landmark GeZero carbon capture project at its Geseke plant in Germany. The company cited the lack of government-backed de-risking mechanisms and immature CO₂ transport and storage networks as the primary reasons for the pause. Consequently, this infrastructure deficit implies that smaller independent operators cannot rely on carbon capture to save their uneconomic inland facilities.

Competitive Dynamics and Corporate Survival

12.
First mover advantages: Decarbonization frontrunners are positioned to capture substantial pricing premiums that will drive significant margin expansion. Bernstein Research argues on 26 August 2026 that Holcim has successfully banked enough carbon allowances to cover its European emissions until at least 2031. This extensive carbon buffer means that Holcim can pass through carbon cost inflation in its pricing while avoiding immediate compliance cash costs. Therefore, these dynamic market conditions will generate exceptional cash flow generation for the decarbonization leaders.
13.
Other side of the story: While rising carbon costs are expected to consolidate the European cement market, unlisted independent operators may survive much longer than anticipated by adopting low-cost clinker substitution strategies. Schwenk Zement in Germany reported in February 2026 that it has successfully replaced over 95% of its coal consumption with alternative fuels. Furthermore, Schwenk is actively rolling out clinkerreduced cements with a clinker content below 60% across all its German sites. This means that independents can dramatically lower emissions without capital-intensive carbon capture investments. Therefore, these operational optimizations could allow agile independent producers to maintain positive operating cash flows, thereby delaying the anticipated wave of sector consolidation.

Section 3 – Local Market Dynamics and Pricing Power Since 2023

Section summary
  1. Consolidation in local cement markets has successfully defended pricing power across continental Europe since 2023.
  2. High-concentration regions like Spain demonstrate superior profitability compared with highly fragmented markets like Germany.
  3. Input cost inflation in under-consolidated regions has compressed margins despite modest positive price adjustments.

Local Consolidation and

1.
Import Mitigation Optimized distribution: Holcim acquired an undisciplined local competitor in Valencia in late 2022 to eliminate low-cost imports from Tunisia. According to Bernstein Research's report on 21 May 2026, shifting import volumes back to local clinker production restored regional price discipline. Consequently, this micro-consolidation turned Valencia into one of Holcim's most profitable regional markets in Europe, which significantly supported underlying earnings per share.
2.
Aggressive rationalization: Heidelberg Materials has taken a leading role in optimizing its European production network by permanently decommissioning underutilized assets. Deutsche Bank's report on 28 May 2026 notes that the group permanently shuttered plants in Beffes and Villiers-au-Bouin in France alongside a facility in Paderborn, Germany. This capacity rationalization is expected to increase regional plant utilization rates and support future margin expansion across Western Europe.
3.
Structural pricing: Vertical integration and supply concentration allow European building materials leaders to maintain robust pricing power even during deep volume downturns. Morgan Stanley argues in its note of 14 October 2026 that pricing discipline has enabled companies to lift prices against flat or falling volumes since 2023. Therefore, this strategic shift from volume to value is expected to insulate operating margins and protect EPS from cyclical demand shocks.

Pricing Trends and

4.
Regional Divergences Regional pricing: Cement price developments remain highly localized and closely correlated with the underlying degree of market consolidation. Morgan Stanley's report of 5 October 2026 highlights that annual cement prices rose 7.0% in Spain and 4.4% in Italy, compared with a meager 1.3% increase in fragmented Germany. This pricing strength means consolidated regions are far better positioned to deliver robust margin progression than fragmented peers.
5.
Deflationary pressure: The UK cement market stands out as a major underperformer due to weak residential demand and elevated import volumes. Jefferies Research reported on 30 September 2026 that UK cement prices fell by 0.8% year on year, whereas Spanish cement prices increased by 6.5% over the same period. Thus, the lack of pricing discipline in fragmented import zones will likely depress near-term operating earnings for UK-exposed players.
6.
Aggregates resilience: Aggregates pricing continues to outperform cement due to localized supply dynamics and significant high transport costs. Morgan Stanley's tracker from 5 October 2026 indicates that Swedish aggregates pricing accelerated by 7.9% year on year in August 2026, while Swedish cement prices rose by 3.8%. Consequently, companies with a higher aggregates mix will experience stronger price-cost coverage, which directly enhances long-term return on capital.

Leader Margin Dynamics Central

7.
European squeeze: Marginal price increases in highly fragmented markets have failed to cover the rapid escalation of operating costs. Buzzi's half-year report on 4 August 2026 disclosed that its Central European revenues fell 6.9% to 733 million euros, while EBITDA plummeted 20.7% to 186.5 million euros. This severe profit contraction shows that low consolidation levels prevent effective price pass-through, which materially dampens consolidated earnings per share.
8.
Benelux margin compression: Severe volume declines and weak demand in Luxembourg and the Netherlands have dramatically impaired operating profitability for regional leaders. Buzzi's half-year report of 4 August 2026 disclosed that cement deliveries fell 12.0% in the region, causing Benelux EBITDA margins to contract by 665 basis points. This decline highlights that weak fixed-cost absorption can completely wipe out moderate pricing gains when local activity remains depressed.
9.
Italian margin expansion: Italian margins have rebounded strongly because of tight market concentration and helpful import regulations. Jefferies Research notes on 4 August 2026 that Buzzi's H1 2026 Italian EBITDA rose 10.7% year on year, driving a margin expansion of 470 basis points to 25.6%. This impressive profitability improvement demonstrates that highly consolidated domestic markets can fully absorb cost inflation and generate exceptional cash flows.
10.
Heidelberg profitability: Cost-efficiency initiatives are beginning to yield tangible profitability gains for Europe's largest cement producer. Jefferies Research argues on 1 October 2026 that Heidelberg's third-quarter European EBITDA margin is expected to expand by 99 basis points to 24.1%. This operational progress implies that the company's aggressive restructuring is enhancing structural cost competitiveness and offsetting local volume headwind.
11.
Holcim margin resilience: Sustainable product mix shift has enabled Holcim to expand its operating margins despite negative organic sales volumes. Holcim's half-year results on 31 July 2026 showed European organic sales fell 4.2% while recurring EBIT rose 6.6% year on year to 524 million Swiss francs. This positive divergence proves that sustainable product premiumization can successfully offset volume pressure and expand leader EBIT margins.

Divergent Cases and

Buzzi's interim report on 4 August 2026 shows that its German EBITDA margin contracted by 310 basis points to 10.0%, compared with 13.1% in the prior year. This contraction occurred because intense cost inflation outpaced Buzzi's modest price increases, proving that competitor closures cannot guarantee profitability in highly fragmented regions.

French sequential weakness: Unexpected pricing sequential declines in consolidated markets can temporarily disrupt the margin recovery trajectory. Jefferies Research reported on 30 September 2026 that French cement prices suffered a sharp 3.5% month-on-month decline in August, erasing earlier gains. This unexpected softening indicates that pricing power remains highly sensitive to transient demand disruptions, even in consolidated Western European economies.

Industry Concentration and

14.
Future Outlook Concentration benefits: Long-term pricing discipline in European heavy materials is primarily determined by market concentration rather than the immediate demandsupply balance. Bernstein Research argued on 26 August 2026 that the carbon border adjustment mechanism will accelerate capacity closures among small, undisciplined operators. This capacity reduction means survival will depend on scale, ultimately boosting consolidated industry profitability and lifting long-term EPS.
15.
Other side of the story: While consolidation has successfully defended pricing power in several European regions, persistent cost inflation and low utilization rates could eventually undermine the industry's margin structure. Morgan Stanley's note on 14 October 2026 warns that Buzzi's price pass-through is failing in Germany and Benelux because of weak fixed-cost absorption. Section 5 – Antitrust Interventions and Regulatory Hurdles

Section summary
  1. National competition authorities and the European Commission have actively imposed structural divestment remedies on major building materials transactions since 2023.
  2. Regulators require the carveout of overlapping regional production plants to preserve market competition, which directly reduces transaction synergy values.
  3. Ongoing cartel investigations and historical antitrust fines demonstrate that regulatory oversight remains highly focused on pricing collusion in concrete and admixtures.

European Commission Decisions and

1.
Remedies Severe horizontal overlaps: The European Commission conditional clearance of Holcim's acquisition of Xella demonstrates that structural remedies are mandatory to preserve local competition. According to the European Commission's press release on 12 June 2026, the regulator had concerns that the merger would reduce competition in Romanian autoclaved aerated concrete blocks. To resolve this overlap, Holcim agreed to divest its entire Romanian AAC plant in Adjud. This divestment was necessary to secure Phase I approval but it ultimately reduces the expected regional synergy value.
2.
Global structural carve-outs: The massive integration of construction chemicals assets by Sika highlights how antitrust authorities can severely dilute transaction scale. Sika's media release on 2 May 2023 confirmed that closing the 2.1 billion Swiss francs MBCC acquisition required massive structural remedies. To secure regulatory approval, Sika was forced to sell MBCC's chemical admixtures business in the UK, Europe, USA, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand to Cinven. Consequently, this multi-jurisdictional divestment means that Sika lost substantial high-margin revenue before realizing group-level cost synergies.
3.
Rigid local enforcement: Regional antitrust watchdogs enforce strict standalone operations for divested units to prevent post-merger market dominance. The Australian Competition and Consumer Commission's regulatory undertaking published on 3 May 2023 shows that Sika was legally bound to transfer all associated research and development facilities to Cinven. This rigid structural remedy was designed to maintain a viable independent competitor in Australia. This means that synergy values were heavily diluted because Sika could not consolidate these overlapping advanced technology centers.
4.
Synergy value mitigation: Massive integration and regulatory clearance costs can temporarily depress the short-term earnings accretion of otherwise highly complementary platform acquisitions. Morgan Stanley's report on 3 May 2023 noted that while Sika's MBCC acquisition was structurally compelling, it would only add 2% to Sika's EBIT in the first year. This minimal contribution was caused by the combined burden of forced admixtures divestments and high integration costs. Consequently, long-term synergy realization remains key to reversing these initial transaction headwinds and lifting EPS.

National Authorities and Consolidation Hurdles

5.
Phased regulatory milestones: Complex European cross-border transactions require structured phases to accommodate lengthy national antitrust merger reviews. Zeus broker research on 16 July 2024 noted that SigmaRoc's acquisition of CRH's lime assets was structured in three distinct phases due to carve-out complexities. While early German and Czech phases cleared quickly, the final Polish phase was delayed until August 2024 to obtain national antitrust approval. Consequently, this prolonged regulatory timeline means expected operating earnings were deferred for several months.
6.
British regulatory intervention: The UK's Competition and Markets Authority imposes high hurdles on heavy materials deals to prevent regional price increases. Rothschild and Co Redburn's report of 27 March 2024 highlights that Heidelberg Materials faced intense regulatory scrutiny during its proposed acquisition of the British recycling specialist Mick George. The regulator flagged numerous local aggregates and ready-mix sites for potential competition concerns. Consequently, these heavy regulatory interventions show that further UK bolt-on acquisitions face severe execution risks that threaten future EPS expansion.
7.
Strict past remedies: Historical merger reviews by the Competition and Markets Authority illustrate the persistent regulatory barriers in regional British aggregates markets. Breedon's modern slavery report published on 16 May 2023 noted that the group's acquisition of certain Cemex UK assets was highly restricted by CMA intervention. The regulator blocked any operational interaction between the companies until the full completion of its detailed investigation. This strict ring-fencing meant that integration timelines were delayed by several months, temporarily depressing Breedon's capital returns.
8.
Network configuration adjustments: Major listed producers preemptively close or optimize their internal networks to avoid triggering antitrust reviews when making regional consolidations. Heidelberg Materials disclosed in its half-year results on 14 August 2026 that its network restructuring plan permanent shuttering of underutilized plants in Germany and France helped it reallocate capacity without regulatory friction. By strategically adjusting its footprint before pursuing nearby transactions, the group manages to reduce potential market overlap issues in future deals. This proactive strategy protects group operating margins while avoiding forced divestments.

Pricing Collusion and Antitrust Inquiries

9.
Systemic price collusion: Ongoing antitrust investigations by the European Commission target pricing coordination in key construction chemical segments. Morgan Stanley's report of 20 July 2026 disclosed that the European Commission issued formal Statements of Objections to major manufacturers including Sika and Saint-Gobain's Chryso division. The preliminary regulatory findings indicate that these companies breached EU antitrust rules by colluding on concrete and cement additive prices during 2021 and 2022. Thus, these investigations introduce substantial regulatory risk to future margins unless the firms successfully contest the charges.
10.
Severe cartel penalties: European Commission cartel regulations utilize massive financial penalties to deter anti-competitive behavior in heavy materials markets. Morgan Stanley's update on 20 July 2026 noted that the Commission typically increases cartel fines by a massive one-time deterrent of 15% to 25% of one year's sales. This aggressive penalty calculation makes any proven collusion extremely costly for large diversified groups. Therefore, these punitive legal frameworks mean that unexpected antitrust fines can severely impair cash flows and depress annual earnings per share.
11.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A political dilution of the EU ETS would suppress expected cement price increases and severely impair the earnings growth of first-mover listed majors.
  2. Delays in carbon capture investments and persistent cheap imports through CBAM loopholes pose substantial downside risks to domestic sector margins.

Policy Dilution and Pricing Compression

1.
Delayed regulatory timeline: The potential extension of free carbon allowances represents a major threat to the consensus consolidation narrative. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 19 October 2026 that the European Commission's latest reform proposal seeks to delay the complete phase-out of free allowances from 2034 to 2038. This extended timeline means that the cost pressure required to force marginal independent plant closures is deferred, which will ultimately dampen near-term earnings growth for consolidated market leaders.
2.
Erodable pricing momentum: Any dilution in carbon policy will directly erode the pricing power that major listed cement groups expect to command. Bernstein Research argued on 11 February 2026 that extending free allowances to 2039 would cause the expected European cement pricing CAGR to drop from 5.5% to 3.7% through 2030. This pricing deceleration would prevent listed majors from fully recovering general operating cost inflation, which means that future profit margins would contract below consensus estimates.
3.
Volatile carbon prices: Political interventions to protect local industrial competitiveness are creating a bearish outlook for European carbon prices. Bernstein Research reported on 30 September 2026 that Italy and the Czech Republic are seeking to adjust the EU ETS to ease energy costs and limit extreme price spikes. Lower carbon prices would significantly weaken the financial incentive for small producers to rationalize capacity, unless regulatory enforcement remains strictly uncompromised.

Decarbonization Investment Delays

4.
Capital expenditure hurdles: Decarbonization capital expenditures are being delayed by listed players who refuse to invest without guaranteed financial returns. Bernstein Research noted on 14 September 2026 that Holcim's management will not proceed with carbon capture projects that fail to clear standard payback hurdles if carbon prices drop. These strict investment criteria imply that decarbonization milestones may be delayed , which would expose these groups to sudden regulatory cost shocks if political winds shift.
5.
Infrastructure development bottlenecks: The transition to low-carbon cement is severely bottle-necked by a lack of government de-risking mechanisms and immature transport networks. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 1 October 2026 that the vanguard of the cement decarbonization movement is facing slower implementation timelines. These infrastructure delays mean that stranded asset risks are rising for early-mover plants, unless national governments fully subsidize the transport pipelines.
6.
Squeezed central margins: Margin compression is already manifesting in under-consolidated markets where price increases fail to cover rising operational costs. Morgan Stanley's report of 14 October 2026 noted that German and Dutch cement margins are being heavily squeezed by persistent inflation and low capacity utilization. This margin squeeze demonstrates that pass-through mechanisms are failing in fragmented regions, which will depress Buzzi's and Heidelberg's European operating earnings unless regional volumes recover.

Import Pressures and CBAM Evasion

7.
Persistent import threats: Cheap imports from non-regulated countries continue to disrupt the pricing discipline of European coastal markets. An expert with extensive building materials experience noted on 19 August 2026 that cross-border cement flows are heavily influenced by the control of import terminals. This dependency means that if third-party operators acquire terminal access, domestic pricing power will be broken by a flood of low-cost Turkish and North African clinker.
8.
Weak carbon enforcement: Regulatory loopholes during the CBAM transitional phase are allowing foreign exporters to bypass carbon tariffs. A former ready-mix and M&A executive explained on 29 September 2026 that the current reliance on self-declarations allows importers to declare artificially low carbon content. This weak enforcement means that cheap imports will continue entering Southern Europe, which directly penalizes domestic players like Cementir and Cementos Molins.
9.
Downstream carbon leakage: The exclusion of finished building products from carbon taxes represents a major structural risk for European cement manufacturers. Morgan Stanley reported on 8 September 2025 that the potential exclusion of downstream goods from near-term CBAM reviews would leave European markets vulnerable to carbon leakage. This regulatory gap means that customers will import finished pre-cast concrete blocks rather than raw cement, which will severely depress domestic volumes and undermine local production.

10.
Resilient localized monopolies: Aggregates-heavy building products companies maintain a highly defensive position that insulates them from carbon-driven pricing shocks. Morgan Stanley argued on 9 March 2023 that aggregates are protected by extremely high barriers to entry and localized monopoly dynamics. This insulation means that diversified players like Breedon and SigmaRoc will maintain robust margin protection even if the expected cement capacity rationalization fails to materialize.

Operational cost pressures: High energy tariffs and rising distribution costs continue to challenge the profitability of Western European heavy materials. Rothschild and Co Redburn's report of 13 October 2026 noted that Europe's subdued volumes remain a major constraint on Heidelberg Materials.
