EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Chief Executive Quality and Strategic Delivery

Søren Steenberg Jensen brings exceptionally high industry expertise and extensive product tanker experience from tenures at Maersk, TORM, and Straits Tankers before his executive promotion at Hafnia.

His operational record demonstrates exceptionally high standards of delivery through strategic fleet expansion, including the $863 million acquisition of Chemical Tankers Inc adding 32 modern vessels and the $413 million Scorpio transaction for 12 LR1 vessels, which together generated over $190 million in operational synergies and 31% asset value appreciation in their first year.

Balance sheet optimization enabled unprecedented cash dividends, with a record 90% net profit payout in Q2 2026 distributing $250 million and achieving a 21% annualized dividend yield, while Net Loan-to-Value dropped to a record low 13% from 24.2% in Q1 2024.

Leadership style exhibits direct and plain-spoken communication focused on disciplined commercial execution and operational excellence, with guidance accuracy consistently tracking actual TCE outcomes within narrow margins.

Performance Against Section 16 Leadership Standards

Jensen demonstrates strong drive through foundational AI partnerships deploying Complexio's platform to improve workflow response times and accelerate commercial contract negotiations, and strong capital allocation evidenced by tactical vessel divestments recycling capital through the sale of six older vessels during strong asset market conditions and authorization of up to $100 million for share repurchases.

His speed in decision-making is confirmed by his background in asset management supporting fast and bold transaction decisions for fleet optimization.

However, critics argue outstanding financial delivery was largely driven by macro shipping cycles and extreme geopolitical disruptions rather than operational excellence, with massive earnings spikes sparked by Strait of Hormuz closures and Red Sea vessel diversions that inflated global freight rates.

His emphasis on continuity may hide a reluctance to pursue independent strategic pivots away from parent BW Group's established playbook, and because his CEO tenure began in late 2026, his independent executive decision-making record remains unproven.

Governance Structure and Parent Control Risks

The board maintains a high level of independent oversight with four of six directors qualifying as fully independent, though Chairman Andreas Sohmen-Pao and director Mikael Skov are classified as non-independent due to corporate associations.

Board committees are staffed entirely by independent directors to protect public shareholder interests, with the audit committee comprising two fully independent members possessing necessary financial and risk management skills.

However, BW Group owns a dominant 45% stake exerting substantial corporate voting control over board nominations and transactions, with the July 2024 shareholder rights agreement granting BW Group direct board seat designation rights based on its ownership levels.

The overwhelming voting power of BW Group could marginalize minority public shareholders during critical corporate actions, and BW Group engages in broad shipping activities that may compete directly with Hafnia for asset acquisitions.

Executive compensation lacks individual transparency with only aggregate disclosure preventing individual pay-for-performance verification, and performance-related remuneration lacks any absolute maximum limit, deviating from local governance codes.

Insider Trading Signals and Market Confidence

The overall pattern of share dealing shows significant net selling by primary insiders over the last 12 months, signaling near-term market caution.

CFO Perry Van Echtelt executed sales of 50,000 shares representing 8% of holdings in October 2026 and 90,000 shares in April 2026, while former CEO Mikael Skov executed sales of 1,000,000 shares in April 2026, and primary insiders jointly executed the exercise and sale of 725,019 options in March 2026.

Hafnia's public free float stands at only 52% of outstanding shares compared to peer Scorpio Tankers' much higher 86% free float, constraining liquidity.

Insider selling patterns at Hafnia align closely with heavy selling observed across product tanker peers, including TORM CEO Jacob Meldgaard's sale of 549,177 shares and Oaktree Capital's total exit from TORM.

Expert Validation and Single Largest Credibility Risk

Former maritime managers with 35 years of industry experience agree that Hafnia's commercial pool model provides substantial information and cost advantages, with Hafnia recognized as the world's leading product tanker company within a decade, and running eight pools providing extensive commercial information advantages that directly lower corporate operating costs.

The single largest risk to management credibility is that former managers warn current record earnings are ultimately unsustainable over long-term cycles once geopolitical disruptions resolve, as any potential resolution to Middle East conflicts could rapidly erode the trade inefficiencies currently supporting Hafnia's profitability.

This risk is compounded by the dominant influence of BW Group which could lead to unchecked related-party transactions if parent interests diverge from minority shareholders, particularly as BW Group engages in broad maritime activities that could compete directly with Hafnia's fleet expansion.
