Zegona Communications (ZEG-GB): what the H1 FY27 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the case moderately on balance: the company delivered what the case needed on its own lines and the payout policy cleared above the case's level, but the price has not followed it, the policy was bought by giving up the deleveraging the case assumed, and Digi has gone through two of the case's alarm levels with its funding constraint apparently lifted.

1. What changed

  • The half (six months to 30 September 2026, reported 17 November 2026). Revenue EUR 1,841m, +2.9%. Service revenue +1.1%, after -2.3% in H2 FY26 and against a market expectation of +1%. EBITDAaL EUR 675m, a 37% margin, 38% in Q2 (the review gives EUR 666m in one place; the interim report line and the review's own table give EUR 675m). Operating free cash flow EUR 434m, +5.6%, 6% ahead of Morgan Stanley because capex was 5% below its forecast.
  • Q2. Revenue EUR 925m, +3.4%, ahead of Deutsche Bank. EBITDAaL EUR 351m, +0.6%, in line, with growth the same house calls weaker than expected. Capex EUR 121m, 8.5% below its forecast. Broadband net additions +9k (+1k in Q1) and contract mobile +45k (+37k in Q1), the eighth positive quarter, helped by seasonality and back-to-school.
  • Balance sheet and returns. Net debt EUR 3,263m, EUR 50m above March 2026; leverage 2.4x (Morgan Stanley). EUR 70m of buybacks in the half. The September 2026 framework returns all free cash flow over time, starting with EUR 400m (a EUR 200m ordinary dividend and a EUR 200m buyback), about 10% of market capitalisation and 24% above Morgan Stanley's prior forecast. The leverage target was raised to 2.25x to 2.75x from 1.5x to 2.0x.
  • Against what the case assumed. The case assumed a policy of about 70% of equity free cash flow, at odds of 65 to 35, and leverage falling inside a 1.5x to 2.1x corridor to 1.8x by FY29. The policy came in above the first and removed the second. The case's alarm levels on the company's own lines were not touched: operating free cash flow below EUR 400m for the half, an EBITDAaL margin below 35%, service revenue growth below +1.0% in a quarter (the half-year +1.1% sits just above it, and the review does not split it by quarter). The case's alarm "leverage rising" was.
  • Competitors. Digi Spain, Q3 2026: record broadband net additions of 178k and 362k postpaid mobile; fixed broadband ARPU -8.6% to EUR 12.24; 504 own macro sites added in the quarter, to 1,867; and what the review calls its "recent listing". The case's alarm was Digi net additions above 150k or an IPO revival, and its confirming test was Digi ARPU declines better than -5% by Q4 2026. MasOrange, Q3 2026: retail service revenue -1%, convergent revenue -4%, net porting negative for twelve months.
  • Sell-side. Deutsche Bank Buy and Morgan Stanley Overweight, both reiterated on 17 November 2026, both targets unchanged at 2,200p. Morgan Stanley expects modest earnings upgrades on cost.
  • Price. 1,734p on 16 November 2026, against 1,806p when the case was written; 2.5% above the 200-day average (11.1% then), below the 50-day, relative strength at the 68th percentile (74th then).

2. The challenge

We find three readings the case does not hold. Each is live on this evidence.

The policy did not clear the test; it changed the test. The case's cleared branch was a declared yield on a balance sheet that was deleveraging, and the case withheld the sector's multiple partly because of leverage at 2.33x. What was declared is a larger payout on a balance sheet that is no longer asked to deleverage. The top of the new range, 2.75x, is about the 2.73x at which the case's own adverse scenario called the equity return "highly vulnerable"; that scenario got there through a 3% revenue decline, and the new target allows it by choice. On this reading the extra distribution is not more cash earned. It is debt not repaid, the market can see that, and a higher yield on a more levered claim earns no higher multiple. The evidence so far fits: a return 24% above forecast, both targets unchanged, and a price about 4% lower than before the policy was declared. The case also records a carry paying management 15% of value growth, settled in shares from October 2027; a reader who starts there sees a policy that maximises what is distributed inside that window. For it to be right: the multiple on equity free cash flow stays where it is through the case's own two-quarter window, and leverage sits at 2.4x or above with no stated path down.

The revenue stabilisation is borrowed from MasOrange, and Digi is no longer short of money. The case treats the top tier as support and Digi as bounded by its funding, which it calls the single fact its 'Foundations' most rely on. This half says something different. Digi added a record 178k broadband lines in a quarter in which the company added 9k, so the company is not holding Digi off; it is growing because MasOrange is shrinking. A donor that weak is unlikely to stay passive. The review's own counter-case is MasOrange completing its synergy programme and discounting to win share back, and a company that has just removed its promotional discounts, and that the review places among those at risk of being "trapped in the middle", is where that lands. Digi, for its part, is accepting ARPU declines of 8.6%, worse than the 6.9% the case recorded for Q2 2026, and has listed. For it to be right: the listing brought real proceeds (the review does not give them), Digi's additions stay above 150k, and the company's broadband additions turn negative once MasOrange responds or the seasonal help goes.

The cash beat is a capex underspend sitting on a cost engine that has slowed again. Q2 revenue grew 3.4% and EBITDAaL 0.6%. Almost none of the extra revenue reached profit, either because it was handsets and seasonal wholesale or because lease costs from the fibre sales absorbed it; the review does not say which. Service revenue grew 1.1% with Spanish inflation at 4.9% in October 2026. The half's beat came from spending less capex than expected, not from cost. The case's first Key Driver is a cost programme it already rated as decelerating on EBITDAaL growth of +2.5% in Q1 FY27; +0.6% is slower again. On this reading the path to EUR 1bn of operating free cash flow is being walked by under-spending, in the half the company added technology supply-chain risk to its principal risks and while a power-backup mandate is under review. For it to be right: EBITDAaL growth does not recover in Q3 as Deutsche Bank expects on easier lease comparisons, and capex rises back towards forecasts.

One reading we do not put up: that the top line is only handsets. Positive service revenue and eight quarters of line growth answer it on volume, though not on price.

3. What it does to the case

  • 'Dividends' (RA 1, E 3, BB 1), B, and the Key Driver 'The payout policy': less pressure on level, more on funding. The case's "not A because undeclared and levered" has lost the first word and gained weight on the second. 'Sustainability' (KQ 2), B, rested on leverage "falling towards 1.8x"; that premise is gone.
  • 'Balance sheet' (KQ 11, RA 2, E 1, BB 1), A, and 'Net debt' (KQ 4, RA 2, E 2, BB 1), A: more pressure. Both were rated on a 1.5x to 2.1x corridor and falling debt. Leverage is 2.4x against 2.33x and the target now runs to 2.75x.
  • 'Re-rating' (RA 2, E 3, BB 1), C, and the Key Risk 'The market keeping the earnings lens': more pressure. The policy is declared and the price is lower. The case's alarm is the multiple unchanged one quarter after declaration; that date falls in late December 2026.
  • 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1), C: pressure both ways. The policy branch has cleared, and at 1,734p the price sits below the 1,806p at which the case said the eighteen-month bar clears and above the roughly 1,600p it gave for the three-year bar (in pence; we have no current exchange rate). The return on the case's arithmetic is higher only because the market has declined to pay for it.
  • 'Riskiness' (E 1, BB 4), C, and its 'Pret sandwich' (KQ 11, RA 1), C: more pressure. The correlating mechanism the case names is retail price pressure meeting fixed costs and leverage, and one of the three has been raised deliberately.
  • 'Competition' (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1), D; 'Industry structure' (RA 3, E 1, BB 2), C; 'Foundations' (BB 2), C; the Key Risk 'Digi beyond the low end': more pressure. Each "not lower because" in the case cites Digi's constrained funding. 'No peer problems' (RA 6, E 1, BB 4), B, read the disruptor as the peer in trouble; on this evidence it is MasOrange.
  • 'Growth engine' (KQ 2, RA 2, E 1, BB 4), D: the same to more. 'Revenue and demand cycle' (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1), B, and the Key Driver 'Revenue stability against Digi': less pressure on the reported lines, more on why they hold.
  • 'Upgrade record' (KQ 3, RA 1, E 2, BB 1), A: the same. A cash beat and an in-line EBITDAaL.
  • 'Near steps' (KQ 1, RA 4, E 2, BB 1), A: more pressure. Both near steps are now behind. What remains, RANCo and Vantage, the case rates as uncontracted, and the review puts the Vantage prize at EUR 50m a year against the case's about EUR 60m.
  • The pinned letters, as context. Technical momentum reads D against the record's C, valuation A against C, thematic momentum F against C, and the store's TSR/Valuation B against C.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is built to carry what the brokers do with the print: whether cash and dividend estimates rise for a policy 24% above forecast, whether any target moves off 2,200p, and whether the houses the case could not explain have changed stance. It cannot carry anything new from the company, and it does not answer any of the three readings; broker models of leverage are not a company path.
  • The price itself, to late December 2026 and late March 2027. The case's own one-quarter alarm and two-quarter invalidation on the multiple. This settles the first reading and needs no disclosure.
  • The Q3 FY27 trading statement (date not in our evidence). EBITDAaL growth against Deutsche Bank's expected recovery; capex against forecasts; broadband additions without seasonal help or promotions; service revenue by quarter against the case's +1.0% level.
  • Digi's Q4 2026 results. Net additions against 150k and ARPU against -5%, the case's own tests, and what the listing raised.
  • MasOrange's next results and pricing. Whether it discounts.
  • RANCo. Morgan Stanley looks for early 2027; Deutsche Bank says 12 to 18 months. Not committed.
  • What nothing is scheduled to answer. The company has not committed to a leverage path inside 2.25x to 2.75x, to the bridge from operating to equity free cash flow, to ARPU or churn by brand, or to the wholesale and lease lines that would say why Q2 EBITDAaL barely grew. The third reading can be tested on outcomes and is unlikely to be explained.
