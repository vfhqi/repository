Beijer Alma (BEIA.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results modestly strengthen the operating half of our case and leave the price half, the reason for the park, where it was: one of the two conditions the case set for progress at today's price has been announced, the other has not been shown, and the shares at 310.5 SEK (13 November 2026) sit where the case said the target was already paid for.

1. What changed

  • The appointment. Per-Johan Dahlgren was named permanent chief executive on 22 October 2026, to take office no later than April 2027; the acting chief executive stays until then. The case gave an appointment within twelve months about two in three. The review records a mixed record at his previous companies and a board that expects him to "accelerate the pace of acquisitions".
  • Profit. Q3 2026 adjusted EBITA was MSEK 352, up 18% on Q3 2025, 6% above consensus of MSEK 331; the margin was 16.9% against consensus of 16.0%. Beijer Components' margin was 19.6% against a DNB Carnegie forecast of 18.5%; the case's modal assumed 19 to 20 percent, and recorded 20.0 percent for Q2. Beijer Tech earned MSEK 116 on sales of MSEK 834, about 13.9% on the review's figures; the case's modal had Tech rising to 13 percent.
  • Organic sales did not move. Group organic growth was 1%, in line with consensus. Beijer Tech's was 1%, down from high single digits over the previous four quarters, and helped by one large order. The review does not give Beijer Components' organic growth for the quarter, which is the series the case watches most closely; Components' reported sales were MSEK 1,256, up 2%.
  • Orders did. Bookings were MSEK 2,155, up 22%, of which 12% organic; book-to-bill 1.03x against a three-year third-quarter average of 0.97x. Two distortions are named: customers in Chassis Springs ordered early ahead of a distribution-centre move in December 2026, not quantified; and Beijer Tech's MSEK 887 of orders included a non-recurring MSEK 50 order.
  • No acquisitions in the quarter. The case expected Q3 deals. Management expects to resume in Q4.
  • More restructuring. Two European spring sites close in Q4 2026 at a cost of MSEK 33 (MSEK 21 cash), with margin benefit expected from Q1 2027.
  • Balance sheet. Net debt MSEK 2,302, 1.4x EBITDA; capital efficiency 49.8% against the 50% target. Reported earnings per share of SEK 4.20 carry a net positive MSEK 37 of items affecting comparability (MSEK 70 of insurance compensation less the MSEK 33).
  • Sell-side. DNB Carnegie, the only broker in the review with post-result numbers, raised adjusted earnings per share about 3% in each year (2026 to SEK 15.1 from SEK 14.7; 2027 to SEK 16.9 from SEK 16.4; 2028 to SEK 18.0 from SEK 17.5) and its target to SEK 360 from SEK 351, Buy.

2. The challenge

Reading one: a second round of margin without volume, which the case said was finished. The case holds that "the easy part, the tail cut, is done and the rest needs volume", and so caps Components at 19 to 20 percent. The quarter says otherwise: 19.6% on 2% sales growth, with two site closures still to come and a broker expecting more than 100 basis points from Q1 2027. If that is right, Components passes 20% on a flat top line, the earnings path sits above our modal for another year, and the case's own test of the false friend, organic growth after the cutting ends, matters less to earnings than we assumed. For it to be right: the closures must deliver the saving without losing customers in the move, and steel and energy costs, which the review shows rising in Europe, must pass through.

Reading two: the same facts mean the test never arrives. The case's false-friend hypothesis has a date: tail-cutting ends in late 2026, then organic prints clean. Closing an Alcomex factory and a standard-springs distribution centre in Q4 2026 is more subtraction, and the early ordering flatters Q3 and probably depresses Q4. On this reading the clean print slips into mid-2027 at the earliest, each quarter arrives with a reason not to judge it, and margin keeps being reported before restructuring cost. The case conceded the false friend; it did not allow that the hypothesis could stay unfalsifiable. For it to be right: Components' organic line stays near zero or negative through the first half of 2027 while further perimeter actions are announced.

Reading three: the appointment answers the question the case asked and opens one it did not. The case feared a centraliser or a delay. What the board has described is an accelerator. The case rates the capital rule (surplus to Beijer Tech; Components mends margins before it buys) as the right design and rests on the acting chief executive's discipline. An incoming chief executive hired to quicken deals, with a mixed prior record, after a quarter with none, is plausibly a risk to price paid and to that rule, not to decentralisation. For it to be right: Components resumes buying before its organic line turns, or prices paid move toward the 10x EBITA the case treats as invalidating.

Reading four: the orders are a demand turn. The case rates 'Demand Inflection' F and models organic growth of about 1 percent a year. Organic orders up 12% is not small: strip the MSEK 50 order and it is still high single digits, and the review reports destocking ending across peers. With Components' fixed-cost base, a real turn would make our earnings path too low. We think this the least secure of the four: the early ordering is unquantified, euro-area industrial output fell 0.60% in Q3, and organic sales were 1%. It is possible, not probable.

Readings one and four cut for the company; two and three against. None changes the price argument by itself.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry the other brokers' estimates and targets, and show whether consensus follows DNB Carnegie's 3%. The 24-month-forward consensus stood at SEK 17.2978 on 14 November 2026, against the case's 17.30 SEK. It cannot carry new company evidence; no further results are due before the fourth-quarter report.
  • The fourth-quarter report, which the case dates to February 2027. Book-to-bill and organic orders after the early ordering unwinds (the case alarms below 1.00x); whether deals resumed; Components' organic growth, if disclosed.
  • Q1 and Q2 2027. Whether Components' margin holds above the case's 19 percent and shows the closure benefit; this separates readings one and two.
  • April 2027 and after. The incoming chief executive's first statements on the capital rule and on Components acquisitions. The company has not committed to quantify the early ordering, or to say when the perimeter stops shrinking. Those two are open questions that nothing scheduled answers.
