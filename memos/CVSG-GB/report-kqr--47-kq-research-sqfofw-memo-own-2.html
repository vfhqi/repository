Section 3 – R&D Expenditure Credits and Tax Assumptions

Section summary
  1. CVS Group's earnings benefit significantly from Research and Development Expenditure Credits which added fifteen point seven million pounds to adjusted EBITDA in fiscal year 2026.
  2. The accounting shift to reduced discounting of R&D credits faces compliance risks as HMRC enquiry windows remain open on thirtyeight point three million pounds of cumulative claims.

accounting treatment of research credits

1.
Substantial benefit: CVS Group recognized a net RDEC benefit of fifteen point seven million pounds within its fiscal year 2026 adjusted EBITDA. This credit was slightly higher than the fifteen point one million pounds recognized in fiscal year 2025. Without these credits, the company's underlying operational margin would have declined to seventeen point six percent from seventeen point eight percent in the prior year. This accounting practice means that future adjusted earnings per share remain highly sensitive to any changes in research credit eligibility rules.
2.
Reduced discounting: CVS Group applied a new accounting estimation method in fiscal year 2026 to reduce the discount on its R&D claims. Under this updated method, the company recognized a gross credit of sixteen point nine million pounds rather than the ten point three million pounds under the old framework. This modification allowed management to release six point six million pounds of prior-year provisions directly into EBITDA. Consequently, this accounting change represents more aggressive near-term income recognition that inflates current earnings.
3.
Historical benefit: CVS Group has historically relied on these research credits to support its reported margins since its first claim in fiscal year 2021. In fiscal year 2021, the company's first-ever R&D claim yielded a two million pound benefit to EBITDA. By fiscal year 2025, the net RDEC benefit had scaled to fifteen point one million pounds. This historical trend shows that EBITDA expansion is heavily dependent on non-operational tax credit accounting rather than organic clinic growth.
4.
Peer margin comparisons: CVS Group exhibits a much larger margin benefit from RDEC than standard European animal health peers. For example, French pharmaceutical peer Virbac SA focuses its financial reports on raw product R&D investments that exceed eight percent of sales rather than government credit recognition. No direct public comparisons for UK veterinary peers are available in covering broker research. If these RDEC benefits are stripped out, CVS Group's decade-long margin expansion appears very modest at only one hundred basis points.

hmrc enquiry and clawback exposure

5.
Significant risk: CVS Group faces substantial clawback risks because HMRC enquiry windows remain open on up to thirty-eight point three million pounds of cumulative claims. This exposure represents the maximum amount that could be disallowed if HMRC challenges the qualification of historic veterinary projects. The open enquiry windows cover submitted claims for fiscal years 2019 through 2025, alongside the unsubmitted fiscal year 2026 claim. A negative HMRC ruling would directly reduce adjusted EBITDA and cash balances in future years.
6.
Provision buffers: CVS Group maintains an outstanding provision of two point nine million pounds against its cumulative R&D claims. Under the previous recognition methodology, the company would have held a much larger provision buffer of nine point five million pounds. This lower provision reflects management's belief that their growing history of successful claims reduces overall uncertainty. However, this lower buffer leaves the group highly exposed to tax adjustments if HMRC chooses to tighten its compliance auditing.
7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank suggests that the risk of HMRC clawbacks is minimal because CVS Group has built a highly robust claim methodology with specialist tax advisers. The broker highlights that the status of each submitted claim has been validated against direct HMRC communications. Furthermore, the broker notes that margins do not reflect over-earning even when these tax credits are included. This robust audit history suggests that the current provision level is entirely adequate.

tax rate and minority interest modelling

8.
Tax rate divergence: CVS Group reported a statutory effective tax rate of forty-four point four percent for continuing operations in fiscal year 2026. This high statutory rate was driven by non-deductible acquisition costs and a higher proportion of profits originating from Australia. However, the company's adjusted effective tax rate was much lower at twenty-seven point four percent. Covering brokers use this adjusted tax rate of twenty-seven percent to model future earnings per share.
9.
Minority interest impact: CVS Group models a steady increase in profits attributable to non-controlling interests as international joint ventures expand. In fiscal year 2026, the statutory profit attributable to non-controlling interests was zero point five million pounds. On an adjusted basis, profit attributable to minority interests rose to one million pounds from zero point two million pounds in fiscal year 2025. This rising minority interest acts as a direct drag on adjusted earnings per share attributable to parent company shareholders.
10.
Past precedent: Jefferies highlights that other European healthcare companies have experienced similar earnings volatility from government R&D grants. For instance, Spanish pharmaceutical peer Laboratorios Farmaceuticos Rovi SA adjusted its mid-term earnings per share models upward after receiving a non-repayable thirty-six million Euro R&D grant recognized in other income. This past precedent indicates that governmentbacked research incentives introduce non-operational volatility into corporate earnings models. This comparison highlights how regulatory grant shifts impact veterinary and wider healthcare sectors.
11.
Unupdated forecasts: Broker models from other major houses have not updated their explicit tax rate projections since the preliminary results. Because these brokers have not released refreshed models, the consensus continues to reflect historical statutory tax averages. Investors must wait for upcoming trading updates to see if brokers adjust their adjusted tax rates upward to reflect persistent Australian corporate tax rates of thirty percent.
12.
Other side of the story: While the reduction in R&D credit provisions introduces near-term earnings risk, management's detailed claim methodology is highly defensible. The audit team and internal specialists verified that CVS Group holds a growing body of evidence to support its project classifications. This evidence suggests that clawback risks are heavily overstated by conservative market bears. Consequently, the current provision level of two point nine million pounds is highly likely to remain sufficient to cover any HMRC inquiry.
