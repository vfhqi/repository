Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be answered partly now, not yet in full. EXOR's demonstrated strengths are Ferrari's pricing power, a very low head-office cost base, buybacks at deep discounts and disposals at attractive multiples. They make NAV outperformance plausible. The expected change also depends on a Stellantis recovery, a CNH margin rebuild and value creation from new large stakes, and the record on those is weak or untested. Overall confidence in what the evidence shows is medium.
1.
Starting point: EXOR's NAV per share fell 3.9% in H1 2026 against an 11.8% rise in the MSCI World, after an 8.1% fall in 2025 against a 5.4% benchmark gain. The 2026 to 2028 incentive plan therefore begins from a deficit.
2.
Ferrari strength: Ferrari's Q3 2026 operating profit rose 23% at constant exchange rates to €563 million, driven by mix and customization rather than volume. This is the clearest demonstrated strength.
3.
Cost discipline: Head-office costs were 8 basis points of gross asset value in 2025, below the 10 basis point target.
4.
Buybacks and the discount: The €1 billion April 2025 tender, done at a discount above 50%, added €4.7 per share to NAV. A new program of up to €500 million was announced on September 22, 2026. Even so, the discount stood at 57.6% at June 30, 2026, up from 56% at end-2025.
5.
Disposals and liquidity: The Welltec sale is expected to return 2.4x invested capital. Deployable cash is about €4 billion. Management concedes that a new public stake faces a very high hurdle relative to buybacks, and it has not committed to a split between the two.
6.
New-investment record: Management targets 15% to 20% stakes costing at least €2 billion. The record on newer holdings is mixed. Clarivate lost €63 million of value in H1 2026. Institut Mérieux took a €276 million write-down to €680 million, so unlisted health assets can reprice sharply. Philips added €99 million in H1 2026 after a €697 million drag in 2025.
7.
Stellantis turnaround: Stellantis posted an industrial free cash outflow of about €4 billion in Q3 2026, with US dealer inventory at 99 days in October 2026. Management targets industrial free cash flow recovery only by late 2028. Contrary evidence is that the dividend was suspended and up to €5 billion of hybrid bonds were planned. Precedents are mixed. Ford cut 265k units and normalized dealer stock to 56 days by end-2025. The PSA and Opel integration shows platform consolidation takes several years.

Bernstein assumes an L-shaped agricultural recovery with farmer cash returns negative through 2027. Ferrari is therefore priced for continued delivery, and CNH is not.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating the plausibility of EXOR's expected strategic changes determines whether its discount narrows and its asset base outperforms.

Defining the core investment question

1.
Central debate: "Key Question: how plausible is the change now expected for EXOR N.V. (EXO-NL), given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Significant fluctuations: EXOR experienced a 3.9% decrease in net asset value per share during the first half of 2026, severely underperforming the MSCI World Index which rose by 11.8%. The ultimate path of its net asset value per share over the 2026 to 2028 incentive plan period will depend heavily on the performance of listed assets like Ferrari. If these assets continue to deliver strong price-and-mix earnings, the company can successfully raise net asset value per share faster than global benchmarks.
3.
Negative performance: Stellantis suffered a significant decline in value during the first half of 2026, which dragged down EXOR's overall performance and severely reduced its short-term dividend income. A slow industrial turnaround or failed dealer-inventory corrections at Stellantis will directly constrain EXOR's consolidated earnings and cash flows. However, a restored dividend and positive industrial free cash flow from Stellantis would provide EXOR with massive recovery in dividend income by 2028.
4.
High exposure: EXOR faces substantial investment risks from deploying its capital into large new public stakes at prevailing market prices. Its recent healthcare foray, including raising its stake in Royal Philips to 19% economic rights, exposes EXOR to operational and litigation-related volatilities. If newly acquired stakes underperform or experience write-downs, EXOR faces the threat of further net asset value declines instead of the expected value creation.
5.
Disciplined allocation: EXOR holds cash and cash equivalents that increased to €2.2 billion, bringing its total deployable liquidity to around €4 billion. Investors are closely questioning whether the holding company will prioritize buybacks at deep discounts or deploy this cash into speculative new market stakes. A disciplined focus on repurchasing shares at a persistent discount of approximately 50% historically supports net asset value per share growth.
6.
Unfavourable conditions: EXOR's investment risks are compounded by external macroeconomic pressures, such as potential tariffs on European vehicles and components. A broader slowdown in luxury spending or a stronger euro could also weaken Ferrari's premium pricing momentum. These headwinds could prevent EXOR from achieving its ambitious growth targets and lead to a widening of the discount to net asset value.
7.
Uncertain credibility: CNH Industrial's ability to achieve its 16% to 17% agricultural margin target by 2030 represents another critical variable for EXOR's long-term valuation. Achieving this target is highly dependent on a cyclical agricultural machinery trough turning into a sustained recovery. Any operational failure or competitive pressure from peers will directly hurt EXOR's sum of the parts value and subsequent investment returns.
8.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on November 23, 2026, that Ferrari's solid pricing power, elevated personalization, and fully allocated order book through 2027 provide superior earnings visibility. This strong assetlevel performance suggests that EXOR's expected net asset value growth may be more plausible than skeptics believe, shielding the holding company from wider market declines.
9.
Other side of the story: While EXOR's downside is protected by Ferrari's exceptional brand heritage, its overall trajectory remains hostage to severe operational risks at Stellantis and CNH Industrial. Historical precedents of automotive turnarounds indicate that resolving massive cash outflows and high dealer inventory is rarely achieved within two years. Consequently, EXOR's incentive plan targets may remain out of reach unless its industrial holdings recover far more rapidly than peers.

  1. EXOR faces medium confidence in its ability to outperform the MSCI World due to historic NAV underperformance despite strong underlying assets.
  2. Ferrari pricing power and disciplined head-office costs represent proven drivers of value that are partially offset by listed industrial asset drags.

Plan Targets and Performance Record

Ambitious targets: EXOR reported in its September 2026 half-year report that it has structured its 2026 to 2028 long-term incentive plan around outperforming the MSCI World Index. The company granted performance share units to its executive leadership that vest at the end of 2028 subject to meeting these relative net asset value growth targets. Accomplishing this goal is necessary for executive options struck at an exercise price of 86.46 euros to deliver meaningful value. Consequently, the management is highly incentivized to raise net asset value per share over this three-year period.

Ferrari Pricing and Operational Efficiency

5.
Exceptional pricing: Ferrari reported on November 3, 2026, that it achieved strong price-and-mix-led earnings growth during the third quarter of 2026. Morgan Stanley reported on November 3, 2026, that the luxury car manufacturer achieved a 23% increase in third-quarter operating profit at constant exchange rates to reach 563 million euros. This operational success was driven by rich product mix and customization rather than shipment volumes. This momentum allows Ferrari to support EXOR's asset base and sustain premium valuation levels over the long term.

This level of customization compares favorably to the 19% rate originally assumed in the company's capital markets day plan. This strong brand execution helps insulate EXOR's largest asset from broader luxury sector slow downs seen globally.

This projection sits above Ferrari's own 2030 target of 30% or more, indicating high market optimism. While mix headwinds from new models like the Testarossa replacement could emerge, high-margin F80 supercar shipments should provide support. This robust margin profile ensures Ferrari remains a reliable contributor of dividend income to its parent holding company.
8.
Disciplined expenses: EXOR reported in its 2025 annual report that it has maintained its corporate head-office costs at exceptionally low levels relative to its total asset base. The holding company reported recurring general and administrative management costs of 8 basis points of gross asset value in 2025. This cost efficiency improved from 6 basis points of gross asset value reported in 2024. This performance beats the company's internal target to keep management expenses below 10 basis points of its gross assets.

Capital Allocation and Disposals

Value accretive: EXOR explained in its March 2026 analyst call that it has utilized targeted share buybacks to capitalize on the substantial discount of its listed shares. The company completed a 1 billion euro share buyback tender offer in April 2025 at a discount exceeding 50%. This major transaction added 4.7 euros per share to the firm's net asset value, illustrating efficient capital allocation. Retiring undervalued equity allows management to accrue net asset value value to remaining shareholders.
10.
Renewed repurchases: EXOR announced in a press release on September 22, 2026, a new share buyback program of up to 500 million euros to address the persistent market discount. Chief Executive Officer John Elkann stated that the market price fails to reflect the intrinsic value of the investment portfolio. The company intends to execute these purchases on the open market before publishing its next results in March 2027. These actions demonstrate that buybacks remain a crucial capital allocation strategy for the Agnelli family.

Successful exits: EXOR documented in its September 2026 half-year report that it completed several key portfolio divestments at attractive returns during the first half of 2026. The company reached an agreement in September 2026 to divest its stake in energy technology provider Welltec. This transaction is expected to deliver a multiple of 2.4 times invested capital upon its scheduled completion in early 2027. Monetizing mature holdings at book value increases total deployable cash to approximately 4 billion euros.
12.
Comparable precedent: Impax Environmental Markets reported in its 2025 annual report on March 30, 2026, that it represents a comparable holding entity that successfully utilized intensive buyback programs to manage its share discount. The board authorized its corporate broker to purchase 49.45 million shares, or 20.6% of outstanding capital, during 2025. This extensive repurchase program reduced the average market discount from 9.8% to 7.0%. Ferrari's outstanding price-and-mix growth cannot fully offset severe margin contractions or dividend suspensions at Stellantis and CNH Industrial. Consequently, EXOR's core strengths may prove insufficient to outperform the MSCI unless its major industrial holdings achieve rapid operational recoveries.

Section 3 – Value Creation from Large Scale Market Purchases

Section summary
  1. EXOR faces significant hurdles in replicating the success of its legacy assets through new large scale public market purchases.
  2. Recent underperformance at Clarivate and unlisted healthcare write downs at Institut Mérieux demonstrate the high execution risk of newer capital deployment.

Criteria for New Capital Deployment

1.
Strategic parameters: EXOR Chief Financial Officer Guido de Boer stated during the September 24, 2026, results call that the firm targets acquiring 15% to 20% stakes in large listed companies. This strategy requires an investment size of at least 2 billion euros, representing approximately 5% of its gross asset value. Investors expect these large-scale purchases to diversify the portfolio and establish EXOR as an active shareholder that can add long term value to target companies.
2.
Prudent deployment: EXOR Chief Executive Officer John Elkann emphasized in March 2026 that the company prefers patience over rapid deployment during highly turbulent market conditions. The company intends to use its cash reserves or debt headroom first, but remains open to monetizing existing assets if superior opportunities arise. Analysts observe that this disciplined capital deployment is designed to protect the balance sheet while positioning EXOR for strategic acquisitions when valuations are favorable.
3.
Massive liquidity: EXOR reported in its September 2026 interim presentation that it has built up approximately 4 billion euros of deployable cash following several successful divestments. This capital pool grew from 1.4 billion euros at the end of 2025 due to distributions from Lingotto and structural portfolio simplification. Management claims this financial strength provides the flexibility needed to execute another major investment of the same magnitude as the Philips transaction.
4.
High bar: EXOR admits that acquiring a new public company requires meeting a very high hurdle relative to executing share buybacks. The company's management has not yet committed to a specific allocation split between return of capital and new acquisitions. Observers note that unless EXOR can acquire stakes that yield higher returns than its own discounted equity, new investments may dilute net asset value growth for shareholders.

Morgan Stanley reported on November 4, 2026, that Clarivate has experienced operational difficulties and intense debate regarding artificial intelligence disruption. Section 4 – Stellantis Turnaround Feasibility and Precedents

Section summary
  1. Stellantis faces significant headwinds in achieving an operational turnaround within two years due to high inventory and structural integration issues.
  2. Automotive precedents demonstrate that structural cost-cutting and platform consolidation can restore profitability but require extensive time and run execution risks.

Operational Hurdles and Inventory Trajectory

1.
Severe cash outflow: Stellantis reported a massive industrial free cash outflow of approximately 4 billion euros in the third quarter of 2026. This severe liquidity drain was compounded by high dealer inventories that reached 99 days in October 2026, representing a substantial deviation from normalized operational levels. Management is implementing the FaSTLAne turnaround plan to clear these inventories and restore industrial free cash flow by late 2028. Consequently, the holding company's short-term earnings per share remain highly sensitive to whether these dealer stock corrections succeed.
2.
Slow market stabilization: Jefferies reported on March 4, 2026, that Stellantis' US inventory stood at 332k units, which equated to 83 days of supply. This high level was particularly pronounced in premium divisions, where Ram full-size inventory reached 106 days and Jeep dealer stock stood at 108 days in early 2026. To address these issues, the company has taken aggressive pricing cuts and adjusted production shifts to match actual customer demand. These inventory drag corrections are crucial because persistent oversupply severely dilutes vehicle margins and holding company returns.
3.
Pragmatic leadership adjustments: Stellantis appointed new regional leadership under Antonio Filosa to transition the North American product portfolio toward market-appropriate pricing. Expert interviews from October 2026 indicate that the previous leadership team underestimated North American market needs by pushing expensive electric vehicles that consumers rejected. While the new team is working to re-establish regional empowerment and brand prioritization, executing this transition remains a daunting task. Achieving these goals is essential to prevent further operating margin degradation over the next 18 months.
4.
Supplier relationship strains: Stellantis pursued aggressive industrial synergies and structural cost savings that severely strained its broader supply chain. Former directors noted in June 2025 that excessive costsqueezing targets led to multiple vendor bankruptcies and commercial underperformance. These supply chain disruptions directly impacted vehicle quality and delayed key product launches. Resolving these vendor disputes is necessary for the company to stabilize its manufacturing operations and support future EPS growth.

Precedents of Historical Automotive Turnarounds

5.
Successful platform sharing: Peugeot Invest and its operating predecessor PSA completed a highly successful integration of Opel following its acquisition from General Motors in 2017. Former M&A directors noted in November 2026 that PSA restored Opel to profitability by rapidly implementing purchasing synergies and streamlining redundant corporate layers. However, this historical turnaround suggests that combining complex vehicle technologies and physical platforms typically takes several years. This precedent shows that while cost-cutting delivers near-term cash, long-term platform consolidation is slow to materialize.
6.
Prolonged margin recovery: Renault Group navigated a complex operational restructuring between 2020 and 2024 to restore its operating margins. Industry experts noted in November 2026 that Renault lowered its break-even point by outsourcing small vehicle development and reducing fixed assets. Despite these cost successes, Renault remains highly vulnerable to intense pricing competition and European regulatory compliance penalties. This precedent indicates that automakers must maintain high capital discipline to sustain industrial cash flows during cyclical downturns.
7.
Drastic capacity rationalization: Ford Motor Company executed a painful strategic reset in late 2025 by taking a massive 19.5 billion dollar asset impairment charge. This sweeping restructuring involved cancelling three electric vehicle programs, ending F-150 Lightning production, and exiting underutilized joint venture plants. Ford also slashed its global production plans by 265k units in 2025 to aggressively clear dealer stocks. These decisive actions allowed Ford to normalize its dealer inventory to 56 days by the end of 2025.
8.
Synergy execution limitations: General Motors achieved substantial operational savings through structural restructuring and asset sales between 2009 and 2014. Financial records show GM continuously optimized its capital allocation, culminating in the complete sale of its loss-making Opel division in 2017. This clean exit allowed GM to defend its highly profitable North American truck franchise and expand margins. This historical comparison suggests that Stellantis may need to rationalize its weak brands to protect its core cash-generating divisions.

9.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank reported on February 16, 2026, that Stellantis decided to suspend its dividend and issue up to 5 billion euros in subordinated perpetual hybrid bonds. This conservative capital action indicates that the manufacturer's short-term industrial cash burn is far more severe than previously assumed. The dividend suspension directly undermines a core pillar of EXOR's expected financial recovery, proving that operational turnarounds face structural blocks that limit immediate shareholder returns.
10.
Brand identity dilution: Stellantis utilized common vehicle platforms across its highly fragmented portfolio of 14 distinct brands. Former planning managers stated in June 2026 that using identical platforms and shared components led to significant brand dilution. Customers have struggled to differentiate premium models like Alfa Romeo from mainstream offerings, weakening Stellantis' overall pricing power. Unless management can establish unique brand identities, achieving a sustainable operational turnaround remains unlikely within two years.
11.
Severe software deficits: Stellantis experienced chronic delays in developing its proprietary software-defined vehicle platforms. Former engineering leaders noted in June 2026 that the company's traditional bureaucratic steering committees and slow decision-making cultures hindered digital innovation. Consequently, the automaker has been forced to rely on third-party software partnerships that dilute long-term margins. This technological gap represents a major structural barrier that hinder long-term competitiveness against agile Chinese competitors.

Peer Margin Benchmarks

This profitability level contracted significantly from the 10.5% segment margin reported in 2024 as shipment volumes fell. This severe contraction demonstrates that CNH remains highly vulnerable to volume declines during cyclical downturns, making the 16% to 17% target look highly optimistic.

CNH Industrial segment adjusted EBIT margins for 2025 and 2024 Superior peer execution: Deere and Company reported a robust operating margin of 15.4% for its Production and Precision Agriculture segment in the fiscal year 2025. This performance declined from a peak margin of 21.7% in 2024 as market demand normalized.
