Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the behavior of forward price-to-earnings multiples during structural transformations is essential because consumer staples valuation is heavily driven by volume-led organic sales growth.
  2. Structural rebasing from divestitures and demergers directly impacts earnings-per-share trajectories and cost of capital, presenting both opportunities and risks for global consumer staples investors.

Section 2 – Nestlé Turnaround Cycles and Valuation Drivers

Section summary
  1. Operational turnarounds at Nestlé are fundamentally driven by the acceleration of organic volume growth.
  2. Structural portfolio optimization and divestitures temporarily diluting earnings frequently precede multiple expansion.
  3. Adjusted core valuation multiples excluding associate holdings align Nestlé closely with consumer staples peers.

Campaign of 2017 to 2019

1.
Activist pressure: Activist pressure from Third Point starting in June 2017 forced Nestlé's management to adopt its first formal operating margin target of 17.5% to 18.5%. CEO Mark Schneider implemented these targets with increased urgency, driving structural changes across the portfolio. This cultural transformation aimed to restore investor confidence and accelerate top-line execution.
2.
Record margins: Nestlé successfully delivered on its operating margin targets in 2019, reaching an underlying trading operating margin of 17.6% one year ahead of schedule. This 60 basis point improvement was driven by factory footprint optimization, manufacturing overhead reductions, and purchasing consolidation. Consequently, underlying earnings per share grew by 11.1% in constant currency, demonstrating the success of the operational efficiency program.
3.
Strategic divestitures: Nestlé accelerated its capital recycling by divesting non-core underperforming assets like the US confectionery business to Ferrero for CHF 2.8 billion in cash. This transaction allowed management to refocus on high-growth categories such as Petcare and Coffee, which offered superior margin structures. Divestitures acted as a margin tailwind, helping Nestlé hit its profitability targets early.
4.
Accelerated returns: Nestlé utilized divestment proceeds to return significant capital to shareholders, completing a mammoth share buyback program in December 2019. During 2019 alone, the group repurchased CHF 9.7 billion of shares, which contributed 1.9% to underlying EPS growth. Simultaneously, net debt decreased to CHF 27.1 billion as strong free cash flow generation offset shareholder returns.

Transition of 2025 to 2026

5.
Strategic shift: The appointment of Philipp Navratil as CEO in 2026 marked a strategic shift to stabilize Nestlé's core execution and restore volume led growth trajectory. Management prioritized "real internal growth" (RIG) over pricing, targeting sustainable RIG above 2% in the medium term. This operational pivot aimed to address prior execution missteps and defend market share against private labels.
6.
Encouraging rebound: Nestlé delivered an encouraging turnaround in Q3 2026, with organic sales growth accelerating to 4% and real internal growth reaching 2.8%. This performance was driven by structural recoveries in US Petcare and Europe, where temporary de-listings and inventory destocking headwinds began to fade. However, pricing moderated to 1.1%, reflecting the planned transition toward volume-led growth.

Valuation Milestones and Multiple Behavior

9.
Severe contraction: Nestlé's forward multiple reached historical lows of approximately 14.9 times during periods of prolonged peak operational stress and execution struggles. This valuation contraction was driven by persistent execution issues, distributor inventory build-ups, and successive downward earnings revisions. Investors demanded a steeper discount as dividend coverage deteriorated and leverage remained elevated.
10.
Optimistic re-rating: Valuation multiples typically inflected upward before actual estimate upgrades materialized once volume growth turned positive again. For instance, following the Q3 2026 volume beat, Nestlé's headline multiple re-rated to 17.9 times forward earnings. This initial expansion reflected market optimism regarding execution and the stabilization of Petcare demand.
11.
Delayed stabilization: Sell-side earnings-per-share estimates stabilized only after consecutive quarters of sustained positive volume delivery and execution consistency. While organic sales growth forecasts for FY26 began trending toward 4.0%, actual EPS estimates remained flat due to currency headwinds and portfolio updates. This delay suggests that multiple expansion precedes the bottoming of the earnings revision cycle.
12.
Contrary evidence: Western Europe scanner data showed Nestlé value sales actually declined by 0.3% value in late October 2026 as volume growth moderated. This deceleration suggests that the volume-led recovery remains fragile and subject to regional consumer headwinds. If consumer demand in key categories like coffee remains soft, multiple expansion could stall prematurely.

Fair Multiple and L'Oréal Stake

13.
Distorted valuation: Current broker consensus values Nestlé's headline multiple at approximately 17.6 times forward earnings, which represents an eight percent sector premium. However, this multiple is heavily distorted by Nestlé's 20.1% associate stake in L'Oréal. Adjusting for this stake reduces Nestlé's core forward P/E multiple to approximately 16.4 times.
14.
Tax adjusted valuation: Morgan Stanley applies a mark-to-market valuation to the L'Oréal stake, adjusting for a 50% capital gains tax rate of 30%. This rigorous treatment yields an adjusted core valuation that aligns Nestlé in line with its consumer staples peers. Gradual monetization of this stake remains a key strategic option to fund future share buybacks.
15.
Execution hurdles: To exceed its historical median forward multiple of 17.7 times, Nestlé must deliver sustainable real internal growth above 2%. Brokers remain cautious, noting that free cash flow coverage of the dividend limits strategic flexibility. Rebuilding gross margins while navigating commodity inflation remains a critical threshold for sustainable multiple expansion.
16.
Other side of the story: Investors may overvalue the impact of the volume turnaround while ignoring the structural drag of prolonged high balance sheet leverage. If Net Debt to EBITDA remains near 2.85 times, Nestlé cannot easily resume share buybacks without monetizing its L'Oréal holding. Consequently, the forward multiple may remain capped near its sector-average level until substantial deleveraging occurs.

Section 3 – Structural Carve-outs and Demergers: Unilever and Danone

Section summary
  1. Structural turnarounds at Unilever and Danone demonstrate that portfolio separations and execution focus are major drivers of forward multiple expansion.
  2. Demergers and divestitures of lower-growth categories temporarily depress headline earnings but position Remaining operations as higher-margin pure-plays.
  3. Valuation re-ratings typically occur when organic volume growth inflects positively, even during periods of mechanical earnings rebasing.

Unilever Portfolio Reshaping

1.
Significant demerger: Unilever completed the strategic demerger of its Ice Cream business, operating as The Magnum Ice Cream Company, in December 2025. This transaction aimed to simplify the group's corporate structure and eliminate a historically capital-intensive drag on operating margins. Management rotated approximately 15% of the corporate portfolio in 2025 to sharpen focus on core categories.
2.
Transformational combination: Unilever reached an agreement in March 2026 to combine its global Foods division with McCormick in a highly anticipated structural partnership. This divestment represents the final stage of Unilever's transition into a pure-play household and personal care company. Dedicated integration teams are advancing regulatory clearances to support a transaction close by mid-2027.
3.
Accelerated execution: Under CEO Fernando Fernandez, Unilever implemented its Growth Action Plan to stabilize regional market execution and drive competitive brand reinvestment. Senior leadership changes and structural simplifications under the program helped remove distributor channel conflicts and restore historic gross margin levels. Investors responded positively to the accelerated pace of portfolio pruning compared to prior sluggish exits.
4.
Optimistic outlook: Deutsche Bank Research raised its investment recommendation on Unilever to Buy in late 2026, citing improved earnings visibility. The broker highlighted the structural benefits of Unilever's digital marketing transformation and systematic white-collar productivity gains. This operational transition provides substantial near-term defensive liquidity, which helps insulate investors from broader macroeconomic headwinds.

Valuation Dynamics and Rebasing

5.
Severe dilution: Structural analysis from Rothschild & Co Redburn indicated that the planned McCormick transaction would reduce Unilever's pro forma 2026 EPS by 32%. This steep decline includes significant stranded costs that management plans to mitigate via share consolidations. Investors accept these transaction mechanics because the remaining core personal care business carries structurally superior organic growth.
6.
Valuation compression: Research from Jefferies took a more cautious stance, retaining an Underperform rating on Unilever due to competitive pricing pressures. The broker warned that lapping temporary 2026 tailwinds could result in slower volume growth than consensus estimates. Furthermore, higher capital expenditure and advertising requirements might limit the pace of underlying operating margin expansion.
7.
Re-rating trajectory: Standalone valuation metrics showed Unilever's forward multiple adjusting before actual transaction closures. Prior to the New York investor day, Unilever traded at 17 times forward earnings, which implied approximately 18 times forward earnings excluding the Foods business. This multi-stage re-rating demonstrates how the market prices structural clarity well ahead of final accounting adjustments.

Restored competitiveness: CEO Antoine de Saint-Affrique launched the Renew Danone turnaround strategy in March 2022 to address a decade of severe operational underperformance. The strategic turnaround focused on restoring competitiveness in core dairy segments and active rotation of underperforming brands. Initial execution steps required reinvesting 100% of realized overhead savings back into product innovations.

For example, the Spanish yogurt portfolio was cut from ten brands to five core platforms. Simultaneously, the company scaled up high-growth platforms such as YoPro and GetPro to align with healthy eating trends.
11.
Systematic reinvestment: Corporate financial updates confirmed that Danone consistently increased its marketing and promotional spending through Chapter 2 of Renew Danone. The business completed key acquisitions like MADE Group and Huel to broaden exposure to active nutrition. These targeted investments were funded by a continuous white-collar productivity and factory footprint consolidation program.
12.
Encouraging momentum: Industry experts note that Danone's structural changes successfully shifted corporate accountability from centralized teams to local operating business units. Consequently, local management teams recovered market share across key retail categories like clinical and medical nutrition.

Volume Recovery and Multiples

13.

This operational consistency helped narrow Danone's historical 20% valuation discount relative to global consumer staples peers. Adjusted recurring operating margins improved by 44 basis points to 13.4%, matching consensus targets.
14.

15.

The broker raised its target price to 80 euros, implying a target multiple of 20.6 times on normalized 2026 earnings. This constructive stance reflects fading operational risks and visible volume acceleration across North American creamers.
16.

If birth rates in China continue to decline, highmargin Specialized Nutrition sales will face unavoidable pricing and volume headwinds. Consequently, the forward multiple could compress back toward 16.5 times as peak protein demand dynamics begin to normalize.
