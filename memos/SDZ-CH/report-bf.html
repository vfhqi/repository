Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. the global off-patent market enters a historic super-cycle driven by a massive USD 320 billion patent cliff
  2. high capital barriers and manufacturing complexity are consolidating the biosimilar market into an oligopolistic structure
  3. Chinese penicillin dumping created severe pricing deflation in Europe, but long-term margins are protected by specialized mix

Global market size and growth tailwinds

1.
Massive patent cliff: Off-patent opportunities represent an unprecedented USD 320 billion in upcoming biological patent expiries through the next decade.
2.
Global market scale: The generic drug industry is projected to expand from USD 431 billion in 2025 to USD 575 billion by 2030.
3.
Accelerating biosimilar adoption: The global biosimilar market is projected to surge from USD 35 billion in 2025 to USD 97 billion by 2030.
4.
US expansion outlook: The US biosimilars market is forecasted to scale from USD 23 billion in 2025 to over USD 90 billion by 2034.
5.
Sustained industry growth: Sandoz estimates the off-patent market is growing at 5% long-term versus 10% for the high-value biosimilar sub-segment.
6.
Long-term patent exposure: The upcoming decade features USD 660 billion of branded sales losing exclusivity across the US and Europe.

Structural consolidation and scale barriers

7.
Oligopolistic market structure: Biosimilar categories mature rapidly and are dominated by just 1-4 players capturing over 10% volume share each.
8.
Substantial capital barriers: Developing a biosimilar requires USD 100 million to USD 200 million compared to only USD 1 million for small-molecule generics.
9.
Extreme volume concentration: Market leaders in successful biosimilar launches frequently command greater than 20% volume share of the total drug pool.
10.
Competitive Asian entrance: South Korea's Samsung Bioepis represents the Asian competitor most likely to achieve meaningful scale in regulated Western markets.
11.
Shrinking generic options: The lack of recent small-molecule industry innovation is creating a future volume headache for non-integrated generic players.
12.
High bioprocessing standards: Monoclonal antibody manufacturing presents quality and capex barriers that restrict the market to well-capitalized players.
13.
Entrenched distribution moats: Successful commercialization requires long-term hospital relationships across approximately 40 European markets that take years to build.

14.
Destructive import redirection: US tariffs forced Chinese suppliers to redirect penicillin volumes into Europe causing sharp price deflation in Q1 2026.
15.
Indian regulatory pressure: India's introduction of minimum pricing regulations restricted Chinese imports, further exacerbating excess supply in non-regulated European channels.
16.
Sandoz revenue drag: The penicillin pricing headwind created a <300 bps drag on Sandoz Generics top-line growth in Q1 2026.

Industry-wide antibiotic stress: Peer Orchid Pharma experienced 12% price erosion and 10% quantity erosion on a nine-month basis in Q3 FY26 78 79 p p g g Q; y g Sandoz Q1 2026 net sales breakdown by product franchise and geographic region.

Cyclical margin recovery: Orchid's EBITDA margin recovered sharply to 11.3% in Q4 FY26 from 0.8% sequentially, signaling stabilization.

Stable European margins: Vertically integrated peer Krka targets commoditised branded generics while maintaining a mid-term EBITDA margin floor above 25%

Premium pricing potential: Pure-play biosimilar developers like Alvotech target premium profitability with EBITDA margins of 40% to 45% by 2028.

Section 5 – Customer Side Analysis

Section summary
  1. Sandoz faces high customer concentration in the US via major consortia, but is mitigating channel power through innovative private-label PBM partnerships.
  2. European markets are highly fragmented and tender-driven, where Sandoz leverages its commercial scale and broad portfolio to secure dominant volume share.
  3. Customer switching barriers are reinforced by supply security and regulatory streamlining, which accelerates biosimilar substitution while reducing development costs.

Buyer profiles and US PBM channels

1.
Concentrated US purchasing power: Sandoz's US generics and biosimilars are marketed through three massive retail purchasing consortia.
2.
Identified major players: The primary retail generic distributors and GPOs driving US volume are Clarus One, Red Oak, and WBAD.
3.
Significant single-customer concentration: A single global customer accounted for 12% of total Sandoz net sales in FY2025, up from 11% in FY2024.
4.
Top three exposure: In historical disclosures, the top three customers accounted for 12%, 7%, and 6% of net sales, respectively.
5.
Distorting rebate structures: US pharmacy benefit channels are heavily controlled by three major PBMs that command over 90% of the market.
6.
Originator rebate trap: Traditional PBM rebate structures historically favored high-priced originator biologics because PBMs financially benefited from larger rebate spreads.
7.
Innovative private-label mitigation: Sandoz bypassed traditional PBM barriers by entering a co-manufacturing and private-label commercialization agreement with CVS Health's subsidiary, Cordavis. - Conference Preferred market access: The Cordavis agreement grants Sandoz's Hyrimoz preferred access to 65% of CVS Caremark's commercial template lives. - Sales Revenue Rapid volume conversion: Following the April 2024 formulary placement, Hyrimoz captured 14% of all US adalimumab claims within its first month.
8.
Fragmented medical benefits: In contrast to pharmacy benefits, physician-administered biologics in clinics and hospitals are governed by a buy-and-bill model. - Conference Provider margin drivers: Hospital and clinic adoption in the medical benefit space is driven directly by reimbursement economics and provider cost savings.

Fragmented European procurement: The European market spans over 40 individual country markets, each requiring localized sales forces and domestic procurement systems.
10.
Localized channel access: Navigating these 40 distinct commercial environments requires extensive government relations, hospital tender-management expertise, and local sales teams.

Portfolio packaging advantages: Sandoz utilizes its extensive product catalog to bundle generics and biosimilars, offering a one-stop solution that retail pharmacies prefer.
12.
Cross-selling substitution: Having a broad portfolio of traditional generics helps Sandoz drive biosimilar substitution to its own brands in retail channels.

German sick-fund tenders: In Germany, Sandoz's second-largest global market, the company negotiates directly with individual public health insurance funds (sick funds).

17.
Intractable pricing pressure: Hospital-administered tenders suffer from aggressive price-cutting by non- integrated Asian competitors like Celltrion and Biocon.
18.

Switching barriers and regulatory substitution rules

19.
High clinical switching barriers: Once a hospital or healthcare provider transitions patients to a biosimilar, they are highly reluctant to switch suppliers again.
20.
Supply security premium: Supply reliability is the primary concern for hospital GPOs, as product shortages trigger severe contractual penalties.
21.
Regulatory substitution rules: European countries increasingly permit pharmacy-level substitution of biosimilars, a trend that favors established historical players.
22.
US interchangeability edge: Sandoz secured first-to-market advantage for Jubbonti and Wyost as the first FDA-approved interchangeable denosumab biosimilars.
23.
Streamlined clinical development: The European Medicines Agency confirmed that comparative clinical efficacy trials may be waived for well-characterized biosimilars.
24.
Substantial cost reduction: Waiving Phase 3 trials lowers the development barrier for biosimilars, though manufacturing complexity and commercial scale remain high.
25.
Pipeline expansion potential: This regulatory shift lowers the peak revenue threshold required for Sandoz to profitably target smaller biologic molecules.
26.
Litigious originator defense: Branded originators deploy aggressive patent litigation and commercial contracting to delay biosimilar adoption.
27.
Stelara private-label dispute: Johnson & Johnson filed a lawsuit seeking to enjoin Sandoz and Samsung Bioepis from executing a private-label bStelara deal. Other side of the story: private-label PBM contracts grant extreme channel leverage; if partners demand deeper discounts, they could compress Sandoz's biosimilar margins.
