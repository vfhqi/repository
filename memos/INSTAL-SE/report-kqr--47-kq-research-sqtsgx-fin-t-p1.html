2.
Operating leverage: Fixed-cost operating leverage is the extent to which revenue changes flow into EBITA because costs do not move proportionately within the relevant period, usually one year. Instalco’s model is not a pure fixedcost model because materials, purchased services and subcontracting can flex with projects, while employee costs, local management, premises, vehicles, systems and central functions are slower to remove. The key analytical issue is therefore economic cost behaviour rather than the accounting label attached to each expense.
3.
Incremental margin: Incremental EBITA margin means the additional EBITA divided by the additional revenue between two periods, rather than the reported EBITA margin on total sales. It is the most direct test of whether returning volume is absorbing existing employees and overhead, although comparisons must control for mix, pricing, project completion, bad debts, acquisitions and exceptional items. A high incremental margin can be temporary if it reflects easy comparisons or released provisions rather than durable productivity. Cost elasticity and utilisation: Cost elasticity measures how much a cost changes when activity changes, while utilisation measures the proportion of available employee or production capacity generating billable work. Low utilisation leaves salaries and local overhead spread across fewer revenue-producing hours, so modest volume recovery can create disproportionate EBITA improvement until capacity becomes constrained. Conversely, a downturn can produce negative operating leverage if employees, premises and project supervision remain in place while revenue falls.
4.
Restructuring savings: Restructuring savings are recurring reductions in future operating expense after a programme has been implemented, whereas cost avoidance means preventing an expected increase or temporarily not incurring a cost. Cost elimination is permanent removal of a role, subsidiary, contract or activity, but the accounting benefit may be delayed by notice periods, closure costs, project completion and redeployment. The report therefore distinguishes announced savings from delivered savings and tests whether benefits survive a normalised volume environment. - Earnings Calls EPS transmission: EPS transmission is the path from revenue, utilisation or cost action to profit attributable to Instalco shareholders after amortisation, interest, tax, minority interests and share dilution. This matters because a higher EBITA forecast does not translate one-for-one into EPS when Fabri adds non-controlling interests, acquisition-related amortisation or debt-funded interest expense. Instalco’s Q2 2026 diluted EPS was SEK 0.45 versus SEK 0.42 a year earlier, despite EBITA rising to SEK 274 million from SEK 225 million, illustrating the need to follow the whole income statement.
5.
Business-model relevance: Instalco’s federation of more than 150 subsidiaries combines local customer relationships and profit responsibility with group purchasing, finance, acquisitions and business development. That structure can create operating leverage through shared knowledge, procurement and resource allocation, but it also duplicates finance and administration and limits the scope for central cuts without weakening local accountability. Sweden’s roughly 71% share of 2025 sales makes Swedish utilisation, pricing, labour agreements and legacy contracts the principal group EPS determinants.

Instalco’s decentralised operating model and central support functions Mix and risk: Service and renovation provide more recurring or less new-build-sensitive demand than new production, while 43% of 2025 sales came from fixed-price projects and 57% from cost-plus, partnering and other remuneration forms. Fixed-price work increases exposure to wage, material, subcontractor and execution-cost inflation, whereas service and partnering can provide better repricing or cost transparency. Acquisitions can add profitable capacity and diversify geography, but they also introduce goodwill, amortisation, minority claims and debt-funded cash requirements.

6.
International and balance-sheet effects: Fabri reported EUR 86 million of 2025 sales and a 12.7% EBITA margin, but only 51% ownership is consolidated from August 2026, so its contribution to group EPS is reduced by minority interests and affected by acquisition financing. Instalco’s Q2 2026 net debt to EBITDA was 2.6x, above its 2.5x target, making interest costs and deleveraging important constraints on the pace of further acquisitions or large restructuring expenditure. The analytical horizon is therefore 18 months to four years, separating near-term utilisation and exceptional-item effects from durable margin, wage and capital-allocation consequences.
7.
Related questions and method: Closely related questions concern whether Swedish margin recovery is sustainable, whether wages can be passed through fixed-price contracts, whether backlog quality and project controls are improving, whether bad debts normalise, and whether low-margin contracts signed in 2024 and 2025 roll off without replacement. They also concern how far back-office and procurement consolidation can proceed, whether country management adds more cost than it removes, whether Fabri’s German economics are repeatable, and whether entrepreneurs remain committed when central oversight becomes more hands-on. Company targets and management explanations are treated as claims, broker estimates as scenarios, peer evidence as comparative evidence, and historical precedents as tests of durability rather than proof of future performance. The central judgement is whether Instalco can reach approximately 8% EBITA in Sweden and the group by 2028 through utilisation, execution and normalisation alone, or whether price, mix, wage pass-through and selective structural cost reduction are also required.

Section 2 – Instalco Cost Structure and Operating Leverage

Accounting cost base and economic flexibility Instalco’s FY2025 accounting cost structure comprised materials and purchased services of SEK 6,379 million, other external costs of SEK 1,123 million, employee benefit costs of SEK 5,004 million, and depreciation, amortisation and impairment of SEK 541 million against net sales of SEK 13,598 million. These figures correspond to approximately 46.9% of sales for materials and purchased services and 36.8% for employee costs, while the company’s presentation rounds the underlying economic categories to 35% materials, 40% employees, 16% subcontractors, 2% transport equipment and 7% other. The difference shows why accounting classifications should not be treated as a direct fixed-versus-variable split, since subcontractor costs are embedded partly within purchased services and other external expenses rather than disclosed as a single audited income-statement line.

Materials are economically the most variable major cost because they are purchased in response to project activity, but their flexibility is constrained by project specifications, delivery commitments and customer timing. The company’s 2025 report states that purchasing decisions consider total cost, installation efficiency, reliability, durability, logistics and security of supply, implying that a low-price substitution is not always operationally available after a contract has been awarded. Materials therefore provide strong short-term cost elasticity on new work, but much less protection on fixed-price projects already in progress, where procurement savings may prevent margin erosion rather than create additional margin. Subcontractors provide a second important flexibility layer because external capacity can be reduced faster than permanent staffing when project volumes fall. Morgan Stanley’s 17 March 2020 analysis of SPIE found that approximately 38% of its cost base came from employees on its own payroll and that this share was 7% to 8% lower than in prior cycles because the company had increased its use of subcontracted labour. The comparison supports the inference that Instalco’s approximately 16% subcontractor share of costs is a meaningful shock absorber, although it does not establish how much of Instalco’s employee base can be replaced without impairing quality, customer relationships or project control.

Employee costs are less fixed than the 36.8% accounting ratio suggests, because field labour, temporary staffing, overtime and some project-linked personnel can be adjusted with workload. However, salaried managers, estimators, project leaders, finance staff and skilled technicians create a slowermoving cost layer, particularly in a federation where each local company retains operating responsibility. Instalco’s FY2025 average employee count was 6,042, down from 6,139 in 2024, but the company does not disclose a sufficiently detailed split between field employees, salaried employees, temporary labour and subcontractors to calculate a precise one-year fixed-cost percentage.

Utilisation, productivity and operating leverage The strongest evidence of economic operating leverage is the movement in revenue per employee rather than the reported cost percentages. DNB Carnegie calculated that Instalco’s Q3 2025 rolling-twelve-month revenue per employee was SEK 2.22 million, only 2% above Q4 2019, while personnel expense per employee had increased approximately 18% over the same period. The same analysis found that revenue per employee rose 15% to 20% between 2021 and 2023 before falling 11% from its Q3 2023 peak, which explains why lower activity translated into disproportionate margin pressure despite a relatively flexible reported cost base.

Instalco’s recent order-book data indicate that capacity absorption is improving, but the available evidence does not provide a complete employee or backlog-per-employee series from 2019 through Q2 2026. At Q2 2026, the group had 6,085 average employees, 6,298 employees at period-end and an order backlog of SEK 10,937 million, equivalent to approximately SEK 1.74 million of backlog per period-end employee calculated from the reported figures.

The backlog was 17% above the prior-year level, while DNB Carnegie described backlog per employee as one of the most useful forward indicators for margin improvement.

The Q2 2026 result demonstrates positive but not unlimited drop-through from returning volume. Net sales increased SEK 362 million year on year to SEK 3,874 million, while EBITA increased SEK 49 million to SEK 274 million, implying a calculated incremental EBITA margin of approximately 13.5% for the quarter. This is consistent with utilisation-driven operating leverage, but it is materially below a theoretical full fixed-cost dropthrough because materials, purchased services, subcontractors, project costs and variable selling costs rose with activity.

The same pattern appears in the first half of 2026, although seasonal effects complicate interpretation. Mix, contracts and working capital Instalco’s mix provides partial protection against volume declines because service and renovation represented 37% and 33% of FY2025 sales respectively, while new production represented 30%. The company states that smaller renovation and service assignments are less dependent on new construction starts, and service reached 35% of sales in Q2 2026. This mix lowers revenue volatility relative to a pure new-build contractor, but service work can still carry fixed technician capacity and may not absorb all project employees when larger assignments finish.

Contract structure limits the extent to which higher volume automatically improves margins. Fixed-price projects represented 43% of FY2025 sales, while cost-plus and other remuneration forms represented 57%, so more than half of sales had some mechanism for cost transparency or reimbursement, although partnering contracts may still include fixed-price elements and execution risk. Instalco recognises fixed-price project revenue over time using percentage of completion, meaning errors in estimated costs to complete can affect both current EBITA and future revisions to contract profitability.

Working capital is another form of operating leverage because strong growth can require cash before customer collection. At Q2 2026, working capital was SEK 321 million, or 2.3% of rolling twelve-month sales, compared with 1.6% at year-end 2025 and 2.4% in Q2 2025. Accounts receivable were SEK 2,009 million and contract assets SEK 808 million, while the company said Q2 operating cash flow was weakened by capital tied up in receivables after strong invoicing, so higher sales can temporarily increase financing costs rather than immediately translate into EPS.

Bad debts are economically variable but can be highly discontinuous. Instalco’s provision for doubtful debts increased from SEK 24 million at year-end 2023 to SEK 72 million at year-end 2024, and the company recognised a SEK 64 million Northvolt receivable write-down in Q1 2025. DNB Carnegie estimated that normalising baddebt provisions could improve EBITA margin by approximately 40 basis points, but that benefit is a reduction in exceptional leakage rather than structural operating leverage and cannot be assumed to recur every year.

Customer concentration and structural limits Customer concentration is moderate, which reduces the risk that one project cancellation suddenly strands the entire cost base. Instalco reported approximately 2,000 customers in 2025, with the five largest accounting for approximately 14% of sales and the largest customer contributing less than 5%. An expert interview with a Skanska production manager nevertheless indicated that large construction groups often award work through regional project managers, so a reported group-level customer concentration measure may understate local relationship dependence.

Instalco’s decentralisation creates both operating leverage and operating friction. The model centralises purchasing, acquisitions, finance, communications and strategy while leaving customers, sales, production, employees and profit responsibility at local units. DNB Carnegie argues that many subsidiaries still manage their own finance functions and that co-locating back-office work, improving resource planning and reducing idle time could raise revenue per employee without cutting productive technicians.

Contrary evidence: the observed revenue-per-employee decline indicates that Instalco’s flexibility has not prevented negative operating leverage in the recent downturn. DNB Carnegie found that revenue per employee fell 11% from its Q3 2023 peak, while personnel expense per employee had risen substantially since 2019. The implication is that the group can obtain meaningful margin recovery from better utilisation, but a return to 8% EBITA cannot safely be attributed to volume alone unless local overhead, staffing productivity and project execution improve together. Other side of the story: the model has demonstrated a substantial capacity to flex through subcontractors, selective project intake and service mix. Instalco closed or merged eight loss-making subsidiaries and adjusted employee and subcontractor capacity during the late-2024 downturn, while the Q2 2026 recovery produced a 7.1% group EBITA margin and 13.5% calculated incremental EBITA margin. The evidence therefore supports meaningful one-year operating leverage, but not a predominantly fixed-cost model or a durable assumption that every additional krona of revenue will generate a high incremental margin.

Section summary

Swedish labour framework

Sweden has no statutory national minimum wage, so minimum pay for relevant installation occupations is primarily determined through collective agreements rather than legislation. Bravida reports collectivebargaining coverage of 80% to 100% for employees in Sweden, Norway and Finland, while coverage in Denmark is lower and not disclosed within the same range. This means Swedish employers generally retain managerial flexibility over deployment, scheduling, overtime and recruitment, but cannot treat collectively agreed wage floors as optional where the relevant agreement applies.

The retrieved evidence confirms that Swedish collective agreements due for negotiation in 2025 covered a broad part of the labour market, but it does not contain the signed electrical or plumbing-and-ventilation agreements themselves. The evidence therefore does not independently validate the reported approximately 8% increase in minimum wages for the spring 2027 rounds or the estimates that actual employee-pay increases may be closer to 3%.

Those figures should remain scenario assumptions until the relevant agreements, wage ladders, implementation dates and employer-side interpretation are available. employment law does not make the entire employee-cost line fixed for four years, because employers can reduce headcount, decline temporary labour, limit overtime and reorganise operations when demand weakens. The practical adjustment is slower than the accounting cost classification suggests because notice periods, consultation obligations, redeployment, recruitment scarcity and the need to retain project-management capability delay the benefit of reductions. Instalco’s reduction of approximately 110 employees and 100 subcontractors during its 2024 programme demonstrates that capacity can be removed, but also that the process involves restructuring costs and delayed delivery. The labour constraint is more acute for skilled technicians than for general administrative roles, because technical installation projects require certified and experienced electricians, plumbers, ventilation specialists, supervisors and project managers. An expert interviewed in October 2026 described qualified installation and commissioning teams as a strategic procurement risk for SPIE, with workforce availability increasingly more important than obtaining the lowest contractor rate. For Instalco, aggressive permanent headcount reductions could therefore lower near-term cost but damage future utilisation, tender capacity and customer service when the market recovers.

Wage settlement transmission

The most relevant historical wage comparison is the Swedish 2023–2024 agreement, which Morgan Stanley described as a two-year settlement providing approximately 4.1% wage growth in 2023 and 3.3% in 2024. That settlement was materially below the approximately 8% minimum-wage increase now attributed to the prospective 2027 rounds, although minimum rates and average actual pay are not equivalent measures. The comparison suggests that the 2027 headline could overstate Instalco’s consolidated payroll impact if most employees already earn above the agreement floor and receive negotiated increases closer to the reported 3% actual-pay estimate. 178 2023–2024 wage round nevertheless created a recurring cost increase even where volume was weak, because employee pay does not fall automatically when project activity declines.

Instalco’s employee costs represented approximately 36.8% of FY2025 sales in the accounting data used elsewhere in this report, so a 3% wage increase applied broadly across the employee base would mechanically equal roughly 1.10% of sales before productivity, pricing, mix or staffing offsets, calculated as 36.8% multiplied by 3%. A wage-floor increase of 8% applied to the same entire employee-cost base would imply approximately 2.94% of sales, but that is a stress calculation rather than a forecast because it assumes every employee receives the full increase. impact on reported margins is likely to be smaller than the mechanical payroll calculation when activity recovers, because higher utilisation spreads salaried supervision, premises and local administration across more revenue.

Instalco’s Q2 2026 Swedish sales increased 10.5% and Swedish EBITA margin improved from 6.6% to 7.0%, showing that operational recovery can more than offset ordinary wage pressure in a favourable quarter. The result does not show that wages were fully passed through, because the company did not disclose the wage, price and productivity contributions separately. Bravida’s experience provides a useful peer test because its Swedish EBITA margin was 6.7% in 2025 despite Swedish sales declining from SEK 14,118 million to SEK 13,373 million.

Fixed-price contract exposure

Instalco recognises installation revenue over time using percentage of completion, with progress calculated from costs incurred relative to estimated total costs at completion. This accounting method makes wage and material assumptions economically important before a project is finished, because a revision in estimated costs can change current-period revenue, margin and provisions. It also means that a fixed-price contract signed in 2026 can carry wage exposure into 2027 and 2028 even when reported sales have already begun.

Bravida’s disclosed contract framework illustrates the main risk allocation, with partnering generally using costplus arrangements, subcontracting commonly using fixed prices, design-and-build typically using fixed prices, and smaller service work priced through hours, materials and price lists. Long-term projects and service agreements often include index clauses for wages and materials, but the company separately warns that fixed prices can expose it to major price increases in the supply chain.

Instalco’s 2025 mix of approximately 43% fixed-price work and 57% cost-plus, partnering and other remuneration forms therefore provides some protection, but does not eliminate exposure because partnering arrangements can still contain target prices, incentives and execution risk.

The practical pass-through depends on contract duration, customer bargaining power, procurement timing and the specificity of the escalation clause. Deutsche Bank noted in 2022 that Swedish and Danish markets often lacked contract indexation and that rapid increases in electrical, sanitary and ventilation materials made the pricing environment unpredictable. A 2026 contract without automatic wage or material indexation therefore requires Instalco either to price expected inflation upfront, secure supplier quotations, negotiate change orders or absorb the difference.

The timing mismatch can be material on projects extending beyond one year, because wage agreements may change during execution while the customer price remains fixed. Instalco’s Östersund renovation project with expected completion in autumn 2027 and its Stockholm renovation project with expected completion in 2029 illustrate how long technical installation commitments can remain exposed to labour availability, productivity and cost escalation. Long duration increases the value of indexation and early procurement, but it also increases the risk that a weak tender decision locks in an inadequate margin for several reporting periods. Materials inflation creates a parallel risk because copper, electrical equipment, pipe, ventilation products and energy-intensive components may move differently from wages. Veidekke reported that Swedish construction-cost inflation was expected to rise toward spring 2027 and that material prices, although relatively stable, could increase as energy and electricity prices rose.

Even if actual wage growth is only 3%, a simultaneous material or subcontractor shock can still compress fixed-price margins unless procurement was locked at award or the contract includes a functioning escalation mechanism.

Pricing power and service repricing

Service work should provide better repricing opportunities than long fixed-price projects because smaller assignments are often billed through hourly rates, materials and price lists. Scenario implications through 2030

A 3% actual-pay scenario would create an initial mechanical pressure of approximately 1.10% of Instalco sales if the entire 36.8% employee-cost ratio were exposed, calculated from the FY2025 accounting mix. If half of that pressure were recovered through price, productivity and utilisation, the residual margin effect would be approximately 0.55 percentage points, calculated rather than reported. That residual would consume more than half of the 100-basis-point gap between Instalco Sweden’s 7.0% Q2 2026 margin and an 8% target if no other improvement occurred.

An 8% wage-floor scenario is materially more demanding because the mechanical exposure would approach 2.94% of sales if applied to the entire employee-cost base, calculated from the same accounting ratio. The scenario is likely conservative because minimum-wage changes do not automatically apply in full to employees already above the floor, but it shows why the 2027 agreement cannot be dismissed as immaterial without the wage distribution by occupation and subsidiary.

The earnings effect would be amplified if fixed-price projects signed in 2026 extend through the agreement date without indexation or customer-approved change orders. Under a favourable scenario, Swedish utilisation improves, backlog converts at acceptable margins, service and partnering gain share, and pricing discipline offsets most of the wage increase. DNB Carnegie’s September 2026 view expects Instalco’s margin to approach 8% as Instalco 2.0 effects, recovering Swedish activity and improving pricing conditions combine.

The evidence supports a plausible 2027–2028 EBITA recovery, but it does not establish that the recovery can occur without price or mix assistance because the broker explicitly includes improving pricing conditions. Under a less favourable scenario, wage inflation is closer to the headline minimum increase, fixed-price projects absorb the cost, and customers use weak tender conditions to resist repricing. DNB Carnegie identifies industry-wide price pressure from robust infrastructure and public-sector volumes alongside weaker private construction demand, which can leave contractors competing for work while labour costs continue to rise.

In that case, Instalco could experience positive revenue growth but limited EPS conversion because higher payroll, subcontractor rates, project provisions, interest expense and minority interests absorb the EBITA improvement. 209 EPS sensitivity should therefore be expressed through margin and pass-through rather than a single wage assumption, because Instalco’s broker model shows that higher EBITA does not flow one-for-one into EPS after updated amortisation, financial and minority assumptions. Other side of the story: labour inflation may be less damaging than the stress cases imply because the market is already constrained by skilled-worker shortages, which can strengthen the pricing position of established installers with reliable delivery capacity. ’s experience indicates that scarce skilled labour can coexist with rising revenue per employee and margin expansion when companies move toward higher-value services and enforce pricing discipline.

Instalco’s own emphasis on value-added pricing, disciplined project selection and service stabilisation provides a credible route to offsetting ordinary wage growth, although the evidence is insufficient to conclude that the 2027 agreement will be fully passed through.

What the model protects

Instalco has more than 150 subsidiaries, with local companies responsible for customers, sales, production, employees and profit, while the central organisation provides procurement, finance, business development, acquisitions and coordination. Instalco’s own 2024 model description says central coordination already negotiates input and insurance contracts, supports resource planning and provides business-development assistance, while subsidiaries retain their names, brands, cultures and project responsibility. This means further centralisation should be judged against an existing shared-services base, so the remaining opportunity is likely narrower than a simple comparison with a fully decentralised contractor.

The local CEO is not merely an administrative role because Instalco explicitly links subsidiary autonomy to entrepreneurship, customer proximity and fast decision-making. Low-risk efficiency pool

Finance is the most credible area for incremental savings because Instalco’s local entities use a group-wide reporting system, but still perform their own bookkeeping and financial reporting. A centre-led model could standardise chart-of-accounts governance, accounts payable, payroll controls, month-end routines and credit monitoring without transferring customer or project decisions to headquarters, improving recurring EBITA while preserving local accountability.

ERP and IT consolidation could create a similar benefit through common data, automated invoicing, purchaseorder controls and project-margin reporting. Procurement should be expanded through a centre-led rather than fully centralised model because Instalco already has centrally negotiated supplier agreements, while local companies remain close to customers and project requirements. The relevant opportunity is better spend visibility, preferred-supplier compliance, payment terms and category negotiation, whereas forcing every local purchase through headquarters could slow urgent work and reduce flexibility.

InstallatørGruppen provides numerical evidence for the potential scale of procurement and commercial coordination, reporting DKK16 million of portfolio-company wholesaler savings in 2025, more than DKK8 million saved on insurance, car leasing, telecom and fuel, and DKK94 million of internal cross-sales, up from DKK43 million in 2024. Caverion reported writedowns of EUR 27.1 million in 2017, EUR 9.3 million in 2018, EUR 17.1 million in 2019, EUR 12.8 million in 2020 and EUR 4.0 million in 2021, while also noting forecast changes across more than 2,000 projects.

Eltel’s project experience likewise shows that a handful of large delayed projects can create material exposure and consume working capital before customer claims are resolved. - Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls Instalco reports fewer major negative deviations after introducing stronger project selection and early follow-up, but it does not publish project-loss frequency, provisions by cohort, positive and negative forecast changes, or the distribution of margin revisions.

A quarterly project-risk appendix or audited bridge from original tender margin to final margin by project cohort would make the durability of Instalco 2.0 easier to assess. modelling EPS remains less certain than EBITA because Fabri creates minority interests, acquisition financing increases debt, and acquired intangible assets create amortisation. DNB Carnegie raised 2026–2027 EBITA estimates by 2% to 7% after Q2 2026, but explicitly stated that the EPS effect was less pronounced because of updated amortisation, financial and minority assumptions.

Instalco’s diluted EPS increased to SEK 0.72 in the first half of 2026 from SEK 0.57, while EBITA increased to SEK 475 million from SEK 348 million, demonstrating that operating improvement does not transmit one-for-one to attributable earnings.

A robust EPS model would require a quarterly bridge from EBITA to EBIT, interest, tax, minority interests, amortisation, warrants and shares outstanding, together with explicit scenarios for Swedish margin, wage passthrough, Fabri ownership and net debt. The three outstanding warrant programmes and the stepwise Fabri ownership structure make diluted attributable EPS especially sensitive to assumptions that are not visible in the operating-margin disclosures. The next two quarterly reports, Fabri’s first consolidated results and a broker model update separating operating from below-EBITA effects would provide the most useful resolution.

The common view of gradual margin recovery toward 8% is plausible but not secure, because faster utilisation and cleaner execution could lift EPS sharply while weak volumes, fixed-price leakage, project losses or entrepreneur attrition could keep margins near 6.5% or below. Upside deviation: operating leverage arrives faster An upside outcome would arise if Swedish activity accelerates before Instalco has to rebuild its cost base, allowing existing project managers, technicians and local overhead to absorb higher revenue. Q2 2026 already showed the mechanism, with group sales up 10.3% to SEK 3,874 million, organic growth of 8.4%, and EBITA up 22% to SEK 274 million. The calculated incremental EBITA margin was approximately 13.5%, but that level would probably fade as additional employees, subcontractors and supervision became necessary.

The strongest upside signal is not the reported quarterly margin alone but the combination of higher backlog, improving backlog per employee and available capacity. DNB Carnegie reported that order backlog per employee reached SEK 1.57 million in Q4 2025, up from SEK 1.49 million in Q3, and interpreted the improvement as evidence of further organic growth and margin expansion in 2026. If backlog converts at current project quality, Swedish EBITA margin could move above the broker’s 7.2% 2027 forecast without large central cost cuts. 320 A faster recovery would be amplified if pricing also improves rather than merely stabilises.

DNB Carnegie’s January 2026 site-visit evidence found that project prices had stabilised, tender activity had increased and market conditions had begun to tighten, although pricing remained difficult. A tighter subcontractor market caused by Nordic data-centre and infrastructure construction could improve Instalco’s negotiating position, because customers would value delivery capacity more than the lowest bid. Service growth would make the upside more durable by reducing dependence on new production and creating more frequent repricing opportunities.

Service represented 35% of Q2 2026 sales and grew 9% in absolute terms, while the company’s FY2025 report described service and renovation as more resilient than new construction. The upside would therefore be stronger if returning volume is concentrated in service, renovation, industrial work and partnering rather than low-margin fixed-price new build.

Durable bad-debt normalisation could provide another earnings lift, although this is a quality improvement rather than operating leverage. Other external expenses rose from roughly 7.5% to 8.0% of sales during 2022 to 2024 to approximately 8.5% to 9.0% during late 2024 and 2025, with bad-debt provisions identified as a principal reason. DNB Carnegie estimated that normalising annual bad debt could improve EBITA margin by approximately 40 basis points, equivalent to roughly SEK 54 million on FY2025 sales. 328 329 A successful roll-off of low-margin legacy contracts would make the recovery more durable than a simple volume rebound.

Instalco reported in April 2026 that fewer significant project deviations were visible after stronger project selection, risk assessment and early follow-up. If contracts awarded during the 2024 and 2025 pricing trough finish without further provisions, the group could retain the benefit even if market growth later normalises. Upside transmission to EPS and stakeholders The operating upside would reach EPS through lower bad debt, higher EBITA and potentially lower leverage, but not one-for-one because Fabri creates minority interests and acquisition financing raises interest expense.

DNB Carnegie raised 2026 and 2027 EBITA estimates by 2% to 7% after Q2 2026 but said the EPS effect was less pronounced because of updated amortisation, financial and minority assumptions. The most favourable case is therefore an 8% group EBITA margin combined with lower net debt, rather than 8% EBITA achieved through debt-funded acquisition growth.

Fabri could strengthen the upside case if its reported profitability remains close to the 2025 level after consolidation. Fabri reported EUR 86 million of sales and EUR 10.9 million of EBITA in 2025, implying a 12.7% EBITA margin, and Instalco began consolidating 51% of Fabri from 1 August 2026. However, only 51% of Fabri’s profit belongs economically to Instalco shareholders, so the group EPS effect will be lower than the headline German EBITA contribution.

Customers could reinforce the upside by awarding more multidisciplinary work to suppliers that can combine electrical, heating, plumbing, ventilation and industrial capability. Instalco’s prison and detention-facility project in Skövde involved several subsidiaries and a reported contract value of approximately SEK 100 million, illustrating how cross-selling can raise utilisation without proportionately increasing group overhead.

Major Nordic contractors and public-sector customers have an incentive to favour reliable integrated capacity when labour shortages make project delay more costly than a modest price premium.

Suppliers and subcontractors could also shift economics in Instalco’s favour if scarce capacity allows contractors to negotiate better customer pricing while maintaining supplier relationships. A construction-industry expert reported that Q2 2026 project volumes were up 10% to 15% year on year, while electrical labour and equipment costs were increasing and overall project pricing was up approximately 3% to 5%. That combination would support Instalco’s margin if customer pricing rises faster than wage and equipment costs, but it would hurt if subcontractors captured the improvement instead.

Downside deviation: low utilisation persists The downside case is that the 2026 recovery proves concentrated in selected regions and projects while Swedish private construction remains weak. DNB Carnegie described robust infrastructure and public-construction volumes alongside weaker private construction demand, creating industry-wide tender pressure and margin compression. Instalco’s 30% exposure to new build, together with its late-cycle installation position, means positive order intake would not necessarily prevent a renewed utilisation trough. Fasadgruppen is the clearest warning that decentralisation does not eliminate negative operating leverage. Fasadgruppen’s Q1 2026 adjusted EBITA margin fell to 0.5% when organic sales declined 11.7%, before recovering to 7.6% in Q2 as delayed work entered production. Instalco could experience a similar pattern if local staffing is retained for expected projects that are delayed, cancelled or awarded at uneconomic prices. A renewed downturn would make the 2024 restructuring insufficient rather than excessive. The programme closed or merged eight loss-making subsidiaries, affected more than 100 employees and terminated contracts with approximately 100 hired workers, but management said its SEK 30 million benefit would build gradually as projects were completed. If volumes weaken again, the group could face a second round of closures, with new restructuring charges arriving before the first programme’s full benefits have been demonstrated. - Earnings Calls Wage inflation could create a downside even if revenue grows. A calculated 3% increase across the FY2025 employee-cost ratio of 36.8% would equal approximately 1.1% of sales before productivity or pricing offsets, while an 8% increase would equal approximately 2.9% of sales under the same simplifying assumption. Fixed-price contracts signed in 2026 without wage escalation would absorb the difference unless Instalco secures change orders, improves productivity or reallocates work to subcontractors.

Project losses would be more damaging than ordinary wage inflation because they can erase the contribution from several profitable subsidiaries at once. DNB Carnegie identifies execution errors and miscalculations on larger projects as Instalco’s principal operational risk, particularly where complexity increases the financial consequence of a mistake. Caverion’s historical project write-downs, including EUR 27.1 million in 2017, EUR 9.3 million in 2018 and EUR 17.1 million in 2019, show that stronger controls reduce but do not eliminate this risk. Customer insolvency remains a second-order risk even after Northvolt has rolled off. Instalco recognised a SEK 64 million Northvolt receivable impairment in Q1 2025 and said customer bankruptcies also caused project delays and suboptimal capacity utilisation. A broader Swedish construction failure cycle could therefore damage EPS through both receivable provisions and unrecovered employee capacity.

Competitive and strategic downside Bravida has both the incentive and the operational reach to intensify price competition in Sweden. DNB Carnegie expects Bravida to enter a multi-year recovery period and sees data-centre construction as a potentially major source of future EBITA growth. If Bravida uses its larger service base, white-collar resources and Nordic reach to pursue strategic volume, Instalco could face lower tender prices precisely when it needs pricing to offset the 2027 wage round.

Assemblin Caverion could exert similar pressure through its combination of Assemblin’s project capability and Caverion’s service platform. A competitor described Caverion as particularly strong in service and Assemblin as strong in project work, implying that the combined group can contest both recurring service and multidisciplinary project tenders. This would make it harder for Instalco to improve margins through mix alone unless its local companies retain superior customer relationships or execution quality.

Nimlas and InstallatørGruppen could intensify competition for both customers and acquisition targets. Nimlas increased Q1 2026 adjusted EBITA margin to 6.0% from 4.8% despite a 1.7% organic sales decline, demonstrating that it can improve profitability while continuing to acquire companies. DNB Carnegie describes InstallatørGruppen as a rapidly scaling, decentralised owner-driven platform with a large acquisition runway, so competition for entrepreneurial targets could raise purchase prices and dilute Instalco’s future returns.

SPIE presents a more constructive competitive threat because it has shown that sustained margin improvement can come from selectivity, mix, pricing and acquisitions rather than volume alone. Deutsche Bank reports that SPIE’s EBIT margin increased by 260 basis points between 2020 and 2025 and that the group has priced services ahead of inflation while moving toward higher-value activities. If Nordic customers increasingly reward technical depth, lifecycle service and complex industrial capability, Instalco may need greater investment in engineering, systems and training, limiting the amount of overhead it can remove. Burkhalter offers a different strategic challenge because its decentralised model combines local autonomy with central procurement and support. If Burkhalter’s model continues to deliver acceptable margins through central systems and procurement without sacrificing local leadership, it would weaken the argument that Instalco must choose between decentralisation and efficiency. Conversely, if centralisation proves expensive or slow, it would reinforce Instalco’s decision to pursue lighter shared-service improvements rather than material local-company consolidation.

Capital allocation, lenders and entrepreneur retention Acquisition dilution is a material alternative downside to the common recovery case. Instalco financed the EUR 35.7 million purchase of an additional 27% of Fabri through its existing credit facility, while Fabri’s remaining shareholders retained a significant economic interest. A high reported EBITA margin could therefore coexist with weaker attributable EPS if minorities, acquisition amortisation and interest costs absorb the incremental earnings.

Leverage constrains how much downside Instalco can absorb before lenders become a strategic influence. Net debt to EBITDA was 2.6x at Q2 2026, above the company’s 2.5x target, while the credit facility was SEK 3.4 billion and extended to 2028. The company still reported covenant headroom, but weaker EBITA, a workingcapital build or further acquisition spending could increase interest expense and force slower M&A or asset sales.

Working capital could be an early downside signal that consensus misses. Q2 2026 operating cash flow fell to SEK 104 million from SEK 202 million despite higher EBITA, because strong invoicing increased capital tied up in receivables. If overdue receivables begin rising again, the apparent EBITA recovery would transmit poorly to EPS and could increase lender concern before reported margins deteriorate. Entrepreneur departures represent a strategic downside that may not appear immediately in financial statements.

DNB Carnegie warns that a more hands-on management approach could cause subsidiary managing directors to lose commitment or entrepreneurial spirit if strict initiatives are implemented without preserving local autonomy. The risk is economically significant because Instalco’s acquisition model depends on founders retaining customer relationships, project-selection judgement and responsibility for local employees.

The company has attempted to mitigate this risk through incentive programmes and continued local ownership. Instalco’s three outstanding warrant programmes could issue up to 6.95 million new shares and include subsidiary CEOs and other key employees, aligning retention with group value creation but creating potential dilution. The upside is stronger retention and execution, while the downside is that incentives add dilution without solving dissatisfaction if local decision rights are perceived to be shrinking.

Indicators deciding between the hypotheses The high-margin hypothesis would gain credibility if Swedish EBITA margin remains above 7% through seasonally weaker quarters, revenue per employee continues rising, backlog per employee stays high and working capital does not expand disproportionately. DNB Carnegie’s Q3 2026 framework expects group EBITA margin to expand by 90 basis points year on year, supported by 10% organic growth, while Sweden remains the principal earnings driver.

Delivery of that outcome would show that Q2 was not merely a seasonal or exceptionalmargin event. The common-view hypothesis would be weakened if Swedish margin remains near 7% despite double-digit organic growth. That result would imply that higher volume is being absorbed by wages, subcontractor inflation, legacy contracts, project execution or added country-level overhead rather than flowing through to EBITA. It would also suggest that the final 100 basis points to 8% requires pricing, mix or further structural cost reduction.

The downside hypothesis would gain credibility if backlog growth becomes concentrated in Norway or Germany while Swedish backlog stagnates, because group growth could then mask the key Swedish margin problem. The Q2 backlog increase was driven primarily by Norway but also by Sweden, so the next decisive evidence is countrylevel order intake, backlog per employee and margin on newly awarded Swedish work. The 2027 labour round should be judged through actual payroll cost, not the headline minimum-wage increase.

The decisive disclosures would be average wage increases by occupation, the proportion of employees affected, the share of fixed-price backlog lacking indexation and the amount recovered through customer pricing. A 3% actual-pay outcome with effective pass-through would favour the recovery case, whereas an 8% cost increase without indexation would materially challenge it.

Fabri’s first consolidated quarters will decide whether German growth is additive or dilutive to EPS. The relevant indicators are Fabri EBITA margin, cash conversion, backlog margin, net debt, acquisition spending and the minority-interest charge after consolidation. A reported margin near 12.7% with strong cash conversion would support the upside case, while margin compression or working-capital consumption would make the German platform a source of earnings volatility.

Contrary evidence: the strongest evidence against a severe downside outcome is that Q2 2026 showed 8.4% organic growth, a 17% backlog increase, a 7.1% group EBITA margin and a 7.0% Swedish EBITA margin, while the company reported fewer significant project deviations and no comparable rise in older overdue receivables. These data indicate that Instalco 2.0 is already producing operational benefits, so a downside case based solely on persistent under-utilisation would require the recovery to reverse or the 2027 cost and project risks to overwhelm the current improvement. Other side of the story: the common expectation remains defensible because independent broker modelling places 2027 group EBITA margin near 7.8%, close to but below the 8% target, rather than assuming an immediate full recovery. The most balanced range is therefore approximately 7.5% to 8.2% EBITA margin through 2027– 2028, with higher EPS conversion if bad debts normalise and leverage falls, and materially lower conversion if wage inflation, fixed-price leakage, project losses or minorities absorb the operating improvement.
