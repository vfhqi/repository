Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The rapid US rollout of KeriMedical's TOUCH thumb prosthesis serves as a critical growth engine and commercial gateway for Medartis to cross-sell its high-margin APTUS fixation portfolio.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Pivotal investigation: "What evidence exists that Medartis Holding AG's (MED-CH) US TOUCH thumb-prosthesis accounts adopt its APTUS hand plating and other fixation products, what is TOUCH's US price and the hospital's procedure economics compared with trapeziectomy, how are ambulatory surgery centres adopting it, and is any competitor (Stryker, Acumed, Skeletal Dynamics, Zimmer Biomet or others) developing a thumb carpometacarpal prosthesis for the US market?"

Strategic Importance of TOUCH

2.
Strategic gateway: The US rollout of the TOUCH thumb prosthesis represents a commercial breakthrough in the large and highly underserved US extremities market. Medartis acquired 100% ownership of KeriMedical in July 2025 to accelerate this launch after securing US regulatory clearance. Success in this market is expected to act as a catalyst to broaden the company's surgeon base.
3.
Accelerating volumes: Commercial demand for the TOUCH device in the US is progressing faster than the company's original internal projections. Management initially projected 1,200 units for 2026, raised it to 1,800 in August, and further upgraded it to 3,000 units in November. This rapid adoption supports the company's organic sales growth acceleration.

Cross-Selling and Margin Impact

4.

The goal is to first win hand surgeons with the joint implant and subsequently expand sales into APTUS screws and plates. This clinical pull-through strategy could meaningfully expand Medartis' high-margin Upper Extremities franchise.
5.
EBITDA expansion: High-volume adoption of the TOUCH prosthesis creates operational leverage that directly improves the group's profit margins. Management expects that overachieving sales targets will lift its core EBITDA margins to the upper end of its guidance. Stronger volume scaling is critical to offsetting heavy commercial investments in surgeon education and sales specialist recruitment.

Section 3 – US Pricing and Procedure Economics: TOUCH vs. Trapeziectomy

Section summary
  1. The TOUCH thumb prosthesis commands high US commercial pricing that requires transitional reimbursement pathways to offset substantial hospital implant acquisition costs.
  2. Clinical registries demonstrate that total joint arthroplasty provides significantly faster short-term functional recovery than traditional trapeziectomy, though long-term clinical equivalence and higher revision risks remain key economic concerns.

US Commercial Component Pricing

1.
Severe expense: Medartis's TOUCH system carries high component costs in the United States compared to standard open resection techniques. The cementless TOUCH cup is commercially priced at USD 3,095, the neck and liner at USD 2,995, and the cementless stem at USD 2,895. These component costs sum to an upfront implant price of USD 8,985 for a complete total joint arthroplasty. This high pricing could restrict early hospital margins unless local commercial pricing structures or bulk purchase discounts are negotiated.
2.
Reimbursement pathways: Medartis utilizes specific regulatory mechanisms to ease the financial burden on healthcare facilities adopting the TOUCH prosthesis. The manufacturer pursued a transitional device passthrough payment status with the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services in mid-2026. Securing this status is essential to cover high component expenses in outpatient hospital departments. Without specialized reimbursement, standard facility payments under existing outpatient prospective payment systems are insufficient to absorb the unit cost.
3.
International precedents: Medartis's international commercial execution demonstrates that delayed reimbursement decisions directly hinder patient flow and implant volume. In Australia, despite positive clinical feedback, the TOUCH prosthesis experienced pending reimbursement decisions throughout fiscal 2025. Management anticipated a final decision in the first half of fiscal 2026 to list the device on the Prescribed List. This precedent illustrates that reimbursement lags slow regional uptake and negatively impact near-term corporate earnings.

Surgical Efficiency and Recovery Times

4.
Rapid rehabilitation: Arthroplasty procedures utilizing the TOUCH dual-mobility prosthesis offer patients earlier hand mobilization than traditional resection techniques. Clinical reviews indicate that total joint replacement preserves thumb length and maintains optimal joint biomechanics. These mechanical advantages translate into faster post-operative rehabilitation timelines for active patient populations. Consequently, patients return to daily activities within weeks rather than the multiple months typical of traditional ligament reconstruction.
5.
Temporary superiority: The United Kingdom Hand Registry recorded clinical outcomes for 948 total procedures, comprising 710 trapeziectomies and 238 total joint arthroplasties. Registry analysis indicated that total joint arthroplasty offers a statistically faster return of hand function within the first six months. However, this clinical advantage was not sustained beyond six months post-operatively. This clinical convergence suggests that the high initial cost of the implant primarily purchases a more rapid early recovery.
6.
Contrary evidence: Systematic medical reviews in late 2025 concluded that while total joint replacements show superior short-term recovery, they do not outperform trapeziectomy over longer clinical horizons. Long-term clinical registries demonstrate that traditional trapeziectomy with or without ligament reconstruction achieves equivalent pain relief. Furthermore, joint replacements carry a higher rate of implant-specific complications such as aseptic loosening and dislocation. These structural failures often require secondary procedures that undermine the initial economic value of the implant.
7.
French transition: The French orthopaedic market underwent a massive surgical shift away from Gervis's traditional 1947 resection technique over the last two decades. Retrospective database analysis of French hospital discharges showed that total surgical procedures increased from 7,564 in 2009 to 20,406 in 2024. By fiscal 2024, trapeziometacarpal arthroplasty became the dominant technique, accounting for 75.1% of all surgeries. This national precedent demonstrates that surgeons will aggressively adopt joint prostheses despite the availability of traditional, cheaper alternatives.

Long-Term Margins and Clinical Outcomes

8.
Durable outcomes: Trapeziectomy with or without ligament reconstruction and tendon interposition remains the historical gold standard for thumb base osteoarthritis. Network meta-analyses compiled through late 2024 showed that resection techniques have a significantly lower reoperation risk. Because of these durable outcomes, some clinical researchers argue that there is still insufficient evidence to recommend joint replacement routinely. This clinical conservatism poses a persistent barrier to Medartis's commercial marketing campaigns in the United States.
