Tax rate progression and losses

Elevated tax rates: TT Electronics experienced a massive tax drag when its FY2025 adjusted effective tax rate soared to 57.1% due to North American losses. Interim tax reduction: The group's interim tax rate showed substantial progress, falling from 60.0% in H1 2025 to 36.3% in H1 2026. Conditions for US tax loss recognition

3.
Unrecognised tax pool: TT Electronics disclosed a massive gross pool of unrecognised tax losses totaling £282.4m at the end of FY2025. The group's annual report of 25 March 2026 indicated that £225.1m of these unrecognised losses reside in the US. This unrecognized US tax loss includes £26.3m of capitalised R&D expenses and credits. 4.
Strict recognition conditions: Under the IAS 12 accounting standard, deferred tax assets can only be recognised when utilisation becomes probable. Management noted in the 2025 annual report that the five-year strategic plan does not yet support US asset recognition. This lack of recognition stems from historical losses and near-term uncertainty in North American profitability. If the US division achieves consistent profitability, management can restore these assets to the balance sheet.
5.
Historical asset write downs: The group's current tax issues trace back to significant write-downs in prior years as North American performance deteriorated. TT Electronics derecognised £16.0m of US deferred tax assets as of 31 December 2024. Management followed this with a further derecognition of £2.7m in FY2025 due to a weak near-term outlook. Reversing these write-downs will require a sustained trend of operational turnarounds.
