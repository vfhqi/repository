Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence demonstrates that PGE's valuation multiple is fundamentally shaped by the December 2028 coal capacity contract expiration, and historical precedents across European utilities confirm that forward multiples compress significantly as dated earnings losses approach, typically 12 to 24 months in advance, unless offsetting regulatory support or strategic transition catalysts emerge to stabilize cash flows and restore investor confidence in the long-term earnings trajectory. PGE's forward price-to-earnings multiple has historically fluctuated between a peak of approximately 11x during periods of elevated domestic power prices and strong regulatory support mechanisms, and a trough of around 3x during commodity price collapses and severe consensus earnings downgrades driven by regulatory intervention risks and coal sector restructuring uncertainty. Despite substantial coal restructuring headwinds, PGE's share price surged 83.6% absolute year-to-date through October 2026, trading at 7.5x forward P/E, as the market re-rated the stock based on the prospective resumption of a 5% dividend yield and strategic transition plans toward regulated networks and renewables before regulatory support mechanisms were fully finalized. This dramatic rally illustrates that strategic credibility can support valuation premiums even during periods of fundamental earnings uncertainty, particularly when management commits to capital discipline and shareholder distributions. Polish utilities TAURON and Enea faced scheduled termination of their five-year capacity market contracts at the end of fiscal year 2025, which drove sell-side analysts to project severe earnings declines starting in 2026, and consequently investors began de-rating both utilities well ahead of the contract expiration date, compressing their forward EV/EBITDA multiples to near-historical lows. CEZ implemented a revised coal fleet depreciation schedule creating a cumulative CZK 13 bn headwind to net income, with sell-side estimates showing the steep earnings drop almost 18 months before the regulatory transition, and the forward price-to-earnings multiple initially rising to 23x on depressed earnings before normalizing as the market priced in lower long-term dividend payouts. These precedents confirm that earnings visibility deterioration triggers multiple compression regardless of share price levels, and that accounting transitions can create temporary multiple distortions that obscure underlying valuation trends until normalized earnings emerge.
  2. The valuation multiple of PGE and peer utilities is deeply linked to dated regulatory support changes, such as the December 2028 coal capacity contract expirations, which threaten long-term earnings per share.

RWE secured final regulatory clearance for its multi-billion lignite phase-out compensation package, with the European Commission approving EUR 2.6 bn in state aid for RWE and EUR 1.75 bn for LEAG in December 2023, and this state compensation reduced the financial burden of decommissioning fossil assets and helped RWE preserve stable credit ratings during its green transition program. Estonia secured European Commission state aid clearance for a EUR 750 mm strategic reserve on October 28, 2025, to guarantee electricity supply during peak winter demand, with this long-term framework running until 2035 and ensuring that participating assets receive stable availability payments outside the competitive wholesale market.

These government compensation mechanisms demonstrate that state-backed capacity frameworks can provide critical earnings floors during decarbonization transitions, but their value depends heavily on timing, scale, and regulatory certainty, with early announcements typically preventing severe valuation collapses while delayed or uncertain support prolongs multiple compression. Bernstein Research valued PGE's core regulated distribution network at an EV/EBITDA multiple of 7x for fiscal year 2026, reflecting stable utility cash flows and insulation from power market volatility, demonstrating that regulated assets command higher valuation premiums than merchant-exposed segments.

Bernstein Research valued PGE's conventional generation using a high WACC of 20% to reflect massive CO2 risks, while the district heating business was valued at a lower 12% WACC due to more stable municipal frameworks, indicating that high carbon liabilities compress generation values substantially. Bernstein Research derived an enterprise value of PLN 51.3 bn for PGE, subtracting net debt of PLN 3.4 per share and pension liabilities of PLN 4.8 per share, resulting in a sum-of-the-parts target price of PLN 14.2 per share, revealing that balance sheet liabilities severely limit equity value expansion.

This sum-of-the-parts analysis highlights the structural valuation divergence between clean and conventional assets within integrated utilities, with regulated networks and contracted renewables supporting premium multiples while legacy coal generation trades at deep discounts that constrain consolidated group valuations until asset separation or restructuring occurs. 8 9 PGE lowered its consolidated fiscal year 2030 EBITDA target to PLN 15 bn from the previous PLN 17 bn, with Bernstein Research reporting that this reduction reflects lower projected earnings from gas and renewable generation, although the new target remains highly aligned with the Bloomberg consensus estimate of PLN 15.2 bn, representing more realistic power price assumptions by management.

This alignment indicates that consensus estimates already incorporate the substantial earnings headwinds from coal contract expirations and the slower-than-initially-planned ramp-up of merchant renewable capacity, meaning that further negative surprises would require either deeper regulatory setbacks or material execution failures on the green transition strategy that management has committed to under Strategy 2035.

Polish grid operator PSE was actively negotiating with Brussels in early 2026 to extend capacity market support for coal units past 2028, and if approved, this derogation extension would preserve Conventional Generation cash flows and defer the expected decline in earnings per share. Although PGE has historically traded at a deep valuation discount to European utility peers, regulated networks and contracted renewables are projected to contribute approximately 63% of group EBITDA by 2030, and this strategic pivot toward infrastructure-led assets should reduce earnings volatility and permanently re-rate PGE's valuation multiple toward developed market peer levels.

This conflicting evidence between near-term regulatory uncertainty and long-term structural transformation creates a fundamental tension in valuation frameworks, where pessimistic scenarios focus on the immediate coal cliff and policy execution risks, while optimistic scenarios emphasize the eventual elimination of carbon risk discounts and multiple expansion toward pure-play renewable and regulated utility peers once the transition is complete. Although dated losses of regulatory support historically compress earnings and depress immediate valuations, utilities accelerating their coal exits such as Uniper and PPC successfully repositioned themselves as sustainable investments, and by shedding highly volatile and carbon-intensive conventional generation these utilities eventually eliminated their fossil-fuel risk discounts, with the transition toward regulated networks and contracted renewables ultimately driving long-term multiple expansion and shareholder returns that far exceed the initial earnings shock.

Verbund traded at 18.4x forward earnings for fiscal year 2026, and although earnings were projected to fall 25% because of the excess profit levy, the stock maintained a premium valuation due to its pureplay renewable status, demonstrating that investors pay premium multiples for low-carbon assets even when near-term earnings are declining. These peer precedents confirm that valuation cycles reflect strategic positioning rather than just near-term earnings trajectories, with utilities that credibly commit to decarbonization pathways capturing structural multiple premiums that compensate long-term investors for enduring temporary earnings volatility and regulatory disruption during the transition phase.

PGE's post-2028 capacity support remains highly uncertain as negotiations with Brussels over long-term strategic reserve mechanisms continue, with the approved capacity market extension only protecting conventional revenues through December 2028 under the estimated PLN 9 bn draft law, and without a formalized capacity framework beyond 2028 analysts cannot safely project PGE's cash flows, depressing long-term earnings per share visibility for investors.

PGE's capital deployment trajectory for its offshore wind portfolio remains unclear after the strategic deferral of key project phases, with the lack of offshore wind development having an estimated PLN 0.8 per share negative impact on valuation, and a detailed capital expenditure update from management in 2027 being necessary to confirm the return profiles of green assets and size future earnings. These critical information gaps surrounding regulatory frameworks and capital allocation timelines explain why PGE trades at a substantial discount to its theoretical sum-of-the-parts value, as investors demand large risk premiums when neither the timing of conventional earnings erosion nor the scale of replacement green cash flows can be projected with confidence, creating a valuation range that will remain wide until Polish authorities and European regulators finalize post-2028 support mechanisms and PGE demonstrates credible execution on its offshore wind commitments.

PGE's long-term earnings could dwarf consensus expectations if the utility successfully executes its ambitious Strategy 2035 targets, with management targeting group EBITDA of over PLN 30 bn by 2035, nearly double the broker's conservative estimate of PLN 16 bn which primarily reflects grid visibility limitations, and achieving this target would result in substantial earnings per share outperformance relative to what sell-side models currently assume.

PGE planned PLN 210 bn in cumulative capital expenditure through 2035, with over 70% of this budget protected by regulated returns or contracts for difference, and under this scenario the group's forward multiples would re-rate toward regulated European utility peer levels, expanding investor returns. This alternative scenario analysis suggests that the current valuation reflects deeply conservative assumptions about both regulatory outcomes and execution capabilities, and any positive resolution of post-2028 capacity support combined with on-schedule delivery of offshore wind projects could trigger a rapid multiple expansion toward the

15x to 18x range observed at pure-play renewable utilities, representing substantial upside to the current 6.4x forward P/E that would reward investors willing to underwrite PGE's strategic transformation through a volatile transition period characterized by simultaneous conventional asset decline and green capacity scaling.

Section 2 – Historical Valuation Cycles of PGE

Section summary
  1. We have high confidence that PGE's valuation cycles are driven by power price spreads, capital expenditure peaks, and coal restructuring risks, as evidenced by broker historical trading bands.
  2. Precedent case studies demonstrate that forward valuation multiples compress significantly before the scheduled termination of capacity contracts and regulatory support.
  3. Utilities that accelerate their conventional energy exit schedules eventually capture valuation premiums as clean-energy assets dominate their earnings mix.

Historical Multiple Ranges

1.
Elevated valuations: Morgan Stanley reported on May 11, 2014, that PGE's forward price-to-earnings multiple rose to a material premium over its historical average of 10.7x. Across eight relevant sources covering three peer companies and two historical precedents, PGE's valuation fluctuated significantly. This peak occurred as domestic power prices rose to a premium over German prices, lifting investor sentiment. No named companies were left out of this valuation cycle analysis because of data availability.
2.
Moderate multiples: ING Wholesale Banking reported on December 21, 2012, that PGE traded at a normalized forward EV/EBITDA ratio of 4.2x for fiscal year 2012. This valuation reflected a low point in earnings as domestic electricity prices fell, which drove consensus estimates lower for the subsequent fiscal year. However, PGE's valuation expanded to 5.5x forward EV/EBITDA for fiscal year 2013 as the market anticipated a rebound in power prices and cost reductions. This expansion shows that investors pay higher forward multiples as costreduction plans begin to support underlying EBIT forecasts.
3.
Steady valuations: ING Wholesale Banking reported on July 15, 2014, that PGE traded at a normalized EV/EBITDA multiple of 6.2x for fiscal year 2014 and 6.4x for fiscal year 2015. These multiples aligned with local peers, supported by the implementation of an operating reserve mechanism that elevated domestic spot prices. Despite positive earnings adjustments, a potential share overhang from state-owned shares limited further multiple expansion. This indicates that regulatory reserve mechanisms can stabilize multiples even when massive capital expenditure programmes are introduced.
4.
Depressed valuations: Morgan Stanley reported on September 14, 2015, that PGE's valuation fell to a forward consensus P/E of 9.8x, approaching its historical troughs. This low multiple was driven by a commodity price collapse and severe consensus earnings downgrades. Investors demanded a higher risk premium due to the uncertainty surrounding state-mandated coal sector restructuring and potential utility mergers. This cyclical trough highlights how regulatory and sector intervention risks can compress forward earnings multiples even when stock prices seem tactically attractive.

Peer Benchmarks and Precedents

This valuation represented a clear premium over PGE's 4.2x multiple, which was justified by CEZ's superior asset quality. In contrast, Polish peer Enea traded at a substantial discount of 3.4x to 4.1x EV/EBITDA, which analysts considered excessive. These regional variations show that investors apply substantial asset-quality premiums within Central and Eastern European utility valuation cycles.
6.
Historical precedent: Morgan Stanley reported on May 11, 2014, that CEZ traded at a forward price-to-earnings multiple of 12.6x, representing an 8% premium to its historical average. This premium was driven by low variable nuclear costs and post-peak free cash flows, which compared favorably to PGE's capital-intensive commitment to build the 1.8GW Opole project. At the same time, developed European peers traded at a higher average multiple of 15.8x. This precedent shows that utilities with low-carbon generation profiles command premium multiples over capital-intensive coal-heavy peers.
7.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research reported on October 19, 2026, that PGE's share price rose by 83.6% absolute year-to-date, trading at 7.5x forward P/E for 2026. This significant share price rally occurred despite a lack of final coal asset restructuring, showing that the market can re-rate PGE's multiple on the prospective resumption of a 5% dividend yield. This shows that investors may reward strategic transition plans before regulatory support mechanisms are fully finalized.
8.
Current valuation: Bernstein Research reported on October 19, 2026, that PGE's target price of PLN 14.2 represented a 14% downside to its market price of PLN 16.5. This target was based on conservative assumptions about future power prices and merchant renewable returns, despite a high 2030 EBITDA target of PLN 15 bn. Although the shares have historically traded at a discount, the resumption of dividends from fiscal year 2026 earnings supports a more positive outlook. This demonstrates that capital discipline can support higher multiples despite substantial investment program requirements.
9.
Other side of the story: Although PGE has historically traded at a deep valuation discount to European utility peers, its green transition strategy could structurally eliminate this gap. Bernstein Research reported on October 19, 2026, that regulated networks and contracted renewables are projected to contribute c.63% of group EBITDA by 2030. Polish and Czech Regulatory Cliff

1.
Significant disruption: TAURON and Enea faced the scheduled termination of their five-year capacity market contracts at the end of fiscal year 2025. Bernstein Research reported on May 8, 2025, that these Polish utilities would lose the majority of their capacity revenues as coal-fired units rolled off support. This anticipated support loss drove sell-side analysts to project severe earnings declines starting in 2026. Consequently, investors began de-rating both utilities well ahead of the contract expiration date, compressing their forward EV/EBITDA multiples to near-historical lows.
2.
Accelerated depreciation: CEZ implemented a revised coal fleet depreciation schedule that created a cumulative CZK 13 bn headwind to net income. Morgan Stanley reported on November 28, 2024, that this accounting change led to significant consensus earnings downgrades for the 2024 to 2026 period. Sell-side estimates began showing the steep earnings drop almost 18 months before the regulatory transition. The forward price-to-earnings multiple initially rose to 23x on depressed earnings before normalising as the market priced in lower long-term dividend payouts for shareholders.
3.
Taxation pressures: CEZ shares experienced a transition from a stable dividend play to a complex transformation story during the 60% windfall tax period. Morgan Stanley reported on January 13, 2025, that the corporate tax surcharge on exceptional energy profits constrained cash distributions and depressed the 2025 net income target. The share price reflected the tax impact early, resulting in limited dividend yield premiums over Czech sovereign bonds. Total shareholder returns from 24 months before to 12 months after the levy introduction remained highly volatile as regulatory uncertainty dominated the investment landscape.
