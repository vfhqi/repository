Avanza (AZA-SE): what the Q3 2026 results, and the response to them, do to the existing case

29 October 2026

These results leave the case where it was, with the pressure running slightly against it: the case was written on this quarter and already holds the three readings of the monetisation miss that the sell-side divides over, and what the twelve days since have added — a consensus that has not moved, a price now below the case's own SEK 360 marker, and two readings the case does not hold — presses on Foundations' Robustness B and Case Riskiness B without moving either.

What changed

The quarter, reported on 16 October 2026, was a 2% miss concentrated in monetisation. Operating income SEK 1,283m against the company-compiled consensus of SEK 1,310m from 18 contributors; operating profit SEK 920m against SEK 935m; net profit SEK 790m against SEK 801m; EPS before dilution SEK 4.90 against SEK 4.99, up 12% year on year. Every core revenue line missed: net brokerage SEK 308m against SEK 323m (-5%, flat year on year), currency-related income SEK 197m against SEK 213m (-8%), fund commissions SEK 244m against SEK 252m (-3%), net interest income SEK 464m against SEK 473m (-2%). Other income of SEK 69m against SEK 48.9m, from Avanza Markets, and costs of SEK 362m against SEK 373m absorbed most of it; the operating margin was 72% against 71% expected, with costs up 12% year on year and the 9% Swedish cost-growth guide for 2026 unchanged.

The volume engine ran the other way. Savings capital SEK 1,277bn, up 21% year on year; net inflows SEK 23.1bn, up 61%; fund inflows a record SEK 18bn, 32% of the Swedish fund market's; customers 2,391,100, up 8%. Behind the gap: gross brokerage income per turnover fell to 10.3 basis points from 11.0 in Q2 as Private Banking and Pro customers' share of trading rose to 30% from 26%; the fund margin fell to 22.2 basis points from 22.9; annualised income per customer fell 6% in the quarter to SEK 2,170; deposits fell to 8.9% of savings capital. Transactions fell 8% quarter on quarter while turnover per trading day was 11% above Q3 2025. Management called the yield decline temporary market sentiment.

Set against the case: all of it is in the case, which was written on this print at SEK 360.1. The case reads about two-thirds of the compression as structural, has 2027 as the trough year (revenue +8%, costs +13%, operating profit +6%, margin 68.2% against a consensus 69.5%), carries FY2027 EPS of SEK 20.2 against a consensus SEK 20.87, and gives a 60% probability that consensus is cut by more than 2% in the next two rounds.

The response is the part the case could only forecast. DNB Carnegie, on 19 October, called the result "incrementally negative", cut adjusted EPS to SEK 18.90 from SEK 19.04 for 2026, SEK 19.29 from SEK 19.34 for 2027 and SEK 20.79 from SEK 20.93 for 2028, trimmed its target to SEK 425 from SEK 430 and kept Buy, still carrying 17% upside to 2027 EPS if first-half activity per user returns and about 4% more if three-month STIBOR follows the forward curve to 2.25%. Deutsche Bank kept Buy and SEK 440 and published no revised estimate table, having gone in at SEK 1,470m of operating income and SEK 931m of net profit, about 17% above consensus. Jefferies kept Underperform at SEK 335 against a SEK 376.30 reference price, 11% downside. On the dashboard at 28 October, consensus EPS stands at SEK 21.13, the FY2027 mean the case cited, unchanged since before the print; EPS revisions are +2% over one and three months, the target price -1% on both, the buy share 47%, the consensus target SEK 412.58 and the revision-momentum score 6; the sell-side earnings momentum pin has moved from A to B, which is where the case said it would mark it. The shares are SEK 355.0 against SEK 376.30 the day before the print, about 6% lower, and 1.7% below the 200-day average of SEK 361.27; the current P/E is 15.8x, the 10th percentile of the stock's own ten years; technical momentum D, valuation A.

Nordnet reported on 21 October: operating income SEK 1,550m, up 20%; operating profit SEK 1,096m, up 24%; margin 71% from 69%; its German launch is set for November 2026. Deutsche Bank's September tracker has Avanza's total trades down 10% year on year against Nordnet's 5% and flatexDEGIRO's 3%, daily trading activity per customer down 15% across all the platforms it tracks, and Avanza's customer growth of 8% against Nordnet's 12%.

The challenge

Where the case already stands, first. The sell-side's three positions on the miss are the case's three branches. Deutsche Bank's view that the effects are not yet shown to be permanent, and DNB Carnegie's that the miss was sentiment with 17% upside if activity returns, are the case's 25% bull tail and the cyclical branch of its first judgement, worth +4.8% to FY2027 EPS. Jefferies' structural decline in income per customer is the fully structural branch, -4.2% to about SEK 19.4, which the case notes is DNB Carnegie's number. The case's base sits between them. On results morning we declined to put up the trading-cycle-top reading because the case then held it at 25% odds; the new case has gone further and made the structural reading its base. The obvious alternatives are therefore not new to the case and we do not put them up. Two readings are.

First: the yield compression is competitive price-taking, not strategic mix, so the drift is not bounded. The case reads the fall in yields as the mix the strategy builds on purpose — affluent customers paying half-price currency conversion, index funds, Sigma at half the incumbent's price — and carries the revenue margin drifting 1.2 basis points a year, while itself saying that drift is the one judgement in its base that leans on reasoning rather than on the 2023 to 2025 record of 2 to 3 basis points. The research carries evidence the case does not: a former SAVR commercial executive describing SAVR and Montrose undercutting on foreign-exchange fees and product cadence after years in which the duopoly faced little direct competition, and the reading that disruption in this sector shows first as lower revenue per trade rather than as lost customers. In its strongest form: the half-price affluent tier is not a design choice but the price at which Avanza keeps its most contestable cohort, so mix and competition are one thing seen from two sides. The brokerage yield fell 0.7 basis points and the fund margin 0.7 basis points in a single quarter, against a case that reads about a third of that as reversing (its brokerage line runs from 10.6 basis points toward 10.0; its fund margin loses about 0.6 basis points a year). If Q3's yields are the floor from which the drift starts rather than a trough to recover from, the case's own down-trigger — a Q1 2027 brokerage yield below 10 basis points — is hit inside the horizon; Foundations' Robustness B's "a revenue margin the moat does not protect" becomes the headline rather than the qualifier; and the fair multiple's claim that quality of this order has earned 20x to 21x leans on a quality the unit economics no longer show. For it to be right: the brokerage yield has to fall again in Q4 with the Private Banking and Pro share of trading flat — price, not mix — or Avanza has to move its 25 basis point standard currency fee, the SEK 100m to 150m step-change the case names. Against it: Nordnet expanded its margin to 71% in the same quarter, which is not what a price war looks like; Avanza raised its mortgage rate by 10 basis points, which is pricing power exercised where it has it; churn is 0.7%; and yield by cohort is not disclosed, so the test is indirect. Plausible, and it presses on the judgement the case itself calls its weakest.

Second: the Swedish platform is nearer its limit than the cost record shows, and the decision to build Denmark on a separate platform is the evidence. The case's Foundations B rests on a cost line met four years running, a cost-to-savings ratio of 14 basis points and one Java stack deploying in minutes; its Case Riskiness B says no franchise risk; and it names a documented outage under load as the one operational risk with no disclosed data. The research carries a former Avanza business developer saying the Swedish infrastructure was not built for the company's present scale, that high-traffic events had caused outages, and that recent customer-facing innovation had been limited while resources went to stability and back-end work. Management's own stated reason for a separate, market-agnostic Danish platform was that adapting the Swedish system would consume Swedish development capacity and risk disrupting the domestic business. Costs rose 12% year on year with information technology at 15% of nine-month costs; period-end headcount went to 812 from 709; cloud migration is expensed as it goes; and management said efficiency gains are redeployed because there are more initiatives than capacity. In its strongest form: the 8% cost framework was set before the re-platforming need was visible; the Swedish base now carries a cloud migration, stability work and Denmark's internal build at once; and the 2027 Swedish cost guide on 22 January 2027 prints above 8.5%, the case's own negative trigger, against a central case of 7% to 8%. For it to be right: that guide, or a disclosed incident. Against it: the case reads nine-month costs of SEK 1,145m as on the 2026 guide; the Danish 2026 cost fell to SEK 40m from SEK 50m because internal work replaced external; the Q3 materials show no outage and supply no uptime data either way; and one former employee is one witness. Possible rather than plausible as an earnings reading — the case bounds each point of Swedish cost growth at SEK 16m, SEK 0.09 of EPS — but it is the only new evidence on the operational side of Case Riskiness B, and it runs the wrong way.

Readings we considered and set aside. That the leverage ratio is rationing the wallet-share engine rather than opening a 2028 buyback: Avanza paused new mortgages for customers with SEK 3m to SEK 10m of savings and raised the rate 10 basis points while the ratio stood at 4.8%, up from 4.4% at end-2025, and the call gave no capital-allocation bridge; but loans to the public are SEK 38.2bn against SEK 1,277bn of savings capital, and the case carries the buyback as upside to the return, not inside it. That Avanza is losing Sweden to Nordnet: Nordnet's +24% operating profit against Avanza's +13% in the same quarter is the case's own counter-case, the tracker's 15% per-customer activity decline across all platforms leaves most of the gap in total trades down to Nordnet's faster customer growth (12% against 8%), which the case holds, and nothing in the research separates Nordnet's Sweden from its other three markets, so the Sweden-specific reading cannot be made from it. That generative search bypasses Avanza's direct funnel, as Jefferies notes neither Avanza nor Nordnet surfaces in searches for Swedish AI investment platforms: there is no account-level evidence, 0.7% churn is the current answer, and the number that would show it, customer acquisition cost, is not disclosed.

The two readings we put up on results morning. The first, that Nordnet's commission-free campaign meant the duopoly had started using price in Sweden, has lost force on the evidence now in hand and we drop it in that form: Nordnet's margin rose, Avanza's yield fall is attributed by management, Deutsche Bank and DNB Carnegie to mix and volatility, and the per-customer activity decline was sector-wide. What survives of it is the first reading above: price-taking at a tier, not a price war. The second, that the rate lever had turned from a cleared concern into a driver the case did not model, has been absorbed: the case now carries a 4 November hike at 60% to 70% pass-through, a second at 40% odds, and the published asymmetry of SEK 1.1bn a point up against SEK 420m a point down. It is no longer a challenge. What the quarter adds is that deposits fell to 8.9% of savings capital as customers went risk-on, so a hike would land on a smaller base, which the case holds in its third judgement. The 'Macroeconomic' C carried over from the September memo still reads the Riksbank as on hold and neutral; the new memo's text has moved past it.

What it does to the case

  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C: the same. Written on these results; nothing in the response adds a force the case did not weigh. The sell-side earnings momentum pin's move from A to B on 27 October is the mark the case said it would make.
  • Foundations' Robustness B: more pressure, not through a grade. Both readings press on its two stated qualifiers, the revenue margin the moat does not protect and the cost line's record. Neither is evidenced beyond one quarter's yields and one witness.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness C: the same. The case said not crisp until after 22 January 2027. The multiple condition is firmer, the 10th percentile from the case's 12th; the concern has not cleared in estimates, with consensus FY2027 at SEK 21.13 and revisions +2%.
  • Case Riskiness B: slightly more pressure. The second reading bears on "no franchise risk" and on the operational data gap the case itself names; Q3 supplied no uptime or incident data.
  • 'The fair multiple' (60, C) and 'Value chain, business model, financial profile, management' (78, B): more pressure from the first reading, which goes to whether the quality the 19.5x rests on is a franchise quality or an earnings quality.
  • 'Forecast financials' (42, D): more pressure on the drift assumption; the single-quarter moves in the brokerage yield and the fund margin are each about a year of the case's path.
  • 'Scope for upgrades' (35, D) and 'Current momentum' (35, D): the same. The sell-side has not cut twelve days on, and the case expected the cut in January, not now. Technical D, the revision score 6 from 10, the buy share 47% from 50%: the direction the case expected, arriving.
  • The share price against the case's levels. SEK 355.0 is below the "bullish below SEK 360" level the case locates at the 200-day average and 1.7% below that average; the case's fair trough band of SEK 310 to 345 sits 3% to 13% below it; the 50-day at SEK 383.18, the case's close-above trigger, is not near. What would settle it

The questions left open on results morning, first. The print lines are answered above: every revenue line below its bar, costs below theirs the right way, and net interest income's miss coming from deposits rather than lending. The broker response: nobody chased Deutsche Bank's above-consensus numbers, and Deutsche Bank held them without publishing a reset; DNB Carnegie kept its conditional upside, now 17%, while trimming by under 1%. Sigma: SEK 710m of assets under management one month after the 31 August 2026 broad launch, charged at 0.55% to 0.85%, which answers the scale question; customer count, margin, retention and payback are not disclosed, and nothing in the quarter's materials promises them. Denmark against its envelope: unchanged at SEK 120m to 150m through an H2 2027 launch, SEK 40m of it in 2026; nothing before the launch can adjudicate it. Two questions remain where they were: the Riksbank decides on 4 November 2026, and the October monthly statistics arrive on the company's first-week pattern.

What settles the first reading: the October and November statistics, read for brokerage-generating notes per customer and the foreign share; then the preliminary statement of 22 January 2027, read for the brokerage yield against 10.3 basis points with the Private Banking and Pro share of trading beside it, the fund margin against 22.2 basis points, and income per customer against the case's SEK 2,300 and SEK 2,050 markers. Yield down with mix flat confirms the reading; yield up with mix flat is the case's recovery. The cohort-level yield that would settle it directly is not something the company discloses, so this stays an inference from two quarterly numbers. Any move on the 25 basis point standard currency fee would settle it at once.

What settles the second: the 2027 Swedish cost guide on 22 January 2027 against the case's 8.5% negative trigger and 7% to 8% central case, and the first Danish platform disclosures in H1 2027. Uptime, incident and recovery data would settle the operational half; they were not supplied with Q3 and are not a disclosure the company makes, so short of an outage visible in the monthly statistics this question has no scheduled answer.

The case's own sequence — cuts into the mid-January compilation, then the print, then the spring — is unchanged by anything above. The response so far says the cuts have not started, which is what the case expected at this date.
