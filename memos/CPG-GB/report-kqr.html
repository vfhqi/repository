Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary

Section 3 – Revenue Exposure and Volume Dynamics Across Segments

Section summary
  1. Corporate caterers have distinct vertical profiles with varying exposures to highly resilient defensive segments.

  3. Office occupancy trends have partially decoupled from catering revenues due to corporate premiumization initiatives.

Segment revenue distributions and defensive mix

1.
Differentiated segment distributions: Compass Group maintains a highly balanced portfolio that dampens exposure to macroeconomic cycles. According to segment disclosures from Bernstein Research on 11 September 2026, Compass Group derives 40% of its revenues from the Business & Industry division. The remaining 60% of revenues are distributed across highly defensive segments, including 22% in healthcare, 19% in education, and 13% in sports and leisure. This diversified segment distribution ensures that revenues remain insulated from office employment downturns.
2.
Concentrated vertical exposure: Sodexo possesses a slightly more concentrated corporate profile compared to its larger peer. Disclosures analyzed by Bernstein Research on 11 September 2026 indicate that Sodexo's Business & Administrations division represents 47% of its total group revenues. This is offset by defensive exposures consisting of 24% in healthcare and seniors, and 20% in education. This heavier concentration in corporate contracts means that Sodexo faces greater relative office volatility than Compass Group.

Distinct travel-centric model: DO & CO operates under a completely distinct commercial model that bypasses traditional corporate cafeteria risks entirely. Segment reports from DO & CO on 12 February 2026 reveal that the Airline Catering division accounts for 77.8% of group revenues. The rest of the business comprises International Event Catering at 14.7% and Restaurants, Lounges & Hotels at 7.5%. Section 4 – Acceleration of First-Time Outsourcing in Non-Office Sectors

Section summary
  1. Non-office sectors are experiencing an acceleration in first-time outsourcing driven by operational complexities.
  2. Compass Group is capturing a disproportionate share of institutional wins compared to Sodexo.
  3. Mounting financial pressures are forcing healthcare and education institutions to transition away from self-operated kitchens.

Institutional transition speeds and metrics

1.
High self-operation: In the massive US healthcare foodservice segment, around 65% of hospitals still choose to run their kitchens in-house. An expert with 15 years of healthcare nutrition experience noted on 21 December 2023 that approximately 64% to 66% of hospitals are self-operated. This high self-op rate means contract caterers have substantial room for market penetration over the next three years.
2.
Stagnant outsourcing: The K-12 public school catering market remains heavily dominated by self-operation with only slow progress toward privatization. An expert with 30 years of transportation experience explained on 16 June 2026 that around 60% of school services remain insourced. This slow transition is because school districts face significant political and labor challenges that hinder conversion initiatives.
3.
Rapidly expanding: Unlike primary schools, higher education institutions have rapidly moved toward outsourcing to improve their financial positions. An expert in school food service management estimated on 23 July 2025 that higher education has transitioned to being 80%+ outsourced. This massive shift demonstrates that universities are embracing specialized external partners to manage their complex operations.
4.
Consolidation trends: Large-scale healthcare facilities are increasingly turning to major contract caterers to streamline their auxiliary services. Morgan Stanley reported on 21 May 2026 that the big three contract caterers now serve 56% of the top 50 US hospitals. This consolidation demonstrates that large institutions prefer scaled operators that can deliver reliable service.
5.
Accelerating gains: Within the competitive higher education landscape, Compass Group is capturing significant market share directly from self-operators and lagging competitors. Morgan Stanley reported on 21 May 2026 that Compass increased its share of the top 50 US colleges from 16% to 24% since 2023. This rapid expansion demonstrates that Compass's collegiate dining models are highly attractive to large academic systems.

Macro economic and operational drivers

6.
Escalating costs: Persistent food and labor inflation has made self-operated kitchens financially unsustainable for many non-profit hospitals. Morgan Stanley argued on 9 October 2025 that elevated inflation forces selfoperators to bear massive volatile cost burdens alone. Because contract caterers have established purchasing networks, they can easily shield clients from these price spikes.
7.
Growing complexity: Increasingly strict nutritional guidelines and allergen labeling laws are driving public schools to seek professional food operators. Jefferies reported on 7 November 2024 that strengthening regulations are making institutional kitchen management highly complex for non-specialists. Unless schools outsource, they must invest heavily in specialized dietitians and compliance staff.
8.
Pressured budgets: Public entities often outsource their catering facilities to avoid the high capital costs of modernizing aging kitchen equipment. Deutsche Bank noted on 1 October 2026 that self-operated systems are highly sensitive to capital expenditure constraints. Outsourcing allows hospitals and schools to defer expensive kitchen infrastructure investments to well-capitalized catering partners.

Competitive win rates and GPO scale

9.
Dominant capture: Compass Group continues to capture the largest portion of first-time outsourcing wins through its massive scale. Morgan Stanley reported on 21 July 2026 that 51% of Compass Group's new contract wins are coming from clients outsourcing for the first time. This high rate of conversion means Compass is actively expanding the total addressable market.

Underperforming peer: Sodexo has struggled to match the aggressive first-time outsourcing conversion rates of its primary competitor. This slow recovery suggests Sodexo is lagging behind Compass in commercial execution.

Sales deficiencies: Former employees highlight that Sodexo has struggled to convert self-operated contracts due to a weak internal sales culture and lower headcount. Bernstein Research explained on 28 May 2026 that Compass Group has replicated its dominant Foodbuy purchasing model across major European markets.
