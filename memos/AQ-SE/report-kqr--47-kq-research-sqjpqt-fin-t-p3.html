EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report examines the critical question of which feedback loops–reinforcing or balancing–will dominate AQ Group's business dynamics during 2027-2028, and how these systemic forces will shape the sustainability of its earnings per share trajectory. The core inquiry focuses on identifying which feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops).

The answer to this question carries profound implications for the group's ability to meet broker consensus estimates and maintain its historical compounding performance. The evidence suggests that both reinforcing and balancing loops operate simultaneously, creating a dynamic tension that will determine whether the company can sustain its aggressive growth trajectory or face operational constraints that curtail expansion.

AQ Group achieved organic growth of 18% in the third quarter of 2026, driven by high demand in data centres and defence systems, demonstrating powerful reinforcing momentum from structural tailwinds. However, this rapid expansion simultaneously triggers balancing mechanisms that absorb liquidity and strain operational execution. The company's operating cash flow for the first nine months of 2026 fell to SEK 631 million from SEK 799 million in the previous year due to working capital build, as growth in inventories and accounts receivable absorbed cash, illustrating how growth creates immediate financial constraints.

The reinforcing loops center on three primary mechanisms that accelerate organic momentum. First, the datacentre capacity and design-win loop creates powerful network effects where early design-stage integration secures proprietary component slots with major infrastructure providers, particularly through the ABB partnership route to hyperscalers. AQ Group acquired the assets of Norlake in Ohio on 21 September 2026 for SEK 133 million to double its North American inductive manufacturing footprint, with the acquired factory capable of generating annual revenues of SEK 300 million at full utilization, unlocking significant mid-term earnings potential.

This capacity relief removes a critical bottleneck, as the group's existing inductive component factory in Virginia is approaching its capacity ceiling with annual sales near USD 50 million in 2026, and this capacity constraint would cap near-term organic volume growth unless supplementary capacity is rapidly integrated. The strategic importance of this loop is amplified by ABB acting as a main route to hyperscaler deployments, with

ABB reporting triple-digit growth in its data-centre electrification orders in its third-quarter results, with electrification orders reaching USD 7.7 billion, representing 70% organic growth, indicating that hyperscaler demand is still accelerating.

Second, the counter-cyclical M&A and local-for-local reinforcing loops have historically allowed AQ Group to compound returns through strategic acquisitions during industrial downturns. AQ Group historically leverages industrial downturns to release working capital and accumulate cash reserves for inorganic expansion, and during these periods of slower organic volume growth, cash flow builds and creates substantial capital allocation flexibility, with management successfully utilizing this counter-cyclical cash build to fund five acquisitions in 2024 when organic growth slowed down.

The financial mechanics are compelling: DNB Carnegie assumes an average acquisition multiple of 6 times EBITA for the company's transactions, which mathematically translates into a steady-state return on capital employed of 16.7% for the group's investments. This low-multiple entry strategy is complemented by a global local-for-local manufacturing footprint spanning 17 countries, which positions the company to capture structural demand from regionalized supply chains.

Rising geopolitical risks are driving massive investments in regional manufacturing capacity, and AQ Group's strategic presence in 17 countries isolates its industrial customers from disruptive tariffs and trade conflicts, enabling regional manufacturing capacity investments and accelerating regional market share gains.

However, these reinforcing loops face significant balancing constraints that could dominate if operational execution falters. The most immediate balancing mechanism is working capital absorption during rapid scaling. AQ Group faces a significant balancing loop where rapid organic expansion in high-growth segments drains operational liquidity by tying up cash in trade receivables, with operating cash flow for the first nine months of 2026 falling to SEK 631 million from SEK 799 million in the prior year period due to this working capital build.

This cash drain reached extreme levels during peak growth periods, as the group's interim report showed operating cash flow of only SEK 23 million compared to SEK 232 million in the second quarter of 2025, highlighting that growth segments absorb immense liquid funds before yielding actual cash earnings. The severity of this balancing loop is confirmed by peer comparisons, with Volex recording a disappointing cash conversion rate of 63.9% in fiscal 2026 compared to 67.2% in fiscal 2025, indicating that accelerated data-centre infrastructure scaling universally requires heavy upfront capital commitment.

A second critical balancing loop emerges from delivery precision slippage and operational strain during rapid production ramps. AQ Group's interim report showed that on-time delivery precision fell to 91.1% in the third quarter of 2026, remaining well below the 98% target, and this operational slippage could jeopardize future project re-awards with demanding industrial customers. The operational challenges are compounded by quality complaints in the fourth quarter of 2025 that impacted earnings negatively, indicating that accelerated engineering ramps risk operational friction unless testing protocols are enhanced.

Management bandwidth is further stretched by multiple simultaneous turnarounds, including Time 24, acquired in May 2026 for a nominal sum but requiring SEK 50 million in debt redemption and new leadership to address backlogs, with this restructuring diverting operational resources from core plants and delaying consolidated margin expansion.

Customer concentration risks are particularly acute given the group's top ten customers accounting for more than 50% of total net sales, with a single major customer historically representing 13% to 18% of group revenues, meaning that earnings remain highly vulnerable to any spending cuts by these industrial giants.
