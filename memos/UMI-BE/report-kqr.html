Section 3 – PGM Pricing, Recycling Volumes, and Metal Hedging

Section summary
  1. Platinum-group metal deficits remain structurally supportive for the near-term earnings of regional refiners and recyclers.
  2. High price volatility and operational metal losses are squeezing refining margins despite robust trading activities.
  3. Slower scrap collection growth and lower catalytic loadings are limiting the expansion of secondary metal recycling volumes.
  4. Hedging programs protect short-term refining cash flows but expose long-term earnings to structural commodity price declines.

Market deficits and metal price dynamics

1.
Supportive structural deficits: Platinum-group metal markets are exhibiting highly resilient fundamentals despite broader global economic challenges. Morgan Stanley's price deck of October 6, 2026, forecasts a supportive pre-investment platinum deficit of 333 koz for the year, which represents 5% of annual demand. This deficit is significantly larger than copper's 0.2% deficit, which means that near-term metal prices should remain high enough to protect underlying refining margins.
2.
Divergent pricing drivers: Industrial demand from emerging AI technologies is offsetting the gradual decline in traditional automotive catalyst requirements. Jefferies argues in its October 9, 2026 note that platinum remains the most constructive metal due to glass and electrical applications. This growing non-automotive industrial consumption will support Umicore's and Johnson Matthey's specialty recycling earnings even as the internal combustion market contracts.
3.
Volatile minor metals: Surging minor metal pricing is creating mixed margin environments for specialty processors and downstream industrial consumers. Euroland Corporate reports on September 18, 2026, that the dramatic price surge of ruthenium has significantly squeezed the operating margins of regional chemical manufacturers. This price appreciation benefits primary refiners like Johnson Matthey but increases raw material sourcing costs for downstream users unless they thrift metal content.
4.
Shifting supply balances: The global platinum market is transitioning from multi-year structural deficits toward temporary seasonal surpluses. The World Platinum Investment Council's quarterly report of September 9, 2026, outlines that while the full year 2026 will see a 265 koz surplus, the second half faces a deficit of 283 koz. This extreme volatility will continue, driving elevated precious metal trading and management profits for Umicore.

Secondary recycling volume dynamics

5.
Lagging metal recovery: Secondary metal collection volumes are expanding globally but failing to translate into equivalent refined output. The World Platinum Investment Council notes on September 9, 2026, that global recycling supply rose 9% year-on-year, but lower catalytic loadings are limiting recovered metal volumes relative to physical scrap. This trend suggests that recycling plants must process higher physical volumes to maintain historical PGM production.
6.
Price-driven scrap flows: High PGM basket prices are successfully incentivizing the release of accumulated secondary catalyst inventories. Morgan Stanley notes in its July 8, 2026 report that London Platinum Week discussions focused on rising recycling volumes in 2026, which are highly sensitive to market pricing. This close correlation indicates that secondary volumes will decline quickly if PGM prices retreat, compressing overall recycling asset utilization.
7.
Unbalanced scrap growth: The expansion of secondary refining volumes is heavily weighted toward specific metals rather than the entire PGM basket. The World Platinum Investment Council expects on June 11, 2026, that platinum recycling will grow at a 2.4% compound rate through 2030, compared to a faster 6.1% growth rate for palladium. This divergence reflects the historical acceleration of palladium-heavy gasoline vehicles entering global scrap networks.

Operating performance and margin comparison

8.
Elevated operating margins: Refiners are extracting high returns from PGM services despite encountering significant operational headwinds. Johnson Matthey's annual report of June 8, 2026, highlights that its PGM Services division achieved a 28.3% margin in fiscal year 2026. This performance indicates that complex refining operations remain highly lucrative compared to standard chemical peers, supporting EPS during the transition.
9.

Johnson Matthey disclosed on June 10, 2026, that it recognized a £48 million operational metal loss at its US refinery, driven by elevated commodity prices during its biennial stocktake. This issue shows that refining earnings are highly sensitive to operational containment, which will pressure near-term EPS.
10.
Strong recycling results: Complex recycling and trading operations are delivering substantial earnings upside for European materials technologies. Deutsche Bank reports on July 31, 2026, that Umicore's first-half recycling EBITDA beat consensus by 12% to reach €259 million. This outperformance suggests that Umicore's precious metals management team has successfully navigated volatile trading curves, which protects dividends from near-term battery materials declines.

Capable complex flowsheets: Flexible metallurgical processing systems allow dominant recyclers to extract high value from low-grade secondary feeds. Belmont Metals publications of September 1, 2026, describe Umicore's Hoboken facility as the largest recycler of complex secondary materials because its flowsheet recovers 17 distinct metals. This unique capability gives Umicore a significant competitive advantage over traditional copper smelters, protecting its long-term market share.

Deutsche Bank noted on July 31, 2026, that Umicore's second-half 2025 recycling earnings declined significantly because of lower hedged price contracts. This highlights that hedging programs can act as a drag on earnings unless managers accurately forecast price trends and timing.

This historical collapse implies that current PGM deficits will eventually give way to structural surpluses once the automotive transition resumes.
