Section 3 – Wide-bandgap technology and deposition tool dynamics

Section summary
  1. The transition of silicon carbide manufacturing from 150 millimetre to 200 millimetre wafers is accelerating across major European players despite near-term yield hurdles.
  2. Strong order momentum in optoelectronics helps deposition toolmaker AIXTRON offset the cyclical trough in silicon carbide tool shipments.
  3. Stranded-portfolio risks are intensifying as legacy silicon power portfolios face marginalization from rapid wide-bandgap adoption in AI data centers.

Wafer size transition and qualification timelines

1.
Accelerated timeline: STMicroelectronics plans to achieve 100% silicon carbide production on 200mm wafers by the 2027 to 2028 timeframe as outlined in its November 2024 Capital Markets Day.
2.
First release: Infineon Technologies has released its first 200mm silicon carbide products manufactured in its Villach facility in Austria in the first quarter of 2025.
3.
Capacity consolidation: Wolfspeed has fully transitioned its device fabrication to its Mohawk Valley facility operating exclusively on 200mm substrates following the closure of its Durham 150mm fabrication facility in 2026.
4.
27 10K Foundry expansion: X-FAB has prioritized its capital expenditure to transition its Lubbock facility into a dedicated silicon carbide foundry hub with a capacity target of up to 18,000 wafers per month.

Epitaxial progress: Epiworld International reported that its 8-inch silicon carbide epitaxial wafer sales surpassed all of its 2025 volume during the first half of 2026.
6.
Advanced scaling: While the industry focuses on 8-inch conductive substrates, Epiworld has already secured its first small-batch 12-inch orders in 2026.

Deposition tool dynamics and stranded portfolio risks

7.
Strong backlog: AIXTRON's G10 series has seen robust adoption driving its equipment order backlog to EUR 456.9 million as of June 30, 2026.
8.
Pre-payment benefits: AIXTRON's customer advance payments reached EUR 197 million in mid-2026 to provide substantial non-debt funding for its production ramp.
9.
Inventory buildup: To support shipping schedules, AIXTRON increased inventories to EUR 318 million by June 2026.

Idle tool risk: Despite stable demand, AIXTRON faces cyclical underutilization of installed GaN tools before next-generation 800V architectures ramp up.

Stranded portfolio risk: Legacy 150mm silicon fabrication lines face high risk of becoming stranded assets as margins face severe dilution from cheaper wide-bandgap alternatives.

  2. Capacityreservation agreements secure long-term volumes but leave pricing market-based, exposing suppliers to pricing erosion once massive industry capacity ramps after twenty-seven.

Section 7 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. The systemic pivot to 800V DC power architectures dramatically reshapes upstream grid dependencies, legacy silicon margins, and advanced packaging materials, far exceeding the direct boundaries of the server rack.
  2. Severe thermal runaway and electrical safety risks shift the industry bottleneck from wafer fabrication capacity to advanced substrate thermal management and solid-state circuit breakers.

Infrastructure displacement and materials impact

1.
Dramatically reduced copper: Decreasing distribution current by over ninety percent allows operators to shrink copper busbar diameters to fourteen millimetres, reducing cabling weight and layout complexity inside high-density AI server halls.
