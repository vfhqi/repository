1.

2.
Surging profitability: Highly efficient station operations helped Fastned more than double H1 2026 operational EBITDA to €37.4 million, achieving an impressive 46.7% operational margin
3.
Rising unit revenues: Scaled commercial density drove Fastned annualized revenue per station to €363,000 in H1 2026, representing a strong 18% sequential increase
4.
Heavy capital drain: Aggressive network rollouts kept Fastned free cash flow negative at €-55 million in H1 2026, driven by heavy expansion capex of €45 million
5.
Attractive site IRRs: Unsubsidized site economics for Allego ultra-fast charging sites deliver highly attractive 10-year IRRs of 29.7% with a brief 4.2-year payback period
6.
Private backing support: Private infrastructure funding provides capital resilience, with Meridiam fully funding Allego through €150 million in convertible debt to expand ultra-fast corridors
7.

9.
Regulatory synchronization: Mandatory driver rest periods under European safety laws perfectly allow opportunity charging during required breaks because drivers must take a 45-minute break every 4.5 hours of driving

Siting optimization models: Integrated highway corridor models show co-locating chargers with rest areas eliminates transport delays by matching vehicle recharge cycles with mandatory stationary rest

Section 7 – Data gaps 8 findings

Section summary
  1. Systematic data gaps are best resolved by integrating S&P Global Mobility network datasets with targeted private operator expert interviews.

Tracking limits in fast charging

1.
Unverifiable competitor metrics: Alpitronic private financial data remains completely shielded from public disclosures, preventing direct margin comparisons with Kempower's cost structures, which can only be resolved by commissioning regional competitor expert interviews
2.
Dynamic pricing transparency: Public network operators run highly volatile local charging tariffs that complicate real-time utilization modeling, creating margin forecasting errors that require systematic web scraping of operator APIs
3.
Granular network utilization: Publicly available datasets fail to capture exact site-level charger throughput, limiting predictive cash flow modeling unless analysts license S&P Global Mobility tracking databases
4.
Uncertain subsidy distribution: Federal and state funding disbursement timelines for fast-charger corridors are highly unpredictable, distorting deployment speed estimates which can be resolved by monitoring national alternative fuel filings

Grid and equipment backlogs

5.
Opaque transformer capacity: Lead times for heavy high-voltage grid equipment are highly variable, stretching visible delivery horizons unless resolved by monitoring localized developer grid filings
6.
Service contract escalators: Long-term service agreement indexing to commodity inflation is highly opaque for private projects, which requires conducting structured utility procurement interviews
7.
Restructuring execution timelines: Wind turbine turnaround schedules are highly sensitive to legacy project litigation, introducing severe earnings volatility that requires tracking municipal offshore project permits

Analytical deficits or blindspots

8.
Other side of the story: : Sceptical analysis reveals these apparent data gaps are often analytical blindspots where investors ignore public filings to focus on short-term macro sentiment, failing to parse explicit company backlogs
