[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Var Energi is a late-cycle yield case dressed as a corporate-change case. The company has done everything it said it would, the market has paid for all of it and for a commodity spike on top, and at NOK 53.2 the only leg of the return that survives a normal oil and gas price is a cash yield of about 9% a year.

  • The internal change was real and has already travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • A new chief executive from September 2023 doubled production from 202.5 kboepd in Q2 2023 to a record 406 kboepd in Q1 2026 through two start-ups and the Neptune Norge acquisition, took leverage from 0.9x to 0.4x, paid 18 consecutive stable or rising distributions, and in July 2026 agreed to buy BlueNord to lift the long-term plateau to about 450 kboepd.
    • Resources, choices and actions became record volumes, a USD 3,719m revenue quarter in Q2 2026, an 84.5% EBITDA margin and sell-side FY2026 EPS estimates up 86% in a year.
      • Nothing in the chain is still waiting to happen.
  • The force that now drives the outputs is external, cyclical and nobody's decision.
    • Realised prices of USD 101 per boe in Q2 2026, Brent at about USD 104 to 106 and TTF at EUR 80 to 88 per MWh (September 2026 readings) sit well above every broker's long-term deck of USD 70 to 75 Brent and USD 10 per mmbtu gas from 2028.
    • The Q2 2026 net profit of USD 839m beat consensus of USD 482m by 74%, on a USD 268m non-cash exchange gain and a 65% tax rate.
      • The brokers themselves attribute their upgrades to FX and price, not to operations.
  • On the framework's metric the outputs decline from here on every deck, including ours.
    • Consensus EPS (brokers' basis, USD) falls from 0.768 in FY2026 to 0.489 in FY2027 (−36%) and 0.350 in FY2028 (−28%)
    • Consensus free cash flow falls from USD 3,072m in FY2026 to USD 956m in FY2027 as Norwegian cash tax catches up
    • The base portfolio declines 8 to 9% a year.
    • So the near-term upgrade cycle is probable and we rate it B, and it does not change the shape of the three-year path, because the sell-side is raising numbers it does not treat as the run rate: the FY2028 consensus line fell 15.7% over twelve months while FY2026 rose 54.4% over six.
  • The price already assumes the spot deck persists.
    • The shares are up 71.2% in twelve months to a 24-month-forward P/E of 12.8x, the 97th percentile of five years and the 99th of ten, the highest multiple and percentile in the cohort, 26.2% above the 200-day average and 1.5% above the consensus target.
    • The mechanism matters more than the percentile: the multiple is 12.8x because the price embeds spot while the forward earnings embed the deck.
      • If the deck is right the price falls to the earnings
      • If spot is right the earnings rise to the price
      • Either way the multiple compresses.
  • The return therefore fails the bar on the modal path and only reaches a D with the multiple held at the top of its range.
    • From NOK 53.2 the modal return is about −11.9% a year over eighteen months and −2.1% a year over three years: a cash leg of about 9% a year that is real, an earnings leg that is flat on the 24-month-forward convention, and a re-rating leg from 12.8x to the stock's own 'defendable' band of 8.8x that is −31%.
    • With the multiple unchanged at 12.8x the eighteen-month return is about 9.4% a year, still below the framework's C anchor.
      • The case clears the 20% bar on the modal path only below about NOK 34 to 37.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', and the fit stops where the pattern's payoff should begin.
    • Seven of the twelve internal forces are present and the change is fully propagated, but the pattern's name ends in major EPS trajectory upgrades, and the trajectory from here is down.

  • The case is therefore not compelling at NOK 53.2.

    • It is a reasonable yield holding on a pullback for an investor who wants the commodity exposure, which is not a Viewforth case
    • It becomes one below about NOK 34 to 37, or if Brent near USD 100 holds through 2029.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material but its materiality has already been spent: strong internal forces ('Change Forces', E 1, B) drove the outputs through all six layers over 2023 to 2026, the market has paid for it, and from here the outputs decline on the framework's metric ('Financial Outputs', E 2, D) while the 'Modal return' (E 3) sits below the framework's F anchor.

    • The parts combine as a chain that holds at its first link and breaks at its second and third, so the thesis the price now needs is a commodity price, not a company change.
    • Not F, because the cash return of about 9% a year is probable, the balance sheet is strong and near-term estimate upgrades are likely.
    • Not C, because a C needs a plausible path to 13 to 16% a year, and on our arithmetic that needs either the multiple to hold at the 97th percentile for eighteen months or Brent near USD 95 to 100 through 2029 to 2030.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case for the stock is that consensus is a floor, not a ceiling.
    • Every broker's deck sits at USD 70 to 75 Brent and USD 10 per mmbtu gas from 2028 while spot is USD 104 to 106 and TTF EUR 80 to 88 per MWh
      • FY2027 consensus EPS has risen 22% in six months and 14% in the last month
      • If the Hormuz premium and Europe's storage deficit persist through 2027, FY2027 EPS is USD 0.75 or more, the stock is on about 7 times that figure, the dividend rises with 25 to 30% of cash flow, and the "high multiple" is an artefact of stale decks.
    • We concede the near-term upgrade cycle (B at RA 1, E 2) and that 2026 to 2027 distributions are safe on the balance sheet.
    • We hold our rating for two reasons.
    • First, the framework values the shares at each horizon on the earnings two years beyond it, so FY2029 to FY2030 earnings for the eighteen-month horizon, and no broker, no forward curve and not we put Brent above about USD 75 in those years.
    • Second, the group's own history is the base rate for a supply shock's duration: gas realisations of USD 204 per boe in Q3 2022 fell to USD 73 by Q1 2026.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the internal forces are many, real and already propagated to the financial statements (production doubled from 202.5 kboepd in Q2 2023 to 406 kboepd in Q1 2026; leverage 0.9x to 0.4x; a transformational merger agreed), while the external forces are strong but cyclical and two-way: a commodity and FX tailwind that the forward curve and every broker's deck expect to fade, set against cost, carbon and demand headwinds.
  • Internal forces: at least seven of the twelve screen forces are present and resourced, which clears the framework's screen rule of three, and they have reached the sell-side's estimates.
  • External forces: the dominant force now acting on the outputs is the commodity price, with under two years' visibility
    • The supply chain and the cash-tax lag act the other way.
  • Not A, because the strongest force now acting on the outputs is the commodity price, which is nobody's decision, and the internal forces have largely done their work: management described the company as "fully de-risked with major projects complete" on the Q3 2025 call.
  • What it means for the case: the forces are strong enough to have built a case, and the one still accelerating is the one the company does not control.

  • Price tailwind: realised prices were USD 101 per boe in Q2 2026, crude USD 110 per barrel (a USD 6 premium to Dated Brent) and gas USD 91 per boe

  • Demand headwind: European gas demand fell 0.3% in the first eight months of 2026, residential demand 2.3% (Bernstein).
  • Not B, because a force the framework's own 'Demand Inflection' test calls cyclical, with no two-year visibility, cannot carry a B on strength.

  • Not B, because structural demand is falling, so the force is a supply shock, not a demand swing with two years' visibility.
  • Direction now: a tailwind on price, a headwind on volume.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Var Energi is a late-cycle yield case dressed as a corporate-change case.
