HVDC transmission shift – structural transition: Extra high-voltage direct current (HVDC) technology is rapidly solidifying its role as the dominant architecture for offshore wind evacuation and cross-border interconnectors due to superior long-haul efficiency. Subsea and underground grid developments increasingly prioritize direct current over alternating current (AC) because direct current transmission has no reactance, eliminating distance-related voltage drops. This structural preference is illustrated by projections that expenditures for subsea HVDC installations in the EMEA region will reach approximately EUR 6.5b in 2027, representing 86% of the year's total subsea cable capital deployment

Distance-related line losses – reactive power bottlenecks: The technical limitations of conventional highvoltage alternating current (HVAC) subsea cables become severe beyond a threshold of 100 to 150 kilometers. When running subsea AC links, reactive power is generated which opposes power flow, resulting in up to 30% of the transmission capability being lost at a cable length of 100 kilometers. This reactive effect makes HVDC the structurally preferred technology because it eliminates reactance entirely, enabling efficient bulk power transfer over extremely long subsea routes
