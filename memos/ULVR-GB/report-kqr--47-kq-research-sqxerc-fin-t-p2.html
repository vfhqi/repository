Section 2 – Pricing Power under Commodity Inflation and Cost Recovery

Section summary
  1. Unilever took massive price increases during the 2021 to 2023 cycle but suffered significant volume declines, taking eight quarters to restore gross margins to pre-pandemic levels.
  2. Recovering guided 2027 commodity inflation will require a shift back to price-led growth, risking volume momentum across developed markets.

Historical Pricing Precedents

1.
Rapid pricing: Unilever responded to the unprecedented post-pandemic global supply chain crisis by aggressively elevating customer retail prices to counter rapidly rising commodity costs. The group successfully implemented a 2.9% underlying price growth in 2021 while maintaining a positive volume growth of 1.6% across its global divisions. However, pricing actions accelerated to an intense 11.3% in 2022, which caused the group's overall underlying volume growth to fall to negative 2.1% as consumers began pushing back.
2.
Severe volume: Unilever registered a consolidated underlying price growth of 6.8% in 2023, but this aggressive pricing severely depressed consumer demand across mature developed markets. European retail operations experienced a sharp 12.8% underlying price hike that directly triggered a severe volume decline of negative 7.7% for the full year. Conversely, Latin American operations demonstrated far greater demand resilience, achieving a 4.8% volume expansion alongside a 9.6% underlying price increase.

3.
Delayed recovery: Unilever suffered severe profitability compression as the cumulative cost of raw materials rose much faster than global pricing actions could land in the market. The group's gross margin fell to 41.3% in the first half of 2023, remaining a massive 270 basis points below 2019 levels. It ultimately required eight quarters of sequential price increases and intense structural productivity programs for gross margins to recover back to the pre-pandemic 44% milestone in 2024.
4.
Lagged adjustments: Unilever utilizes highly structured procurement strategies to mitigate short-term material volatility, though these vendor agreements introduce significant cost-recovery delays. The group typically resets supplier and packaging contract pricing on a rigid six-month lag to manage commodity risk. This structured delay prevents immediate gross margin stabilization when petrochemical derivatives, crude-derived chemicals, packaging polymers, or palm oils spike unpredictably.
5.
Defensive formulation: Unilever also manages raw material cost volatility by proactively reformulating products to substitute expensive chemical inputs before pricing must be adjusted at retail. The company developed new technologies to rapidly alter ingredient specifications for power brands like its core Lifebuoy soap products. These procurement and design interventions helped restore the group's consolidated gross margin to prepandemic levels and fuel brand investment.

Inflation Recovery Projections

6.
Escalating inflation: Unilever faces renewed profitability pressure as corporate management projects 2027 net material inflation to reach between €550m and €750m on its primary cost base. To protect its underlying operating margin, the company expects the 2027 operating algorithm to be heavily driven by price increases rather than volume. This pricing-heavy strategy risks pushing volumes below the group's strict multi-year framework floor of 2% underlying volume growth.
7.
Contrary evidence: Unilever has occasionally failed to recover cost increases fully when aggressive pricing actions directly triggered immediate consumer pushback or retailer friction. In the first half of 2026, the group's consolidated gross margin contracted by 70 basis points year-on-year to 46.8% because commodity inflation and calibrated promotions compressed profitability in Home Care. This margin compression proves that the company cannot always pass through raw material spikes without sacrificing immediate profitability.
8.
Anemic growth: Management recognized that the group previously suffered from a decade of poor financial execution that severely constrained its pricing power. This historical pattern created a vicious circle where anemic volume growth and margin declines depressed overall brand reinvestment. The group currently aims to break this historical cycle by shifting its operational focus to net savings rather than gross productivity targets.
9.
Strained elasticity: Financial analysts express caution regarding Unilever's ability to maintain volume growth while simultaneously elevating prices to cover 2027 material inflation. Deutsche Bank Research models a 4.6% underlying sales growth for 2027, which incorporates a strained 3.0% pricing contribution alongside a low 1.0% volume expansion. If consumer elasticity worsens under cumulative inflation, this pricing-heavy strategy could easily drag overall volume performance into negative territory.
10.
Vulnerable margins: Valuation models indicate that Unilever's margin recovery will be highly back-half weighted due to initial pricing delays and seasonal tariff offsets. Bernstein Research expects a 30 basis points margin contraction in the first half of 2027 as rising raw material costs outpace early price increases. Unless pricing actions land faster, the group's full-year margins will remain compressed because the company has limited self-help productivity levers remaining.

Section 5 – Price Elasticity, Premiumization, and Wellness Portfolio Resilience

Section summary
  1. Historical elasticity data reveals that Unilever faces a critical pricing threshold of 2.5%, above which volume growth drops significantly.
  2. Premium wellness and beauty portfolios offer structural price inelasticity but face growing margin risks from private label alternatives and high concentration.
  3. Competitors are executing margin and promotional resets to stimulate volume growth, intensifying the promotional reinvestment needed for Unilever to hold market share in 2027.

Elasticity and Pricing Thresholds

1.
Moderate elasticity: Jefferies Research highlights that Unilever has historically climbed the wall of commodity inflation with positive net pricing results. Implied group price elasticity over twenty years has been as low as negative 0.5 under different conditions. This relatively low elasticity indicates that price increases have remained net positive for the group's top-line revenue.
2.
Severe resistance: Deutsche Bank Research warns that Unilever historically faces negative volume elasticity when pricing moves above critical levels. The group has generally seen volume growth slow to under 2% when pricing reaches 2.5% or more. This historical boundary suggests that 2027 volume growth targets of over 2% will be difficult to hit if pricing must accelerate.
3.
Divergent categories: Redburn Atlantic notes a clear divergence in elasticity across Unilever's distinct operating segments. While the Ice Cream and Nutrition divisions experienced high elasticity, the beauty and personal care units remained resilient during periods of elevated pricing. This resilience allowed these categories to return to positive volume growth even under high prices.
4.
High vulnerability: Former consumer goods executives observe that price sensitivity varies significantly depending on the specific body part targeted. While facial and children's categories are relatively price-inelastic, body care products are highly sensitive to retail price changes. Slight price increases in commoditized body categories have historically triggered immediate and substantial volume losses.
5.
Historical elasticity: Bernstein Research highlights that the volume impact from pricing since 2008 has historically remained limited below a critical threshold. Elasticity becomes highly evident only above 3.5% price growth when the pricing-to-volume relationship turns strongly negative. This precedent suggests that Unilever can safely implement small price increases, but hikes exceeding this level will trigger severe demand contraction.

Wellness Portfolio Resilience

6.
Contrary evidence: Wellness price increases did not dent volume sales in core nutrition and multivitamin categories where consumers prioritized health in January 2026.
