What the company does

Demant is a vertically integrated Danish hearing healthcare group with three legs: wholesale hearing aids under the Oticon, Bernafon, Sonic and Philips brands, over 4,500 retail clinics, and diagnostics, where it leads with over 40 percent equipment share at 10 percent of sales. Full-year 2025 revenue was 22,971 million Danish kroner at a 17.2 percent margin before special items. The United States is 32 percent of sales, Europe 44.6, and one Polish plant makes 90 percent of global volume.

A plausible investment thesis

The mechanism is internal and mechanical. A category-leading custom product takes premium share, a cost programme and the German retail conversion lift the margin, and a falling interest bill turns that into earnings per share faster than revenue grows. The memo of 8 October carries adjusted earnings per share of 14.2 kroner in 2026, 16.6 in 2027 and 19.1 in 2028 from 11.74 in 2025, about 17.7 percent a year and 2 to 5 percent above consensus.

Four drivers to track, all dated. Oticon Zeal's share of United States Veterans Affairs custom units, 23.1 percent by July 2026 from 19.2 a year earlier at 695 dollars, a 42 percent premium to legacy devices. Wholesale organic growth of 8 percent or better with the gross margin held, at the third-quarter statement in early November. The cost programme, 300 million kroner of savings in 2026 of 500 million by 2028. And the KIND German clinics, now guided to 325 million of 2026 profit with internal supply going from 25 percent to over 95.
