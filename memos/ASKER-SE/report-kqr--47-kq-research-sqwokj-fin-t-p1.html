Section 2 – Cost Structure and Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. Distributor margins vary widely between reported and standardized measures due to net accounting treatment of third-party logistics.
  2. Asker demonstrated modest operating leverage in first half 2026, which lags the historical margin expansion seen at peers like AddLife.

Benchmark distributor cost splits

1.
High cost of goods: Brand-neutral medical-supplies distributors typically face significant variable product costs that dominate their total expenditure. DNB Carnegie's report of 22 October 2026 indicates that Accendra Health had product costs representing 53% of its total revenue. This high cost of goods sold restricts the absolute dollar margin available to cover downstream overhead. Unless a distributor can secure manufacturer pricing concessions, future earnings per share expansion remains constrained by these product costs.
2.
Significant operational overhead: Warehousing and selling expenses form the bulk of a distributor's non-product costs. DNB Carnegie's report of 22 October 2026 shows that Accendra Health spent 39% of its revenue on general and administrative overhead. This substantial overhead demonstrates that operations require careful volume scaling to cover fixed logistics assets. Therefore, managing these operational cost lines tightly is critical to protecting operating profits during volume down-cycles.
3.
Asker cost allocations: Asker's historical financial structure shows a similar concentration of expenses within product purchases. Asker's prospectus of 17 March 2025 reveals that cost of goods sold accounted for 64.7% of its total operating expenses in 2024. Selling expenses and administrative expenses made up 24.9% and 9.4% of total costs respectively. This allocation indicates that Asker remains highly sensitive to product cost inflation, which directly impacts operating leverage.
4.
Impact of agent accounting: Transitioning to net revenue recognition for logistics services significantly alters reported distributor margins. Asker's annual report of 10 February 2026 notes that the group adopted net recognition for all third-party logistics contracts under agent principles starting in 2024. This change reduces reported net sales while leaving gross profit absolute figures unchanged. Consequently, this accounting adjustment artificially inflates the reported gross margin percentage relative to traditional peers.

Comparing gross and operating margins

5.
Gross margin comparisons: Reported gross margins often show large discrepancies when compared with standardized database measures. Asker's annual report of 10 February 2026 reported a gross margin of 41.1% in 2025. However, FactSet standardized database metrics of early 2026 report Asker's gross margin as 36.1% due to differing service cost classifications. This standardized figure is lower than AddLife's reported 2025 gross margin of 38.1% from its annual report of 4 April 2026. Therefore, the underlying profitability is less exceptional than reported.
6.
EBITA margin lag: Higher gross margins do not automatically translate into superior operating profitability if operational costs remain high. DNB Carnegie's report of 10 September 2026 highlights that Asker achieved a 9.5% adjusted EBITA margin in 2025. This margin lags behind its closest peer AddLife, which achieved a 12.1% adjusted EBITA margin in the same period. This operating margin gap indicates that Asker carries higher operational costs that offset its pricing advantages.
7.
Contrary evidence: Subdued demand and strikes can temporarily break the typical margin expansion trajectory during economic up-cycles. AddLife's interim report of 21 October 2026 shows that strikes in Spain held back medtech sales growth and dampened medtech margins to 11.3% in the third quarter of 2026. This disruption shows that external labor disputes can offset positive product mix improvements. Consequently, investors cannot assume that operating leverage will consistently deliver margin expansion.

Testing H1 2026 operating leverage

8.
Modest operating leverage: Asker showed positive but limited operating leverage during the first half of 2026. Asker's interim report of 21 July 2026 reported 5% organic sales growth and 7% organic adjusted EBITA growth. This performance represents an incremental adjusted EBITA margin that only slightly exceeds the group's base margin. This modest leverage suggests that organic volume gains alone are insufficient to drive rapid margin expansion.
9.
Peer leverage comparison: Peer groups have shown stronger organic margin progression when executing structured operational improvements. AddLife's interim report of 21 October 2026 shows that its adjusted EBITA grew 11% on 4% organic sales growth in the third quarter of 2026. This translates to an adjusted EBITA margin of 11.5%, up 40 basis points year-on-year. This performance proves that AddLife extracts stronger operating leverage from its organic sales than Asker currently does.
10.
Uniphar margin expansion: Irish medtech distributors have also demonstrated robust organic growth profiles that highlight superior margin momentum. Deutsche Bank's report of 8 September 2026 notes that Uniphar achieved 6.9% organic gross profit growth and 11.2% adjusted earnings growth in first half 2026. This growth was supported by geographic expansion and a scaling operating model. This momentum implies that Uniphar is successfully leveraging its infrastructure to grow earnings ahead of its underlying revenues.

Mid-term profit and margin drivers

11.
Mix shift margin benefits: Shifting the product portfolio toward advanced medical devices can improve overall operating margins over time. Carnegie's report of 6 May 2025 argues that Asker's increasing focus on acquiring companies distributing equipment and devices should have an incremental positive effect on margins. This fragmented supply base allows Asker to protect its profit margins from severe pricing pressure. Consequently, further acquisitions should strengthen Asker's procurement synergies over the next 18 months.

Volume leverage limits: Niche medical products often limit the degree of unit cost reductions that distributors can achieve through scale. An expert interview with a director at Stryker Corporation on 22 May 2026 explained that specialized surgical tools do not benefit from traditional low unit cost manufacturing scale. This dynamic means that distributors cannot rely solely on volume growth to reduce unit purchasing costs. Thus, margin expansion must be driven by operational efficiency rather than supplier discounting.

Cash generation and deleveraging: Strong organic cash flows are vital to support the acquisition pipeline that drives overall earnings growth. DNB Carnegie's report of 18 September 2026 highlights that AddLife's operational progress during 2023-25 significantly improved its balance sheet to a net debt multiple of 2.2x. Other side of the story: Steady volume growth in defensive healthcare markets provides reliable downside protection that mitigates operating risks. DNB Carnegie's report of 10 September 2026 emphasizes that Asker operates in a non-cyclical end-market with highly predictable revenue streams. Section 3 – Self-Help Restructuring and Central Cost Precedents

Section summary
  1. AddLife's structured self-help initiatives achieved substantial margin expansion by eliminating unprofitable digital units and central corporate overhead.
  2. Lean management structures can safely reduce central distributor costs without compromising strict operational or financial controls.
  3. The divestment of underperforming units provides immediate capital inflows to accelerate future high-margin acquisitions.

AddLife restructuring and self-help execution

1.
Successful Camanio closure: AddLife successfully completed the closure of its underperforming Camanio homecare division in September 2024. The structured wind-down completely eliminated SEK 60m of annual losses and delivered an annual cash flow improvement of SEK 90m from the fourth quarter of 2024 onward. This self-help action demonstrated that terminating proprietary digital projects can rapidly restore subsidiary profitability.
2.
AddVision operational turnaround: AddLife executed a comprehensive reorganization of its AddVision eye surgery business during the second half of 2023. The restructuring measures dismantled the centralized head office to restore a highly decentralized model across local subsidiaries. DNB Carnegie reported on 5 September 2025 that these streamlining efforts successfully turned the subsidiary from loss-making to profitable. Consequently, decentralized restructuring proved that local autonomy enhances sales agility.
3.
Realized corporate savings: The restructuring of AddVision delivered annual cost savings of SEK 20m starting in early 2024. AddLife's management confirmed during the Q2 2024 earnings call that they met and exceeded this saving target through efficiency gains. These actions required a modest restructuring reserve of only SEK 8m in late 2023. This indicates that targeted central cost elimination carries a very short payback period for medtech compounders.
4.
Restructuring delivery timeline: The implementation and delivery of AddLife's self-help program followed a highly disciplined and rapid multi-quarter timeline. Restructuring of AddVision and Camanio began in late 2023, with Camanio's complete decommissioning finalized by September 2024. This timeline shows that complex operational closures require approximately 12 months to completely eliminate costs. Consequently, distributors must anticipate extended transition periods before restructuring savings are fully realized.
5.
Severe upfront write-downs: Executing these large-scale restructuring programs required substantial non- recurring asset impairments in late 2023. AddLife recognized write-downs and one-off costs totaling SEK 134m during the fourth quarter of 2023. These charges included a SEK 106m impairment of intangibles and SEK 19m of property write-downs within Camanio. Thus, material profit improvements require absorbing significant upfront capital hits before savings materialize.

110 - Earnings Calls Gradual margin recovery: AddLife's structural self-help program directly enabled the group to expand its adjusted EBITA margin from 10.5% in 2023 to 12.1% in 2025. This margin improvement occurred despite muted market volume growth. Therefore, focused pruning and restructuring represent highly effective tools to lift distributor profitability without relying on organic volume.

Central function dismantling and management lean-sizing

7.
Lean group management: AddLife's corporate headquarters has been optimized to consist of a lean team of just two executive individuals. RedEye reported on 18 September 2026 that this transition maximized executive alignment and operational agility across the decentralized group. This ultra-lean structure contrasts with historical peer setups where multiple regional directors were employed. Consequently, AddLife's model demonstrates that scaled distributors can function efficiently with minimal central staff.

Thus, distributors can safely reduce central headcount by delegating operational authority to local managers.
9.
Contrary evidence: Deficiencies in financial monitoring can arise when central staff lines are reduced too aggressively in highly decentralized organizations. AddLife's annual report of 25 April 2023 warned that decentralized structures place high demands on financial reporting and risk inadequate operational control. The parent company relies on strict monthly financial reporting and structured KPIs to maintain good insight into subsidiary performance. This rigorous tracking allows group executives to identify potential challenges before they impact consolidated earnings. Therefore, a distributor can successfully reduce central headcount only if it preserves highly disciplined reporting protocols.
11.
Asker head office opportunity: Asker Healthcare's central cost structure presents substantial scope for administrative cost reduction modeled after AddLife's lean precedent. DNB Carnegie highlighted on 10 September 2026 that Asker's 50 subsidiaries could be governed with fewer central employees by deepening its decentralized model. Reducing its central headcount closer to AddLife's lean setup would expand Asker's operating margin. The restructuring of AddVision and Camanio reflected this exact strategy by phasing out weaker digital projects. Thus, pruning unprofitable product lines is more effective than expanding volume in commoditized segments.
15.
Strategic portfolio optimization: AddLife's decentralized subsidiaries systematically optimize their product portfolios by removing high-volume, low-margin products in favor of advanced medical solutions. DNB Carnegie reported on 5 September 2025 that this optimization work supported continuous operating margin expansion within the Medtech business area. Consequently, product pruning represents a low-risk strategy to enhance return on working capital.

Therefore, self-help margin improvements may be partially offset by structural sales losses if major product lines are discontinued.
