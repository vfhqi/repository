Section 2 – Growth Dynamics and the InnovaMatrix Reset

Section summary
  1. The termination of the InnovaMatrix drag by fiscal year 2027 will resolve the wide discrepancy between Convatec's reported and organic growth rates.
  2. Slow conversion of hospital Group Purchasing Organization contract wins into community patients reflects structural friction in post-acute channels.
  3. Coloplast is actively counteracting Convatec's competitive inroads by redirecting DKK 500 million in savings primarily into US Chronic Care.

Distinguishing the InnovaMatrix Drag

1.
Diverging growth metrics: Convatec recorded an organic revenue growth rate of 5.0% excluding the InnovaMatrix skin substitute drag in the first half of 2026. However, the group reported organic growth of only 1.8% when including the severe impact of InnovaMatrix. Bernstein Research noted on 5 August 2026 that the 94% collapse in InnovaMatrix sales created a massive 3.2% headwind to group organic growth. Commentators must distinguish these metrics because the legacy drag will disappear in 2027 as the product becomes immaterial to group sales figures.
2.
Improving growth visibility: Morgan Stanley research highlighted on 24 February 2026 that the downward adjustment of the InnovaMatrix base simplifies the medium-term organic trajectory. The bank noted that the skin substitute reset will make this segment a meaningfully smaller driver of group growth going forward. This base reset implies that group organic growth will naturally accelerate to 6% to 8% from 2027 onward. Investors who separate reported figures from underlying metrics will gain confidence in Convatec's core operating momentum.

Converting Acute Contract Wins

3.
Securing acute contracts: Convatec has significantly improved its acute-care market access by securing major ostomy contracts. DNB Carnegie reported on 22 October 2026 that the group obtained a Captis and Vizient ostomy contract in November 2025, followed closely by Premier and Premier AscenDrive agreements in February 2026. These contract wins should support long-term new-patient acquisition as hospital access improves. This institutional traction positions Convatec to capture higher acute-care market share over the coming years.

This competitive convergence has prompted Coloplast to intensify its commercial execution in the underpenetrated US market. The resulting margin pressure on both players could constrain near-term earnings per share expansion across the chronic care sector.

Section 4 – Regulatory, Pricing Reforms, and Payer Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Imminent European price reforms and the proposed French device tax are projected to exert manageable near-term pricing pressure on chronic care franchises.
  2. Bipartisan legislative efforts to delay Medicare competitive bidding for five years could remove a persistent valuation overhang and protect group earnings.
  3. Scaling own-brand manufacturing and leveraging 180 Medical's dominant distribution share will help Convatec mitigate potential US reimbursement headwinds.

European Pricing and Fiscal Pressures

1.
Decreasing prices: Coloplast anticipates that upcoming healthcare pricing reforms in France and Germany will create modest pricing headwinds in fiscal year 2026 and 2027. These country-specific regulatory pressures are expected to keep broader chronic-care pricing neutral to negative over the coming years. Convatec has historically navigated similar European price cuts by driving volume growth through product mix improvements and marketing. This commercial agility indicates that localized European pricing pressure is unlikely to derail group-level earnings per share growth.
2.
Taxing devices: France has proposed a new 3.6% tax on 70% of reimbursed medical device spending in its draft 2027 Social Security budget. This proposed levy is company-specific and effectively represents a 2.5% tax on reimbursed amounts before adjustments. Jefferies estimates the potential impact across its covered medtech names at 45 million euros, or roughly 0.5% of projected 2027 pre-tax profits. This highly targeted measure is unlikely to trigger a sector de-rating because the direct earnings impact remains extremely manageable.

Medicare Competitive Bidding Timelines

3.
Delaying reforms: Morgan Stanley reports that a bipartisan US bill introduced on October 9, 2026, proposes a five- year pause for ostomy and urological supplies from competitive bidding. This legislative delay would defer the scheduled January 2028 inclusion of chronic care products to December 2032. Enacting this bill would provide a mid-single-digit percentage upside to consensus 2028 earnings estimates for Convatec. Removing this regulatory overhang would narrow the persistent valuation discount that has depressed Convatec's stock price relative to its European peers.
4.
Consolidating suppliers: Medicare is seeking to transition its ostomy reimbursement to a nationwide Remote Item Delivery competitive bidding program by 2028. This structural design will dramatically consolidate the supply chain, awarding only six nationwide contracts for ostomy products compared to over 800 current suppliers. Convatec anticipates that this change could restrict patient access to clinically appropriate custom prescriptions as contract suppliers face intense pricing pressure. This regulatory change is expected to disrupt product continuity unless policymakers establish strict safeguards for individualized chronic supplies.
5.
Sizing headwinds: Convatec has historically guided to a 1% to 2% reduction in group revenues during the initial year of competitive bidding implementation. Morgan Stanley estimates that this exposure translates to a 30 million to 60 million dollar sales headwind for the company. Assuming a 70% to 90% operating profit dropthrough, this would result in a 3% to 8% drag on consensus 2028 earnings per share. However, because current consensus figures only partially reflect these headwinds, the downside risk is largely factored into current market valuations.

This extensive scale positions 180 Medical uniquely to capture significant volume gains as smaller regional distributors exit the market under competitive bidding. To mitigate pricing cuts, the distributor plans to accelerate its patient conversion to Convatec's own-brand catheters, which currently represent half of sales. This vertical integration will retain more product economics within the group and help insulate corporate margins from retail pricing compression.
7.
Challenging leaders: Convatec and Coloplast compete directly in the US ostomy market as challengers to the private industry leader, Hollister. Hollister maintains a dominant 50% US ostomy market share, well ahead of Convatec's stabilized 20% share and Coloplast's 15% to 20% share. To defend their relative positions, manufacturers with vertically integrated retail arms, such as Convatec's 180 Medical, will hold a significant post-acute advantage over non-integrated peers like Hollister. This structural distribution strength will determine which chronic care players can successfully capture market share during upcoming regulatory consolidations.
