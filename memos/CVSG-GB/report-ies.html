CVS Group (CVSG-GB): what the 25 November 2026 trading update does to the case

These results strengthen the case modestly on timing and leave it where it was on earnings and ratings: the organic print the case expected to look soft came in better than the case allowed and the chief executive search is in its final stages, while the balance sheet reached the 2.0x guardrail two years before the case's model has it there, and no rating would move a grade today.

What changed

Company figures are for the four months to 31 October 2026, published 25 November 2026.

  • Revenue +6.2%. Like-for-like +2.4%, with July depressed by extreme heat and 3.0% for August to October. Adjusted EBITDA +5.9%, the gap to revenue attributed to one fewer working day. The case expected group like-for-like of about 1.5% against a 2.7% comparative and said the print would be read as deceleration.
  • Healthy Pet Club revenue +4.3%; members 506,000 (508,000 at 30 June 2026); the Advanced tier, launched 1 July 2026, had 20,000 members at 31 October. The case's alarm is 500,000 members; its Advanced test is 25,000 at the March interims, from about 14,500 when the memo was written.
  • Acquisitions £22.9m of initial consideration: two UK practices on four sites for £16.0m, the first UK completions after a pause of over two years, and three Australian practices for £6.9m, taking Australia to 38 practices on 64 sites and over 10% of group revenue, with a further Australian deal signed to complete by December 2026. The case carries about £50m a year.
  • Bank-test leverage 1.94x at 31 October 2026, from 1.63x at 30 June 2026, on the acquisitions and the £50m buyback, now extended to January 2027. Management would consider exceeding the 2.0x guardrail temporarily only for highly attractive acquisitions. The case's model has 1.85x at June 2027 and 1.74x at June 2028.
  • Confidence in meeting full-year consensus reiterated: twelve analysts at £751.0m revenue, £150.7m adjusted EBITDA, 95.6p adjusted EPS. The case holds £149m and 93p to 94p and expected consensus to drift to 92p to 94p by March.
  • The chief executive search has entered its final stages; Richard Fairman stays until a successor is appointed. Final dividend 9.0p (+5.9%) approved.
  • No profit or cash flow statement, no price-volume split, no practice-level or Australian like-for-like.

Sell-side: Deutsche Bank kept Buy and 1,700p, called the update reassuring and de-risking, expects consensus unchanged, judged the 3.0% run-rate broadly in line with consensus, called the leverage fully expected, and asked whether clinical volumes are flat behind 6.2% revenue growth. Jefferies kept Hold and 1,400p, read 2.4% as only marginally ahead of the soft 1.5% second-half FY2026 trend, stressed leverage close to the self-imposed 2.0x limit, and saw 3% upside from 1,365p. Neither moved an estimate. Pets at Home's joint-venture vet division, which the case watched for a UK volume read-across, reported consumer revenue +2.7% for its first half.

The shares closed at 1,369p on 25 November (1,315p at the memo), 9.0% above a 200-day average of 1,256p, relative-strength percentile 67 (52 at the memo), P/E 12.7x at the 19th percentile of its own ten-year history (13th at the memo). The technical momentum pin has moved from D to C; sell-side earnings momentum stays D; valuation is pinned A. None of the price levels the case states has been crossed: the fair trough of about 1,150p, the 1,070p stress level, the 1,770p to 1,990p private-market range.

The challenge

The case built its next six months on three prints that would look worse than the business. The first did not. That admits two readings the case does not hold, and one it does.

Reading one: the organic engine has turned a half-year before the case allows, and the window is now rather than March to September 2027. The case's mechanics for a 1.5% November print were that Animed and the Laboratories would normalise while practice like-for-like improved only from 1.0% to about 1.4%. A group figure of 2.4% against a 2.7% comparative, and 3.0% for three clean months, is not that arithmetic. By the case's own conversion, group like-for-like above 2.5% means practice like-for-like near 2%, above the case's 1.4% for FY2027 and in the upper half of the 1.5% to 2.5% ceiling it puts on practice growth until FY2029; and that is the figure the case said would move its FY2027 EPS toward 96p and the breadth of revisions to neutral four months early. Revisions were neutral. Membership fell 2,000 in four months against 11,000 across FY2026, so the published-price churn the case feared is not visible yet. The Advanced tier is at 20,000 after four months against a 25,000 test set for the six-month mark. The second company event the case needs, a named chief executive, is in its final stages. On this reading the case's judgement that the entry is more likely between March and September 2027 is late by two prints, the probability-weighted return should sit nearer the 14.92x leg than the case weights it, and the risk the case itself names, missing the first 10 to 15% on an appointment as happened at Pets at Home, is live. For it to be right: the 3.0% must be at least partly volume rather than the summer price rise the company described as slightly above historical rates; the July exclusion must be fair rather than convenient; and the practice segment, not Animed or the Laboratories, must be carrying the group figure. Nothing published tells us any of the three. Deutsche Bank's own question, whether volumes are flat behind the 6.2%, is the case's reading of the business, and it is as unanswered as before.

Reading two: the balance sheet, not the chief executive, is now the constraint on the growth loop, and the case's mechanical EPS leg is less mechanical than it says. The case's reinforcing capital-allocation loop supplies about 70% of EBITDA growth to FY2031 and rests on about £50m a year of deals plus a 55% chance of buyback renewal. Four months in, £22.9m has gone out (about three times that annualised, our arithmetic), another Australian completion is signed for December, the buyback runs to January, and bank-test leverage is 1.94x, past the case's modelled June 2027 year-end of 1.85x and 0.06 turns short of a guardrail management will cross only for an exceptional deal. The case's own sensitivities say what a slower loop costs: halving the deal pace from FY2028 is about 1p in FY2028 and about 3.5p in FY2029; no buyback renewal is about 1p in FY2029 and about 5p by FY2031; together they run FY2029 EPS toward the 103p no-unannounced-deals floor the case itself computed, which is below consensus. The case's counter-case for a slower loop was a successor who prefers reinvestment; this reading needs no successor, because the guardrail does the slowing on its own, and it interacts with the case's R&D-credit view: if the credit steps down by the £3.2m the case expects, bank-test EBITDA falls and leverage rises with nothing spent. For it to be right: the 1.94x must not be a working-capital peak that unwinds by June (the case expects the FY2026 drug-procurement timing outflow to partly reverse); cash conversion must stay near 70% rather than recover; and management must hold the guardrail rather than use the latitude it has just restated. Deutsche Bank calls the leverage fully expected and Jefferies calls it a constraint on acquisitions without capital increases; both are true, and the case has priced the first and not the second.

The reading the case already holds, which these results do not disturb: that the near-term cut comes from the R&D credit, not from like-for-like. The update carries no profit statement, so nothing here tests the £6.6m release in the FY2026 comparative, and no broker named it. EBITDA +5.9% against revenue +6.2% fits the case's flat H1 margin and the company's working-day explanation alike. Of the three forces the case named for cuts, the like-for-like optics have failed, and the H1 margin and the credit wait for 4 March 2027. The case's below-consensus FY2027 is untested, not weakened.

We do not put up a third threat. A reading that the 3.0% is pre-elasticity price that volume will punish later is the case's own balancing loop, not an alternative to it, and the membership figure argues against its timing.

What it does to the case

The case's trigger of group like-for-like above 2.5% was tripped on the three-month run-rate and missed on the four-month headline. The rating's core, the credit-driven cut at the interims, is untested. Within grade, but the closest to moving.
  • Where the stock is in its lifecycle (55, D) and Setups' Fit/Crispness (68, C): pressure toward Turning Up and a crisper fit. Both items that blur the setup, the flat organic engine and the empty chair, moved toward resolution; neither is resolved, since 3.0% is a three-month group figure rather than the practice print the case names and the chair is still empty.
  • Current momentum (75, C): less contradiction between the measures and the narrative. Relative strength 67 against the case's 70 trigger; the 200-day rising in 5 of the last 12 months against the case's 9; technical pin D to C. Within grade.
  • Operating environment (60, D) and the Demand exposure (D, "soft and not turning"): the second-half FY2026 run-rate of about 1.5% that anchored "not turning" has been followed by 3.0%. Pressure toward less adverse; a full grade needs the price-volume split the company does not publish.
  • Riskiness and simplicity (72, C) and Case Riskiness (81, A): slightly more pressure. Leverage of 1.94x is still comfortable against the 3.25x covenant, but the headroom to the self-imposed guardrail has gone, and the two tails the case carries separately, the appointment's reception and the credit, are unchanged. Within grade.
  • Internal change forces (55, C) and Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (60, D): the funded mechanics are executing faster than modelled, which is the part the framework weighed positively, while the next round is now rate-limited. The two pressures run opposite ways; net the same.

  • Fair multiple (90, A) and Total shareholder return (77, B): the price has taken about 5 points of the modelled return (about +32% to the case's £18.14 from 1,369p against +37.8% from the memo's price, arithmetic on the case's own value) while raising the probability of the two events that unlock the multiple. The multiple is a little less cheap. Both within grade; the pressure on TSR runs both ways.
  • Conservatism of guidance (44, D): the same. Comfort with consensus restated and nothing raised, the tracker pattern the case describes.

What would settle it

  • The second Earnings Review, four working days on. It will carry whatever estimate and target changes the covering houses make after the update, so it settles the one thing this piece cannot: whether consensus holds at 95.6p and £150.7m, as Deutsche Bank expects, or begins the drift to 92p to 94p the case expects. It will not carry a price-volume split, a practice-level like-for-like, an Australian like-for-like, the H1 R&D credit, or a leverage figure later than 31 October, because the company has published none of those and nothing in four days changes that. It settles neither reading above.
  • The January half-year trading update. Half-year group like-for-like against a 2.7% comparative; members against the 500,000 alarm; the Advanced count against the 25,000 test; leverage at 31 December after the December Australian completion and the buyback's final weeks; deals done. That is the first test of reading two. It is probable it again omits practice like-for-like and the price-volume split: the company has never given them in a trading update and has not committed to, so reading one's central question is one nothing scheduled answers directly.
  • The interims on 4 March 2027. The H1 R&D credit in the notes (above £7m is a repeat, below £5.5m a problem), H1 EBITDA against the case's about £72.0m (below £70m is Stalling risk), the clinical staff ratio against 34.3% (above 34.5% says wages are winning; at or below 34% with practice like-for-like above 2% says leverage has turned), non-clinical cost of sales against 21.4%, and Australian consideration since July against the case's £15m signal. This is where the case's below-consensus leg is tested and where reading one is most likely settled by inference even without the split.
  • The appointment. Undated, now in its final stages, the one event that can settle reading one's second condition in a day in either direction and pull the case's window forward before any print.
