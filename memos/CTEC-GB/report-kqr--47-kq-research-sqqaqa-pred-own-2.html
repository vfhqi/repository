Section 3 – CMS Bidding Window, Registration, and Payment Methodology

Section summary
  1. CMS has structured the upcoming 2028 competitive bidding round under a unified administrative timeline to consolidate the post-acute medical supply market.
  2. The pricing methodology uses the 75th percentile of winning bids with subsequent inflation adjustments to mitigate severe reimbursement cuts for national distributors.

CMS Timeline and Registration

1.
Structured timeline: CMS has established a clear operational schedule leading up to the formal implementation of the competitive bidding program. The official timeline dictates that bidder registration opens on December 1, 2026, which marks the first administrative milestone for suppliers. This registration phase is followed by a designated bidding window opening on January 5, 2027, and closing on March 10, 2027. This structured rollout ensures that prospective distributors have sufficient time to prepare and submit their bids before regulatory changes take effect in 2028.
2.
Controlled implementation: The federal government plans to announce the winning bidders and publish final payment rates in the late summer or early autumn of 2027. These awarded contracts and new payment schedules are set to take effect on January 1, 2028, representing a permanent transition. Furthermore, CMS will allow a six-month transition grace period for contract suppliers to obtain clinical orders for transferring beneficiaries. This transition period is designed to prevent immediate patient disruptions as Medicare consolidated from hundreds of regional providers to selected winners.
3.
Initial scheduling: Coloplast confirmed that the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services will implement the competitive bidding program for ostomy and urology supplies no later than January 1, 2028. The manufacturer expects that the bidding registration and window will determine which suppliers remain active in the Medicare network. Consequently, this administrative process will impact the entire supply chain from production to patient delivery.
4.
Sequential milestones: Morgan Stanley reported that the upcoming regulatory timeline allows a multi-year adjustment period for both manufacturers and Medicare beneficiaries. Analysts highlighted that the bid registration opening in late summer or early fall of 2026 leads directly to contract awards in 2027. This pacing is critical because it gives major players time to consolidate and restructure operations before the 2028 deadline.

Payment Methodology and Pricing

5.
Controlled pricing: Morgan Stanley analyzed the revised payment methodology and determined that Single Payment Amounts will be set at the 75th percentile of winning bids. This calculation method is designed to reduce severe net price reductions compared to previous rounds that utilized lower bid thresholds. Additionally, the program will allow positive adjustments for inflation starting in the second year of the active contract. These regulatory adjustments are intended to lower overall beneficiary copays while preserving the long-term solvency of the Medicare Trust Funds.
6.
Aggressive bid protection: DNB Carnegie highlighted that the 75th-percentile pricing methodology acts as a regulatory backstop to limit the impact of outlier bids. This rule prevents exceptionally low bids from unilaterally driving down the final national reimbursement rate for all selected contractors. However, the protection remains limited if unusually aggressive bidding is widespread among the dominant national platforms. Consequently, the pricing outcome will depend heavily on the bidding discipline of the largest market participants.
7.
Regulatory reforms: Jefferies reported that the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services introduced several program changes to mitigate direct downward pricing pressure on distributors. The key revisions include implementing the 75th-percentile rule for pricing calculations and allowing consumer price index inflation adjustments starting in contract year two. Analysts expect these reforms to reduce the downward pricing pressure that historically devastated distributor margins in earlier rounds.
8.
Severe pricing cuts: Bernstein Research estimated that the competitive bidding program could force a 30% reimbursement cut at the distributor level. This pricing pressure contrasts with a projected 15% reduction for the manufacturing sector, which is typically more shielded. Consequently, these deep cuts represent a typical historical regulatory headwind for post-acute medical supply markets.

This model creates a single nationwide competitive area rather than localized geographical zones. Under this structure, contract suppliers are required to deliver items directly to the homes of beneficiaries throughout the country.
