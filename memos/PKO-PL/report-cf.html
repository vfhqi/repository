EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

PKO Bank Polski has undergone significant leadership and governance transitions over the last twelve months, with CEO Szymon Midera assuming the presidency in June 2024 and bringing over 20 years of commercial banking expertise, while the Polish State Treasury triggered a near-complete Supervisory Board turnover in early 2024, replacing eight of ten members and installing state-appointed leadership under Chair Katarzyna Zimnicka- Jankowska. The sudden dismissal of deputy CEO Marek Radzikowski in March 2026 temporarily fragmented operational oversight, forcing CFO Krzysztof Dresler and deputy CEO Michał Sobolewski to absorb interim responsibilities across international banking and operations areas. Despite these governance disruptions, internal promotions for the CFO and CRO roles preserved institutional knowledge and risk-management continuity, with Piotr Mazur maintaining his position as Chief Risk Officer since January 2013.

The bank adopted an ambitious 2025-2027 strategic plan titled "#1, full stop" in October 2024, concentrating on seven core business pillars to consolidate regional leadership, while activist investor presence remains absent from the shareholder registry. Structural optimization included the PKO Masterlease merger completed in February 2026, streamlining vehicle leasing and fleet financing services across individual and SME clients, and a corporate sales reorganization that transformed enterprise banking offices into six corporate macroregions dividing Poland into three strategic action areas since Q3 2025. Digitally, PKO partnered with Allegro in November 2025 to launch the "Allegro Klik" payment solution, targeting acquisition of one million new banking customers within three years, with CEO Midera publicly committing to attracting a "minimum of 2 million customers" as users of the service.

(Poland) External demand forces remain exceptionally robust, with customer financing growing 13.4% year-on-year to PLN 328.5 billion by June 2026 and retail household deposits expanding 12.4% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, increasing retail deposit market share. Competitively, PKO maintains a dominant 24% loan market share and approximately 22% of system deposits, significantly outpacing rivals such as Pekao (16% loan share) and anchoring a low-cost funding advantage.

However, regulatory headwinds intensified sharply, with a new banking bill raising Corporate Income Tax to 30% in 2026 and cumulative system-wide CHF provisions reaching PLN 100 billion, with another PLN 20 billion remaining to be booked. The National Bank of Poland cut rates to 3.75% in March 2026 before pausing indefinitely, creating anticipated cumulative net interest margin compression of 56 basis points over the 2025-2028 rate cycle.

Financially, customer financing expanded 13.4% year-on-year to PLN 328.5 billion by June 2026, but PKO's net interest margin contracted to 4.35% in 1H26, directly impacted by interest rate cuts. Despite margin pressures, reported ROE reached 18.4% in 1H26, comfortably exceeding strategic minimum targets of 18%, while the cost-to-income ratio achieved 31.6% in 1H26, retaining PKO's cost efficiency leadership within European banking.

Capital buffers fluctuated but recovered, with the CET1 ratio rebounding to 15.55% in Q226 after dipping to 14.99% in Q126, and cost of risk remained benign at 30 bps in 1H26, with NPLs stable at 3.43%. 14 15 Historically, the bank achieved a record net profit exceeding PLN 10 billion in 2025 versus historical baselines, and the second quarter of 2026 delivered a net profit beat of 2% against company consensus. However, legal risk provisions for Swiss franc mortgages reached PLN 4.4 billion in 2025 alone, with management confirming that 2025 represented the last year of record-breaking write-offs for CHF mortgages.

These provisions showed a material quarterly decrease to PLN 297 million in Q2 2026, easing the bottom-line drag, while best-in-class cost control delivered an outstanding cost-to-income ratio of 29.5% in fiscal 2024, maintaining operating efficiency among the best in European banking. 17 19 - Analyst/Investor The most recent earnings print in Q2 2026 saw reported net income reach PLN 2,769 million, exceeding company consensus by 2%, with Group PBT landing 4% above average consensus estimates due to broad operational outperformance.

Net interest income finished 2% above company consensus reflecting resilient commercial loan pricing, while mutual funds and brokerage fees surged 25.8% year-on-year to PLN 297 million. Operating expenses including regulatory costs came in 4% below consensus expectations, and actual credit provisions were 41% lower than consensus expected, indicating benign credit conditions. The Tier 1 capital ratio recovered to 15.55%, rising 56 basis points sequentially, with sequential capital accumulation aligned with Morgan Stanley estimates of 15.52%. 21 22 23 Relative to peers, PKO BP dominates the Polish banking system with a PLN 608 billion balance sheet versus Pekao's PLN 368 billion, and holds a 30.1% market share in new mortgage sales as of 2025. PKO BP's loan book grew 13.5% year-on-year in Q2 2026 versus Pekao's 8.5% growth, while Pekao's retail deposit base grew at only 4.6% year-on-year in first half 2026, lagging PKO BP's double-digit trajectory. PKO BP's CET1 ratio reached 15.55% in second quarter 2026, 405 basis points above regulatory minimums, and maintained a benign cost of risk of 30 bps in Q2 2026 with stable non-performing loans. Liquidity metrics remain superb, with PKO BP maintaining a conservative loan-to-deposit ratio of 70% alongside a 267% Liquidity Coverage Ratio.

Sell-side commentary identifies the forward EPS trajectory as highly concentrated around five distinct macroeconomic and operational levers, with normalizing legal risk charges providing a substantial earnings tailwind as CHF litigation burdens decline. Morgan Stanley models the terminal policy rate reaching 3.25% by the third quarter of 2027, driving a 56 basis point contraction in PKO net interest margin by 2028, but robust 9% annual loan growth over the projected 2026-2028 period is forecast, with PKO maintaining strong retail lending market leadership by capturing a 30.1% mortgage share recently.

Cumulative CHF legal provisions are projected to reach PLN 2.25 billion over the 2026-2028 period, with annual CHF provisions falling to PLN 370 million in 2027. The bank achieved an operating cost-to-income ratio of 31.1%, comfortably beating the 35% strategic target, and operating opex declined 4% below consensus expectations during the second quarter of 2026. Management maintains a conservative but highly credible communication style, consistently achieving and exceeding major performance targets, with ROE reaching 18.4% in 1H26 versus the 18% target, and executive leadership under CEO Szymon Midera demonstrates conservative planning assumptions, consistently delivering positive earnings beats versus consensus.

Financial sensitivities show that parallel interest rate declines of 200 basis points down are projected to reduce interest income by PLN 1,721 million, with management guiding to a 15 bps NIM decline per 100 bps rate cut. However, the bank ranked among the most resilient European institutions in the EBA stress tests conducted in 2025, with adverse stress scenarios projecting the CET1 ratio remaining at 16.40% in 2027, well above regulatory minima. - Earnings Calls Historical delivery consistently outpaces conservative strategic plans, with PKO consistently beating market expectations on net income and attaining its 2027 market share targets ahead of schedule, demonstrating strong retail lending momentum.

Operational cost control sustained cost-to-income ratios below 32%, retaining best-inclass European cost efficiency, while normalizing mortgage litigation provisions helped cushion bottom-line results sequentially by reducing quarterly legal costs. Across the last three fiscal years, retail loan originations sustained double-digit market share expansion, outperforming Polish banking sector peers, and cost of risk consistently undershot historical bank guidelines, resulting in extremely benign credit provisions.

The primary forward risks center on compounding regulatory and fiscal headwinds–specifically the 30% CIT hike, pending CHF litigation, and potential MRR tax–which could outweigh cyclical loan volume recoveries, and compounding interest rate cuts below 3% combined with rebounding credit defaults would completely erode the core net interest margins, creating a down-cycle scenario. Historical regulatory shocks including the PLN 3.1 billion credit holidays in 2022 demonstrate severe policy-driven volatility that remains an ongoing structural vulnerability.
