1.
Subdued UK demand: Fevertree Drinks PLC experienced significant pressure in its home market due to ongoing consumer headwinds. According to the company's 2025 preliminary results, UK revenue declined by 2% as the local on-trade channel contracted by 9% over the year. Ongoing inflation, elevated labor costs, and tax burdens continued to weigh on premium on-trade volumes, though a flat second-half performance provided some stabilization.
2.
Sharp peer declines: Fentimans Ltd struggled severely under similar UK macroeconomic pressures that impacted the premium adult soft drink segment. The company's filings at Companies House show that Fentimans reported a statutory loss of 1.73 million pounds in fiscal year 2025, down from a profit of 1.01 million pounds in 2024. This performance reflects how intensely rising operating costs and weak consumer demand have squeezed premium soft drink margins.

Strong premium growth: Britvic PLC demonstrated notable resilience in the premium mixer category despite the challenging UK retail climate. The company reported in May 2024 that London Essence grew revenue by 24% as it secured massive new distribution points in the grocery and hospitality sectors. Management noted in its fourth-quarter 2025 earnings call that on-trade channels remain under pressure as consumers limit discretionary spending. Despite these headwinds, Carlsberg managed to gain market share in the UK by focusing on its premium and craft portfolios.

RETAILER ACCOUNTS AND BDO AUDIT

1.
High audit scrutiny: Fevertree Drinks PLC faces significant evaluation from its auditors regarding complex trade- spend arrangements. The company's independent auditor BDO treated the completeness of customerarrangement accruals as a key audit matter in the fiscal year 2025 annual report. This focus was necessary because these promotional discounts and marketing contributions directly impact reported revenue and operating profits. Inaccurate calculations could lead to an overstatement of financial performance or a management override of controls.
2.
Decreasing accrual balances: Fevertree's balance sheet reflected a notable reduction in liabilities associated with retail promotional deals. The company reported that customer-arrangement accruals declined to 11.1 million pounds in 2025 from 17.4 million pounds in the prior year. This liability represented a significant portion of the group's total accruals of 19.2 million pounds. However, the public disclosures did not provide a specific geographic breakdown between the UK and European retail channels.
3.
Rigorous audit validation: BDO performed comprehensive procedures to verify that all promotional liabilities were fully recorded. The audit team sampled significant new contracts, assessed older accrual balances for potential release, and scrutinized post-year-end entries for completeness. Following these detailed investigations, the auditor reported no evidence of inappropriate revenue recording for the period. This validation provides reassurance that the reported 11.1 million pound balance represents an appropriate estimate of retail liabilities.
4.
Consequential accounting treatment: The classification of retail payments has a direct effect on Fevertree's top- line presentation. Payments for distinct marketing services are recorded as administrative expenses, whereas standard promotional discounts are recognized as revenue reductions. Because retailers control when they invoice for these promotional activities, management must estimate outstanding customer-arrangement claims at year-end.
