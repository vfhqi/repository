Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. Orsted has stabilized its capital structure under the leadership of CEO Rasmus Errboe through DKK 46 bn of divestments and the completion of its DKK 60 bn rights issue.
  2. Project-level farm-downs and partnerships will remain the central pillars for capital recycling and risk mitigation as Orsted executes its 8.1 GW construction portfolio.
  3. Reinstatement of the 2026 dividend is supported by a robust funds from operations to adjusted net debt ratio that remains comfortably above the 30% credit target.
  4. Regulatory milestones in the United States and Europe have significantly de-risked the offshore pipeline despite rising interest rates and persistent tariff pressures.
1.
Strategic leadership transition: Orsted stabilized its executive management after a period of intense operational disruption by appointing Rasmus Errboe as the new Group President and Chief Executive Officer in February 2025. The board also expanded the Group Executive Team to include specialized Chief Development and Chief Generation Officers. The management reorganization focused corporate resources on core offshore wind segments.
2.
Successful capital recycling: Orsted completed its cornerstone divestment program by selling its entire European onshore renewable platform to Copenhagen Infrastructure Partners in April 2026. The transaction was valued at EUR 1.44 bn and helped the company exceed its DKK 35 bn divestment target by securing DKK 46 bn in total proceeds across 2025 and 2026. The completed sale successfully strengthened the corporate financial foundation ahead of upcoming development cycles.

The partnership transaction will close simultaneously with the project reaching commercial operations, which was postponed from the third quarter to the fourth quarter of 2026. Leveraging project-level partnerships allows Orsted to share substantial construction risks with incoming partners while preserving capital.

Compounded commodity risks: Turbines, foundations, and inter-array cables are highly intensive users of raw materials and collectively represent approximately 70% of offshore wind project capital expenditure. Supply-chain pricing pressure will directly affect balance-of-plant costs unless developers secure long-term fixed-price contracts.

Turbine manufacturers are highly unlikely to reduce pricing until they fully restore their own profitability. Procurement shifts in supplier bargaining power limits developers' ability to lock-in capex prior to final investment decisions.
32.
Other side of the story: Orsted's supply chain pressures could ease significantly if global turbine manufacturing capacity expands faster than current industry projections. Slower turbine platform scaling and size stabilization around current models may also help suppliers achieve greater industrial scale. Sector operational maturity would drive down unit manufacturing costs and restore developers' pricing power during upcoming procurement rounds.

1.
Positive regulatory resolution: Orsted achieved a major milestone as the United States Treasury officially approved the 10% Energy Community investment tax credit bonus for the newly commissioned Revolution Wind project. Federal clearance immediately unlocks DKK 1.9 billion in tax credits to de-risk the balance sheet. Analysts at Morgan Stanley raised their earnings per share projections following this significant credit validation. Robust operational cash flows should now support the planned reinstatement of the 2026 dividend.

Tactical spending deferral: Orsted lowered its full-year 2026 capital expenditure guidance by DKK 5 billion to a range of DKK 45 to 50 billion. Management explained that deferred payment schedules for key offshore projects drove the near-term reduction. Delayed capital outflows will shift cash requirements into 2027 rather than delivering structural cost savings. Total expected investment budgets for the active construction pipeline remain completely unchanged.
3.
Substantial balance sheet recovery: Orsted successfully restored its capital structure following the completion of its major capital allocation programs. Group funds from operations to adjusted net debt reached 37.3% to comfortably exceed the investment-grade rating requirement of 30%. Large cash reserves of DKK 107.6 billion insulate the company from immediate refinancing pressures. Divestment execution risks have diminished following completed asset sales across the European onshore platform.
4.
Critical growth vacuum: Orsted faces a severe earnings cliff next decade as lucrative legacy wind subsidies begin to roll off. The developer is projected to lose approximately DKK 11 billion of subsidies between 2030 and 2040. Annual capital expenditure must average DKK 35 billion next decade to offset this revenue decline. Secular growth will flatten unless the company wins major awards in upcoming bidding rounds.

Sizable upcoming tenders: Orsted must compete in more than 15 GW of offshore wind capacity tenders scheduled through 2027. Securing new projects is vital to backfill the pipeline as current construction programs conclude. Group President Rasmus Errboe remains confident in replenishing the development pipeline at profitable returns.

Rationalized growth narrative: Financial markets remain divided between bullish execution views and bearish pipeline growth concerns. Operational evidence strongly favors the disciplined capital allocation strategy over aggressive volume expansion. Bypassing highly competitive Danish auctions protects the group from value destruction despite the near-term growth vacuum. Successful execution of remaining construction projects represents the primary driver to rebuild investor trust.

Rising capital costs and supply-chain bottlenecks threaten to inflate the remaining Sunrise Wind capital budget. Low return spreads on future projects will fail to offset subsidy roll-offs as lucrative historical contracts expire. Project internal rates of return near 4% remain uncompetitive against elevated utility sector yields.

Securing new offshore wind tenders at healthy return spreads would immediately de-risk the long-term outlook. Declining interest rates and supportive European policy frameworks would accelerate the corporate valuation re-rating. Refinancing the balance sheet to a net cash position by 2029 provides a strong financial foundation.

Rising long-term interest rates would force further non-cash impairments across the United States asset portfolio. Bidding aggressively in upcoming tenders at low return spreads would destroy shareholder value.

Sizable debt maturities and hybrid resets totaling DKK 48 billion through 2030 expose the firm to costly refinancing. Refinancing these liabilities threatens to escalate the interest burden unless capital expenditure declines as planned.

The next major disclosure must reveal whether the developer can successfully secure high-margin capacity in upcoming European tenders. Achieving annual investment of DKK 35 billion is necessary to halt the earnings decline from expiring subsidies.

Maintaining the funds from operations to adjusted net debt ratio remains vital to secure rating headroom.

Other side of the story: Orsted's successful turnaround could stall completely if intense competitive pressure in future auctions compressed project returns below capital costs. Underwhelming capacity additions will fail to generate attractive upside as current construction programs conclude.
