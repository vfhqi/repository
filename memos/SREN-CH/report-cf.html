EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Swiss Re's investment narrative centers on a strategic refocusing toward core reinsurance operations following the comprehensive exit from underperforming digital platforms and the dissolution of redundant divisional structures under the NEXT program. The Group delivered exceptional first-half 2026 net income of USD 2.8 billion, representing a 9% year-on-year increase that positions the firm well on track toward its full-year target of USD 4.5 billion.

However, this outperformance was heavily driven by historically benign natural catastrophe losses of USD 169 million, representing only 20% of the USD 836 million budget, while normalizing for these favorable conditions reveals a weaker underlying combined ratio of approximately 86% compared to management's guided target of below 85%. The Group faces accelerating reinsurance pricing pressures, with mid-year June and July renewals recording a net price decline of 5.3% after adjusting for elevated loss cost assumptions, which compressed Property and Casualty new business contractual service margin by 28.3% to USD 1.6 billion.

Despite these cyclical headwinds, Swiss Re maintains robust capital adequacy with an estimated SST solvency ratio of 264% as of July 2026, comfortably exceeding the 200% to 250% board target range, and has executed 60% of its USD 1.5 billion share buyback program by mid-year. The sell-side consensus exhibits increasingly bearish sentiment, with Bloomberg's aggregate rating distribution shifting to 2 Buys, 9 Holds, and 8 Sells as of August 2026, while Morgan Stanley maintains an Underweight rating citing weaker underlying combined ratios and unjustified valuation premiums relative to peers Munich Re and Hannover Re.

The core investment thesis rests on Swiss Re's ability to defend underwriting margins through disciplined portfolio pruning and operational self-help initiatives while navigating a softening reinsurance pricing cycle. The Group has upgraded its run-rate cost savings target to USD 500 million by 2028 from the original USD 300 million, focusing on non-client support functions to insulate primary market-facing operations.

Leadership transitions brought Andreas Berger as Group CEO in July 2024, bringing strong technical underwriting credentials from his prior role leading Corporate Solutions, while Velina Peneva's appointment as Life and Health Reinsurance CEO effective October 2026 positions specialized asset management expertise to drive the division toward its USD 1.7 billion net income target. The strategic withdrawal from iptiQ resulted in realized transaction losses totaling USD 210 million across the American carrier sale to Wilton Re and European P&C divestiture to Allianz Direct, but eliminated structural drag from underperforming digital platforms.

The Group's Property and Casualty Reinsurance division achieved an outstanding combined ratio of 76.7% in the first half of 2026, though this performance benefited from exceptionally low catastrophe activity that is unlikely to persist through the Atlantic hurricane season. Meanwhile, Life and Health Reinsurance delivered net income of USD 1.0 billion, up 21% year-on-year, supported by favorable experience variances of USD 107 million and improving US mortality trends.

The materiality of strategic change is substantial, reflecting a fundamental repositioning away from diversified digital insurance platforms toward concentrated core reinsurance capabilities. The NEXT program successfully dissolved the Global Clients and Solutions business unit, reallocating underwriting responsibilities directly to core segments and removing redundant institutional layers. Swiss Re's US casualty market share was deliberately reduced from 17% to approximately 5% to mitigate exposure to persistent North American social inflation running at 6% to 7%, though the Group remains cautiously skeptical that these adverse trends have stabilized.

The acquisition of QBE's Global Trade Credit and Surety business for an expected USD 200 million in annual revenue diversifies specialty lines with uncorrelated risk profiles, while strategic alliances with GNP Seguros in Mexico and Bajaj General Insurance in India position the Group to capture emerging market protection gap opportunities. However, the strategic focus on core reinsurance amplifies exposure to cyclical volatility, as demonstrated by the 8% decline in Property and Casualty Reinsurance revenue to USD 8.2 billion driven by renewal pruning and lower cedent volumes.

The Group's investment portfolio benefits from elevated interest rates, with recurring yields expanding to 4.2% and Q2 2026 reinvestment yields reaching 5.2%, providing a robust earnings buffer against softening premium rates. The top ranked key drivers of forward EPS trajectory, ordered by estimated impact magnitude, comprise: (1) Reinsurance pricing cycle deceleration, with net rate declines of 5.3% translating into an estimated 3.5 percentage point increase in the combined ratio, potentially reducing group earnings by 3% for each 1 percentage point deterioration based on Morgan Stanley sensitivity analysis; (2) Operational self-help cost savings targeting a USD 500 million run-rate by 2028, focused on support functions and corporate bureaucracy simplification without impacting client-facing teams; (3) US casualty reserve adequacy, with the Group having proactively strengthened reserves by approximately USD 500 million in long-tail IBNR during Q2 2026 after historic social inflation challenges depressed 2024 results; (4) Natural catastrophe loss experience relative to budget, where the extraordinarily benign H1 2026 claims of USD 169 million represented an 80% underspend that is unlikely to repeat given seasonal hurricane exposures and structural climate change trends driving expected claims to USD 2.9 billion annually; (5) Life and Health Reinsurance underwriting margin resilience, with the division targeting USD 1.7 billion net income supported by favorable US mortality experience and completed portfolio reviews eliminating underperforming segments; and (6) Capital return execution, with Swiss

Re having completed 60% of its USD 1.5 billion share buyback by July 2026, underpinned by robust SST solvency of 264% and AM Best adjusted financial leverage of only 12.1% at year-end 2025. The recent financial delivery highlights demonstrate mixed underlying momentum beneath headline beats. The Group return on equity reached 22.7% for the first half of 2026, while Property and Casualty Reinsurance net income grew 18% to USD 1.4 billion and Corporate Solutions net income increased 14% to USD 490 million with a combined ratio of 86.1%, comfortably below its 91% long-term target.

However, Group insurance revenue contracted 3% to USD 20.3 billion, with the Property and Casualty division experiencing an 8% top-line decline that reflects active treaty pruning to defend margins amid soft pricing. The 28.3% contraction in Property and Casualty new business contractual service margin to USD 1.6 billion signals materially weaker forward earnings power as softer renewal pricing earns through the portfolio.

Life and Health Reinsurance beat consensus expectations by 29% according to Jefferies analyst Philip Kett, though this outperformance was partially offset by investment result underperformance of 20% due to declining private equity valuations in the second quarter. The normalized underlying combined ratio of approximately 86% identified by Morgan Stanley analysts suggests that the reported 76.7% figure masks structural margin compression once catastrophe luck and reserve releases are adjusted.

The forward guidance credibility exhibits moderate reliability based on historical tracking patterns. Swiss Re achieved its 2025 net income target of USD 4.8 billion, beating the guided threshold of USD 4.4 billion, and delivered Property and Casualty combined ratios of 79.4% against a target of below 85%. However, the Group missed its 2024 net income target by USD 400 million due to decisive US casualty reserve strengthening actions, while Life and Health Reinsurance fell short of its 2025 target by USD 300 million following comprehensive portfolio reviews.

The 2026 full-year guidance appears achievable given first-half delivery represents approximately 63% of the annual target, though this assumes normalized catastrophe losses in the second half and continued containment of casualty reserve development. Management's upgraded cost savings target to USD 500 million demonstrates credible commitment to operational self-help, with over USD 100 million already delivered in the first half tracking ahead of schedule.

Sensitivity analysis indicates that each 1 percentage point combined ratio deterioration reduces Group EPS by approximately 3%, while a 50 basis point expansion in recurring asset yields would add approximately USD 1.5 billion to investment income. The most significant execution risk centers on mid-year renewal pricing momentum, where the 5.3% net rate decline represents accelerating headwinds that could overwhelm cost efficiency gains if catastrophe losses normalize.

The sell-side positioning reflects growing skepticism about valuation sustainability amid deteriorating pricing fundamentals. Morgan Stanley's Hadley Cohen maintains an Underweight rating with a CHF 115 price target, arguing that Swiss Re trades at an unjustified premium to peers Munich Re and Hannover Re despite facing greater cyclical exposure and weaker earnings diversification. Morgan Stanley's FY27 and FY28 estimates sit 5% below consensus, incorporating conservative assumptions on soft renewals and potential casualty reserve pressures.

Jefferies' Philip Kett holds a Hold rating with a CHF 130 price target, acknowledging strong Life and Health results but cautioning that pricing continues to accelerate its downward decline, which will weigh on future contract profitability. The Bloomberg consensus aggregate shows 2 Buy, 9 Hold, and 8 Sell ratings as of August 2026, representing a meaningful deterioration from 12 Holds and 5 Sells at the start of the year.

The average consensus price target declined from CHF 138.9 to CHF 129.6, implying a negative total return of 0.7% for shareholders. Swiss Re currently trades at 10.7x one-year forward earnings, directly aligned with peer

Hannover Re's multiple but below Munich Re's premium valuation despite the latter's superior solvency of 304% and more diversified earnings profile generating 53% from non-cyclical sources versus Swiss Re's 44%. The aggregate risk-reward dynamics present a challenging asymmetry for investors. On the reward side, Swiss Re offers robust capital return capacity through its USD 1.5 billion buyback program and sustainable dividends supported by strong solvency buffers, while operational self-help initiatives targeting USD 500 million in run-rate savings provide downside earnings protection independent of market cycles.

The Life and Health Reinsurance division delivers stable earnings diversification with improving mortality trends, and elevated investment yields of 4.2% create a structural tailwind as the portfolio continues repricing at higher rates. The strategic repositioning toward core reinsurance eliminates drag from unprofitable digital ventures and positions the Group to benefit when pricing cycles eventually inflect positively.

On the risk side, Swiss Re faces material exposure to accelerating pricing pressure, with the 28.3% contraction in new business contractual service margin signaling compressed future profitability that will flow through results over the next 12 to 24 months. The exceptional H1 2026 underwriting results relied on benign catastrophe activity that represents an 80% budget underspend unlikely to persist, particularly given peak Atlantic hurricane season exposure and structural climate change trends driving higher expected losses.

US casualty reserve adequacy remains a persistent vulnerability despite proactive strengthening actions, with North American social inflation running at 6% to 7% and management expressing skepticism about stabilization. The peer-relative positioning shows Swiss Re lagging Munich Re on both solvency metrics and earnings diversification, while trading at similar valuation multiples that fail to reflect this quality gap.

The sell-side consensus has turned materially more bearish, with eight Sell ratings and a negative implied total return suggesting limited upside potential at current levels absent a favorable shift in reinsurance market fundamentals.

The Group's new business contractual service margin collapsed to USD 2.1 billion from USD 3.1 billion, with P&C Re specifically experiencing a severe 28.3% contraction to USD 1.567 billion, signaling materially weaker forward earnings power embedded in newly written treaties. Mid-year renewals exhibited nominal price declines of

1.2%, which when adjusted for elevated loss cost assumptions of 4.2%, translated into net price erosion of 5.3% – a pricing velocity substantially worse than peer resilience and indicative of intensifying competitive capitulation pressures. Swiss Re's SST solvency ratio of 264% trails Munich Re's superior 304% solvency, highlighting a relative capital adequacy gap of 40 percentage points despite both firms exceeding regulatory minimums.

Sell-side consensus has deteriorated sharply, with the rating mix shifting from 12 Holds and 5 Sells in January 2026 to 9 Holds and 8 Sells by August, alongside declining average price targets from CHF 138.9 to CHF 129.6, reflecting eroding institutional confidence in the Group's ability to defend margins through the accelerating pricing downcycle.

Morgan Stanley's normalization analysis reveals that stripping out benign catastrophe tailwinds exposes a weaker underlying combined ratio of approximately 86% versus management's guided targets, suggesting current reported profitability is heavily dependent on unsustainable claims fortune rather than structural underwriting excellence. Historic US casualty reserve strengthening actions during late 2024 to combat North American social inflation of 6% to 7% caused P&C Re to miss its combined ratio target of 87% in 2024 due to 10.2 percentage points of reserve additions, establishing a troubling pattern of reserve adequacy failures that required comprehensive remediation.

Life and Health Reinsurance missed its FY25 net income target of USD 1.6 billion by USD 300 million, demonstrating execution shortfalls in the division positioned as a stable earnings diversifier. Strategic exits from iptiQ resulted in cumulative recognized losses totaling USD 210 million across the Wilton Re transaction (USD 125 million) and Allianz Direct divestiture (USD 85 million), crystallizing meaningful capital destruction from the failed digital platform initiative and raising questions about prior strategic capital allocation discipline.

Outliers worth noting

Swiss Re experienced extraordinarily benign natural catastrophe losses of only USD 169 million in H1 2026, representing an 80% shortfall against the budgeted USD 836 million, with the primary event being Storm Kristin's landfall in Portugal during January 2026. This extreme deviation from normalized expectations masks underlying underwriting performance and raises questions about whether current combined ratio achievements reflect structural margin improvements or temporary environmental tailwinds.

The 28.3% contraction in Property and Casualty Reinsurance new business contractual service margin to USD 1.567 billion coincided with mid-year treaty renewals that saw net pricing decline by 5.3% after adjusting for elevated loss cost assumptions of 4.2%. This pricing divergence appears statistically severe relative to historical renewal cycles and suggests an accelerating shift in reinsurance market dynamics that contradicts the benign catastrophe environment.

Group insurance revenue contracted by 3% to USD 20,264 million while P&C Reinsurance revenue specifically declined 8% to USD 8.2 billion, both substantially underperforming peer volumes during the same period when Munich Re sustained higher absolute volumes despite similarly challenging market conditions. Life and Health Reinsurance delivered USD 107 million in favourable experience variances and net income that beat consensus by 29%, representing an unusual positive deviation that Jefferies analysts characterized as showing stable and reassuring assumption changes. This substantial outperformance stands in contrast to the segment's historical volatility and prior-year underperformance, raising questions about the sustainability of mortality experience trends.

Swiss Re's estimated SST solvency ratio of 264% as of July 1, 2026 exceeds the Group's own board target range of 200% to 250% yet trails Munich Re's superior 304% solvency reported in the same period. This competitive solvency gap appears inconsistent with Swiss Re's comparable business mix and suggests divergent capital management philosophies or hidden balance sheet quality differences between top-tier reinsurers.
