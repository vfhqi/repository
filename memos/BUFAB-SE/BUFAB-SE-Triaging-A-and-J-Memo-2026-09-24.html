[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Bufab is a self-help margin case that has completed and is now priced as a compounder. At SEK 145.40 the shares already pay for the margin, the organic recovery and the announced deals, so what is left on our modal path is about 4 percent a year against the 20 percent bar. The business is compelling; the investment at this price is not, and the same case at SEK 95 to 110 a share would be.

  • The change is internal, dated and real.
    • A new chief executive in August 2022 repriced the product on the value it delivers rather than on its cost, raised the adjusted EBITA margin target from 12 to 14 percent in December 2023, sold the capital-intensive manufacturing units in 2024 and 2025 and redirected acquisitions to platforms with margins above 14 percent.
    • The screen's decision rule is met twice over: a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase, and at least three further strong forces (strategy, pricing standards, perimeter reduction, acquisitions).
    • None of the false-positive red flags fits: twelve quarters of gross-margin data say this is not a slide-deck transformation
      • the units sold were about 5 percent of sales
      • leverage is 2.4x adjusted net debt to EBITDA, inside the 2.0x to 3.0x target.
  • The external forces are mixed and do not carry the case.
    • Consolidation toward scaled distributors and regulatory complexity (CBAM, CSRD) favour Bufab, but end-market demand is polarised rather than inflecting: defence, semiconductor equipment and mechatronics are strong, construction, kitchens and automotive are weak, European PMIs sit below 50, and the SEK and freight are headwinds.
    • Q2 2026 organic growth of 5.3 percent was, in the chief executive's words, primarily market-share gains and the ramp of previously secured large contracts, not a broad recovery.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's people (chief executive, a returned CFO), its choices (targets, strategy), its actions (divestments, pricing tools, platform deals), its key operating metrics (gross margin 33.2 percent in Q2 2026 from 29.3 percent at the end of 2023; organic growth 5.3 percent), its financial statements (adjusted EBITA margin 15.0 percent in H1 2026 from 11.9 percent in FY2024; cash conversion 90 percent; leverage 2.4x from 3.0x) and the sell-side's estimates (FY2027 consensus EPS up 13.8 percent in twelve months).
    • Nothing of the change remains to be discovered.
      • That is the fact the whole record turns on.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on every line the framework names, and the improvement ahead is smaller than the improvement behind.
    • Revenue growth has gone from minus 7.4 percent in FY2024 to plus 11.1 percent in Q2 2026
    • the margin sits at the top of the 14 to 16 percent range the research gives for pure-play C-parts distributors
    • our modal EPS growth falls from about 24 percent in FY2026 to about 16 percent in FY2027 and about 12 percent in FY2028.
    • Three consecutive quarterly beats (6, 9 and 3 percent above consensus adjusted EBITA) and revisions all one way make further upgrades likely over six to twelve months, but the one-month revision has slowed to about plus 1 percent and the next step needs the Q3 report on 2026-10-23 to beat again.
  • What is left is a good business compounding earnings at about 10 to 12 percent a year from 2027.
    • Organic growth of 4 to 5 percent, acquisitions adding 3 to 5 percent a year on the deal record (five deals since 2020 against a compounder peer median of 58; deal cash SEK 54 million in H1 2026 against SEK 714 million in FY2025), margin flat at about 16 percent, and a flat share count.
    • On the reported basis our modal path takes EPS from SEK 3.30 in FY2025 to about SEK 5.30 in FY2028, 17.1 percent a year, but the rate is carried by the first year and settles at 10 to 12 percent.
  • What the price assumes: the margin, the organic recovery, the announced deals and the compounder rating.
    • SEK 145.40 is 25.9x the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of SEK 5.61, the 98th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range and the 95th percentile of five years, against a five-year median of 19.13x
    • the shares are up 53.0 percent over twelve months and the consensus target (mean SEK 142.00) sits below the price.
    • Bufab trades 3 to 10 percent below Addtech (28.7x), Lagercrantz (27.9x) and Lifco (26.8x), and those peers are at their 53rd to 62nd own-history percentiles, so the discount to the compounders is the smallest it has ever been while the compounders themselves are mid-range.
      • The sell-side that recommends the stock defends a 10 percent discount as deserved.
    • The one thing the price does not fully assume is an acquisition pace of 8 percent a year
      • consensus FY2028 revenue growth of 5.8 percent carries little beyond what is announced.
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the multiple is the swing factor.
    • The three legs on our modal path sum to about 4 percent a year over three years and about 0 percent over eighteen months: a cash return of about 1 percent a year, earnings growth on the 24-month-forward figure of about 8 percent a year, and a re-rating of about minus 4.6 percent a year from 25.9x to a blended 22.5x.
    • With the multiple held at 25.9x the return is about 8.9 percent a year, still below the framework's D anchor of about 12 percent.
      • At the five-year median of 19.1x it is minus 3.9 percent over three years.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through from EBITA to EPS (net financial items SEK 200 million, tax 25 percent, amortisation SEK 140 million, a 10 percent stronger USD) takes FY2027 reported EPS from about SEK 4.75 to about SEK 4.00 and the three-year return to minus 1.6 percent a year.
      • The rating does not change
      • the earnings path has room to disappoint by a sixth on lines management does not control, at a multiple that has no room at all.
  • The pattern fits crisply, at the wrong point.
    • 'Major Corporate Change' fits at B: every mandatory condition is met with dated evidence, and the outputs followed in the order the chain predicts.
    • The pattern pays at input-plus-early-output
    • this case is at full output.
    • 'Compounder' is the secondary fit at C: a two-year beat record and a mis-modelled line (outer-year acquired growth) we can identify but do not ourselves model above consensus.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the inputs through the outputs to the estimates: nothing in the 'Foundations' argues against the earnings path.
    • It breaks at the price: the market has confirmed the earnings thesis so completely that the return thesis fails.

  • The case is therefore a business we would want to own at a price we cannot pay.

    • We are not betting
    • we are waiting.
    • The bet is only available below about SEK 110 with the earnings path intact.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material to the earnings and is fully propagated, so it is not material to the return at this price: the forces and the outputs would each earn a B, the return earns an F, and the Building Block's question is whether the change is material enough to drive a 20 percent-plus return, which at SEK 145.40 it is not.

    • Not C, because the modal return of about 4 percent a year is below the framework's D anchor of about 12 percent on every path we can build from the evidence, and no path reaches 10 percent a year without the multiple holding at a level the stock has occupied for only 5 percent of the last five years.
    • Not F, because the inputs and the outputs are genuine and the same case at SEK 95 to 110 a share would be a B.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest, is the bull's: Bufab has become a Swedish compounder, deserves the set's multiple of about 28x rather than a discount, and will compound EPS at the 15 percent a year it targets.
    • It compounded EPS at 17 percent a year for the decade to 2025
    • acquired growth of 8 percent a year (the sell-side's model), organic growth of 4 to 5 percent, margins to 16 percent and operating leverage carry it forward.
    • On that path FY2028 EPS is DNB Carnegie's SEK 6.22, the 24-month-forward figure at September 2029 is about SEK 9.15, and at 28x the shares are worth about SEK 256 plus SEK 5.15 of dividends, plus 80 percent, about 21.6 percent a year.
      • Even with the multiple held at 25.9x it is about SEK 237 plus SEK 5.15, about 18.5 percent a year.
    • The evidence for it: the 17 percent record
      • three consecutive beats
      • a fragmented market with targets at 6 to 7.5x EV/EBITA
      • the CBAM barrier as a hidden organic driver
      • the sell-side's own conclusion of a 25 percent-plus internal rate of return on a three-year view.
  • What we concede.
    • The record is real.
    • The market is fragmented enough.
    • The sell-side's outer-year revenue growth of 5.8 percent (FY2028) and 5.7 percent (FY2029, one broker) does look low for a serial acquirer.
    • If the deal pace doubled we would move our earnings path from about 10 percent to about 13 percent a year and our odds on the compounder rating holding from about 30 to about 50 percent.
  • Why we hold our view.
    • The decade's 17 percent was built partly on the margin lever (adjusted EBIT margin 8.7 percent in FY2019 to 11.7 percent in FY2025 on the FactSet basis; adjusted EBITA 11.9 to 15.0 percent since 2024), which is now at the category's ceiling of 14 to 16 percent.
    • The deal pace since 2020 is a fifth of the peers' and slowed further in H1 2026.
    • And the bull's own arithmetic needs both 15 percent compounding for five years and a re-rating to the top of the peer set to clear the bar, whereas our modal path needs nothing to go wrong to return 4 percent.
  • A second competing hypothesis is the risks research's FALSE FRIEND: acquired growth masking organic decay, motivated by an EBITA-weighted incentive plan, with working capital at 38 percent of sales and cash conversion at 58 percent in Q1 2026.
    • We concede the working-capital and incentive facts.
    • We hold that the research's own disconfirming test, organic growth near the historical 5 percent regardless of acquisitions, was met at 5.3 percent in Q2 2026, and that cash conversion recovered to 90 percent in that quarter.
    • It is a risk to monitor, not the modal case.
  • Where the case against would win.
    • Two deals in H2 2026 adding SEK 500 million or more of annual sales, with organic growth above 4 percent and the gross margin above 32.5 percent through the Q3 and Q4 reports, would make the bull's path the modal one
    • the price we would then pay is still the question, because the shares at 25.9x already carry most of it.
