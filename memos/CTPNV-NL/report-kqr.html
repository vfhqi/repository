Section summary
  1. CTP and VGP maintain highly profitable development spreads with yields on cost reaching 10.0% and
  2. CEE logistics rental growth is showing signs of stabilizing in late 2026 before resuming an upward trajectory in 2027.

Market Rental Growth Stabilization

1.
Moderating rental expansion: CTP reported strong like-for-like rental growth of 4.8% for the first nine months of 2026. However, a Bernstein broker report from October 29, 2026, noted that average rents agreed on new leases were only 1% higher year-on-year when adjusted for country mix. This data indicates that the massive rental growth seen in previous years is now stabilizing across core CEE hubs like Prague and Bucharest.
2.
Shifting tenant terms: VGP experienced a notable deceleration in its reletting rental growth during the first half of 2026. A Deutsche Bank Research report dated August 20, 2026, highlighted that rental uplifts on VGP's re-lets moderated to 6% in the first half of 2026, down from 18.5% in the first four months of the year. This sharp drop supports the view that CEE logistics rents are entering a phase of late-cycle stabilization before growth resumes.
3.
Contrary evidence: CTP experienced negative rental pressure in its Romanian portfolio during the second quarter of 2026. A Morgan Stanley broker note from July 30, 2026, revealed that the group took a valuation write-down in Romania driven by a 5% reset in estimated rental values due to intense local market competition. This decline shows that CEE rental growth is not uniform and can face downward pressure where developer competition is high.

Development Yields on

4.

Other side of the story: While localized rental pressure in Romania and slowing reletting spreads suggest a broader rental plateau, underlying tenant demand remains highly supportive. Section 4 – Structural Demand: Nearshoring and Asian E-Commerce

Section summary
  1. Structural nearshoring and e-commerce regulatory shifts are driving unprecedented industrial space requirements in Central and Eastern Europe.
  2. The cancellation of the EU duty exemption forces Asian etailers to transition rapidly toward local European fulfillment centers.

Nearshoring and

1.
Industrial Relocation Accelerated reshoring: CTP is benefiting significantly from the strategic relocation of Asian automotive and electronics manufacturers to the Central and Eastern European region. According to CTP's third-quarter 2026 presentation, Asian tenants now account for 12% of total portfolio GLA, mostly concentrated in manufacturing and automotive supply chains. This growing cohort represents approximately 16% of all leases signed by the group over the last 24 months, indicating a major shift in the occupier base. These tenant relocations are highly supportive of long-term standing portfolio occupancy and development demand.
2.
Massive investment: Chinese electric vehicle and battery manufacturers are rapidly building out production capacity in Europe to bypass escalating trade barriers. A regulatory news report from October 18, 2026, confirms that Chinese battery and EV firms are developing 26 European manufacturing plants, including CATL's €7.3 billion gigafactory in Debrecen, Hungary. This local manufacturing push is highly favorable for regional industrial park operators, as European battery self-sufficiency efforts have collapsed. These localized supply chains will generate substantial secondary logistics demand from Tier 1 suppliers.
3.
Technical specifications: VGP is positioning its development pipeline toward advanced manufacturing and specialized automated industrial spaces to capture this relocation wave. A Bernstein broker report from September 3, 2026, highlights that VGP's landbank and property tours showcase deep brownfield development expertise required by technology-intensive tenants. This specialized focus allows VGP to secure highspecification pre-let projects that support superior development margins over commodity logistics. Consequently, the group expects robust occupier demand to continue across its core German and CEE platforms.
4.
Contrary evidence: Persistent demographic declines across Central Europe threaten to derail the long-term nearshoring narrative by creating severe labor constraints. A BNP Paribas macro report from March 18, 2026, notes that rapid demographic contraction could trigger severe wage inflation by 2030, raising concerns about structural manufacturing competitiveness. These adverse demographic trends are expected to deteriorate further across Poland and the Czech Republic, potentially limiting the regional expansion of energy-intensive assembly lines.

European Customs Reform Impacts

5.
Regulatory pressure: The European Union's aggressive elimination of low-value tax exemptions has disrupted the direct-to-consumer shipping models of Asian e-commerce giants. A Morgan Stanley broker report from October 7, 2026, explains that the removal of the €150 exemption on July 1, 2026, added a flat €3 customs duty per item category. This regulatory shift is compounded by a permanent €2 handling fee effective November 1, 2026, raising the total fixed-charge burden on cheap imports dramatically. To mitigate these high costs, platforms must transition to bulk import models.
6.
Massive warehousing: Chinese e-commerce platforms are scrambling to secure large-scale logistics space in Central Europe to transition toward local fulfillment models. According to CTP's Q3 2026 financial highlights, Temu has responded to the new duties by leasing over 90,000 square meters across three locations in Poland. This rapid expansion represents a physical transition from direct shipping to local inventory holding, boosting Polish warehousing demand. Such large-scale commitments from major e-tailers are directly supporting CTP's development pipeline absorption.
7.
Surging take-up: Asian e-commerce logistics operators are rapidly expanding their physical footprints in Germany to maintain efficient localized distribution. A German city survey report from October 22, 2026, highlights that Asian logistics provider Goodcang leased nearly 87,000 square meters of space across three German locations. This transaction helped propel the share of Asian companies in total German logistics take-up to 15% in the third quarter. These moves underscore Germany's critical role as a centralized hub for e-commerce operators navigating new trade policies.
8.
Other side of the story: While the customs reform has driven an immediate surge in warehouse leasing, the severe drop in e-commerce volumes could eventually hurt logistics demand. A Deutsche Bank report from October 29, 2026, reveals that Temu's European sales plummeted 39% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026 following the implementation of the €3 duty. Analyzing 12 relevant sources, 2 core companies, and 1 comparable past case shows that if this steep volume contraction persists, Asian platforms may drastically scale back their European expansion plans. Thus, the initial warehousing rush could give way to space consolidation and higher regional vacancy.

Section 5 – Valuation Yields and the Impact of Romanian Markdowns

Section summary
  1. Localized valuation markdowns in Romania have had a minor impact on broader CEE logistics yields.
  2. Independent appraisers maintain conservative exit yields compared to aggressive direct transactions in the market.

Romanian Portfolio Write-downs

1.
Marked depreciation: CTP recorded significant valuation write-downs in its Romanian standing portfolio during the first half of 2026. According to a CTP press release dated July 30, 2026, the developer's standing assets revaluation declined by €146.80 million, primarily driven by a valuation decrease in Romania. This negative performance dragged CTP's total net valuation result on investment property to negative €83 million during the same period. Consequently, this write-down reduced CTP's EPRA net tangible assets per share by 0.80% to €20.23, impacting overall investor sentiment.
2.
Competitive pressures: CTP suffered from falling estimated rental values in Romania as developer competition intensified. A Jefferies broker report from August 4, 2026, noted that CTP acknowledged a 5% reduction in Romanian estimated rental values due to increased local market competition and softer leasing conditions. This competitive environment aligns with the broker's cautious stance on Romania, where they estimate a negative 18% reversionary potential. These regional pressures demonstrate that rental growth is not uniform across Central and Eastern Europe.
3.
Isolated drag: Bernstein Research argues that the negative valuation movements in CTP's Romanian portfolio are highly market-specific. A Bernstein report from August 7, 2026, stated that Romania represents 15% of CTP's gross asset value, with estimated rental values declining 4.50% over the period. The analyst views this as an isolated issue rather than evidence of broader weakness across CTP's wider European platform. Despite these write-downs, occupier leasing demand firmed in the region as management successfully defended headline rental rates.
4.
Contrary evidence: CTP experienced a stabilization in its standing asset revaluation drag during the third quarter of 2026. A CTP press release from October 29, 2026, showed that the standing assets revaluation drag widened only slightly to €150.30 million for the nine-month period, compared to €146.80 million in the first half. This minimal incremental write-down of €3.50 million indicates that the Romanian markdown has largely run its course. Thus, the negative valuation pressure appears to be stabilizing, allowing the developer to focus on future delivery completions.

Valuer Exit Yields by

5.
Country Conservative appraisals: CTP maintained relatively conservative portfolio yields across its broader Central and Eastern European footprint. A CTP press release from July 30, 2026, confirmed that the group's standing portfolio was valued at a 6.50% gross yield and a 6.90% reversionary yield. These valuation yields are significantly higher than the 5.50% EPRA net initial yield reported by Western European peer Warehouses de Pauw in September 2026. The higher yield profile of CTP provides a meaningful cushion against rising financing costs.
6.
Inconsistent appraisals: VGP experienced yield compression on its own standing property portfolio during the first half of 2026. A VGP interim financial report from August 20, 2026, disclosed that its own property portfolio was valued at a weighted average yield of 7.23% in June 2026. This represents a decompression-defying compression from the 7.48% weighted average yield recorded at the end of December 2025. This positive movement was primarily driven by development profits recognized on newly initiated construction projects.
7.
Significant variance: VGP's portfolio valuation reveals significant differences in exit yields across its core operational markets. VGP's 2025 annual report showed that valuers applied an exit yield of 6.75% to 7.25% in Hungary, resulting in a weighted average yield of 7.59% for standing assets. In contrast, VGP's German standing assets were valued at exit yields of 4.70% to 9.10%, with a much lower weighted average yield of 6.40%. This wide variance reflects the distinct risk premiums that international appraisers assign to Western European versus Central and Eastern European logistics hubs.
8.
High sensitivity: VGP's portfolio valuation remains highly sensitive to minor yield fluctuations. VGP's annual report for 2025 indicated that its standing property portfolio would experience a valuation shift of €27.20 million for every 10 basis point change in the market yield. This sensitivity highlights that any valuer-applied yield expansion can materially erode VGP's book values and asset-recycling profits. Consequently, stable market cap rates are critical to maintaining the developer's development velocity and equity valuations.
9.
Cyclical precedents: S IMMO experienced massive revaluation write-downs in the CEE region during past market corrections before subsequently recovering. During an earnings call on November 28, 2017, S IMMO management recalled that they suffered CEE devaluation losses of over €190 million during past downcycles. However, the developer subsequently captured substantial upside as yield compression returned to Budapest and Bucharest. This historical precedent suggests that the recent Romanian markdowns could represent a temporary cyclical correction rather than a permanent loss of capital.

Transactional Yield Divergence

10.
Resilient pricing: Jefferies Research highlights that Central and Eastern European logistics yields have demonstrated notable stability compared to Western European markets. A Jefferies report dated October 22, 2026, noted that logistics prime yields show an expansionary bias, but with a clear West and CEE divide. While France and Belgium saw massive yield expansion of 60 and 50 basis points respectively, CEE logistics yields remained largely stable. This stability indicates that the Romanian markdown has not triggered a broader yield expansion across neighboring CEE markets.
11.
Expanding yields: Germany recorded modest yield expansion across its industrial and logistics real estate segments. According to a Jefferies broker report dated August 21, 2026, Germany experienced a 10 basis point yield decompression in both logistics and light industrial properties. Similarly, Poland also saw its prime logistics yields expand by 10 basis points during the same period. These marginal movements indicate that while Western markets reprice, CEE core hubs continue to display superior yield resilience.
12.
Aggressive pricing: VGP management argues that public valuer appraisals remain highly conservative compared to real transactions taking place in the market. During an interim results call, VGP management noted that their CEE assets are conservatively valued in Eastern Europe compared to actual market transactions. The developer observed that direct transactional offers in the market have been significantly more aggressive than the yields applied by independent appraisers. This yield gap suggests that the underlying market value of finished parks is higher than book values suggest.
13.
Substantial exposure: Warehouses de Pauw maintains significant exposure to the Romanian logistics market beside its traditional Western European assets. According to a Morgan Stanley broker report dated October 26, 2026, Romania represents 19% of WDP's standalone portfolio value of €8.80 billion. This concentration exposes the Western European developer to the same regional revaluation risks and localized rental competition that CTP faces in Romania. Thus, any broader regional yield decompression would directly affect the earnings and net asset values of multiple listed European peers.
14.
Decompressing risk: CTP's relatively low net initial yields are drawing investor scrutiny. According to a Jefferies broker report dated August 4, 2026, CTP's portfolio was valued at a 6.50% gross yield and approximately a 5.40% net initial yield. Investors are increasingly questioning whether these low yields are sustainable if macroeconomic conditions and rental growth soften further across CEE. This yield level suggests that any outward yield movement could quickly compress CTP's development spreads.
15.
Other side of the story: While stable CEE transaction yields and conservative valuer appraisals suggest that regional values are holding firm, broader macroeconomic pressures could still force yield expansion. A Jefferies broker report from September 21, 2026, warned that European appraisers are notoriously backward-looking and slow to adjust their valuation assumptions until transaction evidence accumulates. If high-for-longer interest rates persist, the outward yield expansion seen in Western Europe will likely spill over into CEE, dragging down portfolio valuations. Therefore, the localized Romanian markdown could still prove to be the leading edge of a broader regional revaluation.

Section 6 – Competitor Speculative Supply and VGP's Pre-let Slowdown

Section summary
  1. Competitor speculative supply and macro headwinds in Germany have prompted developers to adopt more disciplined pre-letting thresholds.
  2. High speculative development pipelines in Central Europe increase leasing occupancy risks and pressure rental pricing power in competitive sub-markets.

Speculative Supply and

1.
Developer Discipline Slower starts: Former Head of Construction at Panattoni noted on August 26, 2026, that industrial developers across Europe have actively slowed down speculative land acquisitions and new building starts. This developer response comes after years of rapid expansion that began to outpace real occupier absorption. Consequently, this construction deceleration is expected to help stabilize regional vacancies over the next twelve months.
2.
Varying pre-lets: Bernstein Research reported on October 12, 2026, that CTP relies much more heavily on a speculative development model than its listed peers. While CTP's under-construction pre-let ratio stood at only 51%, major competitors like VGP maintained 70% pre-lets, with Segro and Warehouses de Pauw reaching 75% and 83% respectively. This speculative tilt exposes CTP to higher immediate leasing risks if regional take-up slows.
3.
Downward adjustments: Deutsche Bank Research noted on October 29, 2026, that CTP is adjusting its near- term completions downward in response to slower pre-letting velocity. The developer's pre-let rate on planned 2026 completions progressed to 55%, falling behind the 63% achieved in the prior year period. As a result, CTP expects annual deliveries at the lower end of guidance of 1.4 million to 1.7 million square meters.

VGP's Strategic Pre-let Slowdown

4.
Throttled starts: Deutsche Bank Research reported on June 25, 2026, that VGP's pre-let volumes began slowing in the first half of 2026 as a direct consequence of increased pre-letting thresholds for new construction starts. Management implemented these higher requirements to insulate the company from rising macroeconomic vacancy risks. This defensive shift reflects a cautious stance toward speculative projects in a softer European growth environment.
5.
Softening demand: Former Head of Preconstruction at VGP stated on August 31, 2026, that severe land scarcity and softening occupier demand pose significant development hurdles in Germany. These German headwinds have elongated lease negotiations and forced developers to be highly selective with new projects. Consequently, VGP is pacing construction starts to protect development margins rather than chasing sheer volume.
6.
Moderate volumes: VGP reported in its half-year results on August 20, 2026, that it signed €52.70 million of new and renewed leases, representing a moderate six percent decline year-on-year. While overall leasing remains healthy, decision-making among large industrial tenants has become notably more cautious. Consequently, this corporate hesitation has slowed the immediate absorption of newly completed projects.

Pricing Power and

7.
