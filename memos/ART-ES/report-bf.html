EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Arteche Lantegi Elekartea operates as a specialized global manufacturer of electrical equipment across power generation, transmission, and distribution sectors, structured into three core business segments that enable the Basque company to remain highly competitive against much larger Tier I sector peers. The company maintains a top three global position in the instrument transformer market and holds the number one global position in auxiliary relay sales volume.

This diverse geographic footprint protects total earnings from regional economic slowdowns, while revenue visibility remains exceptionally high due to mandatory grid code compliance and green energy connection rules. The company recently upgraded its revenue outlook to between 565 and 590 million euros following a forty-four percent net profit increase in the first half of 2026, with projected EBITDA margins rising to a range of 16.3% to 17.8%.

Business model and organization strengths

Arteche secures revenue through a balanced mix of client types, led by power utilities at 30% of sales, with most sales consisting of one-off capital equipment contracts for substation projects that reduce concentration risk as the top five customers represent less than 25% of total sales. Revenue predictability remains exceptionally high because long-term master agreements with leading global power utilities typically span two to three years and incorporate automatic renewal clauses, while demand is structurally insulated from short-term economic cycles.

The company wins and retains clients primarily through a complex technical homologation process, having successfully secured product approvals with more than 1,500 utilities globally, which represents the primary barrier to entry for new electrical equipment competitors. Arteche has positioned itself as the preferred supplier of optical current instrument transformers for major global players, with technological leadership proven by successful deliveries to nuclear fusion research projects in the United States.

Financial profile versus peers and capital discipline

Arteche experienced robust top-line growth over the historical period, reaching 282 million euros in revenue by 2021 from 205 million euros in 2017, successfully demonstrating resilient organic revenue development despite temporary economic challenges. The company recorded a strong recovery in its direct margins, which improved by 342 basis points in the first half of 2023, achieved through active sales price updates and operational productivity improvements that offset severe raw material inflation for copper and steel.

Operating profitability compares favorably with historical peer averages in the capital goods sector where EBITDA margins typically hover around 12% to 13%, with increasing demand for high-voltage products expected to drive overall margins higher over the next three years. The company prioritized inventory control to optimize operational cash generation, avoiding the severe working capital strains experienced by Spanish peer Tubacex whose working capital swelled to 384.1 million euros.

Arteche successfully reduced its net financial debt from 119 million euros in 2017 to 51 million euros in 2021, improving the leverage ratio from 5.3x to 2.0x EBITDA and providing substantial balance sheet flexibility to execute the inorganic strategy.

Industry structure and competitive positioning

The global electrical equipment industry is experiencing a multi-decade expansion cycle driven by electrification and data center demand, valued at US$1,660.2 billion in 2025 and projected to grow to US$4,151.6 billion by 2034, representing a structural shift toward a prolonged power infrastructure upcycle. The electrical systems market is dominated by a few multinational players with Schneider Electric holding 51% market share, followed by Siemens at 37% and ABB at 32%, with this high level of market concentration limiting competitive entry from unapproved manufacturers. Major electrical equipment manufacturers are prioritizing profit margins over sheer sales volume, with HD Hyundai Electric and LS Electric adopting a selective order-taking strategy because highvoltage transformer demand exceeds supply, allowing manufacturers to reject low-priced tenders and focus on highly profitable advanced specification orders. Strong operating leverage and pricing power have pushed the industry's margin ceiling to record levels, with high-voltage power systems margins reaching 25% due to improved realizations and fixed-cost leverage, with manufacturers reinvesting these profits to lock in long-term earnings growth.

Value chain dynamics and bargaining power

Upstream procurement for grid equipment relies heavily on raw materials like copper, steel, and aluminum, with the global copper supply facing a widening deficit by 2035 due to secular electrification demand. Equipment manufacturing remains the most lucrative stage of the electrical grid value chain, with peer specialist Trench Group projected to generate an outstanding 37% adjusted EBITDA margin while Arteche improved its own direct margin to 37.3 percent in fiscal year 2025.

Customer concentration at the utility level is highly stable and backed by long-term contracts, with Arteche's top five utility clients maintaining continuous commercial relationships for more than 30 years secured by two-to-three year master agreements with automatic renewal clauses. Large conglomerates are vertically integrating specialized suppliers to secure supply chains during the grid upcycle, with GE Vernova announcing a 9 billion dollar acquisition of Trench Group to bring bushing and coil capacity in-house and improve supply-chain certainty.

The single most damaging value chain change for Arteche would be an abrupt freeze in utility capital expenditures, as grid investments are structurally slow to materialize due to regulatory approvals and permitting delays that would prevent conversion of the record backlog into recognized revenue.

Customer relationships and switching costs

Arteche distributes its products across a balanced mix of client types, with utilities representing 30% of total revenues, followed by distributors at 25%, EPC firms at 20%, and OEMs at 15%, protecting overall operating cash flows from isolated downturns in any single industrial channel. Low customer concentration ensures that the loss of any single account cannot severely impair mid-term earnings, with the group's top five customers maintaining active purchasing agreements for more than thirty years and over 55% of customers who placed orders in 2022 having conducted business in the previous year.

Arteche benefits from substantial customer switching costs driven by intense technical qualification cycles, as obtaining technical product approvals can take several years of testing and evaluation, creating deep customer lock-in that prevents unapproved rivals from bidding. The company possesses robust pricing power through structured contractual pass-through mechanisms, with the vast majority of long-term utility contracts incorporating specific raw material pass-through clauses that protect gross profit margins from commodity shocks.

Supplier dynamics and distribution channels

Arteche relies heavily on key metals and industrial materials including copper, aluminum, and electrical steel processed using casting, stamping, foundry, and drawing technologies, with these inputs essential for highvoltage and medium-voltage product lines. The group manages upstream procurement through localized supplier partnerships, prioritizing purchasing raw materials from local businesses near its regional manufacturing plants to optimize transport logistics and strengthen regional supply security through highly collaborative local supplier relationships. Power grid equipment manufacturers are grappling with unprecedented supply chain constraints for critical high-voltage components, with global lead times on transformers rising to two to four years, forcing orders to be booked far in advance and representing a persistent delivery risk. Arteche utilizes a diversified, multi-channel distribution model, distributing 25% of total product volume through third-party electrical distributors while delivering the remainder directly to power utilities at 30%, EPC contractors at 20%, and OEMs at 15%, which limits dependence on single channels.
