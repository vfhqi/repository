Section 2 – The Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation Fibre Joint Venture

Section summary

Strategic Alliance and

1.
Structure Structured alliance: Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation signed a non-binding term sheet in June 2026 to merge their respective fiber subsidiaries. This agreement combines Vodafone's Fiber2All and PPC's FiberGrid into a unified 50:50 joint venture to establish a scaled wholesale platform. This structure intends to provide neutral, open-access wholesale fiber services to all retail operators in Greece. The arrangement allows both companies to consolidate their market presence.
2.
Balancing contributions: The joint venture structure addresses the unequal network footprints contributed by each partner. To equalize the ownership at 50% each, Vodafone Greece will pay a cash consideration of €130 million directly to PPC. Morgan Stanley's note of 5 August 2026 explains that this payment compensates PPC for contributing its substantially larger physical network to the new entity. This financial mechanism ensures equal control over the wholesale entity.
3.
Combined network scale: The combined entity will start with a substantial network footprint. Vodafone Group reported on 27 July 2026 that their combined fiber-to-the-home businesses currently cover more than 1.6 million homes. Specifically, PPC FiberGrid contributes 1.30 million ready-for-service lines, while Vodafone's Fiber2All contributes 0.55 million ready-for-service lines. This scale establishes the joint venture as a formidable competitor to OTE.
4.
Utility core strategy: PPC's 2030 Strategic Plan focuses heavily on central and southeastern Europe expansion. Vodafone Group reported on 10 June 2026 that PPC is investing €24 billion in renewables, storage, and data centers. This massive investment plan requires PPC to preserve its capital, which the fiber joint venture facilitates by sharing the financial burden with Vodafone. This allows PPC to focus its core resources on regional energy dominance.

Roll-out Targets and

5.
Timetable Timeline to completion: The non-binding heads of terms signed in June 2026 initiated a structured path toward final completion. Both parties are currently conducting detailed due diligence and drafting definitive binding agreements. Public Power Corporation's investor presentation on 5 August 2026 indicates that the partners target closing the transaction in H1 2027. Regulatory approvals and customary closing conditions must be completed before finalization.
6.
Ambitious build targets: The joint venture intends to accelerate the deployment of high-speed fiber across Greece. Public Power Corporation's interim report on 5 August 2026 indicates that PPC FiberGrid alone targets covering over 3.8 million households by the end of 2028. This target represents a significant increase from its footprint of 2.05 million households in mid-2026. This rapid expansion could alter the competitive balance of the Greek telecom market.
7.
De-risked capital expenditure: Partnering with Vodafone allows Public Power Corporation to optimize its balance sheet. The joint venture de-risks PPC's fiber capex by sharing costs with an established telecom provider. This shared investment approach preserves PPC's balance sheet capacity to fund other critical initiatives, such as its €24 billion energy program. Consequently, the alliance improves overall project returns and lowers execution risks.

Vodafone Group's trading update on 27 July 2026 confirms that forming the joint venture is strictly subject to required approvals from competent authorities. The partners expect that the Greek telecommunications regulator EETT will review the market concentration impacts of the transaction. If approved, the merger will reshape the national wholesale market by mid-2027.

Peer Comparisons and Industry Precedents

9.
Infrastructure peer comparisons: The scale of the Greek joint venture's roll-out target is highly ambitious compared to its regional peers. While the Vodafone-PPC joint venture targets 3.8 million homes, peer infrastructure ventures like Glaspoort in the Netherlands, a 50:50 joint venture between KPN and pension fund ABP, targeted a smaller scale of 750,000 households and 225,000 businesses. Deutsche Telekom's GlasfaserPlus joint venture with IFM in Germany targets a larger 4 million lines. This suggests that the Greek venture is positioned to be a major utility-led player.
10.
Operational progress comparison: The physical deployment progress of the Greek joint venture partners compares favorably with other European fiber players. By mid-2026, PPC had reached 1.30 million ready-forservice homes, whereas Open Dutch Fiber passed more than 1 million homes by late 2025. Similarly, Delta Fiber in the Netherlands passed 1.7 million homes by the same period. These comparisons demonstrate that PPC's aerial fiber-to-the-home roll-out has achieved market-leading execution speed in its early phases.
11.
Fibre joint venture trends: Fibre joint ventures have been highly active across Europe as operators seek off- balance-sheet financing. Bernstein Research's report on 15 November 2025 highlights that European incumbents are transferring ownership of their fiber networks to joint ventures with third parties to manage high deployment costs. Examples of this trend are visible in advanced fiber markets such as Spain and Germany. These precedents suggest that the Vodafone-PPC alliance aligns with a broader European trend toward infrastructure sharing.
12.
Comparable past cases: The SIRO joint venture in Ireland provides an excellent precedent for a telecom-utility partnership. Formed between Vodafone and the Electricity Supply Board (ESB), SIRO deploys fiber over ESB's existing pole network to reduce rollout capital expenditures. Just like the Greek joint venture, SIRO operates as a wholesale open-access provider to multiple internet service providers. This precedent suggests that the Vodafone-PPC alliance can successfully leverage utility infrastructure to achieve rapid deployment.

Section 3 – JV Wholesale Price List and Commercial Position

Greek Wholesale Tariff Dynamics

1.
Regulated baseline pricing: Hellenic Telecom maintains a stable regulated baseline tariff for its fiber-to-the-home lines. During the Q2 2024 earnings call on 7 August 2024, Hellenic Telecom management confirmed that the nominal regulated price for wholesale fiber products is typically between €12 and €13 depending on the specific speed tier. This established benchmark serves as the pricing ceiling against which all alternative networks must compete within the Greek telecommunications market. Consequently, any new venture must price its wholesale products below this range to attract retail service providers.
2.
Discounted wholesale framework: Hellenic Telecom introduced an approved discount scheme to secure its retail partners and improve network utilization. During the Q2 2024 earnings call on 7 August 2024, Hellenic Telecom reported that its wholesale scheme offers discounts of up to 28% off nominal regulated rates. This framework is conditional on operators meeting specific minimum volume commitments over the contract duration. Thus, the effective wholesale rate paid by major operators under this agreement falls to approximately €8.64 to €9.36.
3.
Incumbent volume locking: The volume discount strategy has successfully locked in Hellenic Telecom's wholesale customers. Deutsche Bank reported on 14 November 2024 that both Vodafone Greece and Nova signed these volume-based wholesale agreements. This commercial lock-in makes it difficult for alternative infrastructure providers to divert existing subscriber volumes in the short term. Therefore, Hellenic Telecom has successfully insulated its wholesale revenue stream from immediate competitive churn.
4.
Joint venture pricing pressure: The newly proposed Vodafone-PPC joint venture is expected to introduce aggressive wholesale pricing. Morgan Stanley noted on 16 September 2026 that the joint venture could become a viable competitor in the fiber space once operational. By combining Vodafone's retail customer base with Public Power Corporation's extensive physical network, the entity can support lower unit wholesale prices. This pricing strategy could threaten Hellenic Telecom's long-term wholesale margins.

Tenant Acquisition and Alternative Operators

5.
Unacceptable pricing terms: Public Power Corporation struggled initially to attract tenants due to uncompetitive independent wholesale pricing. Deutsche Bank's report on 3 December 2024 noted that Nova's parent company, United Group, publicly stated that Public Power Corporation's initial wholesale fiber prices were unacceptable to Nova. This commercial friction occurred because alternative operators had already agreed to mutual wholesale access on cheaper terms. Consequently, Public Power Corporation failed to secure any major retail tenants during its initial standalone wholesale roll-out phase.
6.
Forced retail disruption: Initial wholesale failures forced Public Power Corporation to deploy a highly disruptive retail pricing strategy. Deutsche Bank's weekly update on 24 October 2025 explained that Public Power Corporation entered the retail broadband market with prices roughly 50% below those of incumbent operators. This aggressive retail move was designed to force alternative operators like Vodafone and Nova to wholesale its fiber-to-the-home network. However, this strategy failed to immediately break Hellenic Telecom's wholesale dominance.
7.
Open access positioning: The new joint venture structure is designed to resolve past tenant acquisition struggles by operating as an open-access wholesaler. Public Power Corporation's presentation on 5 August 2026 indicated that the joint venture aims to build an open-access wholesale fiber platform to host multiple tenants. By merging with Vodafone's existing fiber assets, the joint venture secures an immediate anchor tenant. This structural shift makes the platform significantly more attractive to alternative operators like Nova.

Southern European Peer Market Dynamics

8.
Low-cost rollout precedent: Spanish fiber rollouts demonstrate how low deployment costs can drive extremely low wholesale and retail pricing. Morgan Stanley highlighted on 21 May 2024 that Spanish fiber deployment costs are as low as €70 per line. These low capital requirements allow Spanish operators to earn attractive returns even with very low retail and wholesale average revenue per user. This represents a stark contrast to the high-cost Greek market where civil works are more expensive.
9.
Spanish pricing benchmarks: Spanish pricing trends suggest that highly competitive overbuilt markets lead to severe downward pressure on tariffs. Deutsche Bank reported on 23 July 2024 that Spanish brands like Lowi and Digi offer highly discounted fixed broadband rates of €25 to €35 per month. These low retail tariffs are supported by highly attractive wholesale terms on shared networks. This precedent suggests that if the Greek joint venture successfully scales, wholesale prices will inevitably fall.
10.
Spanish wholesale models: The Spanish wholesale landscape features successful pure wholesale players that could serve as a model for Greece. Deutsche Bank's report on 26 June 2020 highlighted Onivia as Spain's first independent neutral wholesale-only provider of fiber services. Onivia scaled rapidly by acquiring existing fiber lines from MasMovil and providing open-access wholesale terms. This model demonstrates that neutral wholesale platforms can successfully exist alongside traditional vertically integrated incumbents.
11.
High rollout barriers: Greece's high deployment costs make replicating the low-cost Spanish pricing model extremely difficult. Bernstein Research's report on 12 June 2025 noted that Greece belongs to a cluster of European nations with high rollout costs due to limited reusable ducts and poles. These high physical barriers to entry reduce the risk of extensive infrastructure-based overbuild. Consequently, Greek wholesale tariffs are expected to remain structurally higher than those observed in Spain.

Contrary Evidence and

12.
Commercial Challenges Contrary evidence: The pricing power of the Vodafone- PPC joint venture is severely limited by Hellenic Telecom's aggressive customer retention strategies. Morgan Stanley reported on 21 April 2026 that Hellenic Telecom has successfully minimized subscriber churn by focusing on re-contracting existing customers in Public Power Corporation's footprint. This defensive customer retention focus means the new joint venture will struggle to attract retail subscribers even if it offers lower wholesale pricing. Consequently, the commercial threat of the joint venture's price list may be smaller than anticipated.
13.
Technical connection gaps: Technical limitations in the physical network deployment represent a major commercial hurdle for the joint venture. Deutsche Bank reported on 3 December 2024 that Public Power Corporation's rollout focused on bringing fiber to apartment ground floors but lacked the fully-functioning telecom network required to connect individual units. This means that retail partners must invest significant additional capital to complete the final drop connection to the subscriber. Therefore, the joint venture's actual wholesale value proposition remains incomplete compared to Hellenic Telecom's active network.
14.
Anchor tenant limitations: The joint venture's immediate wholesale monetization is restricted by Vodafone's existing long-term contractual commitments. Hellenic Telecom's Q4 2024 earnings call on 26 February 2025 confirmed that Vodafone Greece is already a committed wholesale tenant on its network. Under the 2024 agreement, Vodafone is bound by minimum volume commitments that carry financial penalties. This commercial lock-in prevents Vodafone from rapidly migrating its existing subscriber base to the joint venture network.

Commercial Position and Structural Impact

15.
Joint venture viability: The joint venture consolidates the alternative fiber market to create a viable wholesale platform. Morgan Stanley's report on 16 September 2026 stated that the joint venture will ease competitive overbuild worries by merging competing build plans. This consolidation improves overall capital efficiency and stabilizes market pricing. Therefore, the joint venture is highly likely to achieve commercial viability once the transaction completes.
16.
Other side of the story: Despite short-term contract protections, the joint venture represents a formidable longterm wholesale threat to Hellenic Telecom. Deutsche Bank's report on 11 May 2026 noted that Public Power Corporation's primary goal remains to force operators to lease its network rather than Hellenic Telecom's infrastructure. Once the existing 2024 wholesale agreements expire, both Vodafone and Nova will have powerful financial incentives to migrate their subscribers to the joint venture's open-access network. Consequently, Hellenic Telecom faces a severe decline in national wholesale revenue that will negatively impact its long-term earnings per share.

European Commission Antitrust Evolution

5.
Antitrust leadership transition: The European Commission's approach to telecommunications consolidation has entered a transitional phase that could favor scaled infrastructure alliances. Morgan Stanley reported on 16 September 2026 that Teresa Ribera succeeded Margrethe Vestager as Competition Commissioner in late 2024. This change is highly significant because Vestager consistently blocked horizontal mergers during her ten-year tenure, which means the new administration represents a more supportive operating environment for infrastructure-sharing joint ventures.
6.
Shifting merger guidelines: The European Commission is currently modernizing its horizontal merger guidelines to prioritize long-term dynamic investment over short-term price fluctuations. Morgan Stanley's report on 8 April 2026 noted that draft guidelines were scheduled for mid-2026 with final adoption targeted for late 2027. Under these updated guidelines, the commission will consider how combined platforms improve national digital competitiveness. This regulatory shift should facilitate smoother clearance for the partners as they seek to deploy high-speed fiber coverage.
7.
Lobbying for policy reform: European telecom operators are actively lobbying for merger control reforms to facilitate market consolidation and dynamic efficiency. Bernstein Research reported on 23 January 2025 that high-profile reports by Enrico Letta and Mario Draghi have urged the European Commission to modernize its antitrust stance. This political pressure is designed to encourage investment in very high-capacity networks. Therefore, the shifting political climate suggests that the joint venture will face less regulatory resistance than historical four-to-three mobile mergers.
8.
Behavioral remedy template: Recent regulatory approvals in major European telecom markets indicate that authorities increasingly favor behavioral commitments over structural network divestments. Deutsche Bank reported on 29 October 2025 that the European Commission is utilizing the UK mobile merger as a template for future transactions. This template relies on binding network investment guarantees and open wholesale access to protect consumers. Consequently, the Vodafone-PPC joint venture is highly likely to secure approval if both partners commit to ambitious physical roll-out targets.

Competitor Reactions and Market Impact

9.
Incumbent defensive positioning: Hellenic Telecom has successfully leveraged its premium network status to protect its high-margin retail broadband subscribers from alternative platforms. Morgan Stanley reported on 16 September 2026 that OTE's best-network position allows it to monetize data volume growth and transition prepaid customers. This defensive customer retention strategy supports OTE's projected 2026 EPS of €1.51 even as competitive builds accelerate. Thus, OTE's strong brand equity acts as a primary shield against disruption from alternative wholesale networks.
10.
Extreme market concentration: Hellenic Telecom continues to capture nearly the entire cash flow pool of the Greek telecommunications sector despite aggressive competitor expansion. Deutsche Bank reported on 29 May 2025 that OTE earned 65% of total market revenue and 99% of operating free cash flow in FY24. This leaves Vodafone Greece and Nova with a combined cash flow share of just 1% during that period. Consequently, the joint venture represents a combined effort to break OTE's infrastructure monopoly by sharing heavy rollout costs.
11.
Divergent operator models: Alternative operators are pursuing divergent infrastructure strategies in response to OTE's retail dominance and regulatory pricing reviews. Deutsche Bank's note on 3 December 2024 explained that Nova's parent company expressed strong dissatisfaction with PPC's standalone wholesale rates. This commercial friction shows that competitor alignment is difficult to achieve.
