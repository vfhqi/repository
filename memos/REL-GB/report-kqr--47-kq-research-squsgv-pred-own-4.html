Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding Exhibitions dynamics is crucial to assessing RELX's multi-segment valuation and overall EPS trajectory.
  2. While some investors query segment fit, Exhibitions remains a highly cash-generative asset that boosts group margins.

The Key Question

1.
The Key Question: "For RELX PLC's Exhibitions division (RX), what share of revenue and profit comes from Middle East events, how are 2027 rebooking rates and exhibitor numbers developing for the Arabian Travel Market and other Gulf shows, how large is the biennial event-cycling effect on RX's reported revenue in odd years such as 2025 and 2027 compared with even years, and what do Informa's and its Saudi joint venture Tahaluf's recent event deferrals imply for RX's 2027 revenue and margin?"

Portfolio Structure and Segment Mix

2.
Consolidated revenue contributions: RELX's business mix is highly diversified across several key sectors, with electronic delivery driving most of the group's total revenue. According to RELX's Annual Report published on June 23, 2026, the group reported total FY25 revenue of £9,590 million, with the Risk segment contributing 36%, Scientific, Technical & Medical contributing 28%, Legal contributing 19%, and Exhibitions representing 13 percent of segment revenue (calculated). This multi-segment model reduces overall operational volatility, which protects adjusted earnings per share from downturns in any single end-market.
3.
Divisional profit distribution: The operating profit profile of the group exhibits a similar divisional structure, where high-margin data-led divisions dominate the overall contribution. In its interim results published on July 23, 2026, RELX reported total H1 2026 adjusted operating profit of £1,727 million. Within this total, the Risk segment delivered £1,810 million in H1 revenue, Elsevier STM delivered £1,370 million, Legal delivered £959 million, and Exhibitions contributed £575 million in revenue. This divisional balance supports consistent earnings growth because the robust margins in electronic divisions offset transactional volatility in events.
4.
Exhibitions strategic valuation: The Exhibitions division, operating as RX, is valued at a lower multiple compared to the higher-growth data and analytics segments. Deutsche Bank's report on July 23, 2026, values the Exhibitions division at 15x EV/EBIT, compared to 22x for Risk and 17x for Legal. This lower multiple is applied because live events are perceived as more transactional, although RX exhibits superior operating margins compared to traditional B2B peers. Understanding RX's performance is crucial for investors because segment margin expansion directly enhances consolidated valuation and EPS growth.

Investor structural concerns: Some market participants question whether the diversified multi-segment portfolio of RELX is optimal or if certain divisions should be separated. Although management is unlikely to divest RX, this business complexity creates a valuation discount that caps the stock's P/E multiple.
6.
Compounding EPS trajectory: The overall EPS growth of the group remains highly robust, driven by organic progress and a very active capital return program. Rothschild & Co Redburn's financial model on October 22, 2026, projects that adjusted diluted EPS will grow from 127.91p in FY25 to 139.64p in FY26e, and reach 156.66p in FY27e. This compounding trajectory is supported by ongoing share buyback programs totaling £2,250 million. Consequently, any unexpected operational drag in RX could alter the timing of buybacks and impact future EPS growth.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that the volatility of the Exhibitions business is actually overemphasized by the market, meaning that the division is more stable than commonly perceived. Redburn Atlantic's report on February 25, 2025, highlights that the standard deviation of RX's revenue and adjusted operating profit growth, excluding event-cycling, is in line with other group divisions. This suggests that the transactional nature of live trade shows does not introduce excessive risk to RELX's consolidated earnings. Therefore, the market's discount on RX's earnings contribution may be unjustified.

According to the RELX Annual Report published on September 29, 2026, RX generated total revenue of £1,186 million in the fiscal year ended December 31, 2025. This global base means the division has significant scale to absorb regional shocks, supporting stable overall group adjusted earnings per share unless multiple major geographies contract simultaneously.

Geographic revenue concentration: The division's geographic profile remains heavily weighted toward established western markets, although emerging markets represent the fastest-growing component. In its annual filing published on September 29, 2026, RELX reported that Europe accounted for 36% of RX's FY25 revenue, while North America contributed 20%. The remaining 44% of revenue was derived from the rest of the world on an event-location basis. Consequently, the vast majority of RX's cash flows are generated outside of volatile emerging regions, shielding consolidated adjusted profit margins from localized geopolitical downturns.

Deutsche Bank's research on July 23, 2026, noted that this 39.3% margin was ahead of the broker's 39% forecast due to strong operational gearing. This structural profitability ensures that RX remains highly cash- generative and accretive to group cash flow even during temporary regional disruptions.

Saudi infrastructure costs: The margin profile of exhibitions in emerging Middle Eastern markets is often diluted by high setup costs. A report by Bernstein Research on July 16, 2025, noted that margins for Saudi Arabian shows are historically lower than the divisional average. The analyst explained that Saudi Arabia does not yet possess sufficient permanent facilities, requiring organizers to build expensive temporary structures for major events. Consequently, RX's slower expansion into Saudi Arabia compared to Dubai has actually protected its short-term adjusted operating profit margins from regional dilution.

The broker explained that these displaced events carried high marginal profitability of 60%, meaning H1 margins were mechanically depressed. However, because these revenues recover in the second half, this timing drag does not alter FY26 group adjusted EPS.

Contrary evidence: Some industry data suggests that the direct negative impact of Middle East disruption on RX's revenue growth has been more severe than management initially indicated.
