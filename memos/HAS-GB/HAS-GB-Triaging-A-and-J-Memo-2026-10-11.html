[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Hays is a cost-rebase self-help case dressed as a cyclical recovery, and at 64.25p the market has already paid for the recovery in full. The company has cut £115m from its cost base since FY24, exited 13 countries and replaced its chief executive, so that any recovery in German and UK hiring would lift the conversion margin from 5.4 percent to 11 to 13 percent and roughly triple earnings per share by FY29; but the shares have risen 117 percent in six months to 16.7 times consensus earnings two years forward, the 93rd percentile of their own five-year range, so on our modal path the 'Modal return' is about 7 percent a year, because the multiple's compression toward its median takes back most of what the earnings growth gives.

  • The change forces are internal, and they are real.
    • A chief executive change (Mark Dearnley, interim from 27 Feb 2026, permanent from 18 May 2026) was followed within three months by the Momentum strategy of August 2026, £50m of annualised structural savings secured three years early with a further £50m targeted for FY27, a consultant base cut 41 percent from its peak to 5,194, and a footprint cut from 33 countries to 16 core markets.
    • Four of the twelve internal forces are strong and the screen's decision rule passes.
    • Delivery: £115m of cumulative savings since FY24, 74 offices closed, 13 countries exited
      • Six European countries sold in June 2026 for about £4m, seven more (about £70m of fees) under review.
    • The qualification: the chief executive change was forced by a resignation, the new chief executive has no recruitment operating record, and the two headline targets (productivity up more than 50 percent, conversion 25 percent-plus) carry no dates.
  • Every material external force is a headwind, and the internal change is running against them rather than with them.
    • Permanent fees fell 12 percent and German net fees 9 to 10 percent in FY26
    • The UK private sector has shed about 110,000 jobs since the start of 2026
    • Artificial-intelligence platforms are taking share in junior white-collar roles
    • UK employer costs rose from April 2026 and Dutch self-employment enforcement cut a peer's Dutch fees 30 percent.
    • The one plausible turn: the company's own cycle.
      • Group fees fell 5 percent in the fourth quarter after 8 percent in the third, July and August traded in line, and the blue-collar generalists (Adecco organic +5.6 percent in the second quarter of 2026, Randstad Germany +4 percent) say the industry's trough is behind it.
  • The change has reached one line of the outputs and stopped there.
    • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain it has travelled from resources (the chief executive, the cut headcount) through choices (Momentum, the exits) to actions (the savings, the closures), into one key operating metric (consultant productivity, up for eleven consecutive quarters) and one financial-statement line (the conversion margin, up 70 basis points to 5.4 percent, with FY26 operating profit of £48.6m beating a £43.5m consensus on fees down 8 percent).
    • Where it has not reached: net fees (still down 5 percent in the fourth quarter and in line through August), free cash flow after £89.6m of exceptionals (net cash down £16.9m to £20.1m), and the sell-side's estimates, which are down 47 percent over twelve months for FY27 and up 8 percent over three.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share of 1.2p in FY26 rising to 1.7p in FY27, 2.8p in FY28, 4.3p in FY29 and 5.1p in FY30 on our modal path, with the conversion margin at 7.2, 9.3, 11.8 and 13.0 percent
    • Consensus is 1.8p, 3.1p and 5.3p for FY27 to FY29.
    • The composition: almost all of the growth is margin recovery and the tax rate normalising from 45 percent
      • Revenue contributes about 1 percent a year because our fee path is £935m in FY29 against £905.5m in FY26 after about £70m of exits.
      • That is the growth the framework trusts least.
    • Why we sit at the bottom of the broker range for FY29 (4.5p to 6.5p): we hold conversion below 12 percent where consensus implies about 14 percent, because procurement discounting, boutique share loss, junior-role automation and a 41 percent smaller consultant base argue that fee growth has to be bought with headcount, and that the second £50m of savings is partly reinvested.
  • What it costs. 64.25p is 16.7 times consensus earnings two years forward (3.84p) and 20.1 times our own two-year-forward figure of 3.2p
    • The 93rd percentile of the stock's five-year range, the 87th of two years and the 81st of ten
    • Above the ten-year 75th-percentile band of 15.50 times and below the 90th of 18.30.
    • Peers trade at 8.6 times (Adecco), 13.9 times (PageGroup) and 11.2 times (Randstad), at the 29th to 50th percentiles of their own histories.
    • What is priced: the cost savings, the exits, a German recovery beginning within two quarters, conversion recovering to about 14 percent by FY29 and the tax rate normalising.
      • What is not priced: a second year of German decline, a structural ceiling on conversion from procurement and artificial intelligence, or FY27 exceptionals larger than the market expects.
  • The return does not clear the bar, because the multiple compresses as the earnings arrive.
    • On our modal path the value including cash returns is 71.2p at eighteen months and 79.3p at three years, 7.1 and 7.3 percent a year against the 20 percent bar.
    • Earnings two years forward grow 1.6 to 1.8 times
    • The multiple falls from 20.1 times our earnings to 13.5 and 13.0 times, the pattern of a recruiter's forward multiple, which peaks at trough earnings
    • The cash return is under 1 percent a year.
    • Even on the conceded anchor (the ten-year 75th percentile of 15.50 times, if the temporary mix shift and the cost rebase are treated as structural) the return is 17.3 percent a year at eighteen months, a C, not a B.
    • The price at which the case clears the bar on the modal path is about 54p, where the shares traded in June 2026.
  • The chain breaks at German Temp & Contracting fees, and that is the branch on which the case is a buy.
    • We put the odds that German Temp & Contracting like-for-like fees are positive by the FY27 interims on 25 Feb 2027 at about 45 percent, and the odds that the cost base holds (FY27 conversion above 7 percent) at about 75 percent.
    • On the cleared branch (both, about one in three) the shares are worth about 94p at eighteen months, 29 percent a year
    • On the uncleared branch (two in three) about 47p, a loss of 27 percent
    • The branch-weighted value is 63p, slightly below today's price.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', moderately.
    • The inputs are broad and delivered and the outputs are early
    • The "major EPS trajectory upgrades" the pattern needs have not begun.
    • The two trough patterns ('Trough on Trough', 'Major CfC Clearing') fitted this stock crisply between 28.68p and 50p from April to June 2026 and no longer do, because their mandatory condition on the multiple fails at the 93rd percentile: the price has moved the case out of the pattern in which it was a buy.

  • What the case therefore is.

    • A real cost-driven recovery in earnings, sold at a price that already assumes the volume recovery which no print has yet shown.
    • From 64.25p we are betting against the market's bet: that a 93rd-percentile multiple on trough earnings compresses toward its median as the German recovery arrives, or further if it does not, and that the earnings growth from £100m of cost out, large as it is, cannot outrun that compression to 20 percent a year.
    • We are bearish from here and would park the case until either the price or the German fee line moves.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material on the inputs ('Change Forces' at C with the internal forces at B) but not yet on the outputs ('Financial Outputs' at D: fees falling, estimates cut, one line of the outputs moved) and not at all on the return from this price ('Modal return' at F): the materiality this Building Block measures is the materiality of what the change would do to our return, and the price has consumed it.

    • The parts combine so that the strongest Element is bounded by the weakest
    • The forces are real but the return they buy at 64.25p is not.
  • Not C: a C would need the 'Modal return' in the framework's C band of about 13 to 17 percent a year, which needs the multiple to hold near 15.5 times
    • The own-history and peer evidence says it will not.

  • Not F: the internal change is strong, delivered and resourced, and the operating leverage is arithmetic rather than narrative

    • At 50p the same thesis would clear the bar.

    • The case against our view runs in both directions, and the stronger of the two is the bull case, because it is the one that would move capital the other way.

    • The bull case at its strongest, which is Deutsche Bank's with the operating leverage done properly: Hays has taken £115m of cost out of an £857m cost base and secured a further £50m, so on fees merely flat at £835m the FY27 operating profit is £78m, not the £61.8m consensus

      • On a German recovery of the kind Randstad has already printed (+4 percent in the second quarter of 2026) FY28 operating profit is £89m to £100m at a conversion of 10 to 11 percent, rising toward the 25 percent-plus of the Momentum target by FY30 as the matching engine lifts productivity 50 percent on a stable headcount
      • Earnings per share of 3.1p in FY28 and 5.3p to 6.5p in FY29 put the shares at 10 to 12 times FY29 earnings, cheap for a business returning to 20 percent conversion
      • The premium to peers is earned by the 64 percent temporary mix, the German leadership and the proprietary platform
      • German fiscal and labour reforms are the catalyst, and the sell-side's estimates, having halved, have nowhere to go but up.
      • What we concede: the cost arithmetic (the £78m on flat fees is right before cost inflation and reinvestment)
        • That Hays has executed the downturn better than PageGroup or Robert Walters
        • That the three-month revision trend has turned
        • That the premium to the blue-collar generalists is partly justified by mix
        • And that on the cleared branch, about one in three, the return is about 29 percent a year.
      • Why we hold our view: the competing hypothesis prices the branch and not the odds.
        • The German recovery it needs is possible (45 percent by our reading) and not yet in any Hays print
        • Conversion of 25 percent was last reached in 2008, and the structural forces the research documents (supplier-funded managed-service agreements on upwards of 90 percent of global contracts, boutiques in technical roles, platforms in junior roles, clerical fees down 38 percent since FY19) argue for a ceiling nearer 12 to 15 percent
        • A business at 10 to 12 times FY29 earnings on the bull case is at 20 times our two-year-forward earnings today
        • And the pattern of a recruiter's multiple is to compress as the recovery arrives.
      • No cover claim is available: Germany's £41.2m of FY26 divisional operating profit at 7 times (the lowest named multiple in the comparison set's research) is about £290m, and at the 12 times cycle-average EV/EBIT Morgan Stanley cites about £495m, against an enterprise value of £1,039m

      • The average fee growth of 2 percent masks a 14 percent volume collapse

      • The cost savings have been bought with £120.3m of exceptional charges over two years
      • Net cash has fallen from £300m to £20.1m
      • The German engine lost 24 percent of its operating profit in FY26 with engineering fees down 27 percent in the third quarter
      • And Jefferies' street-low 28p target prices a structural decline in profits.
      • What we concede: the first four as facts, and that the price has run on a recovery no print has yet shown.
      • Why it changes our modal return little: the competing hypothesis is right about the quality of the recovery signals and wrong about the operating leverage, which is arithmetic (a 75 percent people cost base, £100m of cost out of an £857m cost base)
        • Our modal path already assumes conversion recovers only to 11.8 percent by FY29 against the 20 percent of the last cycle, and our rating already rests on the multiple, not the earnings.
      • Where it would move us: on the invalidate level.

  • Hays is a cost-rebase self-help case dressed as a cyclical recovery: a specialist recruiter at trough earnings has cut £115m from its cost base since FY24, exited 13 countries, replaced its chief executive and set a strategy of focus, so that its 75 percent people cost base would turn any recovery in German and UK hiring into a conversion margin of 11 to 13 percent from 5.4 percent, tripling earnings per share by FY29.
  • The change has done one thing to the outputs so far: operating profit rose 3 percent on fees down 8 percent in FY26, the conversion margin gained 70 basis points and second-half operating profit rose 42 percent.
  • It has not yet done anything to net fees (still down 5 percent in the fourth quarter and in line through August), to free cash flow after £89.6m of exceptionals, or to the sell-side's estimates, which halved for FY27 over twelve months and have risen 8 percent over three.
