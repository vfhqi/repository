Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. AT&S entered its FY 2025/26 earnings update on a strong recovery trajectory driven by high-end substrate demand, following years of cyclical downturn and balance sheet pressure.
1.
Established industry leadership: AT&S operates in the technology sector and semiconductor industry as a globally leading manufacturer of high-end integrated circuit substrates and complex printed circuit boards. The company focuses on developing and industrializing high-tech interconnection solutions for digital megatrends. The firm's business model positions it as an advanced technology partner rather than a pure contract manufacturer for leading chipmakers.

2.
Diversified production footprint: The company maintains a geographically resilient footprint with operational manufacturing facilities spanning Austria, China, India, and Malaysia. Research and development operations are highly concentrated at the Leoben-Hinterberg headquarters in Austria, which serves as a global competence center. This diversified global production footprint reduces geopolitical dependencies while allowing the group to capture regional growth opportunities.
3.
Broad end-market exposure: AT&S serves critical digital industries including mobile devices, automotive, aerospace, industrial, medical, and high-performance computing. The group is deepening its engagement in high-performance computing platforms optimized for artificial intelligence applications. Its advanced substrates are critical enablers of next-generation server processors and accelerator architectures.
4.
Solid market share: In the global Ajinomoto Build-up Film substrate market, AT&S held an estimated 8% market share in 2025, according to Jefferies. This positioned the group behind tier-1 market leaders like Taiwan-based Unimicron at 16% and Japan-based Ibiden at 10%. However, AT&S remains ahead of other rising competitors such as Nan Ya PCB at 7% and Zhen Ding at 3%.
5.
Severe cyclical downturn: The global substrate industry experienced a pronounced cyclical correction between 2023 and 2024. This industry downturn was driven by a post-pandemic contraction in personal computers and traditional server markets. AT&S and its peer group suffered from industry-wide capacity overbuilding starting in 2022, which severely depressed utilization rates and market pricing.
6.
Operational inflection point: Despite a challenging currency environment, AT&S achieved a notable operational turnaround in the second half of fiscal year 2025/26. Implementation of strict cost-saving and efficiency initiatives allowed the group to return to positive net profitability in the third and fourth quarters. Management successfully stabilized business development by pivoting toward value-based growth and disciplined investment prioritizations.
7.
Historical price collapse: Mirroring the cyclical semiconductor downturn, AT&S shares suffered a severe correction over the past two years. After trading at elevated levels, the share price collapsed to a 52- week low of EUR 11.05 during the market bottom. This deep valuation discount reflected intense concerns over structural oversupply and the company's elevated debt levels.
8.
Rapid equity recovery: In the six months leading up to the earnings announcement, AT&S shares staged a remarkable upward trajectory. The equity experienced a sharp rebound, rallying more than 26.8% absolute in a single month. This dramatic recovery was fueled by stabilizing channel checks and growing market excitement surrounding AI-driven packaging technologies.
9.
Broker rating upgrade: Deutsche Bank analyst George Brown upgraded AT&S to Buy from Hold on February 2, 2026, with an initial price target of EUR 50. This upgrade was prompted by emerging pricing tailwinds in high-end substrates and the group's unique packaging innovations. The analyst emphasized that the market was underappreciating AT&S's vertical power delivery and optical transceiver opportunities.
10.
Broker target hike: On February 4, 2026, Deutsche Bank analyst George Brown raised his price target further to EUR 55, reiterating a Buy rating. This target adjustment followed solid third-quarter results that demonstrated strong progress on the Kulim plant ramp-up. The analyst highlighted that improving execution at Kulim supported a positive adjustment to medium-term profitability forecasts.
11.
High debt burden: Prior to the earnings update, AT&S's balance sheet remained a primary source of concern for the investment community. In early 2026, the group operated with a highly leveraged financial profile. Deutsche Bank reported a highly stretched net debt to equity ratio of 190.1% for fiscal year 2025/26.
12.
Steep valuation discount: Historically, AT&S has traded at a massive discount compared to its closest Japanese and Taiwanese peers. Deutsche Bank noted that AT&S's target multiples were established at a 50% to 60% discount. This valuation gap was justified by lower historical exposure to high-growth artificial intelligence graphics processing units compared to leaders like Ibiden.
13.
Strategic expansion ambitions: Masterlink Securities noted that Taiwanese competitor Zhen Ding was aggressively implementing a long-term strategy to capture over 10% of the global printed circuit board market. The peer's corporate trajectory included major equipment installations slated to trigger a massive NT$60 billion substrate investment program over four years. This rapid manufacturing build-out was strategically aimed at establishing Zhen Ding as one of the top three substrate suppliers globally.
14.
Other side of the story: While stabilizing fundamentals and broker upgrades suggest a strong turnaround, the company's immense customer concentration leaves it highly vulnerable to unexpected market shocks. The top five customers account for a massive 76% of total revenue. If any of these key customers experience a sudden market share loss or reduce orders, AT&S's expensive manufacturing expansions could quickly become underutilized assets, crushing its projected earnings recovery.
