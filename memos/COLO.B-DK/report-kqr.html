4.
Guidance reset: Coloplast officially revised its long-term financial ambition on November 2, 2026, lowering its organic revenue growth target to 5-7% per year from the previous 7-8% range. The company also adjusted its long-term profitability goal, targeting a flat EBIT margin in the mid-20% range over the period instead of absolute EBIT growth in line with or above revenue. This reset has lowered consensus expectations and increased buy-side scrutiny on near-term EPS growth.
5.
Reimbursement pressure: The guidance reset was primarily triggered by regulatory changes in US outpatient biologics reimbursement that severely impacted Kerecis growth. Bernstein Research noted that Kerecis grew by approximately -3% in fiscal year 2025/26 compared to the initial impairment test expectation of 25%. Controlling Ownership Structure

1.
Stable family control: Coloplast maintains a tightly controlled ownership structure where the founding family and associated holders of class A shares command massive voting power. These holders of non-negotiable class A shares own forty-four percent of the total share capital but control sixty-seven percent of all voting rights in the company. This concentration of voting power among core family interests means that external public investors have limited ability to influence corporate strategy.
2.
Shifted voting control: Coloplast completed a significant transition in its ultimate controlling framework at the start of the current financial year to secure long-term stability. The company's remuneration report reveals that controlling ownership transferred from NPLH Holding ApS to the Coloplast Fonden as of October 1, 2026. This foundational change consolidates long-term stewardship over the voting shares under a dedicated foundation structure.
3.
Strong institutional core: Coloplast Fonden exerts direct authority over the organization's corporate governance through its structured holding entities. The company's latest annual report shows that Coloplast Fonden, operating through CF Holding af 2026 ApS, indirectly controls fifty-one point thirty-six percent of the voting rights. This framework, combined with joint capital ownership alongside the Aage og Johanne Louis-Hansens Fond, ensures that key strategic decisions remain stable.
4.
Minimal treasury influence: Coloplast maintains a small pool of shares in its treasury that does not impact voting outcomes. The parent company financial statements state that the firm held two million eight hundred thirtyone thousand eight hundred sixty-two class B shares as treasury stock on September 30, 2026. These treasury holdings represent one percent of share capital and carry zero voting rights.

Section 4 – US Medicare Competitive Bidding and Regulatory Dynamics

Regulatory Implementation and Legislative Delay

1.
Heightened regulatory concern: Coloplast investors are actively monitoring the US Medicare competitive bidding program scheduled for implementation on January 1, 2028. This program represents a significant pricing headwind because Medicare seeks to address excess chronic care spending. Consequently, the upcoming bidding process introduces substantial long-term risk to intimate healthcare pricing. Management has already incorporated the anticipated impact of these pricing reforms into its rebased 2030 financial ambitions.
2.
Bipartisan delay legislation: The US Congress introduced the bipartisan Access to Medical Supplies Act, known as H.R. 10787, on October 9, 2026, to address supply risks. This legislative proposal seeks to establish a five-year competitive bidding pause specifically for ostomy and urological supplies. Lobbying efforts have intensified in recent months as the bill remains at an early stage. However, the ultimate passage of the bill remains highly uncertain because the legislation is still in its earliest parliamentary stages.
3.
Cautious baseline retention: Financial analysts maintain a cautious baseline model that assumes the bidding program will proceed as planned on January 1, 2028. DNB Carnegie research on October 22, 2026, incorporated the competitive bidding impact directly into its long-term financial models. While a legislative postponement would provide an incremental positive catalyst, the base case remains implementation from January 2028. Therefore, the strategic planning period for 2026/27 and 2027/28 remains clouded by this regulatory shadow.
4.
Standardized billing timelines: The formal administrative process for the competitive bidding program is scheduled to accelerate rapidly over the next 12 months. The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services will open bidder registration on December 1, 2026, to initiate the process. Subsequently, the official bidding window will run from January 5 through March 10, 2027. This structured timeline means that winning suppliers and final contract payment rates will be announced by early autumn of 2027.

Financial Exposure and Pricing Scenarios

5.
Manageable corporate exposure: Coloplast possesses a relatively insulated financial position compared to its direct peers because of its manufacturing focus. DNB Carnegie estimated on October 22, 2026, that approximately six percent of Coloplast group revenue is exposed to US Medicare reimbursement. This exposure is split between 5% through external independent distributors and 1% through Comfort Medical. By comparison, peer Convatec faces a larger direct threat because 9% of its total revenue is exposed.
6.
Moderate financial headwinds: Quantitative scenario analysis indicates that the potential impact on Coloplast's group earnings is relatively contained under base assumptions. DNB Carnegie projected on October 22, 2026, that a 20% reimbursement cut would create a zero point four percent revenue headwind for Coloplast. This pricing pressure would translate to a 30 basis point EBIT margin drag on a full-year basis. Consequently, the base-case financial damage appears highly manageable for a scaled manufacturer.
7.
Severe downside risks: Extreme scenario modeling highlights that a wider pricing deterioration remains possible if competitive bidding proves exceptionally aggressive. DNB Carnegie outlined a bear-case scenario on October 22, 2026, assuming a thirty percent Medicare reimbursement cut. Under these severe conditions, Coloplast would face a 15% wholesale average selling price reduction. This would ultimately expand the full-year group revenue headwind to 1.2% and drag EBIT margins by 85 basis points.
8.
Mitigating volume gains: Coloplast plans to utilize its direct catheter distribution business to offset a portion of the expected pricing pressure. Comfort Medical currently maintains an estimated five to ten percent share of the US catheter distribution market. Management expects Comfort Medical can capture market share from smaller distributors who fail to secure Medicare contracts. If successful, these volume gains will help spread fixed operational costs across a larger patient base.
