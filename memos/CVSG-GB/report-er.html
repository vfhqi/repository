Company overview – established platform: CVS Group operates as a leading veterinary services provider in the United Kingdom and Australia, offering clinical care across its companion, equine, and farm animal practices. Section 4 – Financial delivery

Section summary
  1. CVS Group met full-year earnings consensus but delivered softer organic growth in the second half, offset by capital-intensive acquisitions in Australia and a rising leverage profile. Reporting period details – FY26 trading update: This review covers the full financial year ended June 30, 2026, which was reported as an unaudited full-year trading update on July 23, 2026.

Top-line growth and organic trends

Revenue delivery – slight consensus miss: Total group revenue for FY26 increased by 5.9% year-on-year to £712.8m, compared to £673.2m in FY25. Consensus deviation – top-line softer: The actual revenue print of £712.8m was slightly below published consensus expectations of £714.2m going into the print. Organic deceleration – H2 growth slowdown: Group like-for-like sales growth for the full year came in at 2.1%, representing an improvement against the flat 0.2% delivered in FY25.

Broker perspective – organic deceleration: Deutsche Bank's Slutzkin noted that the 2.1% full-year like-for-like rate implies a softer organic growth rate of approximately 1.5% in the second half of the year compared to the 2.7% growth reported in the first half. Broker perspective – consumer headwinds: Jefferies analyst Vane-Tempest calculated that like-for-like growth in the UK Companion Animal division slowed to 1.4% in the second half, heavily impacted by weak UK consumer confidence and weather-related routine appointment cancellations.

Profitability and margins

Adjusted EBITDA – in line with expectations: The group expects to report an adjusted EBITDA of approximately £141.5m for FY26, representing a 5.1% increase over the £134.6m delivered in the prior year. Consensus deviation – earnings hit the target: The estimated £141.5m adjusted EBITDA was exactly in line with the company-compiled market consensus average of £141.6m, which was constructed from ten analyst estimates ranging from £140.4m to £142.3m.

EBITDA margin – guidance range defended: The group's adjusted EBITDA margin was maintained at 19.9% for the full year, representing a tiny decline of 10 basis points compared to the 20.0% margin achieved in FY25. Operational leverage – cost mitigation: Management successfully defended margins near the lower end of its 19% to 23% medium-term guidance range, offsetting substantial UK minimum wage increases and higher Employer National Insurance contributions.

Cash generation, leverage, and balance sheet

Balance sheet leverage – significant increase: Net debt increased to £199.6m as of June 30, 2026, representing a substantial increase of £68.2m compared to £131.4m at the end of FY25. Leverage ratio – pushing toward ceiling: The group's leverage ratio rose to 1.63x as of June 30, 2026, up from 1.18x in June 2025, driven by the ongoing £50m share buyback program and Australian practice acquisitions. Capital expenditure – disciplined spending: CVS Group invested £36.4m in capital expenditure in FY26, representing approximately 5.0% of revenue, down from the £43.1m spent in FY24.

Cash conversion – target reiterated: While full-year audited operating cash conversion is pending, the first half of the year delivered 75.0%, which is well ahead of the group's long-term capital markets day target of greater than 70%.

Segmental delivery

Geographical shift – Australia scaling rapidly: Revenue from Australian operations grew by £27.0m to reach £79.1m in FY26 compared to £52.1m in FY25, now representing approximately 11% of total group sales and 16% of group EBITDA before central overheads.
