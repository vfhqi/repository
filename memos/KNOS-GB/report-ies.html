Kainos (KNOS-GB): what the H1 FY2027 results, and the response to them, do to the existing case

These results and the response to them weaken the case modestly: the case was written on these results and already holds the margin squeeze as its own mechanism, but the same evidence undercuts the one part of the margin recovery the case called near-mechanical, the sell-side has given the margin no sponsor, and the shares have since fallen to where the case's own flat-margin path takes them.

What changed

The case already takes these results in. The evidence is set out here so that this piece stands alone, with what has moved since the case was written marked at the end.

  • Six months to 30 September 2026, reported 9 November 2026: revenue £268.8m, up 37% from £196.1m, against Deutsche Bank's £256.1m. Adjusted pre-tax profit £41.7m, up 30% from £32.0m, against £39.5m expected. Adjusted diluted EPS 27.4p, up 45%. The case treats revenue as known and in consensus; this is why.
  • Group gross margin 43.1%, from 48.0%. Adjusted pre-tax margin 15.5%, from 16.3%. Digital Services revenue £162.5m, up 57%, at a 31.9% gross margin from 35.8%. Delivery contractors in Digital Services 316, from 139; subcontractors 8% of headcount, from 4%. Revenue delivered through partners and contractors £49.2m, from £15.1m, at gross margins of 9.6% (partners) and 24.9% (contractors) against 39% on the company's own staff. Management's rule: contractor levels stay high while revenue growth is above 20%. The case holds all of this, including the rule, which it has made the mechanism of its margin recovery.
  • Workday Products revenue £48.0m, up 22%; ARR £96.0m, up 24%, with £100m expected in December 2026 and a new target of £200m by 2030. Gross margin 74.3%, from 77.7%, on loss-making installations of the newer products. Research and development spending up 33% to £11.4m. Only four European countries enacted the Pay Transparency Directive on 11 June; management now puts that opportunity into calendar 2028.
  • Workday Services revenue £58.3m, up 9%; utilisation 73%, from 75%; fixed-fee work 43% of divisional revenue, from 34%; thirteen contracts above $1m, from seven.
  • Backlog £853m, up 26%, with 80% scheduled for delivery over two years. Headcount 3,885, up 24%, of which permanent 3,569, up 19%; retention 91%. Cash and deposits £86.5m, from £105.5m; cash conversion 62%, from 48%. Interim dividend 10.4p, up 6%. The buyback was not renewed after £40m returned since September 2025; a further £19.4m goes into the Belfast headquarters in the second half and £12.9m in FY2028.
  • The response. The shares fell 11.8% to 1,084p on 9 November. Deutsche Bank, the only broker whose post-results note has been retrieved (nothing from Stifel, Jefferies, Barclays, Shore Capital or Canaccord), kept Hold and a 1,260p target, raised revenue 2% to 3% for FY2027 to FY2029, left adjusted pre-tax profit unchanged with margins at 15.2% to 15.4%, called the fall unduly harsh, and said further re-rating requires evidence of margin stabilisation. It puts the shares on 19x earnings and 13x calendar 2027 EV/EBIT at 1,084p, and justifies the premium over peers by Kainos having one of the best services margins globally.
  • Since the case was written at 1,090p, the shares have slipped to 1,067p (18 November 2026), below the 50-day average of 1,224p and 13% above a rising 200-day average of 944p. The dashboard's pinned letters have moved: technical momentum C, where the case recorded A; sell-side earnings momentum A, where the case recorded C; valuation A, on a P/E of 16.3x at the 13th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range. These are given as context, not graded here.

The challenge

First, the readings put up on the morning of the results, tested against the case as it now stands and the evidence now in hand.

The reading that growth and margin recovery had become alternatives rather than companions is no longer a challenge: the case has absorbed it whole and made it the thesis, with Digital Services growth falling below 20% in H1 FY2028 as the trigger that releases the margin. Its remaining unheld variant, that growth stays above 20% through FY2028 because an £853m backlog with 80% deliverable within two years and over £190m of pending awards keep arriving, is probable rather than possible, but the case prices it as capped upside and its FY2028 margin step does not depend on contractor release. We drop it.

The reading that the business had changed shape rather than merely delayed its margin is half absorbed. The case holds the three delivery margins, classes 40% of the partner increase as permanent, and never returns the margin to FY2024's 20.2%. The half it does not hold is what the new shape does to the multiple, and that half has gained force. It is the second reading below.

The reading that the software mix was running backwards has lost force as a margin argument: the case answers it on its own arithmetic, with Products' contribution margin only five points above Digital Services', so that mix barely moves group margin in either direction. What survives of it is a different and stronger point about Products' cost base. It is the first reading below.

The reading that the fall was simply the case's entry point arriving referred to thresholds the case no longer states. What the case states now is a price map, and the price is set against it at the end of the next part.

Two readings remain that the case does not hold.

1. Products' cost base is outrunning its revenue, and that removes the part of the margin recovery the case called near-mechanical. The case leans on FY2028 lightly: most of that year's margin gain comes from Workday's fixed annual fee spread over 18% more Products revenue and from central costs growing slower than revenue, with contractor release contributing under £1m. It states the condition: this holds as long as Products' research and sales spending grow no faster than its revenue. In the half the case was written on, research and development spending rose 33% against Products revenue growth of 22%; a divisional chief executive and leaders for product, marketing and customer success have been hired; the newer products are being installed at a loss; and management has raised its ambition to £200m of ARR by 2030, which is a statement of intent to invest, not to harvest. A fee of about £7.8m a year spread over 18% more revenue is worth about a point of divisional margin; research spending of about £23m a year growing eleven points faster than revenue takes about two points back (our arithmetic, treating the spending as expensed and as Products'). The case's downside for Products assumes slower growth and a stuck margin from FY2029 and costs 6% of FY2029 EPS. It does not contemplate FY2028's step failing, nor the half of the four-year margin gain that it attributes to Products outgrowing its own costs failing with it. What would have to be true: that management funds the £200m target through FY2028 at the rate it funded this half, which is probable given the hires already made and the target just raised; and that the Pay Transparency delay holds Products growth in the low twenties or below while the spending runs ahead. If both hold, the case's FY2028 gap to consensus closes to nil and the margin case rests entirely on contractors from FY2029.

2. The premium multiple was paid for the margin, and the margin is now ordinary. The case holds that the market's premium over peers, 17.4x against about 13x on two-year-forward earnings, is earned by returns, cash and growth, and sets its floor in a sell-off at 14.5x to 15.4x, the stock's own five-year tenth percentile. The one broker to respond says the premium is supported by Kainos having one of the best services margins globally, and values the services business at 14x EV/EBIT against 10x for global peers on that basis. That justification has gone: the group's EBITDA margin sits at the 29th percentile of the universe on the case's own rating, the adjusted pre-tax margin is 15.5% and falling, and the broker now holds it flat for three years. The market's behaviour supports this reading over the case's: it took 12% off the shares on a profit beat, so it sold the margin and not the earnings, and it has slipped further since. The case prices its flat-margin path at 15x to 16x on forward EPS of about 71p, giving 1,060p to 1,130p; at the peers' 13x on the same earnings the path is about 920p, a return of about -10% with the dividends, rather than the case's +1% to +8%. The case's own observation that nine of sixteen peers sit in the bottom 15% of their ten-year ranges cuts the same way: own-range cheapness is a sector condition, and Kainos's own 13th percentile is no longer a Kainos-specific anchor. What would have to be true: that the margin stays flat through May 2027 and November 2027, which the only published forecast assumes and the case gives one chance in four; and that the market anchors to peers rather than to Kainos's history while it does, which is what a market that has stopped forecasting the margin would plausibly do. We cannot knock this down on the evidence. The strongest reply is the case's own: returns on equity above 40%, net cash and 14% EPS growth are not peer-like. But the market has just shown it is not paying for those while the margin falls.

A third reading, that the UK fiscal tail is a cancellation and not a deferral, is raised by the Public Interest Test now applied to central government service contracts above £1m and by a stated plan to halve consultancy spend. The case holds the tail as a one-year deferral. We do not put this up as a challenge, because the case's defence is strong and the evidence does not yet contradict it: digital programmes are how departments deliver savings, every past pause has deferred rather than cancelled, and the sovereign and onshore requirements the review describes favour Kainos. It belongs among the open questions.

What it does to the case

The letters of record are the Rater's: the four Master Ratings at C, C, C, C; the memo's own letters were B, B, C, C.

  • 'Forecast financials' (topic 6), B. Under more pressure. The first reading puts the FY2028 margin step the case called near-mechanical in doubt, and the condition the case attached to it was already failing in the half it was written on. The EPS growth that sets the grade, about 14% a year, would fall toward 12% if half the margin gain went; that is the bottom of the band, not out of it.
  • 'Internally driven change' (topic 2), C, and Master Rating 1, 'Thesis's Change Forces' Strength', C. The same. The rating already says the forces have reached revenue and not profit; nothing in the response changes that, and the staffing lever is unchanged.
  • 'What turns revenue growth into profit growth' (topic 5), D. More pressure from the first reading: the case's second route to operating leverage, Products outgrowing its own costs, is running the wrong way. Already at D.
  • 'Scope for upgrades' (topic 7), D. More pressure. One house has responded, it holds the margin flat for three years, and it has said re-rating waits on margin evidence. The case's "sell-side will follow and will not lead" is confirmed, and there is no one yet to follow.
  • 'Current momentum' (topic 8), B. More pressure, and this is the rating most likely to move a grade. The case placed it at the floor of B with the 50-day average already lost and the price a fifth off its high. Since then the shares have fallen further, the technical momentum letter has moved from A to C, and the sell-side letter has moved to A on revisions the case itself describes as sales-led. The case's stalling marker, a fall through the rising 200-day average without company news, has not been met: the price is 13% above it.
  • 'Riskiness' (topic 9), C, and Master Rating 4, 'Case Riskiness', C. More pressure on the multiple tail from the second reading: the case's sell-off floor of 14.5x sits above where peers trade. The body of the cone is unchanged. Not a grade.
  • 'The fair multiple' (topic 11), C, and 'Total shareholder return' (topic 12), C. Pulled both ways, and roughly cancelling. The lower price lifts the return on every path the case draws; the second reading takes the flat-margin path from +1% to +8% to about -10%. Weighted on the case's probabilities, the 18-month return stays near the case's +13% (our arithmetic). The dispersion is wider; the centre is not lower.
  • Master Rating 3, 'Setups' Fit/Crispness', C. Slightly less pressure. The market has now named margin dilution as its reason for selling, which is the concern a 'CfC Clearing' setup needs to have formed; the straddle the rating describes is resolving toward that fit.
  • Master Rating 2, 'Foundations' Robustness', C. The same. Nothing in the response bears on returns, cash, retention or alignment.
  • Price discipline. 1,067p sits inside the band the case gives its flat-margin path at May 2028, 1,060p to 1,130p. On the case's own arithmetic the market is pricing the flat margin now, and the return from here on that path is the dividend. The price is a third above the case's pause price of about 800p and 20% to 25% below its delivered path of 1,340p to 1,430p. The case said the skew becomes unusually positive at a price that already assumes a pause; this price does not.

What would settle it

The questions left open on the morning of the results, and what is now known:

  • Whether FY2028 EPS consensus holds. The broader sell-side response has not been retrieved. The case records consensus FY2028 EPS at 59.4p on 13 November, the brokers' range at 56.4p to 62.0p, and FY2028 revenue already at the model's £607m. Not settled by this review; the case's own record is the answer for now.
  • Whether anyone models an FY2028 margin above 15.4%. The only published forecast does not, and the case records the implied consensus FY2028 EBITDA margin flat at 15.9%. Not settled; probably no.
  • Whether targets have come down toward the price. Deutsche Bank's 1,260p has not; the case records the median at 1,260p and slipping. Not settled.
  • Whether contractors are falling. Nothing before May 2027 can show it.

What settles the two readings, and when:

  • December 2026. ARR passing £100m. It confirms Products' revenue line and says nothing about its cost base, which is the first reading's subject.
  • Around 20 April 2027, the year-end update. A few sentences. "In line" is expected and tells us nothing; "ahead" would be the earliest sign that the contractor count has peaked.
  • Mid to late May 2027, FY2027 results. The first hard evidence. For the staffing lever: contractors at 31 March 2027 (the case's markers are a visible peak for the better and above 350 for the worse; the broker's are below 5% of headcount and a Digital Services gross margin above 35%), the second-half adjusted pre-tax margin (below 14.5% would lead the case to cut FY2028), and FY2028 guidance. For the first reading: Products' gross margin against the case's 75.5% recovery marker, and Products' research and sales spending against its revenue. The company reports research and development spending and divisional gross margins; it has not committed to a divisional cost base or contribution margin, so the first reading may have to be inferred from Products' gross profit growth against reported research spend.
  • The second reading is settled by the market, not the company. If the margin is flat in May 2027 and the shares hold the case's 14.5x floor, the case's anchor is right; if they trade toward peers at about 13x, the premium was the margin. The one broker to publish has set margin stabilisation as the condition for its own re-rating, so the earliest the premium can be re-earned is May 2027 and more probably November 2027, when the first half in which the 20% trigger is met is reported.
  • Open questions nothing yet promises to answer. The staff-type margin split that separates in-house from partner margin was given this time; there is no commitment that it is repeated, and without it the structural and temporary parts of the dilution cannot be told apart. Management has put no date and no number on the margin recovery, only the 20% growth condition; the case gives a stated margin framework one chance in four within eighteen months. Whether the Home Office (£111m) and FCDO (£84m) awards the case counts are signed by 31 March 2027 would probably be announced; the Public Interest Test's outcomes on Kainos's own frameworks would probably not. The Pay Transparency Directive's enactment across member states runs through 2027 and is outside anyone's control here.
