The split is on the return's own basis: the two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple and two-year-forward earnings per share, today against each horizon. The multiple and earnings parts multiply (1 + one, times 1 + the other, less 1, is the share price's change); dividends add to that.

The operating drivers behind the inputs

Revenue - Revenue is mostly pass-through of wholesale power, fuel and CO2. - FY2026 (+6.7%): H1 +5.6% actual (Q1 +1.7%, Q2 +10.6%). H2 lifted by the gas-price shock and higher wholesale prices. - FY2027 (+3%): the January 2027 household tariff rise; 2027 base forward PLN 590/MWh; Baltica 2 first power. Partly offset by business customer losses (-5% to -10% a year) and lower coal output. - FY2028-FY2029 (-0.5%, -3%): wholesale prices normalise; coal output falls (Dolna Odra coal units close 2026; Rybnik 2027-2028; solar 25.6 GW); Baltica 2 adds about PLN 1.4bn a year of revenue at full output. - FY2030 (+1.5%): indexation and new assets.

EBITDA: two clocks - Regulated and contracted, rising: - distribution grows about 4% a year: asset base +7% to 8% a year; allowed return falls only gently from 12.96% in 2025 because the 2026 rise in Polish ten-year yields (about 6.4% in October 2026) feeds the 18-month formula; - Baltica 2 from 2027: first power H1 2027, full operation end-2027, about 3 TWh a year PGE share at about PLN 470 to 490/MWh indexed; - Rybnik CCGT capacity payments of about PLN 300m a year from 2027; - Żarnowiec battery from Q2 2027; - gas peakers of about PLN 0.5bn a year from around 2030. - Coal, falling: - 2026 is lifted by the gas shock; - the 2027 supplementary capacity payment (1,859 MW, about PLN 600m) lapses after 2027; - the five-year coal capacity contracts end in 2028; - from 2029 coal is negative. I assume about PLN 0.5bn a year of replacement support. Management assumes minus PLN 3.3bn in 2030, Bernstein minus PLN 1.8bn. - Lignite costs are largely fixed, so they do not fall with volume. - Supply: improves slowly as the household tariff moves towards cost, but stays below cost through the 2027 election. - Policy scenarios are not in the base: a windfall levy of PLN 0.5bn to 1.5bn a year in 2027-2028; a wider household tariff gap; a higher energy-sector tax.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
