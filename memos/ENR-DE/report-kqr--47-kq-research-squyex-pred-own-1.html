Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding contract liabilities is vital because prepayment unwinding directly dictates Siemens Energy's cash flow, liquidity runway, and sum-of-the-parts equity valuation.
1.
Verbatim key question: "What is the composition of Siemens Energy's contract liabilities (€22.3bn at Q3 FY2026) and net working capital by segment, how much is customer advance payments, reservation fees and milestone billing, how are these expected to unwind as the gas turbines booked in FY2025 to FY2026 are delivered, and what did management say at the FY2026 results on 11 November 2026 about cash conversion and working capital for FY2027 to FY2028?"

Financial mechanisms and valuation impacts

2.
Significant valuation impact: The massive accumulation of customer prepayments drives Siemens Energy's net cash position, but these advance payments represent a temporary operating liquidity buffer rather than structural cash. Morgan Stanley highlights that a bearish reverse stress-test involving a complete unwind of these prepayments would trigger a €16.2bn cash outflow, dragging the share price down to €108. This means that tracking working capital is crucial for investors to assess whether the group's current valuation premium can be sustained as contract liabilities are executed.
3.
Direct earnings impact: The rate at which contract liabilities unwind directly affects reported margins and earnings per share through percentage-of-completion accounting dynamics. As Siemens Energy executes its backlog, the unwinding of customer prepayments triggers revenue recognition while drawing down raw inventories. If execution delays or quality issues occur during delivery, provisions for onerous contracts can escalate rapidly, severely diluting EPS.

Rebranding and segment restructuring

4.
Corporate rebranding transition: Siemens Energy is currently preparing to transition to an independent brand named Omterra, which will eventually unite all business units under a single identity. CEO Christian Bruch stated in July 2026 that strategic and financial independence makes it the right time to retire the licensed Siemens brand as the agreement approaches its expiration. This rebranding to Omterra will gradually occur over an estimated eighteen-month transitional phase, severing the group's final legacy connection with Siemens AG.
5.
Strategic segment separation: The legal and operational separation of the Transformation of Industry business is another closely related strategic milestone currently underway. Siemens Energy plans to carve out this industrial energy solutions segment to establish it as a standalone entity, with deconsolidation expected by mid-2027. Deutsche Bank notes that this divestment will simplify the reporting structure and mechanically improve group margins by at least fifty basis points.
6.
Other side of the story: While management boasts that its massive backlog and advance billing mitigate execution risk, critics argue that such heavy reliance on customer deposits masks structural cash flow weaknesses. A former CFO of GE Aviation warns that power equipment firms struggle to generate structural cash flow over the long term, making high advance payments a double-edged sword. If orders slow down or projects face delays, these contract liabilities will rapidly unwind into massive cash outflows, severely threatening Siemens Energy's liquidity.

Section 2 – Segmental Composition of Contract Liabilities and Working Capital

Section summary
  1. Siemens Energy's segments exhibit highly divergent balance sheet structures due to varying prepayment intensities.
  2. Highly negative segment assets in gas and grid highlight their massive customer-funded cash buffers.

Segment assets and negative asset structure

1.
Negative segment assets: Siemens Energy's Gas Services segment reported negative segment assets of €2,635 million at June 30, 2026, compared to positive assets of €1,083 million at September 30, 2025. This negative figure stems from the segment's massive customer prepayment and reservation fee balances that are netted against gross assets under the group's accounting policies. The rapid accumulation of these customer advance payments significantly strengthens the segment's operating cash flow.
2.
Rising prepayment buffers: Siemens Energy's Grid Technologies segment recorded negative segment assets of €1,940 million at June 30, 2026, deepening from negative €386 million at September 30, 2025. Company management confirmed that this shift was primarily driven by higher project prepayments on transmission and substation contracts. These pre-funded orders secure the segment's liquidity runway and mitigate the risks of executing multi-year grid expansion projects.
3.
Stable asset footprints: Siemens Energy's Transformation of Industry segment maintained a positive asset footprint of €1,905 million at June 30, 2026, compared with €1,689 million at September 30, 2025. Unlike Gas Services, this business area experiences lower advance payment funding levels and higher working capital outflows. Deconsolidation of this standalone unit, expected by mid-2027, will eliminate these cash flow drag effects from the consolidated group balance sheet.
4.
Operational cash drains: Siemens Energy's wind turbine segment, Siemens Gamesa, reported positive assets of €751 million at June 30, 2026, recovering from negative €1,236 million at September 30, 2025. The segment's historical cash flow has been severely handicapped by massive cash outflows from quality mitigation programs. Continued provisions for turbine repairs have dragged down net working capital and hindered the segment's return to sustainable cash generation.

Consolidated contract liabilities and working capital cash flows

5.
Massive liability expansion: Siemens Energy's consolidated contract liabilities reached €28,071 million at Q3 FY2026, up from €22,321 million at the end of FY2025. The group's quarterly interim report shows that operating net working capital was boosted by a €5,359 million cash inflow from contract liabilities during the first nine months. This cash inflow reflects the strong customer down payments and reservation fees received on gas turbine and grid projects.
6.
Growing inventory requirements: Siemens Energy's positive contract liability cash inflow was partially offset by a €1,335 million inventory build-up during Q1 to Q3 FY2026. At the same time, contract assets consumed €1,469 million of operating cash as project execution advanced ahead of billing milestones. This inventory build-up is necessary to support the group's ramp-up in deliveries, which means cash conversion will depend on timely milestone achievements.
7.
Excepting cash generation: Siemens Energy recorded a pre-tax free cash flow of €7,163 million for the first nine months of FY2026. This performance was led by Gas Services, which generated €5,473 million in pre-tax cash, while Grid Technologies contributed €3,471 million. These strong segmental inflows were offset by Siemens Gamesa's pre-tax cash drain of €1,717 million.

Section 3 – Analysis of Customer Advances, Reservation Fees and Milestone Billing

Section summary
  1. Customer prepayments and structured slot reservation fees provide robust cash buffers and mitigate execution risks.
  2. Commercial down-payment structures are highly protective but expose suppliers to future delivery execution obligations.

Customer advances and down payments

1.
Prepayment buffer role: Siemens Energy's contract liabilities are heavily supported by upfront customer advance payments that secure production slots. In the Q4 FY2026 preclose call, management stated that these advance payments and reservation fees provide robust economic protection against cancellations. This commercial framework ensures that the company's operating cash flow remains protected even during periods of macro-market volatility. For the group's earnings per share, these prepayments act as a critical operating liquidity buffer across cyclical turns.
2.
Gas turbine contract dynamics: Siemens Energy has successfully utilized prepayment requirements to fund raw material procurement and engineering work before manufacturing starts. In the Q4 FY2026 pre-close call, the company confirmed that customer commitments, including reservation agreements, reached 95 gigawatts as of Q3 FY2026. This massive volume of commitments provides strong multi-year revenue visibility for the Gas Services segment. Timely execution of these contracts will determine the future trajectory of earnings per share growth.
3.
Comparative down payments: GE Vernova enforces highly rigorous down-payment terms on its gas turbine orders to secure its backlog. Bernstein's note of 23 July 2026 indicates that GE Vernova receives 15-20% down payments on SRAs to drive strong cash generation. These down payments represent a substantial cash windfall that improves working capital efficiency. Consequently, this downpayment strategy provides a massive operational cash buffer that supports its capital-light capacity expansion.
4.
Strategic grid customer terms: Grid developers are increasingly offering more flexible prepayment structures to secure critical high-voltage transmission equipment. A renewable energy developer manager explained that strategic utility customers are trading better terms for forecast visibility and long-term volume commitments. These commercial negotiations focus heavily on capacity reservation and cancellation terms rather than price discounting. This structural flexibility helps Siemens Energy protect its backlogged earnings from project delays.

Slot reservation agreements and fees

5.
Structured slot reservations: Siemens Energy utilizes slot reservation agreements (SRAs) as formal commercial pathways to firm orders rather than speculative options. In the Q4 FY2026 pre-close call, management clarified that slot reservations convert into firm orders within six to twelve months. This conversion mechanism protects the factory queue and ensures optimal capacity utilization. For investors, the steady conversion of these agreements secures long-term service revenue streams.
6.
Premium reservation pricing: Customer slot reservations often command premium pricing due to the extreme supply constraints in the gas turbine market. A former Head of Growth at Siemens Energy noted that slot reservation fees are common for expedited deliveries, often representing a five to ten percent premium. These fees allow large utilities to secure manufacturing slots amid extended three-to-five-year lead times. This pricing leverage suggests that new gas turbine bookings carry superior margin profiles.
7.
Competitor SRA structures: GE Vernova's commercial backlog shows an even higher concentration of slot reservations compared to Siemens Energy. Morgan Stanley's report of 21 August 2026 highlights that 56% of GE Vernova's commitments are slot reservations, whereas Siemens Energy's share is only 31%. This means GE Vernova has built more front-end customer cash before formal order booking. For Siemens Energy, focusing on rapid SRA-to-order conversion reduces the risk of cancellation.
8.
Data center prepayment trends: Hyperscalers and data center operators are increasingly willing to use their massive balance sheets to fund upfront deposits. Morgan Stanley's note of 15 October 2026 indicates that order deposits can reach 20- 40% of the total purchase price for equipment delivered five years ahead. This trend benefits major power equipment suppliers by front-loading their operating cash inflows. This suggests that data center demand provides highly favorable working capital terms.
9.
Utility versus data center bookings: Siemens Energy's gas turbine backlog remains highly insulated from potential data center sector financing issues. Morgan Stanley's note of 26 October 2026 argues that 76% of this backlog is allocated to stable traditional utility customers. This high share of utility contracts provides exceptional protection even if hyperscalers delay their project build-outs. Consequently, the group's near-term earnings remain highly protected against data center execution challenges.

Milestone billing and risk mitigation

10.
Milestone billing protection: Multi-year transmission and substation contracts rely on structured milestone billing to mitigate execution and commodity price risks. A Channel Expert at an energy transition advisory firm noted that T&D equipment contracts have shifted toward milestone-based escalation and upfront capacity reservation payments. This structure passes commodity and scheduling risk directly to the customer. Consequently, milestone billing helps protect Siemens Energy's margins against inflation.
11.
Wind turbine execution risks: The wind segment exhibits a vastly different risk profile where upfront advances fail to fully insulate the supplier. A former VP at Siemens Energy pointed out that the wind business has high execution risk and low initial profit margins. Technical reliability issues and delayed deliveries can quickly consume the initial prepayment buffer. This means wind segment working capital remains highly vulnerable to quality mitigation program costs.
12.
Contrary evidence: Not all major equipment manufacturers enjoy stable working capital structures, as shown by Alstom's severe seasonal cash outflows. Deutsche Bank's report of 6 October 2026 points out that Alstom's FCF is highly negative in the first half due to unfavorable timing of down payments. This indicates that milestone-based billing can create massive cash flow volatility if project execution is delayed. Therefore, prepayment-driven working capital models are vulnerable to sudden execution bottlenecks.
13.
Peer milestone comparisons: Other international competitors also leverage strong prepayments to secure their balance sheets. Mitsubishi Heavy Industries reported that contract liabilities rose significantly to ¥2,483,221 million as of June 30, 2026. This cash collection has driven a massive expansion in its cash balances and net cash position. This peer evidence confirms that the global power equipment market is highly supply-constrained.
14.
Backlog cash generation: Strong prepayment terms help power equipment OEMs fund their necessary capacity expansions without relying on external debt. Bernstein's note of 14 May 2026 explains that Siemens Energy leverages stellar order growth to secure attractive down payments. These down payments generate robust free cash flow that supports capital allocation. This cash generation provides a buffer that protects the group's EPS runway.
15.
Other side of the story: While upfront customer advances and reservation fees strengthen Siemens Energy's near-term liquidity, they represent a significant liability that will unwind during turbine delivery. Morgan Stanley's report of 16 October 2026 warns that a complete reversal of prepayments would result in a €16.2bn operating cash outflow. If order momentum declines, the group's balance sheet will face severe working capital pressure as obligations are met. This makes the current cash buffer highly contingent on continuous order intake.

Section 4 – Unwinding of FY2025-FY2026 Gas Turbine Bookings and Cash Flow

Section summary
  1. Multi-year gas turbine deliveries will drive revenue and cash conversion starting in fiscal year 2027 as capacity expands.
  2. Our overall confidence in this unwinding timeline is high due to clear delivery visibility extending past 2030.

Impact

Delivery schedules and capacity expansion

1.
Accelerated delivery timeline: Siemens Energy is prepared to dramatically accelerate its gas turbine delivery schedule to convert its massive order backlog. The company plans to increase its deliveries of large gas turbines to 50 units in fiscal year 2027 compared to 35 units previously. This capacity ramp-up will be supported by the restart of F-Class assembly operations in Charlotte to optimize manufacturing throughput. - Earnings Calls Timely factory execution is vital because it determines the speed at which upfront customer deposits are recognized as revenue.
2.
Expanding medium turbine capacity: Siemens Energy is also expanding its capacity for medium-sized gas turbines to meet urgent power requirements. The first phase of the Finspång expansion adds 30 additional medium gas turbines to the 50 units produced annually before. This expansion will increase deliveries by more than one gigawatt compared to previous quarters. Faster manufacturing of these medium units will accelerate the unwinding of associated contract liabilities into reported revenue.
3.
Selective customer targeting: Siemens Energy enforces a highly disciplined project-selection process to secure the quality and viability of its backlog. The company focuses on multi-unit deals and works with a limited number of clients to optimize execution. Around 76% of the company's gas turbine backlog is allocated to highly stable traditional utility customers. This customer mix significantly reduces the risk of cancellation compared to speculative developers or volatile hyperscalers.
4.
Extended delivery schedules: The extreme tight supply conditions in the gas turbine market mean that delivery lead times are highly extended. Siemens Energy is currently fully sold out of gas turbine capacity until fiscal year 2028. Customers are rapidly booking the remaining manufacturing slots for fiscal year 2029 and fiscal year 2030. These extended lead times mean that the cash flows from bookings will unwind gradually over many years.

Revenue translation and service flywheel

5.
Gradual revenue recognition: Siemens Energy's revenue recognition from the massive gas turbine backlog follows a structured percentage-of-completion timeline. Out of the year-end fiscal year 2025 backlog, Gas Services is expected to convert approximately €11 billion into revenue in fiscal year 2026. The division plans to convert an additional €9 billion of backlog into revenue during fiscal year 2027. This multi-year execution schedule provides highly visible, stable revenue streams for the group over the next several years.
6.
Deferred service profits: The high-margin benefits of Siemens Energy's massive turbine bookings will experience a multi-year execution lag. Management notes that installing large frames takes 12 to 24 months depending on project complexity. The subsequent commercial warranty phase normally lasts for another 24 months. This means that the full margin benefit of today's bookings will materialize three years after installation.
7.
Profitable service flywheel: The delivery of gas turbines initiates a highly profitable, long-term service relationship with customers. Siemens Energy's long-term service agreements (LTSAs) now have an average duration of 17 years. The company estimates that the lifetime service opportunity is approximately €400 million per gigawatt. This high-margin service revenue will gradually materialize over the next two decades as turbine delivery, installation, and warranty phases conclude.
8.
Comparable peer cash conversion: Competitor GE Vernova also experiences a strong cash inflection from upfront deposits and slot reservation agreements. GE Vernova enforces down payments representing approximately 20% of turbine selling prices to support robust cash generation. The peer maintains a massive cash balance of over $13 billion to fund its ongoing capacity expansion. This strong customer funding across competitors confirms that the global power equipment sector is highly supply-constrained.
9.
Heavy peer prepayment trends: Mitsubishi Heavy Industries also utilizes a prepayment-heavy commercial model to fund its power equipment manufacturing. The peer reported a substantial expansion in its contract liabilities to ¥2,483,221 million at June 30, 2026. This rising balance has successfully driven a massive increase in the peer's consolidated cash reserves. These peer trends show that global equipment manufacturing relies heavily on customer financing.

Precedent turbine cycles and operational risks

10.
Historical integration setbacks: Historical precedents show that rapid turbine booking expansions can lead to severe structural and financial challenges. General Electric's acquisition of Alstom in 2015 nearly doubled its turbine installed base but triggered massive overcapacity. Subsequent structural challenges in fossil power generation caused global sales of large turbines to decline drastically. This overcapacity and poor execution severely impacted profitability and forced extensive restructuring of GE's power division.
11.
Unfavorable wind dynamics: The execution of renewable turbine backlogs typically carries significantly higher risk than gas turbine segments. Vestas Wind Systems managed contract liabilities of €9,813 million at March 31, 2026 to fund working capital. However, wind OEMs remain highly vulnerable to supply chain complexities and technical product resets. This high risk means that upfront wind prepayments are often consumed by unexpected warranty provisions.
12.
Long-term pricing pressure: The current tight turbine supply-demand balance faces normalization risks as global capacity continues to expand. Morgan Stanley warns that gas services new equipment margins could decline to 10% by 2035. This margin erosion would be driven by industry overcapacity and price cost turning into a headwind on new orders. Consequently, Siemens Energy must maintain strict capacity discipline to avoid severe long-term profitability dilution.
13.
Contrary evidence: Ambit Capital argues that transmission orders and margins will peak soon, followed by no order growth until fiscal year 2029. The broker expects downward risk to pricing in fiscal year 2027 and fiscal year 2028 as new capacities come online. This competitive headwind could lead to under-utilization and operating deleverage challenges across major segments. Such stagnant order activity would trigger rapid net working capital outflows as existing contract liabilities unwind.

Post-delivery cash conversion and working capital trajectory

14.
Long-term cash generation: Despite normalization risks, Siemens Energy's massive backlog ensures highly resilient operating cash flows over the next decade. The group is projected to generate approximately €10 billion in free cash flow annually between 2031 and 2035. This strong performance is expected even with contract prepayments unwinding at a rate of €1 billion per year. Robust service margins will provide an important counter-balance to the eventual normalization in equipment margins.
15.
Other side of the story: Critics warn that Siemens Energy's reliance on advance payments masks the risks of executing highly complex, long-term turbine installations. If the group faces unexpected technical failures or execution delays during delivery, turbines could suffer margin dilution. Furthermore, a sharp decline in new bookings after 2026 would trigger massive working capital cash outflows. Therefore, the group's highly favorable cash flow profile remains dependent on perfect operational execution.
