#0 Summary of key insights, implications and conclusions

The verdict: Zegona pays a well-protected cash yield of about 10%, but the business underneath is not improving. At 1,746p the shares are fairly priced, and the positive skew lies in decisions by management and its partners, not in estimates that are too low. Hold the shares as a yield anchor; build with conviction only below about 1,500p, or once RANCo (the planned radio-network joint company with MasOrange) is signed.

The seven insights that matter

  1. 1
    Zegona's story has turned from "re-rating" to "yield", and the turn is already priced. The 24 September 2026 capital allocation framework (€400m of returns for FY27, all free cash flow returned over time, and a higher leverage target of 2.25x-2.75x net debt to EBITDAaL) was the catalyst Viewforth's September memo awaited, yet the shares fell on the day. With 79% of thirteen brokers rating Zegona a Buy, the bull case is known and owned.
  2. 2
    Financing, not operations, locks in the next 18 months of cash. Equity free cash flow rises by more than a third in FY28 because the €3.7bn refinancing cut annual interest by €60m and €100m of one-off refinancing fees do not recur. EBITDAaL grows only about 2%, and is roughly flat without a non-cash release of deferred fibre-sale proceeds.
  3. 3
    From FY29, growth rests on a small net of two large cost flows that Zegona does not disclose: about €250m of gross savings from the second cost programme against about €160m of inflation on a €1.5bn indexed cost base.
  4. 4
    Per-share growth will pause in FY29 because of dilution, which the market may misread as an operating slowdown. The Management Incentive Scheme settles in about 26m new shares from 15 October 2027, reversing more than two years of buybacks.
  5. 5
    Consensus is more likely to fall on cash and rise on distributions. Consensus free cash flow for FY28-FY30 sits 7%-10% above our base case, for nameable reasons (tax, interest, working capital, timing of savings). The likeliest upgrade is a bigger buyback funded from the new leverage corridor.
  6. 6
    On cash, Zegona is about fairly valued; the model's headline return of +172% should be set aside. An equity free cash flow yield of about 10% on FY28 already reflects the base case. Our return is about +9% over 18 months and 12%-13% a year over three years: adequate, but short of the roughly 20% a year that marks a compelling case.
  7. 7
    The asymmetry lies in management and partner choices, not in forecasts. The upside sits outside the base case: RANCo (€100m-€150m a year to Zegona), a deeper rent cut from the tower owner Vantage, up to €0.8bn of extra returns from the leverage corridor, and corporate action in 2027-2029. The downside is a correlated shock (Digi escalating on price, churn after the January 2027 price round, a higher Euribor) worth about −25% on the shares.

Implications for action

At 1,746p the 18-month range is balanced: about −25% to +30%, centred on +5% to +10%. This is not an unusually skewed situation worth pressing.
About 1,450p-1,500p makes the case compelling: there the base case alone returns about 20% a year over three years. Likely weak moments: the H1 FY27 results (late November 2026), the January 2027 price round and the October 2027 share issue.
Paying up now needs one of three events: a signed RANCo agreement; an FY28 return above about €450m; a January 2027 price round that lifts revenue without lifting churn.
Three signals would turn the view negative: an H1 FY27 EBITDAaL margin below 36%; fixed broadband lines falling two quarters running; a back-book price rise below 3%.

EBITDAaL is Zegona's own profit measure, EBITDA after lease costs. Equity free cash flow (EFCF) is the cash left for shareholders; the case lives on EFCF per share. The end notes define both.

#1 Operating environment: a mild headwind that has worsened slightly in six months

Judgement: Zegona's external environment constrains the case rather than driving it. Nothing external is lifting demand; Digi's low-cost owned fibre network erodes Zegona's position slowly; and inflation raises Zegona's indexed rents faster than its prices.
The insight most readers miss: inflation is a net negative for Zegona, not a tailwind. Each extra point of Spanish inflation adds about €15m to indexed costs but only about €8m of price that survives customers trading down to Lowi and Digi, a net loss of roughly €7m of EBITDAaL.
Implication for results: organic revenue growth of about 1% a year and no margin help from price. The environment offers no route to a positive operating surprise and some risk of a negative one.

Score: 36. A mature, overbuilt single-country market with a structurally advantaged challenger and indexed rents puts Zegona in the lower half of European listed companies on external tailwinds.

#2 Internal change forces: still the engine, but most of the big change is delivered and priced

Judgement: internal change still drives Zegona and is superbly resourced, but its focus has moved from operations to the capital structure. The large changes are delivered and explain the 37% share price rise over the past year. What remains is smaller, slower and needs partners.

What is still ahead

Most of Zegona's remaining value is optional, and the options need MasOrange, Vantage or a regulator to say yes. Inside our base case, the remaining operating gain is under a tenth of today's EBITDAaL. Outside it, RANCo, a full Vantage cut and the leverage corridor could add €130m-€200m of EBITDAaL and €0.5bn-€0.8bn of returns.

The problems, weighed honestly

  • The first cost programme is spent: EBITDAaL growth fell from +16.3% (Q4 FY26) to +2.5% (Q1 FY27).
  • The incentive scheme is expensive and certain: 15% of value created above a 5% a year hurdle, about 13.5% of enterprise value (top 1% in Europe), mostly to two executives.
  • Leverage is rising by policy just as interest rates rise.
  • Earnings quality is weak: 49% of €504m of receivables are over 180 days old (peers 15%-25%) against a €117m allowance; about €100m a year of non-cash fibre-proceeds release sits inside EBITDAaL.
  • Service is showing early strain: fixed-line complaints rose from 5.34 to 8.36 per 10,000 lines in 2025.
  • Finetwork: Zegona lost control of the smaller operator in court, putting €30m-€40m of fees at risk.
Expect the leverage corridor to be used. The incentive scheme counts distributions as value created, so returns funded by extra debt raise the executives' payout; spare capacity and incentive point the same way.
Implication for results: management's own actions will land on time and operations will track consensus, so positive surprises will be capital actions, not earnings.

Score: 60. Top-quartile breadth and delivery of change, held back because what is left is either already priced or depends on partners.

#3 Industry, advantage, finances and management: a business that defends rather than compounds, run by top-decile allocators

Judgement: Zegona is a mid-market number three in an overbuilt, commoditising market. It has one genuine moat (business services, about a third of revenue), a cash margin flattered partly by accounting choices, and a top-decile capital-allocation team that will extract more cash than the market position deserves for a few more years.

Industry structure: supportive at the top, hostile at the bottom

  • Spain is concentrated but overbuilt, so scarcity never disciplines price: the top four hold over 90% of retail revenue, yet there are 87m fibre lines for 31m subscribers (2.8x, against 1.2x-1.5x in France and Germany).
  • Digi shrinks the revenue pool with every customer it wins: it has 14% of fixed connections but only 6%-7% of revenue, at about half the market's revenue per user.
  • Zegona is "stuck in the middle" (Morgan Stanley) between Telefónica at the premium end and Digi at the low end, renting fibre from ventures its two main rivals control.
  • Portugal shows who pays when Digi arrives: Vodafone Portugal, the number three, lost 14% of EBITDAaL in a half-year while NOS, the number two, gained. Zegona has the same mid-market profile.

#4 Guidance and delivery: formal guidance is low and safe; the €1bn ambition is a stretch

Judgement: Zegona will very likely meet its low formal guidance, while its ambitions above it are more likely to slip quietly than to be raised. The record is excellent but short, and was earned in a new owner's easiest phase. Expect guidance met, little chance of an operating beat, and a good chance of a beat on distributions.
Formal guidance is easy to meet: an operating free cash flow margin above 21% (Q1 FY27 22.3%), €400m returned for FY27, a progressive dividend and leverage of 2.25x-2.75x. Only a 3% revenue shock would breach the margin floor.
The undated ambitions are a genuine stretch: €1bn of operating free cash flow "within two to three years" needs EBITDAaL about €210m above FY26, or 10%-14% growth a year. The last two years' 38% a year came from one-off levers (headcount, selling the network, capital spending cuts) that are now spent.

Zegona is precise where the cash thesis lives and silent where the bear case lives: it upgrades by raising ambitions at set-piece events, but discloses no churn by brand, no bridge from operating to equity free cash flow and no current hedge ratio.

#5 Pricing power, leverage, cost-out and feedback loops: the mechanisms largely cancel

Judgement: Zegona's profit mechanisms largely cancel each other out, so per-share growth comes from financing and buybacks, not operations. Pricing power offsets cost indexation, operating leverage cuts both ways on a flat top line, and cost savings arrive late and net of inflation.

Pricing power: weak and nominal

The 3.9% back-book price rise of January 2026 did not lift revenue per user, because front-book discounts (the main brand's entry bundle cut from €55 to €35), the shift to the low-cost brand Lowi (~30% of customers) and Digi's frozen prices absorbed it. Digi says it will never raise Spanish prices, Spanish contracts lack the indexation clauses that give Proximus and Orange Belgium real pricing power, and the European Court of Justice ruling invites challenges to unilateral rises.

Leverage and cost savings: strong mechanics, weak net effect

  • Operating leverage amplifies downside more than it drives upside. One point of revenue (~€36m) moves EBITDAaL by €25m-€32m and equity free cash flow by 5%-6%, but revenue grows under 1% a year from FY28.
  • Financial leverage now works through buybacks. Net debt is held at about €3.3bn, and buying shares on a ~10% cash yield with debt costing ~4.3% is strongly accretive per share.
  • Cost savings are real but late: we assume €250m gross by FY31, about €90m-€100m net of inflation. The most deliverable lever is replacing Vodafone Group's transitional IT and shared-service agreements, which is in management's own hands.
  • Precedent is cautious: Telia ran three programmes and two failed, and no example shows a second programme, after a 28% staff cut, producing net EBITDAaL growth much above 2% a year.

Profit keeps outgrowing revenue only if three conditions hold: savings beat inflation, interest rates stay contained, and there is no price war.

Score: 42. Profit outgrows revenue, but through contracted financing and buybacks rather than pricing power or self-reinforcing operating leverage, and the operating mechanisms cut harder on the way down.

#7 Scope for upgrades over the next 6-12 months: distributions likely up, cash estimates likely down

Judgement: the one probable upgrade is to distributions, and the likeliest negative re-pricing is of cash quality. Operating guidance will be met, not raised, and our cash forecasts sit below consensus for reasons we can name, so the gap should close downward.

The likeliest raise is the FY28 return, set at the FY27 results in June 2027 (45%-55% likely): an extra €100m-€300m of buyback, 2%-6% of market value. Deutsche Bank calculates that running leverage at the corridor's midpoint lifts cumulative returns to 35% of market value by March 2029, against 20% in its base case.

Sell-side estimates

  • Revenue and EBITDA: held or nudged up.
  • Free cash flow: about a 50% chance that FY28 consensus falls at least 3% by June 2027, because brokers carry too little tax, interest from older models that assumed deleveraging, and earlier cost savings.
  • Dividends: FY27 is certain at €0.90; FY28-FY30 consensus is slightly high.
  • EPS will rise mechanically in FY29-FY30 when brokers model the end of acquisition amortisation. Read that rise as accounting, not momentum.

The bull narrative is crowded (79% Buy, up 29 points in six months; mean target €25.70), and what it has not yet absorbed is mostly negative:

  • the ~€100m a year of non-cash fibre release inside EBITDAaL, without which cash-basis EBITDAaL is flat to down;
  • ageing receivables;
  • the true hedge ratio (~40% fixed, not the 70% often cited);
  • the FY29 per-share pause.

The dated events that will test the view

Score: 38. Limited room for operating or estimate upgrades and a crowded narrative, partly offset by a genuine chance of a distribution upgrade.

#9 Riskiness and simplicity: predictable near-term cash, but correlated risks on a levered equity

Judgement: Zegona's FY27-FY28 cash is more predictable than most levered telecoms', but its share price is less predictable than the average European listed company's. The risks are correlated, they sit on a levered, high-beta equity, and disclosure is thinnest exactly where the risk sits.
1The net of savings over inflation, which decides growth after FY28.
2Spanish pricing against Digi.
3Euribor, and the multiple paid for yield stocks.
4Management's distribution choices: the leverage corridor and the incentive settlement.
The danger is that the fragile requirements fail together. Inflation feeds cost indexation and downtrading at the same time, and Digi pressure tends to arrive with weaker pricing. Precedent shows how far it can go: Orange Spain's EBITDA fell 30% in the 2020-2022 Spanish price war, beyond our adverse case.

Trackability is good on volume, price and rates, but poor on cost, hedging and working capital, which appear only in the annual report. A large miss will show within a quarter; a slow erosion of cash quality only at the year end.

Thresholds that would change the view:
  • an H1 FY27 EBITDAaL margin below 36%
  • quarterly revenue growth below +1%
  • no back-book price rise in January 2027, or one below 3%
  • fixed broadband net additions negative for two quarters
  • an H1 working-capital outflow above €100m
  • a disclosed hedge ratio below 50%

Score: 45. Simple and mostly contracted near-term cash, but correlated risks on a levered, high-beta equity whose key drivers are undisclosed.

#10 Lifecycle stage: stalling, with a cash-return floor

Judgement: Zegona's shares are stalling, with a cash-return floor beneath them, and the Master Dashboard's rule agrees. Estimates are flat to down, the price has been flat since spring, the payout failed to re-rate the shares, and operating growth has slowed from +16% to +2.5%.
✗ Reinforcing needs rising estimates and fresh good news; the big news is priced and estimates are drifting down.
✗ Thesis broadening needs a confirmed new leg such as RANCo or a Vantage deal; none exists yet.

#11 The fair multiple: about a 10% equity free cash flow yield, not the 22.12x guideline

Judgement: the guideline fair multiple of 22.12x two-year-forward P/E is far too high for Zegona, for mechanical reasons rather than reasons of opinion. Fair value is an equity free cash flow yield of about 10% (range 9%-11%), which equals about 10x the model's EPS: roughly where the shares trade.

The guideline takes the 40th percentile of the stock's own ten-year P/E history, as the dashboard prescribes for a Stalling stock, giving 22.12x, 42% above today's 15.6x.

The Phase principle is right, but the history it is applied to is not: a stalling stock deserves the lower part of its range, measured on the operating company's own history.
  • A discount to the sector is warranted by leverage (equity is ~60% of enterprise value), a single market, the 15% incentive carry, poor earnings quality and low growth; a deep discount is not, given a credible all-cash payout and an outstanding capital allocator.
  • Zegona is fair, not cheap, on base-case cash: about a 9.9% equity free cash flow yield on FY28, and 6.4x adjusted enterprise value to EBITDAaL against 6.8x for peers (Morgan Stanley).
  • The yield shifts with partner deals and shocks: a signed RANCo and use of the corridor would earn a ~9% yield; a rate shock or the adverse case would push it to 11%-12%.
  • The dashboard's valuation rating of A is an artefact of the same distorted history.

Score: 55. Cheap against the sector on cash yield, about fair for its leverage and growth, and nowhere near the 22x the guideline implies.

#13 Summary conclusion

Zegona is now a high, well-protected cash yield run by top-decile capital allocators, on a mid-market Spanish number three that is slowly losing ground. The first, mechanical leg of value creation (cost out, network sold dear, shares cancelled, debt repriced, payout set) is complete and in the price. The next leg depends on partners and on a net of savings over inflation that the company does not disclose.

Where the skew becomes unusually positive

On price: below about 1,500p, where base-case cash alone returns ~20% a year. The H1 results, the January 2027 price round and the October 2027 share issue are the likeliest moments to buy there.
On news: a signed RANCo agreement, or an FY28 return above ~€450m that shows the leverage corridor in use.

End notes

How to read the numbers

EBITDAaL is EBITDA after lease costs, Zegona's own profit measure (€1,341m in FY26, the year to March 2026). Brokers' EBITDA is before lease costs (€1,829m); the gap is mostly tower and fibre rent.
Operating free cash flow (OpFCF) is EBITDAaL less capital spending (€763m in FY26). Equity free cash flow (EFCF) is what remains for shareholders after interest, tax, leases, working capital and one-offs (about €312m in FY26).
EPS has two bases: the model's is before amortisation of the intangibles from the 2024 acquisition, consensus's after it. They differ by more than €1 a share, so P/E multiples on the two bases cannot be compared.
