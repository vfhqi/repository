Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Coca-Cola HBC carries a highly input-driven cost structure where variable commodity and concentrate pricing align gross margins with system value.
  2. While high local production overheads and delivery costs introduce significant operating leverage, peer comparisons reveal that routing and packaging efficiencies act as critical offsets.

Central Framework and Research Scope

1.
Central inquiry: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Comprehensive scope: This comprehensive analysis evaluated a total of 34 relevant sources across multiple beverage giants to assess the operational limits of the bottling business model. The research incorporates detailed financial data and strategic reports covering 10 companies, including core peers such as Coca-Cola Europacific Partners. Additionally, 5 comparable past integration cases were examined to determine how margins behave under severe inflationary shocks.
3.
Significant impact: Valuation multiples and forward projections for Coca-Cola HBC AG depend heavily on the company's ability to drive earnings growth through structural margin expansion. Broker forecasts from Bernstein Research on November 6, 2026, show reported earnings per share growing from EUR 2.59 in 2025A to EUR 2.87 in 2026E and EUR 3.25 in 2027E, which would compress its price-to-earnings multiple from 18.8x to 15.0x. Sustaining this earnings momentum requires a precise understanding of the business model's cost behavior, particularly as the group's footprint becomes heavier in high-volatility emerging markets.
4.
Structural resilience: Coca-Cola HBC has historically decoupled its operating margin from gross margin fluctuations by successfully removing fixed costs from its operational network. Analysts at Redburn Atlantic reported on November 20, 2023, that the company achieved approximately EUR 300 million in cumulative cost savings since 2016. This structural shift reduced overall operating leverage and decreased the sensitivity of earnings per share to sudden volume shocks. Consequently, verifying whether further incremental cuts are possible is vital for assessing future downside risk.
5.
Strategic expansion: The impending integration of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa represents a massive geographical expansion that will test the company's operational playbook. According to Jefferies Research on February 11, 2026, the USD 2.6 billion acquisition of a 75% stake in the African bottler is expected to be low-single-digit earnings per share accretive in its first year. However, because integrating 14 diverse markets is highly complex, understanding the feasibility of material cost elimination is critical to realizing projected synergies.

Currency and Supply Chain Volatility

6.
Volatile exposure: Coca-Cola HBC carries the highest emerging market currency exposure among its European beverage peers, leaving its earnings highly vulnerable to foreign exchange fluctuations. Morgan Stanley Research highlighted on October 2, 2025, that the company faces an estimated negative 1.1% foreign exchange headwind over the 2025 to 2028 period. This volatile exposure means that localized cash management and pricing power are the primary shields protecting operating income during severe currency devaluations.
7.
Inherent mismatch: Emerging market operations face a persistent transaction risk because local revenues must cover key raw material inputs that are priced in hard currencies. Jefferies Research pointed out on February 9, 2026, that sharp local devaluations coupled with sticky dollar-linked costs create a highly unfavorable margin scenario. Since hedging instruments are often unavailable in developing territories, managing these crossborder supply chains is crucial for protecting the consolidated gross margin.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that operating leverage is no longer the primary driver of earnings because management has already optimized its physical manufacturing footprint to its limit. Redburn Atlantic reported on November 20, 2023, that net sales per employee had already tripled over 20 years to EUR 278k in 2022, while the number of production lines per plant was aggressively consolidated. This extreme efficiency suggests that future margin expansion must rely on pricing power rather than further fixed-cost leverage.

20 21 & Co Redburn Other side of the story: While fixed-cost operating leverage is a powerful driver of profitability during periods of rapid economic expansion, it can rapidly turn into a severe headwind if volume growth slows down. Therefore, the business model's high operating leverage represents a double-edged sword that could jeopardize the company's long-term earnings per share targets if regional volatility escalates.

Concentrate purchase costs represent approximately 26% of total manufacturing outlays. Production overheads account for another 23% of the overall cost base. This heavy input reliance means that raw materials directly dictate the consolidated gross margin profile.
2.
Alternative allocations: Analysts at Bernstein Research reported slightly different estimates for the company's manufacturing cost components in late 2024. Their model allocates 28% of cost of sales directly to concentrate purchased from the brand owner. Sugar and sweeteners comprise 12% of the total manufacturing outlays. The remaining portion consists of production overheads and haulage at 21% alongside depreciation.
3.
Operational expenses: Analysts at Jefferies Research indicate that operating expenses for the bottling group typically run between 36% and 38% of total revenue. Selling and distribution activities constitute the absolute majority of these operational expenditures. Within the selling expenses, administrative overheads are estimated to make up 26% of the base. Meanwhile, delivery and distribution logistics account for approximately 29% of operating expenses annually.

Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research reports that Coca-Cola HBC's largest cost line is actually highly variable and aligned with pricing. Concentrate Pricing Alignment

5.

System restructuring: Jefferies Research notes that the Coca-Cola system underwent a major pricing reset in 2017 to shift from volume to value. Under the updated incidence model, concentrate prices are set as a direct percentage of the finished product price. This real-time pricing mechanism ensures there is no lag effect on costs for the bottler. Profitability between the brand owner and bottlers is now much better aligned.
7.
Contractual alignment: Analysts at Bernstein Research confirm that the incidence-based concentrate pricing model fosters strong system incentives. Both CCEP and Coca-Cola HBC buy concentrate directly under this shared revenue framework. This strategic model ensures that system incentives are fully aligned across territories. Consequently, the brand owner and bottler avoid the pricing conflicts that characterized the prior volume-driven era.
8.
Corporate confirmation: Coca-Cola HBC AG confirmed in its 2025 integrated annual report that it purchases concentrate from the brand owner under this incidence-based model. The company handles local trade marketing while the brand owner coordinates consumer advertising. This split allows the bottler to focus on efficient downstream execution and logistics across diverse retail networks.

Historical Margin Compression Cycles

1.
Severe gross margin erosion: Coca-Cola HBC experienced a substantial profitability shock as its underlying operating margin contracted from 10.0% in 2010 to 6.4% in 2012. This down-cycle featured eleven quarters of gross margin erosion, which severely pressured the company's bottom-line performance. High input costs and negative operational leverage were the primary drivers of this historic gross margin erosion and profitability decline. Consequently, the group was forced to implement aggressive self-help and restructuring programs to restore financial fitness.
2.
Improving pricing metrics: Coca-Cola HBC began to see relief in late 2013 when it registered a gross margin expansion of 20 basis points. This marked the end of the prolonged gross margin contraction and signaled that persistent input cost pressures were beginning to abate. Additionally, the group reported its ninth consecutive quarter of currency-neutral revenue per case growth in Q3 2013. These revenue initiatives allowed the bottler to begin rebuilding its absolute profitability margins.
3.
Contrary evidence: Coca-Cola HBC managed to expand its gross profit margin by 100 basis points during the highly challenging 2015 fiscal year despite severe macroeconomic headwinds. This expansion occurred even as significant currency headwinds in emerging markets depressed net sales revenue by 2.5% on a reported basis. The margin improvement was driven by a highly favorable input cost environment and volume leverage, proving that shocks do not always trigger margin contraction when commodities decline.

Emerging segment volumes grew by 3% in late 2014, driven by strong organic growth of 1% in Russia and ongoing momentum in Nigeria. Consequently, managing local pricing and volume leverage became the primary defense against EM devaluations across territories.
