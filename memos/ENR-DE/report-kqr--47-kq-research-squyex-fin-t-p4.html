Section 2 – Transformation of Industry Separation

Section summary
  1. The strategic separation of the Transformation of Industry division aims to unlock substantial valuation upside by refocusing resources on pure-play electrification.
  2. Commercial negotiations and operational carve-outs will dictate the separation timeline, with no final decision expected before mid- calendar 2027.
  3. Retaining a minority stake ensures that Siemens Energy maintains customer trust in long-term service agreements while capturing future equity appreciation.

Timing and Strategic Rationale

1.
Accelerated carve-out preparations: Siemens Energy officially initiated preparations for the legal and operational separation of its Transformation of Industry division on August 25, 2026. Christian Bruch, the Chief Executive Officer, stated that keeping the current structure would limit what the division can achieve because corporate investments are prioritized for higher-payback power generation and transmission. This portfolio simplification allows the parent to focus capital on higher-margin segments, which means the group can accelerate its core electrification strategy over the next 18 months.
2.
Extended decision timeline: Management clarified during its pre-close webcast on September 30, 2026, that no final transaction decision will be made before mid-calendar 2027. Jefferies notes that this ongoing review evaluates whether Siemens Energy remains the best long-term owner to deliver profitable growth. This extended timeline reflects the operational complexity of carving out 17,000 employees, which means immediate EPS impacts will be deferred until fiscal year 2028 when deconsolidation is expected to complete.
3.
Contrary evidence: The initial proposal to spin off the Transformation of Industry division in June 2026 faced immediate resistance from internal stakeholders. Reports from Manager Magazin on June 19, 2026, revealed that both the supervisory board and the works council raised objections to the IPO concept and demanded that alternative transaction structures be evaluated. This internal pushback represents a material risk of execution delays, which means the group must navigate complex labor negotiations before securing a final board agreement in 2027.
4.
Refocused market profile: The planned deconsolidation of the industrial energy segment will eliminate Siemens Energy's oil, gas, and petrochemical process exposures. Deutsche Bank argues that this exit will simplify the group's corporate reporting structure and mechanically improve its medium-term margin profile by at least 50 basis points. Consequently, the separation makes the company's financial profile much more comparable to GE Vernova, which could substantially reduce the valuation multiple gap currently separating the two capital goods peers.

Transaction Structure and Accounting

5.

Financial precedents from GE Aerospace show that its spun-off energy unit, GE Vernova, was successfully classified as discontinued operations on a retrospective basis to reflect the strategic shift. Once Siemens Energy commits to a firm exit plan, the segment's assets and liabilities will be reclassified as held-for-sale, removing historical operational volatility from continuing EPS.
6.
Retained minority interest: Siemens Energy intends to retain a meaningful minority stake in the standalone business rather than pursuing a complete exit. According to its September 30, 2026 pre-close call, this structure is designed to preserve customer trust in long-term industrial service agreements while participating in future value creation. Morgan Stanley notes that retaining a strong minority stake supports operational continuity but could complicate the ultimate deconsolidation process, which means investors must scrutinize the exact voting rights agreements established during the transaction.

Jefferies expects that crystallizing this value through a majority stake sale or spin-off will significantly boost parent liquidity. Since the division's margins have successfully rebounded from breakeven to double digits, the transaction proceeds will likely be directed toward increasing potential for shareholder returns or funding the capital-intensive Grid segment.
8.
Divestment accounting precedent: Precedents from ABB Ltd demonstrate how major industrial separations are handled under corporate accounting frameworks. During its 2025 divestment of the Robotics division to SoftBank for $5.4 billion, ABB classified the segment as discontinued operations because it represented a major strategic shift. This accounting treatment isolates transaction-related cash tax outflows, which means Siemens Energy will likely exclude one-time separation costs of several hundred million euros from its normalized operating EPS.
