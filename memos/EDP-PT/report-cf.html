Appendix

Outliers worth noting

EDPR's net debt balance of €8.7 billion in June 2026 translates to an elevated 4.2x EBITDA leverage ratio, placing the company in the top quintile of European utility leverage and substantially above the sector median range of 2.5x to 3.5x. This outlier position stems from aggressive growth capital deployment coinciding with temporary working capital absorption, creating a compressed deleveraging pathway that relies heavily on volatile asset rotation proceeds.

Cross-reference to Section 4 Financial Profile Change and Section 6 Most Recent Earnings Delivery reveals that this leverage spike occurred despite management's stated commitment to conservative balance sheet management, suggesting either execution timing mismatches or structural underestimation of capital intensity requirements during the renewable build-out phase.

EDPR maintains 85% fixed-rate debt protection, representing an extreme outlier in the top 5% of European renewable developers who typically maintain 60-70% fixed-rate mixes. This exceptionally conservative hedging posture contrasts sharply with peers like Acciona Energía and Orsted who accept greater floating-rate exposure to benefit from potential rate declines. The structural rigidity embedded in EDPR's liability profile may shield nearterm earnings from rate volatility but simultaneously eliminates optionality to benefit from declining yield curves, potentially constraining relative return performance if the European Central Bank easing cycle accelerates through 2027-2028.

Cross-reference to Section 3 External Change (Macro) and Section 4 Financial Profile Change demonstrates how this hedging philosophy diverges materially from sector norms.

The Board reduced from 15 to 9 members for the 2024-2026 mandate, representing a 40% contraction that places EDP in the bottom 5% of European utility board sizes. This streamlined governance structure sits in stark contrast to peers like Iberdrola, Enel, and RWE who maintain boards of 12-16 directors to ensure adequate committee coverage and stakeholder representation. While management frames this as enhancing agile decisionmaking, the outlier positioning raises questions about whether adequate oversight capacity exists for a €15+ billion enterprise value entity operating across multiple complex regulatory jurisdictions.

Cross-reference to Section 1 Leadership and Governance Change reveals this board compression coincided with significant regional leadership departures, potentially compounding governance capacity constraints during a critical growth phase. EDPR's 11% headcount reduction to 2,572 consolidated employees, accompanied by voluntary turnover rising to 9.1%, positions the company as an extreme outlier among renewable developers pursuing aggressive capacity expansion.

Peers like RWE and Orsted typically increase headcount by 5-10% during comparable growth phases to support project execution and operational scale-up. The simultaneous compression of workforce alongside ambitious gross investment targets of €7.5 billion creates a paradoxical staffing profile that may signal either exceptional productivity gains through digitalization or dangerous operational leverage that could manifest as execution bottlenecks.

Cross-reference to Section 1 Leadership and Governance Change and Section 2 Strategy Structure and Ambition Change reveals this workforce reduction occurred during the same period management committed to accelerating North American development, suggesting potential misalignment between strategic ambition and organizational capacity.
