Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating SMA Solar's turnaround plausibility is crucial for investors as segment recoveries and policy shifts dictate future earnings.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Key challenge: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"

Financial Impact on Operating Segments

2.
Sustained weakness: SMA Solar's financial performance hinges on transitioning its Home and Business Solutions segment from steep losses toward its targeted fiscal year 2027 breakeven corridor of EUR 300m to EUR 400m in revenue. The division's operating model shifts hardware production to partners while SMA retains software development to lower fixed costs. Resolving this turnaround will decide whether the company can rebuild its depressed earnings per share over the next 3 years.
3.
Volatile margins: SMA Solar's Large Scale and Project Solutions division remains the core driver of group profitability despite experiencing a significant operational EBIT decline in the first half of 2026. Segment EBIT fell to EUR 78.3m from EUR 113.4m in the prior year period due to lower capitalized development and rising service expansion investments. Investors must determine if utility-scale margins can stabilize near historical mid-teens levels to support long-term earnings.
4.
Subdued demand: SMA Solar faces structural macro headwinds in its residential market due to high interest rates for households and lower electricity export remuneration in Europe. These factors have severely compressed demand for traditional rooftop PV installations, forcing a shift toward self-consumption and integrated storage systems. If distributor restocking fails to gain momentum, HBS revenue could fall below the target corridor and delay profitability.

Regulatory Dynamics and Peer Exposure

5.
Accelerated protectionism: Evolving regulatory policies like the United States Federal Communications Commission Covered List from July 2026 and European Commission funding restrictions create substantial tailwinds for Western suppliers. The European regulations are scheduled to fully exclude high-risk inverter suppliers from publicly funded projects by April 2027. These shifts could structurally reallocate market share away from dominant Chinese competitors like Sungrow toward compliant OEMs.
6.
Intense competition: SMA Solar competes globally against central inverter peers like Spain's Power Electronics and restructured residential competitors like SolarEdge Technologies. Power Electronics maintains minimal reliance on Chinese supply chains to capture United States market share while SolarEdge has restructured repeatedly to lower its costs. Assessing whether SMA has the inherent capability to outperform these rivals is a critical driver for investor valuation.
7.
Instructive precedents: European wind equipment manufacturers Nordex and Vestas serve as key operational precedents of successful self-help margin recoveries after facing severe supply-chain shocks. These peer companies executed comprehensive cost-saving programs and renegotiated backlog pricing to restore structural profitability. Investors are watching if SMA can mirror these precedents to establish sustainable earnings growth without needing to acquire new technological strengths.

Risks and Counter-Views

8.
Other side of the story: Skeptics argue that SMA Solar's turnaround rests on strengths it has not yet demonstrated, such as holding premium pricing against low-cost Chinese rivals in open export markets. If HBS supply constraints persist or if United States service quality issues recur, operating margins could fail to recover despite the company's EUR 250m cost-saving target. Consequently, earnings per share remain highly vulnerable to cyclical solar downturns and competitive pricing pressures.
