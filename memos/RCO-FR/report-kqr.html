IFRS Equity Classification and Coupon Treatment

1.
Corporate accounting: Rémy Cointreau completed its inaugural EUR 250 million hybrid bond issue on September 30, 2026, carrying a fixed coupon of 6.75%. Under IFRS rules, the company accounts for these deeply subordinated bonds entirely as equity on the balance sheet. This classification means the annual coupon payments of approximately EUR 16.88 million do not flow through the consolidated income statement. Instead, the payments are processed directly as equity charges, which can confuse standard earnings per share metrics.
2.
Accrual modeling: Morgan Stanley applies an accrual methodology to evaluate the dilutive impact of the hybrid bond on earnings per share. The analyst notes that because the cost of the hybrid is charged directly to equity under IFRS, standard net income appears inflated. To correct this, Morgan Stanley calculates the final adjusted earnings per share after deducting the hybrid charge net of tax. This adjustment ensures the financial model captures the true cash cost of the perpetual instrument.
3.
P&L treatment: Jefferies notes that the EUR 16.88 million annual coupon does not flow through the profit and loss statement. The broker integrates the hybrid into its model by focusing on capital structure adjustments and interest expenses. However, any subsequent refinancing of senior debt using these proceeds changes net financial charges due to early repayment fees. This approach keeps operating profit metrics clean while adjusting final cash flow expectations.

Broker EPS Modeling and Forecast Impacts

4.
EPS dilution: Bernstein updated its financial models to fully incorporate the dilutive effects of the new hybrid bond. The analyst reduced the group's projected earnings per share by 3.10% for FY2026-27. This adjustment reflects the net cost of the hybrid coupon before the positive operational changes materialize. In contrast, Bernstein raised its FY2027-28 EPS estimate by 2.40% due to favorable foreign exchange tailwinds.
5.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley initially modeled the hybrid bond coupon on a net-of-tax basis but subsequently discovered this calculation methodology was incorrect. The broker corrected its model to reflect the gross hybrid bond costs instead of net costs. This correction reduced the projected earnings per share for affected beverage companies. Standardizing these accounting treatments remains a challenge across different investment platforms.
