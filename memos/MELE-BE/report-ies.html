Melexis (MELE-BE): what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case: modestly on the operating evidence, because the margin recovery the case leans on slowed to a crawl, and more visibly on momentum, while the lower share price reopens the valuation question the case had closed against itself.

What changed

The case treated this print as the first clean read on the 41% gross-margin guide, and named as its most likely error that one quarter of margin expansion had been taken as enough, "which a single weak third quarter would undo". The quarter was neither weak enough to undo it nor strong enough to confirm it.

  • Sales: EUR 223.70m for the third quarter of 2026, up 3% sequentially and 4% year on year, against company-compiled consensus of EUR 223.60m. In line, and inside the guided range.
  • Gross margin: 40.70%, up 10 basis points sequentially and 20 basis points below consensus of 40.90%. The case's sequence was 38.6% for FY25, 39.9% and 40.5% in the first two quarters of 2026. The steps are shrinking.
  • Operating result: EUR 41.30m, an 18.50% margin, 1.20% below consensus. Diluted earnings per share EUR 0.78, 5.61% below consensus of EUR 0.83.
  • Guidance: second-half sales of EUR 445m to EUR 455m, gross margin about 41% and operating margin about 18%, all reiterated. That implies fourth-quarter sales of about EUR 226.30m, 1.48% below prior consensus of EUR 229.70m, a fourth-quarter gross margin of 41.30% (a 60 basis-point step after a 10 basis-point one) and an operating margin of 17.50%, lower than the third quarter's.
  • Inventory: EUR 261.50m, down from EUR 270.40m at end-June. Management says the fall is mainly a value effect from lower supplier wafer prices, not physical liquidation, and that it intends to rebuild physical inventory.
  • Mix: record revenue in China; Europe, Middle East and Africa fell to 27% of third-quarter sales from 31% a year earlier. Humanoid robotics moved into production volumes, at about 1% of sales on Morgan Stanley's sizing.
  • Pricing and costs: the 2027 customer pricing round is unresolved and management takes no position. Assembly inflation and gold are cited as margin drags, and mature-node foundry price rises of 3% to 10% are reported from January 2027.
  • Sell-side response: ratings unchanged, targets down. Bernstein to EUR 87 from EUR 97, with FY27 earnings per share cut to EUR 4.01 from EUR 4.46. Jefferies to EUR 78 from EUR 94. Deutsche Bank to EUR 65 from EUR 75, while raising FY27 earnings per share to EUR 3.37 from EUR 3.18 on currency. Morgan Stanley unchanged at EUR 65. Median target about EUR 71.50. The review's own Jefferies and Morgan Stanley FY26 figures conflict between text and table, so we carry those two brokers' estimates as unsettled.

The challenge

Reading one: the margin recovery was the yield fix, and the yield fix is spent. The case holds that a 200 basis-point yield fault was repaired, that the gain is banked, and that gross margin continues from there toward 45%. The alternative is that the repair was the whole of the recovery. The inventory disclosure makes this sharper than it looks: supplier wafer prices fell enough to lower the value of inventory by itself, yet gross margin rose only 10 basis points. A falling input cost that does not reach the margin line has gone somewhere, and the candidates are assembly inflation, gold and price given away to customers. If so, the 40% to 41% range is the run rate before the 2027 price round and the January 2027 foundry increases, not a staging post. For this to be right, the fourth-quarter 41.30% would have to be missed or reached on mix alone, and the 2027 round would have to land outside the 3% to 4% historical norm the case uses as its yardstick. We cannot knock this down on the evidence; the 60 basis-point step required in one quarter is the largest ask in the guide.

Reading two: China is the cause of the margin problem, not the answer to the volume problem. The case books the China restructuring as a change force and Chinese substitution as revenue at risk. It does not hold the reading that winning in China is itself what caps the margin. The quarter fits it: growth came from the region where the review describes product auctions, trade-down to cheaper vehicles and local rivals, while the European share fell four points, and sales arrived in line with gross margin light. The BYD agreement carries no volume or price commitment, and the first locally made product exists to match local cost. On this reading the case's two fulcrum drivers, volume and gross margin, pull against each other, and each record China quarter moves the 45% target further away. It requires China gross margin to sit below the group's. The company does not disclose that, so the reading is plausible and unproven.

Reading three, in the other direction: the case's valuation objection has lapsed. The case said the entry point had gone, at EUR 70.75 with the multiple at the 53rd percentile of its own history, and failed both cyclical setups on the bottom-quartile test. The price was 57.1 on 13 November 2026, 19% below the case's reference and 11.4% below the 200-day average, with the multiple at the 14th percentile of its ten-year history. Tier 1 inventories are under five weeks and have been flat for about six months, so the reported sales are consumption only and no restocking is yet in the numbers. In its strongest form, this is the trough-on-trough turn the case graded as secondary, now with the price condition met. It requires the earnings behind the multiple to hold, and Bernstein has just cut FY27 by a tenth. It also requires the cheapness to be specific to Melexis; Deutsche Bank attributes its target cut to a de-rating of the whole peer group.

One obvious alternative fails for now: that robotics has stopped being a distraction. Volumes are real and earlier than expected, but the business is about 1% of sales, Jefferies places material revenue nearer 2028, and the lead customer reports hand-reliability problems. The case's reading stands.

What it does to the case

  • E1.RA1 Change forces present [B]: more pressure. The internal change force is delivering smaller increments each quarter.
  • E1.RA2 Robust base [D]: mixed. The headwinds are unchanged; the absence of a multiple cushion, one of the three reasons for the grade, no longer holds at this price.
  • E8.RA4 Change-from-current clearing in a high-quality compounder [B]: more pressure. The clearing is slower than a primary setup wants, and growth lags peers the review cites at 13% and 15%.
  • E8.RA5 and E8.RA6: less pressure on the valuation leg, which was the reason both failed.
  • E8.RA8 Freedom from false-friend case setups [D]: more pressure. The restocking illusion is harder to dismiss with Europe shrinking in the mix. The inventory test has lost its meaning: the case re-looks below EUR 200m, and management now intends to build, so that threshold is unlikely to be met by design. The robotics distraction eases slightly, since both targets that carried it were cut. Whether this reaches the case's own park trigger is the memo's judgement, not ours here.
  • E7.RA5 Conservative investor relations [B]: slightly more pressure. An in-line quarter and a fourth-quarter guide below consensus is not the beat-the-guide pattern the grade rests on.
  • E11.RA5 Supportive supplier side [D]: more pressure from foundry and assembly cost; E11.RA2 [D]: the same.
  • MR1 Technical momentum [C] and MR2 Sell-side earnings momentum [F]: the pinned letters now read F and D, as at 15 November 2026. Most of the price fall came before the print; the shares closed at EUR 59.10 the day before it.
  • The case's bullish trigger (gross margin above 42% with inventory below EUR 250m) and bearish trigger (second-half gross margin below 40% with a further Chinese share gain) are both unmet.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry settled consensus estimates and targets, and so whether the Bernstein cut to FY27 is the pattern or the outlier. It carries no new company disclosure and nothing on price.
  • The fourth-quarter release, expected in early February 2027. This is the test of reading one: the 41.30% gross margin, the outcome of the 2027 price round against the case's 3% to 4% norm, and the first FY27 guide.
  • What nothing is committed to answer. The company has not committed to a numerical price disclosure, to regional margins, or to a robotics revenue figure. Reading two therefore has no scheduled answer; the nearest proxy is whether gross margin and the China share of sales rise together or apart.
  • The case's own outstanding work bears directly on reading one: the decomposition of the gross-margin bridge by yield, mix, volume and price, and the re-run of the false-friend test due by 30 November 2026.
