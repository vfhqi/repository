Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Structural supply deficits in high-voltage cables will sustain Cenergy's elevated margins through 2029 as capacity expansions fail to meet demand. Analysis of the Heuer matrix identifies Hypothesis 1 as the clear winner with the absolute lowest disconfirming evidence count. Cenergy Holdings' cables segment achieved an adjusted EBITDA margin of 19.5% in H1 2026, up from 16.3% in the prior year, with pricing per kilometer of high-voltage cables representing approximately 2.3 times the levels observed in the 2019-2020 period. This report examined three competing hypotheses to explain the sustainability of these exceptional profitability levels: a structural demand-supply gap persisting through 2030, a temporary arbitrage closing by 2027-2028 as competitors add capacity, or a mixed scenario with tight HVDC markets but loosening HVAC and onshore segments.

The Heuer ACH matrix analysis reveals that Hypothesis 1 (structural tightness) accumulates only one disconfirming evidence item, compared to eight for Hypothesis 2 (temporary arbitrage) and zero for Hypothesis 3 (mixed dynamics). The structural thesis carries high plausibility based on multiple reinforcing factors. Hellenic Cables recently qualified its 525 kV HVDC XLPE subsea cable systems, establishing extremely high technical entry barriers, while the record EUR 3.4 billion cables backlog disconfirms the temporary arbitrage thesis by securing project workload through 2030.

Long-term TSO capex plans, including Germany's EUR 360 billion grid program, confirm demand outruns planned industrial capacity additions. The implied margin path supports sustained elevated profitability. The highly plausible structural thesis supports an explicit cables segment adjusted EBITDA margin trajectory of 18% in 2027, 18.8% in 2028, and 19.5% in 2029 as high-value offshore execution and US operations scale up, with turnkey offshore subsea projects expected to consistently deliver project-specific EBITDA margins of 15%-25%.

This trajectory reflects Cenergy's transition toward premium HVDC projects, which remain insulated from competitive pressure due to stringent qualification requirements and extended delivery lead times. The 6-18 month risk/reward profile presents favorable asymmetry with identifiable monitoring triggers. Cenergy's recent landmark EUR 1.5 billion Corinth-Kos HVDC contract win reinforces its transition toward high-margin energy projects. On the upside, major interconnections with public-utility grid operators provide exceptionally stable long-term visibility because these clients possess superior creditworthiness compared to offshore wind developers, while entering the high-margin North American utility market allows Hellenic Cables to expand its international footprint. However, key risks include normalization of working capital back to the targeted medium-term range of 6% to 9% of revenue, skilled blue-collar labor shortages representing a primary operational bottleneck that could delay planned factory expansions, and inherent subsea vessel dependency because Cenergy does not own cable-laying vessels.

Critical monitoring triggers include tracking quarterly working capital intensity, observing execution timelines for competitor capacity additions particularly NKT's Karlskrona facility, and monitoring regulatory progress on European offshore wind projects. The robust EUR 3.4 billion cables backlog supports a clear decision to proceed with the investment over the next 6 to 18 months. The primary invalidation scenario would emerge if prolonged regulatory delays in offshore wind licensing force European transmission operators to defer cable deliveries, creating temporary oversupply conditions that prompt competitors to discount pricing aggressively to maintain factory utilization rates.
