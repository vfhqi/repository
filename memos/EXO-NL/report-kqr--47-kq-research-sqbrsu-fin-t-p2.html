Section 2 – Ferrari and the Luxury Pricing Frontier

Section summary
  1. Ferrari possesses robust pricing power supported by a strict value-over-volume strategy and unparalleled brand desirability.
  2. Secondary hybrid residual values are stabilizing across global markets, reducing transaction risks for prospective new-vehicle buyers.
  3. Flexible surcharges and high passthrough rates insulate Ferrari's operating margins from US import tariffs.

Historical Pricing Resilience

1.
Persistent pricing power: Ferrari demonstrated high pricing resilience during the 2008 to 2009 global financial crisis when volume fell significantly. Stellantis reported in March 2010 that Ferrari's 2009 revenues fell 7.4% on a 4.5% shipment decline to 6,235 vehicles, outperforming its global luxury reference segment which collapsed by approximately 35%. This resilience indicates that Ferrari can protect its average transaction values during deep economic contractions. Consequently, this strong baseline suggests that future downturns are unlikely to severely impair Ferrari's margins or long-term earnings per share.
2.
Strong mix offset: Ferrari navigated the 2020 pandemic downturn successfully by prioritizing high-margin vehicles like the Monza SP1 and SP2 to offset lower shipments. Jefferies reported on November 3, 2020, that Q3 2020 shipments declined by 7% but unit revenues increased by 10% to €314k due to mix. Positive mix and price contributed €46 mm in the quarter to counteract volume losses. This performance demonstrates how Ferrari leverages limited-edition models to sustain EPS even during global production halts.
3.
Enriched value growth: Ferrari achieved massive top-line expansion between 2023 and 2026 driven predominantly by average selling price increases rather than unit shipments. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that price/mix was the main driver of revenue growth over the last three fiscal years, and expect this trend to persist through 2026. For example, 3Q26 price/mix was up 10.9% while total shipments contracted by 1% to 3,368 units. This structural focus on value over volume supports high-single-digit compound EPS growth despite flat shipment volumes.

Luxury Valuation Benchmarks

4.
Superior operating premium: Ferrari boasts a superior valuation and margin profile compared to traditional soft luxury benchmarks like LVMH and Hermès. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that Ferrari trades at 34x to 38x 2027E P/E, which is a warranted premium to wider luxury peers. Ferrari's 2025 operating margin reached 29.5%, outperforming LVMH at 22% but trailing Hermès' industryleading 41%. This outstanding profitability allows Ferrari to command luxury-sector premium multiples, safeguarding its market capitalization.
5.
Resilient target growth: LVMH and Hermès face significant top-line pressures in key markets like China, highlighting the relative stability of Ferrari's order book. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported in October 2026 that Hermès' organic growth slowed to 4% in Q3 2026, leading to a 5% to 10% cut in Hermès' EPS estimates. Meanwhile, LVMH's fashion and leather goods margins are projected to decline to 34% in 2026 due to lack of operating leverage. These comparisons highlight Ferrari as a rare haven of earnings visibility in a volatile consumer landscape.
6.
Exceptional margin performance: Ferrari remains the most profitable manufacturer among luxury automotive peers, including Porsche AG and Lamborghini. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that Ferrari achieved a 30% operating margin in 2025, surpassing Lamborghini's 24% and Bentley's 8%. Meanwhile, Porsche recorded a 15% EBIT margin, which illustrates the severe cost pressures facing high-end German manufacturers. This high-margin structure means Ferrari can generate superior free cash flow to reinvest in its upcoming electric product portfolio.
7.
Stark luxury divergence: Aston Martin and other struggling premium brands face severe profitability pressures, contrasting sharply with Ferrari's financial strength. Bernstein Research reported on October 29, 2026, that Aston Martin registered an adjusted EBIT loss of £25 mm in Q3 2026 with a negative operating margin of 15% in 2025. Unlike its peers, Ferrari possesses unparalleled price elasticity of demand that shields its bottom line. This performance indicates that Ferrari will continue to increase its earnings per share while rivals dilute their exclusivity to survive.

Secondary Market Residual Dynamics

8.
Stabilizing hybrid values: Ferrari's hybrid models, specifically the SF90 Stradale, initially faced severe secondary market depreciation that concerned luxury collectors. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that SF90 and 296 GTB/GTS residual values have begun stabilizing after a period of rapid depreciation. A luxury automotive general manager observed in October 2026 that the depreciation on hybrid models has caused clients to become more selective about specification and timing. Consequently, stabilizing these used values is crucial to protecting new-car price increases and collector loyalty.
9.
Recovering range residuals: Secondary market transaction data suggests that the worst of the hybrid depreciation cycle has passed for range models like the 296. Jefferies reported in November 2026 that resale values of recent range models have stabilized across Germany, the UK, and the US. Additionally, pre-SF90 second-hand models are appreciating strongly in the US market, which anchors pricing power for upcoming launches. This secondary stabilization rebuilds confidence in core vehicle equity, supporting robust margins and future EPS.
10.
Sound used trends: Ferrari's Purosangue SUV residual values are showing healthy demand across global markets despite concerns over luxury vehicle depreciation. Deutsche Bank reported in November 2026 that residual values remain very sound across all models and countries. A former luxury car CEO noted in April 2026 that early Purosangue models sold on the used market above $600,000, which is unsustainable for regular range models over the long term. However, managing these used values ensures that Ferrari maintains its structural pricing power for upcoming model facelifts.

Electrification and Tariff Pass-Through

11.
Locked electric allocations: Ferrari's all-electric Luce EV, priced at approximately €550,000, has generated robust demand ahead of its delivery ramp-up. Deutsche Bank reported on November 3, 2026, that the Luce is already fully allocated for 2027 with orders extending into 2028. Management confirmed in February 2026 that they will never force clients to buy the Luce to qualify for other range allocations like the 849 Testarossa. This voluntary demand profile mitigates major brand dilution risks as Ferrari enters the luxury electric era.
12.
Constructive buyer mix: The commercial strategy for the Luce electric supercar aims to expand Ferrari's addressable market by attracting new consumer cohorts. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that Luce orders are progressing in line with plan and include a diverse mix of repeat and first-time buyers. While some dealers must do more to develop demand, client test drives in Q1 2027 should generate incremental interest. This customer recruitment strategy is designed to protect Ferrari's high operating margins without diluting brand exclusivity.
13.
Efficient tariff pass-through: US import tariffs on European vehicles have been successfully managed by Ferrari through transparent and flexible pricing surcharges. Deutsche Bank reported in November 2025 that when tariffs were lowered to 15%, Ferrari reduced its US price surcharge to a maximum of 5% from the previous 10%. Ferrari's management confirmed that these tariffs had no discernible impact on client order intake or cancellation rates. Consequently, this high tariff pass-through capability preserves Ferrari's core profitability margins in North America.
14.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies reported on November 22, 2026, that upcoming model launches are set to dilute Ferrari's positive price/mix in 2027. Specifically, the lower-priced 296 Challenge Stradale, priced at €291k, and the Amalfi ramp-up are expected to dilute average revenues per vehicle below the €515k expected by consensus. This potential mix dilution indicates that pricing power may face near-term headwinds as the special series mix normalizes.
15.
Other side of the story: Ferrari's transition to hybrid and electric vehicles introduces unique residual-value risks, including battery degradation and rapid technological obsolescence, which could weaken collector demand. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that traditional buyers remain highly skeptical of the Luce EV, leaving investors to seek clearer evidence of end-client demand. If battery failures or high replacement costs depress secondary transaction values, wealthy collectors may refuse future hybrid allocations and seek simpler internal combustion models. Therefore, Ferrari's pricing power faces long-term structural threats if its electrification strategy fails to deliver the emotional driving engagement of its heritage V12 engines.
