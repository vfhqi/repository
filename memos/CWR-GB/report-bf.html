Outliers worth noting

Ceres Power operates at a world-leading gross margin of 70% in fiscal year 2025, placing it in the extreme top percentile of clean technology peers and far above the sector median of approximately 20-25%. This structural advantage stems from the capital-light licensing model that extracts high-margin royalties while shifting manufacturing capital expenditure entirely to industrial partners, contrasting sharply with pure-play manufacturers like ITM Power that reported gross losses of £6.5 million and electrolyser peers like McPhy exhibiting extreme capex-to-sales ratios exceeding 100%.

The licensing economics enable near-100% gross margins on future royalty streams with potential expansion to 98% by 2035, fundamentally differentiating Ceres from capital-intensive manufacturing competitors across the value chain. This outlier position, documented in Section 2 and Section 4, reflects a deliberate strategic choice to occupy the highest-margin licensing bottleneck rather than downstream integration stages.

Ceres maintains a robust cash cushion of £83.3 million with zero long-term financial debt, positioning it in the top quartile for balance sheet liquidity among pre-profit clean technology developers. This sharply contrasts with peers like McPhy facing critical going-concern distress and an industry backdrop where high interest rates compressed 2030 global electrolyser forecasts from 85GW to 30GW and green hydrogen costs remain 37-84% above parity.

The debt-free structure, combined with an opportunistic £100 million equity placement in June 2026 to ensure balance sheet resilience, provides exceptional runway for the restructured operating model targeting 20% opex savings and reduced cash outflows to £19.2 million. However, this liquidity advantage remains vulnerable because negative free cash flow of £20.1 million in 2025 exposes high underlying cash burn that persists until licensees achieve mass-market royalty generation.

This outlier balance sheet strength, documented in Section 2 and Section 9, creates a critical time buffer against industry headwinds but does not eliminate execution dependency on third-party manufacturing partners detailed throughout the value chain analysis in Section 4 through Section 8.
