Section 2 – Cyclical Dynamics: Upcycle vs Historical Downcycle

Section summary
  1. Severe capacity constraints and multi-year lead times have shifted grid pricing power heavily toward manufacturers during the recent upcycle.
  2. Historical precedents from the 2010 to 2017 downcycle show that grid equipment prices remain highly vulnerable to overcapacity and competitive pressure.
  3. Recent regional destocking in short-cycle distribution equipment highlights emerging cracks that could eventually compress manufacturer margins.

Capacity Scarce Upcycle

1.
Stretching delivery lead times: Grid equipment manufacturers are securing unprecedented pricing power because transformer lead times have extended up to four years. Morgan Stanley reported on 21 May 2026 that overall lead times on large transformers have stretched to between 2.5 and 4.5 years, meaning that orders placed today are booked for 2027 or 2028 delivery. This capacity scarcity supports higher prices, which will significantly expand the net profit margins of manufacturers unless a sudden economic contraction halts utility grid upgrades.
2.
Climbing equipment prices: Transformer prices have spiked dramatically during the capacity-scarce upcycle, establishing a highly favorable seller-dominated market. Morgan Stanley reported on 11 February 2025 that power and distribution transformer prices surged by 75% to 90% in mid-2024 compared to 2020 levels. This persistent pricing momentum will drive significant earnings per share upgrades, because manufacturers can easily pass through raw material cost increases.
3.
Exploding utility orderbooks: Global grid developers are aggressively booking manufacturing slots years in advance, which reduces their price sensitivity. Bernstein Research reported on 22 October 2026 that generator step-up transformer demand grew by 274% since 2019, while substation transformer lead times exceeded 160 weeks. This massive backlog secures long-term revenue visibility, which means that manufacturer earnings will remain insulated from short-term economic fluctuations.
4.
Expanding cable backlogs: Sold-out high-voltage cable manufacturers are experiencing strong pricing power because of the massive grid reinforcement cycle. DNB Carnegie reported on 6 August 2026 that leading cable producers like NKT and Prysmian are securing backlogs with delivery times of five years or more. 5.
Contrary evidence: Procurement experts warn that a pricing bubble might be forming in the U.S. transformer market. A procurement director at Rosendin noted on 29 January 2026 that massive capacity additions from Asian suppliers could soon ease the extreme transformer undersupply. This potential capacity influx means that grid equipment pricing could normalize much faster than manufacturers expect, dragging down earnings.
6.
Locked procurement capacity: Utility buyers are increasingly willing to pay a premium to lock in long-lead equipment capacity. An energy sourcing lead at Duke Energy stated on 29 October 2026 that utilities are less cost-conscious and are prioritizing long-term partnerships and delivery certainty over price. This collaborative environment supports stable manufacturer pricing, which means that earnings per share remain protected from intense price negotiations.

Section 3 – Pass-Through Mechanics and Cost Inflation

Section summary
  1. Arteche's historical margin contraction reveals a structural lag in pass-through mechanisms that leaves short-term earnings vulnerable to sudden commodity spikes.
  2. Diverse indexation frameworks and varying fixed-price exposures among peers dictate how quickly raw material inflation is passed through to utility customers.
  3. Severe localized currency devaluations in emerging markets introduce transaction and hyperinflationary headwinds that compress margins despite robust local pricing.

Pass-Through Lags and Margin Compression

1.
Historical margin compression: Arteche's financial trajectory from 2020 to 2022 highlights how a rapid surge in raw material, component, and logistics costs can overwhelm contractual pass-through clauses. The company's reported direct margin fell from 34.6% in 2020 to 29.7% in 2022, representing a severe contraction of 490 basis points. This compression occurred because global supply chain bottlenecks and inflationary spikes in copper, steel, and freight outpaced the indexation formulas embedded in older framework agreements. Recovery took two full years, with the direct margin inflecting to 32.1% in 2023 and finally reaching 35.5% in 2024 as higherpriced orders entered execution.
2.
Typical contract indexation: A significant portion of Arteche's backlog of 417.2 million euros at mid-2026 is tied to long-term utility frame agreements. While some of these contracts feature indexation clauses linked to key raw materials such as copper, aluminium, and steel, others remain fixed-price, particularly in short-cycle distribution and relays. Even when indexation exists, a typical lag of 6 to 12 months occurs between the spot commodity price increase and actual invoice adjustments, which exposes earnings to near-term cash flow volatility. Consequently, unhedged components, local factory energy, and rising labor costs are rarely covered by indexation, requiring manual price list updates.
3.
Contrary evidence: Management claims that its pass-through mechanics and hedging programs are fully capable of insulating the business from raw material volatility. Arteche's 2024 financial presentation asserts that its procurement strategies and reverse factoring lines have successfully stabilized the supply chain and protected margins. However, the severe margin degradation observed in 2021 and 2022 proves that these mechanisms face structural limits when commodity inflation is compounding rapidly. This mismatch shows that during a hyper-inflationary commodity shock, hedging and indexation can only delay, rather than entirely prevent, a compression in operating profit.

This risk-sharing mechanism is vital to defend developer margins, because unexpected equipment exclusions can severely inflate project costs and delay critical grid connections.
