EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business Model and Market Position

1.
Corticeira Amorim operates as the uncontested global leader in cork processing, leveraging a fully verticalised model that spans from agroforestry ownership through finished closure manufacturing to worldwide distribution. The company manages 8,181 hectares of forest estates, manufactures over 5.2 billion stoppers annually, and serves approximately 25,200 clients across 96 countries, creating extreme customer fragmentation that fundamentally shields the business from individual buyer leverage. Revenue composition reflects this closure dominance, with Amorim Cork representing 80.30% of sales, followed by the newly integrated Amorim Cork Solutions at 18.70%. The organizational structure underwent significant transformation in January 2025 when management consolidated non-cork operations into Amorim Cork Solutions to target industrial, flooring, and aerospace applications. However, consolidated turnover contracted from €985.47 million in FY23 to €861 million in FY25, primarily driven by structural destocking following post-COVID inventory adjustments that reduced cork volumes from 6.0 billion units in 2022 to 5.3 billion in 2024.

Vertical integration – strategic forest control: Amorim's fully integrated vertical business model spans forestry, processing, manufacturing, and global distribution, anchored by 8,181 hectares of directly owned agroforestry assets and centralized procurement through the Amorim Florestal unit. Industry Structure and Competitive Dynamics

7.
Market concentration and closure substitution: The aggregate global market for wine closures and caps reached 28 to 30 billion units in 2023, though total wine and sparkling bottles dropped to approximately 17.5 billion units, revealing severe structural oversupply across all closure technologies. Guala Closures holds a leading 30% aggregate global market share in spirits closures and controls over 60% of safety closures by volume, creating formidable adjacent competition in premium segments where Amorim seeks expansion. Roll-on aluminum wine closures represent over 8 billion units globally and continue expanding by capturing share from traditional natural cork stoppers, demonstrating persistent substitution pressure that threatens cork's longterm volume base. The cork segment itself exhibits Oeneo SA as the most formidable tier-2 challenger, with its highly successful Diam brand commanding significant customer loyalty and approximately 2 billion units sold worldwide during 2025-2026.
8.
Value chain economics and margin distribution: High-margin pools concentrate in premium bar tops and highly customized safety closures, with complex anti-counterfeiting closures generating structurally superior margins. Entry into the glass container stage faces heavy upfront capital and maintenance costs, high transportation costs restricting local radius sales, and energy costs comprising 10% to 20% of sales, creating capital-intensive barriers fundamentally different from cork's agricultural constraints. Industry-level success factors emphasize robust energy hedging to limit price volatility and price adjustment formulas to pass through cost inflation, though these formulas require several quarters to reflect raw input changes. Amorim's margin profile across value chain stages reveals material stage-level variance: upstream agroforestry captures estimated 60-70% gross margins, industrial closures achieved 52.90% in FY24, while the newly formed Industrial Solutions segment reported negative 0.16% EBIT margins in FY25, highlighting structural underperformance in non-cork applications.

Customer Dynamics and Switching Economics

9.
Extreme customer fragmentation: No single customer represents more than 2% of Amorim's total sales, creating unprecedented buyer dispersion that fundamentally eliminates concentrated negotiating leverage. The group serves approximately 25,200 active clients distributed across 96 countries, with over 93% of sales generated outside Portugal. This massive global client base contrasts sharply with typical packaging industry concentration, where top-10 customers commonly represent 40-60% of revenues. End-market segmentation reveals the addressable spirits closure market standing above 40 billion bottles, with high-margin spirits closures leveraging aesthetics and cork's premium image, while structural still wine closures face a decline of 1% to 1.50% annually through 2030, creating divergent secular trajectories across core segments.
10.
Technical qualification barriers and pricing mechanics: Wineries require rigorous sensory and chemical validation before selecting a closure, with NDTech individually inspecting natural stoppers to guarantee releasable TCA below 0.5 nanograms/litre, establishing stringent technical switching costs that protect incumbent suppliers. Sales prices are defined taking into account raw material acquisition costs from annual harvesting, with prices set based on estimated cork availability during the summer campaign, creating inflexible annual pricing cycles that limit intra-year margin adjustment but ensure structural cost pass-through over multi-year periods. Rising raw material costs force selective price adjustments, which can prompt customer pushback and increase the risk of customers switching to screwcaps, revealing the delicate balance between margin protection and volume retention in a market facing substitution threats.

Strategic Risks and Structural Evolution

11.
Secular demand headwinds and category growth divergence: Still wine closures face structural decline of 1% to 1.50% annually through 2030, while sparkling wine closures are projected to grow 1% to 1.50% annually and high-margin spirits bar tops are expected to expand at 2% annually. This category bifurcation creates strategic tension between defending traditional still wine volumes and allocating resources toward premium growth segments. Post-COVID inventory adjustments caused cork volumes to drop from 6.0 billion in 2022 to 5.3 billion in 2024, demonstrating cyclical volatility layered atop structural decline. The forestry innovation pipeline offers potential relief, as drip irrigation reduces the first cork harvesting cycle from 25 years to approximately 12.5 years, potentially doubling supply responsiveness to demand shifts while simultaneously reducing climate vulnerability.
12.
Vertical integration risks and adjacent market opportunities: Sourcing centers on natural cork harvested from Mediterranean oak forests vulnerable to rising temperatures, creating concentrated climate risk despite procurement diversification across 3,603 suppliers. Geopolitical conflicts cause shipping disruptions restricting container availability for USA and Asia routes, while regional operations face a highly limited number of viable transport operators, highlighting logistical fragility in global distribution networks. Adjacent market expansion shows promising traction, with Amorim Cork Solutions established in January 2025 representing 18.70% of FY25 revenues, though the segment reported negative EBIT margins. The Scott Premium Closures 50/50 joint venture formed in April 2026 is projected to add €15 million to FY26 sales, representing strategic North American consolidation to combat market fragmentation. Long-term sustainability positioning provides regulatory tailwinds, as strict global climate policies mechanically increase demand for low-carbon circular cork closures, while good forest management creates opportunities to monetize CO2 sequestration, potentially unlocking high-margin carbon credit revenues.

Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Corticeira Amorim exhibits several exceptional characteristics that position the company at the statistical extremes of its industry peer group and broader packaging sector. The company's profitability profile sits in stark contrast to glass packaging peers, with Vidrala delivering a 30.10% EBITDA margin in FY25, nearly double Amorim's 16.40% consolidated margin, representing a massive profitability outlier achieved through high-scale facilities and unhedged energy cost corrections.

This margin gap highlights the structural cost pressures inherent in cork processing relative to optimized glass container manufacturing, where consolidation and operational leverage have driven exceptional returns. Amorim's working capital intensity represents another striking outlier, with the company carrying 183 days of net working capital compared to peer averages of 13.53 days for Verallia, 69.35 days for Vidrala, and 52 days for O-I Glass.

This extreme divergence reflects the fundamental economics of cork processing, where raw material procurement occurs during concentrated summer harvesting campaigns and requires extended storage and stabilization periods before industrial processing. The prolonged inventory cycle creates a structural cash conversion disadvantage relative to just-in-time glass manufacturing operations, though recent working capital optimization efforts have begun to narrow this gap.

The company's vertical integration depth stands as an unmatched industry outlier, operating a fully integrated model spanning 8,181 hectares of agroforestry assets, centralized raw material procurement through Amorim Florestal, and consuming 90% of raw material output internally through the manufacturing business.
