Section summary

Resolving Theon's net working capital trajectory is critical for unlocking substantial cash flow, directly impacting EPS growth, debt deleveraging, and valuation multiples, yet excessive cash optimization could hurt sales if supply bottlenecks worsen. Theon International operates with net working capital of approximately 40% of revenue, driven by inventory of tubes and components held for priority supply, receivables from the Hensoldt-Theon joint venture, payment terms of armed-forces customers, and advance payments to suppliers. Despite explosive revenue growth, net working capital absorption declined to 41.0% of sales in 2025 from 44.2% in 2024, driven by tighter operational discipline and enhanced inventory management across the business. The analysis of fifteen relevant documents including company filings, broker reports, and expert interviews reveals the structural drivers behind this elevated capital intensity and the feasibility of management's medium-term target below 35%.

Theon secured its primary component pipeline by purchasing a strategic 9.8% equity stake in its main supplier Exosens in early 2026 for 268.70 million Euros, solidifying a long-term supply agreement extending through the end of 2030 that ensures uninterrupted access to image intensifier tubes and mitigates major bottlenecks in global defense procurement. Theon committed to purchasing well over 400,000 image intensifier tubes through its extended commercial framework with Exosens over five years, covering approximately 55% of Exosens forecasted tube production capacity over the planning period. This strategic relationship addresses the severe global supply constraint where image intensifier tubes account for up to 65% of night-vision device material costs, but it also introduces significant working capital requirements.

Theon integrated its component supply by acquiring a controlling stake in German manufacturer Harder Digital, subsequently increasing its ownership to 65.22% through a capital increase in July 2026, allowing the company to insource critical tube manufacturing capabilities and reduce its single-supplier dependency. Harder Digital doubled its annual production capacity after installing two new manufacturing lines, and Theon committed an additional 14.98 million Euros in share premium to support further development expected to triple Harder Digital revenues to approximately 50 million Euros by 2028.

While vertical integration strengthens supply security, it also introduces significant capital allocation risks and execution complexities, with the cash consideration of 268.70 million Euros for the Exosens stake increasing financial leverage and diluting short-term earnings per share.

Sales of goods to the Hensoldt Theon NightVision joint venture increased to 34.59 million Euros in the first half of 2026 compared to 17.15 million Euros in the first half of 2025, supporting the historic OCCAR procurement program and confirming sustained demand for night-vision products across NATO member states. The comparative analysis reveals significant structural differences across peers. The likelihood of reaching management's medium-term target below 35% appears moderately feasible but dependent on multiple catalysts aligning successfully.
