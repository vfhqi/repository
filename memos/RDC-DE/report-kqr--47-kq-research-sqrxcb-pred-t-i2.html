Section 2 – The 2026 Margin Improvement and Cost Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Redcare's 2026 margin improvement reflects a structural shift toward targeted digital marketing that more than offsets the gross margin dilution from lower-margin prescription sales.
  2. The accounting treatment of Rx bonuses directly dilutes gross margins but enables significant marketing spend leverage, while Sevenum's automation will provide a critical margin catalyst in 2027.

Analyzing the 2026 Margin Drivers

1.
Significant margin expansion: Redcare Pharmacy achieved its highest adjusted EBITDA margin in ten years during Q2 2026 by delivering a 3.5% margin. This profitability milestone was driven by scale efficiencies and a sharp reduction in marketing spend, which declined to 5.1% of revenue. Operational improvements in both DACH and International segments helped the firm meaningfully expand its underlying profitability.
2.
Efficient marketing spending: Redcare Pharmacy experienced sequential acceleration in its core business while concurrently optimizing its promotional expenditure. The marketing ratio declined by 210 basis points to 5.1% in Q2 2026 from 7.2% in Q2 2025. Analysts attribute this progress to a more efficient and digital marketing mix, which helped the group decouple revenue growth from marketing spend.
3.
Contrary evidence: Redcare Pharmacy experienced a sharp margin disappointment in Q1 2026 when its adjusted EBITDA margin of 1.7% missed consensus expectations of 2.0% due to persistent gross margin pressure. This softer performance was driven by intense non-prescription pricing competition and the full impact of the newly introduced Rx bonus. This outcome suggests that marketing savings may not always be sufficient to offset underlying gross margin dilution.
4.
Gross margin dilution: Redcare Pharmacy saw its group gross profit margin decline from 23.8% in Q2 2025 to 22.1% in Q2 2026. This sequential dilution was primarily driven by the rapid growth of the prescription drug business, which carries structurally lower margins than non-prescription items. Management highlighted that product mix shifts will continue to depress gross profit margins even as absolute gross profit euros continue to expand.

Rx Bonus Accounting and Seasonality

6.
Direct revenue reduction: Redcare Pharmacy accounts for its prescription medication customer bonuses as sales deductions rather than marketing costs. Under IFRS 15 rules, these cash incentives are recognized as a direct reduction of the product's transaction price. Treating bonuses in this manner compresses the reported gross margin but artificially lowers the apparent marketing ratio.
7.
Pronounced margin seasonality: Redcare Pharmacy experiences substantial seasonal variations in its operating profitability due to winter health trends. Demand for pharmaceuticals and cold remedies typically peaks during the first and fourth quarters of the year. However, this high seasonal volume often requires elevated promotional spend, which can compress the sequential EBITDA margin.
8.
Aggressive winter campaigns: Redcare Pharmacy saw its adjusted EBITDA margin compress to 1.6% in Q4 2025 despite robust operational momentum. This margin contraction was driven by aggressive marketing pushes and intensified customer bonus programs deployed to hit annual prescription targets. This historical pattern suggests that strong Q4 volume growth frequently occurs at the expense of short-term profitability.
9.
Peer margin benchmarks: Redcare Pharmacy targets a long-term group adjusted EBITDA margin of 8% which exceeds peer expectations. Competitor DocMorris AG has historically stated that it does not expect its own over-the-counter business to generate EBITDA margins above 2% to 3%. This discrepancy highlights that Redcare relies on its superior scale and diversified offering to achieve its ambitious profitability goals.

This massive investment is scheduled to double the location's order fulfillment capacity by early 2027. Substantial labor savings: Redcare Pharmacy expects the Sevenum automation project to reduce fulfillment labor requirements by 70% per order once fully operational. Depreciation expenses have historically increased as major capital projects are capitalized. However, the labor cost reductions are expected to fully offset this impact as overall capital expenditure rolls off.

Precedent Pilsen facility: Redcare Pharmacy successfully utilized a similar logistics model when it launched its Pilsen distribution facility in Q4 2025. The Pilsen site added 15 million parcels of annual capacity while significantly reducing operational costs due to lower regional wage rates. Section 3 – Below-EBITDA Definitions and Convertible Debt Terms

Section summary
  1. Redcare's below-EBITDA adjustments mask significant non-cash expenses, notably the discrepancy between the convertible's coupon and its IFRS effective interest rate.
  2. Unrecognized Dutch tax losses of over three hundred million euros represent massive potential earnings upside once sustained profitability is established.

Intangibles and EBITDA Adjustments

1.
Operational adjustments: Redcare Pharmacy excludes non-cash acquisition-related expenses and share-based compensation to present its adjusted EBITDA results. According to the Redcare Q3 2026 report, the company reported adjusted EBITDA of 72.7 million euros for the first nine months of 2026, which is reconciled by adding back 2.2 million euros in stock options and project costs. This adjusted metric is widely used by sell-side analysts to evaluate core performance, but it obscures the recurring nature of share-based incentives on longterm shareholder dilution.
2.
Amortization impact: Redcare Pharmacy experiences substantial non-cash headwinds from the amortization of acquired intangible assets originating from past business combinations. The Redcare 2025 annual report shows that deferred tax liabilities of 9.2 million euros are primarily linked to temporary differences in intangible assets because accounting amortization exceeds tax-deductible amortization. Because sell-side analysts often add back this amortization to calculate adjusted EPS, the reported figures can overstate the company's ultimate cash-generating capacity.
3.
Contrary evidence: Redcare Pharmacy's below-EBITDA accounting definitions are dismissed by some analysts who argue that operational cash flows are more critical than accounting metrics. Deutsche Bank reported in October 2026 that Redcare's Q3 cash flow reached 15 million euros, marking the second consecutive quarter of positive net profit. This performance suggests that underlying cash generation is inflecting positively, meaning that the technical adjustments below the EBITDA line may have a diminishing impact on equity valuation.
4.
Valuation premiums: Redcare Pharmacy trades at a valuation premium that reflects its faster growth despite persistent accounting losses. Carnegie research from January 2025 noted that Redcare was trading at 32 times 2026 expected EV/EBIT, which is higher than Nordic peers at 11.5 times but justified by Redcare's larger total addressable market. This indicates that investors are willing to overlook near-term accounting adjustments in favor of long-term structural growth potential.

Convertible Debt Accounting Mechanics

5.
High effective interest: Redcare Pharmacy faces a significant non-cash financing charge due to the accounting treatment of its senior unsecured convertible bonds. The Redcare Q2 2026 report discloses that the 300 million euro bond issued in April 2025 carries a 1.75% annual coupon but has a 5.16% IFRS effective interest rate. This accounting mismatch arises because IFRS requires separating the compound instrument into liability and equity components, thereby depressing GAAP earnings per share.
6.
Put option risks: Redcare Pharmacy must navigate potential refinancing hurdles associated with the investor put option embedded in its 2032 convertible bonds. Management noted in the 2025 annual report that bondholders can exercise a put option on April 16, 2030, to redeem the bonds at an accreted redemption price of 110% of the principal. If the stock trades below the 173.74 euro conversion price, this option represents a substantial medium-term refinancing risk.
7.
Historical refinancing: Redcare Pharmacy has a track record of proactively managing its debt maturities to prevent near-term liquidity pressure. In July 2025, the company used the proceeds from its 300 million euro bond to repurchase approximately 70% of its outstanding 2021 convertible bonds due in 2028. This transaction successfully extended the company's debt maturity profile and proactively addressed investor concerns about upcoming refinancings.
8.
Non-cash expenses: Redcare Pharmacy recorded significant non-operating losses during its refinancing activities that temporarily weighed on net income. The company recognized an accounting loss of 3.82 million euros in 2025 from discounting revised cash flows on its remaining 2021 convertible bonds. This shows how technical accounting adjustments under IFRS 9 can introduce unexpected volatility into reported EPS figures.

Dutch Tax Regulations and DTAs

9.
Tax loss hurdles: Redcare Pharmacy holds massive tax loss carry-forwards that remain unrecognized on the balance sheet due to strict accounting standards and Dutch fiscal limits. The Redcare 2025 annual report details 311.9 million euros of unused Dutch tax losses for which no deferred tax asset has been recognized. Under Dutch rules, loss offsets are restricted to 50% of taxable profit exceeding 1 million euros, which will slow the transition to tax-free profitability.
10.
Volatile effective taxes: Redcare Pharmacy experiences extreme volatility in its effective tax rate due to the treatment of its convertible debt repurchases. The Redcare Q2 2026 report reveals that the effective tax rate rose to 41.2% in H1 2026 from negative 66.9% in H1 2025. This massive shift was driven by the prior year's release of deferred tax liabilities on repurchased bonds, illustrating how accounting adjustments distort intermediate EPS.
11.
Local tax rates: Redcare Pharmacy operates across multiple jurisdictions with different statutory corporate income tax rates. The company's 2025 annual report indicates that the anticipated applicable tax rate on Dutch taxable profits is 25.8% compared to a higher rate of 32% for its German business operations. This geographic tax mix complicates sell-side models and requires careful regional forecasting to project accurate consolidated net income.

Remuneration and Access Platform Visibility

6.
Escalating legislative opposition: CSU Bundestag member Stephan Pilsinger launched a legislative campaign in late 2026 to ban cross-border pharmacies from waiving statutory patient co-payments. This social law amendment seeks to close the marketing loophole that has fueled Redcare's rapid prescription customer acquisition. Unresolved legal compatibility: The legal enforcement of a national co-payment waiver ban against EU-based online pharmacies remains highly controversial under cross-border trade laws. Any German social law amendment must be designed to be strictly compatible with European internal market freedoms to avoid immediate legal challenges. Because prior attempts to regulate foreign mail-order practices have faced severe setbacks, the political desire for equal competition does not guarantee a legally robust outcome.

If insurances collect co-payments directly from patients, online pharmacies would no longer have any operational mechanism to offer immediate waivers. However, this reform represents a massive administrative intervention that would disrupt the entire statutory medication provision system. Consequently, the complexity of this collection shift makes it highly unlikely to be implemented in the near term.

Supportive remuneration adjustments: The German Federal Cabinet approved a ministerial regulation in mid-2026 to adjust the prescription-based pharmacy remuneration package. Under this approved framework, the fixed remuneration per prescription medication, known as the Fixum, rose to nine euros on July 1, 2026. The regulation dictates that this fee will rise further to 9.50 euros on January 1, 2027. 171 structural fee increase provides a predictable revenue lift that will materially support intermediate earnings projections.
