EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Key Question asks what the evidence says about the specific inputs that will drive Volati's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, covering preference shares, net financial items, acquired intangible amortization, effective tax rates, minority interests, items affecting comparability, dividend policy, warrant dilution, goodwill impairment sensitivity, 2026 acquisition contributions, broker EPS definitions, and normal working capital and capex levels.

Based on the comprehensive evidence gathered across nine detailed sections, the Key Question can be partly answered now, with high confidence on several critical inputs but notable gaps remaining on credit facility covenants, full purchase-price allocations, and management's preference share redemption intentions. Volati retains 1,603,774 preference shares outstanding following the Salix spin-off, carrying an expensive annual dividend burden of SEK 64 million (SEK 40 per share paid quarterly), with the current redemption price standing at SEK 625 per share for a total redemption cost of approximately SEK 1.0 billion.

This contrasts sharply with peer Sdiptech's successful March 2026 redemption of all 1,750,000 preference shares for SEK 184 million at SEK 105 per share, demonstrating how a serial acquirer can streamline its capital structure. However, Volati's stretched leverage profile with net debt to EBITDA reaching 4.8x including preference shares severely restricts its capacity to replicate this refinancing strategy near term.

Volati established a new credit facility with Nordea and SEB effective 15 June 2026 to support continuing operations, with DNB Carnegie modeling a constant borrowing rate of 4.5% reflecting the higher risk profile after losing Salix's diversified cash flows. Net debt fell to SEK 1,331 million from SEK 2,081 million at end-2025, primarily because Salix repaid internal loans prior to listing, though the net debt-to-EBITDA ratio remains elevated due to the continuing group's lower EBITDA base. The 2025 annual report disclosed that a one percentage point interest rate change on pre-separation net debt impacts post-tax profit by SEK 17 million, meaning ordinary EPS remains highly vulnerable to STIBOR movements. Lease commitments remain substantial with projected depreciation of SEK 238 million in 2026 and lease interest expenses of SEK 20 million.

The 2026 acquisitions significantly expanded Volati's platform capabilities: Interket (SEK 494 million consideration, SEK 450 million annual sales) represents 26% of Ettiketto's quarterly sales, NicEtikett adds SEK 35 million of regional label sales, and Tramex contributes SEK 90 million of sales at adjusted EBITA margins exceeding 20%. These transactions added SEK 106 million of intangible assets and SEK 215 million of goodwill, with acquisition-related amortization rising to SEK 46 million for the first half of 2026, creating a larger future amortization drag that will cause reported ordinary EPS to diverge further from adjusted EBITA over the next three years.

Platform goodwill is heavily concentrated with Ettiketto holding SEK 237 million and S:t Eriks holding SEK 274 million, but impairment testing shows substantial headroom with post-tax discount rates between 10.1% and 12.8%, and a one percentage point sensitivity test resulting in zero impairment.

DNB Carnegie forecasts Volati's effective tax rate at 21.4% for 2026, stabilizing at 22.0% for 2027 and 2028, consistent with the historical 21.8% rate in 2025, protecting future ordinary earnings from severe tax volatility. The Salix spin-off significantly reduced put option liabilities to SEK 48 million from SEK 287 million, as discontinued operations accounted for SEK 239 million of total obligations, leaving the continuing group with minimal balance sheet risk from future minority buyouts.

All outstanding warrant programs from 2022 to 2026 total 1.34% dilution if fully exercised, including the newly authorized 2026/2030 series representing 0.59% dilution, presenting relatively well-contained long-term share count pressure. Items affecting comparability totaled SEK 19 million in restructuring costs during the first nine months of 2026 (primarily Ettiketto's facility consolidation) plus SEK 8 million in Q3 transaction costs alone.

The Salix spin-off generated a massive SEK 4,044 million capital gain recorded under discontinued operations, which must be excluded from continuing earnings metrics. DNB Carnegie models net working capital falling from 30.5% of sales in 2025 to 19.1% in 2026 and 10.7% in 2027, with capex-to-sales stabilizing at 3.0% annually, suggesting freed cash from inventory liquidations will support debt reduction. The post-separation dividend policy targets 10% to 30% of net profit, compared to the SEK 2 ordinary dividend paid for 2025, with DNB Carnegie projecting this will fall to SEK 1 in 2026 and further to SEK 0.52 by 2028 due to the loss of Salix's earnings pool.

The most critical finding concerns the massive divergence between broker EPS definitions. DNB Carnegie forecasts adjusted EPS of SEK 1.98, 2.30, and 2.82 for 2026 to 2028, while Nordea's forecasts are substantially lower at SEK 0.62 for 2026, rising to SEK 2.02 in 2027 and SEK 2.70 in 2028, creating a SEK 1.36 discrepancy in 2026 that presents high analytical uncertainty for ordinary shareholders. The primary driver of this gap is DNB Carnegie's adjusted net profit methodology, which adds back acquisition-related amortization of SEK 100 million, while Nordea appears not to add back these non-cash charges, causing reported EPS to be severely depressed by historical and new purchase-price allocations.

Both brokers deduct the SEK 64 million annual preference dividend and minority interests from net profit to calculate earnings attributable to ordinary shareholders, with this priority obligation directly constraining ordinary EPS expansion. DNB Carnegie's line-by-line reconciliation shows adjusted EBITA of SEK 321 million in 2026, less SEK 19 million items affecting comparability and SEK 100 million acquisition amortization to reach reported EBIT of SEK 203 million, then less SEK 76 million net financial items and taxes at 21.4% plus SEK 13 million minority interests, yielding continuing net profit of SEK 87 million before adding back post-tax amortization of SEK 78.60 million to arrive at adjusted EPS of SEK 1.98. DNB Carnegie models free cash flow after preference dividends at negative SEK 16 million in 2026 before recovering to SEK 145 million in 2027, indicating M&A flexibility is highly constrained unless operational cash generation improves dramatically.

The analysis reveals three major forecast risk concentrations. The highest forecast risk resides in the recovery timing of S:t Eriks and Tornum Group, as continued weakness in these cyclical platforms offsets growth in Ettiketto and Corroventa, with both brokers' EBITA assumptions facing severe downward revisions if agricultural and construction end-markets fail to recover. DNB Carnegie's own model showing negative free cash flow of SEK -16 million for 2026 after deducting lease payments and interest suggests that adjusted EPS figures may mask severe capital constraints and leverage pressures that ordinary shareholders cannot ignore, potentially validating Nordea's more conservative SEK 0.62 ordinary EPS projection.

If STIBOR remains elevated longer than brokers model, each 100 basis point increase reduces post-tax earnings by SEK 17 million, which would depress ordinary earnings per share well below expectations given Volati's leveraged balance sheet.

Critical information gaps remain that prevent full resolution of the Key Question. Final purchase-price allocations for Interket and Tramex are preliminary, with individual amortization schedules unavailable until the 2026 annual report, leaving future amortization run-rates uncertain. The specific operational and financial covenants of the new Nordea and SEB credit facility remain undisclosed, meaning the group's actual borrowing capacity could be tighter than modeled unless management clarifies these terms.

Nordea's exact modeling definitions and adjustments for its SEK 0.62 EPS forecast are not fully detailed, requiring access to Nordea's full equity research reports to explain why the two brokers' forecasts are so far apart. Management has not provided a definitive timeline or strategy for redeeming outstanding preference shares, with direct management interviews required to confirm whether Volati intends to refinance them using cheaper bank credit.

In summary, while the evidence provides robust answers on tax rates, minority interests, warrant dilution, goodwill resilience, and acquisition contributions, ordinary shareholders face material uncertainty regarding the actual earnings power of continuing operations due to the wide divergence in broker methodologies, unresolved refinancing intentions for expensive preference shares, and high sensitivity to interest rate and cyclical platform recovery paths that could cause material deviations from consensus expectations over the forecast horizon.
