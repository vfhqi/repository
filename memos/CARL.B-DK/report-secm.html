1. The verdict

The Consumer staples tailwind is supportive and moderate today, and it is a weaker foundation for a stock bought on 18 November 2026 than the Healthcare tailwind. The base expectation is that the Consumer staples tailwind stays supportive and moderate over the next three months and fades to neutral by twelve months, unless the full-year results of February and March 2027 start to lift estimates. The median Consumer staples stock is 9 points ahead of the index over three months and 9 points ahead over six months, second of 16 Industries over one month and over three. The Relative Strength is real. The Relative Strength is also almost entirely a higher multiple, earned in October and November 2026, on estimates that have not moved.

Throughout, the index is the Stoxx Europe 600, the universe is the roughly 1,300 European stocks in our files, the multiple is the two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple, and a percentile places a multiple in the stock's own ten-year range, where 0 is the cheapest. The Industry has 72 stocks in six Sectors: Beverages (12), Tobacco (6), Household products (6), Food retail (13), Food production (23) and General retail (12).

Richard's three questions, answered for European Consumer staples:

  • Fundamental improvement (question 1): present, weak and narrow, and not yet visible in estimates. Fundamental improvement is the only durable source and the smallest of the three
  • Consensus 2027 earnings per share for the median Consumer staples stock has not moved over one month, three months or six months, and 30 stocks were downgraded against 26 upgraded over three months
  • The improvement that exists is a recovery in volumes at a handful of the largest companies, mostly in emerging markets, and part of that recovery was weather and timing (company and broker reports, October and November 2026)
  • Money returning to an out-of-favour Industry (question 2): present and strong. The source is less used up than Healthcare's against the stocks' own history and about as used up against the market, and most of the room left is in Beverages
  • The median multiple sat at the 18th percentile of its own range in May 2026 and sits at the 30th on 18 November 2026, with 39 of 68 stocks still below the 35th percentile
  • Against the universe, about two-thirds of the valuation gap of the median stock has already closed. Weighted by company size, as brokers measure the sector against MSCI Europe, about half has closed (section 3.2)
  • Defensive buying (question 3): present, strong in October and November 2026, and the least durable. The buying was money leaving technology shares, and it was not fear of a recession
  • The median Consumer staples stock gained 5 points against the index in October 2026 and 4 more in November 2026 to the 18th, after a net loss of 3 points over May to September 2026
  • Global consumer staples had their best month since 1998 in October 2026 while global technology shares fell more than 13%, and only 3% of fund managers expected a recession in November 2026 (public sources, section 3.3)

The three answers read together: Richard's test was that three strongly positive answers are the most supportive case. European Consumer staples gives one weak positive answer and two answers that are strong today and temporary. The two temporary sources are also one event seen from two sides: the money that left technology shares in October 2026 went to the steady earners that were cheapest and least owned, which were Consumer staples and Healthcare, and passed over Utilities and Telecoms, which were neither cheap nor unloved. The Industry therefore has one support beside fundamentals and not two, and that one support depends on what happens to technology shares (section 3.3).

The difference lies in the estimates. Healthcare therefore has a small durable source that has begun to work, and Consumer staples has a small durable source that has not.

What the verdict means for a stock bought now:

  • The Industry will help for weeks or a few months more, and should not be counted on beyond that. The help so far has been a higher multiple from a low base, bought mostly on days the index fell, with no rise in estimates. The same combination reversed in April 2026, after two strong months in February and March 2026
  • The durable case for any single stock has to be the Investment Analyst's own: a company whose earnings will be upgraded. The market already discriminates on exactly that: the most upgraded third of Consumer staples stocks rose 23% over six months and the most downgraded third rose 3%. The gap of 20 points is the market's general pattern and is not special to the Industry: across the whole universe the gap was 19 points
  • Beverages: the Sector where the Industry can still add most through valuation, and the Sector where estimates give the least help. Nine of 12 stocks sit below the 35th percentile of their own ranges, and a move to the cheap end costs the median stock nothing. A Beverages stock bought now is helped by the multiple only while money keeps arriving, and is carried further only by its own delivery
  • Food retail: the Sector with the best trading news in the Industry and the least valuation room. The price rise in Food retail is still mostly a higher multiple, and the United Kingdom grocers with the best news trade near the top of their own ranges, so the Industry has little left to give those stocks
  • Household products: a rebound from a deep low while estimates are still being cut. The improvement sits in two companies, Unilever and L'Oreal
  • Food production: little Industry tailwind. Estimate upgrades in Food production have mostly not been paid for, which is the reverse of the Beverages pattern and is the Sector's interest for stock-specific work

The counter-case in brief (section 6). The bearish counter-case is that the Relative Strength is a six-week move out of technology shares, and that technology shares need only steady for the gain to be returned. The bullish counter-case is that the recovery started from a relative valuation low of about 25 years and that 2027 estimates are about to rise.

Confidence: medium on the diagnosis and on the three-month verdict, and low beyond three months. The monthly timing, the flat estimates, the valuation percentiles and the behaviour on falling days all point the same way. Confidence falls beyond three months because our evidence on who owns European Consumer staples after August 2026 is limited to short interest and fund flows, and because the history of past strong runs counts only four cases for the Industry (section 7).

3. The three sources of the Relative Strength, tested

Each of Richard's three sources is tested below on two things: how strong the source is on 18 November 2026, and how long the source can last. Section 3.4 then combines the three, Sector by Sector.

3.1 Fundamental improvement

Verdict: present, weak and narrow. Fundamental improvement is the only durable source, and the estimates do not yet show it. Durability is the highest of the three if the improvement arrives, and the evidence that it is arriving is thin.

The reasoning has three steps. Estimates for 2027 have stopped falling and have not started rising. The share price gain is therefore a gain in the multiple, and against the rest of the market earnings have held the Industry back. The operating news behind the estimates is better at four or five of the largest companies and no better, or worse, across most of the rest.

Why the better volumes have not reached estimates. The connection between the three steps is cost. Volume is recovering because companies stopped leading with price, and companies are now being pushed back to price by energy and oil-linked costs. Unilever said on 28 October 2026 that pricing had "stepped up as planned" to offset higher commodity costs, mainly in Home Care and Personal Care. Morgan Stanley put cumulative hedged cost inflation for 2025 to 2027 at 3.1% of sales for Unilever, 1.9% for Beiersdorf, 1.7% for Reckitt Benckiser and 0.9% for L'Oreal (7 October 2026, via AlphaSense). Where costs are falling, in coffee and cocoa, retailers are asking for the saving back in lower shelf prices. The timing of the two cost movements is against margins in the first half of 2027. The rising inputs arrive first, as hedges expire: Deutsche Bank puts Unilever's net cost inflation at €700 million to €1.0 billion in FY2027, from oil-derived ingredients (11 November 2026, via AlphaSense), and brokers name pulp at Essity, aluminium at Carlsberg and a rise of 4% to 5% in cost per case at Coca-Cola Europacific Partners. The benefit of cheaper cocoa reaches margins only in the second half of 2027, because of hedging delays (DNB Carnegie, 6 November 2026, via AlphaSense). Analysts therefore see better volume, see the margin being spent or squeezed, and leave earnings per share where it was.

The one measurement that says fundamentals matter inside the Industry. The stocks whose 2027 estimates were raised most did far better than the stocks whose estimates were cut most.

  • The most upgraded third of Consumer staples stocks rose 23% over six months and the most downgraded third rose 3%, a gap of 20 points. The universe's gap is 19 points (a rise of 11% against a fall of 8%), so the gap is the market's general pattern and is not special to Consumer staples
  • The rank correlation between a stock's six-month price change and its estimate revision is 0.39, where 1 would mean the two rankings match exactly and 0 would mean no relation. The universe's figure is 0.33

The comparison with the universe carries the insight. The market sorted Consumer staples stocks by their revisions about as much as the market sorted every other stock. What differed is the level: the most downgraded third of Consumer staples stocks still rose 3%, while the most downgraded third of the universe fell 8%. An Industry-wide lift of roughly 11 points was laid over every Consumer staples stock, good or bad, and the stock's own revisions then decided the rest. The Industry-wide lift is the tailwind this memo judges. The sorting by revisions is the Investment Analyst's territory, and the sorting is working normally.

Durability of fundamental improvement. Fundamental improvement would last the 12 to 24 months Richard holds a stock, if the improvement comes. Three things would have to happen for the improvement to come at Industry level: volumes in developed markets turn positive, the new price rises for energy costs hold without losing the volume just regained, and the savings from falling coffee and cocoa costs stay with the producers. None of the three is in evidence on 18 November 2026. The first dated test is the run of full-year results and 2027 guidance in February and March 2027.

3.2 Money returning to an out-of-favour Industry

Verdict: present and strong. Against the stocks' own history the source is less used up than Healthcare's, with a little over a third of the room used; against the market the source is about as used up as Healthcare's, with about two-thirds of the gap closed. Most of the room left is in Beverages. Durability is medium, because a cheap starting point sets up a recovery and does not sustain one.

How out of favour the Industry was:

  • Share prices: the median Consumer staples stock rose 9% in the three years to May 2026 against 37% for the universe, fell 4% in the year to May 2026 against a rise of 12%, and rose 12% in the five years to May 2026 against 35%
  • The multiple: the median Consumer staples multiple sat at the 18th percentile of its own ten-year range in November 2025 and in May 2026, when the median multiple in the universe sat at the 42nd and the 45th
  • Outside views of the same thing: J.P. Morgan Asset Management wrote on 30 April 2026 that European staples were at or close to a 25-year low in valuation against the rest of the market, and Banque de Luxembourg Investments wrote on 17 June 2026 that global staples had moved from a premium of 15% to 20% to the world index in the 2010s to a discount
  • Ownership: a net 32% of global fund managers were underweight staples in July 2026 and a net 19% in August 2026 (Bank of America Global Fund Manager Survey, reported by Trustnet, 18 August 2026)
  • The selling before the rally: net institutional ownership of European staples fell by $25 billion in the twelve months to May 2026, and active managers did 97% of that selling (Rothschild & Co Redburn, 26 May 2026, via AlphaSense). Hedge funds had pushed the sectors that trade like bonds deep into short positions by 31 July 2026 (Jefferies, 27 October 2026, via AlphaSense)

The reasons the Industry fell out of favour are operating reasons as well as market reasons. Brokers and fund houses give a consistent list: rising bond yields from 2022, money concentrating in technology shares in 2024 and 2025, growth that relied on price rises until shoppers resisted, the spread of supermarket own-label products, less drinking among younger adults, weight-loss drugs, and two years of estimate cuts at companies such as Nestle (Banque de Luxembourg Investments, 17 June 2026; broker notes of July to October 2026 via AlphaSense). The list matters for durability. A multiple that fell for market reasons alone can recover on market reasons alone. A multiple that fell partly because growth slowed needs growth to return before the multiple can go all the way back.

3.3 Defensive buying

Verdict: present, strong, and the cause of the timing. Defensive buying drove October and November 2026, which is where the whole six-month gain was made. The buying was money leaving technology shares and was not fear of the economy. Durability is the lowest of the three.

The timing. Public sources describe from the outside the same weeks that section 2 measured.

  • The Nasdaq 100 peaked on 6 October 2026. In October 2026 the MSCI global consumer staples index rose 10.6%, its best month since 1998, global healthcare rose 9.8% and global technology fell more than 13% (European Business Magazine, 10 November 2026, citing the Financial Times)
  • Citi wrote on 9 and 10 November 2026 that defensive sectors had beaten cyclical sectors by up to 15 points in a month, in what Citi called a "steady market, violent rotation"
  • Deutsche Bank wrote on 18 November 2026 that Healthcare and staples had gained most from money leaving banks and technology, and on 11 November 2026 described Unilever as a hedge on liquidity against the risks of artificial intelligence (both via AlphaSense). Jefferies named short covering as a further cause (12 November 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • The causes named for the technology sell-off were doubt about spending on artificial intelligence data centres, the debt used to fund that spending, and, in November 2026, fear that the result of the United States midterm elections of 3 November 2026 would slow the building of data centres (Reuters, 16 October 2026; Kitco, 5 November 2026)

The economy was not the worry. Only 3% of fund managers expected a recession in the November 2026 Bank of America survey (Fortune, 17 November 2026). The index made a record close on 11 November 2026 (Saxo, 12 November 2026), eurozone output grew 0.4% in the third quarter of 2026 against 0.2% expected (Eurostat via Associated Press, 30 October 2026), and the European Central Bank and the Bank of England were raising interest rates.

Buying that comes from doubt about one crowded group of shares lasts as long as that doubt, and goes only where the sellers of those shares choose to put the money.

Three consequences follow.

  • Defensive buying and the return of money to an out-of-favour Industry are one event. The seller of technology shares in October 2026 wanted steadier earnings and bought them where they were cheapest. Without the low starting multiple the money would have gone elsewhere or stayed away, as it stayed away from Utilities. Without the technology sell-off the low multiple would have stayed low, as it stayed low from November 2025 to May 2026. Richard's questions 2 and 3 have a single answer for Consumer staples
  • Bond yields do not explain the move. Five months of data are too few to settle the question, and they are enough to say that the Industry is not trading on yields as it did when it moved with Utilities. Morgan Stanley reads the move the same way: staples rose despite United States bond yields above 5%, and not because of them (2 November 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • The link to technology shares now runs both ways. An Industry that rose because technology shares fell is exposed to technology shares recovering. The correlation with Healthcare is the measure to watch, because a fall in that correlation would show the two Industries going back to trading on their own news

Bernstein expected the move to last under six months (13 November 2026, a United States note).   • Further support: Citi expects defensive shares to keep beating cyclical shares into 2027 (reported 10 November 2026). None of the three is fear of a recession, so the reading that the buying was money leaving crowded shares stands
  • The next dated test: the large technology companies' results in late January and February 2027, when spending plans for 2027 are confirmed or cut. In the November 2026 Bank of America survey, 52% of fund managers expected at least one of the large cloud-computing companies to announce a cut in capital spending within six months (Fortune, 17 November 2026). 4. The Industry through Richard's eleven ratings

Each rating is judged at Industry level for one purpose: what the rating says about whether the tailwind continues. The ratings are Richard's, with his titles. Figures already given in sections 2 and 3 are not repeated. Where the Industry-level evidence is thin, the section says so. Facts from our Sector notes are as at 15 October 2026.

1. External headwinds/tailwinds

Verdict: neutral for earnings, supportive for share prices over three months. The largest external tailwind is a market event, and the largest external headwinds are operating ones. The costs that are falling are being handed to retailers, and the costs that are rising need price increases that shoppers resist.

  • Tailwinds:
  • Money leaving technology shares (section 3.3)
  • Falling coffee and cocoa costs. Jefferies expects coffee prices to fall about 20% in 2026 and 11% in 2027 (3 November 2026), and Deutsche Bank puts cocoa futures about 40% below their peaks (16 November 2026; both via AlphaSense)
  • Growth in emerging markets, where the volume recovery is concentrated
  • Headwinds:
  • Energy and oil-linked costs. Deutsche Bank put a basket of crude oil, palm oil and aluminium nearly 20% higher than a year earlier on 9 October 2026, and Bernstein put aluminium as a 27% cost headwind for European brewers in 2027 (13 August 2026; both via AlphaSense). Hedges expire through 2027
  • Rising interest rates. The European Central Bank and the Bank of England have both raised rates in 2026, and markets priced further rises on 13 and 17 November 2026 (Trading Economics). The precedents of 1994 and 2022, when rates drove the market down, were poor for defensive shares (AlphaSense sector report, October 2026)
  • A threat to the cocoa tailwind from a strong El Niño, with a fall of 21% to 25% in Ivory Coast production forecast for 2026/27 (public-source report, 18 November 2026)

2. Internal change forces/risks

Verdict: supportive for stock-specific work, weak for Industry estimates before 2027 and 2028. Internal change is the Industry's best fundamental feature: it is broad, dated and in management's hands. Internal change reaches earnings per share slowly, because much of the saving is reinvested, so internal change is not a reason for the Industry's multiple to keep rising in the next six months.

  • Portfolio change runs through four Sectors. Unilever is combining its Foods business with McCormick by mid-2027; Reckitt Benckiser has sold its Essential Home brands; Essity is preparing to sell Consumer Tissue in the first half of 2027; Nestle is leaving four businesses; Sainsbury is selling Argos, due to complete in February 2027; and Associated British Foods plans to separate Primark in December 2027 (our Sector notes, 15 October 2026; AlphaSense digest)
  • Cost programmes are large. Nestle targets CHF 3.0 billion of gross savings by the end of 2027 and is cutting 16,000 roles. Diageo targets $1 billion of savings by FY2029, at a cash cost that sources put at $850 million to $1.2 billion (Jefferies, 5 November 2026, via AlphaSense; Diageo annual report, 17 August 2026). Pernod Ricard has a €1 billion programme to its 2028 financial year (broker notes via AlphaSense)
  • The saving is not reaching profit in full. Jefferies estimated on 28 October 2026 that about a quarter of Heineken's gross savings of more than €3 billion had reached operating profit, and that Unilever reinvests half or more of its savings (via AlphaSense)
  • Risks: Unilever expects €400 million to €500 million of overheads to be left behind by its separation (our Household products Sector note, 15 October 2026), and restructuring costs cut cash flow in the year they are spent

3. Value chain dynamics and industry structure

Verdict: neutral for the Industry and uneven within it. Margin is moving along the chain from the brand towards the shelf and towards specialist makers on long contracts, so structural change moves earnings between Sectors and does not raise the Industry's total.

  • Grocers are gaining power over brand owners. Own-label food is more than half of United Kingdom grocery sales, and store brands hold 31% of household product sales in Western Europe (our Sector notes, 15 October 2026). Grocers also sell advertising to the brands on their shelves
  • Falling input costs flow through to the shelf. Western European retail coffee prices fell about 6% on a year earlier, which supported volume growth of about 5% (Jefferies, 30 October 2026, via AlphaSense). The producer keeps the volume and gives up the price

4. Strong business SRCA and BTE; management; financial profile

Verdict: supportive of a floor under share prices and not of a rising multiple. The businesses are high in margin and cash, low in growth, and were priced for decline. Cash returns at low multiples raise earnings per share without growth, which limits the downside and does not create upgrades to sales. The Industry-level evidence on management quality is thin.

  • Business quality: the median operating margin of the 32 stocks with consensus forecasts is 12%, and 15% to 16% in Beverages. Sales grew 3% a year over the last three reported years. Heineken's earnings per share grew 1.4% a year from 2019 to 2025 (Deutsche Bank, 5 October 2026, via AlphaSense). The market had moved large Beverages companies from a 50% premium to the market in 2019 to an 11% discount (Bernstein, 12 October 2026, cited in our Beverages Sector note)
  • Financial profile: buybacks are running or returning. Imperial Brands announced £1.5 billion for its 2027 financial year, Tesco £950 million, Haleon £500 million (a headline only), and Unilever plans €6 billion over 2026 to 2029 (public sources, October 2026; AlphaSense digest)
  • Earnings quality needs care. Nestle's first-half 2026 margin included a one-off pension credit, and part of Coca-Cola HBC's 2026 improvement came from currency and lower finance costs (broker notes via AlphaSense)

5. Pricing power and operating leverage (and others)

Verdict: neutral, leaning against for mass brand owners. Weak pricing power is the main reason the better volumes are not becoming upgrades. The Industry's earnings respond to volume, and volume is exactly what the next round of price rises puts at risk.

  • Pricing power is weaker than in 2022 to 2024. Deutsche Bank finds that Unilever's volume growth has fallen below 2% whenever its pricing exceeded 2.5% (28 July 2026), and Bernstein's scanner data of 14 October 2026 showed Unilever's volume down 2.3% as price and mix rose 4.5% (both via AlphaSense). Danone's third-quarter growth of 4.0% was 3.6 points of price and 0.4 points of volume and mix (DairyReporter, 28 October 2026)
  • Pricing power survives where a shopper cannot swap to a cheaper copy: skin care and oral care that professionals recommend, medical nutrition, the Coca-Cola bottlers' drinks and cigarettes (our Sector notes, 15 October 2026)
  • Operating leverage is high where margins are thin. Half a point of operating margin moves Tesco's adjusted earnings per share by 14% and Hilton Food's by 31% (our Sector notes, 15 October 2026). At the large brand owners the leverage is spent on advertising
  • The reinforcing loop the brand owners want is savings paying for advertising, advertising winning volume, and volume spreading fixed costs. The new price rises forced by energy costs threaten the loop at its second step

  • Track record: Nestle cut guidance twice in 2024, and Rothschild & Co Redburn described two years of earnings downgrades on 27 July 2026 (via AlphaSense)

Appendix G. Method, sources and limits

The measurements are the authority for every price, revision and multiple in the memo, and the tests built on them show how a group of shares behaved, not why investors bought it. Appendix G sets out what was measured, how the three sources were tested, and where the evidence is thin.

What was measured, and from which files

  • Share prices and the index: our price files hold daily prices for 1,358 European stocks and the Stoxx Europe 600 to the close of 18 November 2026. Relative Strength is a stock's return less the index's return over the same period, in percentage points. One, three, six and twelve months are 21, 63, 126 and 252 trading days, and the monthly figures use calendar months
  • Multiples: our valuation files hold each stock's two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple (the share price divided by the earnings per share that consensus expects two years ahead) and the multiple's range over ten years. A stock's percentile is the place of today's multiple in the stock's own range, with 0 the cheapest. Sixty-eight of the 72 Consumer staples stocks have a multiple
  • Estimate revisions: FactSet consensus for calendar year 2027, as at 17 November 2026: the change in earnings per share and sales estimates over one to twelve months, the stocks upgraded and downgraded over three months, and analysts raising estimates in the last 45 days less analysts cutting, as a share of all estimates
  • Consensus forecasts: FactSet consensus by financial year, held in our files for 32 of the 72 stocks. The first financial year not yet reported is FY2026 for 23 of the 32 stocks and FY2027 for nine

How groups were formed, and why three figures can differ

  • The median stock is the middle stock of a group, so one extreme mover cannot shift the median
  • An equal-weighted basket averages every stock in the group each day, with each stock's daily move capped at 25%. The daily tests need a basket, and a few large movers can pull a basket
  • Weighting by company size uses each stock's market value at 31 August 2026 and shows what an investor in the index experienced. The ten largest stocks are 69% of the market value of Consumer staples, led by Nestle at 15% and L'Oreal at 14%. Brokers weight by size and compare with the market: on the brokers' measure European staples are still cheap, at the 20th percentile of a 15-year range against MSCI Europe (Morgan Stanley, 2 November 2026, via AlphaSense). Against their own ten-year ranges the ten largest companies sit at about the 41st percentile weighted by size, against the 27th for the median stock (our calculation), so the room left from the multiple is by company and not by size
  • The three measures can differ widely: over six months General retail is (1) point on the median stock, 10 points on the equal-weighted basket and 13 points by size
  • The split of a basket's gain by falling and rising days adds up daily differences, so the split's total can differ from the compounded basket figure: for General retail over six months the split adds to 7 points and the compounded figure is 10
  • The three-month figure has two values: the median Consumer staples stock is 9 points ahead of the index on the window of 63 trading days and 8 points ahead on a window of three calendar months. The memo uses the 63-day figure

The tests for the three sources, and what each can show

  • Fundamental improvement is tested by revisions to consensus 2027 estimates, by whether the most upgraded stocks rose most, and by splitting each share price change into forward earnings and the multiple. Forward earnings are the share price divided by the two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple. Each of the three figures is the median of a group, so the two parts need not add up to the price change: for Household products over six months the figures are 16%, 1% and 12%. Forward earnings also rise because the two-year window moves on to a later year, with no analyst changing a number
  • Money returning to an out-of-favour group is tested by how far the group lagged in the three years to May 2026, by the percentile of the multiple, by whether the cheapest stocks rose most, and by the value of a move in the multiple. The value of a move in the multiple is the change in share price if a stock's multiple moved to the cheap end, the 35th percentile, the middle or the 75th percentile of its own ten-year range with earnings unchanged. A low multiple can also reflect lower growth, and none of the tests observes money moving
  • Defensive buying is tested by the month in which the gain came; by the basket's beta, which is the basket's move for a 1% move in the index; by the split of the basket's gain between days the index fell and days the index rose or was flat; and by the correlation of the basket's daily moves against the index with those of Healthcare, Telecoms and Utilities. The tests show that a group behaved defensively and do not show why investors bought the group
  • Past episodes: for each month-end over ten years, the test takes the six-month return of the group's median stock less that of the median stock of the whole universe. A strong episode is a six-month figure in the top 15% of the group's own history, and the test records what followed over three, six, twelve and 18 months

The limits

  • Few large down days: the index fell 1% or more on nine days in six months, so the split of gains rests mainly on the 58 days of any fall
  • Past episodes are few, use today's members only, and use a different yardstick: Consumer staples has four earlier strong episodes in ten years, one of them March 2020, and the Sectors have five or six each, too few to support a probability. The test of past episodes runs to September 2026, before the gain of October 2026, and companies that failed or were taken over are missing from the history. The test of past episodes ends at a month-end and compares with the universe's median stock, so the test can disagree with Relative Strength against the index, as the test does for General retail (Appendix F)
  • Forecasts for 32 of 72 stocks: consensus forecasts by financial year are held for eleven of 12 Beverages stocks, four of six in Tobacco, one of six in Household products, four of 13 in Food retail, eight of 23 in Food production and four of 12 in General retail
  • One month can dominate: weighted by size, Consumer staples gained 8.5 points on the index over six months, and 7.7 points of the gain came in October 2026
  • The start date matters: the median Consumer staples stock is 5 points ahead of the index from four months before 18 November 2026, 9 from six months and (0.5) from nine months
  • Small groups: a median of six stocks, as in Household products, moves when one stock moves, and the counts quoted inside Appendices D to F are our own calculations from stock-by-stock figures rounded to whole points
  • Bond yields were not re-checked: the public-source researcher collected the ten-year government bond yields on 17 November 2026
