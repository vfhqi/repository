Cellavision (CEVI-SE) — what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results strengthen the existing case materially, and in a way the case did not anticipate: the business cleared most of what the Park was waiting for while the share price took most of the return the Park was meant to capture.

What changed

The case was rated on the first quarter of 2026: a 7% organic sales decline (the case carried 6.6%), a 26% organic fall in EMEA on Sysmex destocking, a single-day share-price crash and a new chief executive not yet in the chair. It asked for three things before re-opening: EMEA order stabilisation in the second or third quarter of 2026, FDA 510(k) clearance for the Bone Marrow Aspirate application on or ahead of a mid-2026 timeline, and strategic continuity in Steve Ferguson's first full quarterly print.

What the company reported on 29 October 2026 for the quarter to 30 September 2026:

  • Net sales SEK 200m, up 13.6% reported and 13.4% organic, against a Modular Finance consensus and DNB Carnegie estimate of 9% organic and SEK 193m. The second quarter of 2026 had already printed SEK 200m.
  • Americas SEK 89m, up 32% organic. APAC SEK 24m, up 91% organic, despite continued cost containment in China. EMEA SEK 86m, down 10% against a Q3 2025 comparison of SEK 96m that the review describes as exceptionally strong distributor stocking. Management stated that the Sysmex inventory adjustment in Europe has concluded.
  • Instruments SEK 105m, against DNB Carnegie's SEK 104m. Reagents SEK 45m, up 12% year on year and 10% above the broker's SEK 41m. Software and other SEK 49m.
  • EBITDA SEK 64m, up 28%, 14% above consensus of SEK 56m; margin 32% against 28%. Excluding SEK 4m of chief-executive transition costs, EBITDA SEK 68m and a 34% margin. Price increases implemented in the second quarter reached full effect. R&D fell to 19% of sales from 24%; management now expects a normalised 20% to 21%. Operating expenses flat at SEK 82m.
  • Gross margin 66% against 69%, on amortisation of capitalised development rising to SEK 5m from SEK 2m, mix and currency. Capitalised R&D SEK 16m against SEK 14m.
  • Net profit SEK 40.17m, up 28%; EPS SEK 1.68. Operating cash flow SEK 68.11m against SEK 29.63m, including a SEK 12m working-capital release. Cash SEK 219m, no interest-bearing debt.
  • The FDA review of the Bone Marrow Aspirate application is ongoing; management's objective is clearance before the end of 2026. A Capital Markets Day on 17 February 2027 is to present strategy through 2030. The 15% organic growth and above-30% EBITDA margin targets are unchanged.

Sell-side response: DNB Carnegie raised its target to SEK 200 from SEK 185 on 30 October 2026, lifted 2026 to 2028 adjusted EPS by 3% to 6% and kept its Buy; the shares were SEK 181.6 at the report. The review found no post-earnings note from any other broker. The broker's own two open questions are whether Americas can hold 32% against a record prior year, and when the FDA clears the application.

The share price: the dashboard has the shares at SEK 204.0 on 26 November 2026, 33.6% above a 200-day average of SEK 152.69, 95th percentile relative strength, up 69.2% over six months, against a 52-week high of SEK 207.0. The case was written at SEK 148.4 and leaned on a SEK 211 broker target and a forward P/E it read at the floor of the ten-year range. The price now sits above DNB Carnegie's new SEK 200 target and within 4% of the case's SEK 211; the dashboard's P/E is 23.1x at the 20th percentile of its own ten-year history.

The challenge

The case's own strongest counter-view, that Sysmex's EMEA pull-back was structural rather than timing, has lost on the evidence. Two consecutive SEK 200m quarters, management's explicit statement that the adjustment has concluded, and an EMEA decline of 10% against a stocked comparison rather than a repeat of 26% are what the case said timing would look like. We do not put it up again. Three readings the case does not hold are harder to dismiss.

First: the beat is price and mix, not conversion volume. Instruments landed SEK 1m above the broker's number; almost all of the SEK 7m sales beat came from reagents and software, and management credits price increases that reached full effect in this quarter, plus improved price realisation, for the operating leverage. The case's thesis is a manual-to-digital conversion runway, and a conversion runway shows up in instrument units, which did what was expected. If price is carrying several points of the 13.4%, underlying volume growth is probably nearer the 5% to 9% the company has delivered against its 15% target in every disclosed year, and the delivery-congruency failure the case flagged has not been cured, only dressed. Add that the case's "most certain driver", R&D normalising from 22% of sales toward 18% to 19% by 2027, has already been spent: R&D is 19% now and management says the sustainable level is 20% to 21%, so this line is likely to give back rather than give more. The next leg of margin must then come from gross margin, which fell 300 basis points, or from opex leverage on volume the company has not yet shown. For this reading to be right, price would have to account for most of the gap between the 9% expected and the 13.4% delivered. The company does not disclose the split, so the report cannot rule it out.

Second: Americas is the next false friend. The case named distributor channel fill as a live negative guardrail, citing precautionary stocking in late 2025 ahead of tariff uncertainty. The Q3 2025 EMEA figure of SEK 96m was that stocking; it produced a 26% decline two quarters later and a 10% decline a year later. Americas has just printed 32% organic growth on strong instrument placements and large connected platforms through the same indirect partners, with a US launch of the Bone Marrow Aspirate application expected on FDA clearance. Partners building instrument inventory ahead of a software launch is the mechanism the case warned about, relocated to a different region. For this to be right, Americas instrument volumes would need to fall sharply against this comparison in 2027 while reagent and software growth held, and the first readable sign would plausibly be Sysmex's own commentary rather than Cellavision's. The review itself ranks the sustainability of the 32% as the single most important open question, which is to say the beat is not yet proven to be demand.

Third: the clearing event has cleared, and it cleared without us. Half of the case's reason to Park rather than kill was mispricing: a 21.78% single-day crash, a P/E read at the floor of its range, a 53% discount to peers, a SEK 211 target. At SEK 204 with a 23.1x multiple and the only published target below the price, the secondary setup the case identified, a huge change-from-current clearing event in solid quality, is complete, and the primary setup, change-from-current clearing in a high-quality compounder, is most of the way through. What re-opens now is not the case the memo rated. It is a company at a full multiple with a single broker, over 83% of revenue through one distributor on the case's figures, and a competitive picture the review has widened: Ozelle integrating cell imaging into the blood-count analyzer itself, an AI classification model validated across three Japanese hospitals, pay-per-slide pathology, Beckman Coulter's own microscopy line, while management still names manual microscopy as the main competitor. The case does not accommodate this because its clock was set to evidence, and the evidence arrived with a 69% six-month move attached. For this reading to be wrong, the remaining return would need to come from the FDA clearance and from the subscription-licence shift the review says consensus ignores, neither of which is in the SEK 200 target arithmetic yet.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days from now, carries whatever estimate and target changes land after DNB Carnegie's 30 October note, any second broker that re-initiates, and the price against the SEK 200 and SEK 211 marks. It does not carry a price-versus-volume split, which the company does not disclose; it does not carry Americas instrument units; and it does not carry the FDA decision unless it falls inside the window. It is the right input for re-rating MR2 and the valuation side of E1, and it settles none of the three readings above.

  • The first reading, price not volume, is only indirectly testable. Reagent sales holding above SEK 45m sequentially in the Q4 report on 4 February 2027, the review's own tripwire, would say the installed base is growing; instruments beating estimate for a second quarter would say conversion is accelerating. No disclosure the company has committed to gives the split, so this stays open as a judgement, not a fact.
  • The second reading, Americas channel fill, is tested by the Q4 Americas figure against a record comparison and, earlier, by Sysmex's own commentary on hematology orders and inventory, which the case already named as the lead indicator. The first clean read is probably the Q1 2027 report, where the EMEA comparison turns easy at the same moment the Americas comparison turns hard; a quarter in which EMEA looks strong and Americas stalls would need to be read for what it is.
  • The third reading, the event has cleared, is settled by what the Capital Markets Day on 17 February 2027 does to the driver count and the 15% target, and by whether FDA clearance arrives by the end of 2026 as management targets. Clearance adds a US software upgrade cycle that is not in a SEK 200 target; a slip past early 2027 is the review's own named signal of a commercial roadblock, and the case's trigger would then have failed twice.
