Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external forces are lifting OEM's 2026 earnings well above trend, but almost everything that is lifting them is cyclical or temporary (the industrial recovery, refilling and pre-buying, the Irish data-centre project, a weaker krona), while the durable external supports (automation, electrification, consolidation) are worth about 5% to 6% organic growth a year, not 14%. The rate of external improvement peaks around Q4 2026, so 2027 will show arithmetic deceleration that the market may mistake for a turn. The force most likely to move the share price over the next 6 to 18 months, the discount rate acting on compounder multiples, runs against the company. The operating model should take the cyclical lift in FY2026 and the first half of FY2027 and then revert to through-cycle rates; it should not extrapolate Q3 2026.

C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

A601st of 11

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. In the product market, competitive intensity is stable and benign, and OEM is currently winning share. In the market for acquisitions, intensity is rising. The change that matters for the model is therefore not price competition but the price OEM pays for growth.

Why.

  • Product market: OEM's 14% organic growth in Q3 compares with Addtech's 1% to 2%, Indutrade's 5%, Lagercrantz's 6%, Bufab's 5.3% and Beijer Ref's 8% in their latest quarters. Competitors are margin-first serial acquirers and sub-scale family firms; no price war occurred through the 2023 to 2025 downturn, and Lagercrantz is raising prices to offset inflation.
  • Digital competitors (Digi-Key, Mouser) are widening industrial catalogues, which bears on OEM Electronics and standard components (about 9% of sales), not on the customised and design-in business.
  • Market for acquisitions: the large Swedish compounders have historically focused on product-owning targets, which left pure distribution targets less contested. OEM has now widened its own mandate to product-owning niches, so it is entering the peers' hunting ground. Experts describe Nordic multiples moving from 4x to 6x toward 8x to 10x EBITDA; private-equity platforms are moving into the lower mid-market; small bilateral deals below EUR 50m still trade at stable multiples.

For the model. No pricing pressure on gross margin from competitors in the base case. Assume acquisition multiples drift up from about 6.5x toward 7x to 8x EBITA over the forecast period, which lowers the return on each acquired krona rather than revenue.

C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

B552nd of 11

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. The net effect is a tailwind over the next six months and a mild headwind risk thereafter. The tailwind is currency: a weaker krona lifts sales on translation and, because currency clauses re-price sales quickly while inventory was bought at older rates, helps the gross margin for a quarter or two. The headwind risk is renewed cost inflation (freight, energy, wages) that has to be passed on case by case, because contracts carry no inflation clauses. Supplier concentration is low and stable.

Why.

  • Currency structure: purchases are 59% EUR, 21% USD and only 10% SEK; purchased goods are about 64% of sales; no financial hedges are used; currency clauses cover 68% of sales (71% in 2024) and adjust 80% to 100% of moves above a 2.25% threshold; there is about a three-month inventory lag. In 2025 a strengthening krona cut reported sales by 3% and caused the Q2 2025 EBITA miss of 16%; in Q3 2026 currency turned positive and DNB Carnegie cites it as a reason for its upgrade.
  • Inflation: Asia to North Europe freight about 25% above a year earlier in late summer 2026; Brent at about USD 104 in September and USD 111 in the week of 23 October; European input-cost inflation accelerating in September.
  • Supply: more than 400 suppliers, none above 3% of sales; some components are single-sourced in Asia; losing one key supplier can cost hundreds of customers.
  • The Q3 evidence fits this reading: the gross-margin proxy rose about 1.1 points while the EBITA margin was flat, so operating costs rose about as fast as the gross-margin gain.

For the model. Treat the currency gain as temporary: do not capitalise it into the 2027 to 2029 margin. Hold the gross margin at about 36.5% with a symmetric band of about 50 basis points for currency.

C-07

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

C+453rd of 11

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Macro forces help the company's profit and loss account now and hurt its valuation. At company level, the industrial recovery and a weaker krona lift 2026 earnings, and net cash removes the interest-rate channel. At sector and industry level, the force that bites hardest is the discount rate: Swedish compounders trade on their multiple more than on their earnings, and that multiple is hostage to bond yields.

Why.

  • Company: 81% Nordic sales tie revenue to Swedish and Finnish industrial production; Sweden's manufacturing PMI is 58.1; currency is unhedged.
  • Sector (industrial distribution): distributors move with factory orders one to two quarters behind; the September PMIs support Q4 2026 and Q1 2027.
  • Industry and market: the United States 10-year yield is near 5.10%, markets price up to three European Central Bank rate rises by mid-2027, Nordea expects the Riksbank to raise its policy rate to 2.25% in 2027, Brent is at USD 111, and the weekly Fitness for Fighting digest calls the regime risk-off and broadening, with its Momentum tilt moved to Underweight. In the 2022 sell-off the compounders' median multiple halved from 30x to 15x (Redeye). The gap between compounders' earnings yield and the 10-year euro swap rate narrowed in Q3 2026 (DNB Carnegie), and Addtech and Lagercrantz fell about 5% over three months.

For the model. Use the macro tailwind in 2026 and early 2027 earnings, and carry the macro headwind in the valuation work (the third and fourth sets), not in the operating forecast.

What other material external dynamic, positive or negative, is at work?

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Four further dynamics matter, two positive and two negative. Positive: the succession wave among family-owned small distributors (a steady supply of targets), and the market's current preference for "newly lit" acquirers whose earnings can outrun a de-rating. For the model. No profit and loss line; these dynamics set the acquisition supply (supportive) and the valuation risk (negative).

C-03

To what extent is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved, and in which direction does it act on the company?

F529th of 11

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. The disruption force is disintermediation and price transparency: suppliers selling direct, large customers buying through procurement portals, global digital catalogues, and in time artificial-intelligence procurement tools and software ecosystems that fix component specifications before a distributor is involved. It acts against the company, slowly, and it is neither strengthening measurably nor being disproved. It is a three-year margin risk on the standard-product share of sales, not an 18-month revenue risk.

Why.

  • Evidence that it exists: a former OEM Electronics sales manager says large customers bypass distributors and extract price concessions of about 3% a year (Danfoss and Grundfos named); a Jefferies automation expert reports hardware margins falling three to five points a year where distributors do not add integration or service; Rothschild & Co Redburn describes online transparency weakening breadth and scale as barriers.

For the model. No explicit disruption charge in the base case to 2028; treat it as one of the reasons not to model real price increases and as a 20 to 50 basis-point downside to the gross margin in a bear case.
