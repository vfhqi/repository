The company faces massive uncollected state subsidy receivables totaling RON 2,544 million while consolidated EBITDA increased by 7.7% in H1 2026, creating a severe decoupling between accrual accounting profits and actual operational cash collection patterns.

The utility confronts acute transaction FX risks as all cash revenues are received in Romanian Leu while major capital equipment procurement and EUR 500 million green bond obligations require Euro cash outflows, expanding net statement of financial position exposure to negative RON 3,820 million in

2025. Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Across the comprehensive investigation into Societatea Energetica Electrica, three statistical outliers emerge as genuinely extreme cases that deviate substantially from regional utility peer norms and warrant heightened analytical scrutiny. The first outlier concerns the complete absence of published short-seller reports or activist campaigns, which stands in stark contrast to the significant operational and balance sheet vulnerabilities documented throughout the analysis.

Despite RON 2,544 million in uncollected state subsidies, rising unhedged EUR debt exposures reaching RON 3,820 million, and structural transaction mismatches between RON revenues and EUR capital expenditures, no formal bearish institutional campaigns have materialized against the company. This absence is particularly striking given that Western European utilities with far less severe balance sheet imbalances routinely face active short-selling pressure.

The void of hostile scrutiny may reflect the structural illiquidity of the Bucharest Stock Exchange rather than fundamental strength, but the divergence from typical market disciplining mechanisms represents a genuine statistical anomaly when benchmarked against comparable European utilities with similar sovereign exposure concentrations. The second extreme outlier relates to the explosive expansion of net foreign currency liability exposure over a compressed three-year period.

Electrica's net EUR-denominated short position surged from RON 348 million in 2023 to RON 3,820 million in 2025, representing an increase of approximately 997 percent in just two fiscal years. This rate of unhedged foreign currency balance sheet accumulation dramatically exceeds peer utility patterns, with domestic peer CNTEE Transelectrica maintaining minimal EUR liabilities of only RON 2 million and peer Transgaz carrying moderate unhedged EUR loans of RON 1,208 million representing 29 percent of total borrowings.

The velocity of Electrica's foreign currency liability expansion positions the company as a severe outlier within the Romanian state-regulated utility sector, where peers have deliberately maintained conservative local-currency debt profiles despite access to cheaper European Investment Bank financing. This differential trajectory suggests either fundamentally different capital allocation priorities or substantially weaker balance sheet risk management compared to the domestic peer median.

The third critical outlier manifests in the acute divergence between reported profitability metrics and actual operational cash generation patterns. During the first half of 2026, the company reported consolidated EBITDA growth of 7.7 percent and net profit expansion of 8.6 percent year-on-year, yet simultaneously experienced a net cash decrease of RON 312 million depleting immediate corporate liquid reserves. This simultaneous occurrence of robust accrual-based earnings alongside severe cash consumption represents an extreme statistical divergence from typical utility operating patterns, where regulated distribution businesses normally convert EBITDA into predictable operating cash flows with minimal timing lags.

The magnitude of this cashversus-earnings disconnect substantially exceeds peer utility norms and creates a deceptive false-friend indicator where superficial financial strength masks underlying liquidity strain driven by uncollected sovereign receivables and regulatory recovery timing mismatches.
