Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The pricing premium of package holidays represents the critical buffer protecting tour operators from volatile seat-only fare declines and relentless jet fuel cost inflation.
  2. Mix shifts toward flight-only bookings or unhedged capacity expose leisure travel operators to severe margin dilution and downside earnings per share revisions.

Core debate and pricing resilience

1.
Targeted inquiry: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector H.11. Airlines and travel, with Jet2 (JET2-GB) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: In bookings so far for winter 2026-27 and summer 2027, how have package holiday prices moved against seat-only fares at Jet2, TUI and easyJet holidays, and is the package price premium holding as households trade down? How fast is the mix at Jet2 shifting from packages to flight-only, especially at its new London Gatwick base, and what does that shift do to its margin? What synergies and margin gains do brokers model for Norwegian Air Shuttle from Nordic Leisure Travel Group once the purchase closes, and how do Nordic package prices compare with seat-only fares on the same routes?" is the core debate that will shape investor sentiment and valuation multiples for European leisure travel companies.

Morgan Stanley reported that Jet2's ticket-only fares fell by mid-to-high single digits while package prices remained flat to up low-single digits. This trend means that a faster shift towards flight-only sales directly dilutes operating margins because packages generate crucial accommodation revenues. If flight-only bookings continue to outpace packages, Jet2's earnings per share will face negative revisions.

Morgan Stanley notes that achieving a 3.0% operating margin in the Markets and Airline division would increase TUI's earnings per share by 40%. However, progress is slowed by intense competition in the UK market and rising fuel cost headwinds. This indicates that TUI's outer-year earnings will remain under pressure unless dynamic packaging offsets airline capacity risks.

This trend could ultimately depress earnings per share if operators cannot pass through hotel cost increases to consumers.

Section 2 – Package vs Seat-Only Pricing and Premium Resilience

Section summary
  1. Our confidence in the resilience of package pricing is medium because UK license capacity growth is moderating to four percent while flight-only fares remain under significant pressure.
  2. Stronger tour operator supply discipline helps protect package margins, but households trading down could erode the traditional pricing premium if capacity is not tightly managed.

License capacity and market shares

1.
Moderating capacity growth: The Civil Aviation Authority published the October 2026 ATOL register showing that aggregate UK package holiday licensed capacity has grown by 3.0% year-on-year to 34.6 million passengers. Deutsche Bank reported in October 2026 that this growth reflects the ongoing popularity of traditional package holidays and expansion in city-break bundles. This capacity expansion indicates that package demand remains structurally resilient even as consumers look to lock in travel costs.
2.
Disciplined market leaders: Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that market leader Jet2 expanded its ATOL authorisations by a conservative 2.9% to 7.25 million passengers, representing a 21% market share. The same broker noted that TUI maintained a flat licensed capacity at 5.9 million passengers, which equates to a 17% market share. This disciplined capacity management suggests that both major players are prioritising yield over volume growth to protect their core margins.
3.
Rapid challenger expansion: The Civil Aviation Authority register from October 2026 shows easyJet holidays increasing capacity by 11.4% to 3.9 million passengers, representing an 11% market share. Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that easyJet holidays continues to take market share by converting existing airline customers to packaged products. This capital-light expansion allows easyJet holidays to consistently capture profitable market share without incurring dedicated fleet risk.

Diverging pricing dynamics

4.
Resilient package premium: Morgan Stanley reported in September 2026 that Jet2's package holiday pricing remained flat to up low-single digits while its seat-only fares declined by midto-high single digits. The broker highlighted that bundled holidays successfully protect operators from volatile seat-only pricing declines. This price dynamic ensures that Jet2 can maintain resilient operating margins even when standalone airline tickets face downward pressure.
5.
Persistent yield pressure: Deutsche Bank projected in July 2026 that Jet2's average flight-only pricing would fall by 7.8% for the fiscal year ending March 2027 while package pricing would rise by 3.0%. To support its average load factor in a highly competitive market, the company continues to invest heavily in promotional flightonly pricing. This downward ticket yield pressure means that Jet2's overall flight margins will degrade unless package holiday volumes expand.
6.
Dynamic margin preservation: TUI AG noted in its September 2026 trading update that its average selling prices have remained resilient due to own-risk capacity reductions and growth in dynamically packaged products. Deutsche Bank confirmed in September 2026 that TUI's holiday experiences division continues to deliver rate growth despite Markets and Airline division softening. These actions show that flexible dynamic packaging preserves margins even when consumers show signs of value consciousness.

Booking momentum and trading

7.
Soft winter outlook: TUI AG reported in September 2026 that booked revenues for the Winter 2026/27 season were down 7% overall, driven by a 9% decline in the UK and a 4% decline in Germany. Deutsche Bank highlighted in September 2026 that these booking patterns reflect shorter consumer lead times amid economic uncertainty. This subdued winter outlook indicates that operators must carefully manage winter schedules to prevent extensive off-season discounting.
8.
Encouraging lates recovery: Morgan Stanley observed in September 2026 that UK package-holiday demand has remained resilient with late bookings driving positive momentum. The broker noted that Jet2's booked passengers through August 2026 rose by 8.8% year-on-year, outstripping its seat capacity growth of 7.6%. This robust close-in volume recovery suggests that households prioritize their annual holidays despite general discretionary spending constraints.
9.
Contrary evidence: Rothschild and Co Redburn reported in October 2026 that rising accommodation cost inflation is outstripping tour operator pricing power, resulting in margin compression. The broker warned that average European short-haul fares fell by 0.5% in September 2026, driven by a 3.7% decline in low-cost carrier pricing. This downward price pressure suggests that the package premium is eroding as operators absorb hotel cost increases.
10.
Steady summer planning: Morgan Stanley stated in October 2026 that UK consumer holiday intentions for 2027 remain solid with early bookings pacing ahead of historical levels. Travel agents surveyed by the broker reported a strong wave of early bookings for Summer 2027, driven by budget-conscious households trying to lock in pricing. This forward booking visibility suggests that operators can secure advanced customer deposits to stabilize their working capital.

Low cost carrier dynamics

11.
Aggressive capacity addition: Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that short-haul narrowbody capacity growth continues to outpace UK demand, driven by Wizz Air's massive 141% winter seat expansion. The broker warned that more seats chasing weak UK demand leaves pricing as the primary clearing mechanism for short-haul carriers. This excessive winter supply threatens to undermine seat-only fare stability across the entire European aviation sector.
12.
Disciplined flight cuts: Ryanair Holdings PLC announced in October 2026 that it is cutting selected winter flight frequencies by 2% to manage its unhedged fuel exposure. Bernstein Research reported in October 2026 that Ryanair's peerless cost minimization model allows it to remain profitable even during high jet fuel cost cycles. This industry leading cost structure ensures that Ryanair can maintain highly competitive entry fares while weaker European short-haul carriers fail.
13.
Standalone yield risk: Morgan Stanley reported in September 2026 that European short-haul fares are experiencing negative yields due to regional overcapacity. The broker highlighted that standalone airlines are highly vulnerable to these yield declines because they lack package holiday revenue buffers. This structural exposure means that pure-play airlines face severe margin dilution compared to integrated travel groups during down-trading cycles.

Core package pricing resilience

14.
Strong consumer prioritization: Morgan Stanley's AlphaWise consumer survey in October 2026 revealed that 86% of European travelers intend to prioritize an annual holiday despite rising living costs. The broker noted that 61% of respondents plan to cut back on other non-essential spending, such as eating out or buying clothing, to protect their travel budgets. This strong consumer commitment explains why package holiday demand remains structurally robust in a challenging macroeconomic environment.
15.
Other side of the story: Bernstein Research warned in February 2026 that TUI's reliance on integrated aviation capacity exposes it to severe cost inflation that could wipe out package holiday pricing gains. The broker argued that traditional operators are losing market share at an increasing rate to agile online travel agents. This structural shift means that traditional package premiums may collapse if dynamic competitors aggressively discount flight-only components to capture value-conscious households.

Section 3 – Jet2's Mix Shift and London Gatwick Margin Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Jet2's package booking mix fell by over three percentage points to sixty-three percent in the fiscal year ended March 2026 as low-margin flight-only volumes grew fifteen times faster than packages.
  2. Structural profit margins face severe pressure because flight-only ticket pricing is structurally low-margin or breakeven at best, whereas packages are highly accretive due to negotiated hotel discount margins.
  3. Performance at the new London Gatwick base is pacing ahead of expectations with a higher package holiday mix and plans to expand to seven aircraft for Summer 2027.

Booking mix shift and volume trends

1.
Declining package holiday mix: Jet2 reported that its package holiday customer mix represented 63.3% of overall passengers flown for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, down from 66.5% in the fiscal year ended March 31, 2025. Flown passengers grew by 5% to 20.83 million in the fiscal year 2026, with package holiday customers increasing by only 1% to 6.62 million. This flat package trajectory indicates that the group's rapid volume growth is being driven primarily by standalone seat sales.
2.
Rapid flight-only growth: Jet2 experienced a substantial 15% increase in flight-only passenger volumes to 7.64 million passengers for the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, up from 6.62 million in the prior year. Chief Executive Officer Steve Heapy stated during the Q4 2026 earnings call that demand for the flight-only product was strong as customers booked closer to departure and sought last-minute deals. This shift illustrates the group's willingness to add airline seats even if they do not convert to higher-value packaged bookings.
3.
Broker analysis on mix: Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that Jet2 is continuing to grow its overall airline rapidly while managing the packaged component more conservatively. The broker highlighted that while Jet2 carried 6.6 million package customers in the fiscal year 2026 (up 1% year-on-year), its flight-only passenger volumes rose by 15%. This divergence has major profit implications because packages generate the accommodation income required to support higher group margins. Broker warning on dilution: Rothschild & Co Redburn reported in November 2025 that Jet2's share of package customers fell by over 3 percentage points year-on-year as airline seats shifted to flight-only. The broker warned that this shift is highly negative from a margin perspective because the flight-only business is essentially breakeven at best. This contrasts with easyJet, which has seen continued expansion in its package mix as it builds out its easyJet holidays business.

Operational and structural margin impact

4.
Package component cost economics: Rothschild & Co Redburn reported in June 2024 that flight costs represent only about 25% of the total price of a package holiday, meaning that flights are not where tour operators generate significant margin. Accommodation is far more relevant to profitability because the room rate is the largest component of the headline package price. Integrated operators like Jet2 generate better margins only when they negotiate deep, direct discounts on hotel inventory. 98 99 & Co Redburn Airline risk exposure: Rothschild & Co Redburn reported in June 2024 that Jet2 is the most exposed UK tour operator to weaker airline fares because it operates 100% of its own airlift. If short-haul pricing deteriorates, flight-only yields fall rapidly, whereas TUI's partially flexible flight capacity and On the Beach's asset-light dynamic model leave them less exposed to airline risk. Consequently, an oversupply of short-haul seats directly dilutes Jet2's standalone flight margins. 101 102 & Co Redburn Dynamic package revenue management: Rothschild & Co Redburn reported in May 2026 that Jet2 utilizes dynamic revenue management across its combined flight-and-hotel inventory to absorb operating cost inflation. Over 80% of group revenue comes from packaged products rather than flight-only tickets, allowing pricing, inventory, and service decisions to run through a single operational stack. When bookings shift to flight-only, Jet2 loses this single-stack dynamic pricing buffer. 104 105 & Co Redburn Peer margin comparison: Morgan Stanley reported in September 2026 that standalone short-haul airlines are highly vulnerable to negative yields because they lack package holiday revenue buffers. In contrast, integrated travel groups can cross-subsidize flight capacity with high-margin holiday ancillaries. However, if Jet2's package mix continues to decline, its structural profitability will increasingly resemble a lower-margin standalone low-cost carrier.

Despite Gatwick seats going on sale late in the booking cycle, average load factors remained in line with established London bases. This positive momentum prompted the company to increase its scheduled Gatwick fleet to seven aircraft for Summer 2027.

CEO Steve Heapy highlighted that the Gatwick winter program is designed to capture growing demand for winter sun, ski holidays, and festive city breaks. However, the substantial capital expenditure required for this southern expansion will continue to weigh on group free cash flow.

Section 5 – Nordic Package vs Seat-Only Fare Comparisons

Section summary
  1. Nordic package holidays maintain a strong pricing premium over seat-only fares due to superior regional consumer purchasing power and vertical integration through concept hotels.
  2. Standalone ticket yields face significant pressure from regional short-haul overcapacity, making the acquisition of Nordic Leisure Travel Group critical to protecting consolidated margins.

Regional consumer dynamics and premium resilience

1.
High profit potential: The Scandinavian region has historically represented one of the most lucrative source markets for holiday operators due to robust consumer purchasing power. An expert interview with a former Loveholidays director on October 18, 2023, revealed that Nordic travelers exhibit a strong willingness to spend substantial sums on Southern European trips. This high-spending demographic enables tour operators to command resilient pricing compared to more price-sensitive Western European markets. Consequently, maintaining a package presence in this region supports higher consolidated margins.
2.
Weakening seat yields: Norwegian Air Shuttle is experiencing significant downward pressure on its standalone flight fares for the winter 2026-2027 season. DNB Carnegie reported on October 28, 2026, that the airline's passenger yields are suffering from regional overcapacity and currency fluctuations. To protect its profitability, the carrier has been forced to implement winter monthly capacity reductions of 25% to 40% compared to October levels. These standalone ticket yield declines mean that Norwegian's flight-only segment will see diminished profitability unless offset by high-margin package sales.
3.
Resilient package premium: The pricing premium for Nordic package holidays remains highly robust when contrasted against the declining yield environment of standalone flights. Morgan Stanley reported on September 18, 2026, that Western Mediterranean seat-only fares fell by mid-to-high single digits while package prices remained unchanged. This pricing divergence demonstrates that packages effectively insulate operator margins from intense low-cost carrier fare competition. This dynamic suggests that Nordic Leisure Travel Group's packaged products can successfully maintain their price premium even as Norwegian's flight yields soften.
4.
Intense regional competition: Norwegian Air Shuttle and its soon-to-be-acquired Nordic Leisure Travel Group operate in an exceptionally competitive regional landscape. A Norwegian Air Shuttle prospectus published on October 16, 2026, notes that the group competes directly with SAS and low-cost giants like Ryanair. In the package segment, the group also faces formidable competition from TUI and Apollo. This crowded competitive environment means that erosion of the package premium would immediately impact Norwegian's post-acquisition profitability.

Yield pressures and vertical integration

5.
Steep yield declines: Standalone airline ticket yields in the Nordic region are projected to face continued erosion through the end of the year. DNB Carnegie reported on October 28, 2026, that Norwegian's ticket yields are modeled to decline by 2.7% year-on-year for the fourth quarter of 2026. This downward trend is driven by winter fare pressure and a stronger NOK. These declining yields highlight the strategic necessity for Norwegian to integrate Nordic Leisure's higher-margin packages.
6.
High concept profitability: Nordic Leisure Travel Group relies heavily on its proprietary hotel portfolio to maintain its high package price premium. A Norwegian Air Shuttle presentation on October 28, 2026, indicated that Nordic Leisure's 26 concept hotels generate 60% of its gross profits. These specialized accommodations allow the operator to command premium package prices that far exceed standalone flight-andhotel components. Integrating these properties allows Norwegian to bypass low-margin seat-only economics on overlapping routes.
7.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie warned on October 28, 2026, that jet fuel cost inflation is escalating faster than airline ticket yields, which could severely compress the package holiday premium. The broker expects third-quarter adjusted EBIT to land 5% below consensus because of these unhedged fuel cost pressures. This indicates that rising operational costs threaten to erode the projected margins of the integrated group. Consequently, macro headwinds may offset the benefits of Nordic Leisure's resilient package pricing.
8.
Softening forward visibility: Consumer booking behavior in the leisure sector is shifting toward shorter lead times, complicating pricing strategies. Morgan Stanley reported on August 12, 2026, that customers are booking much closer to departure dates, which historically forces operators to rely on tactical discounting. This trend is reflected in lower customer deposit growth across major European operators. Consequently, if Nordic households delay booking their winter holidays, the package premium could face severe discounting pressure.

Mediterranean benchmarking and competitive landscape
