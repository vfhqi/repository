CVS Group (CVSG-GB): Sell-side review to 25 November 2026: why the average price target fell while estimates rose

A light sell-side review written by the Investment Analyst in chat on Friday 27 November 2026, at Richard's request, and filed here at his request. It is not the full Sellside Analysts' Memo that the Sellside Analysts' Memo SOP builds; it answers four questions only. Consensus figures are FactSet's: the PMS Per-stock Dashboard data (built 27-Nov-2026 01:50 UK, data to 25-Nov-2026), FactSet's daily and monthly sell-side files, and the broker sheets in the FactSet model for CVS Group (refreshed 19-Nov-2026). Prices and targets are in pence; EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) is in pounds. Financial years end on 30 June (FY2027 is the year to June 2027). Figures marked "our arithmetic" are calculated by the Investment Analyst from FactSet's figures.

The answer in brief

  • Price target: most of the fall in the average is not brokers cutting. It comes from a thin June starting point and from low-target Hold houses joining the average, Stifel above all. Three houses cut in the last 100 days, all named here: Barclays (30p), Shore Capital (to 1,250p) and RBC (100p).
  • FY2029 EBITDA: this is mostly not a real cut. The June reading came from very few analysts, and the three-years-forward line has dipped at the same time of year in each of the last four years.
  • Earnings per share Net Upgrade Breadth: it was negative, at its worst at the end of September after the full-year results, and was back to neutral on 25 November.
  • Estimates up, targets down: both moves are largely changes in which houses FactSet counts, not changes of mind. On 3 November, RBC's estimates (the highest of any house) and Stifel's target (the second lowest) both joined the count. RBC lifted the consensus estimates, and Stifel lowered the average target.

1. The average price target since June 2026

How FactSet counts. FactSet includes an analyst's target or estimate only if it is dated within the last 100 days. After the March interims, most CVS Group estimates aged out of that window by late June, so June's readings rest on very few houses.

June is not a fair starting point. The average target was 1,707p on 29 June on 6 houses, and 1,750p on 1 July on 4 houses. The first full reading after the 23 July pre-close update is 1,666p on 30 July, on 10 houses.

2. FY2029 EBITDA since June 2026

The June figure was not a real consensus. FY2029 consensus EBITDA read exactly £180.0m at the end of June. At that point FY2029 was four years beyond the last reported year (FY2026 was not reported until 24 September), so very few houses forecast it. FactSet's analyst count for FY2029 has only been stored since 28 October: it was 6 then and 11 on 25 November. The round £180.0m suggests one or two houses in June.

On fuller samples, FY2029 EBITDA has barely moved:

The real risk to FY2029 is ahead, not behind. The Investment Analyst's summary of 26 November noted that net debt reached 1.94 times EBITDA at 31 October, close to management's own limit of 2.0 times. If that limit slows acquisitions, FY2029 earnings per share could move toward the case's floor of 103p, which is below consensus.

4. Why targets fell while estimates rose, and the stated logic

Most of the divergence is measurement. RBC's estimates are the highest of any house (FY2029 earnings per share 129.5p, about 15% above consensus). RBC's return to the 100-day count on 3 November lifted the consensus estimates. Stifel's 1,300p target joined on the same day and lowered the average target.

The houses' numbers show two different kinds of move.

  • The low end is driven by earnings. Shore Capital forecasts no earnings growth in FY2028 (95.4p in both FY2027 and FY2028) and 97.8p for FY2029, about 13% below consensus. Stifel's FY2029 forecast is 109.0p, about 4% below. Their low targets rest on lower earnings, not only on a lower multiple.
  • RBC's cut is driven by valuation. RBC cut its target by 100p while keeping the highest estimates of any house. That is a lower multiple or a later timing for the re-rating, not lower earnings.

What the houses have said publicly:

  • Jefferies moved to Hold on 9 July and cut its target from 1,420p to 1,350p, later raising it to 1,400p. Commentary at the time read the July move as valuation discipline rather than a change in its view of the business. At the September results Jefferies said investors should wait to see who the new chief executive is. On 25 November it kept Hold and 1,400p because routine visit volumes at UK practices remain weak, and noted the target was only 3% above the share price.
  • Barclays upgraded to Overweight in late July, citing "visibility and durability", then trimmed its target by 30p on 28 September.
  • Stifel's 1,300p target is below the 1,369p closing price of 25 November. A Hold target below the share price means the house sees the shares as fully valued after their rise.

What is not available in any source we could reach:

  • The written reasons of Shore Capital, Stifel and RBC for their targets.
  • Shore Capital's previous target.

The Investment Analyst's reading (inference, labelled as such). The estimate rises come from growth CVS Group has already paid for: acquisitions (£22.9m in the four months to October) and the £50m buyback. As the APM PMS Q&A SOP note of 23 November put it, analysts are raising revenue and cutting value. What the Hold houses are pricing lower is the multiple, and in Shore Capital's case the earnings too. The shares rose about 24% from 2 October to 13 November while estimates stayed flat. Meanwhile:

  • Like-for-like sales growth (2.1% in FY2026) is below the company's own medium-term range of 4% to 8%.
  • The company still has no chief executive.
  • Net debt is close to the 2.0 times EBITDA limit.
  • The Competition and Markets Authority's remedies (published price lists and a cap on prescription fees) are just taking effect.

The counter-case. The Investment Analyst's memo of 4 November predicted that the Buy ratings, sitting ahead of the estimates, would be resolved either through estimates catching up or targets coming down. Stifel's initiation and the RBC and Shore Capital cuts may be the start of targets coming down.

  • What would confirm it: more Buy-rated houses trimming targets, or consensus earnings per share falling at the January half-year trading update or the interims of about 4 March 2027, when the research and development tax credit is expected to step down.
  • What would refute it: a named chief executive, plus like-for-like growth at the practices above about 2%.
