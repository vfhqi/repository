Section summary
  1. Zegona's incremental cost reductions rely on replacing transitional service agreements and utilizing network virtualization and AI customer care.
  2. European operator precedents show that multi-wave opex programs can deliver rapid savings but often face offset from inflation or integration complexity.

1.
Systematic transition: Across 6 comparable past operator cases and 10 relevant broker sources, Zegona is actively replacing transitional service agreements with Vodafone Group through internal systems and independent third-party solutions.
2.
Operational restructuring: The operator has implemented over 700 technical efficiency initiatives that focus heavily on IT system consolidation and supplier contract renegotiations.
3.
Infrastructure virtualization: To drive operational efficiencies and reduce energy costs, Zegona leverages Microsoft's cloud platform to migrate physical data centers to virtual environments.
4.
Supplier optimization: Zegona's strategy of bypassing its legacy tower vendors has already yielded ten-year agreements with alternative providers to optimize ongoing commercial terms.
5.
Digital care: AI integration in customer care remains early-stage, but management expects digital channels to reduce opex by lowering manual customer support volumes.

Precedents of Tele2's Freya Program

6.
Personnel rationalization: Under Xavier Niel's Freya vehicle, Tele2 rapidly executed a 15% headcount workforce reduction targeting personnel in Sweden and the Baltics.
7.
Contract overhaul: Tele2 successfully renegotiated half of its largest contracts within the first 14 months of Freya taking control.
8.
System consolidation: Tele2 successfully migrated its B2C customer database to a single IT support stack to simplify convergence cross-selling.
9.
Margin expansion: These rapid cost-saving measures allowed Tele2 to deliver an organic EBITDAaL growth rate of 15% in the second quarter of 2025.
