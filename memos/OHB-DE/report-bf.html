This concentration significantly exceeds typical sector diversification patterns and leaves the group structurally vulnerable to single institutional procurement decisions, as detailed in Section 5 – Customer Side Analysis.

The finding is particularly concerning given that three primary clients account for individual customer revenues of EUR 511 million and EUR 165 million in Space Systems, and EUR 52 million in Access to Space, each representing over 10% of their respective segment revenues, underscoring the dependence on a handful of sovereign accounts. Material costs represent the largest cost category, structurally accounting for 59% to 66% of total sales historically, placing OHB at the upper end of material intensity compared to more vertically integrated peers who internalize greater value.

This elevated material dependency reflects the group's largest space infrastructure programs being structurally executed under fixed-price arrangements, exposing core margins to inflationary pressure, as examined in Section 6 – Supplier + Distributor + Channel Analysis. The combination of high material costs and fixed-price contracts creates a structural margin vulnerability that is amplified by critical bottlenecks including onboard power arrays and high-precision optics representing the primary pacing items delaying final satellite integration phases, while alternative component selection remains highly restricted by explicit customerimposed limitations on approved space suppliers.
