[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Renishaw is a demand-inflection case in a high-quality cyclical, compelling as a thesis and not as an entry. An AI-driven expansion of semiconductor equipment spending has reached the encoder division that earns nearly half the group's divisional profit, and a 70 to 75 percent drop-through on the extra volume has turned it into a 30 percent rise in adjusted earnings per share, to £1.79 in FY2026. At 5,660p, after a 61 percent rise in twelve months to the 81st percentile of the stock's own five-year multiple, the shares already pay for the part of that change we can prove and for the part we cannot, so the modal return from here is negative over eighteen months and about nil over three years.

  • The change is external, cyclical and large.
    • Semiconductor wafer-fabrication-equipment spending is in a sector-wide upswing that the customers' own capital plans carry two years out, and it has reached the division that matters most
    • Wafer-fabrication-equipment spending was framed at +12 percent in 2026 and +22 percent in 2027 before the results (Jefferies, 2026-01-25)
      • ASML raised its 2026 sales guidance to EUR 43bn to EUR 46bn and is adding 30 percent EUV manufacturing capacity for 2027
      • TSMC's 2026 capital expenditure is estimated at US$64bn, +57 percent
      • Applied Materials is preparing to double quarterly system output by 2028 on customer forecasts to 2030
    • Position Measurement, which sells the encoders inside lithography, inspection and packaging tools, grew revenue 26 percent to £260.9m in FY2026 (29 percent at constant currency) and its margin rose 490 basis points to 27.4 percent
      • it is 32 percent of revenue and about 47 percent of divisional operating profit (calculated)
    • The force touches about a third of the business
      • European machine-tool and automotive demand, low-end Chinese competition and export controls act on the rest, and the semiconductor tailwind outweighs them today
  • The internal change is secondary, but real, and it survives a downturn.
    • Six of the framework's twelve internal forces are live at moderate strength, which passes the screen's three-strong rule, and none is first-order
    • A £21m fixed-cost saving from a 7 percent headcount cut, the closure of a loss-making drug-delivery line, an external chief financial officer from April 2026, incentives re-weighted to cash conversion and return on invested capital, a run of new products, and a 70.0p special dividend that ended a decade of cash hoarding
    • The cost saving is 2.6 percent of revenue (calculated) against a £41m rise in adjusted operating profit, so the larger part of the output change came from the cycle, not from these forces
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up, and the change has travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It is in the resources, the choices, the actions, the key operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates
    • Adjusted operating margin 18.7 percent in FY2026 from 15.7 percent, 21.2 percent in the second half
      • return on invested capital 17.5 percent from 13.2 percent
      • revenue £815.8m, +14 percent after three years at 1 to 3 percent
      • adjusted earnings per share £1.79, +30 percent
    • FY2027 consensus earnings per share rose 32.3 percent in twelve months to £2.21 (2026-09-25)
      • the sell-side's targets followed the price rather than led it
  • What we think it earns is one more strong year and then a plateau.
    • Our modal path is FY2027 earnings per share of £2.13 (+19 percent), FY2028 £2.30 and FY2029 £2.24 as the semiconductor cycle plateaus in calendar 2028, which is 7.8 percent a year over three years, below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band
    • Consensus carries £2.44 for FY2028 and £2.73 for FY2029 (three brokers), 15.1 percent a year, which is the run-through reading of the cycle
    • We put the odds of a plateau in calendar 2028 at about 60 percent, probable, and of a run-through at about 40 percent, possible, because Renishaw's two prior up-legs each lasted about two years before revenue went flat or fell, the order book gives about one quarter of visibility, and a semiconductor-manufacturer executive puts true demand at 30 to 40 percent of the order surge because of triple-ordering (expert call, 2026-09-22)
  • What it costs is the whole case.
    • At 5,660p the shares trade at 22.54x consensus 24-month-forward earnings of £2.51 (2026-09-25), the 81st percentile of their own five-year weekly range against a median of 19.46x, and 24.8x our own 24-month-forward figure of £2.29 (calculated)
    • Deutsche Bank values the shares at a 41 percent premium to the capital-goods sector and holds them on that ground
      • the one dashboard peer, Judges Scientific, is on 16.2x at the 19th percentile of its own ten years and is a poor comparator in a drawdown of its own
    • The catalysts are in the price: the FY2026 beat, the special dividend, the capacity budget and the "strong start" to FY2027 were announced two days before a price 2.6 percent below the 52-week high
  • The return fails the 20 percent bar on every path we can defend, and the multiple is the swing factor.
    • On our modal path and the five-year-median multiple of 19.5x the 'Modal return' is -14.0 percent over eighteen months (-9.6 percent a year) and -1.9 percent over three years (-0.6 percent a year), dividends included
    • The sixty-forty blend of our plateau and run-through branches gives about 1.1 percent a year over eighteen months
      • the consensus path with today's multiple held gives 12.4 percent a year, a D
      • the run-through branch alone gives 16.2 percent a year, a C
    • The cash return is the only positive leg and it is small (about 2.6 percent a year including the special)
      • the earnings leg is slightly negative at eighteen months because the market already discounts FY2028 and FY2029
      • the re-rating leg is -13.5 percent at 19.5x and +19.8 percent at 27.0x, so the multiple decides the return and it is industry-driven
  • The chain holds from the customers' capital plans to the divisional margin, and it breaks at the cycle's length and at the price.
    • Every link from the equipment makers' plans to the tool builders' orders to encoder volume to a 27.4 percent divisional margin is in the accounts with a date
    • what is not in the accounts is whether the volume runs through calendar 2028, and the price assumes it does
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', crisply and late.
    • The mandatory market-wide demand explosion passes and three of five conditions are at A or B
    • the pattern's payoff, major earnings-trajectory upgrades, has already arrived, which is why a crisp fit and a poor entry coexist

  • The case is therefore a thesis to admire and to watch, not to fund at this price.

    • It clears the bar at an entry below about 4,400p on the blend of our branches, or about 3,700p on our modal path, or if two consecutive readings show the cycle running into 2029 with the multiple held

  • Rating = C, because the three Elements combine as a chain and the last link binds: the input forces are B (a sector-wide, evidenced, two-year semiconductor force plus six moderate internal forces), the outputs are B (already delivered through every layer and still rising in FY2027), but the 'Modal return' is F at 5,660p because the change has been paid for, and the 'Investment Thesis' measures materiality, so the expected change from here (one more 19 percent earnings year and then a plateau) is not material enough at this price to deliver 20 percent a year.

    • The rating is not D because the change itself is real, large and visible in the accounts, so a price about a third lower would make the same thesis a B
    • it is not B because a thesis whose modal return is negative fails the Building Block's own question at its third limb.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The competing case is the sell-side's bull case in its strongest form: that this is not a cycle but a structural step-up.
  • Wafer-fab-equipment demand is being driven by AI data-centre capital spending with customer plans to 2030 (Applied Materials), advanced nodes need about 30 percent more metrology per wafer and hybrid bonding three to four times the wafers per bit, Renishaw holds the number one and number two positions in the encoders that go into those tools, the company is doubling encoder capacity, the cost base has been cut by £21m, the additive business has turned profitable on defence demand, and a new chief financial officer has begun returning cash.
  • On that reading consensus FY2029 earnings per share of £2.73 is conservative, mid-20s margins are "feasible" (Jefferies), the ten-year median multiple of 22.4x is fair for a business that has re-set its margin structure, and the shares at 5,660p offer 6,090p to 6,750p of target with upgrades to come (Jefferies, Peel Hunt, UBS).
  • We concede three things: FY2027 is probably delivered at or near consensus
    • the internal changes are real and would survive a downturn
    • and if the cycle runs into 2029 the case clears the bar (our run-through branch, 16.2 percent a year over eighteen months).
  • We hold our view because the evidence for the cycle's length is the customers' capital plans, which a semiconductor-manufacturer executive says are inflated by triple-ordering to 30 to 40 percent true demand (expert call, 2026-09-22)
    • because sensor tool vendors are holding elevated component inventory with a "highly probable" near-term plateau (expert call, 2026-09-02)
    • because Renishaw's own last two up-legs each lasted about two years before revenue went flat or fell (the FY2018 and FY2022 peaks)
    • because the order book gives one quarter of visibility
    • and because at the 81st percentile of its own five-year multiple the price already carries the structural reading.
  • The structural case is possible, not probable
    • the price treats it as probable.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption forces in the encoder end-market are net tailwinds that are strengthening: gate-all-around and backside-power nodes bring about 30 percent higher metrology intensity than FinFET (Jefferies, 2026-06-10)
    • hybrid bonding for high-bandwidth memory uses three to four times the wafers per bit of DDR5 (Bernstein, 2026-09-24)
    • wafer warpage is forcing a shift from point sensors to area scanners that doubles sub-system component demand (expert call, 2026-07-13).
  • The disproving side: sub-nanometre X-ray metrology is a competitive alternative to optical inspection for 3D structures (Morgan Stanley, 2024-12-03)
    • hardware-agnostic software platforms are what large customers now ask for (expert call, 2026-02-02)
    • optical systems are displacing contact probes in shop-floor metrology.
    • These are slow and Renishaw is answering them (MODUS IM, Renishaw Central, the purchase of the remaining 30 percent of Metrology Software Products for £2.2m in June 2026)
  • The rating is not A because the same disruption that lifts encoder demand (advanced packaging, area scanning) is also what an X-ray or software-led incumbent could use to displace optical positioning over a longer horizon.
