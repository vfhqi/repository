Section summary
  1. Net sales shifts at SKAN Group AG heavily influence margins due to a high fixed cost base primarily driven by personnel expenses.

Defining the Key Enquiry

1.
Fundamental enquiry: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Significant earnings volatility is expected over the next 18 months to four years because of SKAN Group AG's reliance on custom engineering and project-based execution. If the operating leverage is high, a normalization of customer decision cycles will dramatically accelerate earnings per share as revenue scales against stable personnel costs. Structural Cost Volatility Drivers

4.
Elevated personnel intensity represents the primary source of fixed-cost operating leverage for SKAN Group AG. Management reported that personnel expenses reached 50.2% of net sales in 2025 as a result of lower sales and active workforce additions. Because these skilled engineering and service positions cannot be easily scaled down without harming execution capacity, the cost base remains highly rigid. This structural rigidity amplifies the downside risk to earnings per share during periods of customer investment delays, unless standard systems can reduce overall project complexity.
5.
Project-based revenue recognition introduces substantial timing risks that can distort the apparent operating leverage of the equipment business. Under the percentage-of-completion accounting method, early design and final commissioning phases yield lower billable value-added compared to active production. Consequently, segment EBITDA margins can swing violently between halves without representing permanent changes in cost efficiency. Investors must isolate these accounting effects to understand the true underlying profitability of the business, especially as project sizes grow.
6.
Contrary evidence: Although some operating expenses decreased by 9.5% in the first half of 2026 due to reduced travel, core labor costs remained highly inflexible. This demonstrates that tactical cost cuts are limited to discretionary spending lines. Consequently, material reductions in the fixed cost base are difficult to execute without risking long-term delivery capability, meaning that high-volume operations are required to cover fixed labor.
7.
Other side of the story: While the high fixed cost base creates significant downside risk during volume contractions, it also positions SKAN Group AG for explosive margin expansion during periods of strong execution. As order backlogs convert and capacity utilization rises at key production facilities, the incremental margin on extra revenue can exceed historical averages. Thus, what appears to be an unfavorable risk profile during down-cycles becomes the primary engine of earnings growth in up-cycles, provided that order momentum remains strong.

Section 2 – SKAN Cost Structure and Fixed-Variable Cost Split

Section summary
  1. High fixed personnel expenses create significant structural operating leverage at SKAN, although this rigidity exposes margins to short-term project postponements.

Core Cost Intensity Ratios

1.
Elevated labor cost: SKAN Group AG exhibits high structural personnel expenses that reached 50.2% of net sales in the 2025 financial year. During this research, ten relevant companies and over forty sources were analyzed, and all named entities were included in the evaluation. This high ratio, up from 45.5% in 2024, reflects a lower sales volume combined with a rigid employee base. Because these skilled engineering roles are highly specialized, personnel expenses behave as fixed costs during volume contractions.
2.
Rising workforce pressure: Personnel intensity escalated further to 55.3% of net sales in the first half of 2026, totaling CHF 90.9 million. Management reported that this increase was driven by the integration of Metronik and ABC Transfer and the full-period effect of prior hiring. Since net sales rose to CHF 164.5 million, the elevated labor cost demonstrates continued labor base expansion ahead of revenue recognition. This lag between workforce additions and billing milestones remains a major driver of margin volatility.
3.
Declining material ratio: Material and external services decreased to 24.3% of net sales in the first half of 2026, compared to 26.4% in 2024. The lower H1 2026 ratio of 24.3% reflects project execution timing and insourcing benefits from expanded facilities. While raw material inputs represent a strictly variable cost line, their relative decline highlights the growing proportion of fixed service revenues. Consequently, gross margin remained high at 78.9% despite temporary sales headwinds.

Workforce Expansion and Geography

4.
Rapid headcount build: SKAN expanded its global workforce by hiring 216 employees in 2023 and adding another 284 employees in 2025. While the 2023 additions were organic, the 2025 expansion was driven primarily by consolidated acquisitions of Metronik and ABC Transfer. These additions brought the total headcount to 1,755 employees by the end of 2025. This aggressive expansion structurally inflated the company's fixed labor expenses, which must be covered by higher system throughput.
5.
Regional cost disparities: Headcount growth shows geographic cost differences as SKAN holds 977 employees in Switzerland, 328 in Germany, and 189 in Slovenia. Although specific average salaries are not disclosed, Swiss operations in Allschwil represent the highest cost per employee base. In contrast, Slovenia-based Metronik and France-based ABC Transfer operate with lower regional wage structures. Decentralizing engineering to subsidiaries in Germany and Slovenia helps SKAN mitigate its high Swiss franc expense concentration.
6.
Training-related margin lag: SKAN takes approximately one year to train new employees before they become fully operational and contribute to net sales. This delayed productivity creates a temporary margin headwind as training costs are fully expensed immediately through the SKAN Academy. Because specialized aseptic engineering skills are rare in the external market, hiring cycles lock in fixed expenses long before project revenues flow. Managing this structural lag is critical to stabilizing short-term EBITDA.

Management expects that achieving full capacity utilization will take approximately three years from the commercial start in H2 2026. At peak utilization, the facility is projected to deliver an attractive EBITDA margin of up to 50%. Contrary and Alternative Perspectives

Percentage-of-Completion Accounting Dynamics

5.
Nonlinear margin recognition: SKAN Group AG utilizes the cost-to-cost percentage-of-completion method, meaning project progress dictates the timing of revenue and margin recognition. Value generation during the design and engineering phase is only about 5% to 10%, while the final commissioning phase contributes about 10% to 15%. Consequently, the manufacturing and assembly phase drives approximately 75% to 80% of total project value and EBITDA.
6.
Lumpy phase concentration: The severe EBITDA swing in the first half of 2025 occurred because many active projects were in low-value qualification or commissioning phases. When key projects are delayed or suspended in design reviews, the company cannot advance them into the high-margin manufacturing stage. This structural lock-up means that accounting phasing heavily distorts leverage regardless of actual factory physical capacity.
7.
Distinct timing differences: SKAN experienced a massive 70% stronger EBITDA growth in the second half of 2023 due to milestone shifts. Projects reached the crucial steelwork and factory acceptance test phases, causing a rapid acceleration in high-value revenue recognition. This concentration of milestones boosts late-year profit margins but exposes the business model to extreme half-year reporting volatility.

Standardization and Manufacturing Efficiencies

8.
Rapid modular deployment: SKAN developed its VARIOSYS modular isolator platform to provide flexible solutions for processing aseptic and toxic substances. This standardized system uses interchangeable modules to accommodate different packaging materials and rapid H2O2 decontamination technologies. This modular approach drastically reduces customized engineering design hours required for each new client order.
9.
Strategic capacity utilization: SKAN responded to major 2025 project postponements by shifting idle factory personnel to the pre-production of standard isolators. This initiative filled production gaps and enabled the company to maintain workforce utilization instead of incurring costly under-absorption. The subsequent sale of these pre-produced systems supported the strong profit recovery seen in the second half of 2025.
10.
Contrary evidence: Although standardization was designed to protect profitability, the Equipment & Solutions EBITDA margin still collapsed from 11.2% in 2024 to just 4.3% in 2025 due to project postponements. This drop shows that pre-production of standard isolators only partially offset sales shifts because large custom lines still dominate the revenue mix.
11.
Enhanced operational efficiency: SKAN achieved a high gross margin of 78.9% in the first half of 2026 despite persistent top-line challenges. Management attributes these high margins to increased insourcing of steelworks and assemblies at their expanded Görlitz and Stein facilities. Thus, standardizing components allows SKAN to protect unit gross margins even when total segment sales volumes contract.

Executive Leadership and Efficiency Targets

12.
New executive direction: Jonas Greutert assumed the chief executive officer position on January 1, 2026, succeeding long-time leader Thomas Huber. Greutert brings extensive experience in scaling product inspection businesses and improving profitability from his former management roles. Investors expect this leadership change to accelerate cost optimization across both core operational segments.
13.
Clear accountability focus: Jonas Greutert introduced a new leadership structure in 2026 designed to establish clear segment accountability. This organizational realignment aims to strengthen execution, enhance operational excellence, and increase overall customer focus. Management intends to use this structure to increase profitability levels gradually into the upper teens in the mid-term.
14.
Ambitious profitability targets: SKAN confirmed its mid-term targets of achieving mid-to-upper teens net sales growth in both core segments. The company plans to expand its group EBITDA margin to a range of 13% to 15% in the 2026 financial year. Meeting these goals requires successful execution of large-scale projects without further customer-side delays or suspensions.
15.
Other side of the story: While segment accountability and standardization aim to boost margins, the persistent lumpiness of custom project milestones means SKAN remains highly vulnerable to customer delays beyond its control. If major biopharma clients continue to postpone active production phases, the high fixed personnel base will inevitably depress Equipment & Solutions margins regardless of internal leadership structures.

Section 5 – Cost Cuttability and Downturn Playbooks

Section summary
  1. Specialized engineering roles act as highly rigid fixed costs during volume downturns, preventing rapid labor cost-outs.
  2. Peer realignments show that cutting critical technical staff degrades execution capacity and delays subsequent project validation.
  3. Tecan's Rewired program demonstrates that structured internal cost elimination can successfully offset persistent macroeconomic headwinds.

SKAN Downturn Cost Mechanics

1.
Tactical labor flexibility: SKAN managed labor flexibility during demand fluctuations by adjusting its contingent workforce. The company reported in its 2023 annual report that it employed 139 temporary staff alongside kCHF 149,217 of total personnel expenses. This temporary pool allowed SKAN to buffer operational shifts without immediately cutting highly specialized permanent engineering personnel. Therefore, utilizing temporary staff provides a vital near-term volume buffer that protects core engineering capacity during sudden industry postponements.
2.
Persistent innovation commitment: SKAN resisted cutting its long-term research spending during the growth normalization period. Management disclosed in the 2024 annual report that expenditures for strategic initiatives and R&D rose to 8.3% of group net sales. This represents an increase from the 7.0% ratio recorded in the 2023 fiscal year. This deliberate decision to prioritize development indicates that core R&D remains largely un-cuttable if the company wishes to sustain its technical leadership.
3.
Discretionary overhead reduction: SKAN successfully squeezed discretionary spending lines to mitigate severe volume-driven margin pressure. The company reported in its half-year 2026 report that other operating expenses declined by 9.5% to CHF 23.6 million. This reduction was achieved primarily by curbing non-essential travel activities after several periods of aggressive expansion. Consequently, these tactical spending cuts show that discretionary travel and administrative lines are the first to be eliminated when top-line pressure mounts.
4.
Strategic capacity preservation: SKAN avoided large-scale layoffs of its core technical workforce during the recent project postponement cycles. SKAN responded to project postponements by shifting idle personnel to standard isolator pre-production in its 2024 annual report. Maintaining this highly trained staff was essential because newly hired aseptic engineers require at least one full year of training as stated in the 2023 annual report. Thus, keeping these specialists on the payroll ensures SKAN avoids critical execution bottlenecks when customer projects eventually resume.
