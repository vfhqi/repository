Section 2 – Tax Trajectory and German Loss Utilisation

Section summary
  1. NKT's extremely low effective tax rates will persist in the low-20s until German tax loss carryforwards are fully exhausted by late 2028.
  2. Post-utilization, NKT's tax rate is set to inflect sharply upward to approximately thirty percent, representing a significant consensus headwind.

Historical Tax Profile and German Loss Background

1.
Declining tax burden: NKT's historical effective tax rate experienced a rapid downward trend, reporting tax rates of 24.20% in FY2021, 29.60% in FY2022, 20.20% in FY2023, 13.90% in FY2024, and 6.50% in FY2025.
2.
Capitalization windfall: This downward trajectory was driven primarily by the capitalization of German deferred tax assets from loss carryforwards as the operational outlook for the high-voltage business in Cologne improved.
3.
Legislative catalyst: In Q1 2024, newly enacted German tax legislation enabled NKT to capitalize a higher amount of losses carried forward, resulting in a temporary tax rate of only 3% in Q2 2024.
4.
Operational turnaround: Management notes that the utilization of these German tax assets is heavily dependent on a successful turnaround and operational execution of the Cologne-based high-voltage business.
5.
Profitability drive: In Q4 2025, NKT recognized a tax income of EUR 37m due to increased capitalization of German assets triggered by improved operational performance.
6.
German tax unit focus: The majority of NKT's deferred tax assets are concentrated in the German tax unit, reflecting the history of the Cologne operations and the critical role Germany plays in NKT's overall tax planning.
7.
Swedish and Danish asset utilization: In contrast to Germany, NKT's Swedish and Danish tax loss carryforwards were fully utilized in FY2023 and FY2024 respectively, removing other local tax shields.

German Loss Carryforward Status and Utilization Projections

8.
Accumulating losses: The German tax loss carryforwards have grown steadily, rising from EUR 311.80m in 2021 to EUR 387.90m in 2022, EUR 448.70m in 2023, EUR 480m in 2024, and finally reaching EUR 546m in FY2025.
9.
Asset recognition: At year-end 2025, NKT recognized a deferred tax asset of EUR 160m against a total deferred tax value of EUR 164m, leaving only EUR 4m uncapitalized.
10.
Low-20s guidance: Management has guided that investors should expect an effective tax rate in the low- 20s for FY2026, including any German capitalisation effects.
11.
Standard rate baseline: Management previously indicated that the standard long-term normalized corporate tax rate is approximately 22% prior to any German loss carryforward adjustments.
12.
Unlimited carryforward period: A supportive structural factor is that German tax losses have no expiry date, meaning NKT can carry them forward indefinitely until they are completely utilized by Cologne profits.
13.
Deferred tax liabilities offset: Historically, some German tax assets were offset by deferred tax liabilities arising from timing differences in revenue recognition, illustrating the interconnectedness of operational milestones and tax accounting.
14.
Capacity ramp timing: According to DNB Carnegie, the Cologne expansion will become operational by 2027 but will only achieve full operational and financial impact from 2028 onwards.
15.
Estimated exhaustion: Given the gradual ramp-up of the Cologne capacity, the massive EUR 546m German tax loss carryforwards will likely be fully utilized by the end of FY2028 as earnings peak.

Section 3 – Hybrid Instrument Accounting and Dilution Dynamics

Section summary
  1. NKT's March 2026 green hybrid refinancing successfully lowered its coupon rate to 5%, reducing the structural EPS drag from prior instruments.
  2. Under IFRS, the hybrid's equity classification bypasses the income statement but directly reduces the net profit attributable to ordinary shareholders.

Structural Terms of NKT's March 2026 Green Hybrid

1.
Favourable refinancing: In March 2026, NKT priced its new EUR 150m denominated green callable subordinated capital securities with an annual coupon rate of 5% until the first call date, replacing the older EUR 150m hybrid issued in September 2022 that carried a high 7.24% rate.
2.
Extended duration: The green hybrid features a maturity date of 19 March 3026, giving the security a perpetual 1,000-year term, with NKT retaining the option of first call on 19 March 2030.
3.
Subordinated status: The green hybrid is deeply subordinated and senior only to ordinary shares, and is classified as equity capital under IFRS because the group can defer coupon payments at its sole discretion.
4.
Discretionary cancellation: Payment of interest is at NKT's discretion, and deferred coupons are cancelled upon maturity unless NKT pays dividends to shareholders, which triggers mandatory payment of outstanding coupons.
5.
Refinancing execution: During the first half of 2026, NKT's transactions with owners included repurchasing EUR 128m of prior hybrid capital and issuing the new green hybrid capital.

IFRS Net Income Distribution and EPS Mechanics

6.
Direct equity charge: Because the hybrid is classified as equity, coupon payments of EUR 11m in FY2025 do not reduce reported net result but are charged directly to retained earnings.
7.
Attributable profit split: In the reported income statement, the FY2025 net result of EUR 275m was allocated between EUR 264m to equity holders of NKT and EUR 11m to hybrid holders.
8.
Basic share counts: Basic EPS of EUR 4.93 for FY2025 was calculated using a weighted average of 53.55m shares, which excludes 168,496 average treasury shares from the 53.72m issued share base.
9.
Diluted share counts: The diluted share count of 53.64m in FY2025 was impacted by share programmes dilutive effect of 87,159 shares, keeping diluted EPS at EUR 4.92.

Peer Hybrid and Convertible Debt Comparison

12.
Convertible debt contrast: Unlike NKT's equity-accounted hybrid, peer Nexans utilizes OCEANE convertible bonds which require split accounting under IAS 32 into separate liability and equity components.
13.
Amortized cost accounting: For Nexans, the liability component of its OCEANE bonds is measured at amortized cost, and the associated interest expense is recognized directly through the consolidated income statement.
14.
Diluted EPS adjustments: For dilution calculations, Nexans' basic EPS is adjusted by adding back the net interest expense of convertible bonds, provided the effect is not anti-dilutive.
15.
Hybrid leverage metrics: For peer Prysmian, analysts at Deutsche Bank Research treat half of the hybrid bond as debt and half as equity to adjust leverage ratios when modeling its Atkore acquisition.

Section 5 – Operational Ramp-Up Costs and Phasing

Section summary
  1. NKT's strategic growth is temporarily masked by two percentage points of group margin dilution in FY2026.
  2. These front-loaded operational ramp-up costs will reverse rapidly from mid-2027 as expanded high-voltage capacities enter commercial service.

Phasing and Magnitude of NKT Dilution

1.
Dilutive headcount: According to NKT company reports, hiring and pre-operational training costs diluted the group's operational EBITDA margin by approximately one percentage point throughout FY2025.
2.
Steepening dilution: Management guides that this temporary margin dilution will increase to around two percentage points in FY2026 if hiring intensity continues as planned to support the upcoming capacity additions.
3.
Quarterly cost-phasing: According to DNB Carnegie, pre-operational expenses rose from a one hundred basis point drag in Q3 2025 to a one hundred and fifty basis point impact in Q4 2025 as employee onboarding intensified.
4.
Segmented pressure: According to NKT interim statements, the Distribution division experienced a margin decline to 11% in Q2 2026 due to temporary costs from the capacity ramp-up in Denmark.

Accounting Treatment and EBITDA Adjustments

5.
Unadjusted accounting: According to NKT annual reports, pre-operational labor and training costs are not adjusted out of operational EBITDA or adjusted EBITDA figures because they represent ongoing operating costs.
6.
Integrated sell-side: Sells-side analysts at Jefferies Research incorporate these pre-operational costs directly into their core margin models for FY2026, viewing them as standard costs of expansion.
7.
Adjusted underlying performance: Analysts at DNB Carnegie attempt to calculate an underlying margin that excludes these temporary start-up penalties to show that Transmission's core pricing power is actually expanding.

Reversal Timeline and Peer Benchmarks

8.
Scheduled margin recovery: Management expects the dilution to reverse starting second-quarter 2027 as the Karlskrona expansion commences commercial operations and begins generating high-margin revenue.
9.
Sector-leading profitability: Analysts at Jefferies Research project that NKT will deliver the highest high- voltage margins in the sector by FY2028 as fixed cost absorption improves significantly following the asset ramp-up.
10.
Peer labor friction: Peer manufacturer Nexans experienced historical margin pressure due to ramp-up issues in the United States arising from a lack of local technical expertise.
11.
Diversified scale buffer: Peer competitor Prysmian manages its massive capital expansions without reporting explicit group-level margin dilution because its diversified business model absorbs start-up costs more effectively than pure-play NKT.
12.
Other side of the story: Extended dilution duration: Sceptics at DNB Carnegie point out that if the commissioning of NKT Eleonora or the Karlskrona towers faces operational delays, the dilutive cost drag will extend into FY2027, which would severely disappoint current consensus expectations for a swift margin recovery.

Section 6 – Foreign Exchange Exposure and Hedging Framework

Section summary
  1. NKT's extensive transaction hedging framework effectively insulates its massive backlog from short-term currency volatility.
  2. Unhedged Swedish Krona operational cost exposures at the Karlskrona hub present material headwinds if the Krona strengthens against the Euro.

Transaction Hedging Portfolio and Key Exposures

1.
Structured currency risk: The group's central treasury policy dictates that all known transactional currency risks are hedged once they are quantified and identified by subsidiaries.
2.
Robust USD buy-hedges: To manage raw material procurement priced in USD, NKT maintained outstanding forward contracts to buy USD 673m at 1.085 average rate for maturities under one year as of late 2025.
3.
Significant GBP buy-hedges: To support long-term offshore interconnectors and wind farm projects in the UK, NKT locked in a forward portfolio to buy GBP 199m at 0.869 for maturities exceeding one year.
4.
Symmetric USD sell-hedges: The group balanced its procurement hedges with forward contracts to sell USD 728m at 1.080 for maturities under twelve months to cover project-related dollar revenues.
5.
Counter-balancing GBP positions: The transaction portfolio also holds contracts to sell GBP 261m at 0.859 under one year, protecting euro-equivalent margins against sterling devaluations.
6.
Refined hedge accounting: By designating only the spot element of forward contracts, the group reduces P&L volatility because forward points are deferred in equity inside the cost of hedging reserve.

Swedish Krona Sensitivities and Karlskrona Costs

7.
Concentrated operational hub: NKT's primary subsea high-voltage production hub in Karlskrona, Sweden, utilizes the Swedish Krona as its functional currency, creating structural mismatch risks since longterm customer contracts are euro-denominated.
8.
Elevated capital commitments: The ongoing factory expansion requires major localized capital expenditures, illustrated by a SEK 700m construction contract signed with Swedish developer Skanska to complete the extrusion towers.
9.
Substantial earnings sensitivity: Sensitivity testing demonstrates that a 6% strengthening of the Swedish Krona against the Euro boosts earnings before tax by EUR 69m, while a 6% weakening reduces earnings by the same amount.
10.
Offsetting equity impact: The spot sensitivity for SEK is highly asymmetric, as a 6% currency appreciation negatively impacts equity by EUR 9m via translation reserves.
11.
Minor USD earnings-sensitivity: In contrast to Swedish Krona exposures, a 6% shift in the USD spot rate has a minor EUR 3m impact on earnings due to highly matched cash flows.
12.
Negligible GBP earnings-impact: A 5% fluctuation in the British Pound has zero impact on earnings before tax because transaction hedges cover standard project execution.

Overall, NKT's EPS trajectory is highly predictable and largely resolvable through its massive backlog, but the investment case depends critically on resolving the medium and low confidence dimensions through primary research channels, including expert calls on Cologne operational execution, field visits to Karlskrona to assess labor cost inflation, and continuous monitoring of Slovak and Czech regulatory rulings scheduled for late 2026 and 2027.
