Addtech (ADDT.B-SE): what the Q2 FY2026/27 results do to the existing case

The results leave the case on the business where it was, a shade weaker on the quality of the margin and a shade firmer on the organic line; what has moved, and moved a long way, is the price side of the case, which now sits within 3% of the level at which the case says it progresses.

1. What changed

Addtech reported the three months to 30 September 2026 on 22 October 2026.

  • Organic growth was 2%, after 0% in the first quarter, against tough comparisons in Energy and sawmills. Acquisitions added 5% and currency 1% (SEK 53 million). Net sales were SEK 5,907 million, up 8.39%, in line with consensus.
  • Reported EBITA was SEK 987 million, up 16.94%, at a 16.7% margin against 15.5%. That includes a net SEK 55 million positive in Safety from earn-out revaluations and restructuring (DNB Carnegie values it at SEK 57 million). Without it EBITA was about SEK 930 million, up about 10%, and about 1% below consensus, on mix and somewhat elevated costs in Process.
  • The underlying margin is therefore about 15.7% (our arithmetic: about SEK 930 million on SEK 5,907 million), roughly 20 basis points above last year, not 120. The evidence we hold is inconsistent on this point: one passage describes the 16.7% as excluding one-offs, which the reported figures do not support.
  • Group book-to-bill was positive, with a sequential improvement in order intake across most business areas; no figure is given. Management says grid projects are tilted to the second half.
  • Return on working capital was 82% against 77%. Operating cash flow for the half was SEK 1,332 million against SEK 1,336 million, flat while half-year EBITA rose 13.99%; inventory rose on order intake and supply disruption. Net debt was SEK 5,044 million, 1.4x EBITDA.
  • Sell-side response: one house. DNB Carnegie kept Buy and a SEK 395 target (cut from SEK 410 on 5 October 2026, before the results), left adjusted EPS at SEK 11.8 and SEK 13.1 and moved the third year from SEK 14.4 to SEK 14.5. No other broker published. The dashboard at 9 November 2026 shows consensus EPS down 1% over one month and the consensus target at SEK 382.7.
  • Price: SEK 322.0 on 9 November 2026, against the case's reference of SEK 343.8, and below the 50-day, 150-day and 200-day averages (0.9% below the 200-day). Current P/E 26.1x.

Against what the case assumed: organic at 2% is the bottom of the case's 2% to 4% range and arrives a quarter earlier than the case needed it. The case's alarm (organic at or below 0% with book-to-bill below 100%) did not trigger, and the case's organic invalidation, which required both this quarter and next at or below 0%, can no longer be met; that is arithmetic. The case's progress condition of 3% or more for two consecutive quarters has not started counting.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the leg the case treats as delivered is the one that stalled. The case holds that the change has reached margin, capital efficiency and estimates, and that organic volume is the one layer still open. This quarter reverses the order. Organic turned positive on schedule; the underlying margin, at about 15.7%, is well short of the case's 16.6% path for the year and of the 16.6% the case records for the first quarter, in a quarter with positive organic growth and gross margin described as stronger. Half-year cash flow did not grow at all. In its strongest form: the structural margin step is smaller than the reported series shows, the reported series is being held up by items that are not operating, and the case's 12% earnings path loses its two points a year of margin.

The earn-out item deserves separate weight. An earn-out is released when an acquired company is not expected to earn what was promised at purchase. A net SEK 55 million, after restructuring costs that reduce it, is about four times the SEK 13 million the case already carries, and it sits in the segment carved out a year ago. The case's counter-case reads earn-outs as flattering the margin. It does not read them as evidence about the acquisition engine, which is the case's second key driver and the subject of its own invalidation test (post-acquisition organic growth falling). For this reading to be right, the release must reflect acquired businesses underperforming, not cautious initial provisioning, and the Process costs must persist. We cannot tell which from what we have.

Reading two: the cycle turns and Addtech still does not reach 3%. The case is built on two branches: organic confirms at 3% or more, or it stagnates at 0%. It has no middle. This quarter is the middle. The review shows Lagercrantz printing 12% organic growth in its comparable quarter against Addtech's 2%; the case recorded that gap as 6% against 0% and left open whether it was mix and comparisons or local share loss. The gap has doubled in points with the PMI above 53. In its strongest form: the grid, defence and data-centre pockets are too small a share of the group to carry it, construction, sawmills and Process are not comparison effects, and organic settles at about 2%. Then the invalidation never fires, the cleared branch never confirms, and the case sits parked indefinitely on a business the case's own growth test puts at the C boundary (organic averaging below 2%). For this to be right, the third and fourth quarters, with easier comparisons and the grid tilt, would have to print 2% again.

Reading three, the other way: the market has already priced the uncleared branch, and the case is waiting for the last 3%. The P/E is 26.1x against the case's 28.6x and below the case's 27.2x modal blend; the de-rating the case assumed over eighteen months has more than happened in five weeks. On the case's own unchanged eighteen-month value of SEK 412.0, SEK 322.0 gives about 19% a year, and on its three-year value of SEK 483.6 about 15% a year (our arithmetic). The case's first trigger, about SEK 313, is 2.9% below the price, with the invalidation route now closed and broker estimates unchanged. For this to be right, the fall must be about rates and the cohort, not about Addtech: DNB Carnegie's target cut cited yield spreads, which supports it; a relative-strength percentile of 30, down from the case's 59, with most of the dashboard sector on stronger technical letters, argues against it.

Readings one and two are not independent of three. If either is right, the price fall is information and the case's "at or below SEK 313 with the case intact" fails on its second clause. The obvious fourth reading, that the 1% miss shows demand weakening, fails on the evidence: orders, book-to-bill and the organic print all improved.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry what brokers have done with estimates and targets and whether any house other than DNB Carnegie has published. The dashboard suggests the answer is probably "very little". It will not carry any new company fact, and it is unlikely to explain the earn-out release or give the quarter's gross margin unless a broker has pulled them out.
  • The earn-out cause. Which acquisitions, and whether the release reflects missed targets, is not something the company has committed to disclose. This is an open question that no scheduled event answers. The nearest proxy is whether further revaluations appear in the third quarter.
  • Gross margin for the quarter, against the case's alarm of more than 50 basis points of sequential compression without volume. It is not in the evidence we hold.
  • The third-quarter report, February 2027. Organic growth against 3%, with easier comparisons and grid deliveries; the underlying margin against 16.6%; cash flow. It is the first quarter that can count toward the case's two-quarter condition, so the cleared branch cannot confirm before the year-end report in May 2027.
  • Peers' organic prints over the same period, for whether the gap to Lagercrantz is comparison or share.
  • The price against about SEK 313, daily, and whether it gets there with estimates still unchanged.
