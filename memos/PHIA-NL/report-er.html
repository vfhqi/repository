Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Royal Philips has successfully transitioned into a focused health technology company with a more consistent track record of earnings delivery under its new management team.
  2. The stock has shown significant relative outperformance against key peers like Siemens Healthineers, though it continues to trade at a modest discount due to sector de-rating and China headwinds.
  3. Ambitious midterm targets for fiscal year 2028 are supported by robust productivity programs and innovation-led order momentum, despite ongoing competitive threats in China.

The company operates through three primary segments, which are Diagnosis and Treatment, Connected Care, and Personal Health. Mixed fundamentals: The financial fundamentals of Philips over the last three years show strong recovery alongside persistent headwinds. Group organic sales growth reached 3.5% year-to-date, demonstrating solid resilience. However, the company continues to suffer from market share loss in Imaging to core competitors. Significant outperformance: The share price of Philips has demonstrated notable relative strength against major medical technology peers. Philips has outperformed Siemens Healthineers by approximately 20% yearto-date in calendar year 2026. This performance reflects growing sell-side credibility and consistent operational delivery by management. Investors have reacted positively to the company's improved risk profile.

Cautious valuation: The stock of Philips trades at approximately 15x forward price-to-earnings for calendar year 2027. Persistent headwinds: The Chinese healthcare market presents a significant and prolonged growth headwind for medical device exporters. This ongoing weakness is likely to weigh on near-term international sales.
11.
Deliberate strategy: Philips has adopted a highly realistic and cautious approach to its business operations in China. The company is not positioning for a rapid market recovery and has prepared for subdued demand. Instead, management is focusing on differentiated premium technologies such as helium-free MRI. This selective participation is designed to protect margins and long-term profitability.
12.
Manageable inflation: The impact of rising helium prices on Philips represents a small and manageable headwind. However, 3.0T systems remain dependent on conventional helium-based designs for the foreseeable future. Furthermore, if China's centralized procurement continues to delay order processing, sales growth will cap. This combination of risks could prevent the stock from achieving its target valuation.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Our central conclusion is that Philips' EPS trajectory over the next 18 months to three years is positive but costled, and less steep than the headline 2028 ambitions suggest. Management aims to deliver mid-single-digit sales growth and mid-teens EBITA margins by 2028, and that margin target exceeds the sell-side consensus expectation of 13.6%. The third quarter of fiscal 2026 was margin-led. The adjusted EBITA margin expanded by 70 basis points year-on-year to 13.0%, surpassing consensus.

Adjusted basic EPS of EUR 0.40 beat the EUR 0.35 consensus. Revenue, however, was weaker: comparable sales growth of 2.7% slightly missed the 3.1% consensus. The recovery is real, but it rests on self-help, a resilient consumer segment and legal de-risking rather than on broad-based organic acceleration. The first consensus blindspot is the quality and durability of the margin.

Philips delivered EUR 170 million of net productivity savings in the quarter, bringing the year-to-date total to EUR 427 million. One broker judged the quality of this expansion mixed because it relies on cost-cutting rather than broad-based organic growth. The reiterated full-year outlook includes a one percentage point benefit from United States tariff refunds; excluding it, margin guidance is 12.5% to 13.0%. Broker EPS paths for 2026 and 2027 sit close together: Morgan Stanley's forecasts are EUR 1.77 for fiscal 2026 and EUR 1.79 for fiscal 2027, and Bernstein's are EUR 1.74 and EUR 1.78.

Deutsche Bank raised its 2026 EPS forecast by 12% solely for the net impact of tariff refunds and left 2027 and beyond largely unchanged. We read this as one of two things. Either 2026 carries support that will not repeat, or 2027 under-models the 2028 margin ambition. The report cannot fully resolve which.

The second blindspot is the divergence between orders and revenue in Diagnosis and Treatment. Management cited 8% comparable order intake growth, and nearly 20% order growth in Diagnosis and Treatment offset a midsingle-digit decline in Connected Care. Yet the division's comparable sales were negative 0.3% against a consensus of positive 2.8%, and slow backlog conversion remains a primary near-term sales constraint.
