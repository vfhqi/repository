EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Luciano Vasques brings deep operational leadership and over 30 years of global upstream experience to Ithaca Energy following his appointment as Chief Executive Officer in October 2024, coinciding with the transformational combination with Eni UK.

His career encompasses successfully delivering multibilliondollar developments globally across Eni's operations, and since taking office he has expanded full-year combined revenue to $2,946.50 million in 2025, representing more than double 2024 revenue.

Executive execution under Vasques has achieved record production scale while meeting all cash distribution commitments, completing all structural integration activities within eight months of closing the transformational Eni UK combination, and delivering the promised $500 million annual cash distribution in both 2024 and 2025 fiscal years.

His proven track record includes overseeing the giant Kashagan oil field in Central Asia and leading the successful integration of Neptune Energy into Eni UK, though concerns remain that his prior roles may have lacked standalone commercial profit and loss accountability within Eni's parent-company structure. Broker sentiment toward leadership is generally positive but cautious.

Jefferies analysts Mark Wilson and Sasikanth Chilukuru act as prominent supporters maintaining a buy rating and 350p price target, while DNB Carnegie analyst Christian Klingenberg Theodorsen initiated coverage with a buy recommendation and 325p price target.

Morgan Stanley analysts Martijn Rats and Guilherme Levy maintain a more cautious equal-weight rating with 286p target.

Analyst perceptions shifted positively over the last 18 months following successful Eni UK integration and international diversification into Canada, with the Canadian acquisition upgrading medium-term production outlooks to 140-150 kboe/d.

Analysts warn that persistent regulatory headwinds and UK tax changes could prevent the company from narrowing its valuation discount to European peers. Corporate governance presents significant structural weaknesses stemming from concentrated parent control.

The 14-member board includes three executive directors, five nominated non-independent directors, and six independent directors, with executive and nominee directors comprising 57% of total board seats, limiting independent minority influence.

Delek Group and Eni SpA control a combined 86% stake in the company, leaving a narrow public float, with the Amended Delek Relationship Agreement permitting Delek to appoint up to three directors and the Eni Relationship Agreement securing up to two board nomination seats.

Board independence stands at 43%, which fails to meet the 50% threshold recommended by the UK Corporate Governance Code.

Key governance committees remain fully independent, with Deborah Gudgeon chairing the Audit and Risk Committee and Lynne Clow chairing the Remuneration Committee, ensuring unbiased oversight of executive compensation.

Ithaca recorded related-party hydrocarbon sales amounting to $1,039 million to Eni during the 2025 fiscal year, alongside exploration and subsurface technical support secured through Eni service agreements on arm's-length terms.

While these relationships deliver operational benefits, the heavy concentration of Delek and Eni representatives raises legitimate questions about minority shareholder protection despite formal relationship agreements. Executive compensation structures demonstrate partial alignment with shareholder interests.

Benchmarking studies position Ithaca's maximum incentive opportunities at the lower quartile compared to FTSE-listed peers of similar size, with CEO Luciano Vasques receiving total remuneration of GBP 1.496 million in 2025 during his first full year of service.

The annual bonus relies heavily on operational parameters, with operations and safety events comprising 60% of the 2025 corporate scorecard, while long-term incentive plans align directly with investors by basing 100% of vesting performance on relative total shareholder returns.

Scorecards successfully drove carbon reductions, lowering gross operated greenhouse gas intensity from 23.9 kgCO₂e/BOE down to 17.2 kgCO₂e/BOE.

Despite these performance-linked frameworks, most non-executive directors hold nominal equity, with Assaf Ginsburg's 110,000 shares representing the largest independent holding, while executives hold small positions, recently acquiring tiny matching shares under the Share Incentive Plan in October 2026. 21 Insider share dealings reveal minimal open-market conviction among executive leadership.

Standing holdings and SIP actions suggest limited direct personal capital commitment from top executives, with outright shareholdings remaining small and direct purchases of only 40,000 shares by Friedman in late 2025.

Remuneration committees granted performance share awards in the form of nil-cost options in April 2026, with CEO Luciano Vasques receiving 563,764 performance share awards and Yaniv Friedman and Iain Lewis each granted 469,804 performance shares under the same plan.

The absence of substantial open-market purchases by executives contrasts unfavorably with the company's strong operational delivery and indicates that leadership may lack sufficient skin in the game to fully align with minority shareholders.

Expert perspectives and external commentary provide mixed validation of management narratives.

An international drilling expert with 40 years of experience identified high regulatory and political risk and contrasted the UK's difficult landscape with Norway's industry-friendly regulatory environment.

A Mayfair Energy Ventures president stated that North Sea combinations drive substantial operational synergies and deliver enhanced market trading power, though large-scale regional mergers face potential challenges if combinations trigger critical antitrust regulatory actions.

Industry experts validate the strategic rationale for consolidation but emphasize persistent fiscal and regulatory headwinds that constrain capital deployment flexibility. 25 27 - Earnings Calls Company culture indicators reveal moderately positive workforce engagement balanced against demands for improved leadership visibility.

Recent surveys show a 12% engagement score increase in December 2025 compared to the 2023 survey baseline, with survey response rates reaching 73% in August 2024 and around 90% of employees understanding the refreshed corporate values in 2025.

Total Recordable Injury Rate fell to 1.7 cases per million hours in 2025, down from 2.3 in 2024, and approximately 92% of surveyed employees reported feeling safe at work during the 2025 cultural assessment.

Regulators fined the company GBP 300,000 following a flooded lift shaft incident on the FPF-1 offshore platform.

The absence of public Glassdoor reviews and CEO approval ratings obscures fuller workforce sentiment regarding integration frictions following recent acquisitions. The single largest risk to management credibility in relation to the 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory centers on regulatory and fiscal uncertainty in the UK Continental Shelf combined with execution risks from aggressive international expansion.

Revisions to the Energy Profits Levy raised the marginal tax rate to 78%, clouding longterm capital investment visibility, while campaigners successfully quashed the Rosebank field's consent, preventing any near-term hydrocarbon extraction until regulators issue a revised decision.

Management announced a $860 million acquisition of Suncor Canada offshore assets to diversify production outside the UKCS, establishing a second platform for inorganic growth outside Europe with the transaction adding 103 million barrels of reserves at low cost.

Management's ambitious international expansion and upgraded production targets might expose the company to execution and integration risks, as operating in Canada demands regional and logistical expertise that Ithaca's North Sea-focused leadership team currently lacks.

Should Rosebank face prolonged judicial delays, Cambo fail to achieve final investment decision, or the Canadian integration encounter material cost overruns or production shortfalls, the company's ability to sustain its elevated dividend commitments and achieve medium-term EPS growth targets would face severe pressure.

The confluence of punitive UK taxation, ongoing legal challenges to flagship development projects, and untested operational capabilities in a new basin represents a material execution and regulatory risk cluster that could materially undermine earnings visibility and shareholder confidence over the next three years.

Plausible problems to monitor

The third material weakness centers on concentrated related-party transactions and parent dependency. Ithaca recorded related-party hydrocarbon sales amounting to $1,039 million to Eni during the 2025 fiscal year, representing a substantial portion of total revenue that creates commercial dependency risks. Exploration and subsurface technical support are secured through Eni service agreements on arm's-length terms, yet the sheer scale of these arrangements–combined with a related-party group relief payment amounting to $78 million made to Eni in early 2026 –raises questions about pricing transparency and minority shareholder protection.

While management argues these agreements deliver exceptional technical depth unavailable to independent peers, the magnitude of related-party flows significantly exceeds industry norms for publicly listed independents, where such transactions typically represent less than 10% of revenue. This concentration appears across governance structure, board composition, and customer/supplier relationship sections.

Outliers worth noting

The combination of lower quartile pay positioning and minimal direct share ownership creates a highly unusual alignment structure that raises questions about whether executive incentives are sufficiently tied to long-term value creation for minority shareholders. While annual bonus scorecards emphasize operational safety metrics and long-term incentive plans base 100% of vesting performance on relative total shareholder returns, directors must maintain a minimum shareholding requirement of up to 250% of base salary within five years.

However, standing holdings and Share Incentive Plan actions suggest limited direct personal capital commitment from top executives, with direct purchases of only 40,000 shares by Executive Chairman Friedman in late 2025. This weak direct ownership position contrasts sharply with North American upstream independent producers, where founding or long-tenured executives typically maintain substantial equity stakes representing multiple years of compensation.
