[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Alfen is a self-help recovery laid over a policy-driven Dutch grid demand cycle, and at €11.58 it is not a compelling case. The change inside the company is broad and real, but it has reached the balance sheet and one operating metric and not the profit line, while the shares already trade at 25.3 times twenty-four-month-forward earnings, so the earnings recovery we expect is largely spent paying for the multiple to normalise. The case turns on an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 6% appearing in FY27 and about 8% by FY30, from a company that has set and missed three recovery dates.

  • The change inside the company is broad, real and already visible in what it has done.
    • A new chief executive (1 October 2025), a new chair (April 2026) and an interim finance director (1 July 2026) have restructured the company into three business units (1 May 2026), cut headcount to 909 full-time equivalents at 30 June 2026 from 1,053 at the end of 2024, cut inventory to €72.3m from €95.2m, discontinued the DC charger, halved the revolving facility to €50m, and turned net debt of €20.7m at December 2025 into net cash of €6.2m at June 2026.
    • Four of the twelve internal forces are strong, which passes the screen's rule of at least three.
    • This is not a presentation: the structure, the headcount and the cash are all in the accounts.
    • The one internal signal that points the other way is guidance, which has only moved down: the 2027 targets of €1bn of revenue and a 15 to 20% margin were withdrawn in August 2025, and the FY26 margin range was cut from 5 to 8% to 4 to 7% before being held twice.
  • The external force that matters is a structural, dated grid tailwind, and the company can only convert it as fast as its customers can build.
    • Over 14,000 parties wait for Dutch grid connections
    • the three distribution operators that control about 94% of Dutch secondary substations have approved record capital plans for 2026 to 2028
    • a fast-track permitting regime for grid projects starts on 7 October 2026.
    • Alfen holds about 70% of Dutch secondary substations and delivered 883 in the second quarter of 2026, +27% year on year on stable prices.
    • Management says the fast-track brings no volume in FY2026, because the constraint is technicians and permits, not the size of the queue
      • two operators downscaled 2025 orders for lack of labour.
    • The other two segments face headwinds, not tailwinds: EV Charging revenue fell 17.3% in H1 2026 on volumes -12% and price -6%, with Zaptec growing 26% in the same period
      • utility-scale storage intake fell to €11m in the second quarter against an expectation near €33m, and the backlog fell to €93m from €130m.
  • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has travelled from resources, through choices and actions, into the balance sheet and one operating metric, and no further.
    • Substation volumes and net cash confirm it.
    • Gross margin does not (adjusted, 26.1% in H1 2026 from 30.0% a year earlier), earnings per share do not (negative in FY26 on our path, -€0.09), and the sell-side's current-year estimates do not (FY26 mean earnings per share -€0.149, cut 184% in three months to 18 September 2026).
  • 'Financial Outputs' go down before they go up, and the half now under way is the down.
    • Our path has FY26 adjusted EBITDA of €21.5m (4.7% margin) against €25.5m (5.9%) in FY25, because an €8m personnel-cost overlap, a 5% wage indexation and a €4.5m restructuring charge fall on a second half whose revenue (about €195m) is below H2 2025 (€224m) after front-loaded storage deliveries.
    • From FY27 the path recovers: adjusted EBITDA €30m (6.1%), €35m (6.6%), €42m (7.3%), €49m (8.0%) and €55m (8.4%) for FY27 to FY31, on revenue growing about 7 to 8% a year, giving earnings per share of €0.34, €0.52, €0.72, €0.93 and €1.10.
    • The path sits at consensus on FY26 revenue and EBITDA and on FY27 earnings per share
      • it differs in one place that matters, the depreciation and amortisation line, where we sit between the reported history and what consensus implies.
  • What it costs is the whole re-rating.
    • At €11.58 the shares trade at 25.30 times twenty-four-month-forward consensus earnings of €0.458, the 62nd percentile of the stock's own five-year range and above its five-year median of 19.88x, on earnings that are themselves a recovery.
    • We apply 18 times, between the five-year median and the 25th percentile (15.69x), because about 13 points of gross profit have moved from a 40%-margin charging segment growing 30 to 76% a year to 22 to 23%-margin grid hardware growing 7 to 8%.
    • At eighteen months (March 2028) that gives €13.86, +19.7%, about 12.7% a year
      • at three years (September 2029) €19.08, +64.8%, about 18.1% a year
      • no cash return at either horizon.
    • The earnings component multiplies value by 1.682 at eighteen months
      • the re-rating component multiplies it by 0.711
      • the product, 1.196, is the whole return.
      • Earnings carry it and the multiple subtracts a third of it.
  • The chain holds on grid and on cost and breaks on odds and on arithmetic.
    • Our modal path needs grid revenue growing 8% or more a year (probable, about 0.7), the FY27 margin recovering to 6% as the overlap drops out (plausible, about 0.55), and at least one of charging stabilising (possible, about 0.4) and storage holding €140m at 20% gross margin (plausible, about 0.5).
    • Treated as independent that is about one in four
    • the 20%-a-year branch also needs the exit multiple at the five-year median, about one in eight.
    • The swing factor is the exit multiple: two turns is €1.54, 13% of today's price.
      • Behind it sits the drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share, where depreciation at 22 EURm rather than 18 to 20 EURm cuts the eighteen-month return from 12.7% to 3.8% a year.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', fit C, with 'Major CfC Clearing' the near miss.
    • The inputs are crisp and the maturity is where the pattern expects it
    • the outputs are not, because the margin and the current-year estimates have moved the wrong way since the change began.

  • The case is therefore a park, not a progression.

    • It clears the framework's 20% bar at both horizons only below about €10.50, which is also the sell-side's median target, or on evidence of a Q1 2027 adjusted EBITDA margin of 8% and Q3 2026 storage intake above €50m.

  • Rating = D, because the input forces are strong (B) but the outputs are not yet improving on the lines that matter (D) and the 'Modal return' does not clear 20% a year on our path (D)

    • the parts bind, so the weaker sets the level.
    • Not C, which was live: had the return cleared 15% a year at both horizons on the same evidence, the strength of the forces would have carried a C.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against our D, at its strongest: Alfen is a net-cash utility-supply franchise at the start of a decade-long, policy-mandated grid reinforcement cycle, priced at about half its revenue and about 8 times next year's consensus EBITDA, with a sell-side at zero buy ratings of eight, the highest short position on Euronext Amsterdam (7.6% of share capital, January 2026), a fixed-cost base built for twice today's revenue, a management that has delivered its first-year promises (the three-unit structure on 1 May 2026; the cash), and a 5 GWh sodium-ion partnership with CATL that puts it first in Europe on the cheapest storage chemistry.
    • On that reading the charging and storage noise is what a buyer is paid to tolerate
    • FY27 EBITDA of €33m (Jefferies, 20 August 2026) and a return to the five-year median multiple give a share price near €18 within two years, and a takeover, a buyback or a sodium-ion reference project would bring it forward.
  • What we concede: the franchise is top-decile in its own market
    • the balance sheet removes the financing risk
    • the sell-side has given up
    • the enterprise-value reading is cheap
    • and the new team has done what it said for eleven months.
  • Why we hold our view: the price-to-earnings multiple is not cheap and the framework values on earnings, at 25.3 times twenty-four-month-forward consensus earnings that are themselves a recovery.
    • The profit line is still about one-third charging, which is shrinking at 17% a year with prices falling.
    • The half now under way will show deleverage and higher personnel costs before any improvement.
    • The working-capital cash source is closed by management's own statement that it anticipates no further inventory reductions.
    • The finance seat has changed for the fourth time since January 2024.
    • And the recovery date has moved three times.
  • The claim that the grid franchise alone covers the enterprise value cannot be shown from the evidence, so we do not accept it.
    • The only divisional profit line disclosed is Smart Grid gross profit: H1 2026 revenue of €111.6m at 22.9% is €25.6m, about €51m annualised.
    • If the segment carried operating expenses in proportion to its 43% of revenue (about €43m of the roughly €100m of adjusted operating expenses implied by FY25 revenue of €436m, a 28.1% gross margin and adjusted EBITDA of €25.5m), its EBITDA would be about €8m
    • at ING's 8.5x that is €68m and at Jefferies' post-results peer multiple of 13.4x it is €107m, against an enterprise value of about €241m.
    • No cover on either.
    • The downside is bounded by net cash and by the franchise's value to an acquirer, not by a divisional sum.

    • Utrecht province stopped new or heavier connections in April 2026

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening in the two segments that carry about two-thirds of gross profit and is benign only in grid.
  • Charging competition: Chinese suppliers and local rivals target entry-level buyers
    • entry-level wallboxes have fallen from about €1,000 to under €500
    • Alfen's implied charger price was €968 in the second quarter of 2026, -4% year on year after -8% in the first quarter

  • Storage competition: BYD, CATL and Samsung bypass independent integrators
    • utilities increasingly buy cells directly
    • tenders draw up to 50 bidders
    • integrator gross margins are now 10 to 20%
    • Alfen's storage gross margin fell to 22.8% in H1 2026 from 27.4%.
  • Grid competition: Deutsche Bank describes benign competitor attitudes and pricing discipline
    • second-quarter 2026 substation prices were stable.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = D

  • The 'Foundations' support the grid half of the thesis and not the rest: a real, narrow grid franchise sits inside a business whose other two segments have no moat, whose financial outputs are bottom-quartile, and whose operator record is one of over-promising, so the thesis rests on a new team proving in one year what the old one failed to prove in three.

      • two customers took 34.8% of FY25 revenue.
