[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Kingspan is a demand inflection in one-fifth of the company, captured by a major internal reshaping of the whole, and bought by the market before we got there. The change is real and has already reached the sell-side's estimates; at 101.5 EUR the shares pay for the part of it we can prove, so our modal return from here is 15.2 percent a year over eighteen months against the 20 percent bar.

  • The external change is a structural demand inflection in data-centre infrastructure, and it is already in the company's own order book, which is the test the framework sets.
    • Advnsys, the data-centre division, saw order intake and backlog more than double year on year in the first half of 2026 after +24 percent at the end of FY2025, grew 21.9 percent organically to 1,020.4 EURm of revenue, and has about one year of visibility in the backlog.
    • Behind it sits hyperscaler capital spending projected at 1 trillion USD by 2027 and data-centre capacity growing at a 16 percent compound rate from 100 GW to 214 GW.
    • The force acts on about a fifth of the group: Advnsys was 1,655 EURm of 9,199 EURm of FY2025 revenue, 18 percent.
      • That is the scope that keeps 'Change Forces' at B rather than A.
  • The internal response is multi-dimensional and delivered, not promised, which is why the pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' rather than 'Demand Inflection'.
    • Within a year management folded five divisions into two, cancelled the Advnsys sale with the words 'there is one Kingspan', paused the 650 EURm buyback to fund acquisitions, bought BMC for 850 EURm at under 5x FY2027 EBITDA (completed 2026-09-25), and raised FY2026 trading-profit guidance twice, from 1,050 EURm to 1,125 EURm on 2026-08-07 and then to 1,150 EURm for BMC.
    • BMC doubles the group's data-centre content per megawatt from about 500,000 USD to about 1 million USD, carries about a 30 percent EBITDA margin against the group's 13.2 percent, and adds 4 to 7 percent to FY2027 EPS on three brokers' arithmetic.
    • The piece the screen's archetype wants and this case lacks is a new chief executive: Gene Murtagh has led since 2005, and the team that ran a 10 to 11 percent-margin building-materials consolidator for twenty years is the team running the data-centre pivot.
      • That is a strength for execution and the reason the internal forces are B, not A.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up, and the change has travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain to the sell-side's estimates.
    • Organic growth went from +0.3 percent in FY2025 to +6.2 percent in H1 2026 against a consensus 4.1 percent, with global panel volumes +13 percent
    • Trading profit rose 10 percent and the margin 20 basis points to 10.0 percent
    • The second-half margin is signalled at about 12 percent against a sell-side that had modelled 10 to 11 percent for years.
    • FY2027 consensus EPS rose 17.0 percent in three months and FY2028 consensus EPS 29.5 percent in twelve months
      • Eleven of thirteen brokers are at Buy and none at Sell.
    • What is not yet visible is cash and leverage: FactSet free cash flow was 55.5 percent of net income in FY2025, and net debt to EBITDA rises to about 1.9x at end-FY2026 for BMC before the guided fall to 1.1x at end-FY2027.
  • The other four-fifths of the company is a cyclical building-envelope business selling into a contracting Europe, and the thesis needs it only to hold.
    • Western Europe is 63 percent of revenue, 74 to 75 percent of traditional sales are new-build, and the Eurozone construction PMI was 43.0 in August 2026 with France at 37.3 and the UK in its twentieth month of contraction.
    • The foam core's margin tracks steel and MDI with a two-month lag, so the top line benefits while inputs rise and gives the benefit back when they fall.
    • The case for 'hold' is the company's pattern: panel volumes grew 14 percent since 2019 in an addressable market down 4 to 5 percent, and 13 percent in H1 2026 with the PMI at 43, although part of the second quarter was pre-buying ahead of price rises worth low-to-mid single digits of volume.
  • What we think it earns.
    • On our path earnings per share compound at 18.7 percent a year from 3.70 EUR in FY2025 to 6.20 EUR in FY2028, inside the framework's 12 to 20 percent band and 2.7 percent below the consensus 6.37 EUR
    • Revenue contributes 11.3 points of that, margin 4.8 points, the ratio below EBITDA 2.3 points and the rising share count takes 0.6 away.
    • The two nearer horizons sit above the band (H2 2026 about +30 percent, FY2027 +24 percent) because BMC and the margin step land in them
      • The framework marks that zone down for over-extrapolation risk.
  • What it costs, and where the chain breaks.
    • At 101.5 EUR the shares trade at 16.7x 24-month-forward consensus EPS of 6.09 EUR after rising 42.9 percent in twelve months: below the stock's ten-year median of 18.20x but at the 78th percentile of the last two years.
    • The price has moved with the catalysts, not against them
    • The H1 beat, the guidance to 1,150 EURm and BMC are in it.
    • Valuing the shares at 17.5x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, a blend of a 19.0x branch (40 percent odds) and a 16.5x branch (60 percent), the value including cash returns is 125.5 EUR at eighteen months and 145.8 EUR at three years: 15.2 percent and 12.8 percent a year.
      • The bar is cleared at or below 95.5 EUR on the eighteen-month horizon.
    • The chain holds from the forces to the sell-side's estimates and breaks at the price: a B change at a C return.
      • The consensus path, which grows EPS at 18 percent a year beyond FY2028 on two brokers, would give about 22.5 percent a year at the same multiple
      • Ours grows it at 11 percent, and that difference is the whole gap between a B and a C.

  • The case is therefore compelling as a change and not compelling at the price.

    • It becomes a 20 percent case below about 95 EUR, or if the re-rating branch wins because Advnsys keeps growing above 20 percent and the European base holds
    • The FY2026 results on 2027-02-12 are the test of the margin step.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material on both its Elements, 'Change Forces' B and 'Financial Outputs' B, and has reached the sell-side's estimates, while the 'Modal return' at this price is a C

    • The parts bind on the return, so the Building Block sits at the level of its change and not above it.
    • Not A, because the return fails the 20 percent bar at 101.5 EUR and the forces act on a fifth of the group through an unchanged team.
    • Not C, because both the input change and the outputs rank in the top quarter of the comparison set, and a case whose change has reached the sixth layer is not a mediocre one.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against is that Kingspan is a 74 percent-cyclical, 75 percent-new-build building-materials company whose organic growth was 0.3 percent in FY2025, whose foam core is commoditised and margin-correlated with inputs, and whose US panel penetration has sat at about 20 percent for years
      • That a doubling of a data-centre backlog and one acquisition at the top of the hyperscaler capex cycle have rebranded it as an industrial compounder
      • That the sell-side has herded, with eleven Buys of thirteen and targets +32 percent in three months
      • That the shares have re-rated 43 percent to the 78th percentile of their two-year range
      • And that when European volumes do not turn and hyperscaler capex decelerates, as it must at some point, the multiple returns to the cohort's 10 to 12x and the shares fall 30 to 40 percent, which is where Morgan Stanley's bear case of 68 to 69 EUR and ING's 12.5x over-the-cycle EV/EBITDA both sit.
    • Add the governance file (a dishonesty finding at a subsidiary, an open competition investigation with no provision, a 450 percent pay proposal) and roofing greenfields entering an incumbent market at half the incumbents' margin, and the collection of weaknesses is complete.
    • What we concede.
      • All of the structural facts
      • The price
      • The thin consensus beyond FY2028
      • That the H1 panel volume figure of +13 percent carried a pre-buy of low-to-mid single digits
      • And that our own modal return at this price is a C, which is the competing hypothesis's point made in our own arithmetic.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • The hypothesis's load-bearing premise, that volume growth is masked by price, was tested on 2026-08-07 and failed: 13 percent panel volume growth and 6.2 percent organic growth against a consensus 4.1 percent, in a contracting market.
      • The data-centre backlog is a contracted order book with a year of visibility, not a sentiment.
      • BMC was bought at under 5x, not at a peak multiple.
      • The operator has delivered above guidance in every year of the record.
    • Where the case against is right.
      • It is right about what the price pays for the change, which is why our recommendation is price-conditional rather than bullish.
      • We make no claim that any division covers the enterprise value: at about 21.5 billion EUR against FY2027 consensus EBITDA of 1,777 EURm none does, and we do not argue a bounded downside.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because an external demand inflection in data-centre infrastructure, visible in Advnsys order intake and backlog more than doubling year on year at H1 2026 and in organic revenue growth of 21.9 percent, has been met by a multi-dimensional internal response (the 850 EURm BMC acquisition at under 5x FY2027 EBITDA, two guidance raises, the buyback paused for acquisitions, five divisions folded into two, the Advnsys sale cancelled), and the combination has already travelled the full 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain to the sell-side's estimates, where FY2028 consensus EPS rose 29.5 percent in twelve months.
  • Scope: the forces act on about a fifth of the group
    • Advnsys was 18 percent of FY2025 sales, 1,655 EURm of 9,199 EURm.
  • Leadership: the chief executive and the leadership team are unchanged, so the first layer of the chain, the company's resources, carries no new force.
  • Headwind: the European construction contraction (Eurozone construction PMI 43.0 in August 2026) offsets part of the pull on the four-fifths of the company that sells into it.
  • Not A, because of the scope, the unchanged resources and the live headwind.
    • Not C, because the screen's decision rule of at least three strong internal forces is met with room to spare, and the external force meets the 'Demand Inflection' test on the company's own order book.
  • For the case, the forces are strong enough to carry a 'Major Corporate Change' pattern
    • What they cannot do is lift the whole group's growth rate on their own.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because one force, hyperscaler data-centre capital spending, is strong, structural on the evidence of customer capital spending and order books (hyperscaler capex projected at 1 trillion USD by 2027; global data-centre capacity at a 16 percent compound rate from 100 GW to 214 GW), and meets the 'Demand Inflection' test
    • A second force, European building-energy regulation, is a tailwind in principle and delayed in practice
    • And the macro force on European construction is a live headwind.
  • Direction by force: data-centre demand a tailwind
    • Input inflation a tailwind while it lasts
    • The company's own cycle turning up
    • Regulation net neutral now
    • Competition neutral to mildly tightening in foam
    • Disruption a mild headwind in high-rise and a tailwind in prefabrication
    • European construction a headwind.
  • Not A, because the headwind is live and the company's own cycle outside its technology business is flat.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand in data-centre infrastructure is changing structurally with two-plus years of visibility, and the company's own evidence confirms it: Advnsys order intake and backlog more than doubled year on year at H1 2026 after +24 percent at the end of FY2025, and Advnsys revenue was 1,020.4 EURm in H1 2026, +33.8 percent headline and +21.9 percent organic.
  • Direction: a tailwind, sector-level, evidenced by industry data (hyperscaler capex to 1 trillion USD by 2027) and by the company's backlog, which extends about one year out.
  • Traditional market: the other way
    • Non-residential permits fell 3 percent in Europe, and 74 to 75 percent of traditional sales are new-build.
  • Pre-buying: second-quarter 2026 demand was pulled forward ahead of third-quarter price rises, a low-to-mid-single-digit volume benefit on the evidence of Carlisle and Rockwool
    • Rockwool saw July volumes dip as the pre-buy unwound.
  • Not B, because the inflection is sector-level, structural on the hyperscaler evidence and already in the company's order book, which is the test the framework sets.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because higher-for-longer rates and European construction contraction act on this company more than on the universe: Western Europe is 63 percent of revenue, 74 to 75 percent of traditional sales are new-build, and the Eurozone construction PMI was 43.0 in August 2026 (housing 41.8, commercial 42.3), with France at 37.3 and the UK at 44.3 after twenty months of contraction.
  • Direction: a headwind.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because input inflation is a tailwind for this business model: spot European hot-rolled coil was +20 percent year on year and European MDI +14 percent in August 2026, passed through with a lag of about two months (six to eight weeks), supporting price and the second-half margin.
  • Basket: two-thirds steel, one-third MDI.
  • Fragilities: MDI comes from five global producers
    • The pass-through lag compresses margins for six to eight weeks on a spike
    • Deflation would reverse the top-line benefit.
  • Direction: a tailwind while inputs rise, a headwind on reversal.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the regulatory tailwind is large on paper and the regulatory headwind is specific and live, so the net direction is neutral now and a tailwind from 2028 only if enforced.
  • Tailwind on paper: the EU needs deep renovation rates to rise from 0.2 to 3.0 percent a year by 2030
    • EPBD renovation triggers from 2028 and 2030
    • An ETS2 carbon price from 2028.
    • Brokers have removed the Green Deal from base cases for slow national implementation.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is neither easing nor clearly tightening: European mineral-wool producers mirrored Rockwool's mid-single-digit price rises in H1 2025 and Rockwool refuses to match 20 percent price cuts, but Rockwool observed Kingspan lowering prices to defend regional volume, the European PIR market is in structural overcapacity on a former Kingspan manager's account, and the US roofing entry invites retaliation from Carlisle and GAF, with experts forecasting 6 to 10 percent price concessions.
  • Direction: neutral to mildly tightening in foam
    • Stable in panels.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the live disruption force is regulatory substitution of foam by non-combustible stone wool in high-rise, which is strengthening slowly and may spread to other European markets: foam lost the UK high-rise market after Grenfell, and stone wool's share rose from 8 to 13 percent.
  • Hedges: Kingspan has hedged the bio-based substitution (the Steico stake raised to 61.1 percent in late 2025; HempFlax; Troldtekt) and is a beneficiary of modular data-centre build, expected to be nearly 60 percent of new data-centre builds by 2029.
  • Direction: a mild headwind in high-rise, a tailwind in prefabrication.
