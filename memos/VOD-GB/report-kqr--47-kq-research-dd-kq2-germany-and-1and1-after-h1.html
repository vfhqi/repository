Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be only partly answered. The speed and drivers of Vodafone Germany's profit decline are reasonably well evidenced. Adjusted EBITDAaL fell by 2.4% on a reported basis to €2,139 mm in the half-year. The evidence on whether 1&1 moves its traffic off Vodafone's network is much thinner. 1&1 is building its own network, but it remains heavily dependent on Vodafone's national roaming grid, and the precise rate at which it will develop its own 5G network is not clearly visible. The overall read is a moderate, noncollapsing profit decline today. Its medium-term path depends on 1&1's build speed, regulatory outcomes and a possible Telefonica–1&1 combination, none of which can yet be resolved.
  2. Vodafone Germany's profit trajectory directly determines group earnings per share, dividend coverage, and credit rating over the next three years.
  3. Potential wholesale revenue losses from the 1&1 contract migration represent the most significant threat to Vodafone's mid-term cash flow.
1.
Pace of decline: Half-year service revenue reached €5,483 mm, a 1.1% reported increase but only 0.4% organic growth. The adjusted EBITDAaL margin was squeezed to 35.3%. Service revenue growth swung from +1.2% in the first quarter to -0.5% in the second. The direction is clearly negative and accelerating, though the absolute pace remains modest.
2.
Driver one, the court ruling: Organic adjusted EBITDAaL growth fell by 3.2%, including a negative 2.0 percentage point impact from the European Court of Justice ruling. The reversal of price increases affected 2.5 million fixed-line customers and created a negative €30 mm profit impact in the half-year. The balancesheet risk is larger than the P&L hit so far. Management classifies its legal provision as immaterial, yet a class action covers over 118,500 registered customers and the Düsseldorf court signalled an injunction. The total long-term cash outflow remains unquantified.
3.
Driver two, the fading 1&1 wholesale boost: Mobile service revenue growth turned negative at -1.9% in the second quarter, with a 2.2 percentage point drag from lapping the 1&1 wholesale contract. This is a lapping effect, not yet evidence of 1&1 leaving the network. Driver three, structural fixed-line pressure: The broadband base fell by 198,000 customers in the half-year. The TV base fell by 115,000. The gigabit base fell by 119,000. Deutsche Telekom is rolling out 2.0 to 2.5 million fibre households a year. Deutsche Telekom added 11,000 broadband customers while Vodafone lost 100,000 lines. Offsetting this, organic fixed service revenue still grew 1.1% and OXG has reached 840,000 homes passed.
4.
Peer context: Deutsche Telekom's domestic revenue rose 3.9% and mobile service revenue 2.9% in the third quarter. Telefonica Deutschland is the cautionary precedent. Its revenues fell 3.8% and Adjusted EBITDA fell 8.8% in fiscal 2025. Vodafone is therefore underperforming the incumbent but is not yet showing the damage seen at the former 1&1 host.
5.
What the 1&1 contract and network evidence shows: 1&1 migrated its roaming traffic to Vodafone from August 2024 under an 18-year contract. Pricing reimburses a share of Vodafone's network costs in proportion to 1&1's share of data consumed. 1&1's network covers about 36% of households with roughly 1,500 active sites. BNetzA denied 1&1 sub-1 GHz spectrum and extended incumbents' frequency rights to the end of 2030. UBS expects 1&1 to limit itself to 25% coverage. The evidence so far points to slow offload, but no firm migration timetable has been disclosed. 19 largest tail risk is consolidation, not organic offload: Morgan Stanley reported Telefonica negotiating a potential acquisition of 1&1 valued at €4.5bn to €5bn. Deutsche Bank noted the roaming agreement could revert to Telefonica after regulatory approval, which would mean a complete loss of Vodafone's wholesale revenue from 1&1. However, Telefonica Deutschland has downplayed immediate merger expectations, and Morgan Stanley describes a transaction as speculative. What broker and consensus figures already assume: The cited brokers already price in a continued, gradual decline rather than a cliff. Morgan Stanley projects German EBITDAaL growth of about negative 2% per annum over the next two years, and it expects headline German service revenue growth to keep fading as the initial 1&1 boost subsides. The half-year EBITDAaL was 60 basis points below the Visible Alpha consensus. These forecasts effectively assume the 1&1 contract stays in place with no abrupt traffic loss. A fast offload or a Telefonica deal would therefore be a negative deviation from consensus. Stronger cost delivery would be a positive one. Historical precedents show decay is real but usually gradual: Orange France received €720 mm of roaming revenue from Iliad in 2013. The decay played out over multiple years because building physical sites is technically hard. French mobile service revenues still fell 4.0% year-on-year in the first quarter of 2017. Iliad Italia carried over 50% of its traffic on its own network once it had rolled out 9,000 sites. Vodafone's current 1&1 exposure is earlier in that cycle. Defences exist, such as second brands (Kena and ho in Italy) and regulator-approved extensions of roaming termination periods.
6.
Management defences and group-level cushions: Vodafone plans to cut 2,300 jobs in Germany within two years. Management targets a potential €1 billion in net operating expense savings. It upgraded its fiscal 2027 Group Adjusted EBITDAaL guidance. The interim dividend rose 2.5% to 2.306 eurocents. Leverage rose to 2.5x from 2.2x, and Deutsche Bank expects it to fall below the target range in fiscal 2028 on African growth and UK synergies. German weakness therefore appears manageable for group EPS and dividend coverage in the base case. Citi's 23 November 2026 downgrade over worsening German core earnings shows that sentiment on Germany remains fragile. and next steps. Four gaps stand out. First, the refund cash outflow from consumer claims is unquantified. Second, 1&1's actual traffic-offload rate and site build pace are opaque. Third, the status of any Telefonica–1&1 transaction is unresolved. Fourth, OXG customer take-up rates are unrecorded in public statements. Some peer table figures in Section 2 also appear to mix reporting periods and should be treated cautiously. Subsequent quarterly results, BNetzA and European Commission developments, and expert checks on 1&1's network are the evidence most likely to move this question from partly answered to answered.

Section 3 – Broadband Migration, Cable Dynamics, and Price Increase Rulings

Consumer Customer Base Contraction

1.
Severe customer losses: Vodafone Germany recorded a substantial drop in its fixed consumer footprint during the first half of fiscal year 2027. The company's broadband customer base declined by 198,000 users, consisting of a 98,000 loss in the first quarter and a 100,000 loss in the second quarter. This ongoing contraction was driven by intense competitive intensity and a corporate focus on value optimization over volume. These heavy customer departures will weaken long-term broadband service revenues unless subscriber retention improves.
2.
Squeezed television revenues: Vodafone Germany's fixed-line business faces severe compounding pressure from the structural decline of its television consumer base. The TV customer base declined by 115,000 during the first half of fiscal year 2027, including a 55,000 loss in the first quarter and a 60,000 loss in the second quarter. This contraction was driven by the legislative elimination of forced multi-dwelling unit cable television access. This regulatory change is expected to permanently reduce high-margin TV contributions and accelerate consumer broadband churn.
3.
Historical customer attrition: Vodafone Germany has suffered persistent subscriber losses in its fixed division since implementing major retail tariff changes. Operational reviews indicate that fixed-line revenue growth rates fell by seven percentage points in fiscal year 2025 as customers departed following broadband price increases. This multi-year downward trajectory has been exacerbated by cable TV deregulation and aggressive competitor pricing. This historical trend suggests that fixed customer losses represent a structural rather than temporary commercial headwind.
4.
Volatile wireless net additions: Vodafone Germany's mobile contract customer base experienced highly unstable trends during the first half of fiscal year 2027. The operator reported a net loss of 75,000 contract mobile customers in the half-year period, combining an 85,000 customer decline in the first quarter with a modest recovery of 10,000 additions in the second quarter. This performance was heavily weighed down by intense retail competition across German mobile brands. These weak customer additions will suppress mobile service revenue growth in upcoming quarters.
5.
Accelerating gigabit losses: Vodafone Germany experienced pronounced customer defections on its high-speed network segment during the first half of fiscal year 2027. The gigabit broadband customer base contracted by 119,000 lines, showing a 62,000 customer loss in the first quarter and a 57,000 customer loss in the second quarter. Management attributed this persistent decline to retail pricing actions and promotional activities by aggressive competitors. This negative trend is highly likely to erode core consumer fixed revenues over the next eighteen months.

Cable Versus Fibre Dynamics

6.
Growing technology deficits: Vodafone Germany's hybrid fiber-coaxial network is losing its competitive edge as rivals expand pure fiber networks. Expert assessments indicate that pure fiber-to-the-home technology provides superior download speeds and symmetrical upload speeds compared to legacy cable networks. While cable operators rely on node splitting to maintain speeds, the technology is constrained because cable remains a shared access medium. This performance gap will drive structural market share shifts away from Vodafone's cable infrastructure.
7.
Aggressive competitor deployment: Deutsche Telekom is rapidly expanding its pure fiber footprint to challenge Vodafone's cable dominance. Deutsche Telekom is currently executing an annual rollout target of 2.0 to 2.5 million new fiber households to future-proof its German infrastructure. This rapid expansion represents a significant strategic threat because it targets high-value urban households previously locked into cable agreements. This massive infrastructure rollout is expected to accelerate broadband customer losses for Vodafone Germany.
8.
Symmetrical network advantages: Deutsche Telekom's transition from legacy copper to pure fiber gives it a major competitive advantage over Vodafone Germany's cable footprint. Incumbents possess a structural share donor in markets with high hybrid-fiber coaxial presence because cable lacks symmetrical high-speed capabilities. This structural advantage enables Deutsche Telekom to attract high-ARPU subscribers from cable networks during fiber migrations. This competitive dynamic is expected to dilute Vodafone Germany's premium subscriber base over time.
9.
Intense rural competition: Deutsche Glasfaser is targeting non-cable German regions to secure long-term infrastructure dominance. The alternative operator focuses its fiber-to-the-home build on the thirty-five percent of German homes not covered by cable networks. Deutsche Glasfaser typically targets a forty percent penetration rate within twelve weeks of launching in new municipalities. This aggressive rural expansion will block Vodafone's geographic expansion beyond its existing cable footprint.
10.
Slower competitor rollouts: Alternative fiber network builders in Germany are facing severe capital constraints that could limit their build rates. Rising interest rates and the end of cheap money have forced several alternative operators to scale back their rollout plans. Altnets are now focusing on cash generation and customer growth rather than expensive greenfield projects. This slowing competitor rollout rate will temporarily alleviate overbuild pressures for Vodafone's cable network.
11.
Resurgent fiber joint ventures: Vodafone Germany's joint venture OXG is accelerating its own fiber network deployment to defend its customer base. Vodafone announced that Societe Generale completed the acquisition of a fifty percent stake in OXG to secure stable funding. The joint venture has stabilized its rollout and is currently building at an annual run-rate of 800,000 homes. This accelerated fiber buildout requires substantial capital expenditure outlays that will suppress short-term cash flows.

European Legal Rulings and Refunds

12.
Adverse legal decisions: Vodafone Germany suffered a severe regulatory setback that invalidated its historical fixed-line pricing strategy. The European Court of Justice ruled in September 2026 that telecommunications providers do not possess a unilateral right to amend general terms and conditions. This decision directly struck down Vodafone's unilateral five euro monthly price increases implemented in 2023. This legal precedent will force immediate price reversals and severely diminish fixed-line average revenue per user.
13.
Severe revenue headwinds: Vodafone Germany's financial metrics experienced immediate compression following the unfavorable European legal decision. The reversal of the monthly price increases impacted 2.5 million fixed-line customers, creating a negative thirty million euro profit impact in the first half of fiscal year 2027. This price reduction acted as a direct two percentage point organic headwind to adjusted EBITDAaL growth. The loss of this high-margin revenue is projected to reduce group free cash flow by one and a half percent.
14.
Balance sheet provisions: Vodafone Germany's balance sheet reflects very limited immediate liability provisions despite the high volume of customer refund registrations. Management has recorded a legal provision related to the price rises that is classified as immaterial to the overall group financial statements. This accounting treatment relies on the assumption that only registered customers will successfully claim historical refunds. However, this assumption represents a major risk because a definitive court injunction could expand refund liabilities exponentially across the legacy base.
15.
Escalating class-action risks: Vodafone Germany faces substantial financial exposure from consumer refund lawsuits registered in German regional courts. The Federation of German Consumer Organisations initiated a collective class action representing over 118,500 registered customers as of September 2026. Furthermore, the Dusseldorf Higher Regional Court indicated it would grant an injunction on November 26, 2026, declaring Vodafone's price terms invalid. These compounding legal defeats could trigger massive retrospective consumer refunds and interest payments.

Peer Comparisons and Case Precedents

Divergent peer performance: Deutsche Telekom's domestic fixed division is heavily outperforming Vodafone Germany's contracting broadband segment. Deutsche Telekom reported positive net additions of 11,000 broadband customers during the third quarter of 2026, reversing previous line losses as price rise impacts waned. This positive momentum highlights a stark contrast to Vodafone's simultaneous loss of 100,000 broadband lines. This widening operational divergence confirms that Vodafone is losing core market share to its primary German rival.

This strong growth was driven by a healthy 1.5% expansion in fixed service revenues and high customer additions. This financial strength ensures that Deutsche Telekom possesses superior capital to invest in aggressive fiber overbuilding.
18.
Comparable cable precedents: International cable operators have faced severe commercial pressure to abandon legacy coaxial technology when challenged by pure fiber rollouts. In the United Kingdom, joint venture VMO2 stopped its planned fiber-to-the-home upgrade of its existing hybrid fiber-coaxial network in November 2026 due to capital constraints. This strategic retreat has raised concerns that the operator will lose long-term competitiveness against rapid fiber builds. This precedent suggests that keeping hybrid coaxial networks going ultimately risks structural market failure against pure fiber.

Consolidating wholesale precedents: Alternative fiber-to-the-home deployment models in Europe often culminate in consolidation by the dominant market incumbent. The Reggefiber fiber-to-the-home joint venture model in the Netherlands was ultimately acquired by Dutch incumbent KPN to secure infrastructure control. This consolidation allowed the incumbent to neutralize competitive fiber overbuild and secure high wholesale returns. This Dutch case suggests that independent German fiber builders may eventually be acquired by incumbents, further marginalizing Vodafone's wholesale options.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

Contrary evidence: Vodafone Germany's consumer broadband segment demonstrated robust revenue growth and rising average revenue per user despite experiencing substantial net customer losses. Strategists note that cable networks employing DOCSIS standards are capable of delivering high download speeds that satisfy average customer demand. Customer churn remains low if operators offer attractive multi-play service bundles and maintain high satisfaction levels. This technological resilience suggests that Vodafone's cable network can defend its core broadband base without immediate full-fiber upgrades.

Other side of the story: Vodafone Germany could fully neutralize its broadband customer losses and legal headwinds if its OXG joint venture and off-net marketing partnerships scale rapidly. Vodafone's strategic partnership with Deutsche GigaNetz and marketing agreements with Deutsche Glasfaser are designed to reach up to one million additional fiber homes by 2030. Furthermore, the OXG fiber rollout has resumed its target pace, secured Societe Generale's capital, and reached 840,000 homes passed. This expanded fiber reach, combined with modern node-splitting cable upgrades, will allow Vodafone to capture high-value customers and restore positive long-term EBITDAaL growth.

Section 4 – The 1&1 Roaming Agreement and Wholesale Revenue Decay

Roaming Agreement Mechanics

Persistent transition: 1&1 United Internet migrated its entire mobile roaming traffic from Telefonica Deutschland to Vodafone Germany starting in August 2024.
