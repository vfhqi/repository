Section 3 – Contractual dimension: Lease terms and change-of-control risks

Section summary
  1. Cellnex's master service agreements provide robust cash flow insulation through long-term maturities and strict all-or-nothing clauses.

Cellnex lease durations and termination dynamics

1.
Robust contract duration: Cellnex's master lease agreements with anchor mobile operators generally establish initial non-cancellable terms of ten to twenty years, with total duration extending up to forty years including automatic extensions.
2.
Enforceable portfolio protection: The master service agreements utilize all-or-nothing renewal clauses, meaning anchor tenants must renew or cancel their entire portfolio rather than selectively decommissioning individual sites.
3.
CPI escalation mechanisms: Under standard contractual terms, fees are typically revised based on inflationary indices like the consumer price index or fixed annual escalators ranging from one to two percent.
4.
Extended Spanish template: In Spain, the MasOrange contract renewal in February 2025 unified and extended previous MASMOVIL and Orange agreements until 2048, establishing a strict all-or-nothing termination window in 2038.
5.
Sustained French terms: French master service agreements with SFR run through the end of the 2030s, and over eighty percent of French revenue is insulated from co-tenancy churn as it is derived from anchor contracts.

Change-of-control definitions and operator limits

6.
Strict parent thresholds: Cellnex's debt instruments and bond programs define a change of control as the acquisition of more than fifty percent of voting rights or the right to appoint the majority of the board.
7.
Competitive operator protection: For contracts with anchor customers, change-of-control triggers are generally restricted to cases where the acquiring entity is a direct competitor of the anchor customer.
8.
French transfer protections: Former network executives note that a potential change of control at the operator level does not allow a buyer of SFR to break the contract, as the lease remains binding on the operator.
9.
Asset buyback clauses: If a competitor of an anchor customer acquires significant influence over a local subsidiary, the anchor customer is generally granted a highly specific buyback option to purchase back the local infrastructure.
10.
Bouygues equity options: The Nexloop joint venture with Bouygues Telecom incorporates specific equity call options for Bouygues over Nexloop shares after a twenty-year period or upon specific contractual breaches.

Contrast with Italian Inwit contract disputes

11.
Contested Italian terms: In contrast to Cellnex's stable renewals, Italian tower operator Inwit is facing severe contractual disputes as both of its anchor tenants submitted early master service agreement termination notices.
12.
Unilateral cancellation windows: Fastweb submitted a non-renewal notice to terminate its agreement in March 2028, while Telecom Italia is targeting contract termination by August 2030.
13.
Automatic extension dispute: Inwit argues that the termination of TIM and Vodafone's joint control in August 2022 triggered a change-of-control clause that automatically extended all agreements until 2038.
14.
Ardian shareholder exit: The joint-control dissolution occurred when TIM sold a portion of Daphne 3 to a consortium led by Ardian, which dissolved the joint-control agreements over Inwit.
15.
Inwit legal pushback: Inwit has initiated court proceedings before the Court of Milan, arguing that the early termination notices lack valid legal grounds and violate the valid 2038 agreements.
16.
side of the story: – Vulnerable legal precedents: If Italian courts permanently uphold the early termination rights for TIM and Fastweb, it creates a highly damaging legal precedent that consolidators in France could attempt to leverage to dismantle Cellnex's master service agreements before 2038.
