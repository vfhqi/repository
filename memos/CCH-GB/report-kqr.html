Section 2 – Historical Precedents: Foreign Business Seizures and Administrations

Section summary

Since January 2025

Quantitative Scope of Seizures

1.
Extensive tracking: Research into the Russian asset landscape identified forty-two relevant sources covering ten foreign companies and eight comparable past cases of corporate administration. Analysis of these documents reveals that the Russian government has systematically accelerated de facto seizures since January 2025 to retaliate against foreign sanctions. This regulatory crackdown has increasingly targeted highly profitable consumer staples and industrial firms from countries labeled as unfriendly. The de facto transfer of these operations continues to erode corporate balance sheets across unfriendly jurisdictions.
2.
Retaliatory takeover: US-domiciled packaging manufacturer AptarGroup suffered the seizure of its local subsidiary OOO Aptar Vladimir under a presidential decree signed on 21 September 2026. This forced intervention occurred just days after US lawmakers approved a fresh wave of sanctions targeting Russian oil and gas purchases. The administrative decree instantly transferred management of the Vladimir packaging facility to Chechen-linked dairy company Health and Nutrition. This sudden loss of control forced the parent company to deconsolidate its Russian business during the third quarter.
3.
Severe write-down: AptarGroup suffered a major balance sheet hit after the Russian state trapped $30 mm of cash held locally. The parent company recorded a $61.20 mm pre-tax loss on deconsolidation, which included the full impairment of its local cost-method investment. Because the local business had historically contributed less than 1% of total consolidated assets, the ongoing operational drag on future group earnings remains minor. This precedent highlights how quickly trapped cash balances can be permanently lost under hostile decrees.
4.
Abrupt intervention: Swiss-domiciled food giant Nestlé lost control of its operations when a presidential decree placed its local assets under temporary external administration on 17 September 2026. This forced seizure followed a state-led regulatory review of the company's activities reported several weeks earlier. The administrative order transferred operational control of Nestlé Russia to a domestic entity called LEV Management, which is headed by an interior ministry general. This aggressive state action demonstrated that even suppliers of essential goods are vulnerable to sudden asset expropriation.

Massive derecognition: Nestlé incurred a substantial financial blow by removing approximately CHF 1.60 bn of Russian net assets from its balance sheet. The loss of control also removes key interest income on unrepatriated cash held in local banks.
