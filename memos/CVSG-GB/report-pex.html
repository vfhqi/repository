CVS Group (CVSG-GB): Pre-earnings expectations for the AGM statement and four-month trading update of 25 November 2026

Pre-earnings Expectations, version 1, by the Investment Analyst (project IA - Pre-earnings Preparation), written Tuesday 24 November 2026. The event: CVS Group's annual general meeting (AGM) statement and trading update for the four months July to October 2026 (the first third of financial year 2027, the year to 30 June 2027), due Wednesday 25 November 2026; last year's equivalent statement came at 07:00 UK. Prices are London closes to Monday 23 November 2026 (last close 1,378p). Money is in pounds; per-share figures in pence. No trade call is made here (Richard, 11-Nov-2026: "Q2 = B"; 20-Nov-2026: "Q3 = A", Investment Analyst only).

Terms. LFL: like-for-like sales growth, the company's organic measure. EBITDA: adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. EPS: adjusted earnings per share. CMA: the Competition and Markets Authority, which investigated the United Kingdom veterinary market. R&D credit: the research and development expenditure credit, booked inside EBITDA. Healthy Pet Club: CVS Group's monthly preventive-care subscription.

1. The judgement in brief

  • The headline number is group LFL for the four months, and the bar is about 2% or better, with full-year expectations left unchanged. No broker in our files publishes a four-month figure; the 2% bar is AlphaSense's reading of Deutsche Bank and Jefferies (23-Nov-2026). Last year's four months printed 2.5%; the second half of financial year 2026 printed 1.5%.
  • Our own reading sits below that bar: about 1.5% group LFL, because last year's comparison is the hardest of the year, while the practices' own LFL improves. The Investment Analyst's model (memo of 3-Nov-2026; IR Call Question List of 19-Nov-2026) has group LFL near 1.5% in November and January against 2.5% and 2.7% a year earlier, and the Veterinary Practices division improving from 1.0% to about 1.4%.
  • Why it matters: a 1.5% print would read as deceleration on a business that is improving. The memo calls this the "soft optics" risk.
  • The shares have already moved ahead of the numbers, so "in line" may not be enough. The shares rose about 24% from 2 October to 13 November 2026 while FY2027 EPS consensus stayed flat over three and twelve months; in the last 100 days no broker raised a target price, three cut one, and one rating left Buy on 19 November (the Assistant Portfolio Manager's note on the 23 November close).
  • Why it matters: a re-rating on unchanged estimates needs the statement to start moving the estimates. A neutral statement leaves the re-rating without support.
  • Five things are genuinely new on the day: the four-month LFL (and whether the practices are split out); the full-year wording; Healthy Pet Club members; leverage and buyback progress; and any name for the next chief executive.
  • Everything else is known: the CMA final order (22-Sep-2026, "entirely in line with expectations" said management), the chief executive's retirement (announced March 2026), the £50m buyback, the National Insurance and minimum-wage cost increases, and the September statement that the new year had started with positive LFL.

5. Threats, risks and fragilities

  • Volume. FY2026 practice volumes fell about 3% to 4% while price rose about 4% to 5% (Investment Analyst's reading). A further fall in volume after the summer 2026 price rise is the development the memo says would most damage the case. Each point of United Kingdom volume is about £2.9m of EBITDA and 3.1p of EPS; each point of price about £5.8m and 6.2p.
  • Costs. The National Insurance and minimum-wage increases add about £11m a year on AlphaSense's figures (£8m and £3m); the same report also cites £4m and £8m for an earlier year. Not reconciled. Labour is more than half of a practice's costs (Jefferies).
  • The CMA remedies in practice. Published price lists, written prescriptions and a prescription fee cap (reported at £21). Experts quoted by AlphaSense think the risk of further switching of medicines to online pharmacies is low, because most chronic prescriptions moved before the investigation. The rebranding to "CVS Vets" cost £5.1m.
  • Leadership. The chief executive's retirement was announced in March 2026; no successor is named in our files. The memo puts the chance that a new chief executive resets the 4% to 8% organic growth framework within a year at about 60%.
  • The R&D credit. As section 2: consensus may carry about 3.4p of EPS that we do not.
  • Household demand. Routine and preventive visits are weaker on soft household confidence (Jefferies); Pets at Home reported 1.9% growth in its veterinary revenue from pet owners (date not given in the report).
  • History of sharp falls on news. The shares fell (17.9%) on 7 September 2023, when the CMA announced its review, and (25.1%) on 12 March 2024, when it opened its market investigation, both on more than 13 times normal volume (our price file).

7. The bull and the bear reading of about 2% LFL with expectations unchanged

  • Bull: the hardest comparison of the year was met, the CMA is behind the company, the buyback and acquisitions carry EPS growth that is already largely owned, and the Healthy Pet Club gives a predictable base. The re-rating holds and the next test is the January update.
  • Bear: 2% is below last year's 2.5% and confirms the slowdown; a 24% rise on unchanged estimates has no support from the numbers; the R&D credit and wage costs leave consensus at risk; and there is still no chief executive. The shares give back part of the rise.

8. Questions worth asking management, if offered a call

  • How did the summer 2026 price rise land on volumes in July to October, and what is the split between price and volume?
  • How much of the wage and National Insurance increase has been recovered through fees, and when does the rest come?
  • Where does the chief executive search stand, and has the board's brief changed?
  • What was organic growth in Australia, separately from acquisitions?
  • What is the run-rate of the R&D credit for FY2027, and how much provision remains?
