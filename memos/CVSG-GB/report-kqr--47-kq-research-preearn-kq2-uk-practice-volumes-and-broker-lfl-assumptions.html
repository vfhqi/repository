Section 4 – Impact of the Competition and Markets Authority Final Remedies

Section summary
  1. The publication of the Competition and Markets Authority final order on September 22, 2026, removes the structural overhang for major UK veterinary groups.
  2. Large consolidators are successfully mitigating clinical drug margin pressure by increasing service pricing and leveraging integrated online pharmacies.
  3. We have high confidence in these regulatory impacts because both company reports and broker analyses confirm robust operational adaptations are already underway.

Regulatory Order and

1.
Transparency Regulatory clarity: The Competition and Markets Authority published its final order on the veterinary market on September 22, 2026, marking the end of its investigation. Large groups welcomed the final order because it provides long-awaited regulatory clarity and commercial certainty for their corporate practices. The order sets out binding transparency requirements that must be implemented within a strict timetable. This final de-risking event allows corporate operators to shift their focus back toward growing their clinical operations.
2.
Improved caps: The Competition and Markets Authority amended its proposed written prescription fee cap following strong feedback from the veterinary industry. The final order set the prescription fee cap at 21 pounds inclusive of VAT for the first drug prescribed. Clinicians can also charge an additional twelve pounds and fifty pence for any subsequent drugs prescribed at the same time. This tiered pricing is a significant improvement over the flat sixteen pounds cap originally proposed in the provisional decision.
3.
Elevated friction: Independent and corporate practices anticipate that written prescriptions will generate significant friction and administrative load. Clinicians find that manually processing written prescriptions creates a severe operational burden because they do not integrate well with existing management systems. Since clinical staff must spend extra time handling paperwork, practices are likely to pass these administrative costs onto the pet owners. Consequently, the regulation may inadvertently drive up the total cost of clinical consultations.
4.
Transparent lists: CVS Group has made substantial progress in complying with the transparency requirements by publishing price lists directly on its practice websites. The group successfully implemented online pricing since the end of 2025 to prepare for the final order. Listing common treatment rates online ensures that clients can easily compare the costs of care before visiting. Providing this upfront clarity directly addresses the regulatory demand for improved consumer information.

5.

Pet owners had previously discovered online pharmacies, which mitigated any sudden loss of retail drug volume following the September order. The primary harm from the prolonged regulatory investigation was the erosion of consumer trust rather than direct revenue loss. This suggests that the financial downside of the prescription remedies has already been absorbed.

Visual branding: CVS Group completed the vast majority of its joint branding initiatives to comply with the Competition and Markets Authority guidelines. The group has rebranded nearly all of its first-opinion and referral practices under joint corporate identifiers. While corporate groups are comfortable with these transparency remedies, visible branding could make corporate ownership more obvious to pet owners.
