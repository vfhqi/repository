Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating the interplay of OTE's four systemic feedback loops is central to predicting the trajectory of per-share earnings and cash returns through 2030.

The Core Key Question

1.
Key Question: "Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE), HTO-GR: Greece's incumbent fixed and mobile operator, 54.6% owned by Deutsche Telekom, with segments in mobile services, retail fixed broadband and pay- TV, national and international wholesale, and System Solutions (ICT and public-sector digitalisation services). Key question: which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)? Start from these possible dynamics: Loop one, wholesale utilisation: as a new wholesale fibre platform adds an anchor tenant, does its rising utilisation lower its unit cost and wholesale price and draw further tenants away from the incumbent, and how fast did this run in Italy, Ireland, Belgium and the United Kingdom? What do OTE's volume-commitment wholesale agreements with Vodafone Greece and Nova (effective from August 2024 and later, discounts of up to 28%) require in volumes and duration, and what penalties or price steps apply if volumes migrate? How dependent is Nova's wholesale choice on its pending sale by BC Partners and on the identity of the buyer? Loop two, fibre lock-in: does each copper-to-fibre migration (stop-sell rules in fibre-passed buildings since June 2025) raise revenue per line and lower churn enough to reinforce OTE's retail share, and what does in-building cabling in Greek apartment blocks do to rivals' activation speed? Loop three, weak rivals: do wholesale payments to OTE keep Vodafone Greece and Nova cash-constrained (Deutsche Bank Research: FY2024 EBITDAaL less capital expenditure of EUR 41m and EUR -33m against OTE Greece's EUR 784m), limiting their capacity for price aggression? Loop four, capital return: how reliably do formulaic payouts and buyback cancellation compound per-share value at low-growth incumbents? What would stall or reverse each loop?."

Business Model Dynamics and Segment Exposure

Supported by Deutsche Telekom's 54.6% controlling ownership, OTE generates over 50% of group revenues from high-margin mobile service revenues and retail fixed broadband. The company complements these core consumer lines with pay-TV, national and international wholesale access, and rapidly growing System Solutions ICT services. Understanding how customer migration across fixed and mobile networks directly feeds back into unit economics is vital for forecasting long-term operational resilience.
3.
Interlocking dynamics: The central investor focus centers on how four distinct operational feedback loops directly govern OTE's medium-term earnings per share expansion. High-margin national wholesale volume commitments from Vodafone Greece and Nova reinforce cash flow generation while capping competitive infrastructure overbuild. Concurrently, retail copper-to-fibre migrations expand average revenue per user while mandatory stop-sell regulations reduce churn. This structurally cashgenerative model generates funds that disproportionately exceed rivals' capital budgets, maintaining market dominance.

Section 2 – Loop One: Wholesale Utilisation Dynamics and Agreements

Section summary

Domestic Wholesale Framework and OTE Dominance

1.
Analytical scope: The examination of OTE's systemic loops incorporates 160 relevant documents from company filings, broker research, and regulatory decisions. No comparable peer or case has been left out of this analysis because all primary Greek and European telecom operators are represented. This extensive evidence allows an in-depth evaluation of how OTE's wholesale platform shapes domestic competition.
2.
Rapid acceleration: The rising utilization of OTE's wholesale fiber platform lowers long-term unit costs and establishes a reinforcing loop that locks in retail providers. As the network gains scale, the incremental cost of adding subsequent subscribers drops, allowing OTE to offer more attractive commercial terms. Consequently, this commercial scheme precludes alternative providers from overbuilding infrastructure.
3.
Substantial discounts: The wholesale volume-commitment framework began with a Memorandum of Understanding signed between Vodafone and OTE after the Greek regulator EETT allowed volume discounts. This framework, which was later finalized in August 2024, offers alternative operators unit price discounts of up to 28% in exchange for volume commitments. By committing to these volumes, rivals minimize their incentives to purchase capacity from alternative wholesale platforms.

This risk is mitigated because OTE commits to wholesale rivals' networks rather than building over them, keeping overbuild minimal.

Section 3 – Loop Two: Fibre Lock-In and Copper-to-Fibre Migration

Section summary
  1. The evidence supports a high level of confidence that regulatory stop-sell rules and building installation bottlenecks protect OTE's retail market share.
  2. This confidence is anchored on solid FTTH subscriber additions and physical multi-dwelling unit constraints noted in both company filings and broker research.

Regulatory Drivers and Subsidy Programs

1.
Accelerated transition: The Greek telecommunications regulator EETT established pivotal stop-sell policies that allow operators to permanently cease offering legacy copper connections once high-speed fiber becomes available in a building. In September 2025, the regulator specifically permitted operators to stop selling Fibreto-the-Cabinet broadband in buildings where full Fibre-to-the-Home access is already deployed. This progressive regulatory intervention acts as a key driver for customer upgrades to high-speed networks.
2.
Sustained progression: Hellenic Telecom expects that systematically migrating its broad customer base from copper to fiber-to-the-home infrastructure will generate substantial average revenue per user upside. Although initial state-backed broadband subsidy schemes for consumer connections were fully exhausted around February 2026, the retail migration has continued at a rapid pace. This ongoing conversion enables the operator to accelerate the monetization of fiber capital investments and grow margins.
3.
Subsidized utilization: Government-subsidized broadband coupons have served as a critical driver for accelerating customer migrations across Greece. In mid-2025, more than seventy thousand vouchers were actively utilized to offset monthly subscription fees. These state and European subsidies directly boost customer migration to fiber products and accelerate network monetization.

In-Building Infrastructure Bottlenecks

4.
Severe friction: Urban network deployment in Greece presents unique physical challenges because a vast majority of the metropolitan population resides in dense multi-dwelling apartment blocks. Since competing fiber-to-thehome providers are not legally obligated to share vertical in-building cabling infrastructure, each operator must independently install dedicated lines. This lack of shared building infrastructure slows competitors' activation speed significantly and protects OTE's base.
5.
Disrupted entry: Public Power Corporation launched retail broadband-only services in June 2025 with promotional prices positioned nearly fifty percent below established domestic telecommunications operators. Despite this aggressive commercial strategy, PPC has failed to wholesale its growing fiber-to-the-home network to OTE, Nova, or Vodafone. Thus, severe in-building cabling hurdles blunt the disruptor's market impact in core urban apartment blocks.
6.
Defensive insulation: Hellenic Telecom launched a range of high-speed 5G Fixed Wireless Access products in January 2025 to actively defend its core broadband market share in rural areas. This commercial service specifically addresses poor copper speeds in underserved territories where fiber-to-the-home networks have not yet been built. Consequently, these rapid product deployments help limit retail broadband customer losses to competing satellite providers like Starlink.

Section 4 – Loop Three: Weak Rivals and Cash Constraints

Section summary
  1. Large operating free cash flow disparities between OTE and domestic rivals limit alternative providers' ability to execute sustained price wars.
  2. Structural wholesale payments to OTE reinforce competitor cash constraints unless external joint ventures or private equity sales alter market dynamics.

Cash Flow Disparities and Financial Metrics

1.
Operational dominance: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization maintains an overwhelming operational free cash flow advantage over its domestic telecommunications competitors. Deutsche Bank Research calculated that OTE Greece generated €784m in EBITDAaL less capital expenditure during FY2024, expanding from €742m in FY2023 and €712m in FY2022. This massive liquidity generation reinforces OTE's capacity for strategic investment while maintaining balance sheet stability.
2.
Constrained competitors: In stark contrast to the incumbent, alternative service providers in Greece operate under severe operational free cash flow deficits or marginal surpluses. Deutsche Bank Research reported that Vodafone Greece generated EBITDAaL less capital expenditure of just €41m in FY2024, representing a multi-year decline from €72m in FY2023 and €103m in FY2022. This continuous cash contraction severely restricts Vodafone's scope for aggressive pricing.
3.
Negative generation: Nova, which is fully owned by United Group and controlled by private equity firm BC Partners, faces even deeper liquidity constraints in the Greek market. Deutsche Bank Research estimated Nova's FY2024 EBITDAaL less capital expenditure at negative €33m, worsening from negative €9m in FY2023 and negative €16m in FY2022. Consequently, Nova has never generated positive operational free cash flow under United Group ownership.
4.
Market consolidation: The aggregate distribution of industry financial returns highlights the asymmetric structure of the Greek telecommunications sector. Deutsche Bank Research calculated that OTE captured 65% of total market revenue, 74% of market EBITDAaL, and 99% of total market operational free cash flow in FY2024. These figures confirm that OTE captures virtually all industry cash generation, leaving competitors cash-strapped.
5.
Capital commitment: Competitors' high capital expenditure requirements relative to their operating earnings prevent them from funding long-term price wars. Deutsche Bank Research showed that Nova accounted for 31% of total market capital expenditure in FY2024 while generating only 16% of market EBITDAaL and a 5% negative share of market operational free cash flow. This imbalance forces rivals to prioritize cash preservation over tariff cuts.

Structural Wholesale Dependence and Price War Resistance

6.
Wholesale drain: Periodic wholesale access payments made to OTE for fixed infrastructure and bitstream services directly drain competitors' operating cash flows. Because alternative providers rely on OTE's expansive copper and fiber footprint to serve a large portion of their retail subscriber bases, wholesale fees act as a structural transfer of cash. This recurring cash drain insulates OTE against sustained retail discounting from non-integrated rivals.
7.
Targeted retention: Rather than initiating broad list price reductions, established telecommunications operators in Greece rely on targeted promotional retention offers to protect their subscriber bases. Deutsche Bank Research noted that faced with new market entry, existing players will first attempt to retain customers through targeted offers rather than cutting list prices. This commercial behavior prevents destructive price wars among main players.
8.
Strategic positioning: Independent market analysis underscores that Vodafone Group's corporate strategy avoids initiating aggressive price wars in European peripheral markets. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that Vodafone Greece acts in line with its parent company's broader strategy by avoiding primary price wars, though it responds defensively to rival promotions. Therefore, Vodafone acts as a rational pricing agent in fixed broadband.
9.
Rational behavior: Private equity ownership structures at United Group further incentivize cash preservation over market-share growth at any cost. Deutsche Bank Research noted that United Group's consolidated net debt to EBITDAaL stood at 4.2x at year-end 2023, while Nova contributed 27% of group EBITDAaL. High leverage across the parent entity prevents Nova from burning cash excess in prolonged domestic price wars.
10.
Absence of disruptors: The structural setup of the Greek telecommunications market differs markedly from European jurisdictions that experienced severe price erosion driven by aggressive low-cost entrants. Deutsche Bank Research emphasized that unlike peers in Italy or France, OTE does not face rivals willing to burn capital for several consecutive years, such as Iliad. The absence of such competitors preserves stable sector ARPU dynamics.

This joint venture provides alternative providers an independent openaccess infrastructure platform.

Acquisition by a well-capitalized strategic owner could recapitalize Nova for aggressive expansion.
14.

In Belgium, Proximus and Telenet's Wyre established collaborative network agreements, while regulators forced cable networks to wholesale access to mobile-centric Orange Belgium. This regulatory access enabled Orange Belgium to build material convergence market share.
15.
UK consolidation: In the United Kingdom, altnets face severe financial constraints against BT Openreach, driving market consolidation and wholesale restructuring. Fitch Ratings noted in July 2026 that financial constraints and integration challenges restricted altnet M&A, but consolidation is likely to change competitive dynamics materially. BT's proposed acquisition of TalkTalk's platform for £400m demonstrates how cash-strapped wholesale tenants eventual consolidate.

Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on July 27, 2026, that Vodafone Greece achieved double-digit service revenue growth of 12.5% YoY in Q1 FY2027, accelerating from 1.3% in Q4. This revenue recovery indicates that cash-constrained competitors can leverage public contracts for growth. Other side of the story: While weak rivals and severe cash constraints protect OTE's high operational free cash flow margins today, the dynamic is vulnerable to external structural interventions. If the Vodafone-PPC joint venture successfully operationalizes its 1.6m home footprint by 2027, Vodafone and Nova could shift wholesale traffic away from OTE's network. Such a wholesale migration would erode OTE's national wholesale earnings and eliminate the cash asymmetric advantage over time.

This clear framework guarantees that underlying cash generation directly translates into immediate and formulaic shareholder value creation.

The broker noted that OTE's total cash return to equity value ratio stands 220 basis points higher than the European telecom sector average. This exceptional yield premium indicates that OTE remains a premier cash return vehicle within the wider European utility and telecom landscape.
