Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Castellana Properties exhibits several structural vulnerabilities that merit close monitoring despite the company's otherwise strong operational performance. High leverage ratios present a notable concern, with Net Debt to EBITDA standing at 8.7x, materially exceeding peer Lar Espana's 6.2x and signaling elevated refinancing risk should operating performance deteriorate or interest rates remain elevated. This leverage multiple positions Castellana in the upper quartile of European retail REIT debt burdens and constrains financial flexibility for opportunistic acquisitions during market dislocations.

Short weighted average lease term to break of 2.7 years creates material near-term reversion risk, as anchor tenants can demand aggressive rent concessions if retail sales contract sharply. This abbreviated break profile stands below typical regional shopping center lease structures that commonly secure 4-5 year minimum break terms, exposing Castellana to accelerated downside rental adjustments during economic downturns.

The compressed duration amplifies the company's sensitivity to macroeconomic shocks and tenant bargaining power dynamics.

Aggressive pan-European bidding pressure threatens future acquisition returns, with aggressive multi-national bidding threatening accretive growth, as lower-cost sovereign capital dominates future transactions and midmarket operators will be priced out. Transaction yield compression to slightly above 6% on recent portfolio sales reflects intense capital competition from Norwegian sovereign wealth funds and other low-cost institutional bidders, materially reducing Castellana's ability to source attractively priced assets that generate returns above its weighted average cost of capital.

Cumulative construction input cost inflation has created meaningful project execution risk for the company's repositioning pipeline, as Spanish heavyside building material prices escalated cumulatively by 35% since 2019, substantially above general inflation rates and European construction cost benchmarks. This cost pressure compounds with persistent labor shortages in Spain continuing to hold back construction output significantly above historical averages, creating dual headwinds that threaten budgeted yields-on-cost for major projects like Los Arcos and risk delays to NOI contribution timelines that underpin forward earnings guidance.
