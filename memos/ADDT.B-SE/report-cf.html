EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Overall Materiality of Change

Addtech AB executed a significant organizational restructuring in October 2025, transitioning from five to six business areas and expanding leadership from 12 to 15 business units, representing a material structural inflection point designed to scale decentralized M&A capacity. CEO Niklas Stenberg characterizes the reorganization as highly scalable and capable of supporting a doubling of earnings within five years.

Adjusted EBITA margins reached a record-high 16.0% in fiscal year 2025/2026, marking the fifth consecutive year of margin improvements, signaling structural profitability expansion rather than cyclical timing. The group experienced eleven consecutive quarters of flat or negative organic sales growth up to Q4 2025/2026, creating a notable tension between robust acquired growth and persistent organic headwinds. International sales outside the Nordic countries reached 42% of total sales in fiscal year 2025/2026, demonstrating accelerating geographic diversification that fundamentally broadens the long-term addressable market and deal pipeline.

The combination of organizational capacity expansion, margin outperformance, and geographic shift constitutes high overall materiality of change relative to the historical business model baseline.

Top Ranked Key Drivers

The forward earnings trajectory is governed by six primary drivers ranked by impact magnitude and probability. First, decentralized M&A scalability supports 15 to 20 acquisitions annually, with disciplined pricing discipline maintained at 5x to 7x EBITA multiples, directly underpinning 8% to 10% of forward sales growth. Second, international sales outside the Nordics reached 42% of total sales, where geographic expansion widened Addtech's acquisition net to capture higher quality assets, systematically broadening the addressable market and improving deal quality mix.

Third, the weighted Manufacturing PMI crossed the critical 53 threshold in mid- 2026, with historical performance showing organic growth accelerating two to five quarters after PMI crosses 53, signaling near-term organic revenue inflection that could contribute 5% incremental growth in the second half of fiscal 2026/2027. Fourth, management confirmed that recent gross margin gains represent structural improvements rather than cyclical timings, driven by shifting product sales toward proprietary offerings that drive long-term EBITA margin expansion, supporting 100 basis points of incremental margin over the medium term.

Fifth, the Energy business area remains the largest group earnings contributor representing approximately 30% of EBITA, while Svenska Kraftnät expects investments to increase 50% year-on-year in 2026, creating durable infrastructure demand tailwinds anchored in multi-year grid modernization cycles. Sixth, the newly established Safety segment generates approximately SEK 3 billion to SEK 3.5 billion in annual revenue with estimated EBITA margins of 13.5%, where the top five Safety companies grew EBIT at a notable 16% CAGR from 2014 to 2024, unlocking leadership capacity and M&A pipeline acceleration in high-growth niches.

Recent Delivery Performance

Adjusted EBITA reached SEK 1,026 million in Q1 2026/2027, up 11% year-on-year and beating consensus estimates by 1%, demonstrating consistent execution against expectations. Organic sales growth remained flat at 0% due to persistent project postponements, while acquisitions contributed 6% to total net sales, fully offsetting flat organic sales, highlighting the critical importance of M&A execution in sustaining headline growth momentum during cyclical weakness.

Automation EBITA margins expanded to 13.6% from 10.2% on implemented restructuring programs, proving management's operational agility in defending profitability through cost actions. Operating cash flow reached SEK 605 million versus SEK 477 million last year, while return on working capital reached 81% sequentially this quarter, beating the 45% floor, confirming exceptional capital efficiency and balance sheet strength.

DNB Carnegie increased its target price to SEK 410 from SEK 405 on results, with broker revisions reflecting confidence in accelerating organic revenue growth in H2 2026/2027, indicating improving sell-side sentiment post-earnings. However, EBITA growth reached 12% in fiscal year 2025/2026, slightly below the over-the-cycle profit growth target of 15%, representing a technical miss on the long-term earnings growth ambition, albeit offset by substantial capital efficiency outperformance.

Forward Guidance Credibility

Addtech's overarching financial target is annual earnings growth exceeding 15% over an economic business cycle, aiming to source half of its growth from organic expansion and the remaining half from acquisitions within niche B2B markets, establishing clear strategic guardrails. Return on working capital reached an outstanding 81% in fiscal year 2025/2026, vastly beating the 45% target floor and rising from 68% in fiscal year 2023/2024, demonstrating sustained capital efficiency outperformance that significantly de-risks the quality of earnings.

EBITA growth reached 12% in fiscal year 2025/2026 and 14% in fiscal year 2024/2025, representing minor misses of the long-term earnings growth goal of 15%, highlighting cyclical sensitivity in organic demand that temporarily constrains near-term growth target achievement. A one percentage point interest rate hike reduces post-tax profit by SEK 57 million, exposing material interest rate sensitivity that could compress returns if monetary policy remains restrictive.

The weighted Manufacturing PMI crossed the critical 53 threshold signaling organic acceleration, while balance sheet leverage remained low at 1.3x net debt to EBITDA, preserving acquisition headroom, supporting high forward execution probability as leading indicators turn positive and financial flexibility remains intact. Overall guidance credibility is rated moderately high, anchored by consistent capital efficiency outperformance and structural margin gains, but tempered by near-term earnings growth shortfalls driven by external cyclical factors.

Sell-Side Commentary Consensus

The rating mix per Bloomberg consensus as of July 2026 indicates a strong bias toward BUY ratings following recent upgrades, reflecting improving analyst conviction on the forward outlook. DNB Carnegie's Johan Sundén maintains a BUY rating with a SEK 410 target as of September 2026, arguing that weighted manufacturing PMIs crossing 53 will trigger near-term organic revenue acceleration, capturing the dominant bullish thesis centered on cyclical recovery timing.

DNB Carnegie's Johan Lönnqvist Sundén upgraded Addtech from HOLD to BUY in June 2026, arguing that cyclical tailwinds in the second half of 2026/2027 will drive substantial multiple rerating, signaling meaningful positive rating migration as macro indicators inflect. Consensus trades at a 2027 estimated EV/EBITA of 20.5x, representing a 10% discount to historic averages of 23.3x, suggesting the market is pricing in modest valuation normalization despite improving fundamentals.

The dominant bull case expects manufacturing PMI expansion to drive restocking and double-digit EBITA growth, while the bear case focuses on prolonged customer project delays and sawmill investment freezes, framing the central debate around organic growth timing and magnitude. The consensus is constructively positioned with concentrated BUY ratings and rising price targets, but the tight 10% valuation discount to history reflects lingering uncertainty around organic growth inflection timing.

Fundamental Peer Position

Addtech posted flat 0% organic sales growth during the latest quarter matching expectations, while peer Indutrade experienced a sharp 5% organic growth rebound in its recently reported second quarter, positioning Addtech as a lagging organic performer within the Swedish compounder peer group. Addtech maintained a strong 16.6% EBITA margin during its first quarter, which compares favorably against Indutrade's 14.9% adjusted EBITA margin but trails Lifco's exceptional 23.1% adjusted EBITA margin and Sdiptech's excellent 20.3% adjusted EBITA margin, placing Addtech in the middle tier for profitability.

Addtech reported a highly profitable return on working capital of 81% in 2026, matching Lagercrantz's impressive 81% return on working capital achievement and significantly exceeding Sdiptech's 70% cash conversion sequentially in Q2, confirming top-tier capital efficiency relative to peers. Peer Lifco operates at net debt to EBITDA levels of 1.3x to 1.6x, which is higher than Addtech's leverage profile, while peer Sdiptech reported a significantly higher net debt to EBITDA ratio of 2.8x in mid-2026, establishing Addtech as having a conservative balance sheet relative to the peer median. The overall peer ranking positions Addtech as a high-quality but organically challenged compounder, distinguished by superior capital efficiency and balance sheet conservatism but constrained by below-peer organic growth momentum.

Activist Presence

No active activist presence is flagged in the AlphaSense library for the company, indicating zero material activist involvement in governance or strategic direction. The ownership structure and capital allocation framework appear to operate without external activist pressure, allowing management to execute the decentralized M&A strategy and long-term growth ambitions without short-term interventionist demands.

Key Investment Risks

The primary investment risks cluster around six material vulnerabilities. First, early cyclical industrial exposure has prolonged low organic revenue growth for three consecutive years, with bottom-up analysis showing approximately 60% of top companies having short- to mid-cyclical demand exposure, creating sustained organic growth headwinds if industrial capex recovery delays persist. Second, Swedish serial acquirers trade at rich multiples, introducing short-term multiple contraction risks for the peer group, where increasing numbers of public serial acquirers threaten to drive up multiples paid for quality assets, potentially compressing future acquisition returns and forcing capital allocation into lower-quality targets.

Third, a one percentage point interest hike reduces post-tax profit by SEK 57 million, exposing material earnings volatility to sustained high interest rates that could simultaneously freeze customer capex and erode post-tax profitability. Fourth, extreme decentralization hampers corporate control, where if autonomous divisions stumble, the thin central team cannot easily intervene, creating governance fragility that limits the ability to correct localized underperformance.

Fifth, extreme M&A decentralization lacks central oversight, where rapid international expansion increases the risk of failed integration processes, potentially leading to value destruction from poorly executed cross-border transactions. Sixth, building and installation segments remain a sustained drag on organic volumes, while high timber prices and elevated interest rates continue to freeze new sawmill project decisions, representing concentrated end-market weakness in structurally important verticals that could persist for multiple quarters.

These risks are material but partially mitigated by strong capital efficiency, balance sheet conservatism, and diversified end-market exposure, though organic growth timing remains the dominant near-term uncertainty.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Addtech has experienced eleven consecutive quarters of flat or negative organic sales growth up to Q4 2025/2026, representing the most persistent organic growth stagnation within the Swedish serial acquirer peer group. This extended period of organic revenue weakness significantly lags behind peer Indutrade's sharp 5% organic growth rebound, Sdiptech's outstanding 11% organic sales growth, and Lagercrantz's solid 6% organic revenue growth achieved in comparable reporting periods.

The prolonged organic stagnation documented in Section 5 and Section 7 raises fundamental questions about whether Addtech's decentralized portfolio has sufficient exposure to structurally growing end-markets or whether the company faces deeper competitive positioning challenges within its chosen niches. Addtech's EBITA margin of 16.0% in FY 25/26 trails peer Lagercrantz's higher EBITA margin of 18.1% for the comparable full financial year, while peer Lifco achieved an exceptional 23.1% adjusted EBITA margin during its second quarter.

This profitability gap of 200 to 710 basis points versus best-in-class peers suggests that despite recent margin expansion achievements, Addtech's current portfolio mix and pricing power remain structurally inferior to the most disciplined Nordic compounders. The margin underperformance documented in Section 4 and Section 7 becomes particularly concerning when considered alongside flat organic growth, as it indicates the company cannot rely on operating leverage to close the profitability gap with sector leaders.

Early cyclical industrial exposure has prolonged low organic revenue growth for three consecutive years, with bottom-up analysis showing approximately 60% of top companies have short- to mid-cyclical demand exposure. This disproportionate cyclical sensitivity leaves Addtech materially more vulnerable to manufacturing downturns compared to peers with greater infrastructure, healthcare, or mission-critical maintenance exposure.

The cyclical composition weakness highlighted in Section 3 means that soft sawmill demand and project delays in Energy and Process segments continue to hold back organic volume recovery, creating a structural drag that diversified peers have been able to avoid through superior end-market selection. A 1 percentage point interest rate hike reduces post-tax profit by SEK 57 million, representing material earnings sensitivity that exceeds several direct peers.

While peer Lagercrantz faces MSEK 40 pre-tax exposure, Addtech's higher absolute sensitivity reflects either greater floating-rate debt exposure or lower EBITDA scale relative to borrowings. This balance sheet vulnerability documented in Section 3 and Section 4 creates asymmetric downside risk if central banks maintain restrictive monetary policy for longer than consensus expectations, potentially constraining both acquisition financing capacity and organic demand recovery simultaneously.

EBITA growth reached 12% in FY 2025/2026 and 14% in FY 2024/2025, both falling short of the over-the-cycle profit growth target of 15%. This consecutive two-year earnings growth miss documented in Section 11 demonstrates that management's long-term financial target has proven difficult to achieve even during periods when peer serial acquirers delivered operating earnings surges of 41% in FY 2022/2023. Industry sales slipped 3% to SEK 1,172 million on weak sawmill and mechanical markets, with Industry EBITA margin declining to 20.9% year-on-year from 21.2% previously. This simultaneous revenue and margin contraction in a major business segment documented in Section 6 signals deteriorating competitive positioning or customer wallet-share losses that acquisitions cannot fully offset. The Industry segment weakness becomes particularly troubling when sawmill business revenues fell 30-50% from pandemic peaks, suggesting certain subsidiary portfolios face potential permanent demand destruction rather than cyclical normalization.

Outliers worth noting

The analysis of Addtech AB's financial profile and operational metrics reveals several statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies when benchmarked against peer compounders and sector base rates. Return on working capital reached an extraordinary 81% in FY 25/26, vastly exceeding the company's own long-term target of greater than 45%. This figure represents a dramatic outlier even within the high-performing Swedish compounder peer group, where peer Lagercrantz achieved a comparable 81% return but subsequently felt compelled to raise its minimum P/WC target to over 60% to reflect the new operational reality.

The persistence of such extreme capital efficiency at Addtech raises questions about whether the 45% floor remains relevant or whether management guidance requires recalibration to match demonstrated structural capabilities.

The company's organic growth trajectory presents a puzzling contradiction when examined across the peer landscape. Addtech experienced eleven consecutive quarters of flat or negative organic sales growth up to Q4 2025/2026, yet simultaneously achieved record-high adjusted EBITA margins of 16.0% in FY 25/26, representing the fifth consecutive year of margin improvements. This decoupling of volume stagnation from margin expansion is highly unusual compared to peers experiencing similar cyclical headwinds.

For context, peer Indutrade experienced a sharp 5% organic growth rebound in its recently reported second quarter while reporting a lower EBITA margin of 14.7%, suggesting that Addtech's margin resilience during prolonged volume weakness represents a structural anomaly requiring deeper explanation beyond simple product mix optimization.

The company's interest rate sensitivity appears asymmetrically elevated relative to its conservative leverage profile. A 1 percentage point interest rate hike reduces post-tax profit by SEK 57 million, yet financial debt to EBITDA remained low at only 1.4x at the end of fiscal year 2025/2026. When benchmarked against peers operating at comparable or higher leverage ratios, this sensitivity appears disproportionate.

Peer Lagercrantz estimates MSEK 40 pre-tax earnings exposure while Lifco faces SEK 87 million post-tax sensitivity, suggesting that either Addtech's debt structure carries unusually unfavorable floating-rate characteristics or the reported sensitivity calculations embed embedded derivative exposures not fully disclosed in standard financial statements.

The geographic revenue mix presents an unexplained asymmetry in growth contribution patterns. International sales outside the Nordic countries reached 42% of total sales in fiscal year 2025/2026, representing a substantial international footprint. However, organic sales growth remained flat at 0% due to persistent project postponements despite operating across diverse European geographies that theoretically should provide natural demand diversification.

This suggests either that cyclical exposure is more correlated across Addtech's specific end-markets than typical geographic diversification would predict, or that the company's international expansion has inadvertently concentrated exposure in synchronized weak industrial segments such as sawmills and special vehicles manufacturing. The Safety segment carve-out reveals operational profitability metrics that deviate from typical spinout economics.

Safety generates approximately SEK 3 billion to SEK 3.5 billion in annual revenue with estimated EBITA margins of 13.5%, yet the top five Safety companies grew EBIT at a notable 16% CAGR from 2014 to 2024. This historical growth rate substantially exceeds both the consolidated group performance during equivalent periods and the margins currently reported for the segment, suggesting either aggressive accounting conservatism in segment allocation or unexplained margin compression during the portfolio assembly phase that has not been adequately explained in management commentary.

The company's consensus earnings beat pattern exhibits unusual temporal clustering that deviates from random distribution expectations. Addtech has historically beaten consensus in 12 of 18 years, averaging a 10% EBIT beat, yet this strong track record appears concentrated in specific cyclical phases rather than distributed evenly. The five consecutive years of margin expansion coinciding with eleven consecutive quarters of organic stagnation represents a statistical outlier in the relationship between volume growth and profitability outcomes across industrial distribution business models, warranting investigation into whether structural business model changes or accounting policy shifts have fundamentally altered the earnings generation mechanism.

Finally, the M&A multiple arbitrage claimed by management and brokers appears statistically inconsistent with observed transaction volume acceleration. Proven compounders paid a median EV/EBITA multiple of 6.8x during 2020-2024 versus 7.5x for wider peers, yet CFO Malin Enarson states the structure supports 15 to 20 acquisitions annually, up from 10 to 15. Basic supply-demand economics would predict that doubling transaction velocity within the same fragmented niche acquisition pool should compress multiple arbitrage opportunities rather than preserve them at historical spreads.

The persistence of favorable acquisition economics despite dramatically increased competitive intensity from the expanding compounder peer group represents an unexplained outlier requiring validation through granular deal-level disclosure or independent third-party transaction database analysis cross-referenced in Section 2 and Section 9.
