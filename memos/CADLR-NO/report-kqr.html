While headline day rates like the €490k Inch Cape fixture are observable, the sustainability of margin expansion depends critically on weather risk allocation, cost escalation pass-throughs, and client renegotiation rights that remain opaque even to sellside analysts with management access. The near-term demand trajectory carries medium confidence because it depends on developer behavior under financial stress rather than observable physical constraints. The identified 2028-2029 demand air pocket reflects known auction delays and completed cancellations, but its severity could deepen or dissipate based on policy changes and commodity price movements that are inherently uncertain. Confidence classification also reveals geographic and structural asymmetries in data quality. European and North American project pipelines benefit from transparent permitting databases and regulatory disclosure requirements, while Chinese yard slot allocations and vessel charter terms are deliberately obscured, creating a persistent blind spot in global capacity modeling. Similarly, the foundation installation vessel market exhibits lower transparency than turbine installation because many heavy-lift assets serve dual oil-and-gas and renewables purposes, making it difficult to isolate dedicated offshore wind capacity. Other side of the story: While commercial contract opacity limits forecasting precision, the physical asset deficit is so acute that pricing power may persist even with imperfect visibility into contract mechanics.
