Xvivo (XVIVO-SE): what the Q3 2026 results, and the response to them, do to the case

These results and the sell-side's response to them leave the case where it was, because the case was written three days after the report on this same review and on the two broker notes that followed it; what has moved in the week since is the share price and the revisions, and they move the case's return arithmetic in its favour and its momentum and lifecycle readings against it, by less than a grade either way.

What changed

The results, as the later review carries them:

  • Net sales SEK 252.6 million for the quarter to 30 September 2026, up 34 percent year on year and 32 percent organically in local currencies, against Modular Finance consensus of SEK 238 million (a 6 percent beat), RedEye's SEK 232 million and DNB Carnegie's SEK 231 million. Operating income SEK 28.0 million against consensus SEK 22.9 million (22 percent above); EBITDA SEK 52.0 million against SEK 45.8 million (14 percent above), a 21 percent margin. Earnings per share SEK 1.42 against consensus SEK 0.54, lifted by positive tax effects and higher net financial income.
  • Thoracic SEK 177 million, with lung up 67 percent organically. Two items in it will not recur with the same intensity: SEK 8.5 million of retrospective revenue recognition on finance leases for XPS machines delivered to three organ procurement organisations, and about SEK 10 million of inventory built by the largest US customer, LungBio, which is expected to weigh on fourth-quarter thoracic sales. Excluding the SEK 8.5 million, sales were about SEK 244 million, still 5 percent above RedEye's forecast. EVLP disposable sales to OPOs and transplant centres grew 183 percent in the quarter and 92 percent year to date; three OPO hubs were active with five in the pipeline.
  • Gross margin 70 percent from 75 percent a year earlier, dragged by services, where sales fell 28 percent in local currencies and the gross margin was minus 19 percent. Abdominal gross margin 61 percent from 60 percent, with kidney volume temporarily held below demand by a manufacturing transfer that management expects to be finished by the start of 2027. The 2027 targets (EBITDA margin above 30 percent, EBIT margin above 20 percent) were kept. The CFO seat is still held by the executive who moved to strategy on 1 April 2026.
  • LungBio, approved for its own EVLP platform in June 2026, expects to begin phasing out Xvivo's machines in 2027; the company says sales to it will decline and gives no timeline. The heart system was CE-marked on 11 August 2026 with a controlled rollout to 33 clinics in four countries and French national reimbursement in hand; RedEye expects the first commercial heart sales in December. The FDA accepted the heart PMA in September 2026, with a US launch targeted for late 2027. The US liver route moved from the paused DeLIVER trial to a 510(k) submission in the first half of 2027, with an early-2028 launch estimated; RedEye still expects some confirmatory trial. Traferox received FDA clearance for a cold heart preservation system in September 2026.

The response, which the case assumed would come and has now come:

  • DNB Carnegie reiterated Buy and SEK 330, raised 2026 adjusted EPS by 10 percent to SEK 3.9 on financial income and tax, and cut 2027 by 4 percent to SEK 5.7 and 2028 by 3 percent to SEK 8.6, for lower projected abdominal sales. It argues the LungBio loss is overdone, offset by the OPO hub expansion and price initiatives on the remaining components.
  • RedEye cut its base-case fair value from SEK 385 to SEK 360, moved to a method of 90 percent DCF and 10 percent peer multiples to reflect LungBio uncertainty, kept a bull case of SEK 748 and a bear case of SEK 140, sized the LungBio drag at 5 to 6 percent of 2027 group sales, and raised European-plus-US heart to above SEK 550 million in 2028. Both brokers expect margin pressure in the second half of 2027 from US launch spending. Short interest is about 8 percent.

The challenge

The day-one piece put up three readings. Each must be tested against what is now in hand, and the honest finding is that the case has absorbed all three, because it was rewritten after they were written.

The first reading, that the lung gauge and the lung invalidation had become the same signal because the customer's inventory build and step-down would contaminate the growth line for several quarters, is now the case's first judgement: it sizes the step-down at 40 to 100, calls it the widest operating uncertainty of 2027, and tests lung excluding timing items against 140 in the fourth quarter and 130 in the first quarter of 2027. The later review adds nothing the case lacks: both brokers carry 5 to 6 percent of 2027 sales, which is the case's base, and neither has the contract. We drop it as a challenge. The third reading, that the regulator had stopped slipping and the case's modal regulatory branch might be the wrong one, is also inside the case, which dates heart approval at late 2028 by design, calls that the 60th to 65th percentile of the distribution, and names an earlier decision as the single largest upside to its own numbers. The later review carries no new regulatory fact. We drop it too. The second reading, that hub growth is lower-margin growth, survives only in part: the case now holds thoracic gross margin at 80.5 to 82 percent rather than the mid-80s, which takes most of its force. What survives is below.

Two readings remain that the case does not hold, and one small one.

First, the reset the case scheduled over four reports is running in weeks, and the evidence cannot yet say which of two things that means. The case's structure is a two-step move: FY2027 consensus falls to a base the company can beat, then the multiple re-rates on the lower base, with a stalling interlude of two to three quarters in between and a 70 percent chance of the first net cut at the January report. One week on, the dashboard shows one-month EPS revisions of minus 14 percent against the minus 5 percent the case cites, a consensus EPS reading of 5.24 against the 5.81 post-report mean the case gives for FY2027 (if the two measure the same year, about a third of the case's expected cut has already landed), and a price that on the case's own 24-month-forward EPS of 5.60 is about 36.5x, against the 39.9x the case rated as "mid-range, not its floor" and within a turn and a half of the case's 10th-percentile band of 35.1x. The favourable reading is that step one is being done for the case early and in the price, so the stalling interlude is shorter than the case allows and the return to the case's central value of 428 at March 2028 is now about plus 110 percent from here rather than plus 92 percent, with the fair trough of 110 to 140 at 31 to 46 percent below instead of 37 to 50. The unfavourable reading, using the same facts, is that the market is cutting earnings and de-rating at the same time, which is the counter-case the case names for its return: a regime that refuses re-ratings, in which the multiple on consensus does not hold at 25.5x while the numbers come down and the return is the earnings leg alone. Nothing in the review distinguishes them, because nothing happened to the company between 22 and 29 October; what moved was the brokers' numbers and the market's reading of the customer. For the favourable reading to be right the price has to hold once the January report delivers the amortisation and tax alignment the case expects; for the unfavourable one, the price keeps tracking each cut through January and April. The case's scenario table has no row for the second; its lowest non-loss row assumes the market keeps paying 25.5x on consensus.

Second, the brokers' first cut fell on the segment the case treats as predictable rather than on the one it worries about. DNB Carnegie cut 2027 and 2028 EPS for lower abdominal sales, not for the customer, not for heart, and not for amortisation. The case's five judgements for FY2027 are the customer, European heart, operating costs, the FDA and the krona; abdominal appears as a gross-margin item to monitor and otherwise as a given, with revenue of 318 in 2027 from 263, a 21 percent rise, resting on kidney growing 30 percent a year from early 2027 and European liver 12 percent. The case's own description of the abdominal franchise is the argument against that: contested by cheaper adequate systems, real price falling 1 to 2 percent a year, VitaSmart with NICE funding, a centre running most of its liver cases on OrganOx on cost. The later review adds that kidney is held below demand until the manufacturing transfer ends, that abdominal gross margin was 61 percent, and that the 510(k) route may still need a confirmatory trial. The strongest form of the reading is that the case's claim of a predictable business, revenue forecastable to plus or minus 6 percent, is weakest in the one segment the sell-side has just marked down, and that the FY2027 revenue cone's negative skew is wider than "the customer and the OPO transition". On the case's own arithmetic a 10 percent shortfall on 2027 abdominal is about 32 of revenue, about 20 of gross profit at the case's 63 percent margin, and about 0.5 of EPS, the same order as its named judgements. For it to be right, kidney growth has to stay below the case's 25 percent test in the first quarter of 2027 and European liver has to lose share on price while the transfer is still being blamed.

Third, and smaller, the finance-lease recognition is a policy, not a one-off, and it changes what the lung line measures. The case treats the SEK 8.5 million as a timing item. The later review has RedEye saying new EVLP placements are moving to finance-lease arrangements, recognising the machine's lease value upfront rather than as monthly service revenue, and that this "changes the long-term revenue profile". With five hubs in the pipeline, 2027 lung revenue will carry upfront machine recognitions that are neither one-off nor recurring; if the Q3 figure for three machines is a guide, roughly SEK 3 million each. That is a fifth of the case's base step-down if all five land in 2027, enough to make DNB Carnegie's "overdone" reading look right in reported 2027 lung revenue while leaving a lower recurring base for 2028, which is the year the case's return rests on. For it to matter, the pipeline hubs have to take machines under leases rather than service contracts, which RedEye says they are, and management has to leave the recognitions unflagged, which it did not do in Q3.

Three obvious readings fail. "The beat was timing" fails because the case carries the SEK 19 million and still holds the quarter as a beat. "Services is structural, not temporary", the later review's own blind-spot argument, fails to press the case because the case models services at zero gross margin for two years, calls it worth about one point of group margin either way, and already has the 2027 targets missed by a wide margin and re-dated; a 200 to 300 basis point structural drag would move its 2029 to 2030 margins, not its 2027 to 2028 earnings. "The 2027 targets are at risk" is the case.

What would settle it

The day-one piece left five questions to this review. None is settled by it, and no later review follows.

  • The customer step-down. This review shows both brokers at 5 to 6 percent of 2027 group sales and the company saying only that the phase-out begins in 2027. It does not carry the contract, the fluid volume per run, the price or the hubs' procedure volumes; the review itself names the contractual terms as the first disclosure the market lacks, and the company has not committed to giving them. What settles it is the fourth-quarter report on 28 January 2027 (the case's date), where a run-down of the SEK 10 million of channel stock would first show and lung excluding timing is tested against the case's 140, and the first-quarter report in late April against 138, with below 130 the case's fast case.
  • The hub margin. This review does not report thoracic gross margin on its own, so the question stands. The fourth-quarter thoracic margin, the split of machine against disposable revenue, and whether the fourth and fifth hubs arrive as lease-recognised placements will answer it; the company has not committed to disclosing the split.
  • The regulatory odds. This review carries no FDA communication. Management targets a US heart launch in late 2027, RedEye calls early 2028 more realistic, and the case dates it later still. The case expects a Day-100 communication in early January 2027, unscheduled and price-sensitive; the liver question is settled by the Q-sub outcome and the 510(k) filing in the first half of 2027. European heart is tested at the fourth quarter against the case's 30 and the first quarter against 32; the review's own bar of SEK 15 million for the first quarter is less than half the case's, and the case's is the test.
  • Services. This review carries no bridge from minus 19 percent; management frames the losses as capacity built for the heart launches. The case's test is a gross margin at zero or better in the first quarter of 2027 with named contract conversions.
  • The drop-through. This review answers it in part: DNB Carnegie's 2027 cut was for abdominal sales, not for the 39 a year of CE-mark amortisation or a 20 percent tax rate, so the 8 to 10 percent alignment the case expects has not been done by the market and is left to the January report (D&A at or above 28 in the quarter, a tax charge near 20 percent) and the March annual report's tax note.

Two questions this piece adds. The abdominal line is settled by kidney growth against the case's 25 percent in the first quarter of 2027 and the abdominal gross margin against 62 then 63, with management's "temporary" expiring at the start of 2027 by its own words. The speed of the reset is settled by whether the price holds through the January report or keeps tracking the revisions; the case's own trigger is a close above the 200-day average, now 244.05, held through that report. Before either, TransMedics reports on 5 November, which the case names as the read-across that has moved this stock's multiple in sympathy.
