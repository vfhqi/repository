Section 2 – OEM International's Operating Cost Structure and Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. OEM International operates with a cost structure dominated by commodities and staff costs that limits near-term operating leverage but supports margin stability.
  2. Recent strategic property investments and the upcoming Jönköping warehouse will increase fixed depreciation and lease expenses over the 2027 to 2028 period.

Cost Base Breakdown

1.
Cost split: OEM International reported that commodities were the largest operating expense line in the first nine months of 2026. According to the company interim report on 15 October 2026, commodities cost SEK 2,896m out of SEK 4,614m in net sales, representing 62.77% of total revenue. This means that commodities represent the primary variable cost within the group structure.
2.
Personnel costs: Personnel expenses at OEM International represent the largest fixed cost component that cannot be easily adjusted within a twelve-month period. The company interim report on 15 October 2026 showed that staff costs increased to SEK 712m, representing 15.43% of net sales compared to 15.24% in the previous year. This indicates that personnel remains a highly rigid expense during sudden industrial demand changes.
3.
Other costs: Other operating expenses and depreciation make up the remainder of the cost base and are largely fixed. The company reported in its interim report on 15 October 2026 that other operating expenses rose to SEK 193m and depreciation reached SEK 126m. This shows that other opex and depreciation are fixed within twelve months and will increase with recent expansion.
4.
Broker analysis: DNB Carnegie initiated coverage on 15 September 2025, noting that OEM International cannot easily extract gross margins because some staff costs are included in gross calculations. The broker assumed a gross margin around 30% based on the fact that purchasing commodities constituted 64% of sales in 2024. Therefore, any shift in product mix toward higher-margin private-label products will enhance gross profit and leverage over time.

Section 3 – Historical Performance, Downturn Cost Flexibility, and Q3 2026 Margin

Section summary
  1. OEM International has historically demonstrated high operational flexibility during major industrial downturns by executing rapid restructuring programs to protect profitability.
  2. The minor dilution in the third quarter of 2026 EBITA margin reflects a temporary increase in central costs and the incorporation of lowermargin acquisitions rather than structural pricing pressure.
  3. Decentralised Swedish technical traders exhibit resilient cost structures characterized by capital-light business models and low capital expenditures.
  4. Peer operating margins expanded significantly during the upswings of 2021 to 2022 and 2026, driven by pricing power and mix shifts.
  5. During the industrial destocking of 2023 to 2024, flexible operating models and cost controls limited the negative impact of operating leverage on EBITA margins.

Dilution

Historical Downturn Agility

1.
Severe pressure: OEM International faced a substantial decline in industrial demand during the dot-com bubble collapse as telecom end-markets weakened. According to a Redeye report published on 20 February 2025, group sales declined by approximately 30% between 2000 and 2004. The company reacted swiftly by closing nine unprofitable businesses and reducing headcount by 250 employees to protect its operating margins. Despite these massive volume drawdowns, OEM managed to remain cash flow positive in every single quarter during this difficult period.
2.
Strategic transition: OEM International underwent a long-term operational transformation following the IT crisis to establish structural cost efficiencies. DNB Carnegie initiated coverage on 15 September 2025, noting that extensive streamlining initiatives from 2003 to 2011 successfully expanded the adjusted EBIT margin from 6% to 10.9%. These structural measures included exiting low-margin businesses like Cyncrona, reducing the employee-to-sales ratio, and expanding into higher-margin niches. This indicates that the company's historical cost flexibility was underpinned by disciplined portfolio rationalisation rather than simple headcount reductions.
3.
Rapid restructuring: OEM International experienced a 20% sales decline during the global financial crisis of 2009 due to a sharp industrial recession. To counter this drop, the company implemented a forceful capacity modification plan that was executed over approximately two quarters. The company reported in its 2009 annual report that it reduced its year-end headcount by 103 employees. These rapid restructuring measures delivered SEK 80m in annualised cost savings and kept the full-year operating margin at a resilient 5%.
4.
Swift recovery: OEM International rebounded strongly in 2010 as cost-saving measures and a cyclical demand upturn combined to boost profitability. The company reported in its 2010 financial release that net sales increased by 15% while profit after tax surged by 97%. The operating margin improved from 6% in 2009 to 9.7% for the full year of 2010. This rapid margin expansion demonstrates the high positive operating leverage of the business model once the fixed cost base is trimmed.
5.
Contrary evidence: OEM International did not achieve immediate margin expansion during the sudden economic shock of 2020 despite implementing temporary cost controls. In its fourth-quarter statement published on 18 February 2021, the company reported that full-year net sales remained flat compared to 2019. Although fullyear EBITA rose by 10% to SEK 422m, the board had to eliminate the annual dividend payout to preserve liquidity. This indicates that the decentralised model can experience near-term cash flow constraints before structural cost savings are fully realised.
6.
Marginal dilution: OEM International managed to preserve solid profitability during the industrial destocking phase that characterised the 2023 to 2025 period. In its 2025 annual report, the company reported that organic sales growth slowed to 3% while higher operating costs pressured margins. The group's EBITA margin declined slightly to 15.0% in fiscal year 2025 from 15.3% in the prior year. This moderate margin contraction compared to broader sector peers highlights the resilience of the company's decentralized product pricing model.
