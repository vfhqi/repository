Section summary
  1. Schneider Electric entered its early 2026 reporting cycle with strong organic growth momentum driven by data center electrification.
  2. Sector dynamics favored integrated electrical providers over traditional peers, positioning Schneider for favorable price-cost recovery in the second half of 2026.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Schneider Electric delivered record full-year 2025 revenues of €40,152 million with 8.9% organic growth, followed by an accelerating first-quarter 2026 performance that reached €9,767 million in revenues with 11.2% organic growth. This sequential acceleration in top-line momentum, driven predominantly by the Energy Management segment, represents a fundamental shift in the investment thesis for the 18-month to 3-year EPS outlook.

Management's ambitious medium-term framework targeting 7% to 10% organic revenue CAGR through 2030 is supported by double-digit CAGR in data center markets, creating a structural foundation for sustained earnings expansion that was not fully appreciated by consensus prior to these disclosures.

The most significant consensus deviation centers on the sustainability and scale of data center and grid infrastructure demand. The group's €25,400 million backlog, up 18% year-over-year, provides unprecedented forward visibility into late 2027, fundamentally de-risking the near-term earnings trajectory. The first-quarter organic growth delivery of 11.2% beat sell-side consensus forecasts of 10.1% by 1.1 percentage points, driven by robust volume demand in data centers.

This beat was achieved despite exceptionally tough prior-year comparisons, indicating that the underlying commercial momentum is structurally higher than historical baselines. However, the Industrial Automation segment missed consensus expectations of 5.5% by delivering only 4.4% organic growth due to timing effects in process automation, revealing that the group's earnings expansion remains heavily dependent on the Energy Management division rather than a balanced portfolio recovery.

The core operational change that reshapes the medium-term investment case is the aggressive pivot toward a software-led electrification powerhouse. Schneider Electric signed a definitive agreement to acquire PTC for approximately €21.1 billion, representing a major step toward building a unified digital thread that bridges physical and digital lifecycles across industrial end-markets. This structural shift fundamentally alters the earnings quality and valuation multiple the group deserves, as software and services carry materially higher margins and customer lifetime value than traditional hardware sales.
