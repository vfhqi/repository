Section 3 – German Transmission Tenders and the Underground Cable Priority Pivot

Section summary
  1. The four German transmission operators are scheduled to tender over 125 billion euros of power grid investments through 2030.
  2. Germany's legislative shift away from underground direct-current cable priority will significantly reduce installation costs and accelerate overhead line construction.

Transmission operator capital plans

1.
Massive acceleration: The four German transmission system operators are scheduled to tender unprecedented grid expansion volumes between 2027 and 2030 to support the clean energy transition. Analyst Martin Comtesse of Jefferies reported on September 10, 2026, that Germany's grid operators have announced capital programs exceeding 125 billion euros through 2030. This massive level of grid tendering will provide highly visible revenue opportunities for European contractors, supporting structural EPS expansion.
2.
Substantial long-term: The long-term outlook for German power networks remains highly attractive as transmission operators prepare for multi-decade network upgrades. Analyst Bartlomiej Kubicki of Bernstein reported on February 16, 2026, that cumulative grid investments in Germany are projected to reach 315 billion euros by 2045. This extraordinary scale ensures that tier-one contractors will enjoy highly visible pipelines that insulate earnings from broader macroeconomic volatility.
3.
Elevated asset: Amprion is leading the investment charge among the German operators with massive expansion goals. Jefferies research highlighted on June 23, 2026, that Amprion's regulated asset base is expected to grow from 17 billion euros in 2025 to 90 billion euros by 2035. This represents a compound annual growth rate of 18%, which should drive significant subcontracting volumes for infrastructure specialists.
4.
Growing capital: TenneT is rapidly deploying capital to address severe grid congestion across its German and Dutch control areas. A credit note from ING Wholesale Banking on March 10, 2025, revealed that TenneT has raised its capital expenditure program to 200 billion euros through 2033. This translates to average spending of 20 billion euros per year, doubling its previous deployment rate and providing a highly lucrative environment for utility contractors.

Legislative shift to overhead lines

5.
Structural policy: Germany is fundamentally reforming its grid planning laws to prioritize lower-cost and faster installation techniques. Bernstein reported on May 1, 2026, that the German cabinet approved draft legislation adding 45 transmission projects to the Federal Requirements Plan Act, known as BBPlG. This regulatory update reverses the decade-long preference for underground cabling in favor of overhead direct-current corridors to streamline approvals.
6.
Decisive legislative: The legislative transition away from underground cabling gained official momentum following Bundestag intervention. Jefferies reported on July 14, 2026, that the German parliament approved the new overhead transmission grid policy. This law removes the default requirement for underground direct-current cabling on critical routes, which is expected to support a much faster buildout of the national transmission network.
7.
Immense financial: The German government's primary motivation for shifting from underground cabling back to overhead lines is cost reduction. Analyst Pal Skirta of Bernstein noted on October 22, 2026, that overhead lines reduce transmission costs by approximately 50% compared with undergrounding. This regulatory pivot is estimated to save several billion euros on newly planned direct-current corridors like the DC42 and DC42plus pipelines.
8.
Accelerated grid: The new legislation will accelerate construction across thousands of kilometers of newly planned German power corridors. Jefferies highlighted on April 25, 2026, that the policy switch will affect roughly 4,100 kilometers of transmission lines that are still in early planning stages. While ongoing projects like SuedLink are legally protected, future tenders will shift almost entirely to overhead solutions, transforming the bidding landscape.

Overhead line contractors and positioning

9.
Profitable overhead: Balfour Beatty is exceptionally well positioned to leverage its vertically integrated capabilities as grid operators prioritize overhead pylon lines. Deutsche Bank reported on October 16, 2026, that Balfour Beatty holds a 25% UK transmission market share supported by its in-house steel tower manufacturing subsidiary, Painter Brothers. This vertical integration will protect group margins and support robust earnings per share growth as overhead networks expand.
10.
Dominant European: VINCI is utilizing its massive industrial footprint to capture highly complex international overhead line and converter platform contracts. Deutsche Bank highlighted on July 22, 2024, that VINCI's Cobra IS division has secured major power transmission contracts, including offshore converter platforms for 50Hertz. This strong execution track record in Germany ensures that VINCI remains a primary beneficiary of the overhead transmission pivot.
11.
Strategic acquisition: HOCHTIEF has actively expanded its technical capabilities to target the accelerating German overhead transmission market. The company announced on September 2, 2026, that its subsidiary HOCHTIEF Infrastructure acquired high-voltage overhead specialist Autmatec. This acquisition directly strengthens the group's capacity to deliver integrated overhead line engineering and tower installation services throughout Germany.
12.
Growing contracting: Dalekovod is achieving exceptional commercial success by securing major overhead line contracts with premier Northern European grid operators. Koncar reported in its third-quarter 2026 interim report that Dalekovod secured transmission contracts with TenneT and Svenska kraftnät valued at over 435 million euros. These awards demonstrate Dalekovod's strong capability to deliver high-quality overhead infrastructure in highly competitive European regulatory environments.

Impact on Friedrich Vorwerk

13.
Severe backlog: Friedrich Vorwerk faces immediate operational headwinds due to its heavy focus on underground electricity cabling works. Jefferies reported on July 14, 2026, that Friedrich Vorwerk holds 60% of its order backlog in electricity projects, with roughly half of that volume directly exposed to underground cables. Because future projects will shift to overhead lines, Vorwerk faces a significant medium-term threat to its pipeline visibility.
14.
Negative revenue: The regulatory shift is forcing Friedrich Vorwerk to reallocate its specialized engineering resources away from its primary electricity cable segment. Jefferies noted on July 14, 2026, that the end of underground cabling priority will cause Vorwerk to lose future cable contract opportunities. To mitigate these losses, management must redirect labor capacity back to its traditional natural gas and district heating divisions, potentially dampening EPS growth.
15.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Friedrich Vorwerk's near-term growth remains highly secure despite the legislative pivot away from underground cables. Jefferies reported on April 25, 2026, that the new law has no impact on ongoing mega-projects such as SuedLink and A-Nord. These multi-year underground projects are already legally committed, ensuring stable utility revenues for Vorwerk through the end of the decade.
16.
Other side of the story: Although the overhead transmission pivot is expected to reduce network buildout costs and accelerate developer timelines, ultimate contractor profitability remains vulnerable to acute engineering labor shortages and severe equipment bottlenecks. Morgan Stanley highlighted on May 17, 2024, that TSO capex targets are often unrealistic due to supply chain constraints, meaning that project execution could face multi-year delays regardless of the technology chosen. Contractors who fail to secure specialized overhead labor could see their margins compressed by rising wage inflation.

Consequently, standard contractor margins might fail to reach the projected double-digit targets, which would significantly curtail the anticipated earnings per share growth for the listed players.

Unanticipated execution: Primary grid contractors remain highly vulnerable to severe delivery bottlenecks and complex execution delays on major utility projects. Morgan Stanley research on September 22, 2026, warns that a sudden dearth of massive, high-profile projects could trigger a de-rating of one full turn of EBIT multiples for traditional civil and infrastructure divisions. This multiple contraction would directly impair the sum-of-the-parts valuations of integrated European contractors unless they maintain strict bidding discipline.
3.
Intense capacity: Severe equipment constraints in high-voltage transformers and specialized cables continue to delay critical grid installations by six to twelve months. A Siemens Energy expert call on September 3, 2026, highlighted that while developer demand remains exceptionally robust, actual contractor execution is capped by extremely tight manufacturing capacity bottlenecks at strategic equipment manufacturers. Vulnerable labor: Contractors operating in the European grid market face escalating downside operational risks due to severe shortages of qualified electrical engineering personnel. Select margin: Royal BAM Groep's long-term margin improvement remains exposed to legacy contract provisions and persistent project execution friction. A Jefferies research note on July 14, 2026, indicates that the Bundestag's approved legislative shift away from underground direct-current cabling will trigger extensive community protests and subsequent permitting delays. Agressive competition: Cable manufacturers who invested heavily in subterranean production lines are likely to bid aggressively for the remaining German underground contracts. Consequently, the group will continue to generate highly stable electricity segment revenues through the late 2020s, regardless of the legislative debate.

Lobbying pressure: European cable manufacturers are actively lobbying the German Bundestag to dilute the new overhead transmission grid policy. Bernstein reported on October 22, 2026, that major manufacturers have commissioned independent cost studies to prove that the lifetime cost gap of undergrounding is much smaller than frequently claimed. If these corporate lobbying efforts successfully delay or dilute the final BBPlG requirement plan amendments, the contractor market will experience a slower shift to overhead towers.

Conglomerate discounts and tax headwinds

10.
Severe fiscal: French infrastructure conglomerates face significant earnings downgrades due to the proposed implementation of the new long-distance transport tax. Morgan Stanley reported on October 23, 2026, that raising the TEITLD tax from 4.6% to 12% would inflict an 8% EPS cut on VINCI's base projections. This substantial domestic fiscal burden will continue to depress group sum-of-the-parts valuations and offset the solid organic growth delivered by their fast-growing energy divisions.
11.
Persistent discount: French contracting groups continue to trade at depressed valuation multiples due to heightened domestic political and regulatory uncertainty ahead of the 2027 presidential campaign. A Bernstein report on September 24, 2026, highlights that the market continues to apply stark political risk discounts of 22% for VINCI and 32% for Eiffage relative to their historic multiples. Constrained recycling: Eiffage's capital allocation remains structurally limited by a lack of large-scale, high- return reinvestment opportunities to replace its maturing motorway concessions.
