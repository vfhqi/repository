[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. AIB Group is a good bank fully priced, not a materially changing one. At EUR 11.38 the shares pay about 7 percent a year over eighteen months and about 9 percent over three years on our modal path, against a 20 percent bar, because the return from here is the cash yield and the multiple is more likely to drift down than up. The case is right about the bank and wrong about the price.

  • The change forces are real, but they are mostly exogenous or redistributive.
    • Three forces act on AIB: the Irish State's exit, completed in June 2025, which freed capital policy and partly freed pay
    • A management with a serial record of guiding low and raising, which is now returning more than 100 percent of earnings through dividends and buybacks
    • And a rate environment that turned from a headwind to a mild tailwind during 2026.
    • None of them changes what the bank earns by enough to matter against a 20 percent bar.
    • Twelve-month Euribor is up about 110 basis points since December 2025, and management's year-end 2026 ECB deposit-rate assumption moved to 2.50 percent from 2.00 percent at the H1 2026 results on 30 July 2026.
    • FY2025 distributions were EUR 2.25bn at a 105 percent payout
      • A EUR 1.0bn buyback has run since March 2026
      • The interim dividend rose 58 percent.
    • The one internal force that could change the earnings trajectory, the Strategy 2030 reset, is deferred to the FY2026 results in March 2027.
      • There is no chief executive change, and the finance seat is empty under a pay cap.
  • The change has already travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and beyond it into the price.
    • It shows in the key metrics, the statements, the sell-side's estimates and the share price, which is why there is little left for a new holder to be paid for.
    • Key metrics: return on tangible equity 23.2 percent annualised, cost-to-income 44 percent and a deposit beta of 20 percent in H1 2026.
    • Statements: CET1 16.1 percent before H1 profits (17.8 percent with them), 170 basis points of organic capital generated in the half.
    • Estimates: FY2026 consensus earnings per share up 12.5 percent in twelve months
      • FY2027 up 11.3 percent.
    • Price: up 53.2 percent in twelve months to EUR 11.38 on 2026-10-06, a 24-month-forward multiple at the 96th percentile of the stock's own five years.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are excellent in level and modest in improvement.
    • Net interest income has plateaued near EUR 3.8bn after peaking at EUR 4,129m in FY2024
    • The margin is being held rather than expanded
    • Capital generation is strong and stable.
    • H1 2026 net interest income was EUR 1,871m against EUR 1,874m a year earlier, flat
      • The net interest margin was 2.68 percent for the half with a Q2 exit of 2.71 percent.
    • Earnings per share grew 8.2 percent in the half, to 42.20c from 39.00c
      • On the reported basis FY2026 is a small decline on FY2025, because the FY2025 base carries a EUR 156m exceptional gain.
    • Sell-side upgrades are likely but small: the latest guidance raise was, in the sell-side's own words, already aligned with consensus and worth about 1 percent to earnings.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of about EUR 0.90 in FY2026 rising to about EUR 1.08 in FY2028, about 9 to 10 percent a year, roughly half of it from the shrinking share count
    • About 6 percent a year after that as the buyback slows and the margin plateaus.
    • The components are the wrong way round for the framework's weighting: the share count contributes about 4.7 points a year, revenue about 3.5, the margin about 1.3, and the items below pre-provision profit about minus 0.7.
    • From the FY2025 reported base of EUR 0.933 the three-year rate to FY2028 is only about 5 percent a year, because FY2026 resets the base lower.
  • What it costs: EUR 11.38, or 10.65x consensus 24-month-forward earnings of EUR 1.0687, against a ten-year median of 9.20x and a five-year median of 7.60x
    • 1.6 to 1.9x tangible book for a return on tangible equity the sell-side's base case has normalising toward 16 percent by 2028.
    • The multiple is at the sector's level, not at a discount from which to re-rate: European banks trade at about 10.6x forward, Bank of Ireland at 9.2x, CaixaBank at 10.6x, Nordea at 10.2x, KBC at 10.2x.
    • The ten-year percentile of 65 is a fair corrective to the five-year 96th
      • The multiple is high, not extreme.
    • The contracted positives are in the price: the buyback, the raised guidance and the 2027 to 2028 hedge roll-on are all in consensus, and the two brokers who published on the results both call the upside largely discounted or fully priced.
  • The return does not clear the bar on the modal path, and the swing factor is a multiple AIB cannot control.
    • At a 24-month-forward multiple drifting from 10.65x to 10.0x, with earnings on our path and consensus dividends, the shares return 7.4 percent a year over eighteen months and 8.8 percent over three years: an F on the framework's bands.
    • If the multiple holds at 10.65x the return is 11.8 percent a year over eighteen months and 10.8 percent over three, a D.
    • The case clears 20 percent a year at eighteen months only if the multiple reaches about 12.0x, the ten-year 75th percentile, which we judge unlikely, about one in five
      • In no branch does it clear at three years.
    • Under a plausibly worse drop-through (cost of risk drifting to 45 basis points, the buyback slowing to about EUR 700m a year) the eighteen-month return falls to about minus 0.2 percent a year
      • The F stays an F and the held-multiple D disappears.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Compounder', loosely.
    • A proven serial upgrader of guidance runs a top-decile franchise whose per-share earnings compound through a buyback funded by surplus capital
    • The one line consensus might have wrong is the rate path inside net interest income, and that line is exogenous, partly hedged and small.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from forces to outputs: the state exit, the guidance record and the rate tailwind have produced the capital, the distributions and the estimate upgrades.
    • It breaks at the price, because the market has already paid for all of it and what remains is a 5 percent dividend yield, a buyback inside the earnings growth, and a multiple with more room to fall than to rise.

  • What would make it compelling: a price near EUR 9.50, where the eighteen-month modal path clears 20 percent a year

    • Or Strategy 2030 targets in March 2027 setting a return on tangible equity above 18 percent, which would argue the normalisation the sell-side models is not coming and the multiple can hold or rise.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is not material enough to deliver a 20 percent a year return from this price: 'Change Forces' are a C (real, mostly redistributive or exogenous), 'Financial Outputs' a C (excellent in level, modest in improvement), and the 'Modal return' an F (about 7 to 9 percent a year)

    • The chain holds from forces to outputs and breaks at the price.
    • The Building Block sits below its two C Elements because it measures materiality, materiality is finally measured in the return, and here the parts bind: the weaker sets the level.
    • Not C, which was live only on the argument that today's multiple holds
    • That would lift the return to the D band and the Building Block to C, and we do not take it as the modal case for a stock at the 96th percentile of its own five-year range whose core revenue has plateaued.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our D is the bull case: AIB is a compounder with a cash-plus-buyback yield near 9 percent, in a sector the market is re-rating
      • At 10.6x it trades at the European bank average while earning a return on tangible equity of 23 percent against a sector near 12 to 15 percent
      • Low-beta deposits are a structural, not cyclical, advantage in a consolidated three-bank market
      • Rates are moving its way
      • So the multiple can hold or rise toward the ten-year 75th percentile of 12x, and 10 percent earnings growth plus a 5 percent dividend plus a re-rating gives 20 percent a year.
    • The evidence for it is real: a deposit beta of 20 percent against a Eurozone average near 42 percent through a full cycle of neobank offers
      • Euribor up about 110 basis points since December 2025
      • 170 basis points of capital generated in a half
      • Sell-side momentum, technical momentum and thematic momentum all pinned A on 2026-10-06.
    • What we concede: the quality, the cash yield, the guidance record and the near-term rate tailwind are all real, and the ten-year percentile of 65 means the five-year 96th percentile overstates how stretched the multiple is.
    • Why we hold our view: earnings growth beyond FY2027 falls to about 6 percent a year as the buyback slows and the margin plateaus
      • The sell-side's own base case has return on tangible equity normalising to 15.9 percent by 2028, and 1.6 to 1.9x tangible book is already about what a 16 percent return at an 11 percent cost of equity and 2 percent growth is worth (about 1.55x on the sell-side's own model inputs, our arithmetic)
      • The brokers who know the stock best are at Hold with targets at the price
      • And the odds on the two conditions the bull case needs (earnings on or above our path, probable; a re-rating to 12x, unlikely) multiply to an unlikely branch.
    • The case is right about the bank and wrong about the price.

External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external forces net to a mild tailwind (rates higher for longer, Irish domestic demand, a consolidated asset side), offset by a forming headwind at the one place that matters most, the cost of deposits
    • And no force meets the 'Demand Inflection' test of a sector-level structural or two-year-visible cyclical demand change.
  • Where the tailwind is: the rate curve has moved above management's planning assumption, and AIB is more rate-sensitive than most European banks, so the same move does more for it.
  • Where the headwind is forming: the dominant neobank has moved from payments into deposits and is moving into credit

  • For the case: the forces explain why the outputs are as good as they are
    • They do not describe an inflection ahead.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the disruption force is strengthening, not being disproved: the dominant neobank has moved from payments into deposits and is moving into credit, and the research names AI-enabled deposit sweeping as an unmonitored failure mode.
  • Revolut: about 80 percent of Irish adults
    • Deposit remuneration launched in May 2026
    • A UK licence
    • Credit products planned.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • AIB Group is a cash-return compounder in a fully-valued, post-recovery Irish domestic bank, and it is not compelling at this price.
  • The change was the ending of the Irish State's ownership in June 2025, a management that has guided low and raised for four consecutive years, and a rate cycle that lifted net interest income from EUR 2.8bn in FY2020 to EUR 4.1bn in FY2024 and has now settled it near EUR 3.8bn.
  • The outputs that followed are a return on tangible equity of 23 to 25 percent, a cost-to-income ratio of 44 percent, 170 basis points of organic capital in a half, and a payout above 100 percent of earnings.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain into the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus earnings per share up 12.5 percent in a year) and beyond it into the price, up 53 percent in a year to a 24-month-forward multiple at the 96th percentile of its five years.
  • What is left is a bank growing earnings per share at about 9 to 10 percent a year to FY2028, half of it from retiring shares, and about 6 percent a year after
    • A dividend yield near 5 percent
    • And a multiple that is more likely to drift down than up.
  • On that path the shares return about 7 percent a year over eighteen months and about 9 percent over three years, against a 20 percent bar
    • Even with the multiple held they return 11 to 12 percent, a D.
  • The case fits 'Compounder' loosely, because the upgrader record is crisp and the quality is good but the one mis-modelled line, the rate path inside net interest income, is exogenous, hedged and small.
  • The foundations are robust (B), the operator is good (B), and the case is simple to follow (B)
    • The problem is not the bank, it is what the market is already paying for it.
  • We would own the bank at about EUR 9.50, or at today's price if the March 2027 Strategy 2030 targets set a return on tangible equity above 18 percent.
