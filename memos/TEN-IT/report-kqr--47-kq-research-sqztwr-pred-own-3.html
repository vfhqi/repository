Section 2 – US OCTG Import Volumes and Trade Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Recent trade barriers have dramatically reduced import volumes into the US.
  2. Imposed tariffs and anti-dumping cases directly bolster domestic pricing power.

Scope of trade research

1.
Comprehensive source analysis: In analyzing US OCTG trade dynamics, this research identified 24 relevant sources, four companies, and two historical precedents, all of which were utilized in this section with none omitted. This comprehensive dataset allows for a rigorous cross-examination of monthly import trends, tariff impacts, and pending anti-dumping investigations. Consequently, this synthesis offers high-confidence projections regarding Tenaris's pricing power through 2028.

Monthly US OCTG import volumes

2.
Plunging import volumes: Monthly US OCTG import volumes have declined significantly since the tariff rate was increased to 50% in June 2025. Latest US Census Bureau data for August 2026 shows an overall decrease of 27% month-on-month in imports to 110,000 metric tons. While seamless imports increased 48% monthon-month to approximately 42,000 tons, welded imports fell 44% to 69,000 tons. This indicates that high tariffs continue to restrict the flow of foreign-made pipe into the domestic market.
3.
Seamless import contraction: The first half of 2026 reflected the full impact of US steel tariffs on foreign supply. During this period, seamless imports declined by over 40% compared with the first half of 2025. Because imports act as swing supply, this contraction has directly tightened the domestic market. This supply tightness will continue to support elevated seamless pricing unless operators sharply curb drilling.
4.
Welded import shifts: Welded imports have historically represented a more commoditized market segment sourced from low-cost producers. South Korea and Taiwan collectively accounted for 68% of welded OCTG imports in 2024, according to Vallourec S.A. filings. However, the combination of 50% tariffs and antidumping cases has forced welded imports to contract rapidly in mid-2026. This supply shock allows domestic manufacturers to regain market share and stabilize prices.

Pending anti-dumping and circumvention cases

5.
New trade petitions: On April 2, 2026, the US OCTG Manufacturers Association and partner organizations filed major anti-dumping and countervailing duty petitions. The petitions targeted OCTG imports from Austria, Taiwan, and the United Arab Emirates, alleging material injury. The International Trade Commission subsequently advanced the cases after finding a reasonable indication of injury on May 17, 2026. These cases are highly likely to suppress import volumes from the named nations through 2027.
6.
Alleged dumping margins: The Department of Commerce initiated investigations based on extremely high alleged dumping margins. Specifically, the alleged dumping margins range from 43.64% to 55.16% for Austria, 73.68% to 75.31% for Taiwan, and 124.15% to 126.08% for the United Arab Emirates. Furthermore, the Department of Commerce assigned an interim countervailing duty rate of 10.17% to Austrian producer voestalpine Tubulars on August 31, 2026. These massive margins will make importing from these countries highly uneconomical.
7.
Strengthened tariff rules: The U.S. government has continually closed loopholes in its tariff enforcement mechanisms to support domestic steel mills. On April 2, 2026, the White House strengthened its trade action by applying the 50% tariff rule on the entire product value rather than just its steel component. This regulatory change effectively eliminated the cost advantage of importing semi-finished green tubes for final finishing in the US. This tighter import rule will reinforce domestic seamless pricing power through 2028.
8.
Circumvention and transshipment: Regulators are aggressively targeting the circumvention of tariffs via third- party transshipments. On August 13, 2026, the United States released a landmark report identifying more than 40 countries at risk of transshipment scams involving Chinese-linked steel. The report highlighted that Chinese products have been rerouted through Southeast Asian hubs, undergoing minor final assembly to evade Section 301 tariffs. Enhanced enforcement of rules-of-origin will further block illegal pipe flows, supporting domestic price levels.

Peer comparisons and tariff exposure

9.
Peer domestic insulation: Vallourec S.A. has positioned its US operations to be highly insulated from direct tariff impacts. Vallourec's domestic US sales are entirely supplied through its vertically integrated manufacturing footprint, from steelmaking to finishing. Consequently, Vallourec is insulated from the 50% tariff and gains maximum leverage from the domestic price recovery. This structural advantage will help Vallourec expand its North American profit margins through 2027.
10.
Tenaris tariff headwind: In contrast to Vallourec, Tenaris S.A. faces short-term cost headwinds due to raw material imports. Tenaris imports steel billets for seamless production at its Bay City, Texas, and Ambridge, Pennsylvania, mills from Argentina and Europe. The June 2025 tariff increase from 25% to 50% expanded Tenaris's cost of goods sold by an estimated $140 million to $150 million per quarter. Although domestic price increases offset this, Tenaris's near-term margins are more sensitive to tariff policies.
11.
Offshore premium imports: Despite severe trade barriers, some high-end seamless pipe imports continue to service offshore projects. Tenaris imports specialized, proprietary premium seamless connections from its European facilities to supply offshore projects in the Gulf of Mexico. Because domestic US mills cannot easily produce these specialized grades, these imports are insulated from volume displacement. This specialized import niche will support Tenaris's high-margin offshore revenues regardless of onshore tariff battles.

Historical precedent of tariff-driven shifts

12.
Canadian import protection: Canadian trade authorities have mirrored the US protectionist stance to shield their own domestic markets. On October 23, 2026, the Canadian International Trade Tribunal continued antidumping duties on OCTG imports from seven countries, including South Korea and Taiwan. This regional trade defense prevents cheap Asian steel from being diverted into Canada and transshipped into the United States. Consequently, North American seamless and welded pipe supplies will remain highly balanced through 2028.
13.
Section 232 precedent: The Section 232 tariffs enacted in March 2018 serve as a key historical precedent for trade-driven import shifts. The imposition of a 25% tariff spurred massive domestic steel price increases and led to a rapid recovery in capacity utilization. Under bilateral quota agreements, imports of OCTG from South Korea, Argentina, and Brazil fell more than 50% below 2017 levels. This historical case demonstrates that aggressive trade barriers successfully redirect market share back to domestic pipe mills.
14.
Contrary evidence: A client note from Jefferies Research on December 12, 2018, cautioned that tariffs alone fail to permanently suppress imports if the price spread remains too wide. During 2018, hot-rolled coil tariffs failed to cut import volumes because extremely high US prices kept the import arbitrage highly lucrative. This means that if domestic prices surge too far ahead of global averages, foreign steelmakers will still find import arbitrage attractive and resume shipping.
15.
Other side of the story: While trade barriers restrict imports, they also trigger severe cost inflation and trade retaliation. During the 2018 tariff cycle, Canada and Mexico quickly retaliated with countermeasure tariffs of 25% on US steel, disrupting regional supply chains. This historical precedent indicates that aggressive US protectionism through 2028 could spark retaliatory trade barriers from key partners, ultimately raising Tenaris's global operating costs.
