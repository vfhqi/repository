[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Interpump is a cyclical recovery in a solid, cash-generative, decentralised industrial, bought at a trough multiple. At EUR 36.0 the shares are cheap on earnings that already contain the recovery, the recovery is real and four quarters old, and the modal return is a C against the 20 percent bar because the change is real in direction but not large in size.

  • The change is external and cyclical, and it has already reached the outputs.
    • The mobile-hydraulics destocking cycle that ran for seven quarters has ended, and the larger division has grown organically for four consecutive quarters.
    • Hydraulics, about two-thirds of sales, grew +2.2 percent organically in Q1 2026 and +6.2 percent in Q2 2026, after seven quarters of decline averaging 12 percent contraction.
    • The Hydraulics EBITDA margin rose 30 basis points to 21.2 percent in Q2 2026 while absorbing acquired perimeters
      • capacity utilisation at Walvoil and White Drive is back to about 95 percent
      • book-to-bill is above 1.0 in both divisions.
    • The turn is not broad: construction, earthmoving and trucks are up, agriculture is still deteriorating, and group organic growth was +0.7 percent in H1 2026 because Water Jetting is lapping the H1 2025 Hammelmann order in China.
  • The change inside the company is continuity, not change.
    • The same founder, the same chief executive, the same bolt-on model and the same margin floor
    • two of the twelve internal forces are strong, which is short of the framework's rule of three.
    • Bolt-on acquisitions at an average 5.4x EBITDA add about 2.5 percent to FY2026 sales
      • buybacks stepped up to EUR 47.8m in H1 2026 from EUR 16.59m in all of 2025
      • a trade-working-capital target of 35 to 36 percent of sales from about 40 percent
      • net financial debt down to EUR 305.7m at 30 June 2026 from EUR 396.9m a year earlier, on its way to zero by 2028.
    • Guidance was held, not raised: FY2026 organic sales -2 to +3 percent and an EBITDA margin of 22.0 to 22.5 percent, with a verbal steer to the upper half of the sales range.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for the near term, but not into the multiple.
    • It shows in the company's actions (pass-through of a EUR 6m tariff, utilisation, buybacks), its key operating metrics (four quarters of Hydraulics growth), its financial statements (Q2 2026 EPS +8.2 percent) and the sell-side's FY2026 estimates (consensus mean EPS +8.2 percent over three months to 2026-09-25).
    • It has not reached the market's multiple, which sits at 13.4x consensus earnings 24 months forward, the 6th percentile of the stock's own five-year range, nor the mid-term estimates in size (FY2027 consensus +8.0 percent over three months, still -2.0 percent over twelve).
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving into the target band for two years, and half of the growth comes from below EBITDA.
    • On our path EPS rises from EUR 1.96 in FY2025 to about EUR 2.20 in FY2026, EUR 2.54 in FY2027 and EUR 2.83 in FY2028, about 13 percent a year compound over three years
    • consensus median is EUR 2.18, EUR 2.46 and EUR 2.68, about 11 percent a year.
    • Revenue growth and margin contribute about 6 points a year of the 13
      • falling net interest, slower depreciation growth and a share count shrinking about 1 percent a year contribute the rest, which investors do not extrapolate.
    • The rate fades toward 7 percent a year beyond FY2028 on our path as the cycle effect passes, because the six-year record is EPS compounding at 2.4 percent a year (EUR 1.70 in FY2019 to EUR 1.96 in FY2025).
  • What it costs: a trough multiple struck on recovered earnings.
    • At EUR 36.0 the shares trade at 13.4x consensus EPS 24 months forward of EUR 2.6835, about a fifth below the stock's five-year median of 15.7x and second-cheapest of the thirteen sector peers with a multiple.
    • The window the multiple is struck on is three-quarters FY2028, so the price is cheap on FY2028 earnings, not on today's
      • what is priced is the recovery delivered at a multiple the stock has traded below for only a few weeks in five years
      • what is not priced is any re-rating, any beat, the working-capital release or a larger acquisition.
    • The price moved against the recovery, not with it: the shares fell 22.1 percent over nine months and 7.7 percent over twelve while Hydraulics turned and estimates rose.
  • The return does not clear the bar on the modal path.
    • At a blended multiple of 14.7x on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the 'Modal return' is about 17.6 percent a year over eighteen months and about 12.8 percent a year over three years, a C at both horizons.
    • The re-rating is the swing factor: on the re-rating branch alone (15.6x, the ten-year 35th-percentile band) the eighteen-month return is 22.3 percent a year, a B
      • with no re-rating (13.4x) it is 10.6 percent a year, a D
      • we put the branch at probable, about 60 percent.
    • The earnings path is the fragile leg: a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (tax at 31 percent, higher amortisation, interest not reaching zero, a slower buyback) takes FY2028 EPS about 7 percent lower and the return to 12.0 percent a year over eighteen months and 10.2 percent over three, a D.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', at a C for crispness.
    • An external, transient cause for concern is clearing on hard evidence in an oversold business of B quality
    • the fit blurs because the pattern's name says compounder and this company's EPS fell 23 percent from the FY2023 peak and is flat over six years.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the external force to the near-term estimates
    • it breaks, if it breaks, at the fourth quarter, where Hydraulics has had material order cancellations in each of the last three years, and the confirming metrics (book-to-bill above 1.0, +6.2 percent organic, EUR 716.8m of inventories) are the same metrics a dealer restock would show.
    • Behind that sits the one risk no indicator leads: the founder is 81 and negotiates every deal personally.

  • What the case therefore is: a park at this price, with medium conviction, because the modal path gives a C and clears the bar only on a branch we put at 60 percent

    • it becomes a progression at a price of about EUR 33 or on FY2026 results showing EPS at or above EUR 2.15 with no fourth-quarter cancellation wave.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and already in the near-term outputs, but it is a cyclical recovery plus ordinary self-help in a business whose EPS has compounded at 2.4 percent a year over six years, and the 'Modal return' at our blended multiple sits in the framework's C band over both horizons

    • not B, which was live, because a B needs the modal return at about 20 percent a year and only the eighteen-month horizon on the re-rating branch reaches it, while the three-year modal return of 12.8 percent a year is a C at best.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the recovery is a dealer restock, not end demand, and it reverses in the fourth quarter as it has three years running.
    • The +6.2 percent Hydraulics print, book-to-bill above 1.0 and EUR 716.8m of inventories are consistent with dealer double-ordering after a destocking phase
    • management acknowledged material Q4 order cancellations and postponements in Hydraulics in each of the last three fiscal years
    • agriculture is still going down.
    • Meanwhile Chinese entrants with comparable-performance plunger pumps erode the 40 percent price premium, and dilutive acquisitions such as Teknoice at a 14 percent EBITDA margin compress Water Jetting's 26 to 28 percent margin.
    • On that hypothesis EPS stalls near EUR 2.1 to EUR 2.2, the multiple stays at its trough, and the return is the 1 percent dividend yield.
  • What we concede.
    • The fourth-quarter pattern is real and untested this cycle
    • net working capital was 44.2 percent of last-twelve-month revenue at Q2 2026, the highest in the eleven quarters we hold
    • and the confirming metrics and the restock metrics are the same metrics until the fourth quarter reports.
  • Why we hold our view.
    • The margin floor held at 22.0 to 22.3 percent through the trough (FY2024 and FY2025, company basis)
    • the Hydraulics margin expanded 30 basis points in Q2 2026 while absorbing acquired perimeters, which a restock rarely does
    • capacity utilisation at Walvoil and White Drive is back to about 95 percent
    • and the +6.2 percent organic print came with a still-deteriorating agricultural market, so it is not an agricultural restock.
    • The recovery is four quarters old, not one, and dealer inventories were already at 1.2 months in March 2026 from 2.8 months, which leaves little room for a second destocking of the 2023 to 2025 kind.
  • Where the case against would win, and what it costs us now.
    • A negative Hydraulics organic quarter, or disclosed fourth-quarter cancellations of the 2023 to 2025 kind.
    • The competing hypothesis is why we put the re-rating at 60 percent rather than higher, and why the eighteen-month return is a C rather than a B.
