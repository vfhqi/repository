Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Sell-side attention prior to the earnings release was heavily concentrated on an anticipated downward revision of Coloplast's long-term financial targets under its new leadership.
  2. Severe outpatient reimbursement headwinds in the United States and persistent consumer weakness in China had rendered historical growth ambitions increasingly unrealistic.
  3. Strong operational performance in the United States
  4. Competing peer Convatec presented a contrasting turnaround story of accelerating growth and narrowing margin gaps that pressured Coloplast's historical valuation premium.
  5. Gavin Wood's transition to chief executive prompted a necessary reset of Coloplast's long-term financial expectations to reflect heightened external pressures.
  6. Severe outpatient reimbursement headwinds at Kerecis and weak consumer sentiment in China forced a substantial rebasing of the group's growth and margin targets.
  7. Ongoing operational investments and regulatory challenges are expected to limit near-term profitability before continuous efficiency programs can offset inflation.

Reimbursement and Regional Drags

6.
Severe outpatient disruption: Morgan Stanley analysts focused on the financial damage caused by regulatory shifts in the United States skin substitute market. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services proposed clinical coverage policies that severely disrupted outpatient commercialization. Outpatient sales for the Kerecis biologics business collapsed while inpatient demand remained relatively resilient. Investors feared that these structural reimbursement changes would continue to drag on group profit margins through 2027.
7.
Diluted acquisition returns: DNB Carnegie analysts noted that the high acquisition cost of Kerecis had severely diluted Coloplast's historically exceptional return on invested capital. A massive DKK 3 billion impairment charge against Kerecis goodwill reflected the harsh realities of the US reimbursement landscape. The biologics business was forced to transition its commercial focus from outpatient clinics to inpatient hospital settings. Providers were expected to adapt slowly to the new pricing regulations, limiting the pace of volume recovery.
8.
Challenged integration path: Rothschild & Co Redburn analysts expressed skepticism regarding the long-term profitability of the recently integrated Kerecis business. Pricing reforms in the US biologics sector had forced Coloplast to reduce its fiscal year growth expectations to 0% from an initial 25% target. The need for continuous commercial reinvestment was expected to prevent Kerecis from achieving its pioneering high operating margin goals. Gaining market share under the new fixed reimbursement system remained highly uncertain.
9.
Dragging emerging markets: DNB Carnegie analysts identified China as a persistent drag on Coloplast's global ostomy expansion. Consumer spending weakness and aggressive local competitors like Cofoe reduced market share in the lucrative community sales channel. Coloplast was forced to implement a new sales channel strategy in late 2026 to optimize inventory levels. The associated inventory reductions created a substantial one-off headwind to organic growth.
10.
Weakened growth engine: Morgan Stanley analysts estimated that Chinese ostomy sales had deteriorated from a historical growth driver into a multi-year drag. Increased competitive intensity in the retail market forced Coloplast to re-evaluate its commercial go-to-market structure. Chinese consumer sentiment remained weak, encouraging patients to trade down to lower-priced local alternatives. Market experts predicted that the China ostomy business would remain flat over the medium term.

Domestic Strengths and Peer Dynamics

11.
Strong domestic momentum: Morgan Stanley analysts highlighted the solid performance of Coloplast's Chronic Care business in the United States as a key fundamental driver. The company expanded its market share in the US ostomy segment to approximately 20% by late 2026. Greater commercial access was achieved through critical Group Purchasing Organization contracts and expanded acute care channel momentum. Direct-to-consumer commercial initiatives successfully translated tangible market access into double-digit expansion.
12.
Accelerating hospital share: DNB Carnegie analysts noted that Coloplast had made significant inroads into the US acute care setting since 2019. Market share gains in hospitals allowed the company to capture newly discharged patients before they transitioned to post-acute care. Platinum partnerships with major medical distributors like Medline supported sustained post-discharge patient retention. The post-acute channel remained a critical battleground where Coloplast sought to disrupt the historical market leadership of Hollister.
13.
Rising regulatory overhang: DNB Carnegie analysts analyzed the potential impact of the US Medicare Competitive Bidding Program scheduled for implementation in 2028. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services sought to consolidate ostomy and urological supply providers into a nationwide framework. Bidding rounds had historically resulted in substantial reimbursement cuts that pressured manufacturer pricing. Coloplast was expected to be better shielded from competitive bidding than some smaller, less-integrated market peers.
14.
Temporary regulatory relief: Morgan Stanley analysts evaluated a bipartisan US bill introduced on October 9, 2026, which proposed a five-year pause on competitive bidding. Enactment of the bill would defer the planned inclusion of ostomy and continence supplies until after 2030. Successful passage of this legislation was expected to remove a significant valuation overhang from the sector. The legislative delay promised to reduce risk to medium-term growth for global chronic care manufacturers.
15.
Narrowing competitive advantage: Morgan Stanley analysts noted that the historical execution and performance gap between Coloplast and Convatec had narrowed considerably. Convatec successfully accelerated its organic sales growth toward a 6% to 8% mid-term target following extensive commercial investments. The rising competitive threat in Continence Care was led by Convatec's expanding catheter portfolio. Investors increasingly questioned whether Coloplast's historical valuation premium remained justified as competitors delivered stronger operational results.
16.
Other side of the story: Coloplast's core market leadership could remain highly insulated from temporary reimbursement and geographical headwinds. The recurring daily usage of ostomy and continence consumables creates an exceptionally resilient annuity business model. Global market shares of 40% to 50% in European markets provide unmatched scale advantages and pricing power. Strong clinical evidence supporting premium products like Luja and SenSura Mio should enable Coloplast to defend its premium category positions over the long term.

  3. Strong cash conversion and robust free cash flow provided a key financial anchor despite the operating profit contraction.

This structural preservation allows the graft to mimic native human tissue, enabling faster wound-bed preparation and healing compared to synthetic products. This clinical superiority supports Coloplast's premium pricing strategy and long-term volume expansion.

The comparative study showed that intact fish-skin prepared wound beds and achieved closure much faster than synthetic polyurethane and reconstituted collagen alternatives. Furthermore, the fish-skin grafts achieved lower graft loss and fewer complications than these synthetic products. Successful clinical outcomes will help Coloplast convince hospital procurement committees to adopt fish-skin over cheaper synthetics.

New India-based competitors, including Tiger and LifeCell, are targeting prices as low as USD 20/cm² for clinically acceptable skin substitutes. These ultra-lowcost alternatives pose a direct threat to high-priced amniotic and mammalian products in the outpatient setting. Hospital-based inpatient settings remain protected from this pricing volatility because biologics are typically covered under diagnosis-related groups.
5.
Channel consolidation: Coloplast's strategic focus on the US inpatient wound care channel leverages a more stable reimbursement framework covered by diagnosis-related group payments. The hospital inpatient setting accounts for the vast majority of advanced wound care biologic usage and remains insulated from outpatient feeschedule cuts. Sourcing fish-skin tissue remains highly scalable, allowing Kerecis to deliver an exceptionally high gross margin of approximately 90%. This profitable structure helps fund the division's expanded commercial investments without diluting wider group margins.

Digital disruption: Coloplast is pioneering smart stoma care through its Heylo digital leakage notification system, which is designed to reduce leakage incidents and improve patient quality of life. A pivotal clinical study showed that the Heylo platform, which uses an electronic sensor layer, delivered a 31% reduction in leakage incidents. Connected stoma innovation aims to shift the ostomy care model toward proactive patient management. Commercial breakthrough remains highly dependent on securing standardized reimbursement codes in key healthcare markets.

Delayed adoption: Healthcare professional awareness and clinical adoption of Coloplast's Heylo digital platform remain limited despite its clinical benefits. Wound, ostomy, and continence nurse practitioners have reported a lack of familiarity with the electronic leakage notification system during patient evaluations. The complexity of securing reimbursement for digital medical devices has slow-rolled the system's global rollout. Coloplast must overcome these commercial execution challenges to monetize its pioneering digital investments over the next three years.

While competitors and new entrants captured market share by introducing diverse stoma bag skin tones, Coloplast was slow to offer similar options. Some industry experts suggest that the company over-indexed on complex electronics while missing simpler consumer preferences like matching colors. To address this, the company launched SenSura Mio Black to regain momentum in competitive retail channels.

Because stoma care sensor technology is already well advanced, transferring these connected platforms to wound dressings represents a natural next step. Coloplast's existing digital capabilities could provide a significant first-mover advantage in digital wound management.

Section 16 – Conclusion

The 3 November 2026 release of Q4 and full-year 2025/26 results did not mainly change what Coloplast earned. It changed what shareholders can reasonably expect it to earn. Management targets annual organic revenue growth of 5% to 7%, against the prior Impact4 target of 7% to 8%. Because brokers had largely anticipated this, it was not the real surprise. Deutsche Bank had predicted a 5% to 7% range, and the market had broadly welcomed a rebasing to establish a credible performance trough.

The single most important change is the margin rebasing. Coloplast replaced its prior ambition of absolute EBIT growth in line with or above revenue growth with a constant currency adjusted EBIT margin target in the mid-20s range. That target implies little to no margin expansion from the prior year. It removes operating leverage from the EPS algorithm.

The funding mechanism confirms this. Funding the Journey targets DKK 500 million of annual resources by 2027/28, but it is primarily a resource reallocation tool rather than a standard net cost-saving program, with around 80% reinvested into innovation and commercial expansion. The new guidance is c.9% below previous sell-side consensus operating profit estimates.

The EPS growth path therefore shifts from margin-led compounding to revenue-led, back-end-loaded growth. Brokers' revisions reflect this. Morgan Stanley cut EPS forecasts by 4.3%, 6.8% and 8% across the next three fiscal years. Deutsche Bank forecasts flat 2026/27 adjusted EPS, slightly below the 2025/26 result.

It expects earnings to stay heavily back-end loaded until the biologics business recovers. Capital allocation offers little offset. The leverage goal was set at below 2.0x, versus approximately 1.5x previously, and the dividend was held flat. Lower capex toward 4% of sales is a free cash flow and deleveraging story, not an immediate EPS driver.

The quality of the Q4 delivery reinforces the point. The adjusted operating margin of 24.80% missed the 26.90% consensus on a 150 basis point inflationary headwind. The EPS beat was entirely driven by a lower actual tax expense, and the reported revenue beat came from favorable currency and acquisitions. Cash generation was a genuine strength, with free cash flow of DKK 6.3 billion, up 16%.
