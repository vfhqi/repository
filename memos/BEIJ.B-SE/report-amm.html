APM Mini-Memo: Beijer Ref

The one-line view. Beijer Ref is the world's largest refrigeration and air-conditioning wholesaler, with law-dated replacement demand and a re-lit acquisition engine compounding earnings per share at about 12% a year; the case depends on the market holding or raising its multiple through the hard summer comparison of 2027.

What the company does

A wholesaler to installers. Beijer Ref sells refrigeration, air-conditioning and heating parts and equipment to professional installers from about 450 to 500 branches in 45 countries. Sales grew from 14.8 billion kronor in 2019 to 37.1 billion in 2025: roughly 60% in Europe, where the company is number one, about a quarter in North America, where it holds 1 to 1.5% of a market Watsco leads, and the rest in Asia-Pacific. Between 80 and 90% of sales are repair and maintenance, and 8 to 10% come from the company's own manufacture of natural-refrigerant systems.

Demand is partly written into law. European refrigerant rules halve the fluorinated-gas quota on 1 January 2027 and ban most new split air-conditioning units by 2029; the United States phases down the old gases by 2036. Old systems cannot run the new gases, so installers must replace equipment, and the Investment Analyst's memo of 9 November 2026 finds that regulation already touches 40 to 45% of sales directly.

A plausible investment thesis

Four forces moved together in five months. Organic growth turned from minus 1.4% to plus 8.3% in the quarter to September 2026; acquisitions adding about 3.7 billion kronor of annual sales, about 10% of revenue, were signed in nine months; Jonas Steen, who has run Europe since 2010, became chief executive on 1 November; and Melker Schörling AB took control from EQT in June, removing both a control overhang and a seller of stock.

The route to earnings per share is mechanical. The memo's model has earnings per share rising about 13% in 2026 to 5.81 kronor, 12% in 2027 to 6.51 kronor, and about 12% a year to 2030, with over half of the growth to 2027 already signed in deals. Brokers count acquisitions only on completion, so a running deal engine is a stream of upgrades, and the Sector Digest of 13 October names exactly this as a way to make money in distributors.

Why the market could pay more. Beijer Ref trades at a 20 to 30% discount to the Swedish serial acquirers because its returns on capital are lower. The new owner's other companies run to return and cash targets, and the memo's arithmetic shows that taking five points of revenue out of working capital would release about 2.3 billion kronor and fund 2 billion kronor of acquired sales without new debt. A numerical return or working-capital target, possibly at the results on 29 January 2027, would start to close the discount.

Drivers to track. Organic growth in the two weather-free quarters to December 2026 and March 2027, where above 3% confirms the turn; the acquisition pace against 2 to 2.5 billion kronor of sales a year; the 29 January report for a new return or cash target; and the North American margin.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader'. The earnings step comes from signed deals and a dated replacement cycle rather than from a turn in sentiment, and consensus is still catching up with the deal flow. The fit itself is middling: the memo of 24 September graded the serial-acquirer setup C, and the Rater's Setups' Fit/Crispness of 9 November is B. The weekly Lists judgement placed Beijer Ref first because five of the Eight Master Ratings read A or B and the chart is read as probably in an early uptrend; Richard's interest level rose to B this week.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Rater's Case Riskiness of 9 November is B, B+ on the fine scale, meaning fairly simple and fairly low-risk. The earnings are the simple part: 2027 earnings per share has eight outcomes in ten within about 6% of 6.51 kronor, and no single operating assumption moves the year by more than 3%.

The main risk is the multiple, not the earnings. About half of the acceleration in the quarter to September 2026 was the warmest Western European summer on record, worth about 450 million kronor of sales, so the July and October 2027 reports will show organic growth near zero against that comparison. No broker carries that print, the model sits 4 to 6% below 2027 consensus, and climate wholesalers de-rate together when bond yields rise. The weekly Lists judgement adds that the Industry relative strength Sell Criterion is already met for industrials, and a distributor priced for a mandated cycle has little cushion if cooling demand softens with construction.

What needs to go right. Organic growth above 3% in the quarters to December 2026 and March 2027 proves the turn is more than weather; acquisitions keep adding 2 to 2.5 billion kronor of sales a year within the leverage ceiling of 2.5 times, which is tight at the seasonal peak in the second quarter and about 2.8 times once 3.4 billion kronor of option liabilities are counted; and the 29 January report reaffirms or sharpens the targets. The memo expects estimate revisions to trace a V through 2027, so timing matters as much as direction.

Business quality

Well-moated, with ordinary returns. Customers are fragmented, prices held while volumes fell in 2023 to 2025, and the moats of branch density, training for flammable refrigerants, private label at 25 to 30% of European climate sales and own green manufacturing widen with each regulatory step. The operating margin held at 10.6 to 10.7% through the organic stall and fell by less than a point when revenue fell 5% in 2020.

The financial profile lags the cohort. The operating margin of about 11% compares with 14 to 23% for the Swedish serial acquirers, return on operating capital of 11.2% sits below the company's own 12% target, working capital is about 40% of sales, and new deals earn only 7 to 9% after tax. Governance trails too: dual-class shares, no central internal audit across more than 170 subsidiaries, and incentives rewarding earnings growth alone. The Rater's Foundations' Robustness and Thesis's Change Forces' Strength of 9 November are both C.

Plausible returns and valuation

Priced for delivery. The shares closed at 164.5 kronor on 27 November, 0.8% below their 52-week high, on 22.4 times current earnings, the 31st percentile of the stock's own history. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 27 November is B; the weekly plausible return of 19 November averages 39.5% a year, grade A, but assumes the multiple rises from 22.4 to 34.2 times.

The Investment Analyst does not believe that multiple. At 24 to 26 times the memo gets a return of 28 to 40% to March 2028; on an unchanged multiple 22%; over three years about 15 to 18% a year, which is C against Richard's bar. The entry multiple where earnings growth alone carries the return is 19 to 20 times, and the shares have risen 5% since the memo. Sell-side Momentum is A (weekly, 25 November) and Technical Momentum B (weekly, 27 November), with Jefferies at 190 kronor and DNB Carnegie at 170.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. The replacement cycle is dated by regulation rather than by construction, and European air-conditioning penetration of 23% against 93% in the United States gives a long runway. Cheap Chinese propane units hurt European manufacturers far more than the channel and give Beijer Ref private-label supply. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum of 27 November is B.

Cons. Relative strength for industrials and capital goods read 26 on 27 November, below the Sell Criterion line of 30 for a second week, so the stock must be bought against the tape. Rising yields press on every compounder's multiple, 98% of sales are outside the krona and unhedged, and the North American platform, bought at 12 times earnings against 6 to 8 for European deals, carries the growth story with about 1% market share.

In short

  • Interesting because four forces aligned in five months: an organic turn, re-lit deals, an internal chief executive and a long-term owner, on top of law-dated demand
  • Predictable earnings: about 12% a year to 2030, with over half of the growth to 2027 already signed
  • The risk sits in the multiple: the hot summer of 2026 makes the mid-2027 comparisons hard and yields press on the cohort
  • The unpriced upside is capital efficiency, which the new owner's habits favour and a return target would signal
  • Watch organic growth in the two winter quarters and the 29 January 2027 report for a return or cash target
