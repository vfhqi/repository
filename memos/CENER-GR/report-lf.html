EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Cenergy Holdings operates under operator-track CEO Alexis Alexiou, who brings 30 years of continuous parent-group tenure representing deep internal succession stability, though this alignment means he operates as an ultimate parent group insider, raising concerns that the internal career path and Greek conglomerate alignment may prevent the adoption of outside best practices and independent capital allocation.

The CEO has delivered exceptional operational scale and margin expansion under his leadership, with revenue growing from EUR 1.05 billion in FY2021 to EUR 2.06 billion in FY2025, top-line growth of 15% in FY2025 translating into a 28% increase in adjusted EBITDA reaching EUR 348 million, and adjusted EBITDA margin hitting a record 19.70% in Q1 2026, up 415 basis points year-on-year, while FY2025 net profit reached EUR 194 million, representing 39% year-on-year growth.

Shareholder returns have been phenomenal, with share price appreciation reaching 121.50% over a 15-month horizon from December 2024 to March 2026, though Cenergy trades at 5.30x FY2026 EV/EBITDA, representing a greater than 25% discount to peers who trade at an average multiple of 6.90x. Capital allocation demonstrates prudent leverage management with net debt reduced to EUR 204 million by end-2025, representing a comfortable 0.60x leverage ratio, though the limited buyback activity was structured for remuneration in treasury shares rather than opportunistic capital return, while adjusted Return on Capital Employed exceeded 29% during the first half of 2025.

Strategic execution shows acts-not-talks delivery, with the order backlog expanding from EUR 1 billion in 2021 to EUR 3.40 billion in 2025, strong execution leading management to raise the FY2025 adjusted EBITDA guidance to EUR 335–350 million which was fully achieved at EUR 348 million, and successful delivery on the value over volume strategy pushing margins higher.

CEO Alexiou possesses a highly consistent 24-year operational cables track record before Cenergy, managing Hellenic Cables P&L from 2009 to 2020 and scaling Hellenic Cables into a global high-voltage submarine manufacturer during his tenure. Leadership style exhibits high strategic ambition and a direct, execution-focused communication style backed by a conservative guidance posture, using plain-spoken language during analyst calls, pursuing a value-over-volume strategy to expand high-margin offshore cable market share, approving a major EUR 200 million capital increase quickly to fund US expansion, and demanding superior execution delivering 29% adjusted ROCE in H1 2025.

Sell-side analysts view management with increasing conviction due to reliable target upgrades, though coverage remains highly concentrated among regional specialists, with Axia Ventures Group praising management's strong operational execution following consecutive robust quarterly earnings.

The CFO and senior leadership team maintain stable executive tenure led by risk-focused CFO Dr Alexandros Benos since May 2018, though Cenergy maintains a highly cost-effective pay structure with CEO total compensation of €723,356 in FY2025 and total C-suite compensation of €1,128,164 substantially below European peer averages, while no clawback provisions exist to reclaim paid short-term variable bonuses, and the internal CEO-to-employee pay ratio expanded from 5.80x in 2023 to 8.28x in 2025. Executive succession planning faces high key-person dependency as performance and succession planning are managed on an informal basis by the Board, with the prospectus flagging that losing key senior management members could materially damage operational results.

Board composition reveals a highly insider-dominated board controlled by parent Viohalco which owns a commanding 71.46% stake, with only 4 of Cenergy's 10 board members classified as independent representing just 40% of the total board, materially below the European peer average, as peer Prysmian boasts a much stronger 75% independence level and Nexans maintains an even higher 80% board independence rate. Governance structure shows thin board alignment as non-executive directors do not receive shares as part of their remuneration, representing an explicit deviation from Corporate Governance Code Principle 7.6, meaning directors have minimal skin-in-the-game. Control structures demonstrate absolute parent majority control with all 212,384,903 outstanding shares belonging to a single class and Viohalco maintaining a commanding 71.46% voting control, while sales of goods to related parties reached EUR 229.10 million in FY2025, purchases of services from Viohalco rose to EUR 316 thousand, and Cenergy purchased EUR 46.34 million of services from other related Viohalco subsidiaries.

Litigation profile remains significantly cleaner than European peers, with Cenergy having no pending material antitrust, cartel, or class action lawsuits threatening near-term earnings, while peer Nexans faces ongoing UK collective damages actions and was held jointly liable for a EUR 70.60 million fine in the high-voltage sector, and Prysmian holds EUR 209 million in provisions for antitrust investigations and third-party legal claims. Culture indicators show improving workforce retention though external Glassdoor ratings and CEO approval trends are thin, functional LinkedIn turnover metrics for engineering, sales, and manufacturing are not publicly disclosed, while Cenergy achieved a lower consolidated employee turnover of 14.40% in FY2025 compared to 16.50% in FY2024, with cables segment turnover decreasing to 14.60% in FY2025 from 17.10% in FY2024.

Management commentary reveals high operational confidence backed by successive guidance upgrades, with CEO Alexiou maintaining a highly optimistic tone during Q1 2026 celebrating record operational profitability of EUR 100 million, though management warned that the record 19.70% margin is temporary and will normalize toward 17% by year-end, defensively putting stable in quotes regarding their EUR 3.30 billion backlog, and CFO Benos expressing concern regarding unfair Asian competition. Insider transactions exhibit extremely concentrated parent-group control with minimal open-market transactions, as parent company Viohalco maintains dominant strategic control with a 69.71% voting stake, CEO Alexios Alexiou received 47,613 shares allocated free of charge through an OTC transfer in June 2025, no open-market buys or sales were executed by executive officers over the last 12 months, non-executive directors hold zero shares as of December 31, 2025, representing an explicit deviation from Belgian Corporate Governance Code Principle 7.6.

Third-party commentary validates strong customer execution reliability, with TSO Gasunie selecting Corinth Pipeworks for its next-generation onshore energy transition pipeline framework covering deliveries with an estimated potential total value of EUR 2.50 billion, Alliander signing an eight-year power cable framework agreement with Hellenic Cables, DEME General Manager Philip Scheers publicly praising their consistent and effective collaboration across offshore projects, and Ocean Winds director Pete Geddes citing Cenergy's proven expertise as key for reliable and timely project delivery. Competitor monitoring reveals capacity expansion surveillance by European tier-one cable manufacturers tracking Cenergy's capacity expansions as a potential threat to industry pricing, with DNB Carnegie noting that some investors fear Cenergy's Thiva and Baltimore expansions could tilt the undersupplied market balance, while tier-one incumbents maintain a competitive moat through large installation fleets as Cenergy lacks proprietary cable-laying vessels.

Key-person risk remains high due to unusually concentrated strategic execution authority over both business segments held by CEO Alexis Alexiou and CFO Alexandros Benos, with performance and succession planning managed on an informal basis by the Board, the prospectus flagging that losing key senior management members could materially damage operational results, and recruiting qualified replacements remaining difficult due to the highly restricted regional pool of specialized technical expertise. The overall management quality verdict concludes that superb operational execution and robust backlog delivery are heavily compromised by severe Viohalco group control and a lack of integrated subsea installation fleets, with confidence in management quality and operator-thesis execution capability assessed at medium plausibility given the combination of exceptional operational metrics offset by governance vulnerabilities and structural competitive disadvantages versus tier-one peers.
