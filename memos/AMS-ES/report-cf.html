Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Amadeus exhibits several statistical anomalies that distinguish its performance profile from both direct peers and broader sector base rates, warranting skeptical examination of sustainability and structural drivers. The most striking outlier remains the scale disparity where Amadeus's annual research and development expenditure exceeds the entire market capitalization of its primary GDS competitor Sabre, creating an unprecedented competitive moat that supports Amadeus capturing over 50% of global GDS bookings versus Sabre's structurally declining share.

This resource asymmetry, detailed in Section 7 – Peer-Relative Earnings Momentum, creates a winner-takes-most dynamic rarely observed in mature B2B software oligopolies and raises questions about whether such dominance invites regulatory scrutiny or alternatively represents a permanent structural advantage that justifies valuation premiums. A second material anomaly emerges in profitability dispersion where Amadeus achieved an adjusted EBITDA margin of 39.3% in Q2 2026 compared to Sabre's margin of approximately 20%, representing a nearly 20 percentage point margin gap that persists despite both firms operating in identical GDS distribution economics.

This profitability chasm, examined in Section 7 – Peer-Relative Earnings Momentum, cannot be explained solely by scale or cloud efficiency and suggests either superior pricing power from next-generation retailing platforms or alternatively reflects unsustainable cost deferrals that will normalize as Amadeus invests in non-air verticals. Balance sheet leverage presents a third outlier where Amadeus maintains robust net leverage at 1.0x EBITDA whereas Sabre remains highly levered at over 5.0x EBITDA, creating divergent strategic optionality that fundamentally alters competitive dynamics as referenced in Section 4 – Financial Profile Change and Section 7 – Peer- Relative Earnings Momentum.

This capital structure bifurcation enables Amadeus to pursue opportunistic acquisitions like the EUR 1,200 million Idemia transaction while simultaneously returning capital through dividends and buybacks, whereas Sabre's constrained liquidity forces defensive positioning that risks market share erosion in next-generation airline retailing systems. Geographic booking divergence represents a fourth unexplained oddity where European GDS bookings are down nearly 30% versus 2019 levels while Asia is up 25%, creating a 55 percentage point regional variance that far exceeds normal cyclical patterns and suggests either permanent structural changes in European corporate travel behavior or alternatively reflects temporary airspace disruptions that will normalize.

This geographic disparity, detailed in Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces, raises fundamental questions about whether Amadeus's home-market exposure creates concentrated risk or whether Asian expansion offsets European structural decline.
