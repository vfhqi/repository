The operating drivers behind the inputs

  1. Order intake to revenue. Group order intake fell 18% in Q1 2026, was flat in Q2 and rose about 15% in Q3, taking the year-to-date figure from −12% at June to −1% at late September; the order book was 17.3 weeks at June 2026 (15.7 at December 2025, 17.4 a year earlier). Revenue lags intake by about four months, so FY2026 revenue (−14%) is well below FY2026 intake (roughly flat), and the difference converts in Q4 2026 and H1 2027. I assume H2 2026 revenue of £69.5m (−8% against an H2 2025 that had no coring) with a heavy Q4, then H1 2027 of £62.9m (+13%) including about £4m to £6m of China shipments deferred from 2026, and H2 2027 of £72.4m (+4%) against the Q4-heavy comparison.
  2. Organic growth after the rebound. Excluding coring and acquisitions: −11% (FY2026), +6.5%, +5.5%, +5.0%, +4.5%. Price is about 2.5 points of each year (cost recovery, not real pricing); volume is about 4 points in FY2027, 3 in FY2028 and 2 to 2.5 thereafter. The United States (about 25% of revenue, half academic) is assumed flat through another stopgap funding year; China grows flat to low single digit after the 2026 catch-up; Europe recovers slowly from −20% orders; commercial and industrial (about one-third of revenue) grows mid to high single digit; offshore-wind geotechnical work stays flat to down; about 1 point a year is lost to share erosion at the weaker businesses and in China.
  3. Operating leverage. The fixed cost block (staff £48.5m, other external charges £19.0m, depreciation £4.7m in FY2025) was about £67m in FY2025 and ran at about £63m annualised in H1 2026 after £1.8m to £2.0m of cost actions and the disappearance of volume-linked pay, commissions and travel. How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
