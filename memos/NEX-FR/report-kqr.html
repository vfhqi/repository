Section summary

Transmission Margin Conversion and Peer Benchmarks

1.
Accelerating margin execution: Nexans is experiencing clear margin improvement in PWR-Transmission as its lowest-priced legacy contracts gradually wind down. In the first half of 2026, the company achieved a segment margin of 13.7% despite flat organic revenue growth. This execution illustrates the underlying quality of its backlog, which means that net earnings per share should benefit directly as higher-priced contracts are recognized. However, sustaining this trajectory requires consistent operational execution on remaining subsea projects.
2.
Enhanced vessel capabilities: Nexans expects its newly launched cable-laying vessel Electra to drive significant margin progression from 2026 onwards by reducing reliance on expensive thirdparty charters. The €380 million vessel carries 30% more cable weight than its predecessor Aurora, meaning that empty transport travel is minimized during complex installations. Because Electra is fully loaded on capacity until the end of 2028, Nexans has secured high-visibility cost synergies that will support segment profitability. Consequently, these vessel economics will help bridge the historical profitability gap with larger competitors.
3.
Pronounced margin discount: Nexans' current transmission margins of 13.7% trail its primary high-voltage competitors. For comparison, competitor Prysmian targets a high-teens transmission margin corridor of 18% to 20% under its mid-term targets. Meanwhile, peer NKT expects its Solutions EBITDA margin to reach the mid-20s by 2030. This gap shows that while Nexans' high-teens transmission margin target of over 17% by 2028 is achievable, the company remains the structural laggard among the big three.

Halden Plant Utilization and MI Contract Outlook

6.
Impending capacity risk: Nexans has yet to secure a replacement mass-impregnated order for its Halden plant's specialized lines beyond mid-2028. While the mass-impregnated manufacturing capacity is fully booked through 2027 by an alternative contract, the post-2028 pipeline remains uncontracted. If the company fails to secure new orders soon, Halden could experience severe under-utilization that would drag down fixed cost coverage. Consequently, this lack of medium-term visibility will continue to weigh on investor sentiment and depress the stock's valuation.
7.
Persistent technology niche: Mass-impregnated cable technology remains vital because solid XLPE alternatives are not yet certified for deepwater installations exceeding 1,500 meters. Nexans and Prysmian are the only two global suppliers capable of executing these extremely deep subsea projects. To load its Halden lines, Nexans is actively pursuing alternative Mediterranean projects involving Italy, Morocco, and Egypt. This specialized capability ensures that mass-impregnated lines remain highly relevant for deep-sea links, which means Nexans can protect its niche pricing power.
8.
Skeptical expert assessment: An expert Call with a former Prysmian EPCI Project Director suggested that Nexans faces material under-utilization at its Halden plant. The director stated that subsea tenders have cooled down in Europe and that Nexans has not successfully replaced the massive volume originally allocated to the Great Sea Interconnector. This suggests that Nexans' bargaining power with grid operators is weaker than management's positive commentary indicates.
9.
Contrary evidence: Nexans has lost 3% to 4% market share in highvoltage subsea cables over the past few years due to aggressive competition from NKT. A former Prysmian Director of Strategy noted on 26 August 2026 that NKT's market gains and Nexans' concentrated backlog create structural revenue risks. This market share erosion suggests that Nexans' transmission margin expansion could be offset by volume losses, particularly in competitive Northern European markets.

High-Voltage Supply-Demand Parity and New Entrants

10.
Favorable capacity balance: The high-voltage subsea cable industry is expected to remain structurally undersupplied through 2030 because grid-scale investments continue to outpace manufacturers' capacity expansions. Carnegie argued on 16 October 2025 that European transmission operators' massive multi-year capital plans will prevent any market disruption. This persistent undersupply shifts negotiating power directly to established suppliers, which means that high-voltage pricing will remain robust for several years. Consequently, Nexans' backlog execution remains highly insulated from near-term price erosion.
11.
Rising Asian competition: Newly qualified Asian entrants are rapidly expanding their footprint in Europe by building local high- voltage manufacturing plants. For example, Sumitomo Electric is constructing a £350 million facility at the Port of Nigg, while LS Cable & System is investing €1,090 million in its Port of Tyne project. These entrants will increase regional capacity, but grid operators heavily prioritize established track records. This trackrecord barrier means that these new entrants will face a very gradual market penetration curve before threatening Nexans' position.
12.
Moderate capacity ramp-up: Historical capacity additions demonstrate that cable makers often face initial operational friction when scaling up new manufacturing facilities. In 2023, Nexans experienced significant ramp-up issues at its Charleston plant alongside delays in the EuroAsia contract, which left its expanded Halden facility temporarily under-utilized. This past case suggests that newly built transmission capacity requires several quarters of operational fine-tuning before generating high margins. Consequently, nearterm capacity additions from competitors are unlikely to flood the market with cheap supply.

Great Sea Interconnector Restart Potential

13.
Rebounding project momentum: The Great Sea Interconnector project has gained fresh momentum after the French infrastructure fund Meridiam agreed to acquire a 66% stake in the holding company. This private investment helps de-risk the €1.43 billion subsea project, which is designed to connect the electricity grids of Greece and Cyprus. Greek authorities officially announced in October 2026 that marine surveys along the proposed route will resume shortly. This survey restart is critical to driving long-term confidence in the project's eventual execution.
14.
De-risked baseline projections: Nexans has completely removed the Great Sea Interconnector's near-term execution from its financial guidance, which means that any project restart represents pure upside. Chief Executive Officer Julien Hueber stated in October 2026 that cable production for GSI is targeted to restart in 2028, with no impact on the company's 2028 financial targets. This conservative scheduling protects Nexans' nearterm earnings because alternative projects fully book the Halden plant's mass-impregnated capacity until mid-2028.
15.
Other side of the story: Geopolitical tensions with Turkey and ongoing regulatory disputes could completely derail the Great Sea Interconnector, leaving Nexans' mass-impregnated lines vacant after mid-2028. Although management claims alternative projects can easily fill the capacity, expert insights suggest that replacing a project of this magnitude is highly challenging. Consequently, if the interconnector is canceled and replacement orders fail to materialize, Nexans' subsea margins will face severe downward pressure that would prevent the company from achieving its high-teens transmission target by 2028.

Financial drag: Nexans' bottom-line earnings are increasingly burdened by a substantial step-up in net financing expenses. The group's current and deferred tax charge for the period is calculated by applying the estimated average annual tax rate to the first-half pre-tax income. This geographic shift reduces the proportion of highmargin income from lower-tax jurisdictions, while project taxes in Norway remain highly nonlinear due to project tax cash-outs upon completion, which means cash tax payments will spike as legacy projects wind down. This tax volatility will continue to compress continuing-operations net income even as operational EBITDA grows.

Divergent EPS: Sell-side analysts adjust Nexans' earnings per share using different criteria, creating confusion in the current market narrative. This projection represents a major step-up from previous years, but differs from other analyst metrics that include discontinued operations. Consequently, this adjustment discrepancy means that the market narrative struggles to align on a normalized net income run-rate, which depresses the stock's valuation.
