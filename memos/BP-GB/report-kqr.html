Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The historical relationship between equity valuations and earnings declines during commodity cycle downturns provides critical guidance for oil major investments.

Core Research Question and Context

1.
Critical inquiry: "BP p.l.c. (BP-GB), the London-listed integrated energy major; in 2026 it reports Gas & Low Carbon Energy, Oil Production & Operations and Customers & Products, and from 1 January 2027 it reports two segments, Upstream (oil and gas production including bpx energy, LNG, and gas and power trading) and Downstream (refining, oil and products trading, fuels marketing and convenience retail, aviation fuels, biofuels and the retained 35% of Castrol). How have the share prices and forward price-to-earnings multiples of BP p.l.c. and of Shell, TotalEnergies, Eni, Equinor, Repsol, ExxonMobil and Chevron behaved in the six to eighteen months after past peaks in oil prices and refining margins, in 2008-09, 2011-14, 2014-16 and 2022-23: did forward multiples compress or expand as consensus earnings fell, how far did the shares fall from peak to trough and over what period, how did companies that were raising buybacks at the peak fare against those that were deleveraging, and how did BP's multiple relative to its peers behave through each unwind? What do those precedents indicate for the position of BP's shares in the current cycle, with Brent at about $97 in Q3 2026 and BP's refining indicator margin at $46.70/bbl in that quarter, BP's 24-month-forward price-to-earnings multiple near the top of its five-year range in October 2026, and most European integrated majors trading near the top of their own ten-year ranges of price-to-earnings multiples? Under this model, gas and power trading will be integrated into the Upstream segment, while oil and products trading will reside in Downstream. This strategic realignment aims to maximize value from molecules through trading while providing investors with clearer segment dynamics during a volatile period for earnings per share.
3.
Rapidly improving leverage: Management at BP has prioritized structural cost reductions and rapid deleveraging ahead of its original targets, expecting to reach its net debt goals well before the end of 2027. Morgan Stanley reported that the company's valuation at over a 15% average free cash flow yield remains highly attractive during this commodity upcycle. However, as Brent prices decline towards long-term levels, above-mid-cycle earnings will fade and pressure valuation multiples. Investors must therefore evaluate whether forward multiples will expand or compress as consensus earnings drop over the next 18 months.
4.

The brokerage upgraded its price target for BP by 6% to £5.50 due to this rapid balance sheet improvement. Nonetheless, a substantial part of this cash flow was driven by a historic European refining indicator margin of $46.70 per barrel that is expected to contract. This impending margin normalization represents a key valuation threat that makes historical cycle precedents highly relevant for assessing long-term earnings per share trajectories.

Sustaining capital discipline: Rothschild & Co Redburn raised its target price for BP to 650p, noting that the company's pivot back to oil and gas represents a positive strategic direction. However, Redburn cautions that BP's balance sheet remains weaker than peers and is highly dependent on elevated commodity prices. This structural lever underscores the importance of examining how companies raising buybacks at cycle peaks fare against those focusing on deleveraging.
6.

Section 2 – Historical Precedents: Multiples and Share Price Behavior

Section summary
  1. Historical energy cycle unwinds demonstrate that forward multiples typically expand as consensus earnings contract faster than equity prices.
  2. European majors consistently experience deeper valuation discounts relative to US peers during commodity price corrections.

Valuation Multiple Trends in Downturns

1.
Stretched forward multiples: European integrated majors experienced notable multiple expansion during the commodity price softening of early 2023. As consensus earnings estimates fell, the average 2-year forward priceto-earnings multiple of European majors actually expanded to over 6.20x by late 2022. This valuation countermovement occurred because share prices fell slower than the rapidly collapsing earnings. Investors were therefore pricing in normalized long-term cash flows rather than immediate peak profits.
2.
Sharply declining earnings: Deutsche Bank reported that commodity price volatility in early 2023 forced extensive cuts to 2023 and 2024 earnings forecasts. Despite cutting Equinor's 2023 earnings estimate by 38%, the broker upgraded the stock to buy due to its massive prior price correction. This indicates that forward multiples often expand during earnings contraction cycles as prices stabilize. Ultimately, aggregate valuation metrics across the sector remained highly defensive even at lower price levels.
3.
Severely stretched valuations: Norne Securities highlighted that during the commodity market peak in 2014, Statoil traded at a highly stretched 12-month forward price-to-earnings multiple. The company's valuation reached 11.40x forward earnings, which was significantly above its five-year average of 8.90x. This peak valuation preceded a major earnings downgrade cycle as Brent prices subsequently fell.
4.
Rapidly shifting multiples: Morgan Stanley observed that during the 2011 to 2014 cycle, energy sector multiples expanded relative to the broader market. The blended refining forward price-to-earnings multiples moved up by approximately 2x over a three-year period. This multiple expansion was heavily driven by structural earnings mix changes into higher-valued downstream and retail segments.
5.
Gradually rising benchmarks: DNB Carnegie noted that the long-term 15-year average price-to-earnings multiple for European majors sits at approximately 10x. In late 2026, the overall sector average 2-year forward priceto-earnings multiple has recovered to 10.30x from its historical lows. This recovery represents a return to mid-cycle valuation norms after years of deep discounting.

Conversely, US peer ExxonMobil showed extreme resilience with a decline of only 3.80% over the same period.

BP's shares fell by 12.60% over a two-week period as commodity prices softened. This rapid correction demonstrates how volatile capital flows quickly exit European majors during commodity unwinds.
8.
Substantial earnings cuts: Deutsche Bank reported that global oil market volatility led to severe underperformance for high-beta equities in early 2023. Equinor's stock underperformed the broader European oil sector by 17% in US dollar terms. This underperformance highlighted how rapidly equity markets discount downstream and upstream earnings potential during pricing unwinds.
9.
Resilient global operators: Morgan Stanley noted that international integrated majors experienced varying levels of share price erosion during previous cycle unwinds. During the 2011 to 2014 cycle, companies with high Brent crude leverage faced severe valuation headwinds as capital expenditure peaked. This capital intensity ultimately depressed free cash flow yields and compressed forward multiples.

This structural gap indicates that capital markets undervalue European majors relative to their North American peers.

The peer group's current elevated multiples in 2026 face significant compression risk if commodity prices normalize. Section 3 – Capital Allocation: Buybacks versus Deleveraging at Cycle Peaks

Section summary
  1. Pristine balance sheets at cycle peaks consistently outperform aggressive share buybacks by preserving strategic flexibility for subsequent downturns.
  2. Integrated majors that overcommit to peakcycle share buybacks face severe balance sheet strain and forced program suspensions when commodity prices contract.
  3. Sustained debt reduction during upcycles enables resilient capital returns and counter-cyclical investment opportunities when energy markets normalize.

Capital Allocation Trends in the 2011 Peak

1.
Rapidly expanding capital outlays characterized the peak of the 2011 commodity cycle as companies scrambled to capture high-margin production. Shell increased its capital expenditure guidance for the 2011 to 2014 period to a range of $25 bn to $27 bn. This heavy investment program directed approximately 75% to 80% of total capital specifically toward upstream exploration and production. These massive investments were intended to drive long-term volume growth but ultimately left the company with less flexibility when oil prices subsequently collapsed.
