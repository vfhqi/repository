[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. MOL Hungarian Oil & Gas is a cyclical peak dressed as a change case. A conflict-driven spike in European refining and petrochemical margins made the Q2 2026 earnings, travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain into the sell-side's numbers and into a share price up 91.9 percent in twelve months, and is already reversing. At HUF 5,000 the shares pay 9.2 times 24-month-forward consensus earnings, the 98th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range, for an earnings path that falls, so the 'Modal return' is negative at both horizons.

  • The strong change force is external, and over the horizon it points the wrong way for the case.
    • The Brent-based complex refining margin was USD 20.8 per barrel in Q2 2026 against USD 6.0 in Q2 2025 and a group refinery margin of USD 4.6 (FY2024) and USD 6.2 (FY2025) per barrel
    • The petrochemical variable margin was EUR 548 per tonne in Q2 2026 and EUR 119 per tonne by July 2026.
    • Management itself expects refining margins to contract over the next eighteen months
      • The sell-side attributes the Q2 beat to temporary supply bottlenecks in European middle distillates.
    • The other external forces are headwinds that are intensifying: Hungary extended its 95 percent tax on the Brent-Urals differential through end-2026 and added a 50 percent tax on crude differentials from August 2026
      • Price or margin caps are active in ten of twelve markets
      • The Druzhba pipeline was shut for nearly three months in 2026 and the only alternative route charges three to four times fair transit fees.
  • The internal change is real, modest and slow, and it is not what moved the earnings.
    • A holding structure from 1-Apr-26, an internal CFO succession, a USD 720 million stake in Cyprus's Aphrodite gas field with first gas in 2031, and a 9.1 percent dividend rise are substantive, but there is no chief-executive change, guidance was not raised, and the screen's rule of three strong forces is not met.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have peaked rather than turned up.
    • Q2 2026 Clean CCS EBITDA of USD 1,297 million rose 89 percent year-on-year and reported basic EPS was HUF 316.85 against HUF 59.76
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS rose 54 percent in three months and the buy share went to 55 percent.
    • Consensus itself has EPS falling 15.3 percent in FY2027 and 21.5 percent in FY2028 on the brokers' basis, and the one-month revision to FY2027 has gone flat at minus 0.2 percent.
    • The change has travelled every layer of the chain, from an external force through the operating metrics and the financial statements to the sell-side's estimates and the price
      • What the chain carries is a windfall, not a change the company made.
  • What we think it earns: EPS on the brokers' basis of about HUF 700 in FY2026, HUF 520 in FY2027, HUF 455 in FY2028 and HUF 465 in FY2029, a decline of about 12.7 percent a year over three years
    • Our FY2027 figure sits 17 percent below the consensus median of HUF 625.20.
    • Our path assumes the refining margin at USD 8 to 10 per barrel from 2027, petrochemical margins around EUR 200 per tonne, the Danube refinery at full volumes, half of Tomorrow Downstream's USD 500 million of savings by 2027 and all of it by 2028, and the 95 percent and 50 percent crude-differential taxes persisting.
  • What it costs: HUF 5,000, or 9.2 times consensus 24-month-forward EPS of HUF 541.24 (2026-10-08), the 98th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range
    • 10.6 times on our own earnings path.
    • The stock's five-year median is 5.10x and its ten-year median 5.50x
      • It is at parity with Shell (10.0x) and OMV (10.2x) for a business with a 5.0 percent adjusted EBIT margin (FY2025), a 31.1 percent reported tax rate and a state that takes most of any upside.
    • The dated catalysts (Druzhba restored in April 2026, the Danube AV3 unit back in September 2026, Aphrodite signed, the dividend rise) are in a price that rose 32.4 percent in three months while the consensus target of HUF 4,813.64 sits below it.
  • The return against the bar: the 'Modal return' is minus 23.7 percent a year over eighteen months and minus 6.9 percent a year over three years from HUF 5,000, against the 20 percent bar.
    • The cash return of about 6.0 percent a year on a dividend of about HUF 300 per share is the only leg that works
      • The earnings leg is negative and the re-rating leg is negative, from 9.2x to our 6.5x.
    • The re-rating leg is the swing factor, and the case fails on every multiple from the stock's own median to the majors' average once the earnings normalise: at 9.2x held the eighteen-month return is still minus 5.5 percent a year.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major Corporate Change', badly, at D
    • The breadth of internal change is there, but the output the pattern asks for came from the external margin, not from the inputs.
    • The pattern the market is trading is 'Demand Inflection', and that fails its mandatory condition: the inflection is a supply-side spread without two years' visibility, already fading.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the external force to the sell-side and the price: every layer has moved.
    • It breaks at the step from the outputs to the return: the earnings that the price capitalises are the ones consensus already expects to fall, and the multiple that capitalises them is the highest in a decade.

  • What the case therefore is: the earnings are real, the windfall is over, and the price has not noticed.

    • The one branch on which the case works needs the margin to stay above USD 15 per barrel through 2027 and the multiple to hold at 9.2x
    • The branch is about one in five, and on it the stock is a B over eighteen months.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material and has travelled every layer of the chain, but it is an external windfall that is already reversing, the internal forces are moderate and slow, and from HUF 5,000 the 'Modal return' is negative at both horizons.

    • Not F, because the forces and the outputs are real and large and the cash return of about 6 percent a year is real
    • What fails is the return from this price, not the existence of change.
    • Not C, because the return is negative on the modal path and on every multiple from the stock's own median to the majors' average.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: European middle-distillate markets have tightened structurally, not cyclically.
      • Refinery closures across Europe, sanctions on Russian products, Hormuz risk and a diesel-heavy Central European demand base mean cracks settle well above the old normal, say USD 12 to 15 per barrel, for years.
      • The sell-side's bull framing is a global "Golden Age" of refining (5-Aug-26)
        • Weighted middle-distillate cracks were near USD 45 per barrel in July 2026.
      • MOL is the purest listed expression of it: 48 percent of its production is diesel, its plants sit in the top European net cash margin quartile, and from September 2026 the Danube refinery runs at full capacity again after a year at half, so FY2027 volumes rise even as margins ease.
        • Tomorrow Downstream adds USD 500 million a year by 2027.
      • The balance sheet is at 0.49x net debt to EBITDA with USD 5.0 billion of liquidity, the dividend yields 6 percent and is rising, the Croatian legal overhang on the Chairman-CEO is gone, the foundation dissolution returns 5.2 percent of the shares to treasury, Aphrodite gives a long-dated gas option, NIS a regional consolidation option, and the HUF 126 billion carbon-tax claim is upside.
      • On that reading the historical 5 to 6x multiple was a discount for problems that are being solved, consensus FY2027 EPS of HUF 625 is too low, the sell-side's bull case is HUF 6,307, and a stock at 7.9 times one-year-forward earnings with a 6 percent yield is not expensive.
    • What we concede.
      • H2 2026 will probably be strong and FY2026 consensus will probably rise, on July cracks, the Danube restart and the insurance receipt.
      • The dividend is real and its base part is safe.
      • The balance sheet is clean.
      • The legal overhang is resolved.
      • The mix drifts modestly toward retail.
      • A sustained margin above USD 15 per barrel is possible, at about 30 percent, and on that branch the stock is a B over eighteen months.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • The same company that is the purest beneficiary of a crack spike is the purest victim of its reversal, at USD 117 million of EBITDA per USD 1 per barrel
      • The petrochemical half of the windfall reversed inside one month.
      • The state takes the upside: the 95 percent Brent-Urals tax was extended, a 50 percent crude-differential tax was added in August 2026, caps cost about USD 60 million a quarter, and the risks research's named false friend is exactly this, a crack spike that triggers higher windfall taxes before the cash reaches shareholders.
      • Consensus itself does not believe the bull case: it has FY2027 EPS minus 15 percent and FY2028 minus 21 percent, the consensus target is below the price, and even the sell-side's base target of HUF 5,350 is 7 percent above HUF 5,000.
      • On the bull branch the price is already most of the way there: at 9.2x and 24-month-forward EPS of HUF 700 the eighteen-month value is HUF 6,740, a B
        • But the branch needs both the margin and the multiple to hold and its odds are about one in five.
        • The modal branch, at better than even odds, loses a third of the money in eighteen months.
    • The bounded-downside argument, tested.
      • The bull might argue that the non-refining businesses cover the enterprise value, so refining is free
      • They do not.
      • Enterprise value is about HUF 4,991,000 million at HUF 5,000 (HUF 4,855,172 million at HUF 4,830 on 2026-10-02: market capitalisation HUF 3,870,098 million on 801.3 million diluted shares, plus debt HUF 1,252,990 million, minorities HUF 373,853 million and pensions HUF 45,004 million, less cash HUF 471,783 million and associates HUF 214,990 million).
      • One year forward, Upstream, Consumer Services and Gas Midstream less Corporate give consensus EBITDA of HUF 791,596 million
        • At MOL's own historical 4.0x enterprise value to EBITDA that is HUF 3,166,000 million, 63 percent of enterprise value
        • At Orlen's 5.5x on 2027 estimates it is HUF 4,354,000 million, 87 percent.
        • The cover fails at both.
      • The whole group at three-year-forward consensus EBITDA of HUF 1,128,452 million covers 90 percent of enterprise value at 4.0x and 124 percent at 5.5x
        • Only at one-year-forward peak EBITDA of HUF 1,342,438 million does the group cover its enterprise value at its own historical multiple (108 percent).
        • The downside is bounded, if at all, by the dividend yield, which at HUF 2,500 would be 12 percent.
    • Where the case against would win: Brent-based complex margins above USD 15 per barrel reported for Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 with FY2027 consensus not cut.
      • That would move the thesis to C and the return to B on the bull branch, and it is the test the earnings research itself names.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because crude supply is the structural fragility: Druzhba, historically 80 percent of Hungarian crude, was shut for nearly three months from 27-Jan-26 to late April 2026
    • The only alternative is JANAF's Adria route at three to four times fair transit fees under an unsigned long-term contract and limited to about 80 percent of the refineries' needs
    • And raw-material costs rose 18 percent in H1 2026.
  • Druzhba: restored late April 2026 after a drone strike on Ukrainian pumping infrastructure
    • Refinery throughput minus 20 percent year-on-year in H1 2026.
  • Adria: JANAF fees three to four times fair benchmarks since 2022
    • MOL and Slovnaft complained to DG Competition in March 2026
    • On MOL's tests Adria reliably supplies about 80 percent of Danube and Slovnaft needs, against Croatia's claim of 11.4 million tonnes a year tested.
  • Input costs: raw materials and consumables HUF 3,997,228 million in H1 2026, plus 18 percent
    • Goods for resale plus 66.3 percent on the use of strategic reserves.
  • Mitigation: USD 500 million of debottlenecking capex to process 100 percent non-Russian crude by end-2026
    • Slovnaft's EUR 200 million modification is due in 2027.
  • Not F, because the pipeline is flowing again and the strategic reserves (250 kt borrowed) worked.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening at the margins that matter: Orlen targets 3,500 stations across Central and Eastern Europe by 2030 and can source seaborne Saudi crude under long-term contracts, and low-cost petrochemical imports from Asia, the US and the Middle East are structural.
  • Retail footprint: Orlen targets at least 3,500 service stations across CEE and 10,000 EV charging points by 2030
    • OMV operated 1,689 high-performance charging points at end-2025 against MOL's 296 Plugee chargers.
  • Share: MOL's total regional motor fuel sales fell 2 percent year-on-year in Q2 2026 while the ten-country market rose 1 percent, which the earnings research reads as intensified competition in peripheral countries.
  • Crude access: Orlen secured 55 percent of its crude seaborne under long-term contracts with Aramco and Equinor
    • The bear mechanism is undercutting MOL's retail prices in Hungary and Slovakia with that crude.
  • Not C, because European cracker closures (SABIC Geleen, Dow Bohlen) help regional petrochemical margins only temporarily and increase import dependence.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = D

The 'Foundations' are robust as an operating system and fragile where the thesis needs them: MOL is an integrated, landlocked refiner-retailer with 45 to 60 percent shares in its home markets, a clean balance sheet and a management that kept the plants running through a fire and a three-month pipeline cut, but the actors who set its margins (the Hungarian and Slovak states, JANAF, Ukrainian transit, Asian petrochemical exporters) are outside its control, and the business earns a 5.0 percent adjusted EBIT margin and about 9 percent return on capital through the cycle.
