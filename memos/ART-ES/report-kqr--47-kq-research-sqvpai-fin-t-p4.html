Section 2 – The Effective Tax Rate and Basque Regime

Section summary
  1. Arteche’s effective tax rate is expected to stabilize near 16% over the medium term, supported by the regional Basque tax regime and accumulated deferred tax assets.
  2. Backend-loaded deferred tax asset recovery and global minimum tax regulations limit further down-side tax rate optimization.

Historical tax rate volatility

1.
Fluctuating historical rates: Arteche experienced significant volatility in its annual taxation metrics over the last few fiscal periods. The group recorded an effective tax rate of 14.2% in FY2025. This represents a substantial decline from the 26.3% rate reported in FY2024 and is slightly below the 15.9% rate from FY2023. Such wide fluctuations make bottom-line forecasting difficult because shifting global profit contributions alter the annual blended rate.
2.
Interim tax stabilization: Tax expenses in the first half of the year show signs of stabilization. The consolidated group average tax rate was 16% for the six-month period ended June 30, 2026. This performance represents a positive reduction from the 18% average tax rate reported for the six months ended June 30, 2025. Lower interim tax rates will directly support net income generation unless year-end adjustments reverse these gains.
3.
Sector tax comparisons: Arteche's normalized tax rate of 16% in H1 2026 sits below major utility peers. Iberdrola reported a higher effective tax rate of 24.78% for the six-month period ended June 30, 2026. This rate also reflects a decline from the 28.41% effective tax rate Iberdrola recorded for the same period in 2025. The discrepancy indicates that grid equipment manufacturers enjoy greater tax flexibility than heavy capitalintensity utility peers like Iberdrola.

The Basque provincial tax regime

4.
Provincial statutory advantages: The parent company operates under a unique regional tax environment in northern Spain. Arteche is subject to the corporate income tax legislation of the provincial territory of Bizkaia in the Basque Country. This regional framework utilizes a nominal corporate tax rate of 24% as of 2026. This statutory advantage supports higher earnings conversion for Basque companies than those in common Spanish territories.
5.
Regional tax consolidation: Basque tax regulations allow local corporations to form consolidated tax groups. CIE Automotive also leverages the Bizkaia Regional Regulation 11/2013, modified by Regional Regulation 2/2018, to consolidate its regional subsidiaries. This integration helps optimize the group tax liability by offsetting local losses against profitable operations. Arteche utilizes a similar local consolidation structure which mitigates its domestic tax burden significantly.
6.
Unique regulatory disputes: Basque regional tax systems can introduce jurisdictional friction for large multi- regional companies. Iberdrola experienced historical disputes between the Spanish State tax authority and the Bizkaia provincial tax authority regarding audit and collection jurisdiction. These disputes were resolved through statutory mechanisms in the Economic Agreement with the Basque Country to avoid double taxation. While these disputes are administrative, they highlight the complex regional regulatory landscape that Bizkaia-based companies must navigate.

The company held deferred tax assets of €38.82m as of June 30, 2026, which rose from €35.89m at the end of 2025. The largest components of these assets are tax deductions, which remained constant at €15.08m. These accumulated deductions will shield future cash flows because they reduce the taxable base directly.

Debt Structure and Hedging Profiles

1.
Stable debt composition: Arteche maintained gross financial debt of €143.80m as of June 30, 2026, to fund its ongoing global manufacturing expansions. The group's primary financing relies on syndicated loans, bilateral credit agreements, and official institutional support. This capital structure ensures that the group has adequate diversification of funding sources to mitigate individual refinancing risks. Consequently, interest rate stability will help insulate the bottom-line earnings from sudden capital market adjustments.
2.
Effective interest hedging: Arteche reported that approximately 79% of its active loans are fixed-rate or protected through interest rate derivatives. These hedging structures secured an average cost of debt of 3.60% as of June 30, 2026, slightly up from 3.50% in the prior year. Management targets an average maturity of 4.2 years, which extends to 4.9 years when considering undrawn credit lines. Protecting this proportion of variable-rate exposure mitigates major interest cost increases over the next 18 months.
3.
Peer cost benchmarking: Cembre shows a conservative financial strategy with significantly lower overall financial debt relative to the sector. The company reported non-current bank loans of only €10.13m as of June 30, 2026, with interest rates ranging between 0.37% and 2.07%. Having such low debt means Cembre maintains an optimal liquidity position with zero risk of significant interest rate fluctuations. If Arteche can achieve similar low-cost financing terms, its net profit margins would see immediate expansion.
4.
Peer leverage comparison: Landis+Gyr maintains a more capital-intensive balance sheet with bank debt of $336.02m (calculated) as of September 30, 2026. This total comprises an outstanding multicurrency revolving credit facility of $80.0 million at 4.70% and a term loan of $250.0 million at 5.10% interest. The company's average cost of debt remains higher than Arteche's hedged rate of 3.60%. High statutory debt costs for Landis+Gyr will restrict earnings growth unless it deleverages quickly.

Asset-light debt structures: Alfen illustrates an asset-light corporate structure that relies primarily on working capital facilities rather than long-term bank loans. The company held a committed revolving credit facility of €50 million which remained entirely undrawn as of June 30, 2026. Alfen reported a net debt position of €20.70m at the end of 2025, representing a comfortable leverage ratio of 0.8x EBITDA. Low leverage helps shield Alfen from debt service burdens during transitional operational phases.

Factoring and Supplier Finance Cost

6.
Factoring liquidity support: Arteche relies heavily on off-balance sheet and trade financing programs to manage its working capital requirements. The group's non-recourse factored balances stood at €12.35m as of June 30, 2026, compared to €12.11m at the end of 2025. This factoring agreement allows the company to assign commercial insolvency risks directly to high-credit financial institutions. Utilizing non-recourse lines accelerates cash conversion cycles and prevents the inflation of short-term bank borrowings.
7.
Escalating reverse factoring: Arteche's supplier payments are optimized through active reverse factoring and confirming agreements with local credit institutions. Outstanding reverse-factoring balances increased to €33.85m as of June 30, 2026, up from €25.20m on December 31, 2025. These arrangements do not alter the contractual terms of trade payables but provide essential payment flexibility for suppliers. However, any sudden reduction in supplier payment lines could quickly compress operating cash flows.
8.
Peer receivables optimization: PFISTERER also utilizes trade receivables sales as an alternative financing tool to strengthen its capital structure. The group regularly sells original trade receivables to factoring companies up to a maximum nominal amount of €20.70m. Because del credere risks are transferred to the factor, PFISTERER enjoys no significant default risk exposure on these balances. This strategy provides a flexible liquidity buffer that supports cash generation without increasing statutory bank debt.

Factoring cost sensitivities: Cembre's historical decisions illustrate how changing macro interest environments can alter the economic convenience of trade finance programs. The company cancelled several reversefactoring contracts in 2023 because of the higher interest rates charged by factoring entities. This cancellation caused Cembre's average collection time to increase to 64 days in 2023 from 55 days in 2022. If bank rates remain elevated, Arteche face a similar risk of paying higher trade finance fees to maintain its confirming lines.
