Segments: revenue and margin by segment, where the company reports them

TORM reports two segments: Tanker (about 97% of revenue; FY2025 USD 1,314m) and Marine Engineering (ME Production, scrubbers and marine technology; FY2025 USD 37.2m, partly internal). Marine Engineering is immaterial to the forecast; I hold its external revenue at about USD 25m to 35m a year inside "voyage costs and other" and its contribution at about USD 5m to 10m of EBITDA inside the cost line. The operating drivers behind the inputs

  • Earning days: about 34,200 (FY2026), 34,000 (FY2027), 34,000 (FY2028), 34,500 (FY2029), 34,700 (FY2030), at 97.5% of ship-days. Fleet 95 at end-2026; +4 MR resales in 2027 and +2 in 2028; +8 MR newbuildings in 2029; about three sales of 2005-2008-built ships a year (four in 2029); the two 2030 options are not assumed; the eight LR2 joint-venture newbuildings (2029-2030) are equity-accounted and add no consolidated days.
  • Fleet TCE per day: Q4 2026 about USD 80,000 (53% fixed at 78,419; open days at about USD 62,000 for MRs, 150,000 for LR2s and 95,000 for LR1s); 2027 by quarter 63,000 / 48,000 / 36,000 / 31,000 (disruption into Q1 2027, easing from Q2, routes and the LR2 crossover normalised by late 2027, restocking slowing the fade); 2028 27,000; 2029 26,000; 2030 26,500 (mid-cycle anchored by three-year charters at USD 23,900 to 25,000 for 2015-built MRs and a five-year charter at USD 28,350, plus TORM's premium of about USD 2,500 per day; the 2028-2029 delivery wave offset by scrapping of the 22% of ships aged 20 years or more). Each USD 1,000 per day = about USD 34m of EBITDA and USD 0.33 of EPS.
  • Voyage costs and other revenue (IFRS revenue less TCE): about USD 558m (FY2026; USD 145m in Q4), 470m (FY2027; 135, 120, 110, 105 by quarter), then 420m to 430m a year, falling with bunker prices and voyage lengths.
  • Cash costs (TCE less EBITDA): about USD 379m (FY2026; USD 279m in nine months plus about 100m in Q4), 386m, 397m, 417m, 433m: vessel opex per day about USD 8,000 (2026), 8,350, 8,700, 9,000, 9,300; administration about USD 117m to 125m; less about USD 23m of other operating income.
  • Depreciation: USD 177.6m in nine months of 2026; rising with resales and newbuildings.
  • Net financial items: falling with net debt (FactSet definition, including leases): about USD 450m (end-2026), 320m, 155m, 125m, and net cash of about 80m (end-2030); gross debt held near USD 1.0bn to 1.15bn.
  • Tax: tonnage tax, about USD 4m to 6m a year.
  • Share count: 102.7m at Q3 2026; about +1.0m a year from restricted share units (48,167 issued on 12 November 2026); no share-funded acquisitions; no buybacks. The employees' exercise payments (about USD 20m to 30m a year; USD 29.3m in nine months of 2026) are left out of Part 2 so the model does not count the same shares twice; they are a small upside to cash.
  • Dividends: 75% of adjusted net profit each quarter (Q2 and Q3 2026 at 73% and 75%). The base-case balance sheet would allow more from 2028.
  • Capex: commitments of USD 608.8m at 30 September 2026 (USD 191.2m within one year, 208.8m in years two to three, the balance later) for six MR resales and eight MR newbuildings, plus drydocking and upgrades of about USD 60m to 70m a year; the peak in 2029 is the final newbuilding instalments.
  • Acquisitions less disposals: vessel-sale proceeds (USD 47.3m in Q3 2026 for two ships; about USD 45m to 60m a year after) less equity contributions to the LR2 joint venture (my estimate about USD 125m over 2026-2030; contract terms not disclosed).
  • Working capital: about 0.65 times the last quarter's revenue (receivables follow freight levels): USD 430m at end-2026 (from 339m at Q3), released to about USD 240m at end-2027 (about USD 190m of cash), then about USD 215m to 220m.
  • Leases: small after the 2025 refinancing (purchase options declared on 22 leased vessels) and the post-Q3 buy-back of TORM Alice and TORM Alexandra; office and minor charters only.
