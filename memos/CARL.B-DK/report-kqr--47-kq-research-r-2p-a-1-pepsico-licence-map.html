Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The reallocation of PepsiCo licences determines whether Carlsberg can sustain long-term EPS growth.
  2. Royal Unibrew faces near-term earnings pressure that is already largely discounted by its compressed valuation multiples.

Defining the core questions

1.
Unresolved strategic inquiry: As noted in Jefferies' report of 14 September 2026, the Key Question asks, "Which PepsiCo and other soft drinks bottling licences in Europe, Central Asia and the Caucasus come up for renewal or reallocation between 2026 and 2030, on what terms (length, exclusivity, share of margin) are they being awarded, what operating margin does Carlsberg earn on its licensed soft drinks against its beer, and how does Royal Unibrew plan to replace the Nordic PepsiCo business it loses on 1 January 2029?"
2.
Critical growth window: Jefferies argues in its note of 14 September 2026 that answering this question determines the post-2029 duration of Carlsberg's Story 2 growth window and subsequent consolidation pipeline.
3.
Already priced valuation: Bernstein argues in its report of 11 September 2026 that investors must assess if Royal Unibrew's loss of thirteen percent of net sales is already reflected in its stock price.
4.
Interconnected commercial query: Rothschild and Co Redburn notes on 30 September 2026 that a closely related question is how Coca-Cola plans to reallocate its bottling licences in Denmark and Finland.

Financial and risk implications

5.
Accelerating earnings potential: Jefferies suggests on 14 September 2026 that Carlsberg's expanded relationship with PepsiCo could deliver substantial EPS growth as multi-beverage integration synergies accumulate across fifteen consolidated markets.
6.
Impending margin pressure: Bernstein highlights in its note of 19 August 2026 that Royal Unibrew expects the transition to cause a painful air pocket in 2029 with reduced fixed-cost absorption scale.
7.
Compressed trading multiples: Valuation risk is shown by Royal Unibrew's derating to eleven times forward price to earnings in Bernstein's analysis of 19 August 2026.
8.
Other side of the story: Bernstein cautions in its report of 19 August 2026 that structural margin dilution might limit long-term profitability if Carlsberg faces higher concentrate costs and packaging inflation.

Section 2 – PepsiCo Bottling Licences and Reallocation Landscape

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg is rapidly consolidating PepsiCo territories across Central Asia and the Caucasus while preparing for a major Nordic transition.
  2. The strategic reallocation secures Carlsberg's position as an anchor bottler but inflicts significant volume losses on regional incumbents.

Central Asia and Caucasus expansion

1.
Rapidly scaling Central Asia: Carlsberg took over the exclusive PepsiCo license in Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan on 1 January 2026, driving a seventy percent volume surge in the first half of 2026.
2.
Temporary margin dilution: Jefferies notes in its report of 14 September 2026 that the initial Kazakhstan soft drinks ramp was temporary drag on profitability due to a heavy reliance on third-party toll fillers.
3.
Eliminating tolling drag: To resolve this, Carlsberg opened a three hundred forty-four million dollar production facility in Almaty on 1 October 2026 to eliminate the toll-filler drag and supply Kazakhstan and Kyrgyzstan.
4.
Azerbaijan license win: Carlsberg will become the exclusive PepsiCo bottler in Azerbaijan on 1 January 2027, expanding its expanded local Xirdalan brewery with dedicated soft drink lines.
5.
South Caucasus cluster logic: Jefferies highlights in its note of 5 October 2026 that Carlsberg is acquiring Iberia Refreshments in Georgia and JI Pepsi Cola Bottler Armenia from Revery to construct a regional operating cluster.

Northern Europe strategic swap

6.

Section 3 – Commercial and Financial Terms of Reallocated Licences

Section summary
  1. Reallocated PepsiCo bottling agreements combine exclusive long-term territorial distribution with shared marketing funding and incidence-based concentrate pricing.
  2. Carlsberg's integrated beer-andsoft-drinks route-to-market structure slashes capital expenditure requirements nearly in half compared to standalone bottling networks.

Commercial terms and margin sharing mechanics

1.
Long-term exclusive rights: Carlsberg secures multi-year exclusivity across seventeen total territories, extending its anchor bottling footprint under standard ten-year rolling franchise agreements as reported by Jefferies on 14 September 2026.
2.
Real-time concentrate pricing: Under modernized system terms, concentrate pricing operates on an incidence model where brand owners charge roughly thirty percent of net sales based on real-time pack pricing as analyzed by Jefferies on 21 October 2024.
3.
Co-funded marketing support: Brand owners co-fund regional marketing and channel promotions through dedicated advertising funds, reducing the bottler's net commercial spend as detailed in an expert call with a former PepsiCo executive on 11 March 2026.
4.
Shared cluster production logic: Carlsberg leverages centralized manufacturing hubs in Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan to supply neighboring Caucasus and Central Asian markets at low incremental unit costs as noted by Jefferies on 13 September 2026.

Section 5 – Royal Unibrew's Mitigation and Replacement Plan

Section summary
  1. Royal Unibrew faces an unmitigated 2029 EBIT risk of 22% to 26% following the loss of its Nordic PepsiCo franchise.
  2. The company's multi-pronged replacement strategy focuses on accelerating higher- margin own brands, cutting costs, and pursuing new partnerships.
  3. Valuation multiples reflect a substantial discount, indicating that the loss is largely priced in by equity markets.

Strategic mitigation and operational options

1.
Impending volume gap: The expiring PepsiCo agreements in Denmark, Finland, and the Baltics represent approximately thirteen percent of group net revenue and roughly half of Royal Unibrew's soft drink volume in Northern Europe as noted by Bernstein Research on 11 September 2026.
2.
Severe earnings exposure: On an unmitigated basis, the loss of these franchise contracts and the accompanying fixed-cost scale loss create a potential group EBIT decline of twenty-two to twenty-six percent in 2029 as analyzed by DNB Carnegie on 19 June 2026.
3.
Own-brand growth acceleration: Royal Unibrew plans to mitigate revenue loss by prioritizing core local brands like Faxe Kondi and Jaffa, which offer structurally higher gross margins than licensed portfolios as reported in Royal Unibrew's press release on 21 April 2026.
4.
Volume substitution requirements: DNB Carnegie calculates on 19 June 2026 that Royal Unibrew must replace fifty percent of lost Nordic volumes with proprietary soft drinks to fully offset the EBIT impact.
5.
Non-cola brand momentum: Non-cola carbonated soft drinks show high momentum in Denmark, where Faxe Kondi Pro gained a thirty percent market share in sports beverages as stated in Royal Unibrew's earnings call on 26 February 2025.
6.
Strategic partnership rationale: Bernstein Research reports on 25 September 2026 that Royal Unibrew management sees a rational argument for a Nordic Coca-Cola relationship to leverage existing on-trade distribution networks.

Capacity reallocation benefits: Terminating the PepsiCo partnership frees up substantial production capacity across facilities in Denmark and Finland, reducing near-term capital expenditure requirements according to Royal Unibrew's announcement on 21 April 2026.
