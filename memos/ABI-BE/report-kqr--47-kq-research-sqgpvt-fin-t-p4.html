Section 2 – Net Finance Costs and Mark-to-Market Items

Section summary
  1. AB InBev is successfully driving down its net finance costs through rapid deleveraging and proactive liability management.
  2. The brewer's long debt maturity profile and high proportion of fixed-rate debt insulate its earnings from benchmark interest rate volatility.
  3. Non-underlying derivative mark-to-market adjustments introduce non-cash income statement volatility that analysts consistently exclude from underlying EPS.

Net interest path

1.
Lower interest: AB InBev reported a solid reduction in net finance costs to 908 million USD in 3Q26 from 1,165 million USD in 3Q25. This positive performance was primarily driven by net interest expense falling to 600 million USD compared to 675 million USD in the prior period. This downward trend is expected to sustainably lower the company's financial hurdle and accelerate earnings per share growth over the next three years.
2.
Underestimated savings: FactSet estimated AB InBev's net interest expense at 4,162 million USD for FY2025, but actual results came in significantly lower due to rapid deleveraging. Jefferies reported that actual FY25 net interest expense fell to 2,566 million USD from 2,704 million USD in FY24. This lower baseline interest expense means brokers must adjust their interest projections downward to reflect the faster pace of debt paydowns.
3.
Reclassified items: Anheuser-Busch InBev adopted new accounting standards that reclassify several technical lines between operating profit and net finance costs. Analysts at Jefferies on September 28, 2026, explained that IFRS 18 introduces required subtotals and eliminates the use of special items. These reporting changes alter the visual layout of the income statement but do not change core cash flows or actual interest obligations.
4.
Contrary evidence: Anheuser-Busch InBev experienced a 2.8 billion USD foreign exchange headwind on its net debt in late 2025 despite redeeming 2.7 billion USD of bonds. This currency headwind, reported during the earnings call on February 12, 2026, offset organic deleveraging progress. Consequently, exchange rate fluctuations can unexpectedly inflate reported net debt and complicate interest cost forecasting.

Debt structure and refinancing

5.
Extended maturity: AB InBev maintains a highly insulated debt structure with a weighted average maturity of 13 years and no financial covenants. Management confirmed during the Q4 2025 earnings call that 98% of the company's bond portfolio holds fixed interest rates. This high proportion of fixed-rate debt fully insulates the brewer from volatility in global benchmark interest rates.
6.
Low refinancing: AB InBev faces minimal refinancing pressure because its free cash flow exceeds its scheduled annual bond maturities. Jefferies reported on October 29, 2026, that the group has no bonds maturing in 2026, allowing it to retire expensive debt organically. Deleveraging in this manner will steadily reduce gross interest expenses through 2029 without requiring expensive refinancings.
7.
Euro exposure: AB InBev holds approximately 40% of its total net debt in euros, which introduces translation risk to the balance sheet. Bernstein on February 13, 2026, noted that a 13% appreciation of the euro against the US dollar inflated reported net debt. This exchange rate sensitivity means that unhedged translation fluctuations can distort leverage metrics even as the underlying debt is paid off.
8.
Active liability: AB InBev actively managed its liabilities by initiating 2 billion USD in cash tender offers and redeeming 1.60 billion USD of USD notes in mid-2025. These redemptions, announced on May 13, 2025, focused on retiring high-coupon liabilities to optimize the overall capital structure. This proactive management of outstanding notes is critical to maintaining fiscal discipline and reducing the group's weighted average coupon.

Derivative mark-to-market items

9.
Non-underlying losses: AB InBev reported an 800 million USD non-underlying mark-to-market loss on derivative instruments in 3Q26 compared to a 947 million USD loss in 3Q25. These instruments represent equity swap derivatives used to hedge share-based payment programs and the Grupo Modelo and SAB combinations. Although these losses impact reported net profit, brokers consistently exclude these volatile items from their underlying earnings per share calculations.
10.
Unqualified hedges: AB InBev's equity price risk is managed through equity swaps that do not qualify for hedge accounting under standard regulations. In its interim financial report on July 29, 2026, the company stated that changes in the fair value of these swaps are recognized directly in profit or loss. Consequently, share price movements introduce substantial non-cash earnings volatility that does not reflect core operational brewing performance.
11.
Valuation swings: AB InBev's equity derivative liabilities fluctuated from 5.5 billion USD in late 2025 to 3.2 billion USD as of June 30, 2026. The company reported that a rise in its share price from 54.90 EUR to 72.66 EUR during H126 generated a 2,033 million USD non-underlying gain. These massive non-cash gains highlight how stock price appreciation can artificially inflate reported net finance income.

Peer financing and accounting comparisons

12.
Restructured capital: Carlsberg dramatically reshaped its capital structure by issuing 1.8 billion EUR in hybrid bonds in May 2026 to accelerate deleveraging. Jefferies on September 28, 2026, explained that because IFRS treats these hybrids as equity, their interest coupons do not flow through net finance costs. This accounting treatment allows peer groups to report artificially lower net finance costs compared to traditional debt structures.
13.
Peer comparison: Diageo reported a consolidated net interest charge of 812 million USD for the fiscal year ended June 30, 2026. In comparison, Carlsberg expects its annual net financial expenses, excluding foreign exchange, to land at approximately 1.8 billion DKK. This benchmark illustrates that AB InBev's massive annual net interest burden of over 2.5 billion USD remains significantly higher than global beverage peers due to its legacy acquisition debt.
14.
Refinancing comparison: Morgan Stanley reported on October 20, 2026, that AB InBev has a very low proportion of debt maturing within the next 12 months. Refinancing all short-term debt at 100 basis points above current average costs would impact AB InBev's adjusted pre-tax profit by only 0.3%. In contrast, Carlsberg and Heineken would experience estimated profit impacts of 0.8% and 1.1% respectively, proving that AB InBev has superior near-term insulation against rising market interest rates.
15.
Historic precedent: Carlsberg's refinancing precedent in H126 shows that redeeming legacy EMTN bonds with hybrid proceeds successfully lowered non-FX net finance costs from 2.2 billion DKK to 1.8 billion DKK. Analysts at Morgan Stanley on September 29, 2026, also noted that these hybrid bonds pave the way for a resumption of share buybacks. This restructuring shows how corporate actions can shift financing burdens, though AB InBev remains focused on organic debt retirement instead.
16.
Other side of the story: AB InBev's strategy of relying on organic cash flows to retire debt could be slowed by currency devaluations in emerging markets. While fixed coupons protect against rate hikes, Bernstein on October 30, 2026, pointed out that unhedged international cash flows are vulnerable to translation headwinds. If emerging market currencies weaken significantly, the deleveraging path will be extended and delay anticipated net interest reductions.

Section 3 – Taxation Dynamics and Brazilian Disputes

Section summary
  1. AB InBev faces a structurally higher global tax rate as key tax incentives in South America are narrowed by legislative reforms.
  2. Severe fiscal risks in Brazil, including unprovisioned tax disputes exceeding 53 billion BRL, threaten to disrupt long-term earnings per share.
  3. Brokers are adjusting their cash flow models to reflect elevated tax rates and potential changes to interest-on-capital deductions.

Global tax guidance

1.
Elevated tax: AB InBev reiterated its full-year normalized effective tax rate guidance of 26% to 28% during its third- quarter earnings presentation on October 29, 2026. The company noted that this guidance does not incorporate the potential impacts of future legislative reforms in its operating countries. This guided range is structurally higher than historical levels, meaning that tax expenses will continue to exert downward pressure on EPS over the next three years.
2.
Varying rates: Anheuser-Busch InBev reported a normalized effective tax rate of 26.9% in 3Q26 compared to 25.1% in 3Q25. Management attributed this quarter-over-quarter increase primarily to an unfavorable country mix in its consolidated earnings. Because higher-tax jurisdictions contributed more to the profit mix, the company's normalized tax burden expanded significantly during this period.

Brazilian fiscal reforms

4.
Restrictive laws: Ambev saw its standalone effective tax rate surge from 0.5% in 2023 to 23.8% in 2024. This dramatic increase followed the enactment of Federal Law No. 14,789/2023, which restricted interest-on-capital deductions and eliminated certain government sales tax grants. Because Ambev represents a major portion of South American earnings, this legislative change permanently elevated the group's ETR beginning in 2024.

Proposed levies: Bernstein reported on November 26, 2025, that the Brazilian Senate was evaluating a proposed 10% withholding tax on corporate dividends. While interest-on-own-capital payments are already subject to a 15% withholding tax, cash dividends have historically been fully exempt under Brazilian law. If implemented, this withholding tax will reduce the net cash upstreamed from Ambev to its parent company.

Off-balance sheet disputes

7.
Severe contingencies: AB InBev disclosed in its June 30, 2026, interim report that its unprovisioned tax disputes in Brazil reached 21.5 billion USD, up from 19.0 billion USD in late 2025. The most critical of these cases is a massive dispute over interest-on-capital deductions, which carries an updated possible loss of 30.8 billion BRL. Because the probability of loss is deemed possible rather than probable, the group has recorded no accounting provisions for these potential liabilities.

Ongoing challenges: Ambev received administrative and judicial assessments from the Brazilian Federal Tax Authorities challenging the universal taxation of its foreign profits. While some favorable administrative rulings became final in late 2024, the company continues to face new assessments for subsequent fiscal years. Although the initial financial impact is not material, these rules have significantly increased compliance complexity and the likelihood of audits. Opposing positions taken by tax authorities could force future upward revisions in consolidated tax provisions.

Broker tax modeling

10.
Model adjustments: Morgan Stanley utilizes a two-stage discounted cash flow model for Ambev that incorporates a BRL weighted average cost of capital of 16.5%. The broker's model accounts for structurally higher tax rates in Brazil, reflecting the reduced capacity to distribute tax-deductible interest on capital. These elevated tax assumptions have compressed projected free cash flows and limited the stock's valuation multiple.
11.
Alternative offsets: Jefferies highlighted on January 10, 2024, that Ambev held substantial net deferred tax assets of approximately 5 billion BRL on its balance sheet. Analysts suggest that Ambev can utilize these existing credits to mitigate the cash tax impact of reduced interest-on-capital deductibility. This utilization of deferred assets protects immediate cash flow generation even as the income statement tax rate rises.
12.
Other side of the story: AB InBev's consolidated effective tax rate could remain at the lower end of its guided range if currency translation favors lower-tax regions. While Brazilian tax reforms present a clear headwind, Jefferies noted on November 20, 2026, that stable pricing and premiumization in Mexico could offset South American tax pressures. If emerging market currencies stabilize and country mix shifts favorably, the consolidated group may avoid the worst-case ETR scenarios modeled by bearish analysts.

  1. AB InBev's strategic buyback of packaging plant minorities enhances immediate earnings per share by eliminating legacy dividend and profit leakages.
  2. Listed subsidiaries Ambev and Budweiser APAC remain critical operational drivers but introduce structural minority interest volatility to the group.
  3. Peer groups actively utilize minority buyouts and strategic joint ventures to optimize their regional capital allocations and accelerate deleveraging.

These headwinds demonstrate that high regional ownership exposes earnings to localized volume volatility.

Volatile leakage: Anheuser-Busch InBev's consolidated net profit is subject to significant minority interest leakage from these listed subsidiaries. Upstreamed dividends: Budweiser APAC maintains a robust capital position that supports dividend payments to its parent, Anheuser-Busch InBev. The subsidiary holds over 9.8 billion USD in equity attributable to its holders as of June 30, 2026. This strong balance sheet ensures that cash is successfully returned to the parent, mitigating minority leakage impacts.
