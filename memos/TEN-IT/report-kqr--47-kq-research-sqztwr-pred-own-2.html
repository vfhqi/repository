Section 2 – Strait of Hormuz Closure and Shipping Transit Dynamics

Section summary

Daily Vessel Counts and Transit Volume Drops

1.
Severe maritime disruption: The military confrontation that began on 28 February 2026 transformed the Strait of Hormuz from the world's most critical energy chokepoint into an operationally unviable shipping route. Prior to the conflict, typical daily traffic through the strait ranged from 90 to 120 commercial vessels, mostly loaded with energy commodities. Following the escalation, daily traffic collapsed to fewer than ten vessels as shipping lines suspended operations or rerouted their fleets. This sustained collapse represents an unprecedented shock, considering that approximately 21 million barrels per day (bpd) of crude and refined products–about a quarter of global seaborne oil trade–historically utilized the waterway. Weekly transit collapse: Tracking data from the International Monetary Fund's PortWatch highlighted the extreme scale of the physical blockade. On 30 August 2026, PortWatch recorded only six daily transits compared with the pre-crisis baseline of approximately 85 daily commercial transits. Furthermore, Incred Capital reported on 15 September 2026 that monitored commercial transits averaged just six vessels a day during the first 14 days of September, representing an 81% collapse in seaborne crude and refined product flows down to 3.8 million bpd. While some vessels continue to cross, their captains are reportedly commanding premiums of up to $100,000 a month to navigate the high-risk zone. Inconclusive export metrics: Shipments of refined petroleum products have suffered far more severe, lasting damage than crude oil exports. Emirati and regional pipelines: The United Arab Emirates has successfully mitigated its export risk by utilizing the Abu Dhabi Crude Oil Pipeline (ADCOP), also known as the Habshan-Fujairah pipeline. To build longterm structural resilience, the UAE is fast-tracking construction of a parallel pipeline to double Fujairah's export capacity to 3.6 million bpd by mid-2027. Other regional projects, such as Iraq's Basra-Haditha line and the potential revival of the Kirkuk-Baniyas Mediterranean route, are also being accelerated to bypass the strait.
