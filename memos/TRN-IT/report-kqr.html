Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Snam, Terna, and Italgas face major EPS volatility as ARERA prepares to reset WACC parameters for 2028, rendering regulatory visibility a critical driver of investor sentiment.

Central Research Focus

1.
Central regulatory question: "Italy's regulator ARERA will recalculate the allowed returns of Terna, Snam and Italgas for 2028 to 2030 after a trigger cut of 30 to 40 basis points for 2027; what do brokers other than Bernstein and the regulator's consultation papers now expect for the 2028 method (the sovereign bond basket, the share of the cost of debt set at current market rates, the equity risk premium), how large a change in allowed return do they expect against the 2027 level for electricity transmission and gas transport, and how large is the efficiency clawback expected for Italgas's gas distribution at the 2028 reset?"

Financial Implications for Terna

2.
Capital remuneration risks: Terna's massive investment program remains highly sensitive to the upcoming regulatory WACC resets which determine remuneration on both active assets and work-in-progress. Under the current regulatory cycle, Terna earns 5.5% on commissioned assets while work-in-progress receives a lower 4.1% real pre-tax return. Resolving whether the 2028 methodology preserves these incentives is crucial for forecasting long-term capital deployment.
3.
Amplified earnings exposure: Terna represents the Italian regulated network operator with the highest earnings sensitivity to the WACC reset because of its pure-play business model. Approximately 95% of Terna's EBITDA is generated directly from regulated activities in its domestic market. Consequently, any reduction in allowed returns immediately compresses net income and weighs heavily on EPS growth.

Snam and Italgas Trajectories

4.
Regulated revenue concentration: Snam's financial performance is deeply tied to ARERA's rate reviews because approximately 90% of its revenues are derived from regulated activities. Morgan Stanley highlights that Snam's price target is highly sensitive to the nominal valuation WACC and cost of debt parameters. Any negative deviation in the 2028 gas transport allowed return would instantly dampen Snam's dividend growth profile.
5.
Ambitious plan execution: Snam's strategic plan targets stronger earnings growth but relies heavily on stable regulatory assumptions and a constructive 2028 WACC reset. Snam targets 3% compound annual growth in net income, which could be jeopardized if ARERA implements a tougher transport return. Consequently, the 2028 methodology serves as the key determinant of financial targets.
6.
Efficiency clawback overhang: Italgas faces substantial structural risk at the 2028 reset due to the transition to the new Regulation for Expenditure and Service Objectives (ROSS). Deutsche Bank notes that the regulator's preferred Simplified ROSS framework could claw back historical opex outperformance. This regulatory shift could severely limit future cost savings and depress Italgas's medium-term EPS.

Strategic Importance of Resets

7.
Critical clearing event: The upcoming 2027 trigger cut of 30 to 40 basis points has acted as a persistent overhang for Italian utility stocks. However, resolving the 2028 methodology represents a major clearing event because it establishes long-term rules for sovereign baskets and debt indexation. This milestone will finally let investors re-evaluate the sector's risk profile.
8.
Other side of the story: While the upcoming resets present substantial headwinds, robust inflation linkage and output-based incentives may significantly cushion the bottom-line impact. Terna and Snam continue to generate strong performance under ARERA's output incentive schemes which adds a meaningful premium to tariff returns. High capital expenditure execution and indexed regulatory asset bases could therefore partially offset WACC compression over the next three years.
