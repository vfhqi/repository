EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business Model Transformation and Strategic Positioning

Saga plc has executed a fundamental transformation, transitioning from a capital-intensive underwriting model to a capital-light affinity distribution business by completing the sale of Acromas Insurance Company Limited to Ageas UK for seventy-one million pounds in July 2025, eliminating all underwriting risk and solvency capital volatility from its balance sheet. The group now operates as a pure insurance broker under a twenty-year exclusive affinity partnership with Ageas UK, earning volume-linked commission income on motor and home policies through 2045, securing highly predictable long-term revenues. The travel division remains assetheavy, with Saga owning and operating two young, highly profitable ocean cruise ships that represent the group's primary underlying profit driver, while consolidated travel operations enable unified customer journeys and satisfaction gains. The river cruise division utilizes ten-year charter agreements to expand itineraries without high capital expenditure, recently adding the Spirit of the Moselle, while the holidays business packages thirdparty accommodation and flights into curated travel products.

Financial Performance and Deleveraging Success

Saga completed a new three hundred and thirty-five million pound term loan facility due January 2031, resolving near-term bond maturity risks, while proceeds from the AICL sale and strong cash generation reduced the leverage ratio from 7.0x in July 2023 to 2.7x by July 2026. The group's return on capital employed exploded to 22.2 percent in H1 2026 from 13.5 percent previously, while the continuing business generated available operating cash of one hundred and one million pounds in H1 2026, demonstrating robust cash generation posttransformation.

Ocean Cruise delivers over thirty-five million pounds profit per vessel annually, demonstrating exceptional asset productivity, and management raised full-year underlying profit before tax guidance to sixty-five to seventy million pounds while targeting at least one hundred million pounds underlying pre-tax profit ahead of January 2030. The reported EBITDA margin reached 20.6 percent in FY26, reflecting improving margin quality as low-margin broker underwriting was replaced by high-margin travel fees and predictable insurance commissions.

Demographic and Market Positioning Advantages

Saga targets the UK over-50 population, estimated at 26.7 million individuals in 2025, with households aged 55 to 65 commanding average wealth of five hundred and seventy-five thousand pounds, providing a rapidly expanding and highly resilient consumer base. The proprietary customer database holds 9.3 million records with 7.8 million contactable customers, while the Saga brand commands 93 percent brand awareness in the United Kingdom, creating immediate trust and substantially lowering customer acquisition costs. The ocean cruise segment recorded a peak net promoter score of 82 during H1 2026, with repeat customers accounting for 68 percent of ocean cruise passengers, while river cruise tNPS reached a record 75, representing a massive improvement from historic scores near 15. Motor and home customer retention increased to 85 percent from 77 percent in prior periods, while Saga maintains the direct retail relationship and brand ownership under the twenty-year affinity partnership.

Capital-Light Partnership Economics

The exclusive twenty-year affinity partnership with Ageas UK covers motor and home insurance distribution through 2045, with Ageas assuming all pricing and claims risk, insulating Saga from volatile underwriting cycles, while Deutsche Bank estimates Saga's fixed commission at approximately 8 percent of premium volumes. Private medical insurance is distributed through a solus partnership with BUPA, generating profit shares, with the BUPA PMI partnership delivering two point five million pounds in profit commission during H1 2026. The NatWest Boxed savings partnership attracted over two billion pounds in deposits within six months of launch, with more than 53 percent of new savings accounts representing customers entirely new to Saga, demonstrating successful capital-light growth channels. The river cruise division utilizes long-term ten-year charter agreements to expand capacity without ship-ownership debt, taking delivery of the Spirit of the Moselle in July 2025 on a tenyear charter, with lease liabilities contracted for the upcoming Spirit of the Lorelei ship launch in 2027.

Competitive Dynamics and Industry Structure

The UK population over 50 reached an estimated 26.7 million individuals in 2025 and is projected to add 2.1 million additional individuals by 2035, while UK consumers aged over 50 will account for 63 percent of total spending by 2040. Specialized cruise peers include Fred Olsen, Cunard, P&O Cruises, Riviera, and Viking, with Saga avoiding mass-market pricing pressure by targeting affluent consumers aged over 50 exclusively. The UK personal lines insurance market remains highly competitive on aggregator channels, though recent carrier mergers are expected to tighten commercial retail pricing discipline, while private equity capital fuels intense independent broker consolidation. Post-pandemic ship construction cost inflation prevents competitors from replicating cruise economics, while Saga's bespoke ocean cruise vessels are early in their thirty-five-year life, creating impenetrable entry barriers for boutique cruise operations.

Operational Foundation and Risk Management

Saga is one hundred percent hedged against foreign exchange transaction risks for both FY27 and FY28, while fuel oil is one hundred percent hedged for FY27 and seventy-five percent hedged for FY28, with four hundred and fifty-two forward currency contracts and eighty-five fuel swaps designated by July 2026. Saga maintains a highly negative cash conversion cycle calculated at negative 117 days in FY26, with trade receivable days at 54 days, inventory days at 9 days, and payable days reaching 180 days, providing structural liquidity benefits as customer pre-payments fund travel operating capital.

Mike Hazell brings strong operational discipline as Group CEO appointed in November 2023, executing four strategic pillars including debt reduction and operational simplification, with Saga's five-year turnaround plan significantly ahead of the original trajectory set in 2025.

Long-Term Growth Trajectory and Paradigm Alignment

UK over-65s spend three times more on travel than younger demographic cohorts, while active gym memberships for individuals aged 65 to 74 expanded by 44 percent recently, demonstrating resilient leisure demand among affluent retirees. The Sabre partnership converts Saga to modern API-driven travel distribution, while the Specialist Computer Centres partnership moves the group to standardized infrastructure, mitigating technology disintermediation risks while lowering operating overheads. Saga is launching bespoke pet insurance in partnership with Allianz UK, with partnership expansions generating broking income without balance sheet risk, capitalizing on deep brand affinity and proprietary database penetration. The group's structural evolution toward capital-light financial partnerships unlocks substantial fee-based revenues without underwriting or credit risks, positioning Saga exceptionally well to capture unprecedented demographic growth as the UK over-50 population expands structurally.

Key Risks and Mitigating Factors

The capital-intensive ocean cruise segment remains highly operationally geared, where minor load factor declines impact profit before tax, leaving the group vulnerable to a major demand shock compressing profits due to high asset-intensity. High reliance on third-party capacity providers leaves Saga completely exposed to price increases or underwriting capacity withdrawal by Ageas or Allianz, which could cripple commission streams under the affinity model. Extreme winter travel seasonality and pension deficit drag could reverse working capital gains and delay target leverage reduction, as working capital benefits reverse during winter booking slow periods. Acute weather events introduce heightened risks of cruise itinerary changes, while the UK Emissions Trading

Scheme applies to international shipping from July 2026, representing emerging climate and regulatory risks. Despite these vulnerabilities, the capital-light transformation, proprietary demographic database, exclusive longterm partnerships, and robust hedging programs substantially mitigate operational and financial volatility while positioning Saga to deliver accelerated deleveraging and sustainable profit growth through the aging UK demographic tailwind.
