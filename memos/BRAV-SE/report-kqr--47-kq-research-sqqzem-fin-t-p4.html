Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence shows that Bravida's EPS trajectory from FY2026 to FY2028 can be partly answered now.

The ABEKA divestment gain of SEK 158 million and Swedish restructuring costs create modeling variance among brokers, which distorts Bravida's reported operating performance unless brokers adjust for it. The NOK 4.5 billion Green Mountain contract represents Bravida's direct share and will substantially expand Norway's segment backlog without being diluted by the JV partner's portion. However, critical gaps remain around the exact phasing of data-centre revenues, the realization timeline of Swedish cost savings, and the ultimate resolution of the SEK 800 million Danish arbitration, which collectively prevent a definitive answer to the Key Question at this stage.

DNB Carnegie adjusts the SEK 158 million ABEKA capital gain out of core earnings and models an EO-item net positive impact of SEK 98 million in FY2026, which offsets restructuring costs, making the adjusted EPS forecast more representative of underlying performance than reported EPS. Bravida Sweden expects total restructuring costs to reach SEK 70 to 80 million in FY2026, with SEK 20 million in Q1 2026 and SEK 40 million in Q2 2026, indicating that the restructuring program will weigh heavily on Swedish operating margins until these actions conclude.

Bravida's bank debt is 100% floating and denominated entirely in SEK, making interest expenses highly sensitive to the rate paths of the Riksbank and Norges Bank, which means rate hikes would directly inflate interest costs in FY2027 and weigh on net margins. The ABEKA divestment gain of SEK 158 million is expected to be tax-exempt, as noted in Bravida's Q2 2026 report, which artificially inflates reported net profit margins for the FY2026 period.

The SEK 4.3 billion Green Mountain project is scheduled to begin execution at the end of 2026, with production running continuously throughout 2027 and 2028 before concluding in early 2029, meaning Norway's installation segment will experience a sustained sales lift starting in late 2026. The atNorth contract worth EUR 200 million (approximately SEK 2.2 billion) for the data centre in Kouvola, Finland, will significantly bolster Finnish operating margins because the role of principal contractor allows Bravida to capture a larger share of the project's overall economics. Bravida's merger of its three Swedish divisions into a single country organization will incur total restructuring costs of SEK 90 to 100 million but will yield SEK 65 million in annual savings, which is crucial for expanding Swedish EBITA margins starting in late 2026.

Bravida's translation risk is entirely unhedged for Norwegian, Danish, and Finnish operations, and the company warns that a 10% stronger Swedish krona against the Norwegian and Danish currencies would reduce group EPS by approximately SEK 0.28, which will severely distort EPS performance during periods of krona strength.

Bravida's dividend policy targets distributing at least 50% of annual net profit back to shareholders, with the board proposing SEK 3.80 per share for 2025 at a 63% payout ratio, representing a highly attractive income return profile for long-term equity investors. To achieve full share allocation under the LTIP 2026 program, the group's 2026 EBITA must reach at least SEK 2,144 million, which will trigger maximum equity rewards for senior leaders.

On 8 June 2026, the District Court of Rogaland ordered the New Stavanger University Hospital to pay approximately SEK 320 million for outstanding installation work plus SEK 145 million to cover accrued interest and legal costs, representing a substantial validation of Bravida's project claims after years of negotiations. Bravida disclosed a major dispute with a public sector customer in Denmark involving an outstanding balance of approximately SEK 800 million across two public customers, which remains a long-term drag on working capital efficiency across Denmark.

The report's findings reveal that broker modeling divergence, particularly around one-off adjustments, creates material variance in consensus EPS projections. DNB Carnegie excludes Swedish restructuring expenses to show underlying performance, while Deutsche Bank shifts them above the line, and this lack of modeling agreement distorts consensus EPS calculations unless brokers synchronize their treatment. Data-centre contract phasing represents the most significant structural growth driver, with multi-billion-krona projects scheduled to ramp through 2029, but execution bottlenecks and resource constraints pose material risks.

DNB Carnegie notes that data-centre projects are highly complex and carry elevated execution risks, and a former CEO at Bravida Sverige stated that the company is operationally limited to managing only two hyperscale data centres simultaneously, which implies that taking on multiple hyperscale projects at the same time could strain resources and delay completion. translation exposure emerges as a substantial and unhedged risk to reported EPS, particularly given Bravida's large Norwegian and Danish operations.

Legal dispute resolutions present a bifurcated risk-return profile: the favorable Stavanger ruling unlocks immediate working capital, while the prolonged Danish arbitration creates persistent downside risk through 2028. Bravida's Q4 2025 presentation confirmed that the large unpaid Danish receivable will be processed by an arbitration court in 2028, with management expecting minimal earnings impact, which means no near-term cash collection is modeled from this Danish dispute.

Capital allocation flexibility remains robust due to strong cash conversion and conservative leverage, supporting both shareholder returns and bolt-on M&A, though the ambitious LTIP 2026 EBITA hurdle of SEK 2,144 million signals aggressive internal growth expectations. analysis identifies medium overall confidence in forecasting Bravida's EPS path due to clear operational disclosures but persistent accounting treatment variances and project execution uncertainties. Critical information gaps include the exact quarterly phasing of Swedish efficiency savings, the joint venture economics of Green Mountain, and the ultimate outcome of the Danish arbitration. The resolution of these gaps through subsequent quarterly reports, Capital Markets Day updates, and legal filings will be essential to refine EPS projections and assess whether Bravida can sustain its premium valuation multiples relative to peers like Instalco and SPIE.

Investors should closely monitor Q3 and Q4 2026 interim reports for evidence of data-centre revenue conversion, Swedish margin expansion, and normalized cash flow generation, as these operational milestones will determine whether consensus estimates prove conservative or optimistic relative to actual delivery.

Section 2 – EBITA to EPS Bridge: Restructuring, ABEKA, and Financials

Section summary

One-Off Adjustments and Broker Treatment

1.
Adjusted EBITA variance: The treatment of the ABEKA divestment capital gain of SEK 158 million creates modeling variance among brokers. Bravida reported this SEK 158 million gain in Q2 2026 as group-wide profit. Consequently, this transaction substantially distorts Bravida's reported operating performance unless brokers adjust for it.
2.
Broker gain treatment: DNB Carnegie's report on 28 September 2026 adjusts the SEK 158 million ABEKA capital gain out of core earnings. The broker models an EO-item net positive impact of SEK 98 million in FY2026, which offsets restructuring costs. This means the adjusted EPS forecast is more representative of underlying performance than reported EPS.
3.
Alternative broker method: Deutsche Bank’s note on 11 May 2026 shifts adjusting items above the line. This method integrates restructuring charges and divestments directly into standard operating profits. So, their EPS estimates fell by 6% because they did not isolate these non-recurring events from core performance.
4.
Restructuring cost guidance: Bravida Sweden expects total restructuring costs to reach SEK 70 to 80 million in FY2026. Restructuring costs were SEK 20 million in Q1 2026 and SEK 40 million in Q2 2026. This indicates that the restructuring program will weigh heavily on Swedish operating margins until these actions conclude.
5.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank’s update on 6 May 2026 shows Q1 EBITA grew by 12% year-on-year when excluding the SEK 20 million restructuring impact. This shows that the restructuring costs are already yielding positive efficiency gains that offset their immediate margin drag.

Financing Splits and Rate Path Sensitivities

According to DNB Carnegie’s note on 28 September 2026, the lease interest was SEK -50 million in FY2025. This indicates that a significant portion of financing costs is fixed through lease commitments rather than bank debt.
8.
Floating bank debt risk: Bravida's bank debt is 100% floating and denominated entirely in SEK. This exposure makes interest expenses highly sensitive to the rate paths of the Riksbank and Norges Bank. Therefore, rate hikes would directly inflate interest costs in FY2027 and weigh on net margins.

Historical rate impact: Riksbank policy changes historically impacted Bravida's net financial costs during previous rate cycles. Deutsche Bank's report on 30 November 2023 shows that Bravida's net interest cover was 13.8x in FY2021. DNB Carnegie’s report on 23 January 2026 highlights that Instalco's net financial items absorb a large share of EBIT. This suggests that elevated leverage is a sector-wide issue that restricts free cash flow conversion.

Currency Sensitivity and Translation Risks

Bravida’s annual report of 14 April 2025 details foreign net assets of NOK 1,818 million and DKK 469 million, meaning a 10% strengthening of the Swedish krona results in a substantial negative equity adjustment. This translation effect directly impacts reported net income levels when foreign earnings are translated. Thus, a volatile FX path remains a key variable for consensus EPS revisions over the next 18 months.
