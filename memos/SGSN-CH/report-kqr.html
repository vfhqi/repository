Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Legally mandated EU regulations represent a critical driver of high-margin growth for the European testing, inspection, and certification sector over the next three years.
  2. Emerging compliance frameworks like CSRD and CBAM could significantly expand the valuation multiples of leading testing companies if mandatory pricing remains resilient.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Legally mandated testing: "For SGS, Bureau Veritas and Intertek, what share of 2026 revenue and of organic growth comes from work that European Union law requires an accredited independent body to do (the Cyber Resilience Act for class II important and critical products, the Battery Regulation, verification under the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, the Deforestation Regulation and sustainability reporting assurance), and how fast is each part growing? Which of the groups have applied for or won notified-body designation under the Cyber Resilience Act, and do brokers or experts expect a shortage of designated capacity before the December 2027 deadline? Are prices for these mandatory assessments rising while prices for voluntary and standard certificates fall?"
2.
Investment thesis: Legally mandated testing shares represent a structural driver that can insulate Testing, Inspection, and Certification (TIC) companies from macroeconomic cyclicality. Morgan Stanley's report on October 9, 2026 highlights that upcoming EU regulatory requirements will support organic growth decoupling from global GDP. This regulatory push is highly critical because it directly influences whether the sector can maintain high-single-digit organic growth, which would justify premium valuation multiples and raise long-term earnings per share.
3.
Valuation multiples: Investors focus on how regulatory compliance will support premium multiples like the 22x forward P/E historically commanded by the sector. Jefferies' report on September 22, 2026 argues that high exposure to sustainability and connectivity revenue streams justifies valuation multiples near the top of the historical 18x to 25x range. Consequently, if mandatory testing comprises a substantial portion of sales, it will prolong the high-growth earnings window for SGS, Bureau Veritas, and Intertek, thereby directly boosting forward earnings per share.

Therefore, this massive scale is expected to drive high-single-digit organic growth in the Business Assurance divisions, unless client adoption is severely delayed.

This regulation creates a structurally non-discretionary demand profile for accredited testing providers. Manufacturers must use the ENISA Single Reporting Platform to notify authorities of severe security incidents. This strict requirement drives immediate customer-facing advisory demand for digital compliance services. This front-loaded consulting activity will support corporate assurance margins ahead of the final testing deadlines.

Section 4 – Pricing Trends in Mandatory Assessments vs Voluntary Certificates

Section summary
  1. Mandatory EU regulatory assessments command significant pricing premiums over traditional certifications due to high technical complexity and strict compliance barriers.
  2. Standard testing and voluntary certificates face severe commoditization and price discounting because of intense market competition.
  3. We have medium confidence in these pricing dynamics because regional price wars in mandatory inspections can offset complex assessment premiums.

Commoditization of Standard Certifications

1.
High-value digital services: SGS utilizes its specialized digital trust services to capture high-margin, regulatory compliance opportunities. The company reported in its half-year results on July 24, 2026 that double-digit organic growth in Digital Trust was driven by strong cybersecurity demand. This rapid expansion is a core pillar of the group's Strategy 27 plan to scale high-value services. Consequently, expanding these specialized digital trust platforms will protect the group's pricing power and support operating margin targets.
2.
Eroding standard pricing: Voluntary and standard certificates face severe price compression because of low technical differentiation. An expert from UL Solutions noted in September 2026 that standard certification and inspection services have become completely commoditized. This shift means customers simply select the lowest-cost service provider. Consequently, standard testing margins will contract, which will drag down overall earnings per share unless companies rapidly divest these legacy divisions.
3.
Cutthroat electronics competition: Standard electromagnetic compatibility and lighting tests are experiencing intense price discounting. An expert from Intertek Group highlighted in June 2026 that raising prices in these standard electrical segments is extremely difficult. The market for these standardized certifications is cutthroat, so salespeople frequently offer price concessions to win client volume. This persistent price pressure will continue to dilute consumer product testing margins unless Intertek successfully shifts its capacity to high-value connectivity segments.
4.
Standardization volume reliance: Traditional testing laboratories are forced to compete solely on volume as voluntary compliance schemes lose their premium status. Morgan Stanley's chartbook on October 6, 2026 shows that standard consumer testing growth has slowed down, with organic rates failing to match historical double-digit levels. This structural slowdown occurs because basic compliance no longer offers competitive differentiation for manufacturers. Consequently, groups that fail to transition to mandatory regulatory frameworks will face imminent corporate earnings downgrades over the next three years.

High Barriers and Premium Mandatory Fees

5.
Complex scheme premiums: Mandatory environmental and sustainability audits command high price premiums due to strict technical barriers. A former Bureau Veritas vice president stated in February 2026 that specialized sustainability testing permits companies to charge significantly higher fees. These complex audits require specialized knowledge and proprietary methodologies. This pricing power will insulate corporate assurance divisions from broader macroeconomic downturns, which will directly boost operating margins and expand group earnings.
6.
Complex pricing power: Specialized regulatory compliance frameworks allow testing providers to resist client discounting demands. An expert from Bureau Veritas noted in September 2026 that complex sustainability schemes maintain very strong pricing power compared to traditional Quality, Health, Safety, and Environment certifications. While standard schemes face mid-teens discounting, complex certifications remain highly resilient. Consequently, transitioning the sales mix toward these complex frameworks will support stable margin expansion and lift long-term earnings per share.
7.
Mandatory battery premiums: Accredited testing groups leverage their exclusive notified body status to charge premium fees for new European mandates. Intertek announced in July 2026 that it awarded the first batch of compliance certificates under the mandatory EU Battery Regulation. Because Intertek is one of the few designated Notified Bodies capable of conducting stationary battery assessments, it can set high initial prices. This first-mover pricing advantage will accelerate corporate assurance revenue growth and improve consolidated operating margins.
8.
Mandatory carbon verification: The implementation of strict import verifications creates non-discretionary demand that supports robust fee structures. Intertek announced in October 2026 that it became the first organization accredited by SWEDAC to verify emissions under the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism. This accreditation allows Intertek to provide independent verification of actual emissions across all six regulated sectors. This mandatory requirement ensures stable and highly recurring pricing because European importers face severe financial penalties if they fail to provide verified carbon declarations.

Contrary Pressures and Capacity Shocks

9.
Contrary evidence: Mandatory inspection services can suffer from intense local price competition that completely erodes expected premium margins. An expert from Intertek noted in September 2026 that Bureau Veritas is fighting in price for mandatory inspections with small regional TIC companies in Spain. This aggressive pricing behavior indicates that mandatory status does not automatically guarantee premium rates if the local market is highly fragmented. Consequently, localized price wars could undermine the expected regulatory margin expansion for major testing groups.

Auditor labor inflation: High operational cost inflation is restricting the net profitability of premium mandatory compliance divisions. An expert from Bureau Veritas revealed in September 2026 that regional labor cost inflation of 4% to 5% annually regularly outpaces price increases of 1% to 2%. This structural imbalance forces testing companies to continuously improve internal productivity to defend their margins. Unless pricing power in complex schemes increases further, persistent wage inflation will restrict net earnings per share growth.

Buyer market transition: Increased notified body capacity in older regulatory segments is shifting market dynamics and depressing assessment prices. A former SGS Director of Regulatory Operations noted in September 2026 that the medical certification business is transitioning from a seller's market to a buyer's market. This shift occurs as increased testing capacity and potential deregulation trigger severe pricing pressure. Consequently, legacy regulatory segments will experience falling average selling prices which will compress digital trust and certification margins.

Strategic Portfolio Realignment

12.
Active portfolio rotation: Leading testing providers are actively shedding low-margin, commoditized divisions to protect their corporate pricing profiles. Bernstein Research reported on August 27, 2026 that Bureau Veritas is divesting selected sub-sectors within its Agri-Food and Commodities division, including Oil and Petrochemicals. This portfolio cleanup allows the company to exit cyclical end-markets characterized by challenging pricing dynamics. By rotating capital toward higher-value transition services, Bureau Veritas can achieve superior consolidated operating margins and higher earnings.
13.
Other side of the story: While mandatory environmental and cybersecurity regulations support higher initial prices, long-term pricing premiums will eventually decline as these assessment protocols stabilize and attract more competition. A former Bureau Veritas vice president warned in February 2026 that as new regulations stabilize, the subsequent rollout, training, and standard alignment will inevitably lead to the commoditization of the market. This commoditization cycle historically takes five to ten years to materialize. Consequently, the highly profitable pricing window for these mandatory EU frameworks is structurally bounded, meaning testing groups must continuously innovate to sustain high earnings growth.
