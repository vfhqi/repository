Section 2 – Premium Tubulars and Tenaris (TEN-IT) Landscape

Section summary
  1. Premium tubular capacity remains highly constrained by structural deepwater expansion and trade protectionism, driving a predictable multi-year EPS recovery for Tenaris.

DUPOLISTIC PREFERENCE AND SEAMLESS CAPACITY

1.
Dominant supply concentration: Tenaris and Vallourec supply about half of global seamless OCTG demand, excluding China and Russia.
2.
Vast manufacturing footprint: Tenaris maintains an annual production capacity of approximately 8.7 million tons of steel pipes.
3.
Skewed regional demand: Standard API-spec tubes dominate Asian markets, whereas premium tubulars remain concentrated in the Western hemisphere.
4.
Intense premium pricing: High-spec premium tubulars command a significant pricing premium of up to seven times higher than API-OCTG products.
5.
Increasing technical complexity: Deeper wells and complex drilling environments intensify the mechanical demand for high-pressure proprietary connections.
6.
Tightening import supply: US seamless tubular imports have declined by approximately 34% year-on- year, supporting domestic pricing power.
7.
Non-consensus supply shift: While many expect immediate local capacity reactivations, long-lead capital requirements prevent rapid competitive supply additions.

CONTRACT BACKLOG AND EPS INERTIA

8.
Robust backlog visibility: Offshore pipeline commitments, including the Black Sea Sakarya development, provide firm multi-year revenue visibility.
9.
Formidable regional partnerships: Rig Direct service contracts cover around 90% of US and 80% of South American deliveries.
10.
Escalating deepwater demand: TotalEnergies' Suriname campaign has standardized on Dopeless premium connections for all thirty-six wells.
11.
Divergent price expectations: Morgan Stanley models tube realized pricing rising to $3,410 per ton by 2028 on deepwater mix.
12.
Conservative margin model: Redburn estimates realized tube pricing will plateau around $3,277 per ton in 2028 as land activity softens.
13.
Escalating earnings trajectory: Redburn projections show Tenaris's EPS rising from $1.96 to $2.70 by 2028, yielding 25% plus IRR potential.
14.
& Co Redburn Other side of the story: Prolonged domestic hot-rolled coil inflation in North America threatens to erode tubular manufacturing margins if oil major activity stalls.

Section 3 – Subsea Vessels, Jack-Up Rigs, and Saipem Landscape

Section summary
  1. Subsea vessels represent the tightest capacity segment with multi-year backlog visibility, while the jack-up rig market stabilizes as Saudi Aramco resumptions absorb the ex-Middle East oversupply.

SUBSEA CAPACITY TIGHTNESS AND SAIPEM 7 MERGER

1.
Severe capacity constraints: DNB Carnegie reports that limited global capacity in the Subsea, Umbilicals, Risers, and Flowlines segment caps supply, booking major contractors into 2027 or 2028.
2.
Concentrated fleet dominance: Bernstein Research estimates that the combined fleet of 52 construction vessels will command a massive market share, representing approximately 40% of the world's entire rigid pipe-lay fleet.
3.
Consolidated pipe-lay assets: Jefferies indicates that the combined Saipem 7 fleet will feature eight vessels capable of S-lay, J-lay, or Reel-lay rigid pipe installation out of only twenty such high-value units globally.
4.
Expanding revenue runway: Bernstein Research emphasizes that Saipem and Subsea 7 hold a combined 1H26 backlog of 42 billion euros, which secures extensive work scopes stretching into 2028.
5.
Improving fleet utilization: Jefferies highlights that Subsea 7 achieved a robust vessel utilization rate of 87% in the second quarter of 2026, up from 79% in the prior quarter.
6.
Non-consensus margin accretion: Bernstein Research highlights that Subsea 7's backlog continues to increase in quality regarding both terms and margins, contrary to fears of cyclical peaking.

JACK-UP RIG RECOVERY AND DRILLING DYNAMICS

7.
Resilient shallow-water supply: Bernstein Research reports that the global marketed jack-up fleet has contracted to 363 active units, maintaining a tight committed utilization of 89% to 90% as older units retire.
8.
Strategic portfolio realignment: Saipem announced the successful sale of its shallow-water drilling business to ADES for 285 million dollars, reinforcing its pivot to high-complexity deepwater segments.
9.
Protected operational continuity: Morgan Stanley comments that Saipem secured a bareboat charter agreement with ADES to continue operating the Perro Negro 10 jack-up rig under its existing client commitments in Mexico.
10.
Strong backlog accumulation: Borr Drilling reports a solid contract coverage of 73% for 2026 at an average dayrate of 134,000 dollars, driven by 21 new contract commitments year-to-date.
11.
Rising logistics demand: Great Eastern Shipping notes that its offshore vessel commercial utilization improved to 92% in fiscal year 2026, generating a 24% year-on-year revenue increase for its offshore vertical.
12.
Highly visible capacity tightness: High confidence in the structural tightness in subsea vessels and premium jack-ups is anchored by detailed multi-year vessel schedules and contract coverage disclosures.
13.
Other side of the story: DNB Carnegie warns that the premium jack-up market has deteriorated over the last year, creating downward pressure on near-term earnings as consensus models overly optimistic 2027 recoveries.

Section 4 – Shiplifts, Automation Systems, and Nekkar Landscape

Section summary
  1. Global shipyard slot constraints and naval rearmament have created unprecedented demand for advanced marine shiplifts, positioning Nekkar's Syncrolift division as a critical infrastructure gatekeeper.
  2. Intellilift is rapidly commercializing its rig-agnostic software suite, creating a high-margin recurring revenue engine that buffers the lumpiness of commercial shiplift projects.

SYNCROLIFT DEFENCE WIN AND SHIPYARD TIGHTNESS

1.
Severely constrained yard capacity: Noble Capital Markets reports global shipyard slots are largely committed out to 2029 or 2030 across major container, tanker, and LNG vessel programs.
2.
Structural rearmament tailwinds: Zeus Capital argues that multi-year European rearmament pivots focus from simple orders to execution and industrial production constraints.
3.
Dominant niche position: RedEye highlights Syncrolift's commanding market position, holding 60% of global shiplifts and 70% of ship transfer systems.
4.
Naval upgrade pivot: Syncrolift secured a USD 10.5 million Middle East naval modernization contract in June 2026 to rigidify an existing articulated platform.
5.
Commercial backlog expansion: Syncrolift was awarded a EUR 8.5 million contract for a 7,000-tonne shiplift and transfer system in Egypt.
6.
Submarine handling specialization: Nekkar notes Syncrolift remains uniquely protected because it is the only shiplift manufacturer with submarine handling experience.
7.
All-time high pipeline: RedEye notes that while Syncrolift's near-term revenues are volatile, its tender pipeline stands at NOK 8.7 billion.

INTELLILIFT DRILLING AUTOMATION AND SAAS SCALING

8.
SaaS platform scaling: Nekkar's Intellilift grew its live SaaS contracts from two to five rigs during the second quarter of 2026.
9.
Large-scale framework success: The landmark 10-year InteliWell agreement covers eleven offshore rigs and is set to roll out over a 30-month period.
10.
Fleet-wide technological upgrades: Expert insights from a Transocean manager suggest operators willingly pay higher dayrates for automation that delivers first oil faster.
11.
Competitive platform stickiness: Expert insights from an ADES commercial director show high switching costs make digital drilling platforms sticky once adopted.
12.
High-margin division profitability: RedEye models Intellilift's EBITDA margins rising to 39% by 2027 as high-margin SaaS software revenue scales up.
13.
Strategic software collaboration: The InteliWell joint venture incorporates real-time AI solutions from Viasat and drilling assets from Transocean.
14.
Other side of the story: RedEye cautions that near-term award timing delays could still depress group earnings if shipyard newbuilds face further deferrals.

Section 6 – Data gaps

Section summary
  1. While material data gaps in international pricing persist, overall offshore vessel and premium tubular capacity remains highly constrained

STRUCTURAL OPACITY IN PRICING AND MARGINS

1.
Opaque international pricing: Morgan Stanley highlights that wider international OCTG markets remain highly opaque with very limited pricing or supply-demand data available.
2.
Unclear subsea pricing: DNB Carnegie notes that the subsea sector is structurally less transparent on pricing than drilling, making contract margins harder to track.
3.
Opaque margin progression: Bernstein Research indicates that more than sixty percent of subsea project margins are recognized during the final installation phase, creating near-term forecasting opacity.
4.
Hidden contingency releases: DNB Carnegie reports that although subsea projects recognize constant margins via percentage of completion, the actual upside remains concentrated in third-year contingency releases.

PRIVATE CAPACITY AND NAVAL DEFENCE BLIND SPOTS

5.
Undisclosed vessel drawings: Jefferies reports that major operators see no specific newbuild pipelay vessels, but unannounced speculative designs may exist on private drawing boards.
6.
Hidden shipyard schedules: Nekkar's Syncrolift secured a commercial contract with Egypt's South Red Sea Shipyard, but the broader expansion timelines of private yards remain difficult to verify.
7.
Opaque naval procurement: Nekkar highlights that government infrastructure projects and submarine base upgrades have extremely long planning cycles and inherently lower transparency in procurement.
8.
side of the story: If unannounced private capacity plans are more advanced than expected, sudden vessel supply additions could erode the pricing power of listed European operators.

Section 7 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. Uncontrolled Asian shipyard expansion threatens to prematurely resolve the offshore vessel capacity bottleneck by introducing massive speculative new builds.
  2. Failed merger clearances would reintroduce destructive price competition among standalone subsea EPCI contractors, eroding sector margins.

SPECULATIVE VESSEL SUPPLY AND MERGER DISRUPTIONS

1.
Accelerating shipyard deliveries: Chinese yards command the means to expand global supply as they captured over four-fifths of global newbuilding orders in early 2026.
2.
Ambitious shipyard capex: Kawasaki Heavy Industries possesses the motive and opportunity to double domestic construction capacity through a 100 billion yen revitalization program.
3.
Destructive standalone competition: Regulatory vetoes on the Saipem merger would reintroduce aggressive bidding behaviors as uncombined entities struggle to utilize specialized pipe-lay fleets.
4.
Defensive strategic reactions: TechnipFMC retains the motive to execute consolidated transactions to protect its market leadership if the Saipem 7 combination successfully closes.
5.
Outlier: Onshore price wars: Cheap Asian engineering competitors threaten high-complexity onshore projects like urea plants, capping the margins of mature European onshore segments.
