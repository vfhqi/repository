Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Billerud AB's planned Gävle recovery boiler replacement represents a critical capital investment exceeding SEK 3bn that will shape the company's long-term cost competitiveness and operational profile in Region Europe through 2030.

Industrial and Strategic Significance

1.
Strategic inquiry: "What size, timing, phasing and financing is expected for Billerud AB's (BILL-SE) Gävle recovery boiler replacement (stated as above SEK 3bn, decision in 2027, production targeted around 2030, Capital Markets Day December 2024); which technical options and partnerships are being considered; what did the Frövi recovery boiler cost and how was it delivered; and what do brokers and management say about its effect on capex, depreciation, net debt, the dividend and any share buyback from 2027 to 2030?"
2.
Critical infrastructure: Billerud pulp mills depend entirely on solid recovery boilers to act as the chemical and energy recovery hubs for integrated operations. These systems process black liquor to regenerate cooking chemicals and generate high-pressure steam, which directly lowers net operating costs. Upgrading these assets is crucial because efficient chemical recovery reduces caustic purchase costs and optimizes longterm earnings per share.
3.
Sizable investment: Billerud plans the Gävle recovery boiler replacement as its only major capital project in Europe over the medium term. Management estimates that the scale of this project will exceed SEK 3bn and target production around 2030. This massive investment will determine the long-term cost position of Gävle and impact the group's cash flow.
4.
Contrary evidence: none found, and searched company filings, transcripts, and broker research from January 2021 to November 2026.

Financial Implications and Precedents

5.
Strategic shift: Billerud previously scaled back its North American transformation at Escanaba from a SEK 7bn conversion to a SEK 1.4bn upgrade program. This decision frees up significant capital, which directly supports the financing of Gävle boiler without overstretching the balance sheet. Completing Escanaba upgrades by 2027 enables Billerud to redirect its cash flows toward European mill competitiveness and secure future earnings.
6.
Favorable flexibility: Billerud maintains a healthy balance sheet with net debt to EBITDA at 2x as of late 2026. Financial analysts suggest that the company possesses the flexibility to sustain its dividend and buybacks despite the upcoming Gävle outlays. This cash flow resilience means that the boiler replacement will not necessarily disrupt shareholder return policies.
7.
Successful execution: Billerud completed a similar recovery boiler project at its Frövi mill in central Sweden during late 2023. The Frövi project cost SEK 2.6bn and delivered on time and budget despite supply chain disruptions. Analyzing the Frövi precedent helps investors evaluate the operational risks and capital requirements of the larger Gävle installation.
8.
Other side of the story: Billerud faces structural cost pressures in Europe that could complicate the massive Gävle boiler capital commitment. Unforeseen cost overruns or operational downtime at Gävle would severely depress group earnings per share through 2030. Consequently, this project represents a significant strategic hurdle unless European pulpwood costs decline and market margins recover.

Section 2 – Gävle Recovery Boiler: Size, Phasing, and Financing Plans

Section summary
  1. Billerud AB expects its Gävle recovery boiler replacement to exceed SEK 3bn, with a final investment decision in 2027 and production starting around 2030.
  2. The group's disciplined capital allocation, including a reduced North American investment scope, ensures sufficient financial capacity to fund the Gävle boiler replacement.

Strategic Context and Timeline

1.
Operational necessity: Billerud identifies the replacement of the Gävle recovery boiler as a critical long-term priority because the existing boiler is approaching its end of life. Management highlighted during the December 2024 Capital Markets Day that the asset must be replaced to protect the mill's integrated pulp production. This investment is essential to preserve Gävle's annual production capacity of 755 kt of virgin-fibre paper and packaging materials. Resolving this bottleneck will ensure stable long-term pulp selfsufficiency and support future earnings per share.
2.
Clear timeline: Billerud plans to reach a final investment decision on the Gävle recovery boiler replacement in 2027. According to presentation slides from the December 2024 Capital Markets Day, the project represents the only major strategic investment planned for Region Europe. Management intends to use the period leading up to 2027 to explore different technical options and optimize costs. This planned preparation aims to mitigate project execution risks before committing capital.
3.
Targeted execution: Billerud targets commencing production from the new Gävle recovery boiler around the year 2030. Management confirmed during the December 2024 investor presentation that the current operational lifecycle of the old boiler allows for this timeline. Analysts from Jefferies noted in September 2026 that a formal decision is anticipated to align with Billerud's disciplined capital spending. Keeping the timeline focused on 2030 allows the group to avoid near-term operational disruptions at Gävle.
4.
Long-term planning: Billerud has evaluated options for the Gävle pulp supply and recovery boiler systems since at least 2020. During the fourth-quarter 2019 earnings call, management indicated that Gävle was under deep analysis alongside the Frövi boiler. Former executives noted in early 2021 that while Frövi required immediate investment, Gävle's boiler allowed for more preparation time. This extensive preparation indicates that the 2027 decision timeline is the result of systematic asset planning.

Technical and Sizing Parameters

5.
Configuration choices: Billerud is analyzing several technical replacement configurations for Gävle's boiler system. Management stated during the December 2024 Capital Markets Day that the mill currently operates two older boilers. The company is evaluating whether to proceed with a direct one-to-one replacement or an optimized intermediate solution to maximize cost efficiency. This technical choice will directly determine the final capex requirement and the long-term energy self-sufficiency of the Gävle mill.
6.
Sizable outlay: Billerud expects the capital expenditure for the Gävle recovery boiler replacement to exceed SEK 3bn. Financial presentations from the December 2024 Capital Markets Day outline this project as the primary European allocation choice. Broker estimates from Jefferies in October 2025 model the total cost at exactly SEK 3bn. This multi-billion SEK commitment makes the Gävle upgrade the largest European capital project for Billerud over the next decade.
7.
Measured benchmarking: Billerud previously completed a recovery boiler replacement at its Frövi mill in late 2023. Press releases from July 2023 indicated that the Frövi boiler was delivered slightly under its planned budget of SEK 2.6bn. The Gävle boiler's estimated cost of above SEK 3bn reflects its larger scale and capacity requirements compared to Frövi. This historical precedent suggests that Billerud possesses the execution capabilities to deliver major boiler projects successfully.
8.
Capital optimization: Billerud reduced the scope of its North American Evolution program to free up capital for the Gävle boiler. Management abandoned the major Escanaba cartonboard conversion in May 2024, shifting to a modest SEK 1.4bn upgrade. This strategic pivot prevents Billerud from overstretching its financial resources during the 2027 to 2030 period. Consequently, the group can fund Gävle's replacement while maintaining disciplined capital allocation.

Jefferies models show that Gävle strategic outlays will begin ramping up in 2028 at approximately SEK 0.5bn annually. This disciplined base spending is significantly below historical depreciation levels, helping the company preserve cash. Therefore, Billerud can absorb the boiler's strategic capex without elevating its overall investment profile.
11.
Strategic focus: Billerud is prioritizing profitability and capital efficiency over volume growth across its entire operations. CEO Ivar Vatne emphasized in December 2024 that the updated "Way Forward" strategy targets highly disciplined spending. DNB Carnegie highlighted in October 2026 that Scandinavia's elevated wood costs require Billerud to focus on high-return assets. This strategic focus ensures that investing in the Gävle boiler takes precedence over lower-yielding expansion projects.
12.
Regional boundaries: Billerud's strategic capital deployment is strictly partitioned between its European and North American regions. Under the updated 2024 strategy, North American strategic capex is capped at SEK 1.4bn through 2027. DNB Carnegie analysts noted in late 2024 that this modest cap protects Billerud's balance sheet capacity. By limiting North American expansion, the group safeguards the necessary capital required for the Gävle boiler replacement.

Cash-flow generation: Billerud expects its revised financial targets to provide ample liquidity to finance the Gävle replacement internally. Management introduced targets in December 2024 of an EBITDA margin above 15% and cash conversion above 80%. Achieving these goals would generate substantial operating cash flow to cover strategic investments. This internal cash engine minimizes the need for dilutive equity or high-cost external debt.

Section 3 – Technical Options and Vendor Partnerships Under Consideration

Section summary
  1. Billerud AB is evaluating advanced high-efficiency recovery boiler configurations from Andritz and Valmet to optimize steam parameters and chemical recovery at its Gävle mill.
  2. Key technical considerations focus on high steam temperatures up to 515 degrees Celsius and achieving caustic soda recovery rates exceeding 95 percent.

Vendor Landscape and Core Technologies

1.
Promising partnerships: Billerud is exploring high-capacity recovery boiler designs from market leaders Andritz and Valmet to secure its operations at Gävle. While Andritz offers its specialized High Efficiency Recovery Boiler design, Valmet provides tailored modular configurations with advanced automation. Deciding on the vendor is critical because these complex installations are planned to operate for the next forty years with minimal interruptions.
2.
Proven technology: Supplier Andritz presents its proprietary High Efficiency Recovery Boiler technology as a leading option for the Gävle replacement. This design has a strong reference base in Scandinavia, including Billerud's successful installation at the Frövi mill in late 2023. Implementing this technology would optimize black liquor combustion and maximize green electricity generation to lower mill-wide operating costs.
3.
Alternative configurations: Competitor Valmet offers high-power recovery boilers equipped with specialized non- condensable gas handling and advanced emission control systems. Its technical package typically features electrostatic precipitators for dust removal alongside advanced process controls and training simulators. Choosing Valmet would allow Billerud to maximize steam and energy efficiency while meeting stringent European emission standards.
4.
Component integration: Industrial supplier Alfa Laval is also under consideration to provide essential secondary components such as heat exchangers and specialized boilers. The group's extensive thermal transfer portfolio includes highly efficient spiral and tubular heat exchangers that optimize mill-wide heat networks. Incorporating these components helps reduce overall energy losses by recovering waste heat and recycling process water.
5.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie notes that Billerud has not yet announced any official technical partnerships or final equipment specifications for the Gävle boiler as of late 2026. This lack of detail means the strategic preparation remains in an early, flexible stage ahead of the 2027 investment decision. Consequently, the actual capex and operating savings remain highly uncertain for financial models.

Thermodynamic and Chemical Recovery Options

6.
Thermodynamic optimization: Billerud is analyzing advanced steam parameters of up to 110 bar pressure and 515 degrees Celsius for the Gävle boiler. Precedents like the OKI mill demonstrate that these extreme steam data maximize green electricity generation and power-to-heat ratios. Operating at these high parameters would significantly improve Gävle's energy self-sufficiency and lower its dependency on external grid electricity.
7.
Recovery efficiency: Modern recovery boiler systems can achieve chemical recovery efficiency rates for caustic soda that exceed 95 percent. For example, Kuantum Papers utilizes Andritz technology to recover and reuse over 96 percent of its caustic soda from black liquor solids. Achieving this high efficiency at Gävle would substantially reduce fresh chemical purchase costs and boost the mill's operating margins.
8.
Operational safety: Technical options under consideration include advanced ash leaching or crystallization systems to manage non-process elements like chloride and potassium. Valmet's ash crystallizer technology removes these harmful elements to reduce recovery boiler plugging and corrosion. Implementing such systems would lengthen intervals between maintenance shutdowns and optimize the long-term availability of the Gävle mill.
9.
Environmental performance: Integrating multi-fuel or biomass capabilities is a key technical focus to minimize fossil fuel usage during Gävle's boiler start-ups. Billerud's Gruvön mill previously upgraded its burners to allow biooils in its recovery boiler, demonstrating a precedent for fossil-free operation. Utilizing these advanced fuel systems would directly lower direct carbon emissions and align with Billerud's science-based environmental targets.
10.
Other side of the story: Implementing high-temperature and high-pressure steam parameters increases the accumulation of harmful non-process elements like chloride and potassium. If Billerud does not invest in expensive auxiliary ash treatment systems, the Gävle boiler could experience rapid plugging and severe corrosion. This technical risk would compromise overall boiler availability, leading to unscheduled downtime and depressed pulp production.

Section 4 – Precedent Analysis: The Frövi Recovery Boiler Project

Section summary
  1. Billerud AB's successful delivery of the SEK 2.6bn Frövi recovery boiler provides a strong operational template for the upcoming Gävle installation.
  2. The larger Gävle project faces similar execution challenges but requires a larger capital investment exceeding SEK 3bn.

Budget Performance and Phasing

1.
Contextual baseline: This precedent analysis of the Frövi recovery boiler project uses a total of five distinct source documents. The research identified two key precedents, including the Frövi installation and the 2016 boiler breakdown incident, utilizing both company records and broker reports. No relevant historical precedents were excluded from this analysis because all available data directly informs execution risk. This robust baseline ensures a highly representative comparative assessment for the Gävle project.
2.
Successful execution: Billerud completed the Frövi recovery boiler project within the planned budget of SEK 2.6bn. The capital expenditures were distributed across multiple years, starting with SEK 0.4bn in 2021 and SEK 1.1bn in 2022. The final phase required SEK 1.0bn in 2023 and a remaining SEK 0.1bn in 2024. This phased approach suggests that Billerud can manage multi-year capital outlays without disrupting its broader financial commitments.
3.
Capital progression: Billerud scheduled the final Frövi boiler payment of SEK 100m for 2024 to conclude the strategic capital cycle. Management then established a group base capital expenditure target of SEK 2.2bn annually starting in 2024. This shift to baseline spending demonstrates how completing the boiler freed up financial resources for other business areas.
4.
Comparative execution: Billerud successfully managed the Frövi project timeline even as North American operations faced unexpected disruptions. While the Escanaba mill was temporarily closed due to a blastomycosis outbreak in early 2023, the European region kept Frövi on schedule. This regional operational separation protected the boiler's progression and insulated the European business from domestic headwinds.
5.
Contrary evidence: Broker Jefferies highlights that while the Frövi project was successful, major capital expenditures historically muted Billerud's free cash flow generation. The analyst noted that future cash flows would continue to lag earnings due to the heavy strategic spending required for these installations. This perspective indicates that even on-budget delivery can constrain corporate cash flow during intense investment cycles.

Construction Delivery and Operation

6.
Efficient management: The construction of the Frövi boiler involved 1.2 million working hours executed by 2,500 people. Billerud initiated the project assembly in April 2021 and successfully put the boiler into production by September 2023. The operational handover from the supplier to the mill line organization was officially completed in November 2023. These timeline milestones demonstrate that Billerud possesses strong project execution capabilities under complex circumstances.
7.
Advanced technology: Billerud partnered with Andritz to install high-efficiency recovery systems at the Frövi facility. The installation included a highdensity concentrator that raised black liquor dry solids from 72% to 82% to maximize steam efficiency. Despite supply chain bottlenecks during the war in Ukraine, the concentrator launched on schedule in October 2022. This technical setup provides a reliable template for optimizing the future Gävle boiler parameters.
8.
Regulatory pressure: Billerud committed to the Frövi recovery boiler replacement primarily because its existing environmental permit was expiring in 2023. The historical boiler, supplied in the 1960s, could not comply with modern emission standards or support future expansion. Investing in modern, fossilfree systems allowed the mill to secure its long-term license to operate.
9.
Severe disruptions: Billerud previously faced substantial operational risks when the historical Frövi recovery boiler failed in late 2016. The unexpected breakdown forced a premature shutdown that reduced board machine capacity by up to 70% as the mill relied on expensive external pulp. Repair costs and lost volumes negatively impacted quarterly earnings by up to SEK 100m. This historical precedent underscores why timely boiler replacements are critical to prevent catastrophic downtime and safeguard operating margins.
10.
Technical maintenance: Modern recovery boilers operating at high temperatures require advanced systems to prevent rapid fouling and corrosion. Operating without proactive chloride and potassium removal plants can cause unplanned shutdowns and increase chemical costs. Incorporating these auxiliary treatment systems will be essential for Gävle's availability and long-term boiler lifetime.

Scale Comparison and Optimization

11.
Escalating scale: The proposed Gävle recovery boiler replacement is structurally larger and more expensive than the Frövi project. While Frövi has an annual production capacity of 500 kt, the Gävle mill operates at a larger scale of 775 kt. Consequently, the capital estimate for Gävle exceeds SEK 3bn, compared to the SEK 2.6bn spent at Frövi. The increased scale means Gävle will require greater capital allocation flexibility through 2030.
12.
Continuous optimization: Billerud focused on resolving post-commissioning environmental parameters at the Frövi mill after the handover. In 2025, engineers implemented optimization measures on the new boiler specifically to reduce nitrogen oxide emissions. This post-startup calibration highlights that complex recovery boilers require active long-term technical management even after successful physical delivery.
13.
Operational readiness: Billerud mitigated commissioning risks at Frövi by initiating operator training well in advance of the start-up. The mill utilized Andritz Swedish-language e-learning platforms and simulator technology to prepare production teams. This comprehensive preparation shortened the learning curve and minimized costly post-startup downtime during commissioning.
14.
Energy efficiency: The fossil-free Frövi recovery boiler operates on biofuels to minimize the mill's carbon footprint. The integration of the Andritz concentrator allows the mill to increase steam and electricity generation. Achieving these energy gains at Frövi shows how the Gävle project could substantially lower operating costs once operational.
15.
Other side of the story: Broker Jefferies notes that Billerud's prior capital estimates faced upward revision risks due to industry-wide asset inflation. The broker historically modeled the Frövi boiler at SEK 2.5bn before the final cost settled at SEK 2.6bn. If similar structural inflation impacts Sweden's construction sector before 2027, the Gävle project may exceed its current budget of SEK 3bn and depress mid-term returns.
