US Retail Scanner and Sales Performance

1.
Sequential improvement: Recent retail scanner data shows that Rémy Cointreau products are experiencing a slow moderation in their US sales declines. Morgan Stanley reported that the company's US off-trade sales fell by only 0.9% for the four weeks ending November 14, 2026. This represents a solid step up from the deeper 2.8% decline recorded over the longer twelve-week period. This short-term trend suggests that distributor inventory corrections are slowly easing ahead of the holiday season.
2.
Persistent weakness: Broader market indicators confirm that the US cognac category is still facing structural and cyclical headwinds. Deutsche Bank reported that the overall US spirits market remains soft with Rémy Cointreau sales down 1.1% over the latest twelve weeks. This is slightly better than the company's 4.7% decline over the last 52 weeks. The persistent weakness means that legacy distillers must continue to rely on heavy promotion to support volumes.
3.
Divergent pricing: Retail pricing metrics demonstrate a clear difference between Rémy Cointreau and its primary category rivals. Jefferies tracked the average retail price per unit of Rémy Martin at 35.39 dollars, which is the highest price point among the top five US cognac brands. In comparison, the market leader Hennessy has an average unit price of 27.34 dollars. Maintaining this premium pricing threshold is a key risk because pricesensitive consumers may trade down to cheaper alternatives.
4.
Pronounced discounting: Competitors are using aggressive pricing strategies to capture market share in the premium spirits category. Jefferies reported that Courvoisier is positioned as the most affordable major competitor with an average price of 21.59 dollars per unit. Meanwhile, D'Ussé and Martell are priced at 31.73 dollars and 30.54 dollars respectively. This intense discounting by peers places significant pressure on organic margins for Rémy Martin VSOP.

This distribution indicates that Rémy Martin has retained its secondary market position despite severe distributor destocking.

While these sequential declines are less severe than in previous quarters, they are still holding back shipment growth. This suggests that wholesaler shipments will continue to lag behind retail depletions until retail volume demand fully recovers.

Brand outperformance: Non-cognac spirits in the company's portfolio are demonstrating superior resilience compared to the core cognac offerings. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported that the company's results presentation outlined several initiatives to gain market share in the US. Long-Term Outlook and Channel Stabilization

13.
Competitive intensity: Competitive pressure from international rivals threatens to limit the brand's long-term operating margins. Rothschild and Co Redburn warned that major competitors like Diageo and Pernod Ricard are also leveraging innovation to expand profitability. Because of this high competitive intensity, the broker forecasts a 20% operating margin by 2032. This is lower than the company's own target operating margin of 22% or more.

Sequential normalization: Wholesaler destocking is slowly showing signs of reaching a bottom after multiple quarters of aggressive correction. Rémy Cointreau reported that its US inventory coverage remained stable at approximately four months at the end of the second quarter. The resulting market battle will require substantial promotional investment from all industry participants.

Other side of the story: The positive depletion trends reported by management may be temporary and fail to translate into sustained shipment growth. Bernstein Research reported that the critical US cognac market remains in modest sellout decline. If the US holiday season underperforms, wholesalers are highly likely to resume aggressive destocking to right-size inventories.
