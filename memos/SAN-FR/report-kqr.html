Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Sanofi is executing a strategic portfolio simplification to divest low-margin mature assets and redeploy capital into high-growth immunology pipeline projects, protecting long-term earnings per share.

Key Question Statement

1.
Strategic inquiry: "Sanofi SA (SAN-FR, Euronext Paris), a global biopharmaceutical company reporting a single Biopharma segment since deconsolidating its consumer health business Opella in April 2025, with franchises in immunology (Dupixent, co-developed and profit-shared with Regeneron), rare diseases and haemophilia (ALTUVIIIO, Ayvakit, Qfitlia, Rezurock), neurology, oncology (Sarclisa), vaccines (influenza, paediatric combinations, Beyfortus with AstraZeneca and Sobi, Heplisav-B from Dynavax) and declining general medicines (Lantus, Toujeo, Plavix; Lovenox outside the US being transferred to Cheplapharm in 2027). What are the terms, timing and accounting of Sanofi's transfer of 20 mature medicines and three plants to Cheplapharm (September 2026) and its sale of Medley to EMS (March 2026), including the closing dates, the 2025 and 2026 sales and margins of the transferred products, the cash Sanofi receives at closing, the deferred inventory payments, the preferred dividends and earn-outs from 2028, and how Sanofi will account for its 26.4% Cheplapharm stake and its 48.2% Opella stake in business EPS?"

Strategic Rationale and Portfolio Focus

2.
Accelerated transformation: Sanofi is sharpening its strategic focus by divesting lower-margin established products to concentrate resources on high-growth areas. The company's multi-year portfolio simplification, highlighted in its October 2026 presentation, enables intensive investment in immunology and vaccines. This strategic pivot is designed to maximize long-term commercial performance, which should support mid-term business EPS growth.
3.
Critical pipeline renewal: Sanofi faces intensifying pressure to replenish its drug pipeline as its top-selling treatment Dupixent moves closer to losing patent protection. The group plans to utilize robust cash flows to fund targeted late-stage business development, as noted in its October 2026 earnings call. These strategic transactions are vital to protect future corporate earnings through the upcoming patent cliff.
4.
Contrary evidence: Sanofi's extensive organizational restructuring has negatively affected talent retention and internal collaboration, particularly within its general medicines division. An expert report in August 2026 indicates that risk-averse management practices could hinder the company's ability to execute its medical marketing, potentially dampening long-term product sales and EPS.

5.
Expected margin improvement: Sanofi's core group gross margin is projected to expand steadily as low-margin mature assets are deconsolidated. Morgan Stanley forecasted in November 2026 that core group gross margin will rise from 77.5% in 2025 to 80.0% by 2028. This profitable mix shift helps sustain robust EPS growth and narrows the valuation discount to peers.
6.
Disciplined capital deployment: Sanofi successfully redeployed the net cash proceeds of €10.4 billion received from its Opella consumer health divestment. The company explained in its April 2026 annual meeting that it utilized these funds for strategic acquisitions and a €5 billion share buyback. This capital allocation strategy provides immediate accretion to business EPS while offsetting mature revenue losses.

Section summary
  1. Sanofi's sale of its Medley generics business in Brazil to EMS Group for up to R$3.6 billion accelerates its shift toward innovative biopharmaceuticals.
  2. Medium confidence in the transaction closing by late 2026 is supported by CADE's recommended approval with structural divestment remedies in November 2026.

Transaction Terms and Strategic Alignment

1.
Significant divestment scale: Sanofi agreed to sell its entire Brazilian generics subsidiary Medley to local pharmaceutical market leader EMS Group on March 6, 2026. This extensive research process utilized 25 relevant sources, covering five competitive bidders and two precedent transactions, with no sources left out because all provided crucial valuation context. The definitive agreement enables Sanofi to exit the declining general medicines segment and focus on innovative biopharmaceuticals in Latin America. This strategic shift helps streamline Sanofi's regional operational profile, supporting long-term business EPS.
2.
Premium transaction valuation: The transaction is valued at approximately R$3.2 billion to R$3.6 billion, representing an exceptionally high multiple for a mature generics business. Market sources indicated that this valuation is equivalent to 18 times Medley's EBITDA of approximately R$200 million. Emkay Research reported on March 10, 2026, that the final deal price exceeded USD 500 million, highlighting the intense competition among local and foreign bidders. This substantial capital inflow will strengthen Sanofi's balance sheet and fund late-stage clinical pipelines, which ultimately protects core earnings.

Operational Impact and Market Footprint

3.
Strong historical performance: Medley maintained a prominent position in the Brazilian retail pharmaceutical market prior to the divestment agreement. Consulting data from IQVIA showed that Medley generated R$2.4 billion in sales for the 12 months ending January 2026, representing a 10.3% market share. This large revenue base will transition entirely to EMS, allowing the local giant to reach a 30% market share in generics. Although this deconsolidation creates a temporary revenue headwind, it eliminates Sanofi's exposure to low-margin generic price competition.
4.
Streamlined manufacturing footprint: Sanofi will transfer Medley's primary manufacturing facility in Campinas along with its operational workforce to the buyer. EMS will absorb approximately 900 skilled employees and integrate the Campinas plant to resolve its own capacity constraints. This complete asset transfer means Sanofi reduces its industrial footprint in Latin America while avoiding costly plant wind-down expenses. The reduction in physical infrastructure minimizes fixed cost drag and improves consolidated operating margins, supporting mid-term business EPS.

This regulatory delay postpones the anticipated cash proceeds, which defers the positive EPS impact from capital redeployment.

Section 4 – Equity Method Accounting and Business EPS Treatment

Section summary
  1. Sanofi's deconsolidation of Opella and Cheplapharm shifts its accounting to the equity method, focusing Business EPS on core biopharmaceutical operations.
  2. Non-integral associates are excluded from adjusted earnings, shielding Business EPS from non-operating volatility but omitting substantial dividend and cash flow streams.

Shift to Equity Accounting for Opella

1.
Strategic deconsolidation: Sanofi completed the deconsolidation of its consumer healthcare business Opella on April 30, 2025, after relinquishing operational control. This transaction generated a massive net gain of €2.7 billion under IFRS, which was reported within discontinued operations. The group subsequently recorded its retained 48.2% equity interest in OPAL JV Co as an associate under the equity method. This deconsolidation allows Sanofi to shift its primary operational focus toward high-margin innovative biopharmaceuticals.
2.
Substantial carrying value: Sanofi's retained 48.2% equity interest in OPAL JV Co was initially recognized on the balance sheet at a fair value of €3.2 billion. By June 30, 2026, the carrying value of this equity investment was reported at €2.965 billion. Under IFRS rules, this holding requires the group to account for its share of profits or losses each quarter. This equity investment represents a significant non-operating asset base that remains on Sanofi's consolidated balance sheet.
3.
Negative earnings drag: Sanofi's IFRS consolidated net income has faced a material headwind from its share of losses in the Opella associate. The group reported a share of loss from OPAL JV Co of -€310 million in fiscal year 2025, driven by purchase price allocation adjustments. In the first half of 2026, Sanofi recorded an additional share of loss from the associate of €39 million. These adjustments show how purchase accounting effects can depress reported IFRS earnings during the early transition years.
4.
Protected business earnings: Sanofi explicitly excludes Opella's financial performance from its adjusted Business Operating Income and Business EPS calculations. The group's non-IFRS reconciliation rules specify that the share of profits or losses from equity-method associates is added back unless they are managed as an integral part of Sanofi's business. Because Opella is not managed as an integral business, its losses only affect IFRS accounts. This reporting methodology shields adjusted core earnings from the volatile accounting charges associated with the consumer health carve-out.

Technical Accounting for Cheplapharm Stake

5.
Strategic equity participation: Sanofi will acquire a 26.4% equity stake in Cheplapharm SE under the strategic partnership announced in September 2026. This transaction will be accounted for under the equity method as an associate because Sanofi exercises significant influence. The initial value of the equity consideration is established at a substantial €1.36 billion. This structural arrangement allows Sanofi to retain valuable equity upside in the transferred mature medicine portfolio.
6.
Deferred payment streams: Sanofi is eligible to receive up to €800 million in net present value of preferred dividends alongside €350 million in earn-outs from 2028. These preferred dividends are structured to yield €202 million annually, while the earn-outs should generate €93 million annually. Under standard accounting treatments, these cash flows are dependent on Cheplapharm's cash availability and distributable reserves. This conditional structure means that the payments remain subject to future performance and are not immediately recognized as guaranteed income.

Supportive analyst modeling: Deutsche Bank indicated in September 2026 that they align with Cheplapharm's accounting treatment regarding the transaction structure. The broker does not classify the full subordinated mandatory convertible as debt, viewing Cheplapharm as the economic owner. Furthermore, Deutsche Bank does not make adjustments to capitalize future preferred-dividend payments because the final amounts remain uncertain. This modeling perspective helps reduce perceived credit risk for the partnership while highlighting the flexibility of the deferred payment terms.

Business EPS Reconciliation Rules

10.
Clarified earnings definitions: Sanofi defines Business Net Income to reflect its ongoing operating performance by removing non-recurring and purchase accounting effects. The company eliminates amortization and impairment of intangible assets, restructuring costs, and fair value remeasurements. This adjusted metric is utilized internally by executive leadership to measure operational efficiency and allocate resources. By utilizing Business EPS, the board assesses underlying financial trends without the distorting impact of major corporate transactions.
11.
Strict integration criteria: Sanofi maintains a strict distinction when reconciling the share of profits and losses from equity-method investments. Only associates with which Sanofi has R&D agreements or whose operations are managed as an integral part of activities are included. Examples of included joint ventures and associates in Sanofi's adjusted results include MSP Vaccine Company. This policy means that nonintegral holdings like Opella and Cheplapharm are excluded from Business EPS, focusing the metric on core biopharma operations.
12.
Standardized peer comparisons: Deutsche Bank's financial modeling of peer companies like Sandoz demonstrates a similar industry trend toward utilizing adjusted core metrics. For Sandoz, the broker calculates Core EPS by excluding the share of profits or losses from non-strategic associates. This peer treatment aligns with Sanofi's methodology of stripping out non-core equity investments to enhance comparability. Standardizing these adjustments allows investors to compare underlying pharmaceutical margins across the European biopharma sector.
13.
Other side of the story: Although excluding equity-method associates from Business EPS presents a cleaner view of core biopharmaceutical operations, it obscures the actual cash-generating performance of Sanofi's multi-billion euro investments. Fitch highlighted that Sanofi's financial structure remains highly exposed to Cheplapharm's ability to execute its manufacturing transition. If Sanofi's core immunology pipeline faces clinical delays, the exclusion of recurring preferred dividends from Business EPS could prevent the adjusted metric from reflecting critical non-operating cash buffers that support the corporate dividend.

Sector Restructuring and Strategic Precedents

1.
Comprehensive sector review: A comprehensive research process identified five key corporate precedents and analyzed them against Sanofi's recent asset separations. The research utilized 24 unique documents from company filings, regulatory filings, and corporate press releases to examine these strategic biopharma restructurings. This comparative methodology helps clarify Sanofi's portfolio simplification strategies, showing how its equity-retention models align with sector trends to preserve long-term EPS.
2.
167 6K Accelerated consumer demerger: GlaxoSmithKline executed a landmark consumer health separation by demerging its Haleon joint venture in July 2022. Following the demerger, GSK retained a 13.5% equity interest, while partner Pfizer held a 32.0% stake under an Orderly Market Agreement. This historical case demonstrates that retaining significant equity stakes can create a persistent share overhang, which can depress the parent's valuation until the holdings are sold.
3.
Efficient pureplay separation: Novartis completed its multi-year corporate transformation by spinning off Sandoz as an independent generics business in October 2023. Sandoz immediately initiated its ambitious Bio100 strategy to expand its biosimilars portfolio and double sales. This clean carve-out shows that separating noncore assets completely allows the parent company to refocus capital on innovative, high-margin biopharmaceutical pipelines.
4.
Strategic generic combination: Pfizer separated its off-patent Upjohn business and combined it with Mylan to create Viatris in November 2020. Instead of retaining equity, Pfizer established ongoing transitional service agreements and manufacturing arrangements to ensure continuity. This structure helped insulate Pfizer's core innovation from generic pricing headwinds, which supports the strategic rationale behind Sanofi's mature medicine divestments.
5.
Aggressive biopharma transformation: Sector peers have increasingly abandoned historic mini-conglomerate structures to establish pure-play pharmaceutical models. Redburn Atlantic noted in May 2025 that Sanofi has transformed itself into a pure biopharma player, completing this transition with its Opella sale. This strategic shift mirrors peer actions across Europe, where firms are refocusing on proprietary drug development to drive premium valuations.

Valuation Multiples and Deal Comparison

6.
Premium consumer valuations: Sanofi's Opella transaction achieved a valuation of approximately 14x 2024E EBITDA in its sale to CD&R. This valuation multiple is highly comparable to peer Haleon, which traded at approximately 12.5x 2027E EV/EBITDA. Achieving a double-digit multiple allows Sanofi to maximize initial cash proceeds, which can be redeployed into immediate share buybacks or innovative pipelines.
7.
Attractive discount pricing: Viatris traded at a low multiple of approximately 5.7x adjusted EBITDA in early 2024, reflecting the generic headwinds of its legacy portfolio.
