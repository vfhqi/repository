Section 8 – Quality of Bear Evidence

The bear case against Gimv NV is characterized by an almost complete absence of published short-seller reports, marking it as fundamentally distinct from typical bear campaigns targeting listed private equity firms. No hostile activist letters exist, as WorxInvest acted as a friendly strategic anchor investor rather than an adversarial shareholder. This structural void of formal short activism forces the quality assessment to rely entirely on indirect bearish evidence drawn from sell-side skepticism, governance concerns, and operational execution risks rather than the coordinated, research-intensive allegations typical of dedicated short campaigns.

The hard filings-based evidence centers on three core structural weaknesses: the capital increase that diluted NAV and created temporary earnings headwinds from cash drag, historical total shareholder returns that lag the European private equity median, and high operating expenses that historically dragged on returns. These claims are directly verifiable through IFRS financial statements and represent the strongest foundation of the bear thesis.

The EUR 247 million cash proceeds from the rights issue continue to depress short-term return on equity, with over €400 million in idle cash creating persistent drag. The valuation evidence is similarly concrete, with Gimv trading at a steep discount to NAV approaching financial crisis peak levels, though this metric reflects market sentiment rather than intrinsic portfolio deterioration.

Pattern-based allegations dominate the governance and related-party risk category, where evidence quality degrades significantly. The Cegeka transaction generated zero NAV uplift, with the transfer of 25% to WorxInvest executed at book value, creating legitimate questions about transfer pricing but lacking the forensic depth to prove value destruction definitively. The departure of Smart Industries head Tom Van de Voorde creates nearterm platform execution risks, representing a key-man concern that is fundamentally speculative until successor performance can be measured.

The assertion that AI-enabled due diligence might fail to identify portfolio weaknesses remains entirely hypothetical without concrete implementation failures.

Subsequent events have delivered a mixed validation record for bearish claims. The pandemic valuation shock triggered write-downs leading to a negative portfolio return of 10.3% for FY20, but management responded immediately with strict liquidity management and flexible contingency planning, demonstrating reactive resilience rather than structural failure. Management neutralized inflationary pressures in 2H23 through effective pricing policies, and Gimv insulated portfolio companies from rate hikes by restricting average leverage to 1.8x EBITDA, directly refuting claims of unmanageable cost pressures.

However, persistent cash drag from the EUR 247 million capital increase lagged initial deployment expectations, with sell-side analysts revising target prices down to EUR 38.90 from EUR 47, validating the return dilution argument.

Benchmarking the bear case coherence against European private equity peers reveals significant structural weaknesses. Gimv exhibits extremely low foreign currency exposure with unhedged non-EUR assets representing under 4% of equity, while peer Sofina faces massive transaction risk with unhedged US dollar assets comprising 37% of total investments, and 3i Group operates with EUR-denominated assets comprising 74% of the portfolio.

This positions Gimv as a low-complexity outlier where currency risk arguments carry minimal weight compared to peers facing genuine structural FX exposure. The quantified downside scenario projects a 15% write-down across unlisted assets with portfolio company EBITDA growth dropping to negative 5%, contracting the average valuation multiple from 9.7x to 8.5x EBITDA, resulting in direct portfolio fair value falling by EUR 241 million and reducing estimated NAV to EUR 48 per share from EUR 57.2, but this remains a modeled stress scenario rather than observed impairment.

The most diagnostic weakness in bear evidence quality emerges from the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses Matrix, where the FALSE FRIEND profile emerges as winner with the absolute fewest hard inconsistencies, while the YES profile has three hard inconsistencies including elevated leverage and persistent operating cash drains, and the NO profile has three hard inconsistencies because portfolio growth and exits remain active.

This reveals that the bear case lacks internal coherence for an immediate collapse narrative, instead suggesting a slower deterioration masked by optical NAV stability. The quality verdict must therefore conclude that while specific structural concerns around cash deployment efficiency, governance opacity, and key-man risk are well-founded and filings-verifiable, the broader bear thesis suffers from an absence of coordinated research, limited forensic depth on related-party transactions, and a weak track record of predicting actual portfolio write-downs.
