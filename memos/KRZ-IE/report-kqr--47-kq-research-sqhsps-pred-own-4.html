Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary

What do brokers and market forecasters expect for the euro against the US dollar and against Kerry Group plc's (KRZ-IE) main emerging-market currencies (including the Egyptian pound, Turkish lira, Brazilian real, Mexican peso, Malaysian ringgit and Chinese renminbi) for 2027 and 2028? What currency translation does each broker covering Kerry assume in its FY2027 and FY2028 revenue and EPS forecasts? How did Kerry's reported EPS compare with its constant-currency EPS in each year from 2015 to 2026?"

Strategic Pivot and Risk Realignment

2.
Strategic transformation: Kerry Group finalized the divestment of its 70% shareholding in Kerry Dairy Ireland on 31 December 2024 to become a focused taste and nutrition supplier. This structural pivot removes the lowgrowth dairy processing business and reshapes the company into a higher-margin player. By eliminating the capital-intensive dairy segment, the company elevates its baseline EBITDA margins while concentrating resources on its high-value B2B portfolio. Consequently, the group expects this shift to drive high-single-digit constant-currency adjusted EPS growth going forward.
3.
Enhanced profitability: The completion of the dairy divestment delivers an immediate 140 basis points step-up to Kerry Group's consolidated EBITDA margin, moving the 2024 continuing baseline to 17.1%. This structural margin expansion allows the company to target a refreshed EBITDA margin of 18% to 19% by 2026 and 19% to 20% by 2028. Management expects to achieve these aggressive mid-term goals primarily through its Accelerate 2.0 operational excellence initiative. This margin improvement shifts the investment thesis toward high-value B2B ingredients pricing and specialty nutrition.
4.
Contrary evidence: Although the exit from dairy processing was designed to streamline the group's operations, Jefferies research notes that the divestment reduces overall EBITDA estimates by approximately 1% over the next three years. This reduction highlights that the strategic transition brings initial earnings dilution despite the positive long-term margin evolution. Investors must weigh this structural dilution against the anticipated valuation multiple compression versus pure-play peers.

Section summary

Mathematical Sensitivity and Reserve Impacts

1.
Quantified exposure: Kerry Group's official financial disclosures confirm that a 5% euro strengthening against key currencies results in a multi-million euro reduction in reserves. Specifically, the company's 2025 Annual Report shows that a 5% strengthening of the euro against the US dollar and sterling would cause translation reserve losses of €98.1m and €20.5m, respectively. Combining these figures yields a cumulative reserve reduction of EUR 118.6m, validating the mathematical sensitivity benchmark. This reserve volatility reflects the scale of Kerry's net assets held in non-euro jurisdictions.
2.
Minimal transaction impact: Kerry Group maintains a highly localized operating model that reduces transactional risk, leaving translation as the dominant currency exposure. The company's 2025 financial statements indicate that a 5% weakening of the US dollar and sterling would reduce consolidated profit after taxation by only €0.8m. This tiny impact highlights that currency translation represents over 95% of net EPS sensitivity relative to transaction risks. Therefore, reported EPS is highly sensitive to accounting translation but largely insulated from operational cash flow disruption.
3.
Volatile translation reserves: Kerry Group's translation reserve experiences large year-on-year movements driven by the shifting valuation of its overseas operating assets. Annual report disclosures show that the movement in the US dollar from $1.04 at the end of 2024 to $1.18 at the end of 2025 was the primary driver of translation reserve volatility. Consequently, these exchange differences are taken directly to the translation reserve within equity. This accounting mechanism buffers the profit and loss statement from immediate asset revaluation shocks.
4.
Systematic consolidation effects: Kerry Group translates the financial statements of its foreign subsidiaries using average and closing exchange rates for consolidation into euros. According to the company's base listing particulars, monthly average rates are used for income statement items, while closing rates are applied to balance sheets. Decreases in the value of functional currencies against the euro directly reduce the translated value of foreign earnings, creating headwinds. This structural exposure remains a permanent feature of Kerry's global business-to-business taste model.
