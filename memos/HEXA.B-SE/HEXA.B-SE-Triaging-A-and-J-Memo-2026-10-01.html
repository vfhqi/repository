[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Hexagon is a self-help case with a cyclical tailwind: a compelling business change at an uncompelling price. In fourteen months a new chief executive has changed the people, the structure, the profit metric, the perimeter and the cost base, and the outputs have turned; at 98.56 SEK (2026-09-30) the shares, at 24.1 times earnings 24 months forward, already pay for that change and for the 2030 targets being met, so the modal return is about nil over eighteen months and about 4 percent a year over three years, and the case clears the 20 percent bar only below about 75 SEK.

  • The change is internal, broad and already delivered.
    • An activist-prompted board replaced the chief executive (Anders Svensson, from 20 July 2025) and then the chairman (Björn Rosengren, from the April 2026 AGM, after 26 years of Ola Rollén), and the new chief executive's record at Konecranes is of exactly this playbook.
    • Four fifths of the executive team changed
      • A matrix organisation became seventeen divisions with their own profit and loss
      • A 74 million EUR cost programme reached a 73.5 million EUR run-rate by Q2 2026, two quarters early.
    • The Design and Engineering software unit was sold to Cadence for 2.7 billion EUR on 23 February 2026
      • The Octave enterprise software business was spun off on 22 May 2026
      • Net debt fell from 3,356 million EUR at end-2025 to 483 million EUR at end-June 2026.
    • An unmet 30 percent-plus EBIT margin target was replaced by a 24 to 26 percent EBITAC target for 2030, and EBITAC, which deducts capitalised R&D, replaced EBIT1 as the profit metric
      • The sell-side calls the new targets "highly achievable".
    • Nine of the twelve internal forces the framework lists are present and strong, against a decision rule of three
      • The one that moved the wrong way is guidance, which was lowered to a reachable level rather than raised.
  • The external forces net to a modest tailwind that is cyclical in its largest parts.
    • Defence and anti-jamming demand, near-shoring of advanced manufacturing in North America and the electronics capital-spending cycle carried the core to 12 percent organic growth in Q2 2026 against a 6.3 percent consensus.
    • Those forces are small in the mix (defence slightly over 5 percent of sales; electronics 11 percent of Manufacturing Intelligence)
      • The largest end-markets, automotive, European construction and Chinese infrastructure, are still contracting.
    • The company's own cycle troughed in Q1 2025 (organic growth 0 percent; a profit warning) and has turned: 3 percent in Q4 2025, 8 percent in Q1 2026, 12 percent in Q2 2026
      • The turn is two quarters old.
    • Currency is unhedged by policy: a 10 percent adverse US dollar move is calculated at 150 basis points of operating margin.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up, and the change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the people and the choices, in the actions, in the key operating metrics (organic growth 8 and 12 percent), in the financial statements (Q2 2026 EBITAC 244.7 million EUR, 10 percent above consensus, at a 23.3 percent margin; cash conversion 149 percent) and, for one quarter, in the sell-side's estimates (FactSet mean earnings per share up 6.5 to 7.6 percent over three months to 2026-09-25).
    • The one output moving the wrong way is gross margin: 62.3 percent in Q2 2026, down 170 basis points, half currency and tariff, half mix as hardware outgrows software.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of about 0.310 EUR in FY2026 compounding at about 10 percent a year to about 0.414 EUR in FY2029, against consensus of 0.313, 0.340 and 0.365 EUR for FY2026 to FY2028.
    • The growth is revenue-led (organic growth about 5.5 percent a year plus the Waygate step) and margin-neutral at group level, because Waygate's 10 percent EBIT1 margin offsets the core's expansion
      • It sits just below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
  • What it costs is the whole case. 98.56 SEK is 24.1 times consensus earnings 24 months forward (23.0 times on our own), the 82nd percentile of the stock's ten-year range and the 86th of its five-year range, a range earned when about 40 percent of the group's revenue was recurring
    • The core's recurring share is 28 percent.
    • The twenty software peers the stock is filed with trade at a median of about 14 times
      • The industrial-technology peers the research prefers trade at 13 to 18 times EV/EBIT
      • The sell-side's own sum of the parts at 18 times EV/EBIT gives SEK 85.
  • The return does not clear the bar at any multiple the evidence supports.
    • At the ten-year median of about 20 times, the shares are worth about 98.3 SEK including dividends on 2028-04-01 (about -0.2 percent a year) and about 111.5 SEK on 2029-10-01 (about 4.2 percent a year).
    • Even with today's 24.1 times held, the return is 12.9 percent a year over eighteen months and 10.6 percent a year over three years, a D
      • At the 'defendable' 22 times, 6.3 and 7.4 percent a year.
      • The re-rating component is the swing factor, and it is negative.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITAC to earnings per share takes about 7 percent off FY2028 earnings and leaves the rating where it is
      • The adverse case is a drawdown, not a different verdict.
  • The chain holds through five layers and into the sixth, and breaks at the return.
    • Nothing in the business argues against the change
    • The price does.
    • The shares rose 20.8 percent in the three months to 2026-09-30 after the Q2 print, so the price has moved before the park, not against it.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', fitting at B, because the input breadth passes by a wide margin and the outputs are translating, and no other 'Target Setup' fits
    • The fit is not crisper because the earnings trajectory is about 10 percent a year rather than major, and because the pattern's payoff has been taken before the change finished.

  • The case therefore is a good business, well run for the first time in years, that we would own below about 75 SEK

    • At 98.56 SEK we are not betting on it, because the bet would be that the market goes on paying the software-rich Hexagon's multiple for the hardware-heavier one it has become.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material in the business and in the outputs (both 'Change Forces' and 'Financial Outputs' rate B) but does not drive a sufficient return (the 'Modal return' rates F) at this price

    • The Building Block's question includes the return, and the return fails.
    • Not D, because a D would say the change itself is not material, and it is the broadest internal reset in the universe we have rated
    • Not B, because a thesis that returns about 4 percent a year at the modal multiple is not a material case for capital at this price.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our view is the bull case: that we anchor on a history that no longer applies in the other direction.
      • The core, it says, is a 52 percent software-and-services business by the sell-side's wider definition, with 97 percent gross retention, a 26 percent EBIT1 margin, 149 percent cash conversion, a number-one global position in metrology and a chief executive who has beaten in every quarter since the targets were set.
      • It grew 12 percent organically in a quarter in which Trimble grew 12 percent and Sandvik 15 percent
        • The cost programme's full-year effect, Waygate's 16 percent-margin core and the TS20 product cycle are not in a consensus that still carries the Octave strip-out in its six-month numbers.
      • On that reading the multiple is not a legacy but the market pricing a simpler, faster, more honest company
        • DNB Carnegie's SEK 110 is a floor rather than a target, 22 times EBITAC is "undemanding", and a 20 to 25 times multiple held on 10 percent-plus earnings growth gives 12 to 13 percent a year with upside if estimates keep rising.
      • The evidence for it: the Q2 beat across every line
        • The cost run-rate
        • A higher backlog in all three segments
        • The three-month revisions
        • A historical 30 to 40 percent premium to the Stockholm market now at parity
        • And a record in which the company's crises have been "temporary valuation plateaus followed by deep, value-unlocking corporate transformations".
    • What we concede: the business is better run than it was and the targets will probably be met
      • 22 times is 'defendable' on the ten-year bands and we have shown the return at it
      • Consensus FY2027 EBIT (1,195 EURm) looks a few percent low
      • And if organic growth holds at 8 percent-plus with gross margin above 63 percent for a year, our 20 times anchor is wrong and 22 times becomes the floor.
    • Why we still hold our view: the return at the bull's own multiple does not clear the bar.
      • At 22 times held, the eighteen-month return is 6.3 percent a year
      • At today's 24.1 times held, 12.9 percent a year.
      • The bull needs the multiple to rise above a level the stock has exceeded in only 14 percent of the weeks of the last five years, on a company that has just cut its recurring revenue from about 40 percent to 28 percent of sales and reported a gross margin down 170 basis points.
      • The bull's strongest facts are operating facts, and we agree with them
        • Its valuation claim rests on the market continuing to misfile the company, which is possible but not ours to earn.
      • On the research's own bear cases (Morgan Stanley SEK 76 at 15 times one-year-forward earnings; DNB Carnegie's 14 times EV/EBITA on a 25 percent margin) the downside from 98.56 SEK is about a quarter of the price
        • The asymmetry at this price is against us.
        • The sell-side's bounded-downside argument, a sum of the parts at 18 times EV/EBIT giving SEK 85, is itself 14 percent below the price.
    • Where the case against would win: a year of 8 percent-plus organic growth with gross margin above 63 percent.
      • That would make 22 times the floor rather than the ceiling, and even then the return from this price is 6 to 7 percent a year
      • The entry price would move to about 82 SEK, not to 98.56 SEK.

  • Internal forces: alone they would rate A

    • Defence is slightly over 5 percent of group sales.
    • GPS spoofing events exceed 1,000 a day.
  • Tailwind, electronics: demand in China and the rest of Asia drove double-digit organic growth in Manufacturing Intelligence electronics in Q2 2026, tied to the build-out of artificial-intelligence chips and data centres.
    • China grew 10 percent organically in Q2 2026, off a low base after the Geosystems destocking.
  • Headwind, end-markets: automotive metrology volumes stayed weak in Western Europe and North America in Q2 2026
    • The German construction PMI sat at 42.4 in mid-2026
    • Chinese high-speed rail construction fell 85 percent year on year
    • Agriculture (2 percent of sales) is in a deep global recession.

    • A 5 percent move in the euro moves operating earnings by about 70.2 million EUR on 2025 figures
    • The 2025 translation hit to EBIT1 was 75.0 million EUR and the transaction hit 23.1 million EUR.
    • In Q2 2026 currency was a 1 percent headwind to sales, a 50 basis point headwind to gross margin and a 100 basis point tailwind to EBITAC.
  • Not A, because the 'Demand Inflection' test wants a sector-level force with two-plus years of visibility, and the forces here are either small in the mix or cyclical recoveries from a flat 2024 and 2025 (organic growth 0 percent and 2.6 percent).
    • Not C, because a 12 percent organic quarter with a higher order backlog in every segment is not a neutral external environment.
