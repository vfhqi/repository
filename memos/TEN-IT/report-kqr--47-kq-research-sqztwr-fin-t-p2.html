Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding Tenaris's pricing power across diverse cycles is critical for predicting its mid-term earnings per share and risk profile.

Significance of Pricing Power

1.
Investigating pricing power: "How much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: (1) a US onshore upcycle under Section 232 protection, with distributor restocking (2025-26); (2) a US onshore downturn with a falling rig count (as in 2015-16, 2019-20 and 2024); (3) removal or relaxation of US steel tariffs or anti-dumping orders, leading to an import surge; (4) steel input inflation (hot-rolled coil, scrap, tariffed billets and bars) with pass-through lags; (5) long-term national oil company contracts in the Middle East and Latin America during a logistics disruption (the Strait of Hormuz closure since March 2026); (6) a deepwater offshore upcycle that needs high-end premium connections; (7) competitive tenders against low-cost Asian and Indian suppliers in liberalising markets (Argentina)?"
2.
Volatile earnings drivers: Tenaris's pricing power directly influences its margins and subsequent earnings per share developments over the multi-year cycle. Morgan Stanley's report on 14 October 2026 highlights how domestic price increases can outpace input cost inflation. If Tenaris can sustain its average selling prices, its realized profitability will remain highly resilient compared to historical downcycles. This outcome would significantly de-risk the forward EPS outlook for global energy investors.
3.
Cyclical macro exposures: The company's profitability is highly sensitive to the US onshore segment and prevailing import protection. Jefferies' report dated 21 April 2026 notes that revised Section 232 tariffs provide critical support for domestic steel pipe pricing. However, any relaxation of these tariffs could expose the company to low-cost foreign imports. This means that a large portion of earnings remains dependent on trade policy rather than organic demand.
4.
Lagged inflation recovery: Steel input inflation presents a continuous challenge for the company's cost base. According to Rothschild & Co Redburn's report of 25 November 2025, price indices typically have a lagged impact of one to two quarters on Tenaris's realized revenues. If hot-rolled coil inflation continues without corresponding price hikes, margins will inevitably contract. This means that lagged pass-through structures govern short-term profitability fluctuations.
