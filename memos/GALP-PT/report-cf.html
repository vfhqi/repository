EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Galp Energia confronts a pivotal inflection point characterized by abrupt leadership restructuring into a highly unusual co-CEO model alongside co-CFO concentration, coinciding with aggressive upstream expansion in Brazil's pre-salt and Namibia's deepwater frontier. The company operates without activist pressure and is deliberately reshaping itself into an upstream-led Atlantic Basin growth platform, while simultaneously negotiating a complex downstream merger with Moeve to establish two European-scale platforms.

Most recent second-quarter 2026 results delivered replacement cost adjusted EBITDA of €1,272 million substantially in line with consensus, triggering a major full-year guidance upgrade to approximately €4 billion EBITDA, yet management maintains a consistent sandbagging archetype of under-promising and over-delivering across multiple reporting periods. Peer-relative positioning reveals top-quartile EBITDA margins driven by Brazilian pre-salt structural advantages, although sell-side coverage remains highly polarized with 55% overweight ratings, 35% equal-weight, and 10% underweight.

Material risks center on severe port downtime at Sines, restrictive refining margin hedging caps, and concentrated Brazilian fiscal exposure, compounded by Namibian reservoir complexity involving potentially high gas-oil ratios requiring costly reinjection infrastructure.

Materiality of Strategic and Leadership Change spans governance, portfolio architecture, and capital allocation. The abrupt January 2025 resignation of former CEO Filipe Silva following an undisclosed relationship led the Board to appoint co-CEOs Maria João Carioca and João Marques da Silva in April 2025, with Carioca uniquely holding both co-CEO and CFO roles simultaneously. This prolonged transition could take six months to resolve permanently, risking critical Mopane exploration timelines while raising fundamental questions about capital allocation effectiveness under split leadership. Upstream transformation accelerated through the September 2026 completion of a strategic asset swap with TotalEnergies, exchanging 40% operated interest in PEL 83 for stakes in PEL 56 and PEL 91, critically activating a 50% carry on Mopane capex until first oil. Downstream restructuring targets two distinct European-scale platforms through the Moeve combination, establishing cocontrolled RetailCo and minority-stake IndustrialCo, although extensive cross-border due diligence delayed signing final agreements to the second half of 2026. Renewables rebalancing involved acquiring €738 million of operational Spanish onshore wind assets totaling 712 MW to diversify the solar-heavy portfolio, while pro-forma 2026 Renewables EBITDA reaches €110 million and positions the platform for strategic deconsolidation via third-party partnerships.

Top Ranked Key Drivers of Forward EPS exhibit differentiated impact, plausibility, and execution dependencies across the portfolio. Driver One centers on Bacalhau pre-salt production ramp-up delivering over 15% production growth in 2026 as the massive FPSO reaches peak production of 40 kbpd net to Galp by late 2027, underpinned by a low cash breakeven near $20/bbl, carrying high impact, high plausibility, and high confidence dependent on FPSO commissioning uptime.

Driver Two involves Mopane appraisal campaign success following a 57% upgrade to 1.38 billion boe gross 3C contingent resources, with TotalEnergies resuming deepwater drilling in fourth-quarter 2026 while securing 50% capex carry and retaining optionality through a 10% Venus non-operated stake targeting FID in 2026, assessed as high impact, high plausibility, and medium confidence dependent on reservoir quality confirmation.

Driver Three encompasses downstream restructuring through the proposed Moeve merger creating European-scale RetailCo and IndustrialCo platforms, historically yielding 10% EBITDA synergies and enabling RetailCo to lever net debt to EBITDA between three and four times, although extensive due diligence stretched timelines into late 2026, characterized by medium impact, medium plausibility, and medium confidence dependent on cross-border regulatory approval.

Driver Four focuses on Sines lowcarbon execution commissioning the 100 MW electrolyser to replace 20% grey hydrogen by late 2026 and the 270 ktpa HVO/SAF unit in 75/25 partnership with Mitsui starting commercial operations by early 2027, demonstrating medium impact, high plausibility, and high confidence dependent on commissioning timelines. Driver Five addresses renewables platform rebalancing integrating 351 MW of operational Spanish onshore wind capacity, elevating pro-forma 2026 Renewables EBITDA to €110 million, reflecting medium impact, high plausibility, and high confidence dependent on third-party farm-in execution.

Driver Six captures volatile commodity cycle exposure where every $5/bbl Brent change impacts operating cash flow by €65 million, every $5/boe refining margin change creates €120 million EBITDA impact, and every 5 cent EUR/USD shift affects cash flow by €80 million, representing high impact, medium plausibility, and high confidence dependent on global supply-demand balances. Combined upstream resilience and restructuring deals create a plausible de-risking path, yet stacked downside scenarios where Brent drops to $50/bbl would cause free cash flow to fail materialization, while delayed contingent payments from asset sales risk elevating balance sheet gearing.
