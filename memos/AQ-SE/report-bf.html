EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

AQ Group operates as a global contract manufacturer of components and systems for demanding industrial customers, leveraging a highly decentralized operating model across 17 countries to maintain direct customer relationships and rapid local decision-making authority. The business benefits from secular growth tailwinds in power grid electrification, hyperscale data centers, and global defense spending, which offset cyclical exposure to commercial vehicle markets.

The group delivered record net sales of SEK 9,071 million in 2025, though organic growth decelerated to 2.0% from a 2023 peak of 20.7%, while maintaining 13 consecutive quarters of EBT margins above the 8.0% target. However, the financial profile remains highly dependent on underperforming acquisitions, which can create persistent cash flow and margin drags if integration turnarounds stall.

Business Model and Revenue Structure

Revenue generation is primarily transactional but bound by long-term customer relationships through framework agreements, customer forecasts, and specific project orders, exposing the business to short-term pricing passthrough lags. Operations span seven specialized product areas with manufacturing facilities across 17 countries, with the Component segment representing the dominant 85% of total group sales. Customers form long-term partnerships due to high customization requirements and rigorous qualification processes, with product designs co-engineered to make subsequent supplier changes highly disruptive and economically punitive for the customer. Geographic sales are heavily weighted toward Europe, with Sweden representing

27%, other European countries contributing 58%, and rest of world markets accounting for 15% of 2025 revenues.

Financial Profile and Peer Benchmarking

Gross profit margins remained stable at 53.79% in 2025, with EBITDA margins reaching 12.96% and reported EBIT margins of 9.26%, remaining above the long-term historical average. The group maintains a strong net cash position of negative SEK 401 million with an equity ratio of 67.85%, significantly exceeding the corporate target of 40%. Return metrics demonstrate strong profitability, with pre-tax ROCE reaching 16.90%, adjusted after-tax ROIC of 16.70%, and ROE of 14.90% in 2025.

AQ Group exhibits best-in-class profitability and asset turnover compared to direct Nordic peers, achieving superior EBIT margins of 9.26% well above the standard contract manufacturing benchmark of 7% to 8%. Inventory turnover decelerated to 3.20 turns in 2025 compared to a target of 3.50 turns, representing a key operational headwind.

Value Chain Position and Margin Distribution

AQ occupies an intermediate specialist position between raw material suppliers and downstream industrial OEMs, manufacturing custom sheet metal, inductive parts, wiring harnesses, and thermoplastic components while extending downstream into system integration with complete electrical cabinets and automated machines. Upstream vertical integration is limited, as AQ avoids direct commodity sourcing and instead purchases semifinished inputs including sheet metal, insulated copper wiring, and specialized electrical cables from independent suppliers.

The downstream aftermarket parts stage captures the highest margins within the industrial machinery value chain, with downstream industrial OEMs commanding exceptional parts margins averaging 26% from replacement parts, compared to overall truck assembly EBIT margins of 6% to 11%. Pricing power is constrained by contractual frameworks with major OEMs, with contracts including material price adjustment clauses that take 3 to 6 months to pass costs through, creating margin volatility.

Customer Concentration and Switching Dynamics

The ten largest customers accounted for just over half of total net sales in 2025, exposing AQ to individual customer volume adjustments. One individual customer generated revenues exceeding 10% of total group sales, with this single largest customer generating net sales of SEK 1,012 million in 2025. Major long-term accounts include blue-chip industrial OEMs like ABB, Alstom, Hitachi, Scania, and Volvo. Automotive and commercial vehicle contracts are typically awarded for specific vehicle programs spanning five to six years, while general industrial contracts are on average significantly shorter, requiring regular pricing updates. Switching costs are high because products are co-engineered and deeply integrated into customer systems, with early design-phase integration reinforcing relationships and making subsequent supplier changes highly disruptive for the OEM. However, the absence of proprietary IP leaves AQ vulnerable to relentless pricing pressure from consolidated OEMs who can dictate contract terms and utilize dual-sourcing strategies to squeeze margins.

Competitive Positioning and Market Dynamics

AQ operates as the largest and most active consolidator within its direct Nordic contract manufacturing peer group, achieving an adjusted post-tax ROIC of 16.70% and group EBIT margins of 9.26%, beating direct competitor Inission's estimated 8% margin. The European EMS landscape remains highly fragmented with 94% of companies representing small, non-integrated factories, with the market consolidating toward larger regional providers capable of delivering complete system products, as large consolidators increased their market share from 79% to 89% in Europe over the past decade.

High technical barriers require extensive engineering qualifications that lock out new players, with defense and railway markets enforcing strict, nontransferable certifications, while co-engineering components in early design phases embeds AQ directly into the customer's manufacturing process.

Operator Caliber and Capital Allocation

AQ Group demonstrates exceptional operator caliber through its decentralized entrepreneurial culture and a thirty-year history of uninterrupted quarterly profitability, with CEO James Ahrgren, appointed in 2021, successfully scaling annual revenues past SEK 9 billion in 2025. Internal cash flow is prioritized for factory capacity expansions before M&A, with AQ targeting underperforming factories at highly disciplined valuation multiples typically averaging 6x EBITA, executing over eight acquisitions since 2019.

AQ Group has never executed share buybacks in its history, citing structurally low share liquidity, with corporate policy mandating a dividend payout of approximately 25% of cycle-average net income. Financial improvements are highly dependent on management's active restructuring of low-productivity acquired factories, with newly acquired entities initially diluting 2025 EBIT margins by 100 basis points before turnarounds were completed.

Structural Growth Drivers and Paradigm Fit

AQ targets long-term annual sales growth of 15% split between 10% organic and 5% inorganic, with expanding utility grids, wind turbines, and marine electrification fueling double-digit volume growth for industrial transformers, while hyperscale data center applications represent a major growth adjacency expanding rapidly via the company's ABB relationship. European defense spending drives robust, high-margin demand for custom military-grade wiring systems, with long-term global focus on fossil-fuel independence creating structural demand for renewable energy and battery storage systems. Western supply chain nearshoring and regionalization trends directly benefit AQ's localized manufacturing model, with rising demand for local content strengthening the investment case for sub-suppliers with highly regionalized, proximate asset footprints, while local-for-local factories shield the group from escalating universal import tariffs and transport disruptions.

Key Risks and Structural Vulnerabilities

The lack of proprietary intellectual property keeps AQ structurally vulnerable to margin compression dictated by consolidated downstream OEMs, with large industrial buyers always leveraging dual-sourcing options to prevent AQ's adjusted EBIT margins from permanently exceeding 10%. The business model remains fundamentally vulnerable to cyclical swings in heavy industrial demand, as evidenced by the organic sales decline of 5% in 2024 when commercial vehicle volumes collapsed, with rapid raw material inflation exposing the group to severe margin squeeze during the 3 to 6-month contractual pass-through lag. Unexpected technical failures in novel data center transformers could trigger devastating liabilities and erase decades of hard-won brand equity. The structural power imbalance between AQ and its customers makes double-digit operating margins unsustainable, with blue-chip OEMs potentially exaggerating demand forecasts and forcing AQ to hold expensive excess inventory in its warehouses.
