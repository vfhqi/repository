Section 2 – Post-EPL Tax Dynamics and Segment Reorganization

Segment Realignment and

This corporate reorganization under Chief Executive Officer Meg O'Neill aims to eliminate operational redundancies and simplify external financial reporting. Analysts from Jefferies expect this structural shift to improve transparency when evaluating division-specific profit margins. This restructuring means that consolidated segment dynamics will shift as the company optimizes resource allocation.
2.
Strategic integration: BP's new Upstream segment will combine all oil and gas exploration, development, and trading operations under a unified leadership. This segment will integrate Gas & Low Carbon Energy and Oil Production & Operations, including the high- growth US bpx energy onshore business. The unified structure allows BP to streamline its hydrocarbons value chain and leverage global supply, trading, and shipping capabilities. Consequently, the integrated segment will help maximize upstream profit margins and accelerate earnings per share growth over the 2027-2030 period.
3.
Operational focus: BP's Downstream segment will encompass refining, products trading, marketing, convenience retail, aviation fuels, biofuels, and the retained Castrol stake. This division will run parallel to Upstream to capture integrated value from the reservoir to the fuel pump. By streamlining Downstream operations, BP expects to deliver consistent downstream cash flows to support its wider financial framework.

These high UK taxes mean that earnings per share will remain depressed until the windfall levy is dismantled.

This transition suggests that BP can anticipate a lower tax burden in its UK Upstream operations after 2030.

Therefore, enhanced financial flexibility would allow BP to maximize high-margin upstream investments structurally. This shift would help mitigate risks related to mature field decline rates.
5.
Intensified peer competition: If BP achieves a structurally superior financial frame, it will force European peers like Shell and Equinor to re-evaluate their own distribution and capital allocation frameworks. Shell currently distributes 40-50% of cash flow through consecutive buybacks, while Equinor's cash generation remains heavily exposed to high Norwegian tax payment lags. Thus, BP's superior deleveraging profile would compel peers to adjust dividend policies. These regional peer pressures will drive broader strategic changes across the integrated energy sector.

Macro Shifts and Early Indicators

6.
Punitive regulatory actions: The UK government has both the reason and the power to disrupt BP's tax expectations by extending or raising the marginal tax rate under the Energy Profits Levy. The late-October 2026 UK Budget officially confirmed that North Sea producers face a prolonged windfall tax environment through 2030. These regulatory adjustments confirm that EPL liabilities will remain baseline assumptions for several years. This persistent fiscal drag will depress domestic profits.
7.
Stubborn discount rate rigidities: A downward swing in global interest rates would force BP to lower its nominal discount rate for decommissioning provisions, which currently stands at 4.5%. Because decommissioning liabilities are extremely sensitive to discount rate adjustments, any rate cuts would expand the balance sheet liability and raise non-cash accretion expenses. Consequently, global interest rate volatility will threaten the accuracy of finance cost projections. This provision sensitivity remains a key financial variable for long-term planning.
8.
Contrary evidence: BP may fail to experience lower finance costs because its massive lease liabilities remain flat on the balance sheet and are highly insensitive to net debt deleveraging. Redburn notes that total financial obligations are projected to fall to only $34.60 bn, which is at the upper end of management's guidance of $33- 35bn. This stubborn balance sheet rigidity proves that lease interest will limit cash savings. Consequently, these non-discretionary commitments will continue to drag down earnings.
9.
Early operational indicators: The geographical and divisional mix of BP's profits serves as the earliest indicator that the common tax view is wrong. For instance, BP's third-quarter 2026 underlying tax rate fell to 28% due to a downstream-heavy profit mix during a temporary refining margin upcycle. Watching changes in international refining margins represents the best way to anticipate consolidated tax rate fluctuations early. Any sharp deviation in global refining indicators will signal a shift in tax dynamics.
10.
Deferred distribution priorities: BP's decision to prioritize the full settlement of its remaining Deepwater Horizon liabilities represents another shift that could keep buybacks paused longer than common models assume. Management intends to direct its excess cash flow to address approximately $6 bn in remaining state and federal liabilities. Consequently, clearing these legacy liabilities will restrict immediate capital return upside near-term. This strategy suggests that the resumption of capital distributions will remain highly deferred.
11.
Other side of the story: BP could deliver much stronger earnings and a faster tax rate decline if global energy prices remain elevated and downstream assets outpace upstream production. Under these constructive commodity conditions, strong cash generation from international operations would dilute high-tax UK earnings. Consequently, elevated commodity prices would accelerate structural cost reductions and boost intermediate earnings. This scenario would allow the company to easily outperform the more conservative price decks currently assumed.
