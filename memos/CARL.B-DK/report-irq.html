Terms used throughout. EBIT: operating profit on Carlsberg's own performance measures (FY2025 base DKK 13,686m), the basis of guidance and consensus. EBITDA: EBIT before depreciation and amortisation. EPS: the company's adjusted earnings per share (FY2025 DKK 60.81). Leverage: net interest-bearing debt to EBITDA, including leases and half of the hybrid. The hybrid: the EUR 1.8bn hybrid bond, counted as equity, whose coupon (DKK 620m gross a year) sits below net income. IFRS 18: the new accounting standard for presenting the income statement, which moves some items between lines. FY2026: the financial year to December 2026; H1 and H2: first and second halves. CEEI: Carlsberg's Central and Eastern Europe and India region. Western Europe: Carlsberg's Western Europe region. Chongqing: Chongqing Brewery, Carlsberg's 60%-owned listed Chinese subsidiary. Revenue per hectolitre: revenue divided by volume, the best public measure of price and mix. bp: basis points, hundredths of a percentage point.

A first point for Richard before the questions. Carlsberg is held as a Core Investment at 4.50% of the fund, so the questions are weighted to whether to add, hold or cut, and to what would break the case. The memo (3-Nov-2026) calls it "a self-help and capital-return compounder with no demand growth", in the Bottoming phase and turning up on a dated schedule: about 8% a year EPS growth on 3% revenue and 5% EBIT growth, consensus FY2027 EPS right in level (DKK 69.25 against our DKK 70.91) but wrong in composition, and a buyback from August 2027 that no consensus model carries. Its timing call bears directly on adding: the sequence runs the wrong way first (February to April 2027: a conservative opening range, the first clean IFRS 18 year showing the hybrid coupon, revenue cut towards 3%) and the right way second (August 2027: the half-year beat, the upward narrowing, the buyback), so "the entry is better after the spring than before it, unless India lands in December". The call can test both halves of that sequence. The Rater's four ratings: Thesis's Change Forces' Strength 53, Foundations' Robustness 63, Setups' Fit/Crispness 64, Case Riskiness 54.

What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: Carlsberg's earnings over the next eighteen months are set by self-help it controls (the last Britvic synergies, Kazakhstan, deleveraging and a buyback), while its share price answers to three things it does not (China, the 2027 cost round and a sector multiple), and the composition of EPS below EBIT is where consensus is most wrong. Six judgements decide whether to add, hold or cut.

  1. Judgement 1, the FY2027 opening range. We expect 2% to 6% or 3% to 7% organic EBIT growth on 9-Feb-2027 (midpoint 4% to 5%) against consensus EBIT growth of 5.9%, about a 70% chance of opening below consensus. If the range opens at 4% to 7% or narrower, management has broken its own pattern and the spring dip the memo expects is smaller; if the midpoint is below 4%, the downside path (FY2027 EPS about DKK 66.5 to 67) is in play.
  2. Judgement 2, the composition below EBIT. Finance costs, the hybrid coupon (DKK 3.6 a share) and minorities explain why nineteen brokers' FY2027 EPS runs from DKK 65.57 to 75.79 on revenue within 4%. If FY2027 finance costs are guided at DKK 1.6bn or less, the sell-side gains DKK 1 to 1.5 of EPS; at DKK 1.8bn or more, our FY2027 EPS loses about DKK 1.2.
  3. Judgement 3, the buyback's date and size. Base DKK 2bn from August 2027 and DKK 4bn in 2028. A one-year delay takes FY2028 EPS about 3.7% lower; an Indian listing whose proceeds are bought back adds about 2% to FY2028 EPS and could bring the start forward.
  4. Judgement 4, China: cycle or share loss. Base: China volume +2% in 2027 on easy comparatives, China EBIT +3% to 5%. A second weak summer costs about DKK 320m of EBIT (2.1%) but only DKK 1.1 of EPS (1.6%) after Chongqing's 40% minority; a structural 20% fall in China profit costs about 4% of EBIT and 2.9% of EPS and delays the buyback. The shares have moved 5% to 15% on Chinese prints worth 1% to 3% of EPS.
  5. Judgement 5, 2027 price against cost. About 160bp of gross-margin headwind and input inflation near 5.4%; policy is to cover more than 80% in money. Each 0.5 point of price and mix is DKK 300m to 450m of EBIT (2% to 3%): revenue per hectolitre +1.5% against inflation above 5% takes FY2027 EPS to about DKK 68.5 (3.4% lower); price holding at 3% takes it to about DKK 72.9.
  6. Judgement 6, the Kazakhstan step. About DKK 200m of EBIT in 2027 and DKK 100m to 150m in 2028 as toll-filling ends. A CEEI margin above 18% in H1 2027 says it is arriving on time; below it, a point of FY2027 EBIT growth is at risk.

Order of play. Ask how FY2026 lands and how the FY2027 range is built (A1, A2) first, then the lines that are his as treasurer (A4 finance costs and the hybrid, A5 minorities, A6 Sapporo and leverage, A7 the buyback). Then China's fourth quarter (A9) and the 2027 cost round (A3). Then soft drinks economics (B2.1), mainstream price competition (B3.1), the leverage target (B5.1) and China disclosure (B8.1). Use the rest of the hour on Range B, leaving pay and acquisitions to the end or for management.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the FY2026 results and FY2027 range on 9-Feb-2027, the first-quarter trading statement in late April 2027, the H1 results in August 2027 and the third-quarter statement in late October 2027)

A1 ★ Where FY2026 lands. "You narrowed FY2026 to 4% to 6% organic EBIT growth in August and repeated it on 29 October. With China's third quarter and Kazakhstan's toll-filling behind you, are you comfortable in the upper half, and is DKK 13,686 million the right restated base to grow from?"
- Why: FactSet consensus EBIT of DKK 14,472m equals the guidance midpoint exactly; Deutsche Bank reports the Bloomberg consensus at 5.8% organic. We expect 5.5% to 6.0%: about a 1% beat against FactSet and about nil against Bloomberg. Each 1% of EBIT is about DKK 137m, DKK 0.8 of EPS.
- Tell: "upper half" = the beat that no longer creates upgrades, as the memo expects; "the range stands" with weight on China = a 2026 delivered near 5%, and the spring is harder.

A2 ★ How the FY2027 range is built. "When you open FY2027 in February, what are the building blocks we should think about: the synergies left, Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan, the China comparatives and the cost round? And is a range about four points wide still your practice?"
- Why: our FY2027 EBIT increase of about DKK 730m to 790m is synergies +190, Kazakhstan +200, China +100, India +90 and underlying price over cost +150; each DKK 100m of wholly owned EBIT is about DKK 0.59 of EPS. Initial ranges since 2024 opened at 1% to 5% or 2% to 6% and were narrowed up and delivered at the top. Consensus EBIT growth for FY2027 is 5.9%.
- Tell: named blocks summing to about 5% = our base and a range around it; "we will be prudent given China and costs" = the conservative opening and the February dip; any hint of a narrower range = Judgement 1's positive branch.

A3 ★ The 2027 cost round. "How much of 2027's aluminium, glass, energy and malt is now hedged or priced, what input inflation per hectolitre should we model, and is the plan still to cover it in money rather than in gross-margin percentage?"
- Why: 2026 was about 90% hedged; the memo carries about 160bp of gross-margin headwind in 2027 with input inflation near 5.4%. Each 0.5 point of price and mix is about DKK 475m of revenue and DKK 300m to 450m of EBIT (2% to 3%). Our Fevertree work this morning has aluminium and the Midwest premium about doubled since 2024 (Jefferies), with oil at USD 111 fattening the tail.
- Tell: "well covered, inflation mid-single digit, price to follow" = our base; "costs higher than last year's view" = the downside branch of Judgement 5; "gross margin flat" = upside.

A4 ★ Finance costs and the hybrid under IFRS 18. "Net finance cost guidance was cut from DKK 2.2 billion to 1.8 billion. How much of that is IFRS 18 moving items between lines rather than lower debt, where will the hybrid coupon sit in the 2026 accounts, and what is the right all-in finance cost for 2027?"
- Why: the memo reads DKK 150m to 250m of the cut as reclassification, and the combined cost of debt plus hybrid falls only from about DKK 2.34bn to 2.27bn between 2026 and 2027. Our all-in DKK 1,650m against DKK 1,800m to 1,944m at DNB Carnegie, Redburn, Deutsche Bank and Jefferies is DKK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS. The coupon (DKK 477m after tax) is DKK 3.6 a share for any broker who has not deducted it. 84% of debt is fixed; a 1-point rate rise costs DKK 98m a year.
- Tell: "the cut is mostly reclassification, coupon below net income" = consensus EPS has DKK 1 to 3.6 of composition to lose in February, as the memo argues; "genuine savings, about DKK 1.6bn next year" = our EPS is safe and the sell-side gains.

A5 ★ Minorities in 2027. "How should we model minority interests next year, as Sapporo's 25% of Southeast Asia is added to Chongqing, Laos and Malaysia: is about DKK 100 million a year for Sapporo's share the right order?"
- Why: minorities were DKK 1,023m in FY2025, 14.65% of consolidated profit, the highest proportion among European brewers. Our DKK 1,280m against a broker range of DKK 1,004m to 1,293m is up to DKK 2.1 of EPS; the Sapporo charge alone is put at DKK 40m (Bernstein), DKK 150m (earlier work) and DKK 100m (ours), DKK 0.8 of EPS between the extremes.
- Tell: "about 100" = our base; "nearer 150" = brokers below DKK 1,100m cut; a total near DKK 1.3bn = the composition cut the memo expects.

A6 ★ Sapporo's closing and leverage at year end. "Is the Sapporo transaction still on track to close in the fourth quarter, and where should leverage be at the end of 2026 with and without it?"
- Why: about 2.5x at end-2026 if Sapporo closes, about 2.7x if not, from 3.0x at H1 2026 and 3.9x a year earlier. The 2.5x target arriving a year early is the gate to the buyback.
- Tell: "closing in December, 2.5x" = Judgement 3 stays on schedule; "slipping into 2027" = the buyback date moves with it.

A7 ★ What starts the buyback. "You have said reaching 2.5 times will not automatically trigger a buyback. What else does the Board need to see: a period at that level, India's proceeds, China, the rating? Is an announcement in February realistic, or is the half-year the natural point?"
- Why: our base is DKK 2bn from August 2027 and DKK 4bn in 2028, 2.5% to 3.5% of the shares a year, worth 2.5 to 3.5 points of EPS growth that no consensus model carries. A one-year delay takes FY2028 EPS about 3.7% lower. The 2020 buyback was cancelled within weeks of launch, so a restart is not irreversible.
- Tell: "the half-year, subject to conditions" = our base; "February is possible" = the sequence the memo expects reverses and the case to add now strengthens; "not before 2028" = Judgement 3's downside.

A8 The Indian listing. "Where does the Indian listing stand after the updated draft prospectus of 27 October, and would the proceeds go to buybacks or to debt?"
- Why: up to USD 700m, DKK 4bn to 5bn; bought back at about DKK 1,000 it retires 3% to 4% of the shares and adds about 2% to FY2028 EPS net of the new Indian minority (India is about 3% to 4% of group EBITDA). The memo puts a February buyback announcement at about 15% today, mostly through this route.
- Tell: "December or January, proceeds to shareholders" = the buyback comes forward, a reason to add before the spring; "market-dependent, no use decided" = the base holds.

A9 ★ China in the fourth quarter. "Chongqing's third-quarter volume fell about 10% and you said trade inventories were clean by the end of September. How have October and November traded, and how do you split the fall between weather and nightlife spending, and share lost to China Resources and Heineken?"
- Why: China is about 14% of revenue and roughly a fifth of EBIT (about DKK 2.7bn to 2.9bn), two-thirds of it earned in the second and third quarters. Heineken grew in China by double digits in the third quarter while Chongqing fell 9.6%, Tsingtao 9% and Budweiser APAC 16.8%; on-premise's share of Chinese beer has fallen from 51.5% (2019) to 47.5%. On 29-Oct the market sold the brewers with China exposure.
- Tell: "weather and destocking, inventories clean, fourth quarter better" = cyclical, the comparatives arrive from the second quarter of 2027; "competitive in some provinces" = the share-and-channel reading the memo weights more, and a reason to wait for Chongqing's January figures before adding.

A10 ★ Revenue in 2027. "Your 4% to 6% revenue ambition has not been reached since it was set. Is it still the right frame, and how should we split 2027 between price, mix and volume, given the Kazakhstan licence volume laps?"
- Why: organic revenue 2.4% (2024), (0.6%) (2025), 2.7% (H1 2026), 3.0% (third quarter 2026). Consensus carries +3.8% for FY2027 against our +3.1% (Jefferies 2.3%; Redburn cut to 3.0% on 29-Oct). The Kazakhstan licence added 1 to 1.5 points of volume in 2026 and laps in 2027; soft-drink mix costs about 30bp a year of revenue per hectolitre.
- Tell: "about 3%, mostly price" = consensus revenue comes down towards ours (a composition cut, little EPS effect); "the ambition stands for 2027" = upside to the memo's revenue.

A11 ★ The Kazakhstan step. "With the Kazakhstan plant producing since 1 October, how quickly does the toll-filling cost come out, and is a CEEI margin back above 18% in the first half of 2027 a fair test?"
- Why: toll-filling cost 40bp of CEEI margin in H1 2026; we take about DKK 200m of EBIT in 2027 (1.4 points of EBIT growth) and DKK 100m to 150m in 2028. Morgan Stanley reads it as a step to 10% organic EBIT growth in 2027, which the memo calls too high. Peer ramps (Royal Unibrew's PepsiCo ramp, Coca-Cola Europacific Partners in Germany) took two to three years.
- Tell: "full effect in 2027" = the fast end of precedent, our base; "ramp through 2028" = a point of FY2027 EBIT growth moves out a year.

A12 Britvic synergies remaining. "With about 80% of the GBP 110 million of synergies delivered by the end of 2026, what is left for 2027, and in which lines?"
- Why: synergies were DKK 475m in 2026 and DKK 190m in 2027 in our base, and about 3 points of 2026 EBIT growth; after 2027 the named self-help fades to about a sixth of EBIT growth, which no broker quantifies.
- Tell: "the target is a floor, more to come" = some upside to 2027; "done in 2027" = the self-help fade arrives as the memo has it.

A13 Marketing and special items. "Marketing is about 8.3% of revenue against your 8.5% to 9% framework. Will it step up in 2027, and should special items narrow sharply in FY2026?"
- Why: a step to 9% at once is about DKK 650m, 4% of EBIT. Special items were DKK 1.9bn in 2025, a 35% gap between adjusted and reported profit. Both are the memo's tests of whether the new revenue-weighted culture is spending the savings.
- Tell: "gradual, within the framework" and "special items much lower" = the cost culture intact; "a step-up to support growth" = less EBIT leverage in 2027.

A14 Currency and tax. "What translation effect should we model for 2027, and is a 23% tax rate right?"
- Why: translation was raised to +DKK 100m for 2026; the Kazakh and Laotian currencies cannot be hedged; we use a 23% tax rate.
- Tell: a negative translation guide for 2027 = a small EBIT headwind the brokers may not carry.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. China and Asia

B1.1 Premium and channel in China. "Is Chongqing losing share in premium to Heineken through China Resources, and how are you responding in the on-premise channel, which has shrunk since 2019?"
- Why: Heineken's licensed volume through China Resources grew in the mid-twenties; the memo's structural case (low probability, high impact) is Chongqing's mainstream brands losing share in Western China.
- Tell: "share held in our provinces" = cyclical; "pressure in premium in the cities" = part of the fall is share, and China's 2027 recovery is smaller.

B1.2 Vietnam and the rest of Asia. "Vietnamese excise rises about five points a year from 2027. How much price do you expect to take there, and how do Laos and Malaysia look?"
- Why: Asia earns a 26.3% operating margin and an 81.8% return on invested capital excluding goodwill; Vietnam's excise is one of the regulatory steps the memo calls sequenced against the company.
- Tell: "passed through within months, volume dip" = the industry pattern; "margin pressure" = Asia's margin is not as safe as the base assumes.

B1.3 Minority stakes. "Is there any plan to buy in minorities at Chongqing, Laos or Malaysia, or is the Sapporo-style sale of stakes the direction? Or save for management."
- Why: minorities leak 14.65% of profit from the best regions; the Sapporo sale at 21.3x EBIT and the Indian listing are capital recycled now at the cost of leakage later.
- Tell: "no buy-ins planned" = minorities keep rising faster than EBIT, as we model.

B2. Multi-beverage and PepsiCo

B2.1 ★ The economics of soft drinks. "Soft drinks are about 31% of volume and grew 8.5% in the third quarter. What gross margin does the PepsiCo bottling business earn against beer, and would you disclose it, so we can see the density gains in Western Europe?"
- Why: PepsiCo volume comes at about a 20% discount to the group's price per hectolitre (about 30bp a year of mix); the memo's test of density is Western Europe's margin moving from 13.2% (2025) to 14.2% (2027), with soft-drink gross margin disclosed above 30%.
- Tell: "above 30%, rising with density" = the multi-beverage moat proven in money; "lower but higher returns on capital" = a franchise business at a lower margin, as Jefferies argues.

B2.2 The Nordic swap in 2029. "When Danish and Finnish bottling moves from Coca-Cola to PepsiCo in 2029, what are the transition costs and the volume effect, and when will you quantify them?"
- Why: the memo has a net loss of about 1.5m hectolitres and transition costs that turn self-help negative in 2029; the 2019 border-trade precedent cut Danish canning-line volume 30%.
- Tell: "costs in 2028 and 2029, volume recovered by 2030" = a dated dip in FY2029 consensus, which is too high already (DKK 17,518m EBIT, implying +8%).

B2.3 New territories. "Beyond Azerbaijan from January, which PepsiCo territories are open, and how likely is Poland?"
- Why: five PepsiCo markets in 2024, 17 by January 2029; DNB Carnegie puts each mid-sized territory at +4% to 8% of EPS over time; Jefferies calls Poland "not guaranteed". Azerbaijan is breakeven in year one.
- Tell: a named pipeline = each new territory lowering the cost of serving the next, which the memo calls rate-limited, is running faster.

B2.4 Britvic in the United Kingdom. "How is Britvic's United Kingdom margin progressing towards the levels brokers model, and how ready are you for the deposit return scheme in October 2027 and the lower sugar threshold in January 2028?"
- Why: Redburn sees the United Kingdom margin rising from 12% to 15% by 2029; Britvic was bought at 13.6x EBITDA and earns roughly 5% to 7% after tax on our estimate, below its cost of capital; return on invested capital including goodwill fell from 13.8% (2024) to 10.1% (2025).
- Tell: "on track, scheme costs in the plan" = returns rising towards the cost of capital; "investing for share" = Britvic's return stays low for longer.

B3. Western Europe, pricing and competition

B3.1 ★ Mainstream price competition. "In the United Kingdom, Heineken and Molson Coors cut alcohol strength and spent the duty saving on price, and in Poland Asahi has been discounting. Are you matching on price, or giving up mainstream volume to protect margin?"
- Why: Carlsberg leads in only 24% of its volume and is a challenger in 66%; 1% of Western European volume is about DKK 220m of EBIT. Western European beer volume fell 3.9% in H1 2026 and 4.2% in the third quarter. The memo reads pricing as nominal, not real.
- Tell: "we protect value, volume share down" = the memo's reading, EBIT protected; "we will respond" = 2027 price below plan, Judgement 5's downside.

B3.2 Alcohol-free beer. "Alcohol-free beer grew about 11%. What share of Western European volume is it now, and how does its gross profit per litre compare with beer, given it pays no beer duty?"
- Why: the Sector Digest's point is that moderation can be a gain in mix, judged on gross profit per litre; the memo counts alcohol-free among the few real tailwinds.
- Tell: "higher gross profit per litre" = moderation is a mix gain, not just a volume loss.

B3.3 Retailer negotiations. "After the Swiss delisting in late 2025, how are the 2027 negotiations with the large grocers going, and do you expect any listing disputes as you push price?"
- Why: a 2027 price rise is the largest source of variance in FY2027 EBIT; consolidating European grocery presses on a challenger's volume.
- Tell: "settled, no disputes" = price lands early in 2027; "ongoing in some markets" = first-quarter 2027 revenue per hectolitre may print below the memo's +2% test.

B4. Cash and working capital

B4.1 ★ Supplier finance. "Supplier finance has risen to about DKK 3.2 billion with terms up to 180 days. Is that a permanent part of the funding, and what would working capital do if banks or suppliers pulled back?"
- Why: trade working capital weakened to (15.6%) of revenue from (20.1%) excluding Britvic; supplier finance rose 20% and is a quiet source of cash that could reverse, and it bears on the leverage path that gates the buyback.
- Tell: "stable programme, in leverage already" = no change; "we expect to reduce it" = a one-off cash outflow and a later or smaller buyback.

B4.2 Capital spending and cash conversion. "Capital spending runs at about DKK 6.5 billion. When does it step down after Kazakhstan and the PepsiCo plants, and what cash conversion should we expect by 2028?"
- Why: cash conversion of 85% to 90% of adjusted net income rising to 95% to 105% by 2028 as capex and restructuring cash fall; free cash flow after dividends of DKK 2.5bn to 4.5bn a year sets the buyback's scale. Distributable cash is about DKK 2.5bn a year below FactSet's free cash flow (leases, the coupon, minority dividends).
- Tell: "stepping down from 2027" = buybacks at the top of our range.

B5. Capital allocation and the balance sheet

B5.1 ★ The leverage target. "You have called 2.5 times fit for the future business. Is it a level you will hold, so that surplus cash goes to buybacks, or would you go lower, and what does the rating agency need?"
- Why: the memo reads "fit for the future business" as meaning management will not drive leverage below 2.5x; the BBB+ rating was protected with the hybrid. If 2.5x is held, about DKK 4bn to 5bn a year is available for buybacks from 2028; each DKK 5bn repaid instead saves only about DKK 175m of interest (+1.5% EPS).
- Tell: "held at about 2.5x, surplus returned" = the buyback path we model; "lower is better for the rating" = a smaller buyback.

B5.2 The dividend. "Is a payout of about 50% of adjusted net income still the policy once buybacks restart?"
- Why: we model DKK 32 for FY2026 and DKK 34 for FY2027, a 3.4% yield; dividends are about 7% of the 18-month return.
- Tell: "yes, progressive" = no change.

B5.3 Acquisitions after Britvic. "What hurdle does the Board now set for acquisitions after Britvic, and is there appetite for more soft drinks or adjacent brands? Or save for management."
- Why: return on invested capital including goodwill is 10.8% (rolling to mid-2026), and Britvic's initial return is below its cost; a large deal would replace the buyback in our base. Richard has discussed an acquisition idea with the chief executive before; check his own notes before raising a name.
- Tell: "bolt-ons only, buybacks first" = the capital-return case holds; "open to larger moves" = the reason the buyback is not in consensus.

B6. Risks outside the plan

B6.1 Super Bock and other legal matters. "Where does Super Bock's appeal against the EUR 57 million fine stand, and how are the private claims of up to EUR 467 million provided for?"
- Why: a tail to the associates line that the memo lists among the sudden risks.
- Tell: "appeal ongoing, claims without merit" = no change; any provision = a one-off below EBIT.

B6.2 Ukraine, Kazakhstan and Laos. "How do you manage the exposure in Ukraine (about 3% of revenue) and the currencies in Kazakhstan and Laos that cannot be hedged, after Russia?"
- Why: political seizure or war is one of the memo's sudden risks (the Russia seizure of DKK 47.7bn in 2023 is the precedent).
- Tell: "local funding, cash upstreamed" = the exposure is managed.

B7. People and incentives

B7.1 Incentives and succession. "The bonus cap rose to 150% of salary with more weight on revenue. How is the long-term incentive split between revenue, EBIT and returns, and is there depth behind the finance director? Or save for management."
- Why: the memo names the shift from cost-first to revenue-weighted incentives as the mechanism by which the cost discipline could erode; the senior team is small with no visible finance-director successor.
- Tell: EBIT and return on capital still dominating = the cost culture is protected.
