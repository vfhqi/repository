Behind the rating as a whole

Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external environment is a slight net negative for OTE's earnings over 18 months and mildly negative beyond: structural value-upgrade demand, a rational market and favourable regulation are offset by the end of the EU-funded ICT boom, falling energy-hedge cover and the first credible attack on the high-margin wholesale layer (the Vodafone-PPC venture, closing in the first half of 2027). The decisive point for the Consumer is concentration: almost all of 2027's external positive comes through one decision, the size of the early-2027 inflation-linked price step; the main negative for 2028 to 2030 is one rate, the speed at which Vodafone (and possibly Nova) move wholesale lines to the venture. Neither is a cycle; both are decisions by identifiable actors. Separately, the external forces acting on the share price (bond yields, sector de-rating, Greek index reclassification) are larger than those acting on earnings, so the price cone is wider than the earnings cone.

C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

F4016th of 19

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: intensity is rising slowly in fixed broadband and is stable in mobile; the threat has moved from retail price (disproved) to wholesale volume (forming, from 2027), and the first evidence of pressure has now reached OTE's own numbers.

  • Retail attack by PPC (the state-controlled power utility) failed. It launched internet-only fibre in June 2025 at EUR 17.90 for 500 Mbps and EUR 19.90 for 1 Gbps (50% to 59% below incumbents) and had under 11,000 customers at end-2025 and at most 20,000 by March 2026, under 1% of OTE's broadband base, because it has no mobile, no TV and no telecom retail brand while 70% of OTE's broadband customers bundle mobile.
  • But the infrastructure is real and is finding an anchor tenant. PPC's FiberGrid reached 2.3m homes passed, 1.5m ready for service (October 2026), built on electricity poles at about EUR 160 per home against OTE's about EUR 500 per line. Vodafone Greece and PPC signed heads of terms (June 2026) and a term sheet (August 2026) for a 50:50 wholesale fibre venture covering more than 1.6m homes, targeting 3.8m by 2028, closing in the first half of 2027 subject to competition review. Vodafone brings 0.91m broadband customers. This attacks OTE's national wholesale layer (about EUR 239m of revenue at 80% to 85% margin; 48% of rivals' FTTH customers sit on OTE's network).
  • First evidence in OTE's numbers (third quarter 2026): retail fixed service revenue -0.6% despite +52k fibre customers, and the broadband base lost 8,164 customers in one quarter, almost all of the year's loss (Geography Digest, 2 November). Management has also been "re-contracting" customers in PPC areas to hold churn near zero, which is price given back that subscriber counts do not show. This fits the pattern that pricing power breaks before demand does.
  • Mobile is stable and rational. Three operators; Vodafone and Nova raised prices with inflation two years running; PPC's chief executive has repeatedly ruled out mobile; Deutsche Bank judges PPC unlikely to bid for spectrum.
  • Swing factor: ownership of Nova. BC Partners is selling United Group's Greek unit (founder lawsuit pending); a buyer that joins the Vodafone-PPC venture or prices for volume would raise intensity materially. A leveraged buyer would more likely hold prices.
  • European precedent: utility-backed fibre (Open Fiber in Italy, SIRO in Ireland) raised wholesale competition for years, with pressure peaking during construction, but incumbents with strong retail and bundles kept share (eir kept 66% of fibre broadband against SIRO's 27%).

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

C457th of 19

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: a mild net headwind in 2027 after a tailwind in 2026; energy cover falls, wages and content costs rise, while procurement scale and spectrum savings offset part of it. None is large enough to change the case alone.

  • Energy: about 80% of FY2026 consumption hedged, about 66% of FY2027; long-term virtual power-purchase agreements run to 2034 and 2036 (the second began settling in July 2026). To what extent is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved, and in which direction does it act on the company?

F4516th of 19

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: three disruption forces are in play; one is strengthening against OTE (low-cost aerial fibre as a wholesale platform), one has been largely disproved (satellite broadband), and one is unproven but already acting through the valuation (artificial-intelligence switching agents).

  • Aerial utility fibre: strengthening, acting against OTE's wholesale layer. About 30,000 users at end-2024; EUR 40 a month plus EUR 349 equipment; OTE's fixed wireless access (134k, +116% year on year) has slowed Starlink's growth since early 2026 (chief executive, 30 October), and OTE is using subsidised rural fibre to win back some of the 150,000 to 160,000 lines lost to satellite and wireless rivals.
  • Artificial-intelligence switching agents and satellite-to-phone: unproven in operations, real in the price. Barclays (20 October 2026) put 4% to 7% of sector revenue at risk and cut OTE's target about 10% by cutting terminal growth, without scoring Greece (Sector Digest, 3 November). This acts on OTE through the sector's discount rate, not its earnings; a low-debt incumbent with bundles is less exposed, but that is a hypothesis to test against churn and existing-customer pricing in the next two results.

C-04

To what extent is political or regulatory change under way, and how does it bear on the company?

B624th of 19

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: regulation is in an unusually favourable phase for OTE, and the main negatives are already in guidance; the risk is that a state-granted advantage can be switched by decision, and the two live political exposures are the price-indexation clause and the competition review of the Vodafone-PPC venture.

  • Positives in force: a stop-sell of copper-based services where fibre exists (forces migration onto OTE's network); lifting of margin-squeeze obligations on fibre; the wholesale volume-discount scheme (up to 28%) approved by the regulator and the European Commission, which locks Vodafone and Nova into OTE's network; removal of the 10% pay-TV tax (January 2026) and the 5% broadband tax on lines above 100 Mbps (a 6.2% effective retail price cut without lowering OTE's revenue); anti-piracy fines of EUR 750, rising to EUR 1,500; spectrum renewal (900 MHz and 1800 MHz, September and November 2027) proposed at about EUR 78m against EUR 119m paid fifteen years ago, with longer (20-year or indefinite) terms and PPC unlikely to bid.
  • Negatives: the regulator's proposed 10% to 13% cut in fibre wholesale prices, partly offset by a 4% to 5% rise in copper wholesale prices, awaits European Commission review; Deutsche Bank says it is already in the FY2026 free cash flow guidance, and commercial discounts already sit below the proposed ceilings. OTE remains under significant-market-power retail controls.
  • Political exposure: Greek inflation of 5.6% (October) and cost-of-living politics make an inflation-linked price rise in early 2027 visible; no Greek authority has challenged the clause, it is written in at signing (so the September 2026 European court ruling on discretionary clauses at Vodafone Germany should not reach it), but the decision to apply it is now political as much as commercial.
  • Competition review of the venture (Hellenic Competition Commission): remedies could cap the venture's open-access pricing (good for OTE) or, conversely, prompt the regulator to revisit OTE's volume-discount scheme if rivals allege foreclosure (bad).
  • Capital-market regulation: FTSE and STOXX reclassified Greece as developed in September 2026; MSCI inclusion follows in May 2027.
