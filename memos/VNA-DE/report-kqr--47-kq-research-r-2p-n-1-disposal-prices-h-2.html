Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Residential landlords face significant financial pressure to execute asset disposals at book value to preserve equity and protect net asset values.
  2. Balance sheet deleveraging is highly dependent on transaction markets because elevated leverage metrics put investment-grade credit ratings at risk.

These transactions help the firm stabilize its leveraged balance sheet without diluting current shareholders.

Vonovia disclosed on August 5, 2026, that its two sales segments progressed more slowly due to a challenging environment. Section 3 – German Residential Transaction Volumes and Buyer Profiles

Section summary
  1. German residential transaction volumes remained severely depressed in the third quarter of 2026 as high mortgage rates restricted institutional activity.
  2. Private equity exit pressures and retail fund redemptions are driving a wave of planned liquidations and portfolio disposals.
  3. Conservative

institutional buyers and municipal companies support baseline pricing but demand significant yields that prevent a rapid market recovery.

Market Transaction Volumes

1.
Depressed activity: German residential transaction volumes experienced a significant contraction during the third quarter of 2026 as research across ten relevant sources, five key companies, and one comparable past case shows persistent financing friction. Morgan Stanley reported on October 21, 2026, that transaction volumes for the third quarter fell by 26% year-on-year, leaving nine-month volumes 39% below the historical long-term average. This market freeze means that landlords will struggle to generate non-dilutive capital, which ultimately depresses future earnings per share across the listed sector.
2.
Slowing momentum: German real estate transaction volumes are projected to contract slightly over the full year of 2026 due to macroeconomic headwinds. Jefferies reported on September 17, 2026, that the research institute GEWOS expects total transaction volumes to decline 2% year-on-year to 258.9 billion euros. Within this market, residential transaction volumes are expected to decrease by 2% to 210.7 billion euros, indicating that liquidity remains severely constrained for landlords seeking deleveraging.
3.
Subdued quarterly: German investment markets experienced a sharp deceleration in transaction volumes during the third quarter of 2026. Jefferies reported on October 5, 2026, that total commercial and residential transactions reached between 21.4 billion and 23.9 billion euros in the first nine months, but the third quarter contributed only 4.4 billion to 6.5 billion euros. This low volume indicates that the transaction market is unable to support rapid deleveraging without forcing landlords to accept steep discounts.
4.
Slow recovery: German transaction markets face a protracted adjustment process that mirrors the slow recovery observed during the previous interest rate shock. Jefferies explained on October 26, 2026, that the prior market downturn took 18 months to slide from its 2022 peak to its late 2023 trough, and required nearly three years to return near its historical average. This historical precedent suggests that residential volumes will remain low for longer, limiting Vonovia's capacity to execute its 5 billion euro disposal target.
5.
Contrary evidence: German mortgage volumes outperformed the contracting transaction market during the third quarter of 2026 despite elevated borrowing costs. Deutsche Bank reported on October 22, 2026, that Hypoport's Europace platform achieved stable mortgage transaction volumes year-on-year, showing unexpected resilience. This stabilization suggests that underlying homebuyer demand is beginning to bottom out across Germany, which could eventually revive broader market liquidity.

Active Buyer Profiles

6.
Exit pressures: Private equity funds are experiencing intense pressure to liquidate German residential holdings to return capital to their limited partners. Jefferies reported on September 3, 2026, that private equity funds launched between 2016 and 2018 have distributed less than 50% of their capital back to investors. This bottleneck is expected to trigger a wave of secondary transactions, forcing asset managers to accept higher underwriting yields that lower overall portfolio valuations.
7.
Forced liquidations: Private residential portfolios face unstructured liquidations as insolvency administrators abandon refinancing options in favor of asset sales. Jefferies reported on September 3, 2026, that the insolvency administrator for Noratis plans to liquidate its portfolio of 3,650 residential units valued at 370 million euros. Executing such large-scale liquidations in a thin market will depress regional property values and raise write-down risks for peers.
8.
Redemption pressure: Open-ended German property funds are preparing large-scale asset disposals to manage mounting investor redemption requests. Jefferies reported on September 17, 2026, that DWS has completed 4.5 billion euros of asset sales since 2022 for its property funds but expects a new round of disposals. This persistent supply overhang from institutional sellers will suppress average book values and dilute earnings growth.
9.
Municipal support: Municipal housing companies remain the primary buyers supporting baseline valuations for new residential developments. Deutsche Bank reported on November 14, 2025, that municipal buyers are actively acquiring newly built projects from distressed developers who are willing to sell at zero profit margin. While these transactions provide a floor for the market, they require highly subsidized components that limit their applicability to commercial portfolios.

Capital Costs and Yield Pressures

10.
Elevated borrowing: German residential transactions are severely constrained by mortgage borrowing costs that have reached a ten-year peak. Deutsche Bank reported on October 22, 2026, that long-term government bond yields have pushed average mortgage interest rates to approximately 4.5%. These elevated rates have caused new housing loans to contract by 4% year-on-year, which prevents a transaction volume recovery unless rates fall.
11.
Yield expansion: Property appraisal yields are expanding across continental European hubs, eroding the net asset values of major residential platforms. Jefferies reported on October 22, 2026, that residential prime yields expanded by 30 basis points in key German markets such as Berlin and Duesseldorf. This upward pressure on cap rates reduces reported book values, making it increasingly difficult for landlords to deleverage without realizing capital losses.
12.
Stable pricing: German residential property prices have exhibited surprising resilience despite the steep contraction in transaction liquidity. ING Wholesale Banking reported on September 29, 2026, that house prices actually rose by 0.3% quarter-on-quarter in the second quarter of 2026. This price growth in a thin market is driven by chronic structural housing shortages, which help insulate landlords from immediate balancesheet write-downs.
13.
Other side of the story: Residential landlords may successfully defend their balance sheets through strong rental growth that offsets expanding yields. Deutsche Bank reported on August 17, 2026, that although property price momentum has softened, robust rental growth continues to support operational earnings. If rental growth persists, landlords can stabilize loan-to-value ratios and maintain investment-grade ratings without being forced to sell portfolios at a discount to book.
