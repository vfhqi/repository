1.

2.
Dominant market share: Morgan Stanley estimates that the top three European manufacturers control eighty percent of the market, creating high barriers to entry for newer competitors
3.
Massive order backlogs: Prysmian enters the second half of 2026 with a massive transmission segment seventeen billion euros backlog that provides unprecedented multi-year revenue visibility
4.
Extended lead times: An expert from a UK transmission network operator highlighted that cable lead times have ballooned to thirty-six months, delaying several major renewable energy integration projects
5.
Ambitious capacity additions: NKT is executing a two billion euro investment program to expand its Karlskrona factory, which is expected to commence commercial operations in mid-2027
6.
Underestimated pricing power: Jefferies Research argues that the market severely underestimates the structural pricing power of established players who are delivering on highly lucrative backlogs

7.

9.
Targeted regional expansion: South Korean supplier LS Cable has expanded outside Asia by investing nine hundred and twenty-three million pounds in a new subsea cable factory in the United Kingdom

Section 8 – Second and third-order implications 5 findings

Section summary
  1. Successful HVDC execution will unlock substantial cross-business financial synergies and expand Cenergy's valuation multiple toward premium European peers.
  2. Conversely, prolonged qualification delays risk impairing balance sheet capacity and locking the cables segment out of the critical 2026-2028 extra-high voltage grid tender window.

Cross-pillar and valuation implications

1.
Valuation multiple expansion: Achieving a commercial 525 kV HVDC award would likely close the valuation gap between Cenergy and its pure-play cable peers. DNB Carnegie notes that NKT trades at a 22% valuation premium to Cenergy Holdings on 2028 estimated Price-to-Earnings ratios. Valuation multiple expansion (cont.): This discount reflects Cenergy's smaller scale and its integrated steel pipes segment. A successful 525 kV reference project would demonstrate Cenergy's technical parity with premium European high-voltage players. Consequently, this milestone would support a rerating of Cenergy's Cables segment from a mid-tier regional supplier to a core European utility partner. Strategic asset utilization: Increased high-voltage subsea orders will drive maximum capacity utilization at Hellenic Cables' specialized manufacturing facilities. Cenergy's corporate filings show that the submarine cables unit in Corinth is already operating on a high-utilization schedule of three shifts, six days a week. Securing premium 525 kV subsea contracts would maximize this asset utilization while shifting the project mix toward higher-margin work. The resulting operating leverage would support Cenergy's upgraded financial targets, which include an adjusted EBITDA guidance range of EUR 390 million to EUR 420 million
2.
Balance sheet pressure: The massive capital requirements of extra-high voltage projects present a significant risk of balance sheet stretch. Jefferies Research highlights that Cenergy's smaller balance sheet limits its capacity to support the heavy guarantees required for multi-billion euro turnkey contracts. If the company aggressively pursues multiple large-scale 525 kV tenders simultaneously, the associated working capital requirements and performance bonds could strain its liquidity. This capital constraint might force Cenergy to restrict its bidding activity or raise additional equity, potentially diluting near-term investor returns. Cross-segment pipeline pull-through: Success in securing offshore HVDC interconnector awards can create downstream demand for Cenergy's Steel Pipes segment. Corinth Pipeworks has built a strong position in energy transition infrastructure, recently securing the line pipe supply package for the Rovuma LNG project

Large subsea electrical interconnectors often share marine corridors and regulatory permitting pathways with offshore gas, carbon capture, or hydrogen pipelines. By establishing early engineering relationships with major TSOs like Terna or IPTO, Cenergy can position Corinth Pipeworks to capture adjacent pipeline infrastructure tenders.

Value chain and actor dynamics

3.
TSO bargaining power shift: Conservative transmission operators have a strong motive to accelerate Cenergy's qualification to break the pricing power of dominant suppliers. Morgan Stanley notes that the top three European manufacturers control eighty percent of the high-voltage market. This high consolidation has allowed incumbent suppliers to extract meaningful price-led margin expansion on new order wins. To combat this price exhaustion, TSOs like TenneT and Amprion are actively structuring tenders to bring in alternative qualified suppliers. By supporting Cenergy's entry into the 525 kV segment, TSOs can restore competitive bidding tension and lower their long-term grid build-out costs. Marine consortium dependencies: Cenergy's lack of a dedicated cable-laying fleet creates a structural vulnerability that benefits marine installation contractors. Jefferies Research emphasizes that Hellenic Cables' key disadvantage is its lack of direct vessel ownership compared to integrated peers like Nexans. To execute turnkey EPCI contracts, Cenergy must partner with third-party offshore contractors such as Deme Offshore or Jan De Nul. In a tight offshore construction market, these vessel operators hold significant bargaining power. Consequently, any inflation in vessel charter rates or delays in vessel availability could squeeze Cenergy's project margins. Competitive defensive pricing: Established tier-one players may deploy targeted pricing strategies to protect their market share in core regions. Morgan Stanley points out that players like Prysmian have built highly lucrative and resilient order backlogs. If a new entrant like Cenergy threatens their dominant position in key European corridors, these incumbents have the financial flexibility to selectively discount their bids. Competitive defensive pricing (cont.): This defensive pricing behavior could force Cenergy to accept lower initial margins on its first 525 kV awards to build a commercial track record
4.
Outlier: Component standardization constraints: A non-obvious bottleneck for Cenergy's high-voltage expansion lies in its reliance on third-party accessories. NKT's research and development division notes that players like Hellenic Cables do not design or produce their own accessories for extra-high voltage systems. Instead, they rely on specialized component suppliers like Pfisterer to provide compatible cable joints and terminations. If utilities enforce strict standardization rules requiring single-source, fully integrated cable and accessory systems, Cenergy's bidding eligibility could be severely restricted
5.
Other side of the story: Focusing too heavily on high-margin 525 kV tail scenarios may cause analysts to overlook Cenergy's execution risks in its core medium-voltage and land cable segments. The company's recent results show that its solid performance is heavily supported by healthy demand for standard cable products and local grid upgrades. Over-allocating capital and engineering resources to secure a premium HVDC trophy asset could distract management from delivering on standard infrastructure agreements, such as its eight-year framework with Alliander
