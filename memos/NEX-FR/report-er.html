Section 16 – Conclusion

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Nexans reported its interim financial results for the first half of 2026 on July 29, 2026. The company delivered a solid performance that significantly outperformed sell-side expectations, with Q2 organic growth of 2.7% substantially beating the compiled consensus forecast of negative 1.2% contraction. Group adjusted EBITDA reached €387.7 million, representing a 7% beat against the compiled broker consensus of €364 million. Following this strong performance, management upgraded its full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance range to €770-840 million, up from the previous range of €730-810 million.

The most significant positive deviation from expectations emerged in the PWR-Transmission segment, where adjusted EBITDA of €107 million beat consensus of €91 million by 17%, with the 13.7% margin landing 180 basis points ahead of consensus. This margin expansion proved that Nexans' value-over-volume selectivity strategy is successfully protecting profitability despite flat top-line growth. The group also generated free cash flow of €165.5 million in the first half, shattering the sell-side consensus estimate of €70 million. This cash delivery demonstrated exceptional earnings quality and working capital efficiency, removing a key point of investor concern heading into the print. However, not all segments delivered uniformly positive surprises.

PWR-Connect adjusted EBITDA margins fell from 13.6% in H1 2025 to 11.8% in H1 2026, substantially missing Bernstein's compiled consensus expectation of 12.2% due to continued Nordic headwinds. The geographic mix challenge in Connect remains a structural headwind, as rapid volume growth in lower-margin Southern European markets combined with persistent stagnation in the high-margin Nordic building sector acted as a severe profitability drag.

PWR-Grid's 15.4% margin missed consensus expectations of 15.8% by 40 basis points, which Jefferies attributed to near-term project mix. The single most important change in the investment thesis is the structural de-risking of Nexans' subsea transmission backlog. To mitigate the postponement of the €1.2 billion Great Sea Interconnector, Nexans secured preferred bidder status for a replacement subsea project in July 2026, which is expected to keep the massimpregnated transmission line fully loaded until mid-2028 with similar profitability.

This operational milestone removes the severe capacity utilization overhang that had weighed on the stock for over twelve months and validates management's ability to navigate geopolitical project delays. The successful commissioning of the Nexans Electra cable-laying vessel in June 2026 further strengthens execution capabilities and eliminates expensive third-party rental costs. A critical debate persists regarding whether the guidance upgrade represents a structural re-rating or a cyclical peak.

Analysts at Bernstein noted that the €35 million guidance midpoint upgrade was almost entirely driven by the inclusion of the newly acquired Republic Wire business, rather than underlying organic improvements. Bulls argue that Nexans has successfully completed its long-term strategic transformation to become a global pure player in sustainable electrification cable systems, with three core business segments: PWR Transmission, PWR Grid, and PWR Connect.

This pure-play positioning, combined with massive European grid modernization spending and accelerating US data center demand, provides structural multi-year growth visibility.

Bears counter that Nexans' smaller and narrower high-voltage subsea backlog exposes it to heightened project concentration risk relative to Prysmian. If geopolitical conflicts in the Mediterranean permanently derail key interconnection tenders or if the high-margin Nordic residential market fails to recover, the company could face prolonged under-utilization at core plants, severely undermining its ambitious 2028 EBITDA target of €1,150 million.

Deutsche Bank analyst Nabil Najeeb models FY28 group EBITDA at only €933 million, reflecting deep skepticism regarding organic execution limits and warning that if geopolitical delays disrupt subsea cable execution or Nordic building markets fail to recover, the group's highly selective margin strategy could lead to structural under-utilization. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bull case centers on margin sustainability beyond the current capacityconstrained environment.

While transmission margins reached an impressive 13.7% in H1 2026, this performance occurred during a period of unprecedented industry-wide supply shortages. A former chief financial officer at Prysmian Group pointed out that pricing in the low-voltage and medium-voltage markets is structurally vulnerable to normalization as post-pandemic supply shortages ease, and rising competition from mid-sized players will likely lead to a gradual reduction in margins from recent highs. If massive industry-wide capacity additions across Prysmian, NKT, and emerging Asian competitors come online simultaneously after 2028, structural overcapacity could reverse favorable pricing dynamics and compress the elevated margins that underpin current valuation multiples.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: The investment thesis would fundamentally shift if any of three critical variables deteriorate. First, if the replacement Mediterranean subsea contract fails to convert into a signed firm order by the end of Q1 2027, capacity utilization concerns at the Halden plant would re-emerge, threatening the high-teens transmission margin trajectory. Second, if Nordic residential building permits fail to inflect positively by mid-2027, the persistent margin dilution in PWR-Connect would validate concerns that short-cycle profitability has structurally peaked.

Third, if Republic Wire integration encounters unexpected execution delays or fails to deliver the promised €23.6 million in run-rate synergies within three years, it would cast doubt on management's M&A capabilities and undermine confidence in the intensification strategy. Investors should closely monitor four key variables over the next six to twelve months. First, track quarterly subsea backlog evolution and conversion of preferred bidder status into firm contracts to assess whether transmission visibility extends beyond mid-2028.

Second, monitor PWR-Connect segment margins on a sequential quarterly basis to determine whether geographic mix headwinds are stabilizing or accelerating. Third, observe European residential building permit data particularly in Sweden, Norway, and Finland to gauge the timing and magnitude of any Nordic recovery. Fourth, scrutinize Republic Wire quarterly EBITDA contribution and explicit synergy realization metrics to validate whether the acquisition is performing in line with the aggressive integration plan.

The next major inflection point will arrive at the full-year 2026 results in late February 2027, when management will provide comprehensive guidance for 2027 and update its 2028 Capital Markets Day trajectory. The market will demand explicit proof that organic EBITDA expansion can continue without relying on further inorganic contributions. Any signal that the 2028 target of €1,150 million remains achievable purely through organic execution would likely trigger a significant re-rating, while indications that additional M&A is required to bridge the gap would reinforce concerns that the current earnings profile represents a cyclical peak rather than a sustainable structural platform.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Nexans' H1 2026 earnings delivered a solid EBITDA and cash flow beat against consensus, with group adjusted EBITDA reaching €387.7 million representing a 7% beat against the compiled broker consensus of €364 million, and Q2 organic growth of 2.7% significantly outperforming the compiled sell-side consensus which was anticipating an organic contraction of 1.2% in the quarter. Following the strong performance, management upgraded its full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance range to €770-840 million, up from the previous range of €730-810 million.

However, the single most important change to the investment thesis is not this numerical guidance upgrade, but rather the strategic de-risking of subsea project execution through securing preferred bidder status in July 2026 for a replacement Mediterranean interconnector, successfully loading the high-voltage Mass-Impregnated subsea production line until mid-2028.

This capacity loading resolution fundamentally transforms the near-term risk profile by removing the most persistent overhang that had constrained valuation multiples throughout the first half of 2026. Despite PWR- Transmission standard sales coming in flat at €776.8 million organically in the first half, the segment's adjusted EBITDA rose by 21.2% to €106.5 million, pushing segment margins up by 195 basis points to 13.7%.

This margin expansion validates management's assertion that the group's value-over-volume selectivity strategy is successfully protecting profitability even during planned transmission normalization cycles. The completion of the Autoelectric divestment on July 3, 2026 officially exits the group from its last remaining non-electrification business, realizing its long-term ambition of becoming a global pure-player. Combined with the acquisition of Republic Wire in June 2026 for a total enterprise value of approximately €680 million providing Nexans with a major low-voltage manufacturing platform and an extensive distributor network in the United States the company now operates as a structurally simplified, pure-play electrification leader positioned to capture secular energy transition demand.

The strongest counter-hypothesis challenging this optimistic view centers on whether Nexans has reached a cyclical earnings peak rather than a permanent structural re-rating. Analysts at Bernstein noted that the €35 million guidance midpoint upgrade was almost entirely driven by the inclusion of the newly acquired Republic Wire business, rather than underlying organic improvements. Despite PWR-Connect standard sales reaching €1,374.9 million with robust 7.3% organic growth, Connect adjusted EBITDA margins fell from 13.6% in H1 2025 to 11.8% in H1 2026, substantially missing Bernstein's compiled consensus expectation of 12.2% due to continued Nordic headwinds.

Demand was highly dynamic across Southern Europe and Latin America, driven by strong residential volumes in Italy, Spain, and France, however residential building activity in the high-margin Nordic countries remained deeply subdued with no near-term signs of recovery. This persistent geographical margin dilution suggests that short-cycle profitability remains structurally vulnerable to adverse construction market mix effects, potentially capping the group's ability to reach its ambitious 2028 EBITDA target of €1,150 million through organic expansion alone. Furthermore, persistently running a flat organic transmission growth profile while peers like Prysmian and NKT execute multi-billion euro capacity expansions could lead to a permanent loss of market share, and if Nexans' strict pricing discipline causes it to lose subsequent mega-tenders to highly integrated competitors, the group's long-term volume base could erode, leading to structural under-utilization and margin degradation after FY28.
