Section 5 – Historical Post-Overshoot Precedents: 2001 and 2022-23 Comparisons

Section summary
  1. Historical post-overshoot precedents show that structural demand eventually absorbs massive capacity excesses over multi-year horizons.
  2. Unlike the 2001 crash which left eighty-five percent of fiber dark, the 2022-23 post-pandemic cycle corrected rapidly within three years.
  3. Current structural drivers in late 2026 are supported by tight supply dynamics and unprecedented data center deployment volumes.

Post-2001 Telecom Bubble Crash

1.
Extreme capital spending: The late-1990s dot-com boom generated unprecedented capital investments in communications infrastructure. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 27 February 2025 that annual telecom industry capex peaked at approximately $120 billion in 1999-2000, which is equivalent to over $210 billion today. This massive capital deployment led to laying an estimated 80 million miles of fiber optic cable across the United States. Such immense overbuilding created severe manufacturing overcapacity that took years to digest, which means earnings per share for equipment makers collapsed dramatically when the bubble burst.
2.
Severe capacity underutilization: The aftermath of the 2001 telecom crash left the industry with massive unused physical assets. Morgan Stanley highlighted on 17 October 2025 that only 3% to 5% of the optical fiber laid during the boom was actually utilized by 2002. This left a staggering 85% of fiber lines dark as late as 2005. Bandwidth prices plunged by approximately 90% as a direct result of this massive physical glut. This prolonged oversupply prevented any near-term recovery in manufacturing volumes, which is why passive equipment pricing remained depressed for nearly half a decade.
3.
Comparable historic consolidation: Corporate casualties during the post-2001 downturn reshaped the competitive landscape of the telecommunications market. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 27 February 2025 that dozens of telecom operators like Global Crossing and WorldCom went bankrupt. Major equipment manufacturers like Lucent and Nortel collapsed as network construction orders dried up completely. This structural destruction indicates that prolonged capacity overshoots lead to extreme industry consolidation, which ultimately restructures the long-term supplier base before demand can stabilize.

Post-2022-23 Post-Pandemic Overshoot
