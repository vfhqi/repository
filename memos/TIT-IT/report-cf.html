EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 3 findings

MATERIALITY OF CHANGE

The materiality of organizational and structural change at Telecom Italia is exceptionally high, driven by the completion of the landmark NetCo divestment to KKR on July 1, 2024, which generated EUR 13.8 billion in net debt reduction and fundamentally transformed the company into a standalone customer-facing service operation. The historic infrastructure deal valued up to EUR 22 billion achieved a net debt reduction of EUR 13.8 billion for the group, with consolidated leverage reaching 1.94x net debt to EBITDA AL in Q2 2026.

This structural separation represents the single most consequential event in the company's recent history, effectively splitting TIM into a capital-light service company (ServCo) while creating permanent dependence on wholesale infrastructure provider FiberCop for network access. Beyond the balance sheet restructuring, governance complexity escalated dramatically when state-controlled Poste Italiane secured a 66.6% controlling stake in TIM following the first acceptance phase of its voluntary offer in September 2026, introducing a multi-layered conglomerate ownership structure that materially alters strategic flexibility and decision-making velocity.

Executive suite stability improved modestly with Pietro Labriola confirmed for the 2024-2026 term as Chief Executive Officer on April 23, 2024, though second-level organizational realignments continue as the company integrates sovereign cloud platforms and navigates the post-separation operating model. The cumulative effect of these transformations–structural separation, state-controlled consolidation, and wholesale network reliance– fundamentally redefines the risk-reward profile and demands ongoing scrutiny of execution capabilities under the new ownership regime.

TOP FORWARD EPS DRIVERS (RANKED) The forward earnings trajectory for Telecom Italia depends critically on the successful execution of five to eight distinct operational and structural drivers, each carrying materially different levels of plausibility, impact magnitude, and cross-dependencies. Ranked first, domestic retail repricing campaigns targeted 3.3 million fixed consumer lines and 2.1 million mobile lines, with consumer fixed ARPU rising 2% to EUR 33 monthly, demonstrating that back-book pricing adjustments are successfully stabilizing domestic consumer revenues despite intense competitive pressure from utility entrants offering fiber bundles as low as EUR 16.90 monthly.

Second, TIM Brasil postpaid customer lines grew 6.8% year on year, with postpaid accounting for 78% of mobile service revenue, establishing the Brazilian subsidiary as the primary engine of profitable cash generation and positioning it to deliver double-digit operating cash flow growth that funds parent-level distributions. Third, TIM Enterprise cloud revenues grew 18.1% year on year in the first half of 2026, with the contracted enterprise order backlog exceeding EUR 4.5 billion and TIM Enterprise operating 16 integrated proprietary data centers, securing medium-term revenue visibility through sovereign cloud contracts and public sector digital transformation programs backed by state funding.

Fourth, a preliminary RAN sharing agreement covers 15,500 existing mobile sites with joint ventures targeting 6,000 new towers, creating a robust network cost-out runway that aims to deliver approximately EUR 200 million in savings, though execution remains contingent on antitrust clearance and successful migration away from the high-cost INWIT tower platform over a ten-year timeline. Fifth, Italian mobile spectrum bands will expire in 2029, with AGCOM proposing annual fees of EUR 177 million for Telecom Italia's expiring frequencies and new regulations requiring 75% coverage of Italy with 150Mbps download speeds by 2031, representing a material regulatory cash outflow that simultaneously acts as a catalyst for industry consolidation but introduces significant near-term capital consumption.

Additional drivers include the realization of EUR 700 million in Poste Italiane synergies through cross-selling and platform integration, though granular operational visibility remains severely limited, and the successful defense against escalating FiberCop wholesale access fees, which represent an existential threat to domestic margin sustainability given the company's structural single-supplier dependence post-NetCo separation.

RECENT EARNINGS DELIVERY PERFORMANCE

Recent earnings delivery demonstrates operational stabilization at the group level, though performance remains bifurcated between robust international and enterprise segments versus persistently weak domestic consumer connectivity. TIM Group revenues reached EUR 13,734 million in FY25 representing a 3% organic growth CAGR, with TIM Brasil organic service revenue growing 6% year on year in H1 26 and TIM Enterprise service revenues rising 5.7% in H1 26, confirming that structural growth engines are functioning despite mature market saturation and demographic headwinds in Italy.

However, Consumer segment revenues declined 2.7% in H1 26 due to wholesale MVNO phasing contract losses, though consumer fixed ARPU grew 2% to EUR 33 per month supported by sustained consumer retail repricing campaigns, illustrating the persistent tension between volume declines and pricing discipline in the legacy retail business. Reported profitability remains heavily distorted by non-recurring items, as TIM collected EUR 1,012 million in June 2026 for the 1998 Concession Fee dispute while first-quarter earnings were compressed by EUR 210 million of non-recurring personnel provisions for voluntary early retirement exits, though organic EBITDA after lease grew 1.2% year on year in H1 26.

The most recent quarterly performance in Q2 2026 reinforced these trends, with group organic revenue growing modestly but fixed-line subscriber losses accelerating dramatically to negative 76,000 lines due to deteriorating wholesale network service quality from FiberCop, highlighting the operational vulnerability embedded in the post-separation structure. Management successfully delivered against full-year guidance for four consecutive years through FY25, establishing a credible track record, though the heavily back-end-loaded FY26 plan requires flawless second-half execution to offset weak first-half domestic performance.

FORWARD GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY AND MANAGEMENT ARCHETYPE

Management's forward guidance credibility is anchored by a proven four-year track record of consistent delivery against publicly stated targets, qualifying Pietro Labriola's executive team as honest trackers who have successfully navigated the complex NetCo separation and subsequent operational restructuring. Pietro Labriola highlighted that the NetCo milestone is also a new starting point, noting that 2025 confirms a method of execution, signaling a management philosophy that emphasizes incremental operational discipline over aggressive forward guidance sandbagging.

Current full-year FY26 targets call for group revenue growth of 2% to 3%, group EBITDA after lease expansion of 5% to 6%, and standalone equity free cash flow of EUR 1.8 billion, though these projections embed the one-time EUR 1.012 billion concession dispute reimbursement and require domestic EBITDA AL to rise approximately 4% despite first-half underperformance that saw domestic EBITDAaL fall 2.5% versus the 4% growth target.

The sensitivity of forward EPS to key operational variables is material, with revenue changes of plus or minus one percentage point driving approximately 8% EPS volatility, EBIT margin moves of 50 basis points impacting EPS by roughly 6.5%, and Brazilian Real foreign exchange fluctuations of 5% generating 5.4% EPS swings, creating meaningful downside risk if FiberCop wholesale costs escalate or domestic retail pricing power deteriorates further.

Base case EPS projections suggest EUR 0.15 by FY28 assuming modest cloud gains and stable wholesale economics, while bull case scenarios reaching EUR 0.25 depend entirely on the successful realization of EUR 700 million in Poste synergies, and bear case outcomes collapsing to negative EUR 0.05 reflect the catastrophic scenario where FiberCop wholesale cost increases accelerate beyond contractual protections. The archetype classification as an honest tracker rather than a sandbagging management team implies that forward guidance should be interpreted as realistic central-tendency estimates rather than conservatively beatable targets, increasing the probability that external shocks or execution stumbles could trigger meaningful negative revisions.

SELL-SIDE POSITIONING AND CONSENSUS

The sell-side analyst pool covering Telecom Italia is notably small and concentrated, with only three main brokers providing active coverage and nine named individual analysts publishing research, creating limited diversity of viewpoints and heightened sensitivity to individual rating changes. Consensus positioning reflects fundamental disagreement regarding standalone valuation versus takeout optionality, as price targets peak at a high of €6.42 at Deutsche Bank while Jefferies retains a target of €0.28 on ordinary shares, representing a very conservative standalone valuation path, illustrating the extreme dispersion in analyst base case assumptions regarding both intrinsic value and the probability of Poste Italiane completing its voluntary takeover. Deutsche Bank's coverage team, led by Keval Khiroya, Robert Grindle, and Roshan Ranjit, maintains a Hold recommendation arguing that the company is fairly valued as a standalone entity, with standalone domestic operations trading at an elevated 15.8x 2028 EV/OpFCF multiple that represents a massive premium to sector medians. Consensus narrative frameworks center on three competing scenarios: first, that the Poste Italiane tender offer of EUR 1.97 cash plus 0.218 shares presents an attractive exit option for minority shareholders facing persistent governance friction; second, that sovereign cloud synergies of EUR 700 million could unlock material hidden value if successfully executed; and third, that structural dependence on FiberCop wholesale fees combined with mature domestic market saturation renders the standalone equity story fundamentally challenged absent transformative M&A.

Rating migration trends show limited recent activity beyond Deutsche Bank's February 2026 downgrade from Buy to Hold following significant equity outperformance that fully priced in M&A optionality, while Jefferies maintains its Buy rating emphasizing that failure to deliver structural reform could force equity funding requirements. The narrow broker coverage universe and wide target price dispersion underscore the elevated uncertainty regarding forward earnings power and the ultimate resolution of the Poste Italiane consolidation, creating material information asymmetry risks for investors relying on sell-side consensus frameworks.

FUNDAMENTAL PEER POSITIONING

Telecom Italia's fundamental momentum positions it in the middle tier of European telecommunications peers, with group organic revenue growth of 2% in H1 2026 beating mature sector averages near 1% but trailing highperforming operators like Deutsche Telekom's 7.3% organic growth and Iliad's 9.2% expansion. Group organic revenue grew 2% in H1 2026 beating the mature sector average growth, though standalone domestic EBITDAaL margin reached 21% in FY25 lagging the peer median of 30%, exposing the structural margin disadvantage embedded in TIM's low-scale, high-complexity operating model relative to better-positioned incumbents.

Valuation metrics reveal material disconnects between trading multiples and fundamental cash generation, as domestic operations trade at 16.6x EV/OpFCF representing a massive premium vs the sector average, suggesting that equity prices embed significant synergy optionality or takeover premia rather than reflecting standalone intrinsic value. Working capital management represents a persistent area of operational weakness versus peers, with the group net payable balance reaching EUR 2 billion indicating aggressive supplier credit management, while severe working capital cash absorption weighed heavily on group cash generation limiting ultimate equity free cash flows vs peers.

Competitive positioning within Italy has deteriorated following the Swisscom acquisition of Vodafone Italy, which created a fully integrated competitor with unprecedented convergence capabilities across all segments, while utility entrants like Eni Plenitude and Enel continue disrupting fixed markets with aggressively priced fiber bundles. Pricing power comparisons show mixed signals, with TIM successfully executing back-book repricing campaigns targeting 3.3 million fixed consumer lines to support 2% ARPU growth, but facing intensifying pressure from low-cost competitors offering fiber broadband for EUR 16.90 versus national operators charging over EUR 20, compressing the sustainable pricing ceiling and limiting future repricing runway.

Relative earnings momentum ranks Telecom Italia third among the six-peer comparison set, with Orange generating the strongest upward EBITDA revisions year-to-date to lead the European peer group, Deutsche Telekom securing second position on robust U.S. operations, and TIM outperforming only the weaker Swiss and Italian domestic peers.

ACTIVIST PRESENCE AND SHAREHOLDER DYNAMICS

Historical activist and blockholder dynamics have been tumultuous and disruptive, though recent developments suggest resolution as former core agitators exit and alternative restructuring proposals are systematically rejected. Vivendi progressively built its position to 23.75% of ordinary shares, launched a lawsuit seeking to annul the NetCo sale which was declared inadmissible due to lack of standing in January 2025, and finalized the sale of its remaining stake to state-controlled Poste Italiane in December 2025, closing Vivendi's turbulent ten-year involvement, removing the single largest source of board opposition and strategic friction that had paralyzed previous management teams. Merlyn Partners launched a secondary activist campaign in early 2024 after acquiring a 0.53% stake, demanding retention of NetCo and immediate sale of TIM Brasil and domestic consumer assets, but TIM's board formally rejected Merlyn's proposal as inconsistent with delayering targets, with Merlyn ultimately failing to prevent the NetCo sale while maintaining ongoing pressure to divest TIM Brasil by 2025. Current shareholder structure following Poste Italiane's successful voluntary tender offer shows the state- controlled entity holding a commanding 66.6% controlling stake, effectively eliminating traditional activist opportunities but introducing new governance complexity risks as Deutsche Bank Research suggests that managing a listed TIM with minority investors could create persistent governance friction and strategic constraints that limit Poste's ability to unify customer-facing digital platforms. No other investors hold disclosed stakes exceeding the 3% materiality threshold that would indicate active public campaigns or board representation efforts, suggesting that the shareholder base has largely stabilized around the expectation that Poste Italiane will either complete a full takeout or manage TIM as a controlled subsidiary within a broader statebacked conglomerate structure. The absence of traditional activist pressure materially reduces near-term catalysts for forced asset sales, accelerated cost restructuring, or management changes, shifting the primary execution risk from external agitation to internal integration capability and state-imposed strategic objectives.

MATERIAL RISKS (CROSS-REFERENCED)

1.
First material risk: Catastrophic wholesale cost escalation from FiberCop single-supplier dependence [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 3, Section 4]. The post-NetCo separation operating model creates existential vulnerability to escalating wholesale

connectivity fees from FiberCop, which supplies substantially all of TIM's fixed network access under a 15-year Master Services Agreement, with no viable alternative infrastructure or credible competitive supply options given Italy's fragmented regional fiber footprint. TIM signed a 15-year Master Services Agreement to purchase core fixed wholesale access from FiberCop, though worsening network service quality from FiberCop negatively affected domestic fixed additions in 2026, demonstrating that operational execution failures at the separated infrastructure entity immediately translate into retail customer churn and revenue losses at ServCo.

The structural dependence amplifies pricing power asymmetry, as FiberCop can systematically extract value through annual tariff increases, quality degradation that forces TIM to offer compensatory customer discounts, or delayed installation and maintenance cycles that drive subscriber defections to fully integrated competitors like the merged Fastweb-Vodafone entity. Quantitatively, standalone domestic operations already yield a weak unlevered free cash flow of only 3.5% excluding the cash-generative TIM Brasil subsidiary, meaning that even modest wholesale fee escalations of 5% to 10% annually would rapidly eliminate standalone equity value and force either massive customer price increases that accelerate churn or permanent cash burn requiring parental support or equity dilution.

The risk magnitude intensifies because contractual protections under the MSA are opaque, wholesale cost sensitivities are not publicly disclosed with sufficient granularity, and FiberCop's own capital intensity requirements following KKR's acquisition may necessitate aggressive fee increases to generate acceptable returns on the acquired infrastructure asset base.

2.
Second material risk: Poste Italiane integration execution failure and conglomerate complexity paralysis [Cross- ref: Section 1, Section 2, Section 10]. The state-controlled consolidation introduces severe governance and operational integration

risks that could paralyze commercial execution and destroy standalone value for minority shareholders who remain post-tender. Poste plans to integrate TIM into its group to create a state-backed platform company, though Deutsche Bank Research suggests that managing a listed TIM with minority investors could create persistent governance friction and might limit Poste's ability to unify customer-facing digital platforms, highlighting fundamental tensions between state-directed strategic mandates, public interest objectives, and commercial profit maximization that could systematically subordinate TIM's standalone value creation.

The EUR 700 million synergy target lacks granular operational visibility and may prove unachievable if customer overlap is lower than assumed, if regulatory constraints prevent aggressive cross-selling of postal products to telecom subscribers, or if cultural integration challenges create employee attrition and productivity losses during the multiyear combination period. Conglomerate complexity risks extend beyond traditional integration execution to include conflicting public policy agendas around digital sovereignty, national security golden power restrictions, employment preservation mandates, and regional development requirements that could force suboptimal capital allocation decisions or delay critical cost restructurings.

The incomplete nature of the tender offer, which secured only 66.6% acceptance rather than the 90% threshold typically required for compulsory squeeze-out, perpetuates residual minority shareholder populations that retain legal rights to challenge related-party transactions, object to asset transfers between Poste and TIM, and demand fair treatment in any future reorganizations, creating ongoing litigation exposure and governance friction that increases transaction costs and execution uncertainty.

3.
Third material risk: 2029 spectrum renewal capital shock and regulatory intervention disruption [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 9]. The imminent expiration of approximately 70% of Italian mobile spectrum in 2029 represents a material

regulatory-driven capital consumption event that simultaneously acts as a consolidation catalyst but introduces severe near-term cash outflow risks and coverage obligation burdens that could overwhelm standalone equity free cash flow generation. Italian mobile spectrum bands will expire in 2029, with AGCOM proposing annual fees of EUR 177 million for Telecom Italia's expiring frequencies and new regulations requiring 75% coverage of Italy with 150Mbps download speeds by 2031, creating a dual pressure of EUR 177 million in recurring annual cash costs plus incremental capital expenditures for network densification to meet the stringent 150Mbps coverage mandate across 75% of Italian geography within a compressed five-year timeline.

Standard cash auction scenarios project total industry outlays of approximately EUR 2.6 billion for 15-year licenses, suggesting that TIM's proportional share could reach EUR 800 million to EUR 1 billion in upfront payments if AGCOM elects to conduct traditional competitive auctions rather than administrative renewals, representing roughly 40% to 50% of current equity market capitalization and exceeding standalone annual equity free cash flow generation excluding one-time regulatory windfalls.

Regulatory intervention risks extend beyond spectrum renewal mechanics to include ongoing antitrust scrutiny of the 5G RAN sharing agreement with Fastweb and Vodafone, which faces a competition authority investigation scheduled to conclude in April 2027, potentially delaying EUR 200 million in anticipated network cost synergies and forcing structural modifications to the cooperation framework that reduce efficiency benefits or require divestiture of jointly operated assets.

The compound probability that multiple regulatory interventions–spectrum cost shocks, antitrust remedies, and coverage mandate capital requirements– occur simultaneously creates catastrophic downside scenarios where standalone cash generation proves insufficient to fund mandatory obligations, forcing either emergency capital raises at dilutive valuations, asset sales that crystallize losses, or parental bailouts from Poste Italiane that further subordinate minority shareholder interests.

Plausible problems to monitor

Domestic Consumer Revenue Decline Steepening Despite Repricing Campaigns: TIM Consumer service revenues contracted 3.9% year on year in Q2 2026, worsening materially from the 1.8% decline recorded in Q1 2026, despite management executing back-book repricing campaigns targeting 3.3 million fixed lines and 2.1 million mobile lines. This deteriorating trend lags the peer median of flat to low-single-digit growth for European integrated operators and suggests that wholesale MVNO contract losses and competitive utility entrants offering fiber bundles as low as EUR 16.90 monthly are overwhelming domestic pricing initiatives.

The inflection from stabilization to accelerating decline raises material concerns about the achievability of management's 1% to 2% domestic service revenue growth guidance for FY26. [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 7] Enterprise Connectivity Revenue Reversal Eroding Blended Segment Performance: TIM Enterprise connectivity revenues reversed from 0.4% growth in Q1 2026 to a 2.9% contraction in Q2 2026, offsetting the otherwise robust 21.4% year-on-year cloud revenue expansion.

This negative inflection in the higher-margin traditional connectivity business contradicts the low-single-digit positive growth trajectory maintained by comparable European B2B-focused operators and indicates that the newly integrated Fastweb-Vodafone competitor is rapidly closing technical capability gaps in the domestic enterprise market. If this connectivity erosion persists, the highly contracted EUR 4.5 billion enterprise backlog may prove insufficient to sustain overall segment growth momentum. [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 9]

Domestic EBITDA After Lease Trajectory Falling Short of Full-Year Target: Domestic EBITDA after lease declined 2.5% year on year in H1 2026, creating a substantial gap versus management's 4% growth target required to meet FY26 group guidance. This underperformance relative to plan significantly lags the peer median of stable to modest margin expansion achieved by operators like Swisscom (34% EBITDA margin) and Orange (30% EBITDA margin) and implies that back-end loaded opex reductions must materialize flawlessly in H2 2026 to avoid a guidance miss.

The low 21% standalone domestic EBITDA margin already trails European peers by nearly 900 basis points, leaving minimal execution buffer if wholesale cost pressures from FiberCop escalate further.

Persistent Trade Payables Overhang Signaling Structural Working Capital Risk: Group net trade payables reached EUR 2 billion, representing an elevated supplier financing position that materially exceeds traditional European telecom peer norms and suggests aggressive payment term management to preserve liquidity. This stretched payables position indicates that severe working capital cash absorption is constraining ultimate equity free cash flow generation, as noted by Bernstein Research, and creates material counterparty risk if key suppliers such as FiberCop or tower infrastructure partners impose stricter payment conditions.

The reliance on extended payables to meet EUR 1.8 billion FY26 equity FCF guidance (which includes EUR 1 billion of non-recurring concession fee receipts ) raises concerns about the sustainability of organic cash generation absent regulatory windfalls. [Cross-ref: Section 7, Section 4]

Brazilian Currency Volatility Creating Hidden Leverage Re-Gearing Risk: The Brazilian real trades at elevated riskfree rates with 10-year bond yields of 13.6% compared to Italy's 3.4%, introducing significant foreign exchange sensitivity to consolidated earnings and leverage metrics. While TIM Brasil delivered robust 6% revenue growth in H1 2026, any material BRL depreciation beyond the R$/EUR 6.80 warning threshold would mechanically increase reported consolidated leverage above the 1.94x net debt to EBITDA AL ratio achieved in Q2 2026.

This currency exposure is particularly concerning given that standalone domestic operations carry 3.5x leverage excluding Brazil, meaning adverse FX moves could force the domestic Italian rump above sustainable debt service

Antitrust Investigation Delaying Critical Network Sharing Cost Synergies: The AGCM competition authority's investigation into the 5G RAN sharing agreement with Fastweb and Vodafone is scheduled to conclude only in April 2027, creating a multi-year delay in realizing anticipated EUR 200 million annual network cost savings. This protracted regulatory clearance timeline significantly exceeds the typical 12-18 month antitrust review periods for European network sharing deals and introduces execution risk to management's 5% to 6% EBITDA AL growth target for FY26, which implicitly assumes timely cost-out delivery.

If antitrust authorities impose structural remedies or block the arrangement entirely, TIM would remain locked into high-cost legacy tower contracts with INWIT despite pursuing a ten-year migration exit plan, permanently impairing standalone domestic margins relative to the 30% peer median EBITDA margin. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 3]

Outliers worth noting

Telecom Italia exhibits several statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies that deviate materially from peer base rates and historical norms, warranting heightened scrutiny across financial, operational, and valuation dimensions. Extreme standalone leverage disparity emerges when isolating the domestic Italian rump, which carries leverage of 3.5x net debt to EBITDA AL excluding cash-rich TIM Brasil, representing a significant premium to the consolidated group ratio of 1.94x and substantially exceeding the sector norm of approximately 2.0x to 2.5x for investment-grade European telecommunications operators.

This bifurcated capital structure creates an unexplained mismatch between the group's reported deleveraging success and the underlying financial fragility of the core domestic ServCo entity, particularly when considering the structural reliance on wholesale connectivity from a single supplier. [Cross-ref: Section 4] Premium relative valuation anomaly surfaces in the domestic operations trading at 16.6x EV to operating free cash flow, representing a massive premium versus the European telecommunications sector average of approximately 10x to 12x for comparable mature fixed-line service providers.

This valuation multiple appears disconnected from the segment's deteriorating operational fundamentals, including a depressed EBITDAaL margin of 21% in FY25 that materially lags the peer median of 30% and persistent subscriber churn driven by wholesale service quality issues. The elevated trading multiple lacks rational anchoring to either organic cash generation capacity or operational momentum relative to peers. [Cross-ref: Section 7] Persistent structural cost of debt stands at 7.3% on a reported basis post-NetCo transaction, representing an outlier burden approximately 300 to 400 basis points above European telecommunications peer averages of 3.5% to 4.0% despite the group achieving investment-grade credit ratings and executing a historic EUR 13.8 billion deleveraging.

This unexplained interest rate premium suggests either unfavorable legacy debt covenants that were not refinanced during the NetCo proceeds deployment or structural credit concerns regarding the standalone ServCo's cash generation capacity that are not reflected in the reported leverage ratio. [Cross-ref: Section 4]

Elevated trade payables position reached EUR 2 billion on the group balance sheet, indicating aggressive supplier credit management that materially exceeds traditional peer working capital structures typically maintaining trade payables in the range of 30 to 45 days sales outstanding. This stretched supplier financing position represents either an unexplained structural liquidity constraint inconsistent with the group's reported deleveraged balance sheet or a deliberate working capital management strategy that carries substantial operational risk if key infrastructure suppliers demand accelerated payment terms.

The anomaly becomes more pronounced when juxtaposed with the EUR 700 million equity free cash flow reported in H1 2026 that was materially boosted by non-recurring concession fee collections rather than sustainable operating cash conversion. [Cross-ref: Section 7]
