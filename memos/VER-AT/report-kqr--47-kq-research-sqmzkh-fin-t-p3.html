Section 2 – The Hedging and Drought Loop

Section summary
  1. VERBUND AG's advanced hedging strategy acts as a reinforcing negative loop during hydrological droughts, forcing costly spot-market electricity buybacks.
  2. High forward hedge ratios provide cash-flow visibility during normal water years but deepen operating losses when extreme drought limits generation.

Mechanics of the Hedging-Drought Loop

1.
Severe drought: Power generators face severe financial pressure when below-average hydrological inflows reduce generation below pre-hedged volumes. Bernstein Research on September 21, 2026, explained that hydro producers who hedge expected output far in advance must buy back power in the open market when physical generation falls short. This feedback loop increases spot procurement costs and dampens expected earnings per share during high-priced dry periods.
2.
Upward pressure: Spot power markets experience additional upward price pressure when multiple overhedged producers simultaneously buy back electricity during regional droughts. Bernstein Research on September 14, 2026, reported that VERBUND faced monthly gross buyback costs of €50m to €60m to cover its lower-thanexpected run-of-river hydro output. These forced market purchases can escalate wholesale prices and deepen cash-flow operating losses across the sector unless hydrology normalizes.
3.
Financial stress: Hydroelectric operators see their balance sheets deteriorate as negative free cash flows force them to accumulate short-term debt. VERBUND management reported during the Q3 2026 earnings call on November 5, 2026, that dry weather led to negative free cash flows and an increase in net debt and gearing. This liquidity strain raises financing costs and compresses earnings per share unless thermal or grid segments provide a substantial financial buffer.

VERBUND's Historical Experiences

4.
Subdued hydrology: VERBUND experienced significant earnings drag during the 2018 hydrological drought as run- of-river production fell far below historical averages. VERBUND management stated on March 13, 2019, that reported EBITDA fell 6.3% to €864m in 2018 due to decreased hydropower generation in the third and fourth quarters. This historical precedent highlights how poor water inflows depress core generation profitability despite ongoing efficiency gains.
5.
Severe shortfall: VERBUND faced a massive EBITDA headwind of €600m in the first nine months of 2026 due to extremely dry Alpine conditions. Deutsche Bank on September 3, 2026, reported that the cumulative hydro impact and buyback costs reached €600m year-to-date, up from €370m in the first half of the year. This hydrological deficit severely constrained core earnings recovery even though the company benefitted from strong power prices.
6.
Extreme drought: VERBUND's run-of-river hydro coefficient fell to an extremely low level of 0.61 during the first three quarters of 2026. VERBUND's interim group management report on November 5, 2026, showed that the hydro coefficient was 0.61, representing the lowest water supply in around 100 years. This extreme water scarcity forced the group to reduce own generation by 17.6% and buy back substantial volumes at high spot prices.

Section 3 – State Finances and Windfall Levies

Section summary
  1. Austria's deteriorating fiscal deficit increases state incentives to extract utility profits through special levies rather than traditional dividends.
  2. European precedents indicate that state-driven windfall levies create severe earnings asymmetry and limit utility capital reinvestment programs.

Sovereign Fiscal Pressure in Austria

1.
Deteriorating public: Austrian public debt is projected to escalate from 80.7% of GDP in 2025 to 84.2% by 2028. This negative trajectory is driven by persistent budget deficits that are forecast to reach 3.8% of GDP in 2027. These structural deficits increase the state's incentive to extract extra capital from utilities to stabilize sovereign debt dynamics before statutory limits are breached.

This capital requirement increases the fiscal pressure on the Austrian treasury to secure reliable income streams. Consequently, the government has focused on capturing utility rents through administrative measures that directly supplement the national budget rather than relying on standard economic growth.

Persistent regulatory: Austrian authorities extended the Energy Crisis Contribution electricity levy through 2030 to address structural budget deficits. This extended framework applies a 95% tax rate on revenues above predefined price thresholds. This long-term fiscal policy directly suppresses utility profit margins and restricts earnings per share recovery during periods of high electricity prices.

Targeted profit: Austria's government intends to raise approximately €400m annually from the domestic energy sector through 2030 via windfall tax mechanisms. High political: Austrian political debates during periods of high energy prices have historically amplified the risk of windfall tax interventions. This political pressure diminishes the predictability of earnings and raises the equity risk premium.

These retroactive fiscal raids elevated the cost of equity and depressed investor sentiment across the Southern European utility sector.
11.
Aggressive profit: Romania's government imposed a combination of 100% windfall taxes and price caps that significantly reduced utility profitability. SPEEH Hidroelectrica SA paid RON 672m in windfall profit taxes in 2022 and RON 231m in early 2023. These multi-year interventions diluted baseline earnings per share and highlighted the regulatory risks of operating state-owned generation assets.

Section 4 – Battery Entry and Flexibility Spreads

Section summary
  1. Rapid battery storage deployment across European and Australian markets compresses peak-to-trough price spreads and saturates ancillary service markets.
  2. Multi-day and seasonal pumped-storage assets preserve a robust premium over batteries during extended weather-driven generation deficits.

Spread Compression and Saturation Dynamics

1.
Rapid expansion: Declining battery technology costs have driven an unprecedented wave of utility-scale energy storage installations across global power systems. As battery storage capacities multiply, developers actively capture high intraday volatility by charging during low-price hours and discharging during peak periods. However, this rapid entry compresses the very peak-to-valley spreads that incentivized the investments in the first place, eroding long-term merchant profitability. Consequently, the industry is witnessing a structural shift where early storage margins quickly cannibalize their own revenue streams.
2.
Structural shifts: Power market volatility increasingly shifts from a simple capacity challenge to a complex intraday balancing problem. Stationary battery storage systems are highly suited to manage these shortduration fluctuations due to their modularity and rapid response times. But as batteries dominate short-term grid services, ancillary market prices experience severe downward pressure. This saturation forces operators to seek alternative revenue models, relying heavily on wholesale arbitrage and multi-market optimization.
3.
Asymmetric returns: Battery developers face highly unstable revenue streams as even minor capacity additions can lead to disproportionate price declines. While vanilla renewable projects secure predictable returns through long-term power purchase agreements, batteries must stack volatile merchant revenues. These volatile returns raise the cost of capital for standalone storage assets, discouraging traditional utility investments. Therefore, financial markets are increasingly favoring hybrid or co-located solar-plus-storage projects to stabilize cash flows.
4.
Contrary evidence: The rapid deployment of battery storage may not fully eliminate price volatility during extreme weather anomalies or gas supply disruptions. Jefferies Research on April 2, 2026, observed that while battery revenues had softened, UK day-ahead spreads spiked in March 2026 following geopolitical tensions. This indicates that global commodity pricing and fuel supply shocks can temporarily widen price spreads even in highly saturated storage markets. Consequently, batteries still retain periodic opportunities to capture outsized trading margins during system stress events.

Regional Saturation in Finland and Great Britain

5.
Sudden saturation: Finland's power grid has experienced rapid congestion due to a massive pipeline of fast- moving battery and industrial projects. The rapid installation of nearly 2 GW of battery capacity in Finland has caused a dramatic collapse in reserve market revenues. Specifically, Finnish reserve market prices plummeted from historical averages of €10 to €30/MWh down to far below €10/MWh. This extreme price compression illustrates how fast-moving storage can saturate local grid services within a single season.
6.
Market constraints: Finland's transmission system operator has initiated a comprehensive review of grid capacity allocation rules to address the bottleneck. By mid-August 2026, Fingrid had signed connection agreements covering around 5 GW of data centers and over 4 GW of electricity storage. This massive pipeline threatens to increase Finnish power demand by up to 40% by 2030. While rising demand offers long-term arbitrage potential, near-term grid bottlenecks severely limit new storage deployment.
7.
Eroding premiums: Great Britain's battery storage sector serves as a mature precedent for rapid revenue degradation following market saturation. Historically, high frequency response prices in 2021 and 2022 incentivized a massive influx of standalone battery systems. Once these ancillary markets became fully saturated in 2023 and 2024, benchmark revenues dropped sharply. This forced British batteries to shift their focus back toward wholesale trading arbitrage.
8.
Severe write-downs: The transition of British batteries from lucrative ancillary services to highly competitive wholesale trading has severely impacted fund valuations. Gresham House reported that downward revisions to forward battery revenue assumptions had a negative impact on net asset value per share of 66.30p since 2022. This adjustment represented a massive 58% reduction from the fund's historical net asset value. This valuation correction highlights that uncontracted battery storage investments face substantial merchant downside risks.

Battery Expansion in Germany and Australia

9.
Accelerating roll-out: Germany's utility-scale battery market is expanding rapidly as a wave of historical projects enters commercial operations. The country added a record 888 MW of grid-scale batteries in the first half of 2026, surpassing the total installations of 2025. Industry forecasts project German BESS capacity to grow from 3.4 GW in mid-2026 to 15.2 GW by the end of 2028. 176 177 This rapid build-out is expected to flatten Germany's intraday price spreads over the medium term.
10.
High volatility: German battery operations continue to capture lucrative returns in the near term despite the looming threat of saturation. German battery spreads averaged nearly €200/MWh in the second quarter of 2026, driven by frequent negative pricing hours. However, major developers like RWE AG acknowledge that this high profitability will not persist past 2030. Consequently, early movers are prioritizing rapid capital payback before German merchant battery revenues normalize.
11.
Dampened volatility: Australia's National Electricity Market represents the leading global case study of battery- driven spread cannibalization. Large-scale battery capacity has reached approximately 12% of total system capacity in Australia. This substantial battery fleet has successfully flattened pool price volatility and compressed evening peaks. In response, New South Wales cap prices fell significantly, creating earnings headwinds for traditional gentailers.
12.
Compressed spreads: Increased midday battery charging in Australia has successfully absorbed excess solar generation, reducing daytime curtailment. However, this continuous time-shifting has flattened the intraday price curve, with average wholesale prices declining 27% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026. In New South Wales, average price spreads compressed by AUD 33/MWh to a low of AUD 51/MWh. This rapid compression demonstrates that battery density limits peak pricing and erodes merchant arbitrage.

Value and Resilience of Multi-Day Pumped Storage

13.
Alpine advantages: VERBUND AG's Alpine hydropower fleet is uniquely insulated from the near-term spread cannibalization affecting pure-play battery developers. The company's reservoir-backed storage and pumpedstorage hydro assets provide immense operational flexibility. While batteries are highly efficient for shortterm balancing, they cannot compete with large-scale hydro across seasonal horizons. Consequently, VERBUND retains a strong capacity to capture value from seasonal spreads.
14.
Prolonged deficits: Hydropower's strategic moat is most apparent during prolonged periods of low wind and solar generation, known as Dunkelflaute events. In these circumstances, short-duration lithium-ion batteries are quickly exhausted within several hours. Conventional gas plants and large flexible hydro reservoirs must then step in to maintain system reliability. This long-duration resilience ensures that VERBUND's flexible hydro assets command a massive premium over short-duration batteries.
15.
Strategic backbone: Statkraft AS relies on its unique Nordic reservoir capacity to form the financial backbone of its generation portfolio. The company holds approximately half of Norway's reservoirs, representing Europe's largest independent storage capacity. This multi-week storage capability allows Statkraft to optimize generation over several years, achieving realized prices 12.6% higher than average spot prices. Consequently, long-duration hydro assets remain essential for structural grid stability.
16.
Technology limits: Lithium-ion batteries remain economically unviable for durations exceeding eight hours due to high incremental cell costs. Pumped-storage hydro projects possess useful advantages including fifty-year lifespans and established local supply chains. Although upfront capital expenditure is substantial, the long lifecycle yields a highly competitive levelized cost of storage. Therefore, multi-day pumped storage remains the preferred long-duration balancing technology in mountainous regions.
17.
Other side of the story: While flexible hydro assets maintain a clear moat in seasonal storage, the ongoing scaling of multi-day battery alternatives could challenge their long-term dominance. Jefferies Research on March 27, 2026, reported that Form Energy secured an agreement to deliver 12 GW of its 100-hour iron-air batteries to Crusoe starting in 2027. This industrial-scale deployment of low-cost, non-lithium technologies suggests that alternative long-duration battery systems could eventually erode hydro's multi-day flexibility premium.

Section 5 – Grid Asset Growth and Capex Levy Deductions

Section summary
  1. Regulated grid expansions provide utilities with highly predictable earnings growth that reduces volatility and lowers the consolidated cost of capital.
  2. Strategic capital reallocation toward regulated networks and domestic renewable capex offsets helps shield baseline utility earnings from volatile merchant markets.

8.
Effective shielding: Austria's windfall profit levy features structural tax incentives that actively steer utility capital expenditure back into the domestic economy. The Energy Crisis Contribution (EKB-S) allows utilities to claim a substantial tax deduction for investing in local renewable energy assets. This investment offset raises VERBUND's effective realized tax threshold from the statutory €90/MWh up to €103-105/MWh. As a result, the company's expected windfall levy liability for 2026 has been reduced to zero, directly preserving baseline earnings.

Regulatory steering: VERBUND AG's domestic capital allocation is directly influenced by the fiscal structure of the Austrian energy tax regime.
