Ballys Intralot (BYLOT-GR): what the nine-month results, and the fortnight since, do to the case

These results weaken the existing case modestly and in the way it expected: the guidance rebase it put at 65% has happened, by divisional reframing; the margin and the leverage are worse than it carried; the engine's demand is confirmed; and the shares have fallen about 10% toward the case's standalone value, which opens a question the case did not weigh.

What changed

  • Timing. Preliminary nine-month figures on 10 November 2026; the detailed interim statements, presentation and call on 20 November 2026. Nothing has been reported since.
  • The quarter. Third-quarter revenue EUR 286.1m; adjusted EBITDA EUR 88.9m; margin 31.1%, down 540 basis points from 36.5% a year earlier on the old perimeter. Nine months: revenue EUR 830.3m, adjusted EBITDA EUR 273.6m, margin 33.0% against 34.2%. Operating cash flow EUR 206.6m for nine months against EUR 79.1m. The case's quarterly marker for pulling the entry forward was EUR 97m; the quarter missed it.
  • Guidance. Revised on the 20 November call. Interactive full-year adjusted EBITDA is now EUR 270-280m, from EUR 280-295m, a midpoint cut of about EUR 12.5m or 4%; legacy is EUR 110-115m. Management cited slower regulatory approval of market consolidation and weaker US jackpot activity. The review carries no reaffirmation of the EUR 422m headline. The divisional ranges sum to EUR 380-395m. The case's own FY2026 figure is EUR 385m, inside that range; its divisional record was EUR 280-295m plus EUR 100-105m, so the cut fell wholly on the engine and the legacy half was nudged up.
  • The duty. Gross hit about EUR 34m a quarter, EUR 68m over nine months; about 55% mitigated in the third quarter against 65% in the second. Mitigation came from reduced promotional generosity and marketing efficiency. EUR 34m a quarter annualises to roughly EUR 136m; the case's annualised figure was EUR 127m. The review notes mitigation and tax figures differ across sources.

Pro forma adjusted net leverage 4.33x on twelve-month pro forma adjusted EBITDA of EUR 376.7m; the case was written on 4.05x. The challenge

The margin is the new level, not a lag: this reading has gained force. The interactive guidance was cut by EUR 12.5m at the midpoint while UK revenue grew 12.2% on record net gaming revenue. Volume is not the problem; conversion is. The mitigation rate fell from 65% to 55% with a full quarter of the generosity cuts in it, the quarterly margin is 31.1% against the case's 35-36% path, and the gross duty annualises above the case's figure. If that holds, the case's standalone EBITDA of EUR 400m and EUR 408m for FY2027 and FY2028 is too high even if the volume test is passed, and on the case's gearing each EUR 10m is about 4% of the price. The counter is management's own attribution: slower consolidation approvals and US jackpots, neither of which is a margin give-back. For the reading to be right, the third quarter has to carry no seasonal or one-off cost and the 55% has to be the run-rate. The review itself cites seasonal factors, so the reading is strong but not closed.

Leverage rises through the denominator: still open, slightly stronger. Leverage went from 4.05x to 4.33x with the Victoria payment and a lower trailing twelve months. If the fourth quarter resembles the third, FY2026 lands near EUR 363m, below both the guidance range and the case's EUR 385m, and leverage rises again with no change in debt. The new sterling money is priced at SONIA plus 5.5% and nine-month finance costs run at EUR 109.7m, so interest is not the lever that brings it down. Against the facilities test the case cites at 4.75x, headroom is about 0.4x. The objection is liquidity of EUR 472.4m and management's commitment to deleverage from FY2027; a negotiation with lenders is plausible, a liquidity event is not.

The rebase would pass without a price reaction: dropped. The shares fell about 10% to EUR 1.00 and are below both averages. The market reacted.

New reading: the rebase is a sequence, not an event. The case's first entry condition is "rebased standalone numbers at or below EUR 385-400m, so that they can be beaten". The divisional sum of EUR 380-395m meets the words. It does not yet meet the substance. Hitting it needs a fourth quarter of EUR 106-121m against EUR 88.9m in the third and, on the case's record, EUR 100.2m in the first and EUR 84.6m in the second; the case's own EUR 385m needs about EUR 111m. No broker has written, the consensus target has not moved and EPS has been trimmed 7%, so the sell-side has not re-based either. The probable sequence is a second cut, or a fold of FY2026 and the EUR 454m FY2027 target into a combined post-Evoke figure, at the FY2026 results. The case allows for the fold; it does not allow for the rebase arriving in two steps with the entry window between them. For this to be right, fourth-quarter seasonality has to be ordinary and mitigation has to stay at or below 55%.

New reading, pressing the other way: the price, not the calendar, is opening the entry. At EUR 1.00 the shares are 11% above the case's standalone value of EUR 0.90 and 21% below its probability-weighted EUR 1.26. On the case's unadjusted arithmetic the probability-weighted return at today's price is in the mid-twenties, not +13%; adjusting its end values for the interactive cut and for net debt EUR 80m above the case's year-end EUR 1,550m takes that back toward the high teens, still above where the case rated it. One of its three conditions is met in form, the engine's demand is intact in two markets, the legacy half was guided above the case's divisional record, and an insider bought at EUR 1.04. The case sequenced its conditions and dated the entry to the first half of 2027; it did not weigh a first tranche on price when one condition is met and the standalone premium has largely gone. For this reading to be right the fourth quarter has to land in the guidance range, the leverage denominator has to stop falling, and Evoke has to complete on time. The strongest objection is the case's own: a fair trough of EUR 0.49-0.60, flows now running out of the stock rather than in, and a balance sheet that turns a modest miss into a discontinuity. We put the reading up because the case has no answer to it, not because we think it wins.

What would settle it

The questions the first piece left to this review. The full release and call: delivered; it carried the guidance reframing, the 55% mitigation and UK growth on a larger active base, but not BII margin by quarter or mitigation by lever. The broker response: not delivered; the review found none, and the dashboard shows only a 7% EPS trim with targets untouched. The price reaction: settled; the market reacted.

Still open, and what settles each:

  • Whether the margin is the level. The fourth-quarter mitigation rate and the BII margin for the quarter, at the FY2026 results in March-April 2027. Above 65% with actives stable reopens the case's path; at or below 55% confirms the reading. The company gives mitigation as a percentage, not by lever; the split between promotions, direct costs and synergies is a disclosure it has not committed to.
  • Whether the rebase has a second step. The fourth-quarter actual against EUR 106-121m, and whether FY2027 is shown standalone or inside a combined Evoke figure, at the same results.
  • Leverage. Year-end net debt and the twelve-month denominator at the FY2026 results; the covenant note in the annual report, which the company has not committed to and the case has asked for; whether leverage reduction starts in FY2027 as stated.
  • Evoke. Gibraltar court sanction and gaming approvals, the share-versus-cash election and the first synergy phasing, early 2027. The 0.537 exchange ratio is contractual; the phasing is not yet in print.
  • Volume. UK growth against record comparatives in the first-quarter 2027 trading update, with the General Betting Duty step from 15% to 25% in April 2027 behind it.
  • Lottery. Illinois, and the Ohio and Morocco expiries from mid-2027, with Ohio costing about USD 20m of annual operating earnings.
  • The sell-side. Any note after the 20 November call. None is dated; if Deutsche Bank's EUR 1.00 hold and Jefferies' EUR 1.35 buy stand unrevised through the FY2026 results, the market has done the re-basing and the brokers have not.
