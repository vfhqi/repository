What the company does

ABN AMRO is a Netherlands-concentrated universal bank: about 85% of operating income is domestic and net interest income is roughly 70% of revenues. Operating income was 8,716 million euros in 2025, with an all-in cost-to-income ratio of 64.4% and a return on equity of 8.7%. The Dutch State held 24.9% in April 2026 and announced on 7 October 2026 a placement taking that to 10.5%. The market value is 33,991 million euros at 40.99 euros.

A plausible investment thesis

Delivered, not promised. A new chief executive set out at the 25 November 2025 Capital Markets Day to cut 900 million euros of cost and 5,200 roles, pull 10 billion euros of risk-weighted assets out of corporate lending and return up to 100% of generated capital. Five quarters on, 2,340 roles and 300 million euros of savings are done and 9 billion euros of risk-weighted assets is out.

The external leg is the bigger one. The 180 billion euro replicating portfolio, of three-year average duration, rolls maturing hedges on to swap yields near 3%, lifting the liability margin 5 basis points a quarter; commercial net interest income guidance for 2026 went to about 6.8 billion euros on 12 August 2026. The mechanism to earnings per share is revenue growth of 6.7% a year and a cost-to-income ratio falling to 52.5%, which carry 17 of the 20 points a year the Investment Analyst models from 2.45 euros in 2025 to about 4.25 euros in 2028. Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Investment Analyst's memo of 13 October rated Case Riskiness C and Foundations' Robustness C. Executives hold no shares and receive no variable pay. Stage 2 reads Probable today; Stage 3 reads Possible beside it.

Business quality

The strength is structural: three banks hold more than 60% of Dutch mortgages, the deposit beta is 35% to 40%, and CET1 stands at 15.9% against a 13.75% target, with about 7 billion euros of risk-weighted assets released when the DNB mortgage floor expires on 30 November 2026. The weakness is the operator's record: three-year revenue growth of 2.1% a year, a retail cost ratio of 60% against ING's 38%, a missed 2024 cost target and three fines since 2021. Q2 2026 return on equity of 12.1% is the 2028 target reached early.
