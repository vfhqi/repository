Section 2 – Vimian's Cost Structure and Segment Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. Vimian Group's cost base contains a high proportion of short-term fixed personnel and external expenses that restrict margin expansion when organic growth slows down.
  2. Strong operating leverage is evident in Specialty Pharma during high-growth periods but MedTech margins remain highly vulnerable to volume fluctuations.

Group Cost Base Analysis

1.
Rigid cost structures: Vimian's 2025 annual report disclosed raw materials and merchandise of EUR 132.2m, personnel of EUR 108.2m, other external expenses of EUR 81.4m, and depreciation of EUR 37.9m. While raw materials are variable, personnel and external expenses remain fixed in the short term, making it difficult for the group to quickly reduce costs during periods of slow organic growth. Consequently, this rigidity leaves the group's margins highly sensitive to top-line performance.
2.
Slow cost adjustment: Vimian's decentralized segments face challenges flexing personnel and external expenses when organic growth slows down. For instance, Veterinary Services and MedTech require sustained growth investments that cannot be easily postponed without sacrificing long-term market share gains. Therefore, any sudden drop in organic volume directly impacts the segment adjusted EBITA margins before personnel or external expenses can adjust.

1.
Accelerating commercial growth: Vimian transitioned its North American orthopaedics division from inside sales to a direct field sales force in January 2026 to capture deeper market share. According to Vimian's first quarter report on 29 April 2026, this commercial reorganization added temporary fixed personnel costs that initial volumes could not fully absorb, which pressured early segment margins. Consequently, Jefferies noted on 28 October 2026 that a more substantial recovery in North American orthopaedics represents potential margin upside for 2027 once these field investments scale.

Broker DNB Carnegie highlighted on 20 July 2026 that double-digit organic growth finally returned to MedTech in the second quarter, indicating that sales volumes are beginning to absorb the added overhead.

Although this program led to short-term inventory write-downs, it was designed to streamline operations and enhance gross margins without sacrificing core customer relationships.

Limited sales disruption: The elimination of thousands of redundant MedTech products had almost no negative impact on the group's underlying sales performance. During the earnings call on 12 February 2026, the chief executive officer confirmed that the impact on 2025 sales was limited because equivalent, higher-value products were readily available to customers. This transition supports long-term operational efficiency as clients migrate to more profitable, standardized product lines.

Elevated restructuring charges: Simplifying the product portfolio required significant upfront restructuring charges that depressed reported MedTech margins at the end of fiscal year 2025. Vimian reported in its fourth-quarter statement on 12 February 2026 that restructuring costs reached EUR 1.6 million, primarily driven by inventory write-downs from product rationalisation and organizational shifts. These non-recurring items represent necessary costs to establish a fully scalable MedTech platform.
