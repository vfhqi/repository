EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Convatec has successfully pivoted to a highly predictable, repeatable chronic care model, transforming from a stagnant private equity-backed business into a compounding medical technology platform anchored by four defensive divisions –Advanced Wound Care, Ostomy Care, Continence Care, and Infusion Care–that collectively generate over 900 million consumable products annually.

The company operates within highly consolidated oligopolies where Coloplast, Hollister, and Convatec collectively control the vast majority of the global ostomy care market, benefiting from extreme barriers to entry driven by stringent regulatory approvals, sticky clinical relationships with specialized nurses, and multi-year Group Purchasing Organisation contracts.

Leadership has stabilized under CEO Jonny Mason, who worked at Convatec for over four years as former CFO during the turnaround, preserving commercial execution and margin expansion momentum following former CEO Karim Bitar's tragic passing.

The Accelerate strategy targets 6% to 8% organic revenue growth annually starting in fiscal year 2027, underpinned by nearly doubled R&D spending from 2.7% of sales in 2018 to approximately 5.0% currently, delivering eight major product launches between 2022 and 2025.

The business model exhibits exceptional revenue visibility, with over 90% of revenues generated by non-discretionary, recurring chronic care medical consumables, insulated from macroeconomic cyclicality by aging demographics and irreversible clinical needs.

Structural competitive advantages and defensible market positions

Convatec is the undisputed global leader in manufacturing subcutaneous infusion sets, producing approximately two-thirds of the world's supply annually under long-term, sole-source agreements with major automated insulin pump manufacturers, creating a fortress moat in a category delivering stellar 12.5% organic sales growth in 2025, driven by automated insulin delivery systems.

The division benefits from Parkinson's disease diversification, positioning Convatec to become the sole supplier of infusion sets to all three major Parkinson's treatments by late 2026, with non-diabetes subcutaneous therapies exceeding 15% of infusion care sales.

In continence care, owning 180 Medical commands a dominant 40% share of the US continence retail market, enabling Convatec to harvest both manufacturing profits and distribution retail markups while increasing ownbrand catheter sales from 54% to 59% since 2022, establishing a captive double-margin engine that competitors cannot replicate without massive capital deployment.

Advanced wound care is anchored by the flagship Aquacel brand, holding a global market share exceeding 30% in the antimicrobial category, while the next-generation pipeline includes ConvaFoam, which has captured approximately 6% global foam share, targeting a realistic midterm goal of 10%, and ConvaNiox, the first nitric oxide-generating multimodal dressing, demonstrating 60% more diabetic foot ulcers healed and threefold faster healing times in clinical trials.

Ostomy care momentum has accelerated following dual GPO wins with Captis/Vizient and Premier AscenDrive, unlocking access to approximately one-third of US ostomy procedures, ending a five-year contract drought and positioning the division for the critical 2027 launch of Natura Body, targeting the two-piece soft convex ostomy market segment that accounts for approximately 60-65% of the highly lucrative US ostomy market.

Financial turnaround and operational leverage execution

The group delivered reported group revenue of $2,439M in FY25, representing a 6.50% reported increase, with organic revenue growth of 6.40% excluding InnovaMatrix in FY25, consistently outperforming the chronic care market growth rate of 4% to 5%, demonstrating market share capture across all major franchises.

Profitability has expanded materially, with adjusted EBIT margin growing by 110 basis points to 22.30% in FY25, tracking toward the upgraded mid-term target of 24% to 26% by 2027 driven by G&A compression from 13% of sales in 2019 to 7.2% through centralized business services and plant network consolidation from nine sites to seven core facilities, shifting production from the Netherlands and Denmark to Slovakia while increasing highspeed automated lines requiring just two operators instead of ten manually.

Return on capital metrics are improving, with Return on Capital Employed rising above 14% in fiscal year 2025, Return on Invested Capital expanding to 16% in fiscal year 2025, and ROIC forecasted to steadily improve to 24% by fiscal year 2030, although historical returns significantly lag Coloplast's 15% return on invested capital, signaling remaining operational improvement runway.

Cash generation remains robust, with $362M in free cash flow to equity in fiscal year 2025, supporting the completion of a $300M share buyback in 2025 while maintaining net debt of 2.0x adjusted EBITDA for capital structure balance.

The company has proactively hedged input volatility, with over 80% of raw material input costs hedged for fiscal year 2026, and achieved a 60 basis point price gain through dedicated Centers of Excellence, successfully neutralizing the historical 1% annual price deflation seen across categories, demonstrating latent pricing power that management is now systematically exploiting.

Value chain positioning and margin pool capture

Convatec occupies highly profit-favored integrated stages across the chronic care value chain, capturing approximately 60% to 65% gross margins at the automated manufacturing stage protected by robust polymer formulations and proprietary adhesive technologies, while retail distribution through 180 Medical generates approximately 25% gross margins and 10% to 15% EBIT margins, enabling the company to extract dual margins unavailable to unintegrated competitors.

The customer base is highly defensive, with no single customer generating more than 10% of total revenue and a customer churn rate below 1% in the direct-to-consumer home services group, supported by a Net Promoter Score of 80%, reflecting world-class service levels that create extreme switching costs driven by specialized nurse gatekeeper influence and clinical training requirements.

Supplier concentration is manageable, with 42 suppliers representing approximately 80% of total raw material procurement spend, while carrying an average of 5 months of finished goods and key materials inventory insulates production from sudden bottlenecks, and every 1% of inflation above the 3% baseline costs $2M-$3M in FY26 and $7M-$8M in FY27, representing manageable margin sensitivity given hedging protocols.

The distribution channel exhibits shifting power dynamics, with the proposed 2028 US DME competitive bidding program expected to slash registered urology distributors from 3,000 to fewer than 20 national players, creating opportunities for 180 Medical to capture orphaned market volume while swapping out rival products for own-brand catheters like GentleCath, although a 30% retail price reduction could flow upstream, forcing an estimated 15% pricing cut on manufacturers, representing the single largest structural risk to the investment thesis.

Long-term secular tailwinds and paradigm alignment

The chronic care medical consumables sector benefits from irreversible demographic expansion, with the global population aged 65 and older projected to reach 1.9 billion by 2060, driving highly predictable volumes for consumables due to the rising burden of diabetes and cardiovascular diseases, while the medical disposable market reached $596bn in 2025, expanding at a projected 13.6% CAGR through 2034.

Convatec's focus on home care, direct distribution, and infection prevention aligns perfectly with current healthcare efficiency goals, capturing structural value chain shifts toward patient-centric delivery models.

Technological disruption risks remain manageable over the medium term, with continuous biosensor technologies and smart dressings facing severe workflow complexity and high product cost barriers, while the transition to patch pumps is accelerating, driven by increasing patient preference for tubeless form factors, which Convatec has addressed by securing its first patch pump supply agreement in the diabetes space, demonstrating that subcutaneous infusion sets support a wide range of pump designs.

Regulatory barriers remain exceptionally high, with stringent clinical trials and regulatory approvals insulating established medical consumable players from low-cost generic competitors, as the clinical burden of proof demands randomized controlled trials to justify premium clinical pricing, while specialized wound ostomy nurses act as key gatekeepers, making long-term customer switching highly dependent on clinical education.

The competitive profile matches industry success factors, with manufacturing over 1 billion units annually, sustaining an R&D budget near 5% of total sales, and owning 180 Medical commanding a dominant 40% intermittent catheter retail share, positioning Convatec to sustain durable market share within highly consolidated triopolies where chronic care market shares are highly resistant to sudden shifts due to clinical conservatism.

Plausible problems to monitor

1.
Return on Invested Capital deficit: Convatec's historical ROIC significantly lags market leader Coloplast's 15% return on invested capital, highlighting persistent capital efficiency underperformance despite multi-year turnaround efforts. While ROIC expanded to 16% in FY25 with projections to reach 24% by FY30, the company continues to trail Coloplast's peer-leading 31.05% EBITDA margin, exposing structural operating leverage gaps across the value chain.
2.
Continence market share weakness: Convatec holds only approximately 8% of the global continence care market, dramatically trailing Coloplast's dominant 47% global continence share and Hollister's 14% market position, indicating a meaningful competitive deficit in this high-margin chronic care category despite owning the leading US retail distribution channel.
3.
Fragmented wound care positioning: Convatec occupies only approximately 6% global market share in the highly fragmented advanced wound care category, substantially behind Solventum's leading 15% share and Smith & Nephew's 14% of the market, demonstrating limited scale advantages and pricing power in this core division representing 32% of group revenue. Artificially flattered cash flow quality: Apparent earnings and cash flow quality are artificially enhanced by the custom redefinition of free cash flow to equity, which shifts a material $121M of growth capex out of the core cash calculation, disguising higher structural capital intensity versus peers and masking a 27.48% decline in legacy-defined free cash flow to equity from $302M in FY24 to $219M in FY25.
4.
Multi-year GPO contract drought: Convatec failed to win any new US ostomy GPO contracts for over five years prior to late 2025, representing a sustained commercial execution failure that allowed competitors to defend and expand hospital market access while Convatec's US ostomy share stabilized at only approximately 20% compared to Hollister's dominant approximately 40% US ostomy share.

Outliers worth noting

Vertically integrated retail dominance in continence care: Convatec's ownership of 180 Medical represents an extreme structural outlier, generating approximately 90% of continence divisional sales directly and commanding a dominant 40% share of the US intermittent catheter retail market, while competitor Coloplast's Comfort Medical sits at a distant third with only 5% to 10% market share in US retail. This vertical integration allows Convatec to capture both wholesale manufacturing margins and retail distribution markups, creating a fundamentally different profit structure compared to peers who remain primarily wholesale players.

Extreme infusion care market concentration: Convatec manufactures approximately two-thirds of the world's subcutaneous infusion sets annually, establishing the company as the undisputed global leader in this specialized segment. The division holds long-term, sole-source agreements with major automated insulin pump manufacturers, and Convatec is positioned to become the sole supplier of infusion sets for all three major Parkinson's treatments by late 2026.

This near-monopolistic positioning in subcutaneous delivery infrastructure represents a significant structural anomaly where Convatec holds materially higher market share than any comparable chronic care medical device category controlled by competing oligopolists. Persistent return on invested capital deficit versus peer leader: Despite turnaround progress, Convatec's historical returns significantly lag Coloplast's 15% return on invested capital, while Convatec's Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) expanded to 16% in fiscal year 2025.

The company's historical underperformance stems from a decade of private equity ownership that left Convatec with a stagnant, underfunded product portfolio at its 2016 IPO, creating a multi-year structural returns gap that persists despite recent operating improvements and elevated R&D investments.
