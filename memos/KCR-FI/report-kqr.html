Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The key question regarding port equipment market bifurcation is resolvable through segment-level financial projections showing diverging paths for ship-to-shore and mobile yard equipment. US security tariffs of 100% on China-made ship-to-shore cranes will exclude dominant Chinese suppliers from the United States, creating near-monopoly pricing power for Konecranes in a market where Chinese suppliers previously controlled 75% of the US market for heavy cranes. This protectionist barrier translates directly into historic order book highs that reached 1.8 billion euros by mid-2026, establishing predictable revenue visibility for European heavy crane manufacturers over an 18-month to 3-year investment horizon. Simultaneously, the mobile yard equipment segment faces intense disruption from Chinese battery-electric competitors. China dominates the battery supply chain, holding 80% of global cell manufacturing capacity along with 85% of cathode processing, enabling manufacturers like Sany and BYD to deliver massive cost advantages in electric reachstackers, terminal tractors, and yard trucks. These players leverage proprietary battery architectures supporting up to 636 kWh LFP packs integrated with in-house drive systems to undercut Western equipment pricing and compress operating margins for incumbents like Kalmar. Regulatory catalysts intensify this bifurcation. The European Union FuelEU Maritime regulation entered into force on January 1, 2025, imposing strict limits on vessel greenhouse gas intensity with penalties of 2,400 euros per tonne of fuel equivalent for non-compliance. These mandates drive unprecedented infrastructure investment, with providers like Cavotec reporting order backlogs reaching a historic record of 155.8 million euros in mid-2026. However, heavy electrical equipment lead-times remain highly elevated, with power and substation transformers averaging 160 weeks, creating execution risk that threatens deployment schedules.

The strategic purity of European plays varies dramatically across segments. Konecranes ranks as the highestquality play on US protectionism because its heavy crane segment is shielded from Chinese competitor ZPMC by tariffs, though it generates two-thirds of its profits from stable but slower-growing Service operations. In contrast, Kalmar remains highly exposed to market share losses as Sany and BYD undercut electric mobile yard equipment pricing, with its current equipment margins facing immediate compression. Critical data gaps persist around Chinese dynamic pricing structures and transshipment routes. Undisclosed overseas contract pricing structures remain a major information barrier, while recent trade data indicates a massive 22.9 billion dollar surge in de minimis shipments, creating untracked parallel trade routes that obscure true volumes. These gaps prevent complete resolution of the equipment transition timeline and could mask the velocity of Chinese market penetration. Second-order bottlenecks present additional execution risk. Grid connection queues now span up to seven years in core European markets, while factory order books for large transformers are largely filled till 2027, leaving lead times stretched to 36 months. These infrastructure constraints threaten to delay the very electrification investments that regulatory mandates are designed to accelerate, creating temporal misalignment between compliance deadlines and physical deployment capacity. The aggregate thesis exhibits high confidence across protectionist and regulatory dimensions, with 100% US tariffs guaranteeing that Konecranes secures heavy crane orders over the next 18 months and FuelEU Maritime enforcement translating directly into Cavotec backlog growth. However, competitive dynamics in mobile equipment carry elevated uncertainty due to pricing opacity and the potential for Western incumbents to defend aftermarket margins through their large installed bases. The key investment question resolves to whether service revenue resilience can offset equipment margin compression in diversified portfolios, versus capturing pure-play exposure to protected heavy crane segments where tariff barriers remain structurally durable.

Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. The key question regarding port equipment market bifurcation is resolvable through segment-level financial projections showing diverging paths for ship-to-shore and mobile yard equipment.

Strategic context and investor intent

1.
Diverging market paths: The core inquiry examines how US protectionism and emission rules hand ship-to- shore crane markets to European makers while Chinese batteries disrupt adjacent mobile equipment.
2.
Predictable return target: Investors seek to identify a predictable 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory that supports a twenty-five percent IRR potential in capital goods.
3.
Eroding competitive advantage: Analysts must evaluate whether Konecranes and Kalmar can sustain pricing power before new capacity, local assembly, or regulatory loopholes dilute near-term competitive advantage.
4.
Accelerating corporate restructuring: Following Kalmar's spin-off from Cargotec, the sector presents a unique opportunity to contrast heavy port solutions against mobile equipment models.

Step-by-step financial transmission mechanism

5.
Expanding market share: Protectionist barriers in the US redirect massive ship-to-shore orders to Konecranes, significantly increasing port solutions revenue over the next 18 months.
6.
Sustaining pricing power: Restricting cheap Chinese rivals allows European crane makers to price up large port projects, boosting operating margins toward fifteen percent.
7.
Re-rating quality assets: Companies with large service businesses and protected heavy equipment portfolios should re-rate to higher valuation multiples than competitors.
8.
Other side of the story: A sudden global terminal capex freeze driven by trade war escalation would delay port electrification, rendering projected EPS trajectories highly unpredictable.

Section 2 – Market structure and ship-to-shore protectionism

Section summary
  1. US security tariffs on Chinese cranes completely restructure market shares in favor of Western suppliers like Konecranes.
  2. The consolidation of European heavy crane capacity leaves Konecranes with near-monopoly pricing power in the transatlantic market.
  3. Port delivery backlogs and rising input steel costs threaten terminal capex budgets and delay deployment schedules.

Protectionist tariffs and market reallocation

1.
Severe tariff barriers: DNB Carnegie reports that the 100% tariffs on China-made ship-to-shore cranes will exclude dominant Chinese suppliers from the United States.
2.
Historical Chinese dominance: DNB Carnegie indicates that Chinese suppliers previously controlled 75% of the US market for heavy cranes.
3.
Total market size: DNB Carnegie estimates the United States ship-to-shore demand at a modest 10 to 20 cranes annually but representing highly concentrated capital expenditure.
4.
Monopolistic exit dynamics: A senior consultant at an equipment finance firm noted that ZPMC essentially exited the United States market following Section 301 regulatory enforcement.
5.
Western vendor benefit: The same senior consultant at an equipment finance firm observed that Konecranes and Liebherr benefited most from this regulatory exclusion.
6.
Steel inflation impact: A construction company lead highlights that steel price inflation exceeding 15% compounds Konecranes' global supply chain risks despite tariff protections.

Supplier capacity and delivery friction

7.
Strategic portfolio simplification: Kalmar completed its divestment of heavy crane operations during 2024 to focus entirely on lighter mobile cargo handling equipment.
8.
Western duopoly collapse: DNB Carnegie observes that Kalmar's exit from large port cranes leaves few Western heavy alternatives remaining for major terminal operators.
9.
Unprecedented backlog accumulation: Konecranes reported its Port Solutions order book reached a historic high that reached 1.8 billion euros by mid-2026.
10.
Severe delivery delays: A channel expert reports that crane deliveries originally scheduled for late Q1 were pushed back to September due to parts availability issues.
11.
Temporary shipping bottleneck: Kalmar noted that documentation requirements under new tariffs initially delayed US forklift deliveries by four weeks.
12.
Geographic revenue mismatch: DNB Carnegie points out that while US tariff protections boost order books, actual European crane sales growth remains severely delayed by long project execution lead times.
13.
Other side of the story: DNB Carnegie warns that high tariff barriers can disrupt short-term equipment demand if terminal operators freeze capex budgets in response to geopolitical instability.

Section 3 – Mobile yard equipment and the Chinese battery cost attack

Section summary
  1. Chinese OEMs leverage battery supply chain dominance to aggressively disrupt the electric mobile yard equipment market.
  2. Massive battery cost differentials allow Chinese players to compete aggressively on pricing and flexible financing terms.
  3. Western incumbents face intense margin pressure as port operators increasingly prioritize total cost of ownership over brand heritage.

Battery supply chain and cost advantages

1.
Unmatched value control: China dominates the battery supply chain, holding 80% of global cell manufacturing capacity along with 85% of cathode processing according to DNB Carnegie.
2.
Enormous cost leverage: Battery systems represent the single largest cost component for electric heavy transport, where pack sizes typically exceed 500 kWh as detailed by DNB Carnegie.
3.
Proprietary battery architecture: Sany utilizes its proprietary Magic Tower architecture to support up to 636 kWh LFP packs integrated with in-house drive systems, as reported by Carbon Tracker.
4.
Rapid payback profile: Sany Heavy Truck registered 31,200 electric heavy trucks in 2025, demonstrating rapid total cost of ownership payback in highly utilized industrial yard transport according to Carbon Tracker.
5.
Massive scale economies: Sany benefits from CATL, EVE, and BYD volume contracts to secure low-cost LFP cells for vocational equipment, as noted by Carbon Tracker.
6.
Superior energy recovery: Sany launched its 50T electric reachstacker featuring a 512 kWh swappable battery that recovers 0.4 kWh per container descent, according to Sany Group.

Competitive pricing and market disruption

7.
Vocal competitive threat: Sany secured notable European mobile equipment orders, and Konecranes acknowledges that much of their battery technology originates from Chinese suppliers, as discussed in a Konecranes earnings call.
8.
Aggressive financing options: Chinese mobile equipment competitors actively disrupt the Western market by competing aggressively on payment terms and customer credits, as stated by Kalmar's chief financial officer.
9.
Worrying growth divergence: Kalmar's fully electric equipment orders LTM represent only 10% of total orders, which DNB Carnegie notes is below the company's long-term ambitions and leaves it vulnerable to cheap Chinese imports.
10.
Severe margin pressure: Kalmar faces structural headwinds because its electric material handling products remain significantly more expensive, allowing Sany and BYD to capture market share according to DNB Carnegie.
11.
Accelerating global footprint: Sany Marine successfully expanded its international presence by delivering STT4501E electric terminal tractors in Peru during 2026, as reported in a Sany Heavy Industry filing.
12.
Broadening regional adoption: Sany's authorized dealer in the UAE delivered 110 electric heavy trucks to Noatum Logistics in mid-2026, marking a significant step toward zero-carbon port operations according to Sany Group.
13.
Strong R&D commitment: Sany invested 724 million dollars in R&D during 2025, enabling the company to rapidly commercialize intelligent port solutions according to Sany Heavy Industry.
14.
Other side of the story: Kalmar's extensive service network and 70,000 unit installed base may shield its aftermarket margins from direct Chinese battery competition as highlighted in Kalmar investor presentations.

Section 4 – Regulatory catalysts and electrification infrastructure

Section summary
  1. Strict European emissions mandates force immediate terminal capital expenditure to avoid severe non- compliance penalties
  2. Grid capacity constraints and utility substation backlogs represent the primary bottlenecks to widespread shore-side power adoption
  3. Infrastructure providers like Cavotec experience record backlog growth as ship-to-shore power connections transition into mandatory compliance assets

Tightening maritime emissions mandates

1.
Accelerating regulatory compliance: Costamare reports that the European Union FuelEU Maritime regulation entered into force on January 1, 2025, imposing strict limits on vessel greenhouse gas intensity.
2.
Escalating reduction targets: Bernstein Research notes that FuelEU Maritime mandates a two percent emissions intensity reduction starting in 2025, rising progressively to eighty percent by 2050.
3.
Punitive non-compliance costs: Gaztransport et technigaz details that failing to comply with FuelEU limits trigger severe penalties of 2,400 euros per tonne of fuel equivalent.
4.
Mandatory shore-side connection: Under the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation, container and passenger vessels must connect to onshore power at major European ports starting January 1, 2030.
5.
Expanding global adoption: Hanwha Group notes that California At-Berth regulations and Seattle mandates are compressing vessel transition timelines, forcing rapid shore-power hardware retrofits.
6.
Rising equipment demand: Alfa Laval reports that tightening port emission mandates in Europe, California, and China fuel strong demand for hybrid boilers and vessel electrification systems.

Terminal capex and grid infrastructure constraints

7.
Surging infrastructure investments: ABB secured a massive project to build shore-power infrastructure at Rotterdam, covering eight kilometers of berth with one hundred megavolt-amperes capacity.
8.
Severe grid bottlenecks: Luka Koper reports that connecting multiple container vessels requires expanding terminal substation capacity from ten to eighty megavolt-amperes.
9.
Elevating component lead-times: Bernstein Research highlights that heavy electrical equipment lead-times remain highly elevated, with power and substation transformers averaging 160 weeks.
10.
Record backlog growth: Cavotec reported its order backlog reached a historic record of 155.8 million euros in mid-2026, driven by shore-power demand.

CCC SS: MoorMaster order adds to positive momentum

11.
Cavotec annual order intake, sales, and margin trends showing projected recovery and growth through 2028 Accelerating revenue execution: Cavotec achieved a thirty-four percent year-on-year revenue increase to twenty-eight point five million euros in second quarter of 2026.
12.
Strategic horizontal expansion: Cavotec entered an exclusive partnership with EVIAS in September 2026 to deliver ground-based dynamic charging for electric terminal tractors and vehicles.
13.
Other side of the story: Bernstein Research warns that elevated European electricity costs relative to onboard diesel generation create strong financial resistance among shipowners if regulators fail to penalize idle emissions consistently.

Section 5 – European play ranking and pricing power dynamics

Section summary
  1. European port equipment plays exhibit starkly divergent levels of strategic purity and vulnerability to Chinese battery cost disruption.
  2. Konecranes secures unmatched pricing power in protected heavy segments while Kalmar's margins face immediate erosion in mobile equipment.
  3. Project-driven infrastructure providers like Cavotec experience record backlog growth but struggle with near-term execution margins.

Pure-play ranking and strategic exposure

1.
Divergent protectionist purity: DNB Carnegie ranks Konecranes as the highest-quality play on US protectionism because its heavy crane segment is shielded from Chinese competitor ZPMC by tariffs.

Diluted business model: DNB Carnegie highlights that Konecranes is not a pure play as it generates two- thirds of its profits from stable but slower-growing Service operations.

Pricing power and contract structures

Temporary margin dilution: Cavotec Group experienced compressed margins in early 2026 due to mix effects from older projects but expects stronger pricing in second half backlog.

Aggressive customer concessions: DNB Carnegie noted that Chinese mobile rivals are forcing Western players to concede on payment terms to protect their historically dominant market shares.

Other side of the story: Kalmar's massive installed base of sixty-eight thousand active machines may provide a resilient buffer for highly profitable aftermarket services even as Chinese battery-electric OEMs erode its equipment market share.

Section 8 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. Grid connection backlogs and transformer shortages represent the primary bottlenecks delaying widespread port electrification and shore-power integration
  2. Chinese OEMs possess the strategic means, motive, and service partnerships to disrupt European heavy mobile equipment markets

Grid bottlenecks and power infrastructure backlogs

1.
Severely congested grids: Datacloud Global Congress participants report that grid connection queues now span up to seven years in core European markets, forcing developers to seek on-site alternatives.
2.
Elevated component lead-times: Bernstein Research highlights that factory order books for large transformers are largely filled till 2027, leaving lead times stretched to 36 months.
3.
Overwhelming connection backlogs: WindEurope data cited by Morgan Stanley indicates that over 500 gigawatts of wind projects remain stuck in European queues awaiting connection studies.
4.
Slowing terminal expansion: A Siemens Energy project manager notes that heavy electrical equipment constraints delay major grid infrastructure projects by six to twelve months.

Fuel shifts and shipping decarbonization

5.
Accelerating fleet retrofits: Former Rolls-Royce Power Systems directors estimate that up to three- quarters of new vessel orders now specify alternative dual-fuel capabilities to meet international emissions targets.
6.
Escalating fuel transition: The World Bank projects that hydrogen-based fuels will comprise 64% of the global maritime mix by 2050 under carbon neutrality scenarios.

Means, motive, and opportunity (MMO) dynamics

7.
Formidable competitive means: Carbon Tracker reports that Sany has captured a twenty percent market share in China's electric truck segment, leveraging enormous industrial battery scale economies.
8.
Aggressive entry opportunity: Sany utilizes its Putzmeister logistics hub and an Alltrucks partnership to provide maintenance access across 700 workshops to overcome Western service moats.

Non-consensus and outlier signals

9.
Outlier: Severe security restrictions: A United States Department of Energy executive order targets foreign- sourced bulk-power equipment, introducing unexpected national security compliance risks for ports utilizing Chinese battery storage.
10.
Outlier: Battery-buffered infrastructure: XCharge is expanding European assembly of battery-buffered smart charging systems to bypass grid capacity limits and compress installation timelines.
11.
Other side of the story: : S&P Global reports that behind-the-meter co-located generation is unlikely to fully substitute for utility-level power grids because microgrids cannot replicate bulk voltage stiffness.
