Project Billing Cycles and Milestones

1.
Highly structured milestones: Alfen manages its storage project working capital through multiple payment milestones over the lifecycle. The utility-scale storage project cycle typically varies from 4 to 18 months, with a standard average duration of approximately ten months. These milestone payments are billed progressively from order placement through final customer takeover to minimize unbilled work. This sequential billing ensures that cash inflows closely track physical project execution milestones.
2.
Upfront financial security: Alfen mitigates early capital outlays by securing substantial advance payments from utility-scale customers at the order stage. Management notes that battery storage payments are usually collected up front to support early-stage cash management. This upfront collection provides the liquidity needed to secure critical supplier battery components without relying on costly bank overdrafts. Consequently, initial project phases remain cash-neutral or positive before fabrication begins.
3.
Direct procurement alignment: Alfen actively balances its customer payment schedules against downstream supplier commitments to shield operating cash. The company manages liquidity exposure by maintaining strict end-to-end alignment between sales contract payment schedules and supplier procurement contracts. Specifically, battery costs are directly fixed with suppliers after contract negotiations to mitigate raw materials price risk. This matching mechanism prevents the company from absorbing severe working capital mismatches.
4.
Backlog conversion lumpiness: Alfen's project-based business causes order backlog conversion to fluctuate significantly between quarters. Jefferies Research highlighted that front-loaded storage revenues surged 88% in H1 2026, but the low Q2 order intake of only €11m signaled a sharp slowdown for the second half of the year. This lumpiness impairs working capital predictability because down-payment inflows become highly concentrated in specific periods. Consequently, cash flows can swing from highly positive to deeply negative depending on contract signing timelines.

Balance Sheet Impact of Contract Work

5.
Substantial contract liabilities: Alfen records customer down-payments as contract liabilities, representing amounts due to customers for progress billings exceeding recognized revenue. During periods of active order intake, these advance payments accumulate and create a negative working capital effect that acts as interest-free funding. For example, net contract work at the end of 2024 stood at a negative balance of €24.3m. This liability buffer indicates that progress billings of €218.8m outpaced recognized revenue of €194.5m.
6.
Delayed revenue recognition: Alfen utilizes the cost-to-cost method to progressively recognize revenue based on contract costs incurred relative to estimated total costs. Uninvoiced contract assets are classified as amounts due from customers once performance obligations are satisfied over time. These contract assets are transferred to trade receivables only when the right to consideration becomes unconditional through an invoice. Delays in engineering milestones therefore defer the conversion of work-in-progress into trade receivables.
7.
Fluctuating contract balances: Alfen experienced a notable contraction in contract liabilities during the first half of 2026 as legacy projects moved toward completion. Contract liabilities, represented by amounts due to customers, decreased from €47.2m in December 2025 to thirty-two point six million euros by June 2026. Concurrently, amounts due from customers for contract work declined from €21.0m to €19.4m. This dual reduction reflects a temporary pause in large project start-ups amid a shifting backlog timeline.
8.
Limited collection visibility: Alfen does not provide interim balance sheet updates in its quarterly trading statements, making short-term working capital swings difficult to track. Deutsche Bank Research noted that the company did not disclose detailed quarterly updates on working capital or cash positions during Q1 2024. This lack of disclosure leaves investors reliant on semi-annual reporting to evaluate whether down-payments are adequately covering ongoing project execution costs. As a result, sudden working capital deteriorations may only become visible after significant cash burn has occurred.
