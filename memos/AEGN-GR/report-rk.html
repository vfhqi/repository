Section 10 – Pre-Mortem: Executive Summary

Assuming it is now 2028 and Aegean Airlines has experienced a significant business failure, the pre-mortem framework reveals that the FALSE FRIEND profile carries the lowest inconsistency score, best matching the combination of traffic growth and margin compression. This analytical outcome suggests that the carrier's apparent operational strength masked a fundamental deterioration in economic profitability, ultimately leading to its distressed state.

The company appeared to be performing well on headline metrics while underlying cash flow conversion progressively eroded, creating a deceptive signal pattern that lulled investors and management into complacency. The Analysis of Competing Hypotheses framework evaluated three distinct profiles against fifteen evidence items, including six highly diagnostic indicators such as revenue yield per available seat kilometer, free cash flow conversion rates, and sustainable aviation fuel cost pass-through capability.

Third, mandatory environmental compliance costs reached €43.3 million in FY25 imposing a non-discretionary unit-cost hurdle on regional operations, while carbon costs surged to €78.36 million in FY25 reflecting the steady phase-out of free EU-ETS rights. These clusters correlate and compound dangerously, as geopolitical fuel price spikes occurring alongside prolonged aircraft groundings create synchronized operational and financial stress.

The least-monitored early warning signals that ultimately precipitated Aegean's failure centered on three critical blind spots. The FALSE FRIEND failure pathway ultimately materialized through a specific sequence of events beginning in late 2025 and accelerating through 2027.

Initially, scheduled seat capacity expanded by 6% to 21 million seats in FY25, highlighting strong traffic volume momentum despite fleet groundings, which management and analysts interpreted as operational resilience and market share gains. The carrier's balance sheet initially appeared robust as cash and cash equivalents reached €955.13 million in FY25, providing a powerful structural balance sheet against near-term distress, yet this liquidity buffer masked the reality that expanding winter capacity if unhedged EUR/USD depreciates masks underlying net cash erosion, validating the false friend hypothesis. As derivative protection rolled off in outer years and a strong dollar amplifies unhedged fuel costs, accelerating winter margin collapse narratives under geopolitically tight oil markets, the carrier found itself unable to sustain profitability during the critical 2027-2028 period when Athens International Airport slots operated above physical terminal capacity of 26 million limiting Aegean's peak-season expansion window while Wizz Air scheduled unprecedented Q2 capacity growth of 20%, triggering widespread intra-European yield dilution on overlapping routes.

The pre-mortem analysis reveals that Aegean's failure resulted not from a single catastrophic event but from the steady accumulation of structural weaknesses that remained invisible beneath superficially positive operating metrics. The carrier's premium regional positioning appeared defensible through 2025 but proved fundamentally incompatible with the simultaneous pressures of environmental compliance cost escalation, unhedged currency transaction exposure, and intensifying low-cost competition.

By the time management recognized that traffic growth was decoupling from cash flow generation in late 2026, the company had already committed to fleet expansion plans and winter schedule extensions that amplified rather than mitigated the underlying margin erosion, ultimately validating the FALSE FRIEND hypothesis as the most accurate predictive framework for understanding Aegean's demise.
