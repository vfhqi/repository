Section 3 – What the company does 18 findings

Section summary
  1. Solvay operates mature, cash-generative technology platforms with clear regional leadership, but faces intensifying Chinese competition in seaborne markets.

Segment operations and splits

1.
Segmented commodity operations: Solvay operates two main divisions, Basic Chemicals and Performance Chemicals, following the demerger of its specialty businesses into Syensqo
2.
Dominant basic segment: Basic Chemicals represents 61% of group net sales, comprising Soda Ash & Derivatives and Peroxides
3.
Customized performance segment: Performance Chemicals represents 38% of sales, comprising Coatis, Silica, and Special Chem units
4.
Concentrated commodity mix: Soda ash alone accounted for slightly more than 30% of total group sales in 2023
5.
Resilient food application: Bicarbonate represents 11% of net sales, serving mature animal nutrition, food, and pharmaceutical applications
6.
Defensive merchant business: Peroxides represent 18% of net sales, serving paper bleaching, chemical synthesis, and environmental depollution

Revenue dynamics and positioning

7.
High geographic localization: More than 80% of Solvay's sales are regional or local-to-local, protecting it from tariffs
8.
Diverse customer base: Solvay serves essential markets including glass, detergents, automotive tires, and semiconductors
9.
Polyamide chain exposure: Coatis solvents operate primarily in Latin America, where Solvay holds a leading regional position
10.
Bicarbonate secular growth: Bicarbonate volumes remain steady, growing faster than 2% over consecutive quarters for depollution
11.
Global tire demand: Silica accounts for 12% of total net sales, highly integrated into the energy-efficient passenger car tire sector
12.
Cyclical construction headwinds: Flat glass applications for soda ash are heavily exposed to construction market activity

Competitive moat and barriers

13.
Substantial scale advantage: Solvay is the number one global player in soda ash and peroxides capacity
14.
High transportation barriers: High bulk transport costs prevent seaborne players from easily displacing regional synthetic producers
15.
Unmatched process expertise: Solvay possesses a historical 160-year process advantage in synthetic and natural trona extraction
16.
Semiconductor integration moat: Electronic-grade peroxides command premium pricing, requiring extremely high purity for semiconductor fabs
17.
Oligopolistic pricing structure: The synthetic market is highly consolidated among Solvay, WE Soda, and Şişecam
18.
Other side of the story: European synthetic soda ash assets are structurally disadvantaged by high energy and carbon costs versus US/Turkish natural players

Section 5 – The possible investment case 18 findings

Section summary
  1. The investment case is strengthened by structural cost reductions and substantial strategic rare earth processing optionality.

Structural cost-saving execution

1.
Ambitious cost target: Solvay seeks to achieve €350m in cumulative gross annual cost savings by 2028
2.
Execution ahead of target: Management successfully delivered €211m in structural cost savings by end-2025
3.
Support function right sizing: Phasing out the Syensqo Transition Services Agreement allows support function simplification
4.
STAR operations rollout: The STAR Factory program targets fixed cost reductions across 43 plant locations
5.
Sustained target run-rate: The operational excellence programs seek to reduce the corporate cost run-rate below €50m by 2028
6.
Digital integration roadmap: Standardizing ERP systems across European assets acts as a critical enabler for procurement efficiency

Niche industrial expansions

7.
Semiconductor demand capture: Solvay doubled high-purity electronic hydrogen peroxide capacity in China during 2025
8.
Plausibility of demand: Semiconductor-grade chemical expansion has a high plausibility driven by structural fab build-outs
9.
Circular silica commercialization: Solvay initiated bio-circular silica capacity in Italy to address green tire requirements
10.
Failure mode risk: These specialty initiatives could fail if semiconductor and tire markets experience extended demand down-cycles
11.
Organic capacity expansion: Natural soda ash capacity expansion in Green River of 600,000 tons provides highly competitive long-term volume backing

Rare earth strategic optionality

12.
La Rochelle separation option: Repurposing the La Rochelle facility could add €50-100m of annual EBITDA
13.
Western supply independence: Establishing ex-China rare earth separation has a medium-to-high plausibility under Critical Raw Materials regulations
14.
Western supply independence (cont.):
15.
Offtake agreement dependency: This project will fail if Solvay cannot secure long-term offtake commitments with automakers
16.
Independent growth drivers: The cost restructuring, electronic peroxide, and rare earth drivers are structurally independent
17.
Intermittent facility headwind: Local outages like the Saudi HPPO site could slow intermediate chemical contributions
18.
Other side of the story: Persistent overcapacity from Chinese soda ash exports could permanently suppress seaborne margins, neutralizing internal cost savings

Section 7 – Key risks and the other side 12 findings

Section summary
  1. High energy and carbon exposure represents a structural headwind, stretching FCF below dividend commitments.

Structural carbon and energy inflation

1.
European carbon exposures: Coal consumption on synthetic sites creates severe exposure to EU ETS carbon price hikes
2.
Contract indexation limits: European contract pricing is linked to TTF gas, limiting near-term pricing power
3.
Seaborne supply imbalances: Turkish natural capacity expansions from Ciner threaten long-term European supply-demand balances
4.
Uncapped environment provision: Solvay holds large environmental liabilities of €0.5bn on legacy chemical remediation
5.
Regulatory policy pressure: Chemical environmental restrictions (like prospective European PFAS chemical bans) represent persistent litigation headwinds

Dividend sustainability and balance sheet pressure

6.
Uncovered capital returns: FCF of €200m in 2026 is insufficient to cover the proposed €254m dividend
7.
Stranded separation costs: Post-spin TSA stranded costs of €10m per quarter weigh on fixed costs
8.
Fluorine footprint liabilities: Footprint optimization and site closures in Germany create ongoing restructuring cash drains
9.
High pension liabilities: Residual post-demerger pension liabilities of €0.6bn restrict capital allocation freedom
10.
Early warning metrics: Investors should monitor monthly Chinese synthetic export volumes and seaborne pricing benchmarks
11.
False friend signals: Temporary EBITDA beats driven by speculative carbon credit sales can mask underlying structural volume decay
12.
Other side of the story: Rapid Chinese synthetic plant shutdowns driven by government anti-involution guidelines could tighten global supply
