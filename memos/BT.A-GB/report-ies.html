BT (BT.A-GB): what the half-year results of 5 November 2026 do to the case

These results leave the park where it was and narrow it at both ends: they modestly strengthen the cash half of the case by removing or shrinking three of its single-step risks, they do nothing for the earnings line the case is measured on, and they push the first proof of the cash inflection entirely into the second half.

1. What changed

  • A trading beat, on the lines the case already credits. Adjusted EBITDA for the half to 30 September 2026 was £4,097m, up 1%; second-quarter EBITDA of £2,084m beat consensus of £2,020m by 3.2%. Adjusted revenue was £8,750m, up 1%, the first growth in two and a half years, but the beat came from handsets: adjusted service revenue fell 1% to £7,685m. The case assumed flat EBITDA and no revenue engine. This is that, slightly better.
  • Openreach better, retail worse. Second-quarter broadband line losses were 175k against 198k expected and 192k in the first quarter; the full-year expectation stays at about 800k. Openreach broadband ARPU rose 7% to £17.8 a month, take-up reached 41% and Openreach EBITDA rose 4% to £2,238m. Consumer broadband ARPU fell 3% to £40.8, Consumer broadband net additions were -7k, Consumer EBITDA fell 1% and Business EBITDA fell 7%.
  • Cash weak in the half. Normalised free cash flow was £236m against £569m, down 59%, which management attributes to timing (the anchor-price decision and working capital). Reported capital expenditure fell 5% to £2,282m; cash capital expenditure fell only 1% to £2,518m. Net debt was £21,006m at 30 September 2026 against £19,966m at 31 March 2026. Guidance was reconfirmed, not raised: EBITDA of £8.1bn to £8.2bn and normalised free cash flow of about £2.0bn for FY27, about £3.0bn by the end of the decade. The FY27 figure excludes TalkTalk.
  • Three of the case's dated events have happened. Pensions: the triennial deficit fell to £2.7bn from £3.7bn, and £2.1bn of zero-coupon bonds maturing in 2033 go to the scheme in place of the scheduled cash contributions for FY28 to FY31. Total payments to full funding are £282m higher than before. Distributions of up to £1.5bn are permitted in FY27, rising to £2.0bn by FY30. TalkTalk: BT bought it out of administration on 5 October 2026, with 2.5m customers, a cash impact of about £400m in FY27 and a competition-authority public interest review now open. Ofcom: the final decision of 28 September 2026 withdrew the one offer already blocked and approved the two alternatives; the review records no margin-control language. Separately, the competition authority provisionally found against nexfibre's purchase of Netomnia in October 2026, and Virgin Media O2 has halted its fibre upgrade beyond existing commitments.
  • The sell-side split did not close. Morgan Stanley raised its target to 270p from 255p (Overweight). Bernstein stayed at 235p (Outperform). Deutsche Bank stayed at 150p (Sell) and noted second-quarter adjusted earnings per share of 8.2p against its own 9.2p. The median target, about 219p, barely moved. The review holds no post-results consensus earnings-per-share data.

2. The challenge

We see three readings the case does not hold. Two run for the shares and one against.

Reading one: the pension settlement removes the wedge on which the case refused the higher multiple. The case holds 10.0x rather than its conceded 12.0x anchor largely because normalised free cash flow is not shareholder cash, and every cover test in it deducts the case's £780m a year of pension deficit payments through FY30. If the bonds replace those cash contributions for FY28 to FY31, then for four years the company's measure and the shareholder's cash differ mainly by restructuring. Permitted distributions of £1.5bn rising to £2.0bn sit well above the case's own dividend bill of about £850m to £950m a year. The buyback, which the case names as the only step that acts on the share count and puts no earlier than FY29, could plausibly come sooner. For this to be right: the bonds must genuinely replace cash contributions and not sit beside them; the rating agencies and the company's own BBB+ gate must not count the £2.1bn as debt in a way that pushes the leverage test out; and FY27 cash must arrive. The honest counterweight is that this is a deferral, to a 2033 maturity, at a total cost £282m higher, with net debt already up about £1.0bn in six months. The case argues neither side. It was written for a world of annual cash top-ups.

Reading two: wholesale erosion is ending, not steady, and the effect is on the multiple, not on earnings. The case treats line losses of about 800k a year as a standing 4% erosion and gives odds of about 45% that the multiple reverts toward 8.3x if a Key Driver fails. Since then TalkTalk, which the review says accounted for more than half of Openreach's line losses, has come in-house; the scaled second wholesaler has been provisionally blocked; and the largest cable rival has stopped upgrading and may rent Openreach's network. For this to be right, quarterly losses must keep stepping down after TalkTalk's customers have been targeted by rivals, and the December decision must confirm the block. We note the limit of this reading on the case's own arithmetic: each 100k of line losses is about 0.16p of earnings per share, so even a large improvement barely moves the metric. What it moves is the odds between the case's multiple branches.

Reading three: the inflection is being bought, and the case's main tracking series is no longer clean. The better line-loss number arrives beside a £400m outlay on the customer that caused most of the losses. That outlay, like the pension bonds, sits outside the guided cash measure, so the measure drifts further from the shareholder's cash in FY27, not closer. Reaching about £2.0bn requires about £1.76bn in the second half, and management's own explanation of the first half is that working capital recovers. The case's test for May 2027 is £2.0bn with less than £300m of working-capital and factoring inflows. It is plausible that the company hits the number and trips the alarm in the same result, which settles nothing. And the case's thresholds of 700k and 950k line losses a year were set when TalkTalk was an external wholesale customer; with its lines inside the group, losses can migrate from the wholesale count to retail churn and trading losses. For this to be right, TalkTalk's base must leak at retail, or the authority must force a wholesale divestment, as competitors have asked.

One obvious reading fails on the evidence: that revenue has turned. The growth is handsets, service revenue fell, and retail ARPU is falling faster than the case recorded.

3. What it does to the case

  • 'Value chain, industry': less pressure, and the most of any rating. The case withheld a higher grade for three dated fragilities: Ofcom's final decision, the competition decision on nexfibre and Netomnia, and the TalkTalk sale process. Two have resolved and the third is provisionally favourable. A new one replaces them: the public interest review of the TalkTalk purchase itself.
  • 'External forces', 'Competition', 'Political and regulatory': less pressure, for the same reasons.
  • 'No fundamental shocks' and the 'Cockroach' flag: less pressure. The pension step-up and the TalkTalk change of control were two of the case's three sudden risks; neither can now happen in the form the case feared. The margin-squeeze risk passed its dated event.
  • 'Dividends', 'Sustainability', 'Buybacks', 'Shareholder returns': less pressure from FY28, the same for FY27.
  • 'Cash conversion' and 'Conservative guidance': more pressure. The record of meeting cash guidance now rests on one half-year, and on a definition that excludes two more large items.
  • 'Net debt' and 'Balance sheet': more pressure.
  • 'Financial Outputs', 'Goldilocks growth', 'Sell-side upgrades', 'Guidance raised': the same. An EBITDA beat produced no guidance rise and, at one house, an earnings-per-share miss. The pinned sell-side earnings momentum reads F as at the 15 November 2026 snapshot, against D at the memo; we take that as context.
  • 'Modal return' and 'Limited multiple risk': the same grade, more pressure. The price was 215.8p on 13 November 2026. The case re-looks at about 165p; no threshold has been crossed, and the price has moved away from it. The case's unrevised eighteen-month total value of 217.2p is roughly today's price.
  • 'Leading indicators': the line-loss thresholds need restating for a group that owns TalkTalk.
  • 'Investment Thesis', 'Investment Setup', and the park: the same.

Two reconciliations we cannot make from the review: it gives the savings target as £3.5bn and labour resource heading to 65k to 70k on a continuing basis, against the case's £3.7bn and 75,000 to 80,000. The bases differ, so we do not read either as a raise. The 25m-premises milestone is now March 2027; the case had December 2026.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry what brokers did to their estimates and targets: whether the consensus earnings line moved after an EBITDA beat, and how the sell-side treats the pension bonds and TalkTalk in leverage. That bears directly on reading one. It does not carry any new company disclosure: no second-half cash, no competition outcome, no TalkTalk trading.
  • The competition decision on nexfibre and Netomnia, which the case dates to December 2026. It settles half of reading two.
  • The public interest review of TalkTalk. The review gives no date. Divestment or share remedies would revive the counterparty risk in a new form.
  • The next quarterly update: line losses after rivals' offers to TalkTalk customers, Consumer broadband ARPU and net additions. The PSTN switch-off follows on 31 January 2027.
  • The FY27 result, which the case dates to May 2027: normalised free cash flow against about £2.0bn, its working-capital content, TalkTalk's actual cash cost, and the shareholder's cash against the dividend.
  • What the company has not committed to disclose: the working-capital and factoring content of second-half cash, line losses on a basis comparable with the period before TalkTalk, and any leverage conditions attached to the new distribution limits. Reading three may therefore stay open after May, because the figure that would close it is one the reader must rebuild.
