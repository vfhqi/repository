Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The Key Question evaluates Carlsberg's upcoming results and strategic transition to verify if its multi- beverage strategy can offset structural beer volume declines and support compounding earnings growth over the next three years.

Strategic Direction and EPS Drivers

1.
The Key Question: "What do Carlsberg's formal outlook and financial framework, its informal management commentary and the published forecasts of every covering broker imply for each of the company's next four reporting events, and what has changed in outlook, broker forecasts, target prices, ratings and news flow since the Q3 2026 trading statement, and in particular since 1 November 2026?"
2.
Compelling transition: Carlsberg is aggressively pivoting to a multi-beverage model to support compounding earnings growth. This strategic shift follows the transformational acquisition of Britvic, which increases soft drinks exposure to approximately 30% of group volumes. Analysts at Jefferies project that strong cost discipline and potential share buybacks should ensure that earnings per share compound at high-singledigit rates.
3.
Attractive valuation: Morgan Stanley argues that Carlsberg's mid-single-digit operating profit growth combined with share buybacks supports a highly resilient financial algorithm. This algorithmic compounding potential is currently undervalued because the market does not fully price in the structural shift. Reaching the company's target leverage ratio will also pave the way for a resumption of value-accretive share buybacks in the medium term.
4.
Organic growth debate: Bernstein Research notes that the central debate surrounding Carlsberg is its long-term run-rate organic growth. Bottom-up assessments indicate that around 50% of the company's portfolio exhibits moderate growth, while 25% remains in structural decline. Consequently, establishing a consistent pattern of low-single-digit volume growth is critical to dispelling investor skepticism and driving a sustained stock rerating.

The company's net debt to EBITDA ratio reached 3.28x at the end of 2025. Approximately 18% of the company's debt requires gross refinancing by December 2028. Under a hypothetical 6.5% interest rate environment, this refinancing cycle could result in a one percent headwind to earnings growth over the next three years.

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg holds high confidence in its financial outlook based on the reiterated four to six percent organic EBIT growth target and accelerated Britvic synergy realization.
  2. Deleveraging momentum is projected to bring group leverage below the 2.5 times net debt to EBITDA target by the end of fiscal year 2026. Net read: Sustained top-line growth is supported by expanding soft drinks exposure and strategic pricing power despite persistent volumes pressure in China.
