Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. ABB entered the 20 October 2026 results with a stronger structural position and a higher expectations bar, led by Electrification and data-centre demand, while Automation remained the principal earnings uncertainty. Positioning, structurally advantaged: ABB is a global technology company focused on electrification, automation and Motion, with more than 140 years of operating history and approximately 110,000 employees, placing it between diversified industrial peers such as Siemens and Schneider Electric and more specialized electrical-equipment companies. Electrification, primary engine: Electrification supplies low- and medium-voltage distribution, switchgear, protection, building systems, installation products and services, and had become the central growth engine entering the results after Q3 orders rose 70% year on year to $7.67 billion, compared with only 1% growth in Automation.
1.
Motion, resilient contributor: Motion combines motors, drives, generators, traction, powertrain and lifecycle services, and Q3 orders increased 14% year on year while operational EBITA reached $449 million at a 19.1% margin, contrasting with Automation’s $315 million and 14.8% margin.
2.
Automation, cyclical counterweight: Automation covers process, hybrid, marine, ports, measurement, analytics and machine automation, but Q3 orders were broadly stable at $2.11 billion and its $7.1 billion backlog was only modestly above the prior quarter, leaving ABB more dependent on Electrification than its three-business model suggests.
3.
ABB Way, execution model: ABB’s decentralized ABB Way assigns business lines and divisions responsibility for strategy, resources and performance, with approximately 70 business lines operating under stability, profitability or growth mandates after full performance-accountability implementation in 2025.
4.
Decentralization, advantage and risk: The ABB Way is intended to combine local decision speed with group- scale capital, brand and technology, but external commentary has identified the corresponding risk that decentralized operations make supply-chain economies, technology adoption and group-wide software scaling harder than at Siemens or Schneider Electric.
5.
Three-year operating progress, material: ABB reported 2023 operational EBITA margin of 16.9%, while its nine-month 2026 margin had reached 21.4%, a 450-basis-point improvement that supports management’s claim of sustained productivity and mix gains rather than a purely cyclical recovery.
6.
Growth framework, surpassed: Management’s long-term framework remains 5% to 7% comparable revenue growth plus 1% to 2% acquired growth through the cycle, but Q3 2026 revenue grew 14% organically and the full-year guide was raised to the low-teens range, putting current delivery materially above the through-cycle bar.
7.
Margin ambition, upgraded: ABB lifted its medium-term operational EBITA margin target from 16% to 19% to 18% to 22% in November 2025, while Q3 2026 delivered 20.8%, meaning the company was already inside the upper half of the new corridor before the property gain expected in Q4.
8.
Energy-transition exposure, broad: ABB estimates that approximately half of its offering supports electrification and about 35% directly enables higher energy efficiency, giving it exposure to grid modernization, industrial efficiency and decarbonization beyond the narrower data-centre theme.
9.
Data centres, expectations escalator: Electrification’s backlog reached $16 billion, up 85% year on year, and data-centre orders again grew at a triple-digit rate, making near-term revenue more visible but also raising the hurdle for future results because the market was already capitalizing sustained artificial-intelligence infrastructure spending.

Electrification delivered the strongest beat, with Q3 orders reaching $7.67 billion, up 70% comparably and approximately 12% above consensus, while backlog expanded to $16 billion and revenue grew 21% with a 25.6% operational EBITA margin, 20 basis points above consensus. Automation was the principal miss, with orders of $2.11 billion, flat year on year and 9% below consensus, while revenue grew only 5%, 1% below consensus, although the 14.8% operational EBITA margin improved 90 basis points and beat consensus by 20 basis points. Group orders reached $12.06 billion, approximately 6% above consensus, revenue was $9.70 billion and broadly in line, operational EBITA of $2.03 billion was about 1% above expectations, but basic EPS of $0.65 was 18% below DNB Carnegie consensus because of the non-cash Rotork foreign-exchange hedge loss.

Full-year guidance was raised to low-teens comparable revenue growth from low double-digit to low teens, with reported operational EBITA margin improvement of at least 270 basis points expected for 2026, equivalent to more than 21.7%, but approximately 80 basis points of that improvement comes from the CHF240 million property gain, leaving a pre-gain margin of at least 20.9% versus consensus at 20.8%. Q4 revenue guidance suggested comparable growth broadly similar to Q3, or approximately 14%, which was below Deutsche Bank's 16% expectation, although management expected Q4 operational EBITA margin to soften sequentially but less than the normal seasonal pattern.

Deutsche Bank cut 2026 EPS from $3.12 to $3.06 primarily because of the Rotork hedge loss, while leaving 2027 EPS at $3.74, and Morgan Stanley cut 2026 EPS by 0.7% but raised 2027 and 2028 EPS by 2% and 2.8% respectively, reflecting stronger Electrification sales and margins, with 2027 adjusted EBITA at $9.675 billion, 7% above consensus of $9.027 billion.

Morgan Stanley cut its target price from CHF88 to CHF82 and retained Equal-weight, reducing the target multiple from 20.6x to 18.5x 2027 EV/EBITA because peer valuations had de-rated, even though its 2027 and 2028 EPS forecasts increased, while calculating that ABB traded at approximately 24.4x 2027 earnings, around 25% above Electrification peers at 18x to 20x. Bernstein retained Market-Perform and an CHF80 target even after raising 2027 EPS, because ABB remained one of the most expensive European electrical companies and the broker expected tougher data-centre order comparisons, while noting that ABB continues to favour hardware through the Mitsubishi Electric low-voltage joint venture, while Siemens and Schneider Electric were more active in industrial software. Deutsche Bank retained Hold and a CHF76 target, with the shares valued at approximately 17x 2027 EV/EBITA and 23x 2027 earnings, because the operational upside was balanced by already-high expectations and medium-term artificial-intelligence capex risk.

ABB is losing the software race, with Bernstein estimating that Siemens and Rockwell have larger software positions within automation, while Schneider's PTC acquisition adds computer-aided design and productlifecycle-management capabilities to AVEVA, Cognite and its existing automation platform, and Bernstein argued that PTC gives Schneider access to product-design, CAD and PLM data earlier in the customer lifecycle, with cross-selling potential into machine builders, consumer packaged goods and HVAC customers.

ABB's strategy may be improving the portfolio while narrowing its exposure to software and making future EPS increasingly dependent on Electrification demand, successful Rotork integration and Mitsubishi synergies; if data-centre growth normalizes before the new assets mature, the hardware-heavy strategy could produce less recurring growth than the market's premium valuation assumes. The strongest case against the conclusion is that ABB delivered an Electrification order beat, improved 2027 revenue visibility, raised full-year guidance, maintained strong margins, generated robust free cash flow, reported no material tariff impact, and showed diversified infrastructure demand beyond data centres alone, so the market's focus on Automation weakness, capacity constraints and valuation may be underweighting the durability of ABB's structural position in grid modernization, energy efficiency and electrification, particularly because the $16 billion Electrification backlog already underwrites much of 2027 sales and the company has $15 billion to $20 billion of acquisition capacity to deploy toward further Electrification expansion.

One non-obvious reading: ABB's Q3 Electrification pricing broadly offset commodity inflation, and Deutsche Bank expected Electrification price-to-commodity effects to turn positive in Q4, but steel prices were up 32% year to date, copper 18%, aluminum 10% and Brent crude 64%, creating renewed cost pressure across electrical equipment and petroleum-derived materials, making future price-cost protection less certain than the Q3 margin result suggests. The market has focused on data-centre order growth, backlog visibility and valuation, but has under-appreciated that ABB's margin expansion over the next six to eight quarters may depend more on sustained pricing power in an inflationary commodity environment than on operating leverage from volume growth, particularly because the company's gross margin still fell 60 basis points and Motion's margin declined 100 basis points year on year, showing that operational EBITA resilience does not eliminate below-the-line or mix sensitivity. The two most important questions for next disclosures are: First, how much of the $16 billion Electrification backlog will convert into 2027 revenue, what is the data-centre share of that backlog, and will ABB provide a separate data-centre revenue forecast or customer concentration disclosure in the January Q4 results? Second, will ABB deploy a meaningful portion of its $15 billion to $20 billion acquisition capacity toward Electrification or industrial software during 2027, and if so, what deal size, target profile and expected accretion would management consider value-creating relative to the alternative of repurchasing shares at current multiples? 377 Other side of the story: The bullish reading is that ABB can convert the $16 billion Electrification backlog into 2027 sales and deliver EPS above consensus, but the bearish reading is that 2027 growth is already largely reflected, Automation has not recovered, valuation multiples have fallen, and a 2028–29 data-centre capex slowdown could erase the premium before the earnings arrive. ABB's record Electrification orders and diversified infrastructure demand could support materially higher 2027 EPS, but the alternative reading is that customers are committing to projects faster than ABB can ship, while residential, process, mining and Middle Eastern demand remain weak; if backlog conversion disappoints, the order beat will prove more valuable for visibility than for near-term earnings.
