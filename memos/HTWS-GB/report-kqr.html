Section summary

Eastcastle Portfolio Expansion

1.
Accelerated growth: Eastcastle Infrastructure DRC has aggressively expanded its telecommunications infrastructure to address severe coverage deficits across the country. The company secured a 32.8 million USD senior secured loan from the Emerging Africa and Asia Infrastructure Fund in August 2026 to fund its growth. Co-founder Peter Lewis stated in August 2026 that this private debt financing will expand Eastcastle's operational footprint from 1,072 sites to 1,800 towers by the end of 2026. This rapid site deployment could limit Helios Towers' future tenancy growth if mobile operators choose Eastcastle's newer rural locations instead.
2.
Rural prioritization: Eastcastle Infrastructure is focusing its latest capital deployment program heavily outside of major urban hubs in the Democratic Republic of Congo. The Emerging Africa and Asia Infrastructure Fund confirmed in August 2026 that approximately 70% of the 728 planned towers will be located in rural or underserved regions. Co-founder Pankaj Kulshrestha, a former Eaton Towers executive, is utilizing solar panels and lithium battery systems to mitigate unreliable national grid electricity. These sustainable energy upgrades could reduce diesel overheads, thereby increasing competitive pricing pressure on Helios Towers during upcoming lease renegotiations.
3.
Multi-lateral backing: Eastcastle Infrastructure leverages substantial international development funding to support its capital- intensive tower programs in high-risk African territories. The International Finance Corporation provided a 60 million USD loan alongside 34 million USD from Standard Bank of South Africa in 2023 to finance the initial growth phase. Section 4 – Regulatory Impact: Banque Centrale du Congo's Foreign Currency Cash

Section summary
  1. The Central Bank of Congo will enforce a complete ban on physical foreign-currency cash transactions starting in April 2027 to curb massive illicit cash outflows and combat regional money laundering.
  2. Electronic foreign-currency transfers remain fully permitted under the new regulatory framework, leaving the electronic dollarized lease collections of independent tower operators largely unaffected.
  3. Overall confidence in what the evidence shows on the Key Question is medium.

Ban

Central Bank De-dollarization Mandate

1.
Abrupt regulatory restriction: The Banque Centrale du Congo announced an upcoming ban on physical foreign-currency cash transactions starting on April 9, 2027. This sweeping policy will limit all foreign-currency activities strictly to traceable electronic banking channels such as bank transfers, cards, and mobile money platforms. The central bank will also restrict the physical importation of foreign currency banknotes exclusively to itself to restore domestic monetary sovereignty over the economy. This sudden transition forces a rapid modernization of the national payment system.
2.
Rigorous compliance enforcement: The central bank governor André Wamesso reported that 85% of the 10 billion USD in banknotes imported during 2025 completely bypassed the banking system. This massive leakage suggests that uncontrolled cash circulation is actively fueling regional money laundering and terrorism financing. Consequently, the new regulations are intended to help the Democratic Republic of Congo comply with international sanctions and maintain access to global correspondent banks. This regulatory shift is fully supported by the United States Treasury to secure mineral corridors.
3.
Technical strain on institutions: Local financial institutions face severe operational challenges as they rush to upgrade their outdated core banking systems before the deadline. Research shows that up to 50% of core banking platforms in Africa are obsolete, leaving them vulnerable to digital cyber threats. Smaller microfinance networks are pleading for technical assistance to connect to the national interoperable payment switch known as Switch Mosolo. These tech deficits could disrupt local payments if the implementation proceeds without extensive support.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some financial analysis suggests that the de-dollarization measures will have only a minimal effect on actual economic dollarization because of the domestic currency's structural weakness. Despite various historical de-dollarization campaigns introduced since 2012, foreign currencies continue to dominate up to 85% of large transactions and 86.1% of bank deposits. Thus, the central bank's limited reserves will prevent any effective defense of the Congolese franc under persistent depreciatory pressures.

Operational Impact on Tower Infrastructure

5.
Insulated cash collections: Helios Towers remains largely insulated from the physical cash ban because its high-value lease collections are processed electronically. CFO Manjit Dhillon confirmed that the company bills its anchor tenants in US dollars and does not anticipate any shift in currency mix. Since electronic bank transfers in foreign currencies remain fully permitted under the new rules, lease collections are highly secure from physical currency restrictions. This means the ban will not impact the company's dollarized segment revenues.
6.
Complex upstreaming procedures: Helios Towers must continue to document and process all cross-border cash flows through authorized local banks to ensure compliance. Legal expert Amani Cibambo noted that equity inflows, shareholder loans, and dividend repatriation remain strictly subject to the central bank's exchange framework. To prevent trapped value in the country, investors must design robust payment waterfalls and maintain clean tax documentation before distributing returns. Failure to comply with these formal bank procedures could delay international cash upstreaming.
7.
Increased local currency billing: The physical cash ban will force tower companies to settle ground rents and supplier payments through formal digital transfers. Currently, many local ground leases and maintenance services in remote areas rely heavily on physical transactions. Transitioning these microtransactions to electronic banking or mobile money will increase near-term administrative costs for infrastructure managers. However, this digital shift will ultimately improve transaction transparency and reduce the risk of localized cash theft.
8.
Accelerated mobile money adoption: Major mobile operators like Vodacom and Orange expect the cash ban to accelerate the usage of their digital financial services. Active mobile money subscriptions in the country rose by 18.3% to over 34 million, generating 477.4 million USD in transaction revenue. This regulatory push toward electronic payments is driving massive mobile money growth for carriers like Airtel Africa. Increased mobile wallet adoption will provide operators with extra liquidity to meet their dollarized tower lease obligations.

This policy successfully redirected informal remittance channels into the traceable financial system, bolstering national foreign exchange reserves. Similarly, the Congolese central bank is restricting physical banknotes to shift transaction volume into formal banks and traceable networks. 12.
Other side of the story: While the physical cash ban aims to modernize the Congolese financial system, it poses a severe threat of operational disruption for rural tower maintenance. Furthermore, any aggressive enforcement of dedollarization that restricts electronic US dollar holdings would quickly trap corporate value, forcing tower companies to accept devalued local currency that impairs earnings per share.
