Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

The aggregation of these signals suggests that HHLA faces a compound crisis rather than a series of independent challenges. The operational bottlenecks from terminal automation are self-inflicted and multi-year in duration, meaning that near-term profitability will remain under severe pressure regardless of macro conditions. The capital intensity required to complete these modernizations is unsustainable relative to current cash generation, forcing reliance on debt markets or public-sector support at a time when interest rates remain elevated and municipal budgets face constraints.

The competitive positioning disadvantage from river depth and infrastructure constraints is structural and cannot be resolved through internal actions alone, requiring sustained public investment in dredging and rail networks that faces political and environmental opposition. The governance uncertainty from minority shareholder litigation creates strategic paralysis where MSC cannot fully integrate operations or commit additional capital until legal clarity emerges.

Taken together, these plausible problem to monitor indicate that HHLA's business model is experiencing simultaneous stress across multiple dimensions –profitability, cash flow, competitive position, execution credibility, and strategic clarity. The company operates in a mature, capital-intensive industry with limited pricing power, high fixed costs, and intense regional competition, making it exceptionally vulnerable to operational missteps or cyclical downturns.

The fact that margins and returns are declining during a period of elevated storage fees from geopolitical shipping disruptions suggests that underlying operational performance is even weaker than reported figures indicate. When these temporary windfalls normalize, HHLA will face the full impact of its structurally uncompetitive cost base and disrupted operational capacity.

Outliers worth noting

HHLA's group profit after tax collapsed by 89.3% to €3.4 million during the first half of 2026, representing an extreme earnings deterioration that vastly exceeds typical cyclical downturns observed in comparable European port operators. This profitability collapse drove the operating EBITDA margin to 17.2%, sitting substantially below Port of Rotterdam's calculated 62% operating EBITDA margin, creating a 45-percentage-point margin differential that cannot be explained by normal operational variance or geographic positioning alone.

The magnitude of this underperformance suggests fundamental structural dysfunction rather than transitory operational friction, particularly when considered against the backdrop of HHLA's intensive capital expenditure program that should theoretically enhance rather than degrade operational efficiency.
