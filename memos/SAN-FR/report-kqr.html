Section 2 – The 2027 Growth Step-Down and Revenue Headwinds

Section summary
  1. Favorable pricing releases and non-recurring adjustments in late 2026 will create high baseline comparisons that artificially depress reported revenue growth rates throughout 2027.
  2. Completed asset divestments and the termination of the lucrative Regeneron research reimbursement alliance will remove over two billion euros in high-margin revenues in early 2027.

Gross-to-Net and Reimbursement Phasing

1.
Pricing normalization: Favorable price releases in 2026 will create exceptionally high baseline comparisons that depress reported top-line expansion in 2027. Sanofi specialty care leadership telegraphed that the positive grossto-net tailwinds from 2026 will not recur once the company anniversaries these benefits. The return to historical pricing patterns will act as a major brake on near-term revenue acceleration. This transition suggests that 2027 reported performance will step down as these pricing benefits exit the baseline.

Adjustments fading: Transitory gross-to-net provision adjustments provided an artificial boost to third-quarter performance that will not persist. Deutsche Bank reported that approximately two percentage points of the thirdquarter revenue growth, representing three hundred million euros, was driven by one-off gross-to-net adjustments. Since these adjustments are non-recurring, their absence will lead to a visible deceleration in the first half of 2027. Reimbursement cliff: Steep operational funding cliffs will materialize in early 2027 from the completed repayment of the Regeneron alliance development balance. Sanofi management confirmed that the company must actively absorb a 1.1 billion headwind to business operating income due to the end of the Regeneron R&D cost reimbursements. Although underlying product demand remains strong, losing this major funding offset will compress operational profit margins. This transition will require superior cost discipline across the remaining biopharma divisions to sustain overall corporate profitability.

alliance shift: Completed development balance repayments under the Monoclonal Antibody Alliance will significantly increase the cost burden on Sanofi. The loss of these incoming offsets will directly reduce the net margins the company retains on Dupixent commercialization. This development represents a severe structural profitability drag that will peak in the first half of 2027.

Divestment Fallout and Lovenox Transfers

5.
Divestment drag: Significant reported revenue contractions will materialize in 2027 from the planned exit of dilutive legacy medicines. Sanofi disclosed that the divestments of mature assets, including the Cheplapharm partnership and Medley sale, will trigger a sales impact of approximately 1.3 billion euros. This planned decline is necessary to simplify the portfolio and focus resources on innovative immunology franchises. However, the immediate loss of scale will depress the reported top-line expansion rate during the transition year.
6.
Lovenox transfer: Accelerated volume declines will impact the General Medicines division as Lovenox operations outside the United States are handed over in early 2027. Sanofi confirmed that the commercial transfer of enoxaparin and other legacy medicines to Cheplapharm will begin in the first quarter of 2027. These mature products generated combined annual sales of roughly 1.2 billion euros in 2025, which will completely disappear from Sanofi's reported base. This transaction will cause a sharp reported sales drop that is highly concentrated in the first half of 2027.
7.
Opella dilution: Significant reported revenue and scale dilution will occur when the transaction to sell the consumer healthcare unit Opella finally closes. News reports confirmed that Sanofi finalized a share purchase agreement to sell a fifty percent controlling stake in Opella to CD&R for ten billion euros. This business accounted for roughly eleven percent of Sanofi's total sales in prior periods. Divesting this stable cash generator rebases Sanofi's group revenues downward and increases near-term volatility.

Sarclisa Reversals and Historical Precedents

8.
Sarclisa adjustments: Unfavorable gross margin adjustments have emerged from the subcutaneous formulation reversals for Sarclisa. Jefferies reported that Sanofi's second-quarter gross margins benefited from a two hundred million euro reversal related to subcutaneous Sarclisa. While this temporary adjustment flattered near-term profits, it highlights the inherent volatility of clinical development accounting. This accounting reversal will create a challenging baseline comparison that will penalize reported operating leverage in 2027.
9.
Restructuring postmortem: Severe pipeline productivity challenges at Sanofi mirror the historical restructuring hurdles faced by European peers. Rothschild & Co Redburn pointed out that Sanofi is undergoing a rigorous R&D postmortem similar to the strategic overhaul completed by AstraZeneca in 2011. Historically, rebuilding R&D productivity from failures requires extensive leadership transitions and timeline delays. This past case suggests that Sanofi's pipeline turnaround will take several years to translate into material earnings.
10.
Contrary evidence: Strong demand-driven growth in immunology assets could entirely offset the combined impact of the 2027 divestments. Morgan Stanley upgraded its long-term estimates, noting that underlying Dupixent volume and newly launched specialty medicines will expand the baseline. This organic acceleration could mitigate the 1.3 billion euro divestment impact and stabilize reported growth. Consequently, the feared growth cliff in 2027 may be shallower than consensus models currently predict.

Jefferies Research highlighted that Sanofi trades at an attractive multiple of just 7.5 times its forecast price-to-earnings ratio. This multiple represents a steep 20% discount to the broader European pharmaceutical sector. This expansion will be supported by a flat R&D budget and tight control of administrative expenses. Therefore, the margins will improve each year to support profitable growth even if reported sales decelerate in early 2027.

This baseline suggests that the step-down is a temporary accounting variance rather than a structural deterioration of Sanofi's long-term earnings trajectory.

Section 3 – Dupixent Growth Profile and Profit-Sharing Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Divergent growth trends show Dupixent total volumes rising from chronic patient accumulation despite falling new start market shares.
  2. Rebased profit-sharing alliance terms limit Sanofi's incremental margin expansion as partner reimbursements end and global splits equalize.

Volume Growth and Patient Dynamics

1.
Decelerating starts: Sanofi faces a widening divergence between new patient starts and total accumulated volume. Dermatologists report that Dupixent's first-line share of biologic-naive patient starts has fell from 80% or 90% historically to between 30% and 40% today. This trend occurs as newer competitors enter the dermatology market with less frequent dosing schedules. However, the massive chronic base of over 1.5 million active patients continues to expand total volumes. Consequently, long-term cumulative volume growth remains highly resilient despite the drop in divergence between new patient starts.

Indication expansion: Sanofi is leveraging new regulatory approvals to offset the deceleration in standard atopic dermatitis starts. Global net product sales of the drug increased 35% year-over-year to 6.5 billion dollars in the third quarter of 2026. This expansion is heavily supported by successful launches in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and chronic spontaneous urticaria. These new indications provide a vast pool of biologic-naive patients that rejuvenates the top-line growth curve.

Favorable pricing: Sanofi recorded a 10% beat on product sales during the third quarter of 2026 due to underlying demand and temporary adjustments. Morgan Stanley noted that the outperformance was driven by solid volume growth alongside positive gross-to-net revisions. These adjustments included tighter controls on inappropriate 340B rebate utilization. However, management cautioned that these pricing tailwinds are non-recurring and will not continue expanding.
5.
Contrary evidence: Rapidly increasing physician preference for ultra-long-acting competitors could trigger massive chronic patient switching and disrupt the volume baseline. Deutsche Bank reported that dermatologists expressed significant enthusiasm for AbbVie's next-generation antibody zumilokibart due to its three-month dosing schedule. If patients actively abandon daily or biweekly regimens for these convenient options, Sanofi's total accumulated volume would contract sharply. This threat suggests that chronic patient loyalty is highly vulnerable to competitive convenience breakthroughs.

Profit-Sharing Mechanics and Alliance Math

6.
Balanced split: Sanofi shares the financial rewards of its primary blockbuster equally within its largest geographic market. SEC filings confirm that Sanofi and Regeneron equally split all profits and losses from commercialization in the United States. Since the US market accounted for 5.0 billion dollars of the 6.5 billion dollar total in Q3 2026, this equal split dictates the economics of the majority of global sales. This balanced structure means Sanofi retains exactly 50% of the core domestic operating profit.
7.
Sliding scale: Sanofi retains a larger portion of product profitability in international territories under the original alliance terms. Regulatory documents show that international profits are shared on a sliding scale starting at 65% for Sanofi and ending at 55% for Sanofi. This structure leaves Regeneron with 35% to 45% of ex-US profits. Consequently, Sanofi retains a larger portion of ex-US profits than it does on US sales.
8.
90 91 10Q Reimbursement ending: Sanofi faced a structural shift in cash flow dynamics following the complete repayment of the historical development balance. Regeneron fully repaid the remaining 595 million dollar development obligation as of June 30, 2026. While this repayment formerly reduced Regeneron's profit share, its completion means Regeneron now receives its full unreduced share of alliance profits. For Sanofi, this transition removes a major cash inflow and raises reported other operating expenses.
9.
Partner expansion: Regeneron is experiencing significant margin expansion as it transitions to recording its full share of alliance profits. Regeneron reported that its share of collaboration profits rose to 2.57 billion dollars in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 1.45 billion dollars in the prior year. This 77% growth reflects the double benefit of rising sales and the end of development reimbursements. This shift demonstrates that the partner is capturing a rising portion of total economics.
10.
Margin compression: Sanofi's reported biopharma margins face near-term pressure from the rising profit share paid out to its partner. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported that Sanofi's other operating expenses rose 28% due to higher profit-sharing payments to Regeneron on account of increased sales. This payment offset some of the operating leverage achieved from corporate cost discipline. Therefore, every incremental euro of Dupixent sales triggers a substantial profit outflow that limits Sanofi's margin expansion.

Expanded Alliance and Next-Generation Pipeline

11.
Strategic extension: Sanofi and Regeneron expanded their 20-year collaboration in October 2026 to add next- generation long-acting antibodies. Under the updated agreement, Sanofi paid a 1.0 billion dollar upfront fee to secure access to four early-stage immunology assets. This expansion aims to add next-generation longacting antibodies to develop longer-acting successors. However, these preclinical programs require years of clinical validation before generating commercial revenues.
12.
Global parity: Sanofi agreed to a simplified 50/50 global profit split for all new assets commercialized under the expanded alliance. Press releases confirm that both development and commercialization costs, as well as future profits, will be shared equally on a global basis. This contrasts with the original sliding scale that favored Sanofi in international markets. Consequently, Sanofi agreed to a simplified 50/50 split that reduces its relative share of ex-US profitability.
13.
Favorable terms: Jefferies Research views the expanded agreement terms as highly favorable for the partner due to the enhanced international economics. The broker calculated that the 5 to 15 percentage point increase in Regeneron's ex-US profit share would translate into a 15 dollar net present value increase. This analysis underscores that Sanofi conceded valuable international margin to secure long-term pipeline defense. This concession reflects Sanofi's urgent need to diversify away from its 2031 patent cliff.
14.
Other side of the story: Sustained label expansion and the massive size of the existing patient base will maintain Dupixent as a high-margin cash cow despite partner outflows. Morgan Stanley upgraded its long-term estimates, noting that higher Dupixent sales and operating leverage will expand Sanofi's business EPS growth through 2030. This growth remains highly visible because the drug's clean safety profile and multiple indications create a powerful commercial moat. Thus, the franchise will continue driving near-term profitability even under the rebased profit-sharing terms.

Section 4 – Margin Expansion Constraints and Launch Portfolio Contribution

This price erosion is expected to persist across the biopharmaceutical portfolio as gross-to-net pricing benefits from earlier periods begin to normalize. Consequently, the company must rely on organic volume expansion to sustain its top-line growth trajectory. This shifting pricing dynamic underscores why cost discipline is critical to achieving near-term margin expansion.

Disciplined R&D spending: Sanofi's new research leadership plans to keep the global R&D budget flat despite an ongoing push for increased scientific rigor. This spending constraint is a key element of the company's operational excellence and cost control strategy. Management seeks to improve pipeline quality by implementing a rigorous technical review board to manage risk. Consequently, flat R&D budgets will prevent structural expense growth from limiting operating leverage as new launches scale.
3.
Controlled administrative costs: Sanofi continues to enforce strict cost discipline across its global administrative infrastructure. The company reported that selling and general expenses decreased by 0.9% at constant exchange rates in the third quarter of 2026. This expense reduction successfully reduced the SG&A-to-sales ratio by 2.4 percentage points. Maintaining this disciplined cost base is vital for expanding operating margins each year through 2030.

Contrary evidence: Heavy profit-sharing obligations with Regeneron could entirely offset internal cost discipline and restrict Sanofi's post-2027 operating margins. Morgan Stanley warns that higher Dupixent sales trigger automatic increases in other operating expenses due to partner payouts. This contract structure means that profit-sharing outflows will grow alongside sales, creating a margin drag. Consequently, internal administrative savings might not translate directly into bottom-line margin expansion.

Incremental Contributions and Launch Execution

5.
Sizable sales ambitions: Sanofi targets twenty-five billion euros in Dupixent sales and ten billion euros in launch portfolio sales by 2030. This long-term revenue blueprint is designed to help the group secure sustainable growth beyond the patent cliff. However, because Dupixent's profits are shared with Regeneron, the launch portfolio must supply a disproportionate share of incremental profits. This division of labor makes successful commercial execution of new launches critical.
6.
Accelerating launch momentum: Sanofi's non-Dupixent pharmaceutical launches achieved 31.4% sales growth in the third quarter of 2026, reaching 1.4 billion euros. This growth was led by the strong commercial uptake of ALTUVIIIO, Ayvakit, and Rezurock in key markets. For example, Rezurock sales grew 43.0% to 165 million euros, demonstrating high demand. These proprietary specialty medicines represent the most direct path to high-margin incremental operating profit.
7.
Strong specialty execution: Sanofi is achieving significant volume expansion through its haemophilia and pediatric vaccines portfolios. Sales of the hemophilia franchise ALTUVIIIO increased by 21.3% to 453 million euros in late 2026. Additionally, the pediatric RSV immunization Beyfortus provides vital cash flow support during respiratory seasons. These assets generate high-margin revenues that do not carry the same profit-sharing burden seen with Dupixent.
8.
Sustained operating leverage: Morgan Stanley forecasts that Sanofi will achieve a 5% revenue and 7% business EPS compound annual growth rate from 2027 through 2030. The broker expects that continued Dupixent expansion and new specialty launches will drive steady operating margin expansion through cost discipline. Under this base case, the core group operating margin is modeled to rise to 28.4% by 2028. This shows that the launch portfolio is vital for offsetting near-term pricing headwinds.

Rothschild & Co Redburn notes that Novartis's target is supported by deep cuts in third-party spend and steady portfolio simplification. In contrast, Sanofi's projected operating margins remain in the high-20s across the same time horizon.
