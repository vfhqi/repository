Xvivo (XVIVO-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results strengthen the existing case, modestly and unevenly: the two dated events the case was parked for, the European CE mark on the heart system and a lung print above 30 percent, both landed its way and the price sat at the case's entry level going into the print, while services failed the case's one remaining condition and the largest customer's insourcing moved from a risk to an announced fact, so the pressure is upward on two Elements and on no Building Block.

What changed

  • Sales SEK 252.6m for the quarter to 30 September 2026, reported 22 October, against SEK 232m (Redeye), SEK 231m (DNB Carnegie) and published consensus of SEK 238m: a 6 percent beat to consensus. Organic growth 32 percent in local currencies (33 percent excluding heart-trial revenue) against a prior-year quarter of -5 percent. The case had lung growing about 30 percent through 2027 and was looking for lung disposables above 30 percent at this report.
  • Thoracic SEK 176.7m against Redeye's SEK 154m, growth 51 percent; lung SEK 155m against SEK 133m; United States EVLP disposable sales +183 percent in the quarter and +92 percent year to date. Of the thoracic figure, about SEK 19m was timing: SEK 8.5m of initial XPS finance-lease recognition and about SEK 10m of inventory built by the largest US customer.
  • That customer has received approval for its own EVLP platform. Xvivo expects sales to it to decline, with the relationship continuing at a lower value because STEEN Solution and other components remain in the approved platform. Timing and size were not quantified. DNB Carnegie's pre-results estimate was 5 to 6 percent of 2027 revenue exposed.
  • Heart SEK 22m against SEK 21m expected (SEK 24m in the second quarter on the case's figures). CE marking of Heart Assist Transport in August 2026, ahead of the fourth quarter DNB Carnegie had assumed; French reimbursement in hand; a staged rollout from eight existing centres to about 30 priority centres in France, the Netherlands, Austria and the United Kingdom. FDA receipt of the PMA in September, Breakthrough designation, 60 further continued-access patients with CMS cost recovery; commercial launch "targeted" for the end of 2027 subject to approval, wording narrower than before. Liver: the 215-patient DeLIVER trial paused in favour of a possible 510(k) submission in the first half of 2027 after a comparable HOPE device was cleared, with an early-2028 US launch estimated; in no broker's numbers.
  • Abdominal SEK 62.2m against SEK 63m; growth 12 percent after 26 percent in the second quarter; kidney SEK 22m (+19 percent organic, after 72 percent) and liver SEK 40m (+9 percent); management cites seasonality and a manufacturing transfer and expects higher growth again in early 2027. Abdominal gross margin 61 percent.
  • Services SEK 13.7m, -28 percent organic, gross margin -19 percent against +37 percent a year earlier and Redeye's +15 percent; contract conversion "taking longer than originally anticipated"; FlowHawk growth 0 percent in the quarter after 48 percent in the second. Management now frames the surgical capacity as infrastructure for the US heart launch.
  • Group gross margin 70 percent against 73 percent expected and 75 percent a year earlier; thoracic 80 percent from 89 percent, on services losses, a higher share of machine sales and inventory write-downs. EBITDA SEK 52.0m against SEK 43.7m (Redeye), SEK 44m (DNB Carnegie) and about SEK 46m consensus, a 21 percent margin that management attributes partly to seasonally low summer activity, with investment to continue. EBIT SEK 28m against SEK 23m consensus. Reported EPS SEK 1.42 against SEK 0.14, including about SEK 7.1m of exchange gains on cash.

Cash SEK 313.2m, calculated net cash about SEK 193m, EUR 12m undrawn on a EUR 20m facility maturing January 2028. Nine-month capitalised development SEK 131.7m. The company expects about SEK 39m a year of CE-mark amortisation over ten years.

  • Sell-side: one accessible post-results note, Redeye's, which read the quarter as a sales and profitability beat, expected a 5 to 7 percent share-price rise, kept its SEK 145, 385 and 785 fair-value range and published no revised EPS; the analyst discloses a shareholding. The challenge

Three readings press on the case, two against it and one for it, and none is the bear reading of "a timing-inflated beat", which the case already holds as its false-friend hypothesis and its drop-through sensitivity.

First, the lung confirmation and the lung invalidation are now the same signal. The case holds customer insourcing as a separate, future, possible event, with "the customer's own system replacing the XPS in its programme" as the invalidation level, and reads lung disposable growth as the gauge: above 30 percent confirms, below 20 percent for two quarters refutes. This quarter shows the replacement in progress rather than possible, and about SEK 10m of the print was that customer stocking up before it switches. Three things follow that the case never put together. The gauge is contaminated for several quarters: inventory in now, run-down next, then the step-down, so a 183 percent print and a sub-20 percent print can both be noise about the same customer. The size has never been reconciled: on the case's own figures the customer was SEK 132m of SEK 812m of 2025 revenue, about 16 percent, against DNB Carnegie's 5 to 6 percent of 2027 revenue exposed, and the gap is whatever "a lower value" turns out to mean, with only the fluids certain to stay. And the case's bearish trigger could fire in 2027 for a reason it would want to look through, with hub demand intact beneath it. For this reading to be right, the step-down has to fall mainly on the XPS and its disposables rather than on STEEN Solution, to land inside 2027, and to be larger than what three activated hubs add, whose procedure volumes the company does not disclose. Each of those is plausible; the company has said nothing that rules any of them out.

Second, the hub route to market is lower-margin growth, not razor-and-blade growth. The case's mechanism is that lung volume converts to earnings at a rising margin, and its gross-margin risk is framed as abdominal and services outgrowing lung. It does not hold a quarter in which thoracic grows 51 percent and its gross margin falls nine points, to 80 percent, on machine mix, finance-lease recognition and write-downs; that is exactly the case's thoracic alarm line, and the group's 70 percent sits exactly on the consolidated one. The strongest form of this reading is that growth through OPO hubs means placing XPS machines ahead of the disposables that pay for them, so the lung franchise's margin runs at 80 rather than 85 percent for as long as hubs are being added, which is as long as the case wants. Five points on consensus' FY2027 thoracic revenue of SEK 817m is about SEK 41m, roughly three points of EBITDA margin on our path's SEK 1,190m of FY2027 revenue, which is the whole of our margin gap to consensus a second time. For this to be right, the fourth and fifth hubs the case wants have to arrive as further lease-recognised placements, the write-downs have to recur, and the 183 percent disposable growth has to follow the placements more slowly than a razor-and-blade model implies. One quarter is not proof, and a placed machine is the precondition of years of consumables; but the case's own alarm lines are the evidence that it was not expecting this.

Third, and for the case, the regulator has stopped slipping and our modal branch may be the wrong one. Our path sits 20 to 35 percent below the sell-side's, and the whole of the gap between our 26 percent upside and their 40 percent is the US heart eighteen months later and margins two to three points lower, resting on the generalisation that every regulatory date in this company's record has slipped. In one quarter three dates did not: the CE mark in August, ahead of the fourth quarter DNB Carnegie had assumed and inside the seven-in-ten odds the case gave it by year end; the PMA received in September, with Breakthrough status and a continued-access extension; and the liver route moving from a paused 215-patient trial to a possible 510(k) filing in the first half of 2027 with an early-2028 launch, faster than the 18-to-24-month de novo shortcut the case had as its upside. The case accommodates a fully cleared branch at about 35 percent and 38x inside a 30x blend; it does not accommodate one quarter moving the weights. If the fully cleared branch becomes the modal one, the case's own arithmetic gives 35x on 13.7 SEK, 480 SEK at three years, 26 percent a year from 240 SEK and more from 226.6 SEK. For this to be right, the FDA has to decide within the roughly fifteen months to the company's end-2027 target without a major deficiency letter, and the 510(k) predicate has to be accepted. Against it, in the same evidence: the company's launch language got narrower, not wider; European heart sales of SEK 22m fell from SEK 24m under a rollout deliberately staged to eight centres first, so consensus' SEK 89m for FY2026 needs about SEK 34m in the fourth quarter on the case's figures; and the 510(k) exists because a rival HOPE device was cleared, which means the regulatory moat the case rated as an advantage held is lower in liver than it assumed, at the moment Bridge to Life has raised USD 110m and won NICE funding.

Two obvious readings fail on the evidence. "The beat was timing" fails as a full explanation because thoracic net of the SEK 19m was still above Redeye's estimate and disposables grew 183 percent, and the case already carries the timing point. "Services has failed" is true but already inside the case, which modelled stabilisation at best and wrote the alarm for it; what is new is the framing of services as launch infrastructure, which puts a negative margin on the whole eighteen-month horizon rather than on a turnaround that is late, and we take that up below.

What would settle it

  • The customer step-down. Its timing and size, which the company has not committed to disclosing. The fourth-quarter report is the first place a run-down of the SEK 10m of channel stock would show, and the first half of 2027 the first place the step itself could. The second review, four working days on, carries whatever DNB Carnegie and Redeye do to their lung lines and 2027 EPS for it, the results-day price, and any target change; it does not carry the customer's own plan or the hubs' procedure volumes, which management withholds until six months of data exist. A gauge that cannot read for two quarters is the open question nothing on the calendar answers.
  • The hub margin. The thoracic gross margin and the split of machine against disposable revenue in the fourth quarter, with the fourth and fifth hubs the case expects within six months of September; two quarters at 80 percent would make the reading probable, a return toward 85 percent would retire it.
  • The regulatory odds. The FDA's first substantive response on the PMA, and whether the company confirms the 510(k) submission timetable with the predicate accepted; neither is dated. European heart sales in the fourth quarter and the first half of 2027 are the adoption test the staged rollout sets.
  • Services. Fourth-quarter organic growth against the case's alarm, and any contract conversion the company names; a bridge from -19 percent to breakeven has not been promised.
  • The drop-through. The amortisation line from the fourth quarter, which should show the SEK 39m a year starting, and the brokers' depreciation and amortisation for FY2027 and FY2028 in the second review, which says whether the gap the case named is being closed by the market or left to us.
