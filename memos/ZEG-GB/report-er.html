Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

1.
Promising leadership: Zegona Communications initiated a search process through Russell Reynolds Associates to appoint a new independent non-executive director. The new director is expected to chair the Audit and Risk Committee. This leadership transition will ensure strong governance handover before Ashley Martin steps down from the Board. Underpinning this, the company maintains its combined Chairman and Chief Executive Officer structure to facilitate swift decision-making.
2.
Successful monetization: Zegona Communications completed two major fiber joint venture transactions that raised €1.8 billion in upfront cash proceeds. The company finalized the PremiumFiber transaction with MasOrange and the FiberPass transaction with Telefonica in early 2026. External investors AXA Real Estate Investment Managers and Singapore's GIC acquired minority stakes in these ventures. These completed transactions will allow Vodafone Spain to self-fund its growth strategy going forward.
3.
Simplified capital: Zegona Communications utilized its fiber transaction proceeds to execute a €1.4 billion special dividend in early 2026. This distribution fully satisfied and subsequently cancelled the 523 million ordinary shares held by Vodafone Group. Deutsche Bank analyst Keval Khiroya noted that this transaction successfully reduced Zegona's ordinary share count to 236 million. The simplified structure allows the equity market to value the platform more accurately.
4.
Substantial refinancing: Zegona Communications completed a landmark €3.7 billion refinancing package in July 2026. The transaction extended the company's senior secured debt maturity profile beyond five years. This refinancing reduces the company's annual interest run rate from €230 million to €170 million. Global credit markets strongly supported the deal because of the operational progress achieved since the May 2024 acquisition.
5.
Expanding profitability: Zegona Communications delivered impressive operational cash flow margins that reached 25% in the second quarter of fiscal year 2027. The business has grown cash flow margins from only 10% in fiscal year 2024 to 21% in fiscal year 2026. Operational cash flows increased to €434 million for the six months ended September 30, 2026. Margin expansion shows that aggressive selfhelp cost measures are successfully converting stabilized revenues into strong cash flows.
6.
Significant efficiency: Zegona Communications managed more than 400 initiatives to improve operational efficiency across Vodafone Spain. The company recorded an EBITDAaL of €675 million for the H1 FY27 interim period, reflecting a 37% margin. Deutsche Bank analyst Keval Khiroya reported that Q2 EBITDAaL growth of 0.6% was in line with expectations. Cost-saving programs are expected to continue supporting profitability over the next three years.
7.
Enhanced returns: Zegona Communications introduced a generous capital allocation framework that targets returning all free cash flow to shareholders. The Board authorized a €400 million return, consisting of a €200 million ordinary dividend and a €200 million buyback.
8.
Morgan Stanley analyst Harrison Williams estimated that these distributions represent a 10% yield on market capitalization. The progressive dividend and ongoing buyback program provide an attractive yield for longterm equity investors.
9.
Aggressive leverage: Zegona Communications revised its long-term net debt-to- EBITDAaL leverage target to 2.25x to 2.75x in September 2026. The company previously maintained a more conservative target of 1.5x to 2.0x. This relaxed target demonstrates management's confidence in the recurring nature of the company's cash flow generation. Morgan Stanley analyst Harrison Williams noted that the new framework enables ongoing capital returns while maintaining financial discipline.
10.
Accelerating top-line: Zegona Communications grew total revenue by 2.9% to €1,841 million in H1 FY27. This marks four consecutive quarters of top-line stabilization and growth. Service revenues grew 1.1% in H1 FY27, reversing a 2.3% decline in H2 FY26. Morgan Stanley analyst Harrison Williams noted that seasonally stronger wholesale revenues from increased tourism supported the acceleration.
11.
Sustained customer: Zegona Communications stabilized its consumer business by achieving eight consecutive quarters of customer growth under its ownership. Vodafone Spain added 45k contract mobile lines and 9k broadband lines in Q2 FY27. 155 157 156 159 161 162 165 Deutsche Bank analyst Keval Khiroya noted that positive subscriber trends provide strong evidence of retail base stabilization. Sustaining this momentum is critical to offsetting competitive pricing pressure.
12.
Ambitious medium-term: Zegona Communications established a medium-term target to grow annual operating free cash flow to more than €1 billion. The company's annual operating free cash flow was approximately €400 million at the time of the acquisition. Morgan Stanley analyst Harrison Williams modeled that cash generation will grow to €833 million in fiscal year 2027. Attaining the cash goal relies heavily on further cost efficiencies and mobile return improvements.
13.
Intense low-cost: Zegona Communications faces persistent retail pricing pressure in the Spanish telecommunications market. Telecom expert calls highlight that virtual operators are aggressively driving down mobile and broadband prices in Spain. This low-end competition threatens to erode retail average revenue per user across incumbent brands. Zegona is actively countering this risk by utilizing its Lowi brand to target value-conscious customers.
14.
Tactical cost: Zegona Communications is threatening to leave Vantage Towers when the contract expires in 2028 to achieve annual savings of €50 million. The company is demanding a 50% discount on its current annual tower lease payments of €150 million. MasOrange Investor Relations Director noted that while physical migration is highly complex, the threat serves as powerful negotiation leverage. Actually moving thousands of macro sites would carry immense upfront capital costs.
15.
Collaborative mobile: Zegona Communications plans to develop a mobile network vehicle called RANCo to share active infrastructure with other operators. Management believes that sharing mobile networks would be easier to execute with MasOrange than with Telefonica. Deutsche Bank analyst Keval Khiroya noted that any RANCo joint venture agreement would likely take 12 to 18 months to finalize. Finding an external investor in the mobile network vehicle could unlock additional capital.
16.
Favorable macroeconomic: Zegona Communications operates in a Spanish market that exhibits superior population and economic growth compared to other European nations. Morgan Stanley analyst Harrison Williams noted that a strong economic backdrop could provide positive tailwinds for subscriber growth. Continued expansion of the total market size is expected to reduce competitive intensity. This supportive macro context reduces the downside risk to service revenue stabilization.
17.
Other side of the story: Zegona’s leading conclusion that its buy-fix-sell strategy will deliver sustainable cash flow could be wrong if intense retail price wars resume. Aggressive price cutting by Digi Spain could force higher subscriber churn and limit ARPU growth. Failing to meet mid-term operational efficiency goals would quickly prevent Zegona from sustaining its progressive dividend policy.
