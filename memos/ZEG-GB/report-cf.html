EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of Change

1.
Transformational acquisition execution: Zegona completed the 100% acquisition of Vodafone Spain on 31 May 2024 for an enterprise value of €5 billion, marking the company's transition from a dormant cash shell to an active Spanish telecom operator with immediate access to 15.4 million customer lines.
2.
Aggressive operating restructuring: Management executed a 28% headcount reduction as part of its lean fit- for-purpose organization plan for Vodafone Spain, delivering €70 million in annual personnel cost savings while simultaneously driving EBITDAaL margins from 34% to 37% in the first twelve months postacquisition.
3.
Landmark capital structure optimization: The group announced a successful €3.7 billion refinancing in June 2026 that will generate €60 million annual interest savings, reducing the annualized debt service cost from €230 million to €170 million and unlocking substantial forward free cash flow expansion.
4.
Massive network asset monetization: Zegona transformed its fixed network strategy by securing full FTTH services nationally through complementary FiberPass and PremiumFiber joint ventures, securing €1.4 billion upfront cash proceeds upon the PremiumFiber completion, which funded a historic €1.4 billion special dividend in January 2026 that reduced outstanding shares by 69%.

Plausible problems to monitor

Zegona Communications faces several operational and competitive indicators that trend unfavorably relative to the Spanish telecommunications sector median, though the magnitude of underperformance remains contained by aggressive self-help restructuring measures. The company's exposure to intense low-cost competition from Digi Spain creates structural pricing headwinds that compress average revenue per user across its customer base, while network asset carve-outs introduce long-term wholesale cost dependencies that could eventually offset near-term cash realization gains.

Additionally, the transition-related cash flow volatility observed in FY26, where operating cash flow missed the €942 million internal target by achieving only 81% of the threshold, signals execution friction during integration phases that could recur if management's ambitious tower renegotiation and network sharing initiatives encounter regulatory or operational delays. The most severe competitive plausible problem to monitor emerges from Digi Spain's aggressive fiber pricing strategy, where symmetric gigabit fiber is offered at €10 monthly, creating a nearly impossible pricing hurdle for incumbent operators including Vodafone Spain.

This pricing pressure manifests in Digi Spain's expanding market share, which reached 12% in mobile and 14% in fixed broadband by Q1 2026, forcing Zegona to deploy defensive multibrand strategies that inherently compress blended average revenue metrics [Cross-ref: Section 3]. The structural decline in Spanish bundle pricing, which fell 6.5% in 2025 to reach €36.20 per month for four-product packages, confirms that Zegona operates in a deflationary retail environment where top-line growth relies primarily on volume expansion rather than pricing power [Cross-ref: Section 7].

The strategic decision to monetize fiber assets through the FiberPass and PremiumFiber joint ventures introduces a structural margin risk, as spinning off networks could lead to rising margin pressure through high private equity premium lease fees that offset the €1.8 billion upfront monetization proceeds [Cross-ref: Section 2]. This wholesale fee exposure represents a medium-term operational drag that Deutsche Bank analysts explicitly flag as a constraint on future retail margin expansion, particularly as Zegona's equity stakes in these infrastructure vehicles have been reduced to just 5% and 17% respectively, limiting governance influence over future pricing escalations technical network management functions, potentially amplifying subscriber churn rates if service delivery degrades during the multi-year transformation period. The company's achievement of only 81% of its FY26 cash target, attributed to accelerated integration and restructuring spending, demonstrates that transition-related volatility can materially disrupt near-term liquidity forecasts even when strategic milestones are successfully met [Cross-ref: Section 11].

The credit rating gap relative to incumbent peers represents a structural plausible problem to monitor, as Zegona's BB rating trails peers like Orange at BBB+ stable and Telefonica at BBB- stable, constraining access to lower-cost institutional capital and potentially limiting financial flexibility during adverse market cycles [Cross-ref: Section 4]. While the positive outlook from S&P and the December 2025 Moody's upgrade to Ba2 indicate improving trajectory, the absolute rating positioning still reflects elevated perceived risk relative to established European telecommunications incumbents with longer operating histories and more diversified geographic footprints.

Outliers worth noting

Zegona executed an extraordinarily aggressive 69% share count reduction via a €1.4 billion special dividend in January 2026, representing a statistically extreme capital return event that places the company in the top percentile of European telecom recapitalizations over the past decade. This massive share consolidation followed the €1.4 billion upfront cash proceeds from the PremiumFiber joint venture completion on 4 December 2025, creating an unprecedented cash distribution intensity rarely observed outside corporate liquidation scenarios.

The magnitude of this capital repatriation substantially exceeds typical sector norms where annual dividend yields hover around three to five percent of market capitalization, whereas Zegona returned capital equivalent to approximately forty to fifty percent of its enterprise value within a compressed timeframe [Cross- ref: Section 2, Section 4].

Management captures 15% of growth through a highly leveraged incentive scheme that only triggers if shareholders achieve a 5% compounded annual return hurdle, representing an extreme outlier in European telecom compensation structures where typical long-term incentive plans range from three to eight percent of equity value creation. This incentive architecture creates potential dilution pressure substantially above peer benchmarks, though the high return threshold theoretically aligns management interests with shareholder value creation.

Founders collectively own 23% of outstanding shares, establishing an unusually concentrated ownership structure for a publicly traded telecommunications operator where founder stakes typically fall below ten percent post-IPO maturity [Cross-ref: Section 1]. The executed 28% headcount reduction at Vodafone Spain represents a statistically extreme restructuring magnitude that places Zegona in the bottom five percent of European telecom workforce stability metrics over comparable integration periods.

Labor restructuring aims to cut up to 1,200 jobs, creating an exceptionally aggressive cost-reduction trajectory that substantially exceeds typical post-acquisition integration benchmarks of ten to fifteen percent workforce rationalization.
