Section 3 – Openreach wholesale contract terms and change-of-control risk 16 findings

Section summary
  1. Openreach wholesale agreements provide robust baseline pricing protections, but short rolling contract terms leave BT exposed to an accelerated line migration under new PlatformX ownership.

Contractual architecture and rolling term framework

1.
Baseline primary framework: Ofcom regulatory filings confirm Openreach wholesale services operate under the 2019 Primary Generic Ethernet Access contract supplemented by Equinox 1 and Equinox 2 commercial discount agreements
2.
One month contract duration: Ofcom's Telecoms Access Review maintains a strict one-month maximum limit on minimum contract periods for all Virtual Unbundled Local Access services including FTTP and FTTC
3.
Restricted termination penalties: Ofcom regulations prohibit held-to-term early termination penalties beyond the one-month minimum contract window for unbundled wholesale broadband connections
4.
Rolling supply flexibility: Openreach contract terms give wholesale access seekers full operational freedom to cease or transfer lines on 30 days notice without incurring structural breach penalties
5.
Asymmetric contractual duration: Openreach commercial disclosures confirm BT cannot terminate wholesale supply without 12 months notice while wholesale customers maintain standard one-month rolling exit rights

Volume commitments and pricing protections

6.
Baseline rebate mechanisms: Ofcom regulatory consultations establish that Equinox 2 pricing offers tiered rebates conditioned on ISPs meeting historical baseline volumes rather than absolute volume lock-ins
7.
Indexation price ceilings: Ofcom's Telecoms Access Review framework caps standard 80/20 FTTC and FTTP anchor product price increases to annual Consumer Price Index inflation through March 2031
8.
Voluntary volume participation: Rothschild & Co Redburn analysis indicates wholesale customers retain full legal rights to underperform Equinox volume thresholds, forfeiting rebates without triggering default
9.
Cash flow rebate structure: Ofcom filings reveal TalkTalk incurs significant cash flow lag under Equinox pricing due to paying list prices upfront before receiving quarterly rebate disbursements
10.
Equivalence of inputs constraint: BT Group regulatory filings confirm Equivalence of Inputs rules prevent Openreach from offering customized bilateral contract terms or change-of-control penalties to individual communication providers

Change-of-control triggers and migration risks

Non-discriminatory access boundaries: Regulatory filings demonstrate BT cannot contractually compel a restructured PlatformX to maintain line volumes on Openreach without violating non-discrimination rules

Other side of the story: Bullish investors argue that high physical re-contracting costs and network coverage gaps severely constrain how rapidly a new owner can realistically migrate PlatformX lines away from Openreach
