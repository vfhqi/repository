Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Based on comprehensive analysis across all sections of the Linea Directa Aseguradora report, several findings emerge that are materially worse than peer medians or sector base rates and should warrant investor concern. The company's Solvency II ratio of 196.3% trails both Mapfre's 208.8% and Mutua Madrileña's exceptionally high 297% solvency ratio, indicating weaker capital buffers relative to direct Spanish competitors despite management's characterization of the position as robust.

This capital adequacy gap becomes particularly relevant given the company's exposure to cyclical underwriting volatility and persistent claims inflation pressures. Motor insurance policies comprise 81.4% of written premiums, reflecting an extreme revenue concentration that substantially exceeds diversification levels achieved by broader composite insurance peers operating across multiple geographies and product lines.

This concentrated exposure amplifies the company's vulnerability to motor-specific regulatory changes, competitive dynamics, and claims cost inflation in a single product vertical. The concentration risk is compounded by the severe 104.1% combined ratio experienced in 2023, demonstrating how quickly profitability can deteriorate when adverse conditions emerge in the dominant motor segment. The company faces intensifying competitive pressure from financially robust mutual insurers willing to sacrifice near-term margins for market share preservation.

Mutua Madrileña deliberately raised its combined ratio target to 97% to freeze prices for policyholders, creating a strategic pricing headwind that threatens Linea Directa's ability

Spare parts prices are increasing around 10% on average, with paint repair rates rising 7%, creating persistent claims cost inflation that operates as a structural margin headwind. These inflationary pressures in the repair supply chain require continuous premium rate increases to maintain underwriting profitability, yet the company operates in an increasingly price-competitive environment where aggressive rate actions risk policyholder attrition.

The workshop labor and parts inflation dynamic proved capable of pushing the company into technical underwriting losses as recently as 2023, and similar pressures could reemerge if competitive dynamics prevent adequate pricing responses. - Earnings Calls The company's strategic execution carries elevated dependency risk on sustained digital adoption rates and operational technology performance. More than 86% of customers interact digitally, which management positions as a core structural cost advantage supporting the industry-low expense ratio.

However, this digitalcentric model creates vulnerability if customer preferences shift toward higher-touch service channels, technology systems experience service disruptions, or competitors successfully replicate the digital efficiency model while offering additional service features. The heavy reliance on artificial intelligence tools and automated claims processing concentrates operational risk in technology infrastructure that may prove less resilient during stress scenarios.

The Health segment maintains a combined ratio of 125.1%, indicating the business line remains in substantial technical deficit despite sequential improvements. While management expresses confidence in reaching breakeven, the health portfolio continues to generate underwriting losses that dilute overall group profitability. The path to health segment profitability faces headwinds from rising medical provider costs and competitive dynamics with established health insurance specialists, creating execution risk around the strategic diversification initiative that management has positioned as a key growth pillar.

Finally, one board seat remains unfilled following the tragic passing of John de Zulueta, creating a governance capacity gap at a critical juncture when the company navigates competitive pricing pressures, digital transformation initiatives, and strategic diversification efforts. The board vacancy reduces oversight bandwidth and committee expertise availability during a period requiring heightened strategic decision-making and risk governance, particularly as management balances growth ambitions against margin defense imperatives in an increasingly competitive Spanish insurance market.

Outliers worth noting

The company's Return on Average Equity (RoAE) of 23.3% in H1 2026 represents a statistical outlier, substantially outperforming major peer Mapfre which reported a group return on equity of 12.5% for the first half of 2026, positioning Linea Directa in the top tier of Spanish non-life insurers for capital efficiency despite its smaller scale and concentrated market position. This exceptional return profile stems from the combination of superior combined ratio execution and digital cost advantages that amplify underwriting leverage on a relatively modest equity base. [Cross-ref: Section 4] The company's Solvency II ratio of 196.3% sits below both Mapfre's 208.8% coverage and substantially trails peer Mutua Madrileña's ultra-high 297% solvency ratio reported at the end of 2025, representing a notable capital structure outlier that suggests either more aggressive capital distribution policies or structural differences in reserve conservatism and risk modeling assumptions compared to mutual competitors.

While the 196.3% level remains comfortably above regulatory minima, the 100 percentage point gap versus Mutua Madrileña raises questions about differential capital philosophies or hidden risk exposures in a market where catastrophic weather events can rapidly deplete buffers. [Cross-ref: Section 4] The home insurance combined ratio of 86.6% in H1 2026 represents an extreme positive outlier within the Spanish multi-risk property segment, achieving margins that appear structurally superior to typical residential property underwriting economics and suggesting either exceptional risk selection capabilities, favorable geographic concentration away from coastal flood zones, or reserve releases that may not prove sustainable across longer claim development cycles.

This exceptional profitability in a traditionally volatile line warrants scrutiny regarding whether benign weather conditions in the reporting period mask underlying exposure concentrations. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 6]
