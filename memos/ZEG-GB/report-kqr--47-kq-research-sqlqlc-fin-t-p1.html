Section 2 – Vodafone Spain's Cost Structure and Historical Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. Vodafone Spain carries a heavily locked operational cost base that restricts immediate structural leverage.
  2. Decelerating EBITDAaL growth in Q1 FY27 confirms that first-phase restructuring gains are fully in the run-rate.
  3. Under pre-2024 ownership, revenue declines historically exposed deep operating deleveraging risks.

Composition of Vodafone Spain's Cost Base

1.
Classification of primary cost lines: Vodafone Spain's operating cost base is characterized by a high proportion of contracted, inflation-indexed, and structural fixed expenses, with very few lines directly variable with customer volumes. Personnel costs, which landed at €194.32m in the 12 months ended 31 March 2026, are highly fixed and subject to local wage inflation. Tower and site leases represent a massive, contractually locked annual expense of approximately €150m under a 32-year Master Service Agreement (MSA) with Vantage Towers, which is indexed to local CPI and runs until its first exit window in November 2028. Wholesale access and FibreCo fees paid to PremiumFiber and FiberPass sit above EBITDAaL as rigid, contractually escalating operational lease payments. Network operations and energy costs are structural but partially mitigated through long-term, fixed- rate Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) that protect the business from volatile market rates. IT and Transitional Service Agreements (TSAs) with Vodafone Group are contractually fixed over 2 to 10-year horizons, representing a rigid service cost line. Commercial costs, commission payments to distributors, and subscriber acquisition costs (SAC) represent the primary cost categories that remain variable with customer additions. Bad debt expenses, recorded at €1.99m in FY26, are managed closely but fluctuate based on macroeconomic conditions and customer mix.
2.
Structural lease burdens: Prior to the recent FibreCo joint venture transactions, Vodafone Spain's existing annual lease costs exceeded €500m, presenting a significant fixed cost hurdle. Broker assessment of cost base: Morgan Stanley's report of 14 October 2026 forecasts that Vodafone Spain's EBITDAaL will remain broadly flat near-term at €663m because incremental lease costs from the FibreCo sales offset the positive impact of operational efficiencies.

EBITDAaL Growth Slowdown in Q1 FY27

3.
Growth deceleration metrics: Zegona's Q1 FY27 results published on 15 July 2026 showed that while total revenues grew 2.30% year-on-year to €916m, EBITDAaL growth decelerated sharply to 2.50% year-on-year, landing at €324m. This represents a severe drop from the 16.30% year-on-year EBITDAaL growth recorded in Q4 FY26. 44 Run-rate of first cost program: The slowdown in Q1 FY27 EBITDAaL growth indicates that the financial benefits of the first cost-reduction programme–including the 28% headcount reduction–have been fully absorbed into the run-rate.
4.
Wholesale accounting drags: Morgan Stanley's note of 14 October 2026 explains that the flattening of EBITDAaL growth is primarily due to the introduction of new monthly wholesale access fees associated with the Fiberpass and PremiumFiber joint ventures, which offset the underlying opex savings.
5.
Peer comparison of growth: This decelerated EBITDAaL growth of 2.50% in Q1 FY27 brings Vodafone Spain's near-term performance in line with European incumbents. For instance, Telefónica Spain reported a domestic EBITDAaL growth of 2.70% year-on-year in Q2 2026. Meanwhile, Portuguese incumbent NOS reported a Telco EBITDA growth of 0.80% in Q2 2026, reflecting intense local pricing pressures.
6.
Digi Spain's rapid trajectory: In stark contrast to the low-single-digit growth of the consolidated incumbents, low- cost challenger Digi Spain reported a massive Adjusted EBITDA growth of 48.60% year-on-year in Q2 2026, reaching €54m.

Quantifying Operating Leverage and Flow-Through

7.
Stress case flow-through: Zegona's FY26 annual report outlines a severe downside stress case where a €93m revenue decline is projected to reduce EBITDAaL by €81m, revealing a massive 87.10% (calculated) marginal flow-through rate. This metric illustrates the extreme operating leverage inherent in the business model, where almost the entirety of any revenue loss drops straight to the bottom line due to the highly fixed nature of the operating cost structure.
8.
Historical down-cycle precedent: During its pre-2024 down-cycles under Vodafone Group ownership, the business suffered severe margin compression as service revenues fell. For example, in FY23, service revenue decreased by 5.40% to €3,514.40m, driven by heavy pricing pressure and customer migration from premium plans to lower-value segments, which severely diluted margins because fixed infrastructure costs could not be matched.
9.
Portugal post-Digi entry: In Portugal, the launch of low-cost challenger Digi in late 2024 has triggered a similar competitive down-cycle, impacting Vodafone Portugal's financial performance. Morgan Stanley's report of 12 May 2026 highlights that Vodafone Portugal's EBITDAaL growth trend turned deeply negative, declining 14% year-on-year in H2 FY26 after growing 8% in H1 FY26, as competitive pricing pressured mobile ARPU and increased customer retention costs.
10.
Stabilization in Portugal: However, Vodafone Group's trading update of 27 July 2026 noted that while service revenue in Portugal continued to decline, pricing actions initiated in January 2026 have started to support ARPU trends and stabilize the sequential rate of decline.
11.
Contrary evidence: Despite the high risk of operating deleverage under revenue declines, Deutsche Bank's report of 13 August 2026 argues that concerns over Digi's competitive impact are overblown because only about 10% of Zegona's current revenues are exposed to direct Digi competition, and Zegona has successfully lowered its own retail pricing to defensive levels. Other side of the story: While management's aggressive cost-cutting measures and the structural shift to an asset-light wholesale model support near-term free cash flow, the highly contracted and inflation-indexed nature of tower leases and FibreCo rents limits the scope for further material cost reductions. Consequently, any prolonged competitive pricing war led by Digi in Spain could overwhelm these opex efficiencies, triggering a severe operating deleverage cycle that would directly threaten Zegona's target of reaching a €1bn operating free cash flow ambition.
