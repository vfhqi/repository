Section 4 – Germany's Private Medical Fee Reform and Medicover

Section summary
  1. Germany's private medical fee reform (GOÄneu) is targeting a CY28 implementation, shifting reimbursement value from technical diagnostic testing toward patient consultations.
  2. For Medicover, Germany Diagnostics represents 14% of Group revenues, with 42% private-pay exposure directly vulnerable to the proposed fee cuts.
  3. Sensitivity analysis indicates that a 15% to 45% diagnostic tariff cut threatens 1% to 2% of Medicover's Group sales and 4% to 13% of its adjusted EBITDA.

Current Status and Reform Timing

1.
Historical baseline and legislative pressure: The current German private medical fee schedule, the Gebührenordnung für Ärzte (GOÄ), has remained largely unchanged since 1996, keeping private medical tariffs highly rigid and outdated. To address these outdated codes, the German Medical Association (BÄK) and the Private Health Insurers' Association (PKV) jointly drafted a modernized proposal, known as GOÄneu, which was officially approved by the 129th German Medical Assembly in May 2025. While the reform aims to increase overall medical funding by up to 13.2% or 1.9 billion Euros within the first three years of implementation, it relies on substantial structural redistribution across specialties. Consequently, the reform acts as a regulatory mechanism that directly alters the profit margins of private service providers.
2.
Ministerial draft and implementation timeline: Legal enactment of the GOÄneu requires formal legislative action by the German federal government, including Cabinet approval and Bundesrat consent. Although Germany's Health Minister Nina Warken initially aimed for rapid progression of the ministerial draft in mid-2026, the complexity of statutory adjustments has pushed the realistic implementation window to CY28. This multiyear delay is driven by the necessity to coordinate amendments across both the Federal Medical Code (Bundesärzteordnung) and Social Code Book V (SGB V). Because these legislative steps require extensive coordination, diagnostic operators face a prolonged period of regulatory uncertainty. Political friction and potential slippage: Alternative expectations for the reform's timeline highlight the deep political friction surrounding German healthcare policy. In an expert call on 8 March 2026, a former senior executive at Ober Scharrer Gruppe argued that German healthcare politicians are heavily overwhelmed with statutory health insurance (SHI) financial crises and elderly care reforms. The expert noted that because the reform has been languishing in development for over ten years, politicians remain hesitant to prioritize it, creating a high risk of slippage beyond CY28. Under these conditions, the current high-margin private billing framework will remain preserved for several years.

Structural Shift to Talking Medicine

3.
Reallocation of diagnostic value: The core architectural change under the proposed GOÄneu is a decisive transition from multiplier-based billing to predominantly fixed tariffs. This structural shift explicitly devalues technical diagnostic procedures and machine-led testing, instead reallocating financial value toward patient consultation or "talking medicine". Under the legacy system, pathologists and laboratory physicians could charge elevated multipliers of up to 3.5 times the basic EBM rate based on case complexity. This means that the transition to flat-rate pricing eliminates the flexible upside that private diagnostic labs historically relied on to maintain high operational margins. Impact on routine and specialized testing: The technical draft of the GOÄneu compresses the pricing premium that private laboratories previously enjoyed over the statutory Einheitlicher Bewertungsmaßstab (EBM) system. In studies of the proposed tariff book, routine laboratory chemistry and basic high-volume testing are slated to experience the steepest price declines, with a representative basket of common diagnostic tests showing average price cuts of approximately 42%. Conversely, the reform modernizes the code catalog to introduce explicit, fixed pricing for advanced modalities such as genomic testing and specialized next-generation sequencing. Therefore, laboratory operators must aggressively pivot toward highly specialized diagnostic services to defend their profitability. Contrary evidence: While mainstream laboratories face severe pricing pressure, clinical networks that maintain a high volume of direct patient interactions are expected to experience a mild positive revenue impact. In an expert call on 25 February 2026, a clinical ophthalmic surgeon noted that the reform's emphasis on consulting and complex surgical procedures could increase reimbursement for outpatient clinics. The expert indicated that for operators with high private-pay clinical exposure, the moderate increase in consultation-led GOÄ fees would offset any minor technical fee cuts. This means that the negative impact of the reform is strictly localized to asset-heavy, routine diagnostic laboratories.

In FY25, Medicover's German diagnostic business generated 324.9 million Euros in revenue, accounting for roughly 14% of total Group sales and 43% of total Diagnostic Services sales. Private pay channels, which are billed directly under the GOÄ, represented 41.9% of these German revenues, translating to approximately 136 million Euros in direct regulatory exposure. However, the impact on earnings is far more severe, placing a meaningful 4% to 13% of Medicover's Group adjusted EBITDA at risk. In a severe downside scenario, these regulatory headwinds would completely prevent Medicover from achieving its medium-term margin expansion targets. Strategic mitigation and the Indian divestment: To insulate its corporate earnings, Medicover is actively executing a strategic turnaround focused on shifting its business mix toward high-margin fee-for-service (FFS) specialized tests. By targeting specialised lab testing and genetics–which are projected to grow at a 12% to 15% CAGR in Germany through 2028–the company aims to outpace the broader 4.2% laboratory market expansion.
