Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. ClearCorrect's trajectory directly influences Straumann's consolidated margin target of 140 to 170 basis points expansion.
  2. Currency headwinds from the Swiss franc cost base continue to impact reported earnings per share.

Financial Significance of Orthodontics

1.
Crucial investigation: "What is the full profit and loss picture of Straumann Holding AG's orthodontics business (ClearCorrect): revenue by region, gross margin, operating loss in 2024, 2025 and 2026 to date, the savings delivered so far from the transfer of EMEA and APAC aligner production to Smartee and the closure of the Markkleeberg plant, the reinvestment management plans in orthodontics go-to-market, the evidence on whether losses were halved by late 2026 as targeted, and the options (keep, partner, sell to Smartee or another buyer) that management, former executives and brokers have discussed, with precedents of medtech companies that exited aligner or other consumer dental businesses (Dentsply Sirona's Byte, Straumann's own DrSmile sale to Impress in 2024)?"
2.
Diluted profitability: Straumann Holding AG's consolidated financial results are heavily depressed by the ongoing high double-digit million losses within its orthodontics segment. While the core implant business maintains exceptionally strong margins, ClearCorrect's operational drag has historically limited core operating margin expansion below its potential. Consequently, resolving these orthodontic losses is critical for the group to achieve its core EBIT margin targets.
3.
Substantial impact: Morgan Stanley forecasts that Straumann's core EPS will grow from SFr 2.99 in 2025 to SFr 3.45 in 2026. This bottom-line trajectory depends on the group successfully halving ClearCorrect's losses by late 2026. Any delay in execution would directly restrict EPS growth and threaten the company's consolidated targets.
4.
Compressed valuations: Straumann's valuation multiple faces downward pressure as growth shifts toward competitive and volatile segments like clear aligners. Although trading at 26x next-twelve-months price-toearnings, the stock remains vulnerable to investor skepticism regarding diversification outside core implants. Successful execution of the clear aligner turnaround is required to support premium valuation multiples.

Currency Risks and Operational Exposure

5.
Severe currency headwinds: Straumann generates 98% of its revenues outside of Switzerland but maintains a Swiss-franc cost base. This severe mismatch exposed the company to significant currency exchange risks that lowered consolidated revenues by over CHF 100 mm. Operational profitability remains highly sensitive to fluctuations in Swiss franc spot rates.
6.
Strategic mitigation: Straumann is actively reducing its Swiss-franc currency exposure through international manufacturing transfers. By shifting production to Shanghai and the United States, management expects a 3 percentage point reduction in the Swiss-franc cost base. This structural hedge helps protect operating margins against persistent Swiss franc appreciation.
7.
Uncertain macro environment: Weakening consumer demand in priority regions like North America poses a major risk to dental treatments. High inflation and high interest rates have subdued orthodontic case starts and full-arch procedures. These headwinds slow down ClearCorrect's turnaround and complicate the path to breakeven.

Closely Related Strategic Questions

8.
Synergistic platforms: A closely related question is how effectively Straumann's AXS cloud platform can drive ClearCorrect adoption. Customer analysis shows that AXS platform users exhibit substantially higher reorder rates compared to non-users. Integrating scanning and treatment planning is crucial to expanding orthodontic market share.
9.
Contrary evidence: The orthodontics segment was notably absent from Straumann's presentations at the International Dental Show in early 2025, raising investor concerns. This omission suggested that the company might be downplaying its orthodontic challenges while prioritizing its core premium implant innovations. Investors continue to question the transparency of ClearCorrect's standalone financial performance.
10.
Other side of the story: Some analysts argue that Straumann's diversification into clear aligners is a necessary expansion of its total addressable market to CHF 20 bn. Even if ClearCorrect operates at a loss, it secures vital digital entry points into general dental practices. Therefore, the long-term strategic value of keeping the orthodontics business outweighs the short-term margin dilution.

Closure of Markkleeberg Facility

21.
German consolidation: Straumann Holding AG finalized the closure of its ClearCorrect manufacturing site in Markkleeberg, Germany, at the end of the first quarter of 2026. This closure was executed in parallel with the transition of regional production volumes to Smartee's automated platforms. The company engaged in consultations with the local works council to ensure a responsible transition for the workforce at the site. While clear aligner manufacturing was completely phased out, CAD/CAM activities at the Markkleeberg facility were unaffected.
22.
Unit economics: Straumann Holding AG's closure of the high-cost German plant is a milestone for orthodontics profitability. Operating own manufacturing lines in Germany created severe margin drag due to limited scale and high European labor costs. Shifting to an asset-light model allows the company to eliminate these fixed costs and transition to a variable cost structure. This structural change is expected to drive the orthodontics business to break even by the end of 2027.
23.
Asset-light transition: Bernstein Research appreciates the asset-light approach Straumann is using to gain scale and lower manufacturing costs. Under this partnership, Straumann successfully maintains the ClearCorrect brand and clinical interface while capturing the cost savings of automated scale. This structure enables a much faster path to profitability than organic scale could have delivered.

Precedent Direct-to-Consumer Dental Exits

1.
Significant portfolio restructuring: Straumann Holding AG completed the strategic sale of its DrSmile direct-to- consumer aligner business to Impress Group in September 2024. The company initiated formal proceedings in June 2024 to exit the direct-to-consumer channel due to shifting macroeconomic headwinds and tighter online marketing regulations. In return for transferring net assets of CHF 79.3 million, Straumann received a 20% non-controlling stake with a fair value of CHF 82.5 million in the combined Impress entity. This transaction allowed Straumann to maintain involvement as a primary aligner supplier while mitigating direct retail losses.
2.
Substantial discontinued losses: Straumann Holding AG recorded a net loss from discontinued operations of CHF 69.3 million for the fiscal year 2024, which was fully attributable to the DrSmile divestiture. This represented a significant improvement over the massive discontinued operations loss of CHF 202.1 million recorded in fiscal year 2023. The historical performance of DrSmile was heavily impacted by a CHF 153 million impairment charge in 2023 following reduced consumer demand for elective aesthetic procedures. Consequently, exiting this business allowed management to focus resources on B2B professional clear aligner channels.
3.
Abrupt business termination: Dentsply Sirona Inc announced the voluntary suspension of sales and marketing for its direct-to-consumer Byte aligner system and impression kits on October 24, 2024. Following discussions with the Food and Drug Administration regarding patient onboarding workflows, the company permanently ceased offering Byte products to new patients in January 2025. The sudden suspension of this direct-toconsumer clear aligner business significantly reduced group sales and disrupted historical segment performance. This precedent highlights the unpredictable regulatory and clinical risks associated with managing teledentistry platforms.
4.
Severe financial impairments: Dentsply Sirona Inc recorded a massive goodwill impairment of $495 million within its Orthodontic and Implant Solutions segment during the third quarter of 2024. Additionally, the company recognized $187 million in asset write-offs during the fourth quarter of 2024, including a full write-off of the Byte trademark and capitalized software. Dentsply Sirona also recorded an initial $35 million reduction to net sales for expected customer refunds stemming from the cessation of sales. These combined write-downs illustrate the massive capital destruction of Byte relative to its original $1.04 billion cash acquisition price in December 2020.
5.
Operational capability redirection: Dentsply Sirona Inc plans to leverage the demand generation and e-commerce technologies developed by Byte to support its professional-led SureSmile aligner platform. Management has already reallocated key Byte personnel to augment clinical digital workflows and enhance online consumer marketing. However, the company determined that the clinical, regulatory, and financial barriers to reentering the pure teledentistry market are excessively high. This strategic shift indicates that leading dental players are abandoning direct-to-consumer teledentistry models in favor of doctor-directed treatments.
