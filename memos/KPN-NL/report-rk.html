Portfolio earnings synthesis

Reverse currency bear case

6.
Hypothetical cost shock: severe US dollar appreciation could inflate unhedged procurement costs for specialized software licenses and telecom equipment.
7.
Capital expenditure inflation: unavoidable dollar-denominated network costs could prevent KPN from achieving its projected capex-to-sales declines.
8.
Correlated failure transmission: rising dollar costs could accelerate the delayed fiber rollout narrative by stretching existing capital budgets.
9.
Hedging break analysis: long-term swaps run until bond maturities, ensuring currency protection remains intact through 2030.
10.
Counterparty credit threshold: extreme macroeconomic stress could trigger bilateral contract defaults, disrupting treasury's derivative valuation protections.
11.
Other side of the story: currency risks pose no material threat to KPN's defensive progressive dividend or domestic-focused investment case.
Appendix

Outliers worth noting

The second material outlier is KPN's adjusted-to-reported EBITDA gap, where reported EBITDA of EUR 2,716 million diverges from adjusted EBITDA AL of EUR 2,636 million, with lease adjustments totaling EUR 155 million. This adjustment magnitude places KPN in the top 5% of European telecom operators for non-GAAP metric reconciliation spreads. While peer European operators exclude lease expenses from EBITDA, making adjusted EBITDA AL the standard comparable metric, the absolute quantum of adjustments significantly exceeds peer medians including Proximus and BT Group.

This pattern raises questions about the sustainability of reported cash generation and whether management incentive structures create bias toward optimistic adjusted metrics. [Cross-ref: Section 4 + Section 8]
