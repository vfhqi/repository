EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 findings

Bear-case credibility baseline

The bearish thesis against Getinge possesses substantial factual foundations rooted in a decade-long history of regulatory enforcement actions and persistent quality management failures.

The 2015 FDA consent decree was expanded in 2022 to include subsidiary Datascope due to persistent QMS compliance failures, while Getinge has initiated 24 Class I FDA recalls since 2021, representing the most severe category of medical device risk.

Annual compliance remediation costs exceeded SEK 800 million during peak years, creating a material structural drag on operating margins that persists despite management's repeated assertions of improvement.

The FDA advised healthcare providers in May 2024 to transition away from Cardiosave, Cardiohelp, and HLS sets, which created near-term earnings volatility and triggered immediate pauses in US promotional activities.

These regulatory headwinds are compounded by intensifying competitive pressure as Medtronic's VitalFlow system launched in 2024 threatens Getinge's dominant 60% US market share in ECMO, exploiting the promotional vacuum created by quality issues.

The bear case demonstrates high credibility in its assessment of regulatory risks and competitive erosion, though it likely overstates the speed of market share loss given exceptional clinical switching costs.

Winning ACH profile and diagnostic evidence

The FALSE FRIEND profile has only two hard inconsistencies, making it the most plausible investment narrative.

This profile posits that stable recurring consumable revenues actively mask a terminal decline in the underlying hardware installation base, creating a deceptive appearance of business health that will unravel over multi-year horizons.

High recurring ECLS consumable revenues of 72% appear supportive but actually mask a terminal hardware install decline.

The diagnostic evidence supporting this profile includes suspending US marketing for Cardiosave prevents hardware growth while Teleflex expands its clinical install base, while Getinge postponed its new Cardiosave platform development to prioritize fixing existing compliance issues.

The critical distinction between the FALSE FRIEND and outright bear profiles lies in the short-term stickiness of the installed base: exceptionally high clinical switching costs protect short-term consumable revenues despite restricted hardware sales, meaning revenues will remain deceptively stable until the legacy hardware base reaches end-of-life and cannot be replaced due to promotional restrictions.

The winning profile carries only two hard inconsistencies because reinstated CE marks for HLS sets in late 2024 contradict a permanent terminal operational collapse, yet these certifications do not address the deeper strategic problem of blocked US market access and stalled nextgeneration platform development.

Highest-plausibility failure modes

The most credible failure pathways cluster around diverted engineering resources and silent competitive displacement.

Diverted engineering resources to quality remediation permanently stalls the next-generation R&D pipeline for multiple years, as long-term consent decrees create operational reactivity leading to a "good enough" engineering culture, and taking key engineers away for months completely destabilizes core product development projects.

1.
This mechanism is already manifesting: reassessing engineers to historical compliance remediation stalls next-generation product development and innovation cycles, creating a vicious cycle where quality issues consume the very resources needed to develop competitive next-generation products

The second high-plausibility pathway involves unreliable product supply and chronic quality notifications drive clinicians to quietly transition to newer competitor systems, as Medtronic's VitalFlow and Spectrum Medical capture ECLS share while Getinge struggles with product maintenance, and suspending promotional activities halts new customer acquisition while competitors expand footprints.

This silent blacklisting represents an insidious threat because it occurs gradually without dramatic announcements, making it difficult to detect until market share erosion becomes statistically undeniable.

The third critical pathway involves Surgical Workflows faces accelerating exclusion from Chinese public tenders due to strict local manufacturing requirements, as European medical companies without local operations face bans on tenders exceeding USD 6 million.

The joint probability of these failures compounding is assessed as medium-high because unmitigated medtech tariffs combined with persistent SEK strength threaten to structurally cap margins below 14%, while regulatory bottlenecks prevent the commercial response that would normally offset such headwinds.

Key FALSE FRIEND indicators and monitoring framework

The FALSE FRIEND profile generates highly specific monitorables that can validate or invalidate the thesis over coming quarters.

The primary deceptive signal is solid recurring consumable growth of 72% masks a terminal decline in hardware installations, as high near-term consumable pull-through hides market share loss until the old hardware installed base expires, evidenced by divergence between double-digit ECLS consumable growth and declining new hardware order intake.

This divergence represents the single most diagnostic indicator and should be tracked monthly through order book disclosures and capital equipment shipment data.

The second false-friend dynamic involves a technical sales rebound in Surgical Workflows occurs as hospitals address pandemicdeferred capital projects, where temporary volume surges are misconstrued as structural growth acceleration while masking underlying premium price pressure, with sequential order growth flattening rapidly after the release of pent-up replacement demand.

Investors must distinguish between cyclical restocking and genuine market share expansion by examining win rates in competitive tenders and average selling price trends.

The third critical monitorable is the delay in Cardiosave's US 510(k) submission serves as an early indicator of continued regulatory friction, as any postponement beyond the targeted mid-2026 timeline would signal that compliance remediation remains incomplete despite management assurances.

The aggregate monitoring framework should track the ratio of new hardware installations to consumable revenue growth, with any sustained divergence serving as confirmation that the installed base is entering terminal decline.

Additional leading indicators include monthly Chinese public tender win rates for imported hospital equipment fall below historical averages and contraction in Getinge's dominant 60% US market share for portable life-support platforms, both of which would validate the competitive erosion thesis.

Net currency verdict and FX synthesis

The foreign exchange outlook represents a transitioning tailwind that will partially offset operational headwinds but cannot resolve structural competitive challenges.

Foreign exchange headwinds are easing significantly after representing a massive SEK 1 billion drag on group earnings during fiscal year 2025, and if exchange rates stabilize at current spot levels, currency effects are projected to turn into a positive earnings tailwind starting in late 2026.

The baseline quantified sensitivity reveals material earnings leverage to currency movements: export sales generated a net USD transaction surplus of SEK 2,857 million in fiscal year 2025, while consolidated manufacturing operations drove a net EUR transaction deficit of SEK 3,759 million in 2025.

This structural imbalance means USD transaction exposure yielded a net surplus of +2,857 SEK M in 2025 due to export-led sales, positioning Getinge to benefit from USD strength or SEK weakness.

However, the reverse-engineered bear case demonstrates that the worst-case currency scenario combines a depreciating US dollar with unmitigated US import tariffs, where unmitigated exchange headwinds and quality remediation costs could structurally force operating margins down to a flat 12% baseline.

The critical vulnerability lies in centralised European production limits natural hedging, leaving margins exposed to sharp SEK and USD volatility, though expanding US manufacturing footprints could eventually cover 75% of US sales, reinforcing long-term natural hedges.

The net currency verdict is neutral-to-modest-tailwind over the next twelve to twenty-four months, as translation headwinds ease but transaction exposure remains sensitive to policy-driven shocks.

Currency dynamics are unlikely to drive the investment outcome but could amplify margin compression if tariff escalation coincides with adverse exchange movements.

The final synthesis across all dimensions suggests that currency represents a tertiary risk factor subordinate to regulatory execution and competitive positioning, meaning investors should monitor FX primarily as a potential accelerant of pre-existing operational failure modes rather than an independent thesis driver.
