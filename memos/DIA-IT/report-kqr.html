Section 3 – Convertible Bond Terms, Refinancing Route, and Floating-Rate Debt

Section summary
  1. DiaSorin faces escalating interest expenses as its zero-coupon convertible bond approaches maturity in 2028 and its floating-rate debt incurs higher run-rate costs.
  2. QIAGEN and Siemens Healthineers peer precedents indicate that transitioning from low-cost historical debt to standalone market-rate refinancing imposes a significant headwind on subsequent earnings per share.

Convertible Bond Parameters

1.
Favourable terms: DiaSorin S.p.A. issued a €500 mm zero-coupon senior unsecured equity-linked convertible bond on 28 April 2021 to partially finance its Luminex acquisition. These bonds carry no coupon interest and are scheduled to mature on 5 May 2028. The initial conversion price was set at €210.9339 per ordinary share, representing a premium of 47.50% over the equity's volume-weighted average price. This high conversion hurdle suggests that the debt is highly unlikely to convert into equity before maturity.
2.
Unviable options: DiaSorin S.p.A. maintains an optional redemption clause allowing it to redeem the bonds early under specific market conditions. Starting on 26 May 2026, the issuer can call the bonds at principal value if the parity value exceeds €130,000 for a designated period. However, because the underlying share price remains far below the conversion price, this call option is currently out of the money and unviable.
3.
Rising liability: DiaSorin S.p.A. treats the convertible bond as a compound financial instrument split into debt and equity components. The carrying value of the convertible liabilities rose from €476.86 mm at the end of 2025 to €481.77 mm as of 30 June 2026. Because no interest is paid, this instrument shelters DiaSorin from short-term interest volatility but creates a massive refinancing cliff in 2028.
4.
Contrary evidence: DiaSorin S.p.A. did not experience any immediate liquidity stress from its convertible bond during the recent interest rate hiking cycle. The zero-coupon structure insulated the company's cash flow because interests do not accrue on these specific convertible notes. This suggests that the zero-coupon convertible acted as an effective capital shield during macro tightening.

The introduction of this floating-rate instrument directly exposes DiaSorin to high interest rates across the European banking sector.

This sharp rise in financing costs erodes the company's net profit margins and dilutes potential earnings per share growth.
