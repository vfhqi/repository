Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The planned acquisition of Octus expands LSEG's capabilities into high-growth private credit data markets and is key to long-term earnings growth.
  2. Based on research of 30 relevant sources across four companies and three precedent cases, every identified source was used in this evaluation.

The Key Question

1.
Significant inquiry: "London Stock Exchange Group plc (LSEG-GB), a global financial markets infrastructure and data group reporting four segments: Data & Analytics (Workflows desktops including Workspace, Data & Feeds, Analytics), Markets (capital markets including a majority stake in the listed Tradeweb, and post-trade clearing through LCH including SwapClear, with net treasury income on clearing collateral), FTSE Russell (indices and benchmarks) and Risk Intelligence (screening and KYC data); it agreed in October 2026 to acquire the private credit data provider Octus. What regulatory approvals does London Stock Exchange Group's (LSEG-GB) acquisition of Octus require, and what completion date do the company and brokers expect? What do the company and brokers say about Octus's standalone revenue growth, adjusted EBITDA margin, integration costs (including the roughly US$100m of integration and product spend cited for 2027 to 2028), cost of financing and EPS effect in 2027, 2028 and 2029, and which brokers' published forecasts already include Octus?"

Strategic Importance and Investor Rationale

2.
Strategic expansion: LSEG-GB agreed to buy private credit data provider Octus for $3.535 bn to expand its presence in high-growth, illiquid, and private credit data segments. The transaction represents LSEG's largest acquisition in seven years and accelerates its Data and Analytics division's capabilities. This acquisition is critical because it connects public and private credit datasets, allowing customers to source deals and assess credit risk on a single integrated platform.
3.
Targeted penetration: LSEG historically has been under-penetrated in buy-side segments such as hedge funds and alternative asset managers. By acquiring Octus, LSEG gains access to 750 new institutional customers and over 40000 individual users who rely on private credit intelligence. This client base provides substantial crossselling opportunities to distribute LSEG's existing liquid credit products to buy-side professionals.
4.
Strategic comparison and integration synergies of LSEG's acquisition of Octus Financial accretion: LSEG expects the transaction to be immediately accretive to Data and Analytics organic revenue growth upon completion. However, management confirmed that the deal will be neutral to adjusted EPS in 2028, which represents the first full year post-acquisition. This EPS neutrality is driven by c. $180 mm of net financial expenses in 2028 from a 5.5% cost of debt, which offsets Octus's standalone profit contribution during the initial integration phase.
5.
Planned integration: The acquisition is not driven by cost-cutting, as LSEG plans to spend approximately $100 mm on integration and product development across 2027 and 2028. This spending will fund engineering work to move Octus's proprietary datasets into LSEG's core infrastructure. Over the longer term, LSEG intends to merge Octus with existing assets like LPC and Yield Book to launch highly differentiated private credit products starting around 2029.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some buy-side clients report that competing private credit platforms like 9fin are rapidly closing the data coverage gap with Octus. Furthermore, 9fin is perceived by some investment professionals as a more nimble and innovative platform that leverages AI faster. This rising competition could challenge LSEG's ability to maintain Octus's historical double-digit growth rate or achieve projected pricing power.

Section 2 – Regulatory Approvals and Expected Timeline

Section summary
  1. Regulatory clearances in multiple jurisdictions represent the primary hurdle for completing the Octus acquisition within the targeted timeframe.
  2. Management and sell-side brokers express high confidence that the transaction will close in the first half of 2027.
  3. This high confidence is based on the bolt-on nature of the transaction and parallel timelines for comparable precedent acquisitions.

Regulatory Requirements and Jurisdictions

1.
Complex clearance: London Stock Exchange Group's acquisition of Octus is subject to review by competition authorities in key operating jurisdictions. The transaction requires antitrust clearance in the United States and the United Kingdom before the cash consideration can be distributed. These reviews ensure that combining Octus's private credit data with LSEG's public benchmarks does not restrict competition in financial data markets.
2.
Planned funding: LSEG will finance the USD 3.535 billion purchase price entirely through new debt once authorizations are granted. Financial regulators will evaluate the impact of this debt on the group's leverage targets. The transaction cannot proceed to final completion until all applicable regulatory clearances are secured by both parties.
3.
Rigorous scrutiny: Antitrust authorities focus on the potential for market foreclosure when a dominant exchange operator acquires a niche provider. Because LSEG is traditionally more exposed to public markets, the addition of private credit intelligence raises fewer immediate vertical overlap issues. However, regulators will scrutinize whether the deal increases barriers to entry for smaller alternative data platforms.
4.
Coordinated filing: Legal advisors from Latham & Watkins are coordinating the merger control filings across the relevant antitrust jurisdictions. The transaction requires the expiration of standard waiting periods under antitrust laws before ownership can transfer. This coordination is designed to ensure that all filings are processed simultaneously without causing unexpected closing delays.
5.
Strategic divestment: The private equity firm Permira agreed to sell Octus after owning the asset since August 2022. Regulatory clearance represents the final step for Permira to achieve its strategic exit during a period of challenging exit markets. This divestment highlights how the deal timeline is critical for both the buyer's expansion and the seller's capital recycling goals globally.

Expected Timeline and Closing

Section summary
  1. Delayed or rejected regulatory approvals would eliminate the projected interest expense but severely derail LSEG's expansion into private markets.
  2. Competitive pressures from Bloomberg and 9fin would intensify if LSEG fails to resolve integration challenges and customer contract dissatisfaction.

Regulatory and Closing Disruptions

1.
Delayed completion: Delayed completion of the Octus transaction past the first half of 2027 would defer the associated financing costs for London Stock Exchange Group. Morgan Stanley analysts assume that LSEG will issue approximately GBP 2.60 billion of debt at a 5.50% interest rate to finance the cash consideration. If the transaction is deferred, LSEG would avoid the peak leverage of 2027 and delay the projected GBP 144 million annual interest expense. This delay would temporarily preserve capital for share buybacks but defer longterm strategic growth goals.
2.
Complex clearance: Antitrust regulators like the Competition and Markets Authority have the power to block data aggregation or demand painful structural remedies. A former Head of Group Strategy at LSEG recalled that the massive Refinitiv competition review required the divestment of Borsa Italiana to secure clearance. If authorities decide that combining public and private debt datasets creates an unfair data moat, they could demand restrictive licensing remedies. Such regulatory interventions would delay completion, increase transaction costs, and reduce the ultimate EPS accretion of the deal.
3.
Hawkish reviews: Hawkish regulatory scrutiny in the United Kingdom or United States remains a material completion risk that most brokers downplay. Jefferies Research warns that regulatory changes or merger integration failures represent key risks to LSEG's price target. If regulators impose restrictive conditions, it would diminish the strategic value and delay synergies. This regulatory friction could force LSEG to abandon the transaction entirely.

Customer Friction and Contract Churn

4.
Aggressive lock-ins: Octus's high customer retention is partially driven by aggressive, long-term contracts that are difficult to terminate. A former Business Operations Lead at 9fin explained that Octus locks in clients with restrictive termination clauses and auto-renewal policies. If these customers become dissatisfied with LSEG's integration, they may run down their contracts to switch to cheaper providers. This rising dissatisfaction would threaten Octus's projected revenue growth of 13% for 2027.
5.
Competitive alternatives: Alternative private credit platforms are actively exploiting customer frustration with Octus's premium pricing model. A Director at Barings noted that competing platform 9fin is roughly half the price of Octus and is rapidly closing the data coverage gap. If LSEG raises pricing after the acquisition to offset high debt costs, customers could aggressively transition to 9fin. Such churn would prevent LSEG from achieving its targeted EBITDA margin expansion of 300 basis points.
6.
Opaque pipelines: Private credit data aggregation remains highly vulnerable to shifting manager attitudes regarding deal confidentiality. Expert calls from the buy side highlight that some asset managers are increasingly reluctant to share private deal documentation with third-party aggregators to protect their proprietary pipelines. If managers restrict data sharing, Octus's data collection capabilities would shrink. This data opacity would erode the long-term defensibility of LSEG's acquired credit moat.

Competitive Backlash and Peer Dynamics

7.
Workflow consolidation: Bloomberg remains the dominant player in fixed-income workflows and is highly capable of defending its position. A former Director at LSEG noted that Bloomberg has a very strong position in fixedincome trading and analytics. If LSEG fails to innovate and quickly embed Octus data into Workspace, Bloomberg's messaging and data network will continue to lock in users. This failure would result in community dilution and loss of market share for LSEG's desktop segment.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some industry experts believe that LSEG's underlying data and analytics business remains highly resilient despite integration friction. A former Solutions Business Director at LSEG stated that the core market data business remains strong even when facing significant management and product integration challenges. This structural resilience suggests that even if the Octus integration experiences delays, the wider group's double-digit EPS compound growth rate would remain largely intact.
9.
Aggressive peers: S&P Global and other major information providers are investing heavily in private company data to counter LSEG's acquisitions. A former Managing Director at Moody's noted that major players are aggressive in acquiring private market datasets through partnerships to address data opacity. For example, S&P Global's market intelligence segment continues to build out its one-stop-shop ecosystem to compete directly with LSEG and Bloomberg. This aggressive peer expansion would intensify market share battles in private debt.
