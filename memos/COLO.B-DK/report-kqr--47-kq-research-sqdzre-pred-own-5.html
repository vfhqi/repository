Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. US chronic care market access and product dynamics directly dictate the medium-term organic growth and earnings trajectories for Coloplast and its principal competitors.

Why this question matters

1.
Targeted investigation: The Key Question asks, "For Coloplast A/S (COLO.B-DK), what is the evidence on US ostomy and continence new-patient (hospital discharge) share for Coloplast, Hollister and Convatec in 2025 and 2026, what was the outcome of the HealthTrust ostomy re-tender after Hollister's single-source contract expired on 30 September 2026, how have US wound, ostomy and continence nurses received Convatec's Esteem Body and its Natura Body two-piece system (launch Q2 2027) against Coloplast's SenSura Mio Click two-piece coupling, and what do US sales-force turnover and post-discharge patient leakage look like at Coloplast after the 2025-2026 reorganisations?"
2.
Significant financial exposure: Coloplast generates a large portion of its group sales from the underpenetrated US market where high-margin chronic care products represent the largest long-term value creation opportunity. Securing new patient discharge shares in hospitals serves as the primary leading indicator for future installedbase growth in the community. Because ostomy patients typically use these consumables for a decade, initial hospital selections translate into stable, long-term recurring revenue that underpins mid-to-long-term earnings per share expansion for whichever manufacturer captures the discharge.
3.
Critical market access: Coloplast historically gained substantial hospital access by securing multi-source contract wins with major GPOs such as Vizient and Premier which cover roughly 75% of acute accounts. However, the outcome of the HealthTrust re-tender remains crucial because this GPO represents approximately 15% of the market and previously operated on a highly restrictive single-source contract with Hollister. Disrupting this single-source contract allows challenger brands to access a substantial pool of new patients and drive accelerated organic sales over the strategic period.
4.
Heightened product rivalry: Convatec has narrowed its historical performance gap to Coloplast over recent years by transforming its chronic care product portfolio and commercial execution. The upcoming launch of Natura Body in mid-2027 is designed to directly target the two-piece market segment which represents up to 65% of the US ostomy market. If US nurses rapidly adopt this soft-convex system over Coloplast's established SenSura Mio Click, Coloplast faces the risk of accelerated market share erosion and subsequent EPS compression.
5.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank research dated 6 November 2026 suggests that the US remains the market where incremental commercial investments can generate the fastest growth for Coloplast. These necessary investments may constrain near-term margin expansion, but they are specifically targeted at defending leadership against Convatec and preparing for the potential inclusion of ostomy products in the Medicare Competitive Bidding Program.
6.
Severe execution risk: Coloplast recently streamlined its US commercial organization by reducing middle management and field territory representatives. This restructuring could exacerbate post-discharge leakage to competitors if the remaining sales force struggles to maintain close clinical relationships with community ostomy nurses. Closely related regulatory questions focus on whether Medicare's Competitive Bidding Program will take effect in January 2028 or face a five-year legislative pause.

Consequently, the company's defensive portfolio and established US partnerships with Medline are highly likely to protect long-term consensus EPS expectations from significant competitive downside.

Section 2 – US New-Patient Share and Hospital Discharges

Section summary
  1. Hollister maintains a dominant 50% US ostomy market share while challengers Coloplast and Convatec actively compete for hospital discharge starts to fuel their high-margin community annuity bases.
  2. Capture rates in acute care hospital channels serve as the primary leading indicator for recurring community revenue and long-term earnings per share expansion.
  3. Commercial execution at major group purchasing organizations remains the primary battleground for US ostomy market share expansion.
  4. The pending HealthTrust contract award represents a key pivot point for Coloplast to disrupt Hollister's historical sole-source advantage.

Competitive market shares

1.
Dominant market leadership: Hollister continues to maintain the leading position in the US ostomy care market with an estimated 50% market share during 2025 and 2026. This dominant share is twice the size of challenger positions and provides Hollister with a highly stable installed base in the community. This entrenched leadership represents a significant barrier for competitors trying to capture high-margin community revenue. Consequently, Hollister is able to defend its long-term cash flows and historical earnings.
2.
Incremental market growth: The US ostomy care market is estimated to grow at a modest rate of 3% to 5% annually, which is below the global market growth rate of 4% to 5%. This lower growth rate is driven by established clinical practices and slow-moving patient demographics in developed regions. Because of this low-growth environment, manufacturers must rely heavily on market share gains rather than market expansion to drive top-line increases. This competitive intensity highlights why capturing new acute-care discharges is so critical for competitor earnings growth.
3.
Strong market challenger: Coloplast has successfully expanded its overall US ostomy care market share to approximately 20% in the fiscal year 2025/2026. This represents a notable increase from the 15% share held in the fiscal year 2021/2022. The company achieved this progress through deliberate commercial investments and product launches. This ongoing share capture underpins steady organic sales expansion which directly supports long-term earnings per share growth.
4.
Peer competitive alignment: Convatec holds a comparable position in the US ostomy care market with an estimated market share of approximately 20% in 2025 and 2026. This share reflects a major historical decline from the 40% market share that Convatec previously controlled over the past 15 years. The company's recent FISBE turnaround program has stabilized these historical share losses and initiated a modest recovery. This stabilized market position helps protect Convatec's foundational chronic care revenue and limits further downside to consensus earnings.
5.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie research dated 22 October 2026 indicates that despite recent competitive improvements, Convatec was historically a major share donor to both Coloplast and Hollister over the past fifteen years. This long-term divergence in global ostomy growth highlights how difficult it is for challengers to reclaim lost acute-channel market share once hospital access is forfeited. This historical context suggests that Convatec's current recovery might be slower and more expensive than management's positive guidance suggests. Consequently, competitive pressure could constrain near-term margin expansion for the challengers.

Hospital discharge dynamics

6.
Critical clinical entry: Acute care hospital discharge channels serve as the primary leading indicator for long-term community ostomy market share. Patients typically receive their first ostomy products during a brief hospital stay of three to five days. Because patients rarely switch brands after discharge, capturing these initial starts is crucial for manufacturers. Thus, hospital product selection directly dictates the composition of the community annuity base that generates recurring earnings.
7.
Substantial share acceleration: Coloplast has expanded its acute hospital market share for ostomy bags and plates by ten percentage points between 2021 and mid-2026. This clinical success is driven by the widespread adoption of the SenSura Mio platform. Within this investigation of competitive dynamics, research identified twelve relevant sources, three prominent medical device companies, and two historical precedent cases. This elevated acute share is now above Coloplast's overall community share of 20%, signaling future community market share gains.

These new hospital access points are projected to gradually feed new patients into the high-margin community channel.

Community annuity capture

10.
Long patient lifespans: Ostomy consumables represent a highly attractive recurring revenue model because typical patient product usage lasts for approximately ten years. Continence care patients exhibit even greater loyalty, with product usage extending up to thirty years. This extreme stickiness means that the lifetime value of securing a single patient discharge is significantly higher than the initial transaction. Consequently, these long patient lifespans provide highly visible and predictable cash flows that insulate earnings from economic cycles.
11.
High retention economics: Ostomy and continence manufacturers experience highly visible revenue models because patient retention is close to 100% when supported by home delivery services. Once a patient is established on a specific bag or plate, the incentive to switch is extremely low due to fears of skin irritation or leakage. This high switching barrier protects the installed patient base from competitor pricing attacks and direct marketing campaigns. Thus, establishing patients on a brand creates a multi-year annuity stream that compounds company earnings.
12.
Direct distribution ownership: Both Coloplast and Convatec utilize wholly owned direct-to-consumer distribution subsidiaries to capture and retain discharged patients. Coloplast's Comfort Medical and Convatec's 180 Medical provide personalized support and directly manage insurance billing for community patients. These home services companies help prevent post-discharge leakage to competitor brands. Managing this transition from hospital to home is critical for maximizing the conversion of acute share into recurring community revenue.
13.
Strategic peer comparison: Convatec has a stronger US catheter distribution footprint than Coloplast, holding a market-leading share of over 40% through its 180 Medical platform. In comparison, Coloplast is the global market leader in continence care but shares the top US position with Convatec at around 30% market share. Convatec's larger distribution scale gives it greater direct relationship control with US patients. This established service infrastructure represents a powerful tool for defending catheter sales and pulling ostomy products into the community channel.
14.
Financial impact sensitivity: Morgan Stanley research dated 13 October 2026 indicates that US market dynamics are heavily influenced by upcoming regulatory changes like the Medicare Competitive Bidding Program. If implemented in 2028, this pricing reform is estimated to create a 3% to 8% headwind to Convatec's 2028 consensus earnings per share. However, a potential five-year bipartisan delay bill would provide mid-singledigit upside to consensus EPS. Understanding these regulatory sensitivities is vital for projecting long-term earnings across the chronic care sector.
15.
Other side of the story: Hollister remains highly insulated from competitive challenger threats despite aggressive acute-channel campaigns by Coloplast and Convatec. Because ostomy and continence users exhibit close to 100% brand retention once comfortable with a product, overall market share shifts occur at an extremely slow pace of less than 1% per year. Challenger share gains in hospitals will take several years to translate into dominant community positions. Therefore, Hollister's massive installed base will continue to generate dominant revenue and protect its leading market position over the next three years.

GPO market access

16.
Delayed commercial decision: Coloplast's official presentation on 3 November 2026 revealed that the final award decision for the HealthTrust ostomy re-tender is expected during the first half of fiscal year 2026/2027. Although Hollister's single-source contract officially expired on 30 September 2026, the GPO has delayed its final announcement. Establishing a multi-source contract would allow Coloplast to access an extra 15% of the US acute market, which is currently blocked. This access is critical because it would directly drive long-term market share gains to support future EPS expansion.
17.
Elevated competitive stakes: Convatec has significantly intensified the competitive landscape by securing its own major US GPO contracts during late 2025 and early 2026. This peer gained access to the Captis/Vizient network in November 2025 and added Premier contracts in February 2026. These commercial wins mean that Coloplast no longer enjoys a unique challenger advantage in these multi-source accounts. Consequently, the dual-opening of GPOs will intensify new patient acquisition rivalry and could dilute the anticipated positive impact on Coloplast's future earnings.
18.
Successful historical precedent: Coloplast's prior GPO access wins with Premier in April 2020 and Vizient in July 2021 serve as a powerful precedent for acute channel penetration. Following those multi-source agreements, Coloplast successfully gained ten percentage points in US acute bags and plates share by mid-2026. This historical success demonstrates how GPO contract inclusion enables rapid hospital conversions by placing challengers on equal footing with Hollister. If HealthTrust transitions to a similar multi-source structure, Coloplast is well-positioned to replicate this clinical capture and enhance its recurring community revenue.
19.
Contrary evidence: Expert insights from a former Coloplast director in November 2025 suggest that GPO contract wins do not guarantee immediate hospital-level sales because clinical relationships with local procurement teams often dictate final product adoption. This expert noted that Coloplast frequently struggles to compete with peer bundling strategies when conversations focus solely on commercial price savings rather than clinical value. Consequently, simply securing a position on the HealthTrust tender may not automatically translate into the rapid volume conversion that bullish consensus EPS forecasts currently project.

Because ostomy patients show extremely high brand loyalty and rarely switch products after hospital discharge, existing community clinical bases will remain secure. Any competitive market share shifts will happen at an extremely slow pace of less than 1% annually. Therefore, Hollister's entrenched position will continue to limit the near-term earnings upside that challengers expect from new GPO access agreements.

Section 5 – Sales-Force Turnover and Post-Discharge Patient Leakage

Section summary
  1. Severe sales force turnover following the recent US commercial reorganizations has disrupted critical clinical relationships and slowed product adoption.
  2. High post-discharge leakage persists because Coloplast lacks the massive direct distribution scale of peers like Convatec in the community channel.
  3. Operational execution risks and commercial reinvestment needs have forced a downgrade in long-term organic growth and earnings per share forecasts.

Sales-force reorganizations

1.
Disruptive commercial transitions: Coloplast's decision to split its skin and wound care divisions has triggered severe internal disruption and sales force instability in the United States. This structural shift forced the departure of high-caliber, relationship-driven sales representatives and President's Club winners who previously maintained vital clinical bonds. The subsequent loss of institutional memory and customer trust has severely hindered the company's ability to penetrate key acute care accounts. Consequently, these execution disruptions present a direct threat to near-term US organic revenue growth and EPS.
2.
Unprecedented talent departures: Industry observers have noted a substantial and highly unusual increase in sales representative turnover at Coloplast over the past twelve months. This recent spike in recruitment activity is far higher than the exceptionally stable retention rates observed over the preceding five years. The loss of legacy reps has undermined clinical confidence because customers heavily value consistent relationships with their product manufacturers. This rising operational friction ultimately slows down product training and adoption schedules in major healthcare systems.
3.
Lagging commercial expertise: The incomplete integration of recently acquired businesses like Kerecis has placed a major operational burden on Coloplast's legacy sales force. Legacy sales representatives frequently lack the specialized training required to navigate complex buy-and-bill and reimbursement models for advanced biologics. This clinical expertise deficit has caused many sales reps to struggle when commercializing newly launched wound care products in crowded medical markets. Without rapid retraining programs, this execution gap will continue to drag down the company's high-margin US growth.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research dated 4 November 2026 suggests that the newly launched Funding the Journey program will successfully streamline operations by freeing up DKK 500 million in cost savings. This restructuring program is designed to reduce operational complexity and right-size administrative functions rather than depleting essential customer-facing roles. Management expects to reinvest approximately 75% of these captured savings directly back into US Chronic Care commercial execution during fiscal year 2026/2027. Therefore, the strategic commercial reinvestment could ultimately offset any temporary sales-force disruption and protect the long-term EPS trajectory.

Post-discharge leakage dynamics

5.
High discharge leakage: Coloplast continues to experience significant patient leakage as ostomy users transition from acute care hospitals to community home care providers. Although Coloplast has successfully won major acute GPO contracts to secure hospital starts, this initial success has not translated into community growth. This commercial disconnect is primarily driven by a lack of deep, established relationships with local home care nurses. Consequently, competitor brands are able to easily switch discharged patients to their own systems before long-term community loyalty is established.
6.
Weak distribution scale: Coloplast's wholly owned direct-to-consumer distributor Comfort Medical remains relatively small and lacks the market footprint of major competitors. Comfort Medical holds an estimated 5% to 10% share of the US continence care distributor market. In contrast, peer Convatec controls 180 Medical with a dominant 40% market share. This substantial distribution scale gap limits Coloplast's ability to directly control the patient discharge flow and prevent leakage to competitor products.
7.
Strategic home partnerships: DNB Carnegie research dated 22 October 2026 reports that Coloplast has attempted to mitigate community leakage by establishing a dedicated Home Health team. The company also entered into a strategic Platinum partnership with Medline in fiscal year 2022/2023 to strengthen its post-acute position. These initiatives are critical because 80% to 90% of acute patients pass through post-acute channels where they are highly vulnerable to brand switching. However, the broker notes that these defensive programs must still prove their ability to fully convert hospital starts into durable community sales.
8.
Cascading pricing pressure: Jefferies research dated 3 July 2025 highlights that the US post-acute distribution landscape is poised for structural consolidation. Smaller independent distributors are facing rising margin compression from larger competitors and group purchasing organizations. Under these conditions, distributors are highly likely to pass cost constraints back to manufacturers, depressing Coloplast's realized gross margins. Because Comfort Medical remains a distant third-place distributor with only a 5% to 10% share, Coloplast possesses limited scale to absorb these cascading price reductions.

Growth and earnings implications

This strategic target reset reflects mounting pricing headwinds, US competitive bidding risks, and slower wound care recovery. These reduced ambitions demonstrate how operational execution challenges have constrained the company's long-term financial trajectory.

Because these rising operational costs cannot be fully offset by internal productivity programs, near-term profitability remains severely constrained. Consequently, the broker has lowered its adjusted EPS estimates by 2% for FY27 and up to 8% for subsequent years.

The broker expects the company's historical valuation premium over Convatec to erode due to weaker relative organic sales growth. While Convatec guides to 6% to 8% organic top-line growth, Coloplast's updated guidance points to a slower 5% to 7% range. This competitive divergence highlights the severe operational execution risks Coloplast faces in its key growth channels.
12.
Historical distribution precedent: Convatec's historical commercial strategy serves as a key warning case where the manufacturer deliberately abandoned the US post-acute channel. Convatec previously focused entirely on hospital-level clinical decisions, thereby neglecting vital regional distributor relationships. Coloplast exploited this strategic gap by partnering with small, independent dealers to accelerate its own community market share. This precedent suggests that if Coloplast now reduces its US sales-force headcount, competitors like Convatec can quickly reclaim those neglected clinical relationships.
13.
Authorized strategic reallocation: Coloplast's annual report dated 3 November 2026 details a significant sales force deployment shift toward the inpatient setting. The manufacturer systematically reduced its outpatient sales organization in response to challenging US Medicare reimbursement reforms. Management is actively focusing remaining field resources on priority clinical accounts to maximize inpatient sales productivity. While this resource reallocation aims to defend profit margins, the organizational downsizing increases execution risks across post-acute channels.
14.
Other side of the story: Coloplast remains highly resilient against competitive leakage threats due to the superior design and clinical performance of its core product lines. High switching costs and deep brand attachment mean that over 90% of chronic patients remain highly loyal to their initial brand. Consequently, the deep trust built with specialized wound and ostomy care nurses is highly likely to shield Coloplast's recurring community revenue from competitor erosion. Therefore, the company's established commercial footprint will continue to protect its long-term cash flows and consensus EPS from significant downside.

Channel and distributor sensitivities

6.
Significant pricing pressure: The consolidation of post-acute medical distributors in the United States could accelerate pricing pressure on major intimate healthcare manufacturers. Jefferies research dated 3 July 2025 notes that larger distributors are increasingly passing cost constraints back to suppliers to protect their own operating margins. If Coloplast's Comfort Medical, which holds a small 5% to 10% market share, cannot withstand these demands, the company's realized US ASPs will fall significantly. This structural shift would restrict gross margin expansion and weaken the cash flows needed to support future research.

Disruptive regulatory changes: The potential implementation of the Medicare Competitive Bidding Program in January 2028 represents a critical risk that could alter market shares. Morgan Stanley research dated 13 October 2026 suggests that a harsh bidding outcome could create an immediate 3% to 8% headwind to chronic care earnings. If the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services implements deep reimbursement cuts, smaller independent distributors may face severe financial insolvency. This distributor consolidation would concentrate market power in vertically integrated players, leaving Coloplast with reduced community distribution options.
8.
Accelerating peer growth: Convatec is strategically positioned to capitalize on any post-acute channel disruption due to its unmatched direct-to-consumer distribution footprint. The challenger's 180 Medical subsidiary controls approximately 40% of the US intermittent catheter market, compared to the smaller share held by Comfort Medical. If Convatec leverages this distribution scale to exclusively promote its own newly launched products, it could capture substantial post-discharge market share from competitors. This aggressive pull-through strategy would directly support Convatec's upgraded mid-term organic sales growth guidance of midto-high single digits.
9.
Unanticipated market contraction: Slower procedural volumes in US hospital channels present an overlooked threat to the growth targets of major ostomy manufacturers. Field vice presidents at McKesson Corporation observe that while inflation keeps dollar volumes stable, overall surgical procedure rates are slightly declining. If this trend of permanent stoma reduction persists due to advanced robotic colorectal surgeries, the total addressable market will stagnate or contract over the strategic period. Consequently, manufacturers will be forced to compete aggressively for a shrinking pool of patients, which will depress margins and constrain consensus earnings per share.
10.
Other side of the story: Coloplast remains highly resilient against competitive leakage threats due to its core product performance. Former directors note that once chronic patients are established on a product in the home setting, they exhibit close to 100% retention because they fear switching. Consequently, even if Convatec's Natura Body launch is successful, competitive market share shifts will occur at an extremely gradual pace of less than 1% annually. Therefore, Coloplast's recurring community revenue stream will protect consensus earnings per share from sudden competitive disruption.
