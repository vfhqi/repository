2.
Change momentum: Near-term estimate revisions are positive, but the outer-year growth ambition has been cut. Morgan Stanley lifted its 2027 EPS estimate to €4.88 from €4.75, largely on favorable currency spot rates. That upward momentum contrasts with negative earnings revisions across other peer diagnostics manufacturers. The 2027 to 2028 organic sales growth target was lowered to 3% to 6% from 7%.

Guidance trustworthiness: Guidance is moderately reliable. Management sets cautious baselines and resets early rather than missing late. After a 3.9% organic sales decline in Q1 2026, it cut full-year sales expectations from 5% to 7% to 3% to 5%. It then maintained that range, together with 0% to 10% organic EBIT growth, at the half-year. In 2025, actual organic growth of 6.2% landed inside the cut guidance range.
4.
Overall earnings read: Earnings growth now depends more on margin than on volume. Management aims for organic contributive operating income growth higher than organic sales growth in 2027 and 2028. That makes cost delivery the anchor of the earnings case, while the top line is exposed to winter respiratory cycles.

Leadership and ownership backdrop: The CEO, Pierre Boulud, was renewed for four years on February 26, 2026. Institut Mérieux holds 55% of the equity, and Exor plans to raise its stake to 8% from 4%. Governance is therefore stable, but the shift to a results-driven culture carries retention costs (see the key risks below).
