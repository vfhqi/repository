EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Inter Cars S.A. dominates European automotive parts distribution through an agile, segment-focused one-stopshop model executed across a 21-country branch footprint, with scalable digital ecosystems and advanced logistics automation driving strong operating leverage and solidifying defensive aftermarket leadership. The group delivered exceptional long-term performance with consolidated revenue exceeding PLN 21.2 billion in 2025, underpinned by a 16.3% sales CAGR and 16.2% EBITDA CAGR from 2013 to 2025.

Inter Cars exhibits exceptional leadership caliber and structural moats driven by proprietary digital integration and highly automated robotized central logistics hubs, with the alignment of management incentives and asset-light franchisee dynamics ensuring consistent, superior ROCE and organic market share gains across European markets.

Business Model Strength and Market Position Inter Cars distributes spare parts for passenger and commercial vehicles utilizing a comprehensive one-stopshop product range to serve independent workshops, with operations spanning a brick-and-mortar network in 21 European countries. Passenger vehicle parts remain the dominant segment at 55.9% of 2025 sales, with growth driven by expanding assortments and entering new markets. The Inter Cars e-Catalogue serves as the primary B2B transactional engine, supporting over 250,000 active users across Europe and processing roughly 3 million monthly order lines. Inter Cars positions itself as the dominant master distributor in Central and Eastern Europe, with its central warehouses controlling the flow of over two million SKUs.

Financial Profile and Peer Outperformance Consolidated LTM EBITDA reached PLN 1.37 billion by September 2025, with automation initiatives driving Q1 2026 gross margins up to 29.6%. Q1 2026 consolidated net profit surged to PLN 211 million from PLN 157 million, supported by financial expenses dropping to PLN 47 million. Net debt to EBITDA rose to 2.2x by September 2025 due to investment loans but deleveraging efforts compressed the ratio to 1.74x by March 2026.

Inter Cars achieved outstanding sales growth of 12.0% in Euro terms in Q1 2026, significantly outperforming primary Western European peers, driven by international expansion with foreign distribution subsidiaries rising to represent 57.1% of total distribution sales. In contrast, LKQ Europe experienced a 4.0% organic revenue decline in Q1 2026, with Europe segment EBITDA margins falling 150 basis points to 7.8% from 9.3% in Q1 2025.

Industry Structure and Consolidation Runway The European automotive aftermarket at the distribution level represents an estimated €100 billion addressable market, growing at a steady 2.0% CAGR excluding macroeconomic inflation. Deferred purchases of new vehicles are structurally expanding the target market as cars remain on the road longer, with the average age of the European passenger car fleet rising from 10.9 years in 2013 to 12.9 years in 2025, projected to reach 13.6 years by 2030.

The European aftermarket remains highly fragmented with approximately 50,000 independent distributors operating across the continent, while the top 7 European IAM distributors control only about 25.0% of the total distribution revenue pool. Larger distributors utilize acquisitions to drive substantial purchasing, logistics, and cost synergies, with Parts Holding Europe managing to halve its post-synergy acquisition EV/EBITDA multiples by an average of 2.0x. 15 Value Chain Position and Franchise Economics Inter Cars' consolidated master distribution and wholesale operations generate a 29.25% reported gross margin and 3.52% reported EBIT margin.

Downstream sales and deliveries are executed through independent franchise branches, with 664 total branches at the end of 2024, where the parent company provides capital, IT systems, and inventory while keeping physical branches asset-light and shifting variable operating expenses to local partners. The sales margin generated by a branch is divided in a 50-50 ratio between Inter Cars and the partner, motivating local managers to optimize local sales.

Credit risk associated with trade receivables remains highly limited across the entire group, with the customer base large and widely dispersed throughout Poland and international daughter companies, showing no significant concentration of credit risk regarding exposures to individual B2B customers.

Digital Moats and Software Lock-In The Inter Cars e-Catalogue serves as the primary system for B2B search, selection, and purchasing, supporting more than 220,000 active monthly users across 21 markets in 2024, with platforms integrating VIN-based vehicle identification and comprehensive repair-related information to aid professional mechanics. Technical systems are designed to create structural switching costs that secure workshop purchase loyalty, with integrating the garage Dealer Management System directly with Inter Cars via API a key driver of loyalization, reducing the likelihood of garages comparing individual parts prices on rival platforms. Transactional revenue is characterized by highly repetitive B2B ordering behavior, with regular, established customers realizing 50.0% of all group purchase orders. - Earnings Calls Logistics Automation and Scale Advantages Inter Cars has built a significant competitive advantage in Eastern Europe through highly automated logistics hubs, with the newly expanded 114,300 square meter Zakroczym hub featuring the region's first Exotec Skypod system, operating with over 200 autonomous robots navigating modular racking over 11 meters high and housing 100,000 SKUs representing 20.0% of local stock.

A fully automated warehouse near Poznań represents a PLN 600 million total investment, with the group allocating PLN 150 million to Stęszew in Q1 2026 alone and full operational launch scheduled for the second quarter of 2027. A modern warehouse in Romania required a PLN 250 million investment, with the robotized Brașov facility fully operational and stocking goods. Automation drove Q1 2026 SG&A down to 14.4% of sales.

- Earnings Calls Supplier Diversification and Quality Focus The group cooperates with over 2,100 suppliers worldwide, identifying 1,600 of them as key long-term partners, with no single supplier's share of total group sales revenue exceeding 10.0% given the significant diversification of supply sources. Sourced inventory is classified into premium brands, own private label brands, and remanufactured parts, with premium brand suppliers remaining the absolute bedrock of the business, generating over 92.0% of Inter Cars' total consolidated turnover.

High inventory levels, standing at PLN 5.76 billion in March 2026, are strategically held to hedge against supplier price increases, while negotiating power has historically shifted toward large distributors as component producers face limited direct automotive OEM demand.

- Earnings Calls Operator Quality and Management Alignment Inter Cars targets absolute market leadership as the premier and most profitable mobility distributor in Europe, with the company's strategy pivoting heavily on organic expansion, extending its specialized branch distribution model, and deploying advanced logistics automation. For the 2026 operational year, executive options require a minimum 10.0% year-on-year sales revenue expansion, with management feeling highly comfortable hitting this target after generating 13.5% growth in Q1 2026.

Inter Cars successfully refinanced its debt profile by securing a new PLN 4.0 billion syndicated credit facility with eight commercial banks, with the new facility completely unsecured and carrying 20.0% lower bank margins in contrast to previous collateralized debt, boosting financial flexibility by extending debt tenors from three to five years. High operating efficiency enables Inter Cars to consistently deliver a reported post-tax ROIC exceeding 15.0%, with profitability supported by descending SG&A costs as robotized hubs lower fulfillment expenses.

Long-Term Growth Drivers and Disruption Defense High capital barriers and technological complexity insulate Inter Cars from disruption, with new competitors facing insurmountable logistics capital requirements to replicate established master distribution hubs across European markets, while replicating a network of 684 regional branches remains highly improbable for emerging alternative entrants. ADAS technologies increase product complexity and repair severity, raising the overall parts value per vehicle, with sophisticated components like LED matrix headlights commanding up to six times the cost of traditional equivalents, while windshield replacements require mandatory ADAS sensor recalibration, generating incremental, highly profitable service revenue.

The average age of European passenger cars reached 12.9 years, driving persistent non-discretionary repair volumes, with older vehicles requiring more intensive maintenance insulating the aftermarket from general economic downturns, while weak consumer confidence prompts drivers to maintain existing vehicles, expanding the addressable parts market. Fleet transitions to electric vehicles eliminate classical mechanical parts, potentially reducing traditional aftermarket revenues by 25% to 40% lifetime, with battery electric vehicles eliminating complex systems like fuel injection, exhaust layouts, and multi-stage transmissions, as approximately 25% of current aftermarket parts are ICEspecific indicating a long-term decline in traditional engine categories.

Key Risks and Structural Headwinds Strategic logistics investments temporarily elevate debt metrics, yet transition the group toward unmatched automated processing efficiency, though logistics automation projects elevate net debt and support a long-term improvement in operating efficiency. Supplier pricing power faces erosion as consolidated purchasing groups steadily force the harmonization of historical regional price differences, with international suppliers actively negotiating across borders to harmonize prices, aiming to eliminate gray-market imports by 2030.

Strategic consolidation by competitors like MEKO and SAG narrows geographic white space while parts manufacturers launching direct-to-workshop channels pose emerging vertical integration risks, with major automotive component manufacturers expanding direct-to-workshop sales channels, as evidenced by MAHLE shifting to a direct sales approach for its workshop equipment in the UK in July 2026, bypassing separate distributor structures and supporting this direct model through its local Bilston warehouse, mobile service vans, and field engineers delivering on-site training.

The consolidated operating cycle expanded to 178 days in 2024 from 170 days in 2023, with the net cash conversion cycle lengthening to 140 days in 2024 from 135 days in 2023 indicating higher capital absorption, while the average collection period deteriorated to 48 days in 2023 from 44 days in 2022, highlighting growing workshop leverage.
