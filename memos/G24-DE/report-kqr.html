Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The risk of agentic AI disintermediation threatens to erode the consumer-attention monopolies of European classifieds portals, potentially triggering a significant de-rating in valuation multiples and driving substantial long-term downside to earnings per share.

Core Inquiry and Moat Threat

1.
Critical inquiry: "What share of visits and leads at Europe's national classifieds portals now comes from artificial intelligence agents and large language models, and has that share moved since Meta launched its Muse agent? Rightmove reports under 0.5 percent, Scout24 under 1 percent and Swiss Marketplace below 1 percent; we have no figures for Autotrader, Hemnet, Vend or Baltic Classifieds. Do the portals block agents or feed their listings to them (as Autotrader does inside ChatGPT), and is there any sign that estate agents or car dealers are starting to supply listings directly to agents instead of to the portals?"
2.
Escalating risks: The core threat of agentic AI is that platforms could disintermediate classifieds as the primary discovery layer, forcing portals to pay distribution fees to AI agents. Historically, well-established marketplaces have maintained low customer acquisition costs of around 6% of sales due to highly direct traffic. If portals become heavily dependent on third-party AI traffic, their pricing power could weaken, threatening their ability to sustain high-single-digit average revenue per agent growth.
3.
Severe de-rating: Investors fear that if consumers bypass property and automotive portals, the high margins and capital returns generated by gatekeeper pricing power will permanently end. This existential concern has caused several classifieds stocks to trade at a severe discount, with some peers reflecting a sector-low threeyear forward revenue PEG ratio of 0.5x. Even if near-term earnings remain resilient, valuation multiples are unlikely to stabilize until there is greater clarity regarding the adoption slope of tools like Meta's Muse.
4.
Structural displacement: The launch of Meta's Muse highlights the structural risk where AI agents transition from simple discovery to owning the entire transaction itself. Under this scenario, the AI agent could bypass price comparison websites and classifieds entirely, depriving portals of transaction economics while maintaining only high-volume price discovery. Consequently, European classifieds portals could face lower quote-to-sale conversion rates and diminished long-term revenue growth.

24 25 Valuation discount: In Germany, Scout24's share price declined by approximately 10% following the US launch of Meta's Muse agent as investors feared disintermediation. Deutsche Bank argues that this de-rating is unjustified because Scout24 has established a deep real estate ecosystem that extends far beyond simple listings, including software and valuation tools. If AI agents successfully bypass portals, Scout24's

5.
valuation multiple could compress from its expected 18x EV/EBITDA target to historical averages.
6.
Contrary evidence: Practical testing of agentic AI tools like OpenClaw demonstrates that AI agents struggle with real-time data accuracy, confirming that platforms remain superior for discovery and transaction conversion. Jefferies notes that agentic AI represents a technology step-change that will actually make classifieds platforms higher quality by removing micro-frictions and expanding monetization opportunities. Consequently, the threat of disintermediation is exaggerated, and progressive-minded management teams are positioned to lead a strong sector recovery.
7.
Other side of the story: While the market has heavily discounted European classifieds on fears of sudden AI disintermediation, these platforms hold highly defensive moats because AI platforms need comprehensive, current inventory that only structured portals can aggregate. Bernstein projects that an "AI as distributor" scenario would only reduce long-term earnings per share by approximately 3% for both Rightmove and Scout24. Therefore, the existential threat is likely slower and less binary than currently priced, allowing dominant portals to adapt and protect their high margins.

Section 2 – AI Agent and LLM Share of Visits and Leads

Section summary
  1. AI-driven traffic across European classifieds portals remains exceptionally low, with most platforms experiencing less than 1.50% of total visits originating from artificial intelligence agents and large language models.
  2. The launch of Meta's Muse agent has not triggered a sudden traffic surge, and early data suggests that incoming AI queries represent channel substitution from Google search rather than net additive demand.

Traffic Quantification and Peer Comparison

1.
Minimal exposure: Rightmove has seen only a marginal increase in its share of incoming artificial intelligence traffic, which remains a tiny fraction of its overall audience. According to Similarweb data analyzed by Bernstein on October 21, 2026, Rightmove's AI-referred web traffic grew from 0.1% in January 2025 to 1.1% in August 2026. This slow expansion suggests that the threat of immediate portal bypass is likely overblown. Consequently, Rightmove's long-term earnings per share are well-insulated from sudden disintermediation as the platform retains its lead ownership.
2.
Low penetration: Scout24 is experiencing similar negligible traffic levels from large language models, as its core German real estate portal maintains direct audience loyalty. Bernstein's October 21, 2026 analysis of Similarweb metrics shows that incoming AI traffic to ImmobilienScout24 rose from 0.1% in July 2026 to 1.1% in August 2026. This level matches its UK peer Rightmove, showing consistent resistance to AI disintermediation across different European markets. This low share means that Scout24's gatekeeper pricing power and high margins remain largely secure.
3.
Slow growth: Autotrader also reports a very low proportion of audience interactions coming from generative AI assistants, despite being an early technology adopter. Redburn confirms that less than 0.5 percent of Autotrader's traffic is generated by LLMs, indicating that vehicle buyers continue to prefer direct visual browsing. This slow adoption curve suggests that Autotrader's average revenue per retailer is unlikely to face near-term pressure. Ultimately, the threat to the classifieds from agents is low because consumer browsing habits are extremely sticky.
4.
Consistent metrics: European classifieds portals exhibit highly aligned traffic profiles, with Rightmove, Scout24, and Autotrader all recording exactly 1.1% of visits from AI tools in August 2026. This absolute alignment across property and automotive verticals suggests a unified consumer preference for portals over conversational interfaces. With these key peers showing identical low-exposure metrics, there is no evidence that any single vertical is more vulnerable to agentic disruption. Therefore, the sector's valuation multiples should recover as traffic risk proves uniform and manageable.

Regional Portals and the Impact of Meta's Muse

5.
Very low exposure: Hemnet reports that large language model traffic is increasing but remains at an extremely low absolute level. According to a Deutsche Bank research note from October 22, 2026, Hemnet's LLMoriginated traffic is currently north of 0.1% of its total visits. This low volume demonstrates that Swedish property searchers are not delegating their home search to autonomous agents. Because Hemnet maintains dominant pricing power and an unmatched network effect, its near-term earnings growth remains highly insulated.
6.
Negligible referral: Vend reports that its leading Norwegian portal, FINN, experiences almost zero direct traffic from external artificial intelligence tools. Company disclosures from October 27, 2026, confirm that less than 0.5% of FINN's traffic comes from external AI, while 95% of traffic is direct or owned. This highlights that consumers continue to rely on trusted brands and proprietary data for high-value transactions. As a result, Vend's transaction-based monetization models face little risk of being bypassed.
7.
Absent figures: Swiss Marketplace Group and Baltic Classifieds Group show no signs of traffic erosion, though precise AI traffic shares are not disclosed. While Swiss Marketplace operates in a market below 1% AI traffic, Baltic Classifieds remains focused on expanding its dominant local Baltic portals. Analysts at Morgan Stanley note that although AI-driven disruption is the key risk, Baltic Classifieds retains strong growth and high margins due to robust local network effects. Consequently, the lack of precise traffic metrics has not prevented these portals from delivering robust financial performances.
8.
Contrary evidence: Rapid consumer adoption of Meta's Muse agent has ignited fresh fears of portal disintermediation since its September 8, 2026 launch. Sensor Tower data cited by Deutsche Bank on September 25, 2026, shows that Muse achieved over 3 million downloads in its first 16 days, far outpacing typical AI applications. This explosive growth suggests that agentic commerce could scale faster than expected, potentially threatening portal gatekeeper roles if users permanently delegate property searches.
9.
Channel substitution: Sector analysts argue that incoming AI traffic represents a shift in search channels rather than a net-additive source of leads. Redburn notes that portals typically derive only 10% to 15% of traffic from organic Google searches, which AI tools are likely to partially replace. Since consumers are simply migrating from Google to LLMs to find portal listings, the economic impact on portals is immaterial. This channel substitution protects the portals' direct relationships, ensuring stable long-term advertising revenue.

Travel Sector Comparison and Conclusion

10.
Reassuring precedent: Online travel agencies provide a comparable case where agentic AI has failed to disrupt established consumer search habits. For example, the AI agent Instinct generates travel-related volume that equates to a mere 0.25% of Booking Holdings' total web traffic. This shows that even in transactional travel, consumers prefer visual interfaces over simple chat-based assistants. This precedent suggests that high-ASP, visual-heavy classifieds portals are highly likely to retain their direct traffic and listing monopolies.
11.
Other side of the story: Although near-term traffic data suggests that portals are safe, the long-term threat of Meta's Muse evolving into a Western "Super App" remains a structural risk for classifieds. If Muse or a similar platform succeeds in scraping every property online or signing up agents directly, it could deliver supply abundance that replicates portals completely. This shift would erode the portals' attention monopolies and weaken their ability to push double-digit pricing increases. Consequently, terminal growth assumptions and long-term earnings per share could face downward revisions if agentic adoption eventually hits critical mass.

Section 3 – Portal Strategies: Blocking Versus Feeding Listings to AI Agents

Section summary
  1. European classifieds portals utilize structured API integrations and text mining opt-outs to maintain strict control over proprietary listing data while expanding distribution through controlled partnerships.
  2. Technical defenses such as login walls and app-centric user journeys serve as primary barriers against unauthorized scraping by horizontal artificial intelligence crawlers.

Defensive Portals and Technical Barriers

1.
Technical defenses: Technical defenses at Rightmove prevent unauthorized database extraction by limiting the visibility of listing details to public crawlers. Annick Maas at Bernstein Research reports on February 27, 2026, that over 50% of the metadata underpinning a listing is completely unscrapable from the public interface of the platform. This technical barrier prevents general-purpose AI agents from reconstructing Rightmove's database without formal commercial agreements. Consequently, the company's long-term gatekeeper role and high average revenue per advertiser remain protected from unauthorized search aggregation.
2.
Strategic protection: Strategic protection at Vend Marketplaces relies on strict intellectual property protocols to restrict unauthorized model training on its local Scandinavian databases. Analysts at DNB Carnegie report on March 4, 2026, that Vend actively enforces its right to opt-out of training under Text and Data Mining rules. If third-party developers bypass these rules to scrape property or vehicle listings, Vend blocks their access and pursues legal remedies. This defense preserves the exclusive value of Vend's databases and protects its future transaction-based monetization.
3.
Direct app engagement: Direct app engagement serves as an additional natural barrier for portals, preventing search engines and crawlers from easily accessing listing details. DNB Carnegie reported on March 4, 2026, that over 50 percent of traffic at Vend flows directly through its proprietary mobile applications. Because mobile application traffic is shielded behind app interfaces and login screens, it remains highly resistant to external scraping. This app-centric traffic distribution ensures high consumer loyalty and prevents external large language models from diverting users.
4.
Vertical integration: Vertical integration at Swiss Marketplace Group allows the platform to utilize horizontal assets as protective channels rather than exposing proprietary databases. A former CEO of Swiss Marketplace Group explained on January 4, 2026, that the group routes individual listings to professional horizontal marketplaces to extend audience reach. This integration keeps the proprietary real estate and automotive databases secure while leveraging horizontal traffic to drive high-quality leads. Consequently, Swiss Marketplace Group maintains strong margins without risking direct data leakage to external generative tools.

Feeding and Integration Strategies

5.
Collaborative distribution: Collaborative distribution at Autotrader Group allows controlled listing exposure through advanced standard protocols that preserve data ownership. Autotrader reported in its annual report on May 21, 2026, that it developed connectivity using the emerging standard MCP protocol for its live ChatGPT application integration. This structure allows Autotrader to act as a trusted source of truth while keeping the transaction layer firmly on its platform. By supplying structured feeds instead of permitting scraping, Autotrader ensures that AI-driven searches still rely on its proprietary infrastructure.
6.
Controlled integration: Controlled integration at Autotrader protects the platform from presenting full datasets to external search engines. On a conference call on November 6, 2025, Autotrader management explained that they make strategic decisions to open their site partially rather than fully to third-party interfaces. This selective distribution prevents external tools from capturing entire inventory metrics or eroding the value of premium listings. As a result, Autotrader leverages AI as a referral channel while protecting its core advertising economics.
7.
Database protection: Database protection at Scout24 is maintained by treating ChatGPT integrations as restricted wrappers rather than open data repositories. Annick Maas at Bernstein Research reports on February 27, 2026, that Scout24's ChatGPT partnership is strictly structured as an API wrapper that limits data access. The company does not grant external applications unrestricted entry to its underlying real estate records. Because its proprietary datasets remain safely guarded, Scout24 maintains total control over its real estate ecosystem and prevent traffic leakage.

Commercial Terms and Cloudflare Defenses

8.
Modern network: Modern network infrastructure enables precise traffic management by separating search crawlers from training bots. Cloudflare announced on September 15, 2026, that its new AI suite provides three independent traffic controls to manage search, training, and AI agents. This granularity allows adsupported websites to block AI agents and training models while keeping traditional search visibility intact. These controls allow platforms to protect their databases from being mined for free by horizontal model developers.

Cloudflare reports on October 20, 2026, that daily requests from AI agents grew by over 1,700 percent in a single year. This massive surge means that automated agents now represent a substantial share of total internet traffic, making complete blocking commercially impractical. If portals fully block these agents, they risk losing significant referral traffic to more open competitors.
10.
Resilient traffic: Resilient traffic patterns at Baltic Classifieds Group demonstrate that regional monopolies can maintain direct customer relationships without aggressive bot blocking. Baltic Classifieds Group reported on July 2, 2026, that traffic from generative AI sources remained minimal at 0.2 percent of total visits. Because direct visits represent 77% of total traffic, the platform does not face significant disintermediation pressure. This high direct engagement allows the company to focus its AI investments on listings creation rather than building expensive defensive firewalls.
11.
Offensive database: Offensive database building at Hemnet utilizes automatic indexing to aggregate listings directly from competitor and agency websites. Hemnet announced on October 20, 2026, that it would launch automatically aggregated property listings sourced from agency websites to ensure full market coverage. This active scraping strategy allows Hemnet to reclaim supply leadership from its main competitor, Booli. By combining free agent uploads with automated web indexing, Hemnet rebuilds its platform network effects and secures its consumer-attention moat.
12.
Other side of the story: Other side of the story: While portals guard their databases through technical walls and API wrappers, the long-term rise of horizontal agents remains an existential threat. If conversational tools achieve widespread consumer adoption, they could force portals to pay high distribution fees for traffic. Even if portals restrict bot access, agents could pull data from cooperating challenger platforms or directly from cooperative agency networks. Consequently, defensive moats may prove temporary, and portal pricing power could weaken if agentic search dominates.

Section 4 – Direct Listing Supply: Are Agents and Dealers Bypassing Portals?

Section summary
  1. Fragmented supply and unstructured websites prevent real estate agents and automotive dealers from successfully bypassing dominant classifieds portals to list directly with artificial intelligence agents.

High computational costs and data inconsistencies make direct scraping of individual dealer and agency portals highly inefficient compared to utilizing structured platform aggregation.

Feasibility Barriers to Direct Listing

1.
Severe fragmentation: Property listings in the United Kingdom remain highly decentralized across approximately 16,000 distinct real estate agency branches listed on Rightmove. Because of this dispersion, artificial intelligence agents struggle to build a comprehensive property database without utilizing a central portal. Consequently, portals maintain their gatekeeper status, ensuring that long-term earnings per share remain protected from direct bypass.
2.
Highly fragmented: Real estate marketing in Germany displays a similar lack of centralization with about 26,000 agencies operating on Scout24. This structural division makes it exceptionally difficult for external large language models to construct reliable alternative inventories. Therefore, independent developers cannot easily bypass the platform to establish direct agent relationships, securing Scout24's robust listing subscription revenues for the foreseeable future.
3.
Inconsistent listings: Automotive dealers present highly unreliable data formats that make direct dealer web scraping extremely impractical for autonomous systems. Testing of the agentic AI tool OpenClaw revealed that it routed queries to major aggregators like Autotrader 77 times, compared to only 28 direct dealer website links. This heavy reliance on structured aggregators indicates that dealers cannot easily bypass portals due to their own poor website infrastructure.
4.
High compute: Running autonomous agents to index thousands of small business websites creates significant technical inefficiencies and elevated operational costs. Interrogating a small group of structured, aggregated portals remains far more efficient than processing thousands of unstructured endpoints when processing costs are high. This efficiency implies that classifieds platforms will remain the path of least resistance for conversational search engines.
5.
Controlled data: Large-scale horizontal operators maintain a strong supply moat by restricting the historical transaction details they expose to external crawlers. Prosus's OLX division selectively provides listing data to AI agents while withholding highly proprietary insights like pricing dynamics. This strategic filtering forces consumers to transact directly through the platform, protecting commission margins and transactional revenues.

Pre-Market Alternatives and Platform Bypasses

6.
Rising costs: Property sellers in Sweden are increasingly choosing to bypass upfront listing fees by using free localized platforms. Real estate brokers frequently list properties first on their own corporate websites and free sites like Booli before paying for premium portal exposure. This behavioral shift threatens the immediate monetization pipeline of traditional portals, forcing them to introduce zero-cost entry tiers to secure inventory.
7.
Weakened monetization: The growth of pre-market real estate platforms represents a structural bypass where transactions occur before properties reach public portals. This early-stage transaction activity typically exhibits lower consumer intent but successfully absorbs market supply. If portals fail to capture these early listings, their long-term pricing power and advertising revenues could face downward pressure.
8.
Reduced friction: Alternative financing and payment structures allow portals to capture listings earlier in the transaction cycle before sellers consider bypassing. Hemnet's pay-on-sale model, adopted by approximately 45% of property sellers, effectively removes upfront financial barriers to listing. This flexible model helps portals secure early-stage property inventory and prevents sellers from relying solely on free local competitor platforms.
9.
Contrary evidence: Hemnet successfully countered competitive bypass threats by launching free listing formats and automated web aggregation in October 2026. By offering free agent-sourced listings and scraping non-participating agency sites, the portal brought pre-market inventory back into its ecosystem. This aggressive strategic pivot indicates that portals can reclaim listings supply leadership through targeted free offerings.

Although challenger portals offer similar inventory levels, Rightmove retains a massive 90% share of total consumer time spent. Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A rapid shift toward agentic AI discovery could trigger severe structural revenue declines for European classifieds portals by permanently dismantling their pricing power.
  2. If consumers delegate high-value searches to horizontal agents like Meta's Muse, portals may be forced to pay significant distribution fees or face complete bypass.

Devalued earnings: If the alternative scenario of complete agentic disintermediation unfolds, European property portals face severe contraction in baseline profitability. Analyst Annick Maas of Bernstein Research warns that full disintermediation of the classifieds model would drive a 31 to 37 percent decline in expected earnings per share for Scout24 and Rightmove. This negative projection assumes meaningful subscription churn, weaker ancillary revenues, and a dramatic increase in marketing costs as portals struggle to retain direct consumer attention.
