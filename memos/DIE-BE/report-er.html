EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. D'Ieteren Group delivered a resilient FY-2025 performance that exceeded consensus expectations at the operational level, with adjusted profit before tax of €955.6m coming in 3% ahead of sell-side consensus despite a 10.3% reported decline driven by elevated interest costs from the late-2024 recapitalization. The cornerstone Belron asset demonstrated exceptional momentum, with adjusted EBIT margins expanding to 22.9% and second-half margins spiking to 24.4%, rapidly approaching the ambitious 2028 target of greater than 25%. However, the group's cautious FY-2026 guidance initially disappointed markets, with the mechanical EPS cut falling 7% to 9% below prior sell-side expectations despite strong underlying operational trends. The persistent conglomerate discount remains a critical debate, as the stock trades at an excessive 50% discount to net asset value despite rapid deleveraging and defensive aftermarket exposure.

The full-year 2025 results were reported on March 10, 2026, with the group's adjusted profit before tax reaching €955.6m, representing a material beat versus consensus expectations but masking the structural drag from financing costs. Excluding the €159.7m financial charges related to the shareholder reorganization, underlying PBT grew by 3.8% year-on-year at constant currency, demonstrating the fundamental strength of the portfolio's operational engines.

The most significant upside surprise came from Belron's Q1-2026 organic growth of 6.9%, which comfortably exceeded sell-side forecasts of 5.2%, driven by North American claims normalization and accelerating ADAS recalibration penetration. Management's strategic messaging emphasized Belron's structural recovery, with 7.1% constant-currency sales growth and normalized North American conditions in the second half, supporting the full-year adjusted operating margin expansion to 22.9%. 5 The internal operational adjustments across the portfolio revealed a bifurcated performance profile.

At Belron, management successfully executed on its margin expansion roadmap, with ADAS recalibration rates rising to 47.2% in FY25 from 42.2% in FY24, acting as a crucial pricing and mix driver. North American organic revenue growth accelerated to 8.0% in 2H25 compared to 4.0% in 1H25, driven by reduced claims avoidance and supporting the progression toward 2028 profitability targets. The State Farm contract win represents a multiyear volume catalyst, as Safelite's exclusive third-party administrator role for State Farm, which controls 17% of the US auto insurance market, is expected to act as a durable volume driver.

However, Parts Holding Europe delivered more modest but steady performance, with sales growing 6.3% to €2,937.1m and international sales expanding 7.0% organically, while margins remained stable despite acquisition dilution.

The cyclical automotive division faces severe near-term headwinds, with sales declining 5.5% to €4,978.7m yet delivering a temporarily elevated 4.7% adjusted operating margin, which is projected to contract by 120 basis points to 3.5% in FY-2026 as the Belgian market normalizes. Belgian new car registrations declined by 5.8% year-on-year in Q1-2026, matching the FY25 market drop of 7.5%, reflecting structural demand normalization after the post-pandemic boom.

Management's cautious tone on this segment was reinforced by a 6.6% sales drop in Q1-2026 driven by lower volumes and declining average selling prices, with analysts expecting severe cyclical margin compression. Meanwhile, TVH Parts experienced the sharpest execution challenges, with sales rising modestly by 1.1% to €1,705.3m but adjusted operating margin contracting from 15.7% to 13.4% due to weak industrial end-markets and elevated logistics ramp-up costs, prompting a comprehensive leadership overhaul with the appointment of Giuliano Parodi as CEO effective September 1, 2026.

The external environment shifts provided both supportive tailwinds and emerging risks. On the demand side, robust double-digit organic growth in international aftermarket segments is offsetting cyclical domestic automotive contraction, as rising ADAS recalibration rates and aging vehicle parcs reinforce defensive demand profiles. The macroeconomic backdrop showed signs of stabilization, with Belron's net leverage improving from 5.4x to 4.5x despite the massive recapitalization, with leverage expected to normalize to approximately 3x EBITDA by end-2027.

Favorable loan repricings in October 2025 and January 2026, including a 25 basis points margin step-down, are expected to reduce Belron interest expenses by €20m with a 1% positive impact on adjusted PBT. However, cost inflation pressures persist, with motor insurance claims inflation stabilizing in the mid-single digits at around 5% but higher vehicle complexity continuing to pressure underlying repair costs. The competitive landscape remains favorable for D'Ieteren's market-leading positions. Belron maintains a massive operational leverage advantage, posting an adjusted operating margin of 22.9% in FY25 compared to US peers Boyd Group at 18.3% and Driven Brands' Auto Glass Now at 4.3%.

PHE's adjusted operating margin of 9.1% remains comfortably above direct European automotive parts distribution peers, while TVH's 13.4% margin sits comfortably above the 11.9% average of global peers despite recent execution challenges. The technological disruption forces are being successfully monetized, with Belron's ADAS penetration reaching 47.2% in FY25, supported by the European Union's General Safety Regulation 2 mandating advanced driver-assistance systems, providing a meaningful price/mix benefit.

The exclusive mobile static recalibration tool co-developed with Bosch gives Belron a powerful commercial advantage to capture market share in cash and commercial segments.

The regulatory environment continues to provide structural tailwinds, particularly in Belgium where corporate electric vehicles benefit from 100% tax deductibility, pushing battery electric vehicles to represent 34.7% of Belgian registrations. However, this concentration creates vulnerability, as approximately 89% of BEV sales are fleet-driven through corporate accounts, making the automotive division highly sensitive to any future removal of subsidies.

On the supply side, Belron's position as the second-largest vehicle glass buyer globally, purchasing approximately 10 million units, allows it to maintain superior product availability over regional competitors, while Safelite's 80% domestic sourcing strategy minimizes tariff exposure, allowing it to neutralize cost volatility. The sell-side response was cautiously optimistic, with Jefferies raising its target price to €235 from €228, increasing its DCF-based Belron valuation by 5% to €33.8bn or 20x FY26E EV/EBIT.

Bernstein initiated coverage with an Outperform rating and €230 target, arguing the 50% discount to net asset value was unwarranted given Belron's defensive profile, while Deutsche Bank initiated with a Buy rating and €230 target, noting that Belron alone is worth the entirety of the group's current equity value. The governance evolution through the new Operating Committee structure, integrating Investment Directors with the Executive Committee to strengthen portfolio active ownership and accelerate capital allocation, represents a critical step toward institutionalizing best practices and addressing historical underperformance at TVH and Moleskine.

The key risks to the investment case remain centered on execution at TVH and the timing of a potential Belron listing. The elevated debt from the 2024 reorganization has delayed the anticipated timeline for a Belron listing, while the sheer volume of leadership transitions across TVH, Moleskine, and PHE creates near-term execution and cultural integration risks that could delay the multi-year earnings trajectory. The structural interest drag remains significant, with management downplaying that net finance costs evolved negatively, resulting in a 10.3% decline in adjusted PBT, with persistent financial charges continuing to cap reported net income expansion.

If macroeconomic conditions deteriorate or US insurance premiums remain elevated, Belron's volume recovery could experience unexpected volatility, making the ambitious 25% adjusted EBIT margin target for 2028 harder to achieve.

Section 16 – Conclusion

The FY-2025 earnings cycle fundamentally reaffirmed D'Ieteren's structural transformation from a cyclical automotive distributor into a resilient aftermarket services conglomerate, with adjusted profit before tax reaching €955.6m and beating sell-side consensus by 3%, yet the most critical delta from this reporting period was not the absolute profit delivery but rather Belron's accelerating margin inflection in the second half, where adjusted operating margins spiked to 24.4% as North American organic revenue growth surged to 8% driven by insurance claims normalization and ADAS recalibration penetration reaching 47.2%.

This second-half performance trajectory proved that Belron's path to greater than 25% margins by 2028 is not aspirational but structurally embedded, fundamentally de-risking the group's primary value driver and validating the investment thesis that premium aftermarket services can compound through mix-driven margin expansion independent of volume cycles.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish investment case remains the elevated financial leverage introduced by the 2024 family shareholding reorganization, which raised Belron's net debt to 5.5x EBITDA and triggered €180m in additional annual interest expenses, creating a structural drag that management deliberately downplayed during earnings calls while acknowledging that net finance costs evolved negatively and resulted in a 10.3% reported decline in adjusted profit before tax.

This counter-thesis argues that the group's reported earnings trajectory will remain suppressed for at least 18 to 24 months, perpetuating the 45% conglomerate discount and delaying any potential Belron IPO catalyst that could unlock shareholder value. Furthermore, if US auto insurance premiums remain elevated and continue to trigger consumer claim avoidance, Belron's organic growth trajectory could experience unexpected volatility, making the 2028 margin target harder to achieve while the Belgian automotive market faces severe cyclical margin normalization.

The exact criteria and future datapoints that would fundamentally change this analytical verdict are highly specific and measurable. First, if Belron's net leverage fails to delever below 4.0x EBITDA by the end of FY-2026 or if management fails to execute another successful 50 basis point loan margin step-down by October 2026, this would signal structural cash flow deterioration and force a material re-rating of the group's deleveraging narrative.

Second, if ADAS recalibration penetration rates stall below 50% in FY-2026 or if average recalibration pricing deflates by more than 10% due to competitive compression from independent glass repairers, this would undermine Belron's core margin expansion engine and require a fundamental reassessment of the 2028 profitability targets. Third, if TVH's newly appointed CEO Giuliano Parodi fails to restore adjusted operating margins to at least 14.5% by FY-2027, this would confirm that D'Ieteren lacks the active ownership capabilities to successfully turn around underperforming assets, widening the structural conglomerate discount and validating investor skepticism around portfolio complexity.
