Section 2 – Volume and Customer Trends of Chinese Platforms in CEE

Section summary
  1. The introduction of the €3 customs fee on low-value parcels has triggered a severe decline in Central and Eastern European order volumes for Chinese e-commerce platforms.
  2. Our confidence in this volume contraction is high, based on official European import indicators, consumer spending panels, and regional logistics data.

App downloads and monthly active users

1.
Pronounced deceleration: Temu experienced a sharp drop in user acquisition across Central and Eastern Europe following the July tariff implementation. Quantitative metrics from Jefferies on September 16, 2026, revealed that Temu's monthly active users declined by 15% year-over-year in August. This severe contraction suggests that the new tariff has substantially weakened the platform's consumer pull within the regional market.
2.
Marginal contraction: Shein demonstrated greater resilience than its primary competitor but still experienced an overall softening of user engagement. According to Jefferies research on September 16, 2026, Shein's monthly active users fell by 3% year-over-year in August. This trend indicates that even established apparel platforms are struggling to maintain user momentum under the newly restrictive European import regime.
3.
Severe deceleration: Chinese shopping platforms saw a significant slowdown in user metrics as regional regulatory pressures intensified across Europe. Morgan Stanley research on October 15, 2026, noted that the flat customs fee contributed directly to a visible slowdown in European app downloads. This suggests that the cost increases are dampening consumer interest in installing these discount shopping apps.

Card spending and transactional panel data

4.
Decreasing category share: Transaction data indicates that Shein has suffered a severe drop in apparel market share across major European countries. Transactional intelligence from Consumer Edge on October 12, 2026, showed that Shein's apparel spend share fell from 7% in April to under 5% in September. This decline demonstrates that price increases undermine the platform's core competitiveness in fashion retail.
5.
Slumping broadline share: Temu experienced a parallel contraction in its retail categories as consumers balked at the added customs fee. Consumer Edge transaction panel data from October 12, 2026, revealed that Temu's broadline market share decreased from 7% to 4.5% over the summer. This data confirms that higher transactional costs deter shoppers from making frequent low-value purchases.
6.
Shifted purchasing patterns: Shoppers modified their checkout habits by grouping orders to mitigate the impact of the per-category customs charge. Consumer Edge research on October 12, 2026, observed that order counts fell sharply while average ticket sizes rose after the fee took effect. This shift proves that the fixed tariff drives larger basket sizes at the expense of pure transaction frequency.

Parcel volumes and import logistics

7.
Plummeting cargo flows: Customs clearances for inbound small packages collapsed immediately after the European Union eliminated the low-value tariff loophole. Official administrative monitoring cited in news reports on November 1, 2026, indicated that parcel imports valued under €150 crashed by 64% after July. This massive volume drop indicates that the cross-border delivery model faces crisis without rapid logistical restructuring.
8.
Anticipated volume drop: Logistics experts forecast severe near-term disruptions to air freight volumes as the direct shipping model became economically unviable. Broker analysis from Maybank on July 2, 2026, noted that independent advisors expected a 10% to 35% drop in European air e-commerce shipments. This expectation matches the subsequent reality of depressed volumes at major regional airports during the autumn.
9.
Contrary evidence: Consumer sentiment surveys reveal that a substantial majority of shoppers remain highly loyal to Chinese marketplaces despite the added fee. Survey data from NielsenIQ on September 1, 2026, showed that 87% of European consumers continue to use cross-border platforms to combat domestic retail inflation. This persistent demand indicates that the volume drop may be temporary as consumers adjust to higher baseline prices.

Historical precedents and comparisons

10.
Instructive historical precedent: The 2021 abolition of the European VAT exemption serves as a direct analogue for the current customs disruption. PostNL's annual report for 2021 noted that the removal of the €22 VAT exemption caused inbound Asian parcel volumes to fall by 50% to 60%. This historical precedent indicates that regulatory tax changes consistently depress direct-toconsumer cross-border shipments.
11.
Sluggish domestic capture: Local e-commerce players are struggling to capture the customer base leaving Chinese cross-border applications. Retail association reports from Germany on October 12, 2026, indicated that overall market sales fell because local providers could not benefit from the Asian platforms' weakness. This indicates that the regulatory fee may contract the total addressable market rather than immediately boosting domestic operators.
12.
Severe merchant anxiety: Platform business managers anticipate prolonged market share erosion as the direct shipping framework loses its economic viability. An expert call with a Senior Business Development Manager at Temu on July 8, 2026, revealed expectations of near-term GMV declines and temporary market share losses in Poland. This insight confirms that the platform's leadership is deeply concerned about volume erosion during the transition to European warehousing.

Regulatory outlook and pricing dynamics

13.
Secondary administrative barrier: Chinese platforms face further transaction cost increases that will exacerbate the ongoing volume contraction. Morgan Stanley research on October 15, 2026, reported that the European Commission confirmed an additional €2 handling fee starting November 1, 2026. This surcharge will further inflate direct shipping costs and accelerate the operational pivot toward local stock.
14.
Significant margin pressure: Ultra-low-cost platforms are seeing their profitability erode as they absorb tariff costs to defend their market share. Financial reports cited in news articles on July 27, 2026, showed that Shein posted a quarterly loss of $99 million just ahead of its Hong Kong IPO. This financial strain indicates that the platform's high-growth model is becoming increasingly unsustainable under the new tariff rules.
15.
Price escalation effects: Direct import cost increases are being passed onto regional consumers, directly undermining the platforms' low-price positioning. Morgan Stanley research on October 15, 2026, showed that Shein responded to the €3 tariff by increasing prices by 20% across Europe. This pricing shift demonstrates that cost pressures are reducing the price gap between Chinese platforms and local competitors.
16.
Persistent transaction demand: Despite the administrative friction, a massive volume of low-value orders continues to cross European borders daily. News reports on October 30, 2026, highlighted that around 5 million packages still arrive in the EU daily from Chinese marketplaces. This ongoing volume proves that consumer appetite for cheap goods remains resilient even when facing administrative import delays.
17.
Other side of the story: While the customs fee has severely depressed direct-to-consumer imports, the long-term impact remains highly contested due to the rapid adaptation of Chinese supply chains. Morgan Stanley research on October 15, 2026, pointed out that Shein plans to import goods in bulk to bypass the per-item tariff and return to double-digit growth by late 2027. Thus, domestic platforms may only enjoy a temporary competitive respite before localized Chinese inventories restore their cost advantages.

Section 3 – Temu's EU Warehousing Pivot and Cost Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Chinese e-commerce platforms are accelerating their transition to local European warehousing to escape new customs fees.
  2. Bulk maritime imports combined with regional fulfillment hubs yield significant per-order cost savings compared to direct shipping.
  3. Allegro has successfully defended its forty percent share of Polish online retail through steady buyer engagement and strategic loyalty programs.
  4. Merchant monetization remains highly stable despite platform competition, although sellers face localized logistics cost pressures.

Cost economics of warehousing

1.
Substantial savings: Shein can achieve massive operational efficiencies by transitioning from direct air mail to localized bulk imports in Poland. Huatai Research reported on October 24, 2026, that bulk import fulfillment costs range from USD 6 to 7 per order, whereas direct-shipping costs are higher at USD 13 to 14. This structural shift is expected to protect long-term operating margins by mitigating the impact of new European tariffs on individual packages.
2.
Prohibitive charges: Direct cross-border delivery has become financially unsustainable for discount e-commerce platforms due to new European import surcharges. Morgan Stanley research on October 15, 2026, estimated that the combined burden of the July tariff and the November handling fee reaches 50% of the average product selling price for low-value parcels. To bypass these transactional penalties, platforms are forced to accelerate local warehousing investments to stabilize their European unit economics.
3.
Favorable comparisons: European marketplace operators maintain a substantial logistical advantage over cross- border entrants that are only beginning to localize. Amazon reported in a statement sent to La Repubblica on November 1, 2026, that 97% of its European orders are already dispatched from warehouses inside the European Union. This localized density allows the incumbent to guarantee fast delivery times while remaining entirely unaffected by the new import fees.
4.
Improved margins: Shifting fulfillment models can dramatically reduce the total tariff and logistics burden on low- value apparel orders. Huatai Research noted on October 24, 2026, that a bulk import model reduces the combined tariff and fulfillment cost ratio to 15.3% of average order value, compared to 24.17% for direct shipping. Shifting to a local fulfillment strategy could therefore facilitate long-term profitability recovery as the platform scales bulk import volumes.

While this aggressive infrastructure ramp depressed near-term retail margins, it ultimately secured an unassailable competitive advantage across the continent.

Supply chain and capital risks

12.
Operational hurdles: Shifting to a local fulfillment model introduces substantial capital risks and reduces supply chain flexibility for discount platforms. Jefferies research on September 14, 2026, pointed out that transitioning to bulk freight requires significant upfront inventory commitments. This requirement directly undermines the demand-led agility that previously defined the low-cost Chinese cross-border business model.

Escalating expenses: Moving fulfillment operations into European jurisdictions substantially increases the fixed cost base of Chinese e-commerce operators. An expert call with the Director of Delivery Solutions at ASOS on September 29, 2026, highlighted that local warehousing and European labor rates are far more expensive than Chinese alternatives. This structural inflation makes it highly uncertain whether ultra-low-margin platforms can sustain their price advantages over the long term.
14.
Other side of the story: While local warehousing increases the fixed cost base of Chinese e-commerce operators, the sheer manufacturing cost advantage of their Chinese supplier networks remains highly formidable. Morgan Stanley research on October 7, 2026, argued that these platforms do not need to move their actual production ecosystems out of China. By maintaining centralized, demand-led manufacturing while using local warehouses solely for distribution, they can preserve their baseline price leadership against domestic European retailers.

Critical tracking and transactional gaps

1.
Unreported throughput: Financial analysts lack verified regional data on the exact volume of goods moving through localized European fulfillment centers. While Temu has publicly stated plans to transition to local-to-local sales, actual localized warehouse volumes remain entirely undisclosed. Standard disclosures do not segment regional warehouse operations, leaving investors to rely on broad management projections. Regular warehouse auditing or direct platform reports are required to confirm localized supply trends and accurately project mid-term logistics margins.
2.
Integration bottlenecks: Assessing the velocity of merchant acquisition on cross-border platforms represents another critical information deficit. While platforms target rapid onboarding of European sellers, the rate of local merchant integration is slow due to brand trust and logistics friction. Understanding these merchant bottlenecks is essential to predict local growth because local-to-local sales remain highly dependent on domestic seller participation.
3.
Unrecorded cash: High cash-on-delivery preferences across Central and Eastern Europe obscure the true transactional volumes of cross-border discount platforms. Logistics experts observe that alternative data panels, which rely on digital card tracking, systematically underreport transactions in markets where cash remains dominant. Shifting patterns in cash transactions cannot be captured without granular merchant-level reports or localized delivery carrier statements. Consequently, tracking cash transactions is vital to evaluate local market share shifts since the customs fee took effect.
4.
Tracking discrepancies: Data intelligence panels show conflicting trends that limit investor confidence in e- commerce traffic estimates. Analysts note that digital traffic proxies can diverge significantly from actual platform conversion rates, making it difficult to assess buyer behavior. Furthermore, app download metrics remain under pressure globally, but these indicators fail to capture the purchasing behavior of existing, highly active platform users. This leaves a significant gap that hinder precise volume calculations for regional markets during the post-tariff period.

The central EU Customs Data Hub is designed to unify declarations and manage rising e-commerce volumes, but its implementation is phased through 2034. Until this platform becomes mandatory, investors must rely on fragmented national customs disclosures to track inbound package volumes. This means that precise cross-border parcel volumes will remain difficult to compile on a centralized European level.
