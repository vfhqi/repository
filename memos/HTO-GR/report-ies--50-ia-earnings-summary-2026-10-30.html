Hellenic Telecom (HTO-GR): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results leave the case where it was: they land on the case's own numbers, confirm the two things it expected of this quarter (the ICT taper arriving, and silence on the December price step), and press on it only at the edges, where retail fixed is softer than the case allowed and cost-out is stronger.

What changed

Hellenic Telecom reported Q3 2026 on 30 October 2026. Revenue was 882.7 EURm, +1.0% year-on-year, against a Bloomberg consensus of 870.6 EURm (a 1.4% beat), Morgan Stanley's 883 EURm (in line) and NBG Securities' 896.0 EURm (a miss); underlying growth excluding the international wholesale rundown, which ends by the end of 2026, was 5.0%. Adjusted EBITDA after leases was 371.4 EURm, +3.1%, against consensus of 368.7 EURm (+70 basis points), Morgan Stanley's 366 EURm and NBG's 370.2 EURm; the margin rose 90 basis points to 42.1% on personnel and marketing cost control that offset higher energy. The case's model had 371 EURm (+3.0%) for the quarter, so the case's number was hit to within 0.4 EURm. Adjusted net income rose 7.4% to 182.5 EURm; reported EPS of 0.4566 EUR, -38.4%, is entirely the 129.8 EURm one-off tax credit in Q3 2025. Free cash flow after leases was 137.4 EURm, +27.7%, on working capital and lower cash tax. Capex was 176.4 EURm, +17.8% in the quarter but about +1% over nine months. Net debt was 683.0 EURm at 30 September 2026, 0.5x adjusted EBITDA after leases on the company's basis; a 350.0 EURm bond was issued in September 2026 at 3.853%, fully subscribed by Deutsche Telekom, with 150.0 EURm of the maturing 500.0 EURm bond repaid from cash.

Guidance was reaffirmed, not tightened and not raised: FY2026 adjusted EBITDA after leases growth of about 3%, capex of about 600 EURm (the call said "slightly over" 600 EURm), reported free cash flow of about 750 EURm, underlying 575 EURm, total shareholder remuneration of 532 EURm. Nothing was said that commits the company to the CPI-linked tariff increase: NBG Securities records that the revised contracts permit annual CPI-linked adjustments, that management has not committed to executing them, and frames the question as early 2027. Management did say it expects positive operational growth trends to continue in 2027, unnumbered.

On the operating lines: System Solutions revenue was 105.9 EURm, +9.0%, from +52.1% in Q2 2026 and +45.0% over nine months, with management targeting stable ICT revenue and margin into 2027. Mobile service revenue was 296.0 EURm, +2.0%, on 48,744 postpaid net additions (postpaid base +7.5%) and 69,101 prepaid losses. The company's retail fixed services line including Data Com was 264.9 EURm, +2.9%; Morgan Stanley's reading of retail fixed service revenue is -0.6%, which it attributes to PPC's promotional activity. The retail broadband base was 2,377,994 lines, -0.4% year-on-year and -8,164 in the quarter; FTTH was 739,081 lines, +52,250 in the quarter after +62k in Q2, 31% of the base from 21% a year earlier, with active-line utilisation at 44%; FWA was 134,355 lines, +15,461; pay-TV was 802,443 subscribers, revenue +8.9%. Management said competitor pricing is pressuring fixed ARPU and declined to discuss PPC's tariffs; PPC's 500 Mbps plan is 17.90 EUR a month, a 55% discount to incumbent tariffs. Wholesale services were 106.6 EURm, -24.1%, not split between international transit and national wholesale. Headcount was 8,491 in late 2026 from 8,897 in late 2025; the indirect cost ratio is 28% of service revenue. The 900 MHz and 1800 MHz renewal consultations point to about 78 EURm, but management has modelled a 120 EURm spectrum outflow for its mid-term planning. The board proposed the CEO's re-election for a three-year term from 2027. The Vodafone-PPC venture is unchanged: heads of terms, with due diligence and Hellenic Competition Commission clearance pending.

The sell-side response: Morgan Stanley kept Overweight and a 23.80 EUR target, raised FY2026 EPS to 1.53 EUR from 1.51 EUR and trimmed FY2027 to 1.67 EUR from 1.68 EUR. NBG Securities reiterated Outperform. Deutsche Bank did not publish; its last stance was Buy with a 23 EUR target. The consensus target is 20.92 EUR; the closing price set against the targets after the release was 18.57 EUR, against the case's 19.03 EUR on 27 October. A beat the shares did not reward.

The challenge

Three readings the case does not hold, and one obvious one that fails.

First: the core is not flat; cost-out is doing more than the case's 8 EURm a year, and FY2027 may not need the price step to reach +2% to +3%. The case's spine is that FY2026's +3% rests on the December 2025 price step and the public ICT boom, and that without them core EBITDA falls 0.6% to 0.9% a year. Q3 is the first quarter in which the ICT support largely fell away (System Solutions added 8.7 EURm of revenue, which at the case's 10% to 20% incremental margin is about 1 to 2 EURm of EBITDA) and adjusted EBITDA after leases still grew by 11.3 EURm. Take off the case's own estimate of the price step (20 to 24 EURm a year, so 5 to 6 EURm a quarter) and about 4 to 5 EURm is left for cost and volume, against roughly nil on the case's own H1 bridge, where ICT and price were the whole of the 19 EURm. The margin rose 90 basis points with energy going the wrong way, headcount is down 406 in a year, management layers are down from 88 to 68, and the indirect cost ratio is at 28%. If that is structural, the FY2027 bridge gains about 15 to 20 EURm the case does not have, a "+2% to +3%" February guide becomes the central branch rather than the case's 30% one, and the February cut that the case's whole sequence waits for does not come. For this to be right: the Q3 saving has to survive Q4 and a deeper ICT taper; the voluntary-leave charges excluded from adjusted EBITDA after leases have to be no larger than usual (the company's Q3 tables carry the figure, the material we have does not, and the case counts 28.3 EURm excluded in H1 alone); and the 2027 energy hedge at 66% has to hold the energy line. The case's own answer is that the company lands guidance with 10 to 15 EURm of cost timing, and that one quarter cannot distinguish timing from structure. That is true, and it is also why this reading cannot be knocked down from a single quarter.

Second: PPC at retail is not "over"; it has moved from lines to price. The case closed the PPC retail question (about 20k customers, under 1% of OTE's lines) and agreed with the market that it was done. The quarter says the attack is being paid for in a different currency: retail fixed service revenue at -0.6% on Morgan Stanley's reading, with the broker attributing it to PPC's promotional intensity; a broadband base that lost 8,164 lines in the quarter and is 0.4% down on the year; management conceding that competitor pricing is pressuring fixed ARPU and declining to discuss the rival's 17.90 EUR tariff; and a defence built on in-home Wi-Fi upsell and TV bundling rather than on price. In its strongest form this reading says the demand-side element's "price increases held" is no longer true in fixed, that the December step is likely to be applied to mobile only (the case's upside of about 10 EURm from fixed inside the step goes, and the step's political exposure in a 5.0% inflation year rises), and that the retail moat is being spent to hold lines in the same period the wholesale moat starts to erode. For it to be right: the fixed ARPU decline has to persist into Q4 and Q1 2027 rather than lap a mix effect; PPC's own retail base has to be growing beyond the count the case used, and none of the post-results material gives a current PPC retail line count; and the gap between Morgan Stanley's -0.6% and the company's +2.9% for retail fixed including Data Com has to sit in the consumer line rather than in business connectivity. Against it stand Deutsche Bank's view that PPC has failed to dent the three incumbents, Morgan Stanley's that Greek pricing remains rational, and FTTH penetration rising from 21% to 31% of the base in a year, which is not what a base being priced away looks like. It is plausible, not probable, and it is live.

Third: the estimate reset is happening now, quietly, rather than in February, loudly, which changes the timing of the case rather than its content. The case's practical conclusion is that the stock is cheaper to buy after the February 2027 guide, when consensus FY2027 EPS of 1.70 EUR is cut 2% to 3%, and that the crisp setup re-forms then. Morgan Stanley has already moved FY2027 to 1.67 EUR, one cent above the case's 1.66 EUR, on a beat; the dashboard's consensus is 1.696 EUR; and the shares have fallen 7.2% over three months to 18.85 EUR, below the 50-day average of 19.53 EUR, a fall the case attributes to Greek flows rather than to the company. If the rest of the street drifts to 1.66 to 1.68 EUR before February, a "+1% to +2%" guide prints as in line, the February marker the case waits for never arrives, and the cheaper entry the case wants comes without the event that would tell it to act. For this to be right: at least one more house has to cut FY2027 before the FY2026 results, and the dashboard's revision line (0% over one and three months, -1% over six) has to turn negative at the next read. This reading presses on Setups' Fit/Crispness from the other side: the setup may be forming now rather than in three to six months.

The reading that fails is the obvious bull one: that a beat, a 90 basis point margin gain and 27.7% free cash flow growth start an upgrade cycle. The sell-side's own response is a 2 cent raise to FY2026, a 1 cent trim to FY2027, an unchanged 23.80 EUR target and no tightening of guidance. The case already holds that even its bull case produces a held consensus rather than upgrades, and the quarter has confirmed it.

What would settle it

  • The first reading is settled by the FY2026 results and the FY2027 guide in February 2027: whether the guide says "+2% to +3%" with ICT flat to down, and the full-year voluntary-leave charge excluded from adjusted EBITDA after leases. The Q3 charge sits in the company's interim tables; the second review may carry it, the material we have does not.
  • The second reading is settled by Q4's retail fixed service revenue and broadband net additions (February 2027), by whether the December step is applied to fixed contracts (December 2026, visible in Q1 2027 revenue), and by PPC's own retail line count, which is PPC's disclosure, not OTE's, and which OTE's calendar does not provide.
  • The third reading is the one the second review, four working days from now, is built to answer. It should carry the post-results broker estimates and targets as they have settled: whether any house beyond Morgan Stanley has moved FY2027 EPS below 1.70 EUR, whether Deutsche Bank has published and where its rating stands, and whether the consensus target has moved off 20.92 EUR. It cannot carry the December step, the February guide, the Hellenic Competition Commission's decision on the venture or Q4's fixed-line figures. The step is the open question that matters most for 2027 and the one nothing before December answers, because the company has not committed to it; a full memo update should wait for the second review and should not expect it to close that question.
