According to a Mitsubishi HC Capital presentation dated 8 October 2026, U.S. data center power demand capacity is projected to increase from 44.7 GW in 2025 to 94.7 GW by 2027. This massive capacity expansion is fueling an unprecedented demand for specialized, high-density optical cabling and physical infrastructure. Consequently, this structural demand tailwind is highly likely to drive robust long-term earnings per share growth for passive infrastructure manufacturers.

Black Box Network Services reported on 25 August 2026 that artificial intelligence workloads represent about 50% of total data center demand, which materially increases fiber connectivity and cooling needs. Severe power grid shortfalls and lengthy interconnection queues are beginning to limit new capacity additions in primary U.S. markets. Unless these power constraints are resolved through on-site generation, the rate of physical data center construction will slow, potentially impacting near-term component deliveries and dragging down supplier earnings.

High-bandwidth interconnection requirements: High-speed networking between clustered graphics processing units is transforming optical infrastructure design from copper to high-core-count fiber. Yangtze Optical Fibre and Cable reported on 29 September 2026 that global data center optical fiber demand is expected to grow 69% year-on-year in 2026, representing the principal industry driver. These advanced GPU architectures require next-generation multi-core and hollow-core optical fiber networks to solve critical AI latency and scalability challenges. This technological migration will likely preserve high average selling prices for advanced connectors, shielding manufacturers from legacy telecom pricing pressure.
