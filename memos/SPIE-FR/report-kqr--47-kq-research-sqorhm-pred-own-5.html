Section 2 – Allowed Equity Returns and the German Regulatory Decision

Section summary
  1. The German regulator's proposed RP5 returns represent a material step-up from the restrictive fourth regulatory period.
  2. Higher allowed equity returns of approximately 6.5% will unlock substantial transmission and distribution grid capital expenditure.
  3. Accelerated utility spending directly translates to expanding long-term high-voltage order backlogs for SPIE and its peers.

Regulatory Period Five Parameters

1.
Improving regulatory returns: The German Federal Network Agency is modernizing its framework for the fifth regulatory period to address historical under-investment in power transmission. Bernstein Research calculated on 21 October 2026 that the allowed base return on capital in RP5 is likely to offer a nominal post-tax equity return of approximately 6.6% for electricity networks. This estimated return represents a substantial improvement from the restrictive 4.13% baseline return that applied to existing assets in the fourth period. This regulatory shift will significantly expand the investment headroom for grid operators across Germany.
2.
Enhancing financial viability: Grid operators require higher allowed returns to maintain access to capital markets under competitive conditions. Elia Group reported on 10 September 2026 that a nominal cash return on equity of around 8% is necessary to support its massive investment program. The upcoming transition to a standardized WACC-based framework in Germany is expected to deliver this required return through added operational and debt-cost incentives. Consequently, this structural evolution will ensure that major transmission projects remain highly bankable for international investors.
3.
Shifting regulatory methodologies: The German regulator is implementing major changes in its upcoming RP5 framework to modernize cost of capital calculations. Jefferies Research explained on 3 August 2026 that the BNetzA is moving toward using the arithmetic average to determine the market risk premium instead of previous geometric averages. This technical adjustment is expected to lift the allowed post-tax return on equity to 6.5% for all assets. Such a step-change in base returns is crucial for incentivizing long-term private capital deployment into complex grid infrastructure.
4.
Analyzing gas read-across: The regulator's draft gas return decision sparked brief concern in the market regarding potential negative read-across for power network returns. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 14 August 2026 that the draft gas proposal of 4.9% allowed return on equity was disappointing because it captured historical periods of low interest rates. However, the firm emphasized that the regulator is highly likely to apply a higher equity beta for electricity networks. This means the final power regulatory settlement will remain insulated from gas-specific constraints.

Historical and Regional Comparisons

5.
Assessing historical constraints: The previous German regulatory framework was heavily criticized for failing to offer competitive investment returns compared to international standards. Eurogrid reported in its ESG report on 15 May 2024 that the allowed return on equity was slashed from 6.91% in the third regulatory period to only 5.07% pre-tax for the fourth regulatory period. This severe reduction forced operators to appeal the decision and led to subsequent variable adjustments for new investments. The upcoming RP5 period will correct these historical errors by establishing a single, higher baseline return.
6.
Comparing European regimes: German network returns have historically ranked at the lower end of major European jurisdictions. Bernstein Research noted on 19 June 2026 that Germany ranks middle of the pack in terms of allowed returns and regulatory frameworks compared to other global jurisdictions. While other countries offer highly attractive real return frameworks, Germany's nominal-equivalent ROE spread over sovereign yields has historically been less compelling. This regional yield gap explains why the German regulator faced intense industry pressure to reform its cost of capital methodology.
7.
Demanding higher returns: Network operators in other European countries have successfully secured more attractive regulatory parameters to fund their capital programs. Bernstein Research documented on 23 July 2025 that European countries like Austria and Italy consistently offer higher nominal allowed ROEs compared to Germany's fourth regulatory period. This regional disparity has created a competitive challenge for Germany as it seeks to attract international infrastructure capital. The upward revision in RP5 is therefore a necessary convergence to regional averages.
