Alignment of Incentives and Corporate Strategy

Executive incentive structures serve as a critical bridge between corporate strategy and shareholder returns, directly influencing how management prioritises organic growth versus margin expansion. The Hexatronic Group AB board of directors adopted the LTIP 2026 program at the Extraordinary General Meeting on 26 August 2026 to align executive rewards with long-term value creation. This program evaluates performance over a three-year vesting period based on four key metrics: diluted earnings per share (40%), net sales growth (25%), EBITA margin (25%), and sustainability criteria (10%). How these specific thresholds are met will determine executive stock rewards, which directly incentivises management to execute on its strategic pivot.

Establishing clear, realistic thresholds is particularly crucial given Hexatronic's historical volatility and its transition from a pure Fiber-to-the-Home play into a diversified passive fibre infrastructure provider. Redeye's research update on 23 October 2026 indicates that Hexatronic's Data Center and Harsh Environment segments are projected to constitute 50% of group sales by 2028. If the LTIP 2026 targets are set too low, they risk overrewarding management for cyclical market recoveries; conversely, overly aggressive targets could lead to talent attrition during integration periods. Therefore, the calibration of these thresholds relative to broker forecasts and internal financial targets represents a core focus for institutional investors.

LTIP 2026 Metric Calibration

Consequently, any future share allocation remains highly dependent on achieving robust earnings expansion.
2.
Accelerating top-line expansion: The second metric under LTIP 2026 rewards top-line growth to support the group's market expansion strategy. The board allocated a 25% performance weighting to net sales growth requirements during the three-year performance cycle. This metric ensures that management actively pursues organic and inorganic market share. Achieving this target is critical if the company intends to meet its long-term commercial ambitions.
3.
Underpinned margin requirements: The LTIP 2026 includes an EBITA margin criterion to ensure growth does not compromise operational profitability. The plan sets a 25% weighting on average EBITA margin targets ranging from 9.8% to 11.1% over 2026-2028. This range represents a transitional step toward the group's long-term operating profitability goals. Mandated sustainable standards: Hexatronic integrated sustainability directly into its executive compensation model to align with environmental commitments. As a result, the final vesting of performance shares requires the company to meet specific green milestones.

Comparison with Prior Incentive Plans

5.
Identical metric weightings: The metric weights of LTIP 2026 mirror those established in the previous LTIP 2025 program. The LTIP 2025 program allocated 40% to diluted earnings per share targets, 25% to sales growth, 25% to EBITA, and 10% to sustainability. However, the prior plan evaluated performance annually rather than over a three-year average. This structural difference means LTIP 2026 reduces the impact of singleyear market volatility on vesting outcomes.
6.
Variable annual schedules: The previous LTIP 2025 program implemented a complex, tiered vesting schedule across different financial years. For the 2025 financial year, minimum and maximum vesting ranges were set at 10% and 60% of potential allotment. For both 2026 and 2027, the minimum and maximum ranges were lowered to 10% and 30%, respectively. These restrictive annual caps highlight the board's cautious approach during a period of subdued market demand.
7.
Historical program differences: The LTIP 2024 program operated under a similar share-savings framework but with different participation caps. The EGM on 7 May 2024 authorized up to 1,343,596 series C shares to support the LTIP 2024 program and cover associated social costs. Alongside the share-savings program, the board utilized a directed issue of 442,500 subscription warrants. This dual-track incentive strategy has been streamlined in LTIP 2026 to focus primarily on performance shares.
8.
Streamlined allocation rights: Hexatronic altered the maximum performance share allocation per participant in the latest plan. In prior programs like LTIP 2025, participants could receive two, four, or six performance shares per savings share depending on their specific corporate role. Under LTIP 2026, all participants are entitled to obtain up to six performance shares regardless of their seniority. This shift significantly increases the potential upside for mid-level key employees.

Rationale for Incentive Rebasing

1.
Rebased performance schedule: The board of directors proposed measuring performance under LTIP 2026 over a three-year average rather than relying solely on the final fiscal year. According to the company's EGM notice published on 21 July 2026, the performance criteria will be evaluated across the financial years 2026, 2027, and 2028. This structural adjustment reduces the risk of short-term volatility skewing executive payouts because it smooths out annual operational fluctuations. Investors generally support this change as it ensures that the final share awards reflect sustained long-term corporate performance rather than a single year's cyclical peak.
2.
Withdrawn annual targets: The previous incentive proposal put forward in the May 2026 AGM notice measured performance solely on the results achieved in the single year 2028. However, the board decided to withdraw that specific proposal at the Annual General Meeting to refine the metrics. This strategic pause allowed the board to consult with major institutional shareholders and redesign the performance framework to better suit the changing market. The resulting rebased plan was subsequently presented in the updated EGM notice on 21 July 2026.
3.
Shareholder consultation process: The board justified the transition to a three-year average by citing intensive dialogues with Hexatronic's primary institutional investors. As disclosed in the board's official proposal on 21 July 2026, the revised structure was directly shaped by consultation with larger shareholders who requested a more stable governance model. This proactive engagement helped the board resolve outstanding concerns before submitting the final proposal. Consequently, the board expects that this cooperative approach will enhance long-term organizational loyalty and align executive interests with shareholders.

Major Institutional Shareholder Dynamics

4.
Nomination committee composition: The governance structure of Hexatronic is strongly influenced by its largest voting blocks, which are represented directly on the Nomination Committee. As announced in the press release on 16 September 2026, the committee includes representatives from Handelsbanken Fonder, AP3, and Accendo Capital. These three major institutions combined control a significant portion of the company's outstanding votes. Because these holders have a direct say in board appointments, they possess the necessary leverage to enforce strict governance standards on executive compensation.

This substantial position means Accendo Capital has a vested interest in aligning executive payouts with share price recovery. By participating actively in the governance dialogues, Accendo Capital supports the implementation of metrics that drive long-term earnings growth.

Handelsbanken active oversight: Handelsbanken Fonder plays a leading role in Hexatronic's governance through its position as the largest institutional shareholder. Redeye's shareholder analysis on 23 October 2026 shows that Handelsbanken Fonder holds a 5.81% stake in the company. Staffan Ringvall of Handelsbanken Fonder chairs the Nomination Committee, putting him in a key position to guide remuneration discussions. Board leadership co-option: Chairman of the Board Magnus Nicolin is co-opted to the Nomination Committee to ensure seamless communication between the board and major owners. Other side of the story: Although some governance experts argue that a three-year average performance period dilutes the urgency of achieving the company's 2028 targets, this structure actually promotes sustained strategic execution.
