Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Original equipment manufacturers are rapidly shifting to direct-sales models across Europe to capture higher average selling prices.
  2. Direct-to-patient and digital care channels are accelerating in key regions like Germany and Benelux.
  3. Regulatory pressures from the In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation are forcing OEMs to assume closer control of local distribution.
  4. Confidence in the rapid OEM direct-sales transition is high, supported by multi-country timelines from Tandem and Sysmex.
1.
Investigating strategic risk: "Asker Healthcare Group AB (ASKER-SE, Nasdaq Stockholm), a decentralised European distributor of medical supplies, devices and equipment (not pharmaceuticals) to hospitals, primary care, elderly care and homecare, with more than 50 companies in 19 countries in three business areas, North (Sweden, Norway, Finland, the Baltics), West (Benelux, United Kingdom, Ireland, Denmark) and Central (Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France, Poland, Czech Republic, Slovakia), reported product segments Medical Supplies, Devices and Equipment, and Services, growing through organic sales and 10 to 20 bolt-on acquisitions a year. For Asker Healthcare Group AB (ASKER-SE), which original equipment manufacturers in the device and equipment categories Asker distributes in the Nordics, Benelux, Ireland and Germany (endoscopy, surgical instruments and equipment, diabetes devices, wound care, continence care, orthopaedics, laboratory equipment) have announced or implemented direct-sales or direct-to-patient models in Europe in 2024 to 2026, which distributor contracts were terminated as a result (including AddLife's UK endoscopy loss in 2025), what local sales volume each terminated line carried, and which of Asker's larger distributed lines, by manufacturer and country, are above the scale at which an expert formerly at Asker said manufacturers typically reclaim distribution (SEK 20m to 30m of local annual sales)?"

Scenarios for Direct-Sales Transitions

1.
Accelerating transitions: DNB Carnegie notes on 10 September 2026 that Asker's reported earnings quality is already under pressure because real EBITA margins potentially fell to 7.3% if capitalized items are treated as opex. If manufacturers reclaim sales faster than expected, this localized margin erosion will worsen significantly across all territories. Consequently, an accelerated go-direct wave would undermine consolidated group profitability unless the pace of acquisitions accelerates to compensate.
2.
Procurement barriers: Deutsche Bank's report from 7 April 2015 highlights that GPOs pool volumes to negotiate lower prices, which embeds them deeply into hospital supply chains. This structural integration makes it extremely difficult for individual manufacturers to bypass distributors and sell directly. Unless an OEM offers a complete product platform, GPO price agreements protect traditional distributors from disintermediation.
3.
One-stop preferences: Morgan Stanley reports on 13 October 2026 that modern hospitals prioritize vendor rationalization and platform compatibility over isolated devices. This procurement behavior strongly favors full-service distributors who can bundle hardware with consumables and software solutions. Consequently, single-category manufacturers struggle to establish direct-to-hospital channels without substantial distributor partnership support.
4.
