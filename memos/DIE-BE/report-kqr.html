Glass network cost structures

1.
Highly variable cost structure: Belron maintains a business model where the majority of operating costs are variable rather than fixed. Fitch's rating report of 7 December 2023 notes that this flexible structure is supported by low capital intensity of around 1.5% of revenues. This flexibility allows the company to protect its cash flows and maintain stable margins during sudden volume downturns.
2.
Granular job cost dynamics: Standard vehicle glass jobs are dominated by material expenses rather than direct technician labor. An expert interview with a former corporate counsel at Liberty Mutual on 12 April 2026 revealed that glass materials constitute 50% of job costs, while labor accounts for only 10% to 15%. This low labor portion means that wage inflation has a relatively muted impact on overall job profitability.
3.
Direct labor expense flexibility: Belron relies on its ability to manage technician staffing levels as a key lever to balance service capacity with demand. D'Ieteren's management stated during the 9 September 2026 earnings call that direct labor costs typically represent between 15% and 20% of sales. This operational flexibility allows Belron to scale its workforce down during soft volume periods, protecting the group's proportionate earnings.
4.
Franchise cost advantages: Driven Brands utilizes a low fixed-cost franchise model for its paint, collision, and glass networks to drive capital-efficient growth. Driven Brands' annual report of 1 March 2023 highlights that this simple operating model requires minimal labor and carries low fixed costs. This asset-light structure helps insulate parent margins from local volume fluctuations, which supports stable corporate earnings.

44 10K European glass peer gross margins: Cary Group demonstrates similar cost dynamics in its European vehicle glass network.
