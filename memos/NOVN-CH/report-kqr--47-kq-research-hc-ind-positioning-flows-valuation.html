Section summary
  1. European healthcare experienced extreme out-of-favour sentiment before mid-2026, but defensive rotation has triggered a significant positioning reversal by November 2026.
1.
The Key Question: "How out of favour was European healthcare before mid-2026, and how much of that has reversed by November 2026?"

Sector Scope and Anchor

2.
Broad integration: The European healthcare sector encompasses pharmaceuticals, medical devices, contract research, and digital distribution networks. Under this broad footprint, recent recovering investments in pharmaceuticals, biotechnology, and related supply chains like contract research organizations support long-term growth. Understanding these subsegments is critical because style rotation affects diverse segments differently, meaning medtech and tools face distinct operational headwinds. Therefore, analyzing the entire value chain reveals how capital flows across the broader sector.
3.
Stable performance: Swiss multinational Novartis AG serves as an anchor peer within European pharmaceuticals, showing high corporate resilience despite volatile market dynamics. Broker research monitors weekly retail prescription trackers for Novartis AG to gauge underlying volume trends and project forward earnings. This commercial visibility helps establish a baseline for large-cap pharma, highlighting how defensive anchors preserve earnings stability during broader macroeconomic downturns. Consequently, Novartis AG provides a benchmark against which high-growth medtech and life science tools can be evaluated.
4.
Significant revisions: Reversing sector sentiment directly impacts corporate earnings per share expectations as valuations begin to match positive fundamental revisions. For instance, contract development organizations like Lonza Group expect rapid biological pipeline expansion to support double-digit earnings growth through 2030. This positioning shift is crucial because lower discount rates unlock valuation elasticity for healthcare companies. Thus, tracking positioning reversals helps investors identify where earnings growth and multiple expansion are most likely to align.

Related Risks and Related Questions

5.
Elevated risks: Geopolitical policy shifts and upcoming trade tariffs represent significant downside risks that could disrupt supply chains and compress operating margins. Medical technology suppliers like Stryker Corporation face severe margin headwinds if planned medical-device tariffs are implemented near-term. These policies are critical because they inject uncertainty into hospital capital expenditure planning. Unless regulatory risks abate, earnings per share growth across medtech and specialty pharma subsectors could fall short of consensus targets.
6.
Vulnerable spending: Closely related questions focus on whether global research and development budgets will contract further, directly impacting outsourcing providers. Analytical instrument manufacturers and biotechnology firms note that global financing constraints continue to temper order velocity. This issue matters because any prolonged contraction in funding delays order recoveries for tools and manufacturing services. Therefore, the pace of global rate relief remains a key determinant of sector earnings velocity.
7.
Extreme rotation: Extreme style rotation out of technology has catalyzed a defensive migration toward under- owned sectors like healthcare and consumer staples. While European equities benefit from this defensive rotation, global equity investors are actively weighing whether these inflows represent a permanent reallocation. This trend is highly relevant because undemanding valuations contrast with strong earnings revisions. Consequently, the speed of this transition will determine how quickly historical multiple discounts are fully unwound.
8.

German hospital operators and private care clinics face margin compression if public payer settlements fail to compensate for wage inflation. This friction is critical because pricing caps directly limit operating leverage and cash flows. Thus, the sustainability of the sector recovery depends heavily on favorable local policy adjustments.

Section 2 – European Healthcare Sector Performance and Valuation

Section summary
  1. European healthcare relative performance and valuations reached historic lows relative to the broader market by mid-2026 before initiating a structural defensive-led recovery.

Sector Performance Against the Stoxx 600

1.
Declining returns: The European healthcare sector experienced prolonged underperformance relative to the Stoxx Europe 600 index over the five-year period leading into mid-2026. Absolute return indices compiled by analysts show that while the broader Euro STOXX 600 appreciated significantly, European healthcare stocks lagged behind, creating a wide performance gap. This structural underperformance was driven by post-pandemic destocking, funding freezes in life science tools, and a market-wide style rotation favoring cyclical sectors over defensive ones. Consequently, the sector entered the middle of 2026 trading at deeply discounted levels compared to its historical trajectory. Severe lag: Performance data over the three-year period leading to June 2026 highlights the extreme out-of-favour status of European medical technology and biopharma stocks. Charts comparing EU Medtech and EU Pharma against the Euro STOXX 600 show that both subsectors consistently underperformed the broader index. According to historical performance trackers, this multi-year lag intensified in the 12 months preceding mid-2026, leaving active managers heavily underweight. As a result, the relative return of healthcare against the wider European market fell to multi-year lows by June 2026.

Historical Multiple Ranges and Lows

2.
Extreme compression: Valuation multiples for European healthcare compressed dramatically, with price-to- earnings (P/E) ratios relative to the broader market dropping to historic lows. A 20-year absolute P/E historical analysis spanning from 2006 to mid-2026 demonstrates that European Medtech and Pharma multiples fell below their long-term averages. Specifically, NTM P/E multiples for the sector compressed to levels generally lower than their three-year and seven-year historical averages. This multiple contraction occurred despite the fact that consensus expectations for three-year compound annual EPS growth did not experience a corresponding decline. Undemanding valuations: The sector's relative P/E discount versus the broader market reached the bottom of its historical 10-year and 20-year ranges. Analysts noted that the lack of valuation dispersion across heterogeneous healthcare subsectors reflected indiscriminate selling by institutional investors. By June 2026, the absolute P/E of European Medtech had converged near the Euro STOXX 600 index multiple, representing a substantial discount compared to its typical premium. Contrary evidence: Some individual medical technology companies experienced multiple deratings that were completely detached from their underlying financial improvements. This derating occurred despite a 100 basis point increase in its long-term organic growth expectations, which was the only material increase recorded across the entire European medical technology sector. Therefore, the sector-wide valuation compression over-penalized high-quality companies with positive fundamental revisions.

Reversal Status by November 2026

4.
Defensive rotation: Investor sentiment and capital flows began to reverse after mid-2026, driven by a growing preference for stable defensive industries. Market trackers indicated that the MSCI World Health Care Index rose by 7% to 11% following October 1, 2026, highlighting a clear rotation back into defensive equity classes. This supportive macro environment provided spill-over benefits to broader European healthcare listings, helping the sector reclaim some of its lost ground against cyclical indices. However, the scale of the recovery varied widely between large-cap pharmaceutical anchors and high-multiple medical technology companies.
5.
Valuation progress: By November 2026, European healthcare multiples had recovered from their absolute lows but remained at reasonable historical levels. According to an equity strategy analysis, the 12-month forward P/E multiple for European healthcare stood at the 44th percentile of its 10-year history. This multiple sits in sharp contrast to highly expensive defensive sectors like European Insurance, which traded at the 100th percentile of its 10-year range. Consequently, while a portion of the valuation discount has reversed, healthcare still trades at a discount to its historical median. Incomplete recovery: Valuation data for the broader European equity market indicates that healthcare has not yet fully returned to its long-term average multiples. In November 2026, the average P/E for covered European equities was reported at 12.7x. This market-wide multiple is lower than its five-year industry average of 13.8x and its 10-year historical average of 15.6x. Because the broader market continues to trade below long-term historic averages, the relative valuation recovery of healthcare has been partially masked by depressed marketwide multiples.

The 2016 to 2017 Post-Drug Pricing Scare Recovery

1.
Slow stabilization: The global healthcare sector entered 2016 under intense valuation pressure due to US political campaign rhetoric regarding drug pricing reforms. This political overhang triggered a sharp de-rating, causing the MSCI Healthcare index to decline by 8% in 2016 after seven consecutive years of positive absolute returns. However, the resolution of the US presidential election in late 2016 removed the immediate threat of disruptive legislative price controls, setting the stage for a strong valuation rebound. This shift allowed sector earnings to compound against a more stable regulatory backdrop, driving relative multiple expansion as defensive investors returned to the space. Bumpy valuation expansion: The subsequent recovery in 2017 lasted approximately 12 months, characterized by a steady but uneven expansion in relative valuations across pharma and medtech. In Europe, the pharmaceutical sector's price-to-earnings premium relative to the broader European market compressed to just 3% by early 2018, reflecting the persistent structural challenges that lingered even after the initial pricing scare subsided. Medtech outperformed pharmaceuticals significantly during this period, with European medtech rising by 28% in 2017 compared to a 16% gain for European pharmaceuticals and biotechnology. This divergence demonstrated that while defensive assets rebounded, capital favored subsectors with higher insulation from direct government reimbursement interventions. Macroeconomic terminal triggers: The upward trend of the 2017 recovery was ultimately brought to an end by a powerful global macroeconomic synchronized expansion. By early 2018, global GDP growth accelerated to a peak rate of 3.8% in 2017, driving a robust style rotation out of defensive sectors and into highly cyclical industries. 171, the enactment of the US Tax Cuts and Jobs Act in late 2017 catalyzed a massive rotation into financials, small-caps, and deep cyclicals, which stripped liquidity away from defensive healthcare allocations. Consequently, despite solid organic earnings growth, healthcare relative multiples faced a hard ceiling as institutional capital chased higher-beta cyclical alternatives.

The 2018 to 2019 Cycle

2.
Normalization and deceleration: The healthcare sector recovery in 2018 emerged as a classic defensive play against rising global trade tensions and tightening monetary policies. Aggregate healthcare earnings delivered strong absolute performance in 2018, but faced a high base effect that led to a projected deceleration in year-over-year earnings growth heading into 2019. 175 This dynamic forced active managers to focus heavily on earnings visibility and balance sheet quality. As a result, the sector's relative performance became highly stock-specific, with defensive hospital operators and specialty pharma leading the initial leg of the re-rating.
3.
Steady multiple expansion: The 2018 to 2019 recovery cycle lasted nearly 18 months, supported by a highly supportive monetary backdrop as central banks paused their rate hike cycles. The Nordic healthcare sector, for example, maintained strong upward momentum throughout 2019, driven by an attractive combination of structural growth and stable forward earnings estimates. By late 2019, the healthcare sector's forward price-to-earnings multiple traded at a modest premium to its 10-year historical median, though this premium remained lower than those of other expensive defensive sectors like utilities. This disciplined multiple expansion reflected a mature recovery where valuations were backed by visible cash flows. Style rotation termination: The recovery cycle of 2019 was interrupted by a sharp style pivot toward growth and a cyclical "mini-cycle" recovery in late 2019. Easing trade tensions and global monetary easing in mid-2019 fueled expectations of an economic acceleration, prompting a strong rotation from defensive assets into value and cyclical sectors. This shift compressed the valuation multiples of high-quality defensive stocks relative to the broader market as investors positioned for cyclical upside. The cycle was subsequently completely disrupted by the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in early 2020, which entirely restructured global healthcare demand and investment priorities.

The 2022 and 2024 Recoveries

4.
Staffing bottlenecks and pauses: The recovery of 2022 was characterized by a defensive flight to safety as global markets grappled with surging inflation and aggressive central bank interest rate hikes. Healthcare was one of the few global equity sectors to avoid deep negative returns in 2022, outperforming the broader market due to its defensive appeal and inelastic demand. 186, the recovery was structurally capped by severe hospital staffing constraints and high wage inflation, which limited volume growth and compressed operating margins. This bottleneck prevented a full valuation re-rating, as active managers remained skeptical of the sector's nearterm operating leverage.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some major healthcare subsectors experienced severe, multi-year funding and inventory destocking headwinds that completely counteracted the defensive market rotation in 2024. While diversified biopharma benefited from stable demand, the life science tools and diagnostics segment suffered a sharp contraction in capital allocations, with global biotechnology funding falling 25% year-over-year.
