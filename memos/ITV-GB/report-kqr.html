Section 2 – Cross-Border Broadcaster Consolidation and Corporate Action

Section summary
  1. European broadcaster consolidation is accelerating as players pursue cross-border scale and portfolio restructuring to compete against global streaming platforms.

Strategic Rationale and Scale

1.
Defensive consolidation: Broadcasters are seeking scale because traditional linear advertising continues to face structural declines while global streaming competition intensifies, making linear television advertising highly challenging.
2.
Synergy capture: Broadcasters target cost and revenue synergies, such as RTL Group aiming to deliver €250 million annual synergies from its June 2026 Sky Deutschland acquisition.
3.
Operational focus: Separating broadcasting and studio segments allows for pure-play valuations, with ITV positioning its Studios division as a standalone global content creator.
4.
Resource pooling: Combining cross-border ad-tech and digital platforms, such as MFE's planned unified front- end, aims to improve monetization of digital inventory.
5.
Content efficiency: Scale enables broadcasters to amortize high programming and sports rights costs across larger geographic footprints, which could stabilize long-term operating margins.

Section 3 – Streaming Monetization, Ad Tiers, and Content Demand

Section summary
  1. Streaming ad-supported monetization and premium local-content demand are expanding across Europe as commercial broadcasters offset traditional linear advertising declines with digital programmatic inventory and structured pre-financed production models.

Scaling Ad-Supported Digital Monetization

1.
Accelerating digital growth: Streaming advertising tiers are driving robust top-line gains, with RTL Group achieving 27.2% streaming revenue growth to €299 million in H1 2026 compared to €235 million in H1 2025.
2.
Surging platform engagement: Broadcasters are rapidly scaling their ad-supported reach, as illustrated by ProSiebenSat.1's Joyn expanding monthly video users by 15% to 9.5 million in Q1 2026 compared to 8.3 million in Q1 2025.
3.
Robust digital offset: Programmatic ad-supported architectures are successfully defending market share, evidenced by ITVX digital advertising revenues increasing 14% to £130 million in Q1 2026 compared to £114 million in Q1 2025.
4.
Enhancing ad load: Targeted video ad deployment is driving rapid monetisation gains, with TF1+ recording an impressive 37% revenue expansion in Q1 2025 compared to Q1 2024.
5.
Stabilizing digital margins: Streamlined delivery is improving operating margins, with ITVX achieving digital breakeven ahead of target timeline compared to original multi-year loss projections.

Evolving Content Production Dynamics

6.
Selective program commissioning: Global subscription platforms are curbing expensive original orders, leading to a deceleration in global TV production market growth to just 1% in 2025 compared to historical mid-single digit growth.
7.
De-risked operating models: European production studios generate highly predictable margins because their unscripted and scripted projects are predominantly pre-financed by broadcasters and platforms under lower-risk cost-plus structures.
8.
IP catalog accumulation: Scale is driving substantial consolidation among content creators, exemplified by Banijay completed partnership with All3Media in July 2026 to create a powerhouse with €4.4 billion pro forma revenues.
9.
Resilient contract pricing: Producers rely on robust cost-plus pricing agreements to protect margins, with unscripted giant Banijay relying on cost-plus pricing models to hedge against inflation across its 226,000-hour library.
10.
Phased delivery schedules: Content production revenues exhibit significant seasonal volatility, with major deliveries for ITV Studios heavily weighted toward Q4 2026 schedules compared to the first half of the year.

Non-Consensus and Edge Drivers

11.
Tightening localization compliance: The European Accessibility Act enforcement of June 2025 is triggering surging demand for professional subtitles and dubbing across non-English speaking markets.
12.
Deploying production automation: Content creators are utilizing generative artificial intelligence to achieve cost efficiencies, with Fremantle launching its dedicated Imaginae Studios in 2025 to optimize post-production workflows.
13.
Imposing cultural spend: French broadcasters face strict regulatory mandates requiring them to spend 12.5% of revenues on commissioning original local and European works.
14.
Other side of the story: Streamers' selective commissioning and a 24% reduction in global scripted orders in 2025 threaten high-margin European studios which risk closure without regulatory protections.
