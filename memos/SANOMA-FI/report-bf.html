Outliers worth noting

1.
Negative working capital extreme: Sanoma operates with a highly negative net working capital position of minus 17.1% of sales (minus 51 days), representing an exceptional cash collection profile far beyond typical European publishing peers. Peer Pearson operates with positive 45 NWC days due to its higher exposure to corporate and US higher education courseware distribution, while Alma Media maintains minus 20 days. This structural outlier stems from advance subscription payments in both Learning and Media Finland, creating a substantial pre-collection cash buffer that mechanically funds operations before materials are delivered. The extreme position places Sanoma in the top 5% of European media and education companies for working capital efficiency. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 8]
2.
Recurring restructuring intensity: Sanoma has recorded EUR 106.3 million in items affecting comparability in 2025, up from EUR 61.5 million in 2024, representing an unusually high and persistent restructuring burden relative to sector norms. The 2025 charges included a EUR 48 million impairment from exiting Dutch distribution tenders and EUR 32 million in combined Solar program and Tampere print centralization restructuring charges. While peer consolidators typically absorb one-time integration costs during active M&A phases, Sanoma's multi-year pattern of material restructuring charges (spanning Program Solar, print facility closures, and portfolio exits) suggests structural operational churn rather than episodic transformation. This places the company in the bottom 5% of stability among European education publishers. [Cross-ref: Section 2]
3.
EBITDA margin ceiling trajectory: Sanoma Learning is targeting an adjusted operating profit margin clearly above 23% in 2026, positioning it for substantial upward margin trajectory relative to historical performance. However, this target remains materially below global peer benchmarks, with McGraw Hill operating at a high 35.4% adjusted EBITDA margin and Pearson delivering a 22% EBITDA margin across its consolidated portfolio. Sanoma's margin ceiling appears constrained by its smaller scale (EUR 1.3 billion revenue versus multi-billion peer platforms) and continued exposure to lower-margin Media Finland operations. The company operates in the middle quintile of education publisher profitability despite shedding low-margin distribution contracts, suggesting operational or structural constraints limiting margin expansion toward best-in-class levels.
