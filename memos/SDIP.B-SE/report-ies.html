Sdiptech (SDIP.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results strengthen the thesis and weaken the entry: the two Key Drivers the case most needed to see delivered well above our modal path, while the margin, the cash and the price each moved the wrong way by enough to press on the case without breaking any of its stated levels, except one alarm.

What changed

Sdiptech reported its third quarter (July to September 2026) on 23 October 2026.

  • Net sales SEK 1,309m, +4% reported on SEK 1,253m, 7.5% above the Modular Finance consensus of SEK 1,218m. Organic growth in the comparable core was 13% excluding currency (currency +1%), against DNB Carnegie's 6% forecast, the fifth consecutive quarter of rising organic growth. The case's modal assumption is 5% a year, and the case's own concession was that the run-rate is "nearer 6% than 11%".
  • Adjusted EBITA SEK 261m, +8% on SEK 242m, 3% above consensus of SEK 254m. Organic adjusted EBITA growth was 6%. The adjusted EBITA margin was 20.0%, below consensus of 20.9%; the review carries two prior-year comparatives, 19.3% on the reported group basis and 21.3% on the core basis of the earnings presentation. The case's margin leg is +0.13 points a year from a 21.2% LTM core margin.
  • By segment: Supply Chain & Transportation sales +27% to SEK 659m at a margin of 18.5% from 21.0% (DNB Carnegie expected 20.1%), on unhedged raw-material costs on long-lead rail projects at Mecno and low-margin truck-chassis pass-through at Hilltip; management says the pressure continues through Q4 2026 and is adding indexation clauses. Energy & Electrification sales +17% to SEK 293m at 24.2% from 25.8%, in line. Water & Bioeconomy sales +6% to SEK 241m, adjusted EBITA +2% to SEK 49m at 20.3% (20.1% on the interim report's basis), with management conceding that sustained profit growth there is "not yet fully secured"; a new segment head took office on 1 October 2026. Safety & Security adjusted EBITA +31% to SEK 42m at 36.2% from 30.2%, which the chief executive called unsustainable.
  • Operating cash flow SEK 143m from SEK 256m; cash conversion 62% in the quarter (94% a year ago) on inventory and receivables built to support sales, 81% LTM against a 70% to 90% target. The case's LTM figure was 90%.
  • Net debt to adjusted EBITDA 2.90x (2.95x on the call), up from 2.80x at Q2 2026 and down from 3.39x a year ago, after funding two acquisitions. Group ROCE 13.6%, from 13.2% at Q2 2026 and 12.0% a year ago.
  • Two acquisitions closed: A.U.T. Wheels and Castors (GBP 8.5m, August) and Vale Engineering (GBP 10.0m, September), which DNB Carnegie puts at SEK 40m of run-rate profit against the SEK 100m annual target. The multiples paid are not stated.
  • SEK 550m of sustainability-linked bonds redeemed against SEK 400m of senior secured bonds issued, with a SEK 13m one-off charge; net financial items SEK -79m against DNB Carnegie's SEK -70m.
  • Sell-side: DNB Carnegie reiterated Buy, trimmed its target to SEK 340 from SEK 342 on peer multiples, cut 2026e adjusted EPS by 1%, left 2027e unchanged at SEK 19.5, raised 2028e by 1% and raised sales forecasts by 2% across 2026e to 2028e; its M&A-scenario fair value is SEK 366. It models leverage at 3.0x for 2026e, falling to 2.0x in 2027e. Redeye had not published. The dashboard on 29 October shows consensus EPS revisions of +2%, +7% and +8% over one, three and six months, a consensus target of about SEK 313 and a buy share of 100%.
  • Price: SEK 254.8 on 28 October 2026, against SEK 247.8 at the memo; P/E 13.5x at the 52nd percentile of ten years, against the memo's 13.5x and 53rd. The multiple has not moved; the price rise is earnings.

The challenge

Three readings of this quarter are stronger than the case's own, and two of them pull in opposite directions.

First: the growth is dilutive by construction, and the case's margin leg has the sign wrong. The case assumed mix would add margin because Safety & Security (30%) and Energy & Electrification (25%) outgrow Supply Chain & Transportation (19.5% in the case's arithmetic). This quarter the fastest-growing segment was the largest and lowest-margin one: Supply Chain grew sales 27% at 18.5%, a point below the case's own figure for it, while the two thematic segments grew sales 17% and 9%. Add two mechanisms the case rated as C-grade weaknesses and now sees operating together in the largest segment: a pricing lag on long-lead fixed-price work (Mecno), and pass-through revenue that carries no profit (Hilltip's chassis). On this reading 13% organic sales converting to 6% organic EBITA is not a one-quarter anomaly; it is what this portfolio's growth looks like whenever transport leads. The case's +0.13 points a year becomes a minus, the EBITA path runs at or below the case's 11.7% a year while sales run far above it, and the "revenue-led" label the framework favours is doing less work than it appears. For this to be right, Supply Chain must keep outgrowing the group through 2027 with its margin held at 18% to 19% after the indexation clauses are in, and the Safety margin must fall back as the chief executive says it will. Against it: management calls Mecno temporary and dates the recovery to early 2027, DNB Carnegie agrees, and group ROCE still rose 0.4 points in the quarter. We cannot knock this reading down on the evidence, because the one number that would, the Supply Chain margin after indexation, is not yet printed.

Second: organic growth and the acquisition engine are competing for the same balance sheet, and the case treats them as allies. The case's cleared branch takes organic delivery, M&A pace and the return on capital as "driven by the same core delivery and not independent", meaning positively coupled. This quarter shows the negative coupling: 13% growth consumed working capital, operating cash flow fell 44%, conversion dropped to 62%, and leverage rose to 2.90x to 2.95x while two deals were funded. The ceiling is 3.0x; SEK 205m of earn-outs were due in the rest of 2026 on the sell-side's July count; DNB Carnegie's own model has year-end 2026 leverage at 3.0x, which is the case's alarm level. On this reading the faster the core grows, the less room the engine has, and SEK 100m a year of acquired EBITA cannot run alongside double-digit organic growth inside the ceiling without the working-capital release management now promises (a goal of about 20% of sales against the case's 24.7%). This is a reading of the case's "Balance-sheet headroom: finite" driver that the case never drew: the headroom is consumed by success, not only by failure. For it to be right, Q4 2026 must not deliver the seasonal release (Q4 2025 was 134%), year-end leverage must print at or above 3.0x, and the deal count must slow in the first half of 2027. Against it: the LTM figure is 81%, inside the corridor; the build is in inventory and receivables, the kind that normally reverses; and the earn-out write-back mechanism the case names as its strongest bear argument is not visible in this quarter at all, since profit rose on sales, not on revaluations. The false friend is neither confirmed nor refuted here; the headroom squeeze is.

Third, and against the first two: the case is too conservative on growth, and its entry discipline is what would cost it the position. The case took 5% organic, the bottom of the company's band, discounted Q2's 11% as backlog, and set the entry at SEK 230. Two quarters later the company has printed 11% and 13% against comparables the sell-side called tougher, peers Lifco and MedCap printed 1.6% and 3.5%, the engine added SEK 40m of run-rate profit in one quarter, ROCE rose to 13.6% on the way to the case's 14% condition, and the refinancing confirmed the below-EBITA leg: DNB Carnegie's 2027e EPS of SEK 19.5 stands 18% above the SEK 16.5 the cleared branch requires. The case's entry at SEK 230 is 10% below the price while the thesis has been confirmed on its two Key Drivers. The strongest form: a case that is right about the change and wrong about its pace would be right and not own the shares. For this to be right, organic EBITA growth, not organic sales, must rise toward the sales rate in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, which is the first reading's negation. The first and third readings cannot both be true, and the same print decides between them. The case's existing middle reading, that H2 2026 transport revenue is the delayed-delivery catch-up the sell-side flagged in July and the clean run-rate is still about 6%, is also live and is not refuted by a 13% that arrived with a 2.5-point segment margin fall.

Two obvious readings fail on the evidence. That this is a sector tide fails on the segment split: the acceleration is in transport, not in the thematic segments, and two of three named peers grew low single digits. That the margin miss is the start of the case's dual shock fails because currency added 1% and neither the pound nor UK wages appears in the quarter's explanation; the cost problem is raw materials on rail projects, not sterling.

What would settle it

  • The Supply Chain & Transportation margin in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 (reports due in February and April 2027). Above 20% with sales still growing double digits settles for the third reading and against the first; 18% to 19% with the same growth settles for the first. Management has dated the recovery to early 2027. The indexation-clause coverage at Mecno is not a disclosure the company has committed to, so the "why" may never be printed even when the "what" is.
  • Q4 2026 cash conversion and year-end leverage (February 2027). Conversion back inside 70% to 90% for the quarter and leverage under 3.0x after the earn-outs settles against the second reading; leverage at or above 3.0x is the case's alarm and DNB Carnegie's base case at once.
  • Organic growth in Q4 2026 against a 0% organic EBITA comparative: a print that holds double digits on sales and reaches high single digits on EBITA would make the case's 5% indefensible.
  • Water & Bioeconomy at 22% or better under the new head within two quarters, or the invalidation level is met.
  • The second Earnings Review, four working days on, carries what the sell-side does in between: Redeye's note if one is published, any Nordea change, the FactSet consensus for FY2026 to FY2028 after the print (the test is whether the FY2027 median, SEK 16.69 at the memo, moves toward DNB Carnegie's SEK 19.5), and the target spread. It cannot carry a segment margin, a cash figure, a leverage figure, a ROCE by area or an acquisition multiple that this review does not already hold, because none of them is due before the Q4 report in February 2027. It can settle whether the sell-side read this quarter as the first reading or the third; it cannot settle which is true.
