Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
The Key Question: "How exposed is Teleperformance SE (TEP-FR) to a goodwill impairment at its FY2026 annual test, given its cash-generating units, the goodwill allocated to each, the discount rates, growth and margin assumptions and the sensitivities disclosed in its 2025 Universal Registration Document and H1 2026 interim report, the gap between its market value and book equity, and the precedents of Concentrix's goodwill impairments in FY2025 and Q3 2026?"

Financial and Operational Risks

Teleperformance SE holds a massive goodwill balance of €4.28 billion at June 30, 2026, which represents approximately 37% of its total assets and nearly matches its total market capitalization of €4.82 billion. This enormous concentration of intangible assets on the balance sheet makes the company highly vulnerable to even minor downward revisions in its cash flow forecasts or increases in discount rates. A material goodwill impairment would immediately reduce reported net profit and book equity, negatively impacting earnings per share (EPS) and driving down investor confidence.

For an investor, tracking goodwill impairment risk is critical because non-cash write-downs directly impair reported EPS, even though they are frequently added back to calculate adjusted net income. In fiscal year 2025, Teleperformance reported a 13% miss on reported EPS due to below-the-line impairment charges of €87.3 million.

Any further write-downs in fiscal year 2026 will suppress reported EPS expansion and prolong the stock's valuation discount relative to its historical multiples.

Debt Covenants and Capital Structure

A massive goodwill impairment could also threaten Teleperformance's compliance with its financial covenants, which dictate a minimum level of book equity and a maximum leverage ratio. Under its private placement agreements, Teleperformance must maintain consolidated equity above €2.69 billion and keep its consolidated net debt to EBITDA ratio below 2.75x. While its consolidated equity of €3.95 billion is currently healthy, a substantial multi-segment write-down would erode this cushion.

A reduction in equity could also prompt credit rating agencies to re-evaluate Teleperformance’s investment-grade BBB rating. A credit downgrade below BBB- would activate more restrictive covenants across its commercial paper and multicurrency credit facilities. Consequently, management has shifted its capital allocation strategy to aggressively focus on deleveraging, forecasting a reduction in leverage to 1.7x in fiscal year 2026 to mitigate these structural risks.

Comparative Market Valuations

The risk of goodwill impairment is magnified by the persistent valuation discount applied to global customerexperience providers. Global outsourcers trade at an average fiscal year 2026 price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of approximately 7x, which represents a 61% discount to Indian-listed peers trading at 18x. 37 estimates that Teleperformance trades on a fiscal year 2027 adjusted P/E of just 6x, reflecting deep market skepticism about the sector's long-term growth viability.

This depressed market valuation creates a significant disconnect between the company's book equity and its actual market capitalization. Under IAS 36, when a company's market capitalization falls below its book value of net assets, it serves as an explicit indicator of impairment. This disconnect forces rigorous, highly scrutinized quantitative impairment tests at each reporting period, leaving the company with little margin for operational error.

Precedents and Sector Headwinds

The customer-experience sector faces severe secular headwinds from generative AI automation and client pricing pressure, which are squeezing organic growth and operating margins. Peer precedent demonstrates that these headwinds can rapidly trigger massive goodwill write-downs, as seen with Concentrix's recent impairments. These sector-wide challenges make the impairment testing assumptions used by Teleperformance's management a key focal point for investors.
