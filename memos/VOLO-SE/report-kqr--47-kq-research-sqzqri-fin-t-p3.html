Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Resolving Volati's systemic feedback loops is critical because leverage constraints and high preferred dividend commitments directly restrict future acquisitions and earnings growth.

The Key Question

1.
Key Question: "Which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"

Impact on Earnings and Capital

2.
Stretched balance sheet: The current leverage profile severely limits Volati's acquisition capacity and threatens its target of 15% EBITA growth per share. In October 2026, DNB Carnegie reported that Volati's leverage reached a stretched 4.5x net debt to EBITDA including preference shares after the Salix spin-off. This high debt load restricts capital allocation efficiency because available cash flow must service existing obligations instead of funding high-margin add-on M&A. Consequently, delayed acquisitions directly depress future earnings per share expansion.
3.
Elevated cost of capital: The rising swap rates and depressed equity valuations feed back into a higher weighted average cost of capital for Volati. In October 2026, DNB Carnegie noted that compounder multiples have compressed and the valuation of Volati at ~11x 2027e EV/EBITA reflects a high leverage discount. Since equity is expensive and debt is constrained, the cost of funding new acquisitions increases significantly. Unless end markets recover rapidly, this high cost of capital caps potential investment returns and limits shareholder value creation.

Strategic Cash and Preference Shares

4.
Servicing preferred dividends: Volati's financial flexibility is heavily constrained because all preference shares remained with the parent company after the Salix Group distribution. According to Volati's Q3 2026 interim report, these preference shares require an annual dividend payment of SEK 64 million, which equals SEK 40 per share. This structural cash drain reduces the organic capital available to fund acquisitions within the remaining platforms. Therefore, servicing these fixed preference obligations directly reduces earnings attributable to ordinary shareholders during cyclical downturns.
5.
Organic cash conversion: The organic cash generation capacity of Volati's decentralized platforms is the primary balancing force against rising leverage. In October 2026, Volati reported an exceptionally high cash conversion of 93% on a rolling 12-month basis. DNB Carnegie argues that under normal operating conditions, a sustainable cash conversion rate is closer to 50% or 60%. Maintaining strong cash conversion is critical because organic cash flow must rebuild the balance sheet capacity before acquisition activity can safely accelerate.
6.
Scalable decentralized model: A key related question is whether individual platforms can independently source and execute acquisitions while the parent is constrained. In September 2026, DNB Carnegie reported that Ettiketto Group is becoming increasingly self-sufficient, executing eight acquisitions to date and taking on more sourcing responsibilities. Sourcing deals locally lowers execution costs and improves synergy potential. This gradual decentralization could increase the scalability of Volati's model, provided the platforms can self-fund growth without parent capital injections.

The Other Side of the Story

7.
Other side of the story: While leverage appears stretched, seasonally strong cash flows in the fourth quarter and completed divestments could rapidly repair balance sheet capacity. Volati's management stated in October 2026 that cash generation from operations and acquired businesses supports a gradual return to their target leverage range of 2.0x to 3.0x. If cyclical platforms like S:t Eriks recover faster than expected, the resulting EBITDA expansion will automatically lower leverage ratios and restart the acquisition loop without requiring dilutive equity issues.
