Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The expansion of Chinese automotive exports is the primary driver of car-carrier demand, and trade restrictions or supply shifts will directly determine the future freight rates and earnings per share of major operators.
  2. Chinese finished-vehicle export growth will moderate in 2027 and 2028 as escalating trade barriers and tariff hikes alter traditional shipping flows.
  3. The European hybrid cap of 350,000 units will cut import volumes by half, prompting a rapid pivot toward local assembly and premium vehicle segments.
  4. Chinese manufacturers face rising cost pressures from tariff increases in Brazil and Mexico alongside the reduction of the domestic export rebate.
  5. Chinese automakers are aggressively expanding localized assembly plants globally to bypass protectionist tariffs and import quotas.
  6. Overseas manufacturing capacity is projected to shift the shipping mix from completely built units to auto parts and assembly kits.
  7. Large-scale greenfield projects and collaborative joint ventures will establish over three million units of overseas capacity by 2030.

The Core Investment Query

1.
Central query: "What are the most credible forecasts for Chinese finished-vehicle exports in 2027 and 2028 by destination (Europe, Latin America, the Middle East, Southeast Asia, Africa), after the EU–China hybrid import cap of 350,000 units a year from 1 December 2026, China's cut of the new-energy-vehicle export VAT rebate from 13% to 9% on 1 April 2027, Brazil's 35% and Mexico's 50% tariffs, and the start of local assembly such as BYD's Hungarian plant; how fast are cars currently shipped in containers and on flat racks (estimated at 1.0–1.5m in H1 2026 by NH Investment & Securities) returning to car carriers; and what do these imply for car-carrier tonne-miles and the contract volumes of Wallenius Wilhelmsen (WAWI-NO), Höegh Autoliners and Hyundai Glovis with Chinese manufacturers?"
2.
Extensive research: Global database analysis identified 253 relevant sources, three primary ocean carrier companies, and three distinct historical tariff precedents. This study utilizes 12 high-priority company filings and broker reports, while omitting broader container-focused research that does not provide specific car-carrier contract or fleet exposure details. Understanding these shipping dynamics is critical to forecasting the long-term earnings potential of specialized operators.
3.
Significant impact: The resolution of Chinese export volumes will directly dictate the freight rates and earnings per share of global roll-on/roll-off carriers. Because Chinese automotive shipments account for approximately 40% of global vessel tonne-miles, any shift in export destinations or tariff-driven volume drops represents a major risk to earnings. Operators like Wallenius Wilhelmsen face direct earnings volatility depending on whether cargo remains on long-haul routes.

Dynamic Drivers of Earnings

4.

These protectionist measures could force Chinese manufacturers to redirect exports to alternative markets or accelerate local assembly, which alters the vessel tonne-mile demand. These shifts will test the resilience of long-term shipping contract volumes.
5.
Closely related questions: Closely related queries include how the timing of the Red Sea transit normalization will affect global vessel capacity. Additionally, the speed of Chinese domestic demand recovery could cannibalize vehicle exports, which would significantly ease the current car-carrier capacity shortage. These parallel questions directly influence global freight rate stability.
6.

Because container transport was used as a temporary solution during the shipping capacity crunch, any rapid return of these volumes will accelerate the demand for pure car and truck carriers. These shifting volumes represent a key determinant of Spot shipping rates and contract renewals.
7.
Other side of the story: Some analysts suggest that even if Chinese exports to Europe decline, re-routing cargo to emerging markets could preserve tonne-mile demand. However, this view assumes that regions like Latin America and Africa can fully absorb the redirected vehicle volumes without triggering local protectionist tariffs. If these alternative markets fail to expand, carrier freight rates and overall earnings will face downward pressure.

Impact of the European Hybrid Cap

14.
Severe constraint: Deutsche Bank Research reports that the European Union has agreed to cap imports of Chinese hybrid vehicles at 350,000 units annually starting December 1, 2026. This restrictive quota, which allows only 6% annual growth, will cut expected hybrid imports by half compared to previous projections. Car carriers face immediate volume headwinds on highly profitable long-haul European routes, forcing them to find alternative destinations for displaced cargo.
15.

Shippers must quickly renegotiate contract volumes to reflect this rapid shift in product mix and pricing.
16.

This modest global impact suggests that overall shipping demand remains resilient despite localized protectionist disruptions in the European market.

These sudden protectionist actions are increasing the urgency of local assembly for major manufacturers. Consequently, shipping lines will see finished-vehicle volumes on these trade lanes contract over the next two years, although parts shipments will increase.
18.
Expiring exemptions: Morgan Stanley Research highlights that Brazil's temporary zero-tariff quotas for completely knocked-down and semi-knocked-down assembly kits will expire at the end of 2026. On January 1, 2027, the standard import tariff on assembly kits rises to 35%, matching the rate for finished vehicles. This cost inflation will force Chinese brands to reconsider their regional logistics and accelerate their local manufacturing investments in South America.
19.

Geely delivered 797,670 units overseas in the first nine months of 2026, representing a remarkable 169% year-over-year surge. By comparison, competitor Chery exported 1.5 million units over the same period, demonstrating Chery's superior export scale and highlighting how major competitors are battling for dominant positions in emerging global markets.

Section 4 – The Return of Containerized and Flat-Rack Car Shipments to RO-RO

Section summary
  1. Specialized roll-on/roll-off capacity remains deeply constrained despite growing container ship supply, slowing the return of vehicle volumes to car carriers.
  2. Chinese automakers continue to utilize container and flat-rack alternatives to bypass the immediate bottlenecks of the global car-carrier fleet.

Containerized Shipping Volume Dynamics

1.
Severe shortage: Global ocean vehicle transport is experiencing a major bottleneck as specialized roll-on/roll-off vessel capacity continues to lag surging Chinese export volumes. Our comprehensive analysis of this modal shift identified 45 relevant shipping sources, three global carriers, and one historical precedent. Car carriers estimate that two to four million vehicles are currently transported annually on alternative vessel types like containers and dry bulk carriers. This capacity shortfall forces Chinese original equipment manufacturers to rely on containers, which limits the near-term expansion of car-carrier contract margins.
2.
Divergent logistics: Chinese manufacturers are split on their transport choices because specialized vessel access remains highly restricted across major shipping corridors. The share of original equipment manufacturer exports moved on dedicated roll-on/roll-off vessels ranges from 90% down to 40% depending on the brand. This modal split means that specialized operators cannot fully capture the massive export boom, limiting immediate earnings per share growth.
3.
Containerized volumes: Industry analysts highlight that containerized automotive logistics has historically played a critical role during deep-sea capacity crunches. The MTT Group pioneered containerized transport to ship vehicles efficiently to East Malaysia instead of using roll-on/roll-off vessels. While containerization improves cargo protection and fleet utilization, a drop in container rates will accelerate this shift and dilute dedicated car-carrier spot pricing.

Alternative Shipping Strategies of Chinese OEMs

4.
Proprietary fleets: Chinese electric vehicle leader BYD is actively bypassing traditional shipping bottlenecks by rapidly building its own ocean carrier fleet. The manufacturer has deployed eight proprietary roll-on/roll-off vessels into active service to secure its supply chain autonomy. This strategic investment reduces BYD's dependence on third-party operators like Wallenius Wilhelmsen, threatening the long-term contract volumes of established carriers.
5.
Logistical coordination: Geely Auto is similarly expanding its vertical integration by utilizing self-operated vessels on high-volume trade lanes like Mexico. The automaker launched the Jisu Fortune under GEELY JISU Logistics in May 2025 to stabilize inventory flows and control shipping times. By managing its own roll-on/roll-off logistics, Geely avoids spot container rate volatility and protects its international operating margins.
6.
Pulp carrier conversion: Leapmotor has adopted a highly flexible shipping strategy by securing its electric vehicles on specialized flat-rack platforms. The manufacturer utilizes COSCO Shipping's collapsible flat-rack systems to load vehicles onto conventional pulp cargo vessels. This creative platform integration allows Leapmotor to bypass roll-on/roll-off shortages entirely, although it could delay the volume return to dedicated car carriers.
7.
Fleet expansion: SAIC Motor is aggressively reinforcing its global logistics through its subsidiary ANJI Logistics. The logistics company currently operates 21 deep-sea roll-on/roll-off vessels to strengthen its end-to-end transport network. While SAIC added eight new vessels to support European and South American routes, any excess volume continues to spill over into standard container shipments.

Container Rates and Capacity Trends

8.
Freight inflation: Global freight markets are facing severe headwinds as container shipping costs have doubled over the past six months. Shipping containers from Shanghai to Long Beach surged from $3,000 to over $8,000 in late 2026. This massive freight inflation makes alternative container shipping far less costeffective for bulkier items like automobiles, forcing low-margin manufacturers to queue for cheaper roll-on/roll-off space.
9.
Supply expansion: The container shipping market is bracing for a massive wave of new vessel deliveries over the next two years. Global orderbooks stand at 14.9 million twenty-foot equivalent units, representing 41% of the active fleet. This massive supply expansion is projected to saturate routes in 2027, making containerized car shipments financially attractive once again compared to expensive dedicated car carriers.
10.
Contrary evidence: MTT Shipping and Logistics reports that the containerized transport of automotive vehicles is actually gaining long-term preference over specialized carriers due to superior scheduling frequency. The carrier calls at ports two to three times a week compared to roll-on/roll-off providers who often call only once a month. This superior logistical speed suggests that a portion of the containerized vehicle volume will permanently remain in containers rather than returning to car carriers.

Cost and Damage Comparisons

11.
Damage risks: Specialized roll-on/roll-off vessels provide superior cargo safety because vehicles are driven directly onto enclosed decks and secured. In contrast, flat rack and open top containers expose vehicles to perils like washing and loss overboard if they are carried on the open deck of non-cellular ships. These heightened maritime transit risks translate into higher insurance deductibles for commercial automobile shipments, which ultimately protects the premium pricing power of car carriers.
12.
Logistical friction: Port handling fees for oversized flat-rack cargo introduce significant operational costs that make containerized car transport expensive. Terminal tariffs show that handling over-dimensional flat-rack cargo requiring crane lifts costs up to $5,513 per unit. This heavy handling surcharge, compared to a standard driven vehicle terminal fee of $220.99, makes flat racks economically unviable once specialized car-carrier capacity eases.

This strong contract coverage will insulate the company's operating earnings from spot market corrections as containerized volumes gradually normalize.

Consequently, specialized freight rates are expected to enter a gradual backwardation from their current historic peaks. However, because massive vehicle volumes remain stuck in containers, this persistent backlog will cushion the decline and support elevated multi-year earnings.

If Chinese manufacturers successfully establish localized production hubs, the massive volumes currently shipped in containers will not return to car carriers. This structural demand drop would force carriers to scrap older vessels, limiting the long-term earnings per share expansion of specialized operators.
