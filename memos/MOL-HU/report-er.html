Section 16 – Conclusion 1 finding

The Q2 2026 earnings release fundamentally shifted MOL Group's investment narrative by delivering a USD 786 million net profit and USD 1,297 million Clean CCS EBITDA, representing an 89% year-over-year EBITDA surge that dramatically exceeded consensus expectations. This exceptional financial performance was driven primarily by refining margins that more than tripled to USD 20.8 per barrel and petrochemical spreads that jumped 134% to EUR 548 per ton, though management acknowledged these favorable conditions quickly

The single most important delta from this earnings release is the signing of the landmark USD 720 million Sale and Purchase Agreement with Shell to acquire a 35% non-operated stake in the offshore Cyprus Aphrodite gas field, which represents MOL's biggest upstream growth opportunity since 2019 and adds an estimated 104 billion cubic meters of contingent natural gas resources. This transformational transaction structurally reframes the investment case from a cyclical CEE refining story into a long-duration Mediterranean gas repositioning play, though first gas delivery is not anticipated until 2031 with Final Investment Decision expected in 2027. strongest counter-hypothesis to the positive case centers on the temporary and geopolitically dependent nature of the downstream margin windfall that drove the earnings beat.

The massive EBITDA outperformance was almost entirely dependent on volatile geopolitical crises, specifically the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and regional conflicts that temporarily inflated refining and petrochemical spreads. If global refining capacity expansions lead to a rapid crash in crack spreads back to five-year averages, this would severely impair downstream cash flows and leave the expensive USD 720 million Cypriot gas acquisition looking poorly timed.

Additionally, approximately USD 60 million in Q2 Consumer Services earnings pressure was directly attributed to state-mandated fuel price and margin regulations across Serbia, Romania, Croatia, Slovenia, Bosnia, and Montenegro, with these political margin caps structurally capping retail cash flow generation.

Third, definitive resolution of the European Commission investigation into Croatian pipeline operator JANAF's alleged abuse of its transit monopoly, where fees are three to four times higher than market benchmarks.
