First set of questions: TKH Group (TWEKA-NL), answered 22 October 2026

Three notes that govern every answer below

  1. Data vintage. The latest Earnings Review covers the first half of 2026 (six months to 30 June 2026, reported 11 August 2026); I confirmed this from the report's own reporting-period declaration, and it matches the last period in the FactSet block (06/2026). The earlier Earnings Review covers FY2025 (reported 5 March 2026). No third-quarter 2026 trading update is in the files; TKH's third-quarter update is due around 10 to 11 November 2026 and the FY2026 results on 9 March 2027. Everything below stands as at the H1 2026 print plus the dashboard, revision and FactSet readings of 22 October 2026.

  2. Perimeter. The FactSet consensus for FY2026 to FY2029 models the consolidated group as it stands, including Electrification (about 40% of revenue, €604 million in the twelve months to June 2026) and the €250 million of non-core turnover (mostly Digitalization fibre) that management intends to sell. Nothing in consensus reflects the Electrification separation targeted for 4Q26 or 1Q27. The Consumer should build two models: (a) the consolidated group up to the separation date; and (b) the standalone Automation business (Vision Technology plus Automated Machinery, roughly €1.0 billion of annualised revenue at an 18.4% EBITA margin before unallocated central costs of roughly €25 million a year) plus the separation proceeds and their use. Where I give figures for "the group", I say so.

  3. Segment naming. From January 2026 the group reports Automation (Vision Technology; Automated Machinery), Electrification (Offshore Energy; Onshore Energy; Specialty Cables) and Other (Digitalization fibre and residual activities). FY2025 and earlier material uses Smart Vision, Smart Manufacturing and Smart Connectivity; Smart Connectivity contained both Electrification and Digitalization, which is why its 2.3% FY2025 margin and the Electrification figures are not the same series.

External change forces

Group judgement written last by the Investment Analyst; shown first here

The external forces have turned from headwind to tailwind over the last 12 months, and the turn is well-evidenced in order books and tender wins rather than in narrative. The two structural tailwinds (Dutch grid, offshore inter-array) have multi-year visibility and are the reason Electrification can be sold at all; the cyclical tailwind (vision restock) is real but short-dated; the cyclical headwind (tyre-maker capex) is unresolved and is the main swing factor for the business the shareholder will keep. The macro regime (discount-rate shock, refusal of re-ratings) bites the transaction more than the operations. The Consumer should model Electrification on structural demand with a capacity-bound ramp, Vision on a 6-to-18-month restock that fades to mid-single-digit growth, and Machinery on a trough that does not turn before mid-2027 unless the November update shows order intake above €80 million a quarter.

C-01

To what extent is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically, and with how much visibility? (A cyclical swing counts when it has two-plus years of visibility.)

C66

End-market demand is changing in three different ways at once, and the composition has inverted against the last 12 to 18 months. (a) Offshore inter-array cables (Offshore Energy, about €200 million annualised): structural, with visibility beyond two years. European inter-array demand is projected to grow six-fold by 2030 against 2024; the tender funnel TKH is bidding on is 14,400 kilometres across 92 projects, with over 19,000 kilometres through 2030; TKH has won over 80% of the European tenders it entered in 2024 to 2026 (Zeevonk 162 kilometres, Waterkant 130 kilometres, Gennaker 140 kilometres at €50 to €150 million); its Eemshaven plant has 1,200 kilometres of annual capacity against a 600-kilometre target for 2026. Offshore turnover rose 81.6% organically in H1 2026 because supply, not demand, was the constraint. The visibility is high, but the timing of call-offs depends on developers' financing and permitting, which the rates environment is now stressing. (b) Dutch onshore grid cables (Onshore Energy, about €370 million annualised): structural, visibility of several years. TKH holds €1.4 billion of signed framework agreements that are excluded from the reported order book, including the eight-year €650 million Alliander contract running from mid-2026; Dutch distribution operators plan €10.8 billion of capex to 2030 against €5.9 billion in 2025, and onshore turnover grew 26.4% in H1 2026 after the 2024 destocking that management failed to anticipate. The factories moved from severe underutilisation to near-maximum capacity within a year. (c) Machine vision (Vision Technology, about €540 million annualised): a cyclical turn with short visibility, so it does not count as structural under the question's two-year test. Destocking has ended; the Vision order book rose to €170.1 million at June 2026 from €125.8 million at December 2025 (about four months of sales), APAC semiconductor, electronics and battery inspection lead, Europe and Germany lag; the read-across is strong (Jenoptik's H1 2026 orders up 53% and its FY2026 margin guidance raised to about 22% in October; Basler's bookings up 64%; the Eurozone manufacturing PMI at 52.9 in September 2026, the highest since May 2022). (d) Tyre-building machinery (Automated Machinery, about €440 million annualised): a cyclical trough that is still deepening. The order book fell 38% year on year to €259.2 million at June 2026 (from €420.2 million) because Tier 1 tyre makers deferred capex on tariffs, energy costs and site permitting; Goodyear is closing plants and Michelin cut guidance; management says no order has been lost to a competitor and 75% to 80% of unit demand is replacement of an ageing installed base (over 40% of non-TKH machines are more than 20 years old). The turn is not yet visible; the November trading update is the first test. (e) Specialty cables (€90 million) are flat on German industrial weakness; Digitalization fibre (about €220 million, for sale) has turned up on AI data-centre demand and recovered pricing. The judgement: about 40% of revenue now sits on structural demand with two-plus years of visibility, 30% in a cyclical upturn with short visibility, and 25% in a cyclical trough with no turn confirmed. Eighteen months ago the picture was the reverse: machinery was the stabiliser at record backlog, cables and vision were the drag. The skew over the next twelve months is positive, but the cone is wide because the two structural legs are in the business being sold, and the cyclical trough is in the highest-return business that stays.

C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

C54

Competitive intensity is easing where TKH is strongest today and rising where it is weakest, and the easing is time-limited. In subsea inter-array cables intensity has fallen over the last 18 months: the three export-cable majors (Prysmian, NKT, Nexans) are fully booked on 525kV high-voltage work into 2028 and beyond and are not bidding aggressively for inter-array; TKH's 80% win rate and its sole-bidder position in a German tender certified for winter laying are the evidence. The fragility is dated: top-tier cable makers are adding roughly 30% capacity into 2027 (NKT's Karlskrona expansion commissions in Q2 2027), LS Cable and Sumitomo are building inside Europe, and offshore cancellations in the United States free capacity that could be pointed at European inter-array tenders. In Dutch onshore medium-voltage cables the frameworks (30% to 35% supply share) have locked intensity down for several years. In tyre-building machinery intensity is stable at the top (no order lost; Tier 1 share of VMI intake rose from 10% to 55% over ten years) and rising at the bottom (Chinese builders have taken an estimated 15% to 20% of parts of the APAC Tier 2/3 market over five years). In 2D machine vision intensity is rising structurally: Hikrobot and Dahua in standard cameras, software-only and hardware-agnostic entrants, robotics makers buying sensors from chip vendors, and Cognex and Keyence spending 13% to 14% of sales on research against TKH's 5%. Net for the group: slightly easier than 12 to 18 months ago, driven by cable scarcity; net for the Automation business that will remain: unchanged to slightly harder.

C-03

To what extent is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved, and in which direction does it act on the company?

C58

The disruption forces are mostly being proved in TKH's favour over the last six months, with one exception that acts against the remaining business. Strengthening and acting for TKH: the dry-design inter-array cable (no lead or bitumen, installation up to 50% faster, winter-capable, re-engineered from 66kV to 132kV for 15MW-plus turbines) has been validated by the 80% win rate and by H1 2026 output; the UNIXX tyre-building platform (strip winding, combining component preparation and assembly, 2x to 3x the selling price of a MAXX machine, orders above €10 million) has moved from first delivery to repeat orders and to a motorcycle-tyre variant; the GoPxL edge-AI vision software has been unified across the Allied Vision and LMI hardware and extends the addressable market from €3.4 billion to €4.8 billion; software and AI are over 30% of turnover. Strengthening and acting against TKH: commoditisation of 2D camera hardware by Chinese vendors bundling open-source deep learning, and the shift of value to software that robotics integrators can do themselves; Chinese 3D entrants (Mech-Mind, Revopoint) are narrowing the gap below LMI's niche. Being disproved: generic AI entrants displacing industrial vision (deterministic 10-millisecond execution requirements keep them out of the core); 800-volt data-centre architectures (irrelevant to inter-array). The net is positive now and balanced on a three-year view, because the positive forces lift segments whose value is about to be crystallised (subsea) or that are cyclical (machinery), while the negative force erodes the asset the market will be asked to re-rate at a software multiple.

C-04

To what extent is political or regulatory change under way, and how does it bear on the company?

C60

Political and regulatory change is strongly supportive, but the support attaches mostly to the business that is being sold. In Electrification's favour: REPowerEU's binding 42.5% renewable target for 2030 (over €100 billion a year of EU grid investment required); the Dutch government's designation of grid expansion as a national priority (about 15,000 customers waiting, a 120GW connection backlog), which removed local permitting constraints and lifted onshore call-offs in H1 2026; TenneT's €200 billion programme to 2034 with TKH as a preferred high-voltage onshore supplier; nine North Sea countries targeting 15GW a year from 2031; Dutch nitrogen rules worked around by the grid operators (the earlier claim that 60% of projects were delayed 2.5 years has not shown up in the H1 2026 onshore numbers). Also in TKH's favour: EU anti-dumping duties of up to 62% on Chinese fibre, neutralised by moving production to Poland, which Jefferies says added about 100 basis points to connectivity added-value margins. Against TKH: trade-tariff uncertainty is the stated cause of Tier 1 tyre-makers deferring plant decisions, which is the single biggest drag on Automation today; the European Circular Economy Act and CSRD add compliance cost; OECD Pillar Two lifts the tax floor across 34 countries. The important insight for the model is that the regulatory tailwind supports the sale price of Electrification rather than the earnings of the company the shareholder will own after the separation.

C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

❌ D45

Input costs are a mild net headwind, and the mechanism matters more than the direction. Copper is up about 30% year on year, Brent is above $100, and European electricity and gas prices were up 97% and 133% year on year in September 2026. In cables, copper and aluminium are passed through contractually with indexation (lag of one to two quarters on some contracts; secondary materials such as compounds are not fully indexed); the effect of rising metal is therefore inflated revenue and higher working capital, not lower margin, which is one reason trade working capital sits at 18.3% of turnover against a 12% to 15% target and Electrification alone at 25.6%. Unhedged "free stock" exposes TKH to €1.9 million of EBITA per 10% fall in copper, with 1,658 tonnes hedged inside three months. Energy is the real cost headwind: continuous-curing cable lines and Dutch plants are energy-intensive and the pass-through is weaker. In Automation the inputs are electronics, steel and labour; components are outsourced from a diverse base, Vision's added-value margin held at 62.7%, and German wage inflation is the live pressure. Supplier concentration is moderate: a limited number of global metal suppliers; optical preform through the 12.5% Shin-Etsu joint venture (germanium prices doubled on Chinese export limits); cable installation vessels are subcontracted, which is TKH's most consequential supplier dependency because it transmitted €11.6 million of late-delivery penalties in 2025. A 10% rise in the euro cuts pre-tax profit by €8.3 million; H1 2026 currency cost €4.2 million. Net: the group's added-value margin was stable at 50.2% in H1 2026 against 50.7%, so cost inflation is being absorbed, but through cash rather than profit, and the energy and tariff components are headwinds for the next six months.

C-06

Where is the company's own revenue and demand cycle, and to what extent is it turning?

C64

TKH's own revenue cycle turned up sharply in the last six months and is early in its recovery, but the rate of growth has probably peaked. Organic growth ran minus 1.2% in FY2024, plus 4.9% in FY2025 (plus 8.7% in Q4 2025), plus 9.6% in Q1 2026 and plus 18.0% in Q2 2026; adjusted EBITA fell 7.2% organically in FY2025 and rose 43.5% in H1 2026. Three facts qualify the acceleration. First, the comparison base was depressed: H1 2025 return on sales was 9.3% with the Connectivity margin at 1.0%, so H1 2026 is as much a repair as a cycle. Second, the segments are out of phase: Electrification is in year one of a ramp (plus 33% organic), Vision in the first two quarters of a restock, Machinery in the trough. Third, H2 2025 is a tougher comparison (return on sales 12.9%, Q4 alone 14.6%), so H2 2026 organic growth will be lower than H1's 14% even if the business continues to improve; consensus already has FY2027 revenue growth at 6.1%. The company's normal through-cycle growth is low-to-mid single digit; it is running above normal, which is the definition of early-cycle. Skew over twelve months: positive on earnings (Eemshaven utilisation, vision order book), balanced on revenue growth (machinery drag, tougher base).

C-07

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

❌ D45

The macro force is the discount-rate shock described in the Fitness-for-Fighting digest (US ten-year about 5.1%, hiking paths pulled forward, European breadth in the bottom quarter, the sector reading that the market refuses re-ratings and penalises leverage, with Fragility at Double Underweight). It bites TKH at four levels, in this order of severity. (1) Company level, through the separation: a dual-track process in which the public route depends on an IPO window and the private route on leveraged buyers' financing; a credit crack and a 5% risk-free rate lower the proceeds and raise the chance of slippage past 1Q27. (2) Industry level, through customer capex: Tier 1 tyre makers and German machine builders defer orders when financing costs are high; offshore wind developers face financing bottlenecks that can push cable call-offs into later years. (3) Sector level, in TKH's favour: the break-up cohort of diversified industrial groups has been the one part of the sector the market paid for (plus 45.3% over twelve months, TKH plus 23.7% over three months to mid-October), and the sector digest explicitly names TKH as value that does not need a re-rating because the carve-out is worth over half the market capitalisation. (4) Directly through the income statement, where the effect is small: €13.0 million of H1 interest, €1.2 million of net profit per 100 basis points. The short-cycle industrial turn (PMI above 52) is a positive macro offset for Vision. Net: the macro bites hardest at the transaction, not at the operations.

C-08

To what extent is a key driver at the industry or sector level accelerating the company's demand?

C64

Yes, three industry-level drivers are accelerating TKH's demand, and one is decelerating it. Accelerating: grid electrification (Dutch onshore capex growing at an 11% CAGR from €2.8 billion in 2025 to €4.6 billion in 2030; interconnector capex of €6 billion a year to 2030); offshore wind inter-array build (six-fold by 2030); and the AI and semiconductor capital cycle (APAC vision demand; fibre demand and pricing). A fourth, structural labour shortage (working-age population in developed markets falling from 67% to 59% by 2050), supports hands-off automation in both tyre building and vision but moves slowly. Decelerating: global tyre-maker capex. The acceleration is real and measurable in the order books (Vision plus 35% in six months; Electrification offshore plus 81.6%). The caveat is the one that runs through this whole group: two of the three accelerating drivers sit in Electrification.

C-09

What other material external dynamic, positive or negative, is at work?

C52

Four other external dynamics are material. (1) The pricing of the sold asset is set by cable peers' multiples, and those have fallen: Nexans trades at about 7x EV/EBITDA and 14x earnings after a 22% fall from its June peak, Prysmian at about 10.7x 2027 EV/EBITDA, NKT about 10x; a trade buyer or IPO investor will anchor Electrification nearer Nexans than Prysmian, which cuts the proceeds against the sum-of-the-parts cases built in spring 2026 (Jefferies' €79 after a 30% discount, NAV €103.5). (2) Capacity additions by cable peers in 2027 and 2028, and NKT's commissioning in Q2 2027, mark the end of the scarcity window on which Electrification's 19% EBITDA target depends; a buyer will price that. (3) Private-equity appetite for carve-outs is high (Bain's €7.4 billion Everllence deal; Smiths Detection sold to CVC at 12.5x EBITDA), which supports the private route even if the public route closes. (4) HAL Trust's 10.23% stake gives a valuation floor and a patient owner pushing simplification, but under the STAK structure it cannot force pace. A fifth, smaller dynamic: thyssenkrupp's tk accelis listing in late 2026 is the next break-up read-across in TKH's sector and will set sentiment for separation stories into TKH's own announcement window.

C-10

Taking each sector and industry thematic driver in turn, and then all of the above together: how strong are the external headwinds and how strong are the tailwinds at value-chain, industry and sector level, and what is the overall external change picture?

C60

Taking each thematic driver in turn. Value-chain level: grid electrification, strong tailwind; offshore wind, strong tailwind with financing fragility; AI and semiconductor capex, tailwind for Vision and fibre; tyre-maker capex, headwind until tariffs settle; German industrial activity, headwind; energy and metals costs, mild headwind through cash; Chinese commoditisation, slow headwind in 2D vision. Industry level (industrials and capital goods): the short-cycle turn is a tailwind for the restock in Vision; the discount-rate regime is a headwind for customer capex and for any case that needs a re-rating. Sector level (diversified industrial groups): the break-up pattern is the one the market is paying for, and TKH fits it (what leaves is worse than what stays). Overall external picture: moderately positive, with the tailwinds concentrated in the segment to be sold and in Vision, and the headwinds concentrated in Machinery, which is 25% of revenue and historically the highest-margin unit. For the consolidated model over the next 18 months the external forces net to a tailwind worth several points of organic growth above TKH's norm; for the post-separation Automation model they net to roughly neutral until tyre-maker capex turns.

Internal change forces

Group judgement written last by the Investment Analyst; shown first here

TKH is in the middle of the most complete corporate reset in its history, and most of the pieces that can be dated have happened on time since September 2025. The reset removes the two things that made the group unmodellable, a capital-intensive project business with penalty risk and a 25-year chief executive without a successor, and leaves an Automation business whose segment economics are already inside its 2028 targets. The weakness is that everything hinges on a single transaction in a hostile financing regime, that the people delivering it over-promised for three years, and that the accounting and working-capital habits of the old group travel with the new one. The Consumer should model the separation as the base case (two-thirds probability of closing by mid-2027), with a one-third case in which the group is still consolidated through 2027 and the conglomerate discount persists.

C-11

To what extent have the chief executive, or other key leaders (e.g. finance director, operations director, board, division heads, etc.) or other key actors (e.g. activists, major shareholders) changed, and what does the change signal?

C62

The leadership change is the largest in the company's listed history and signals the end of the family era for the business that remains. Guido Grandi (former chief executive of Norma Group and WKW, earlier at thyssenkrupp, KUKA, Ford and United Technologies) joined the Executive Board on 3 September 2026 and becomes group chief executive on 1 January 2027; Alexander van der Lof (chief executive since 2001, 41 years inside TKH, his contract previously extended past retirement age) moves to lead the carved-out Electrification business and stays on the Group board until separation; Van Cortenberghe joined as Electrification's chief financial officer in June 2026; Harm Voortman (Automation, VMI since 2010) was reappointed to 2030; chairman Peter Oosterveer was reappointed and had chaired the temporary Eemshaven committee; Rokus van Iperen stepped down. CFO Elling de Lange (since 2008) stays. HAL Trust holds 10.23% with no board seat. What it signals: (a) the Supervisory Board and HAL have aligned the top of the company with the strategy, which removes the key-person and succession risk the Leadership report rated extreme; (b) the first external chief executive in four decades, with a turnaround background, raises the probability of a harder look at Vision's integration, central costs and portfolio in 2027, and of a reset of the base year ("kitchen sink") at his first results or capital markets day; (c) the dual-chief-executive interregnum from September 2026 to the separation date is a friction risk, and Grandi's departure from Norma over strategic differences is a documented tendency to conflict. For the model: expect a disclosure reset and possibly a restructuring charge in FY2026 or FY2027 results, and a new set of standalone Automation targets during 2027 that may differ from the 2028 ones now in place.

C-12

To what extent have the strategy or the strategic priorities changed, and how well is the change articulated and resourced? (choices)

C70

The strategy changed decisively in September 2025 (Capitalise & Execute 2028) and has been executed step by step since: separation of Electrification via a dual-track process (shareholders approved it at the 3 September 2026 EGM; completion targeted 4Q26 or 1Q27); sale of about €250 million of non-core turnover (Dewetron closed October 2025 for a €35.8 million gain, Alphatronics March 2026, Digitalization regrouped under Other and prepared for sale); consolidation of nine vision brands into two (Allied Vision for 2D, LMI for 3D) with a unified software kit; a plan to sell vision software as standalone licences; an AI hub in Amsterdam; capex cut to depreciation after the €200 million programme; bolt-on buy-and-build in vision after separation. It is well articulated with dated milestones, quantified targets (Automation 5% to 7% organic growth, 17% to 19% EBITA margin, 25% to 30% ROCE by 2028; Electrification over 9% growth and over 19% EBITDA margin) and it is resourced (leverage 1.8x, a €625 million facility, capex done, €1.4 billion of frameworks). Three weaknesses. The targets were trimmed when the strategy was launched (Automation margin 17% to 19% against a prior 22% ambition for Vision; growth 5% to 7% against over 7%; Electrification EBITA 12% to 15% against 15%), so the plan was set to be beatable. The strategy does not say how a €1 billion Automation group with 5% research intensity widens its moat against Keyence, Cognex and Chinese entrants, and the buy-and-build leg is where TKH's record (seven vision acquisitions, sister brands competing, slow integration) is worst. And no stranded-cost, dis-synergy or separation-cost figure has been disclosed, which the Consumer must estimate (my working assumption is €25 to €30 million of central costs of which perhaps half is removable over two years, plus one-off separation costs above the €15 million the Risks report sets as the distress threshold).

C-13

To what extent has financial guidance been raised, or have mid-term targets or capital-markets-day ambitions been set or raised? (choices)

C56

Formal guidance has not been raised; mid-term targets have been reset in a way that is partly a genuine raise and partly a change of basis. FY2026 guidance remains qualitative, "organic growth in turnover and adjusted EBITA", reiterated in August despite an 11% H1 beat, with management citing regulatory restrictions during the dual-track process; against H1 organic EBITA growth of 43.5% the guidance is a floor, not a forecast. In August 2026 the standalone Electrification targets were upgraded to over 9% organic turnover CAGR from a €604.2 million base and an EBITDA margin above 19% (ING calculates about €131 million of 2027 EBITDA). The September 2025 target for the same unit was a 12% to 15% EBITA margin by 2028. Electrification's H1 2026 EBITDA margin was 12.6% against an EBITA margin of 8.0%, so depreciation runs at about 4.6% of sales; 19% EBITDA therefore equals roughly 14% to 14.5% EBITA, the top of the old range rather than above it. That is a modest real upgrade with a larger headline. Automation's 2028 targets were left unchanged even though the segment delivered 18.4% in H1 2026, inside the range two years early. No capital-markets-day ambitions have been set for the post-separation company; that is for Grandi in 2027.

C-14

To what extent are standards improving in execution, operational measures, governance or cost discipline? (choices and actions)

C55

Operational standards are improving; financial discipline and accounting conservatism are not yet.
