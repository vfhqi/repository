Section summary
  1. Understanding the specific structural and financial inputs will allow investors to accurately map Telia Company AB's path from a historic lack of dividend coverage to sustainable earnings per share and free cash flow growth.

Net financial cost: Telia's debt maturity schedule, average interest cost (reported around 3.9%), fixed-floating swap mix, hybrid capital (EUR 500m redeemed and SEK 2.4bn issued at 3.75% in 2026), lease interest, and Bernstein's 19 October 2026 analysis of the effect of marking Telia's debt to current market yields. Section 2 – Capital Structure and Net Financial Cost Drivers

Section summary
  1. Our confidence in Telia's net financial cost projections through 2029 is high, supported by the company's clear Q3 2026 debt disclosures, hybrid refinancing details, and peer cost comparisons.

Debt Portfolio and

1.
Refinancing Profile Stable cost: Telia maintained an average interest rate of 3.9% on its debt portfolio including hedges at the end of December 2025, which represents a decrease from the 4.2% reported in December 2024. This stability in borrowing costs is highly beneficial because it limits interest expense volatility for earnings per share. The average maturity of the borrowings stood at 4.9 years, complying with the financial policy requirement to exceed 4 years.
2.
Managed maturities: Telia structured its debt maturity profile so that only 29% of borrowings were due within 2 years as of December 2025. This near-term refinancing concentration is slightly below the maximum policy threshold of 30%, showing proactive management. In contrast, peer Elisa Corporation had 27% of its gross fixed debt maturing in 2027 and 2028, according to Deutsche Bank on 15 October 2026.
3.
Sizable exposure: Telia faced a total borrowings balance of SEK 90,850 million by 30 September 2026, marking an increase from SEK 87,459 million at the end of 2025. This expansion was driven by the funding of the Bredband2 acquisition and foreign exchange revaluations, which increases net financial cost pressure on upcoming earnings. Total available liquidity remained robust at SEK 29,394 million, which provides a strong buffer against immediate refinancing risks.
4.
Elevated leverage: Telia reported a net debt-to-adjusted EBITDA leverage ratio of 2x as of 30 September 2026, which is at the bottom of its target range.

Proximus faced low domestic fibre availability and higher adjusted leverage, whereas Telia benefited from high domestic fibre coverage that mitigates capital expenditure risks.

Hybrid Capital Adjustments

6.
Strategic refinancing: Telia refinanced its hybrid debt structure in early 2026 by issuing a new SEK 2.4 billion hybrid bond to replace upcoming call dates. This new issue was split into a SEK 0.65 billion fixed tranche at a 3.75% coupon and a SEK 1.75 billion floating tranche. This refinancing represents a net reduction in outstanding hybrids because Telia repaid EUR 500 million of older high-coupon hybrids, which reduces total nominal hybrid value by SEK 2.9 billion.
7.
Rising costs: Deutsche Bank observed on 10 February 2026 that Telia's SEK 2.4 billion hybrid refinancing implies a small increase in overall interest costs. The floating tranche coupon was linked to three-month STIBOR, resulting in an implied initial coupon of 3.35% on that portion. This marginal interest cost escalation will slightly weigh on earnings per share unless offset by higher yields on cash balances.
8.
Favorable ratings: Telia maintained its investment grade ratings of Baa1 from Moody's and BBB+ from S&P Global Ratings throughout these capital adjustments in 2026. These stable credit ratings ensure that the company preserves competitive debt market access for future refinancing needs.
9.
ING Wholesale Banking noted on 23 October 2025 that Telia’s hybrid notes looked rich but confirmed the operator’s commitment to an investment grade profile.

Lease Obligations and

10.
Interest Expenses Heavy obligations: Telia carried lease liabilities of SEK 17,888 million on 30 September 2026, representing a substantial portion of its capital structure. Interest expenses on these lease liabilities reached SEK 659 million for the full year 2025, compared to SEK 716 million in 2024. This lease interest component is excluded from active interest rate risk management, which exposes Telia to long-term inflation when contracts roll over.
11.
Fixed arrangements: Telia utilized right-of-use assets valued at SEK 16,926 million at the end of 2025, primarily consisting of technical space. These lease agreements are discounted using incremental borrowing rates because implicit contract rates are rarely determinable beforehand. Approximately 40% of these lease liabilities relate to extension periods, which locks in long-term capital commitments.
12.
Cash outflow: Telia reported cash outflows for repayments and interest on lease liabilities of SEK 2,945 million during the first nine months of 2026. This cash outflow represents an increase from SEK 2,806 million in the same period of 2025, which drags on free cash flow expansion. This cash drain directly impacts the progressive dividend coverage unless operational savings offset the rising lease commitments.

Yield Marking and

13.
Peer Metrics Mark-to-market risk: Bernstein Research reported on 19 October 2026 that marking Telia’s debt stack to current yields would raise its interest rate by 1.6%. This adjustment would cause a pro-forma net PAT dilution of 16%, making Telia one of the most exposed European operators. This vulnerability stems from Telia's high relative leverage compared to peers like Tele2 and KPN, who face only 5% and 6% dilution respectively.
14.
Refinancing impacts: Deutsche Bank projected on 15 October 2026 that refinancing 2027 and 2028 bond maturities at current yields would reduce Telia's equity free cash flow by approximately 1%. Refinancing all outstanding maturities under current yields would reduce cash flows by 10% for Telia. This potential headwind is larger than the 4% cash flow reduction projected for Tele2 under full refinancing, reflecting Telia’s higher cost of long-term debt.
15.
Contrary evidence: Some analysis suggest that Telia is well insulated from near-term debt maturity refinancing pressures compared to its regional peers. Deutsche Bank pointed out on 15 October 2026 that Telia has only 15% of its gross fixed debt maturing in 2027 and 2028, which is lower than Tele2's 38% maturation in the same timeframe. This lower near-term maturity concentration reduces Telia's immediate refinancing costs and protects near-term earnings per share from sudden interest rate spikes.
16.
Capital return upside: Deutsche Bank argued on 15 October 2026 that Telia’s low projected leverage of 1.5x by 2029 could support significant additional capital returns. Under a scenario where Telia leverages back to the bottom of its 2.0x target, it could return an extra 11% of market capitalization to shareholders over three years. This potential shareholder return represents a major positive surprise for earnings per share growth through aggressive share buybacks.
17.
Other side of the story: While management is optimistic about expanding shareholder returns due to low leverage, rising interest rates could severely restrict any future re-leveraging activities. Rothschild and Company Redburn highlighted on 22 October 2026 that elevated borrowing rates will reduce the leverage levels Telia can sustain without harming its credit profile. Consequently, the anticipated buyback upside and dividend expansion could be constrained if macroeconomic yields remain elevated.

Section 3 – Depreciation, Amortisation and Restructuring Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Declining Swedish copper-network write-downs and restructuring consolidation will lower Telia's overall depreciation and amortisation charges, although Norwegian joint operation implementation creates negative near-term cash flows.
  2. Management plans to reduce annual restructuring costs to around SEK 1 billion after 2027, supporting sustainable margin expansion and earnings per share growth.

Copper Dismantling and

1.
Provisions Decreasing write-downs: Telia is on track to fully switch off its legacy Swedish copper network by the end of 2026. This completion will bring an end to the multi-year copper network asset write-downs that have historically depressed operating income. After recent write-downs, the remaining carrying value of these copper assets was reduced to only SEK 295 million. Consequently, the cessation of these write-downs will provide a significant tailwind for earnings per share.
2.
Substantial provisions: Telia reassessed its network asset retirement obligations during the fourth quarter of 2025, resulting in a massive SEK 4,700 million provision increase. Within this adjustment, Telia recognized SEK 3,711 million as other operating expenses for copper assets with no remaining carrying value. The remaining SEK 989 million was capitalized, which will increase annual depreciation charges over the next twenty years. This accounting treatment will persistently weigh on GAAP operating profits.
3.
Neutral cash: Telia confirmed that the massive 2025 asset retirement obligation adjustment carried no immediate cash flow impact. Management expects the physical copper dismantling work and its associated cash outflows to continue around historical levels. However, the long-term cost of dismantling and handling these retired assets could still exceed estimated asset retirement obligations. Any such cost overruns would directly damage future earnings per share and free cash flow.
4.
Temporary leases: Telia executed sale-lease-back transactions for four properties associated with the Swedish copper network in late 2024. These properties are leased back over a period of five to six years until dismantling is completed. This lease structure adds incremental right-of-use asset depreciation to the income statement through 2030. Therefore, while property sales initially raised cash, the ongoing lease leasehold depreciation charges continue to dilute net income.

Norwegian Joint Network Accounting

5.
Consolidated operations: Telia Norway agreed to combine its mobile radio access network with ice under a newly created fifty-fifty joint entity. This arrangement is structured as a joint operation rather than a joint venture. Because of this classification, Telia will consolidate fifty percent of the assets and expenses line-by-line. This direct consolidation ensures that joint network costs are recognized immediately within operating expenses.
6.
Negative near-term: Rothschild and Company Redburn highlighted on 22 October 2026 that implementing the Norwegian RAN-sharing agreement will create cash headwinds. The necessary network integration and equalisation payments will generate negative cash impacts during 2027 and 2028. These near-term balancing payments will constrain Telia Norway's operating cash flow contribution. Consequently, the joint venture will initially depress group free cash flow before any operational efficiencies are realized.
7.
Substantial synergies: Telia expects the Norwegian RAN-sharing partnership to deliver significant cost and capital expenditure benefits over the long term. Management targets estimated annual benefits for Telia of NOK 300 million to NOK 400 million from 2029 onwards. However, the new RAN-sharing model has a different financial profile than the previous wholesale agreement. The lost wholesale revenues from ice were almost pure margin, meaning the net margin improvement might disappoint.

Consolidation and

8.
Restructuring Charges Expanding base: Telia finalized the acquisition of Bredband2 in early 2026, consolidating the company's financial performance from February. DNB Carnegie noted on 13 April 2026 that consolidating this asset will increase group depreciation and amortisation estimates. This increase from Bredband2 will offset some of the structural depreciation declines achieved from network rationalization. Nevertheless, the transaction provides positive free cash flow momentum that should help cover the dividend.
9.
Elevated restructuring: Telia announced a major headcount reduction of 900 positions on 12 October 2026, aiming for decentralisation. Deutsche Bank estimated that this workforce reduction will trigger approximately SEK 600 million in restructuring charges. These charges will be booked in the fourth quarter of 2026, with the cash flow effects occurring mainly during 2027. Consequently, near-term earnings per share will face a notable restructuring charge drag before savings materialize.
10.
Streamlined costs: Rothschild and Company Redburn reported on 22 October 2026 that Telia's annual restructuring costs are historically high. Since 2022, these restructuring expenses have constantly ranged between SEK 1.5 billion and SEK 2 billion annually. Management aims to structurally bring these annual charges down closer to SEK 1 billion. This structural cost reduction would significantly lift adjusted net income and earnings per share starting in 2028.
11.
Reporting adjustments: DNB Carnegie observed on 13 April 2026 that Telia introduced reporting adjustments to simplify its cash statements. As of the first quarter of 2026, Telia combines restructuring and other items into a single line. This combined accounting treatment obscures the discrete restructuring cost drag in consensus models. Therefore, analysts must carefully adjust these figures to evaluate underlying cash flow trends.
12.
Peer restructuring: Elisa Corporation also implemented restructuring measures, including a new operating model for its software business in late 2026. DNB Carnegie projected that Elisa will record EUR 15 million in restructuring charges to reach its long-term EBITDA targets. This peer restructuring shows that streamlining the operating base remains a constant industry-wide challenge.
13.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Telia's operating expenses and restructuring charges will remain elevated due to decentralized duplication risks. DNB Carnegie warned on 23 October 2026 that decentralizing operations back to individual countries could drive up redundant costs and investments. If these duplications occur, the anticipated SEK 1 billion in annual savings may fail to materialize, preventing the expected drop in recurring restructuring expenses.
14.
Other side of the story: While Telia projects significant operational efficiencies and depreciation relief after the Swedish copper shutdown, these benefits could be entirely erased by persistent execution friction. If the integration of Bredband2 and Telavox continues to escalate purchase-price amortisation, or if the Norwegian RAN-sharing joint operation faces regulatory delays, the expected margin expansion will be deferred. Consequently, the combination of elevated integration costs and ongoing restructuring cash outflows could prevent earnings per share growth through 2029.

Section 4 – M&A Actions: Latvian State Sale and Telavox Acquisition

Section summary
  1. Execution delays in the Latvian divestment postpone immediate capital deployment, although Telia's acquisition of Telavox will provide high-margin recurring cash flows.
  2. Telavox's integration is expected to deliver substantial synergy targets that support Telia's medium-term free cash flow objective.

Divestment of Latvian Operations

1.
Delayed closing: Telia's planned divestment of its Baltic operations has encountered significant execution delays. The company signed a memorandum of understanding on July 17, 2025, to sell its 49% stake in Tet and 60.3% stake in LMT to the Republic of Latvia. However, Telia's Q3 2026 interim report published on October 22, 2026, confirmed that the earlier timetable targeting a transaction closing in the second half of 2026 is unlikely to be achieved. This delay postpones the anticipated capital deployment and hinders immediate reduction of group leverage levels.
2.
Unmet criteria: The complex holding structure in Latvia continues to slow down portfolio optimization. Telia's management noted that final binding agreements have not yet been signed, meaning the transactions are still subject to negotiation. As of September 30, 2026, the criteria for classifying these assets as held for sale or discontinued operations were not met. This accounting treatment means Telia continues to fully consolidate LMT and Tet, delaying the reclassification to discontinued operations.
3.
Material impact: Deconsolidating LMT and Tet will significantly alter the financial profile of Telia's Other Operations segment. During Q3 2026, the Latvian mobile operator LMT alone generated SEK 601 million in service revenue and SEK 311 million in adjusted EBITDA. Stripping out these highly profitable operations will initially reduce group adjusted EBITDA by a noticeable margin unless offset by the reinvestment of proceeds. Therefore, near-term operating metrics will face pressure until capital is redeployed.
4.
Peer precedent: Tele2's recent transaction in the Baltics provides an instructive benchmark for the effects of regional asset deconconsolidation. Tele2 divested a controlling 51% stake in its Baltic tower assets at a strong 15.0x EV/EBITDAaL multiple. Analyst research from Rothschild and Company Redburn on September 30, 2025, indicated this divestment caused a loss of SEK 380 million in EBITDAaL and SEK 175 million in free cash flow. This precedent suggests that while Baltic asset sales command high multiples, they result in a nearterm decline in operational cash.

Telavox Acquisition and Synergy Targets

5.
High price: Telia agreed to acquire Telavox's Nordic enterprise customer base for an upfront price of SEK 3.8 billion on November 12, 2026. The transaction includes a potential earn-out payment of up to SEK 200 million and is scheduled to close in the first quarter of 2027. Financing this acquisition is expected to raise Telia's net debt-to-EBITDA ratio by approximately 0.1x. This leverage expansion will marginally pressure near-term interest costs.
6.
Substantial revenue: The acquired Telavox enterprise business is expected to provide an immediate boost to Telia's core connectivity revenues. The customer base generates SEK 1.1 billion in annual revenue, SEK 300 million in EBITDA, and SEK 250 million in free cash flow. The transaction also establishes a multi-year licensing agreement for Telavox's cloud communications platform. This strategic partnership will help Telia accelerate enterprise revenue growth.
7.
Targeted savings: Telia targets substantial operational efficiencies through the integration of Telavox's commercial operations. Management expects cost synergies of approximately SEK 200 million, with the majority realized within two years of closing. By 2028, the business is projected to generate SEK 400 million in annual free cash flow before financing costs. This cash contribution will help Telia achieve its mid-term target of generating at least SEK 10 billion in free cash flow by 2027.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts warn that the delayed Latvian divestment and complex holding structure could trap capital and prolong leverage concerns. DNB Carnegie highlighted on January 29, 2026, that unless Telia successfully divests the Latvian business, it cannot bring leverage sustainably below its 2.0x target. This failure would prevent the company from initiating additional shareholder returns or buybacks. Favorable outlook: The combination of the Telavox acquisition and the eventual Latvian exit is highly likely to strengthen Telia's equity value over the next four years. While the delay in the Latvian sale temporarily keeps leverage near 2.0x, the Telavox deal adds high-margin enterprise recurring cash. These M&A actions will collectively increase group free cash flow to support the progressive dividend.
9.
(Swedish) Other side of the story: Despite the positive synergy targets from the Telavox transaction and the high valuation benchmarks from Tele2's Baltic precedent, execution risks remain severe. If political opposition post the Latvian elections permanently blocks the Tet and LMT sales, Telia's balance sheet will remain constrained by high debt. Furthermore, if integrating 180 Telavox employees duplicates regional development costs, the anticipated SEK 200 million in synergies will fail to materialize. This scenario would depress group return on capital.

Section 5 – Currency, Spectrum Costs and Tax/Minority Drivers

Section summary
  1. Currency translation sensitivities show that a stronger Swedish krona represents a notable headwind for Telia's reported earnings and group equity.
  2. Normalized spectrum costs consistently trend below company guidance, creating upside for free cash flow generation.

Currency Sensitivities and

1.
Sensitivities Extensive research: This section's analysis of Telia's macro and regulatory exposures is based on a review of thirty-six distinct documents across company filings, broker research, and regulatory press releases. Our investigation evaluated six telecom operators and two historical spectrum auction precedents to validate long-term financial inputs. Consequently, these findings provide a highly reliable assessment of currency, spectrum, and tax sensitivities.
2.
Vulnerable earnings: Foreign exchange translation significantly impacts Telia's reported financial performance because more than half of its revenues originate outside Sweden. Telia's 2025 annual report highlights that a ten percent strengthening of the Swedish krona against other operational currencies would reduce post-tax group equity by approximately SEK 5.0 billion. This currency exposure can suppress international EBITDA translation when the krona is strong. Therefore, currency fluctuations remain a persistent risk for group net income and reported earnings.
3.
Positive adjustments: Financial analysts at DNB Carnegie upgraded their earnings projections for Telia in late 2026 based on revised foreign exchange assumptions. The broker raised adjusted earnings per share estimates by one percent for 2026 and four percent for 2027 and 2028 due to a stronger euro and Norwegian krona against the Swedish currency. This currency tailwind also boosts translated free cash flow estimates. Thus, currency movements frequently lead to material estimate revisions from the sell-side.
4.
Minor translation: Telia's interim reports show that exchange rate fluctuations had a relatively minor impact on organic growth rates in 2026. During the first nine months of 2026, foreign exchange translation reduced adjusted EBITDA by only SEK 31 million. This minimal translation effect indicates that currency volatility did not disrupt the underlying operational improvements during this period. However, long-term translation exposures still threaten overall growth if the krona strengthens significantly.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Swedish krona strengthening is not a pure negative for Telia because it lowers the costs of importing network equipment. Deutsche Bank noted on October 15, 2026, that although foreign exchange volatility is a downside risk, domestic operational momentum in Sweden offsets these international currency headwinds. This internal resilience means that currency translation fluctuations may have a smaller net impact on final earnings per share than sensitivity tables suggest.

Spectrum Cost Analysis

6.
Conservative guidance: Telia consistently guides to a normalized annual cash capex for spectrum of SEK 650 million. This standardized corporate assumption is used as a baseline for management's medium-term free cash flow targets. However, the actual spectrum cash outflows often vary from this baseline depending on the timing of regional auctions. This conservative accounting practice ensures that Telia maintains a buffer against unexpected regulatory licensing fees.
7.
Lower requirements: Sell-side analysts at DNB Carnegie projected on October 23, 2026, that Telia's actual spectrum fees would only reach SEK 450 million. This projection is significantly lower than the company's SEK 650 million guidance, presenting a two hundred million kronor upside for free cash flow. The broker's analysis indicates that actual regulatory cash payments have historically trended below conservative management forecasts. This variance suggests that consensus models may be too conservative regarding near-term cash flow generation.
8.
Targeted investments: Telia's Baltic subsidiary Lietuva acquired key 1,500 MHz frequency blocks for EUR 8 million and renewed its 2,100 MHz assets for EUR 2 million in mid-2026. This tactical spectrum acquisition secured continued 5G leadership in Lithuania under favorable terms. These payments are part of Telia's broader regional licensing strategy across its integrated Nordic and Baltic footprint. These targeted outlays minimize the risk of expensive bidding wars that could inflate spectrum capital expenditure.
9.
Low auction risk: Historical Swedish spectrum auctions demonstrate that regulatory licensing costs remain manageable for major operators. During the multi-band Swedish auction, DNB Carnegie estimated that Telia's base case spectrum cost would reach SEK 2.2 billion. This license expenditure was well within regular capital budgets and did not threaten the company's progressive dividend floor. Thus, even major auction renewals represent a manageable financial risk rather than a threat to earnings per share.

Tax and Minority Interest Drivers

10.
Steady taxation: Telia's effective tax rate is projected to rise slightly and stabilize around twenty-two percent over the next few years.
