The bank reported net profit of SEK 7,195 million, exceeding consensus estimates by a modest 1%, while achieving an adjusted return on equity of 15.5% that substantially surpassed consensus expectations of 14.3% by 124 basis points. This outperformance was driven by exceptionally strong fee income and trading revenue that successfully offset compressed net interest margins and elevated operating expenses from ongoing restructuring initiatives.

The most consequential development was the final resolution of all historical anti-money laundering investigations. Swedbank reached a USD 50 million settlement with the New York State Department of Financial Services on July 16, 2026, officially closing the last regulatory overhang that had suppressed the bank's valuation multiple for over six years. This clearance follows earlier closures by the US Department of Justice, Securities and Exchange Commission, and Estonian authorities, collectively removing the structural compliance risk that dominated sell-side discourse throughout 2024 and 2025. Management emphasized that no operational restrictions or probation periods were imposed, allowing the executive team to pivot its full attention toward commercial execution and shareholder capital returns. Swedbank's commercial momentum demonstrated a decisive turnaround in Swedish mortgage market share. The bank captured 20% of front-book mortgage volume during the first two months of the quarter through its own distribution channels, representing a dramatic recovery from market shares of just 5% in 2024 and 10% in 2025. This rebound was supported by the successful integration of digital mortgage challenger Stabelo, acquired in November 2025, which contributed SEK 17 billion in loan volumes and opened new customer acquisition pathways. However, this volume recovery was achieved in an environment of intense mortgage pricing competition that compressed realized lending margins across the Swedish banking system. The Baltic Banking division remained the bank's structural profitability engine. Lending volumes in Estonia,

Latvia, and Lithuania expanded by 3% sequentially in local currency, maintaining the region's exceptional 19% return on allocated equity. Baltic mortgage and corporate lending growth of 12% year-over-year provided a critical offset to margin pressure in Sweden, reinforcing the strategic importance of the bank's dual-region business model. Nevertheless, the Baltic operations faced localized headwinds from the temporary Latvian bank windfall tax, which extracted SEK 193 million in Q2 2026, and severe Lithuanian pension reform outflows that triggered SEK 18 billion in mutual fund redemptions during the quarter.

Net interest income performance slightly missed consensus expectations despite sequential growth. Net interest income rose 1% sequentially to SEK 11,276 million but fell short of company consensus by 1% as elevated wholesale funding costs and compressed Swedish mortgage spreads offset Baltic volume tailwinds. Critically, Swedbank revised its net interest income sensitivity upward to SEK 3.0 billion for a parallel 50 basis point rate increase, highlighting the bank's exceptionally high exposure to interest rate movements relative to hedged European peers. This unhedged balance sheet positioning creates substantial asymmetric risk if the Riksbank and ECB implement deeper rate cuts than currently anticipated by management's base case scenario. Fee and trading income delivered exceptional upside relative to expectations. Net commission income reached SEK 4,462 million, beating consensus by 4%, driven by rising equity markets and SEK 22 billion in net inflows into Swedbank Robur mutual funds. Net gains and losses from financial transactions surged 54% sequentially to SEK 1,060 million, substantially exceeding consensus of SEK 745 million due to robust fixed income and debt capital markets activity facilitated by the newly launched SB1 Markets partnership. This capitallight revenue strength demonstrates the bank's successful pivot toward advisory and wealth management fee streams that are less sensitive to interest rate volatility.

Operating expenses overshot consensus targets due to restructuring charges and Baltic wage inflation. Total costs reached SEK 7,854 million, which was 4% higher than consensus expectations, inflated by an extraordinary restructuring charge of SEK 860 million. Management is executing a comprehensive efficiency program designed to deliver SEK 1 billion in annual run-rate cost savings by year-end 2028 through IT platform consolidations and a planned workforce reduction from approximately 17,350 to 16,800 full-time employees. While the bank is taking a total SEK 1.3 billion restructuring charge in 2026, sell-side analysts remain divided on whether the anticipated synergies will materialize quickly enough to prevent the cost-to-income ratio from breaching the 0.40 medium-term target in 2027.

Capital adequacy remained robust despite sequential erosion from volume growth. The Common Equity

Tier 1 ratio contracted by 14 basis points to 17.4%, leaving Swedbank with a 260 basis point buffer above regulatory requirements at the upper end of its 100 to 300 basis point target range. Strong lending volume growth added SEK 21 billion to risk exposure amounts, while the temporary Article 3 add-on of SEK 11 billion for Baltic retail exposures pending ECB model approval continued to constrain reported capital ratios. Management reiterated its commitment to distribute 60% to 70% of profits as ordinary dividends and signaled openness to special capital distributions once the DFS settlement is fully absorbed, though no formal announcement was made during the quarter.

Sell-side analysts upgraded ratings and price targets but remained divided on valuation premium sustainability.

Morgan Stanley's Gulnara Saitkulova upgraded Swedbank to Equal-weight from Underweight on September 1, 2026, raising her price target to SEK 386 from SEK 330, citing the bank's operational turnaround and Swedish mortgage market share recovery as core catalysts. Deutsche Bank's Marlene Eibensteiner reiterated her Buy rating and raised her target to SEK 388, emphasizing a turning point in net interest income momentum.

However, DNB Carnegie's Nicolas McBeath and Jefferies' Alexander Demetriou maintained Hold ratings, warning that Swedbank's 12.5x forward price-to-earnings ratio represents a 10% premium to Nordic peers that fully reflects its structural profitability advantages and limits further near-term upside.

The overarching investment debate centers on whether Swedbank's unhedged interest rate sensitivity represents an opportunity or a structural vulnerability. Bulls argue that if the Riksbank and ECB resume interest rate hikes in 2027 due to persistent inflation, Swedbank's high net interest income sensitivity will drive earnings substantially ahead of conservative consensus expectations and support return on equity well above its 15% medium-term target. Bears counter that if policy rates fall below 1% in a prolonged deflationary scenario, Swedbank's return on allocated equity would likely plunge to 11%, eroding its current trading premium and threatening dividend sustainability.

The resolution of historical compliance risks has removed a major valuation anchor, but the bank's future performance remains highly path-dependent on macroeconomic interest rate trajectories that lie outside management's direct control.
