Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Softcat entered the FY2026 earnings event with a premium valuation and exceptional historic momentum, having established a twenty-year track record of double-digit gross profit growth.
  2. Sell-side expectations were high after successive guidance upgrades in H1 and Q3 FY2026, though brokers recently moderated ratings to Hold on valuation concerns.

2.

Its revenue breakdown is highly diversified, with software representing 57%, hardware at 28%, and services comprising 15% of gross invoiced income in fiscal 2025.

Section 16 – Conclusion

However, the strongest arguments against the thesis are equally compelling and cannot be dismissed. Jefferies analyst Brennan criticized the GDT acquisition price, noting that Softcat is paying $1.05 billion for $80 million of EBITDA, whereas peer Computacenter spent only $500 million on US acquisitions generating $350 million in EBIT, highlighting a potentially dilutive capital allocation decision. The GDT transaction introduces leverage to a historically debt-free balance sheet, pushing net debt to 1.3x EBITDA post-closing, which fundamentally alters the risk profile and could trigger permanent multiple compression if integration falters. A single direct customer accounted for £502.2 million, representing a massive 25.4% of group revenue at a considerably lower gross profit margin, exposing the group to severe margin and revenue volatility if this customer scales back spending in FY2027. Additionally, Softcat's primary supply-side dependency remains heavily centered on Microsoft, which historically accounts for approximately 50% of group gross profits, creating acute vendor concentration risk at a time when Microsoft is actively restructuring partner incentives.

The thesis would be definitively proven wrong by several observable data points over the next 18 to 36 months. First, if standalone UK organic gross profit growth decelerates below mid-single digits for two consecutive halves while peer Computacenter sustains high-single-digit growth, it would signal structural market share loss rather than cyclical normalization. Second, if GDT's gross profit growth slows below 10% annually during calendar years 2027 and 2028, it would confirm that the neocloud infrastructure boom was transitory rather than structural.

Third, if Softcat's EBIT-to-gross-profit conversion margin falls below 33% on a sustained basis due to integration costs or employee attrition, it would demonstrate that operational complexity is permanently diluting profitability. Fourth, if net debt leverage remains above 1.0x EBITDA beyond July 2029, it would indicate weak cash generation and challenge management's deleveraging commitments. Finally, if the largest customer's revenue contribution exceeds 30% of group revenues or if this customer relationship terminates unexpectedly, it would expose catastrophic concentration risk.

Looking forward to future earnings calls, investors should focus on several critical questions. What is the sequential quarterly gross profit growth in the standalone UK business excluding the large customer contribution, and how does this compare to the mid-single-digit guidance? What percentage of GDT's FY2026 gross profit growth was driven by neocloud infrastructure sales, and what is the organic run-rate excluding these customers?

What is the pipeline value and conversion rate of cross-selling opportunities between Softcat's UK customer base and GDT's US technical capabilities? The concluding counter-view challenges the prevailing sell-side caution and suggests that the market may be underestimating the structural demand tailwinds from enterprise AI deployment. Enterprise customers are actively prioritizing high-value, complex artificial intelligence compute workloads and hybrid cloud data center modernization over simple transactional commodity refreshes, creating sustained demand for precisely the type of multi-vendor integration expertise that the combined Softcat-GDT entity can deliver.
