[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Carel is a demand-inflection case in a high-quality controls niche, already delivered in the numbers and already paid for in the price. Two structural forces, AI data-centre cooling in North America and the UK and the European F-Gas shift to natural refrigerants, have joined a cyclical recovery in European residential heat pumps to lift organic growth to 23.0 percent and the EBITDA margin to 22.5 percent in H1 2026, and the sell-side has followed; at EUR 27.95 the shares pay about 12 percent a year on our modal path against the 20 percent bar, so the business case is compelling and the share-price case at this price is not.

  • The change is external, structural in two of its three legs, and sector-wide.
    • Data-centre cooling and the F-Gas transition are regulation and hyperscaler capital spending acting on the whole sector, not a company story
    • The third leg, European residential heat pumps, is a cyclical turn with policy visibility.
    • North American revenues grew 55.7 percent at constant currency in H1 2026, with data-centre revenues nearly doubling
      • BELIMO's data-centre revenues grew 93 percent in local currency over the same half, which is the corroboration outside Carel.
    • Refrigeration organic growth was above 30 percent in Q2 2026 under the revised EU F-Gas regulation (published 20-Feb-24, high-GWP phase-out by 2030)
      • Residential heat pumps grew close to 30 percent in early 2026, concentrated in Germany and Northern Europe.
    • The internal forces are real but second-order: a flatter structure, bolt-on acquisitions, a North Carolina plant due early 2027, incentives moved to cash flow
      • None is a chief-executive change or a guidance step-up.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, which is why it is material rather than promised.
    • It shows in the company's actions, its operating metrics, its financial statements and the sell-side's estimates.
    • Organic growth 23.0 percent at constant currency in H1 2026 (25.5 percent reported in Q2)
      • The EBITDA margin 22.5 percent, 350 basis points above a year earlier and above the company's own 19 to 20 percent frame
      • Net profit up 72.5 percent to EUR 45.7m.
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS has risen 25.7 percent over twelve months and FY2027 by 21.6 percent
      • Every visible broker's estimate is dated after the H1 print.
  • The earnings path is inside the target band at eighteen months and at its floor at three years, and the margin carries the difference.
    • Our modal EPS on the brokers' basis runs from EUR 0.650 in FY2025 to about EUR 0.83 in FY2026, EUR 0.95 in FY2027 and EUR 1.06 in FY2028, a little above consensus (EUR 0.821, EUR 0.930, EUR 1.05) on margin, then high-single-digit growth.
    • That is about 14 percent a year over eighteen months and about 12 percent a year over three years from the FY2026 base, built from revenue first and margin second
      • Buybacks contribute nothing.
    • The margin leg rests on holding 22.5 percent against a company frame of 19 to 20 percent
      • The company guides low and beats, which is why we sit above consensus rather than below it.
  • The price already pays for the FY2028 earnings, so the chain breaks at the price, not at the business.
    • At EUR 27.95 the shares trade at 27.6 times earnings 24 months forward, the 43rd percentile of their own ten-year history and within a point of the median
    • The re-rating leg is not ours to earn.
    • The shares returned 23.1 percent over twelve months and 18.0 percent over six, and fell 8.4 percent over the three months after the 4-Aug-26 beat
      • The May 2026 placement at EUR 30.10 says the price was already there before the print.
    • On the framework's valuation convention (today's multiple held and applied to earnings two years beyond each horizon) the modal total return is about 12.5 percent a year over eighteen months and about 11 percent a year over three years, including a cash return of about 1 percent a year
      • A D on the framework's anchors.
  • The case clears the 20 percent bar only below about EUR 24, or on a branch that needs two things at once.
    • Out-year growth of 15 percent a year or more to FY2030 and a multiple of 32 times or more at the horizon together give about 21 to 29 percent a year
    • The conditions are not independent and we put the branch at about 15 percent, unlikely.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', crisply on the mechanism and loosely on one point.
    • Four of the five conditions sit at B or A
    • The hardware is one-to-one and growth consumes capital (EUR 32.2m of working capital in H1 2026)
    • The pattern's own warning applies, an inflection is cyclical until proven structural, and this one has been paid for after a 23 percent twelve-month rise.

  • The case therefore is a strong business at a full price.

    • The chain holds from change to earnings and the market has priced what it can see
    • The return at EUR 27.95 is about 12 percent a year, which is not sufficient.

  • Rating = C, because the input forces (B) and the financial outputs (B) are material and already visible in the statements and the estimates, but at EUR 27.95 the modal return is about 12 percent a year, which the framework anchors at a D, so the chain from change to return breaks at the price.

    • Not B, because the Building Block's question includes a sufficient return and a 12 percent modal return is not sufficient.
    • Not D, because the change itself is real, large and already delivered in the outputs.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: H1 2026 is a cyclical rebound plus a data-centre spike read as structural, and the numbers flatter it.
      • Visibility is three to five weeks
        • Residential demand rebounds from a destocked trough on restored German subsidies that politics can remove
        • EMEA refrigeration above 30 percent in Q2 2026 may be delayed projects catching up.
      • The 22.5 percent margin is volume on fixed cost and reverses with volume, as it did in 2024 (21.1 to 18.1 percent)
        • 15 plants of fixed cost sit under a volume-driven P&L.
      • The earnings base is flattered: a EUR 19.0m revaluation gain on minority option liabilities in 2025
        • R&D capitalised at 15.11 percent against BELIMO's 4.81 percent
        • Inventory provisions at 23.04 percent of gross inventory
        • Kiona impaired by EUR 11.75m and CFM by EUR 3.89m in 2025.
      • Cash flow was helped by an inventory drawdown now reversing: inventory EUR 102.4m at June 2026 against EUR 88.5m at December 2025.
      • The bear model gives EUR 9.36 a share (EUR 0.52 of EPS at 18 times)
        • A residential subsidy reversal or a hyperscaler pause would hit a volume-driven P&L with three weeks' notice.
    • What we concede: the visibility, the inventory build, the Q4-FY2025 earnings base (net income of EUR 31.3m on EBIT of EUR 22.4m), the impairment record and the cyclical component of the inflection.
      • All of it is in our riskiness and our drop-through sensitivity.
    • Why we hold our view on the business: Q2 2026 came after the bear digest and answered its central "deceptive recovery" claim with revenue up 25.5 percent reported and a 23.3 percent EBITDA margin.
      • The structural legs are corroborated outside Carel: BELIMO's data-centre revenues up 93 percent
        • F-Gas in law with quotas cut over 80 percent by 2030
        • The company gaining share in North American CO2 refrigeration during the transition.
      • The 2024 downturn held the margin above 18 percent with no price erosion
        • The balance sheet is net cash (EUR 7.1m at June 2026; EUR 38.0m before IFRS 16).
      • The invalidation thresholds are specific and not close on the Q3 guide: organic growth below 10 percent, margin below 20 percent, working capital above 25 percent of sales, each for two quarters.
    • Where we agree with the bear: the price leaves no room.
      • The competing hypothesis does not need to be right for the return to fail
      • The modal path fails the bar on its own, which is why the return, not the thesis, is the weak link.
