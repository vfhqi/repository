The bear case analysis reveals several persistent plausible problem to monitor where Hacksaw Gaming demonstrates meaningful structural vulnerabilities relative to peer benchmarks and industry norms. These signals represent earlywarning indicators that merit continuous monitoring despite the company's strong operational cash generation and growth trajectory. First, unregulated market concentration emerges as the most severe structural plausible problem to monitor, with Hacksaw deriving exposure from unregulated markets while peers like Evolution faced targeted regulatory scrutiny.

The ACH matrix confirms that unregulated markets represent 88% of revenues, creating a vulnerability that exceeds peer median exposure levels. While the Swedish Gaming Authority initially fined Hacksaw SEK 2.6 million for software distribution non-compliance, the penalty was reduced to SEK 20,000 on appeal, suggesting regulatory friction remains manageable but omnipresent. Second, key personnel dependence represents a governance-level plausible problem to monitor, particularly highlighted by the sudden CEO departure in April 2026.

The creative execution cluster analysis identifies that rapid developer wage inflation and leadership transitions disrupt the vital game pipeline since highly specialized talent remains scarce. Unlike more institutionalized peers, Hacksaw's reliance on founding personnel like Karl Rausing and Marcus Cordes creates single-point-of-failure risk that peer Evolution has largely mitigated through deeper organizational bench strength.

Third, customer concentration risk manifests as a structural plausible problem to monitor despite strong client diversification efforts. The top ten customers represented 44% of consolidated group revenue in 2025, while high customer concentration combined with operator platform insourcing threatens recurring royalty streams if major operators transition to in-house options. This concentration exceeds the peer median threshold for B2B digital suppliers and creates asymmetric downside vulnerability.

Fourth, absent hedging policy emerges as a treasury management plausible problem to monitor, with Hacksaw Gaming having no active hedging policy at the moment while relying entirely on spot conversions and natural operational hedges. In contrast, peer Evolution does not use derivative instruments but peer Kambi manages risk by holding short-term deposits in currencies matching principal cash outflows, avoiding complex financial derivative instruments in favor of conservative cash management balances.

Unhedged player-level translation headwinds will likely persistently drag reported top-line growth below organic volume baselines, though the reporting currency shift to euros in 2026 eliminates historical SEK presentation volatility.

Outliers worth noting

Three extreme statistical outliers and structural anomalies emerge from the comprehensive analysis of Hacksaw Gaming, each representing significant departures from peer benchmarks and industry norms that warrant immediate attention. The first and most severe outlier is Hacksaw's 88% revenue concentration in unregulated or unverifiable jurisdictions, representing an extreme statistical deviation from licensed peer operators.

This concentration exists despite a relatively balanced invoicing currency split of 77% EUR and 23% GBP, creating a fundamental disconnect between contractual presentation and underlying operational reality. The Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix classified this metric as highly diagnostic evidence, with Jefferies applying a 50% WACC beta premium specifically to reflect this structural exposure risk. This outlier directly maps to the pre-mortem failure mode where coordinated global regulatory blacklists targeting offshore supply routes could trigger immediate revenue collapse, as operators would be forced to block Hacksaw content to protect their own licenses.
