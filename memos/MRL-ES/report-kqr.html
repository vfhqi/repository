Section 2 – Spain's Data-Centre Decree and MERLIN Properties' Pipeline

Section summary
  1. Spain's softened data-centre decree drastically reduces the execution risk for MERLIN Properties' development pipeline.
  2. Grandfathering clauses and Portuguese strategic options protect 532 megawatts of MERLIN Properties' planned capacity from compliance penalties.
  3. We have high confidence in these findings, supported by company disclosures and late October 2026 broker analysis.

Restrictive early legislative drafts

1.
Restricting proposals: The initial August 2026 Spanish draft decree threatened to halt massive digital infrastructure investments. Morgan Stanley reported on September 21, 2026, that the proposal demanded 80% hourly matching with new renewables and offered no phase-in period. This stringent framework aimed to link data-centre grid access directly to newly commissioned generation. Such severe conditions would materially escalate power procurement costs and delay development timelines.
2.
Demanding standards: The early legislative text imposed additionality rules that tied data centre expansion to very young renewable projects. Jefferies reported on August 26, 2026, that every single megawatt of new demand had to be backed by a megawatt of green power commissioned within the preceding 18 months. The rule covered all facilities above a 1MW threshold. This immediate operational constraint threatened to severely bottleneck Spanish pipeline execution for several years.
3.
Regulatory comparisons: Spain proposed a framework far more restrictive than neighboring European jurisdictions. Morgan Stanley reported on October 26, 2026, that Ireland allows a six-year ramp-up period to meet its renewable matching targets. Conversely, the initial Spanish decree required simultaneous commercial operation of both the data centre and its dedicated renewable source. This lack of flexibility made Spain the most difficult European market for power procurement.
4.
Past precedent: Similar grid-management interventions in other regions demonstrate that rapid demand growth often forces regulatory pivots. Jefferies reported on September 22, 2026, that Texas paused all new data centre permits in August 2026 to audit grid connections. This temporary freeze impacted both front-of-themeter and behind-the-meter projects. The Texas precedent suggests that unplanned grid capacity constraints eventually require legislative intervention.

Revised October regulatory relief

5.
Softened requirements: The Spanish government unveiled a revised draft in late October 2026 that significantly eases compliance. Bloomberg reported on October 26, 2026, that the updated text extends the transition timeline for developers to meet green targets. Rather than immediate 80% hourly matching, operators must meet 60% of peak-hour demand with renewables until 2029. This requirement will climb to 70% in 2029 and finally reach 80% in 2032.
6.
Elevated thresholds: Raising the applicability limit provides substantial relief to smaller developers across the Spanish market. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 28, 2026, that the capacity threshold for the rules was increased from 1MW to 10MW. This change effectively exempts smaller facilities while focusing regulation on major hubs. The amendment ensures that only truly large data centres face complex compliance audits.
7.
Increased flexibility: The revised draft doubles the eligibility window for qualifying renewable assets to ease sourcing pressures. Jefferies reported on October 27, 2026, that the age limit for associated green projects was increased from 18 to 36 months. Additionally, the new draft exempts solar installations from this age restriction entirely. These modifications reduce near-term execution risks for developers ramping up Spanish platforms.
8.
Alternative pathways: The government introduced specific battery integration rules to help operators meet nighttime demand. A regulatory news report on October 26, 2026, highlighted that developers can utilize energy storage systems to satisfy compliance. Electricity injected from large batteries during nonsolar hours will count as renewable energy. This battery provision promotes localized energy orchestration over speculative grid connection requests.

Quantified pipeline exposure risk

9.
Quantified exposure: MERLIN Properties faces a significantly reduced threat of project disruption under the revised legislation. Bernstein Research reported on October 12, 2026, that a downside scenario under the original draft put 192MW of the company's 724MW pipeline at risk. This exposure represented approximately 27% of its total planned capacity. However, the softened rules mean the execution risk is now materially lower.
10.
Protected capacity: A substantial portion of MERLIN's pipeline is protected by grid connections or construction progress. Bernstein Research reported on October 12, 2026, that the Madrid-Getafe facility of 48MW is already connected and out of scope. Furthermore, the 96MW Bilbao asset is pre-let and expected to be grandfathered due to its advanced construction. These protected assets ensure that legacy development remains on track to generate rent.
11.
Strategic offsets: MERLIN Properties is leveraging its Portuguese portfolio to mitigate any remaining Spanish regulatory drag. Morgan Stanley reported on October 26, 2026, that the company can reallocate capacity to Portugal, where it has 200MW of IT capacity pending grid approval. This geographical diversification protects the long-term rental target of €1.20 billion by 2032. This strategic flexibility ensures that future net asset value is shielded from domestic shocks.
12.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on October 28, 2026, that the revised data-centre decree is expected to have a minor impact because the five-year transition period provides ample time for operators to adjust. The investment barriers highlighted in early drafts have been watered down by utilities' active lobbying and negotiations. These amendments ensure that the regulatory impact will be minor for large integrated players.

Industry pushback and legal challenges

13.
Continued friction: Major technology companies continue to reject the revised framework due to operational risks. A news report on October 27, 2026, indicated that the Spanish data-centre association SpainDC expressed absolute rejection of the updated draft. The main point of contention is the requirement to accept grid disconnections without compensation during power strain. This operational risk could deter hyperscaler capital allocation in Spain.
14.
Disconnection penalties: The transition regime forces data centres to accept operational vulnerabilities in exchange for compliance delays. A news report on October 27, 2026, highlighted that SpainDC considers the disconnection clause extremely grave. Operators must agree that Red Eléctrica can order shutdowns during peak periods. Activating backup diesel generators imposes exorbitant operational costs on tenants.
15.
Impending litigation: Tech giants are organizing a coordinated legal campaign to block the decree. A news report on November 1, 2026, revealed that SpainDC is preparing lawsuits at the Council of State. The association aims to delay the regulation's approval by involving the European Commission. This legal offensive could stall final decree implementation past the elections.
16.
Other side of the story: While tech associations warn of massive investment flight, integrated utilities and prepared developers view the decree as a positive driver for structured growth. Morgan Stanley reported on October 28, 2026, that Spanish utility comments advocate for a softening of requirements to support overall data centre development. Furthermore, the firm's analysts highlighted on September 10, 2026, that integrated business models with access to diversified generation portfolios are best positioned to meet these rules. Consequently, the decree will weed out speculative developers and strengthen the competitive position of well-capitalized, integrated market leaders.

Section 4 – Icade's 130-MW Orly-Rungis Campus Partner Selection

Section summary
  1. Icade is shifting to a joint operating partnership for its 130-megawatt Orly-Rungis campus to target higher yields of 10%.
  2. Negotiations for a joint venture partner remain in progress with actual construction scheduled for completion in 2031.

Strategic partnership structure

1.
High-yield transition: Icade is actively transitioning from a traditional powered shell business model to a joint operating partnership structure. This strategic shift targets an improved yield on cost of approximately 10% on the massive campus. This return profile represents a significant premium over the 5% to 6% yields historically achieved on its legacy powered shells. Transitioning to this high-yield framework is expected to substantially improve future capital efficiency for the developer.
2.
Asset comparison: Icade delivered a 10 MW Equinix powered shell in August 2026 that serves as a benchmark for its historical model. That traditional asset yielded 6.20% on an investment of €36 million under a firm 9-year lease. Conversely, the Orly-Rungis campus is designed to deliver a massive 130 MW of IT capacity over 65,000 square meters. Adopting the joint operating model on this larger site should unlock far superior development margins relative to legacy shells.
3.
Financial metrics: Icade plans to allocate €280 million in capital expenditures to construct the massive Orly- Rungis campus. The completed facility is projected to generate €20 million in annualized revenues. Management intends to pursue a joint venture with a seasoned operator to share these heavy funding requirements. This co-investment approach will allow the group to limit direct balance sheet exposure while maximizing developer fees.

Development timeline and pre-leasing

4.
Regulatory approvals: Icade secured a critical grid connection offer for the 130 MW power demand in 2024. The developer followed this by obtaining a building permit cleared of all legal appeals in 2025. These early achievements significantly de-risk the initial pre-construction phase of the project. Securing early power and permit clearance ensures the development remains protected from regulatory delays that plague peers.
5.
Project timeline: Bernstein Research reported on October 16, 2025, that the Orly-Rungis hyperscale opportunity offers excellent medium-term development potential. The analysts noted that full project delivery is scheduled for 2031 once the necessary power is fully secured. This extended timeline means that absolute rental income contributions will remain minimal over the next five years. Investors must realize that longterm digital infrastructure execution will not drive near-term earnings growth.
6.
Return visibility: Deutsche Bank Research reported on February 20, 2024, that the Orly-Rungis project is a complex development requiring substantial capital. The broker highlighted that the investment plan carries a limited level of visibility on actual long-term returns. These high capital requirements on commercial business parks could strain the developer's balance sheet unless shared. Consequently, the group must secure an equity partner to mitigate capital allocation risks associated with the site.
7.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research reported on October 20, 2026, that there is still no concrete advancement on the selection of a partner. Although Icade initiated the tender process early, negotiations with potential operators have not yielded signed agreements. This lack of concrete progress threatens to delay initial groundwork and push back the scheduled 2031 completion. Any prolonged delay in partner selection will stall the anticipated earnings inflection from the data-centre division.
8.
Overall judgement: Successful implementation of the joint venture model remains highly likely because Icade holds fully permitted land with secured power. However, the actual earnings contribution will remain extremely long-dated given the projected 2031 delivery timeline. While the 10% target yield on cost is attractive, the slow execution pace limits near-term positive impacts on earnings per share. Thus, the Orly-Rungis project remains a distant driver of equity value for patient investors.
9.
Other side of the story: Delays in partner selection could actually benefit Icade by allowing the company to negotiate more favorable leasing terms as demand intensifies. Global hyperscaler demand for secured powered land near Paris is expanding rapidly as central municipal connections become scarcer. By waiting for a more competitive bidding environment, Icade can extract higher equity valuations for its land bank. Consequently, a slower tender process may ultimately secure superior long-term yields on cost exceeding 10%.
