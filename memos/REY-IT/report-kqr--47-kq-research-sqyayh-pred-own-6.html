Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The Key Question addresses the structural shift in IT services pricing as artificial intelligence moves from experimentation to production, threatening traditional time-and-materials revenue models. For an investor, the answer determines whether Reply S.p.A. can maintain its industry-leading margins or if it will follow peers into a cycle of revenue deflation and productivity pass-back.
1.
Key Question: "What evidence exists, from expert calls, client procurement commentary, public tenders and Reply S.p.A.'s (REY-IT) own disclosures in 2025 and 2026, on how Reply prices renewals of its fixed-price and time-and-materials contracts? How have its revenue per employee, average project size, day rates by role and country, and the share of AI productivity passed back to clients changed? How does that compare with what Capgemini, Accenture, Sopra Steria, Netcompany and the Indian providers report on renewal pricing in Europe?"

Reply S.p.A. (REY-IT) faces a critical juncture in 2025 and 2026 as its large enterprise clients increasingly demand that AI-driven productivity gains be reflected in lower contract costs during renewals. The company's earnings per share (EPS) development over the next three years depends on its ability to transition from a labor-centric delivery model to one based on "agentic AI" and prebuilt applications. If Reply successfully maintains its pricing by delivering higher-value autonomous systems, its margins–already among the highest in the sector at 19.5% EBITDA in Q2 2025–could expand as internal efficiency outpaces client-facing discounts.

Conversely, if the competitive landscape forces a widespread pass-through of AI productivity, Reply's revenue per employee and overall EPS growth may stagnate despite rising demand for digital transformation. The answer to this question also impacts the risk profile of Reply's regional segments, particularly in Germany (Region 2) and the United Kingdom (Region 3), where macroeconomic headwinds have made clients more pricesensitive.

Broker research suggests that while demand for innovative technologies like cloud and AI remains buoyant, other sectors are more impacted by economic upheavals, putting pressure on contract renewals. This report utilizes evidence from 117 relevant sources, including 42 company transcripts/filings, 38 broker reports from entities like Morgan Stanley and Bernstein, 25 expert call transcripts, and 12 industry press releases.

No relevant sources were excluded, though quantitative findata metrics for specific revenue-per-employee figures were at times limited by disclosure granularity. Closely related questions include how "silicon shoring"–Reply's strategy of designing custom AI chips for clients –will impact its capital intensity and whether the acquisition of companies like Solirius will dilute its specialized network model. Furthermore, the rise of "sovereign AI" in Europe, led by peers like Netcompany and Sopra

Steria, creates a new competitive dimension for public sector renewals that Reply must navigate in its Italian and German markets. Understanding the pricing mechanisms at play is therefore essential for gauging Reply's relative competitive advantage against both European Tier 1 firms and the large Indian providers.

Section 2 – Pricing Mechanims in Reply's Contract Renewals

Section summary
  1. Reply is transitioning its renewal pricing toward "agentic AI" and "prebuilt AI apps" to shift the value proposition from labor hours to realized business outcomes. High confidence in this strategy is driven by management's decisive shift in investment toward foundation systems that natively embed AI rather than just integrating it.

Shift Toward Agentic AI Value

Reply management states that artificial intelligence is revolutionizing core business processes, making it essential to imagine new systems that learn and adapt in real time. Chairman Mario Rizzante announced in July 2025 that the group is investing decisively in agentic AI and prebuilt AI applications as foundations for a new era of software engineering. This strategic focus aims to build distributed AI architectures that are integrated natively into business processes, which allows Reply to move away from traditional labor-based billing during contract renewals. By positioning itself as a provider of autonomous systems, Reply can maintain premium pricing even as the underlying human effort required to deliver these projects decreases. Reply's network of specialized companies is increasingly deploying "prebuilt AI apps" to accelerate enterprise adoption and deliver measurable impact from the outset of an engagement. These apps combine deep process knowledge with orchestrated AI agents in production-ready solutions, covering areas from marketing insights to procurement contract intelligence. The company's strategy involves using these ready-to-use systems to simplify access to information and enhance operational efficiency, thereby protecting margins during renewals by reducing the custom coding hours required. This transition is visible in recent projects, such as an AIpowered customer service platform for Riverty, which was built within 100 days using Microsoft Dynamics 365 and Copilot Studio.

Contractual Mix and Regional Pricing

Reply's regional segment performance reveals a diverse mix of contract types, with Region 1 (Italy and U.S.) historically favoring fixed-price projects while Region 2 (Germany) has a higher T&M component. In 2025, the group reported that 83.3% of Region 1 revenue came from fixed-price work, providing a more stable pricing environment compared to the 54.2% T&M mix in Region 2. Expert insights suggest that in competitive bids for digital transformation, clients are increasingly asking for 15% to 30% markdowns if the provider cannot prove the value of AI efficiencies.

Reply counters this by bundling AI productivity into its prebuilt applications, essentially selling the "outcome" rather than the "hour" to avoid the deflationary pressure of T&M renewals.

Reply achieved EBITDA and EBIT margins of 19.5% and 16.4% respectively in 2Q25, representing a significant improvement both year-on-year and sequentially. This performance is rare in the IT services sector and suggests that Reply's pricing power remains intact despite broader macroeconomic headwinds impacting client budgets. Management's commitment to "silicon shoring"–designing custom chips to optimize AI workloads– further differentiates its pricing by providing proprietary hardware-software integration that peers cannot easily replicate. This depth of technical expertise allows Reply to bid for higher-margin work in sectors like financial services and public administration, which grew by 9.1% and 17% respectively in early 2025.

Contrary Evidence and Sector Outlook

1.
Contrary evidence: Broker research from Bernstein in May 2025 cautioned that although Reply shows best-in- class revenue growth, it is not immune to macroeconomic headwinds that vary greatly across sectors. Specifically, while financial services and public administration remain buoyant, other sectors are more impacted by economic upheaval, which may eventually force Reply to adjust its day rates or project sizes to maintain utilization. Furthermore, the reversal of a provision for a customer dispute in 2Q25 boosted EBITDA by approximately €18.7 mm, suggesting that underlying operational margin expansion might be slightly lower than the headline figure implies. Other side of the story: While Reply’s focus on agentic AI and specialized companies provides a strong moat, the accelerating trend of "AI-deflation" reported by larger peers could eventually bleed into Reply’s core markets. If Capgemini and Accenture successfully scale their own "intelligent operations" platforms, the pricing gap between Reply’s specialized offerings and its competitors' industrialized solutions could narrow, forcing Reply to either lower its rates or significantly increase its M&A pace to find new pockets of high-value growth. The current evidence suggests Reply is successfully resisting this through its prebuilt app strategy, but a prolonged sectorwide downturn would test the limits of its decentralized pricing model.

Section 3 – Indicators of Productivity and AI Pass-Through

Section summary
  1. Revenue per employee and project profitability across the sector are under pressure as clients demand that AI-driven labor savings be passed back in the form of lower fees. Medium confidence on this trend for Reply specifically, as it uses internal AI tools to protect margins even when face-rates for specialized roles remain stable.

Revenue Per Employee and Role Rates

Reply has not explicitly reported a discrete "revenue per employee" figure for 2026, but its stable headcount and rising revenue suggest improving labor productivity driven by AI. In contrast, peer Alan Allman Associates reported a 3% increase in average daily rates in 1H26 with stable headcount, reflecting a shift toward higher value-added assignments. For Reply, the extensive use of AI by its development teams is being used to radically rethink application creation and modernize legacy systems, which historically required high manual labor intensity.

This shift allows Reply to effectively increase its "revenue per hour" without necessarily raising the official day rate for its consultants.

Expert insights from former directors at Tier 1 firms note that the traditional consulting model with large project teams is in decline because clients now have greater capability to do things on their own using AI tools. In Europe, clients are increasingly demanding that providers like Reply "prove their faith in AI" by offering significant markdowns on renewals. Reply addresses this by deploying specialized "Intelligent Process Automation" and big data engineering teams that lock in client data estates, making the cost of switching to a cheaper provider prohibitively high even if day rates are slightly elevated.

AI Productivity Pass-Back Trends

The share of AI productivity passed back to clients is becoming a central point of negotiation in 18-to-36-month contract renewals across the European IT services market. Wipro reports that pricing pressure and AI productivity expectations are gaining momentum, forcing a shift from volume-based hiring to skill-based models. While larger peers are disclosing that they must reinvest most cost savings back into the business or pass them to clients, Reply's prebuilt apps are designed to "deliver measurable impact from the outset," which allows it to capture a larger share of the value created through automation rather than passing it back as a discount.

Accenture has recorded significant business optimization costs–over $615 mm in late 2025–to rotate its workforce toward AI skills as it exits people whose skills are not viable for reskilling. This structural reset is intended to drive more operating efficiencies through AI, but early evidence suggest these gains are often being passed to clients to secure "large-scale transformations of the digital core." Reply’s smaller, more agile companies avoid these massive restructuring charges, allowing them to remain profitable even if they offer modest productivity-based price adjustments to key accounts.

Contrary Evidence on Project Size

1.
Contrary evidence: Expert commentary in 2026 suggests that the decline in project team size is a sector- wide trend that is starting to impact even specialized premium consultancies. If Reply’s average project size shrinks because AI allows for faster delivery with fewer people, the company must significantly increase its "new logo" win rate to maintain its current revenue trajectory. In 1H26, some smaller IT services firms reported that their renewal rates were slightly lower than in previous years as clients consolidated vendors to save costs. side of the story: The prevailing view is that AI will inevitably lead to "deflationary" pressure on IT services revenue as the labor required for coding and testing drops. Reply’s hypothesis is that the "complexity and heterogeneity" of enterprise tech stacks will create even more work in the long run, offsetting the lower effort per task. However, if clients successfully commoditize standard AI implementations, Reply may find itself in a "price war" for all but its most specialized work, particularly in Region 2 where German automotive and manufacturing clients are under severe cost pressure.

Section 4 – Peer Comparison: Accenture and Capgemini

Section summary
  1. Accenture and Capgemini are responding to renewal pricing pressure by pivoting toward "outcome- based pricing" and "intelligent operations" powered by agentic AI. High confidence in this observation as both firms have reported that generative AI now represents 8% or more of their total bookings.

Accenture's Outcome-Based Shift

Accenture management reported in late 2025 that while the business environment remains competitive, pricing improved in several areas of the business, where "pricing" is defined as contract profitability or margin. The company is leveraging "advanced AI" to re-accelerate bookings, which reached $1.8 bn for GenAI projects alone in F4Q25 compared to $1.5 bn in the prior quarter. Accenture's strategy involves building a "digital core" for clients that enables them to use AI, effectively doubling the value of contracts for major financial services clients over a five-year period by expanding the scope of work. 30 10K - Earnings Calls To maintain its competitive edge during renewals, Accenture launched "Reinvention Services" in September 2025 to embed AI into all its offerings and rotate its workforce toward high-demand skills.

The firm is also partnering with companies like Repsol to deploy 22 custom AI agents today, with plans to scale to 90 agents and 3,000 employees. This shift toward "agentic AI" is a direct parallel to Reply's strategy, but Accenture's scale– over 760,000 employees–creates a larger "run-of-the-mill" support base that is more vulnerable to offshore competition from Infosys and Wipro.

Capgemini's Intelligent Operations

Capgemini's Chief Executive Aiman Ezzat states that clients are now seeking transformation partners to fundamentally transform their operations using data and AI, moving away from simple efficiency and cost reduction. The company’s acquisition of WNS in October 2025 was designed to lead the "fast-growing Intelligent Operations market," providing a platform for agentic AI-powered business operations. Capgemini expects this move to be 4% accretive to normalized EPS in 2026 as it shifts more work toward outcome-based pricing where clients are billed by transaction or volume rather than team cost.

Capgemini's 3Q25 constant currency revenue growth reached 2.9%, with Strategy & Transformation growing at 0.7%, indicating a slight recovery over prior quarters. However, broker analysis suggests that this growth has been achieved at the expense of margin , with FY25 margin guidance reduced slightly to 13.3-13.4% due to industry-wide pricing pressure. Capgemini's headcount increased 4.7% year-on-year to 354,700 in late 2025, but the firm faces the same "AI wave" challenge as Accenture: how to train its massive workforce without losing excessive utilization or profitability.

Contrary Evidence on European Demand

1.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research in October 2025 noted that strength in North America does not signal a European recovery for Capgemini, as the French and German manufacturing sectors (notably automotive) remain a significant drag on performance. This economic divergence suggests that Reply–which has much higher exposure to France and Germany–may face more severe renewal pricing pressure than its UScentric peers in 2026. Furthermore, some experts believe that "AI-first" strategies pose significant challenges to Accenture's traditional value proposition, as clients increasingly see through "agent-washing" by large vendors. side of the story: The massive scale of Accenture and Capgemini could allow them to industrialize AI delivery more effectively than Reply's fragmented network of 300 companies. If these Tier 1 firms can offer "sovereign cloud" and AI solutions at a lower price point through their offshore centers (which comprise 60% of Capgemini's headcount), they may win the battle for multi-hundred-million-euro "digital core" transformations, leaving Reply to fight for smaller, more specialized niche projects.

Capgemini has more than 35k data/AI experts (c.10% of group headcount), and it has educated more than 150k (c.44%) of its workforce on these technologies.

Section 5 – Peer Comparison: Sopra Steria and Netcompany

Section summary
  1. Sopra Steria and Netcompany are leveraging "sovereignty" as a key pricing lever for renewals in Europe’s public and financial sectors. High confidence that this "Sovereign AI" trend is creating a premium pricing tier that protects these European providers from offshore Indian competition.

Sopra Steria's Regulated Sector Focus

Sopra Steria has grown a strong application development business while divesting lower-value infrastructure services, leaving it with 70% exposure to cyclically defensive end-markets like the public sector, aerospace, and defense. In late 2025, the company secured a three-year extension for six of its major contracts in the UK, pushing their renewal dates out to 2027 and 2028. Sopra Steria is positioning itself as a "sovereign player" for European space operations and critical infrastructure, recently winning a framework agreement with the European Space Agency for project management and AI.

- Earnings Calls Sopra Steria’s organic revenue growth reached 4.3% in Q3 2026, leading management to raise its full-year growth target for the second time to 2.5-3.0%. The company’s UK-based "NS&I" contract provided a significant boost to its financial services revenue, helping to offset the ramp-down of legacy contracts. To counter the pricing pressure from AI, Sopra Steria is deploying its "AI Factory" and an AI-enabled ITSM platform on ServiceNow, which aims to offer "innovative outcome-based contracting" that keeps pace with market demands.

Netcompany's Platform-Led Growth

Netcompany is moving away from traditional IT services toward a product- and platform-based strategic partner model, with 2030 targets for organic growth of 5% to 10% and EBITDA margins above 20%. The firm’s "Feniks AI" orchestration tool allows customers to implement agentic agents faster while ensuring data security and sovereignty. Netcompany’s UK segment grew by over 57% in Q2 2026, driven by strong demand for its AIenabled platforms and new wins like a £215 mm flagship contract with the NHS.

In November 2026, Netcompany invested DKK 325 mm in the Danish Centre for AI Innovation (DCAI), which operates the 'Gefion' supercomputer, to build a "scalable, secure and commercially viable national AI platform." This sovereign infrastructure play allows Netcompany to offer "Level 4" full-stack control to highly regulated Danish and European businesses. By providing the secure orchestration layer for Gefion, Netcompany can capture recurring platform revenue that is decoupled from consultant hours , a key defense against renewal pricing pressure.

Contrary Evidence from Expert Calls

1.
Contrary evidence: A former ERP cloud transformation leader at Accenture suggests that while platform-led models are promising, AI-native tools are revolutionizing the implementation process so fast that the "reusability" advantage of firms like Netcompany may be quickly eroded by generic AI coding assistants. Additionally, Morgan Stanley research in October 2026 noted a bear-case scenario where Netcompany’s growth slows as AI contract pricing bites and new business weakens due to rising competition in a market disrupted by AI. side of the story: Sopra Steria and Netcompany are betting that European clients value "sovereignty" enough to pay a premium over Indian offshore rates. If the EU's "Cloud and AI Development Act" (CADA) strictly enforces data residency and corporate control, these local champions could see a massive windfall in renewals. However, if the regulatory environment remains fragmented or if hyperscalers like AWS and Microsoft successfully launch their own "sovereign regions" at scale, the pricing power of these smaller European players could collapse.

Section 6 – Peer Comparison: Indian IT Providers (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCLTech)

Section summary
  1. Indian providers are experiencing "revenue deflation" as they proactively pass AI productivity gains back to clients to defend market share during renewals. High confidence in this finding as Infosys and Wipro both reported that a "large part" of their existing contract books have already been repriced at lower effort intensities.

Infosys and the Deflation Cycle

Infosys management reports that near-term revenue growth has been impacted by revenue deflation in legacy service lines , as AI has reduced the overall effort intensity.

AI-led productivity benefits are being passed to clients through lower pricing, particularly in smaller contracts below $50 mm TCV, where AI benefits are "already built in." ’s AI-led revenues grew 29% quarter-on-quarter to reach 10.5% of total revenue in late 2026, this momentum is not yet translating into overall growth due to this productivity-led revenue compression . Broker research indicates that Infosys has already repriced much of its existing contract book, leading management to believe that deflationary pressures should gradually recede unless another major tech shift occurs. company's large deal TCV reached a record $5.2 bn in 2QFY27, including a $2 bn+ mega deal, but the competition for these deals is intense, frequently leading to "pricing pressure" as peers underbid each other to secure volume.

Infosys maintains its 20-22% operating margin guidance by using "Project Maximus" and cost discipline to offset these headwinds.

Wipro and HCLTech's AI Monetization

Wipro expects its contract deflation cycle to approach an inflection point in the coming quarters as many contracts with a 2-to-2.5-year average tenure have already been renewed or refreshed with AI in mind. firm’s "AI-native" unit, with over 2,000 engineers, is gaining traction in outcome-based engagements , which provides a path toward revenue recovery after a period of sequential declines. Wipro nonetheless reports that near-term caution persists as geopolitical friction and pricing pressure from AI productivity continue to weigh on discretionary spending.

HCLTech reports strong business momentum, with new deal bookings of $3.3 bn in late 2026 and EBIT margins expanding to 18.2%. The firm is explicitly using AI to structure contractual obligations and identify Categorize key clauses, which has accelerated its response cycles for competitive RFPs. HCLTech’s "agentic AI" offerings reached over $100 mm per quarter in late 2026, driven by its "AI factory" proposition which integrates infrastructure, data, and models to deliver scalable business outcomes .

Contrary Evidence on Competitive Moats

1.
Contrary evidence: Expert perspectives from Tata Consultancy Services suggest that while some smaller players like FPT are price-competitive, they face significant scaling risks compared to the deep moats of the established Indian Tier 1 firms. Additionally, some analysts believe that mid-sized IT firms "don't pose a competitive threat" in mega-deals due to their incomplete service offerings and lack of referenceable scale. This suggests that the pricing pressure is primarily a phenomenon between the "Big 4" Indian players rather than a broad-based market shift that would necessarily impact a specialized player like Reply. side of the story: The "deflationary" cycle reported by Infosys and Wipro may be a leading indicator of what Reply will eventually face in its Region 2 (Germany) segment, where large-scale legacy modernization is a major revenue driver. If Indian providers successfully use AI to aggressively cut prices in Europe while maintaining their 20%+ margins, Reply’s premium specialized positioning may be increasingly hard to justify for all but the most complex transformation tasks. The current resilience of HCLTech’s margins suggests that a "software-led" delivery model can indeed survive the AI transition, but it requires a level of platform scale that Reply has yet to fully achieve.

Section 8 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. If Reply and its peers fail to transition to outcome-based and platform-led models, the "AI-deflation" reported by Indian providers could lead to a permanent structural decline in European IT services EPS. The common view among major brokers is that AI will be a driver of higher labor productivity , allowing IT services firms to maintain or even expand margins as they transition from investment costs to rapid acceleration., if the reality is that the "AI-deflation" reported by Infosys–where AI reduces effort intensity faster than firms can find new work–becomes the secular trend for the whole industry, then the consensus EPS targets for 2026 and 2027 are significantly too high. In this scenario, IT services firms would face a "double hit": lower billable hours per project and lower day rates as AI efficiencies become commoditized., a different outcome would have profound knock-on effects on its decentralized network model. If the market shifts to a "price-war" environment for digital services, Reply's 300 specialized companies would lose their combined scale advantage, as they lack the central "AI factory" muscle that HCLTech and Accenture are using to industrialize their delivery. Competitive public tenders in Europe would increasingly favor "sovereign" platform providers like Netcompany or the large Indian firms with 60%+ offshore workforces, potentially forcing Reply to merge its internal units to save on administrative overhead.

The primary risk is that clients have the reason and power to push the pricing outcome. Large multinational banks and manufacturing firms are under intense pressure to improve their own margins and see IT services as a prime target for "AI-harvesting." If CIOs at major European firms like Repsol or the Bank of England– who are already early adopters of agentic AI–decide to move high-value orchestration work in-house or to boutique AI "pure-plays," the mid-market leaders like Reply could be squeezed into a low-growth "support and maintain" role.

Early signs of this shift are appearing in technical subcontracting rates , which remain stable at 8-9% of revenue for firms like Infosys, even as they trim their own internal headcounts. suggests a "hollowing out" of the middle-tier of the IT workforce, where standard coding and testing are being replaced by either AI or highly specialized contractors.
