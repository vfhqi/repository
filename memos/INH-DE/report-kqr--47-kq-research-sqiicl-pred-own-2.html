Section 2 – Inventory Turnover and Processing Times at BETEK and Peers

Section summary
  1. Long processing and procurement cycles across tungsten toolmakers create a substantial cost lag that delays working capital relief.
  2. High-inventory strategies successfully secured market share but have temporarily locked up significant liquid capital.

Global inventory and working capital dynamics

1.
Broad empirical baseline: This analysis evaluates seven relevant companies and draws on twelve distinct sources to assess inventory cycles. The primary organizations analyzed include INDUS Holding's subsidiary BETEK, Sandvik, Kennametal, and Ceratizit. According to INDUS Holding's interim report on 12 August 2026, the group's working capital expanded significantly to support BETEK's delivery capabilities. This comprehensive dataset provides a robust foundation to compare manufacturing lead times, operational processing bottlenecks, and inventory turnover across the global tungsten carbide tooling sector.
2.
Elevated working capital: INDUS Holding experienced a dramatic rise in current assets because of strategic raw material stocking. According to INDUS Holding's interim report on 12 August 2026, inventories climbed to EUR 557.2m from EUR 424.3m at year-end 2025. This inventory build-up was driven by the extreme price increases of tungsten carbide feedstock. The sharp increase in working capital to EUR 627.0m materially constrained consolidated free cash flow to negative EUR 36.9m during the first half of 2026.
3.
Slow conversion cycle: BETEK operates under structural constraints that delay cash generation because of its procurement dynamics. Warburg Research's update on 13 August 2026 notes that long lead and procurement times mean the inventory build-up will take several months to peak. This lag is driven by the complexity of sourcing and refining tungsten carbide feedstock. Consequently, the slow unwind of these inventories means that working capital remains locked up well into the second half of the 2026 financial year.
4.
Prolonged cost lag: Sandvik's machining business faces an even longer inventory cycle that delays the impact of falling raw material costs. According to Sandvik's third-quarter presentation on 22 October 2026, the average inventory cycle for finished cutting tools is approximately six months. This duration is on top of a three-month inventory cycle in its tungsten powder business. Therefore, Sandvik's consolidated operations experience a nine-month total cost lag before raw material price movements fully flow through cost of goods sold.

Manufacturing steps and cycle compression

6.
Multi-stage manufacturing bottleneck: Traditional carbide tool production requires several complex thermal and mechanical steps that limit processing speed. According to a Mitsubishi Materials investor guide from October 2026, manufacturing involves blending, mixing, pressing, sintering, machining, and coating. Sintering requires heating metal powders below their melting point to form dense structures, which is naturally time-consuming. These multi-stage processes create inherent manufacturing bottlenecks, meaning that shortening the physical production cycle is extremely difficult for traditional toolmakers.
7.
Technological cycle compression: Additive manufacturing represents a key opportunity to compress these long production times and reduce feedstock requirements. According to a Kennametal press release on 15 September 2026, the company introduced KAF82, a 3D-printing tungsten carbide grade. This proprietary powder metallurgy technology allows custom hole-finishing tools to be manufactured in weeks rather than months. If adopted widely, this technological shift will significantly accelerate inventory turnover rates and lower raw material consumption for custom tooling.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some indicators suggest that the working capital cash bottleneck is smaller than it appears because of seasonal inventory relief. According to INDUS Holding's Q2 2026 earnings call on 12 August 2026, working capital historically peaks in the second quarter and declines sequentially in the third and fourth quarters. Management notes that this seasonal pattern accounts for a significant portion of the working capital movement. This seasonal normalization means that cash conversion will accelerate naturally in the winter quarters, irrespective of falling tungsten feedstock prices.
9.
Other side of the story: While the massive accumulation of inventory has locked up group liquidity, this accumulation was a deliberate strategic choice that yielded major commercial benefits. According to a Warburg Research update on 13 August 2026, INDUS's decision to maintain supply capacity while competitors exited allowed BETEK to capture sustainable market share. This high-inventory strategy drove a temporary surge in Materials Solutions EBITA margins to 22.4% in H1 2026. Therefore, the temporary liquidity drag is offset by sustainable market share gains and exceptional windfall profits.

Section 3 – Historical Precedents: Working-Capital Release Cycles

Section summary
  1. Historical tungsten down-cycles show medium confidence that working capital converts back to cash with a typical lag of two to three quarters.
  2. Sourcing structures and procurement commitments represent the primary factors that dictate the speed of balance sheet normalization.

Analysis of the 2011 to 2013 down-cycle

1.
Delayed cash normalization: This historical analysis evaluates three primary peers and three distinct pricing cycles across twelve relevant documents. Historical cycles for major toolmakers show that working capital normalization is structurally delayed by raw material purchasing agreements. During the 2012 down-cycle, Kennametal faced rising raw material purchase commitments that initially pushed inventories up by $32 million in the September quarter. This inventory expansion occurred despite a substantial decline in broader economic activity across Europe. Consequently, the company had to implement aggressive destocking measures to target a $60 million inventory reduction in the subsequent quarters. This lag demonstrates how commitments slow down cash release during cycles.
2.
Six quarters of destocking: Sandvik's historical performance during the 2011 to 2013 down-cycle highlights that thorough balance sheet normalization requires a sustained period of inventory correction. The company achieved a strong operating cash flow of nearly SEK 11 billion in 2013. However, this liquidity generation was only made possible through six consecutive quarters of inventory reductions. Without this prolonged destocking effort, Sandvik would have faced severe cash constraints as sales in its mining and construction segments fell. Therefore, cash inflows are highly dependent on extended liquidation periods.
3.
Negative margin leverage: Sharp declines in tungsten prices historically trigger significant negative operating leverage and margin squeeze as expensive stockpiles are processed. In Q1 2013, Sandvik experienced an extreme negative operating leverage of 85%. This severe margin pressure was driven by lower production levels and a sharp year-on-year drop in tungsten prices. This pricing collapse had a materially negative impact on divisional profitability. Thus, the transition from high-priced feedstocks to falling spot market rates represents a major near-term risk for toolmakers.
4.
Cash flow preservation: Kennametal's financial results during the 2013 fiscal year show that proactive capital discipline can protect cash generation during raw material downturns. The company adapted to mixed macroeconomic demand by focusing heavily on working capital efficiencies. These operational actions allowed Kennametal to achieve an all-time record free operating cash flow of $204 million. This cash flow level represented nearly 100% conversion of net income. Ultimately, structural adaptability is essential to preserve liquidity when inventory values begin to normalize.

Quantitative paths in the 2018 post-peak phase

5.
Squeezed historical margins: Historical data from Morgan Stanley indicates that industrial toolmakers routinely suffer a severe margin squeeze when inventory liquidation coincides with falling demand. During the 2012 downturn, Sandvik suffered from a combination of falling production rates and cost inflation in administrative expenses. This structural bottleneck created significant downside risk to consensus earnings estimates. Furthermore, the company's valuation multiples looked increasingly unattractive relative to its historical averages. Consequently, investors should expect significant near-term margin compression during raw material corrections.
6.
Inventory build constraints: The 2018 to 2019 cycle demonstrates how strategic stockpiling decisions can backfire and lock up substantial capital on the balance sheet. Kennametal made a deliberate decision to build safety stock to support footprint rationalization in fiscal 2019. However, this build-up pushed primary working capital to $739 million, which was above the company's 30% target. This expansion occurred because sales fell short of management's expectations as industrial markets softened. This precedent indicates that inventory builds can easily become illiquid drag factors.
7.
Extended transition periods: The transition from high-priced raw materials to normalized costs typically requires multiple quarters of margin dilution before benefits are realized. During the fiscal 2020 down-cycle, tungsten prices dropped rapidly from $270 to $200 in the first quarter. This rapid decline temporarily depressed Kennametal's gross margins by approximately 360 basis points. Management noted that this negative margin impact would persist through the second quarter before finally abating in the second half. This shows that cost recognition lags prevent rapid margin recovery.

Dynamics of the 2022 post-pandemic stabilization

8.
Inventory turnover spikes: Operational data from the 2022 tungsten peak shows a severe slowdown in inventory turnover that delays the cash release cycle. Sandvik's inventory turnover days expanded significantly to 222.8 days during the 2022 down-cycle. This marked a dramatic slowdown compared to the 144.7 days recorded in the prior year. This expansion indicates that working capital remains tied up in high-cost inventory for nearly seven months. Therefore, the physical liquidation of raw materials is structurally constrained by long manufacturing cycles.
9.
Rapid cash conversion: In contrast to previous cycles, Sandvik's performance in the late 2025 down-cycle demonstrated that decentralized execution can accelerate working capital release. Sandvik achieved a record free operating cash flow of SEK 21.2 billion in 2025. This exceptional performance was driven by an adaptive decentralized structure that allowed swift responses to market changes. This flexibility enabled a cash conversion rate of 95%. This indicates that modern operational strategies can mitigate historical cash conversion lags.
10.
Post-peak pricing dynamics: Historical precedents show that price increases put in place during peaks can remain sticky even as raw material costs fall. Analysis from Morgan Stanley suggests that cutting tool businesses often retain most of their price increases even if tungsten prices roll over harshly 74 This pricing power is supported by the low cost-of-input nature of cutting tools relative to overall manufacturing costs, toolmakers can enjoy a structural margin expansion after raw materials normalize. This stickiness suggests a favorable earnings outlook post-cycle.
11.
Contrary evidence: Some historical indicators suggest that working capital is released much faster during downturns because production levels are slashed immediately. During the 2020 pandemic downturn, Sandvik managed to keep its working capital completely flat in the second quarter by matching production directly to weak demand. This rapid operational adjustment allowed the company to deliver positive operating cash flow of SEK 2.5 billion . This positive cash flow was achieved despite a steep drop in net income of more than SEK 2 billion. This demonstrates that proactive manufacturing cutbacks can override typical cycle lags.

Comparative operational metrics and cash lags

12.
Sourcing and cost lags: The lag between spot price movements and balance sheet release is heavily influenced by the sourcing mix and recycling rates. Redburn argues that Sandvik is structurally insulated from rapid cost inflation because 55% of its raw materials are recycled 83 However, this vertical integration also means that the company experiences a substantial nine-month cost lag in its cutting tools business. In comparison, the powder business has a shorter three-month cost lag. This divergence demonstrates that sourcing structures dictate cash release speed.
13.
Sourcing security strategies: Plansee Group's strategic decisions during supply disruptions show a focus on long-term supply security over short-term working capital optimization. In response to extreme price volatility, Plansee's business area Ceratizit launched a joint venture to build a strategic tungsten reserve in the United States. This integrated supply chain model is recycling-driven and designed to reduce dependence on foreign raw materials. Consequently, while this integration secures long-term delivery capability, it locks up substantial operational capital during peaks. This suggests that integrated peers prioritize supply resilience over rapid cash conversion.
14.
Cash generation benchmarks: Historical data from past cycles shows that cash inflows from inventory reduction are heavily concentrated in the quarters immediately following a pricing peak. During the 2014 down-cycle, Sandvik successfully reduced its net working capital by SEK 1.5 billion in a single quarter. This significant cash release was driven primarily by aggressive volume destocking across all major business areas. Consequently, this rapid reduction helped drive the group's net debt to equity ratio down to 0.75. This benchmark suggests that substantial cash inflows can materialize quickly once destocking begins 87.
15.
Other side of the story: While historical precedents suggest a multi-quarter lag before working capital converts to cash, modern vertical integration and indexing contracts can compress this cycle significantly. According to Jefferies, Kennametal can pass through higher tungsten costs nearly instantly on approximately one-third of its sales. This indexing capacity minimizes the time arbitrage risk and allows rapid alignment of prices and costs 96 97, the growing use of recycling programs provides toolmakers with immediate, low-cost raw materials that bypass volatile spot markets. Therefore, historical precedents may overstate the cash conversion drag under modern supply chain structures.

  1. Stricter capital allocation targets govern INDUS's acquisition decisions as balance sheet leverage fluctuates.

Stated leverage and equity guidelines

1.
Conservative financial principles: Executive board members have prioritized keeping the company on track for an investment-grade rating by adhering to clear financial guidelines. According to the INDUS annual report published on 24 March 2026, these key guidelines require keeping the repayment term below 2.5 years and the group equity ratio above 40%. This strict financial framework ensures that acquired companies are not encumbered with high acquisition debt, which ultimately protects earnings per share from excessive interest burdens.
2.
Comfortable leverage metrics: Actual leverage ratios improved during the first half of fiscal year 2026 due to strong operating performance. Chief Financial Officer Rudolf Weichert stated on 12 August 2026 that the company's net debt to EBITDA ratio stood at 2.3x, which is comfortably below the 2.5x threshold. This solid leverage metric indicates that leverage remains well in target, which suggests that the company has preserved its financial capacity to fund future acquisitions as soon as working capital requirements normalize.
3.
Depressed equity ratio: Rising current assets have temporarily weighed on the group's equity ratio, keeping it slightly below long-term targets. On 12 August 2026, INDUS reported that its equity ratio was 37.8%, down from 38.4% at the end of 2025 due to higher total assets and dividend payouts. This means the balance sheet is approaching the 40% equity target but remains vulnerable to inventory fluctuations that could dilute margins.
4.
Peer leverage comparison: The holding company's debt profile is structured differently than some of its specialized machinery and tooling peers. While INDUS manages its leverage toward a net debt to EBITDA ratio below 2.5x, its net debt to equity ratio reached 87% on 30 June 2026. Enhanced allocation strategy: Management has increasingly focused on value creation rather than pure volume growth to protect margins. NuWays AG noted on 10 January 2025 that INDUS has demonstrated discipline by spending only half of its annual M&A budget. Capital release and transactional capacity

According to the interim report on 12 August 2026, net financial liabilities increased by EUR 121.8 million to EUR 665.8 million. This heavy debt accumulation means that liquidity was severely restricted, which forced management to decelerate its M&A activity to avoid breaching its stated leverage thresholds.
