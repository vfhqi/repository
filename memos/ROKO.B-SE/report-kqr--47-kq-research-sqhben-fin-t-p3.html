Liabilities, and Working Capital

Option Liabilities and Capital Consumption

1.
Escalating liabilities: Röko AB manages substantial off-balance sheet risk through its extensive put and call option obligations with minority shareholders in its subsidiaries. These option liabilities stood at SEK 2,798m at the end of September 2026, representing a significant increase of SEK 196m from the start of the year. This massive liability consumes a large portion of the company's financial flexibility because it is included in financial net debt calculations.
2.
Complex accounting: Röko AB evaluates its put and call option liabilities under IFRS 9 by measuring the expected cash outflows based on subsidiary performance metrics. These liabilities are classified as Level 3 in the fair value hierarchy, which requires non-observable market data and estimates of future earnings. This measurement method means that sudden earnings spikes can trigger large, non-cash balance sheet revaluations.
3.
Rising obligations: Röko AB faces a direct financial balancing loop because these minority put and call options rise automatically as subsidiary profitability improves. A sensitivity analysis from the 2025 annual report shows that if subsidiaries' average EBITA increases by SEK 100m, the option liability grows by SEK 191m. This mechanical increase creates a headwind where successful organic growth absorbs a high proportion of incremental leverage headroom.
4.
Diluted equity: Röko AB experiences substantial equity erosion because revaluations of these put and call options are charged directly to balance sheet equity. These non-controlling interest value changes reached SEK 150m in fiscal year 2025, following a larger charge of SEK 239m during fiscal year 2024. This structural accounting treatment weakens the capital base, meaning that relying on option-heavy structures reduces leverage headroom over time.

Constrained structure: Idun Industrier AB exhibits a comparable structural challenge because its minority put and call option agreements also inflate overall group leverage. This expensive funding method means that fixed preference dividends restrict the capital available for reinvestment in ordinary shares.
