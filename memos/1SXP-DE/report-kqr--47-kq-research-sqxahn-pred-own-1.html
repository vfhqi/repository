Initial Facility Parameters and Delay

1.
Ambitious expansion: SCHOTT Pharma announced plans on March 18, 2024, to build a state-of-the-art prefillable syringe manufacturing facility in Wilson, North Carolina, representing a massive expansion into the lucrative North American market. The planned facility involves a total capital investment of $371 million and is designed to create 401 high-wage jobs in the biopharma region. This project aims to help the company triple its U.S. syringe supply by the year 2030 to meet the rising domestic demand.
2.
Advanced capabilities: The planned Wilson facility is designed as the first United States site capable of manufacturing prefillable polymer syringes for deep-cold storage of mRNA medications. Additionally, the site will house the capability to produce glass prefillable syringes designed specifically for high-demand GLP-1 diabetes and obesity therapies. This dual-material focus allows the company to target both rapid-growth biotech segments simultaneously.
3.
Financial backing: To support the $371 million capital deployment, local and state authorities awarded SCHOTT Pharma $21 million in incentive packages. These incentives are coupled with a $40 million state investment in a new biologics training facility at Wilson Community College to prepare the local biomanufacturing workforce. This public-private alignment is intended to subsidize early operational expenses once construction commences.
4.
Delayed timeline: Although SCHOTT Pharma's initial March 2024 press release projected that physical groundbreaking would occur by the end of 2024, no active construction began during that period. By December 2024, corporate management confirmed that the company was still in the final planning phase for North Carolina, making the initial operational start date of 2027 highly unlikely. This timeline shift suggests that commercial supply will be delayed past the original target.
