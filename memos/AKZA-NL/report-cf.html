EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of Change Assessment The investment case for Akzo Nobel centers on a transformational all-stock merger of equals with Axalta Coating Systems, announced in November 2025, which creates a $25 billion enterprise value coatings giant with combined pro forma revenues of $17 billion across 160 countries. This strategic combination represents the most material structural change in the company's recent history, fundamentally altering the competitive positioning, operational footprint, and earnings trajectory.

The merger is complemented by aggressive standalone self-help initiatives, including a completed functional SG&A program delivering €175 million in annualized savings through 2,900 global job cuts and an ongoing industrial excellence transformation targeting €300 million in gross savings by 2027. Active portfolio management has crystallized €900 million in net proceeds from the Indian decorative paints divestment at a premium 25 times EBITDA multiple, with ongoing strategic reviews of Southeast Asian decorative positions expected to generate additional monetization opportunities.

However, the operational environment remains challenged by persistent European volume contraction, with Q1 2026 organic revenue declining 1.0% and decorative paint volumes running approximately 4% below 2019 prepandemic levels due to weak residential construction and renovation demand. The company operates under heightened activist scrutiny, with Cevian Capital securing a 10.15% stake and board representation to accelerate operational performance and merger execution.

Top Ranked Key Drivers (Impact and Plausibility Matrix) The forward earnings trajectory is governed by six critical operational and strategic drivers ranked by estimated financial impact and execution plausibility. Driver 1 is the Axalta merger cost synergies, targeting a $600 million pre-tax run-rate with 90% realization within three years, representing 11% of acquired revenues and exceeding the coatings sector acquisition average of 8%.

This driver carries medium plausibility due to complex dualheadquarters integration requirements, cross-border regulatory clearances spanning the US, EU, and UK, and potential antitrust divestiture pressures on overlapping refinish, powder, and wood coatings businesses representing approximately 10% of combined revenues. Driver 2 is the industrial excellence program, delivering €300 million in gross savings by 2027 through aggressive manufacturing footprint rationalization, including 15 site closures since program inception and three additional shuttered locations in Q1 2026 alone.

This driver exhibits high plausibility as the functional cost-out program has already exceeded initial targets by €25 million, demonstrating proven execution capability despite legacy formulation complexity barriers. Driver 3 is the completed standalone SG&A cost-out initiative, which delivered €175 million in annualized overhead reductions via 2,900 position eliminations representing 8% of total headcount, a structurally deeper cut than typical sector streamlining levels.

This driver carries high plausibility and high confidence as savings are fully embedded in the run-rate and offset structurally elevated European wage and logistics inflation. Driver 4 is pricing power durability and raw material pass-through, where mid-single-digit to low-double-digit price increases must offset high-teens raw material inflation driven by petrochemical derivatives representing 50% of the input basket.

A 1% change in net pricing generates a nominal EBITDA impact of €100 million, but plausibility is constrained by customer price elasticity, competitive intensity from Nippon Paint's aggressive Asian retail price war, and a three to six month inventory lag delaying P&L impact. Driver 5 is regional demand recovery and volume leverage, where 1% volume growth supports approximately €40 million in EBITDA through operating leverage, but low plausibility and low confidence reflect European construction PMIs remaining in contraction at 42.1 in Germany and 39.6 in France and meaningful standalone volume recovery unlikely to materialize until late 2026 or 2027.

Driver 6 is active portfolio pruning and Asian asset monetization, which has already crystallized €900 million from the India decorative paints sale and carries high plausibility as ongoing Southeast Asian strategic reviews may generate an additional $350 million in divestment proceeds, with transaction multiples demonstrating that non-core assets are significantly more valuable to regional strategic buyers than embedded within the group portfolio.
