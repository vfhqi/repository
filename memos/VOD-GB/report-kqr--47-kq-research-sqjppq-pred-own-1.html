This transition means that previous associate contributions will no longer recur in their historic form, thereby shifting investor focus toward clean operational metrics rather than non-controlled earnings.

Strategic leverage goals: Managing Vodafone's leverage is essential for its financial stability, especially after the group's debt spiked following historical acquisitions like Unity Media. Company presentations in July 2026 show that Vodafone is targeting the lower half of its 2.25-2.75x net debt to adjusted EBITDAaL leverage range by the end of FY27. Section 3 – Non-Recurring Items and the FY27 Transition

Section summary
  1. The consolidation of Safaricom and exit of VodafoneZiggo fundamentally restructure Vodafone's earnings profile in FY27.
  2. Ongoing Kenyan litigation over the Safaricom divestiture creates temporary reporting uncertainty but maintains the current consolidated status quo.

Safaricom Consolidation and Stake Mechanics

1.
Consolidated operating scale: Vodafone completed the transition of its Safaricom investment from associate equity accounting to full consolidation effective July 2026. Under the terms of the transaction, Safaricom's operational performance will be fully reflected in Vodafone's financial statements from the second quarter of FY27. This reporting change simplifies the group's financial structure by replacing volatile associate income with predictable operating cash flows, which helps stabilize the group's rebased dividend payouts.
2.
Stake acquisition terms: Vodacom increased its effective shareholding in Safaricom from 35% to a controlling 55% stake on 30 June 2026. The transaction involved acquiring 15% from the Government of Kenya for €1.36 billion and 5% from a Vodafone subsidiary for €0.45 billion. Consequently, the group expects this transaction to shift its earnings mix toward faster-growing businesses, which will materially enhance long-term growth prospects.
3.
Non-cash remeasurement gain: Vodafone recognized a substantial non-recurring gain in its operating profit as a result of the Safaricom control shift. The group discontinued equity accounting for its prior 39.9% associate stake and remeasured it to fair value. This valuation adjustment yielded a €3.0 billion non-cash gain recognized in other income, which temporarily inflates reported operating profit but does not impact cash flow.
4.
Financial services footprint: Consolidating Safaricom dramatically expands the group's footprint in high-growth African financial services. The consolidation increases Vodacom's annual fintech service revenues to approximately US$2.5 billion, representing over 22% of its total service revenues. This integration of M-Pesa is expected to accelerate operating free cash flow growth, which means Vodafone will enjoy reduced reliance on slow European telecom markets.

Legal Challenges in Kenyan Courts

5.
Adverse judicial ruling: The Safaricom consolidation faces ongoing legal challenges following a ruling by the High Court of Kenya on 15 September 2026. The local court ruled against the Government of Kenya's divestiture of its 15% stake to Vodafone Kenya. This judicial decision introduces regulatory and operational friction, which could jeopardize the planned corporate simplification unless resolved on appeal.
6.
Substantive appeal process: Vodacom and other affected parties filed a notice of appeal against the adverse Kenyan judgment in September 2026. The Court of Appeal in Nairobi decided to fast-track the substantive appeal rather than determine a stay application. Consequently, the group remains confident in the strategic rationale of the acquisition, which helps protect near-term operational integration from sudden disruption.
7.
Preservation of status quo: Kenyan judicial orders maintain the current shareholding structure pending the outcome of the fast-tracked appeal. Under the court order, Vodafone Kenya remains the registered holder of the Safaricom shares, while the government preserves the purchase consideration. This legal preservation of the status quo allows Safaricom to continue operating normally under Vodacom control, which prevents immediate disruption to group earnings during the litigation.
8.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank warns that despite the operational consolidation of Safaricom, the Kenyan dividend distribution timing will dilute near-term cash flows. The broker adjusted its FY27 adjusted free cash flow estimates downward by 3% to reflect the specific timing of Kenyan dividends. This means that the transition creates near-term cash flow headwinds, which temporarily offsets the benefits of consolidation until distributions normalize.

VodafoneZiggo Exit and Cash Deleveraging

9.
Portfolio simplification exit: Vodafone completed the sale of its 50% stake in VodafoneZiggo to Liberty Global on 31 July 2026. Under the terms of the agreement, Vodafone received €1.0 billion in cash and a 10% equity stake in Ziggo Group. This divestment represents a key step in Vodafone's strategy to sell underperforming assets, which aims to reduce balance sheet complexity and support deleveraging.
10.
Associate income non-recurrence: The exit from VodafoneZiggo eliminates the group's historical share of results from the Dutch joint venture in FY27. Vodafone reported a share of net loss from VodafoneZiggo of €95 million in FY26, driven by lower operating income. Eliminating these equity-accounted losses will clean up group net income and remove a persistent drag on EPS going forward.
11.
Benelux holding exposure: Vodafone's retained 10% equity stake in Ziggo Group provides continued exposure to the merged Benelux entity. This newly formed entity combines VodafoneZiggo with Liberty Global's Belgian subsidiary, Telenet, with plans for an Amsterdam stock exchange listing in 2027. Consequently, Vodafone can eventually monetize this stake to fund future cash returns, which creates mid-term capital allocation options.
12.
Passive tower monetization: VodafoneZiggo obtained EU regulatory clearance to sell its passive tower assets to DigitalBridge in October 2026. The transaction is expected to generate €669 million in proceeds by the end of the calendar year. This capital injection assists the joint venture in reaching its c.4.5x leverage target, which supports the upcoming corporate spin-off in FY27.
