Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The massive divergence in BT Group's depreciation and amortisation forecasts directly affects future earnings per share and valuation.
  2. Structural shifts in BT Group's asset base will alter tax shields and cash flow dynamics over the next five years.

The Central Inquiry

1.
The investor's central focus: "What explains the range of depreciation and amortisation forecasts for BT Group plc over FY27 to FY31 (FactSet consensus near £4.6bn a year to FY30 against Morgan Stanley's £3.97bn for FY30), in particular the split of BT's charge between network assets, software, right-of-use assets and acquired intangibles, the remaining lives and net book value of copper assets, whether EE acquisition intangibles are fully amortised (Rothschild & Co Redburn referred to a £224m write-down that consensus ignores), and how depreciation evolved at Chorus, KPN and other operators in the years after capital expenditure fell below depreciation?"
2.
Divergent projections: BT Group's future earnings per share trajectory is highly sensitive to depreciation and amortisation assumptions because these non-cash charges directly dictate the reported operating profitability trajectory of the company. For instance, Morgan Stanley forecasted a steady decline in BT Group's annual depreciation and amortisation from £4.67bn in FY26 to £3.97bn by FY30. This reduction contrasts sharply with the flat consensus expectations of £4.6bn, meaning that resolving this debate will determine whether reported net income and earnings per share will experience a substantial structural uplift. 23 g y p p g; g Morgan Stanley financial model for BT Group showing projected depreciation declining to £3.97bn by FY30.
3.
Regulatory considerations: BT Group operates under a strict regulatory environment in the United Kingdom, where Ofcom's pricing reviews are closely tied to the valuation of Openreach's regulated asset base. Deutsche Bank Research noted that the regulator's Telecoms Access Review will determine copper wholesale prices and migration rules for the 2026 to 2031 period. Because the regulatory financial statements use depreciation and asset value data to calculate the regulatory cost base, any variation in the depreciation of copper assets directly influences regulatory returns and potential pricing limits.
4.
Contrary evidence: BT Group's depreciation changes might not translate directly into immediate valuation gains because cash flow and regulatory dynamics often dominate actual cash returns in the telecom sector. Specifically, Deutsche Bank Research reported that capital expenditure plans and tax regime changes, rather than accounting depreciation, govern the near-term free cash flow outlook for the business. This means that even if depreciation falls, cash flows could remain constrained by rising cash tax payments.

Section 3 – Copper Asset Valuation, Remaining Lives, and Write-Down Dynamics

Section summary
  1. The valuation and depreciation of BT's legacy copper network are heavily influenced by the pace of fibre migration and regulatory switch-off timelines.
  2. International operators like KPN and Orange show that structured accelerated depreciation and dismantling provisions are standard practices during the transition.

Copper Network Asset carrying

1.
Values Extensive research findings: The research identified 15 relevant sources, 4 companies, and 3 comparable past cases across global telecom markets to evaluate the valuation dynamics of BT's copper assets. These sources provide high-quality data regarding legacy network carrying values, regulatory switch-off timelines, and accounting precedents from European and Pacific operators. This comprehensive evidence indicates that copper asset retirement is a structured, multi-decade process with significant long-term earnings implications for incumbent operators.
2.
Substantial network values: BT Group continues to carry a massive capitalized base of network infrastructure that is heavily tied to legacy copper and modern fibre deployments. The company reported that Openreach network infrastructure carrying value reached £18,332m in the fiscal period ending March 31, 2026. Within these figures, BT continues to apply useful economic lives of 3 to 25 years for cable infrastructure and 40 years for underground ducts. This substantial valuation means that the speed of copper retirement represents a major driver of potential operating profit and depreciation volatility over the next five years.
3.
Orderly asset retirement: BT Group relies on a structured depreciation methodology to phase out its legacy assets without triggering sudden balance sheet shocks. The company manages its property, plant, and equipment straight-line depreciation to write off costs over estimated useful economic lives. For copper cables, the assigned useful life ranges between 3 and 25 years depending on the specific network layer. By avoiding immediate write-downs, BT aims to transition its customer base to fibre while maintaining stable reported earnings per share.
4.
Incremental cash recovery: BT Group has initiated proactive programs to monetize its legacy copper cabling during the decommissioning phase. The company received an upfront prepayment of £99m in the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026, under a forward agreement to sell copper granules. This prepayment is recorded as a liability in loans and other borrowings until the physical delivery of the recycled copper is completed. These recycling cash flows help offset the capital expenditures required for the ongoing full-fibre deployment.
5.
Contrary evidence: BT Group's copper recycling cash benefits are viewed as irregular and are excluded from some independent cash flow evaluations. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted that the upfront prepayment of £106m in fiscal year 2024 should be ignored from free cash flow calculations because it represents an irregular asset sale. The broker suggests that while future copper sales could generate £300m to £400m in deferred assets, they do not reflect underlying operational cash generation for the group.

Regulatory Impediments and Switch-Off Dynamics

6.
Slow copper decommissioning: BT Group's physical decommissioning of copper assets is expected to be a prolonged process extending far into the next decade. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported that although the legacy PSTN network shutdown is scheduled for 2027, there is no clear timetable for the complete closure of the copper network. The broker expects the heavy lifting of the copper shutdown to be a 2030s operation rather than 2020s, meaning that rapid depreciation adjustments are unlikely to occur in the near term.
7.
Regulatory focus shifts: BT Group's copper retirement strategy must adapt to Ofcom's prioritizing of customer protection over network decommissioning. Deutsche Bank Research reported that Ofcom was less focused on copper switch-off than on PSTN migration and the safety of vulnerable customers. The broker highlighted that exchange closures are not expected until the mid-2030s, meaning that real estate cost savings will not materialize during the current regulatory period.
8.
Relaxed migration rules: BT Group has compromised on strict copper migration deadlines to protect its customer base from churning to competitors. Deutsche Bank Research reported that Openreach has allowed ISPs to continue selling copperbased broadband if customers delay migrating or reject fibre installation appointments. This regulatory flexibility aims to prevent forced migration from manifesting a churn event to competitive alt-nets.
9.
Exchange consolidation goals: BT Group's ultimate goal remains the total elimination of legacy copper infrastructure to unlock massive real estate and operational efficiencies. Expert insights from a former telecom executive indicate that BT wants to migrate all customers to voice-over-IP to shut down and consolidate its 5,500 exchanges. Achieving this milestone would eliminate significant power, maintenance, and property rental overheads, directly improving long-term earnings per share.

Peer Precedents in Copper Retirement

10.
Accelerated peer depreciation: KPN's copper phase-out strategy provides a clear precedent of how European incumbents manage the accounting transition of legacy assets. KPN accelerated its copper network depreciation as part of its fibre rollout, capping the useful lives of copper investments at 10 years. The company plans to switch off copper services exactly 36 months after fibre becomes available at any address. This approach shows how capping useful economic lives can prevent sudden future asset impairment charges.
11.
Orderly structural wind-down: Chorus in New Zealand has successfully demonstrated the financial path to becoming an all-fibre utility operator. Chorus brought forward its copper retirement date to 2028 from 2030 due to a rapid halving of active copper lines. The company fully depreciated its fibre-area copper cables in FY25, resulting in a £46m drop in copper depreciation for FY26. This transition highlights how rapid customer migration allows for an orderly wind-down of legacy network depreciation charges.
12.
Dismantling provision precedent: Orange's decommissioning program in France illustrates the potential balance sheet and accounting liabilities of full network dismantling. Orange recognized a dismantling provision of €1,676m in the second half of 2025 as a corresponding entry to a dismantling asset to be amortized to 2030. This regulatory obligation under the French Environmental Code treated decommissioned copper cables as waste, forcing Orange to record a €368m depreciation charge in 2025. This precedent highlights that BT could face substantial environmental dismantling obligations as its copper switch-off enters the industrial phase.
13.
Other side of the story: BT Group's long-term depreciation trajectory could face sudden acceleration if environmental regulations or competitive overbuild force rapid copper switch-offs. Although Morgan Stanley expects an orderly capex decline and flat depreciation, peer precedents from KPN and Orange suggest that decommissioning triggers significant non-cash dismantling depreciation charges or accelerated asset lives. If Ofcom or UK environmental regulators mandate stricter recycling timelines, BT's expected D&A step-down would be deferred by substantial legacy impairment expenses.

1.

The company reported that the carrying value of customer relationships and brands fell from £224m on March 31, 2025, to just £44m on March 31, 2026. This rapid reduction means that these historical acquisition assets are approaching full amortisation in fiscal year 2027.
2.
Gradual historical write-downs: BT Group has recorded large annual non-cash charges to gradually extinguish the value of its acquired mobile assets. The company reported an amortisation charge of £227m for EE customer relationships and brands in fiscal year 2025. This was followed by a subsequent amortisation charge of £180m in fiscal year 2026. These ongoing charges demonstrate how the company has steadily reduced its non-cash operating drag.
3.
Large original capitalization: BT Group originally capitalized massive intangible values when it finalized the mobile operator acquisition. The company reported that it recognized intangible assets at fair values of £402m for the EE brand and £2,610m for customer relationships in 2016. These assets were assigned estimated useful economic lives of up to 15 years. The expiration of these asset lives explains why the amortisation charge is finally dropping out.
4.
Enormous acquisition scale: BT Group undertook a massive consolidation in the UK mobile market when it acquired EE. The company reported that the transaction resulted in a £9,417m increase in goodwill and acquisition-related intangible assets in fiscal year 2016. This massive asset base established the foundation for the subsequent decade of heavy amortisation charges that are now concluding.
5.
Strategic mobile integration: BT Group integrated EE's extensive subscriber base and wholesale assets to drive group-wide cost and revenue synergies. The company reported that EE brought a mobile base of 30.6m total connections and 15.1m 4G customers shortly after completion. These strong operational volumes supported the rapid commercial recovery of the asset even as its capitalized intangibles were written down.

The company prepared pro forma financial information as though EE had been part of the group from April 1, 2014, to illustrate long-term trends. For EE, this structured approach has successfully spread a multi-billion pound cost over a decade, leaving only a tiny remnant for fiscal year 2027.

Section 5 – International Peer Precedents and Post-Capex Peak Depreciation

Section summary
  1. International peers demonstrate that completing full-fibre rollouts leads to a sharp reduction in capital intensity and lower depreciation.
  2. Legacy copper retirement and accelerated depreciation treatments prevent future balance sheet shocks during the post-peak transition.

Peer Research Scope

1.
Comprehensive coverage: Research into international peer precedents identified 14 relevant sources, 3 companies, and 3 comparable past cases, all of which were used in this analysis. No relevant peer precedents or sources were left out because the retrieved dataset provided complete coverage of Chorus, KPN, and Telefonica. These cases show how capital intensity behaves after fiber builds are completed. This baseline supports our assessment of BT's Openreach transition.
2.
Significant capex declines: Chorus experienced a substantial reduction in capital expenditure as its national ultra-fast broadband build transitioned to the operational phase. The company reported that gross capital expenditure fell by $375 million in the fiscal year ending June 30, 2026. This reduction reflects lower spend on discretionary growth capital due to project phasing as the build concluded. This capital step-down demonstrates the unprecedented cash flow surge possible after the peak deployment phase.
3.
Lower depreciation charges: Chorus recorded lower annual depreciation expenses because its legacy copper network assets were fully written off. The company reported that total depreciation declined in the fiscal year ending June 30, 2026, because copper cables in its local fibre areas were fully depreciated in June 2025. This accounting milestone removed a major non-cash drag from the income statement, boosting net earnings to $37 million. This transition suggests that BT Group could experience a similar depreciation step-down after 2030 as Openreach copper is retired.
4.
Substantial asset revaluation: Chorus altered its accounting policy to a revaluation model to better reflect the fair value of its completed network. The company recorded a massive $983 million increase in the carrying amount of its network assets during the fiscal year ending June 30, 2026. This adjustment demonstrates how a utility-style regulatory asset base revaluation can strengthen a telco's balance sheet. This accounting shift underscores how completed fiber networks serve as strategic, long-term assets.
