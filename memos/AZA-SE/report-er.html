Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The 16 October 2026 results shifted the Avanza investment case from "strong inflows plus resilient monetisation" to "strong inflows, but monetisation must now be proven," because operating income and operating profit were each 2% below the 18-contributor consensus while every core revenue line missed expectations, revealing that excellent customer and savings-capital growth is not automatically converting into earnings at the rate the market had assumed. The most important change is that brokerage income per turnover fell from 11.0 to 10.3 basis points as Private Banking and Pro customers increased their share of trading from 26% to 30%, contradicting Deutsche Bank's pre-results assumption that a richer foreign-trading and affluent-customer mix would largely protect brokerage monetisation and leaving the 18-to-36-month EPS outlook dependent on whether yields recover or remain structurally lower. Q3 delivered a modest published-consensus miss, not a franchise failure, because Avanza reported operating income of SEK1,283m against company-compiled consensus of SEK1,310m, operating profit of SEK920m against

SEK935m and net profit of SEK790m against SEK801m, producing basic EPS before dilution of SEK4.90 versus the broker-reported consensus of SEK4.99, while the result was materially worse against Deutsche Bank's individual SEK1,470m operating-income and SEK931m net-profit forecast that had been approximately 17% above published consensus. Every core revenue line missed expectations, with net brokerage income 5% below consensus at SEK308m, currency-related income 8% below at SEK197m, fund commissions 3% below at SEK244m and net interest income 2% below at SEK464m, while only other income exceeded expectations by 41% at SEK69m, primarily driven by higher Avanza Markets income rather than a recurring earnings stream.

The revenue shortfall was concentrated in monetisation rather than activity or assets, because savings capital rose 21% year on year to SEK1,277bn, platform net inflows reached SEK23.1bn, up 61% year on year, and mutual-fund inflows reached a record SEK18bn, representing 32% of Swedish fund-market inflows, yet brokerage-generating turnover fell 5% quarter on quarter, brokerage-generating notes declined 14% and gross brokerage income per turnover dropped to 10.3 basis points from 11.0 basis points in Q2. Customer behaviour is shifting in ways that reduce near-term revenue quality, because two-thirds of Q3 net inflows came from existing customers rather than new customers according to CEO Gustaf Unger, while annualised income per customer fell 6% quarter on quarter to SEK2,170 and the average fund margin declined to 22.2 basis points from 22.9 basis points as customers favoured lower-cost index products, showing that the strategic shift toward deeper wallet share is proceeding but without proportionate revenue conversion.

Operating expenses provided a partial offset, coming in at SEK362m against consensus of SEK373m, a 3% favourable variance that absorbed much of the revenue shortfall and allowed the operating margin to remain at 72% versus 71% expected, although management retained 9% Swedish cost-growth guidance for full-year 2026 and an 8% annual cost-growth framework through 2030, indicating that the cost beat was primarily seasonal rather than a permanent improvement in the cost base.

EPS forecasts were modestly downgraded, with DNB Carnegie's Keric, McBeath and Jonsson reducing adjusted EPS to SEK18.90 from SEK19.04 for 2026, leaving 2027 at SEK19.29 versus SEK19.34 previously, and cutting 2028 to SEK20.79 from SEK20.93, while the broker retained Buy and cut its target price to SEK425 from SEK430, valuing Avanza at approximately 18 times 2027 earnings and roughly 15% below its historical valuation.

Deutsche Bank's Killian and Goy retained Buy and their SEK440 target price despite acknowledging the revenue and operating-profit misses, because they regarded 21% savings-capital growth, SEK23.1bn of platform inflows and SEK18bn of fund inflows as evidence that the asset-growth engine remained intact, while their unchanged valuation stance indicates that the franchise's savings-capital growth is expected to ultimately outweigh nearterm yield compression.

Jefferies' Roberts, Li, Mills and Gris Trillo maintained Underperform with a SEK335 price target against a reference share price of SEK376.30, implying 11% downside, because the Q3 miss reinforced their pre-existing concern that lower income per customer, competitive pricing, Denmark execution and technology investment could produce weaker sustainable EPS than the growth narrative assumes, showing that the cautious interpretation of Avanza's expansion and monetisation was strengthened rather than overturned by the results.

Guidance provided only a small near-term EPS positive, because Avanza reduced expected 2026 Danish expansion costs from SEK50m to SEK40m after completing more work internally, but retained the total pre-launch budget of SEK120m to SEK150m and expects profitability approximately five years after the planned second-half 2027 launch, meaning the revision improves near-term phasing without removing medium-term dilution risk or changing the strategic investment commitment.

Avanza Sigma reached SEK710m of assets under management one month after its broad launch to Private Banking and Pro customers on 31 August 2026, exceeding what the market could confidently model before launch and opening a new fee stream charging 0.55% to 0.85% for digital discretionary equity management at roughly half the cost of traditional alternatives, but the company disclosed no customer count, acquisition cost, margins, retention or payback target, leaving Sigma as strategically valuable option value rather than a material EPS contributor during the next few quarters according to DNB Carnegie.

Net interest income remains exposed to policy-rate direction, because Avanza stated that a one-percentage-point rate cut would reduce full-year net interest income by approximately SEK420m while a one-percentage-point hike could add approximately SEK1.1bn before volume, behaviour and competition effects, and DNB Carnegie estimated approximately 4% 2027 EPS upside if Swedish short rates follow the forward curve toward 2.25%, although the Q3 NII miss of 2% versus consensus showed that lower deposit volumes and repricing can offset part of that benefit.

The leverage ratio improved to 4.8% from 4.4% at year-end 2025, leaving an estimated SEK46bn of deposit capacity before falling below the 3.5% requirement including guidance, which preserves room for mortgage and deposit growth but makes balance-sheet expansion a future constraint rather than an unlimited EPS catalyst, particularly as management temporarily paused new mortgage applications for customers with SEK3m to SEK10m of savings and raised their mortgage rate by 10 basis points.

Trading activity weakened more sharply at Avanza than at named peers, because Deutsche Bank's September tracker showed total trades down 10% year on year at Avanza versus 5% at Nordnet and 3% at flatexDEGIRO, while Avanza's customer growth of 8% year on year and assets under custody growth of 21% lagged Nordnet's 12% and 28% respectively, suggesting that the cyclical explanation is credible but does not fully exclude relative competitive or customer-mix pressure.

Nordnet's Q3 performance materially exceeded Avanza's, with operating income of SEK1,550m, up 20% year on year, operating profit of SEK1,096m, up 24%, and a 71% operating margin against 69% a year earlier, compared with Avanza's 72% Q3 margin but slower year-on-year growth, while Deutsche Bank's comparison showed Nordnet at a 76% operating margin and approximately 42% return on equity in 2025 against Avanza at 62% and 39%, indicating that regional scale is converting into superior profitability.

Regulatory risk was deferred rather than removed, because the December 2025 political agreement on the European Retail Investment Strategy does not introduce a general European inducement ban and the Swedish Financial Supervisory Authority has said it will not investigate a Swedish ban as part of implementation, but member states retain discretion to impose stricter rules that could affect external funds and Avanza Markets, while political debate over a potential bank tax, investment-savings-account taxation and endowment-insurance interest deductibility creates additional policy uncertainty.

Cost discipline is a deferred promise rather than current operating leverage, because Q3 operating expenses rose 12% year on year despite being 3% below consensus, management retained the 8% long-term Swedish costgrowth framework and expects investment to be heavier at the beginning of the strategy period before reaching 5% by 2030, while Avanza accelerated cloud migration, expanded the workforce from 709 to 812 employees and is building a separate market-agnostic platform for Denmark rather than adapting the Swedish system, creating parallel technology, governance and regulatory complexity before any quantified efficiency benefit has appeared.

Technology and execution risks are increasing, because a former Avanza business developer said the Swedish infrastructure was not built for the company's present scale and high-traffic events had caused outages while limiting recent customer-facing innovation, a Nordea software-development leader judged that incumbent banks can close specialist-platform technology gaps within one to two years if they prioritise the area, and Jefferies noted that neither Avanza nor Nordnet appeared prominently in searches for Swedish artificial-intelligence investment platforms, warning that generative search could bypass Avanza's historically strong direct brand and logged-in distribution without immediate customer-account closure. The competitive balance is shifting, because a former SAVR commercial executive said SAVR and Montrose were offering lower foreign-exchange fees and more frequent product improvements after Avanza and Nordnet had faced limited direct competition, while Avanza's Q3 brokerage yield already fell to 10.3 basis points from 11.0 basis points in Q2, suggesting disruption may first appear as lower revenue per trade and margin compression rather than reported customer losses.

DNB Carnegie's recovery scenario implies 17% 2027 EPS upside if first-half trading activity per user returns and approximately 4% additional upside if Swedish short rates follow the forward curve, making its base case deliberately conservative but also dependent on a recovery that Q3 did not yet demonstrate, while the broker attributed the 6% to 7% brokerage and foreign-exchange commission shortfall to softer broad-retail sentiment and a greater share of lower-priced Private Banking and Pro clients rather than permanent demand destruction.

Deutsche Bank's unchanged SEK440 target and Buy rating are the observable evidence that the valuation conclusion did not change, because the accessible results note does not provide a complete pre-results and post-results EPS, revenue or profit forecast table, while Killian and Goy attributed the weaker quarter to lower activity, client mix, index-fund margin pressure and lower deposits without explicitly classifying those effects as permanent, assuming instead that the franchise's savings-capital growth will ultimately outweigh near-term yield compression.

Management presented Q3 as operationally strong but acknowledged that income declined from Q2 because lower summer volatility reduced trading-related revenue and lower deposits pressured net interest income, while emphasising operating profit of SEK920m, earnings per share of SEK4.90 and return on equity of 44%, which exceeded Avanza's stated minimum return-on-equity target of 35%, although the result was 2% below company-compiled operating-profit consensus, so "strong" meant strong year-on-year profitability rather than an earnings beat.

The strongest case against the cautious conclusion is that Q3 simply reflected a temporary combination of summer volatility, cautious broad-retail customers and an unusually high share of lower-priced Private Banking and Pro activity, because CEO Gustaf Unger said the lower margin reflected temporary market sentiment, lower volatility and an unusually negative quarter rather than a customer-mix shift that should be extrapolated, while DNB Carnegie retained Buy because it saw little evidence that Avanza had lost relevance and attributed much of the weakness to market sentiment, treating the income miss as largely sentiment-driven and cyclical.

What would change my mind: a return of brokerage income per turnover toward 11 basis points, stabilisation of fund margins near 22.9 basis points, recovery in income per customer and net interest income at or above consensus in the January 2027 preliminary statement would demonstrate that Q3 was cyclical, while a further two quarters near the Q3 monetisation levels would overturn the constructive EPS view and support Jefferies' more structurally cautious Underperform stance, because Deutsche Bank's pre-results expectation that richer foreign-trading mix would protect monetisation was not validated and the same customer growth and savings inflows would then produce less revenue and lower EPS than the market's structural-growth narrative assumes.

The two most important questions for upcoming disclosures are whether transactions per customer, brokerage yield and foreign-exchange income recover as market volatility improves, because the preliminary 2026 financial statement on 22 January 2027 and the Q1 report on 16 April 2027 should distinguish temporary activity weakness from structural revenue dilution, and whether mortgage growth is being matched by deposits without consuming excessive leverage capacity while Denmark, cloud migration and Sigma remain within budget and produce measurable productivity or asset-gathering benefits, because management disclosed a 4.8% leverage ratio and only SEK46bn of estimated deposit capacity before the applicable constraint binds. Other side of the story: Avanza could still outperform the cautious conclusion if Swedish risk appetite improves, rates rise without damaging savings demand, trading yields recover and the company converts its large existing-customer wallet opportunity into higher-value pensions, Private Banking, lending and Sigma assets, because the structural franchise case remains intact with 21% savings-capital growth, record fund inflows, 0.7% rolling twelve-month customer churn and approximately one-fifth of Sweden's population already served, while the company's 72% operating margin and 44% return on equity remain exceptional relative to most financial platforms, but Q3 established the franchise strength more clearly than it established the earnings conversion required to justify a sustained re-rating beyond Deutsche Bank's SEK440 target and approximately 18 times 2027 earnings.

Market share, ahead of penetration: Avanza’s 8.7% savings-capital share remained well below its 20.5% penetration of the Swedish population, while its rolling twelve-month share of Swedish savings-market inflows was 11.9%. Deutsche Bank therefore viewed the opportunity as increasingly dependent on capturing a larger share of existing customers’ assets through private banking, pensions and guided savings products rather than relying only on new-account growth.
4.
Customer quality, unusually sticky: Avanza reported rolling twelve-month customer churn of only 0.7% and average daily active users of 490,000 in the third quarter. That retention profile supports the long-term EPS case, but it also means the key question entering the results was whether existing customers would transfer more assets to Avanza, not simply whether headline customer numbers would continue rising.
5.
Savings-market runway, material: Avanza estimates the Swedish savings market at SEK13,900bn, against its own SEK1,277bn of savings capital at the September quarter-end. The company’s stated 2030 ambition is average annual Swedish savings-capital growth of 15% and more than SEK2,000bn of savings capital, implying a materially higher asset base than at the time of the Q3 results.
6.
Revenue history, increasingly balanced: Deutsche Bank found that brokerage represented more than 40% of operating income during 2018 to 2021 but only 27% in 2025, while fund commissions represented 18%, currency-related income 15% and net interest income 35% in 2025. The shift reduced Avanza’s direct dependence on retail trading volumes, although the Q3 miss showed that trading and customer mix still had immediate EPS consequences.

Recent fundamental record, strong: Avanza’s operating income increased from SEK3,900m in 2024 to SEK4,495m in 2025, while operating profit rose from SEK2,621m to SEK3,078m and the operating margin improved from 67% to 68%. Entering Q3, this strong first-half delivery led DNB Carnegie to raise its 2026 to 2028 EPS forecasts by 2% to 3% after the Q2 review.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Elevated earnings bar: Modular Finance consensus from 18 contributors expected Q3 operating income of SEK1,310m, operating profit of SEK935m and net profit of SEK801m, making the central market question whether Avanza could sustain the exceptional momentum seen in the first half.

20.
Capital returns: The market expected Avanza to maintain its 70% dividend payout policy and retain a capital- light balance sheet, but no buyback was embedded as a meaningful EPS catalyst because the company had authorisation to repurchase shares without having repurchased any by the end of September.
21.
Peer positioning: The pre-results comparison was not simply about quarterly earnings, because Avanza represented a domestic wallet-share story while Nordnet represented pan-Nordic scale and German expansion, flatexDEGIRO had the strongest trading resilience, and Fineco offered a more diversified European savings and investment model.

Nordnet contrast: Deutsche Bank rated Avanza Buy at a SEK440 target and Nordnet Hold at SEK360, arguing that Avanza’s domestic white space and below-history valuation offered more upside even though Nordnet had higher profitability and broader geographic reach.

Section 14 – Value chain: political and regulatory

Section summary
  1. The 16 October results did not reveal a new regulatory earnings shock, but political uncertainty around taxation, inducements, pension transfers and leverage has increased the range of possible EPS outcomes through 2029.
1.
Regulatory relief, incomplete: EU inducement risk has been deferred rather than eliminated, because the December 2025 political agreement on the European Retail Investment Strategy does not introduce a general European ban, while member states retain discretion to impose stricter rules and the Swedish Financial Supervisory Authority has said it will not investigate a Swedish ban as part of implementation; Avanza therefore avoided the immediate revenue shock brokers had previously feared, but external-fund distribution and Avanza Markets remain exposed to future national action.
2.
Political tax risk, rising: The election increased policy uncertainty for savers, with CEO Gustaf Unger identifying debate over a potential bank tax, the design of investment savings account taxation and the deductibility of interest on endowment-insurance-backed lending; the 2026 tax-free threshold of SEK300,000 supports customer investment flows versus the pre-2025 regime, but a future reversal or bank levy could reduce saving activity, increase Avanza’s tax burden or weaken net interest income, while management provided no EPS sensitivity.
3.
Tax incentive, supportive: The SEK300,000 ISK exemption lowers entry barriers for Swedish customers from January 2026 and should support long-term equity and fund participation relative to the previous SEK150,000 threshold, which is favourable for Avanza’s customer acquisition and savings-capital growth; however, the OECD cautioned that the benefit may accrue disproportionately to existing wealthier savers, so the policy’s incremental effect on new customer flows remains uncertain rather than a reliable nearterm EPS catalyst.
4.
Pension reform, meaningful upside: Transfer reform could unlock sticky assets because Avanza, Lysa and Nordnet asked the Swedish Ministry of Finance to remove the requirement for a former employer’s signature when transferring certain paid-up unit-linked and endowment-insurance policies; management expected simplification to improve Avanza’s competitive pension proposition, but no reform had been delivered by the Q3 report, so the market could not yet include a material earnings contribution from the SEK1,062bn non-collectively agreed occupational-pension market.
5.
Lending-tax exposure, negative: Endowment-insurance collateral lost tax deductibility when Swedish rules fully took effect on 1 January 2026, because the Swedish Tax Agency’s interpretation treats endowment insurance as unacceptable collateral for the relevant interest deduction; Avanza and other affected parties asked the Ministry of Finance to amend the law, but until then the change can reduce demand for margin lending against such policies and constrain a lending stream that brokers expected to grow with Private Banking.
6.
Prudential burden, manageable but binding: Leverage, not risk-weighted capital, constrains expansion because Avanza’s 30 September leverage ratio was 4.8% against a 3.5% requirement including Pillar 2 guidance, leaving an estimated SEK46bn of additional deposit capacity; the stronger position exceeded the market’s concern that post-Sparkonto+ deposits would immediately restrict growth, but deposit inflows, mortgages and future Danish operations will continue consuming regulatory capacity and could compete with dividends for capital.
7.
Sparkonto+ precedent, costly: Regulation already changed Avanza’s balance sheet when stricter treatment of deposits sourced through third-party investment platforms made the partner-bank model less attractive, contributing to the withdrawal of Sparkonto+ and moving some deposits onto Avanza’s own balance sheet; Jefferies’ Roberts, Li, Mills and Gris Trillo linked the change to higher capital needs and Avanza’s 2025 and 2026 Additional Tier 1 issuance, showing that regulatory interpretation can affect funding, leverage, customer pricing and shareholder distributions even without a formal prohibition.
8.
Data and resilience, material compliance cost: Privacy and operational resilience remain earnings risks as Avanza expands cloud services, artificial-intelligence tooling and a separate Danish platform, because its September 2026 personal-data policy requires GDPR-compliant processing, incident management, data registers and controls over transfers outside the European Economic Area; the earlier SEK15m Swedish Authority for Privacy Protection fine shows that compliance failures can create direct costs and reputational damage, while DNB Carnegie’s Sellander described data breaches as potentially more damaging than routine technology spending.
9.
topic-specific reports Denmark, regulatory execution risk: The Danish launch adds a second compliance regime before revenue begins, as Avanza must adapt its business model, licensing, reporting, customer-protection processes and platform controls to local requirements ahead of the planned second-half 2027 launch; management’s SEK120m–150m total start-up estimate and approximately five-year path to profitability assume orderly execution, while its own report warns that regulatory delays or localisation difficulties could increase costs or reduce growth and profitability.
10.
Other side of the story: The regulatory downside may be overstated because Avanza already operates without commission-driven advice, supports transparent low-fee investing, maintains substantial capital surplus and has no general inducement ban under the agreed European framework; nevertheless, the leading conclusion would be wrong if Sweden later tightens commissions, reverses the ISK incentive, preserves pension-transfer barriers or applies stricter Danish requirements than management’s launch budget assumes.

Section 15 – Value chain: supply side

Section summary
  1. Avanza’s supply-side economics have become more technology- and talent-intensive, with no evidence of a supplier failure but clear evidence that cloud migration, payment commissions, specialist hiring and Danish platform development are raising the cost base before efficiency benefits are proven.
1.
Technology costs, rising: Avanza reported that 9M 2026 operating costs reached SEK1,145m, including SEK176m for information-technology systems and licences, or 15% of total costs, while external services added SEK77m, or 7%; compared with the company’s historical cost development, this indicates that suppliers and technology infrastructure are becoming a larger operating input even though Q3 costs were 3% below consensus.
2.
Cloud migration, expensed early: Avanza said other costs increased primarily because of information- technology and development costs related to its cloud journey, and that its policy is to expense as much technology investment as possible rather than defer it on the balance sheet; the market expected cloud migration to improve scalability and future operating leverage, but the current delivery is near-term EPS pressure without a quantified supplier saving or productivity benefit.
3.
Supplier disclosure, limited: Avanza’s Q3 materials identify technology systems, licences, external services and payment commissions as cost categories, but do not name cloud providers, exchange or clearing counterparties, execution and liquidity providers, data vendors, banking partners, or contractual concentration; consequently, the research does not establish whether Avanza has diversified suppliers, fixed-price protections, material renewal risk or negotiating leverage.
4.
Payment economics, worsening: Avanza stated that other commission expenses increased mainly because of higher payment commissions, while income from Avanza Markets rose to SEK233m from SEK177m and partly offset the pressure; this suggests that transaction and payment infrastructure is becoming more expensive per unit of activity, and the Q3 result gives no evidence that the higher payment cost was temporary or recoverable through customer pricing.
5.
Internal capability, partly protective: Avanza reduced 2026 international-expansion expense guidance to SEK40m from SEK50m after completing more work internally, but retained the total SEK120m to SEK150m investment estimate through the Danish launch; the saving therefore demonstrates some internal engineering and execution capacity, while the unchanged total budget shows that internal delivery lowers near-term external spend rather than removing the underlying resource requirement. Danish platform, duplicated supply burden: Avanza is developing a separate international platform instead of adapting its Swedish system, with SEK14m of 9M 2026 costs attributed to international expansion and SEK3m capitalised; compared with the market expectation of a disciplined Danish option, this creates a parallel technology, testing, control and maintenance burden that could weaken group margins before launch and complicate service reliability across two platforms.
6.
Labour supply, expensive but available: Personnel represented SEK682m, or 60% of 9M 2026 operating costs, and Avanza attributed the increase to more employees and annual salary adjustments; the company reported 812 employees at the end of Q3 versus 709 a year earlier, so the supply of specialist technology and product labour has been secured through hiring, but at the cost of a larger fixed base that must be absorbed by future income growth. Talent leverage, not yet demonstrated: Avanza reported that employee engagement remained strong and that its technology investment should improve scalability, while management expects employee additions in 2027 to be lower than in 2026; the market expected moderation in hiring to support operating leverage, but no current 2026 employee Net Promoter Score, turnover update or quantified productivity measure was disclosed to prove that the enlarged specialist workforce is becoming more efficient. Platform resilience, a supply-side risk: Avanza’s business model depends on the availability, stability, security and capacity of its digital infrastructure, particularly as cloud migration, pre- and post-market United States trading, Sigma and Denmark increase system demands; the Q3 disclosures reported no new outage or service-level failure, but they also supplied no uptime, incident, recovery-time or vendor-service data, leaving resilience risk unquantified rather than removed.
7.
Broker interpretation, cost benefits remain conditional: DNB Carnegie expected Avanza’s separate international platform and artificial-intelligence-assisted development approach to improve build efficiency, but also warned that running dual platforms could be suboptimal over time; against that expectation, Q3 showed the company still expensing technology and development costs while Danish profitability remains approximately five years after launch, so any supply-side margin benefit is a future assumption rather than a current earnings driver. Market infrastructure, no confirmed change: The Q3 report confirms that Avanza launched pre- and postmarket trading in United States securities and maintained strong foreign trading, but the supplied evidence does not identify the relevant exchanges, clearing houses, market makers, execution providers or data suppliers; therefore, no reliable conclusion can be drawn on execution pricing, liquidity concentration, counterparty health or whether the new trading hours improve or worsen unit economics.
8.
EPS implication, initially negative: The visible supply-side changes point to higher personnel, technology, payment, cloud and international-platform costs before corresponding revenue benefits, while the company’s 9% 2026 domestic cost-growth guidance remains unchanged and approximately 80% of Danish investment is expected to be expensed; this makes supply-side execution a modest near-term EPS drag and a potentially important three-year operating-leverage test.
9.
Other side of the story: The adverse supply-side reading may be too conservative if Avanza’s internal engineering capability, cloud migration and artificial-intelligence tools reduce future unit costs faster than the company’s 8% long-term cost-growth framework implies; however, the conclusion would change only after Avanza discloses measurable vendor savings, platform reliability, productivity gains or lower costs relative to savings capital, none of which was provided with the Q3 results.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Section summary
  1. The 16 October 2026 results did not break Avanza’s structural growth thesis, but they changed the near- term EPS debate from “strong inflows plus resilient monetisation” to “strong inflows, but proof is now required that trading yields, fund margins and net interest income can recover.”
1.
Most important change: Q3 showed that Avanza’s excellent customer and savings-capital growth is not automatically translating into earnings at the rate the market expected, because operating income and operating profit were both 2% below the 18-contributor consensus while brokerage income missed by 5%, currency-related income by 8%, fund commissions by 3% and net interest income by 2%.
2.
Quality of growth, downgraded: The market expected a richer foreign-trading and affluent-customer mix to cushion seasonal trading normalisation, but Deutsche Bank’s Killian and Goy instead saw brokerage and currency-related income decline 12% quarter on quarter as the brokerage margin fell from 11.0 to 10.3 basis points, challenging the assumption that mix would protect monetisation.
3.
Structural franchise, still intact: Avanza delivered 21% year-on-year savings-capital growth, SEK23.1bn of quarterly platform inflows and record SEK18bn mutual-fund inflows, which was stronger than the softer twelve-month flow trend that DNB Carnegie had identified before the results and supports continued assetbased earnings growth over the next three years.
4.
Consensus expectations, materially too high in one case: Deutsche Bank’s pre-results model expected SEK1,470m of operating income and SEK931m of net profit, approximately 17% above company-compiled consensus, whereas Avanza reported SEK1,283m and SEK790m respectively, so the results were not merely a small miss against the published average but a substantial failure to meet the most bullish earnings scenario.
5.
EPS forecasts, modestly lower: DNB Carnegie reduced adjusted EPS to SEK18.90 from SEK19.04 for 2026, left 2027 at SEK19.29 versus SEK19.34, and cut 2028 to SEK20.79 from SEK20.93, indicating that the Q3 shortfall reduced the earnings base slightly but did not cause the broker to abandon its longer-term recovery assumptions.
6.
Valuation, modestly reset: DNB Carnegie cut its target price to SEK425 from SEK430 but retained Buy, while Deutsche Bank retained Buy and its SEK440 target, showing that the accessible positive research treated the miss as a timing or cycle issue rather than a permanent impairment of Avanza’s franchise value. Bear case, unchanged and strengthened: Jefferies’ Roberts, Li, Mills and Gris Trillo maintained Underperform and a SEK335 target against a reference price of SEK376.30, implying 11% downside, because the Q3 miss reinforced their concern that lower income per customer, competitive pricing, Denmark execution and technology investment could produce weaker sustainable EPS than the growth narrative assumes. Cost benefit, not enough to reverse the revenue signal: Operating expenses of SEK362m were 3% below consensus and the 72% operating margin exceeded the 71% expected by the market, but management retained 9% Swedish cost-growth guidance for 2026 and an 8% annual cost-growth framework through 2030, so the cost beat appears primarily seasonal rather than a permanent improvement in the cost base. - Earnings Calls Denmark, only a small EPS positive: Avanza reduced 2026 Denmark-related costs from SEK50m to SEK40m, but retained the SEK120m to SEK150m total start-up estimate through the planned second-half 2027 launch and expects profitability approximately five years after launch, meaning the guidance change improves nearterm phasing without removing medium-term dilution risk. - Earnings Calls Strategic options, not current earnings support: Avanza Sigma reached SEK710m of assets under management one month after launch and expands the company into digital discretionary equity management, but no revenue, margin, retention or break-even target was disclosed, so Sigma should be treated as valuable option value rather than a material EPS contributor during the next few quarters.
7.
Rate upside, conditional rather than embedded: Avanza stated that a one-percentage-point policy-rate hike could increase full-year net interest income by approximately SEK1.1bn before volume, behaviour and competition effects, while DNB Carnegie estimated approximately 4% 2027 EPS upside if Swedish short rates follow the forward curve, but the Q3 NII miss showed that deposit migration and repricing can offset part of that benefit.
8.
External evidence, mildly adverse: Avanza’s September trading activity declined more sharply than that of Nordnet and flatexDEGIRO, with total trades down 10% year on year at Avanza versus 5% at Nordnet and 3% at flatexDEGIRO, while customer and asset growth also lagged Nordnet, so the cyclical explanation is credible but does not fully exclude relative competitive pressure.
9.
Non-obvious reading: The strongest underappreciated interpretation is that Avanza may be succeeding strategically while becoming less profitable per customer, because two-thirds of Q3 inflows came from existing customers and fund demand was exceptionally strong, yet annualised income per customer fell 6% quarter on quarter and fund margins declined from 22.9 to 22.2 basis points. - Earnings Calls Strongest case against this conclusion: The bearish reading could be wrong if Q3 simply reflected a temporary combination of summer volatility, cautious broad-retail customers and an unusually high share of lower-priced Private Banking and Pro activity, because DNB Carnegie estimates approximately 17% 2027 EPS upside if first-half activity per user returns and considers the current estimates prudent. What would change my mind: A return of brokerage income per turnover toward 11 basis points, stabilisation of fund margins near 22.9 basis points, recovery in income per customer and net interest income at or above consensus in the January 2027 preliminary statement would demonstrate that Q3 was cyclical, while a further two quarters near the Q3 monetisation levels would overturn the constructive EPS view. Next disclosure priority, trading economics: The preliminary 2026 financial statement on 22 January 2027 and the Q1 report on 16 April 2027 should show whether transactions per customer, brokerage yield and foreign-exchange income recover as market volatility improves, because these figures will distinguish temporary activity weakness from structural revenue dilution.
10.
Next disclosure priority, capital and execution: Investors should also require evidence that mortgage growth is being matched by deposits without consuming excessive leverage capacity, and that Denmark, cloud migration and Sigma remain within budget while producing measurable productivity or asset-gathering benefits, because management disclosed a 4.8% leverage ratio and only SEK46bn of estimated deposit capacity before the applicable constraint binds. - Earnings Calls Other side of the story: Avanza could still outperform the cautious conclusion if Swedish risk appetite improves, rates rise without damaging savings demand, trading yields recover and the company converts its large existing-customer wallet opportunity into higher-value pensions, Private Banking, lending and Sigma assets, but Q3 established the franchise strength more clearly than it established the earnings conversion required to justify a sustained re-rating.
