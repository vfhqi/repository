Planned Capacity Additions to 2030

1.
Ypsomed capacity roadmap: Ypsomed is executing a major capital investment program to expand its autoinjector manufacturing footprint significantly. According to Ypsomed's Q2 2026 earnings call, the company is expanding its installed manufacturing capacity from approximately 350 million units to 1 billion units by the end of the decade. This expansion is supported by a site development package in Solothurn, Switzerland, totaling approximately CHF 200 million, which includes the commissioning of two new high-volume assembly lines and injection molding machines for autoinjectors. Morgan Stanley's research from October 16, 2026 outlines Eli Lilly's site-by-site peak auto-injector capacity, projecting 210 million annual units at peak across facilities including RTP, North Carolina (30 million), Concord, North Carolina (30 million), National Resilience in Ohio (50 million), Pleasant Prairie, Wisconsin (25 million), Rome, Italy (15 million), and Alzey, Germany (30 million). The same Morgan Stanley report indicates Eli Lilly can produce an additional 55 million Kwikpens, bringing its total peak in-house delivery platform to 265 million units. Meanwhile, Novo Nordisk has secured direct excess assembly capacity following its major strategic acquisition of Catalent's fill-finish sites.

Demand Analysis and Equilibrium Timeline

3.
Peptide demand curves: The global demand for GLP-1 autoinjectors is scaling at an unprecedented rate, outpacing immediate manufacturing capability. Systematix Institutional Research notes that the global patient pool using GLP-1 therapies is expected to expand ten-fold from roughly 10 million patients to more than 110 million patients by 2030. Because a standard weekly autoinjector regimen requires 48 single-dose devices per patient annually, this projected patient volume implies a theoretical peak demand of billions of autoinjectors by 2030. 35 Morgan Stanley estimates that total US and ex-US diabesity GLP-1 patient counts will reach approximately 38 million and 68 million respectively by 2030, which will continue to stretch standard glass and component supply chains.
4.
Supply catch-up projections: Most industry experts and analysts expect the severe capacity crunch to persist for the next 12 to 24 months before supply finally matches demand. A senior director at SHL Medical noted that syringe suppliers and specialized glass cartridge makers represent severe industry-wide bottlenecks that have struggled to keep pace with demand. Former program management staff at Stevanato Group observed that raw tube glass manufacturing setups are highly complex and represent a longer-term constraint than plastic injection molding. Consequently, Morgan Stanley notes that supply will remain tight in the near term, with equilibrium only gradually being established as massive multi-year facilities like Concord and Alzey ramp to full utilization between 2025 and 2027.
5.
Past medtech capacity rollouts: The current rapid scaling of autoinjectors represents a unique phenomenon in medtech, with capital expenditure profiles diverging from historical precedents. A former Vice President at West Pharmaceutical Services remarked that during the COVID-19 pandemic, a massive bolus of packaging capacity was installed, which initially helped satisfy early GLP-1 volumes. However, a former CFO of Gerresheimer cautioned that aggressive over-investment based on pandemic-era demand can lead to operational mismatches, prompting peers like Gerresheimer to transition toward higher-margin, core packaging lines like ready-to-fill syringes to optimize returns.

Jefferies notes on September 15, 2026 that Ypsomed utilizes volume-based pricing structures where lower-than-expected production volumes are offset by higher unit prices. This structured pricing means that under-absorption has a limited impact on earnings per share. Therefore, these contracts shield Ypsomed from unfavorable margin compression during market downturns.
2.
Flexible capacity reallocation: The company possesses the contractual and operational flexibility to mitigate localized client volume shortfalls. Jefferies reports on September 15, 2026 that Ypsomed can reallocate its manufacturing capacity when specific customers under-absorb their slots. This capability allows management to secure additional contracts and leverage its broader portfolio to maintain high utilization. Consequently, the operational setup ensures that capacity remains highly utilized and profitable over the long term.
3.
Sticky developer relationships: Customer relationships in the autoinjector industry remain highly insulated from rapid competitive switching once clinical development begins. Jefferies reports on September 15, 2026 that customers are locked in through sticky development and approval processes. Competitive losses are almost entirely confined to the initial platformselection stage rather than post-commercialization. This structural friction ensures that Ypsomed enjoys long-term commercial revenue durability for its primary platforms.
4.
Single-source negotiation leverage: Ypsomed maintains strong bargaining power over pharmaceutical giants through its unique positioning in the supply chain. Jefferies reports on September 15, 2026 that the company benefits from a predominantly single-source supplier model. This pricing power guarantees that Ypsomed can maintain attractive long-term commercial pricing terms before rivals catch up.

The Myth of Rigid Minimum Volumes

5.
Absence of rigid guarantees: Despite common investor assumptions, rigid volume commitments are generally absent from commercial autoinjector supply agreements. An expert call with a former Stevanato Group head of product management on March 10, 2026 reveals that pharmaceutical clients do not commit to rigid minimum volumes. Instead, standard agreements utilize price brackets and rolling 18-month forecasts to manage capacity. This commercial design means that Ypsomed's contracts rely on flexible price brackets rather than take-or-pay fees.
6.
Pricing bracket mechanics: Contract negotiations in this sector focus on price brackets tied to rolling purchase orders. An expert call with a former Stevanato Group product head on March 10, 2026 explains that clients provide only six months of binding purchase orders. Suppliers must negotiate price tiers that adjust dynamically based on realized volumes. This operational framework suggests that Ypsomed faces potential short-term volume fluctuations if customers adjust their forecasts.
7.
Procurement priority dynamics: Pharmaceutical procurement teams prioritize manufacturing reliability and quality over device unit costs during negotiations. An expert call with an associate director at MSD on November 7, 2025 highlights that the length and terms of supply agreements are heavily negotiated to guarantee supply security. This prioritization suggests that Ypsomed can withstand aggressive pricing pressure from emerging low-cost competitors. This focus on security over cost solidifies the durability of Ypsomed's long-term commercial relationships.

Contrary evidence: Ypsomed's commercial contracts face significant pricing headwinds as major pharmaceutical customers demand substantial cost reductions. Jefferies reports on July 9, 2026 that media reports indicate Novo Nordisk has approached its suppliers requesting cost reductions to improve its competitiveness. Because participation in these cost-reduction schemes is tied to broader supplier evaluations, Ypsomed faces severe margins and volume risks if it complies. This indicates that Ypsomed's contractual pricing is highly vulnerable to unilateral customer pressure.
