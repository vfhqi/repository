EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

UIE Plc's H1 2026 financial results revealed a significant earnings contraction driven by non-cash fair value adjustments, despite management's insistence that core underlying operations remain robust. The company's operating income under business reporting plummeted by USD 52.3 million to just USD 10.1 million in H1 2026 from USD 62.4 million in H1 2025, while consolidated net profit declined to USD 10.5 million from USD 61.6 million. This material earnings collapse represents the defining takeaway from the period, though the underlying drivers warrant careful disaggregation between operational performance and portfolio accounting volatility.

This 13% decline to USD 215.4 million was driven by broader Swedish equity market volatility, currency translation headwinds from a 6% SEK depreciation against the USD, and a 27% share price drop in Hexagon following the May 2026 spin-off of Octave Intelligence. Similarly, Greenbridge suffered a negative fair value adjustment of USD 3.3 million despite continued solid underlying growth, caused entirely by compressed software multiples in public markets. These portfolio valuation headwinds accounted for USD 35.3 million of the consolidated earnings decline, exposing UIE's structural vulnerability to macroeconomic factors beyond operational control.

Beneath the headline volatility, United Plantations delivered operationally resilient but financially mixed results. Management proudly showcased all-time high production volumes of crude palm oil and palm kernel during the first half of 2026 as proof of competitive execution. However, United Plantations' consolidated net profit contribution to UIE decreased by 8% to USD 42.7 million in H1 2026 from USD 46.2 million in H1 2025, slightly below buy-side whisper numbers.

This contraction reflected lower realized selling prices for crude palm oil, with average CPO selling prices falling 5% to MYR 4,156 per tonne, which management positioned as an external market factor beyond operational control. More concerning was the downstream refining collapse, where Unitata's operating profit plunged by 84% to MYR 7.6 million in H1 2026 from MYR 46.9 million in H1 2025, primarily caused by realized losses from buying back previously sold crude palm oil futures contracts in a rising market.

The earnings print forced management into a defensive posture during investor communications. Management actively directed investor attention away from the headline net profit contraction, arguing that portfolio assets demonstrated resilience under highly volatile and challenging global market conditions. The company highlighted that the reported net profit of USD 10.5 million was heavily influenced by non-cash adjustments under fair value accounting, presenting this reduction as a reporting technicality rather than a structural deterioration of the business.

However, the board formally guided that net profit will be lower for the full year 2026 compared to 2025, though they framed this outlook as dependent on volatile public equities rather than a structural downshift in business earning power.

Looking forward over an 18-month to 3-year horizon, UIE's EPS trajectory faces material headwinds despite elite plantation operational performance. The company entered H1 2026 with sell-side analysts expecting sustained CPO pricing strength driven by tightening global vegetable oil supply and expanding biodiesel mandates. Analysts at Jefferies raised their 2026 and 2027 average CPO price forecasts by RM50 per tonne to RM4,450 per tonne and RM4,550 per tonne respectively, driven by tightening global vegetable oil supply and intensifying weather anomalies.

However, global vegetable oil demand is increasingly rationed by record-high palm prices, shifting price-sensitive food buyers to soybean oil, with Malaysia's palm oil stockpiles rising 7.5% month-on-month to 2.82 million tonnes in August 2026, significantly exceeding consensus expectations. The structural forces shaping UIE's medium-term outlook present a mixed picture. On the positive side, global palm oil supply growth has structurally slowed to 1.3 million tonnes per year compared to the historical average increase of 2 million tonnes annually, with limited global land availability and moratoriums on new plantings implying future demand must be met through yield increases, which is expected to sustain elevated crude palm oil prices.

United Plantations delivered an outstanding Fresh Fruit Bunch yield of 29.31 metric tonnes per hectare in 2025, significantly superior to peer SD Guthrie's 18.95 metric tonnes per hectare, confirming that the company's agricultural practices continue to widen its competitive lead. This peer-leading productivity provides a defensive moat against commodity price volatility. However, three critical risks cloud the medium-term EPS outlook. First, UIE's consolidated net profit remains highly sensitive to Swedish equity market fluctuations via its strategic investment in Schörling, which suffered a negative fair value adjustment of USD 32.0 million in H1 2026 driven by market-wide volatility, representing an inherent risk of UIE's business model as the holding company cannot influence global public market movements.

Second, elevated costs of agricultural inputs including fertilizers, chemicals, and energy present persistent challenges to the plantation cost structure, with high input inflation weighing on operating margins for Malaysian planters. Third, climate centers project El Niño to persist and strengthen through early 2027, with extreme hot weather leading to increased bunch failure and water stress with a 6 to 12 month lag, representing a near-term threat to volume growth.

The investment case evolution post-H1 2026 centers on whether United Plantations' operational excellence can offset portfolio volatility and input cost inflation. UIE continues to face a significant valuation penalty at the holding company level, with its holding company discount standing at 41% at the end of June 2026, with underlying assets valued at USD 2.812 billion compared to UIE's market value of USD 1.646 billion.

This persistent NAV discount suggests the market assigns minimal value to UIE's active ownership model and views the holding structure as a value-destructive layer. UIE initiated a new share buyback program of up to DKK 150 million to acquire up to 600,000 shares by December 2027, following the completion of a DKK 245 million buyback in February 2026, representing a continuous mechanism to address UIE's persistent net asset value discount.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to a negative medium-term outlook rests on three pillars. First, if Nordic equity markets rebound and Swedish krona currency stabilizes, Schörling's fair value could recover USD 30-40 million in a single quarter, immediately restoring consolidated profitability. Second, United Plantations' 2026 commissioning of its new Seed Production Complex creates a commercial revenue stream that could partially offset refining margin compression.

Third, expanding EUDR compliance requirements structurally advantage integrated, traceable producers like United Plantations, potentially allowing premium pricing that offsets input cost inflation. The key monitoring variables for portfolio managers over the next six to twelve months should include quarterly Schörling NAV movements relative to Swedish equity indices, monthly Malaysian palm oil inventory levels as a leading indicator of pricing pressure, United Plantations' cash cost per tonne trends to assess whether mechanization offsets wage inflation, downstream refining margin recovery at Unitata and UniFuji, and any material changes to Indonesian or Malaysian biodiesel mandates that could alter structural CPO demand.

Additionally, UIE's commitment to resume bi-annual analyst briefings in 2026 provides a critical transparency checkpoint to assess management's confidence in the underlying operational trajectory versus their defensive posture during the H1 2026 release. In conclusion, UIE's H1 2026 earnings fundamentally did not change the long-term agricultural asset quality thesis around United Plantations' peer-leading yields and sustainability credentials.

However, the print dramatically highlighted the earnings volatility embedded in UIE's holding company structure, where 43.76% of major investments are tied to fair-value portfolios that remain hostage to public equity market swings and currency depreciation beyond operational control. The 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory will be determined less by plantation operational performance and more by whether Nordic industrial multiples stabilize, Swedish krona currency recovers, and whether El Niño weather disruptions materialize into volume declines that offset any CPO pricing strength.

Section 16 – Conclusion 1 finding

The H1 2026 earnings fundamentally reframed UIE's investment thesis by exposing the structural volatility embedded within its diversified holding company model, where a USD 52.3 million contraction in operating income to just USD 10.1 million was driven almost entirely by non-cash fair value adjustments rather than operational deterioration. While management emphasized United Plantations' achievement of all-time high production volumes of crude palm oil and palm kernel as proof of competitive execution, the 13% decline in Schörling's fair value to USD 215.4 million, compounded by Hexagon's 27% share price drop following the Octave Intelligence spin-off and a 6% Swedish Krona depreciation, demonstrated that parent earnings remain hostage to public equity market swings over which UIE has zero operational control.

The print definitively confirmed that UIE's consolidated net profit reflects two distinct asset pillars operating on entirely different risk-return trajectories: a stable, cash-generative agricultural anchor delivering record biological yields versus mark-to- market equity portfolios subject to quarterly valuation compression.

What changed most materially was the market's recognition that even elite upstream plantation performance cannot insulate UIE from severe earnings volatility when lower realized CPO selling prices combine with temporary downstream refining hedging losses. United Plantations' wholly-owned Unitata refinery reported an 84% operating profit collapse to MYR 7.6 million due to realized losses from buying back previously sold crude palm oil futures contracts in a rising market, though management expects full recovery in subsequent quarters as contracted finished products are delivered at locked-in margins.

The board's formal guidance that net profit will be lower for the full year 2026 compared to 2025, framed as dependent on volatile public equities rather than structural business deterioration, forces investors to accept that UIE's parent earnings will remain unpredictable over an 18-month horizon regardless of agricultural operational excellence. This earnings print eliminated any remaining illusion that UIE's business reporting presentation can fully shield investors from Swedish equity market cycles and Nordic currency depreciation.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to a bearish UIE view centers on the thesis that structural global palm oil supply constraints, with growth slowing to 1.3 million tonnes per year compared to the historical average of 2 million tonnes annually due to limited land availability and moratoriums on new plantings, will sustain elevated crude palm oil prices and drive a sharp earnings recovery as Schörling and Greenbridge fair values rebound alongside broader equity market stabilization.
