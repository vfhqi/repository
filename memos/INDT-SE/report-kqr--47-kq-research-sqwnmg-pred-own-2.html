EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.
Acquisition valuation structures: Indutrade AB (INDT-SE), a Swedish decentralised industrial group that acquires and owns more than 220 technical-trading and niche-manufacturing companies in more than 30 countries, completed ten major acquisitions from January 2025 to October 2026, including Valveco, Createc, ATM, Sinmed, Axotan, PHACOTEC, Albiox, SLT, Controlin and the pending Euromed in Poland, raising the central question of whether acquisitions historically accounted for more than half of Indutrade's earnings growth can be sustained at disciplined pricing levels. Transaction discipline remains essential because rising deal competition threatens the group's ability to maintain its capital reinvestment flywheel without eroding returns on capital employed.
2.
Implied acquisition multiples: Indutrade paid an aggregate preliminary purchase price of SEK 2,508 million for its nine-month 2026 acquisitions, which includes SEK 387 million of contingent considerations and corresponds to acquired annual sales of SEK 2,060 million, yielding an implied EV/sales multiple of 1.22x (calculated), matching the long-term historical median of 1.2x and supporting stable earnings per share accretion. This disciplined valuation level suggests that increasing deal size has not led to multiple expansion during the 2025-2026 period.
3.
EBITA multiple discipline: Redeye's report on 24 July 2026 states that Indutrade paid a median initial consideration of 7x EBITA between 2020 and 2024, but adding the maximum contingent considerations increases the total median EBITA multiple to 9x, with deal design limiting upfront financial exposure while rewarding targets that exceed profit expectations. The group's official target range of 5 to 8x EV/EBITA remains broadly intact, though the upper bound of 9x including earn-outs reflects heightened seller expectations in competitive segments.
4.
Target margin accretion: Indutrade's management disclosed during the Q4 2025 earnings call that acquisitions achieved a rolling twelve-month EBITA margin of 16.4%, which significantly exceeds Indutrade's long-term group target of 14% over a business cycle, providing immediate margin accretion upon consolidation and supporting accelerated growth in earnings per share. These elevated profitability levels demonstrate that the group successfully targets high-margin niche operators rather than commoditized distributors.
5.
Peer valuation benchmarks: DNB Carnegie's report on 24 September 2026 calculates that proven compounders achieved a median multiple of 6.8x EV/EBITA for acquisitions, which aligns closely with Indutrade's historical initial multiples but sits below the 7.4x average paid by other serial acquirers, demonstrating that Indutrade's disciplined capital reinvestment flywheel remains highly competitive and protecting its long-term ROCE from multiple inflation. This peer comparison confirms that Indutrade has not materially overpaid relative to its Nordic competitors.
6.
Earn-out protection mechanisms: Indutrade's interim report on 15 July 2025 notes that contingent consideration payments are typically due within three years, with payouts initially measured at the present value of the likely performance outcome, ensuring that underperforming targets do not receive excess cash. This contractual structure effectively transfers downside risk to sellers while preserving upside participation, thereby safeguarding the group's return on capital even in highly competitive seller markets.
7.
Market competition stability: Lagercrantz's chief financial officer Peter Thysell noted during the Q2 2026 earnings call on 24 October 2025 that M&A multiples have historically been highly stable with no material competitive impact, meaning that serial compounders can continue to deploy capital without facing margin dilution, which ultimately supports stable earnings per share growth. Despite widespread concerns about private equity bidding wars, mid-market pricing for technical-trading targets in the SEK 30 million to 350 million range has remained remarkably consistent.
8.
Disciplined capital deployment evidence: DNB Carnegie's report on 24 September 2026 highlights that Salix Group paid a weighted-average EV/EBITA multiple of 6.7x for acquisitions completed from 2023 to the first half of 2026, which compares highly favorably with the 7.4x average paid by the broader peer group, allowing serial acquirers to sustain high returns on capital. This empirical peer data reinforces the conclusion that disciplined buyers have successfully avoided multiple inflation.
9.
Size-driven market insulation: Friedrich Vorwerk's representative Tim Hameister noted during a conference on 2 December 2025 that larger targets attract intense competition from private equity firms that pay elevated multiples, but smaller succession-led transactions face far less bidding friction, meaning that industrial groups can successfully secure companies through direct owner relationships. This strategic size positioning explains why Indutrade's focus on smaller acquisitions protects it from the most aggressive financial buyers.
10.
Geographic expansion dynamics: Redeye's report on 23 July 2026 notes that Lagercrantz has successfully expanded its transaction footprint in the United Kingdom and is actively targeting new acquisitions in the Netherlands and Germany, with this geographic pivot helping the group maintain its M&A momentum, although scaling organizational M&A capacity remains a key long-term operational hurdle. Continental European expansion provides a critical pipeline buffer as Nordic markets consolidate, though execution risks increase with geographic distance.
11.
Contrary integration evidence: DNB Carnegie's report on 30 January 2026 notes that Indutrade recorded SEK 125 million of negative one-off items related to customer projects in two UK subsidiaries, with these project deviations suggesting that pre-acquisition due diligence failed to identify deep-seated structural issues, highlighting that earn-outs cannot fully insulate Indutrade from operational losses. This material write-down demonstrates that even experienced acquirers face execution risks that can temporarily offset the benefits of disciplined transaction pricing.
12.
Information gap limitations: While Indutrade reports aggregated acquisition spending, company filings do not disclose the individual purchase prices or EBITA margins for targets like Valveco, Createc, or Controlin, making it highly challenging for investors to verify if the group is strictly adhering to its stated 5 to 8x EBITA range. This disclosure deficit represents the primary limitation of the analysis, requiring investors to rely on aggregated metrics and peer benchmarks rather than transaction-specific verification.
13.
Capital flywheel risk scenario: Indutrade's investor presentation on 4 November 2025 shows that the group deployed 71% of its free operating cash flow into M&A over the past five years, while Redeye's report on 24 July 2026 warns that paying higher entry multiples decreases the financial return on M&A, meaning that the group must either accept lower returns on capital employed or allocate cash to dividends instead of M&A reinvestment. If competition forces sustained multiple expansion beyond 9x EBITA, Indutrade's historical compounding mechanism would face significant structural degradation.
