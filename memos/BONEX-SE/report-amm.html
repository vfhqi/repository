APM Mini-Memo: Bonesupport

The one-line view. Bonesupport is a one-product Swedish medtech whose antibiotic-releasing bone graft is becoming the standard of care for bone infection in the United States, and the case depends on American surgeons and payers adopting the product fast enough to keep estimates rising.

What the company does

Bonesupport is a Swedish orthobiologics company with a market value of about 1.3 billion euros at the share price of 320.6 on 6 November 2026. Its product is an injectable bone graft substitute that fills a bone void and releases an antibiotic as new bone replaces it, so the surgeon treats the infection and rebuilds the bone in one operation. My own reading is that the product is sold as CERAMENT and that bone infection, trauma and infected joint replacements are its main uses. The United States is the growth market: American penetration is below 10% and United States sales grew 31% in the second quarter of 2026.

A plausible investment thesis

Demand-driven. Two events in the autumn of 2026 turned a product surgeons liked into one hospitals are paid to use. The SOLARIO trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine on 17 September 2026, showed that the product cuts the course of antibiotics after bone infection surgery from weeks to days. From 1 October 2026 Medicare moved those cases into its highest-paying groups, so an American hospital now earns more on the operation that uses the product.

The route to earnings per share is short. With a gross margin of 90.8%, almost every extra dollar of United States sales reaches profit, and consensus already expects earnings to compound at about 40% a year. The Sector digest's rule is that revenue lags a payment decision by roughly a year, so the Medicare change should feed United States growth through 2027, and the market's story is that adoption makes the product a standard of care.

The drivers to track are three: the growth rate of United States sales each quarter, the direction of consensus estimates, and whether the gross margin holds as volume rises. The Assistant Portfolio Manager's note of 28 October found that in this Nordic medtech cohort the shares follow the direction of estimates more than the reported quarter, and Bonesupport rose 24.3% on 22 October on a beat with rising estimates.

The Target Setup

No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and this read is my own. The Target Setup is 'Demand-driven EPSU': a clinical result and a payment change are lifting demand for an existing product, and the earnings step-up comes from volume at a very high gross margin rather than from a cleared worry or a corporate change. A memo would decide whether the United States adoption curve is steep enough to make that fit crisp.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple but fragile. The case has one product, one market that matters and one question: how fast American surgeons and payers adopt the product. What needs to go right is that United States sales accelerate through 2027 as the Medicare change works through, that estimates keep rising, and that the gross margin holds.

The main risk is the multiple. The Investment Analyst's note of 31 October found that small, one-product medtech stocks have about four times as many days with a 10% move as large medtech, and that their multiple breaks on the first doubt about the growth path, whether a miss or a beat with a weaker outlook. At 33.5 times earnings, one slower American quarter would cost the shares a large part of the recent rise, and relative strength over eighteen months of only 37 shows how recently the shares were out of favour. The weekly Lists judgement also recorded that a small single-product medtech name is the shape the book already has too much of, with Xvivo held and our market-regime reading arguing for less fragility, so position size, not the case, is the second question.

Business quality

High margin, narrow base. A gross margin of 90.8% is the mark of a consumable with clinical evidence behind it and pricing power, which the Industry digest notes is rare in regulated healthcare. The published trial and the Medicare payment groups are a lead that competitors cannot copy quickly, and my own reading is that no rival antibiotic-releasing injectable graft is approved in the United States. Against that, the company depends on one product and on United States payers, and operating margin, cash and revenue are not in our files.

Plausible returns and valuation

Cheap for its growth, not cheap in its sector. At 33.5 times earnings on 6 November, Bonesupport sits at the 19th percentile of its own ten-year range and has the cheapest valuation reading on the Bench; the medtech consumables Sector median is 17.1 times, but at the second percentile of its own range. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 6 November is A, and the plausible return reading of 29 October is 79% a year, which assumes the multiple doubles to 67 times; I would not lean on that. Consensus earnings growth of about 40% a year on an unchanged multiple would give about 40% a year, an A against your bar, and my own arithmetic says that even a fall in the multiple to 25 times over three years would leave about 27% a year, still an A. Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. The Sector digest's story of a small maker's consumable becoming the standard of care is rated early, and Bonesupport and Xvivo are its purest plays. Who pays decides who can raise prices, and Medicare has just decided to pay more. Leadership in the Nordic medtech cohort rotated on 22 October to the estimate risers, and Bonesupport is one of them.

Cons. The company waits on American payers and hospital purchasing committees, and the revenue lag of about a year means the next two quarters may show more promise than numbers. The Sector is cheap as a whole, so a buyer of medtech has many cheaper worry-clearing franchises to choose from. Small growers' multiples halved after the 2020 to 2021 boom, and some of that was normal maturing rather than the group's wave.
