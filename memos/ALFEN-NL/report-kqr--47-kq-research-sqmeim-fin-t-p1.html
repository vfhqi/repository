Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. High operating leverage makes Alfen highly sensitive to volume swings, meaning small changes in gross profit can cause massive fluctuations in earnings per share.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Central investment question: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Significant operating risk: High fixed costs create material operating leverage that leaves Alfen highly sensitive to revenue fluctuations, as seen during past volume downturns. Deutsche Bank's report on 19 May 2025 noted that Alfen requires substantial top-line growth to leverage its fixed cost base effectively, as slow adoption delays profitability. Consequently, any expansion in gross profit margin will flow through directly to lift the company's operating profit once fixed capacity costs are covered.
3.
Sensitized earnings dynamics: Alfen's historical margins show how rapidly profitability swings when sales volumes drop against a rigid, pre-built operational footprint. Jefferies pointed out on 21 August 2026 that Alfen's adjusted EBITDA margin is expected to fall to a cyclical low of 4.0% in FY2026, compared with former peak margins of 18.0% achieved in FY2022. This compression illustrates how a lack of volume support can trigger severe operational deleverage and crush net income.
4.
Shifting product mix: The structural shift in Alfen's revenue mix toward lower-margin business lines significantly complicates how operating leverage translates into net earnings. Alfen's H1 2026 results revealed that while revenue grew 23.6% to €261.5 million, the group gross margin declined to 26.1% because of a higher share of Energy Storage Systems sales. 17 mix effect means that even if operating expenses are controlled, the actual flow-through to EBITDA is muted unless high-margin EV charging revenues recover.

Speed of grid buildouts

5.
Severe grid congestion: Bottlenecks in the electrical transmission and distribution networks represent a critical barrier to the deployment of Alfen's Smart Grid Solutions. Bernstein's report on 2 October 2026 highlighted that the Netherlands faces material onshore grid congestion, prompting TenneT to propose €50 billion of onshore investments between 2026 and 2035 to reinforce grid capacity. These long-term infrastructure upgrades will ultimately dictate the delivery schedules and overall demand for Alfen's transformer substations.
6.
Grid hardware shortages: Worsening equipment shortages and lengthy lead times for high-voltage transformers present a direct threat to the execution speed of European grid operators. A global data center operator's procurement head noted on 19 October 2026 that transformer lead times have worsened by six to eight months, stretching some delivery times up to five to seven years. 20 22 supply-side delays limit how quickly grid operators can execute substations and connect new projects, directly constraining Alfen's near-term revenue potential.

EV adoption and charging networks

7.
Surging electric vehicle demand: European electric vehicle registrations have experienced a powerful acceleration, which represents the primary driver for Alfen's EV Charging business. Jefferies reported on 15 October 2026 that European battery electric vehicle registrations surged by 59% year-on-year in September 2026, pushing BEV penetration to a record high of 32.6%. While this macro trend is highly favorable, its direct financial benefits to Alfen depend entirely on resolving competitive pressures within the charging hardware segment.
8.
Slowing public deployment: The build rate of Europe's public charging network has reached a historical low due to worsening grid congestion and permitting delays. Deutsche Bank's report on 23 October 2026 revealed that Europe's public charging network added only 6,000 public chargers in Q3 2026, dropping significantly below the five-year quarterly average of 45,000 chargers. If these grid connection delays persist, they will severely limit the growth of public charging networks and drag on Alfen's distributor sales.
9.
Other side of the story: While operating leverage is a major threat during downturns, it can trigger an explosive recovery in Alfen's earnings per share once market volumes rebound.
10.
Jefferies argued on 21 August 2026 that Alfen's profitability is projected to reach a 10.0% EBITDA margin by FY2030, driven by double-digit growth across its core markets. Consequently, if European grid and EV charging investments accelerate faster than expected, Alfen's pre-built capacity will allow incremental revenue to drop straight to the bottom line.
