The operating drivers behind the inputs

  • Revenue growth. FY2026 minus 1.5 percent: Automotive minus 10.4 percent (H1 2026 minus 10.8 percent on deliveries minus 8.3 percent, share 21.5 percent, a Belgian market heading to the company's 400,000-unit plateau, adverse price and mix) against PHE +13.5 percent (organic about 5.5 percent plus seven months of Polaris and Regueira, about EUR 340m of annual sales, consolidated from 29 May 2026). FY2027 +2.1 percent: Automotive minus 2.9 percent (end of 100 percent deductibility for corporate electric vehicles, mobility budgets), PHE +8.8 percent (full year of Spain, organic 4.5 percent). FY2028 to FY2030 +2.4 to 2.9 percent: Automotive +1 to 1.5 percent on a stable market and share near 21 percent; PHE +4 to 4.6 percent (organic about 4 percent and small bolt-ons); parts prices +1.5 to 2 percent a year, passed through.
  • EBITDA margin. Built as the consolidated adjusted operating result plus depreciation excluding acquired-intangible amortisation. The FY2026 dip (7.80 percent) is Automotive's margin at about 2.1 percent (H1 2026 2.1 percent against 4.5 percent; a 24 percent decremental margin); the rise to 8.45 percent (FY2027) and 8.82 percent (FY2028) is the Automotive restructuring (344 roles, site closures, marketing and consulting cuts; about EUR 30m to 35m gross a year, about half in FY2027 after Belgian consultation, the run-rate in FY2028) and PHE's margin rising from 9.2 to 9.6 percent with Spanish procurement synergies. I do not reach the company's Automotive ambition of about 4 percent by FY2028 (pre-pandemic about 3 percent; price and mix still negative).
  • Depreciation and amortisation. About EUR 205m in FY2025 excluding acquired-intangible amortisation (FactSet's total EUR 231m less PHE's EUR 25.9m), plus Spain from FY2026 and a slowly rising asset base.
  • Net financial items. See the bridge below. The large positive figure is the share of Belron's and TVH's adjusted profit before tax. How to read these figures
