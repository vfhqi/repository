MECHANISMS AND SECTOR DYNAMICS

5.

Section 2 – Regulatory architecture: Net-Zero Industry Act and Buy America rules

Section summary
  1. Robust protectionist frameworks are forming a structural wall around the European and US grid markets, effectively isolating local manufacturers from low-cost Chinese and Korean competition
  2. These rules deploy stringent non-price criteria and tiered tariff structures that systematically disqualify foreign bids while enabling Western players to command substantial pricing premiums

EUROPEAN UNION NZIA AND NON-PRICE CRITERIA

1.
Tightening regulation: The Net-Zero Industry Act (NZIA) established a 40% benchmark by 2030 for domestic clean technology manufacturing capacity to secure European energy supply chains.
2.
Stringent criteria: Public procurement rules under the NZIA require authorities to apply non-price resilience and sustainability criteria carrying a 15 to 30 percent weight in clean energy tenders.
3.
Exclusionary thresholds: Supply diversification rules mandate filtering out bids if a single third-country source supplies more than 65 percent of the EU's demand for that net-zero technology.
4.
Gradual implementation: Although regional resilience rules legally took effect in late 2025, state-level implementation is occurring more gradually as member states integrate regional procurement guidelines.
5.
Incremental adjustment: Germany's EEG27 reform and the proposed Grid Package are key state-level instruments undergoing active political negotiations to reshape developer grid responsibilities.
6.
Shifting supply: Turbine and equipment manufacturers are shifting their component sourcing toward India and Europe to satisfy the European Union's rising local content standards.

US BUY AMERICA AND SECTION 232 TARIFFS

7.
Robust defense: The Build America, Buy America Act (BABA) under federal infrastructure law establishes strict domestic content preferences for iron, steel, and manufactured equipment.
8.
Segmented tariffs: The updated Section 232 framework assessed since April 2026 places a temporary 15 percent tariff through 2027 on electrical grid equipment.
9.
Strict enforcement: Under the revised Section 232 metal tariffs, derivative articles containing steel, aluminum, or copper face a 25 percent tariff on full value.
10.
Punitive sanctions: White House proclamations on copper imports impose punitive 50 percent tariffs on foreign-made cables, connectors, and key electrical components.
11.
Severe exclusion: The August 2026 Executive Order 14420 targets foreign bulk-power system equipment operating at 69kV and above on national security grounds.
12.
Extensive scope: Executive Order 14420 broadens import restrictions to explicitly cover grid-connected batteries, inverters, power conversion systems, and security-critical remote-access software.
13.
Declining share: Tighter US cybersecurity requirements for battery management systems are driving a rapid shift to non-Chinese suppliers across utility-scale energy storage projects.
14.
Other side of the story: The protectionist wall contains explicit economic relief valves, as NZIA resilience rules shall not apply if their application forces utilities to absorb a price premium exceeding 10 percent.
