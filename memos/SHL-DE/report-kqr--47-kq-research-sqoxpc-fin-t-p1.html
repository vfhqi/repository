Section 3 – Historical Margin Volatility and Cyclical Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. Historical margin cycles demonstrate that Siemens Healthineers exhibits significant profitability volatility driven by regional headwinds and product mix.
  2. Structural challenges in China and sudden transition drags can severely depress segment operating margins despite underlying volume growth.

Historical Cyclical Performance

1.
Defensive growth: Siemens AG managed to expand its Healthcare sector revenue by 4% to 3.8 billion EUR during the global economic recession in fiscal year 2009. The company reported that its Healthcare business activities were less exposed to short-term economic cycles. This resilience suggests that medical technology maintains a stable baseline of demand during broader market downturns, protecting group earnings per share.
2.
Cyclical contraction: Siemens Healthineers experienced a 10% decline in group adjusted EBIT to 2,230 million EUR in fiscal year 2020. The company reported a group adjusted EBIT margin of 15.4% as the COVID-19 pandemic disrupted routine clinical procedures. This temporary volume disruption demonstrates how fixed-cost operating leverage can pressure margins when clinical access is restricted.
3.
Pandemic tailwind: Siemens Healthineers achieved a record adjusted EBIT margin of 19.1% in the first quarter of fiscal year 2021. The company reported that excellent volume conversion in Imaging and Diagnostics drove a 37% increase in adjusted earnings per share. This surge was propelled by profitable rapid antigen test sales that temporarily masked underlying core cost inflation.
4.
Structural reset: Siemens Healthineers saw group adjusted EBIT fall 16% to 3,081 million EUR in fiscal year 2023, yielding a 14.2% margin. The company reported that the cliff-like decline in rapid antigen test revenues and elevated transformation costs dragged Diagnostics margins to negative 2.6%. This down-cycle reveals the severity of negative operational leverage when high-margin revenue streams suddenly evaporate.
5.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research estimated in September 2026 that core margins would show resilient sequential improvements ahead of full-year results. However, the company's final reported adjusted EBIT margin of 16.2% for fiscal year 2026 ended up slightly below the prior-year level of 16.5%. This shortfall indicates that accelerating supply chain inflation and currency headwinds can override positive volume trends.

Diagnostics Profit Collapse

6.
Profit plunge: Siemens Healthineers reported that Diagnostics adjusted EBIT collapsed by 76% in the second quarter of fiscal year 2026. The company noted that comparable segment sales declined 6.5% during this period due to structural market changes in China. This disproportionate drop highlights how a highly rigid fixed-cost base amplified the profit collapse.
7.
Regional erosion: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in May 2026 that the Diagnostics adjusted EBIT margin collapsed from 6.3% to just 0.9% in the second quarter. The broker explained that steep volume declines in China created severe negative operating leverage. High tariffs further compressed profitability, showing that unfavorable regional sales mix can decimate segment earnings.
8.
Persistent drag: Bernstein Research reported in November 2026 that Diagnostics profitability remained under pressure with an adjusted EBIT margin of 5.4% in the fourth quarter. The broker estimated that ongoing cost reductions and tariff refunds were insufficient to offset lower volume earnings. This sluggish recovery suggests that depressed clinical testing volumes in China will continue to limit near-term earnings per share expansion.

These external pressures offset strong underlying volume growth in the radiotherapy business.
