Section 2 – Valuation and separation timetables

Section summary
  1. Corporate carve-outs and strategic divestments across European component makers legally isolate lower-margin segments, accelerating transition to premium industrial profiles before execution benefits are fully realized.

Separation timetables and structural mechanisms

1.
SKF (Vertevo) timetable: SKF approved the Lex Asea distribution of its automotive business under Vertevo at its October 1, 2026 Extraordinary General Meeting, setting the record date for November 27, 2026 and the first day of trading for December 1, 2026.
2.
SKF capitalization structure: In preparation for the spin-off, SKF plans to make a capital contribution of approximately SEK 5 billion to capitalise Vertevo at zero net debt before listing.
3.
Senior Aerostructures execution: Senior completed the sale of its structurally dilutive Aerostructures division to Sullivan Street Partners on December 31, 2025, receiving upfront net cash proceeds of £95.7 million to reduce net debt and fund buyback.
4.
Coats US Yarns exit: Coats completed the exit of its non-core US Yarns business at Kings Mountain in June 2025 for net cash proceeds of $16 million, representing an valuation of five point three times EV/EBITA.
5.
Trelleborg Wheel Systems divestment: Trelleborg divested its Wheel Systems business to Yokohama Rubber on May 2, 2023, for an enterprise value of €2.1 billion, or SEK 24.425 billion, successfully improving its structural profitability and reducing cyclicality.
6.
Bekaert Latin America portfolio shift: Bekaert finalized the divestment of its Steel Wire Solutions entities in Costa Rica, Ecuador, and Venezuela to Grupo AG on June 30, 2025, for a consolidated enterprise value of $73 million to reduce exposure to commoditized markets.
7.
SFS production streamlining: SFS Group is executing a global streamlining program scheduled to complete by the end of 2027, closing selected sites and cutting CHF 110 million of unprofitable sales to concentrate on high-differentiation applications.

Stranded costs, margin targets and multiple expansion

8.
SKF dis-synergies and costs: Jefferies estimates that SKF will incur SEK 1.0 billion in annualized dis- synergies starting in Q1 2026, which will be largely offset by rightsizing savings of SEK 750 million run-rate by the end of 2025.
9.
Senior margin targets: Deutsche Bank reports that Senior targets double-digit group operating margins and "at least mid-teens" Aerospace margins, leaving the remaining business more predictable and less capital intensive.
10.
Coats margin progression: Coats Group's exit from US Yarns delivered a 100 basis point margin improvement to the group, supporting its accelerated progress toward its medium-term adjusted margin target of nineteen to twenty-one percent.
11.
Trelleborg margin targets and re-rating: Deutsche Bank Research notes that Trelleborg's active divestments expanded its EBIT margin from 14% to a proforma 17%, prompting a multiple re-rating from ten times to twelve times plus EV/EBIT.
12.
Daetwyler ForwardNow progression: Daetwyler consolidated its US footprint by closing its Vandalia, Ohio facility in September 2025, with its ForwardNow program successfully improving the group EBIT margin by two hundred and thirty basis points since inception.
13.
Bekaert transaction one-offs: Bekaert incurred a €40 million one-off impact on the sale of its Latin American SWS businesses, primarily due to non-cash translation adjustments from historical currency devaluations in Venezuela.
14.
AQ Group EBT upgrade: AQ Group's Board of Directors upgraded its pre-tax profit (EBT) margin target from 8% to 10% on July 14, 2026, driven by a strategic shift toward highly complex products.

Section summary

1.
High-value customization: Jefferies highlights that customized engineering solutions with high reliability at a global scale allow SFS to secure premium contracts with significantly reduced customer pricing pressure across its primary divisions.
2.
Rigid pricing clauses: DNB Carnegie notes that AQ Group employs price clauses in its customer agreements but typically requires three to six months to pass raw material cost increases to downstream buyers.
3.
Multi-year price indexing: Jefferies reports that Senior plc has finalized eighty percent of its pricing negotiations to support margin expansion in fiscal twenty-six and twenty-seven.
4.
Direct pass-through structures: Bekaert management indicates that raw material pass-through mechanisms help protect absolute profitability but can create dilutive operating margin impacts during inflationary cycles.
5.

6.
Low cost-share inelasticity: Daetwyler management indicates that its system-critical elastomer components provide high customer value through functionality while representing a minimal share of total product cost.
