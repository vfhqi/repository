Section 2 – Amortisation of Acquired Intangibles

Section summary
  1. Indutrade's acquired intangibles amortisation expands automatically with deal pace and remains a primary driver of the gap between EBITA and adjusted EBIT.
  2. Standardizing peer adjustments is critical because brokers use inconsistent definitions of adjusted EPS when adding back acquisition-related noncash expenses.

Indutrade's amortisation dynamics

1.
Reconciliation gap: The discrepancy between Indutrade's operating profit and adjusted metrics is largely driven by acquisition-related amortisation charges. Indutrade's annual report published on 9 March 2026 shows that out of SEK 706 million in total amortisation of intangible assets, SEK 656 million was directly attributable to acquisitions. This non-cash expense bridges the company's reported 2025 EBITA of SEK 4,450 million down to its operating profit after amortisation. Consequently, forecasting these acquisition-related charges is vital to accurately bridge EBITA to reported earnings.
2.
Amortisation lifespan: Indutrade applies a straight-line amortisation method to allocate the cost of acquired intangible assets over their useful economic lives. According to its annual report published on 9 March 2026, the useful lives of customer relationships, agencies, and trademarks typically range between 5 and 20 years. As transaction volumes pick up, these identified intangibles grow, causing the straight-line charge to escalate alongside the acquisition pace. Thus, rapid deal execution automatically compounds future non-cash expenses and lowers reported operating margins.
3.
Contrary evidence: The impact of amortisation on individual transactions is typically deemed immaterial by management due to the small size of target companies. Indutrade's annual report published on 9 March 2026 states that because individual acquisitions are small, it is not possible to itemise the intangible assets separately. Instead, supplementary disclosures are omitted for these minor items, indicating that amortisation rarely creates significant single-deal volatility. However, the aggregate sum of these minor items still creates a substantial gap at the group level.
4.
Broker adjustments: Sell-side analysts adjust their earnings models to isolate operational performance from non-cash accounting items. DNB Carnegie's report on 5 October 2026 explains that they adjust the book value of capital employed by adding back accumulated amortisation and impairment of intangible assets. This adjustment allows the broker to calculate a clean return on capital employed (ROCE) and maintain valuation comparability across peers. Consequently, many brokers add back the SEK 656 million acquisition amortisation when modeling their adjusted EPS.

Indutrade earn-out remeasurements

1.
Volatile remeasurement trends: Indutrade experiences significant fluctuations in its other operating income due to the ongoing revaluation of contingent considerations. In the second quarter of 2026, the company recorded a negative operating remeasurement of SEK 14 million. This negative impact contrasts sharply with the positive SEK 59 million recorded in the prior year's second quarter. Consequently, these fluctuations create unpredictable adjustments in reported operating profit from quarter to quarter.
2.
Annual revaluation outcomes: The cumulative impact of annual earn-out remeasurements can be substantial for the group's reported financials. For the full year of 2025, Indutrade recorded total contingent consideration remeasurements of SEK 223 million. Out of this total, SEK 208 million was recognized in other operating income and expenses, while SEK 15 million was booked in net financial items. Thus, these accounting revaluations materially alter the statutory EBITA margin unless analysts adjust their core estimates.
3.
Management judgment reliance: The initial estimation and subsequent remeasurement of earn-outs represent a key source of balance sheet uncertainty. Indutrade typically structures its deals with a base consideration and a contingent earn-out based on future earnings. Because these payments depend on subjective future projections, they require a high degree of management judgment at each reporting date. This subjective framework means that final cash outflows can diverge significantly from the initial carrying values.
4.
Contrary evidence: Remeasurement volatility remained largely neutral on a net basis during certain historical quarters because different adjustments offset each other.
