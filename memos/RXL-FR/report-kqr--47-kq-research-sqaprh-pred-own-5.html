Section 2 – Financial Covenants and Leverage Definitions

Section summary
  1. Rexel maintains significant covenant headroom with its leverage ratio of 2.39x sitting well below the 3.50x limit.
  2. Covenant calculations exclude IFRS 16 lease liabilities but include most of Rexel's active securitisation programs.

Senior Credit Agreement terms

1.
Revolving facility details: Rexel entered into a revolving credit facility in January 2024 that was later increased to €900 million in December 2025. This facility, which expires on January 22, 2031, is managed by a syndicate of major European banks including BNP Paribas and Societe Generale. The agreement provides critical liquidity support and carries interest based on Euribor plus a margin determined by credit ratings. This facility represents the cornerstone of Rexel's debt structure and supports operating flexibility.
2.
Interest margin stepups: The margin under Rexel's Senior Credit Agreement varies dynamically between 0.30% and 1.40% depending on ratings. Standard & Poor's and Moody's ratings are used to determine the exact pricing grid quarterly. If Rexel's leverage rises significantly after the GCG transaction, these interest costs could increase margin expenses. This margin structure links Rexel's operational performance directly to its overall weighted average cost of capital.
3.
Leverage covenant level: The revolving credit facility requires Rexel to maintain its leverage ratio below 3.50x tested semi-annually. As of June 30, 2026, the company reported a leverage ratio of 2.39x under these terms. This provides Rexel with substantial headroom against its covenant limits before the GCG transaction closes. It remains highly unlikely that Rexel will breach this threshold in the near term.
4.
Test date schedule: Covenant compliance is monitored on specific test dates occurring on June 30 and December 31 each year. Rexel is required to deliver certified financial compliance certificates to lenders within specified reporting periods. These biannual tests ensure that Rexel retains continuous discipline in its capital allocation decisions. Failure to meet these ratios on test dates would constitute an event of default unless waived.
5.
Covenant flexibility clauses: The Senior Credit Agreement permits the leverage ratio to exceed 3.50x on up to three accounting dates. This flexibility is restricted such that only two of these occurrences can be consecutive. The covenant ratio can rise up to 3.75x on two dates and up to 3.90x on a single date. This contractual mechanism ensures temporary relief during major acquisition periods like GCG.

Contrary evidence: The capital increase of €500 million was required because the GCG deal would otherwise strain covenant limits. Without this equity component, the €800 million in new debt would push leverage close to the restrictive levels. This indicates that Rexel's headroom is dependent on dilutive equity issuance during large transactions.

Treatment of leases and securitisations

7.
Lease accounting exclusion: Covenant calculations are determined on a pre-IFRS 16 basis which means lease liabilities are excluded from adjusted net debt. Adjusted EBITDA is defined under the agreement as consolidated operating income before depreciation and other non-recurring items. Operating lease payments are deducted from operating income to arrive at Adjusted EBITDA. This mechanical calculation isolates Rexel's leverage from the inflation of lease liabilities under modern accounting standards.
8.
EBITDA adjustments: Adjusted EBITDA under the revolving credit facility includes the last 12 months of EBITDA for acquired subsidiaries. It also excludes the non-recurring impact of copper price fluctuations on inventory values. Restructuring and acquisition costs are added back to reflect normalized earnings potential of the group. These adjustments prevent temporary integration costs from artificially inflating leverage metrics.

Securitisation program accounting: Rexel utilizes several securitisation programs that totaled €1,581.5 million in authorized limits as of June 30, 2026. Most of these programs are maintained on-balance sheet, representing €1,354.5 million in drawn funding. On-balance sheet securitisation is treated as financial debt in Adjusted EBITDA calculations. Consequently, these programs directly increase the reported covenant leverage ratio for the group.
