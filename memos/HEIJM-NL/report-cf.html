EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Koninklijke Heijmans NV has executed a fundamental transformation from a cyclical construction contractor into a high-margin service partner, anchored by orderly leadership transitions, a disciplined margin-over-volume philosophy, and strategic pivot toward recurring technical and infrastructure services. The company delivered robust top-line growth of 16% in H1 2026 while expanding underlying EBITDA margin to 9.0% from 8.5%, positioning itself in the top quartile of European construction peers.

This elite profitability profile, with a 9.10% FY25 EBITDA margin exceeding BAM Nederland's Dutch division at 7.20%, reflects rigorous bid selectivity and successful migration away from volatile project-based contracting.

Management has secured substantial growth visibility through strategic acquisitions, with the EUR 296.8 million Van Wanrooij transaction doubling the residential landbank to 29,000 homes and the late-2025 Hegeman acquisition adding EUR 140 million in specialized technical services revenue. Critically, recurring revenue from multi-year asset management and maintenance contracts now represents 35% of total group revenue, with the Working segment achieving 70% recurring business mix, fundamentally de-risking earnings volatility inherent in traditional construction cycles. This strategic repositioning aligns perfectly with secular demand tailwinds from

TenneT's EUR 200 billion grid expansion campaign, public infrastructure investments of EUR 6-7 billion annually, and Dutch defense expenditure rising to 2.7% of GDP by 2030. earnings delivery demonstrates exceptional execution discipline, with H1 2026 revenue reaching EUR 1,517 million (up 15.8% year-on-year), underlying EBITDA of EUR 136 million matching consensus, net profit expanding 24% to EUR 73 million, and the order book climbing 36% to EUR 4.1 billion.

Management consistently demonstrates conservative guidance practices, having raised FY24 EBITDA margin outlook during interim results and historically providing beatable figures that enable subsequent upward revisions, establishing a proven sandbagger archetype. Current FY26 guidance targets revenue of approximately EUR 3,100 million with underlying EBIT margin toward 7.3%, fully secured by a high-quality EUR 4,100 million backlog, providing exceptional forward visibility.

However, structural execution risks persist from extreme high-voltage technician shortages that hinder energy transition project deployment, though these labor deficits paradoxically bolster contractor pricing power when negotiating complex public infrastructure tenders. The broader Dutch housing market faces cyclical headwinds as rising mortgage interest rates cool transactions, though chronic structural supply deficits maintain long-term pricing support if new-build volumes remain constrained by local planning boards. National infrastructure budget shortfalls cause public tender delays while residential mortgage rates recently exceeded 4%, depressing marginal consumer borrowing power and creating near-term volume uncertainty.

Peer-relative analysis confirms Heijmans occupies top-quartile profitability within the wider European construction sector, with H1 2026 revenue growth of 15.8% vastly outpacing the 4.3% peer average and organic sales rising 7.0% demonstrating resilient core market demand dynamics. The sell-side consensus is heavily skewed toward positive ratings with no active sell recommendations, projecting FY26F revenue of EUR 3.11 billion matching management targets, with bulls emphasizing TenneT's massive EUR 200 billion grid expansion and Dutch military budget expansions to 2.7% of GDP by 2030 as primary secular drivers.

Earnings sensitivity analysis reveals group EPS is extremely sensitive to EBIT margin changes where a 50 basis point shift impacts EPS by 7.07%, while foreign exchange exposure remains completely absent due to entirely domestic Euro-denominated operations.

The balance sheet demonstrates exceptional cash conversion efficiency, delivering EUR 85 million operating cash flow in fiscal 2025 and net cash reaching EUR 51 million in H1 2026 despite typical seasonal outflows, supported by contracting models generating substantial prepayments that provide negative working capital to fund landbank development. However, the ambitious transition to emission-free machinery requires EUR 30-40 million annual capital expenditure over depreciation, though the upgraded EUR 210 million Revolving Credit Facility with an additional EUR 150 million accordion provides ample headroom for both fleet electrification and bolt-on acquisitions.

Management's proven sandbagger archetype is evidenced by systematic mid-year upgrades in both FY24 and FY25, with initial FY25 EBITDA margin guidance of "at least 8.0%" raised to "circa 9.0%" and ultimately delivering 9.1%, reinforcing credibility of conservative forward targets.

The investment thesis centers on Heijmans' structural transformation from cyclical volatility to predictable margin expansion, underpinned by five quantitative drivers: Hegeman technical services contributing 5% revenue growth, TenneT grid projects generating 12.5% segment CAGR, defense infrastructure representing EUR 237 million opportunities, suburban housing delivering 2.5% volume CAGR, and timber-frame factory production scaling to 1,000 homes annually.

This margin-over-volume strategy implements zero-profit recognition during the first 20% of large projects to prevent early-stage margin overestimation risks and utilizes clear tender frameworks to dare to say no to high-risk projects, fundamentally differentiating Heijmans from commodity-oriented peers. While technical labor bottlenecks, housing market cyclicality, and public budget constraints present execution headwinds, the combination of negative working capital dynamics, top-quartile margins, secular infrastructure tailwinds, and management's flawless historical delivery track record positions Heijmans to achieve its ambitious 2030 targets of EUR 4.0 billion revenue and 8-10% EBITDA margins through continued selective bidding discipline and recurring service penetration.

Plausible problems to monitor

The Van Wanrooij family share lock-up ended on September 5, 2026, creating near-term technical overhang risks as some investors anticipate selling pressure from former family shareholders who may choose to liquidate their positions, potentially depressing the share price independently of operational fundamentals [Section 2]. The company's growing long-term service commitments lock in fixed labor costs which could become structural margin drags if high inflation outpaces contractual indexation clauses in these recurring multi-year agreements, eroding the profitability of what management presents as a stable revenue base [Section 2].

Heijmans' absenteeism incident rate reached 3.1, significantly missing the 2.0 target despite proactive safety initiatives and level 4 safety ladder certification, suggesting underlying workforce morale or operational stress issues that exceed sector norms [Section 1].

Extreme high-voltage technician shortages and specialized welder recruitment bottlenecks are hindering energy transition projects and constraining pipeline deployment, limiting installation rates for grid operators and driving up labor acquisition costs, which could delay the execution of Heijmans' highest-growth Connecting segment opportunities [Section 3]. The company's fleet electrification program requires EUR 30 million to EUR 40 million annual capital expenditure over depreciation, and these heavy electrification capital requirements could restrict surplus cash distributions if inflationary pressures erode operational cash inflows, limiting shareholder returns during the transition period [Section 4].

Heijmans exhibits pronounced seasonal earnings patterns with revenue and operating profit lower in the first half than the second half, and most free cash flow generation concentrated in the second half, creating predictability challenges and potential investor concern during interim reporting periods [Section 5].

- Earnings Calls H1 2026 underlying EBIT included EUR 4 million in expensed costs for the SAP S/4HANA migration, representing a temporary earnings headwind that will continue through the implementation period and could mask underlying operational performance trends in near-term reported results [Section 6].

Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive analysis of Koninklijke Heijmans NV reveals several statistical outliers and unexplained operational anomalies that warrant investor attention. The first notable outlier concerns Heijmans' extraordinarily high return on average capital employed, which reached 30.1% in H1 2026, rising sharply from 24.7% in the previous year. While Heijmans posted a 9.10% EBITDA margin in fiscal year 2025, exceeding BAM Nederland's Dutch division at 7.20% adjusted EBITDA margin, regional competitor TBI achieved an even stronger 10.30% EBITDA margin in infrastructure, yet neither peer demonstrates comparable capital efficiency metrics.

This extreme ROACE figure appears structurally disconnected from peer norms and raises questions about the sustainability of such elevated returns or whether measurement methodologies differ materially from sector conventions. The lack of disclosed denominators for capital employed calculations prevents direct peer verification of this performance claim, presenting an unexplained gap in comparative analytics that merits further disclosure transparency [Section 7, Section 4].

A second operational outlier emerges in Heijmans' workplace safety performance, where absenteeism incident rates reached 3.1, missing the 2.0 target despite proactive safety initiatives and level 4 safety ladder certification. This represents a 55% shortfall against management's own threshold and contrasts sharply with the company's otherwise disciplined operational culture and margin-over-volume philosophy. The deterioration in safety metrics during a period of aggressive revenue scaling and margin expansion suggests potential trade-offs between growth ambitions and workforce welfare management.

The absence of comparable peer safety data in the construction sector prevents definitive benchmarking, but the magnitude of the miss relative to internal targets signals either inadequate resource allocation to safety programs or fundamental structural challenges in managing labor-intensive project execution at scale [Section 1]. - Earnings Calls The third anomaly centers on Heijmans' acquisition valuation strategy, where management claims that bolt-on acquisitions like Hegeman are bought at low valuation multiples, generating high margin accretion postintegration. However, the Hegeman acquisition brought €140 million turnover at 5.6% EBITDA margins, adding

20% to divisional revenue versus Heijmans' own 7.7% margin, creating a margin dilution dynamic that contradicts the stated accretion thesis. While Hegeman contributed €5 million EBIT in H1 2026, fully absorbing integrationrelated transition costs, the initial margin profile suggests Heijmans acquired a materially lower-quality earnings stream that will require extensive operational restructuring to justify the purchase price.

The apparent contradiction between management's "high margin accretion" language and the acquired business's inferior profitability profile represents either overly optimistic integration assumptions or incomplete disclosure of the true valuation economics, warranting deeper scrutiny of future M&A execution capabilities [Section 5, Section 9].

A fourth statistical outlier appears in the extraordinary order backlog expansion, where the group order book reached €4.1 billion by mid-2026, representing a 36% year-on-year expansion, with management stating that high-quality backlog fully secures updated margin targets. This aggressive backlog build occurred during a period when completed new-build residential volumes declined by 11.6% because complex regulatory rules delay starts, and national infrastructure budget shortfalls caused public tender delays, postponing key civil engineering opportunities.

The simultaneous occurrence of a 36% backlog surge against declining residential completions and public budget constraints presents a mathematical tension that requires explanation–either Heijmans is capturing disproportionate market share from distressed competitors, or the backlog contains significant volumes concentrated in long-dated defense and grid projects that carry elevated execution risk relative to the company's traditional contracting profile.

The absence of detailed backlog aging disclosures prevents assessment of project concentration risk and revenue conversion timelines [Section 6, Section 3, Section 9]. The fifth outlier concerns the remarkably strong correlation between residential permit growth and management confidence, with Dutch residential permit issuances growing at 25.3% year-on-year, supporting mid-term residential volume targets, yet management's residential financial projections assume flat near-term suburban housing volumes, planning for just 1% growth in fiscal year 2026.

This 24 percentage point gap between permit momentum and volume guidance represents either extreme conservatism bordering on deliberate sandbagging, or management possesses non-public information suggesting permits will not convert to actual construction starts at historical rates. Given that revenue and operating profit are lower in the first half of the year than the second half, and most free cash flow generation shifts to the second half of the year, the disconnect between permit activity and volume assumptions could reflect timing lags or structural changes in permit-toconstruction conversion dynamics that have not been adequately explained to investors.

The magnitude of this guidance conservatism exceeds typical sandbagging patterns observed across the peer group and merits explicit reconciliation in future investor communications [Section 10, Section 5, Section 3].
