[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Legrand is a quality compounder with a structural demand inflection in the third of it that serves data centres and a cyclical trough in the rest. At 138.00 EUR the shares pay us for the compounding, about 15 percent a year over eighteen months on our modal path, and not for the price: the multiple has nowhere obvious to go until the market's doubt about the outer years of the data-centre niche is answered. So the case is a C against the 20 percent bar; it becomes a B at about 130 EUR, or at 138 EUR if that doubt lifts.

  • The change is external and structural, and it has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • Demand for data-centre physical infrastructure took data-centre sales from 8 percent of the group in 2018 to 26 percent in FY2025 and 32 percent in H1 2026, growing above 30 percent organically in the half, with orders visible into 2029.
    • It shows in the company's resources (three new Executive Committee members from 2026), its choices (the 2030 targets raised on 2026-09-29 to 6 to 8 percent organic growth a year and a 21 to 22 percent adjusted operating margin), its actions (seven acquisitions in H1 2026 adding about 450 million EUR of sales; a first divestment programme), its key operating metrics (group organic growth 9.8 percent in H1 2026 against a history of about 3 percent), its financial statements (H1 2026 sales plus 13.1 percent) and the sell-side's estimates (FY2028 EPS up 14.7 percent over twelve months to 2026-10-02).
    • The force is sector-level: global data-centre capacity is projected from 87 GW in 2025 to 233 GW by 2030, content per megawatt is rising from 2.5 million USD to 3.3 million USD, and Schneider Electric reported triple-digit data-centre order growth in Q2 2026.
  • The internal change is an acceleration under the same leadership, not a break.
    • The chief executive has been in post since 2018 and the finance director since 2019
    • The strategy is the same one at a higher setting.
    • Guidance has been raised at every level within twelve months, acquisition spending has been raised to around 60 percent of free cash flow, and leverage has been taken to 2.24 times EBITDA at Q2 FY2026 from 1.38 times a year earlier inside a new 1.5 to 2.5 times envelope.
    • That is why 'Change Forces' are B and not A: strong on growth, neutral on margin, delivered by the same people.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up where investors extrapolate and gone backward beneath the operating line.
    • Revenue growth went from an average of about 4.4 percent a year over FY2023 to FY2025 to a consensus 16.6 percent in FY2026 and 11.6 percent in FY2027
    • Brokers' basis EPS grows 16.4 percent and 14.4 percent on the same two years
    • FY2027 and FY2028 estimates are up 8.4 and 10.5 percent over six months.
    • The adjusted operating margin is flat at 20.8 percent in H1 2026 and guided unchanged at 20.5 to 21.0 percent for FY2026 while the sales guide rose, because expedited freight and inventory for data-centre delivery absorbed the operating gearing.
    • Free cash flow was 9 percent of sales in H1 2026 against a historic 13 to 15 percent, trade working capital reached 21.6 percent of trailing revenue at Q2 FY2026 from 17.2 percent at Q4 FY2025, and Q2 2026 free cash flow missed consensus by 37 percent.
      • The cash lines are the live weakness in a record of growth.
  • The other two-thirds of the company sit at a cyclical trough with early signs of a turn, not a turn.
    • Around 60 percent of revenue is tied to buildings
    • European organic sales fell 2.4 percent in H1 2026 and the non-data-centre buildings businesses have recorded negative volume for ten straight quarters.
    • French housing starts were up 31 percent year-on-year in July 2026 and French residential permits up 23.9 percent, and installations lag permits by 12 to 18 months
      • Against that, Eurozone construction confidence fell back to minus five in August 2026 and the French and Italian construction PMIs sit below 40.
    • So the recovery is a plausible 2027 event, about 55 percent on our reading, and the price assumes little of it.
  • The price has accepted the growth and is withholding the multiple.
    • At 138.00 EUR the shares trade at 19.0 times consensus 24-month-forward EPS of 7.262 EUR, the 37th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range, after a twelve-month return of minus 2.7 percent while Schneider Electric rose 7.4 percent, Siemens 9.5 percent and ABB 35.9 percent and now trade at 21.3, 19.1 and 22.9 times.
    • Estimates for the outer years rose 8 to 15 percent while the price went sideways through two guidance raises and a Capital Markets Day.
      • That is the pattern of a market that believes the growth and doubts the terminal value of the data-centre franchise beyond the 800 volt DC architecture transition.
    • The doubt is company-specific, which is why the re-rating is ours to earn and why the mark-to-market risk is Legrand's own rather than the sector's.
  • What the case earns at the price is a C.
    • On our path brokers' basis EPS compounds from 5.08 EUR in FY2025 to 7.55 EUR in FY2028, about 14 percent a year, within two percent of consensus
    • The shares are worth about 170.0 EUR including cash at April 2028 and 197.9 EUR at October 2029 at 19.7 times the earnings two years beyond each horizon, which is 14.9 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.8 percent a year over three years.
    • Earnings growth is the whole return
      • The re-rating from 19.0 to 19.7 times is small
      • The cash yield of about 2 percent a year is secure and immaterial.
    • A B needs either the 21.0 times multiple that the doubt lifting would earn (19.8 percent a year over eighteen months) or an entry at about 129 EUR, 7 percent below the price.
  • The return is more sensitive to the drop-through than to a point of revenue growth.
    • A plausibly worse ratio of EBITDA to EPS, taken from the evidence of H1 2026 (margin flat to plus 20 basis points, net interest about 300 million EUR by FY2028, tax at 28 percent), cuts EPS growth to about 12.2 percent a year and the return to 9.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.9 percent over three years, a D at the edge of F.
    • That is why cash conversion and the margin are two of the three 'Case/SP Key Risks', and why the Q3 2026 results on 2026-11-05 matter more for the drop-through than for the growth.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', not crisply.
    • The mandatory condition passes and the dominant-position condition passes at B
    • One-to-many scalability and fixed-cost leverage fail in a hardware manufacturer with a deliberately variable cost base.
    • The secondary fit, 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', describes the price rather than the change: a high-quality compounder relatively oversold on an identifiable external concern whose clearing evidence is directional now and hard only in 2027.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the external force through the actions, the operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates.
    • It breaks at two points: at the multiple, which the market will not re-rate while the architecture question is open, and at the cash lines, which went the wrong way in a year of record growth.
    • The case is therefore compelling as a business and not compelling as a return at 138.00 EUR.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material in its inputs (B) and its outputs (B) but not in its return (C): at 138.00 EUR the modal case gives about 15 percent a year, and the Building Block measures whether the change is sufficient to drive 20 percent-plus, so the weaker part binds.

    • Not B, because the return is the Building Block's own question and the price answers it
    • B was live and would be taken at about 130 EUR or on the multiple branch.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

    • The strongest case against is the one the risks research itself judges the most internally consistent, the 'False Friend': record near-term data-centre hypergrowth and a record backlog mask two structural problems that arrive after the backlog is delivered.

    • The case against, at its strongest.

      • The building core, about 60 percent of sales, is ex-growth: ten quarters of negative volume and a historical organic growth rate of 3 percent against 5.4 percent at Schneider Electric.
      • The niche faces a technological transition: native 800 volt DC and solid-state transformers would remove the low-voltage distribution layer Legrand sells, with 37 percent of data-centre sales facing an 81 percent revenue drop on Rothschild and Co Redburn's estimate, and the company is excluded from native-DC supplier lists and lacks medium voltage, three-phase UPS and software.
      • The near-term evidence is on its side: the margin did not rise with growth, cash conversion fell, acquisitions dilute 30 to 50 basis points of margin a year, 45 percent of the US cost base is imported, and the PEG ratio de-rated 29 percent since late 2025 while estimates rose, which the bears read as the market seeing it first.
      • In its strongest form it says the right multiple for a business whose growth is borrowed from one niche with a five-year horizon is the 15 times EV/EBIT of Morgan Stanley's bear case, 120 EUR, 13 percent below the price.
    • What we concede.
      • It is internally consistent.
      • The H1 2026 margin and cash evidence supports it.
      • The building core is ex-growth today.
      • The company's answer to the architecture question is a 2027 product, not a 2026 order.
      • We cannot refute it from the printed accounts, which is one reason the rating is C and the recommendation is a park.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • The timeline of the disruption is beyond the eighteen-month horizon on every source including the bears': 100 percent of current data-centre orders are on traditional AC architectures, management expects fewer than 5 percent of racks to be full low-voltage DC by 2030, and solid-state transformers are distant on cost and reliability.
      • The hybrid sidecar phase that precedes it raises content per megawatt by 20 to 30 percent and is additive to busway, racks and cooling.
      • The company is buying its way into the parts of the data centre it lacked (Power Pods, switchgear, load banks, liquid cooling) at about 12 times EBIT and twice its cost of capital.
      • The research's own near-term probability for the disruption is low, and the multiple already sits at the 37th percentile of ten years with estimates rising, so the hypothesis is partly priced.
    • Where it would win.
      • If the European core is still contracting in 2028 when the first native-DC deployments appear, the group would be a mid-single-digit grower on a multiple that should then be the five-year low of 14.85 times, about 109 EUR on today's 24-month-forward earnings of 7.34 EUR.
      • The bounded-downside argument we make is a trough-multiple argument on the whole company's earnings, not a claim that anything covers the enterprise value.

    • Addressable content per megawatt rises from 2.5 million USD to 3.3 million USD.
  • Visibility: the chief executive described order intake as very strong with pipeline discussions extending into 2029 for primary hyperscaler projects.

Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle is turning up and the turn is visible in the reported numbers: quarterly revenue growth went from plus 3.7 percent in Q4 FY2025 to plus 11.4 percent in Q1 FY2026 and plus 14.7 percent in Q2 FY2026, organic growth from 7.7 percent for FY2025 to 9.8 percent in H1 2026 and 10.4 percent in Q2 2026, and full-year organic guidance was raised twice in a year, from 4 to 7 percent to 8 to 10 percent.
  • Two halves: North and Central America grew 24.2 percent organically in H1 2026 (the US alone, 44.4 percent of group sales, plus 26.7 percent)
    • Europe fell 2.4 percent
    • Rest of World grew 2.3 percent with South America minus 3.3 percent and China subdued.
  • The prior trough: FY2023 revenue growth was plus 0.9 percent and FY2024 plus 2.8 percent
    • Q1 2024 sales fell 5.6 percent.
  • Not A, because half the turn is acquired (6.9 percentage points of the 13.1 percent H1 2026 growth) and because Europe has not turned.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the 800 volt DC architecture force is strengthening in the debate (NVIDIA's reference architecture, peers' native DC ecosystems, Schneider Electric's 3.1 billion USD Cognite acquisition in June 2026) but is being disproved in the near term: 100 percent of current data-centre orders remain on traditional AC architectures, management expects fewer than 5 percent of racks to be full low-voltage DC by 2030, and the hybrid sidecar phase raises content value per megawatt.
  • The threat as the bears frame it: Rothschild and Co Redburn estimate that 37 percent of data-centre sales (traditional PDUs and low-voltage protection) face an 81 percent revenue drop under native 800 volt DC, while 58 percent (racks, busway, cooling) gain a 40 percent value uplift, a net 11 percent headwind to the data-centre mix.
    • Deutsche Bank notes Legrand lacks direct partnerships with NVIDIA on native DC and lacks three-phase UPS, medium voltage and software.
  • Timing: management says 800 volt DC will not scale until 2030 and hybrid sidecars represent 10 to 20 percent of new IT loads by 2030
    • Jefferies puts hybrid sidecars at 35 percent of additions by 2030.
    • The company's power sidecar is scheduled for commercialisation in 2027.
    • Solid-state transformers are judged distant on cost and reliability.
  • Protected share: management estimates roughly 80 percent of the product portfolio is structurally protected from the shift.
  • Not B, because the force is net negative in direction for the outer years and the company is excluded from key native-DC supplier lists
    • Not D, because it is not yet in any order book and the hybrid phase is additive.
  • This force is the reason the multiple has not followed the estimates
    • It is read again as the named concern under 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' and as a 'Case/SP Key Risk'.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is stable in the core low-voltage building niches, where national standards, a catalogue of over 300,000 references and distributor lock-in protect position, but is tightening at the two edges that matter for the growth case: hyperscale data-centre procurement, where integrated groups (Schneider Electric, Vertiv, Eaton) bundle white space with grey space and software, and emerging-market switches, where Legrand fell from number one to number four in Indian switch volumes.
  • Hyperscale procurement: expert calls in the research say hyperscalers increasingly prefer single-source procurement of combined white-space and grey-space packages, which favours vertically integrated groups over Legrand's component approach
    • Legrand is losing relative share in the largest hyperscale data centres, where Schneider Electric remains dominant.
  • The company's answer: the chief executive argues customers prefer best-of-breed components over forced modular systems, and the H1 2026 data-centre organic growth above 30 percent beat Vertiv on a two-year compound basis.
  • Not B, because the direction at the edges is tightening, not easing
    • Not D, because the core is not under price attack and the company holds the global number one position in rack PDUs and busways.
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = C

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
