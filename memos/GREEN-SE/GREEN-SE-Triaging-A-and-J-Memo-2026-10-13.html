[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Green Landscaping is a recovery case in a leveraged roll-up, priced as a distressed equity. At SEK 16.2 (2026-10-12) the shares trade at 3.9x earnings two years out, the lowest point in the stock's own history, because the equity is a quarter of an enterprise value that itself trades at an ordinary 8x EBITA; almost any recovery that does not need new equity clears the 20% bar from here. The folder does not yet show that the recovery has started, and the people running it are interim.

  • The change is internal, and it is a reset rather than a build.
    • The chief executive of eleven years left on 16 Jul 2026, the day before the Q2 2026 report, and a board member with a restructuring record became acting chief executive the next day
    • Within two months he had launched the Green Performance programme, taken SEK 79m of one-off charges, filed a police report against the former owner of a German subsidiary and put four country managers into group management (22 Sep 2026).
    • The chairman changed in May 2026 to the chief executive of the largest shareholder.
    • The dividend is zero, buybacks are halted and acquisitions are paused so that debt can fall.
    • The two principal owners bought shares through the fall and held 38.94% of the capital combined by September 2026
      • The departing chief executive sold 38.45% of his holding after his exit.
  • The external forces net to a mild headwind that is only beginning to ease.
    • About 60% of sales come from municipalities and public bodies under three-to-five-year contracts, which grow at about 4% a year and do not move with the cycle
    • The private 40% and the landscaping half of the business are in a contraction that has not turned.
    • Construction generalists with empty order books have bid into Nordic landscaping tenders, so Norway's segment margin fell from 13.4% in FY2022 to 4.1% in Q2 2026, and the first easing (fewer bidders in Norway) is one quarter's observation.
    • Labour and fuel costs rise before annual indexation catches them.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have not yet turned on the profit line.
    • The Q2 2026 EBITA margin fell 200 basis points to 7.0%
    • Q3 2026 carries SEK 79m of charges
    • Leverage was 3.4x net debt to pro-forma adjusted EBITDA at 30 Jun 2026 against a 2.5x target and is forecast by DNB Carnegie to peak at 3.69x at end Q3 2026
    • Consensus earnings per share for FY2026 have been cut 48% in twelve months.
    • What has turned: organic growth (+11% in Q1 2026 and +6% in Q2 2026 after five negative quarters), the order backlog (SEK 8,039m against SEK 7,558m a year earlier) and operating cash flow (SEK 131m in H1 2026 against SEK 61m in H1 2025).
  • The change has travelled three of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The people, the decisions and the actions are all new since July 2026.
    • The operating metrics show it only in organic growth and cash
    • Margins, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates are still moving the wrong way.
    • The first evidence that they have turned cannot arrive before the Q3 report on 23 Oct 2026, and the first full test is the FY2026 report on 3 Feb 2027.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted earnings per share of about SEK 2.50 in FY2026, SEK 3.10 in FY2027, SEK 4.00 in FY2028 and SEK 4.70 in FY2029, about 23% a year from FY2026 to FY2029, against consensus of 2.83, 3.46 and 4.35 for FY2026 to FY2028.
    • The growth is a recovery from a depressed base, not an extrapolated trend: the adjusted EBITA margin moves from about 6.4% in FY2026 to about 7.4% in FY2029, still below the 8% target, and about half of the earnings growth comes from below EBITA, where falling net interest meets fixed amortisation.
      • That is the kind of growth the market pays least for, which is why our fair multiple is 9x and not the five-year median of 12x.
    • Reported earnings per share for FY2026 are about SEK 1.3 on our estimate, because the SEK 79m of charges sits outside the adjusted figure.
  • What it costs: SEK 16.2, or 3.9x the dashboard's 24-month-forward consensus earnings of SEK 4.1952 and about 4.05x our own FY2028 estimate, against a five-year range of 3.70x to 28.43x (median 11.94x; 25th percentile 9.66x) and a two-year median of 9.15x
    • The 18 peers with a reading trade between 4.8x and 17.3x.
    • On the enterprise lens the picture is ordinary: market capitalisation of about SEK 914m plus net debt of SEK 2,668m is about SEK 3,590m, about 8x FY2026e adjusted EBITA of about SEK 445m and about 4.6x consensus EBITDA.
      • The market is pricing the enterprise fairly and the equity as an option on deleveraging.
      • A 10% rise in enterprise value is a 40% rise in the equity
      • A two-turn fall in the enterprise multiple removes most of the equity.
  • The return clears the bar on the modal path with room, and clears it even with no re-rating at all.
    • At a blended multiple of 7.35x the value including cash returns is SEK 37.49 at the eighteen-month horizon (13 Apr 2028), about 75% a year, and SEK 41.39 at the three-year horizon (13 Oct 2029), about 37% a year.
    • With the multiple held at 4.05x throughout, the same earnings path gives about 18% a year over eighteen months and about 13% over three years.
    • The swing factor is the re-rating, and behind the re-rating is the absence of an equity raise: a SEK 500m rights issue at about SEK 12 would add about 42m shares to 56.4m and cut the eighteen-month return to about 27% a year at the blend and about 10% at 6x.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through (the margin recovering only to 6.5% by FY2028 and 6.8% by FY2030, net interest stuck at SEK 140m) gives FY2028 adjusted earnings per share of about SEK 3.07 and, at 6x, about 28% a year over eighteen months and about 15% over three years: a B and a C on the framework's anchors.
      • A slow recovery dents the case
      • A raise breaks it.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major CfC Clearing', and the fit is forming, not formed.
    • A trough multiple, estimates cut in half, a kitchen-sink and a capitulated price are all in place
    • The condition that defines the pattern, that the concern is clearing, has no evidence yet, and the latest evidence (22 Sep 2026 and 30 Sep 2026) added to the concern.
    • The secondary pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', broad in its inputs and without outputs.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the owners through the new team to the actions taken, and it holds at the price, which asks for no recovery.
    • It breaks, for now, at the fourth layer: Norway's margin is 4.1% against a management "normal" of 10%, cash conversion was 28% over five years against the 50% the case needs, and both must mend before the lenders rather than the shareholders decide who owns the recovery.

  • What the case therefore is: compelling on arithmetic and not compelling on evidence

    • A case to watch with dated triggers, not yet a case to own.
    • The framework's park rule is tripped twice (management and business quality both at D), and we do not argue against it.

  • Rating = C, because the expected change is material in size (a doubling-plus of the equity on the modal path from a 0th-percentile multiple, and a return that clears the 20% bar even with no re-rating) but not yet material in evidence: the input forces are moderately strong and led by a caretaker ('Change Forces' at C), the outputs have not turned on the profit line and the estimates are still falling ('Financial Outputs' at D), and the return ('Modal return' at A) is large because the price is distressed rather than because the improvement is demonstrated.

    • The parts combine as a C because the A of the return and the D of the outputs are the same fact seen from two sides: the price assumes the outputs will not improve, and the folder does not yet prove the price wrong.
    • Not B, because a B needs the outputs to have begun to turn (one quarter of margin recovery with cash conversion above 50%), and the last two data points went the other way.
    • Not D, because a D would ignore that, at this price, even a partial recovery is a 20%-plus a year return, and that the people, the decisions and the actions have all changed.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: Green was never a compounder but a multiple arbitrage with a shrinking core.
      • Organic adjusted EBITA fell about 7% a year from 2019 to 2024 while acquisitions added 38% a year of sales to 2023
      • Cash conversion was 28% over five years, not in one bad year but in six
      • Acquired businesses lost an average 4.6 percentage points of margin within two years of purchase as their founders left
      • The controls (a SEK 30m underwriting cap, local finance functions, no internal audit) failed in three countries at once
      • And the man who built the group sold 38% of his shares the moment he could.
      • On this reading the Nordic margin is not in a trough but at its structural level for a labour-heavy, price-taking service business once acquisitions stop flattering it.
        • FY2028 adjusted earnings per share is about SEK 3.0 rather than SEK 4.0, leverage stays above 3.0x, the Q3 2026 peak forces a conversation with the banks, and the equity is refinanced at the shareholders' expense.
        • The research's own analysis of competing hypotheses gives this profile zero hard inconsistencies and puts the bear case at medium-high probability.
    • Our first concession: the record is as stated, and the folder carries no evidence yet of Norway recovering.
      • Every figure in the case against is in the folder.
      • The control weaknesses are real and unchanged in design, and the operator is interim.
      • This is why 'Investment Thesis' is a C and not a B, and why the recommendation is a park.
    • Our second concession: the bounded-downside argument does not hold at a low multiple.
      • Other Europe's EBITA is about SEK 300m (a 17.5% rolling-twelve-month margin on about SEK 1,700m of sales; 59% of group EBITA in 2025), a pre-tax, pre-interest divisional profit, so the like-for-like multiple is an enterprise multiple.
      • At 4x, the price the group pays for private targets, it is worth about SEK 1,200m, a third of the SEK 3,590m enterprise value
      • At 8x about SEK 2,400m, two thirds
      • Only at 12x does it cover the enterprise value.
      • It covers the market capitalisation of about SEK 914m at any of those multiples, but that is not a bound, because the market capitalisation sits beneath SEK 2,668m of debt that ranks ahead of it.
    • Our response: the structural level of this business is not 6%, and the owners are buying.
      • Return on capital employed excluding goodwill was 19% in 2025
      • Other Europe earns 17.5%
      • Germany earns 13.5% to 14.0%
      • Stebule in Lithuania retained all its key Vilnius contracts at margins above the segment average.
      • The Swedish losses were specific contracts, now closed.
      • Organic growth of +6% in Q2 2026 contradicts a shrinking core.
      • The owners who control the board and form its remuneration committee have bought through the fall and have 38.94% of their own money on the line.
      • And the price, at about 8x enterprise value to EBITA, is not asking us to believe in a compounder, only in a business worth its debt and a little more.
    • Where the case against would win: Norway.
      • If the Norwegian margin stays near 4% through 2027, the cash does not come, the debt does not fall, and the multiple stays where the lenders put it.
      • The competing hypothesis is the reason for the park
      • The arithmetic is the reason to watch.

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Contracted public demand

  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Other Europe holding a mid-teens margin
    • The continental and Lithuanian units earn the group's best margins and most of its EBITA, and they are the proof that the unit economics are not those of the Nordic segments.
    • The case needs them to hold a mid-teens margin while the Lithuanian contracts are retendered, so that the Nordic repair is not offset.

  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: A permanent chief executive

    • The appointment of an external operator with a Nordic services record would answer the operator question that keeps business quality at D.

    • It is the inverse of the new team's own review having found everything.
External
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Construction generalists bidding into Nordic tenders
    • Construction companies with empty order books have bid for landscaping and municipal contracts at prices that cover their fixed costs, and customers have let price outrank quality.
    • It is a case and share-price risk of high probability today and the direct cause of the Norwegian margin collapse; the first easing, fewer bidders in Norway, is one quarter old.

    • Swedish building starts and sentiment have turned in the national data, a point made about installers rather than landscapers.
    • If a construction recovery pulls generalist bidders back out of landscaping tenders, Norway's margin could recover without heroics from management; a supporting hypothesis, not evidence about Green.
Internal
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: The management reset
    • An interim chief executive with a restructuring record has launched a performance programme, charged legacy problems in one announcement, appointed country managers and stopped the dividend and acquisitions.
    • It is the change the whole case rests on; it has reached the people, the decisions and the actions and not yet the operating metrics.
    • Nothing in it is quantified yet.

    • It decides whether the case is right and whether the shares re-rate, because it produces the cash that cuts the debt.
    • The Swedish piece is contracted arithmetic; the Norwegian piece is not yet in evidence.
