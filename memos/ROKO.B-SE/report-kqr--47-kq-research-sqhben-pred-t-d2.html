Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the concentration of Röko's financial drivers determines whether its decentralized acquisition model can sustain earnings growth amid cyclical headwinds.
  2. Rapid acquisition-led growth remains central to Röko's strategy but elevates balance sheet risks if capital availability contracts.
  3. Historical pauses at peers Storskogen and Volati demonstrate that high leverage inevitably forces M&A halts and drives significant downward earnings revisions.

Strategic Backdrop and Central Inquiry

1.
Central research: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Decentralized compounding: Röko operates as a Swedish serial acquirer with a portfolio of about 40 European niche businesses divided into business-to-business and business-to-consumer segments. The company has successfully grown its EBITA to approximately SEK 1.9 bn expected in 2027 by deploying capital into highly profitable, asset-light niche companies. This rapid expansion makes understanding the sustainability of its deal-making pace essential for predicting future earnings per share.
3.
Accelerating dealmaking: Broker research highlights that acquisitions are the primary driver of earnings revisions for serial acquirers over the next 12 months. DNB Carnegie notes that Röko is well positioned to double its earnings over the next five years through continued acquisitions without increasing leverage or raising equity. However, any pause in the M&A engine due to tight credit or high leverage represents a key risk to its valuation and EPS growth.
4.
Contrary evidence: Carnegie indicates that despite cyclical end-market risks, Röko has maintained a highly profitable, asset-light portfolio with historical organic sales growth of 6% between 2020 and 2024. This diversified historical performance suggests that the group is less dependent on single-unit drivers than near-term forecasts assume.

Section 3 – ETB Technologies and Refurbished IT Memory Price Cycles

Section summary
  1. The current memory supply shortage has fueled extraordinary organic growth at ETB Technologies, but historical precedents show that memory downcycles inevitably trigger severe revenue and margin contraction.
  2. Reversals in memory-pricing cycles typically span five to seven quarters and inflict substantial sequential declines on refurbished IT distributors, presenting a major risk to Röko's segment margins.

ETB Technologies Organic Contributions

1.
Strong demand: Röko's B2B segment has recently experienced exceptionally strong organic expansion that is heavily concentrated in its subsidiary, ETB Technologies. ETB supplies refurbished IT networking and memory products, which are seeing elevated demand from AI and data center investments. This boom has been supported by global memory shortages that created a highly favorable pricing environment. Therefore, ETB's rapid expansion represents a cyclical peak rather than a permanent structural shift.

During the third quarter of 2026, the subsidiary accounted for approximately 6 percentage points of the group's total 9% organic sales growth. Consequently, any slowing in ETB's operational momentum will severely flatten Röko's organic growth.

Section 4 – Currency Translation and Krona Volatility relative to Organic Growth

Section summary
  1. Unhedged currency exposure makes Röko's translated earnings highly vulnerable to krona volatility, which can frequently mask robust underlying organic growth.
  2. Forecasts showing a persistently weak Swedish krona suggest that serial acquirers will continue to enjoy translation tailwinds, but any reversal represents a major downside risk to adjusted EPS.

Röko's Unhedged Currency Position

1.
Significant risk: Röko faces notable translation exposure because 89.6% of its consolidated net sales are generated in foreign currencies without active hedging. The company's 2025 annual report outlines that this unhedged position makes consolidated earnings highly sensitive to fluctuations in the Swedish krona. Consequently, rapid movements in exchange rates will directly dictate whether reported net income meets baseline investor expectations. This unhedged exposure means that unfavourable currency translation swings can significantly suppress reported adjusted EPS.
2.
Negative impact: Röko reported that currency translation fluctuations had a negative 2% impact on group net sales during the first nine months of 2026. The company's interim report notes that this drag occurred because the Swedish krona appreciated on average against key operating currencies compared to the previous year. This translational headwind shows that even when underlying business units expand, reported financials remain highly vulnerable. Therefore, currency volatility continues to represent a primary shortterm driver of reported EBITA.
3.
Volatile drag: Röko experienced a substantial negative 6% translation impact on net sales during the first quarter of 2026 due to krona strength. The company's Q1 interim report highlights that this headwind restricted reported sales growth to 9% despite a solid 6% organic local currency expansion. Additionally, currency differences negatively impacted net income by an extra SEK 26m on financial items. This volatility confirms that reported quarterly earnings per share are heavily distorted by foreign exchange swings.
