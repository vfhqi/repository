EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of Change Merck has undertaken significant structural reorganization by establishing a dedicated oncology business unit in 2026 to prepare for the Keytruda patent cliff while simultaneously planning to double the Animal Health business from its 2024 base by the mid-2030s. Senior leadership rotations have aligned talent with oncology and pipeline commercialization goals, including appointing Jannie Oosthuizen as EVP and President of Oncology in April 2026.

Management has raised its mid-2030s pipeline sales target to over $70 billion in January 2026, signaling aggressive diversification ambitions. The company has committed more than $65 billion to strategic business development since 2021, representing massive capital deployment to offset future revenue erosion. Regulatory headwinds have materialized with Januvia facing a negotiated 79% price discount under Medicare maximum fair price implementation and increased 340B program utilization negatively impacting overall performance.

Keytruda subcutaneous transition (Qlex) represents a 20% potential impact on forward earnings through lifecycle management [HIGH plausibility], supported by Q2 sales reaching $463 million, beating consensus by 36% after permanent J-code implementation, with clinical trials showing 65% of patients preferring the subcutaneous administration route. Winrevair commercialization holds a 15% impact on EPS with Q2 2026 sales reaching $588 million, up 75% sequentially in the highly lucrative US market [HIGH plausibility].

Lipfendra launch represents a 10% long-term revenue opportunity as the first available oral PCSK9 inhibitor, demonstrating nearly 60% LDL reduction in Phase 3 trials [MEDIUM plausibility]. Sac-TMT oncology pipeline is projected to deliver 10% incremental oncology revenue by the mid-2030s, having achieved its first positive Phase 3 readout in first-line non-small cell lung cancer [MEDIUM plausibility]. Animal Health expansion supports a 5% division sales growth target with management planning to double Animal Health sales from the fiscal 2024 base [HIGH plausibility].

Strategic hematology business development through the Terns acquisition adds TERN-701, demonstrating a 75% major molecular response in previously treated CML patients [MEDIUM plausibility].

Recent Delivery Merck delivered a solid revenue beat in Q2 2026 with worldwide sales of $16.6 billion, beating consensus estimates by 1.8% and growing 5% year-on-year. However, Non-GAAP EPS of -$0.13 missed consensus expectations of -$0.11, driven by $5.7 billion upfront charge for the Terns acquisition. Corporate scorecard performance dropped to 94% of target in FY25, reflecting poor international vaccine volumes with adjusted FY25 scorecard revenue of $63.86 billion failing to meet the $65.63 billion target.

Forward Credibility Merck raised its FY26 sales guidance midpoint to $66.3-67.3 billion, representing a $400 million increase, but full-year adjusted EPS guidance was lowered to $2.71 at the midpoint to incorporate the Terns transaction charge. Bernstein Research notes that Merck's development plans remain much more conservative than peers, with management's FY25 guidance modeling no further Gardasil shipments to China at the lower bound, representing a sandbagged baseline.

Mixed revision quality exists as hedging and out-licensing milestone payments supported a portion of the FY26 raise, while Bernstein warns that U.S. Keytruda growth will moderate as key tumor indications reach peak penetration.

Sell-Side Positioning Morgan Stanley upgraded Merck to Overweight from Equal-weight in August 2026, raising the price target to $179 from $116, modeling flat revenues through the Keytruda cliff with newer pipeline assets providing longterm offset. Jefferies models a ~$13 billion peak cardiovascular pipeline opportunity, indicating significant structural upside, while Bernstein argues the personalized mRNA cancer platform will deliver lower-than-average margins due to profit-splitting with partners. Consensus rating mix reveals 72% Overweight, 28% Equal-weight, and 0% Underweight allocations with price targets displaying wide dispersion from $105 to $179. Fundamental Peer Position

Merck sits in the third quartile of the peer group with Q2 2026 worldwide sales expanding 5% year-on-year, trailing Eli Lilly's market-leading 48% surge. Rothschild & Co Redburn emphasizes that Merck's P/E of 8.5X is close to declining peers Pfizer and Bristol Myers, failing to reflect pipeline value, while near-term EPS momentum remains depressed by a $2.39 per share reduction due to business development transactions. & Co Redburn Activist Presence No active activist presence exists in AS library for Merck, allowing undisturbed execution of corporate restructuring.

Institutional shareholding remains highly supportive of management's capital allocation, reducing potential proxy disruption risks. Material Risks Keytruda composition of matter patents expire in December 2028, exposing half of total revenues to rapid biosimilar market erosion, although secondary methods-of-use and manufacturing patents expire in 2029, fueling multi-year litigation against generic oncology developers.

CMS guidelines subject both intravenous and subcutaneous Keytruda to simultaneous price negotiations starting in January 2029, while Medicare restructuring drives ~$400 million negative impact, compressing net pricing margins on core oral specialty therapies. Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Merck faces several concerning fundamental challenges that are materially worse than peer median performance and sector base rates, centered primarily around its extreme revenue concentration risk and impending patent cliff dynamics. The company's gross margin trajectory has compressed significantly more than peers, declining from 77.5% to 73.5% on a GAAP basis in the most recent quarter, driven by elevated inventory reserves and vaccine write-downs that signal poor demand forecasting and channel management quality.

Keytruda's revenue concentration represents nearly half of total corporate revenue, creating an asymmetric dependency that far exceeds typical pharma portfolio concentration levels and exposes shareholders to severe cliff risk upon patent expiration.

The company's international vaccine distribution remains structurally impaired, with Gardasil sales depressed by 35% in China due to suspended shipments and elevated channel inventories, demonstrating unusually severe distributor relationship challenges relative to peer vaccine franchises. Organizational execution capacity appears constrained by persistent commercial understaffing and structural silos, with expert interviews highlighting that vaccine marketing teams operate on inadequate resources and functional divisions between clinical, commercial, and payer teams hinder efficient product launch coordination more acutely than at peer organizations.

Management's recent delivery track record deteriorated sharply in fiscal year 2025, achieving only 94% of its scorecard target with adjusted revenues of $63.86 billion falling short of the $65.63 billion target, marking a notable reversal from the prior two years of above-target execution and raising concerns about forward guidance reliability. - Earnings Calls & Co Redburn Regulatory pricing pressure appears disproportionately severe, with Januvia facing a negotiated 79% price discount under Medicare maximum fair price implementation and CMS guidelines subjecting both intravenous and subcutaneous Keytruda formulations to simultaneous price negotiations starting January 2029, while increased 340B program utilization negatively impacts overall performance by limiting validation of inappropriate discounts.

The company's earnings quality has deteriorated through frequent and massive business development charges, including a $5.7 billion upfront Terns acquisition charge in the second quarter of 2026 and a $9.0 billion Cidara transaction impact, with these non-deductible write-offs creating a pattern of diluted GAAP profitability that obscures underlying operational trends more severely than peer business development practices.

Outliers worth noting

Merck's Januvia faces an exceptional 79% negotiated price discount under the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare maximum fair price implementation, representing one of the most severe discounts in pharmaceutical pricing history and positioning the asset in the bottom percentile of pricing outcomes industry-wide. This discount magnitude substantially exceeds typical pharmaceutical pricing adjustments and signals unprecedented regulatory pricing power over mature blockbusters. [Cross-ref: Section 3] Keytruda Qlex recorded Q2 2026 sales of $463 million, beating consensus expectations by 46%, placing this subcutaneous formulation launch in the top decile of specialty pharmaceutical uptake velocities within the first year of commercial availability.

The pace of conversion from intravenous to subcutaneous administration far exceeds historical norms for oncology lifecycle management transitions, suggesting exceptional clinical preference dynamics. [Cross-ref: Section 6]
