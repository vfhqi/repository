EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report examines how concentrated or diffuse the drivers are that separate AT&S's next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months. The evidence across eight analytical sections reveals that AT&S's financial trajectory remains highly concentrated around a narrow set of operational and non-operational drivers, creating an exceptionally wide and negatively skewed earnings distribution over the near term.

The most critical finding is that non-operational customer payments provide a substantial near-term lift to AT&S's P&L, with Deutsche Bank attributing €280 million of EBITDA in FY2026/27 guidance directly to these client receipts, which represent contractually agreed contributions that do not reflect sustainable, recurring market demand and significantly inflate the company's near-term earnings per share before operations mature.

Deutsche Bank's modeling assumptions include another €200 million of client payments in FY2027/28, which positively impacts both the top and bottom lines for that fiscal year because the cash is recognized as high-margin revenue, temporarily delaying the projected year-over-year earnings drop-off. This masking effect represents the single most important factor separating AT&S's reported performance from its underlying operational reality.

Customer concentration emerges as the dominant structural driver of earnings volatility. AT&S relies heavily on a limited pool of technology leaders like Apple, AMD, and Intel, with the top five customers accounting for approximately 70% of total revenue in fiscal year 2021, creating significant top-line volatility where any shift in their product roadmaps or inventory levels represents a major risk to long-term earnings stability.

AT&S faced initial headwind delays at its Kulim plant as AMD decided to kick off the facility with its next-generation highperformance chips, pushing the high-volume manufacturing ramp out by one to two quarters, meaning that nearterm revenue recognition will lag behind initial investor expectations. The operational timeline for the Kulim K2 shell facility has been deferred because of customer roadmap changes, with Deutsche Bank adjusting its model to reflect K2 ramp beginning in early 2029 rather than late 2028, reflecting Intel's decision to delay its capacity commitments, which strains near-term earnings growth because it delays the expected sales contribution. - Earnings Calls Pricing dynamics under long-term agreements present a second concentrated driver with ambiguous outcomes. Deutsche Bank projects that ABF substrate pricing will rise 20% to 30% by year-end because of accelerating capacity absorption, with this rapid pricing acceleration being structurally stronger than previous PC-driven cycles and directly benefiting qualified vendors, accelerating microelectronics gross margins across the entire sector.

However, AT&S relies on strategic customer-financed capacity agreements to de-risk its capital expansions, with the firm's Chongqing facility expansion being fully customer-financed with approximately €80 million, although these agreements secure long-term volumes they often lag behind the dynamic industry-wide pricing hikes seen in open spot markets, potentially capping immediate operational pricing benefits unless contract terms permit quarterly renegotiations.

This structural lag means AT&S may capture substantially less near-term pricing upside than peers operating with shorter-term contracts. Facility ramp execution and depreciation dynamics constitute the third concentrated driver amplifying earnings uncertainty. Management confirmed that the certification of the Kulim plant triggered the commencement of noncash charges despite the fact that product qualification is still ongoing, creating immediate headwinds to operating profits before generating corresponding sales.

The guided capital spending program continues to run ahead of immediate operational cash flows, with net capital expenditure of €1.0 to 1.2 billion for the fiscal year, while first-quarter net capital spending was reported at only €34.8 million, indicating severe mismatch that suggests depreciation charges will rise sharply in subsequent fiscal years. AT&S faces a major balance sheet test as its €350 million hybrid bond reaches its first optional redemption date in January 2027, with the interest rate stepping up to approximately 15% from its current 3.25% cost if not redeemed, forcing management to replace the hybrid with standard debt and severely raising net interest expenses while compressing future earnings per share.
