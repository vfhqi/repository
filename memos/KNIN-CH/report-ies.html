Kuehne + Nagel International (KNIN-CH): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results leave our case where it was, because the case was written with them in hand; but the same evidence admits two readings the case does not hold, one against its cash hedge and one against its timing, and neither is yet strong enough to move a rating.

1. What changed

Kuehne + Nagel reported Q3 2026 on 22 October 2026.

  • Group recurring EBIT CHF 435 million against consensus of CHF 398 million, a 9% beat. Net turnover CHF 7,386 million (+22.2% year on year); gross profit CHF 2,388 million (+13.3%).
  • Full-year recurring EBIT guidance raised to CHF 1.5 to 1.6 billion from CHF 1.35 to 1.55 billion, the third raise of the year.
  • Volumes: Sea +4.5% to 1.16 million TEU (consensus +2.0%); Air +10% to 619,000 tons, with air yields +17%. DSV's sea and air volumes fell 3% in the same quarter.
  • Segments: Sea EBIT CHF 172 million (consensus 151), Air 169 (158), Contract Logistics 66 (57), Road 28 (29, a miss). Combined Sea and Air conversion 32% against the 35% target.
  • Costs: 2026 gross savings now above CHF 150 million (previously CHF 120 million); the CHF 100 to 150 million AI target for 2027 was confirmed as before. Headcount rose 9.0% to 92,542.
  • Cash: free cash flow CHF 336 million, helped by cash tax timing that partly reverses in Q4. Net working capital intensity 5.8%, outside the 4.5% to 5.5% corridor, with a core outflow of CHF 281 million against CHF 80 million a year earlier.
  • Sell-side: targets rose and ratings did not. Bernstein to CHF 220 (Market-Perform), Morgan Stanley to CHF 212 (Underweight), Jefferies unchanged at CHF 240 (Buy), Deutsche Bank at CHF 240 (Hold). Morgan Stanley puts the shares at a 17% premium to their through-cycle EV/EBIT.

Set against the case, none of this is a surprise. The case already carries the CHF 435 million quarter as the peak it expects not to be exceeded until about 2029, the three raises, the gross nature of the savings and the working capital breach. The shares closed at CHF 229.4 on 9 November 2026, between the CHF 227.10 at which the case's returns were struck and the CHF 236.7 it quoted as the price, so no threshold the case states has been crossed.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the working capital breach is commercial, not rate-driven, and the case's cash hedge is weaker than it says. The case treats working capital as a function of freight rates: rates fall, receivables shrink, and it has about CHF 260 million released in 2027, lifting free cash flow to about CHF 1.35 billion while EBIT falls. Management gives the same explanation for the breach. But the outflow more than tripled in a quarter in which the company was also winning large accounts, taking share from DSV and growing Sea volume at more than twice the expected rate. It is plausible that part of the outflow is the price of that share: longer terms for large shippers. The sell-side frames the cost at CHF 200 to 300 million of annual free cash flow if intensity stays above 5.5% through Q1 2027. That is about the size of the release the case counts on, so the two would cancel. On the case's own figures the dividend would still be covered, at roughly 1.2 times instead of about 1.5 times, so this is not a dividend risk. It is a risk to the claim that cash moves opposite to EBIT, and to the "strong cash conversion" headlines the case expects in 2027. For this to be right, intensity must stay above the corridor as rates fall. Payment terms are not disclosed, so the reading cannot be tested directly today.

Reading two: the turn is later and sharper than a plateau. The case has EBIT flat at about CHF 1.5 billion in both FY2027 and FY2028 and dates the first negative step to March 2027. The supply evidence carried with these results is more back-loaded than the case's inputs. It has fleet growth of 7% in 2027 and 13% in 2028, where the case uses 9.5% and 12.7%; deliveries of 3.2 million TEU in 2027 and 4.8 million in 2028, where the case uses about 3.1 million and 4.4 million. Schedule reliability was 49.9% in August 2026 with more than 4.3 million TEU tied up; full emissions-trading compliance arrives in 2027 and rewards slow steaming; one estimate puts effective capacity growth at only 3% to 5%. Suez transits are cited at 47% and 54% of 2023 levels, well short of the 75% trigger the case shares with the sell-side. If that holds, unit gross profit survives the 2027 contract season, FY2027 consensus proves about right, and the case's first test fails in a way that looks like disproof. Then 2028's larger wave lands on a cost base that has grown by 9% in headcount and a Contract Logistics depreciation line up CHF 31 million in one quarter. This is not the case's bull scenario, which carries strength into 2028; it is a good 2027 followed by a worse 2028 than the plateau, with the trough the case hopes to buy arriving later and lower. For it to be right, congestion and diversions must absorb the 2027 deliveries but not the 2028 ones.

Readings we considered and do not put up. That owned technology sustains a margin premium of 500 to 700 basis points through a glut fails on the same quarter's evidence: DSV's Air and Sea conversion was 46% against 32%, so there is no premium to sustain. That the beat was low quality because of the CHF 4 million Sea depreciation credit is a fair point about Sea alone: against the CHF 18 to 19 million quarterly expense earlier in the year, the swing is about the size of Sea's CHF 21 million beat. But the case treats it as a reclassification with no effect on group EBIT, and nothing reported contradicts that.

3. What it does to the case

  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (F, 42): the same. Volume, cost delivery and the raise are what the case assumed. Reading two presses on the timing inside the operating-environment topic (D, 34), not its direction.
  • Foundations' Robustness (C, 64): slightly more pressure. The rating leans on cash conversion of 90% to 100%; reading one is aimed at exactly that.
  • Forecast financials (D, 31): the same on level; more pressure on shape. The case holds FY2027 and FY2028 level; reading two says they differ.
  • Scope for upgrades (F, 24): slightly less pressure in the near term if reading two is right, since the case's 65% on a FY2027 range below consensus would be too high; more pressure further out.
  • Case Riskiness (C, 46) and the riskiness topic (D, 45): slightly more pressure. The case says the risk is entirely in earnings expectations and the multiple; reading one adds a cash dimension it does not count.
  • Total shareholder return (F, 20) and the fair multiple (F, 17): the same. From CHF 229.4 the case's 18-month return at its 22.93x guideline is about one point below the +5.1% it quotes from CHF 227.10.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (F, 18): the same today. Reading two would move the date of the 2028 trough-on-trough setup the case names, not its fit now.

The pinned letters stand as given: technical momentum B, sell-side earnings momentum A, valuation F.

4. What would settle it

  • The second earnings review, four working days on. It will carry the settled broker estimates and targets: whether FY2027 and FY2028 consensus moved up with the raise, and whether any house now models working capital or the 2028 supply wave. It will not carry any new company disclosure. It cannot settle either reading; it can only show whether the market's numbers have moved further from the case's.
  • Working capital intensity at the Q4 2026 results, which the case's calendar dates to 2 March 2027, and again at Q1 2027. Back inside 4.5% to 5.5% as rates ease: reading one fails. Still above 5.5% through Q1 2027: it stands. The Q4 free cash flow also shows the cash tax reversal.
  • The FY2027 guidance range on the same date, and Sea gross profit per TEU through Q2 2027 against the case's CHF 440 and CHF 400 lines. A range near consensus with unit gross profit above CHF 440 is consistent with both the case's bull scenario and reading two; only the FY2028 range, which the case expects in March 2028, separates them.
  • Not committed to by the company: payment terms on the new large accounts, the share of Air growth from technology customers, which management declined to quantify, and a net rather than gross AI figure. These are open questions that no scheduled disclosure answers.
