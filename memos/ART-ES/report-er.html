EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Arteche delivered a highly profitable performance in H1 2026, comfortably beating historical baselines and triggering a significant full-year guidance upgrade. The group posted net revenues of €277.9 million, achieving a 10.7% year-on-year top-line expansion compared to the €250.98 million reported in H1 2025. More importantly, absolute EBITDA rose by 29.2% year-on-year to €51.1 million, pushing the EBITDA margin up by 260 basis points to 18.4% of total turnover.

Net profit attributable to the parent company jumped by 44.3% to €30.6 million, translating into basic and diluted earnings per share of €0.54. This exceptional margin expansion represents the single most critical validation of management's structural profitability thesis, demonstrating that operating leverage and product mix optimization are driving sustainable earnings power well beyond historical norms.

The financial delivery prompted the group to upgrade its FY2026 EBITDA margin guidance to 16.3%-17.8%, representing a major step up from its initial 15.8%-16.3% forecast. This guidance increase confirms that the margin trajectory is structural rather than cyclical, reflecting embedded efficiency gains, favorable customer mix shifts toward high-voltage applications, and successful pricing discipline across the portfolio. Order intake grew by 15.7% during the first half of the year to reach €338 million, representing a robust 12.3% expansion at constant exchange rates. The velocity of order intake significantly outpaced revenue recognition, meaning backlog visibility provides highly predictable revenue conversion over the next 18 months.

Despite completing two major acquisitions during the period, the group's leverage ratio stood at a highly conservative 0.5x EBITDA, proving its continued balance sheet flexibility. On June 12, 2026, the company acquired 100% of Kempen-based SEG Electronics for an equity value of €50.6 million, a transaction that fully utilizes cash on hand and available credit lines to strengthen the group's grid automation portfolio in Central Europe.

Additionally, on March 18, 2026, the group acquired a 35% stake in Navarre-based Uptech Sensing, an investment that introduces distributed fiber optic sensors that reinforce the company's high-value digitization offering for transmission lines. These strategic acquisitions fundamentally reposition the company from a pure hardware manufacturer toward an integrated hardware-software grid automation provider capable of challenging Tier I conglomerates on complete digital substation solutions.

The corporate structure underwent a transformative shift during the period. On February 2, 2026, the company officially transitioned from BME Growth to the Main Market of the Spanish Stock Exchange via a direct listing, completed without the issuance of new shares, meaning that current family and institutional shareholders retained their existing holdings. The subsequent €100 million Accelerated Bookbuilding executed on June 25, 2026, was structured as a mixed transaction combining a primary capital increase and a secondary share sale, with approximately two-thirds consisting of existing shares sold by major shareholders, while one-third was new share issuance, successfully increasing the company's free float without altering its core control architecture.

This dual capital markets execution means the stock is now accessible to a much broader pool of international institutional capital, eliminating the structural liquidity discount that historically constrained the valuation multiple. a demand perspective, the investment case is underpinned by unprecedented utility capital commitments and accelerating smart grid modernization. Leading global grid operator Iberdrola successfully completed a €1,500 million senior green bond issue to fund electricity networks in several of its operating countries, confirming that utility-led infrastructure investment is accelerating to accommodate rising electrification demands.

More structurally, on July 28, 2026, the Spanish government authorized an additional €17.9 billion of power grid investment through 2030, allocating €10.2 billion to distribution networks and €7.7 billion to transmission over the next four years, creating an expansive procurement landscape that directly benefits domestic grid component suppliers like the group. Furthermore, in a historic policy shift on April 21, 2026, President Trump signed several Presidential Determinations under Section 303 of the Defense Production Act, formally designating grid infrastructure and its associated upstream supply chains as critical resources essential to national defense, authorizing the Department of Energy to utilize direct purchases and financial instruments to expand domestic manufacturing capacity.

These regulatory tailwinds provide multi-decade structural demand visibility that fundamentally de-risks the revenue trajectory.

The key changes to the investment thesis center on accelerating margin expansion, successful inorganic technology integration, and massive regulatory demand catalysts. The company has transitioned from a regional medium-voltage instrument transformer supplier into a vertically integrated grid automation technology group with leading positions in optical sensing, protection software, and energy storage. The combination of SEG Electronics, Uptech Sensing, and the Teraloop flywheel alliance creates a unique value proposition that allows the company to offer complete digital substation architectures, directly challenging legacy Tier I players on both technical capability and delivery speed.

However, the rapid integration of three disparate technology acquisitions introduces structural complexity, and managing a highly decentralized corporate footprint spanning Spain, Germany, China, and Brazil could overextend the core executive team, meaning that if the group fails to rapidly capture cross-selling synergies between its software automation suite and instrument transformers, the newly expanded capital base could experience a near-term return on capital dilution.

The primary counter-hypothesis to the bullish case is that aggressive capacity expansion by Tier I conglomerates could compress pricing power and market share over the next 24 months. Siemens Energy is aggressively expanding its global manufacturing footprint by 50% to address severe grid bottlenecks, targeting equipment lines that represent approximately 45% of its Grid Technologies division's total annual revenues.

Similarly, Hitachi Energy is deploying USD 180 million to construct a greenfield transformer facility in Finland, aiming to double its global transformer production capacity, allowing the conglomerate to lock in multi-year utility orders ahead of smaller European peers. If these giants successfully execute their capacity ramps while maintaining competitive pricing, Arteche's core pricing power could erode rapidly, particularly in commoditized medium-voltage segments where scale economies dominate.

The most important "what would change my mind" indicator is sequential quarterly EBITDA margin performance through H2 2026 and into early 2027. If the group's EBITDA margin compresses below 16.5% on a quarterly basis despite continued revenue growth, it would signal that the H1 2026 margin spike was driven by temporary favorable project mix rather than sustainable structural efficiency gains. Conversely, if the company maintains or expands margins above 17.0% while simultaneously integrating SEG Electronics and Uptech Sensing, it would confirm that the technology-led product pivot is successfully driving a permanent step-change in profitability.

Additionally, monitoring order intake growth in the Transmission and Distribution Automation segment is critical; sustained double-digit growth rates would validate that the software-hardware integration strategy is winning premium utility contracts, whereas flat or declining automation orders would indicate that legacy hardware competitors are successfully defending their installed base. The direct subsequent action for investors is to closely monitor the Strategic Plan 2030 presentation scheduled for October or November 2026, which will formalize mid-term financial targets and integration roadmaps for the recent acquisitions.

This disclosure will provide critical visibility into management's ambition for automation segment revenue mix, expected synergy realization timelines, and capital allocation priorities between organic capacity expansion and further inorganic growth. Furthermore, tracking regional revenue mix shifts over the next two quarters will reveal whether the group is successfully pivoting toward higher-margin EMEA and APAC markets while managing exposure to more volatile Latin American utility cycles. The investment case now hinges on execution certainty across a highly complex multi-technology integration program, making operational delivery metrics more important than top-line growth velocity.
