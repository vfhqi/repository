Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro) 40 findings

Section summary
  1. global beauty demand is rotating toward science-backed efficacy and premiumization, while mass market channels face persistent volume headwinds
  2. multinational incumbents face accelerating market share fragmentation as agile local Chinese and Korean competitors capture regional consumer cohorts
  3. generative AI and digital marketplaces are rewriting the rules of consumer engagement, although they threaten to dilute traditional luxury brand equity
  4. evolving ingredient safety and environmental mandates are escalating compliance costs and forcing expensive reformulations across global fragrance portfolios
  5. specialised fragrance and luxury supply chains remain concentrated and highly vulnerable to rising geopolitical bottlenecks and freight volatility
  6. the global beauty market is entering a moderating growth phase as the post-COVID demand surge fully normalizes
  7. persistent macroeconomic headwinds, including China's slow credit expansion and US tariffs, threaten near-term beauty sector operating margins

Demand

1.
Accelerating premiumization: global prestige beauty grew 7% in H1 2026, driven by clinical skincare and premium fragrance outperformance
2.
Subdued mass demand: mass market volumes remain under pressure as consumers become selective and prioritize proven efficacy over viral marketing
3.
Skincare normalization: the post-COVID skincare boom has stabilized, with consumer interest transitioning to targeted clinical regimens
4.
Category substitution: historical data shows clear substitution effects where beauty spending rotates between makeup and skincare during cycle inflections
5.
Selective retail growth: specialty beauty brick-and-mortar stores are capturing incremental foot traffic from declining department store formats

Competition

6.
Local Chinese dominance: domestic Chinese beauty brands now control over 57% of their home market, up from 50% in 2022
7.
Chinese brand scaling: local competitor Chicmax targets RMB 30 billion in revenue by 2030, driven by its flagship brand Kans
8.
Korean export surge: K-beauty exports rose nearly 40% in the US and over 90% in Europe, driving intense global competition
9.
Premium market entry: local competitor Proya partnered with Ulta Beauty in August 2026 to accelerate its commercial expansion into North America
10.
Diminishing brand loyalty: younger Gen Z consumers are product-loyal rather than brand-loyal, accelerating the rise of affordable mass-market dupes
11.
Global market fragmentation: low barriers to digital entry allow thousands of indie brands to launch, eroding legacy multinational market shares

Disruption

12.
Accelerated content generation: Gen AI tools enable automated mass production of marketing assets, lowering content creation costs for large portfolios
13.
Predictive formula acceleration: the integration of AI modeling in research has doubled the speed of identifying and testing new active molecules
14.
Digital channel expansion: e-commerce platforms have expanded rapidly, now representing 31% of L'Oreal's total sales in H1 2026
15.
Search engine evolution: if search algorithms shift from SEO to generative engine optimization, large brands must re-engineer their digital assets
16.
Amazon marketplace pressure: digital marketplaces facilitate small-peer growth, neutralizing some of the historical shelf-space advantages of multinational incumbents
17.
Social commerce risk: the rapid rise of platforms like Douyin in China has compressed product life cycles and increased marketing volatility

Regulation

18.
Ingredient ban pressures: upcoming European Union regulations and parallel US efforts require beauty brands to reformulate core lines
19.
Sourcing transparency mandates: tightening global compliance rules around raw materials force brands to implement tracking systems across agricultural inputs
20.
Packaging recycling requirements: new environmental legislation targeting single-use plastics increases operational complexity and capital intensity for mass-market brands
21.
Fragrance allergen rules: stricter labelling and safety laws for fragrance allergens threaten to compress margins across prestige portfolios
22.
Local protectionist barriers: rising regulatory protectionism in North Asia creates administrative hurdles that delay international product launches

Supply

23.
European manufacturing concentration: prestigious fragrance and luxury skincare production remains highly centralized in Western Europe, exposing portfolios to global trade disruptions
24.
Fragrance sourcing specialized: the specialized nature of fragrance raw materials makes rapid supplier diversification nearly impossible during crises
25.
Packaging bottle delays: international trade friction has caused significant shipment delays for premium glass bottles, gift-with-purchase items, and samples
26.
Freight cost volatility: ongoing geopolitical tensions have driven unpredictable shipping rates, creating margin headwinds that cannot be quickly offset
27.
Inventory management risks: supply chain disruptions have forced retailers to adopt more conservative forecasting models, leading to localized out-of-stock issues

Cycle

28.
Slower sector growth: global beauty market growth is normalizing to pre-pandemic rates of 4% to 5%, down from double-digit highs
29.
Promotional intensity rising: retailers are elevating promotional activity and discounting to capture highly selective consumers in a late-cycle environment
30.
Skincare cycle normalization: the premium skincare cycle is decelerating in mature regions, forcing a heavier reliance on volume-driven emerging markets
31.
Destocking headwinds persist: the gap between cosmetics retail sales and beauty import volumes indicates persistent inventory normalization pressures
32.
Makeup demand recovery: the color cosmetics segment is exhibiting a delayed cyclical recovery, led by prestige formats in Western Europe

Macro

Credit impulse warning: North Asia organic growth and beauty imports show a high historical correlation with China's decelerating credit impulses

36.
US tariff headwinds: US import tariffs of 15% on European beauty imports present an estimated 40 basis
