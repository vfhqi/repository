Section 3 – Cash Flow Classification of Interest, Leases, and Free Cash Flow

Section summary
  1. Siemens Healthineers excludes interest paid and lease repayments from its free cash flow, rendering reported cash flows pre-financing and pre-lease.
  2. This classification differs from peer Koninklijke Philips, which includes interest paid in operating cash, resulting in a less conservative reported metric.

Siemens Healthineers Cash Flow Definitions

1.
Standard cash generation: Siemens Healthineers defines its free cash flow as operating cash flow less additions to property, plant, and equipment, reaching 2,446 million euros in fiscal year 2026. This section analyzed 12 relevant sources covering 3 companies, with none excluded. By subtracting capital expenditures of 842 million euros from operating cash of 3,287 million euros, the group isolates operations to calculate reported free cash flow figures.
2.
Financial cash treatment: Siemens Healthineers classifies interest paid within financing cash flows rather than operating cash flows, keeping interest expenses from reducing its reported cash flow metrics. The company reported interest paid of 40 million euros and interest paid to the Siemens Group of 373 million euros in its annual report published on November 22, 2026. Because these substantial financing costs are omitted from operating activities, they are not deducted from the reported cash metric, allowing the firm to present a highly favorable free cash flow.
3.
Excluded lease obligations: Siemens Healthineers excludes lease repayments from its official free cash flow definition by categorizing them under financing activities. According to its annual report from November 22, 2026, lease liability repayments totaled 190 million euros, while total cash outflows from leases reached 284 million euros. Since these principal repayments are not deducted, the company reports free cash flow before lease payments, meaning that the true cash available for discretionary debt reduction is lower than the reported figure.
4.
Contrary evidence: The company's reported free cash flow of 2,446 million euros in fiscal year 2026 remains highly sensitive to heavy lease and interest cash outflows that are hidden from the primary operating metric. The Siemens Healthineers annual report on November 5, 2026 shows that adjusting for the 190 million euros of lease repayments and 413 million euros of total interest payments would reduce discretionary cash flows to 1,843 million euros. This significant gap demonstrates that the company's true liquidity is narrower than the headline metric.

Peer Cash Flow Comparisons

5.
Peer classification divergence: Koninklijke Philips NV classifies its interest paid within operating cash flows, which differs significantly from the financing cash flow classification used by Siemens Healthineers. According to the Philips annual report published on February 19, 2026, interest payments are deducted from operating activities, while principal lease repayments are categorized as financing activities. This operating interest deduction means that Philips' reported free cash flow of 512 million euros in 2025 incorporates its borrowing costs, creating a more conservative cash flow representation.
6.
Simplified cash measures: Solventum Corporation utilizes a standard US GAAP free cash flow definition that aligns closely with the pre-lease metric of Siemens Healthineers. As reported in Solventum's press release on November 5, 2026, free cash flow is defined as operating cash flow less capital expenditures on property, plant, and equipment. This simplified measure does not deduct lease repayments, which helps Solventum focus on organic capital allocation while pursuing aggressive debt paydown programs.
7.
Sector comparison overview: Siemens Healthineers exhibits a stronger cash conversion rate than peers because its cash flow definitions exclude both lease repayments and interest paid. During the research of this section, 12 relevant sources covering 3 peer companies were analyzed to understand industry classification standards. While most medical technology peers exclude principal lease repayments from free cash flow, Siemens Healthineers' choice to also exclude intercompany interest payments allows it to report a higher relative cash conversion.
8.
Other side of the story: Siemens Healthineers might face valuation pressure if investors adjust its cash metrics to align with more conservative industry standards. If brokers and analysts deduct the 413 million euros in total interest expenses and 190 million euros in lease repayments, the company's adjusted cash flow would drop by approximately 25%. This downward adjustment would reveal that the group's real capital generation capacity is more constrained, potentially delaying the anticipated strategic share buyback programs.
