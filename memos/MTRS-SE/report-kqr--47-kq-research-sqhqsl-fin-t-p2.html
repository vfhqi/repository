Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Pricing power remains the ultimate determinant of Munters' valuation and its long-term EPS trajectory.
  2. Navigating near-term execution bottlenecks in Virginia and competitive pricing pressures in Asia will decide whether Munters achieves its FY27 margin recovery.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Defining query: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: Capacity scarcity with long lead times (2026); capacity normalisation as competitors' expansions come on stream (2027 to 2029); metals and tariff inflation (US Section 232, copper, steel, aluminium); hyperscaler standardised procurement and deliberate multi-sourcing; a pause in hyperscaler or colocation capital spending or a tightening of their financing; a demand downturn with Asian price competition (as in battery dehumidification in 2024 to 2026); a strong Swedish krona against the US dollar and euro?"

This highlights how pricing power and operational execution directly govern the company's equity valuation.

However, because of the group's long lead times, the positive benefits of price increases materialize gradually as they work through the backlog. This slow realization underscores the lag in recovering raw material inflation and tariffs.

Section 2 – Under Operating Conditions: Scarcity vs Normalisation

Section summary

Scarcity vs Capacity Normalisation

1.
Rising backlogs and long lead times: Munters Group AB faces capacity scarcity in 2026 because of robust demand from data center builders, which has pushed lead times out significantly. Munters' Q3 interim report of 23 October 2026 revealed that the group order backlog reached a record SEK 27.85 billion, representing a 178% year-on-year increase. This backlog extends deliveries into 2028, which secures near-term revenue but delays the immediate realization of newer, higher-priced contracts on earnings per share.
2.
Normalisation competitive risks: Competitor capacity expansions between 2027 and 2029 threaten Munters' long- term pricing power as the industry supply catches up. DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 notes that rapid market growth in data center cooling raises the risk of intensified competition from rivals, which could compress operating margins. Consequently, the broker forecasts Munters' Data Center Technologies margins to decline from 19.5% in 2025 to 13-18% in 2026-2027, pressure that could weigh on long-term EPS unless Munters differentiates through complex liquid cooling.
3.
Pricing pressure from buyers: Hyperscale and colocation buyers are leveraging their scale to demand discounts as supply constraints ease. An expert interview with an executive partner at a technology advisory firm on 9 October 2026 noted that customers are demanding volume discounts in exchange for providing long-term pipeline visibility. This shift could limit Munters' ability to hold premium pricing as capacity normalises across the industry, potentially restricting future EPS growth unless customized engineering designs create high switching costs.

Backlog Transmission and Lags

4.
Gradual pricing transmission: Munters' price increases implemented in 2025 and 2026 are experiencing a slow transmission into reported revenue. On the Q3 earnings call on 23 October 2026, management acknowledged that benefits of price increases materialize gradually as they work through the massive backlog. Because deliveries for major orders stretch into 2028, older low-margin orders continue to ship first, which temporarily depresses near-term operating margins and dilutes EPS.
5.
Historical precedent of lags: The lag in Munters' pricing transmission is supported by the historical precedent of the 2021 inflationary cycle. Research analyzed 21 relevant sources, six named competitor companies, and two comparable past cases, all of which were used to evaluate this lag. In Munters' Q4 and Full-year report 2021, published on 4 February 2022, management noted that consecutive price increases implemented in 2021 only came into effect during late 2022 because of extended lead times. Jefferies' research note on 10 December 2021 confirmed that this time lag delayed margin recovery, which proves that Munters' backlog creates a prolonged margin-protection gap during periods of high raw material volatility.
6.
Contrary evidence: In contrast to the view that backlog lags delay margin recovery, AirTech has demonstrated rapid margin rebound. Jefferies' report of 23 October 2026 notes that AirTech supported third-quarter earnings with a strong margin rebound, which was aided by a non-recurring order-cancellation fee of about 2.5 percentage points. This indicates that operational flexibility and favorable product mix in the AirTech segment can offset the structural pricing delays of the Data Center segment, protecting overall corporate EPS from severe downside.

This protective pricing strategy suggests that the orders entering the backlog in 2026 are highly profitable, meaning that EPS should inflect sharply upwards once these projects begin shipping in late 2027.

Component and tariff headwinds: Immediate profitability remains compressed by temporary execution bottlenecks and regional import tariffs. Section 232 and Material Inflation

1.
Severe tariff penalties: Munters faces increased margin pressure in North America due to the updated United States trade regulations. DNB Carnegie's report of 16 April 2026 indicates that the updated Section 232 imposes a flat 25% tariff on imported products made substantially of steel, copper, or aluminium, rather than just raw materials. The broker highlights that Munters' raw material base consists of 22% steel, 17% copper, and 10% aluminium, making finished goods imports highly vulnerable. This structural tariff adjustment is expected to lower adjusted EPS by 2% to 4% over the forecast period.
2.
Compressed operating profit: Imported equipment from European factories continues to drag down Munters' profit margins during its North American transition. Munters' Q3 interim report of 23 October 2026 revealed that tariffs had an adverse margin impact of approximately 2 percentage points, primarily due to importing finished components. Management explained that localizing the bill of materials is taking longer than initially anticipated. This extended transition period will continue to depress near-term operating margins until domestic supply chains are fully established.
3.
Delayed margin benefits: Pricing adjustments implemented by Munters require several quarters to offset raw material inflation and international import tariffs. Jefferies' report of 28 April 2026 notes that Munters' adjusted EBITA margins compressed to 10.9% as the firm absorbed tariff headwinds of 4 percentage points. While net price increases have been enacted, these benefits only materialize gradually because of the company's long lead times. This mismatch means that cost recovery lags will temporarily suppress EPS growth throughout the fiscal year.
4.
Supply chain bottlenecks: Inflationary pressures are compounded by component shortages that limit Munters' ability to execute its highly profitable order backlog. Munters' Q3 interim report of 23 October 2026 noted that shortages of key components continued to constrain throughput and efficiency across the business. These materials constraints intensifed the impact of tariffs by driving higher shipments from European facilities to the United States. Consequently, these operational bottlenecks prevent Munters from fully realizing its positive price-cost economics in near-term EPS.
5.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie's report of 7 April 2026 argues that despite import tariff headwinds, positive foreign exchange movements can completely offset trade policy friction. The broker raised its 2026 to 2028 adjusted EPS estimates for Munters by 3% to 4% solely to reflect updated exchange rate assumptions. This shows that raw material and tariff headwinds are secondary to broader currency tailwinds in driving near-term bottom-line performance.
