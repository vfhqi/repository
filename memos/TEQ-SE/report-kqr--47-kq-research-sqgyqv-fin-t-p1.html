Deep-Dive Entity Profiles Teqnion AB – Operating Leverage, Cost Structures, and Portfolio Restructuring Teqnion's decentralized model leaves subsidiary operations highly autonomous, shielding the parent company from central cost inflation. In Q3 2026, central group functions cost -9.5 MSEK, highlighting a lean, nonbureaucratic headquarters. Historically, Teqnion has maintained positive annual cash flows since the GFC, though the 2024 and 2025 period saw considerable earnings compression. The company typically sheds about 25% of its profit in a moderate downturn (as in 2024) and lost nearly 40% of its profit during the more severe 2016 cycle. During the subsequent 2026 recovery, margins rebounded strongly: the group EBITA margin reached 14.1% in Q2 2026 (up from 11.3% in Q2 2025) and stabilized at 14.9% in Q3 2026. - Earnings Calls 38 To eliminate underperforming units, Teqnion recognized a 73 MSEK goodwill impairment in Q3 2025, specifically targeting its struggling housebuilder Hem1 and contract caterer Reward Catering. Reward Catering has since been provisionally liquidated in the Irish High Court following certification concerns and earn-out audit irregularities. Turnaround adjustments at Hem1 and the liquidation of Reward Catering are expected to yield 18 to 24 MSEK in annual earnings/cash flow improvements by 2026. Simultaneously, Teqnion established a central sourcing office in China. By Q2 2026, the office had completed nearly 100 projects across the group, delivering average cost savings of 40% (equating to MSEK 11 annualized), fully hitting its MSEK 10-15 annual target. Teqnion’s 2026 reorganization split the company into two regional segments: Teqnion Nord (Nordics, managed from Solna): Generated Q3 2026 sales of 327.7 MSEK and an EBITA of 46.1 MSEK (14.1% margin). Teqnion Väst (UK & Ireland, managed from Birmingham): Generated Q3 2026 sales of 164.5 MSEK and an EBITA of 36.6 MSEK (22.2% margin). Väst's margins are structurally higher but were aided by nonrecurring positive items in early 2025; they are expected to normalize in the 20% to 25% range over time. Lifco AB – Decentralized Benchmarking and Cyclical Margin Resilience Lifco represents the high-performance benchmark for decentralized compounders, achieving an EBITA CAGR of approximately 20% over the 5, 10, and 19 years leading to 2025. In fiscal year 2025, Lifco's consolidated operating cost structure was dominated by variable materials and goods for resale (11,885 MSEK), personnel costs (5,678 MSEK), other overheads (3,658 MSEK), and operating leases (100 MSEK). At the corporate level, central Group service fees charged to subsidiaries were kept to a modest 89 MSEK. However, its long-term EBITA margin expanded from 14.2% in 2014 to 19.6% in 2020, 21.6% in 2022, 23.2% in 2023, and 22.8% on an LTM basis in Q3 2026. Beijer Alma AB – Industrial Tail-Cutting and Beijer Components Restructuring Beijer Alma's cost structure balances variable raw materials with semi-fixed personnel costs. In its historical baseline, raw materials accounted for SEK 1,311m and personnel expenses stood at SEK 851m.

Because over 70% of sales are generated outside Sweden while most manufacturing is based domestically, Beijer Alma is structurally exposed to transaction currency fluctuations. During the 2008–2009 crisis, Beijer Alma executed a defensive 15-month cost-cutting campaign, preserving an operating margin of 15.2% despite a 14% drop in sales. The group similarly navigated the 2020 COVID downturn and the 2022–2023 slowdown (where margins dipped to 12.5%).

During 2024–2026, the company focused heavily on operational recovery, lifting adjusted EBITA margins from 12.8% in 2024 to 15% in 2025, and hitting 16.9% by Q3 2026.

A key driver of this margin expansion was the restructuring of Beijer Components (formerly Lesjöfors). Following a factory fire in Finland, Beijer Alma launched a SEK 120m action plan in June 2025, which successfully lifted Beijer Components' adjusted EBITA margin from 15.9% in 2024 to 17.7% in 2025.

In early 2026, Beijer Components discontinued SEK 50m of low-margin business lines and scaled back unprofitable US and Turkish operations. This was followed in Q3 2026 by a decision to close two European spring facilities (one Alcomex factory and a standard distribution unit), incurring SEK 33m in restructuring charges (SEK 21m cash impact). This broader cost-out program outperformed internal targets, driving Beijer Components' LTM EBITA margin to 18.9% as of Q2 2026 and positioning it to exceed its long-term 20% margin target as volumes return.
