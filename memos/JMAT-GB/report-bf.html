EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Johnson Matthey is transitioning into a streamlined, cash-focused business centered on circular PGMs and emissions control, having divested Catalyst Technologies to Honeywell. CEO Liam Condon launched a strategic refresh to create a highly focused, lean and cash-generative business, simplifying what was historically a complex conglomerate into a three-segment operator anchored by Clean Air autocatalysts, PGM Services refining and recycling, and nascent Hydrogen Technologies. The group occupies highly advantaged positions within a disciplined oligopoly structure, though it faces structural headwinds from the long-term decline of internal combustion engines and severe near-term setbacks in hydrogen commercialization.

Business Model and Strategic Positioning

Core Clean Air segment provides catalysts for emission control after-treatment systems in light and heavy-duty internal combustion engine vehicles, delivering sales excluding precious metals of £2,123 million in fiscal year 2026, down 8% year-on-year. Clean Air underlying operating profit reached £307 million in the same period, with margins expanding 270 basis points to 14.5%. Integrated PGM Services provides precious metal refining, recycling, and fabrication, serving as the foundational ecosystem for the entire group.

The group agreed to sell its high-growth Catalyst Technologies segment to Honeywell International for £1.325 billion, with this segment experiencing weak performance as fiscal year 2026 underlying operating profit plummeted 51% to £44 million. Operational separation is complete, with the transaction expected to finalize by the end of August 2026, as Clean

Air operates on point-in-time product sales of bespoke catalysts to global automotive original equipment manufacturers. Pricing incorporates a pass-through of precious metal costs, meaning the group is insulated from direct commodity price volatility. Revenue model resilience is secured by metal pass-through clauses and multi-year OEM contracts, though the group remains structurally exposed to terminal ICE decline.

Financial Profile and Peer Benchmarking

Underlying operating profit increased 14% to £340 million in fiscal year 2026, up from £299 million in fiscal year 2025, with efficiencies driving a 270 basis point margin expansion to 14.5% in the dominant Clean Air division. Reported operating profit fell 65% to £161 million, down from £454 million in the prior fiscal year, weighed down by £192 million of major impairment and restructuring charges in fiscal year 2026, with the group recording a reported loss for the year of £91 million.

Average working capital days excluding precious metals increased to 62 days in fiscal year 2026, up from 52 days, while total inventories decreased to £865 million and trade and other payables increased to £2,154 million, providing a valuable cash cushion of £135 million in net inflows. The group implemented aggressive targets to raise cash conversion from 20-30% in FY24/25 to over 80% by FY26/27, with free cash flow rising 163% to £168 million in fiscal year 2026, representing still-modest cash conversion of approximately 49%.

Group return on capital employed increased to 15.5% in fiscal year 2026, up from 12.0% in fiscal year 2025, though ROCE remains capped by dual-running refinery costs and a temporarily elevated capital employ base of £2,200 million, with the group targeting a return on capital employed of 20% over the mediumterm. Underlying profitability shows robust operational progress, but reported earnings remain depressed by heavy non-cash charges, while cash conversion remains below ambitious targets due to elevated working capital and refinery capex overruns.

Value Chain Power Dynamics and Competitive Position

The automotive catalyst market is structured as a disciplined oligopoly including Johnson Matthey, BASF, and Umicore, with the highly consolidated structure enabling robust pricing discipline and value-based contract structures among the three main players. Recycling serves as a vital PGM source, contributing 16% of platinum, 27% of palladium, and 30% of rhodium, with Sibanye-Stillwater controlling a leading 22% market share in North America and 8% of global PGM recycling volumes.

The midstream refining stage captures superior economics, where precious metal services command gross margins between 35% and 45%, with Johnson Matthey refining roughly 4 million ounces of PGMs annually with a market value of approximately £6 billion, and the group refining 80% of its required metals internally. Johnson Matthey is the largest recycler of secondary platinum group metals in the world, commanding a dominant 40% global market share in secondary PGM recycling, maintaining more than double the capacity of its nearest recycling competitor.

Upstream miner Sibanye-Stillwater reports that global autocat recycling volumes remain heavily subdued due to elevated vehicle prices extending road lifetimes, with persistent collection headwinds as low price incentives fail to motivate yard collectors. Johnson Matthey occupies the most profit-favoured midstream refining and catalyst fabrication stages, securing oligopolistic pricing power and circular supply security, though secondary feedstock constraints and downstream OEM leverage present ongoing challenges.

Operator Quality and Foundation Strength

CEO Liam Condon has transformed Johnson Matthey's culture since joining in March 2022, shifting focus from capital-heavy growth to cash generation, achieving a simplified corporate structure by divesting non-core, lowerreturn businesses to unlock substantial shareholder value, and doubling down on core chemistry and refining where the group maintains clear global leadership. In January 2026, the Group Leadership Team was streamlined from nine to six members to improve decision-making speed, with Alastair Judge succeeding as Chief Financial Officer to oversee the group's new cash steering model, Richard Pike becoming Chief Operating Officer to drive process and operational efficiencies, and this restructuring program successfully delivering a 15% corporate headcount reduction in support functions.

Executive remuneration has been redesigned to align with the board's strategic focus on sustainable cash generation, with 80% of the 2026/27 annual executive bonus directly linked to underlying profit and free cash flow, and long-term performance share plans tied directly to underlying EPS growth and total shareholder return versus peers. Johnson Matthey operates as the largest recycler of secondary platinum group metals in the world, commanding more than double the capacity of its nearest recycling competitor, sourcing 80% of its precious metals internally, with the recycling moat protected by high technological barriers in separating chemically similar metals to 99.95% purity.

Autocatalyst formulations are deeply integrated into the customer's specific engine and exhaust architectures, securing long-term customer lock-in, with technical qualification processes taking three to five years making mid-lifecycle catalyst substitution prohibitively expensive for OEMs, and the group's emissions control revenues secured under long-term contracts spanning five to seven years. Management has established conservative financial targets and aligned executive incentives with cash generation, while core refining and catalyst businesses possess durable competitive moats, though near-term returns remain constrained by elevated refinery capex and mechanical improvement depends on heroic execution rather than structural tailwinds.

Structural Risks and Long-Term Disruption

The transition away from internal combustion engines is moving slower than historical targets, expanding the Clean Air runway, with hybrid platforms growing fast as evidenced by nine European platform wins representing 25% of the 2028 market, and Deutsche Bank research highlighting that a delayed EV transition provides a much longer cash generation runway. Hydrogen market growth has deteriorated sharply due to delayed regulatory incentives and high production costs, with slower medium-term growth expectations leading the group to book a £54 million impairment on Hydrogen Technologies, and in response the group is refocusing hydrogen investments on near-term opportunities in the Chinese market.

The group's simplified business model remains structurally exposed to a terminal decline in automotive internal combustion engine platforms, with structural margin risk as accelerated battery electric vehicle adoption could compress Clean Air operating margins rapidly, and ongoing footprint consolidation highly dependent on continuous restructuring charges that could consume much of the cash. The group's simplified business model remains structurally exposed to a long-term terminal decline in internal combustion engines, with autocatalyst manufacturing capacity utilization collapsing and destroying group cash flows if global battery electric vehicle adoption accelerates unexpectedly, and further fit-out delays at the new UK refinery prolonging dual-running costs, locking up working capital and capping ROCE improvement. Extended ICE longevity and data-centre stationary emissions growth provide intermediate cash runway, but stagnant hydrogen commercialization represents a major strategic setback, and the core business faces existential risk from faster-than-expected EV adoption that could collapse autocatalyst volumes before alternative engines reach commercial scale.
