EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Chief Executive and Delivery Record

1.
Bernd Montag: The chief executive shows relentless drive and strategic clarity, having led the company since its 2018 IPO and successfully integrated the Varian acquisition.
2.
Exceptional qualities: Montag demonstrates exceptionally high standards in Imaging, but the persistent structural decline in the Diagnostics segment challenges his record of adaptable execution.
3.
Delivery record: Management has consistently met or exceeded guidance in the synergetic core, although Diagnostics headwinds forced multiple revenue guidance downgrades throughout fiscal year 2026.

Governance and Credibility

4.
Governance robustness: The company is transitioning to a standalone setup by refreshing the Supervisory Board with independent experts to replace departing Siemens AG representatives.
5.
Share dealing: Insider activity signals neutral conviction with routine selling, as recent transactions mainly involve the automatic disposal of shares to cover tax obligations.
6.
Expert views: Industry experts confirm Siemens' technological leadership in CT and MRI, while questioning the feasibility of achieving mid-teens margins in the standalone Diagnostics business.
7.
Credibility risk: The ambition of mid-teens Diagnostics margins is the most likely forecast to prove wrong because of structural pricing pressure in China.

Section 1 – Chief Executive's Career and Background

Section summary
  1. Bernd Montag is a Siemens lifer whose career has developed exclusively within the imaging and healthcare technology sectors of a single conglomerate.

Career Path and Education

1.
Long-term tenure: Bernd Montag has spent his entire career within the Siemens organization, having joined the group in 1995 after completing his studies in physics at the University of Erlangen-Nuremberg.
2.
Functional development: The chief executive's experience developed through quality and sales roles in the hearing aid business before he moved to the imaging systems unit in 1999 to serve as a product manager.
3.
Division leadership: Montag assumed overall responsibility for computed tomography in 2004 and was named President of the imaging and therapy division in 2008, managing diagnostic imaging and image-guided therapies.
4.
Appointment circumstances: The supervisory board appointed Montag as chief executive of Siemens Healthineers in February 2015, three years before he led the company's initial public offering in March 2018.
5.
Other side of the story: Job titles suggest business leadership early in Montag's career, but the centralized structure of the pre-2018 Siemens Group may have limited his direct responsibility for independent profit and loss before the carve-out.

Section 4 – Chief Executive's Leadership Style

Section summary
  1. Bernd Montag employs a polished yet direct communication style and shows an appetite for bold, portfolio-shaping decisions during periods of industry transition.

Decision Making and Communication

1.
Decision speed: Bernd Montag demonstrates bold decision making by initiating the Diagnostics carve-out and rebranding the company simply as Healthineers to establish a distinct identity from Siemens AG.
2.
Risk appetite: The chief executive shows high risk appetite by exiting the commoditized general ultrasound market to refocus resources on higher-margin procedural ultrasound within the Precision Therapy segment.
3.
Owner mindset: Management thinks like an owner by launching the Transform4Future productivity program, which targets over EUR 300 mm in savings to offset stranded costs from the upcoming corporate separation.
4.
Guidance accuracy: The company consistently sets prudent guidance, as shown by fiscal 2026 adjusted earnings per share of EUR 2.42 landing in the upper half of the initial EUR 2.20 to EUR 2.40 range.
5.
Direct quote: Bernd Montag stated that Healthineers is who we are, engineers and pioneers in healthcare, during the Q4 2026 call to explain the rationale for the corporate rebranding.
6.
Direct quote: The chief executive noted that synergies are limited between the synergetic core of Imaging and the Diagnostics division, justifying the decision to prepare the latter for a standalone future.
7.
Direct quote: Montag emphasized that shaping the future means more than providing clarity, it requires positioning the company for the next phase with a different shareholder structure.

Section 9 – Governance Structure and Warning Signs

Voting Rights and Ownership

Founder control: Siemens AG holds a majority stake in the company, which stood at approximately 73% in mid-2025, although this has decreased from 85% at the time of the initial public offering.
2.
Takeover defenses: Provisions in the articles of association may delay or prevent a merger or change in control, even when terms are favorable, by making it more difficult to replace the board.
3.

5.
Ethics reporting: Systems actively prevent failure through compliance programs that include educating employees and performing risk-based due diligence on the supplier and customer base.

Section 16 – Management Quality: Evidence For and Against

Section summary
  1. Bernd Montag shows strong drive and capital discipline in the core business, but his adaptability is tested by the structural decline of the diagnostics market.
  2. The strongest case against management involves the deteriorating quality of earnings and the risk that Diagnostics margins will fail to reach mid-term targets.
  3. The evidence favors the chief executive's ability to deliver the core plan, although the transition to a standalone entity will likely delay earnings per share growth.

Qualities of the Chief Executive

1.
Relentless drive: Bernd Montag shows strong relentless drive by consistently expanding the company's innovation leadership and maintaining a leading market share in high-growth Imaging segments.
2.
Capital allocation: Management demonstrates disciplined capital allocation by prioritizing de-leveraging to 2.5x net debt to EBITDA before considering strategic share buybacks for cancellation.
3.
Exceptional standards: The company shows exceptionally high standards in its synergetic core, with Imaging achieving 8.10% growth and 21.90% margins that outpace major peers.
4.
Adaptability: Montag demonstrates partial adaptability as he took several years to move toward a formal separation of the Diagnostics business despite its persistent underperformance.

The Case Against and Gaps

5.
Fragile quality: The adaptability of the chief executive is most fragile because the long-awaited Diagnostics carve-out introduces significant execution risk and substantial near-term stranded costs.
6.
Words against reality: Management re-confirmed double-digit EPS growth ambitions while at the same time guiding for flat 2027 earnings due to a series of clearly defined headwinds.
7.
Governance support: The refresh of the supervisory board supports good behavior by adding independent clinical and financial experts to oversee the company's increasingly independent path.

Verdict on Delivery

8.
Evidence for: Bernd Montag's strong track record of execution in Imaging and the successful integration of Varian provide confidence that he can deliver the core business strategy.
9.
Evidence against: The deterioration of earnings quality and the significant increase in adjusted items for fiscal 2027 challenge the management's story of underlying operational strength.
10.
Other side of the story: Strategic logic of separation is sound, but the time lag between materializing stranded costs and realizing productivity savings could create a potential drag on earnings through 2029.
