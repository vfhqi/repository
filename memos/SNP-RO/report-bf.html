EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

OMV Petrom displays superior financial performance among Central and Eastern European regional peers, achieving industry-leading return on invested capital of 14% and maintaining robust double-digit EBITDA margins exceeding 18% in recent years. Multiple broker filings confirm consistent margin and ROIC reporting between company books and analyst research, with Tupras standing out as the only peer achieving a comparable estimated 18% return on capital due to strong domestic diesel shortages.

The company possesses a fortress balance sheet, maintaining a strong net cash position of RON 5,176 million with minor long-term lease liabilities making up the entirety of its debt profile in 2026. However, massive capital spending on the Neptun Deep gas field will drive temporary negative free cash flows through 2026, with total investment budgets rising 11% to RON 9.98 billion for 2026 expenditures and upstream exploration and development consuming 60% of total capital budgets.

Management quality is exceptionally strong, with CEO Christina Verchere steering OMV Petrom to grow regional gas production significantly by 2030 while adjusting strategic plans to focus on traditional high-return energy projects over immature technologies. Verchere possesses over twenty years of experience with an international energy supermajor, and long-term executive remuneration is strictly tied to OMV Petrom share performance and ESG achievements. Project execution is outstanding with major milestones achieved on time and within budget across both gas and green sectors, as Verchere has kept the flagship Neptun Deep project on time and on budget for 2027 and construction of the Petrobrazi SAF and HVO unit is progressing rapidly within budget. High-barrier vertical integration and unrivaled regional infrastructure scale protect OMV Petrom from external competitive threats, operating as the largest integrated energy producer in Southeastern Europe with flagship gas projects that will lower overall production costs to nine USD per barrel. 6 - Analyst/Investor The business model of OMV Petrom is highly robust with strong vertical integration and strategic gas growth in the Black Sea region.

Upstream operations are centered in Romania and Bulgaria, managing 146 commercial oil and gas fields under 13 units, with all upstream oil and gas volumes distributed internally to own downstream operations in Refining and Gas centered around the Petrobrazi refinery, which has an annual processing capacity of 4.5 million tons of crude. The Neptun Deep deepwater gas project is on track for first gas in the first half of 2027, with plateau production projected to decrease upstream production costs from USD 18 to USD 9 per boe, while the company is investing EUR 750 million at Petrobrazi to become Southeastern Europe's first major biofuels producer by 2030.

Stated capital allocation policy commits to a progressive base 5% to 10% annual increase in dividends, delivering a highly competitive total dividend yield of 5.8% in mid-2026 with discretionary gross special dividends of RON 0.0112 per share, while share buyback activity remains completely absent reflecting prioritization of direct dividend cash distributions.

OMV Petrom maintains its premier position as the largest integrated energy producer in Southeastern Europe, operating filling stations across Romania and neighboring countries through approximately 780 stations. The Romanian refining sector remains highly concentrated as four operational refineries process all domestic crude, with Rompetrol operating the Petromidia refinery representing the largest domestic facility, while the top three retail fuel brands command the vast majority of sales in the Romanian downstream market where OMV Petrom maintains a retail market share of approximately 30%. Deepwater gas extraction remains the most lucrative stage because plateau production drives down unit costs, with Petrom's Neptun Deep project set to decrease upstream production costs to USD 9/boe, while downstream processing margins have risen substantially as diesel and jet fuel margins improve year-on-year and tight global refining capacity keeps the regional margin ceiling well above historical mid-cycle averages, with Petrom achieving a refining margin of USD 41.80 per barrel in late 2026.

OMV Petrom holds an attractive integrated position where upstream gas and retail channels capture the largest share of value-chain profits, operating as a fully integrated energy major spanning upstream extraction with 50% estimated gross margin and 25% estimated EBIT margin, logistics with 30% and 15% respectively, refining with 15% and 5%, and marketing with 10% and 3%, with upstream extraction earning an estimated 60% share of the chain's profits.

Digital loyalty applications have secured two million active retail users across Romania and regional stations, with the Petrom loyalty app exceeding 800,000 active users within year of its domestic launch, while fuel products are distributed to millions via 780 regional filling stations under dual brands with retail fuel throughput reaching an average of 5.9 million liters per station in Romania and inelastic retail fuel demand rising 3% year-on-year in Q2 2026 despite high prices. - Analyst/Investor - Earnings Calls Neptun Deep deepwater gas development requires unmatched capital expenditures of EUR 4 billion to achieve production with OMV Petrom and Romgaz holding equal equity of 50% each in the development project, while heavy regulatory and environmental permit approvals represent substantial administrative barriers to entry for offshore explorers as repeat presidential and local elections continue to generate volatile regulatory interventions. Accessing the Transgaz network via the Tuzla-Podisor pipeline creates vital infrastructure bottlenecks that secure domestic gas market share, with construction of the gas measurement station at Tuzla advancing towards upcoming testing phases. Slower than expected regional passenger EV fleet expansion prolongs the demand for traditional transportation fuels until 2030 as lower relative regional motorization rates provide room for robust fuel sales through the decade, while management maintains an 11 billion EUR total budget allocating 25% to mature green technologies with capital systematically reallocated away from unproven green tech into high-return E&P, and regional solar and wind power assets under development exceed 1.5 gigawatts of capacity including final investment decisions at Gabare with 600 megawatt-hours of battery storage.
