Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The investment case for SCHOTT Pharma rests on whether the market correctly understands the transition from a capital-heavy investment phase to a high-margin GLP-1 delivery leader.
1.
The Key Question is: "SCHOTT Pharma AG & Co. KGaA (1SXP-DE), a German maker of primary packaging for injectable drugs, 77% owned by SCHOTT AG (Carl Zeiss Foundation), fiscal year ending 30 September. It reports two segments: Drug Containment Solutions (glass vials, ampoules and cartridges, increasingly as sterile ready-to- use formats) and Drug Delivery Systems (glass and polymer prefillable syringes). Key question: how comprehensively are these dynamics articulated in the current narrative of sell-side commentary and of investor commentary: How well known are these dynamics to the sell-side and to investors? (1) The FY2026 compensation payment and write-down: their opposite effects on FY2027 revenue growth and on EBITDA. (2) Equity-accounted joint-venture income (SCHOTT Pharma India; Empha in Italy) inside the company's EBITDA, and the differences between brokers' EBITDA bases. (3) The capital-spending peak in FY2027 (finance director, 12- Aug-2026) against consensus capital spending falling in FY2027, and the free cash flow inflection after it. (4) Synchronised capacity additions in glass syringes and ready-to-use formats, and the price pressure on new tenders after Stevanato's freed capacity. (5) The 26.8% group EBITDA margin in the third quarter of FY2026, with production-optimisation costs and under-used polymer lines. (6) The status of the North Carolina syringe plant. (7) The December 2027 renewals of the SCHOTT AG tubing framework agreement and the EUR 412m revolving facilities. (8) GLP-1 format shifts from syringes to vials and multi-dose cartridges, and whether they move volume within SCHOTT Pharma's own portfolio or away from it. (9) Ready-to-use cartridge tightness (Stevanato's EZ-fill cartridges booked through most of 2027; Deutsche Bank, November 2026) against falling ready-to-use vial prices. (10) Customer concentration in GLP-1 with Eli Lilly. (11) Capital allocation as capital spending tapers, under a KGaA structure with no buyback, and any plan by SCHOTT AG for the free float. (12) The basis of the reported tax rate (23.5% in FY2025). For each: which analysts (Deutsche Bank, Jefferies, UBS, RBC Capital Markets, Stifel, Barclays, Oddo BHF, EQUI.TS, Bernstein, Morgan Stanley) and which investor commentators discuss it, since when, with what view and how prominently??"

The answer to this question determines whether SCHOTT Pharma is entering a period of significant earnings surprises or if the market is already correctly calibrated to its complex financial structure. For investors, understanding the articulating narrative is critical because SCHOTT Pharma's low free float (18.1%) means that even small shifts in institutional perception can lead to outsized share price volatility. If the market is ignoring a capital-spending peak in FY2027, there is a risk that upcoming free cash flow (FCF) figures will disappoint relative to consensus, potentially delaying the deleveraging narrative.

Conversely, if the tightness in ready-to-use (RTU) cartridges is under-appreciated, SCHOTT Pharma may have more pricing power than the currently cautious "price pressure" narrative suggests. Related questions concern the durability of the High-Value Solutions (HVS) shift, which has moved from 39% of sales in FY2022 to approximately 59% in late FY2026. The transition of the CEO and the alignment of management incentives–which are 30% weighted toward Operating Free Cash Flow (OFCF) and 30% toward EBITDA margin–also remain central to the investment debate.

Furthermore, the impending December 18, 2026, BIOSECURE Act list remains a looming macro factor for all European packaging players with Chinese supply chain exposure. side of the story: The market may be correctly ignoring some of these granular dynamics because they are immaterial compared to the massive absolute growth in GLP-1 demand. If Eli Lilly continues to gain significant U.S. market share, the precise timing of a capital spending peak or a joint-venture income inclusion may be viewed by investors as "accounting noise" that does not change the structural upside of the HVS portfolio.

Section 2 – The Compensation Payment and FY2027 Growth Headwind

Section summary
  1. The sell-side narrative on the EUR 15 mm one-off payment is highly consistent among top-tier brokers, who clearly articulate it as a growth headwind for FY2027. Confidence is high due to explicit management transparency.

Management Disclosures and Broker Recaps

SCHOTT Pharma management confirmed a one-time revenue contribution of approximately EUR 15 mm to be recognized in Q4 FY2026, serving as a compensation effect for a previously under-performing glass syringe customer. This payment was a primary driver for the company to raise its full-year revenue and margin guidance in mid-2026. Management has been transparent that this creates a year-over-year headwind for FY2027 revenue growth, effectively de-risking the volume outlook while complicating comparisons.

Deutsche Bank analyst Falko Friedrichs has articulated this dynamic prominently since the July 2026 resolution, specifically noting that the payment equates to about 1.5% of group sales. He expects this one-off to lead the company to guide FY2027 organic growth at 5-7%, slightly below its medium-term 6-8% target. This view is echoed in his October 2026 downgrade note, where he treats the payment as a non-recurring event for modeling purposes.

Consensus and Pricing Impact

Jefferies also addresses this dynamic, citing management's comment that the loss of a vaccine customer and the subsequent resolution were key drivers of the mid-term guidance reset. While the broker recognizes the visibility gained through the new "take-or-pay" structure, it notes that investors remain focused on near-term delivery to rebuild confidence. The articulation here is clear: the FY2026 boost is a "borrowed" success that will temper the optics of FY2027 growth. Contrary evidence: While the sell-side broadly recognizes the headwind, Deutsche Bank notes that the new contract structure "effectively de-risks the volume outlook," suggesting that the long-term benefit of the partnership resolution may outweigh the mathematical headwind in investor minds. In a report dated October 9, 2026, the broker emphasizes that the strengthened partnership is a significant net positive for the investment case, potentially muting the negative impact of the growth headwind.

Comparable Past Cases

A comparable past case occurred within the Drug Delivery Systems (DDS) segment in late FY2025, where a oneoff inventory write-down on customer-specific syringes impacted margins. Management at that time indicated that, excluding this effect, margins would have been broadly in line with the prior year, a phrasing they are using again for the current compensation payment. This suggests a pattern where management uses one-offs to bridge gaps in underlying operational performance.

Other side of the story: The impact of the compensation payment may be less pronounced than analysts expect if underlying GLP-1 volume growth accelerates faster than the 1.5% headwind. If Eli Lilly's demand exceeds the "take-or-pay" minimums, the mathematical headwind becomes irrelevant. Most brokers are modeling a conservative 2027, but a "clean" beat could occur if market demand for RTU formats continues to outpace the one-off adjustment.

Section 3 – EBITDA Basis and Joint-Venture Income

Section summary
  1. The inclusion of joint-venture income in EBITDA is a documented but poorly articulated dynamic in investor circles, creating a risk of basis mismatch between broker models. Confidence is medium.

The Definition of EBITDA

SCHOTT Pharma defines EBITDA as operating profit before depreciation and amortization, explicitly stating that it "includes the share of the net income / loss of the joint ventures." The company's primary joint ventures are SCHOTT Poonawalla in India and Soffieria Bertolini in Italy. In FY2022, these joint ventures contributed approximately EUR 13 mm, or 10.3% of net income, indicating their materiality to the reported bottom line.

Jefferies is one of the few houses that has deep-dived this structure, noting that the Indian JV operates four plants and offers a full range of containment solutions. However, in more recent Q3 FY2026 results notes, the specific contribution of JV income to the 26.8% EBITDA margin is not broken out by most sell-side analysts. This lack of continuous articulation suggests that investors may be overestimating the organic margin of the core German business.

Peer Benchmarking and Reporting Basis

Comparing this to Stevanato, which also uses high-value solutions (HVS) to drive margins, the articulation of JV income is less a point of contention for the Italian peer. SCHOTT Pharma's reported FY2025 EBITDA margin of 28.4% is often compared directly to Stevanato's 26.8% Q3 2026 midpoint guidance without adjusting for the JV income inclusion. This creates a "valuation trap" where SCHOTT Pharma appears more profitable on a consolidated basis than it is on a standalone manufacturing basis.

1.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank notes in October 2026 that SCHOTT Pharma's improved cash generation was driven by "optimized financing structure" and "lower tax payments," rather than highlighting JV income. This suggests that professional analysts may be more focused on cash conversion than on the specific EBITDA components.

Articulation Gaps in Sell-Side Models

While Jefferies acknowledged the JV structure at initiation in November 2023, its recent August 2026 "First Look" note on Q3 results focuses almost entirely on production optimization costs and under-utilization. The specific dynamics of Empha in Italy are virtually unmentioned in the 2026 sell-side corpus. This gap in the narrative means that a sudden downturn in the Indian or Italian markets could surprise investors who view EBITDA as purely representative of the European and U.S. operations.

2.
Other side of the story: The inclusion of JV income in EBITDA is standard for many European KGaA structures and might be "transparent enough" in the annual reports for sophisticated investors. If the JVs are growing at the same rate as the parent (around 6-8%), the inclusion does not distort growth trends even if it slightly inflates the absolute margin figure. Long-term JV partnerships benefited EBIT and Net Income by €13m, representing 10.3% of NI in FY22.

Section 4 – The FY2027 Capex Peak and FCF Inflection

Section summary
  1. A significant disconnect exists between management's guided FY2027 capex peak and consensus expectations of falling spend, representing a major unarticulated risk. Confidence is medium.

Management Guidance vs. Consensus

SCHOTT Pharma's finance director stated on August 12, 2026, that capital spending is expected to peak in FY2027. This directly contradicts the consensus narrative, where analysts like those at Jefferies have discussed a "moderation from recent peak levels" as being appropriate for the business. Jefferies specifically reduced its price target in early 2026 due to the historically high capital intensity of the business, yet it still expects some moderation that management now says isn't coming until after FY2027. - Earnings Calls 51 Deutsche Bank models a gradual improvement in cash generation but does not explicitly flag the FY2027 spending peak as a primary risk factor in its October 2026 downgrade.

Instead, the broker highlights a 49% year-over-year surge in free cash flow (FCF) for the first nine months of FY2026, which may be leading investors to believe the "worst" of the spending is already over. This mismatch in timing could lead to a negative surprise when FY2027 guidance is formally issued.

Peer Comparison and Investment Cycles

Stevanato provides a relevant comparison, as its Fishers (IN) plant ramp-up has been a central theme for investors. Stevanato reached gross-profit breakeven at Fishers in Q3 2026, providing a clear inflection point that the market rewarded. SCHOTT Pharma, by contrast, is still in the "pre-peak" phase for its next major capacity additions in Hungary and Serbia, suggesting its FCF inflection will lag Stevanato's by 12 to 18 months.

1.
Contrary evidence: SCHOTT Pharma management claims their growth is "self-funded" from operating cash flow, which may mitigate investor concern over a capex peak. In its FY2025 presentation, the company highlighted a cash conversion rate of over 80%, suggesting that even at peak spending, the liquidity risk is lower than for peers like Gerresheimer, which faces higher debt from acquisitions.

The Unarticulated FCF Inflection

The "inflection" narrative–the idea that FCF will jump significantly once capex tapers–is a common trope in sellside research for other sectors but is notably thin for SCHOTT Pharma. For example, First Watch Restaurant Group and Targa Resources have clearly articulated their capex-to-FCF inflection stories in late 2026. SCHOTT Pharma investors, however, appear more focused on the HVS revenue mix (57%) than on the cash flow implications of the FY2027 peak.

Other side of the story: The consensus for falling capex in FY2027 might be based on an assumption that management is being "traditionally conservative" by guiding for a peak. If construction in Serbia or Hungary finishes ahead of schedule, the peak might actually occur in late FY2026, aligning with the current broker models.

Section 5 – Synchronised Capacity and Syringe Price Pressure

Section summary
  1. While the market is alert to Stevanato's GLP-1 issues, the narrative on "synchronised capacity" and widespread price pressure for SCHOTT Pharma remains largely unarticulated. Confidence is low to medium.

The Stevanato Contagion Narrative

The sell-side is acutely aware of Stevanato's GLP-1 customer cut on November 3, 2026, which led to an expectation of low-single-digit (LSD) growth in 2027.

However, Deutsche Bank explicitly argues that Stevanato's issues are "idiosyncratic" and "not representative of strong underlying market fundamentals." This has created a protective narrative around SCHOTT Pharma, with analysts suggesting that Stevanato's freed capacity will not necessarily lead to a price war. 65 An expert Vice President at Aptar Pharma articulated a more cautious view in May 2026, predicting that "true overcapacity" could reach 20% by 2030 as "everybody wanted a piece of the GLP-1 cake." This expert expects this will eventually shift pricing power to pharma companies, but notes that annual low- to mid-single-digit price increases are still possible for now due to high switching costs. This specific warning of a 20% surplus is not yet widely echoed in current sell-side reports.

Capacity Additions and RTU Tightness

Management at SCHOTT Pharma reported that their capacity expansion for polymer syringes in Germany is expected to triple capacity by 2027 and is "already fully sold out." This sell-side narrative of "sold out" capacity directly conflicts with the investor fear of synchronised overcapacity. Furthermore, Deutsche Bank reported in

November 2026 that Stevanato's EZ-fill cartridges are booked through most of 2027, suggesting that while vials are seeing price pressure, cartridges remain extremely tight. Contrary evidence: An expert at Novo Nordisk noted in May 2026 that Stevanato is experiencing the "most notable overcapacity," while SCHOTT Pharma dominates the R&D site business, which is inherently higher margin and less volume-sensitive. This suggests that SCHOTT Pharma may be insulated from the "commodity" pricing pressure that hits bulk syringe makers first.

The Impact of Annex 1

The EU GMP Annex 1 guidelines and the FDA's August 2026 container-closure guidance are cited by experts as structural drivers that force pharma companies to adopt RTU packaging for contamination control. This regulatory push is a "silent" driver of demand that many sell-side reports mention in passing but few quantify as a primary defense against price erosion. Stevanato management specifically highlighted Annex 1 as supporting the conversion from bulk to RTU formats in their Q3 2026 call. Other side of the story: If Novo Nordisk or Eli Lilly successfully shift more volume to oral GLP-1s, the "tightness" in cartridges could disappear overnight. Jefferies notes that oral GLP-1 penetration expansion is a risk that could lead to a re-rating of the entire injectable packaging space. This "oral threat" is a prominent part of the investor debate, even if the sell-side remains optimistic about the injectable "trough" being behind the industry.

Section 6 – Segment Margins, Polymer Utilization, and the NC Plant

Section summary
  1. The Q3 FY2026 EBITDA margin of 26.8% is widely discussed as a trough, but the specific status of the North Carolina plant remains a notable narrative gap. Confidence is medium.

The Q3 Trough Narrative

SCHOTT Pharma's adjusted EBITDA margin of 26.8% in Q3 FY2026 (down 550 basis points) is articulated by Deutsche Bank as being impacted by three temporary factors: inventory impairments, lower polymer utilization, and production optimization costs. Analysts generally accept management's view that this decline is not structural. Jefferies identifies "additional production optimization costs" in Q3 as a primary cause of the margin move, which aligns with the company's "bridge year" guidance for 2026.

The under-utilization of polymer lines is a recurring theme, with Deutsche Bank noting that Drug Delivery Systems (DDS) profitability was hit by "weaker polymer utilization" in the first half of the year.

However, by August 2026, the narrative shifted to "increasing dynamics from non-mRNA in polymer," suggesting that the company is successfully diversifying away from its COVID-era dependency. This recovery is wellarticulated but remains a key variable for FY2027 margin expansion.

The North Carolina Plant Gap

The status of the North Carolina syringe plant is significantly less articulated in the 2026 narrative than other expansion projects. While filings from 2023 mentioned site expansion, recent 2026 broker reports from Deutsche Bank and Jefferies focus almost exclusively on Hungary, Switzerland, and Serbia. This suggests that investors may be unaware of the plant's current operational status or its contribution to the U.S. "nearshoring" strategy.

1.
Contrary evidence: Expert calls with former Gerresheimer and Aptar executives highlight that "U.S. manufacturing presence" is becoming a critical selection criterion for pharma companies. If SCHOTT Pharma is not articulating its North Carolina status effectively, it may be losing the "perception battle" to Stevanato, whose Fishers, Indiana plant is a central part of every broker note.

Management Incentives and Alignment

The narrative on management's ability to hit the "increasing towards 30%" EBITDA margin target by 2029 is supported by a 57% HVS mix, which carries margins roughly 10 percentage points above the group average. Deutsche Bank notes that management's incentives are now aligned with shareholder value through the ROCE and OFCF targets approved in February 2026. This alignment is a growing part of the "institutional credibility" narrative that the sell-side is pushing to counter previous guidance disappointments.

Other side of the story: The "production optimization" costs may be more permanent than the "one-off" label suggests. As SCHOTT Pharma shifts toward more complex sterile formats, the baseline for operational expenditure may be structurally higher. If polymer utilization does not recover to pre-2026 levels due to the decline in mRNA vaccines, the 30% margin target could remain elusive.

Section 7 – Renewals, Liquidity, and Capital Allocation

Section summary
  1. The impending 2027 renewals and the tax rate basis are the least articulated dynamics, while the low free float is a widely known but unresolved barrier. Confidence is low for renewals/tax and high for liquidity.

The December 2027 Cliff

There is almost no articulated sell-side or investor commentary regarding the December 2027 renewals of the SCHOTT AG tubing framework agreement and the EUR 412 mm revolving facilities. These are critical "tail risks" because SCHOTT Pharma's competitive advantage is built on its strategic relationship with its parent for glass supply. A failure to renew on favorable terms or a spike in borrowing costs upon the RCF renewal could materially impact EPS in FY2028, yet this remains a "non-topic" in analyzed broker notes.

The Liquidity and Free Float Barrier

The "extremely low trading liquidity" of SCHOTT Pharma (18.1% free float) is prominently articulated by Deutsche Bank, which has repeatedly called the stock "un-investable for a vast number of institutional investors." The broker explicitly states that the majority owner, SCHOTT Group, "urgently needs to address" this issue. However, there is no evidence in the narrative of a concrete plan by SCHOTT AG to increase the free float in the near term, leaving investors in a "wait-and-see" mode.

The Tax Rate and Capital Allocation

The 23.5% tax rate reported for FY2025 is a "black box" in current commentary. No covering house from the list (including UBS, Barclays, or Morgan Stanley ) has articulated the drivers of this rate or its sustainability relative to the statutory German corporate tax rate. Similarly, the capital allocation narrative under the KGaA structure is focused on "self-funding" growth rather than returns to shareholders. With no buybacks possible and a modest dividend (EUR 0.18 in FY2025), the narrative is purely a "growth through capex" story. Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank notes that cash generation in 2026 was aided by "lower tax payments," suggesting that the company may be benefiting from tax credits related to its heavy investment cycle in Hungary and Serbia. If these credits are one-time in nature, the normalized tax rate could be significantly higher, presenting a hidden headwind to EPS that no analyst has yet quantified. Other side of the story: The low free float may actually be a "moat" against short-selling, as the lack of borrowable shares limits aggressive downside bets. While institutional investors may find it hard to enter, the concentration of ownership with the Carl Zeiss Foundation provides long-term stability that a more fragmented share register might lack during the current capital spending peak.

Section 9 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A negative surprise regarding the FY2027 capex peak or a spike in the tax rate could derail the stock's recent recovery, as these dynamics are currently unpriced. If the market's common view–that the capital spending cycle is moderating and that the syringe market is "tight"–is wrong, the impact on SCHOTT Pharma's earnings could be severe. Most sources currently expect a smooth "inflection" to higher free cash flow (FCF) as the company hits its 2029 targets.
1.
However, if the FY2027 capex peak leads to a second "bridge year" of zero or negative FCF, institutional investors who recently returned to the stock after the July 2026 resolution may exit again, potentially causing the share price to re-test its EUR 12.96 trough. A hidden risk is the Eli Lilly concentration. While currently viewed as a strength (Lilly accounts for 2/3 of GLP-1 exposure), any regulatory setback for Zepbound or a shift toward orforglipron (Lilly's oral GLP-1) would disproportionately hit SCHOTT Pharma compared to more diversified peers like Becton Dickinson. The market narrative currently treats this concentration as a "guaranteed" growth driver, but it has the power to push the outcome away from expectations if Lilly seeks to diversify its supply chain away from European glass providers to avoid geopolitical or tariff risks. Early signs that the outcome may differ include Stevanato's recent report of "variable compensation tied to lower GLP-1 volumes," which most analysts dismissed as idiosyncratic. If SCHOTT Pharma's upcoming FY2026 year-end results show a similar volume moderation that isn't fully offset by the EUR 15 mm compensation payment, it would indicate that the "syringe price pressure" narrative is more pervasive than the sell-side currently admits. Furthermore, a sudden jump in the reported tax rate toward the 30% statutory level would represent an immediate 6-8% headwind to EPS that is not currently articulated in any identified broker model.

Other side of the story: The "un-investable" liquidity dynamic may be resolved by a secondary offering from SCHOTT AG in 2027, which would be a massive positive catalyst ignored by those who focus only on the 18.1% free float. If the parent company decides to unlock value before the 2027 renewals, the resulting influx of institutional capital could lead to a permanent re-rating that makes current accounting concerns irrelevant.
