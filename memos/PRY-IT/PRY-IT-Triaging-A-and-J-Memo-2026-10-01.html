[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Prysmian is a demand-inflection case in a first-rank operator, compelling on the business and not compelling at the price. At EUR 124.0 the shares already pay for the earnings we believe in, so the return from here turns on a multiple at the 90th percentile of its own ten-year history being held, which we think unlikely.

  • The change is external, sector-level and structural, and it is already in the order book.
    • Two demand forces, Western grid reinforcement and the AI data-centre build-out, have run into capacity-constrained oligopolies in which Prysmian is the largest and most integrated player, so price and volume have risen together.
    • High-voltage awards exceeded EUR 15bn in the first nine months of 2024 against a EUR 2bn to 3bn annual market in the late 2010s
      • transmission operators now reserve capacity five years or more ahead.
    • The Transmission backlog stands at EUR 18bn to 20bn (mid-2026), roughly four years of Transmission revenue, and the company holds about 40% of the high-voltage market.
    • In data-centre fibre, Prysmian is one of three producers with a US footprint and signed a ten-year supply agreement with Molex in July 2026 worth up to EUR 5.5bn, with EUR 550m paid up front.
  • The internal change amplifies the external force rather than creating it.
    • A chief executive appointed on 18 April 2024 has added Encore Wire (July 2024), Channell (June 2025), ACSM (February 2026) and Atkore (agreed 3 August 2026, enterprise value about EUR 3.3bn), raised FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance twice to EUR 2,800m to 2,900m, and reached the 2028 targets in 2026.
    • He is a 36-year insider, so this is acceleration of an existing direction, not a reset
      • that is why 'Major Corporate Change' is the secondary pattern and not the primary.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on every line that matters, and the sell-side has already caught up.
    • Revenue rose 15.4% in FY2025, adjusted EBITDA 24% and adjusted EPS 45%
    • organic growth accelerated to 9.4% in Q2 2026
    • Transmission margins reached 21.2% and Digital Solutions 23.8%.
    • Consensus FY2028 EPS has risen 39.5% in twelve months and the dashboard's six-month EPS revision is +17%, so the upgrade wave the pattern predicts has happened.
    • The near term offers little further room: the guidance range tops out at EUR 2,900m against consensus EUR 2,858m
      • the Q2 2026 group margin missed by 80 basis points
      • the one-month FY2026 revision was -1.3%.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, which in a demand-inflection case means the trajectory upgrades are largely behind, not ahead.
    • It shows in the company's people, its choices, its actions, its operating metrics, its financial statements and the sell-side's estimates, and in a share price up 42% over twelve months.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted EPS of EUR 4.30 in FY2025 rising to about EUR 6.90 in FY2028, roughly 17% a year, then slowing to high single digits by 2030 as high-voltage capacity catches up and US low-voltage margins normalise.
    • Our path sits 2% below consensus in FY2027 and 4% below in FY2028, because we hold the Electrification margin near 9% to 10% rather than consensus 10.8% and let Encore normalise toward 11% by 2028.
    • The disagreement with the sell-side is about 2029 and beyond, which is exactly where the backlog's protection ends and the capacity response begins.
  • What it costs: EUR 124.0 (2026-09-30), or 17.5x consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 7.08, the 90th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range, against a ten-year median of 14.4x, Nexans at 12.2x, NKT at 16.1x and an 11-peer median of 15.7x.
    • On a one-year-forward footing the stock is on about 20x
      • on our own path the same price is 20.7x FY2027 and 18.0x FY2028 earnings.
    • The market found this case in May 2026, when the shares rose from EUR 94.96 (30 March 2026) to EUR 156.90 (11 May 2026) on the hyperscaler news
      • they have since fallen 21% with sell-side momentum still at A, which is what a priced catalyst looks like.
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the leg that fails is the multiple, not the earnings.
    • On our modal path, with the multiple settling at the ten-year 75th percentile of 16.0x, the 'Modal return' is 2.8% a year over eighteen months and 5.4% a year over three years, against the 20% bar.
    • The earnings leg alone adds 12.3% over eighteen months and 25.0% over three years
      • the de-rating from 17.5x to 16.0x takes 8.6% away
      • the dividend adds 1.7% and 2.7%.
    • Hold the multiple at 17.4x and the eighteen-month return is 8.6% a year, still an F
      • consensus earnings and the multiple held give 18.9% a year, a B.
      • Every 1x on the multiple is about EUR 8 a share.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', crisply on the mandatory condition and on three of five conditions, with 'Major Corporate Change' as the secondary fit.
    • The fit is B rather than A because the inflection reaches about 60% of profit, the business is capacity-bound rather than one-to-many, and the pattern is fully mature on the chain.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the forces through the outputs to the estimates: the backlog is contracted, the operator is of the first rank, and the earnings compound at about 17% a year on our path.
    • It breaks at the price: 40% of EBITDA sits in commoditised low-voltage wire held by tariffs and a cycle, the premium mix is not rising (Transmission plus Digital Solutions are 43% of consensus FY2026 segment EBITDA and 44% of FY2028), and the multiple prices none of that.

  • The case therefore is a good business parked on price.

    • On our modal path the shares clear 20% a year over eighteen months below about EUR 98 (13.9x the current 24-month-forward EPS)
    • on consensus earnings, below about EUR 113.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material (forces A, outputs B) but at this price it does not drive a sufficient return (F)

    • the Building Block measures materiality including sufficiency, and the return binds.
    • Not B: B would need the 'Modal return' at the C band at least, and it is an F on every row of our arithmetic but the ones that assume consensus earnings and no de-rating.
    • Not D: the earnings leg alone is top-decile against the comparison set, and a price about 20% lower restores the case without any change to the business.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The competing hypothesis, at its strongest: consensus is right or low, and the multiple deserves to hold.
    • The backlog makes Transmission earnings contractual to 2028
    • the Molex agreement and Claremont make Digital Solutions earnings contractual to 2031
    • Atkore adds high-single-digit accretion before USD 150m of synergies
    • the chief executive has beaten every range he has set.
    • On that path EPS compounds at 19% to 24% a year to FY2029 (consensus EUR 8.94), a stock at 17x 24-month-forward earnings sits on a price-to-earnings-growth ratio below one, and the Capital Markets Day in H1 2027 resets targets to 2030 and 2031.
    • The return at consensus earnings with the multiple held is 18.9% a year over eighteen months, a B.
    • The evidence for it: the FY2025 beats
      • two guidance raises in H1 2026
      • a free cash flow guidance upgrade 27% above pre-results consensus
      • a 95% share-price run to July 2026 that rewarded the pattern once already
      • Deutsche Bank at EUR 167, UBS at EUR 175 and Equita at EUR 178
      • and the risks report's own finding that the bear case has no unifying mechanism.
  • What we concede.
    • On consensus earnings with no de-rating the case clears the bar
    • our modal path is below consensus in every year
    • and the sell-side has been too low on this company for three years.
    • We also concede that against global electrical peers the stock is on a 20% discount, wider than the 15% norm, which is the one measure on which it is not expensive.
  • Why we still hold our view, first: the FY2029 consensus of EUR 8.94 rests on four brokers.
    • The 13-broker FY2028 median of EUR 7.20 is only 4% above our EUR 6.90
    • the disagreement is about 2029 and beyond, where the backlog's protection ends and NKT's Karlskrona (Q2 2027), LS Cable's Virginia plant (H1 2028) and a top-tier capacity addition of about 30% into 2027 arrive.
  • Second: the mix is not moving toward the premium segments.
    • Transmission plus Digital Solutions are 43% of consensus FY2026 segment EBITDA and 44% of FY2028, because Encore and Atkore add to the low-voltage half
    • so the argument for a structurally higher multiple has no numbers behind it.
  • Third: the market has already paid once.
    • The re-rating from EUR 94.96 to EUR 156.90 between 30 March and 11 May 2026 came on the hyperscaler news
    • the shares have since fallen 21% while sell-side momentum is still pinned at A, which is the shape of a catalyst that has been priced.
  • Fourth: the drop-through.
    • The Atkore funding and the EUR 5bn capex programme take about 11% off EPS on a plausibly worse EBITDA-to-EPS ratio, which alone would turn a C return into an F
    • the sensitivity is shown in the 'Modal return' Element.
  • The second competing hypothesis is the bear one: Morgan Stanley's EUR 95, or EUR 3.2bn of 2027 EBITDA at 10x EV/EBITDA.
    • We think it unlikely on the earnings, because consensus FY2027 EBITDA is EUR 3,546m and the H1 2026 run-rate supports the guidance, and possible on the multiple alone, because 10x EV/EBITDA is near the stock's own trough.
    • It is a reminder that the mark-to-market risk is the live one.
  • Where the competing hypothesis would win: a Capital Markets Day in H1 2027 that sets 2030 targets with Transmission and Digital Solutions above half of EBITDA, or a FY2028 print near the top of the brokers' range (Goldman EUR 8.50, UBS EUR 8.32).
    • Either would change the branch odds on the multiple and we would re-rate the case.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because three copper-saving or fibre-substituting technologies are being proved rather than disproved (800 VDC data-centre architecture, high-temperature superconducting cable targeted for commercial use by 2030, hollow-core fibre), but none reaches volume inside the eighteen-month horizon.

  • Backlog protection: hyperscalers resist redesigning architectures mid-deployment, which protects the backlog over 12 to 24 months on Bernstein's reading.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
