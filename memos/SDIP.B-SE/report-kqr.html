Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Sdiptech's massive exposure to sterling and highly sensitive variable interest rates make its upcoming earnings per share growth heavily dependent on macroeconomic shifts.
  2. Fluctuations in interest and exchange rates directly affect Sdiptech's acquisition capabilities and financial costs, which are critical to its serial compounder model.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Defining the challenge: "Sdiptech AB (SDIP.B-SE), a Swedish serial acquirer of about 35 niche infrastructure product companies in 13 countries, with about half of sales in the United Kingdom, reporting four business areas: Supply Chain & Transportation (transport refrigeration, rail, lifting, road maintenance), Energy & Electrification (power connectors, power quality, temporary power, EV charging through Rolec), Water & Bioeconomy (water treatment, subsurface repair, bioeconomy) and Safety & Security (perimeter security, clean air, secure communications). For Sdiptech AB (SDIP.B-SE), whose 2025 annual report states that a 10% move in the exchange rates of the British pound and the euro against the Swedish krona would have changed 2025 operating profit by SEK 83m and pre-tax profit by about SEK 44m, whose currency derivatives mature within twelve months, and whose bank debt of about SEK 2.8bn carries variable rates with interest-rate swaps covering 40% to 45% of utilised credit, what do the major banks and forecasters (Nordea, SEB, Swedbank, Handelsbanken, Danske Bank, Barclays, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and others) as of October and November 2026 forecast for the Riksbank policy rate, three-month STIBOR, the Bank of England base rate, GBP/SEK and EUR/SEK through the end of 2027 and into 2028, with each forecaster's date and reasoning; how have Sdiptech's reported net sales growth, adjusted EBITA and net financial items moved quarter by quarter with GBP/SEK, EUR/SEK and STIBOR since 2022, as disclosed in its interim reports; and what did comparable krona-reporting companies with large sterling exposure (for example Lifco AB, Rökko AB, Teqnion AB, Beijer Alma AB, Lagercrantz Group AB) report as translation and interest effects on earnings in 2025 and 2026, with dates and sources?"
2.
Significant currency sensitivity: Sdiptech operates as a highly decentralized infrastructure group where international operations generate the majority of earnings. According to the company's 2025 annual report, a 10% change in the average exchange rates of all currencies against the Swedish krona would impact operating profit by SEK 83m. This currency volatility is particularly critical because approximately half of group sales are generated within the United Kingdom. Changes in the GBP/SEK rate will therefore directly determine the translated value of these foreign earnings.
3.
Substantial interest exposure: Sdiptech's extensive leverage profile remains highly sensitive to fluctuations in market interest rates. The company's credit facility and sustainability-linked bonds carry variable rates tied to benchmarks like STIBOR. Interest expenses to credit institutions represent a major expense item, reaching SEK 206m in fiscal year 2025. Consequently, any upward movement in borrowing costs will negatively affect pre-tax profit margins and net income.

Impact of Interest and Exchange Rates

4.
Leverage and swap coverage: Sdiptech relies on bank debt totaling SEK 2.28bn drawn on its long-term credit line to fund acquisitions. To mitigate rising borrowing costs, the group maintains interest-rate swaps covering approximately 40% to 45% of its utilised credit volume. This means that a large portion of its outstanding liabilities remains fully exposed to variable interest rate hikes. Higher rates will immediately increase interest costs on the unhedged debt, directly impacting earnings per share.
5.
Short-term derivative risks: Sdiptech actively manages its transactional foreign exchange risks through financial hedges. The group has entered into currency futures in GBP and EUR to reduce exchange rate impacts on its operating result. However, these currency derivatives mature within twelve months, which exposes the company to refinancing risks. If the krona depreciates dramatically after these hedges roll over, the group will face much higher hedging costs.
6.
Strategic acquisition headwinds: The execution of Sdiptech's compounder business model depends entirely on its capacity to successfully complete earnings-accretive transactions. Elevated interest rates increase the cost of capital, making new acquisitions more expensive and harder to finance. When borrowing costs rise, the group's net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio is pushed upward. This financial constraint ultimately limits the pace of M&A, which is the primary driver of earnings growth over the long term.
7.
Contrary evidence: Sdiptech can reduce its overall interest expenses by refinancing expensive debt at more favorable terms as it grows. In September 2026, the company successfully refinanced its previous outstanding bond with a new sustainability-linked bond under improved terms. This refinancing activity resulted in lower net financial estimates for the 2027 and 2028 periods. This suggests that corporate refinancing actions can partially offset broader macroeconomic rate pressures.
8.
Other side of the story: Although interest rates and currency fluctuations present notable risks, Sdiptech's highly decentralized portfolio of niche infrastructure companies provides a strong defensive buffer. Its subsidiaries typically enjoy high margins and non-cyclical demand. If market rates remain elevated, Sdiptech's organic earnings growth could still sustain its capital allocation model without heavy reliance on new debt. Therefore, operational performance might prove far more resilient than interest rate sensitivities suggest.

Evaluating Key Information Gaps

1.
Uncertain swap coverage: Sdiptech manages interest exposure through swaps covering 40% to 45% of credit volume, but disclosures do not specify the exact strike rates of these hedges. The latest 2025 annual report outlines general maturities of 1 to 3.5 years without presenting the underlying fixed swap rates. Understanding the precise swap rates is critical because it determines how much interest expense will rise under variable contracts.
2.
Derivative rollover gaps: Sdiptech utilizes short-term currency derivatives to hedge translation risk, but reports do not disclose the precise rollover schedule. The 2023 annual report specifies that these hedges mature within 12 months but lacks details on monthly contract expiries. Reviewing future quarterly interim reports is required to track derivative refinancing terms and pricing shifts over the next fiscal year.
3.
Variable margin opacity: Sdiptech's credit agreements include variable margins tied directly to its net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio. The 2024 annual report states these margins adjust dynamically, but the exact pricing tiers remain undisclosed. Waiting for future annual filings is necessary to calculate the marginal interest cost on new debt accurately.
4.
Currency growth details: Sdiptech does not provide a segment-level breakdown of organic sales growth by operating currency in its reports. While the third quarter report of 2026 mentions 13% organic growth, it does not isolate sterling performance from other currencies. A direct expert call with management would close this gap by revealing regional organic sales trends and underlying volume developments.
5.
Earnout translation formulas: Sdiptech's contingent considerations are highly sensitive to exchange rate fluctuations because most earn-outs are paid in GBP. The Q3 2026 interim report lists SEK 720 million in outstanding earn-outs but does not share the translation formula. This gap could be resolved by waiting for future balance sheet updates detailing paid considerations and exchange adjustments.
6.
Sustainability target adjustments: Sdiptech's credit agreements are linked to sustainability targets that can adjust the interest margin upwards or downwards. The 2025 annual report outlines these terms but fails to disclose the specific KPI milestones required. Analyzing subsequent sustainability reports would clarify if Sdiptech can secure cheaper debt financing through these targets over time.
7.
Contract pricing power: Sdiptech’s subsidiaries operate in niche markets, but disclosures do not specify their long- term contract indexation clauses. DNB Carnegie highlights that while these companies have strong margins, their ability to pass on rising interest costs is unquantified. Organizing expert calls with subsidiary managers would confirm their underlying pricing power and resilience against inflation.
8.
Tax policy implications: Sdiptech’s earnings are highly sensitive to corporate tax rates, particularly within the United Kingdom market. RedEye reported that the UK tax rate hike to 25% negatively impacted Sdiptech's earnings per share expansion. Gaining full visibility on future UK fiscal policy adjustments would help analysts forecast long-term net margins under changing tax environments.
9.
Peer currency exposure: Comparative analyses of peers are limited because some companies do not disclose localized UK revenue. The interim reports of Lagercrantz indicate zero translation impact but do not detail their UK segment sales. Examining upcoming annual filings of peers would provide better market structure comparisons for institutional investors.
10.
Other side of the story: Although the lack of precise disclosures on derivative strike rates and variable credit margins limits absolute visibility, Sdiptech's historical performance shows that these factors are secondary to operating performance. DNB Carnegie argues that the group's high EBITA margins and robust cash conversion are the primary drivers of its re-rating potential. Therefore, operational strength will likely overshadow any minor interest hedges or currency translation discrepancies.

Financial Impacts of Higher Rates

1.
Elevated financing pressure: Sdiptech's interest expenses will escalate significantly if Swedish STIBOR rates remain structurally higher than the 2% bank consensus. According to Sdiptech's October 2026 prospectus, a 1% rate hike increases annual interest costs by approximately SEK eleven million. Consequently, a 2% surprise increase would reduce pre-tax earnings by SEK 22 million unless offset by further swaps. This rate headwind directly threatens the group's net margins and future earnings per share.
2.
Elevated capital costs: Sdiptech's valuation remains highly sensitive to changes in its weighted average cost of capital. RedEye recently lowered its weighted average cost of capital assumption to 9.1% from 9.3% WACC because Sdiptech secured cheaper bond financing in September 2026. However, if STIBOR surges unexpectedly, Sdiptech's borrowing costs will rise and force analysts to raise their cost of capital models. This upward revision would trigger a downward re-rating of Sdiptech's base case valuation.
3.
Restricted leverage capacity: Prolonged interest rate pressure would strain Sdiptech's capital allocation model due to its high debt load. Sdiptech's net debt to adjusted EBITDA reached 2.95 times in September 2026, which is close to management's 3.0 times leverage ceiling. Under a higher-for-longer rate scenario, the rising cost of debt would limit Sdiptech's ability to fund new acquisitions using its credit facilities. Consequently, this financial constraint would slow the group's long-term EBITA compound growth rate.
4.
Valuation multiple compression: High interest rates pose a substantial threat to the valuation multiples of highly leveraged serial acquirers. DNB Carnegie notes that Sdiptech's valuation remains highly sensitive to benchmark interest rates. If STIBOR diverges upward from projections, investors will likely demand higher equity risk premiums, causing multiple contraction across the sector. This de-rating would prevent Sdiptech from achieving its target valuation of 13.6 times EV/EBITA.

Currency Devaluation and Translation Effects

5.
Severe translation headwinds: A sudden devaluation of the British pound or the euro against the Swedish krona would severely impact Sdiptech's translated profitability. Sdiptech's 2025 annual report outlines that a 10% adverse currency shift reduces operating profit by SEK 83m. Because Sdiptech derives roughly half of its revenues from its British subsidiaries, any sterling weakness will directly compress consolidated earnings. This exchange rate volatility remains a key downside risk for EPS development through 2027.
6.
Sterling volatility risks: Sdiptech's heavy geographic concentration in the United Kingdom makes its earnings particularly sensitive to sterling fluctuations. DNB Carnegie notes that Sdiptech has around 50% of its group sales in the UK market. If sterling weakens significantly against the Swedish krona, translated operating profits will decline and limit Sdiptech's reported EPS growth. Consequently, currency translation represents a major risk if exchange rates diverge from bank consensus.
7.
Tax and wage friction: Sdiptech's British operations face structural cost headwinds that could compound the negative effects of any currency devaluation. RedEye highlights that previous UK tax rate increases to 25% and minimum wage hikes have historically dragged Sdiptech's net earnings down. If the sterling devalues while these domestic cost pressures persist, the profitability of the UK divisions will deteriorate rapidly. This double impact would severely depress Sdiptech's converted earnings per share.

Knock-on Effects on M&A and Peers

8.
Divergent peer capacity: Macroeconomic shifts will alter the competitive dynamics among Swedish serial acquirers, favoring companies with lower leverage. DNB Carnegie reports that Röko's net debt to EBITDA reached 2.6 times in late 2026, which limits its near-term acquisition capacity compared to less-leveraged peers. In contrast, Beijer Alma's leverage is projected to remain stable at around 2.0 times during the same period. Consequently, Sdiptech could face intense competition from better-capitalized peers for high-quality niche infrastructure assets.
9.
Peer leverage buffers: Swedish peers are taking proactive measures to manage their leverage profiles ahead of potential macroeconomic shocks. RedEye reports that Lagercrantz's net debt rose to 2.1 times EBITDA after acquisitions, which management considers comfortable but worth monitoring. Because Lagercrantz maintains a lower leverage ratio than Sdiptech's 2.95 times, it is better positioned to withstand sudden interest rate shocks. This structural leverage difference could allow Lagercrantz to capture market share during a downturn.
10.
Rising deal multiples: Increased competition from international buyers and well-funded local serial acquirers could drive acquisition multiples higher. A former unit head at Lifco noted that valuations for target companies have increased due to intense competition in Scandinavia. If Sdiptech is forced to pay higher multiples, its return on capital will deteriorate over the next 18 months. This competitive pressure would ultimately slow Sdiptech's acquisition pace and reduce its long-term EPS compounding rate.

Early Warning Signs and Alternate Views

11.
Negative volume signals: Monitoring organic order intake and sector-specific demand will serve as early warning signs of a departure from bank forecasts. DNB Carnegie notes that weak demand in construction-related niches has already pressured several peer groups. If organic order intake fails to outpace invoiced sales, Sdiptech's organic growth momentum will reverse. This slowdown would indicate broader macroeconomic deterioration, signaling that bank growth forecasts are overly optimistic.
12.
Other side of the story: Although a deviation from the central bank rate path poses risks, Sdiptech's diversified portfolio provides strong operational resilience. Sdiptech's core business areas focus on non-cyclical, structurally growing end-markets that can sustain high profit margins. Furthermore, if rates remain elevated, Sdiptech can adjust its capital allocation and focus on organic cash generation rather than M&A. This defensive flexibility suggests Sdiptech's long-term EPS growth remains protected even in turbulent macroeconomic environments.
