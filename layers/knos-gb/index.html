Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external forces have moved decisively in Kainos's favour on volume and visibility and against it on price. For modelling purposes: treat FY2027 and FY2028 revenue as demand-backed (a TCV backlog of £853m, 80% due within two years, plus £195m pending, against FY2027 revenue of about £545m), with the fiscal channel (2027 Spending Review, Treasury approval gates) as the main external swing variable for FY2028 and beyond. Do not assume any external help for margin: competitive pricing in public frameworks and the Workday ecosystem, plus AI-driven decline in hours, mean services prices are at best flat in real terms. Products demand is structural but tied to one platform owner whose roadmap and valuation are external variables. The external environment for the shares is less friendly than for the business: a high discount rate and a risk-off regime penalise small growth stocks.

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

F3824th of 31

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Competitive intensity is rising in both of Kainos's services markets and is a real headwind to price. It is concentrated at the standardised, smaller and framework-tendered end of each market. At the large, complex, prime-contractor end, where Kainos is now winning, intensity is lower and some forces are turning in Kainos's favour. Direction: worse for price over 18 months to four years; neutral to slightly better for share at the top end.

Reasoning.

  • UK public digital: global integrators displaced from weak commercial demand (Capgemini, PA Consulting, Accenture) have underbid by 5% to 10%, bidding at 65% to 85% of full-price costing; Kainos's share of DOS contract value fell from about 10% to 4.6% in 2023 and 2024 as it refused to cut rates. Entrants are widening: FPT on G-Cloud 15; Infosys's 15-year £1.2bn NHSBSA contract; TCS bidding low for volume; Netcompany scaling in UK public sector; BJSS.
  • Workday: UK or EMEA partner count up from 60 to 114; standard implementation pricing down 20% to 25% a year; EMEA price pressure about 10%; Workday holds its own services revenue flat (about $710m in FY2027) while pushing standard work to partners; Workday GO cuts implementation to 30 to 60 days; 26% of partners say the programme needs improvement.
  • The best evidence that price competition has not yet reached Kainos's own delivery: Digital Services gross margin on employee-delivered revenue held at 39.0% (39.5% a year earlier); the fall in the division's margin to 31.9% is mix (contractor revenue at 24.9%, partner revenue at 9.6%), not price.
  • Forces favouring Kainos at the top end: larger, more complex work where few can bid as prime (13 Workday Services deals above $1m against 7; DEFRA £150m); the Procurement Act's most-advantageous-tender basis; social value weighting doubling to 20% from January 2027; a new "UK supplier" definition by January 2027. If commercial IT demand recovers (Accenture Q4 FY2026 +7% in constant currency), integrators may pull capacity back from public bids.
  • Products: competition is growing from a small base (Strada Verify and Opkey against Smart Test; Workday's own agents; Trusaic and Syndio on the Workday Marketplace against Pay Transparency).
C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

C+509th of 31

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. The main input is skilled people. Over the last 6 months the net effect has been a headwind through availability (Kainos could not hire permanent staff fast enough and bought capacity from contractors and partners at much lower margins) and through employer National Insurance; underlying wage inflation has been a tailwind (low single digits, a loosening UK labour market, junior-weighted hiring). Over the next 6 to 18 months the balance should turn mildly favourable as the hiring pipeline converts, but management has tied contractor reduction to growth slowing below 20%, so the availability headwind lasts as long as demand stays strong. Supplier concentration risk sits with Workday, not with labour.

Reasoning.

  • Availability: a contractor deploys in four weeks, a permanent hire takes about five months. Contractors rose from 69 (March 2025) to 259 (March 2026) to 316 (September 2026). Digital Services partner and contractor revenue was £49.2m in H1 FY2027 against £15.1m; gross margin is 9.6% on partner revenue, 24.9% on contractor revenue and 39.0% on employee revenue. Management classes about 60% of the increase as temporary burst capacity and 40% as structural (partners working under Kainos as prime).
  • Price of labour: core wage inflation moderated to low single digits; average salary fell 2% on junior mix; UK temporary and permanent placements both contracted in October 2026, easing hiring; employed staff rose 11% in six months; 200 early-careers hires planned; retention 91%. Polish delivery costs are unhedged in zloty (GBP/PLN 5.31 to 4.94 over FY2023 to FY2026) in a tight Polish and Romanian labour market.
  • Policy cost: employer National Insurance, 0.8 points of PBT margin in H1 FY2027.
  • Workday: a fixed fee of $10m (about £7.8m) a year replaced a 20% commission, a fixed cost that falls per unit as Products grow; £23.6m minimum spend commitment, £5.0m outstanding at 30 September 2026; an eight-year agreement with a three-year break clause available to Kainos.
  • Peers show the problem is sector-wide in a demand recovery: Made Tech's contractor share went 9% (FY2024), 19% (FY2025), 14% (H1 FY2026); Adesso's hiring freeze raised its subcontractor costs 16%.

What the scores mean in this layer

Finish each answer and each judgement on a group or set with a score from 1 to 100; a higher number is more positive for an investment case in the company.

For the third set's questions, the score says how positive the thing the question asks about is for the predictability of the company's financial forecasts over the next 18 months, and so for an investment case in the company: 100 the most positive, 1 the most negative, 50 neither.

For the fourth set's questions, the score says how favourable for an investment case in the company the gap is between our assessment and what current guidance, sell-side estimates and the stock market narrative already reflect: 100 the most favourable, 1 the least, 50 neither.

The Investment Analyst's opening notes

Reading notes for the Consumer

1. What is new since the second set was written, and changes the picture.

  • Consensus has already moved to our revenue. The dashboard's revision file (read 12 November) gives the FactSet mean for FY2028 as revenue £606.6m, EBITDA £96.3m and EPS 60.3p. Three days earlier the mean was £592m, £95.6m and 59.2p (median £586m, £97.0m, 59.4p). The second set's FY2028 revenue is £606.7m. Its statement that it is 3.5% above consensus on FY2028 revenue is therefore no longer true. Consensus now implies an FY2028 EBITDA margin of 15.9%, the same as FY2027. The whole difference between our forecasts and consensus over the next 18 months is one item: whether the margin rises in FY2028.
  • The UK fiscal calendar is now dated. The next Spending Review has been pushed back to autumn 2027 (ING, 21 October 2026); a fiscal gap of £25bn to £40bn must be closed in it (Deutsche Bank, 28 October 2026); unprotected departments face real-terms cuts of about 2.6% a year on current plans (Morgan Stanley, 29 October 2026). The review therefore falls inside the 18-month window, in H2 FY2028.
  • NHS England is to be abolished and merged into the Department of Health and Social Care by April 2027. The precedent is Kainos's own: when NHS Digital merged into NHS England, healthcare revenue fell 31% to £20.4m in H1 FY2024.
  • The Workday break clause is Kainos's option, not Workday's, and is first exercisable in August 2027 (Deutsche Bank, 17 September 2024); the £23.6m minimum spend covers the first three years. One channel check (2 November 2026) says qualifying "Built on Workday" products are exempt from Workday's new charges for API consumption.
  • Management has a stated staffing target: contractor spend at 5% and partner spend at 12% of revenue (H1 FY2027 presentation), worth about 3 points of gross margin. I read this as Digital Services revenue and gross margin, because the 3 points reproduce on that basis; the two together were 30% of the division's revenue in H1 FY2027.
  • Kainos has released contractors quickly before: from 209 to 42 in FY2024 (7% of staff to 1%), in a year when revenue grew 2%.

2. Where I disagree with the first or second set (the reasons are in the answers).

  • The second set's base case puts the first margin step in H1 FY2028, the first half in which the trigger is met, although its own advice (P-06) was to treat the timing as optimistic by one to two halves. I move the step by about one half (note 4; D-01, D-02).
  • Both sets treat "about July 2027" as a window in which Workday might change its terms. It is Kainos's break right; the Workday risk is undated and sits in Workday's product roadmap (D-01, D-02).
  • The first set (C-04, C-32) attributed the 85% healthcare growth largely to trusts procuring ahead of the reorganisation. The AlphaSense evidence attributes it mainly to national programmes already mobilised (Digital Health Checks, vaccinations, population health) across 50 customers. The risk to FY2028 is therefore the re-contracting of national programmes when their customer, NHS England, ceases to exist, more than an air pocket at trusts (D-01, D-04).
  • The first set (C-70, C-71) called guidance conservative on revenue. That conservatism has now been absorbed by consensus for FY2028 (I-01, I-02).
  • The second set (P-04) flagged Workday's API charges as a cost risk outside the base case; the exemption evidence lowers it. The risk both sets under-weighted is Workday's native agents, which act on the two oldest and most profitable products (D-02).
  • The AlphaSense report on plausibility says the £200m ARR target needs 24% a year. From £96.0m in September 2026 to £200m in December 2030 is about 19% a year (my arithmetic), as the first set said.

3. The gaps between the second set's cross-check and the Financial Analyst's model. The summary shows no year in which the gap is large, and none of the gaps shows that an input is wrong.

  • EPS (model 1% to 2% above the cross-check: 0.54 against 0.534 in FY2027, 0.91 against 0.891 in FY2031). The model starts from FactSet's 117.9m basic shares at March 2026 and turns each buyback input into shares at the price it holds (1124p). The cross-check uses the diluted weighted average (117.6m in FY2027) and assumed the FY2029 to FY2031 buybacks at rising prices. The model therefore retires about 8.5m shares in FY2029 to FY2031 against about 5.5m in the cross-check (my reconstruction), which is why the gap widens to 2% by FY2031. This is a mechanical result of a flat price against growing earnings, not an input error. Rounding of growth rates adds £0.2m to FY2027 revenue, EBITDA and pre-tax profit.
  • Free cash flow (model higher by 1.7 in FY2027, 1.5 in FY2028, 0.5 in FY2029). The model's FY2027 figure reproduces exactly as EBITDA 87.3, less tax 22.2, plus net financial items 2.9, less capital spending 28.0, lease payments 1.8 and a working-capital outflow of 2.5: 35.7. The cross-check also deducts the cash cost of the Davis Pier post-combination pay (about £1.5m a year to September 2028), which is excluded from adjusted EBITDA and has no input line. It is real cash; the cross-check is the better figure.
  • Net cash at March 2027 (model 69.3; second set about 75). The model's figure is FactSet's net cash after £6.1m of lease liabilities (it starts at 82.9, not the £89.1m of cash). The other two differences cancel: the model pays the whole FY2027 dividend of 31.4p in the year (about £36.8m) where cash paid is the FY2026 final plus the FY2027 interim (about £35.1m), and it omits the Davis Pier pay.
  • Two basis breaks in the model's table that the memo must not quote. The FY2026 column is FactSet's own basis (EBITDA £55.8m, 12.9%; EPS 0.36, after share-based payments), and the forecast columns are the adjusted basis. The model's "+50.7%" EPS growth for FY2027 and the margin step from 12.9% to 15.9% are artefacts. Like for like, FY2026 adjusted EBITDA was £68.3m (15.8%) and adjusted EPS 41.1p, so FY2027 EPS growth is about 30%. The Financial Analyst should show FY2026 on the adjusted basis in the forecasts-of-record table, or mark the break, so that the growth line and the template's trailing-earnings split of the return are not read across two bases.

5. Cautions on the evidence.

  • AlphaSense holds research for only two of the eight houses that cover or covered Kainos (Deutsche Bank and Rothschild & Co Redburn's former coverage). Every "sell-side" statement in the reports is in practice Deutsche Bank's. No investor commentary (social media, fund letters) was found at all.
  • Unreconciled items. The 0.8-point fall in H1 FY2027 adjusted PBT margin (16.3% to 15.5%) is attributed wholly to employer National Insurance by the first set and wholly to lower interest income by one AlphaSense report; neither can be all of it. One report gives H1 FY2027 net finance income as £0.4m (£1.1m a year earlier), where the second set assumed about £1.6m; if the report is right, H1 EBITDA was nearer £43.7m (16.3%) than £42.5m, with no effect on pre-tax profit. The Home Office contract is described as four years in the interim report and 60 months in the presentation. The second set labels central costs "net of interest income"; its arithmetic only reconciles if they are before interest (segment contributions 116.1, less central costs 34.0, plus net financial items 2.9, equals PBT 85.0). Its tables show Digital Services contribution margin at 23.0% in FY2030 while P-04 says it is held at 22.5%.
  • The 26% tax rate guided for FY2027 is the reported effective rate. The adjusted rate has not tracked it closely (my arithmetic: about 26% in FY2026 against 27% reported; about 23% in H1 FY2027 against 24%). I keep the second set's rates and treat the adjusted rate as uncertain by a point either way (each point is 1.4% of EPS).
