Element E1 — Required input change forces D 4 findings
Required Input Change Forces -- We are looking for credible external tailwinds (regulatory, structural, or cyclical) AND active internal change programmes (turnaround, restructuring, re-acceleration); both must be real and sufficiently strong. Avoiding cases where change is incremental or forced.
Judgement summary
  • E1 verdict. D Arkema's external change forces are volatile-or-uncertain rather than compelling — the acrylics windfall is already fading and the construction recovery its own report grades LOW plausibility — while its base is not robust: over half of group sales sit against the most structurally impaired end-market in the cohort, and the shares have already re-rated hard before the case is proven.
1.
E1 RA1 — Change forces? — summary: C Real internal change (leadership succession, resegmentation, self-help) is credible and clean; external tailwinds are genuine but either fading or low-plausibility, a softer picture than Trelleborg's already-delivered portfolio uplift.
1.1
E1 RA1 CQ1 — Strong external change forces / tailwinds? C Upstream acrylic spreads and EV battery PVDF adoption are the two named external tailwinds, and both carry live decay or dependency risk. Asian acrylic spreads fell 3% sequentially in June 2026 after their early-2026 spike, and US spreads fell 7%; Jefferies "expects pricing momentum to peak in Q2 2026, posing margin risks into H2."
1.1.1
Construction, the largest single external force, is graded LOW plausibility by the source report itself: building and construction is 27% of group sales, residential is 55% of that exposure, and broker consensus assigns low near-term probability to a Western recovery given restrictive rates and German industrial production contracting 2.8% year-on-year.
1.1.2
EV battery PVDF adoption is real but MEDIUM plausibility, dependent on gigafactory build-out: Rothschild & Co Redburn "warns EV battery adoption delays would materially depress PVDF performance polymer demand"; the Calvert City facility only started up mid-2026.
1.1.3
Cohort comparison, cross-driven: EMS-Chemie's external driver (automotive lightweighting / metal replacement) is a comparably structural but comparably automotive-cyclical-exposed theme (~50% of EMS-Chemie revenue in automotive); Trelleborg's external tailwind is not really a forward force at all — the portfolio mix shift into medical and aerospace is already executed, delivering a record 18.3% EBITA margin now, not a hoped-for future one; Hexpol's driver (M&A into a $240bn fragmented thermoplastics market) is comparably execution-dependent to Arkema's.
1.1.4
Cross-driver dependency check: the source report's own joint-probability arithmetic across Arkema's independent driver estimates (70%/80%/50%) yields a 28% compound success rate — a low joint probability that several of the positive drivers must co-occur.
1.2
E1 RA1 CQ2 — Strong internal change forces? B Leadership succession, portfolio resegmentation and a credible cost programme are real, largely already-executed, and clean relative to the cohort. Laurent Tellier's COO succession is structured (25 years at Saint-Gobain, named successor already in place for his old role), unlike the abrupt, HIGH-materiality CEO transition Hexpol's own report flags for Henrik Elmin.
1.2.1
The 85% Specialty Materials resegmentation is a genuine structural change, not aspiration: commodity acrylics and legacy refrigerants were regrouped into a separate Primary Materials segment, isolating cyclical exposure for the first time.
1.2.2
The EUR 250 million self-help programme has a credible delivery record: EUR 90 million delivered in 2025 against target, and management is targeting the full programme two years ahead of schedule — a track record the report itself treats as "highly credible based on historical delivery patterns."
1.2.3
Counterpoint inside the cohort — EMS-Chemie's internal change signal is materially worse, not better: five different chief financial officers since 2022 is flagged as one of EMS-Chemie's three most critical weak signals, a governance-continuity problem Arkema does not share.
1.2.4
Held off A: none of Arkema's internal changes are transformational in the way Trelleborg's completed Wheel Systems divestment was — Arkema's resegmentation moved a reporting line, Trelleborg's divestment permanently changed the earnings mix and already shows up in the margin print.
2.
E1 RA2 — Robust base? — summary: D The base fails more of its own tests than it passes: construction concentration is a real headwind (not an absence of one), delivery has required two guidance resets in three years, and the shares have already moved a long way before any of this is proven out.
2.1
E1 RA2 CQ1 — Absence of external headwinds? D This is not a business with an absence of headwinds — over 50% of sales are exposed to weak construction and general industry (Jefferies), on top of a 5.1% negative USD/EUR currency drag in the most recent quarter alone. Add PFAS litigation risk (192 individuals filed suit over the Lyon site in January 2026, ECHA's restriction process due in 2026) and the Vencorex/hydrogen supply shocks that already forced 154 job cuts at Jarrie.
2.1.1
Cohort comparison: every cohort name carries a currency headwind (EMS-Chemie versus the Swiss franc, Trelleborg and Hexpol versus the Swedish krona, both a 9% translation drag in recent quarters), so currency alone does not separate Arkema from the set. Construction concentration does separate it — none of the other three cohort names carry a comparably large single depressed end-market; Trelleborg has already de-risked its most cyclical exposure (agriculture) via divestment.
2.2
E1 RA2 CQ2 — Well-invested base? B This is a genuine relative strength. Around EUR 1 billion is committed across a dozen growth projects — Calvert City's 15% PVDF expansion already online mid-2026, a 20% Changshu PVDF expansion for 2028, the Singapore polyamide facility tripling capacity, Serquigny Pebax +40%, Beaumont DMDS +35% — backed by 3.10% of sales on research and development across 1,800 researchers and 17 centres.
2.2.1
A real, evidenced competitive-timing edge exists, not just capex volume: Syensqo "delayed its $850 million Augusta US PVDF plant indefinitely due to slowing EV adoption and regulatory uncertainties," which Bernstein says "gives Arkema a window to secure major US customer qualifications" — a specific, sourced, peer-relative fact rather than a generic capex claim.
2.2.2
Held off A: the Singapore polyamide ramp is itself a caution on execution risk within this same "well-invested" story — Jefferies notes "new plants are ready, customer demand is not," with project EBITDA below EUR 35 million flagged as the warning threshold — so the base is well-invested but not yet proven to monetise on schedule.
2.3
E1 RA2 CQ3 — Past/present fundamental delivery congruency with thesis? C Management is an honest guidance-resetter, not a consistent deliverer against its original numbers. FY2025 EBITDA of EUR 1,251 million missed the initial FY25 guidance midpoint by -21.8% (calculated) before being met against a revised, lowered range; FY2024 missed its original midpoint by -4.25% (calculated); only FY2023 was in-line against the original guide.
2.3.1
The Q1 2026 beat itself carries a sustainability flag from the source report: "the Q1 beat was heavily driven by temporary March volume strength following weak January and February trading, raising questions about sustainability into the second half" — this is the report's own scepticism, not an external add.
2.3.2
Cohort comparison: EMS-Chemie is characterised as a "conservative sandbagger" that consistently beats deliberately low guidance — stronger delivery congruency evidence than Arkema's pattern of resetting the target itself; Trelleborg's digest carries no flagged miss; Hexpol's own report explicitly grades its most recent quarter "MIXED" (slight misses on revenue and EBIT), putting it in the same tier as Arkema on this specific test.
2.4
E1 RA2 CQ4 — Large change-from-current / mispricing? D The "still-cheap, still-to-re-rate" case is weak on the evidence actually in the reports. The shares are up 18.9% over six months and 18.6% over nine months, and moved 5.25% on the single day the master-dashboard price snapshot was taken (30-Jul-26) — a stock that has already re-rated hard before Triaging even began.
2.4.1
The discount narrative inside the source report is internally inconsistent, which itself argues for caution rather than conviction: one line states the historical 23% discount to specialty peers "has narrowed to 7%," while a separate line states Jefferies puts the current discount at 21% — a same-report contradiction flagged here rather than silently resolved (see F.2).
2.4.2
The forward P/E of 10.3x sits at the 54th percentile of Arkema's own ten-year range — roughly the middle of its own history, not a statistically cheap entry point, even before any cohort-relative comparison.
2.5
E1 RA2 CQ5 — Low trading multiple? C Low in absolute cross-sector terms, unremarkable on Arkema's own history. 10.3x forward P/E looks inexpensive next to typical pure-specialty multiples in the high teens, but the 54th-percentile own-history reading above is the more decision-relevant number for a Triaging-stage entry-point read.
2.5.1
Genuine gap, flagged rather than papered over: none of the three live cohort peers' current P/E multiples were sourced in this pass (EMSN-CH, TREL.B-SE and HPOL.B-SE compact digests do not carry a comparable current-P/E line, and their own master-dashboard factset-valuation.json records were not pulled this session). A true cohort-relative multiple comparison is a data gap for Early-Stage Assessment, not something to estimate here.
3.
Counter-view considered (E1): The strongest case against a D here is that Arkema's self-help delivery record (EUR 90m of EUR 250m target already banked, historically ahead of schedule) and its evidenced PVDF timing edge over Syensqo are exactly the kind of company-specific, non-macro change forces the Triaging test is meant to reward, and a pure focus on construction exposure understates how isolated that exposure now is post-resegmentation. Rejected as the primary read because the two RA2 tests that most directly measure "is there a robust base to build on" — absence of headwinds and genuine mispricing — both land at D on their own evidence, and the case-change story still leans on an acrylics tailwind the source report itself expects to fade into H2 2026.

Element E5 — Thematic fit D 6 findings
Thematic Fit -- We are looking for positive fit against all three active portfolio thematics: T1 (bear market resilience), T2 (AI disruption insulation or tailwind), T3 (Iran / oil-price environment).
Judgement summary
  • E5 verdict. D Two of the four required scenarios are genuinely, richly evidenced in the source reports (the Middle East/energy shock and the macro paradigm-fit tests) and both land negative-to-mixed; the third (bear-market resilience) is evidenced and also weak; the fourth (AI disruption) has almost no direct evidence in either report and is graded on a thin base, flagged as such rather than argued with false confidence.
1.
E5 RA1 — Suitability for bear market / economic downturn: D Real defensive characteristics exist (geographic balance, working-capital discipline, a 2023 downturn where guidance was actually met) but they sit on top of the weakest margin cushion and highest leverage in the cohort, which is what would matter most in an actual downturn.
1.1
E5 RA1 CQ1 — Does the earnings base hold up in a genuine downturn? D The direct evidence is mixed but tilts negative: Arkema was "one of the few diversified chemical players to fully deliver on its initial full-year guidance range throughout the deep cyclical downturn of 2023," a genuine positive data point. Group EBITDA still fell 17% to EUR 1,251 million in the FY2025 downturn year — "demonstrating superior resilience vs Wacker's 42% collapse," but a 17% fall is still a 17% fall, not resilience in absolute terms.
1.1.1
Leverage is the sharper problem going into any downturn test: net debt and hybrids at 2.8x trailing EBITDA is explicitly "materially higher than peer Clariant's 2.03x," constraining the balance-sheet flexibility a downturn would demand.
1.1.2
Commodity intermediate exposure is a direct, named downturn amplifier: "low-cycle monomer margins drag down group profitability mechanically during macroeconomic downturns" (Rothschild & Co Redburn) and roughly 50% of group revenue remains commodity-exposed, "materially more exposed to cyclical intermediate volatility" than pure-play peers Sika and Syensqo who were named explicitly as the comparison.
1.1.3
Genuine mitigants, not dismissed: geographic balance across North America (34%), Europe (33%) and Asia (28%) is designed "to protect against regional macroeconomic recessions"; working-capital discipline (88% cash-to-EBITDA conversion vs a 70% target) is a real cash lever a downturn would let management pull.
1.1.4
Cohort comparison: EMS-Chemie's 29.1% EBIT margin and Trelleborg's record 18.3% EBITA margin both sit well above Arkema's 13.8% EBITDA margin — a materially thicker cushion before either peer would face the kind of guidance-reset pressure Arkema has already shown twice in three years even in a milder environment than a true downturn.
2.
E5 RA2 — Suitability for AI disruption [C, thin evidence — flagged]: Neither source report discusses artificial-intelligence-driven disruption directly; grading this from a genuinely absent evidence base risks manufacturing a view the sources do not support, so this is graded cautiously and the gap is stated plainly rather than papered over.
2.1
E5 RA2 CQ1 — Is Arkema exposed to AI-driven demand growth or AI-driven substitution risk? C The only concretely sourced link is a data-center cooling and thermal-management platform, "targeting over 100 million euros in sales by 2030" — real, but immaterial in scale against a roughly EUR 9 billion revenue base (about 1%). Polyimide films for smartphones and electronics via PI Advanced Materials is a tangential, non-AI-specific electronics exposure.
2.1.1
No evidence of disruption risk either way: neither report flags AI-driven substitution of Arkema's own products or processes, nor AI-accelerated competitor R&D, as a threat; this absence is itself informative (a specialty materials business is not an obvious AI-disruption target) but is not the same as positive evidence of "suitability."
2.1.2
Held at C, not higher: a single, small, forward-dated revenue line (2030 target) is a real but minor optionality, not evidence of a structural AI-driven tailwind comparable to, say, the PVDF/EV-battery story which is separately and much more richly sourced under E1.
2.1.3
Explicitly ungradeable-with-more-confidence: a robust RA2 read would need dedicated research (a self-initiated query is the right tool here, not an assumption) — flagged as a genuine candidate for a follow-up query at Early-Stage Assessment rather than guessed at now.
3.
E5 RA3 — Suitability for Iran oil-price induced inflation or demand deterioration: C This scenario is, unusually, already partly LIVE in the source material rather than hypothetical — the "late February 2026 Middle East crisis" is cited repeatedly as a real, dated event, and Arkema's exposure to it is genuinely two-sided.
3.1
E5 RA3 CQ1 — Does an oil/energy price shock help or hurt the case? C Both directions are directly evidenced in the same report, which is itself the finding: the shock has already delivered a windfall (the acrylic-spread spike that drove the Q1 2026 beat is explicitly tied to "dependency: macro supply disruptions") while simultaneously "the late February 2026 Middle East crisis inflated global supply chain, energy and logistics costs" group-wide. The structural cost disadvantage is separately sourced and would only worsen in a fresh shock: "European gas prices remain over four times higher than highly advantaged US shale gas levels" (Jefferies).
3.1.1
A genuine relative-benefit angle exists and should not be discounted: "inflated Asian energy costs diminish import pressure from Asia, supporting European asset utilization" (Bernstein) — a European-based producer can gain competitively if the shock hits Asian competitors harder.
3.1.2
Demand-destruction risk is the other side and is explicit: sustained energy inflation would extend, not relieve, the German industrial production contraction (-2.8% YoY) and Western construction weakness already dragging the case, per the same driver analysis underlying E1 RA1.
3.1.3
Held at C, not D or B: genuinely two-sided with real evidence on both sides in the same source, rather than a clean directional read — a further oil-price spike plausibly helps the upstream acrylics windfall continue a little longer while simultaneously hurting the construction-linked 50%+ of sales; net effect is ambiguous, not clearly positive or negative.
4.
E5 RA4 — Paradigm fit from business perspective summary: D This Required Attribute is directly and explicitly answered by the source material's own words, and the answer is a structural mismatch with the current environment, not a resilience story.
4.1
E5 RA4 CQ1 — Does Arkema's business model fit favourably with the current macro paradigm? D The Business Foundation report states plainly that "nominal global chemical industry growth (excluding pharmaceuticals) tracks global GDP growth closely at a 1.63x ratio" — the sector is a geared, not hedged, play on GDP. The same report's own §10 verdict: "Arkema's heavy exposure to cyclical construction and manufacturing creates material headwinds in a higher-for-longer interest rate environment."
4.1.1
This is not a generic macro statement — it is Arkema-specific and current: the 1.63x GDP gearing sits on top of the same >50%-of-sales construction and general-industry concentration already identified as the weakest point in E1 RA2, so the paradigm-fit problem and the base-robustness problem are the same underlying exposure viewed two ways, not two independent negatives.
4.1.2
No credible offsetting paradigm-fit positive was found in either source report — the growth adjacencies (batteries, data centres, Pebax, polyimide films) are real but are described throughout as niches inside a group still majority-exposed to the GDP-geared commodity-and-construction base, not a paradigm shift away from it.
5.
Counter-view considered (E5): The strongest case against a D verdict is that three of Arkema's four growth adjacencies (batteries/PVDF, data centres, high-performance polyamides) are structurally counter-cyclical to the GDP-linked commodity base being graded down here, and a thematic read anchored only on the legacy 50% could underweight how fast that mix is shifting (85% Specialty Materials by 2026). Rejected as the primary read at Triaging because the >50% commodity/construction-and-industrial exposure is still the majority of revenue TODAY, the mix shift is a target for 2026-2028 not a present fact, and three of the four required scenarios (RA1, RA3, RA4) each independently surfaced the same structural vulnerability from a different angle — a convergence across independent tests, not a single pessimistic read amplified three times.
6.
Downstream actions (E5): (1) Run a self-initiated research query on AI-linked specialty-materials demand (data-centre thermal management, semiconductor-adjacent polymers) before Early-Stage Assessment, since RA2 is currently graded on a genuinely thin base; (2) At Early-Stage Assessment, re-test RA3 explicitly against how the Middle East situation has actually evolved since this Triaging pass, since it is a live, dated, fast-moving input rather than a stable structural read.
