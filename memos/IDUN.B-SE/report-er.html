Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Sell-side attention before the third-quarter update centered on whether Idun Industrier could rebound from its weak second-quarter performance.
  2. Analysts closely monitored the upward trend of the Swedish Manufacturing PMI as a key leading indicator for organic growth.
  3. Elevated leverage and the integration of recent high-margin acquisitions remained central to market discussions regarding capital allocation.
  4. Valuation comparisons highlighted that Idun Industrier traded at a notable discount to its larger serial acquirer peers.
1.
Focused operations: Idun Industrier acquires high-quality small and medium-sized manufacturing, industrial trading, and industrial service businesses, often with high market shares in their respective niches. The decentralized group has a portfolio of approximately 20 companies, with the funeral coffin market leader Fredahl Rydens being the largest subsidiary. Corporate managers encourage co-ownership with entrepreneurs in group companies to align long-term interests with those of external shareholders. Decentralized operations rely heavily on subsidiary employees who remain close to customers and suppliers.
2.
High concentration: Idun Industrier operates primarily within the Swedish and Nordic capital goods and industrial services markets. Sweden remains the company's largest market, accounting for 73% of re-trailing-twelvemonth sales. The broader Nordic region represents more than 90% of consolidated revenues, which exposes the company heavily to regional industrial production cycles. Decentralized regional focus distinguishes the group from more international peers.
3.
Solid expansion: Idun Industrier grew net sales by 4.8% to SEK 2,298 million in 2025, up from net sales of SEK 2,192 million in 2024. Historical net sales reached SEK 2,063 million in 2023. Acquired companies contributed the majority of sales growth over this period. The company targets at least fifteen percent annual growth in operating profit EBITA.
4.
Slower profitability: Idun Industrier achieved EBITA of SEK 316 million in 2025, representing a 2.4% increase compared to SEK 308 million in 2024. Historical EBITA stood at SEK 283 million in 2023. The group's adjusted EBITA margin contracted slightly to 13.7% in 2025, compared with 14.1% in 2024. Management expressed confidence that recent acquisitions would gradually lift margins back toward historical levels.
5.
Improving earnings: Idun Industrier increased goodwill-adjusted earnings per share by 19.8% to SEK 13.9 in 2025, up from SEK 11.6 in 2024. Optimized interest expenses supported the positive development. Net debt rose to SEK 873 million in 2025 from SEK 665 million in 2024. Financial leverage remained under control at three point four times EBITDA in 2025.
6.
Lower scale: Idun Industrier is considerably smaller than established Swedish serial acquirers such as Lifco and Indutrade. Lifco achieved net sales of SEK 28,251 million and EBITA of SEK 6,318 million in 2025. Indutrade delivered net sales of SEK 32,229 million and EBITA of SEK 4,446 million in 2025. Idun Industrier also carries a lower EBITA margin profile than Lifco's 22.4% in 2025.
7.
Structural differences: Idun Industrier reports financial statements under the Swedish K3 standard, which complicates direct comparisons with IFRS-reporting peers. The group owns approximately 85% of its consolidated EBITA, whereas peers own nearly 95% to 100%. Decentralized minority ownership aligns interests with subsidiary managers but hampers near-term growth potential. Analysts adjust valuations to reflect these structural nuances.
8.
Disappointing returns: Idun Industrier experienced share price underperformance over the 12 months preceding October 2026. The stock delivered a negative 1.23% absolute performance and a negative 12.13% relative performance against the market. Carl Korsheden of DNB Carnegie noted in late September 2026 that the stock suffered a significant de-rating over the year. High leverage and slower organic growth during 2025 contributed to this weak performance.
9.
Short-term weakness: Idun Industrier's share price traded down by 14% year-to-date in April 2026. The stock continued to trade weakly into its second-quarter report, declining by 4% year-to-date by August 2026. Carl Korsheden of DNB Carnegie stated that the share price notably underperformed adjusted EPS revisions. Divergent market trends created an attractive valuation multiple of 12 times enterprise value to EBITA ahead of the thirdquarter report.
10.
Fluctuating expectations: DNB Carnegie sponsored research maintained an optimistic view of Idun Industrier in mid-2026. Carl Korsheden of DNB Carnegie raised the company's fair value range to SEK 355-470 in May 2026 after a strong Q1 report. Carl Korsheden maintained a fair value range of SEK 360- 460 in July 2026. Ambitious fair value ranges reflected expectations of accelerating organic growth and acquisitions.
11.
Lowered bar: DNB Carnegie adjusted its valuation range downward following a weakerthan-expected second-quarter report in August 2026. Carl Korsheden of DNB Carnegie decreased the fair value range to SEK 310-450. Carl Korsheden fine-tuned the range to SEK 320-430 in late September 2026. Valuation adjustments followed postponed assumptions for organic growth.
12.
Cautiously optimistic: DNB Carnegie retained a BUY equivalent rating on Idun Industrier throughout the six months leading up to October 2026. Carl Korsheden of DNB Carnegie emphasized that the company possessed a high-quality portfolio and a solid operational track record. The analyst expected a strong third-quarter rebound to restore confidence. Positive outlooks was supported by improving Swedish manufacturing purchasing managers indexes.
13.
Other side of the story: Elevated leverage and a heavy reliance on acquisitions could expose Idun Industrier to significant operational and financial risks. If credit markets tighten or acquisition multiples expand, the company may struggle to find attractive targets. Consequently, any disruption to the M&A flywheel would directly impair the long-term earnings per share trajectory.
