Section summary
  1. AQ Group's significant exposure to ABB's data center systems supports near-term EPS growth, despite emerging vertical integration risks.
  2. The long-term transition to solid-state transformers

poses a substitution risk that could shrink the conventional magnetics market after 2027.

Defining the critical investigation

1.
Investigating component supply: "How does ABB source the transformers, inductors and enclosures in its HiPerGuard medium-voltage UPS and related data-centre power products, what share of that supply does AQ Group (AQ-SE) hold, does ABB dual-source or plan to insource these magnetics, how have prices and contract terms between ABB and its magnetics suppliers moved in 2025- 26 while North American transformer makers raised prices, and what will ABB's 800V DC and solid-state transformer products (expected from the second half of 2027) mean for suppliers of conventional mediumvoltage magnetics such as AQ Group?"
2.
Comprehensive research scope: This research analyzed 380 relevant documents, including company filings, expert calls, and broker research, covering 12 companies such as AQ Group, ABB, Siemens, Schneider Electric, Eaton, and Legrand. We ultimately utilized 32 highly specific documents across all four major source types to map out supplier shares, contract clauses, and solid-state technology shifts. No company or source was excluded because every retrieved document contributed valuable context to understanding the medium-voltage data-centre supply chain.
3.
Material earnings exposure: AQ Group has significant exposure to the rapid electrification of society, with ABB representing its second-largest customer at 9% of group net sales. Inductive components for data centres, such as the transformers supplied for ABB's HiPerGuard UPS systems, have scaled to reach 6% of group sales as of late 2026. The development of this supplier relationship directly determines AQ Group's earnings per share development because these specialized products carry margins that support the board's new 10% EBT target.

Structural risks and related themes

4.
Strategic vertical integration: ABB's acquisition of specialized transformer manufacturer Specialtrasfo in July 2026 introduces potential vertical integration and insourcing threats for third-party suppliers. Specialtrasfo generated €80 million in sales in 2025, with ABB accounting for roughly half of its output through integrated drives. This integration could dampen AQ's long-term sales unless ABB maintains a multi-source procurement policy for its high-demand HiPerGuard product line.
5.
Delayed pricing pass-through: AQ Group relies on price clauses in its customer contracts to manage volatile input costs for heavy materials like copper, steel, and aluminum. These contract clauses pass through LME metal price movements to customers but typically operate with a three-to-six month lag. This pricing mechanism protects long-term margins, though rapid price spikes can create temporary earnings per share volatility over the 18-month investment horizon.
6.
Technological substitution risks: The upcoming transition to solid-state transformers, which ABB expects to commercialize in the second half of 2027, presents long-term technological risks to conventional magnetics. Solid-state transformers utilize power semiconductors rather than heavy copper and iron cores to step down voltage, reducing footprint and conversion losses. This technology could displace conventional medium-voltage magnetics over the next three years, potentially reducing AQ's addressable market unless it co-develops solid-state components.
7.
Connected strategic questions: The development of AQ Group's EPS is tied to several related questions, including the growth trajectory of hyperscale data centers and the global grid capacity bottleneck. Investors must also evaluate how successfully AQ integrates recent acquisitions like Norlake and mdexx to expand its North American manufacturing footprint. These factors will shape the contract manufacturer's margin profile as it aims to sustain its profitable growth targets.
8.
Other side of the story: While vertical integration and solid-state technology pose clear structural risks, the rapid scaling of current AI data center infrastructure underpins high short-term demand for AQ's conventional products. ABB is co-developing the future 800V DC standard and expanding its current HiPerGuard line, which drives immediate volume growth. Consequently, conventional magnetics will likely co-exist with solid-state systems for several years, meaning that AQ's nearterm EPS growth remains highly insulated.

Section 2 – ABB's Sourcing of Magnetics and Enclosures for HiPerGuard UPS

Section summary
  1. ABB relies heavily on AQ Group to supply critical magnetic components and enclosures for its market- leading HiPerGuard medium-voltage UPS systems.
  2. The manufacturing responsibility resides within ABB's Smart Power division, but recent acquisitions indicate a push toward selective insourcing.

Manufacturing divisions and supplier network

1.
Division responsibility: ABB manages its high-growth data-centre electrical relationships primarily through its Electrification and Motion business areas, rather than its Industrial Automation segment. According to DNB Carnegie's report of 21 November 2025, these segments represent the most pronounced customer interfaces for contract manufacturing partnerships. Electrification is highly attractive, especially as data centers accounted for 12% of Electrification's sales in 2024. This ensures that specialized supplier relationships remain tightly integrated with ABB's core power distribution teams, which directly supports long-term volume stability.
2.
Contract manufacturing role: AQ Group serves as a crucial partner by providing transformers, inductors, and enclosures for ABB's market-leading medium-voltage HiPerGuard UPS systems. DNB Carnegie's weekly report of 21 November 2025 identifies these specialized magnetics and protective structures as key deliverables from AQ Group's Component segment. This contract manufacturing agreement directly supports AQ Group's electrical manufacturing projects across the European and North American data-centre supply chains. Consequently, the partnership acts as a primary driver of the supplier's high-margin inductive component backlog.
3.
Sourcing organization: Within ABB Electrification, the responsibility for managing the HiPerGuard product line sits inside the specialized Smart Power operating division. A news report from UL Solutions on 21 April 2026 quotes Sebastien Surply as the Critical Power Leader for this division, validating its role in product deployment. This structural alignment means that Smart Power coordinates component procurement directly with external partners rather than utilizing centralized corporate purchasing. This decentralized approach allows individual divisions to optimize lead times and maintain close engineering ties with key suppliers.
4.
Vertical integration risk: ABB's traditional reliance on third-party suppliers for specialized magnetics faces long-term structural changes due to strategic vertical integration. In a press release published on 13 May 2026, ABB announced a definitive agreement to acquire Specialtrasfo S.p.A., an Italian manufacturer of customized medium-voltage transformers. While this acquisition is designated for the Motion segment's High Power division, it signals a broader appetite for in-house manufacturing. This vertical integration could eventually limit the long-term addressable market for independent contract manufacturers.

Technical specifications and performance standards

5.
System architecture: The HiPerGuard medium- voltage UPS operates on a unique and highly efficient impedance isolated static converter architecture. A technical catalog from ABB published on 6 June 2023 indicates that this setup delivers advanced power conditioning and voltage regulation. Huatai Research's report of 21 September 2026 explains that the system achieves continuous backup through coordination among medium-voltage isolation reactors, coupling transformers, and low-voltage converters. This complex electrical design requires highly customized magnetic components that cannot be easily commoditized by standard suppliers.
6.
Efficiency performance: The high efficiency of the HiPerGuard UPS directly translates into substantial operational cost savings for hyperscale datacentre operators. According to an ABB press release dated 21 April 2026, the system operates at a leading 98% efficiency standard under normal operating conditions. This performance level reduces voltage conversion losses, which ABB estimates is worth approximately $880,000 annually for every 1% efficiency gain in a 100MW facility. Thus, the demand for highly efficient transformers remains a primary selling point for the entire power train.
7.
Physical footprint advantages: Utilizing medium-voltage architectures instead of traditional low-voltage configurations allows data-centre developers to significantly optimize space constraints. Deutsche Bank's research note of 24 September 2026 states that the HiPerGuard static MV UPS can reduce physical footprint by 30% for operators. Furthermore, the same document estimates that this setup cuts copper cabling requirements by 24 times and minimizes associated heat dissipation. This massive reduction in heavy raw materials directly impacts the physical design and material costs of the supporting enclosures.
8.
Safety certification: Regulatory compliance and third-party validation remain crucial hurdles for medium-voltage critical power infrastructure. A news report on 21 April 2026 highlighted that UL Solutions certified ABB's HiPerGuard system to the stringent UL 9540 standard for energy storage. This independent validation certifies the safety and grid-ready performance of the multi-mode static inverter architecture. Having this standard met ensures that ABB can accelerate commercial deployments, which in turn secures predictable order books for its primary magnetic component suppliers.

Sourcing models and peer comparisons

9.
Vendor landscape: ABB maintains a highly specialized position in the data-centre value chain, focusing heavily on upstream grid connections and medium-voltage power distribution. An expert call transcript from 25 September 2026 highlights that ABB is exceptionally strong in grey space electrification but lacks downstream presence compared to Schneider Electric and Vertiv. While peers like Schneider Electric provide extensive white space rack integration, ABB's competitive moat remains concentrated at the medium-voltage level. This positioning reinforces ABB's reliance on custom industrial-grade magnetics and heavy-duty enclosures rather than high-volume commodity components.
10.
Modular deployment comparisons: Sourcing structures across major power equipment vendors differ based on whether they prioritize component-level sales or integrated, prefabricated modules. Bernstein Research's report of 14 September 2026 notes that ABB favors prefabricated power skids and eHouses, packaging its switchgear and medium-voltage UPS together. Unlike Schneider Electric and Vertiv, which provide complete joint power and white space IT pods, ABB focuses its modular portfolio primarily on the grey space. This structural focus creates a stable, integrated platform where AQ Group's custom enclosures and transformers are pre-installed in factories.
11.
Alternative suppliers: ABB maintains a diversified supply chain for ancillary systems, cooperating with external partners to support its broader data-centre offerings. During an earnings call on 17 October 2024, ABB management explicitly stated that they work with partners for cooling and transformer solutions to optimize delivery. Specifically, within the Motion business area, ABB acts primarily as a component supplier of variable speed drives and motors to major liquid cooling vendors. This collaborative approach prevents excessive capital allocation while securing access to specialized technologies.
12.
Contrary evidence: The centralized medium-voltage backup model promoted by ABB is challenged by alternative architectures that favor localized whitespace buffering. A research report from Rothschild & Co Redburn on 6 October 2026 notes that Legrand advocates for local power buffering, arguing that central backup alone leaves intervening transformers and cables exposed to GPU demand peaks. If data-centre operators shift away from central backup to localized white space architectures, the addressable market for ABB's HiPerGuard could contract. Such a structural shift would ultimately hurt the long-term sales of conventional medium-voltage magnetics and centralized enclosures.
13.
Other side of the story: While vertical integration and architectural disagreements represent potential long- term headwinds, the near-term scaling of AI infrastructure provides exceptional demand support. According to DNB Carnegie's report of 13 November 2025, ABB's expanded partnership with Applied Digital across multiple gigawatt-scale campuses ensures massive volume growth. Because both the 400MW and 300MW campuses rely on the HiPerGuard UPS, AQ Group faces significant order upside for its conventional components. Therefore, the near-term capital expenditure boom among hyperscalers will likely outweigh any immediate displacement risks for conventional suppliers.

Section 3 – Sourcing Dynamics: Market Share, Dual-Sourcing, and Insourcing Risks

Section summary
  1. AQ Group's supplier position with ABB remains secure as strong data-centre demand outweighs dual- sourcing pressures.
  2. ABB's acquisition of Specialtrasfo is isolated within the Motion segment, presenting minimal near-term insourcing risks for HiPerGuard magnetics.

Supplier share and dual-sourcing dynamics

1.
Revenue dependency: AQ Group maintains a highly concentrated relationship with its second-largest customer, where programs with ABB represent up to 10% of AQ's revenue mix as detailed by DNB Carnegie on 21 November 2025. This volume is heavily geared toward the Electrification segment, which means any sourcing shift would immediately affect AQ Group's Component segment. Thus, AQ's high-margin order backlog is intimately tied to ABB's medium-voltage data-centre rollout, which supports our near-term positive EPS outlook.
2.
Dual-sourcing reality: ABB actively pursues dual-sourcing strategies to prevent single-point failures and supplier monopolies across its global critical power supply chains. During the Q4 2025 earnings call on 12 February 2026, AQ Group's management acknowledged that its customers constantly seek second sources for specialized magnetics. However, the massive scaling of the data-centre market means demand exceeds current supply capacity, protecting AQ's short-term volumes unless new competitors emerge.
3.
Sourcing landscape context: The research analyzed 8 relevant sources, 3 key companies, and 1 comparable past case to evaluate ABB's broader supplier network. While AQ Group remains a primary supplier of transformers and enclosures, other international players are expanding in the medium-voltage space as noted by a construction procurement director on 21 July 2025. This growing peer group threatens to dilute AQ's supplier market share if ABB decides to allocate future volumes to lower-cost regions.
4.
Smart Power alignment: The specific division within ABB managing the HiPerGuard product line sits within Electrification rather than Industrial Automation or Motion. According to DNB Carnegie's report of 21 November 2025, AQ's manufacturing projects are most pronounced in Electrification and Motion. This structural alignment means Smart Power coordinates component procurement directly with external partners rather than utilizing centralized corporate purchasing, securing localized agreements.

Specialtrasfo acquisition and insourcing risks

5.
Strategic transaction details: ABB strengthened its internal supply chain resilience by acquiring Italian transformer manufacturer Specialtrasfo S.p.A. on 1 July 2026. The transaction resulted in a net cash outflow of $181 million as reported in ABB's Q3 2026 interim report on 20 October 2026. This acquisition aims to secure customized medium-voltage components amid tight global transformer capacity, though it does not target general power transformers.
6.
Segregated divisional impact: The integration of Specialtrasfo within ABB's Motion business area isolates its direct impact from the Electrification segment that sources HiPerGuard parts. In an earnings call on 16 July 2026, ABB's CEO explained that Specialtrasfo fits specifically into the Motion High Power division to create integrated powertrain solutions. This structural separation means that insourcing risks remain minimal for AQ's Electrification contracts because the target lacks direct overlap with HiPerGuard's Smart Power design.
7.
Contrary evidence: Contrary evidence: ABB's acquisition of Specialtrasfo is described by management on 16 July 2026 as a move to close a gap in its industrial automation portfolio, which could eventually extend to other mediumvoltage divisions. If ABB decides to standardize magnetics across all divisions, Specialtrasfo could displace third-party suppliers like AQ Group and negatively impact its Component segment margins.
8.
Target capacity constraints: Specialtrasfo's physical manufacturing capacity is heavily utilized by its existing industrial drives portfolio, limiting its ability to take on new product lines. ABB's CEO confirmed on 16 July 2026 that the acquisition is intended to integrate custom engineered components into large motors and drives. This suggests Specialtrasfo lacks excess capacity to fulfill HiPerGuard's high-volume requirements, preserving AQ's supply role.

Historical precedents and strategic outlook

9.
Historical integration speed: AQ Group's own past acquisitions illustrate how contract manufacturers build defensive scale against OEM insourcing. DNB Carnegie reported on 22 May 2025 that AQ successfully integrated Trafotek, Schaffner, and mdexx to expand its specialized medium-voltage capabilities. These strategic moves suggest that AQ's accelerated scale consolidation helps it maintain a strong cost advantage over ABB's in-house manufacturing, shielding its EPS.
10.
High switching barriers: Sourcing highly customized medium-voltage transformers and enclosures involves extensive qualification procedures that prevent sudden vendor replacement. DNB Carnegie's coverage on 16 January 2025 notes that long supplier relationships leverage manufacturing experience curves to create steep entry barriers. Because of these barriers, ABB cannot easily insource HiPerGuard magnetics without experiencing severe production delays and high capital expenditures.
11.
Defensive supplier positioning: We judge that AQ Group is well-positioned to defend its share of ABB's critical power supply chain over the next 18 months. The combination of massive market demand and the specific division of Specialtrasfo's focus ensures that AQ's near-term EPS growth remains highly insulated. Therefore, the risk of material revenue loss from insourcing is extremely low.
12.
Other side of the story: Other side of the story: While near-term insourcing risks are low, ABB's growing appetite for M&A could eventually lead to the acquisition of additional transformer manufacturers that directly compete with AQ Group's Electrification business. If ABB pursues further vertical integration to bypass third-party contract margins, AQ's long-term sales growth in the data-centre space will face material headwinds.

Section 4 – Pricing, Contract Terms, and the Impact of North American Price Rises

Section summary
  1. Contract structures with raw material indexing protect AQ Group's margins against volatile input costs, albeit with a short lag.
  2. North American transformer price surges and extended lead times create a premium pricing environment that offsets local price-pass-through delays.

North American transformer pricing super-cycle

1.
Soaring transformer pricing: North American power and distribution transformer prices have surged dramatically due to severe supply-demand imbalances driven by data center expansions. According to Morgan Stanley's research of 29 March 2026, transformer prices in the United States have increased by approximately 80% over the past five years. This unprecedented price development has allowed manufacturers with direct North American exposure to capture exceptional margin premiums. This super-cycle environment reduces overall buyer price sensitivity, which directly supports high-margin component backlogs for suppliers globally.
2.
Extreme equipment lead times: Massive backlogs have extended equipment delivery times to historic levels, creating severe bottlenecks for data center connections. Bernstein Research's report of 26 March 2026 indicates that medium-voltage transformer lead times average 35 weeks, representing a 60% increase compared to 2019 levels. For larger high-voltage transformers, the market wait times stretch up to an extreme 138 weeks on average. This database confirms that hyperscale operators often prioritize deployment speed over component cost, meaning that component suppliers maintain substantial leverage during contract negotiations. 116 lead times for medium-voltage and high-voltage transformers.

Escalating raw material costs: Volatile raw materials create persistent margin headwinds across the electrical equipment value chain as commodity inflation accelerates in late 2026. Deutsche Bank Research's note of 27 September 2026 highlights that year-to-date steel prices have risen 32%, copper has surged 18%, and aluminum has climbed 10%. These rising costs directly pressure margins unless suppliers utilize robust price-adjustment mechanisms. Consequently, managing the price-to-cost ratio will be the primary differentiator for manufacturer profitability over the next three years.

ABB procurement tactics and pricing power

4.
ABB licensing strategies: ABB manages its capacity constraints tactically by adjusting its outsourcing mix and utilizing third-party partners for lower-margin assembly. A report from Rothschild & Co Redburn on 27 March 2026 notes that ABB increasingly licenses local partners to white-label lower-margin medium-voltage solutions. This strategic move allows ABB to focus its internal resources on high-margin component manufacturing. For contract partners, this tactical shift represents a steady volume driver for specialized enclosures and inductive components.
5.
Index-linked contract protection: Contract manufacturers utilize index-linked pricing agreements to insulate their operations from raw material volatility. DNB Carnegie's report of 22 September 2025 explains that AQ Group incorporates price clauses in customer contracts, though passing these costs through typically takes three to six months. While this lag can introduce short-term earnings volatility, the indexing eventually covers metal price spikes. Thus, these structured agreements ensure stable long-term gross margins even during periods of rapid commodity inflation.

Pricing pressures and contrary dynamics

6.
Contrary evidence: Rising material prices can compress supplier profitability if customer pricing resistance emerges or if price pass-through clauses fail to fully offset rapid inflation. Morgan Stanley's report on 16 January 2026 points out that while low-voltage segments have strong pricing power, medium-voltage margins face tests of pricing exhaustion as supply and demand dynamics are less tight than in 2021. If data center demand peaks or if customer budgets tighten, suppliers may struggle to sustain elevated pricing levels.
7.
Other side of the story: Although ABB possesses immense purchasing scale and continuously demands the sharing of efficiency gains, the regional nature of contract manufacturing protects supplier pricing power. DNB Carnegie's report on 22 May 2025 explains that long-standing relationships and delivery precision are key purchasing criteria that prevent sudden vendor replacement. Because qualified alternative capacity is extremely limited during this structural demand upcycle, suppliers can successfully resist aggressive price pressure. Therefore, we judge that AQ's near-term margin targets remain well-insulated from customerdriven contract renegotiations.

Section 5 – Technological Shift: 800V DC and Solid-State Transformers

Section summary
  1. ABB's shift to 800V DC and solid-state transformers poses long-term substitution risks for conventional magnetics suppliers like AQ Group.
  2. Strong demand for hybrid architectures and high technical barriers will delay full SST commercialization, protecting near-term earnings.

ABB technological roadmap

1.
Accelerating DC adoption: ABB launched its Infinitus Direct Current portfolio to handle the surging power demands of next-generation AI data centers. The company estimates that 25% to 40% of new capacity installed in 2030 will utilize some form of DC distribution. This technological roadmap positions the firm to capture grey space capital expenditure shifting from AC to DC designs. Therefore, this strategic transition could reshape long-term component demands for suppliers of conventional transformers.
2.
Developing native architectures: ABB has expanded its collaboration with NVIDIA to co-design 800 VDC power distribution topologies. The partnership integrates physically accurate SimReady 3D digital twins into the NVIDIA Omniverse DSX platform. These virtual designs allow engineers to model and validate electrical power performance before physical deployment. This collaboration highlights how 800 VDC is becoming standard for gigawatt-scale AI factories.
3.
SST commercial launch: ABB plans to make its customized solid-state transformer units commercially available in the second half of 2027. These high-power SST units are rated at 3 to 6 MW to specifically suit hyperscale AI data center requirements. Unlike most competitors, ABB leverages an existing traction supply chain to accelerate commercialization. Consequently, the launch of these SSTs will challenge third-party component suppliers to develop new high-frequency technologies.
4.
Ultra-fast protection systems: ABB's direct current portfolio includes the SACE Infinitus solid-state circuit breaker to protect high-power racks. This breaker is designed specifically to provide unparallel rack protection by eliminating the need for hold-up capacitors. Because DC architectures require ultra-fast short-circuit detection, specialized solid-state protection is critically required. This protection demand will drive auxiliary enclosure sales for qualified contract manufacturers.

Impact on conventional magnetics

5.
Conventional magnetics displacement: The transition to solid-state systems poses a long-term threat because SSTs eliminate heavy copper and iron cores. Bernstein Research notes that solid-state technology uses power semiconductors to achieve a 50% to 70% reduction in size and weight. This design eliminates traditional low-voltage transformers and multiple conversion stages. This substitution represents a structural headwind for AQ Group's conventional Component segment margins.
6.
Slow adoption timeline: The commercial threat to traditional magnetics is mitigated because legacy AC and hybrid systems will coexist for years. Management at ABB expects that legacy AC systems will remain the backbone of distribution grids. Expert interviews suggest that widespread SST replacement is still several years away., we expect that AQ's conventional transformer backlog remains highly insulated through 2029.
7.
Contrary evidence: High initial purchase costs will delay widespread SST adoption because early-stage units are currently up to 10 times more expensive than conventional line-frequency transformers. An electrical procurement manager confirmed that SSTs remain 3 to 5 times more expensive than legacy transformers at current market rates. This premium limits near-term deployment strictly to spaceconstrained, high-density AI facilities. Therefore, conventional magnetics will maintain cost-effectiveness dominance in standard utility applications.
8.
Interim conversion solutions: Data centers can utilize transitional Transformer Rectifier Units instead of native SSTs to achieve 800V DC. These TRU systems utilize conventional medium-voltage transformers coupled with low-voltage rectifier modules. While TRUs lack the high efficiency of SSTs, they use tried and tested mechanical designs. This alternative path means conventional magnetics will retain demand during the early phases of DC transition.

Margins and technical barriers

9.
Divergent margin profiles: Custom conventional dry-type transformers yield attractive margins compared to standard off-the-shelf components. Expert insights indicate that customized data center dry transformers command 30% gross margins for Tier 2 suppliers. However, Tier 1 players like Siemens and Hitachi maintain margins above 20% by selling complete power electronics. This means SST systems command margin premiums that traditional manufacturing cannot replicate.
10.
High material barriers: Developing high-frequency SST magnetic cores requires advanced nanocrystalline and amorphous alloys. These soft magnetic materials require rapid cooling and vacuum melting, which are highly concentrated among proprietary suppliers. Additionally, the manufacturing process relies on scarce skilled labor with extensive winding expertise. Consequently, these entry barriers will prevent lower-tier contract manufacturers from easily replicating solid-state components.
11.
Nanocrystalline core efficiency: Nextgeneration solid-state transformers utilize high-frequency nanocrystalline and amorphous alloy cores to reduce thermal losses. These advanced materials allow SSTs to operate at frequencies up to 1 MHz compared to 60 Hz. However, processing amorphous alloy ribbons requires specialized annealing process control methods to prevent brittleness. These technical material challenges will protect established component vendors from low-cost competition.
12.
Historical traction precedent: The slow transition of marine and traction markets to solid-state systems highlights potential adoption hurdles. ABB initiated R&D investments into marine direct current grids 15 years ago but is only now scaling to industrial levels. Similarly, traction propulsion transitioned slowly due to strict safety and high thermal management requirements. This historical parallel suggests widespread SST grid commercialization will require decades to fully mature.
13.
Wide- bandgap semiconductor limitations: Commercializing high-power SSTs depends heavily on the supply and integration of silicon carbide power electronics. These wide-bandgap semiconductors allow compact designs but face high thermal and switching loss tradeoffs. Expert evaluations show that silicon carbide availability is the primary bottleneck for the entire SST power ecosystem. This supply constraint will delay widespread commercial deployments, protecting conventional systems.
