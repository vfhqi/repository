EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 17 findings

1.
4iG Nyrt completed a transformational corporate governance and operational restructure in January 2026, transitioning from a dual CEO model to a vice-presidential structure aligned by four strategic business pillars: Telecommunications (split into ComCo commercial operations and 2Connect infrastructure), Space & Defence, IT, and International Digital Infrastructure. This restructuring represents a material strategic inflection designed to enhance capital market transparency, unlock operational synergies, and separate asset ownership from commercial execution across a diversified conglomerate spanning mature regional telecommunications markets and high-growth defense verticals. Management projected that the organizational transformation would enhance Enterprise Value by HUF 400 billion by unlocking operational synergies, though execution complexity remains elevated given the heavy integration burden from recent acquisitions including a 74.34% majority stake in Rába Járműipari Holding Nyrt acquired in January 2026 and a USD 100 million strategic capital contribution in Axiom Space completed in Q1 2026. The change materiality is highly significant across governance, strategy, capital structure, and external value-chain forces, positioning 4iG as a unique hybrid telecommunicationsdefense-space integrator within Central Europe
2.
Change Materiality Assessment: The Group's structural transformation represents the most significant strategic reorganization since its formation, converting 4iG from an operating company into an investment holding platform with centralized treasury and strategic oversight functions. The restructure converts 4iG Plc into an investment holding company to separate ownership and daily operations, enabling independent capital allocation and performance tracking across four distinct verticals. The sweeping organizational changes risk disruption, and centralizing power under Jászai's 52.76% control could weaken minority shareholder protection, introducing governance concentration risk that stands above regional peer norms. Externally, 4iG faces unprecedented defense demand with Hungary's military frameworks providing up to EUR 4 billion in call options through 2030, including EUR 1.5 billion for Tatra military truck deliveries and EUR 2.5 billion for Gidran 4x4 modular combat vehicles, creating a material growth opportunity offset by execution complexity and geopolitical dependencies. The magnitude of concurrent internal restructuring and external opportunity exploitation places exceptional strain on management bandwidth, working capital cycles, and financial flexibility
