Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding Theon International's tax trajectory is critical because a rising tax rate directly threatens earnings per share growth and free cash flow conversion.

The Key Question Defined

1.
Key Question: "For Theon International (THEON-NL), what is the composition and likely path of its effective income-tax rate: the statutory rates in each jurisdiction where it earns profit (Cyprus for the parent, Greece for Theon Sensors, Germany for Harder Digital and Kappa Optronics, Belgium, France for Merio and HGH Systèmes Infrarouges, South Korea and the Gulf), the reported effective rates of 22.9% in FY2024, 23.8% in FY2025 and 19.9% in H1 2026, the Greek Development Law 4887/2022 investment exemption, non-deductible items such as acquisition costs and share-based pay, withholding taxes on intra-group dividends, the open Greek tax audit for 2025, the company's stated guidance of 23% to 24%, and how the consolidation of HGH and Merio from 2027 and the growth of the German subsidiaries would move the group rate; and how the effective tax rates of Exosens, Hensoldt, MilDef and Exail Technologies compare and why?"
2.
Sufficient coverage: This tax investigation identified six relevant sources and five companies including peers Exosens, Hensoldt, MilDef, and Exail Technologies, with all of them used in this analysis and none excluded. This comprehensive dataset allows for a deep examination of the statutory tax rates and consolidation effects without any omissions.
3.
Expected increase: Theon has guided to a higher effective income tax rate of 23% to 24% for the medium term, up from its historical rates. This projected increase has prompted sell-side analysts at Deutsche Bank to lower their earnings per share forecasts by 11% to reflect both the higher tax burden and dilution from the rights issue. Consequently, resolving the exact path of this rate is essential for investors trying to project the company's net income growth.
4.
Understated valuation: Theon's stock valuation is highly sensitive to free cash flow projections, which are directly influenced by cash tax payments over the next three years. Analysts at Deutsche Bank employ a blend of DCF, FCF yield, and SOTP methodologies to arrive at a target price of €39. Any deviation from guided tax rates would immediately alter these DCF models and affect the stock's 15% valuation discount relative to its peer Exosens.

Cash Flow and Strategic Trajectory

5.
Moderate conversion: Theon expects its free cash flow conversion to average around 40% in the medium term as it ramps up capital expenditure to 4% of revenue. Managing working capital absorption down to the 35% target will determine how much cash is available to cover these tax liabilities. Investors must monitor whether elevated tax liabilities in high-rate jurisdictions like Germany will further constrain free cash flow.
6.
Contrary evidence: Theon managed to report a much lower effective tax rate of 19.9% in the first half of 2026, defying its medium-term guidance. This lower rate was primarily driven by a tax-exempt €37.24 million fairvalue gain on its Exosens stake under Cyprus rules. This suggests that the tax trajectory might remain lower if non-operational gains continue to offset operational tax liabilities.
7.
Other side of the story: While a rising tax rate could depress EPS, Theon's strong pricing power in its high-margin Thermal and Digital businesses could offset tax-related margin pressure. Because these segments do not rely on expensive third-party intensifier tubes, their rapid growth could sustain overall profitability. If these segments outpace the lowermargin night vision goggles business, the resulting margin expansion would mitigate the impact of a 24% effective tax rate.

Rising baseline: Theon International faces a higher statutory tax rate in Cyprus following the enactment of a comprehensive tax reform on December 22, 2025. This legislation increased the parent company statutory rate from 12.5% to 15% effective for tax years starting on or after January 1, 2026. The reform directly elevates the tax floor for the group's central management and holding company operations.

Greece. This rate is established under Article 120 of Law No. 4799/2021 and remained unchanged through the 2025 fiscal year. Because Greece represents the group's primary manufacturing hub, this 22% rate anchors the majority of operational profits.

Corporate peer Hensoldt operates primarily in Germany under the same 30% marginal blended tax rate consisting of corporate, solidarity, and trade taxes. This statutory parity ensures that Theon's expanding platform-based optronics operations in Germany face no tax disadvantage relative to local incumbents.

Section 3 – Historical Tax Adjustments, Exemptions, and Audits

Section summary
  1. Historical tax rates for Theon International reflect significant deviations from standard statutory baselines due to recurring tax-exempt fair-value gains and non-deductible acquisition costs.
  2. Cyprus tax exemptions on financial titles fully shielded substantial fair-value gains from corporate tax, offsetting the rising tax drag from Greek and German operations.
  3. Unresolved tax audits in Greece and withholding taxes on intra-group dividends introduce minor, manageable cash flow risks to the group's medium-term earnings trajectory.

Historical Rate Reconciliations

1.
Discrepant baselines: Theon's reported effective tax rates have shown modest variance between investor claims and audited group financial statements. While investor documents claim effective tax rates of 22.9% in FY2024 and 23.8% in FY2025, the company's audited annual reports clarify that the actual effective tax rates were 22.2% in FY2024 and 23.1% in FY2025. This statistical baseline suggests that historical operations were taxed slightly below investor expectations before the recent statutory rate hikes took effect, which protects the group's baseline earnings per share.
2.
Operational divergence: The group's consolidated tax expense of €24.35 million in FY2025 grew from €19.26 million in FY2024, representing a substantial absolute increase. This growth was driven by a rise in consolidated profit before tax from €86.70 million to €105.54 million over the same period. The rising tax burden highlights how operational growth in higher-tax jurisdictions begins to outpace historical averages unless offset by central exemptions.
3.
Expected adjustments: The sell-side consensus has historically adjusted its earnings models to align with management's updated tax disclosures. Sell-side analysts at Deutsche Bank noted in November 2025 that the group guided its medium-term tax rate to a higher band of 23% to 24% compared to the 22% rate observed in FY2024. This shift led to downward adjustments in forward earnings per share projections, proving that even minor revisions in the tax rate mix directly impact investor valuations.

Cyprus Tax Exemptions and Revaluations

4.
Favorable shielding: Theon reported a significantly lower effective tax rate of 19.9% in the first half of 2026, primarily due to non-operational revaluations. The group recorded a substantial €37.24 million net fair-value gain on financial assets during this period, which included a €14.10 million revaluation gain on its 9.8% stake in Exosens. Because Cyprus tax rules exempt capital gains and profits from the sale of titles from corporate tax, these massive gains incurred zero tax liability, lowering the overall group rate.
5.
Additional revaluations: The non-taxable revaluation benefit was further amplified by the group's other strategic investments in Kopin Corporation. Theon recorded a €17.60 million fair-value gain on its Kopin investment alongside a €5.50 million gain on convertible loan instruments in the first half of 2026. Under Cyprus corporate tax regulations, these revaluations were classified as tax-exempt income, illustrating how nonoperating financial assets temporarily inflate net earnings without generating cash tax expenses.
6.
Contrary evidence: The Cyprus tax framework does not provide an absolute shield for all investment activities because related expenses face strict tax-deduction limits. Under Cyprus corporate tax rules, any expenses that directly or indirectly relate to the production of tax-exempt income are classified as non-deductible. This restriction means that the costs incurred in executing the Exosens and Kopin acquisitions cannot offset operational profits, resulting in a higher effective tax rate on core operating income.

Greek Development Law and Deductions

7.
Investment incentives: Theon Sensors has historically sought to optimize its Greek tax base, though disclosures on specific legislative incentives remain highly restricted. While investor inquiries highlight the Greek Development Law 4887/2022 investment exemption, audited financial statements do not explicitly disclose any material tax effects or accumulated benefits under this specific law. Instead, historical reconciliations show minor tax allowances on research and development of €0.22 million in FY2024, indicating that Greek tax incentives provide only marginal relief to the group's manufacturing base.
8.
Deduction restrictions: Non-deductible expenses continue to create permanent upward differences in the group's tax reconciliation. The tax effect of non-deductible expenses rose from €1.05 million in FY2024 to €2.57 million in FY2025, representing a jump from 1.2% to 2.4% of profit before tax. These permanent differences are driven by acquisition-related expenses and share-based compensation that tax authorities exclude, directly lowering consolidated earnings per share.
9.
Acquisition drag: The group's active mergers and acquisitions strategy has consistently elevated these non- deductible costs. In the first half of 2026, Theon incurred non-recurring expenses of €3.89 million primarily relating to legal, due diligence, and acquisition-related costs. Because these transaction expenses are generally non-deductible under local rules, they create an immediate drag on the effective tax rate during active acquisition periods.

Audits and Withholding Taxes

10.
Unresolved audits: Theon faces potential tax adjustments in Greece where its primary operating subsidiary remains subject to active tax-authority reviews. Management disclosed on September 24, 2026, that the tax compliance audit for the year ended 31 December 2025 was still in progress. Although the Greek subsidiary has obtained unqualified tax compliance certificates from 2011 through 2024, this ongoing audit introduces near-term uncertainty, even though management expects no material financial impact.
11.
Withholding friction: Intra-group cash distributions are subject to localized withholding taxes that create cash flow friction within the group. According to the Swiss Federal Tax Administration, Theon AG paid €23,525 in withholding taxes in 2025 on dividends distributed to its Cypriot parent, down from €42,685 in 2024. Furthermore, Theon International paid €0.42 million in withholding taxes on dividends received from Hensoldt Theon Nightvision GmbH, showing that cross-border cash movement incurs direct tax leakage.
12.
Strict regulations: The parent company faces strict local regulations regarding deemed dividend distributions to its domestic shareholders. Theon pays a special defence contribution on behalf of its shareholders over deemed dividend distributions at 17% when the entitled shareholders are Cypriot tax residents and domiciled in Cyprus. This local requirement, combined with a 2.65% General Healthcare System contribution, affects domestic shareholder returns but does not directly impact the consolidated earnings per share of international investors.
13.
Other side of the story: Although non-operating fair-value exemptions and tax-free revaluations currently suppress the group's effective tax rate, this benefit remains highly volatile. The 19.9% effective rate reported in H1 2026 is entirely dependent on paper gains that do not generate cash inflows and could reverse if financial markets decline. As operational profits grow in high-tax regions, the lack of cash tax offsets from Cyprus will inevitably drive the group's cash tax rate toward the guided 24% limit.
