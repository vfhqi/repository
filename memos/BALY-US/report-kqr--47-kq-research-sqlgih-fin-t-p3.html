Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Feedback loops of leverage and capital commitments dictate Bally's financial path. The balance between regional asset decay and Chicago's growth will determine near-term earnings.

Strategic Financial Questions

1.
Critical inquiry: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"
2.
Severe pressure: Bally's earnings per share are highly sensitive to lease expenses and cash interest because high fixed charges raise the financial break-even point. Deutsche Bank Research estimates that Bally's lease obligations will expand from $121 million LTM to $303 million by 2026, while cash interest expenses are projected to reach $290 million. This massive surge in fixed costs suppresses discretionary free cash flow and increases the volatility of future earnings per share.
3.
Complex structure: Bally's operates a highly segmented business model whose digital expansion has historically acted as a cash drag rather than a growth driver. Jefferies Research reported that the North America Interactive segment incurred a $12.3 million EBITDA loss in the fourth quarter of 2024, which was worse than their estimated loss of $0.6 million. These ongoing digital losses restrict the capital available to maintain the core Casino and Resorts segment properties.

Scope of Precedent Analysis

4.
Elevated leverage: Bally's high debt levels limit its ability to navigate economic downturns and execute key development projects without diluting existing shareholders. Jefferies Research indicates that the company's lease-adjusted net debt to EBITDAR ratio is expected to peak at 7.7x in 2024 before potentially declining to 6.5x in 2025 as sale-leaseback proceeds are applied to debt repayment. However, the massive long-term rent increase associated with these transactions creates a structural drag on cash flow.
5.
Historical precedents: Structural over-leverage in regional gaming historically leads to severe distress and complex restructurings, as demonstrated by several comparable past cases. This report evaluates the restructuring of Station Casinos between 2009 and 2011, the Caesars Entertainment Operating Company bankruptcy of 2015, and the capital structure failure of Revel Atlantic City. These cases illustrate how controlling shareholders, landlords, and lenders behave when casino operators face distressed debt situations.
6.
Extensive scope: Bally's comprehensive research report synthesized evidence from 110 distinct documents, including company filings, expert call transcripts, broker reports, and regulatory announcements. The analysis tracks 11 companies and 4 comparable historical restructurings to map how these feedback loops operate in practice. By evaluating diverse sources, the report assesses whether Bally's can execute its development plans or if it will slide into an underinvestment spiral.

Risks to the Thesis

7.
Financial obligations: The landlord loop represents a critical balancing dynamic where sale-leasebacks provide short-term liquidity but permanently elevate the operator's operational cost structure. Deutsche Bank notes that Bally's lease-adjusted leverage profile is severely impacted by these transactions because lease capitalization at eight times rent adds substantial implied debt to the balance sheet. This structural leverage increases cash interest costs and limits Bally's financial flexibility.
8.
Contrary evidence: Bally's development pipeline may be less risky because key projects are heavily supported by partners and structured capital. An expert interview on July 10, 2023, revealed high optimism regarding the Las Vegas and Chicago opportunities, viewing them as positive, near-certain drivers of incremental regional gaming revenue. This view suggests that the positive deleveraging loop from Chicago could initiate much faster than consensus expectations.
9.
Other side of the story: Bally's underinvestment spiral and landlord lease loops could be offset by a rapid and successful ramp-up of the permanent Chicago casino to generate enough EBITDAR to fund deleveraging. Deutsche Bank estimates that the permanent Chicago casino, due to open in 2027, could generate $250 million in annual property EBITDAR. If these projections are met, the cash flow would easily cover Bally's increased rental burdens and allow the operator to refinance its expensive unsecured debt.

Section 2 – The Negative Loop: Leverage, Rent, and Underinvestment Spiral

Section summary
  1. High fixed lease and interest payments trigger a regional underinvestment loop at Bally's. Neglecting physical assets and gaming floor technology drives market share loss to better-capitalized competitors.

Step-by-Step Mechanics of Decay

1.
Severe friction: Bally's high debt leverage and growing lease obligations restrict its operational cash flow and limit capital allocation. Jefferies Research estimated in 2024 that Bally's lease-adjusted net leverage would remain elevated above six times over the near term. This cash flow drain limits the capital available for updating regional gaming properties, initiating a downward spiral of property decay. This trend directly reduces future regional earnings per share.
2.
Escalating leasehold: Bally's annual lease payments to Gaming and Leisure Properties reached $173.8 million in 2025 and are subject to constant inflation escalation. Bally's Corporation reported in its 2024 annual report that these triple-net rent obligations can materially limit its ability to reinvest in its regional gaming properties. Because these lease payments are highly fixed, any regional revenue drop severely impacts operating profitability and increases default risk. This dynamic compounds the difficulty of servicing unsecured debt obligations.
3.
Deferred maintenance: Bally's capital expenditures have faced structural reductions as the company diverts cash flow to fund its expensive urban development pipeline. Bally's Corporation reported that its capital expenditures fell to $199.8 million in 2024 from $311.5 million in 2023. Because capital is preserved for major projects like Chicago, the company has deferred critical slot machine upgrades and property refreshes at its regional properties. This underinvestment weakens regional property appeal and threatens long-term EBITDAR generation.

This projection reflects how intensive development costs consume the cash flow generated by stable regional assets. As cash is consumed by development, maintenance capital at regional casinos is capped, leaving properties exposed to competitive encroachment. This imbalance leads to volatile earnings per share.
