EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Vaudoise Assurances exhibits highly stable executive leadership with deep-tenured insiders, structured board governance succession, and talent retention as a key competitive advantage, positioning the cooperativecontrolled Swiss insurer as a resilient regional player navigating intensifying market consolidation. The group is executing a transformative diversification strategy, deploying CHF 309 million to acquire 92.27% of Procimmo and positioning itself with over CHF 9 billion in real estate assets under management, marking a material strategic pivot toward capital-light fee income generation.

However, the announced merger of Helvetia and Baloise creates a dominant competitor controlling 20% of the Swiss insurance market, substantially elevating competitive pressures on mid-cap regional players like Vaudoise. Core underwriting performance demonstrates robust momentum, with non-life gross written premiums growing 7.2% in first half 2025 and the combined ratio improving to 96.7% from 98.3% in the prior year, driven by favourable natural catastrophe variance and successful motor tariff adjustments.

Yet investment returns are deteriorating, declining to 1.4% in first half 2025 compared to 1.9% in the prior period, reflecting weakening interest rate pressures across Swiss portfolios. Despite this margin pressure, the group maintains an extraordinary SST solvency ratio of 344% at end-2025, far outperforming larger peers Swiss Life at 210% and

Baloise at 210%, providing a substantial capital cushion for strategic investments and shareholder distributions.

The materiality of strategic change is high. The Procimmo acquisition represents a major capital commitment that CEO Jean-Daniel Laffely explicitly frames as transforming Vaudoise into a key player in third-party property management, fundamentally reshaping the group's revenue mix beyond traditional underwriting operations. Concurrently, the launch of "Vaudoise InvestPlus" in partnership with Zuger Kantonalbank and the CEO's emphasis on providing modern and efficient banking solutions signals an aggressive push into adjacent financial services, creating multiple new revenue vectors while introducing execution complexity and integration risk.

Top Five to Eight Key Drivers ranked by impact and plausibility are as follows. First, motor and property pricing adjustments are the highest-impact driver, with CEO Laffely stating Vaudoise had no choice but to adapt tariffs due to auto losses, generating 6.8% motor premium expansion in first half 2025, carrying high plausibility given documented execution momentum. Second, German-speaking regional expansion delivered 9.5% premium growth in 2025, now representing nearly 40% of total premiums, offering high plausibility as a sustained organic growth engine supported by ongoing agent recruitment.

Third, Procimmo third-party real estate scaling involves raising the stake to 92.27% and controlling over CHF 9 billion in assets, but carries medium plausibility due to lengthy integration timelines and dependency on Swiss property market stability. Fourth, cooperative member premium retrocessions of CHF 40 million providing a 10% motor premium discount carry high plausibility as a loyalty mechanism, though this amount is lower than the CHF 44 million distributed in the prior period, signaling potential margin constraints.

Fifth, Prevanto pension consulting integration strengthens actuarial advisory leadership in Switzerland, offering high plausibility for steady fee generation with low execution risk. Sixth, TrendValor tranche scheduling shows extreme volatility, with single premiums declining 7.2% in 2025 after surging 46.4% in first half 2024, carrying medium plausibility due to high dependency on Swiss interest rate cycles and unpredictable retail appetite.

These drivers collectively position Vaudoise as a consolidation play and fee-diversification compounder, though stacked downside scenarios involving severe weather events and property yield compression could materially compress consolidated earnings.

Recent delivery over the last three years reveals a consistent pattern of premium outperformance offset by chronic underwriting margin misses. Consolidated turnover grew from CHF 1,300 million in 2023 to CHF 1,518.8 million in 2025, while consolidated net income reached a historic CHF 156.3 million in 2025, expanding 16.73% compared to the restated 2024 baseline. However, the non-life combined ratio exhibited volatility, reaching 96.4% in 2025 compared to 97.9% in 2024 and 96.6% in 2023, with natural catastrophe claims declining in 2025 but severe storm and hail events in 2023 impacting motor underwriting margins.

Non-life premium growth consistently exceeded historical guidance of 4.0% to 4.5%, reaching 5.1% in 2024 and 7.5% in 2025, yet combined ratios consistently failed to hit the strategic target of 90%, with the average L3Y ratio settling at 96.5% versus Baloise's superior 92.9% baseline. This bifurcated performance trajectory indicates strong commercial execution but structural underwriting challenges driven by persistent motor claim inflation and natural catastrophe exposures.

Forward guidance credibility is moderately high for top-line growth but structurally weak for underwriting margins. Management historically projected non-life premium growth of 4.0% to 4.5%, yet delivered 5.1% in 2024 and 7.5% in 2025, confirming a conservative sandbagger archetype that systematically under-promises on revenue targets. Conversely, the long-term target of a 94% non-life combined ratio remains difficult to achieve, with actual ratios reaching 96.6% in 2023, 97.9% in 2024, and 96.4% in 2025, representing consistent misses of 2.4 to 3.9 percentage points.

Standard guidance explicitly excludes major natural catastrophe storms and severe financial market volatility, introducing substantial caveats that limit comparability during volatile weather years. Earnings sensitivity analysis reveals that a +/- 50 bps combined ratio change impacts EPS by approximately +/- 3.5%, while a +/- 20 bps shift in asset yields represents a CHF 15.2 million pre-tax profit impact on the CHF 7.6 billion investment portfolio, underscoring material exposure to both underwriting execution and interest rate movements.

Sell-side positioning is absent due to thin analyst coverage, with no substantive broker research identified in the AlphaSense pool, leaving the company under-researched by institutional standards and creating limited external validation of management's strategic narrative. This coverage gap reflects Vaudoise's mid-cap regional positioning and concentrated cooperative ownership structure, which together reduce sell-side commercial incentives for sustained coverage.

Fundamental peer positioning reveals leading organic growth but lagging underwriting efficiency. Vaudoise delivered 7.5% non-life premium growth, outperforming Swiss Life's 3.0% expansion and comfortably exceeding the 4.5% global real growth rate benchmark. However, Vaudoise's non-life combined ratio of 96.4% lags Baloise's superior 92.9% combined ratio, representing a material margin disadvantage of 350 basis points.

Larger European peers Zurich and Allianz accelerated non-life revenue by 11.4% and 6.8% respectively in first quarter 2026, demonstrating scale-driven momentum that Vaudoise cannot replicate given its regional Swiss concentration and CHF 1.5 billion revenue base. Activist presence is zero. Mutuelle Vaudoise Société Coopérative controls the group with 67.6% of capital and 91.2% of voting rights, secured through unlisted Class A shares with privileged voting rights, creating an impenetrable governance fortress that eliminates any realistic prospect of hostile activism or proxy contests.

No participation disclosures were filed under Swiss LIMF rules, proving absolute stability in the ownership register. This concentrated cooperative structure ensures strategic continuity but also removes external governance pressure that might otherwise accelerate operational improvements.

Key risks cluster around four dimensions. First, intensifying market consolidation via the Helvetia-Baloise merger creating a 20% market share behemoth poses existential competitive risk to Vaudoise's regional franchise. Second, severe automobile repair and spare parts inflation and rapidly escalating claims costs could completely offset pricing actions, destroying motor underwriting profitability. Third, extreme natural catastrophe sensitivity to Swiss storms, which inflated the 2023 combined ratio by 5.5 percentage points, creates binary earnings volatility in adverse weather years.

Fourth, integrating external real estate platforms while funding heavy digital operating expenses could temporarily depress near-term operating margins, particularly if Procimmo integration encounters execution delays or Swiss property markets weaken. A compound risk scenario involving simultaneous severe weather, runaway motor inflation, and property yield compression would rapidly deplete underwriting profits, leaving the CHF 309 million Procimmo capital outlay unprotected by core earnings generation.
