EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Overall conclusion. Billerud is in a cyclical, self-help-driven earnings recovery, not a structural re-rating of its earnings power. The recent outperformance reflects a cyclical rebound from the end of destocking rather than a structural expansion. Stable Nordic wood costs in late 2026 offer temporary margin relief only.

The report's judgement is that management has de-risked the strategy and delivered on costs. However, wood-cost exposure, European overcapacity and the loss of free carbon allowances cap how far margins can recover. Consensus EPS for this year and next. The report provides no full-street consensus series, so the EPS picture rests on DNB Carnegie figures.

The base case points to a recovery in 2027 clean earnings per share to SEK 5.12, compared with SEK 1.41 in 2026. After the Q3 beat, DNB Carnegie's Tomi Railo raised his 2026 adjusted EPS forecast by 49% to SEK 1.55, so the 2026 figure is still moving. The step-up into 2027 is steep. It depends on disciplined process execution across all board mills.

It also assumes stabilizing Swedish pulpwood costs and steady food and beverage packaging demand. Estimates have been volatile throughout the year. Railo cut 2026 adjusted EPS estimates by 30% to 75% in April and raised projections by about 10% in October.

Ranked main drivers of future earnings. The ranking weighs both earnings impact and plausibility. 1. Nordic pulpwood cost relief is the largest and most plausible driver. Every 10% change in Nordic wood costs moves annual EBITDA by about SEK 0.9 billion. Pulpwood prices have fallen roughly 20% from their peak, and the CEO expects stability in the fourth quarter.

2. The SEK 800 million fixed-cost program is a high-plausibility internal lever. It targets annualized savings of SEK 800 million from 2027, with SEK 550 million in 2026 and a further SEK 250 million in 2027. Savings of SEK 150 million were delivered in both the second and third quarters of 2026.

3. The US Evolution program is a high-plausibility but slower-burning driver. It carries a targeted investment of SEK 1.4 billion over 2024 to 2027, and early customer qualification trials support its plausibility. The Section 9 section cites SEK 1.3 billion for the same program, a small inconsistency in the sources.

4. European demand and pricing recovery is potentially large but has low short-term plausibility. Persistent overcapacity keeps near-term confidence low. The CEO notes that liquid packaging board has been strong, but that cartonboard overcapacity remains a challenge.

5. The loss of free CO2 allowances is a near-certain headwind, not an upside driver. Jefferies estimated the historical net result from selling surplus emission rights at SEK 507 million in fiscal 2025. The regulatory timeline is already locked in.

Driver interactions. The drivers do not simply add up. Wood-cost relief and European price recovery can interact negatively, because producers often give back cost relief to customers through lower pricing in oversupplied cycles. By contrast, cost savings and US upgrades reinforce each other, since European savings fund North American reinvestment. The combined probability of success is constrained, because wood relief, cost delivery and US execution must occur together to offset the carbon-allowance loss.

Operating profit of SEK 311 million beat the SEK 286 million consensus by 8.74%. EPS of SEK 0.85 beat the SEK 0.58 consensus by 46.55%. The regional split was uneven. European EBITDA of SEK 734 million beat consensus by 7.62%. North American EBITDA of SEK 472 million missed consensus by 12.75%. The Escanaba shutdown cost SEK 104 million against a budget of SEK 90 million. Cash generation was strong, with operating cash flow after investments of SEK 712 million and cash conversion of 127%.

It posted a 28% adjusted EBITDA miss against Visible Alpha consensus in Q2 2025, and a 23.80% miss in Q1 2026. Improved execution points to greater control over internal cost-out than over market pricing. The previous SEK 1.5 billion efficiency program closed one year ahead of schedule.
