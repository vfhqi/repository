Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics 10 findings

Section summary

  2. regional fragmentation triggers mid-market consolidation
  3. severe physical constraints and rising rack densities shift value toward secured power capacity and advanced liquid cooling

1.
Scale deficit: Europe operates with less than 10GW of data center capacity, representing a massive gap against the 60GW deployed in the US
2.
Growth path: The European data center market is projected to expand at a 21% annual growth rate through 2030
3.
Global capacity: Global data center power demand is projected to double from 95GW in 2025 to 174GW by 2030
4.
Regional concentration: European digital infrastructure remains heavily clustered inside the five core FLAPD markets of Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris, and Dublin. Secondary market growth: European capacity development is gradually migrating toward secondary markets like Spain, Norway, and Northern France due to land and power availability
5.
AI leasing surge: European AI-focused colocation signings reached a record 420MW in the first half of 2026, up from 89MW in H1-2025

Value chain economics and consolidations

6.

Asset carve-outs: Telcos are increasingly carving out their physical data center operations through sale and lease-back deals to unlock high premium multiples. Margin distribution: Platform-as-a-Service layers generate much higher investment returns than bare-metal Infrastructure-as-a-Service by bundling software ecosystems. IaaS margin pressure: Incremental AI infrastructure revenues carry lower margins, with pure GPU server rentals yielding gross margins well below 30%

Hyperscaler dominance and success factors

7.
Massive market share: The top three US hyperscalers command a dominant 85% share of the European cloud infrastructure market. Individual dominance: Within Europe, AWS is the largest player with a 45% market share, followed by Microsoft at 27% and Google at 13%
8.
Pipeline control: US tech giants already own 35% of European capacity and account for nearly half of the region's upcoming construction pipeline

Power constraints: The availability of grid-connected electricity has emerged as the primary bottleneck for new European data center deployment. Conditional survival: If local providers cannot secure high-power

Other side of the story: aggressive power regulations and European grid bottlenecks could halt capacity expansions entirely, rendering local players unable to fulfill critical sovereign contracts
