EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Signify's ongoing leadership transitions at Board of Management level have significantly elevated near-term execution risks during a critical corporate restructuring phase, with a new CEO appointed in September 2025, a permanent CFO confirmed in October 2024, and aggressive leadership turnover potentially disrupting operational continuity.

The company faces persistent organic revenue contraction of 3.4% in FY25 and 3.6% in Q2 2026, alongside severe margin compression from 9.9% in FY24 to 8.9% in FY25 and further deterioration to 6.1% in Q2 2026, driven by intensified Chinese competitive pressure as US tariffs redirected excess LED capacity into Europe, exacerbating localized pricing pressure and forcing domestic manufacturers to double lighting export volumes.

Strategic Change and Portfolio Segmentation Management formally split the portfolio into Build and Harvest mandates at the June 2026 Capital Markets Day, with the Build portfolio representing 72% of 2025 sales targeted to reach circa 80% by 2029, while targeting Build organic growth acceleration to circa 2% during 2025-29 from 0% in 2024-25, with no active activist presence identified. The company initiated a EUR 180 million cost-saving program in January 2026 structurally impacting circa 900 employee roles, closed redundant conventional lighting plants to consolidate the manufacturing footprint, while cash flows were weighed down by EUR 29 million in restructuring payouts during the second quarter of 2026. The dividend policy was reset to 40-50% payout of continuing income representing a circa 40% year-on-year dividend cut, while management suspended the share repurchase program to prioritize balance sheet strength and flexibility during the transition.

Top Key Drivers Ranked by EPS Impact The forward earnings trajectory depends on cost resizing self-help (estimated +12% EPS impact), where Signify launched a EUR 180 million cost-reduction program targeting 900 global roles with high plausibility and high confidence as an independent execution driver. However, Chinese pricing pressure represents a -10% EPS impact with high plausibility and medium confidence dependent on tariff policy, while European building recovery carries an estimated +8% EPS impact but with low plausibility and low confidence due to dependency on macro interest rates.

Connected destocking normalization offers a +6% EPS impact with medium plausibility and medium confidence dependent on consumer sentiment, OEM turnaround provides a +4% EPS impact with medium plausibility but low confidence due to sourcing costs, and conventional declines impose a -5% EPS impact with high plausibility and high confidence driven by regulatory phase-outs.

Consumer adjusted EBITA margin fell to just 3.0% declining by 440 basis points year-on-year as connected lighting sell-out remained healthy but lower retailer sell-in volumes drove heavy factory under-absorption costs, while higher input costs and raw material inflation outpaced consumer price increases creating a negative price-cost lag. OEM comparable sales fell by 12.0% in Q2 2026 due to the loss of two large customers though OEM adjusted EBITA margin sequentially improved to 4.6% via proactive fixed-cost base restructuring, while Conventional segment adjusted EBITA margin expanded to 18.1% driven by specialty lighting growth.

Rothschild & Co Redburn forecasts the FY26 adjusted EBITA margin at 7.7% sitting at the lower end of guidance and below the 8.0% consensus, while Morgan Stanley forecasts a 7.3% Adjusted EBITA margin running below the company's guidance floor of 7.5% due to slower cost-saving phasing and pricing headwinds. The second material risk relates to the aggressive cost-cutting program and dividend reset potentially failing to stabilize the financial profile if European construction markets stagnate further and Chinese pricing pressure accelerates gross margin erosion, particularly given the back-end loaded recovery assumptions proving completely unachievable if European construction stagnation persists, which would trigger a material guidance cut at the third-quarter results. Plausible problems to monitor

Signify exhibits seven material plausible problem to monitor that position the company below peer median performance and sector base rates across operational, financial, and strategic dimensions. These signals reflect structural vulnerabilities that compound execution risk and limit the probability of achieving management's medium-term turnaround targets. Severely Concentrated Executive Leadership represents a governance plausible problem to monitor as Signify operates with only two Board of Management members following the sudden December 2025 departure of Professional Business CEO Harsh Chitale, while the newly created Chief Growth Officer role remains vacant nine months after its establishment.

This concentration falls far below the European Capital Goods peer median of four to five executive board members and creates single points of failure during a critical restructuring period (see Section 1). Persistent Organic Revenue Contraction constitutes a fundamental plausible problem to monitor as Signify recorded negative comparable sales growth of minus 3.4% in FY25 and minus 3.6% in Q2 2026, representing three consecutive years of structural top-line decline.

This contrasts sharply with US peer Acuity Brands' positive 6.8% net sales growth in fiscal 2026 and positions Signify in the bottom quintile of European lighting and capital goods peers on revenue momentum (see Section 4, Section 5, Section 7).

Severe Operating Margin Compression emerges as a profitability plausible problem to monitor with Signify's adjusted EBITA margin deteriorating from 9.9% in FY24 to 8.9% in FY25 and collapsing further to just 6.1% in Q2 2026. Acuity Brands achieved an outstanding 18.7% adjusted operating margin in Q4 2026, while the European Capital Goods

Elevated Financial Leverage represents a balance sheet plausible problem to monitor as Signify's reported net debt to EBITDA ratio rose from 1.26x at year-end 2024 to 1.50x at year-end 2025 and reached 1.70x by mid-2026. This compares unfavorably to Acuity Brands' net cash position and voluntary debt reduction of USD 200 million in fiscal 2026, while the European Capital Goods median leverage stands at a healthier 1.30x (see Section 4, Section 7).

Consumer Segment Margin Collapse signals acute operational distress as Consumer adjusted EBITA margin plummeted to just 3.0% in Q2 2026, declining by 440 basis points year-on-year due to severe retailer destocking and factory under-absorption costs. This represents the weakest Consumer profitability in Signify's post-IPO history and sits materially below the 7% to 9% historical range that characterized stable Consumer operations (see Section 6).

Escalating Restructuring Cash Drain constitutes a cash generation plausible problem to monitor as restructuring payouts expanded from EUR 14 million in Q2 2025 to EUR 29 million in Q2 2026, representing a 107% year-on-year increase. This drain occurs while Signify's pro-forma free cash flow is already heavily absorbed by annual lease obligations of circa EUR 72 million, leaving minimal residual cash after the reset dividend and eliminated buybacks (see Section 4, Section 5, Section 6).

Deteriorating Sell-Side Positioning reflects a capital markets plausible problem to monitor with Signify's rating mix showing 38% Sell ratings and 54% Hold ratings as of October 2026, while consensus price targets compressed to a range of EUR 12.30 to EUR 23 following multiple downward EPS revisions of up to 11.8%. The resulting persistent minus 50% to minus 52% EV/EBITA discount to European Capital Goods peers signals exceptionally weak institutional sentiment and limited technical support from systematic equity flows (see Section 8, Section 11).

These seven plausible problem to monitor form a reinforcing pattern of structural underperformance that distinguishes Signify from both its direct US peer and the broader European capital goods sector median, creating compounding downside risks to the achievement of FY26 guidance and 2029 strategic targets outlined at the June 2026 Capital Markets Day.
