IMCD (IMCD-NL): what the nine-month results of 5 November 2026 do to the case

These results leave the case broadly where it was and tilt slightly against it: the headline landed exactly where the case expected, but the composition raises a question about cost drop-through that the case held only as a counter-case.

1. What changed

  • EBITA on the number. Q3 2026 operating EBITA was €140.2 million, up 18%, exactly in line with the Visible Alpha consensus of €140.2 million. The case expected about €140 million.
  • Gross profit ahead. Q3 gross profit was €333 million against a €328 million consensus. Organic gross profit growth accelerated to 9.6% from 7.3% in Q2 2026, above the case's 7-9% range and above its 8% line. The gross margin was 25.1%, up 80 basis points on Q3 2025, against a 24.6% consensus.
  • Revenue slower. Organic revenue growth fell to 5.6% from 7.4% in Q2 2026. Management described Q3 pricing as back to "stable and normal" after a volatile Q2; brokers describe underlying volume as stable. The review carries no statement that volumes grew.
  • Conversion at the bottom of the case's range. The conversion margin was 42.1%, above Q3 2025 but below a 42.7% consensus. The case's lines were 44% (volume is carrying costs) and 42% (costs are running above 3%). The print is 0.1 points above the lower line.
  • Regions diverged. Asia-Pacific organic gross profit grew 20.1% and organic EBITA 27.6% (5.1% in Q2 2026), ahead of Azelis' 14.2%. EMEA organic revenue growth slowed to 5.6% from 10.3%, with organic gross profit up 8.3% against Azelis' 2.1%. Americas organic revenue grew 1.5% and organic EBITA 1.1%.
  • Cash and balance sheet. Q3 free cash flow was €94 million, lower than Q3 2025 on working capital; nine-month free cash flow was €317 million, up 12%. Working-capital days were 74 at end-September 2026 against 71 a year earlier. Leverage was 2.6x EBITDA at 30 September 2026 against 2.8x at December 2025. Nine-month cash conversion was 73%; management expects the full year in the high-80s to low-90s per cent.
  • An outlook statement. The company now expects positive organic operating EBITA growth for FY2026. The case assumed no outlook would be given.
  • Sell-side response. Targets and ratings did not move on the day: Jefferies Buy, €122; Deutsche Bank Buy, €123; Morgan Stanley Equal-weight, €110. Deutsche Bank reads the conversion shortfall as evidence that Q2's conversion was flattered by one-offs. Morgan Stanley reads it as arithmetic from a strong gross profit print.

2. The challenge

Reading one: operating leverage is asymmetric, and the case's lever overstates the upside. This is the strongest challenge. The case holds that price drops through to EBITA at close to 100% and that EBITA moves about 2.3x gross profit. This quarter does not behave that way. Gross profit beat consensus by about €5 million and EBITA beat by nothing, so the extra gross profit did not reach EBITA. Organic gross profit grew 9.6% and organic EBITA 13.7%, a ratio of about 1.4x. On the case's own lever formula that implies organic cost growth of roughly 6-7% in the quarter (our arithmetic, and approximate), against the case's 2.75-3.0% and against management's description of very low-single-digit own-cost growth for the nine months. The case's own record shows the same shape: Q1 2026 on the way down ran at about 2.5x, Q2 on the way up at about 1.5x. If this is right, costs follow gross profit up through bonus accruals and variable pay, but price leaves at full drop-through on the way down. The FY2027 cone is then skewed lower than the case's symmetric EUR 5.33 to EUR 6.74 around EUR 6.15, and the conversion recovery toward the case's 45% is slower. The "arithmetic headwind" explanation does not rescue the case, because a higher gross profit lowers conversion only if costs rise with it. For this reading to be right, the cost growth has to be recurring rather than a one-quarter bonus true-up. Management's nine-month cost statement is the evidence against it, and the two cannot both hold without a large Q3 step in accruals.

Reading two: the gross profit acceleration is an inventory and spread windfall, so the give-back is bigger than the price line. Organic revenue slowed while organic gross profit accelerated, and the gross margin rose 80 basis points. A pure pass-through of supplier inflation would raise revenue faster than gross profit, which is what the case models (a gross margin of 24.6% for FY2026, drifting down). The opposite happened. One plausible cause is stock bought at earlier cost and sold at replacement-cost prices, in a quarter when inventory was deliberately held high and working-capital days rose to 74. Jefferies reports wholesaler chemical sales up about 10% against retailers' inventory growth of 5%, and warns of a reverse bullwhip. If so, the case's argument that the destocking leg is absent is weaker than stated: the excess may sit on IMCD's own balance sheet. The air pocket the case allows for the quarter of the turn (its figure: up to 100 basis points of gross margin) would then start from an inflated base. For this to be right, the margin gain has to be concentrated in semi-specialties and to fade as supplier prices stop rising. Against it, Jefferies expects upstream prices to keep rising into the first half of 2027.

Reading three: the acceleration is share and mix, a source the case's price-versus-volume frame has no line for. Asia-Pacific grew organic gross profit 20.1% and beat Azelis, where the case has IMCD losing that regional race and has Asia-Pacific margins drifting down. EMEA also outgrew Azelis by about six points. Pharmaceutical volumes, including Signet in India, normalised after tariff disruption in late 2025. If the gain is mandate wins and richer mix, it is neither borrowed price nor cyclical volume, it is more durable than the case's FY2026 bridge allows, and the gross margin does not have to drift down. For this to be right, Asia-Pacific has to hold double-digit growth once comparisons toughen; Morgan Stanley notes the region's comparisons were easy. A rebound from a tariff-depressed base would be a fourth non-repeating source, not a durable one.

Readings two and three explain the same margin gain in opposite ways, and the review cannot separate them. Reading one stands on its own and is the one we would weight most.

One obvious alternative fails on the evidence: that volume has arrived. Nothing in the review claims it, and conversion at 42.1% is far from the case's 44% line.

3. What it does to the case

  • Operating leverage and cost-out (56, the case's lever of 2.3x and near-100% price drop-through): more pressure. This is where the quarter bites.
  • The case's judgement that organic costs grow 2.75-3.0% a year: more pressure. The conversion threshold was not crossed, but the arithmetic behind it points to the wrong side.
  • Case Riskiness (48) and the riskiness topic (45): slightly more pressure. A lower-skewed cone and working-capital days of 74, against the case's watch level of 70 or below, both bear on it. The day counts may not be on the same definition.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (55): the same. The recovery is still not shown to be volume. The tailwinds topic (57) is the same, with the timing of the price give-back possibly later than the case's bear path assumes.
  • Foundations' Robustness (64): slightly less pressure. Leverage of 2.6x is ahead of the case's path, no impairment is expected where Azelis took €350 million, and IMCD outgrew Azelis in two regions.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (68): the same. The loose element, price rather than volume, is no tighter.
  • Guidance and track record (45): marginally less pressure. A first directional outlook exists, though "positive" for the full year is a low bar after nine months and says little about Q4.
  • Scope for upgrades (30) and current momentum (77): the same to slightly better. The case expected a small FY2026 nudge up and no cuts, and day-one targets were unchanged.
  • Share price. The shares closed at €103.05 on 10 November 2026, above the 50-day average of €99.09; the case's trip of a close below the 50-day was not triggered. The price sits far above the case's fair-trough range of EUR 78-84. The pinned sell-side earnings momentum letter is A (10 November 2026) against the B the case recorded; technical momentum is B, unchanged.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry broker estimate and target changes made after the print, and Brenntag's results and capital markets day, which the case dates to 11-12 November and treats as the frame for 2027 pricing. It is unlikely to carry a cost breakdown, a volume-price split, or anything about Q4. It can tell us whether brokers cut conversion assumptions for FY2027; it cannot tell us who is right.
  • Full-year results on 19 February 2027. Three tests. Q4 conversion and the organic cost growth it implies, which settles reading one. Full-year cash conversion in the high-80s to low-90s per cent from 73% at nine months, which requires the Q3 inventory to turn into cash and tests reading two. The gross margin against 25.1%, which separates a windfall from mix.
  • Asia-Pacific over the next two prints, against tougher comparisons, for reading three.
  • The volume-price split. The company has not committed to disclosing one. The central question of the case therefore stays inferred, from Brenntag, Azelis and the conversion margin, until the first-quarter and half-year prints of 2027, which the case dates to late April and late July.
