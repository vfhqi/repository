Section summary
  1. Accounting shifts in development cost capitalization have introduced substantial volatility into DiaSorin's reported operating expenses.

  3. Management relies heavily on discretionary cost deferrals and manufacturing footprint consolidation to protect near-term margins when revenues fall short.

R&D Accounting and Capitalisation Dynamics

1.
Decreasing capitalized development: DiaSorin experienced a sharp contraction in its capitalized development outlays during the first half of fiscal year 2026. Financial reports indicate that the company capitalized €20.76 million in development costs in the first half of 2026 compared to €34.31 million in the first half of 2025. This significant reduction reflects a transition out of heavy platform development into commercial launch phases. Consequently, this accounting change puts pressure on near-term reported profitability as more development outlays are expensed immediately.
2.
Escalating expensed research: DiaSorin has witnessed a corresponding rise in expensed R&D outlays as capitalization thresholds are no longer met. Corporate filings show that research and development outlays charged directly to the income statement rose to €21.83 million in the first half of 2026 compared to €20.07 million in the first half of 2025. Additionally, amortization of previously capitalized development costs grew from €10.67 million to €11.14 million over the same period. This dual expansion in expensed and amortized R&D will likely continue to weigh on reported operating margins.
3.
Normalizing development intensity: DiaSorin management expects R&D expenditures to stabilize as key platform development cycles conclude. Executives noted during the 2026 Investor Day that clinical trial costs and material consumption are entering a normalization phase after launching three platforms. Management plans to transition R&D spending from double-digit percentages of revenue down into the single-digit range. This strategic normalization is expected to offset rising amortization headwinds over the next three years.
4.
Shifted capital allocation: DiaSorin is redirecting its free cash flow away from heavy R&D capitalization and toward direct shareholder returns and debt management. Morgan Stanley reported on May 26, 2026, that the company's capital allocation will focus on capital expenditure and footprint optimization. If no suitable bolton acquisitions arise, excess cash will likely be funneled into share buyback programs. This reallocation suggests that EPS expansion will rely increasingly on capital restructuring rather than organic margin leverage.
5.
Contrary evidence: DiaSorin's absolute R&D investments actually declined slightly to €46.77 million in the first half of 2026 from €47.21 million in the first half of 2025, which contradicts the view that total innovation spending is growing out of control. This minor reduction demonstrates that management is successfully controlling the absolute cash outflow of its research programs. Thus, the rise in expensed R&D is entirely an accounting reallocation effect rather than an expansion of underlying operational cash burn.

Price Indexation and Inflation Pass-Through

6.
Impaired pricing power: DiaSorin faces significant difficulties in passing raw material and supply chain inflation through to its customer base. Morgan Stanley highlighted on May 26, 2026, that escalating polymer and polypropylene prices could inflict an unhedged €8 million to €10 million annualized cost impact. These extraordinary inflationary pressures are not currently embedded in the company's mid-term guidance. This vulnerability implies that unhedged input cost inflation could severely compress operating margins.
7.
Superior peer indexation: Peer diagnostics companies demonstrate far more robust pricing power through active contract negotiations. Corporate transcripts from bioMérieux show that the company achieved a 2% overall price increase in the first half of 2023 to offset inflation. Furthermore, bioMérieux secured price increases of 4% in microbiology and over 5% in industrial applications. This comparison indicates that DiaSorin's pricing power remains structurally weaker than its closest European peers.
8.
Complex contract barriers: Sectors suppliers face significant hurdles due to the rigid nature of long-term laboratory contracts. Stratec management explained that laboratories typically operate under predefined three-year pricing agreements with diagnostic suppliers. This commercial setup makes it extremely difficult to implement price increases in the middle of active contract cycles. Consequently, suppliers are forced to absorb near-term input inflation until these contracts come up for renewal.
9.
Active price adjustments: Diagnostic tool providers utilize multiple mid-year price adjustments to defend their gross margins. QIAGEN implemented two distinct price increases in 2022 that delivered a net positive pricing impact of 300 basis points.
