Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

How concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months? This question sits at the heart of evaluating Hexatronic Group AB's (HTRO-SE) earnings trajectory as the company emerges from a severe European fiber-to-the-home destocking cycle and navigates an unprecedented North American recovery alongside explosive data center growth.

The answer directly determines whether Hexatronic's recent massive earnings beat in the third quarter of 2026, where actual diluted EPS of SEK 0.81 beat consensus estimates of SEK 0.59, powered by the Data Center segment growing 107% year-over-year, represents a sustainable inflection point or a temporary surge vulnerable to sudden reversal. Understanding the concentration versus diffusion of earnings drivers is critical because Hexatronic's North American recovery remains highly concentrated on a handful of large customers whose capital expenditure plans are volatile, with sales falling by 26% year-over-year in late 2025 because key regional customers suddenly deferred their orders, demonstrating that volume surges can quickly reverse during sudden customer-level spending pauses. - Earnings Calls The range and skew of plausible outcomes matters profoundly for Hexatronic's EPS development because the company operates at the intersection of multiple cyclical forces with fundamentally different volatility profiles.

On the other hand, Hexatronic reported a 9% year-over-year contraction in its European Fiber Solutions sales during the third quarter of 2026, primarily driven by weak demand in the key UK and DACH regions, with management expecting European FTTH market challenges to persist in the near term as operators remain cautious. cyclical linkages to EPS development flow through three distinct channels with materially different historical volatility patterns.

Section 2 – North American Fiber Solutions and Post-Destock Surges

Section summary
  1. Hexatronic's North American Fiber Solutions segment is experiencing a rapid post-destocking volume recovery driven by direct-sales frame agreements with emerging carriers.
  2. Historical peer evidence shows wide year-two performance dispersion where duct makers face price erosion while advanced connectivity players enjoy sustained margins.

Hexatronic Direct Sales Momentum

1.
Accelerating volume expansion: Hexatronic Group reported a dramatic turnaround in its North American operations as sales grew 51% year-over-year in the third quarter of 2026. This rapid recovery was fueled by robust demand in both fiber-to-the-home and transport networks. The strong growth prompted management to reverse its previous decision to downsize the Clinton facility in South Carolina. Consequently, North America has now officially surpassed Europe as the company's largest reporting region.
2.
Direct sales model advantages: Hexatronic's unique direct-sales strategy successfully targets emerging private- equity backed carriers directly in North America. This model helps bypass the massive inventory swings typically seen with third-party distributors. Unlike competitors who rely on distribution partners, Hexatronic delivers directly to customers and experiences milder destocking cycles. These direct relationships provide greater long-term visibility into carrier deployment schedules.
3.
Secured frame commitments: Hexatronic secured several major system agreements that bolster its multi-year expansion plans in the United States. For example, the company signed a three-year frame agreement worth SEK 400 million with Novos Fiber in mid-2024. These long-term agreements ensure stable volume deployment through local manufacturing facilities. This local production approach allows Hexatronic to remain fully compliant with federal BABA regulations.

Impact on Recoveries and Deferred Revenue

7.
Supply capped recoveries: Historical precedents indicate that supply-constrained recoveries typically result in deferred revenue rather than permanently lost orders. For instance, semiconductor equipment manufacturers built massive backlogs when supply constraints limited their shipping capabilities. These backlogs created stable deferred revenue cushions that supported subsequent quarters. Similarly, RedEye noted that Hexatronic's supply constraints will likely damp near-term growth rather than threaten it because the global fiber market is completely sold out.
8.
China supply relief: Supply bottlenecks in the global fiber market are expected to ease gradually as new manufacturing capacity comes online. Analysts expect global fiber supply to increase by 10% to 15% in early 2027 and another 20% to 25% in mid-2027. Much of this incremental capacity is expected to emerge from Chinese manufacturers. This Chinese supply is now increasingly acceptable in Western markets because of the severe physical shortage of local glass.
9.
Contrary evidence: Severe supply-side constraints can sometimes lead to permanently lost market share if frustrated customers migrate to vertically integrated competitors. Some network operators have already cancelled outstanding cable orders when non-integrated suppliers failed to guarantee delivery timelines. These operators chose to pay higher prices to integrated providers who maintain direct control over raw glass drawing. Therefore, prolonged shortages pose a genuine structural risk of permanent customer loss for non-integrated cable manufacturers like Hexatronic.
