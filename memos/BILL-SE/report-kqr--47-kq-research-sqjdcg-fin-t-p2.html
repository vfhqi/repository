Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Evaluating Billerud's pricing power across volatile Nordic fiber costs and global packaging capacity is essential for predicting its earnings development.
  2. Fluctuations in contract structures and tariff barriers will directly shape peer margins and sector returns through 2027.

The Core Pricing Power Question

1.
Crucial question: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: Rising Nordic wood costs with weak demand (2021 to mid-2025, after the Russian wood import ban); falling Nordic wood costs with storm-felled supply (late 2025 to 2026); European overcapacity in folding boxboard and cartonboard (the Oulu ramp-up and Chinese exports, 2025 to 2027); tightening virgin kraftliner and sack paper supply after North American closures (2025 to 2026); new recycled containerboard capacity entering Europe (2027); a strong Swedish krona against the euro and US dollar; US import tariffs on paper and board (from August 2025); high European gas prices raising recycled producers' costs (October 2026); consumer down-trading and slower plastic-to-paper substitution; liquid packaging board contract renewal rounds?"

Earnings Sensitivity and

2.
Peer Dynamics Vulnerable earnings: Billerud faces massive earnings sensitivity to wood raw materials and currency changes because of its asset footprint. DNB Carnegie reported on 2 October 2026 that a 10% change in fiber prices impacts Billerud's pre-tax profit by SEK 1,380 mm, while a 10% move in the Swedish krona impacts profits by SEK 640 mm. These shifts are highly material given that Billerud is trying to defend its operating margins amid elevated Nordic wood costs. Understanding whether Billerud can hold its liquid packaging board prices as wood costs roll over will dictate its EPS trajectory through 2027.
3.
Depressed margins: Stora Enso and Metsä Board exhibit high sensitivity to pulp and fiber costs which directly threatens peer margins. DNB Carnegie estimated on 2 October 2026 that a 10% change in wood costs delivered to Finnish mills impacts Metsä Board's EBIT by EUR 50 mm, while a 10% wood price decline affects Stora Enso's EBIT by EUR 238 mm. Peer margins have suffered as peak wood costs in mid-2025 severely squeezed mill profitability. Resolving whether peers can retain the benefits of falling wood costs in 2026 is central to predicting regional EPS recovery.

Operational Risks and

4.
Sector Headwinds Looming overcapacity: European paperboard producers face elevated risks from Stora Enso's massive Oulu mill expansion and new tariffs. Jefferies reported on 7 August 2026 that Stora Enso is ramping up its 750 kt Oulu consumer board machine, which threatens to worsen regional overcapacity and depress folding boxboard prices. Furthermore, US import tariffs of 15% and rising freight costs present severe headwind risks for exporters like Metsä Board. If these capacity additions and trade barriers prevent pricing power from recovering, the sector faces prolonged downside risk to consensus 2027 earnings.
5.
Debated assets: Nordic forest owners face intensive investor debate surrounding forest balance sheet values and potential asset separations. Jefferies noted on 12 January 2026 that investors are questioning the appropriate liquidity discount for forest assets, which historically shielded Holmen and SCA from downstream margin compression. Stora Enso is planning a major separation of its Swedish forest assets in the first half of 2027. How these forest valuations and demergers develop represents a critical related question that will redefine the sector's capital allocation and relative returns.

Potential for Resilient Mitigation

6.
Other side of the story: Billerud and its integrated peers might demonstrate superior resilience if supply discipline and structural plastic-to-paper substitution offset cost headwinds. Morgan Stanley noted on 31 July 2026 that despite low capacity utilization, the packaging industry successfully lifted containerboard prices, indicating better-than-expected price discipline. If sustainable packaging regulation accelerates and competitors implement significant capacity closures in Europe, Billerud's pricing power may hold up far better than historical precedents suggest. This outcome would drive significant EBITDA upgrades and surprise the cautious sell-side consensus.

Section 2 – Nordic Wood Costs and Fiber Supply Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Research across 21 relevant sources, 4 core companies, and 2 comparable past cases shows that rising Nordic wood costs after the Russian import ban squeezed margins but initiated strategic repricing and self-help programs across regional producers.
  2. Declining wood costs from late 2025 to 2026 driven by stormfelled timber offer operational relief but trigger strong expectations of subsequent product price concessions.

Fiber Supply Shock and

1.
Price Surges Severe inflation: Billerud experienced unprecedented raw material cost increases in Europe following the 2022 ban on Russian wood imports. Nordic wood prices surged to record levels, which heavily compressed the gross margins of non-integrated packaging producers. This structural supply shock forced Billerud to aggressively reprice its long-term contracts to offset the massive input headwinds. This represents a significant operational risk that depressed group earnings during the peak cost cycle.
2.
Structural deficit: The Russian import ban permanently removed approximately 10% of the regional pulpwood supply from the Nordic fiber market. This severe structural wood deficit could not be mitigated by increasing local forest harvesting or shifting regional sourcing. Consequently, pulpwood raw material costs in Sweden and Finland doubled compared to 2021 levels by mid-2025. This massive fiber cost escalation severely threatened the competitive position of European paperboard mills.
3.
Vulnerable integration: Billerud remains highly vulnerable to raw material cost inflation compared to its forest-owning Nordic peers. Peers like SCA own 2.6 million hectares of land and maintain a 60% wood self-sufficiency rate to shield their downstream mills. Stora Enso also possesses massive forest assets and manages wood supply through structured regional procurement. Without substantial forest holdings, Billerud must rely heavily on expensive private timber and Baltic imports.

Falling Costs and

4.
Storm Influx Rapid softening: Nordic wood costs began to turn during the second half of 2025 as industry demand weakened. Pulpwood prices in Finland fell by approximately 20% from their peak in mid-2025 as producers curtailed operating rates. Billerud anticipated that these falling fiber prices would provide substantial variable cost relief throughout 2026. This pricing correction helped stabilize regional production margins after years of intense cost pressure.
5.
Storm windfalls: The severe impact of storm Johannes in late 2025 generated a massive volume of salvage wood in Sweden and Finland. This sudden influx of storm-felled timber put immediate downward pressure on stumpage costs and increased local pulpwood availability. Holmen reallocated harvesting resources to clean up third-party storm damage while reducing its own forest harvesting. This localized supply surge helped depress Nordic pulpwood and sawlog prices into early 2026.

Historical Precedents and

8.
Pricing Sensitivities Delayed relief: Historical precedents show that wood cost changes impact paperboard producers with a substantial lag of three to six months. Billerud experienced this delay in late 2025 when initial spot price drops took several months to reflect in the cost of goods sold. In prior cycles, wood costs eventually moderated in tandem with softening end-market product pricing. However, Billerud now faces pressure to hold its liquid packaging board prices despite the falling cost base.
9.

Financial analysts estimate that every 10% decline in Nordic pulpwood prices represents a SEK 900 mm EBITDA tailwind for Billerud. This wood price drop delivers a corresponding EUR 100 mm EBIT benefit to peer Stora Enso. This massive leverage makes Billerud a primary beneficiary of Nordic cost relief if product prices hold.
10.
Other side of the story: Billerud's cost relief from falling wood prices may be entirely negated if regional overcapacity forces the company to pass all savings back to customers. Industry overcapacity in cartonboard and liquid packaging board could trigger intense competition and rapid price erosion. If Billerud cannot defend its end-product pricing, the margin expansion from cheaper fiber will not materialize. This scenario would prevent any meaningful EPS recovery despite lower stumpage costs through 2027.

Section 3 – Liquid Packaging Board Contracts and Market Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Commercial negotiations for liquid packaging board contracts shifted toward annual structures to protect margins from Nordic cost swings.
  2. The supply disruption at Nippon Paper's Dynawave mill removed significant global capacity, supporting regional pricing power.
  3. Confidence in Billerud's pricing power remains medium as falling wood costs and competitor expansions trigger concession demands from buyers.

Contract Terms and

1.
Annual Negotiations Resolute shift: Billerud permanently transformed its commercial sales framework by transitioning all of its liquid packaging board volume to one-year terms negotiated annually. Chief Executive Officer Ivar Vatne confirmed in a January 2026 earnings call that 100% of these volumes now sit on annual contract cycles rather than multi-year pricing agreements. This structural commercial modification was implemented across Region Europe's customer base to allow Billerud to adjust prices more rapidly in response to volatile fiber costs.
2.
Substantial gains: Billerud achieved a successful contract repricing round for its European operations at the start of the 2025 fiscal year. Management announced in February 2025 that concluded commercial agreements would yield a positive EBITDA contribution of SEK 500 mm to SEK 600 mm. This major profitability reset was driven by a combination of targeted price hikes and optimized mix agreements with large buyers.
3.
Stretched lag: Historical precedents indicate that liquid packaging board agreements carry substantial pricing transmission delays during intense regional cost inflation. Jefferies noted in October 2022 that long-term commercial contract structures create temporary margin squeezes when wood prices rise quickly. Peer Stora Enso highlighted timing delays to pass through prices on longer-term contracts in liquid packaging board, proving that historical contract rigidity prevented packaging producers from immediately passing on wood inflation during the 2022 cost wave.

The 2024 Escalation and Wood Cost Softening

4.
Realized progress: Billerud demonstrated solid pricing power in early 2024 by securing a critical price escalation compared to its peers. DNB Carnegie reported in March 2024 that Billerud achieved a 9% year-on-year price increase for its liquid packaging board, while peer Metsä Board reported stable board pricing for the same period. This robust upward pricing adjustment was crucial to stabilize margins against surging Nordic fiber prices.
5.
Softening trend: The pricing premium achieved during the prior commercial rounds began to roll over as regional fiber dynamics eased. Jefferies reported in February 2026 that liquid packaging board average selling prices for Billerud were projected to decline by 3% to 5% year-on-year. This initial pricing moderation reflects intense customer demands for price relief as wood costs fell from record peaks across Sweden and Finland.
6.
Contrary evidence: Billerud's main peer Stora Enso maintained a highly rigid consumer packaging pricing posture that resisted rapid downward adjustments as raw material costs declined. DNB Carnegie reported in April 2019 that Stora Enso's long-dated consumer board contracts typically insulate its average selling prices from immediate market corrections. This contract layout means that consumer board prices lag raw material cost movements by several quarters during regional cost deflation.

Peer Dynamics and Global Supply Disruptions

7.
Market dominance: Peer competitor Stora Enso maintains a commanding lead as the largest global producer of liquid packaging board. DNB Carnegie reported in April 2019 that Stora Enso is the global market leader in liquid packaging board and a market share leader in virgin fiber consumer boards. This massive scale gives Stora Enso significant influence over contract pricing and industry standards.
8.
Severe disruption: The global liquid packaging board market experienced a massive supply shock in mid-2026 due to an operational suspension at a major North American facility. Nippon Paper reported in August 2026 that a catastrophic chemical tank collapse on May 26, 2026, forced the complete closure of its Nippon Dynawave Packaging mill in Washington State. This unexpected suspension removed a significant portion of highly specialized global packaging capacity from active circulation.
9.
Tightened balances: The suspension of the Washington packaging mill dramatically altered global supply dynamics and benefited Nordic competitors. Morgan Stanley reported in June 2026 that Stora Enso is the world's #1 player in liquid packaging board, while the suspended Nippon Paper Dynawave mill accounted for approximately 8% of global supply. This severe supply shortfall triggered immediate replacement sourcing from Northern Europe, which supported regional capacity utilization and pricing frameworks.
10.
Prolonged constraints: The operational suspension at Nippon Paper's US subsidiary is projected to keep global board availability highly constrained throughout the next contract round. Nippon Paper announced in November 2026 that its subsidiary would record a substantial operating loss of JPY 21 bn due to the ongoing suspension. This prolonged outage keeps the broader market tight and limits buyer switching opportunities through 2027.

Converter Perspectives and

11.
Risk Mitigations Defended margins: Downstream packaging converters utilized long-term commercial agreements to insulate themselves from short-term board price volatility in the European market. Elopak reported in February 2026 that European liquid packaging board contracts are negotiated with the opportunity to adjust prices annually. This pricing mechanism protects downstream converters from intra-year cost fluctuations in key packaging substrates.
12.
Exposed cost: Downstream packaging converters face high sensitivity to liquid packaging board prices due to its dominant share of production costs. DNB Carnegie reported in February 2026 that liquid packaging board accounts for approximately 50% of Elopak's total costs. This heavy cost exposure forces converters to negotiate highly predictable annual price structures to protect their margins.
13.
Surcharge mitigation: Downstream buyers in the Americas faced immediate cost escalation following the regional supply disruptions in 2026. Elopak reported in August 2026 that it was forced to implement raw material surcharges to compensate for constrained board supplies. These surcharges allowed the company to pass on higher input costs to downstream customers and preserve operating margins.
14.
Locked structures: Large consumer packaging buyers leverage multi-year relationships to guarantee supply security rather than chasing spot price reductions. Jefferies noted in August 2024 that SIG Group utilizes multiyear volume agreements with Stora Enso and Billerud to manage its raw material risk. This purchasing strategy ensures stable input cost forecasting but reduces the buyer's ability to demand immediate price cuts.
15.
Other side of the story: Billerud's transition to annual contracts may severely backfire if falling wood costs and peer capacity additions prompt aggressive pricing concessions from competitors. Stora Enso's massive packaging footprint allows it to absorb lower margins, which could force Billerud to match deep discounts to defend its market share. If Billerud must concede substantial price relief during the 2027 negotiation rounds, its European division EBITDA will experience significant downward pressure.

Section 4 – Folding Boxboard and Cartonboard Capacity Pressures

Section summary
  1. European cartonboard markets face severe oversupply as Stora Enso's Oulu mill ramps up and Chinese imports elevate regional inventory levels.
  2. Wood cost relief in 2026 is being partially conceded to buyers as overcapacity erodes the pricing power of regional fresh-fiber board producers.
  3. Nordic peers and North American bleached board competitors are adjusting their grade mixes and geographical focus to defend margins.

Oversupply and

1.
Capacity Additions Gradual expansion: Stora Enso continues to ramp up its converted consumer board machine at the Oulu site in Finland. This major investment will add 750 ktpa of new consumer board capacity once the line is fully completed during 2027. Morgan Stanley reported on 10 November 2025 that this massive expansion will add substantial competitive pressure to a weak European packaging market. This represents a key drag on regional operating rates.
2.
Rising imports: European packaging markets face increased competitive headwinds from surging Chinese virgin cartonboard volumes. Chinese virgin cartonboard exports escalated from 1.90 million tonnes to 3.80 million tonnes in 2025. Industry contacts indicate that more Asian board is arriving in Europe despite higher logistical expenses. This influx has forced regional producers to compete directly against low-priced imports to maintain their market shares.
3.
Low utilization: European folding boxboard operating rates are projected to remain at depressed levels due to chronic oversupply. Jefferies reported on 7 August 2026 that regional operating rates will hover around 78% to 81% over 2026 to 2027. The broader European cartonboard market requires approximately 1.50 million to 2 million tonnes of permanent industry capacity closures to restore healthy operating balances. Without such rationalization, product pricing will continue to be set by the cost curve in an oversupplied environment.

Conceded Savings and

4.
Pricing Pressure Softening prices: Billerud faces intense customer demands to pass through recent wood cost relief in the form of lower product pricing. Jefferies reported on 9 January 2026 that folding boxboard prices are expected to decline by 4% year-on-year. The bear case for fresh-fiber board producers is that regional cost relief is given back to converting customers because of persistent overcapacity. This dynamic prevents Billerud from capturing the full margin benefits of cheaper pulpwood.
5.
Pricing lag: European cartonboard pricing adjustments typically track raw material cost movements with a substantial lag. Morgan Stanley noted on 4 February 2026 that lower pulpwood costs take three to six months to feed into the P&L of regional board producers. However, folding boxboard prices came under immediate pressure in early 2026 as overcapacity intensified. This pricing pressure prevents producers from retaining their raw material savings during the cost unwind.
6.
Contrary evidence: Expert insights suggest that folding boxboard and cartonboard prices might remain stable because extremely depressed industry margins prevent further price reductions. A former Vice President at Stora Enso stated on 24 December 2025 that cartonboard companies are suffering from such low profitability that customers do not dare ask for lower prices. Furthermore, major competitors like Metsä Board cannot afford to lower prices due to severe profitability pressures.

This tariff-driven volume decline forced Metsä Board to absorb the impacts or redirect exports back to Europe where overcapacity is already high. Consequently, Husum mill operating rates remained low and the integrate continued to lose money in late 2026.

Jefferies reported on 4 October 2021 that Holmen's premium solid bleached board is priced at a stable premium to the benchmark folding boxboard index. During the third quarter of 2026, Holmen maintained largely stable board pricing despite cautious consumer spending. This premium focus allows Holmen to avoid intense price competition on commodity board grades.

Section 5 – Kraftliner, Sack Paper, and Containerboard Market Shifts

Section summary
  1. European virgin kraftliner and semichemical fluting prices decoupled from recycled testliner benchmarks after 2021 due to energy advantages and tight supply.
  2. Unprecedented capacity rationalization and mill closures in North America dramatically reduced unbleached kraftliner export volumes to Europe.
  3. Mondi leveraged its dominant unbleached sack paper position to lead Q4 2026 price hikes, which Billerud tracked despite severe European energy inflation.

Spreads and

1.
Capacity Shifts Decoupled pricing: Virgin containerboard and recycled testliner prices exhibited significant divergence during the packaging cycle since 2021. Unbleached kraftliner prices rose rapidly by 60% from their late 2020 low of EUR 581 a tonne to a peak of EUR 750 a tonne in late 2022. However, a subsequent severe destocking cycle and weaker macroeconomic demand in Europe forced recycled testliner prices down by EUR 230 a tonne. This structural volatility demonstrates how different fiber grades respond independently to shifting end-market conditions.
2.
Energy advantages: The physical differences between virgin and recycled containerboard manufacturing drive contrasting cost structures and margin paths. Recycled testliner is highly energyintensive, typically requiring approximately 2 to 2.50 megawatt-hours of electricity to produce one tonne of paper. Modern virgin kraftliner mills require no external power because energy is embedded in the wood feedstock and separated as lignin. This structural energy integration insulates virgin-fibre producers like Billerud and Svenska Cellulosa AB from extreme energy price volatility.
3.
Capacity threats: The European containerboard pricing landscape faces medium-term oversupply headwinds from several planned recycled capacity additions. German packaging producer Palm announced the conversion of its King's Lynn newsprint machine in the UK to produce 700 ktonnes of recycled containerboard in early 2027. This massive single-site conversion will add approximately 1.60% new capacity to the already oversupplied 44 million tonne European market. These lowcost recycled additions threaten to erode regional operating rates and squeeze the pricing premium of virgin containerboard.
4.
Delayed entrants: The market penetration of new recycled capacity has been partially mitigated by extensive construction delays. Prominent recycled containerboard projects like Eren Paper in the United Kingdom have experienced prolonged operational start-up delays. This temporary delay has prevented a severe supply deluge and extended the pricing window for existing operators. However, the eventual ramp-up of these massive open-market machines is highly likely to trigger aggressive price competition by late 2027.

Transatlantic Supply Dynamics

5.
Historic closures: North American containerboard producers executed unprecedented supply discipline throughout 2025 and 2026 to address weak box demand. Major US packaging companies permanently shuttered substantial high-cost capacity, representing over 9% of the regional asset base. Georgia-Pacific permanently retired its 1 million ton Cedar Springs virgin mill, while International Paper closed its Red River and Riceboro facilities. These aggressive structural closures effectively rebalanced the domestic market and pushed US containerboard operating rates into the mid-90s.
6.
Shrunk exports: The aggressive rationalization of the US manufacturing footprint significantly curtailed unbleached kraftliner export volumes destined for Europe. Monthly US export shipments to the European Union and the UK dropped from over 50 ktonnes to approximately 29 ktonnes by mid-2026. This sharp export contraction removed substantial marginal supply from the European virgin market to support regional price recovery. This transatlantic tightening demonstrates how North American structural self-help directly bolsters Billerud's European competitive position.
7.
Squeezed imports: Mexican packaging converters faced growing difficulty securing unbleached kraftliner imports as North American supply balances tightened. US containerboard shipment volumes to Mexico declined sharply from late June 2026, forcing regional buyers to search for alternative paper. This localized supply squeeze pushed US export kraftliner prices up by USD 75 a tonne to reach USD 750 a tonne. Although some Mexican buyers attempted to source replacement paper from Brazil and Europe, available global supply options remained highly constrained.
8.
Fluting stability: Billerud's containerboard division benefits from the highly stable supply and demand dynamics of its semichemical fluting grade. Unlike recycled containerboard, the semichemical fluting market has experienced virtually no major capacity additions over the last thirty years. Semichemical fluting operating rates are projected to improve steadily through 2027 as European transport packaging demand recovers. This niche product exposure shields Billerud from the severe overcapacity plaguing commodity testliner and kraftliner grades.

Sack Kraft Price Leadership

9.
Leader initiatives: Mondi leveraged its dominant position in unbleached sack kraft paper to lead European price adjustments in late 2026. Mondi successfully secured a EUR 50 a tonne quarterly increase for external unbleached grades to protect its margins. This price adjustment lifted benchmark unbleached sack kraft paper to a midpoint of EUR 1,025 a tonne. Mondi's strong commercial positioning ensures that niche specialty packaging grades achieve rapid cost pass-through compared to fragmented recycled categories.
10.
Tracking increases: Billerud successfully capitalized on this supportive pricing environment by raising its own packaging paper prices in late 2026. Billerud implemented sequential price increases for fluting, liner, and brown sack and kraft paper grades during the third quarter of 2026. Billerud also announced further targeted upward price revisions exiting the period to offset elevated Nordic production costs. This commercial coordination indicates that Billerud possesses solid near-term pricing power within its integrated European fiber segments.
11.
Transmission delays: The financial benefits of rising unbleached sack paper prices face temporary lags before fully reflecting in producer earnings. Mondi typically experiences implementation time lags of several quarters because commercial contracts govern downstream deliveries. Consequently, the full positive EBITDA contribution of these Q4 2026 price hikes is not anticipated to materialize until the second quarter of 2027. This contractual lag means that Billerud must absorb short-term cost inflation before capturing downstream pricing gains.
12.
Resilient demand: The demand outlook for specialty kraft paper remains supported by expanding e-commerce volumes and plastic-to-paper substitution. Mondi reported solid 8% volume growth for unbleached kraft paper, driven by robust customer adoption of paper mailer bags. The ongoing structural transition away from single-use plastics provides a.

Energy Inflation and

14.
Cost Pressures Gas headwinds: Extreme natural gas price inflation severely deformed the European recycled containerboard cost curve in late 2026. Natural gas prices on the Dutch TTF index surged to EUR 100 per megawatt-hour in November 2026 due to geopolitical conflict. Since every EUR 10 per megawatt-hour gas increase adds approximately EUR 15 a tonne to cash costs, recycled producers faced severe margin compression. Consequently, analysts estimated that approximately 20% of the European recycled containerboard cost curve was operating at cash-negative margins.
15.
Lowered guidance: The severe energy inflation forced major integrated packaging producers to significantly reduce their full-year earnings expectations. Smurfit Westrock lowered its fiscal year EBITDA guidance from USD 4.90 billion to approximately USD 4.70 billion in late 2026. The company cited an unprecedented USD 700 million year-on-year freight and energy headwind, including a USD 230 million energy hit in Q4 alone. This massive cost inflation highlights why European recycled box plants must aggressively implement paper price increases to defend their margins.
16.
Contrary evidence: European recycled containerboard prices could experience rapid downward corrections in 2027 if winter natural gas costs moderate. Sell-side analysts forecast that the EUR 60 to EUR 80 a tonne September 2026 price increases will be given back in Q2 2027.
