Competitor and regulatory disruptions

5.
Disruptive regulator influence: The Hellenic Telecommunications and Post Commission possesses both the regulatory mandate and the power to push OTE's operational results away from consensus projections. OTE reported on July 29, 2026, that regulatory approval of wholesale discount agreements is critical for sustaining infrastructure utilization rates. If the regulator delays approvals or demands deeper discounts to protect smaller competitors, OTE's wholesale revenues will decline rapidly. This regulatory friction could severely impact OTE's domestic margins, compressing future earnings per share.
6.
Aggressive network overbuilding: The potential creation of a single wholesale FTTH platform by Vodafone Greece and PPC presents an unprecedented long-term threat to Hellenic Telecom's infrastructure monopoly. Euronext Athens reported on August 5, 2026, that the proposed 50:50 joint venture merges the operations of PPC FiberGrid and Vodafone Fiber2All to create a neutral wholesale provider. If completed, the venture will establish a highly formidable competitor targeting 3.8 million homes passed by the end of 2028. This massive footprint expansion would bypass OTE's networks, diluting wholesale demand and dragging down earnings per share.

Contrary evidence: Hellenic Telecom's retail fixed service revenue is already showing signs of weakening, contrasting with the optimistic growth expectations held by major broker houses. Morgan Stanley reported on October 30, 2026, that retail fixed service revenue growth fell to negative 0.6% in the third quarter of 2026 from positive 1.1% in the first quarter. This unexpected contraction demonstrates that pricing pressure from competitors is already impacting OTE's core top-line performance. Such ongoing negative revenue trends could eventually depress earnings per share if fixed-line customer churn rises.
8.
Robust cash protection: Hellenic Telecom's defensive structural positioning suggests that even extreme competitive scenarios are unlikely to completely derail its earnings per share trajectory. Deutsche Bank argued on May 11, 2026, that OTE's exceptionally low leverage of 0.2x net debt to EBITDAaL provides a powerful financial buffer against market shocks. This pristine balance sheet ensures that OTE can sustain its extensive share buyback program even during periods of operational pressure. Consequently, share count reductions will continue to support earnings per share, offsetting minor operating income declines.
9.
Other side of the story: While consensus views assume a stable competitive environment, aggressive overbuilding by the Vodafone-PPC joint venture could permanently lower the return on fiber assets across Greece. Morgan Stanley reported on September 16, 2026, that the proposed joint venture might ease overbuild concerns through market rationalization. However, if the venture aggressively targets OTE's core urban customer bases, it will spark destructive infrastructure duplication. This would depress utilization rates below OTE's current 41.7% and impair long-term asset valuations, ultimately dragging down future earnings per share.
