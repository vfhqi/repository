Executive Summary

Section summary

The European engineered heavy process plant supplier landscape presents a complex investment case where robust structural catalysts in green steel and hydropower coexist with cyclical headwinds in automotive and biomaterials segments. The peer group comprising Andritz, Danieli, Dürr, Valmet, and OC Oerlikon demonstrates differentiated trajectories shaped by their exposure to multi-year decarbonization projects, resilient service revenues, and varying degrees of Chinese competitive pressure. A concentrated long-only fund demands stable cash flows before allocating capital to support a predictable double-digit return target. The investment thesis rests on whether record order backlogs translate into predictable earnings expansion or whether project execution risks, margin compression, and cyclical capital expenditure lulls undermine profitability targets. Danieli maintained a massive backlog of nearly €6bn in its Plantmaking segment as of mid-2026, driven by electric arc furnace transition, while Andritz experienced an 81.8% surge in Hydropower order intake in the first half of 2026, reaching €2,445.9m driven by grid stability demand. These order inflections provide multi-year revenue visibility but introduce execution risk concentration.

The service and automation transformation across the peer group provides crucial earnings insulation. Andritz generates nearly 58% of pulp and paper revenue from services, which yield three times higher margins than new capital equipment sales, while Valmet generated approximately 89% of its operating profits from services and automation, which underpins its financial stability during capital expenditure lulls. This structural margin expansion contrasts sharply with cyclical capital equipment volatility, where Valmet experienced a 25% decline in biomaterial equipment orders in early 2026, showing that pulp overcapacity remains a drag and Dürr recorded a

€94.9M goodwill impairment in 2026, revealing structural electric vehicle headwinds.

Green steel megaprojects represent the most significant structural growth catalyst. Danieli secured a major direct reduction contract with Salzgitter Flachstahl for its SALCOS program in Germany to build a two million tonne direct reduction iron facility to deliver zero direct carbon emissions, while Thyssenkrupp's tkH2Steel project in Duisburg, Germany, utilizes a 2.5 million tonne hydrogen-ready direct reduction plant with up to €2 billion in federal and state funding de-risking the massive capital outlay. Jefferies highlights that European steelmakers are planning around 20 to 25 major low-carbon steel projects, supporting 50 million tonnes of EAF capacity by 2030. These projects benefit from favorable regulatory frameworks, including Germany's €50 billion Carbon Contracts for Difference scheme directly supports local heavy industry transitions, de-risking low-carbon technology investments across hard-to-abate industrial sectors.

The competitive landscape reveals selective Chinese encroachment in commodity segments but enduring European technological moats in high-complexity applications. German supplier Dürr defends its dominant 40% to 50% market share in automotive paint shops by delivering highly complex turnkey project execution that lowcost competitors cannot replicate, while Swiss surface specialist OC Oerlikon protects its market position with advanced physical vapor deposition coatings, which represent a fraction of customer costs but are absolutely mission-critical for operational lifetime in aerospace and semiconductors.

However, Chinese players are selectively encroaching on commodity components, as evidenced by Southern and Eastern European wind developers increasingly using aggressive commercial offers from Chinese turbine manufacturers to extract massive pricing concessions from established European suppliers. 10 & Co Redburn Material data gaps persist around backlog profitability, revenue conversion timing, and Chinese competitive winrates.

Analysts struggle to dissect the exact profitability of Danieli's €6bn backlog, specifically how inflationary DRI equipment contracts convert to margins, while investors cannot precisely forecast when Andritz will recognize Hydropower revenues because its backlog-to-revenue conversion rate dropped below 60%. True penetration rates of Chinese process plant equipment in Central Europe remain undocumented, requiring targeted regional expert calls.

These gaps constrain near-term earnings forecasting precision despite improved order visibility. Second-order implications include accelerated corporate restructuring driven by shareholder activism and defensive regulatory pivots. European shareholder activists capitalize on cheap market valuations to demand prompt corporate breakups and asset divestments, while EU policymakers utilize reformed public procurement acts to exclude non-reciprocal foreign bidders from domestic industrial tenders.

The green transition trajectory faces execution risks from European contractors facing severe project delays and cost overruns as sustainable steel and green cement carry premiums of 40% to 100% and decarbonization projects facing severe infrastructure bottlenecks because European renewable energy installations face a grid queue six times larger than annual deployment.

The investment case ultimately depends on whether record backlogs and service mix improvements offset cyclical capital equipment weakness and execution margin pressure. The peer group demonstrates divergent positioning, with Andritz and Danieli capturing multi-year green infrastructure projects, Valmet and OC Oerlikon leveraging service-led margin resilience, and Dürr navigating automotive sector volatility. The 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory appears partially resolvable for service-heavy peers with diversified end-market exposure but remains contingent on megaproject execution timing, subsidy disbursement schedules, and the pace of Chinese competitive displacement in mid-tier market segments.

A concentrated equity portfolio requires close monitoring of quarterly backlog conversion rates, project-level margin disclosures, and regulatory policy implementation to assess whether structural tailwinds materialize into predictable earnings growth or whether cyclical and execution risks dominate near-term financial performance.
