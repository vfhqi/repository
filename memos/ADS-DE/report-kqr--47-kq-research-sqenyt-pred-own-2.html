Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Standardized free cash flow definitions diverge significantly across sell-side brokers and historical disclosures because of varying lease, intangible, and seasonal working capital treatments.

Defining the

1.
Key Question Investigating metrics: "What are adidas AG's (ADS-DE) annual lease payments (repayment of lease liabilities and interest on lease liabilities), right-of-use depreciation, capital spending on intangible assets, and the seasonal pattern of operating working capital (third-quarter end against year-end, 2019 to 2026); and how do adidas's own free cash flow, FactSet's free cash flow definition and brokers' free cash flow estimates (Bernstein, Jefferies, Morgan Stanley, UBS) differ in their treatment of leases, intangibles and working capital for 2025 to 2028?"
2.
Strategic leases: Lease accounting standards dictate how adidas AG structures its capital commitment disclosures and reports operational cash generation. Under IFRS 16 rules, the lease repayments are split into redemption payments and interest expenses, which directly shifts cash flows from operating activities to financing activities. Analysts track lease interest and principal payments to establish whether rent is treated as an operating expense or financing outflow in valuation models. This accounting choice alters reported EBITDA and changes the baseline scale of valuation drivers.
3.
Valuation variance: Adjusting for lease obligations changes the perceived enterprise value of adidas AG and alters peer comparisons. For instance, capitalizing leases adds billions in debt-like liabilities, which directly depresses the equity valuation unless models offset this by boosting operating cash flows. Jefferies highlights that including lease liabilities in net debt metrics increases total net debt from €1,024 million to €3,937 million as of December 2025. This variance explains why cash flow definitions must be standardized for valuation accuracy.
4.
Intangible investments: Capital spending on intangible assets such as software directly influences the amortization profile and operating profit margins of adidas AG. Under IAS 38, development costs for internally generated software are capitalized on the balance sheet, which temporarily supports reported earnings per share. This capitalization of IT systems means that future amortization will weigh on profit margins as assets are deployed. Capitalizing these costs boosts short-term cash flows from operating activities while increasing investing cash outflows.

Seasonality and Cash Flow Divergence

5.
Seasonal swings: Operating working capital seasonality causes major fluctuations in cash availability and short-term leverage for adidas AG. Typically, inventory builds up significantly ahead of the holiday season, which leads to peak working capital requirements at the end of the third quarter. Wholesale payment terms with retail partners also delay cash collections, meaning that operating cash flows are seasonally depressed prior to year-end collections. Understanding these dynamics helps investors assess whether liquidity pressures are structural or purely temporal.
6.
Broker metrics: Sell-side analysts apply divergent free cash flow definitions to adidas AG, creating varied projections for investor returns. Jefferies defines free cash flow by explicitly subtracting repayments of lease liabilities from operating cash flows, resulting in a forecasted free cash flow of €2,395 million for 2026. This conservative treatment reflects actual cash rent commitments and directly impacts the perceived equity free cash flow yield of the firm.
7.
Alternative definitions: In contrast to peers, some brokers focus on Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF), which ignores certain financing cash outflows. Bernstein Research forecasts FCFF for adidas AG to reach €1,337 million in 2026 and €2,367 million in 2027. By utilizing FCFF, this methodology does not deduct lease repayments or interest expense, which leads to a higher reported free cash flow metric than equity-focused models.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some brokers argue that lease accounting changes have a minimal net impact on operating profit and valuation models for adidas AG. In the 2019 earnings call, management noted that lower rent expenses under IFRS 16 were mostly offset by higher right-of-use depreciation, creating a positive operating profit impact of only around €10 million. Section 3 – Capital Spending on Intangible Assets

Section summary
  1. adidas AG manages its capital spending on intangible assets by prioritizing internally generated software capitalization while peers show divergent IT expensing policies.

Capitalization and Accounting Policies

1.
Systematic software capitalization: adidas AG capitalizes development costs for internally generated software under IAS 38 when technological and economic feasibility is demonstrated. The company amortizes these capitalized intangible assets on a straight-line basis over a defined useful life of 3 to 10 years once they are ready for use. This treatment removes immediate development expenses from the income statement, which temporarily supports reported earnings per share. However, this policy results in a future amortization drag on margins as these technology assets are systematically deployed across the global network.
2.
Declining software additions: adidas AG recorded additions of €25 million to internally developed intangible assets in 2025, representing a decrease from €37 million in 2024. These assets are primarily related to internally generated software and digital platforms designed to enhance e-commerce operations. Despite lower additions, the net carrying amount of internally developed intangibles increased to €81 million in 2025 from €74 million in 2024. This trend suggests that adidas is maturing its existing digital tools rather than launching massive new development projects.

Moderating intangible spending: adidas AG reduced its total annual cash investments in intangible assets to €114 million in 2025, compared to €121 million in 2024. The research analyzed four sportswear companies across 10 relevant documents and one historical ERP migration case. This represents a significant contraction from the €141 million spent in 2023. German GAAP differences: adidas AG reported in its separate German HGB financial statements that it capitalized €20.05 million for internally generated software in 2023. Additionally, the company recognized €11.61 million for internally generated software under construction during the same fiscal year. Under HGB regulations, these capitalized internally generated assets restrict the distribution of reserves by €50.30 million to protect corporate creditors. This structural difference highlights how local statutory rules can limit dividend flexibility even when IFRS cash metrics appear robust.

Sportswear Peer Comparisons

5.
Longer amortization horizons: Nike Inc. capitalizes major software purchases and internally developed software under US GAAP and amortizes them on a straight-line basis over 2 to 12 years. This amortization range is slightly longer than adidas's 3 to 10 year useful life policy for software. Nike also capitalizes direct external development costs and specific internal payroll expenses associated with software projects. This accounting policy permits slower straight-line depreciation on the income statement, which can help shield short-term operating overhead margins during technology transitions.
6.
Operative IT expensing: Puma SE altered its accounting approach in 2026 by recognizing certain IT and digital investments directly as operating expenses rather than capitalizing them. This policy shift increased general and administrative expenses but allowed Puma to reduce its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to under €150 million. This decision represents a more conservative cash classification than adidas's continued capitalization of software development. This difference in methodology can distort direct peer EBITDA and margin comparisons across the sportswear sector.
7.
Emerging peer capitalization: On Holding AG capitalized CHF 2.70 million in intellectual property and customized software in 2025, which increased from CHF 1.40 million in 2024. This scale of investment is incredibly small compared to the €114 million that adidas invested in intangible assets in 2025. This comparison demonstrates that smaller sportswear brands face far lower balance sheet drag from legacy IT systems. Consequently, these emerging competitors can maintain higher asset turnover ratios and agile operating structures.
8.
Expensed brand presentations: Nike Inc. expenses all retail brand presentation and marketing costs when the presentation is complete, rather than capitalizing them on the balance sheet. Nike also carries substantial off-balance sheet endorsement contract obligations that totaled $15.50 billion as of May 31, 2026. Puma SE similarly expenses its licensing and advertising agreements, disclosing €2.73 billion in commitments as of December 31, 2025. This practice highlights that brand-related spending remains largely expensed across the sportswear industry, leaving capital spending focused on technology.

Section 4 – Seasonal Patterns of Operating Working Capital

Section summary
  1. adidas AG experiences pronounced seasonal working capital swings, with operating working capital peaking at the end of the third quarter due to inventory stocking for the holiday season.
  2. Wholesale partners representing about 57% of sales drive higher trade receivables during Q3, which subsequently liquidate into cash by year-end to fund operating cash flows.

Seasonal Working Capital Swings

1.
Elevated seasonal requirements: adidas AG experienced a substantial operating working capital expansion to €6,682 million by the end of the third quarter of 2026. This peak compared to €6,179 million in the prior year's third quarter, representing an increase of 8%. This upward movement illustrates how working capital peaks in Q3 before year-end cash releases.
2.
Improving capital efficiency: adidas AG recorded average operating working capital as a percentage of sales at 23.9% for the trailing twelve months ending September 30, 2026. This metric stood at 21.9% in the prior year's third quarter, showing a 2 percentage point increase. Management actively monitors this KPI to track how efficiently capital is locked up in product cycles.
3.
Historical baseline changes: adidas AG reported operating working capital of €5,556 million at the end of December 2025, which rose from €4,306 million at year-end 2024. The 29% expansion was driven by investments in inventory to support planned sales growth. This trend indicates that operating requirements fluctuate significantly between Q3 peaks and December balance sheet dates.
4.
Pandemic disruption baselines: adidas AG managed through severe operational disruptions in 2020, during which average operating working capital as a percentage of sales jumped to 23.5% from 18.1% in 2019. Broad-based store closures led to a sharp revenue decline and elevated unsold inventory. This extreme period demonstrated how external shocks distort seasonal working capital management.

Inventory Dynamics and Holiday Prep

5.
Growing inventory volumes: adidas AG carried inventories of €5,739 million on September 30, 2026, compared to €5,471 million at the end of the third quarter of 2025. The company built up these product volumes specifically to support healthy growth and upcoming seasonal sales. This build-up reveals how inventory expansions precede peak winter holiday demands.
6.
Promotional risk indicators: Morgan Stanley notes that elevated footwear inventory and discounting across parts of Europe are expected to remain headwinds through the holiday season. The broker highlights that retailers are planning 2027 orders more conservatively due to recent inventory builds and slowing consumer demand. This caution suggests that unplanned inventory accumulation increases retail markdown risks.
7.
Clear recovery paths: Jefferies Research notes that margins should improve quickly as brand heat remains favorable and local management utilizes manufacturing assets. The broker models an EBIT margin recovery to 10.3% in 2027 and 10.7% in 2028. This projection assumes that inventory clearance runs smoothly without compromising brand equity.
8.
Contrary evidence: adidas AG reduced its inventory days lasting through proactive management and successfully cleared excess stocks during 2024. Annual report figures show inventories rose only 10% to €4,989 million in December 2024 despite significantly stronger underlying sales. This shows that seasonal builds do not always lead to margin-damaging stock surpluses.

Wholesale Partner Receivables

9.
Rising trade receivables: adidas AG reported accounts receivable of €3,661 million at the end of September 2026, reflecting a 7% increase from €3,428 million in September 2025. These balances seasonally contract by December, as seen in the €2,634 million year-end receivable balance of 2025. This contraction shows that wholesale collections accelerate post-holiday delivery phases.
10.
Wholesale expansion effects: adidas AG trade receivables grew 27% to €2,413 million at the end of 2024 from €1,906 million in 2023. The company attributed this change to higher wholesale sell-in and a more sizable wholesale partner business. This expansion illustrates how wholesale partner terms delay cash collection relative to direct-to-consumer channels.
11.
Declining historical collections: adidas AG reported accounts receivable of €2,175 million at the end of December 2021, compared to €1,952 million in December 2020. Strong top-line growth drove receivables higher, which was partially offset by Reebok reclassifications. The historical data confirms that receivables track top-line momentum closely across multiple seasons.

Payables and Supplier Credit

12.
Flat trade payables: adidas AG recorded accounts payable of €2,717 million as of September 30, 2026, showing minimal change from the €2,720 million reported in 2025. These payables typically increase during seasonal sourcing peaks and are subsequently paid down by year-end. This pattern suggests that supplier credit helps buffer the working capital demands of inventory builds.
13.
Sourcing volume fluctuations: adidas AG accounts payable decreased 6% to €2,910 million at the end of 2025 from €3,096 million in 2024. Management noted that this decrease was driven by more evenly distributed sourcing volumes compared to previous years. This change reflects how production schedules impact credit terms and cash requirements.
14.
Historical payable swings: adidas AG reported that accounts payable rose 36% in December 2024 to €3,096 million from €2,276 million in 2023. The company experienced higher sourcing volumes as retail demand recovered, which expanded supplier liabilities. This increase shows how supplier credit acts as a critical financing source during growth phases.

15.
Seasonal cash generation: adidas AG registered cash flows from operating activities of €1,971 million during the first nine months of 2026. This operating performance rebounded sharply from a negative cash flow of €275 million during the same period in 2025. The comparison shows that working capital liquidations generate massive cash inflows in later quarters.

Therefore, relying heavily on year-end cash inflows leaves the firm highly vulnerable to seasonal delivery delays or sudden retail demand contractions.
