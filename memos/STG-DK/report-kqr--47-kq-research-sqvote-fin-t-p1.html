Cost Base Structure

1.
Steady procurement: Scandinavian Tobacco Group manages leaf sourcing through a global network of over five thousand suppliers. The company does not engage in tobacco farming but purchases raw leaves directly to maintain structural flexibility. While leaf procurement functions as a variable expense, raw materials inventory remains extremely high to protect against unexpected supply disruptions. This buffering strategy dilutes direct variable cost benefits during volume downswings and complicates short-term operating leverage.
2.
Elevated payrolls: Staff expenses at Scandinavian Tobacco Group reached DKK 1,880.9 million in 2024 compared to DKK 1,748.2 million in 2023. Labor costs in the Dominican Republic, Honduras, and Nicaragua are highly intensive because handmade premium cigars require extensive manual craftsmanship. These rolling expenses behave as semi-fixed local rolling labor costs due to rigid local employment arrangements. Thus, volume declines generate immediate operating deleverage across these Central American premium facilities.
3.
Rigid overheads: Selling and administrative expenses at Scandinavian Tobacco Group carry substantial lease liabilities that increase structural overheads. The group reported lease obligations of DKK 410.7 million in 2024 to support its commercial presence. These lease commitments represent highly rigid fixed rental costs for thirteen US retail superstores. Consequently, retail operating leverage remains highly sensitive to retail traffic shifts and consumer sentiment trends.

Footprint Rigidity

4.
Complex conversion: Manufacturing conversion at Scandinavian Tobacco Group is split between highly automated machine-rolled sites and manual premium facilities. Machine-made cigarillo production in Belgium and Italy relies on high-speed equipment with minimal direct unit labor. However, these automated plants carry high depreciation and maintenance overheads that act as fixed costs. Therefore, machine-rolled profit margins contract rapidly during structural volume declines because fixed equipment costs cannot be easily scaled down.
5.
Fragmented footprint: Sourcing binders and wrappers from Indonesia and Sri Lanka while processing them in Belgium creates immense footprint complexity. Sourcing raw materials from over forty countries further increases logistics overheads and freight exposures. This fragmented supply chain locks in substantial fixed international distribution costs for machine-rolled cigars. Consequently, the European Branded division is highly vulnerable to transportation price shocks and shipping delays.
6.
Low cost leverage: Monthly agricultural minimum wages in Honduras were set at US$345 in 2025. While local Central American wages are low compared to European standards, they still represent significant absolute fixed operating outlays for premium handmade operations. Because premium cigar crafting is completely un-automated, manufacturers cannot easily dilute these wages through technology. Thus, premium cigar margins remain permanently exposed to local wage inflation.
