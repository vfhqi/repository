Part 1: Sector-Wide Cost Structures, Margins, and Capacity Cycles

The global electrical transmission and distribution (T&D) equipment sector is characterized by high capital intensity, long backlog execution periods, and varying degrees of fixed-cost operating leverage. Historically, the sector operates in extended 4-to-6-year boom-and-bust cycles. 1. The 2008–2011 Boom & 2013 Price War: In the 2008–2011 cycle, over-leveraged mid-tier capacity additions led to industry-wide price wars and a severe margin collapse in 2013 Grid equipment margins are historically volatile and have frequently dropped well below 16%. 2. Fixed-Price Contract Lag Precedents: In inflationary up-cycles, long-term fixed-price contracts with limited escalation clauses (historically representing 70% of Landis+Gyr's backlog, for example) expose manufacturers to severe margin pressure as raw material and spot-freight costs inflate. 3. Capacity Constraints vs. Demand: In the current electrification supercycle (2023–2026), capacity–rather than demand–is the primary binding constraint, pushing lead times for large power transformers out from 2 years to 5 years.
