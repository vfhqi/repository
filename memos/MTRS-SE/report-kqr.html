Section 3 – Working Capital, Cash Flow, and below-EBIT Leakage

Section summary
  1. Munters' operating working capital is kept exceptionally low by a temporary customer-advance float, risking cash outflows when order intake normalizes.
  2. Severe below-EBIT leakage from variablerate net interest and high tax rates continues to dilute Munters' earnings per share.
  3. Strong Swedish krona translation exposure creates significant downside risk to Munters' operating earnings and cash flow generation.

Working capital efficiency and normalization risk

Munters Group achieved an operating working capital (OWC) as a percentage of sales of 4.2% in Q3 2026. This exceptional level represents a notable improvement from 8.3% in Q3 2025 and 11.6% in Q4 2024. However, this current level sits far below management's long-term average OWC target range of 10% to 13% of net sales. The primary driver keeping OWC suppressed is a massive influx of advances from customers, which reached SEK 4,094m in Q3 2026 compared to SEK 2,109m in Q3 2025. This customer-advance float acts as a short-term cash buffer, but it creates material balance sheet risks when order intake eventually normalizes.

Below-EBIT financial leakage and tax structures

Munters suffers from substantial below-EBIT financial leakage that dilutes the translation of operating profit into net income. For the full year 2025, Munters recorded net financial items of SEK -413m, driven primarily by variable-rate debt expenses. DNB Carnegie's financial statements show that net interest expenses are projected to remain elevated at SEK 466m in 2025, before slightly recovering to SEK 413m in 2026. Because the majority of Munters' long-term bank loans carry variable interest rates, the group remains highly exposed to central bank rate policies. A one percentage point increase in global interest rates would impact Munters' net financial income by SEK -52m.

The effective tax rate for Munters presents another source of earnings leakage, consistently exceeding Sweden's corporate tax rate. Munters reported an effective tax rate of 31% for both FY2024 and FY2025. This high tax rate is driven by unrecognized deferred tax assets on losses in Sweden and Germany. In comparison, Belimo reported an effective tax rate of 18.6% in 2025 and 16.7% in 2024. Systemair recorded a tax rate of 25.2% in Q1 2026/27, down from 27.3% in the prior year. This regional tax mismatch means that Munters converts a smaller portion of its EBIT into net income compared to European peers.

- Earnings Calls Items affecting comparability (IAC) further complicate Munters' reported earnings quality. For the full year 2025, IACs totaled a negative SEK -324m, up from SEK -240m in 2024. These charges include restructuring activities of SEK -77m, M&A expenses of SEK -40m, and contingent considerations of SEK -207m. In Q3 2026, Munters recognized an additional SEK -64m in IACs, including SEK -23m for restructuring and SEK -5m for M&A. These recurring adjustments indicate that "non-comparable" costs are a persistent drag on EPS.
