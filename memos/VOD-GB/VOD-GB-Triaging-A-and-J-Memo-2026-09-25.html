[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Vodafone is a self-help case that the market has already paid for. Three years of exits, a UK merger bought out in full and 11,000 fewer roles have travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain into the sell-side's estimates, and the share price has travelled it faster: at 125.3p and 11.3 times two-year-forward earnings, the 95th percentile of the stock's own five years, our modal path returns about 2 percent a year over eighteen months against the framework's 20 percent bar, while the German consumer business, about 40 percent of underlying operating profit, is still shrinking. The case cleared the bar at about 98p in July 2026; it does not at this price.

  • The change is internal, broad and executed, which is the case's strength.
    • Eight of the twelve internal forces rate B or better, against the framework's screen of at least three, and the largest of them are finished rather than promised.
    • Spain and Italy sold for EUR 13.0bn
      • Three UK merged and the remaining 49 percent bought for GBP 4.3bn, completed 30 July 2026
      • Safaricom consolidated from 30 June 2026
      • net debt cut from a EUR 52.0bn peak in July 2019 to EUR 25.4bn at 31 March 2026
    • 11,000 roles removed against a 10,000 target
      • a EUR 1.0bn net European opex reduction targeted for FY27 to FY30 and GBP 700m of UK synergies by FY30
    • The chief executive is three and a half years into the job, so the chief-executive force itself is no longer new
      • what is live is integration and cost-out
  • The external forces net to a mild headwind, concentrated in the largest market.
    • Regulation has turned into a tailwind on consolidation and spectrum, and Africa grows at 12.6 percent organic, but German fibre and German competition run against the company and are strengthening.
    • Deutsche Telekom has passed 13.6 million households with its own fibre at 17.5 percent penetration, and charges a 51 percent premium on 1Gbps plans
      • Vodafone's German broadband net losses were -98,000 in Q1 FY27, worse than the quarter before
    • Emerging-market currency translation took 5.4 percentage points off reported growth in FY26 and now acts on about a third of adjusted EBITDAaL
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned and the sell-side has followed.
    • The four key outputs are improving against the last three years in the reported accounts, and estimates have risen on every window.
    • Organic service revenue +5.2 percent and organic adjusted EBITDAaL +6.2 percent in Q1 FY27
      • the FY26 adjusted free cash flow of EUR 2,621m was the first growth in four years
    • FY27 guidance raised on 27 July 2026 to EUR 13.0bn to 13.3bn of adjusted EBITDAaL and EUR 2.6bn to 2.9bn of adjusted free cash flow, with the upper end expected
      • consensus FY2028 EPS +16.0 percent in twelve months to 2026-09-11
  • The change has reached the last layer of the chain, which is also where the case's materiality runs out.
    • Because the upgrades have been made and the price has moved 46.2 percent in twelve months, what is left to be paid for is the tail of the pattern: the UK synergy ramp and the opex programme, both already in the sell-side's models.
  • Our earnings path grows about 12 percent a year, at the bottom of the framework's band, below consensus's 15 percent.
    • The gap is below the EBITDA line, the part investors do not extrapolate.
    • Our path: FY2027 EUR 0.106, FY2028 EUR 0.121, FY2029 EUR 0.135, FY2030 EUR 0.150 on the brokers' adjusted basis
      • consensus EUR 0.106, EUR 0.130, EUR 0.140 and a thin two-broker EUR 0.171
    • Consensus's FY2028 step of +22.7 percent leans on lower tax and depreciation while UK capital expenditure peaks in FY27, Safaricom's EUR 1.1bn of EBITDAaL carries a large minority, and the sell-side itself models German EBITDAaL down 1.2 to 2 percent a year
    • Only about 45 percent of our 12 percent comes from revenue and margin
      • the rest comes from depreciation, interest, tax, minorities and the share count
  • What it costs: 125.3p, 11.3 times the consensus blend of FY2028 and FY2029 earnings, 11.9 times our own.
    • That is the 95th percentile of the stock's five-year range of 7.00x to 12.03x (median 9.23x), above Deutsche Telekom at 9.8x and BT at 10.7x, level with Orange at 11.4x, and above the block's median target of EUR 1.48 against a price of EUR 1.53.
  • The 'Modal return' does not clear the bar, and the multiple is the swing factor.
    • At a multiple blended between a held 11.3x and a de-rated 9.5x, applied as 10.0x, the return is +3.2 percent over eighteen months (2.1 percent a year) and +21.1 percent over three years (6.6 percent a year), an F on the framework's anchors.
    • At a held 11.3x the same earnings path gives about 10.4 percent a year, a D
      • only consensus's two-broker FY2030 figure and a held multiple together reach 20.1 percent a year, and that is what the price assumes
    • The cash return of about 3.3 percent a year is real and covered
      • the earnings leg is plausible
      • the re-rating leg is negative and probable, because the multiple holding needs German retail to stabilise and the UK synergies to land at once, which we put at 26 percent
    • On the plausibly worse drop-through, no improvement in net income as a share of EBITDAaL, the eighteen-month return is -9.3 percent at 10.0x and +1.9 percent at 11.3x, F either way
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' in its late stage.
    • The fit is crisp on inputs and outputs
    • the payoff, major EPS trajectory upgrades, has largely been taken in the twelve months to September 2026.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the chief executive's actions through the operating metrics, the financial statements and the estimates.
    • It breaks at the price, because a change the market has paid for is not material to the return, and it is fragile in Germany, where the base has shrunk at about 95,000 broadband lines a quarter for six months and the wholesale revenue that has masked it laps from Q2 FY27.

  • The case therefore is a well-executed restructuring whose earnings upgrades are real and whose price already carries them: worth watching for the price at which it clears, about 98p to 110p, and not worth progressing at 125.3p.

  • Rating = D, because the 'Investment Thesis' measures materiality along the whole chain and the chain breaks at its last link: the input forces are strong (B) and the outputs are improving (B), but the 'Modal return' they leave at 125.3p and 11.3x is single digits a year (F).

    • The parts do not average
    • the third Element binds, because a change the market has already paid for is not material to the return.
    • Not C, because C would need the modal return to reach the framework's D band on the central path, and it does so only if the multiple holds at the 95th percentile of its own history
    • not F, because the change is real, executed and in the estimates, and the case clears the bar at about 98p to 110p, a price the shares traded at ten weeks before the dashboard's date.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The competing hypothesis, at its strongest: we are anchoring the multiple on a history that no longer applies.
    • The five-year range of 7.00x to 12.03x was set by a Vodafone that cut its dividend twice, lost EUR 4.2bn in FY25 and shrank.
    • The new group grows service revenue at 5 percent, has a third of its EBITDAaL in a 12 percent grower, converts to free cash flow at 7 to 8 percent of its market value (consensus EUR 2,664m to a guided EUR 2,900m on EUR 36.0bn), and has a 19.9 percent shareholder with a record of forcing value out of European telecoms.
    • On that reading 11x to 12x is the new median, not the top, and the eighteen-month return at 11.3x on consensus's path is 20 percent a year.
  • What we concede.
    • The mix argument is real.
    • It is why our uncleared-branch anchor is 9.5x rather than the 9.23x median, and why we name 10.18x, the 75th percentile, as the floor the concession supports.
  • Why we hold.
    • The cohort's incumbents trade at 8x to 11x
    • the mix shift is a third of profit, not the whole
    • the FY2030 consensus that makes 20 percent rests on two brokers
    • the German retail base is still shrinking at about 95,000 broadband lines a quarter
    • and the return the competing hypothesis reaches needs every one of its conditions to hold at once.
    • Our response is the blend, which gives the cleared branch its 26 percent and the case its D.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the disruption that matters, Deutsche Telekom's fibre overbuild of Vodafone's German cable footprint, is strengthening, and Vodafone's response is capital-light and slower.
  • The overbuild: Deutsche Telekom has passed 13.6 million households with its own fibre and has 2.2 million active fibre customers, 17.5 percent penetration, as of its 6 August 2026 release.
  • The response: the OXG joint venture has passed 840,000 homes and expects over 1.2 million by the end of FY27
    • wholesale off-net through Deutsche GigaNetz covers 500,000 homes
    • the cable footprint is 25 million homes.
    • Gigabit cable net losses were -61,000 in Q1 FY27.
    • Dell'Oro expects a DOCSIS 4.0 upgrade but says fibre into homes is eventually needed.
  • Satellite: direct-to-device is complementary rather than a replacement, on latency and line of sight
    • Vodafone holds 3.8 percent of AST SpaceMobile and 50 percent of SatCo, and Ofcom approved VodafoneThree's direct-to-device service on 900MHz in April 2026.
  • Hyperscalers: they hold over 85 percent of European cloud and use Vodafone as a channel (a ten-year Microsoft partnership; Google Cloud) rather than competing.
    • The risks research rates Big Tech disintermediation low over eighteen months, and we agree
    • the live disruption is fibre.
  • This is the finding that makes the German foundation fragile and it recurs at 'Foundations' and at 'Simplicity'.

  • The UK: Virgin Media O2 cut 1.1Gbps broadband to GBP 26.99 a month
    • giffgaff cut unlimited data to GBP 14
    • Sky Mobile has a GBP 1 plan
    • MVNOs are forecast to take 30 percent of consumer mobile by 2031 from 24 percent.

  • Portugal and South Africa: Digi's entry
    • Vodafone's Portuguese top line -2.7 percent in Q1 FY27 against -3.5 percent the prior quarter
    • Vodacom's data subscribers -5.7 percent while Telkom's grew 15.5 percent.
  • Direction: a headwind in the UK, Portugal and South Africa
    • neutral to easing in German pricing
    • a net headwind.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)

  • This is a self-help case, the framework's 'Major Corporate Change', in its late stage.
  • Over three years Vodafone sold Spain and Italy for EUR 13.0bn, merged and then fully bought its UK business, took control of Safaricom, cut 11,000 roles and cut net debt from EUR 52.0bn to EUR 25.4bn.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: from a chief executive who acts, through decisions on perimeter and cost, into actions completed by 30 July 2026, into the operating metrics (organic service revenue +5.2 percent and organic adjusted EBITDAaL +6.2 percent in Q1 FY27), into the financial statements (FY26 adjusted free cash flow of EUR 2,621m, the first growth in four years) and into the sell-side's estimates (FY2028 EPS +16.0 percent in twelve months).
