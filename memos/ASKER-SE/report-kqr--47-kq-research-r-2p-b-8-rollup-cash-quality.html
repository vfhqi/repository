Section 4 – M&A Valuation Multiples and Competitive Dynamics in Medical

Section summary
  1. Multiples for small European medical supply targets remain tightly anchored in the 5x to 8x EBITDA range, despite rising competitive intensity.
  2. Disciplined consolidators like AddLife and Asker enforce strict return thresholds to protect organic cash flows and sustain multi-year EPS growth.
  3. High-growth specialities and platforms command premium multiples, whereas commoditized distribution faces stricter capital constraints.

Distribution

Historical valuation multiples and deal discipline

1.
Prevalence of historical ranges: Small-scale acquisitions of medical supplies distributors across the Nordics, the United Kingdom, and Ireland have consistently traded within historical valuation parameters. AddLife's CEO Fredrik Dalborg stated during the RedEye Serial Acquirers Conference on 18 March 2025 that the group historically paid 7 to 8 times EBITDA for small and medium-sized targets. This transaction discipline remains active as companies seek to insulate their balance sheets from multiple inflation. Consequently, preserving this entry multiple range is critical to maintaining high return on working capital and supporting double-digit EPS compounding.
2.
Acquisition criteria and hurdle rates: Serial consolidators employ highly structured financial criteria to prevent overpaying in competitive bidding environments. Uniphar's H1 2026 report on 8 September 2026 reiterated that the company enforces a strict hurdle rate of 12% to 15% Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) within three years of any acquisition. This programmatic discipline restricts the maximum multiples acquirers can justify for targets with limited synergy potential. Unless substantial cost or commercial synergies are realized, high entry multiples directly threaten the long-term compounding viability of the business. Decentralized target identification: Sourcing deals off-market allows disciplined consolidators to bypass expensive, highly competitive auction processes. Uniphar's CEO Ger Rabbette explained during the Q4 2024 earnings call on 25 February 2025 that working with target management teams before formal negotiations allows them to secure off-market deals at highly attractive valuations. This relational pipeline mitigates the pressure of rising multiples in the broader broker-led transactions market. Maintaining this private channel ensures that tuck-in transactions continue to accrue immediate earnings benefits to the parent company.

Premium pricing and platform exceptions

3.
Surpassing standard historical ranges: While standard bolt-on acquisitions remain highly disciplined, platform deals and specialized niche companies frequently command premium valuations. Asker's CEO Johan Falk noted during the Q4 2025 earnings call on 10 February 2026 that the group typically pays 6 to 8 times operating profit for normal deals but will selectively pay more for larger platforms. These larger acquisitions are designed to establish geographic footholds in new European markets. This means that while standard cash-generative distributors are bought at low multiples, platform assets dilutively pressure near-term return on capital.
4.
Specialized diagnostic and clinical premiums: Targets possessing proprietary technology, high-margin product mixes, or strong defensive barriers command valuations at the top end of the historical range. AddLife's Q1 2026 transaction updates highlighted the acquisition of CoaChrom Diagnostica in April 2026, an Austrian coagulation diagnostics niche player bought at an EV/EBITDA multiple of under 7 times. Similarly, AddLife's acquisition of Danish patient positioning manufacturer Opitek in December 2025 was completed at under 7 times EV/EBITDA. These transaction data points confirm that high-margin niche targets can still be secured within the 5 to 7 times operating profit framework.
5.
Broker observations on target discipline: Sell-side analysts actively monitor these valuation thresholds to evaluate balance sheet health and EPS accretion quality. A research report by DNB Carnegie on 10 September 2026 observed that AddLife’s current management team, which took over in 2022, has successfully pivoted its M&A strategy back to basics by focusing on small and mid-sized targets valued at 5 to 7 times EBITA. This strategic focus reduces execution and leverage risks compared to peers pursuing larger, more expensive targets. Consequently, this disciplined pricing preserves AddLife's balance sheet capacity, positioning it to compound earnings without expanding its leverage profile.
