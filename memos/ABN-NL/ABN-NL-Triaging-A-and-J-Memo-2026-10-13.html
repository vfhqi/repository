[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. ABN AMRO is a self-help case riding a contractual rates tailwind, in a mature Dutch deposit bank, bought after the re-rating. The change is compelling as a company change and not compelling as an investment at EUR 40.75, because the price already capitalises the fast part of the earnings path at a top-decile multiple and leaves the slow part, plus a 4% to 5% dividend yield, to pay us about 3% a year over eighteen months. The case clears the 20% bar only below about EUR 32, or if consensus earnings are delivered and the multiple holds at the top of its ten-year range; neither is our modal case.

  • The change is internal and external at once, and both parts are delivered rather than promised.
    • A new chief executive (appointed 23 April 2025) set a plan at the 25 November 2025 Capital Markets Day to cut EUR 900m of cost and 5,200 roles, pull EUR 10bn of risk-weighted assets out of corporate lending and return up to 100% of generated capital
    • At the same time the EUR 180bn replicating portfolio, with a three-year average duration, began rolling maturing hedges on to swap yields near 3%.
    • Cost plan: 2,340 of the 5,200 roles are gone and EUR 300m of the EUR 900m of savings is realised, 45% of the plan in five quarters
      • EUR 9bn of corporate risk-weighted assets is out since the Capital Markets Day, 63% of the EUR 10bn.
    • Hedge roll: the liability margin rose 5 basis points quarter on quarter in Q2 2026, and commercial net interest income guidance for FY2026 went to about EUR 6.8bn including NIBC from EUR 6.4bn standalone on 12 August 2026, about EUR 120m above the pre-results consensus of EUR 6.68bn.
    • Capital: CET1 is 15.9% against a 13.75% target
      • The DNB mortgage risk-weight floor expires on 30 November 2026 and releases about EUR 7bn of risk-weighted assets, about 50 basis points of CET1
      • The commitment is at least EUR 7.5bn of distributions over 2026 to 2028.
    • Acquisitions: Hauck Aufhäuser Lampe (legally merged June 2026, EUR 68m cash) and NIBC (closed 1 August 2026 at 0.85x book for EUR 960m) add scale in wealth and Dutch mortgages.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on every line the company reports.
    • Q2 2026 net profit was EUR 781m, up 29% year on year and 15% above consensus
    • Net interest income EUR 1,700m, up 11%
    • Fees EUR 617m, up 25%
    • Operating income EUR 2,424m, up 13%, against costs down 1.3%, so pre-provision profit rose 36% to EUR 1,123m
    • Cost of risk 4 basis points
    • Return on equity 12.1%, which is the 2028 target already.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's people, its plan, its actions, its liability margin and headcount, its reported profit and the sell-side's estimates: consensus EPS for FY2026 to FY2028 rose 18% to 22% in twelve months, and the shares rose 68.5% in twelve months to EUR 40.75.
    • The layer that is left is the one the pattern pays for least: the next upgrade needs a beat against estimates that already sit above management's own 2028 targets on income, efficiency and return on equity.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share of EUR 2.45 (FY2025, reported) rising to about EUR 4.25 in FY2028, about 20% a year, and then 7% to 8% a year as the hedge roll matures.
    • Our path against consensus: our FY2028 figure sits 6% below the consensus median of EUR 4.54, because we carry a cost of risk of 14 basis points against the street's about 12 and the Q2 print's 4, a cost base of EUR 5,565m (flat plus wage drift) and revenue EUR 134m below consensus for the mortgage margin and Clearing normalising.
    • The two coveted components carry it: revenue growth of 6.7% a year and a cost-to-income ratio falling from 64.4% to 52.5% contribute 17 of the 20 points a year
      • Buybacks add 2.4 points
      • A normalising provision line takes 0.6 away.
  • What it costs.
    • EUR 40.75 is 9.5x 24-month-forward consensus earnings of EUR 4.28, the 90th percentile of the stock's own five-year range (4.64x to 10.32x, median 6.81x) and the 84th percentile of the last two years
    • On the ten-year bands it sits between the 'prudent' median of 8.20x and the 'defendable' 75th percentile of 9.90x.
    • On book it is 1.36x FY2026 consensus book value per share of EUR 30.01, which is about what a 12% return on equity at a 10% cost of equity with 2% growth is worth, so the price already assumes the 2028 target is met and held.
  • The return does not clear the bar, because the re-rating leg runs the wrong way.
    • On our modal multiple of 8.7x applied to the 24-month-forward earnings at the eighteen-month horizon (EUR 4.70) the shares are worth about EUR 40.9 in April 2028, plus EUR 1.70 of dividends: EUR 42.6, 3.0% a year.
    • Over three years, 8.5x on EUR 5.23 plus EUR 5.80 of dividends is EUR 50.3, 7.2% a year.
    • Held multiple: even at today's 9.52x the eighteen-month return is 9.1% a year and the three-year return 10.9% a year, a D at best.
    • The bull branch: consensus earnings delivered and the multiple at the ten-year 'defendable' band of 9.9x gives 19.6% a year over eighteen months
      • We put that branch at about 20%, because it needs two conditions at once in a sector where two thirds of the 95 banks already sit at or above the 80th percentile of their own ten-year ranges and the Banks row lost its Overweight in the week of 2026-10-09.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', and the fit is crisp (B) but late.
    • A chief-executive change with a guidance increase and at least five other strong internal forces, outputs translating on every reported line, the change at full output along the chain.
    • The pattern's payoff, major EPS trajectory upgrades, has been paid
    • What the pattern does not say is what the upgrades cost on the multiple, and that is where this case fails.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the people to the sell-side's estimates without a weak link
    • It breaks at the price, because the market has capitalised the contractual two to three years of the roll at a multiple that assumes the growth continues after the roll is complete, and on our path it does not.

  • What the case therefore is.

    • A good company change bought late: material to the company, not material to us at this price.
    • We would pay about EUR 32, which is 21% below the 2026-10-12 close and where the modal path clears 20% a year over eighteen months.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the company ('Change Forces' B, 'Financial Outputs' B) and not material to us at this price ('Modal return' F)

    • The return binds the rating down from its parts and the forces hold it off the floor.
    • Not a B: the Building Block measures materiality including the return, and a 3%-a-year modal return is not material.
    • Not a D: EPS from EUR 2.45 to EUR 4.25 in three years with every layer of the chain moved is not a thin change.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: ABN AMRO is one of the cleanest self-help ideas in European banking, as Morgan Stanley and Jefferies make it, and the market has not finished paying for it.
      • Payout: a sector-leading total payout yield above 10%, with Jefferies counting EUR 8.6bn of distributions over 2026 to 2028 and Morgan Stanley 40% ahead of consensus on 2027 to 2028 buybacks.
      • Returns: a 14% to 14.6% return on tangible equity in 2028 on 1.35x 2027 tangible book, with the cost-to-income ratio heading to 52%.
      • Conservatism: consensus net interest income still rests on guidance that assumes zero replicating-portfolio growth and 100% pass-through on interest-bearing deposits, both of which Q2 2026 disproved.
      • Overhang to catalyst: the State's sell-down from 24.9% to 10.5% turns an overhang into consolidation optionality.
      • The multiple: a market that pays eleven to twelve times for a bank re-read as a five-year earnings grower, as the sector digest cites Morgan Stanley
        • On consensus earnings and a 9.9x multiple the return is about 20% a year over eighteen months, and at 11x it is higher still.
        • The evidence for all of this is the Q2 2026 print itself and the delivery record since 2024.
    • What we concede.
      • The earnings leg is more secure than most banks' because it is contractual for two to three years
      • Management has beaten every guide it has set since 2024
      • The cost of risk and other income are the only lines we have normalised down and we may be too prudent on costs
      • The multiple's floor is higher than the five-year history suggests, and we have named the anchor that concession moves us to (9.9x) and shown the return there (11.9% a year over eighteen months, a D).
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • The bull return needs two conditions at once: consensus earnings, which sit above management's own 2028 targets on income, efficiency and return on equity, and a multiple at or above the ten-year 75th percentile in a sector where two thirds of the 95 banks are already at the 80th percentile or higher and the Banks row lost its Overweight this week.
      • We put that branch at about 20%.
      • The asymmetry decides it: the mark-to-market downside to a fair trough multiple is 25% to 30% with earnings unchanged, the upside to the bull branch is about 30%, and the modal outcome is near zero.
      • The floor is our entry: the dividend yield of 4% to 5% and a fair value of about 1.25x book at a 12% return on equity put a floor near EUR 30 to EUR 32, which is also the Jefferies bear case at 1.0x 2028 tangible book (EUR 30)
        • That is the same arithmetic as our entry level, which is the point of waiting for it.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the internal forces are among the broadest in the sector (a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase plus cost, perimeter, acquisition and capital-return actions, all delivered rather than announced) and the dominant external force, the replicating-portfolio roll, is a strong tailwind, while the Dutch mortgage-margin squeeze and this week's European credit stress are headwinds that keep the Element off an A.
  • External against internal: the largest single force on earnings is the swap curve, which the company does not control
    • The forces the company does control are the cost base, the perimeter and the payout, and those are the ones that make the pattern a corporate-change pattern rather than a rates trade.
  • Direction: two external forces are tailwinds (rates and the swap curve; the dated capital release), two are headwinds (mortgage competition and mix; the wage round), and one is a live risk this week (European credit and sovereign stress).
  • Not an A: the mortgage-production share fell from 20% to 18% in one quarter, and the force that lifts earnings most is one the company receives rather than makes.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because two strong tailwinds (the rates and swap-curve regime feeding the replicating portfolio; a dated regulatory capital release) outweigh two live headwinds (Dutch mortgage competition and the Collective Labour Agreement wage round), and the demand picture (a housing shortage, growing deposits) is supportive rather than inflecting.
  • Hedge roll, tailwind, strong: the replicating portfolio of EUR 180bn (about 70% of client deposits) has a three-year average duration with 40% to 45% repricing within twelve months, and rolls maturing hedges into swap yields near 3%
    • The liability margin rose 5 basis points quarter on quarter in Q2 2026
    • The ECB deposit rate is 2.50% after a 25 basis point rise in September 2026, with a further rise to 2.75% expected in December 2026 by Deutsche Bank and Morgan Stanley.
  • Capital release, tailwind, dated: the DNB mortgage floor expires on 30 November 2026, releasing about EUR 7bn of risk-weighted assets, about 50 basis points of CET1
    • The Basel IV output floor is not expected to bind.
  • Mortgage margin, headwind: asset margins fell 2 basis points sequentially in Q2 2026 on competition from pension funds and insurers at 10- to 30-year maturities and a higher share of state-guaranteed NHG loans
    • Management expects the compression to persist or accelerate after the floor is removed.
  • Wage round, headwind: the Collective Labour Agreement round resumed in September 2026
    • Each 1% of wage inflation above the bank's 2% hypothesis adds up to EUR 30m a year of cost
    • Unions have asked for up to 5.5%.
  • Not an A: European credit cracked in the week of 2026-10-09 and the Banks row moved from Overweight to Neutral through the sovereign-bank loop
    • That is not this company's own research, but it is a live external force on a bank's funding and multiple.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the macro force acting on this company more than on the universe is the euro short rate, and it is acting in the company's favour: 70% of revenue is net interest income, and the rate path sits above the level at which the hedge roll would start to reverse.
  • Sensitivity: a 10 basis point savings-rate cut adds EUR 100m of net interest income and a 10 basis point rise costs EUR 135m
    • Net interest income at risk under a 200 basis point parallel fall is EUR 599m.

    • That mechanism is the same one the group research documents for other euro-area deposit banks.
  • Dutch economy: GDP rose 0.4% quarter on quarter in Q2 2026
    • Rabobank expects 1.3% growth in 2026 and 1.1% in 2027.
  • Not an A: the same sensitivity cuts the other way if the cycle turns, and 40% to 45% of the book reprices within twelve months.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • ABN AMRO is a self-help case riding a contractual rates tailwind, in a mature Dutch deposit bank, bought after the re-rating.
