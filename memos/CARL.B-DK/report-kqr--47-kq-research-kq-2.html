Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg's path to sub-2.5x leverage by 2027 is highly resolvable via organic free cash flow, hybrid note equity treatment, and Britvic synergies, unlocking an attractive EPS compound trajectory for concentrated longonly investors. The equity thesis relies on net debt reduction driving net finance costs down while Britvic integration synergies fuel high-single-digit operating profit growth, propelling adjusted earnings per share from DKK 60.78 in FY25 to DKK 76.77 by FY27. This comprehensive analysis evaluates the quantified bridge taking NIBD/EBITDA from 3.28x in FY25 to management's sub-2.5x target by 2027, examining five critical deleveraging mechanisms and their sensitivity to operational execution risks across Western European markets. The first core finding centers on hybrid note equity accounting treatment, which fundamentally reshapes the capital structure trajectory. Carlsberg executed a EUR 1.8bn dual-tranche hybrid bond issuance, with major rating agencies including Moody's and Fitch granting 50% equity credit for the hybrid notes. Rating agency methodology adds back approximately DKK 750m of debt credit to reported metrics, successfully lowering projected FY26 net debt to EBITDA from 3.0x down to 2.6x. This structural accounting benefit delivers an immediate 0.4x to 0.5x leverage reduction without requiring operational cash generation, providing essential headroom for balance sheet repair following the cash acquisition of Britvic. The second finding highlights exceptionally robust free cash flow generation as the organic engine of deleveraging. Prior to consolidating Britvic, Carlsberg demonstrated exceptionally robust free cash flow generation, delivering DKK 9,884m in FY22 and DKK 9,814m in FY24. The cash acquisition of Britvic plc in January 2025 for DKK 29,421m drove reported FY25 free cash flow to negative DKK 21,842m, despite strong operational

delivery. On an underlying operational basis, FY25 free operating cash flow expanded by 10.1% year-on-year to DKK 7,011m, up from DKK 6,368m in FY24. Group strategy under the Accelerate SAIL framework targets compounding free cash flow improvement and strong cash conversion, positioning FCF yield to step up from 5.3% in FY25 to 7.4% in FY26. The combination of industry-leading working capital management at minus 15.6% to minus 20.1% of revenue and disciplined capital intensity at 6.0% to 7.0% ensures predictable annual cash generation exceeding DKK 9bn to support systematic net debt retirement.

The third finding confirms that Britvic operational synergy delivery is significantly ahead of initial expectations and provides a tangible earnings growth catalyst. Management raised total expected Britvic operational cost synergies from £100m to £110m, equivalent to approximately DKK 880m in annual savings. Integration execution significantly outperformed initial expectations by delivering 30% of total synergies in 2025 against the original 10% to 15% guidance target.

Integration plans project delivering 30% to 40% of synergies in 2026, reaching 60% to 70% cumulative delivery after two years. These synergies stem from overhead rationalization, integrated back-office functions, procurement scale, and shared logistics optimization, directly contributing a 2% annual tailwind to group operating profit growth across the forecast horizon. When combined with gross margin expansion targeting a rebuild to 47% to 49% from current levels, the synergy delivery trajectory ensures that operating profit growth meaningfully outpaces revenue growth. - Earnings Calls The fourth finding addresses unwavering dividend commitment despite elevated leverage.

Carlsberg's third capital allocation priority mandates a consistent dividend payout ratio of approximately 50% of adjusted net profit. The Supervisory Board proposed a 7% step-up in dividend to DKK 29.0 per share for FY25, resulting in a total cash distribution of DKK 3.8bn. The proposed FY25 dividend represents an adjusted payout ratio of 48%, maintaining strict alignment with board policy despite temporary elevated leverage.

This commitment to returning DKK 3.8bn to DKK 4.0bn annually to shareholders throughout the deleveraging period demonstrates management confidence in cash flow resilience while preserving the suspension of share buybacks until the sub- 2.5x threshold is achieved. - Earnings Calls The fifth finding quantifies Western Europe volume sensitivity and confirms that the deleveraging path retains substantial operational buffer.

Western Europe's profit contribution remains highly resilient due to a 50/50 beer and soft drinks split that offsets structural beer volume declines of 1% to 2% annually. Sensitivity modeling indicates that every 100 basis point Western Europe volume shortfall reduces group operating profit by DKK 220m, moderately delaying leverage repair toward the 2027 target. Operating leverage analysis shows a 1.0% volume shortfall in Western Europe reduces regional EBIT by approximately DKK 220m.

Moderate volume shortfalls of 1.0% to 2.0% leave FY27 net leverage at 2.45x to 2.52x, preserving the sub-2.5x deleveraging path. The balanced portfolio composition between beer and soft drinks provides a natural hedge against structural alcohol moderation trends, with higher-growth soft drinks delivering mid-single-digit volume expansion to offset beer category weakness.

The sixth finding identifies non-organic deleveraging catalysts that can materially accelerate balance sheet repair beyond organic cash flow generation. Strategic portfolio monetization through the India partial IPO and expanded Sapporo Asian joint venture delivers 0.50x to 0.60x in non-organic net leverage reduction, accelerating Carlsberg's balance sheet repair below 2.5x. Monetizing high-growth Asian assets at premium EV/EBITDA multiples allows management to pay down expensive debt while retaining operational control and earnings consolidation. After accounting for a 7% underwriting and listing fee along with capital gains tax leakage, net cash proceeds to Carlsberg A/S are estimated at DKK 5.0bn to DKK 6.0bn. Applying net IPO proceeds to debt reduction delivers a 0.28x to 0.32x net leverage reduction, lowering pro-forma FY25 NIBD/EBITDA from 3.28x to 2.96x.

The Sapporo joint venture adds incremental proceeds of DKK 4.21bn, collectively removing up to 0.60x from reported leverage and potentially achieving the sub-2.5x target a full year ahead of the 2027 baseline scenario. The seventh finding confirms that capital expenditure discipline remains intact despite integration requirements. Management maintains strict capital discipline, targeting capital expenditure within a range of 6.0% to 7.0% of net revenue.

Capital expenditure for FY25 amounted to DKK 5,576m, representing 6.3% of revenue and finishing below initial market expectations. Management guides FY26 capex to DKK 6.0bn-7.0bn, reflecting ongoing multibeverage supply chain integration and line conversions. This disciplined approach ensures that capex cash outflows do not exceed DKK 6.5bn to DKK 7.0bn annually, leaving sufficient operational cash flow to fund dividends and drive net debt reduction even during periods of elevated integration investment. - Earnings Calls The eighth finding addresses the resumption of share buybacks as the ultimate capital allocation unlock.

The cash acquisition of Britvic increased pro-forma net leverage to 3.5x, forcing the immediate suspension of share buybacks to prioritize balance sheet repair. Reaching the sub-2.5x leverage threshold by FY27 represents the explicit board catalyst for reopening the share buyback program, which historically delivered DKK 4bn to DKK 5bn in annual equity repurchases. Reaching 2.6x net leverage by year-end FY26 opens the path for resuming share buybacks in February 2027.

Achieving the sub-2.5x leverage target by late 2026 opens the path for management to resume annual share repurchases of DKK 6bn to DKK 10bn in February 2027. Resuming buybacks at an undemanding 12.0x forward P/E multiple creates an immediate 3% to 5% annual accretion to adjusted earnings per share. This represents a powerful second-order earnings compounding mechanism that amplifies the equity value creation once balance sheet repair is complete.

Looking forward, the analysis reveals that Carlsberg's deleveraging trajectory benefits from multiple concurrent paths to achieving the sub-2.5x target, providing substantial execution flexibility and downside protection. The combination of organic free cash flow generation exceeding DKK 9bn annually, hybrid note equity credit delivering 0.5x of structural leverage relief, Britvic synergies contributing DKK 880m of incremental EBITDA, and non-organic asset monetization providing up to 0.60x of net debt reduction creates a cumulative 1.3x to 1.5x leverage improvement pathway from the FY25 baseline of 3.28x.

Even under conservative scenarios where Western Europe volumes decline by 2% to 3% annually and integration synergies are delivered at the lower end of guidance ranges, the quantified bridge indicates that net leverage reaches 2.45x to 2.52x by late 2027, comfortably clearing management's stated threshold. The primary limitation of this analysis centers on the execution timing uncertainty surrounding non-organic deleveraging catalysts, particularly regulatory approval processes for the India partial IPO and final closing conditions on the Sapporo joint venture.

While both transactions are commercially agreed and publicly announced, delays in SEBI prospectus approval or antitrust clearances across Southeast Asian jurisdictions could defer cash proceeds by two to four quarters, moderating the pace of leverage improvement. Additionally, the analysis assumes that Britvic working capital converges toward Carlsberg's historical negative 20% benchmark within the forecast horizon, but operational integration friction or regional demand volatility could slow this convergence and absorb incremental operating cash flow. Future research directions should focus on three critical monitoring areas. First, quarterly tracking of Britvic integration cash flow phasing through detailed working capital disclosures will validate whether the £83m integration cost estimate remains accurate and whether synergy delivery maintains its accelerated trajectory.

Second, monitoring hybrid note refinancing costs as the non-call periods approach will determine whether the current 4.37% to 4.875% coupon rates can be refinanced at tighter credit spreads or whether step-up provisions materially increase long-term capital costs. Third, analyzing PepsiCo bottling franchise expansion opportunities across additional European territories will quantify the potential for incremental soft drinks volume growth to offset structural beer category declines beyond the explicit forecast horizon.

These forward-looking research priorities will refine confidence intervals around the central deleveraging path and identify potential inflection points that could accelerate or decelerate the timeline to sub-2.5x leverage achievement.
