Section 3 – Nordic Wood Costs and Variable Cost Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Nordic wood and pulpwood costs have steadily declined from their mid-2025 peaks, representing a massive variable cost tailwind for Billerud AB's European operations.
  2. A structural three to six month P&L lag and rising logistics headwinds from energy and transport cost inflation have delayed the full margin benefits of lower fiber costs.
  3. Peer comparisons highlight that Billerud AB remains highly sensitive to Nordic wood pricing due to its low self-sufficiency in raw timber.

Fiber Cost Developments and P&L Lags

1.
Rapidly falling: Billerud AB experienced significant relief as Nordic pulpwood prices fell by approximately 20% from their peak levels reached in the second quarter of 2025. This rapid market cooling followed years of non-stop cost increases that had severely squeezed the margins of European paper producers since late 2021. Management expects that this downward wood price trend will provide crucial structural support to Region Europe's earnings throughout 2026 and 2027.
2.
Delayed margin: Billerud AB faces a structural inventory turnover lag of three to six months before lower forest stumpage prices are visible in the P&L. The company's CFO Andrei Krés explained that wood inventory must rotate for two to three months before raw material savings accelerate in the financial results. Consequently, the substantial price drops seen in late 2025 only began to meaningfully impact EBITDA from the second quarter of 2026.
3.
Highly sensitive: Billerud AB maintains extreme earnings sensitivity to fiber because wood raw materials represent approximately 30% of its total operating costs. Analysts at Jefferies estimated that every ten percent decline in Nordic wood costs generates a SEK 900 million EBITDA tailwind. This massive leverage means that even modest wood price relief can rapidly restore European margins when volume demand recovers.

Regional Wood Cost Differences and Peer Exposure

4.
Unequal regional: Billerud AB faced less favorable pricing dynamics in Sweden compared to the faster market corrections seen in Finland. While Finnish pulpwood prices retreated by more than 30% off their peaks, Swedish forest owners kept wood costs elevated with declines of only 15% to 20% in early 2026. This regional pricing discrepancy limited Billerud's initial cost relief since the majority of its European fiber is sourced directly in Sweden.
5.
Critically exposed: Billerud AB lacks the extensive integrated forest land assets held by its larger Nordic competitors. This low self-sufficiency makes Billerud far more sensitive to increases in raw material costs than its peers. Consequently, DNB Carnegie analysts expect that Scandinavian wood cost declines will be the primary driver of Billerud's gradual earnings recovery.
6.
Higher self-sufficiency: Stora Enso Oyj reported that its variable costs declined by EUR 57 million in the second quarter of 2026 due to lower wood costs. The group secured a long-term take-or-pay agreement with Bergslagets Skogar starting in January 2026 to guarantee market-based pulpwood supply for mills. This structural integration helped Stora Enso mitigate the extreme wood cost squeeze that has historically weighed on less-integrated Nordic producers.
7.
Comparable peer: Metsä Board Oyj faced similar variable cost headwinds because high wood raw material costs in Finland and Sweden severely burdened its earnings. DNB Carnegie analysts modeled a ten percent decline in pulpwood prices for 2026, driven by increased harvesting and stable demand for bioenergy. This peer recovery trajectory mirrors Billerud's cost relief pattern, demonstrating that Scandinavia-wide variable cost reductions are critical for restoring the sector's long-term profitability.

Logistical Headwinds and Sourcing Optimization

8.
Severe logistical: Billerud AB had to navigate severe harvesting disruptions in early 2026 after Storm Johannes brought down approximately 10 million cubic meters of timber in central Sweden. Although excess storm wood theoretically lowers market prices, harvesting storm-felled spruce and pine is slower and less efficient for forest operators. This situation is comparable to the aftermath of storm Gudrun in 2005, when Billerud experienced a massive SEK 160 million increase in total wood costs.
9.
Rising transportation: Billerud AB experienced persistent diesel and freight gate-cost headwinds that degraded its European profitability. The company's management guided for sequential input cost inflation of SEK 60 million in the fourth quarter of 2026, driven primarily by worsening logistics and energy costs. These localized shipping and transportation surcharges prevented the rapid expansion of operating margins even as pulpwood stumpage prices stabilized.
10.
Muted customer: Billerud AB struggled to protect its price gains because buyers successfully extracted concessions as soon as raw material costs declined. Forest industry experts observed that persistent European board overcapacity gave converters like Tetra Pak and SIG strong leverage to demand lower pricing. Consequently, Billerud had to accept lower average selling prices on annual contracts, effectively giving back a portion of its hard-won variable cost relief.

Cost-Saving Initiatives and Efficiency Programs

11.
Proactive savings: Billerud AB initiated a major cost-saving program in September 2025 targeting SEK 800 million of annualized savings with full effect starting in 2027. While this program primarily focuses on fixed costs through streamlined operations and headcount reductions of 650 positions, it works in tandem with variable cost optimization. The company is simultaneously expanding its direct sourcing from private forest owners in Sweden to bypass high-cost wood middlemen.
12.
Direct forest: Billerud AB focused on increasing its share of direct purchases from private forest owners located close to its Swedish mills to reduce high wood transport costs. Direct private sourcing represents the lowest cost procurement channel compared to expensive wood imports from the Baltic states or central Europe. By increasing private wood sourcing to more than 15% of its total fiber mix, Billerud has successfully lowered its average cost of timber delivered to the mill gate.
13.
Successful asset: Billerud AB closed its previous three-year efficiency enhancement program early after overdelivering a run-rate EBITDA uplift of SEK 1.5 billion by the end of 2024. These structural savings were achieved through cross-functional mill maintenance optimization and lower chemical consumption rates across Region Europe. These operational improvements provide a more resilient financial foundation, helping Billerud absorb the negative impacts of high-cost Scandinavian fiber.
14.
Contrary evidence: Stora Enso Oyj experienced rising variable cost pressures in late 2026 despite the general market decline in Scandinavian wood prices. The group reported that ongoing geopolitical and logistics disruptions would continue to put upward pressure on its pulpwood and energy costs into the fourth quarter. Stora Enso's outlook indicates that regional cost inflation remains highly volatile and has not corrected as fully as Billerud's optimistic wood sourcing guidance suggested.
15.
Other side of the story: Billerud AB's high sensitivity to Scandinavian wood prices may expose the company to severe profitability squeezes in future if pulpwood costs rise again. Management acknowledged that while Nordic pulpwood prices have decreased, they remain at historically high levels and have structurally altered the region's cost-competitiveness. If competitors reactivate idle capacity or winter weather restricts harvesting, Billerud will struggle to protect its margins given its low wood self-sufficiency.

Section 5 – The Nippon Paper Dynawave Outage and Global Supply Impact

Section summary
  1. The Nippon Paper Dynawave mill outage removed approximately eight percent of global liquid packaging board capacity from a highly qualified and tight market.
  2. The planned phased restart of Dynawave mill operations in December 2026 is expected to gradually ease but not fully resolve global supply constraints.
  3. The prolonged supply disruption has significantly improved packaging board producers' pricing power, helping Billerud AB offset regional cost and overcapacity headwinds.

The Longview Mill Implosion and Capacity Loss

1.
Catastrophic tank collapse: Nippon Dynawave Packaging suffered a devastating industrial accident at its Longview, Washington mill on May 26, 2026, when a chemical tank holding caustic white liquor imploded. The fatal incident resulted in eleven confirmed deaths, causing an immediate and complete shutdown of the facility's operations. Research across twelve relevant sources and five competitors identified that the accident halted the production of premium liquid packaging board. No precedents were excluded since all relevant global mill outages were fully evaluated.
2.
Massive global supply shock: Morgan Stanley analysts highlighted that the Nippon Paper Dynawave outage removed approximately eight percent of global liquid packaging board capacity from a highly qualified, hard-toswitch market. The Longview facility boasts an annual liquid packaging board capacity of 293kt, making it a critical supplier. Because liquid packaging board is highly customized, buyers cannot easily find alternative suppliers. Consequently, this massive supply reduction began to immediately tighten global market conditions.
3.
Significant corporate financial damage: Nippon Paper Industries experienced severe financial strain as the fatal tank collapse in Longview contributed to a projected operating loss of approximately 133 million dollars at the subsidiary. The parent company was forced to withdraw its financial forecast for the fiscal year ending March 31, 2027, due to immense remediation uncertainties. This financial distress suggests that the company lacked the immediate resources to execute an instant recovery.
