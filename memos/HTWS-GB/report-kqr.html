3.
Expanding infrastructure partnerships: Airtel Africa is actively pursuing multiple sharing arrangements to expand its physical footprint across emerging African markets. Operational and Environmental Impacts

6.
Driving environmental sustainability: The World Bank reported in December 2024 that active infrastructure sharing significantly mitigates the carbon footprint of emerging mobile networks. Implementing active sharing can reduce 4G wireless backhaul baseline emissions by thirty-seven percent. These shared efficiencies help regional operators promote highly sustainable network growth pathways across Sub-Saharan Africa.
7.
Optimizing asset utilization: Telecommunications operators are increasingly utilizing active network sharing to limit duplicate physical site construction. This practice allows multiple carriers to consolidate active electronics on a single macro tower, bypassing the need for redundant passive builds. Consequently, independent tower companies must adapt to a landscape where co-locations drive the majority of growth rather than greenfield construction.
8.
Contrary evidence: Network sharing agreements can significantly cannibalize passive infrastructure demand when operators consolidate duplicate locations and trigger lease churn. Morgan Stanley warned that network sharing and carrier consolidation historically represent material risks that increase the potential for lease cancellations. This consolidation pressure could depress long-term organic tenancy growth if regional customers share physical electronics.
9.
Quantifying sharing efficiencies: The World Bank highlighted that multi-tenant tower models significantly enhance passive infrastructure efficiency and reduce operational emissions. Helios Towers estimates that adding a second tenant on a tower reduces average emissions per operator by thirty-seven percent. This direct correlation incentivizes both operators and regulators to favor high-density shared tower platforms over single-tenant sites.
10.
Growing active network modifications: Mobile network operators are prioritizing tower amendments and active equipment upgrades over building completely new physical sites. Commercial managers expect steady activity in equipment updates to accommodate 5G densification and regional spectrum refarming. These active modifications allow carriers to maximize existing infrastructure capacity efficiently without incurring new lease expenses.
