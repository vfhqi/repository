[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Technoprobe is a 'Demand Inflection' that has already happened and has already been paid for. The physics of testing AI accelerators has taken revenue from EUR 628m in FY2025 to a guided EUR 1,050m to 1,100m in FY2026 and the EBITDA margin from 32.1 percent to a guided 46 to 48 percent, and the change has travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain into the sell-side's estimates and into a share price up 200 percent in twelve months. At EUR 33.08, 37 times our 24-month-forward earnings, the shares return about -11 percent a year over eighteen months on our modal path, so the business is compelling and the investment at this price is not.

  • The change is external, sector-level and of the first rank, and it is a tailwind.
    • Test content per AI accelerator rises with thermal design power, pad pitch and chiplet count, so probe card consumption grows at a multiple of chip units
    • Deutsche Bank models Technoprobe's AI GPU probe card sales at a 130 percent compound annual growth rate over 2023 to 2026E against Nvidia GPU unit growth of about 50 percent.
    • AI data centre applications exceeded 50 percent of group revenue in H1 2026
      • Q2 2026 revenue was EUR 277.1m, +64.3 percent year on year.
    • The force is confirmed outside the company: Advantest raised its CY2026 SoC tester market forecast by 19 percent to USD 10.5bn to 11.5bn in July 2026 with 18 months' visibility, and Teradyne raised its FY2026 outlook to USD 5.02bn to 5.22bn.
    • The company recorded an order backlog for the first time in its history, with commitments extending well into 2027.
  • The internal change is real but follows the demand rather than leads it.
    • Capital expenditure has been raised to EUR 350m through Q1 2027, targeting a 140 percent capacity increase over end-2025 and a EUR 1.6bn annualised run-rate, 100 percent self-funded
    • guidance was raised twice in three months
    • four new product lines are resourced and dated.
    • None of this would have happened without the external force, which is why 'Change Forces' is A on the strength of the external force and the internal forces are B.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have improved further and faster than any in the comparison set, at every layer of the chain.
    • H1 2026 revenue +42.4 percent, EBITDA margin 44.4 percent from 32.6 percent, net profit +315 percent
    • FY2026 consensus earnings per share +140.5 percent over twelve months
    • seven of seven brokers at Buy.
    • The one caution is the framework's own: growth above 40 percent is very likely to return to base rates, and this company's FY2023 showed the symmetry (revenue -25.4 percent, EBITDA -50 percent).
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share of about EUR 0.53 in FY2026 rising to EUR 0.78 in FY2027, EUR 0.91 in FY2028 and EUR 1.00 in FY2029, about 23.6 percent a year over three years, almost all of it revenue.
    • We sit at the consensus mean for FY2027 (EUR 0.786) and in the bottom quarter of the range for FY2028 (consensus EUR 1.06 median), because the capacity beyond EUR 1.6bn is unannounced, Nvidia dual-sourcing is on schedule for H1 2027, and the margin mix turns toward lower-margin lines.
  • What it costs.
    • EUR 33.08 is 37.2 times our 24-month-forward earnings of EUR 0.89, at the stock's ten-year 'mania' band of 38.50x
    • 31.84 times the consensus figure of about EUR 1.04, the 74th percentile of five years
    • and a current P/E of 35.3x, the highest in the 19-stock comparison set, whose median is 24.7x.
    • The price has risen 200 percent in twelve months and 119 percent in six
      • the contracted catalysts are in it.
  • The return does not clear the bar on any multiple in the stock's own history.
    • On our path at 27x, the ten-year 'prudent' median, the shares are worth EUR 27.81 in April 2028 and EUR 28.25 in October 2029: about -10.9 percent and -5.1 percent a year, with no cash return.
    • At the 'defendable' 32.3x the eighteen-month return is about +0.4 percent a year
      • at the 'mania' 38.5x about +12.9 percent a year, still a D.
    • Being right with the sell-side (FY2029 earnings per share EUR 1.48 on one broker) at the stock's median multiple earns about 13.4 percent a year, a C
      • being wrong with it, on the risks research's arithmetic, costs more than half the price.
  • The chain holds from the force to the estimates and breaks at the price.
    • The price below which the case clears the bar on our modal path is about EUR 21 over eighteen months and about EUR 16 over three years
    • below about EUR 23 the eighteen-month return is a C.
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', fitted crisply.
    • The mandatory demand explosion is sector-level and evidenced by three equipment makers' guidance and a first-ever backlog
    • the dominant position captures it disproportionately
    • fixed-cost leverage turns it into a 49 percent EBITDA margin.

    • it is a case to park and to re-open at about EUR 21 to 23, or on a disappointment that resets the multiple.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand for advanced probe cards is changing structurally: test intensity per device rises with thermal design power, pad pitch and chiplet count, so probe card consumption grows at a multiple of chip units.
  • Content growth: Deutsche Bank models Technoprobe's AI GPU probe card sales at a 130 percent compound annual growth rate over 2023 to 2026E against Nvidia GPU unit growth of about 50 percent.
  • Device physics: chiplet disaggregation steps probe card demand up 1.5x to 2x per device
    • Rubin runs at 1,400W against Hopper's 700W
    • probe counts are above 20,000 against 3,000 to 4,000 on older architectures.
  • Visibility: an order backlog recorded for the first time in the company's history, with commitments extending well into 2027
    • TSMC's advanced nodes at near 100 percent utilisation through 2027.
  • Cyclical caution: management disclosed an expected Q4 2026 pause in major AI GPU mass production ahead of 2027 architecture transitions
    • consumer mobile and PC, 30 to 40 percent of revenue, show only slight growth.
    • We treat the swing as cyclical until proven structural, as the framework asks
    • two years' visibility is met.
  • Not B, because the swing has two-plus years of visibility and is confirmed by three independent equipment makers' guidance.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening from 2027 by customer policy rather than by competitor merit, while through H1 2026 Technoprobe lost no material share.
  • Share held: Technoprobe holds over 60 percent of non-memory vertical MEMS and 34 percent of total logic probe cards, with zero material share loss at Nvidia through H1 2026.
  • FormFactor: it is running pilot qualifications of its Apollo MEMS card at Nvidia and is capacity-constrained until Farmers Branch, Texas ramps in H1 2027
    • Deutsche Bank's model carries a 20 percent share loss at Nvidia by 2028
    • FormFactor itself expects a 70:30 split in its favour once qualified.
  • MPI: it targets 3.5 million MEMS pins a month by late 2026 and holds over 80 percent of custom ASIC probing with forming-wire technology
    • patent litigation runs in Taiwan (Technoprobe's injunction against MW90, MW120 and MW130) and in East Texas (MPI's countersuit of 2026-04-17).
  • TSMC share: already compressed to 60 percent from 90 percent in FY2017.
  • Not D, because the market is growing faster than any share loss modelled, and the WinWay alliance (a minimum USD 60m over five years, about EUR 60m of 2026 sales) blocks the lower tier.
  • The customer's blessing on the second source is what makes this a headwind rather than a nuisance
    • it is the one external force the case cannot argue away.

Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because one macro force acts on this company far more than on the universe, and against it: about 90 percent of revenue is invoiced in US dollars against over 55 percent of costs in euros, with no contractual pass-through and no hedge accounting, so dollar weakness took EUR 30m from H1 2026 revenue (about 9 points of growth) and EUR 23m from EBITDA.
  • Macro tailwind: hyperscaler capital spending heading toward USD 1 trillion by 2028, decoupled from GDP.
  • Sensitivity: a 5 percent euro move costs about EUR 4m of profit on the company's own sensitivity, small against about EUR 500m of EBITDA.
  • Not D, because the headwind is a translation of a dollar business, not a loss of demand.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because every disruption force in the value chain (chiplets, 3D hybrid bonding, co-packaged optics, 16-high HBM stacks) increases wafer-level test complexity in Technoprobe's favour, and the two substitute technologies have been shown not to substitute.
  • Substitutes tested: non-contact e-beam inspection cannot perform functional AC/DC parametric testing or deliver high current under thermal stress
    • built-in self-test carries a die-area penalty and stays in trailing-edge logic.
  • Disruption against: cantilever cards are being prolonged at HBM through sacrificial pads, to avoid the 6x cost premium of vertical MEMS.
  • Not A, because the memory transition is the one place where the incumbents' technology could be stretched rather than replaced.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because vertical integration has turned the supply side from a constraint into an advantage: in-house MLO and MLC substrates (over EUR 150m invested since 2021) deliver 100,000-plus-pin cards in 8 to 12 weeks against competitors' 20-plus-week external substrate lead times, and raw materials fell from 24.1 percent of sales in H1 2025 to 20.6 percent in H1 2026.
  • Concentrations: palladium, silicon nitride plates and Taiwanese substrate processing.
  • Not A, because the Taiwan concentration is a single point of physical failure.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
