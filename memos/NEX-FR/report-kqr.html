Section summary

  2. Geopolitical delays at the Great Sea Interconnector have been successfully mitigated by securing replacement high-voltage orders for the Halden plant.

Halden and Subsea Utilization

1.
Encouraging progress: Nexans is close to finalizing a replacement mass-impregnated subsea cable order for the Halden plant to offset the Great Sea Interconnector postponement. Chief Executive Officer Julien Hueber expressed strong confidence in securing the final award before year-end 2026. Investors closely monitor this development because it will fully load the mass-impregnated line through 2027 and remove major capacity utilization uncertainties.
2.
Rapid mobilization: Nexans has already initiated engineering preparation works for the replacement Mediterranean mass-impregnated project ahead of the official contract signing. This proactive phase is designed to allow immediate manufacturing startup as soon as the final award is announced. The swift turnaround is crucial because securing this contract will protect the high-voltage segment's mid-term earnings from unabsorbed fixed cost pressures.
3.
Competitive capacity: Nexans faces pressure to load its subsea capacity because peers like NKT maintain massive high-voltage backlogs reaching €13.0bn at the end of Q2 2026. While NKT leverages its long-term backlog to drive faster earnings expansion, Nexans relies on short-term replacement tenders to avoid Halden under-utilization. This backlog disparity suggests that Nexans must win immediate contracts to prevent margin dilution relative to peers.

Transmission Margin Trajectory

4.

The positive surprise was driven by premium backlog execution and underlying improvements in project mix.

Reaching this target is essential for the group to narrow its valuation discount.

Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research reported on 29 July 2026 that prolonged Halden under-utilization and weaker customer bargaining power could severely pressure margins. US Footprint and Republic Wire Integration

Strategic acquisition: Nexans completed the acquisition of US-based low-voltage cable manufacturer Republic Wire in June 2026 for an enterprise value of €680 million. This deal establishes a critical platform for Nexans to target highly profitable US grid and data center verticals. This capacity ramp-up will allow the group to capture rapid US data center demand.

Until low-voltage construction inflects, Nexans' financial profile will likely remain inferior to premium peers.
