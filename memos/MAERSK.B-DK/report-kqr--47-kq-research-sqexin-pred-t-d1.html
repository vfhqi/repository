Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Assessing Maersk's operational plausibility through upcoming cyclical pressures is crucial for determining its mid-term earnings trajectory.

Defining the core investment debate

1.
Crucial investigation: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Severe pressure: Anticipated cyclical overcapacity in the global container shipping market makes Maersk's mid- term earnings outlook highly uncertain. Analysts warn that the global cellular container ship orderbook has risen to a record 44% of the active fleet, which will drag down freight rates as vessels are delivered through 2028. Determining whether the company's cost-out capabilities can protect profits remains the most critical factor for predicting earnings per share development over the next three years.
3.
Significant downside: Normalisation of geopolitical disruptions could expose underlying industry overcapacity and trigger severe valuation de-ratings for Maersk. While near-term rates remain buoyed by port congestion, any resolution of these bottlenecks is expected to drive negative free cash flow starting in 2027 as networks normalise. If these temporary disruption-led earnings fade, the stock faces substantial downside risks as freight rates head downward into the upcoming supply wave.

Structural risks and alliance changes

4.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that persistent terminal congestion and resilient demand could continue to support elevated freight rates and delay the normalisation of the container market. Indeed, port bottlenecks remain highly elevated globally, which could continue to constrain effective vessel supply even as the massive newbuild orderbook is delivered. This alternative view suggests that congestion-led earnings could persist longer than bearish consensus models currently project.

25 Threatening supply: The acceleration of global fleet capacity growth represents a major structural hurdle that Maersk's cost-out measures must overcome. Global container fleet capacity is projected to expand by 7.4% in 2027 and 13.2% in 2028, outstripping structural demand growth of around 4% in each of those years. This

5.

6.
Structural realignment: The transition to the Gemini Cooperation with Hapag-Lloyd in 2025 introduces a completely restructured hub-and-spoke operational model. This cooperation covers approximately 22% of global container capacity, routing deep-sea loops through specific equity-affiliated transshipment terminals. Investors must assess whether this hub-reliant network can deliver structural cost benefits or if it introduces high operational risks as the broader shipping market faces severe overcapacity.

Uncertain transformation: Maersk's strategic pivot toward becoming an integrated container logistics provider has faced skepticism regarding its ultimate profitability. Since 2019, the group has spent over $8 bn on acquisitions to build its Logistics & Services division, yet margins remain below targets. Section 2 – Ocean Unit Cost Flexibility and Fleet Charter Constraints

Section summary
  1. Historical Ocean unit cost reductions have been driven heavily by temporary factors like bunker prices and congestion relief. Rising charter constraints and massive committed lease liabilities threaten to limit Maersk's cost flexibility during the 2027-2028 supply wave.

Historical cost performance and savings attribution

1.
Decreasing unit costs during the 2023 downturn showcased Maersk's focus on operational discipline. The company reported that its Ocean unit cost at fixed bunker decreased by 14% to $2,280 per FFE in Q4 2023. However, much of this performance was driven by a lower time charter equivalent, terminal costs, and higher volumes rather than permanent structural changes. This demonstrates that past operational progress depended heavily on volume scaling rather than standalone cost reductions.
2.
Attributing savings in past cycles shows that fuel prices often played a dominant role. During the 2009 global financial crisis, Maersk achieved a 15% reduction in total unit costs per FFE, but unit costs excluding fuel fell by only 5%. This historical precedent indicates that bunker efficiency and fuel price drops represent the largest components of Maersk's unit cost flexibility.
3.
Evaluating the 2023 cost performance reveals that actual fixed opex fell short of initial models. Analyst reports from Jefferies in February 2024 noted that Maersk's full-year average operating costs at fixed bunker reached $2,371 per FFE, which sat significantly above their modeled estimate of $2,100 per FFE. This discrepancy suggests that fixed network costs are stickier than analysts expect, even when headcount reductions and vessel idling are aggressively pursued.

Fleet charter constraints and lease commitments

4.
Lengthening charter durations are set to lock in high operating expenses for Maersk over the medium term. The company is committed to long-term vessel leases, with its charter of the Maersk Puelo from Costamare running until at least January 2027 with options extending to September 2031. These rigid time-charter terms mean Maersk cannot rapidly shed expensive capacity as freight rates converge toward historic cyclical lows.
5.
Expanding lease liabilities are mounting just as the industry enters a projected oversupply phase. Maersk's total commitments across segments, which stood at $13.4 bn at the end of 2025, surged to $19.5 bn by the third quarter of 2026 due to investments in dual-fuel vessels and concession contracts. This huge capital overhang represents a significant barrier to cost flexibility during the expected supply wave.
6.
Analyzing fleet growth plans shows that Maersk's operational constraints limit its near-term flexibility. According to Morgan Stanley in November 2026, Maersk's high utilization rate of 96% reflects a capped fleet that lacks the flexibility to navigate port disruptions without ordering more vessels. This suggests that Maersk's pivot back to fleet growth will prolong negative free cash flows through 2027 as new capacity is delivered.

Comparative fleet flexibility and peer structures

7.
Contrasting fleet structures reveals that ZIM maintains a far higher exposure to spot charter volatility. As of December 31, 2025, ZIM operated 128 vessels, of which 99 were chartered from third parties, representing an extremely high charter-in ratio. This means ZIM enjoys greater cost flexibility in downturns as leases expire, but suffers severely when charter rates spike.
8.
Comparing peer capacities highlights Hapag-Lloyd's structurally superior position regarding asset ownership. While Maersk operates a mixed fleet with substantial charter commitments, Hapag-Lloyd operates a younger fleet of modern large vessels that are mostly owned, reducing its long-term charter opex risk. This ownership structure gives Hapag-Lloyd more stable long-term unit costs compared to charter-heavy carriers.
9.
63 64 Evaluating competitor strategies highlights MSC's aggressive volume-first approach. Reports from Bernstein in November 2026 indicate that MSC holds the largest active fleet of 7.3 mm TEUs and the largest orderbook, driving industry-wide price pressure. This competitive threat makes it difficult for Maersk to maintain its target 14% market share without sacrificing pricing power in Ocean trades.

Cost-out programs and target delivery track record

10.
Reviewing historic cost programmes demonstrates that Maersk can deliver on overhead reduction targets. In the 2016 downturn, Maersk Oil reduced its opex by 19% in the fourth quarter, achieving a total 36% cost reduction compared to its 2014 baseline, which exceeded management's initial target. This proves the group's capacity to execute aggressive structural cost-out measures.
11.
69 70 - Earnings Calls Contrasting recent restructurings reveals a mixed record of corporate overhead delivery. The February 2026 program promised annualized savings of $180 mm through a 15% reduction in corporate overheads and the closure of 1,000 corporate positions. However, these savings are frequently offset by restructuring charges, such as the $350 mm incurred during the 2023 workforce downsizing. This indicates that corporate cost-outs do not immediately translate into improved net margins.
12.
Contrary evidence: Operational efficiencies from Maersk's new network structures have delivered significant immediate cost savings. Broker reports in August 2026 showed that Gemini cost benefits were realized just above the upper end of their prior expectations, helping to offset broader inflationary headwinds. This demonstrates that network synergies can cushion unit cost inflation even during periods of severe port congestion.
13.
76 77 Predicting future margin delivery remains highly challenging given Maersk's rigid cost structures. While cost programmes help lower corporate overhead, Ocean opex is dominated by vessel operations, port handling, and network fuel costs that cannot be easily downsized. Consequently, Maersk's ability to hold Ocean EBITDA positive through the upcoming supply wave will depend entirely on rate normalisation rather than corporate simplification.
14.
Other side of the story: Maersk's aggressive investments in green fuel technology and vessel retrofits could structurally lower slot costs and insulate the company from rising carbon fees. The company has executed more than 1,500 retrofit projects across 200 chartered vessels to improve fuel efficiency and expand cargo capacity. These proactive environmental measures could allow Maersk to outcompete less-efficient peers as EU ETS and FuelEU Maritime regulations phase in.

Section 3 – Routing Normalisation Dynamics and Gemini Cooperation Structural

Section summary
  1. Suez Canal routing normalization is accelerating and will release substantial vessel capacity back into a structurally oversupplied market. The Gemini Cooperation's hub-and-spoke model aims for superior reliability but faces execution risks at key transshipment terminals.

Benefits

Congestion and routing cost dynamics

1.
Rapidly rising unit costs in Q3 2026 showed the financial impact of the Cape of Good Hope rerouting. According to Bernstein Research on November 9, 2026, Maersk's Ocean unit costs at constant energy prices increased by 5% year on year due to terminal congestion and lower volumes. This cost inflation was driven by higher container handling fees and network inefficiencies. Consequently, these rising operational expenses are likely to depress Maersk's near-term profitability before routing normalises.
2.
86 Easing port congestion represents a critical driver for near-term network cost relief. Maersk management noted during the Q3 2026 earnings call on November 5, 2026, that shorter transit distances through Suez will mitigate terminal bottlenecks by allowing more effective fleet redeployment. This operational shift is expected to reduce container handling delays and lower bunker consumption. Ultimately, these efficiency gains should help rebuild operating margins in the Ocean segment.
3.
88 - Earnings Calls Accelerating Suez transits are quickly releasing effective vessel capacity back into the global market. Morgan Stanley reported on October 5, 2026, that weekly Suez containership transits surged 62% week on week to 752k TEU, representing about 54% of pre-disruption levels. This rapid capacity return on Asia-Europe routes is already starting to roll over spot freight rates. This capacity influx will likely accelerate rate normalisation into 2027 as the supply wave intensifies.
4.
Rising network costs are closely tied to geopolitical rerouting and temporary surcharges. Jefferies Research highlighted on October 20, 2026, that diverting voyages around Africa adds up to two weeks per voyage and significantly increases total fuel consumption. While carriers have recovered these expenses through temporary surcharges, these mechanisms will disappear as routes normalise. This normalisation will remove key rate supports and expose the underlying vessel oversupply.

Gemini network design and structural benefits

5.
Projected operational savings from the Gemini Cooperation remain highly dependent on terminal execution. Bernstein Research on August 14, 2026, estimated that the partnership with Hapag-Lloyd could deliver $820 mm to $1.10 bn in annualized EBIT benefits through improved asset productivity. However, this hub-and-spoke model concentrates vessel calls at specific transshipment hubs like Tanjung Pelepas. Any operational failure at these critical hubs could trigger network-wide delays and increase costs.
6.
95 High reliability targets represent the primary commercial hook for the Gemini network. Jefferies Research reported on October 27, 2025, that Gemini aims to achieve 90% schedule reliability by utilizing nine controlled terminals on Asia-Europe routes. This target sits well above historical industry averages of around 50% that have frustrated shippers. Delivering on this promise will be essential for maintaining premium freight rates in a loose market.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some channel checks indicate that the Gemini Cooperation is failing to deliver its expected operational synergies. An ecommerce industry expert noted in an interview on July 28, 2026, that Gemini is not creating the anticipated synergies and has caused Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd to lose market share to competitors. This finding suggests that Gemini's hub-and-spoke model might actually alienate shippers who prefer direct-call networks.
8.
100 inventory predictability is a key benefit highlighted by major retail customers. Dollar General management stated at the TPM26 conference in March 2026 that Gemini's reliable 90% schedule transit performance has helped reduce pipeline inventory variability and associated safety stock costs, according to a news report on March 17, 2026. This customer feedback confirms that operational reliability can drive container volume stickiness even during broader market downturns.

Comparative alliance networks and competitive strategies

9.
Contrasting operating models show that MSC is pursuing a different competitive strategy. Flexport reported in an investor publication on November 4, 2024, that MSC has chosen to focus on direct port-to-port connections instead of intermediate transshipment hubs. This independent approach avoids the transshipment fees and congestion risks associated with Gemini's hub-and-spoke network. Consequently, MSC can leverage its massive scale to offer direct and highly flexible port pairs.
10.
Massive direct-service coverage gives the Ocean Alliance a dominant scale advantage. COSCO Shipping Holdings reported in its 2025 annual report on June 26, 2026, that its Ocean Alliance DAY10 product operates over 500 direct port-to-port services with a total capacity exceeding 5.30 mm TEUs. This extensive network maintains a leading position in both scale and service frequency. This direct-call model will continue to challenge Gemini's market share on East-West trade lanes.
11.
Collaborative slot exchanges help Premier Alliance partners optimize their asset utilization. Rebranded in February 2025, the Premier Alliance has partnered with MSC for slot exchanges on Asia-Europe trade lanes to plug capacity gaps, according to a report by Laufer Group on January 16, 2025. This flexible cooperative structure allows ONE, HMM, and Yang Ming to reduce operating costs. These partnerships will help Premier Alliance defend margins during the 2027 capacity wave.
12.
Resilient contract structures are helping Hapag-Lloyd protect its near-term Ocean revenues. Deutsche Bank Research noted on November 3, 2026, that Hapag-Lloyd has upgraded its 2026 guidance due to strong contract rates and resilient demand. While spot rates are rolling over, these long-term contract rates provide a temporary buffer. However, this protection will fade as upcoming contract renewals reflect lower spot levels in 2027.
13.
Other side of the story: Gemini's network configuration could prove highly resilient and structurally cheaper than peer alliances during a severe downturn. Hapag-Lloyd CEO Rolf Habben Jansen stated in a Laufer Group presentation on January 31, 2025, that Gemini's hub-and-spoke model requires fewer port calls, which improves asset utilization and allows more cargo to be transported per ship. If this operational efficiency is fully realized, Maersk could structuraly lower its slot costs and maintain positive Ocean EBITDA through 2028.

Section 4 – APM Terminals Pricing Power and Revenue Resilience in Overcapacity

Section summary
  1. Global terminal operators face significant margin compression when localized port capacity expansions outpace medium-term demand.
  2. Geographically diversified gateway portfolios show greater pricing resilience than transshipment-heavy networks during shipping downturns.

Historical precedents of terminal downturns

1.
Severe margin compression during overcapacity periods has historically devastated terminal returns: Research across 18 relevant sources, 4 primary peer terminal companies, and 2 comparable past cases reveals that APM Terminals suffered a massive 33% decline in EBIT per TEU during the 2016 downturn. This severe pricing pressure contributed to a near-halving of the segment's return on invested capital down to 6.3% during that period. Consequently, these historical results show that overcapacity can quickly erode terminal pricing power and profitability before cost controls take effect.

Strategic shifts toward operational collaboration represent a defensive response to overcapacity: To counter severe overcapacity, APM Terminals pivoted away from capital-intensive expansion toward cost leadership and maximizing utilization. Jefferies

2.
reported on January 9, 2017, that this strategy involved redirecting Maersk Line volumes to equity terminals to optimize asset utilization. This corporate collaboration aims to protect the terminal business model during prolonged cyclical downturns.
3.
Resilient revenue expansion has recently characterized APM Terminals' organic operational performance: APM Terminals posted record quarterly revenues in the third quarter of 2026, supported by a 5% top-line increase. This growth occurred despite lower volumes in Bahrain because it was driven by higher revenue per container move and the ramp-up of a new terminal in Rijeka. This recent achievement indicates that selective tariff increases and expansions can sustain top-line results.

Peer comparisons and operational dynamics

4.
Contrary evidence: Terminal operators frequently lack the structural pricing power required to fully offset severe industry overcapacity. DP World explained in its 2024 annual report that when port authorities simultaneously tender multiple expansion projects, local capacity easily exceeds medium-term demand. This localized oversupply directly intensifies operator competition, resulting in weak pricing power, revenue losses, and depressed returns on investment. Thus, terminal tariffs remain highly vulnerable to competitive price discounting during downturns despite attempts to maintain yield discipline.

Softening EBITDA margins highlight the pressure that local volume contractions exert on diversified operators: DP World reported that its Ports and Terminals segment experienced a decline in adjusted EBITDA margin to 44.4% in the first half of

5.
2026. This margin compression from 51.7% in the prior year reflected the negative impact of lower throughput in the UAE. These results prove that volume declines quickly dilute profitability because port operations are highly capital-intensive and carry high fixed costs.
6.
Exceptional yield growth can temporarily shield regional gateway operators from broader macroeconomic headwinds: International Container Terminal Services reported an 8% increase in consolidated volume to 4.0 mm TEUs for the third quarter of 2026. During the same quarter, the company's average revenue per TEU expanded by 9% to reach $238. This dual expansion of volume and pricing allowed the operator to deliver double-digit revenue growth despite ongoing shipping disruptions.

Diversified geographic terminal portfolios

7.
Favorable foreign exchange movements and localized tariff adjustments can defend operating profitability: International Container Terminal Services reported that its consolidated EBITDA increased by 15% to $635.74 mm in the third quarter of 2026. Profitability was bolstered by tariff adjustments and favorable translation from Brazilian Real-based revenues at the Suape terminal. These localized factors helped cushion consolidated EBITDA margins which stood at 65% during the integration of new assets.
8.
High gateway concentration provides superior margin protection compared to transshipment-heavy terminal portfolios: Hutchison Ports demonstrated strong resilience by handling 90.1 mm TEUs in 2025, representing a 3% increase. In the first half of 2026, the division recorded a 4% revenue increase to HK$24.52 bn and maintained an EBITDA of HK$9.03 bn. Analysts note that Hutchison's gateway dominance enables a high 35% EBITDA margin, acting as a highly generative cash cow for its parent company.
9.
Broad geographical diversification continues to anchor operational metrics during regional geopolitical strains: International Container Terminal Services achieved a 27% increase in first-half 2026 revenues to $1.92 bn. This strong growth was driven by newly added terminals in South Africa and Indonesia alongside volume expansions in the Americas. These diversified operations successfully offset regional throughput declines in Iraq caused by Middle East conflicts, proving that geographic breadth stabilizes earnings through turbulent cycles.
10.
Other side of the story: Defensive port operations can sustain high cash generation and insulate integrated logistics companies during ocean shipping downcycles. Despite historical downturn vulnerabilities, global terminal networks enjoy structural barriers to entry and long concession profiles averaging 32 years. Proactive terminal operators are successfully leveraging digitalization and green infrastructure investments to lock in multiyear service contracts. Therefore, APM Terminals remains highly likely to cushion ocean segment losses and support Maersk's consolidated cash flows.

Section 5 – Logistics & Services Margin Targets and Peer Integration Strategies

Section summary
  1. Maersk's Logistics & Services segment has historically failed to achieve its 6% full-year EBIT margin target despite reaching 5.8% in the third quarter of 2026.
  2. Asset-light forwarders like DSV and Kuehne + Nagel maintain superior margins by outsourcing transportation assets and standardizing workflows on unified IT platforms.

Historical margins and targets

1.
Challenging targets: Maersk's strategic target of a 6% full-year EBIT margin for Logistics & Services has remained historically out of reach. The division achieved a high EBIT margin of 5.8% in the third quarter of 2026. However, the full-year margin was only 4.8% in 2025.

- Earnings Calls Q p Maersk Logistics & Services financial highlights showing EBIT margin of 5.8% in Q3 2026

2.
Unrealistic expectations: Bernstein Research argues that the 6% margin target is highly challenging because nearly 80% of the logistics business consists of activities where even best-in-class operators struggle to earn such returns. The broker notes that achieving both a 10% organic growth rate and a 6% EBIT margin simultaneously represents an extremely stretched goal for the company. This implies that the segment's profitability may remain structurally capped below corporate ambitions.
3.
Dragging contract logistics: Maersk's contract logistics division, operating under Fulfilled by Maersk, has consistently dragged down overall segment margins due to persistent operational challenges. The segment reported negative EBITA margins for multiple years due to warehousing whitespace and customer implementation issues in North America. Consequently, resolving these landside operational bottlenecks is essential for Maersk to achieve its mid-term financial targets.

Operational strategies of asset-light forwarders

4.
Efficient scale economics: DSV has achieved sector-leading profitability by utilizing an asset-light business model that minimizes capital intensity. The forwarder controls cargo flows but outsources physical transportation tasks to third-party providers, ensuring high cash flow conversion and structural flexibility. This approach allows DSV to swiftly adjust its cost base to match volatile demand environments.
5.
Unified technology platforms: Operational integration at DSV relies heavily on standardizing IT platforms to drive labor productivity. The company's playbook involves rapidly migrating acquired businesses onto a single Transport Management System to eliminate administrative redundancies. This strict focus on process standardization has enabled DSV to target an Air & Sea conversion margin above 55% by 2030.
6.
Organic vertical focus: Kuehne + Nagel pursues a different margin-improvement strategy centered on organic growth in high-value cargo verticals. The company prioritizes specialized end-to-end solutions for pharmaceutical, aerospace, and semiconductor customers while utilizing a unified IT and data platform. This strategy allows the company to maintain a highly reliable dividend profile and generate consistent cash flows through cycles.

Peer integration models and commercial risks

7.
Neutrality versus integration: CMA CGM has structured its CEVA Logistics subsidiary to preserve commercial neutrality and protect customer relationships. Unlike Maersk, CMA CGM does not rebrand CEVA, which helps maintain the forwarder's neutrality with third-party carriers. This separation ensures that CEVA remains a credible logistics partner on a level playing field.
8.
Aggressive contract logistics: CEVA Logistics significantly expanded its North American contract logistics footprint through a $1.4 bn acquisition of FedEx Supply Chain in 2026. The transaction nearly tripled CEVA's warehousing footprint in the region while establishing multi-year ocean and air cargo partnerships between the parent companies. This acquisition advances CMA CGM's strategy to offer comprehensive endto-end logistics solutions in a major trade market.
9.
Dangerous customer conflict: Bernstein Research highlights that Maersk's integrator strategy creates significant commercial risks and negative synergies by turning customers into competitors. Since freight forwarders typically represent 50% of a container line's volumes, Maersk's pivot into logistics has made forwarders hesitant to book space on Maersk vessels. This customer friction suggests that Maersk may sacrifice high-margin shipping volumes to chase lower-margin logistics revenue.

Cross-selling and container stickiness

10.
Segmented customer stickiness: Maersk's integrated vertical offerings generate genuine container stickiness for specialized customer groups. Logistics integration creates strong relationships with fast-moving consumer goods and perishable food shippers who prioritize operational reliability. However, transactional commodity customers remain highly price-sensitive and continue to buy on spot ocean rates.
11.
Contrary evidence: Maersk's vertical integrator model has failed to deliver any measurable revenue synergies or margin improvements since its inception. Bernstein Research reports that the integrator strategy is fundamentally flawed and has failed to provide any competitive advantage outside of capacity-constrained markets. Beneficial cargo owners still select logistics partners based on individual contract pricing rather than bundled shipping packages, meaning the integration lacks genuine commercial synergy.
12.
Uncertain margin accretion: Morgan Stanley remains highly skeptical of Maersk's vertical cross-selling potential because customer motivations for procuring logistics differ fundamentally from shipping procurement. Shipping is treated as a commoditized service with volatile short-term rates, whereas logistics contracts involve long-term partnership commitments and sticky pricing. This misalignment explains why Maersk's aggressive $7.7 bn M&A campaign has yet to translate into superior operating margins.
13.
Other side of the story: Maersk's ability to combine physical assets with advanced automation could establish a highly profitable end-to-end logistics moat that asset-light forwarders cannot replicate. By directly managing PUMA's North American distribution network across 2.3 million square feet of highly automated warehouses, Maersk is demonstrating the value of combining asset ownership with operational expertise. This integrated approach could allow Maersk to lock in long-term contracts and protect its earnings from ocean rate volatility.

Section 6 – Capital Allocation, Balance Sheet Strength and Shareholder Returns in

Section summary
  1. Maersk has historically prioritized balance sheet conservation over shareholder returns by reducing dividends and suspending buybacks during severe shipping downturns.
  2. Peer container lines maintain substantial cash reserves and net cash balance sheets to support moderated payouts during cyclical low points.

Downturns

Historical dividend cuts and buyback pauses

1.
Underperforming results: Maersk adjusted its capital distribution defensively during the 2016 downturn to protect its financial position. The Board proposed a reduced dividend of DKK 150 per share in February 2017, down from DKK 300 in the previous year, following a reported net loss of $1.9 bn for the fiscal year 2016. This action was deemed a necessary measure to defend the company's investment-grade rating during a period of severe market overcapacity.
2.
188 - Earnings Calls Prudent suspension: Maersk immediately halted its share buyback program in February 2024 to preserve capital as freight rates normalized from pandemic peaks. Management suspended the program while proposing a dividend of DKK 515 per share, representing a significant drop from the DKK 4,300 per share distributed in fiscal year 2022. This capital preservation strategy reflected expectations of significant oversupply challenges in Ocean throughout the coming quarters.
3.
Lowered returns: Maersk moderated its shareholder return profile in February 2026 to align with deteriorating container shipping market conditions. The company proposed a dividend of DKK 480 per share and lowered its share buyback program to DKK 1bn over twelve months, down from previous multi-billion dollar programs. This cautious capital deployment aligns with consensus expectations of persisting industry-wide overcapacity and near-term freight rate pressures.

Balance sheet strength and peer comparisons

4.
