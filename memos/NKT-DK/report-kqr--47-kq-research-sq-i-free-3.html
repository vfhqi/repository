Section 2 – Share price and valuation correlation with peers and sector

Section summary
  1. NKT’s historical performance has been closely tied to the European electrification cycle, yet its high- voltage pure-play status warrants a premium that is currently suppressed by capital expenditure timing.

Multi-year correlation and performance trends

1.
Sustained outperformance: Long-term share price performance indicates that NKT has outperformed peers since 2019, delivering an 8-fold return compared to a 4-fold return for Nexans and a 3-fold return for Prysmian.
2.
Divergent trajectories: Broker analysis reveals NKT significantly underperformed its key European peers during 2024, trailing Prysmian by nearly 70 percentage points and Nexans by 25 percentage points.
3.
Sector-led momentum: Strategic re-rating has propelled Prysmian to exceptionally strong performance, transitioning from a cyclical cable player into a structural electrification leader.
4.
Earnings-driven divergence: Financial forecasting indicates NKT will deliver the fastest earnings growth in the sector, supported by its pure-play transmission portfolio.
5.
Delayed capacity: Relative performance is constrained by NKT's capital expenditure cycle, as its major Karlskrona expansion will not be commercially active until Q2 2027.

Relative valuation and forward multiples

6.
Valuation premium: Multiple comparisons from Jefferies show NKT trading at a 2027 forward P/E of 23x, which is a premium to Prysmian's 18.80x and Nexans' 15.80x.
7.

Discounted growth: Valuation modeling suggests NKT is the cheapest on growth-adjusted terms when evaluated on growth-adjusted terms using its PEG ratio.

EBITDA concentration: Geographic and product exposure differs significantly, as NKT concentrates 70% of its EBITDA in high-margin transmission projects compared to much lower levels at peers.

Section 7 – Data gaps

Section summary
  1. Structural visibility into NKT's medium-term margin progression remains constrained by limited project-level financial disclosures and highly unpredictable variation order patterns.

Corporate disclosure limits and blind spots

1.
Opaque project-level profitability: Limited disclosure restricts project-by-project margin tracking because NKT does not break down Transmission revenues or profitability for individual contracts in its public reports.
2.
Unpredictable variation margins: Unpredictable contract adjustments cloud short-term execution trends as variation orders are completely excluded from management guidance despite driving significant margin volatility.
3.
Unclear capex escalation: Disappointing capex overruns raise questions about budget discipline because management never disaggregated the Karlskrona overruns into distinct scope changes versus cost inflation.
4.
Short-selling lending opacity: Restricted lending data limits visibility into short interest because European exchanges do not consistently disclose lending metrics or active security lending activity for NKT.
5.
Post-2030 pricing transition: Unclear long-term pricing limits terminal value visibility because there is minimal data on pricing power sustainability post-2030 once peers complete their ongoing capacity expansions.

Resolution methodologies and tracking tools

6.
Structured project monitoring: Rigorous project tracking requires monitoring physical milestones such as commercial start of Karlskrona tower expansion in Q2 2027 and the delivery of the NKT Eleonora vessel.
7.
Competitor margin benchmarking: Comparative financial tracking provides critical margin read- throughs by benchmarking NKT's Transmission margins against already-ramped capacity expansions at Prysmian and Nexans.
8.
Vessel satellite tracking: Alternative AIS data helps verify fleet utilization by tracking the real-time installation campaigns and offshore operations of NKT's cable-laying vessels to anticipate margin surprises.
9.
Early execution indicators: Proactive risk monitoring helps detect project slippage because tracking contract margin attrition serves as the primary warning indicator for execution issues.
10.
Other side of the story: Bullish analysts claim these data gaps are insignificant due to NKT's secure EUR 13bn backlog, but this view ignores that severe project penalties from unmonitored execution delays would quickly wipe out projected EPS gains.
