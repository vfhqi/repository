(BGN-IT), an Italian private bank and wealth manager, 50.2% owned by Assicurazioni Generali, distributing through about 2,470 self-employed financial advisers. Section 2 – Management Fee Margins and Product Mix Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Banca Generali's gross management fee margin has exhibited structural stability around 140 to 144 basis points since 2015, defying broader European fee compression trends.
  2. Stability in recurring margins has been sustained by a deliberate shift toward higher-margin in-house funds and sophisticated financial wrappers, offsetting cyclical macro headwinds.
  3. Favorable asset mix adjustments have successfully shielded core revenues during equity drawdowns and interest rate cycles, while direct pricing power remains limited.

Historical margin evolution and macro cycle resilience

1.
Gross margin stability: Banca Generali’s gross management fee margin on average managed assets has shown exceptional long-term stability, hovering between 1.40% and 1.44% across multiple macroeconomic cycles since 2015.
2.
Pandemic dip and recovery: During the height of the Covid-19 pandemic in Q2 2020, the gross management fee margin experienced a temporary dip to 1.36% due to tactical cash allocations. However, it quickly recovered to 1.37% by Q3 2020 and 1.43% by 2021 as markets stabilized. - Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls Drawdown resilience: During the severe market drawdown of 2022, where global equity and bond markets fell simultaneously, Banca Generali's management fee margin on managed assets remained highly resilient, closing the year flat at 1.43%.
3.
Sovereign yield crowd-out: In the high interest rate environment of 2023 and 2024, retail client focus shifted heavily toward Italian sovereign bonds (BTPs), causing deposit outflows. This retail BTP "crowd-out" led to client assets migrating toward basic administration (AuC), which dragged down the blended yield on total client assets, even though the margin on the managed portion remained stable at 1.40% to 1.43%. - Earnings Calls Post-crisis normalisation: In the first half of 2026, the annualized asset management margin picked up to 1.44% (representing 1.44% of average managed assets), supported by renewed inflows into higher- margin managed solutions.

Product mix shifts versus pure pricing power

4.
Product-mix driving margins: The resilience of Banca Generali's recurring margin is primarily a function of product mix optimization rather than direct price increases. Over the last decade, the bank has aggressively shifted client assets away from lower-margin third-party funds and traditional insurance policies into higher-margin structures.
5.
Wrapper and fund expansion: From 2018 to 2021, the share of traditional, lower-margin life insurance products fell by 6 percentage points, while higher-margin financial and insurance wrappers rose to represent 38% of managed solutions. - Analyst/Investor Luxembourg platform optimization: Growth has been highly geared toward the bank's Luxembourg manufacturing hub, BG Fund Management Luxembourg (BGFML), where retail-class in-house funds (such as the LUX IM SICAV) grew to EUR 15.0 billion by 1H 2026, capturing a larger share of the value chain. Core vs. blended dilution: While the core management fee margin remains highly stable, a notable shift has occurred toward advanced advisory services (BG Personal Advisory) on assets under custody (AuC), which are charged at a lower margin of approximately 50 basis points, diluting the blended fee yield on total financial assets.
6.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank's report of 3 September 2026 calculated a slight quarter-on-quarter reduction in Banca Generali's average recurring asset management margin, which declined by 2 basis points to 1.35% in Q2 2026 due to strong inflows into lower-margin automatic investment strategies.

Peer fee structures and margin comparisons

7.
Sector margin comparison: The Italian wealth management sector exhibits highly divergent recurring fee margins based on vertical integration and asset mix.
8.
Banca Mediolanum pricing: Peer Banca Mediolanum commands the highest recurring fee yield in the sector, averaging 2.02% to 2.12% from 2024 through H1 2026. This is driven by an equity-heavy asset allocation (over 60% equity exposure) and a high concentration of complex, insurance-wrapped unit-linked products.
9.
Fineco transparent pricing: FinecoBank serves as the low-fee, transparent pricing benchmark, holding pre-tax investing margins steady at a much lower 73 basis points in 2024. Fineco relies on its high-scale digital platform and the penetration of its in-house manufacturing arm, Fineco Asset Management, to offset competitive fee pressure.
10.
Asset mix differences: Jefferies' research of 6 February 2023 notes that while Banca Mediolanum had approximately 55% of client assets in equity-linked exposures, Banca Generali’s equity exposure was significantly lower at 25%, explaining the structural gap between Mediolanum’s ~200 basis points margin and Generali’s ~140 basis points margin. Other side of the story: While peer margins have begun to contract–with Banca Mediolanum's average recurring fee declining from 214-215 basis points in 2023 to 198 basis points in H1 2026–Banca Generali has successfully defended its 141-143 basis points core margin target, proving that its conservative, wrapper-based model has higher pricing durability during market volatility.

Section 4 – Performance Fees and Retrocession Structures

Section summary
  1. Performance fee restructuring has successfully improved the quality and predictability of Banca Generali's earnings
  2. Banca Generali's retrocession revenues face long-term regulatory scrutiny from European value-for-money frameworks
  3. High-water mark mechanisms limit the short-term recovery of performance fees after severe market drawdowns

Performance fee mechanics and peer comparison

1.
Fee parameters: Banca Generali's Luxembourg funds apply a 20% performance fee with no hurdle rate under daily crystallization.
2.
High water marks: The Lux IM SICAV utilizes a high-water mark equivalent to maximum inception NAV.
3.
High-on-high mechanism: The BG Collection funds utilize a five-year high-on-high mechanism with annual crystallization.
4.
Peer coverage: Peer Banca Mediolanum applies performance fees on managed assets covering c.80% of its products.
5.
Mediolanum hurdles: Mediolanum's products apply a 1% to 5% hurdle rate with a 1% NAV cap.

8.
Recovery constraints: Jefferies notes that performance fees are constrained by high-water marks limiting immediate upside after drawdowns.
9.
Contrary evidence: Banca Generali's performance fees in first half 2026 surged to EUR 125.8 million.

Retrocessions and regulatory compliance

Section 5 – Low-Margin Channels, Client Segmentation, and adviser Competition

Section summary
  1. Lower-margin products and partnerships present a structural headwind, diluting Banca Generali's blended fee yield over the next four years despite driving robust overall asset inflows.
  2. High-net-worth fee concessions and adviser recruitment incentives are escalating distribution costs, limiting the net profitability of new asset gathering across Italian wealth networks.
  3. Corporate takeover battles have directly elevated competitor operating expenses and disrupted short-term asset gathering, while triggering intensive defensive retention programs.

Margin dilution from lower-margin strategic channels

1.
Segregated asset structures: Banca Generali's strategic "Insurbanking" partnership with Alleanza targets total volumes of €7.0 billion to €8.5 billion by 2030, but introduces distinct lower-margin components.
2.
Low-margin investment components: The investment component of these hybrid products, marketed under the Stile Unico unit-linked wrapper, is projected to generate €2.5 billion to €3.0 billion in volumes by 2030 at an average net margin of 60-65 basis points.
3.
Banking product margins: The banking and Assets under Custody (AuC) component, structured under the Conto Unico current account, targets €3.0 billion to €3.5 billion in volumes by 2030 with an average net margin of 80-90 basis points.
4.
Irish active ETF internalization: To defend against margin compression, Banca Generali completed its acquisition of Dublin-based active ETF platform Investlinx in July 2026 to internalize manufacturing margins on its clients' €7.3 billion ETF exposure.
5.
Strategic fee projections: Morgan Stanley's analysis of 24 February 2026 estimates that the partnership could generate €33 million in fee income from €5.0 billion of unit-linked assets at 65 basis points, alongside €32 million from €3.5 billion of current accounts at 90 basis points by 2030.
6.
Dilutive advisory migration: Deutsche Bank's report of 22 May 2023 cautions that the rapid penetration of advanced advisory services, which charge a lower fee-on-top of approximately 50 basis points, may dilute the blended fee yield on total financial assets.
7.
Contrary evidence: In the first half of 2026, Banca Generali’s average management fee margin on Private Banking assets (excluding hybrid products) remained highly stable at 1.44%, defying the dilutive impact of lower-margin channels.

Client fee concessions and peer segmentation

8.
Grandi Patrimoni private platform: Banca Mediolanum launched its dedicated "Progetto Grandi Patrimoni" private banking platform in May 2025 specifically to cater to high-net-worth individuals, which typically command heavier fee discounts than retail accounts.
9.
Recurring fee compression: Morgan Stanley's note of 3 September 2026 highlights that Banca Mediolanum's average recurring fee yield declined from 214-215 basis points in 2023 to 198 basis points in the first half of 2026 due to rising HNW client concentration and low-fee product shifts.
10.
Rising adviser payouts: Morgan Stanley's report of 3 September 2026 notes that competitor adviser acquisition costs have risen, with the payout to family bankers escalating from 33% of gross commissions in 2023 to 36% in the first half of 2026.
11.
Platform scale advantages: Jefferies' research of 7 May 2026 outlines that FinecoBank is able to grow its asset base above peer averages due to a highly competitive, low-fee transparent pricing structure supported by an efficient digital platform.
12.
Corporate contract discussions: Banca Generali's distribution contracts do not contain rigid indexing clauses but instead rely on general good-faith renegotiation clauses to address regulatory changes or pricing adjustments.

Cost impacts of recruitment competition and corporate bids
