Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The potential sale of Nova represents a pivotal event that could reshape OTE's high-margin domestic wholesale revenue and broader competitive dynamics.
  2. Any migration of Nova or Vodafone lines to alternative fiber infrastructure directly threatens OTE's long-term earnings per share growth.

Financial exposure and valuation risk

1.
Strategic inquiry: "Where does United Group's sale of Nova, Greece's third operator, stand, who are the likely buyers, and what would each kind of buyer mean for the wholesale lines Nova and Vodafone Greece rent from OTE today?"
2.
Severe revenue erosion: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE) faces significant risk to its earnings per share if major wholesale partners migrate their active lines to competing networks. Wholesale revenue historically generated a highmargin portion of OTE's total EBITDA, meaning any major customer churn directly reduces operating cash flows. Consequently, Deutsche Bank research suggests that domestic wholesale declines represent a primary downside threat to OTE's financial performance over the next three years.
3.
Negative dividend pressure: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization remains a favored stock among income-focused investors due to its superior shareholder remuneration policies. A sudden loss of high-margin wholesale agreements could reduce the company's free cash flow generation and restrict its capacity for future payout increases. Investors therefore monitor these wholesale dynamics closely because they directly dictate OTE's long-term ability to grow its annual dividend payments.
4.
Significant valuation compression: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization trades at specific multiples that reflect its established position as the undisputed market leader in Greece. Intense competition in the wholesale fiber space or the entry of aggressive infrastructure buyers could cause investors to de-rate the stock. Ultimately, a shift in Nova's wholesale partner of choice could contract OTE's historical forward price-toearnings and EV/EBITDA multiples significantly.

Broadening the competitive scope

5.
Evolving tariff frameworks: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization has sought to lock in its primary retail rivals by introducing volume-based wholesale fiber discount schemes. These frameworks offer unit price discounts of up to 28% to discourage alternative operators from building overlapping infrastructure. The pricing of these wholesale fiber lines remains a critical battlefield for OTE's market share protection.
6.
Intertwined mobile networks: Vodafone Greece and Nova operate their joint mobile network infrastructure through a shared vehicle named VICTUS Networks. This joint venture manages radio access and transmission network sharing nationwide to reduce capital expenditures for both operators. Any change in Nova's ownership structure could disrupt this critical mobile network sharing alliance in Greece.
7.
Alternative platform threats: Public Power Corporation continues to expand its alternative fiber-to-the-home footprint, threatening to provide a viable wholesale alternative. The utility company plans to reach over 3.8 million households by the end of 2028 and is in talks for a joint venture with Vodafone. This overbuild represents a significant alternative wholesale fiber network threat to OTE.
8.
Other side of the story: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization may find its financial outlook insulated from Nova's sale because both major rivals have already signed long-term wholesale agreements. Management estimates that the bulk of competitor active fiber lines will remain on OTE's network due to high migration hurdles. Therefore, OTE retains a strong defensive moat against immediate wholesale churn regardless of who buys Nova.

Section 2 – The Nova Sale Process, Bidders, and Timetable

Section summary
  1. The sale process of United Group's Greek operator Nova is drawing strategic interest from major global telecommunications companies.
  2. Potential bidders favor asset-light business models while regulatory authorities closely monitor the transaction for competitive compliance.

Transaction overview and scale

1.
Strategic divestment: BC Partners has appointed JP Morgan and Morgan Stanley to initiate the sale of United Group, which owns Nova Greece. This analysis utilizes ten distinct sources covering five companies and two precedents to evaluate the regional telecom landscape, leaving out Bulgarian media assets to focus purely on telecommunications. The transaction represents a major restructuring of Southeast European telecommunications, where Greece serves as United Group's largest single market. Ultimately, the deal could redefine OTE's future wholesale revenue streams significantly.
2.
Ambitious valuation: United Group is seeking an enterprise valuation of approximately EUR 8 bn, representing an 8x multiple of its 2023 EBITDA. Sell-side advisers are targeting both global private equity buyouts and strategic regional operators. However, some international private equity firms may avoid the bidding due to the specific macroeconomic risks of the Balkan region. This high valuation could limit the pool of buyers capable of acquiring Nova outright.
3.
Robust growth: United Group has demonstrated solid financial expansion, reporting a 7% increase in revenue to EUR 2.74 bn for the first nine months of 2023. The group's adjusted EBITDA grew by 10% to EUR 751.2m over the same nine-month period. These numbers reflect a healthy business model but also require substantial capital to sustain infrastructure investments. Consequently, any buyer must balance growth potential against the high capital expenditure requirements of fiber networks.

Prospective bidders and strategies

4.
Strategic interest: Saudi Telecom Company has emerged as a primary contender to purchase United Group to expand its footprint beyond the Middle East. The company already owns United Group's regional towers through its infrastructure subsidiary TAWAL, which acquired 4,800 tower assets for EUR 1.22 bn. This existing relationship gives the Saudi operator a strong baseline understanding of the target's regional operations. A successful acquisition would accelerate stc's transition toward a more diversified global telecom player indeed.
5.
Careful selection: Saudi Telecom Company's management has publicly stated that they will only pursue acquisitions that strictly enhance margins and free cash flow. The company intends to maintain a highly selective investment filter, actively walking away from transactions that fail to meet these criteria. This cautious stance is reflected in their latest commercial and wholesale revenue growth of 5.1% in late 2026. Consequently, the Greek fixed-line market must present demonstrable and sustainable wholesale margins to secure a bid.
6.
Alternative options: Emirates Telecommunications Group, known as e&, has also been linked with a potential acquisition of United Group. Although the chief executive of e& initially dismissed these market rumors, the company continues to evaluate strategic mergers across its geographic footprint. Financial analysts note that e& could achieve significant network and operating synergies in overlapping markets like Bulgaria. Therefore, a rival bid from e& remains a highly plausible alternative exit route for BC Partners.

Infrastructure models and asset shifts

7.
Asset-light transition: United Group has already shifted toward an asset-light infrastructure model by selling its tower portfolio to Saudi Telecom Company's TAWAL. This divestment unlocked EUR 1.22 bn in capital to help deleverage the group's balance sheet. Telecom operators across Europe increasingly utilize these carve-outs to monetize passive assets and focus on active services. This trend suggests that a new strategic owner might continue to divest Nova's physical network assets over time.
8.
Contrary evidence: No definitive buyer has yet signed a binding agreement for United Group or its Greek subsidiary, Nova, as of late 2026. Media reports indicate that some international investment funds, such as Gemcorp, have already dropped out of related bidding processes. This lack of finalized agreements indicates that the sale process remains highly complex, meaning United Group may struggle to complete the divestment. Ultimately, the transaction may not occur within the originally anticipated timetable or valuation range.

Section 3 – Greek Fixed Wholesale Market Dynamics and OTE Agreements

Section summary
  1. OTE's new FTTH wholesale volume-commitment framework stabilizes its market position and mitigates long-term revenue erosion.
  2. The potential Vodafone-PPC joint venture creates an emerging second wholesale network but faces regulatory and execution hurdles.
  3. European precedents show that alternative fiber networks inevitably cause gradual customer churn and significant pricing pressure for established incumbent operators.
  4. Incumbents can successfully mitigate wholesale revenue erosion by executing long-term volume-commitment agreements and commercial joint ventures in underserved areas.
  5. Our confidence in these wholesale migration precedents is high, based on robust financial data from Telecom Italia and Deutsche Telekom.

Market structure and wholesale framework

1.
Structured analysis: Financial research into the Greek fixed line market identifies 15 relevant sources, 4 core companies including Hellenic Telecommunications Organization, Vodafone Greece, Nova, and Public Power Corporation, and 1 comparable past case. This specific analysis utilizes 12 of these sources and all 4 named companies to evaluate wholesale market dynamics, leaving out early spectrum consultations from 2023 because they do not impact fiber commercial terms. The analysis confirms that OTE successfully established a highly defensive fixed wholesale model in Greece.
2.
Commercial commitments: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization secured long-term stability by signing its main retail competitors, Vodafone Greece and Nova, to its new Fiber-to-the-Home pricing framework in September 2024. This commercial framework offers volume-based unit price discounts of up to 28% in exchange for long-term subscriber commitments. Consequently, the pricing scheme acts as a powerful commercial incentive that prevents alternative wholesale operators from easily poaching OTE's existing wholesale customer lines.
3.
Defensive alignment: Deutsche Bank Research reports that OTE's wholesale framework commits the incumbent to wholesale the FTTH networks built by Vodafone Greece and Nova instead of deploying overbuilding infrastructure. This bilateral infrastructure commitment ensures that both alternative operators can secure appropriate financial returns on their respective local fiber rollouts. Ultimately, this collaborative arrangement reduces the commercial incentive for Vodafone or Nova to source their wholesale lines from hostile network builders like PPC.

Management financial updates and ARPU

While this high-margin revenue stream had been declining by around EUR 15m annually due to competitors expanding their own networks, the commercial FTTH agreements have successfully stabilized these losses. The broker expects that national wholesale sales will continue to stabilize, minimizing the downward pressure on group earnings.

Vodafone and PPC joint venture

11.
Strategic partnership: Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation signed a non-binding heads of terms in June 2026 to explore a 50:50 joint venture. The proposed venture aims to merge Vodafone's Fiber2All and PPC's FiberGrid operations to create a unified wholesale open-access FTTH platform. On a combined basis, their respective fiber networks already passed more than 1.6 million homes in mid-2026, creating a formidable second wholesale fiber network.

This aggressive expansion provides Public Power Corporation with the physical scale needed to challenge OTE's dominant national fixed line position.

Regulatory hurdles: NBG Securities research reports that the proposed joint venture between Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation remains subject to regulatory review by the Hellenic Competition Commission. The transaction continues to undergo mutual commercial due diligence and detailed design ahead of a potential definitive agreement. Analysts observe that because the joint venture would control a significant fiber network covering over 1.5 million homes, the commission will likely scrutinize the competitive market impact closely.

Italian and Irish migration timelines
