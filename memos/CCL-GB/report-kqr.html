Section 2 – Global Cruise Order Book and Capacity Growth (2027-2030)

Section summary
  1. Tight global cruise shipyard capacity limits overall industry supply growth to under four percent annually through 2030, establishing a highly favorable pricing window.

Tightening Global Cruise Order Book

1.
Constrained supply: The global cruise industry benefits from a historically thin order book that prevents oversupply and underpins operators' pricing power over the next five years. Morgan Stanley Research reports that the global cruise order book representing roughly 190,000 lower berths is equivalent to only 25% of current fleet capacity compared to nearly 50% in 2019. This supply constraint means major operators can prolong their strong ticket pricing window well past the 2027 season.
2.
Moderate expansion: Global ocean cruise capacity additions in 2027 will remain highly controlled and sit well below long-term historical averages. Bernstein Research expects the 2027 cruise order book to add 28,800 berths to industry capacity, which translates to about 3.8% supply growth for the year. This rate of growth represents a major deceleration from the 5.2% annual average capacity increase that the global industry recorded over the past 25 years.

Fincantieri SpA reports a profound backlog of 73.9 billion euros with visibility extending up to 2039 across its shipbuilding divisions. These lengthy backlogs mean cruise operators cannot easily secure new building slots to rapidly expand their fleets before the end of the decade.

Merchant Demand and Chinese Competition

4.
Global yard utilization: Global shipbuilding utilization is approaching historic peaks, which leaves very few idle slots for unexpected cruise vessel additions. Wärtsilä reports that global shipyard capacity has recovered to approximately 85% of its previous peak and is expected to reach 97% by 2030. This expansion is primarily driven by existing Chinese yards ramping up production to meet resilient cargo vessel demand. However, because cruise construction requires highly specialized labor and supply chains, merchant yard expansions do not create interchangeable slots for passenger vessels.
5.
Specialized capacity constraints: Chinese shipyards are aggressively expanding their physical capacity to capture global market share, but their focus remains on merchant ship designs. Deutsche Bank Research observes that while China dominates merchant vessel ordering, European yards won all cruise ship contracts during the first half of 2026. A new Chinese production line designed for tankers or container ships cannot be easily converted to build complex cruise ships. Therefore, the rapid expansion of Asian shipbuilding capacity provides no near-term relief for cruise lines seeking faster deliveries.
6.
Cargo demand: The surge in merchant shipping orders continues to crowd out non-merchant construction slots across the global maritime industry. Morgan Stanley reports that global ship orders during the first nine months of 2026 rose 31% year-on-year, driven by product tankers and gas carriers. In contrast, global cruise ship orders declined 16% over the same period, reflecting a highly disciplined and constrained ordering environment. This divergence shows that merchant demand is consuming shipyard engineering resources and limiting options for cruise capacity expansion.

Distributive building: Shipbuilders are attempting to bypass local labor bottlenecks by adopting decentralized construction strategies rather than expanding existing yards. Huntington Ingalls Industries is expanding its use of outsourcing and distributive shipbuilding to supplement its internal capacity and improve throughput. However, this decentralized model is highly vulnerable to supply chain disruptions and cannot easily be adapted to complex cruise ship assembly.

Global network: Fincantieri is investing in a global network of yards to rebalance its workload and expand its physical capacity limits. By shifting simpler hulls to these global sites, Fincantieri hopes to free up its premium European yards for advanced cruise assembly.
