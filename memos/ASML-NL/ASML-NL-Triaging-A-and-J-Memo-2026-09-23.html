[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. ASML is a 'Demand Inflection' case in a monopoly, read late. The artificial-intelligence build-out has made the only maker of EUV lithography tools supply-limited into 2027, with customers pre-paying to hold their place, and at 1,507.4 EUR the shares already carry the two years of growth we can see. On our path the return from here is single digits a year, unless the 2028 order book and the June 2027 Capital Markets Day prove the growth structural rather than cyclical.

  • The change is external, sector-wide and paid for by the customers' own cash.
    • Lithography intensity is rising at logic and memory at the same time, and the evidence is at the customer, not only at the company.
    • TSMC raised its 2026 capital expenditure to USD 60 to 64 billion from USD 52 to 56 billion
      • SK hynix holds long-term agreements with about ten customers carrying deposits
      • Deutsche Bank's wafer-fab-equipment forecast rose to USD 193 billion for 2027 and USD 220 billion for 2028.
    • Management guided memory-related system sales up more than 75 percent and advanced logic system sales up more than 25 percent in 2026, and said it is close to receiving all the orders needed to cover 2027 low-NA EUV capacity of about 85 systems.
    • Contract liabilities of 18.9 billion EUR at end-2025 (58 percent of revenue) are non-refundable down payments
      • the customers finance the capacity build.
  • The company's response is a change force in its own right, at scale.
    • On 15 July 2026 it raised FY2026 revenue guidance to 43 to 45 billion EUR (35 percent growth at the midpoint, 11.4 percent above the consensus of the day) and gross-margin guidance to 54 to 56 percent from 51 to 53 percent.
    • It committed to lift low-NA EUV capacity from about 65 systems in 2026 to about 85 in 2027, with about 110 under investigation for 2028, and DUV immersion from 130 to about 170 with about 220 under investigation.
    • Value-based pricing is applied in real terms, 5 to 8 percent a year with zero discounting, and the 1,700-position delayering is a cost-discipline action with savings deferred to May 2027.
  • The 'Financial Outputs' have already done what the pattern asks of them.
    • Q2 2026 revenue rose 21.3 percent, gross margin reached 54.0 percent against 51 to 52 percent guided, EBIT margin 37.1 percent, and EPS rose 29 percent.
    • The sell-side has re-based: FY2027 consensus EPS mean up 74.7 percent in twelve months to 52.95 EUR, FY2028 up 98.6 percent to 68.56 EUR (2026-09-18).
    • The upgrade wave has crested: one-month revisions are plus 0.1 to plus 1.3 percent, so the next leg needs new information, which arrives on 14 October 2026 and in January 2027.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The resources (a chief executive two years in post) and choices (capacity, pricing) are made
    • the actions (the build, the delayering, the buyback) are under way
    • the key operating metrics (EUV units, gross margin, installed-base software sales) have moved
    • the financial statements show it
    • the sell-side has re-based.
    • Nothing is still to propagate except the 2028 decision and the 2030 framework.
  • The growth sits far above the framework's 12 to 20 percent band, and the framework treats an inflection as cyclical until proven structural.
    • FY2026 EPS rises about 58 percent on our path (39.0 EUR against 24.73 EUR), then about 32 percent to 51.5 EUR in FY2027 and to 62 EUR in FY2028, before a plateau at about 68 EUR in FY2029 as the capacity built through 2028 is absorbed.
    • Consensus, on eight brokers, has 91.64 EUR for FY2029, and consensus FY2029 revenue of 77,921 EURm sits above the company's own 2030 framework ceiling of 60 billion EUR
      • the structural case requires management to have under-called its own market by about a third.
    • The base rate inside this company is against extrapolation: growth years have come in pairs and triples followed by a digestion (plus 33 percent in FY2021 then plus 14 percent; plus 30 percent in FY2023 then plus 2.6 percent).
  • What it costs is the point.
    • At 1,507.4 EUR the shares stand on 23.8 times consensus earnings twenty-four months forward, the stock's own ten-year median, after an 86.7 percent twelve-month rise
    • the multiple has not expanded with the growth, it has sat at the median while earnings caught up, because the market, like us, is reading the inflection as cyclical.
  • The 'Modal return' fails the bar on our path, and the re-rating is the swing.
    • Over eighteen months the earnings the market prices (twenty-four months forward) grow about 9.7 percent (63.354 to 69.5 EUR), the multiple slips to 22.4x (minus 5.9 percent) as forward growth decelerates to single digits, and cash adds about 1.0 percent: plus 4.3 percent in total, 2.9 percent a year, and 6.0 percent a year over three years.
    • The case clears the 20 percent bar on our own path at about 1,200 EUR, and at the current price only on the consensus 2029 path, which we put at about 30 percent: possible, not probable.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through (higher amortisation, a 21 percent tax rate, a slower buyback) takes FY2028 EPS to 57.4 EUR and the eighteen-month return to minus 3.3 percent
      • the rating does not change.

  • So the chain holds at the forces and the outputs and breaks at the return.

    • The case is compelling as a business and not compelling as a return at this price
    • the pattern fits crisply, and the pattern's own hazard, growth above 40 percent reverting to base rates, is live.

  • Rating = C, because the Building Block asks whether the expected change is material along its whole chain, from forces to outputs to a modal return of 20 percent-plus a year, and the chain holds at the first two links ('Change Forces' A; 'Financial Outputs' B) and fails at the third ('Modal return' F): the parts bind and the weaker sets the level, because a thesis whose change is already in the price is material as an event and immaterial as a return.

    • It is not D because the forces and the outputs are among the strongest in the universe and an entry about 17 to 20 percent lower would clear the bar on our own path
    • it is not B because "compelling once the price falls" is not a return we can bank today.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The bull's reading is that we are under-calling 2029.
  • The 2028 capacity of about 110 low-NA systems will be ordered by mid-2027 as 2027's 85 were
    • value-based pricing on the NXE:3800F and 3800G lifts ASP 5 to 8 percent a year
    • Installed Base Management grows with the fleet at about 11 percent a year
    • DRAM EUV layers rise from three to eight
    • and the 2030 framework is raised at the 10 June 2027 Capital Markets Day to a range whose midpoint is above 70 billion EUR.
  • On that path FY2029 EPS is 90 EUR-plus (consensus 91.64 EUR on eight brokers) and the shares at 1,507.4 EUR stand on about 16.5x FY2029 earnings, a multiple with no precedent in the stock's own history for a monopoly growing 30 percent.
  • We concede that this is the consensus of the houses that have been right for two years, that the 2027 order book was filled before the tools were built, and that our FY2029 plateau is a base-rate judgement rather than a datum.
  • We hold our view for four reasons.
  • The base rate inside this company is strong: growth years have come in pairs and triples followed by a digestion.
  • Two cyclical supports sit inside the current numbers: about 200 basis points of software-upgrade gross margin, and a second-half China re-acceleration of more than 130 percent that the 20 percent full-year share requires.
  • The eight-broker FY2029 consensus sits above the company's own stated 2030 ceiling.
  • And the framework tells us to treat an inflection as cyclical until proven structural
    • the proof is the 2028 order coverage, observable from January 2027, and it is the single datum that would move our FY2029 from 68 EUR toward 85 EUR and this rating from C to B.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = A

    • the two headwinds (China normalisation; the MATCH Act) are real but sized at about a tenth to a sixth of revenue against a 35 percent growth year.
  • Stage of the forces: their weakness is not strength but stage
    • they have already reached the sell-side's estimates and the share price.
  • The rating is not B because the framework's 'Demand Inflection' test (sector-level; structural, or cyclical with two-plus years' visibility; evidenced by customer capital spending and order books) is met on every limb, with the evidence at the customer and not only at the company.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand is changing structurally and cyclically at once, at sector level, with two-plus years of visibility: management guided memory-related system sales to grow more than 75 percent in 2026 and advanced logic system sales more than 25 percent (15 July 2026), said it is close to receiving all the orders needed to cover 2027 low-NA EUV capacity of about 85 systems, and backed the 2027 and 2028 expansions with non-refundable customer down payments.
  • Customer capital: TSMC raised its 2026 capital expenditure guidance to USD 60 to 64 billion from USD 52 to 56 billion (July 2026)
    • TSMC's N2 node entered volume production ahead of schedule at 3 percent of wafer revenue in Q2 2026.
  • Memory intensity: EUV layers rise from three exposures at 1b DRAM to eight at 1d
    • 1c DRAM needs five to six EUV layers a wafer
    • DRAM EUV exposures are forecast to expand 4.5 times over five years
    • management's own frame is DRAM lithography spending growing 15 to 25 percent a year over 2025 to 2030 and advanced logic 10 to 20 percent.
  • Industry spending: wafer-fab-equipment spending of about USD 145 billion in 2026 (up 16 percent), forecast at USD 193 billion in 2027 and USD 220 billion in 2028
    • Tokyo Electron confirmed record equipment demand on 30 July 2026.
  • Visibility: backlog 38.8 billion EUR at end-2025 with record Q4 2025 bookings of 13.2 billion EUR
    • customers share multi-year cleanroom projections longer than historical norms.
  • The edge of visibility: not all 85 low-NA tools for 2027 have firm finalised orders and the 2028 capacity of about 110 is under investigation rather than committed (Jefferies, July 2026), so 2028 is the visible edge and beyond it the case is the AI capital-spending cycle itself.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because a live legislative proposal, the bipartisan MATCH Act introduced in April 2026, would ban all immersion lithography shipments and servicing to China and is estimated by Jefferies to put about 15 percent of ASML's revenue at risk
    • Dutch rules already require licences for NXT:1970i and 1980i shipments and prohibit upgrades that lift throughput more than 1 percent on installed Chinese tools
    • and management's own China guidance (about 20 percent of 2026 revenue from 33 percent in 2025) requires system sales to China to grow more than 130 percent in the second half of 2026 after 14 percent of system sales in Q2 (Rothschild & Co Redburn).
  • Sovereign funding: the offset is a tailwind on the customers' side: the US Commerce Department's 10 percent Intel stake for USD 5.7 billion
    • EU Chips Act 2.0 targeting 120 billion EUR by 2035
    • South Korea's USD 880 billion decade plan.
  • Passage odds: Deutsche Bank judges the MATCH Act a diplomatic lever whose window before elections is constrained and standalone passage unlikely without concessions (April 2026).
  • Exposure: ASML's China exposure of 16 percent in H1 2026 is the lowest among wafer-fab-equipment peers (Applied Materials 27 percent, Lam Research 30 percent), and China is excluded from EUV entirely, so the growth leg is unaffected.
  • Not F because the size is bounded and the growth leg is untouched
    • the direction is a headwind and its timing is the unknown.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle turned up in 2025 (revenue plus 15.6 percent after plus 2.6 percent in 2024) and is now accelerating hard (Q2 2026 plus 21.3 percent; FY2026 guided plus 35 percent at the midpoint; consensus FY2027 plus 27.1 percent), which puts the company in the middle of an upcycle whose third year, 2028, is the last one with capacity visibility.
  • Before Q2: Q1 2026 revenue 8,767 EURm, plus 13.2 percent
    • Q4 2025 bookings 13.1 to 13.2 billion EUR, which Jefferies called the peak.
  • After Q2: 2027 capacity almost fully ordered
    • customer discussions already shifting to 2028 (CCB International, 16 July 2026).
  • Not A because a cycle in its second acceleration year with 30 percent-plus growth is closer to its peak than its trough, and the framework treats an inflection as cyclical until proven structural.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because competitive intensity is unchanged where the profit is (EUV, 100 percent share; installed-base services) and tightening only at the mature-node DUV edge in China and in back-end packaging, which together are a minority of revenue.
  • Chinese entry: Shanghai Aishengna, integrating Shanghai Yuliangsheng and SMEE technology, began mass production of domestic immersion DUV tools on 27 July 2026 with a plan of five units in 2026 and 20 in 2027, at estimated yields of 10 to 20 percent against ASML's 90 percent-plus and throughput of 10 to 30 wafers an hour against 200
    • Chinese local suppliers were expected to hold 25 percent of the domestic market by late 2026.
  • Nikon: plans to lift ArF scanner sales to ten systems in FY2027 from three and is developing a new-platform immersion scanner with prototype deliveries in 2028 and a commercial ramp in FY2031
    • yen weakness of 31.5 percent over five years helps Canon and Nikon price mature tools.
  • Where it lands: the Netherlands' share of Chinese lithography imports hit a record 89 percent in 2024
    • the MATCH Act itself is the accelerant for domestic substitution because it forces Chinese fabs to accept inferior tools.
  • Back-end: ASML's XT:260 i-line scanner (shipped Q3 2025) attacks Canon's packaging stepper position rather than the reverse.
  • Not A because the Chinese state-backed entry is now a fact with a shipment plan and a policy tailwind behind it, and mature DUV (about 10.3 billion EUR of immersion revenue in FY2025) is where it lands.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because every lithography substitute the research examined is being disproved for leading-edge volume production over the horizon, while computational lithography lowers the cost of adopting advanced nodes
    • the partial bypasses are real and are why this is not A.
  • Substitutes disproved: Canon nanoimprint (particle defects, unsuited to 3nm and 2nm logic)
    • the xLight free-electron laser (less than 50 percent chance of commercial deployment in US fabs by 2030 per Jefferies, and a source that would have to be integrated into ASML systems)
    • Substrate's X-ray tool (a decade-long gap in subsystem maturity).
  • High-NA deferral: the disruption that has bitten: TSMC has deferred High-NA past 2029 (A14 and later), Samsung has deferred its 1.4nm node two years to 2029 and mass-production High-NA to about 2030
    • Intel is the only high-volume adopter today (18A layers dual-qualified on the EXE platform, 15 July 2026).
    • Consensus carries only 1,400 EURm of High-NA revenue in each of the next two years.
  • Partial bypasses: 3D DRAM limiting EUV layers
    • advanced packaging shifting capital to deposition and etch
    • Low-NA double patterning delaying High-NA.
  • Direction for the case: a tailwind on the core (nothing displaces EUV over three years)
    • a headwind on the High-NA leg of the long-term story.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the supply side is not a cost problem (ASML pushes cost-down onto sub-tier suppliers, who report a margin squeeze) but a volume ceiling: Carl Zeiss SMT optics are the single tightest bottleneck in the EUV flow, there is zero fungibility between High-NA and Low-NA optics, and low-NA lead times are 12 to 18 months with High-NA at 18 to 24.
  • Raw materials: helium (Ras Laffan strikes, March 2026; one week of working inventory) and China's rare-earth controls, suspended to 10 November 2026.
  • Reading for the case: a constraint that supports pricing and visibility (it is why 2027 is sold out) while denying the 2028 upside beyond about 110 systems
    • direction mixed.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the macro force acting on ASML more than on the universe is the funding of AI capital spending (Jefferies: consensus memory forecasts ex-China imply about USD 2 trillion of US AI capital spending by 2027, which faces funding challenges if AI monetisation disappoints; hyperscaler capital spending projected above USD 1 trillion by 2027), a two-sided exposure the universe does not share.
  • Rates and currency: second-order
    • euro billing shields translation, a 10 percent move in foreign currencies moved 2025 net income by only 1.6 million EUR, and the transaction risk sits with customers' purchasing power.
  • Backdrop: global GDP growth of about 3 to 3.5 percent for 2026 is neutral
    • the compound scenario in which a DRAM downturn coincides with China localisation is judged low near term and rising beyond 24 months.
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because one other external dynamic is material: the physical bottleneck has moved from tools to cleanroom construction and skilled construction labour (Sunwah Kingsway, 23 July 2026) and, at home, to housing for thousands of new specialists in the Netherlands, so that revenue recognition can slip on customer readiness even when tools are built.
  • Two-sided: it is why software productivity upgrades sell at 100 percent gross margin
    • it is a timing risk to the 2027 to 2028 ramp rather than a demand risk.

  • The artificial-intelligence build-out has lifted lithography intensity at logic and memory at the same time, and ASML, the only maker of EUV tools and the maker of most DUV tools, is supply-limited into 2027 with customers pre-paying to hold their place.
  • The company answered on 15 July 2026 with a guidance raise 11.4 percent above consensus (43 to 45 billion EUR of 2026 revenue; 54 to 56 percent gross margin) and a 30 percent capacity build for 2027 with another 30 percent under investigation for 2028.
  • The change has run the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: the choices and actions are made, the operating metrics and financial statements show it (Q2 2026 revenue plus 21.3 percent, gross margin 54.0 percent, EPS plus 29 percent), and the sell-side has re-based (FY2028 consensus EPS mean up 98.6 percent in twelve months).
  • The shares have followed, up 86.7 percent in twelve months to 1,507.4 EUR (2026-09-22), yet the multiple has not: at 23.8 times consensus earnings twenty-four months forward the stock stands at its own ten-year median, because the market, like us, is treating the inflection as cyclical.
  • Our modal path puts EPS at about 39.0 EUR (FY2026), 51.5 EUR (FY2027) and 62 EUR (FY2028), near consensus for 2026 and below it after, then a plateau at about 68 EUR in FY2029 as the capacity built through 2028 is absorbed
    • consensus has 91.64 EUR for FY2029 on eight brokers, above the company's own 2030 revenue ceiling.
  • On our path the twenty-four-month-forward earnings the market will be pricing in eighteen months are only about 10 percent above the figure it prices today, so the 'Modal return' is single digits (2.9 percent a year over eighteen months; 6.0 percent over three years) and does not clear the 20 percent bar
    • it clears the bar only on the consensus 2029 path, which we judge possible (about 30 percent), not probable.
  • The business and the foundations are the best in the universe
    • the case is compelling as a business and not compelling as a return at this price.
  • The entry at which it becomes both is about 1,200 EUR, or the current price once the 2028 orders and a raised 2030 frame are in hand.
Setup verdict: The sorting-box verdict: primary 'Target Setup', any secondary, and the mechanism tying Thesis and Foundations to the pattern (Part 2, E 3, BB 4)
  • The primary 'Target Setup' is 'Demand Inflection', fitted crisply: the mandatory demand explosion is sector-level and evidenced by customers' committed capital with two years' visibility
    • the position is dominant and captures the incremental demand disproportionately because EUV is where the intensity lands and China is excluded from it
    • the structure and behaviour are oligopolistic
    • the fixed-cost leverage is demonstrated at a 60 percent drop-through
    • only one-to-many scalability is a C, because a scanner is bespoke hardware.
  • There is no secondary.
  • The mechanism tying the 'Investment Thesis' and the 'Foundations' to the pattern is that a monopoly at the gate of a funded build-out converts each unit of incremental demand to revenue at a rising price and to profit at a rising margin, and the customers finance the capacity themselves.
  • The pattern's known hazard is also this case's: the framework treats an inflection as cyclical until proven structural and warns that growth above 40 percent reverts, and the fit is read at the last layer of the chain, after the estimates and the price have moved.
  • 'Compounder' fits at C, a correctly-modelled high-quality business
    • 'Major Corporate Change' at C, a response to demand misfiled as transformation
    • the two clearing patterns and 'Trough on Trough' at F.

Congruence: The congruence judgement: does the whole hang together across past, business, value chain and market environment; where ratings disagree, why (Part 4, E 3, BB 4)
  • The whole hangs together, and the one place the ratings disagree is explained.
  • With the past: the thesis is a continuation of a decade in which revenue rose from 11,820 EURm (FY2019) to 32,667 EURm (FY2025) and EPS from 6.16 to 24.73 EUR through two export-control regimes and a 2024 digestion year
    • the base rate inside that record, growth years in pairs and triples followed by a digestion, is also the reason we model a 2029 plateau, so the past supports both the thesis and our caution about its extrapolation.
  • With the business: the thesis relies only on strengths the business demonstrably has (the monopoly, the pricing power, the operating leverage, the installed-base annuity) and not on High-NA, the one strength not yet proven.
  • With the value chain: the thesis sits with the demand force, the customers' funding and the suppliers' captivity, and against only the regulators
    • it needs the regulatory force not to tighten by statute within the horizon, not to reverse.
  • With the market: here the ratings diverge and the divergence is the case.
  • 'Foundations' A and 'Investment Setup' A sit beside 'Investment Thesis' C and 'Investment Case' C, and earnings momentum A sits beside technical momentum C and valuation D.
  • The market has rewarded the pattern (equipment peers up 58 to 200 percent over twelve months) and ASML (plus 86.7 percent) while holding ASML's multiple at its ten-year median as earnings doubled
    • that is the market treating the inflection as cyclical, which is exactly our reading, so the disagreement between growth and multiple is explained rather than unexplained.
  • The ratings do not contradict each other: the A ratings say what to own, the C ratings say at what price, and the D on the multiple says the price is full against our path and fair against consensus.
  • Where we would be wrong is if the market is right to hold the median multiple because growth is structural, in which case the C ratings are too low, not the A ratings too high.

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Customer pre-funding
    • Customers pay non-refundable down payments to hold capacity and finance the build.
    • It is why 2027 is sold out and why the demand evidence is cash rather than narrative.
    • The balance has risen to a record and is the first thing that would reverse if orders were deferred.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Monopoly structure
    • One supplier of EUV inside a five-firm oligopoly that prices for margin.
    • It is what converts incremental demand into revenue at a rising price.
    • Share keeps drifting toward the largest and most advantaged player.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Sole-source optics
    • One supplier makes the lenses and mirrors, and its failure would stop the company.
    • The risk is low in probability, catastrophic in magnitude and stable in trend.
    • It is not the inverse of a Driver; it is the shape of the value chain.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Customer concentration
    • Four customers are more than half of revenue and one region is a quarter.
    • A single-customer capital-spending freeze or a Taiwan event would hit the case at once.
    • The trend is stable; the exposure is the mirror of the co-investment moat.
Business
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Installed-base annuity
    • Service and software revenue grows with the fleet independent of the unit cycle.
    • Its software upgrades sold at full margin are the reason the gross margin moved a year early.
    • It is repeatable while cleanrooms are constrained and cyclical when they are not.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Fixed-cost leverage
    • Operating expenses grow slower than sales and the company expects a high drop-through on incremental revenue.
    • It is what turns the demand explosion into margin expansion.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Software-upgrade cyclicality
    • A part of the gross margin rests on productivity upgrades that customers buy because they cannot get cleanrooms.
    • As fabs finish, that demand eases and the margin gives some back.
    • It is the inverse of the annuity Driver at its cyclical edge; probable in part, moderate in size, worsening as the build-out matures.
Leadership and management
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Capacity decision and execution
    • Management committed to a large capacity build on customer money and shifted priority to shipment execution.
    • The 2028 decision is the next act of the same kind.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Delayering during the ramp
    • The organisational change was scaled back and deferred while a large ramp is under way.
    • The risk is slower savings and execution friction rather than a break in the case.
External
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: China and export policy
    • The China share must more than double in the second half to meet guidance, under a licence regime that already binds.
    • It decides whether the near-term guidance is met and whether a statutory ban is priced.
    • It bears on both the case being right and the share price.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: AI capital spending
    • Customers' capital-expenditure raises and memory long-term agreements are the demand force itself.
    • It is strengthening in the data and two-sided in sentiment.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: High-NA adoption
    • A second product cycle that is live at one customer and deferred at the two largest.
    • It matters for the long-term story more than for the three-year case.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: A statutory ban
    • The MATCH Act would remove a material share of revenue and servicing by law.
    • Passage within the horizon is unlikely but bipartisan; the magnitude is large; the trend is toward tighter controls.
    • It is the tail of the China Key Driver and threatens both the share price and the case.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Memory cycle softening
    • A DRAM downturn from the second half of 2027 would coincide with the year our path plateaus.
    • Its probability is judged low near term and rising beyond two years; it is a share-price risk that becomes a case risk if it meets China localisation.
Internal
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Value-based pricing
    • Prices rise in real terms on new models with zero discounting, framed around customer productivity.
    • It is the mechanism by which the monopoly captures the demand at a rising price.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Buyback pace
    • The programme is authorised and cash-funded and lowers the share count a little each year.
    • It is a small, certain contribution to the return.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Capacity at the top of the cycle
    • The company is building for a 2028 that is investigated, not ordered, and its own filings warn of write-downs if demand fails to match.
    • Probability is possible, magnitude large, trend unknown until the 2028 decision; it is the inverse of the capacity Driver.
Key operating and financial metrics
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Low-NA EUV units and capacity fill
    • The number of systems shipped and whether the 2027 and 2028 capacity is ordered in full.
    • It is the single datum that would move our 2029 view and the thesis rating.
    • It bears on both the case being right and the share price.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Installed Base Management revenue
    • The quarterly run-rate of service and upgrade revenue tracks the annuity and the software cycle.
    • A sharp sequential fall would confirm the bear reading of the margin.

    • The optics partner's output caps how fast capacity can be added.
    • It supports pricing and visibility and denies upside surprise.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Cleanroom and housing timing

    • A timing risk to the ramp, not a demand risk; probable in small measure, modest in size.
Key financial statement items
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Gross margin
    • The margin carries the mix shift to EUV, value-based pricing and the software-upgrade component.
    • It decides whether the earnings path holds through 2028 and gives back only a little in 2029.
    • It bears on both the case being right and the share price.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Free cash flow and working capital
    • Cash is lumpy because down payments arrive with bookings and inventory builds with the ramp.
    • It is not the case, but it is where a deferral would show first.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Drop-through below EBITDA
    • Higher amortisation, a higher tax rate or a slower buyback would trim earnings per share by a few percent.

  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Upgrades on the 2028 decision

    • It is the one live trigger for a further upgrade leg.

    • Consensus for 2029 sits above the company's own long-range ceiling, so the estimates carry a structural re-basing management has not yet described.
    • The risk is that 2029 plateaus and the estimates come down; probable on our path, large in size, and the trend is flat estimates after a steep rise.
    • It is a share-price risk first and a case risk if the plateau arrives early.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Flat revisions
    • The upgrade wave has crested and the one-month moves are near zero.
    • Momentum that has stopped rising is the first half of a market catch-up pattern.
Cash return, earnings growth, re-rating
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Earnings growth
    • Revenue and margin carry the return and are the components investors extrapolate.
    • On the forward footing the market prices, the growth left over the next eighteen months is single digits.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Cash return
    • Dividends and buybacks add about a point a year, small and certain.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: A cycle-driven de-rating
    • The multiple is industry- and cycle-driven, sits at its own long-run median, and can fall to its trough bands on unchanged earnings.
    • Probable in part as forward growth decelerates, large in size, and the peers' multiples are more likely to compress than expand.
    • A share-price risk, not a case risk.
