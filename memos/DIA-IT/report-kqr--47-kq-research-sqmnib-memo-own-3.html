Section 2 – Installed Base Dynamics and Migration Plan

Active Installed Base Dynamics

1.
Extensive base: DiaSorin commands an active installed base of more than 7,000 LIAISON immunoassay systems globally as of 2025. Management notes this extensive footprint is critical for securing recurring consumable revenues through a locked-system model. Upgrading this base with the next-generation LIAISON XL starting in 1H27 represents the core commercial priority.
2.
Broad exposure: Morgan Stanley research in 2020 estimated that approximately two-thirds of immunoassay instruments are placed under reagent-rental contracts. Under this structure, DiaSorin provides the analyzer with no upfront cost to the laboratory in exchange for multi-year committed consumable purchase agreements. This contract structure directly influences future capital intensity during platform transitions.
3.
Direct purchases: Morgan Stanley noted that approximately one-third of customers choose to buy instruments outright rather than accepting reagent rentals. Direct equipment sales represent roughly 10% of total company revenues, with the remaining 90% generated by recurring reagents and services. This mix provides a stable base of fully amortized older systems that do not require immediate replacement capital.
4.
Tactical positioning: DiaSorin strategically deploys the smaller LIAISON XS analyzer to target lower-volume hospital laboratories, particularly in the United States and European markets. This specialized strategy allows the company to penetrate physician offices and smaller clinics where total laboratory automation is unnecessary. The XS platform helps defend high-margin specialty testing niches without requiring the intensive capital deployment of larger systems.
5.
High connectivity: DiaSorin reported during its 2026 Investor Day that approximately 25% of its LIAISON installed base is already connected to laboratory automation tracks. The high-throughput segments of the market increasingly require total lab automation integrations to remain competitive. Managing these automated accounts represents a critical retention hurdle during the upcoming system migration.
6.
Sticky customer: DiaSorin leverages its extensive specialty testing menu to secure high customer retention rates during platform migrations. By offering unique diagnostic assays that larger competitors lack, the company maintains pricing power during contract renewals. This specialty positioning reduces the risk of defection when transitioning laboratories to the next-generation analyzer.

Section 3 – Historical Precedent: The LIAISON to LIAISON XL Transition (2010–2015)

Section summary
  1. The historical transition from LIAISON to LIAISON XL provides a clear precedent of revenue acceleration.
  2. Upgrading customers to the LIAISON XL platform delivered a twenty to thirty percent top- line expansion.
  3. Margins faced compression due to elevated depreciation charges as the installed base grew rapidly.

Analyzer Placements and Rollout

1.
Rapid rollout: DiaSorin initiated the commercial launch of the next-generation LIAISON XL platform in late 2010 after receiving the CE mark. The company placed 128 units in 2011 during its initial launch phase across Europe and Australia. These placements successfully demonstrated high customer interest in specialty panels such as HIV and Hepatitis. This rollout laid the foundation for a sustainable long-term consumable revenue stream.
2.
Accelerated expansion: DiaSorin expanded its placements significantly in 2012 by installing 477 new LIAISON XL systems. This expansion brought the platform's total installed base to 605 units in major global territories. The rapid adoption was driven by launching the platform in high-volume regions like the United States and China. This successful strategy protected high-margin specialty testing accounts from competitive threats.
3.
Strong momentum: DiaSorin continued to drive instrument installations and grew the LIAISON XL base to over 1,500 systems by September 2014. The overall installed base of the LIAISON family reached nearly 6,000 units by the end of 2014. This steady progress confirmed that LIAISON XL met laboratory needs for medium and large diagnostic facilities. This expansion supported solid consumable sales growth across key geographies.
4.
Massive replacement: DiaSorin accelerated its substitution program in 2015 by placing over 620 new LIAISON XL systems. The company completed the year with over 2,300 LIAISON XL units installed globally. Management actively de-installed 160 older LIAISON systems during the year to upgrade customers to the newer platform. This transition secured long-term customer relationships for another five to seven years.

Reagent Economics and Revenue

5.
Positive pricing: DiaSorin experienced a significant increase in reagent revenue per analyzer after upgrading customers to the newer platform. Upgrading an older system to a LIAISON XL typically generated twenty to thirty percent more revenue per box. This increase occurred because customers agreed to adopt broader specialty menus and guarantee top-line growth. This positive pricing dynamic helped offset the financial impact of platform cannibalization.
6.
Productive menus: DiaSorin increased its average annual revenue per installed system to approximately 81,000 euros in 2010. This productivity level was up from 76,000 euros recorded in 2009. The improvement reflected optimization of the installed base and high test yields from Vitamin D assays. This expanding menu of specialty assays supported stable group revenues despite competitive pressures.
7.
Sustained margins: DiaSorin reported net revenues of 404.5 million euros in 2010 with a gross margin of 70.4%. Gross margins improved to 71.3% in 2011 as higher-margin CLIA assays grew. However, margins declined to 67.3% by 2014 due to higher LIAISON XL depreciation charges and product mix changes. This margin pressure shows that platform upgrades demand disciplined capital management.
8.
Elevated capital: DiaSorin maintained stable capital expenditures of 28.9 million euros in 2011 compared to 28.4 million euros in 2010. However, depreciation charges rose subsequently as the placed instrument base expanded. This trend reflects the capital intensity of placing systems under rental models. This elevated asset base increased long-term depreciation and put near-term pressure on operating margins.

The agreement required DiaSorin to commit to minimum purchase volumes over the contract term. However, both companies agreed these commitments were significantly below typical capital investments required for independent manufacturing. This structure helped protect DiaSorin from large changes in net invested capital.
10.

In 2013, Stratec SE completed two specialized versions, including Laboratory Automation System compatible systems to dock with Inpeco tracks. This engineering collaboration helped DiaSorin penetrate highthroughput core laboratories.
11.
Current expectations: Stratec SE reported in November 2026 that its next-generation immunoassay platform has transitioned to series production design transfer. This upcoming system is designed as a drop-in replacement that extends the customer's addressable market through higher throughput. It is expected to help DiaSorin defend its high-volume diagnostic accounts starting in 2027.
12.
Broker outlook: Deutsche Bank Research reported in October 2026 that DiaSorin's planned launches of the new LIAISON XL and molecular systems should provide a tailwind. The broker noted that volatile customer ordering patterns and weakness in service parts represent ongoing risks. These variables could create a highly back-end-loaded revenue profile for the manufacturer. This potential volatility may delay the positive financial impact of the platform transition.
