Section 2 – Political, Consumer, and Regulatory Stance on Inflation Indexation

Section summary
  1. Greece's elevated inflation environment of autumn 2026 has intensified political focus on consumer living costs.
  2. While consumer protests have escalated, formal regulatory caps on telecom indexation have not yet been implemented.

Macroeconomic Pressure and Social Backlash

1.
Pressured consumer environment: Greek consumer price inflation surged to 5.1% in September 2026 compared to 3.7% in August 2026 according to the Hellenic Statistical Authority. This sharp acceleration placed the nation among the highest inflation rates within the European currency bloc. The Bank of Greece reported on 23 October 2026 that services and energy are the primary drivers of this inflationary pressure. Such cost-of-living strain could restrict household budgets and affect telecom pricing power.
2.
Rising public discontent: High inflation triggered significant public resistance across major Greek metropolitan areas. Student organisations and trade unions staged nationwide demonstrations in Athens on 21 October 2026 to protest against rising costs. Activists in Syntagma Square demanded immediate government action regarding expensive housing and general consumer price hikes. If these social tensions escalate further, political pressure may force the administration to intervene in private sector contract adjustments.
3.
Direct regulatory precedent: Greek authorities have a history of implementing strict market interventions to protect consumer purchasing power. The centre-right New Democracy government introduced the household basket in November 2022 to regulate essential goods prices. Furthermore, Development Minister Takis Theodorikakos confirmed on 30 June 2026 that tackling high living costs remains a critical priority. These precedents indicate that the government possesses the political will to cap private corporate margins if pricing disputes threaten social stability.
4.
Elevating budget pressures: Greek economic performance in 2026 remains highly vulnerable to external price shocks in international energy markets. The Bank of Greece reported on 22 October 2026 that Middle East conflicts have disrupted energy flows, boosting domestic fuel prices. This energy cost spike directly squeezes household savings, which dropped to negative levels in recent quarters. Consequently, these energy cost increases leave consumers with less disposable income, which may dampen demand for premium services.
5.
Contrary evidence: Macroeconomic resilience remains strong in the Greek private sector, potentially muting the political drive for immediate telecom price caps. The Bank of Greece reported on 23 October 2026 that non-financial corporate savings rose to 8.1% of GDP in the first quarter of 2026. This robust performance indicates that inflationary pressures have strengthened firms' overall pricing power. Consequently, the government may avoid aggressive price interventions as strong corporate performance supports economic expansion.

Regulatory Oversight and European Precedents

6.
Changing legal frameworks: European legal developments could reshape contract indexation across the entire Greek telecommunications sector. The Court of Justice of the European Union issued a landmark ruling on 10 September 2026 concerning inflation pricing at Vodafone Germany. While Greek regulatory bodies have not yet explicitly applied this case to local contracts, the precedent challenges automatic tariff adjustments. This legal shift could eventually restrict Greek operators from implementing automatic backbook price increases without allowing penalty-free termination.
7.
Sector pricing dynamics: Inflation-linked pricing strategies have supported the financial performance of major domestic telecom operators. Morgan Stanley reported on 21 April 2026 that all three major mobile operators implemented indexation over the past year. This coordinated pricing behavior helped protect mobile margins against severe cost-push inflation. However, any regulatory action to ban indexation would disrupt this stable trend and hurt long-term average revenue growth.
8.
Peer pricing initiatives: Competitor actions show that price indexation has become standard practice in the Greek telecommunications market. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 17 March 2026 that Nova and Vodafone Greece raised prices in line with consumer inflation for two consecutive years. This consistency allowed these operators to maintain profitability despite rising operational and energy costs. Consequently, Hellenic Telecom faces significant pressure to match peer pricing moves to sustain its relative market valuation advantages.
9.
Tightening consumer oversight: Regulatory bodies in Greece maintain active supervision over unfair contract terms and pricing policies. Vodafone Greece submitted consumer regulation memorandums to EETT following hearings regarding retail customer complaints. Furthermore, Greek legislation demands standardisation of billing documents and pre-contractual offers to protect consumer rights. If EETT or the Consumer Ombudsman launch formal investigations, telecom operators face severe compliance risks that could depress mobile service revenues.
10.
Divergent regulatory policies: European regulators exhibit varying approaches to inflation-linked retail pricing adjustments. In Germany, regulator Bundesnetzagentur allowed lighter fixed-line rules but scrutinised back- book inflation increases. Conversely, the United Kingdom banned inflation-based retail contracts, forcing operators to state price changes in clear currency terms. These shifting regional policies suggest that Greek authorities might follow restrictive European precedents, which would limit retail price indexation flexibility.

Complex antitrust challenges: The Greek regulatory landscape exhibits a high level of scrutiny regarding market power and anti-competitive practices. The Hellenic Competition Commission noted that major infrastructure operators have previously faced antitrust investigations for selling below cost. This intense scrutiny of dominant market players suggests that OTE must tread carefully when coordinating pricing adjustments with competitors. Therefore, aggressive pricing strategies could attract costly regulatory litigation and limit pricing flexibility over time.

Rising travel inflation: Consumer price trends are further complicated by the rapid growth of services inflation in tourism-related sectors. The Bank of Greece noted on 12 October 2026 that surging hotel prices and airfares driven by strong tourist demand have accelerated HICP inflation. This tourism-driven price increase impacts local consumer perceptions of inflation, even as underlying core items remain stable. Consequently, the public debate around the cost of living could intensify, and prompt further political scrutiny of telecom contract terms.

Restrictive pricing frameworks: Comparative regulatory models in adjacent utility sectors show a trend toward standardized consumer protection rules in Greece. The Regulatory Authority for Waste, Energy and Water enforces prescriptive rules requiring suppliers to offer fixed-price contracts. Section 3 – EETT Decisions on Fixed Wholesale Tariffs

Section summary
  1. EETT's upcoming wholesale tariff rebalancing directly reshapes Hellenic Telecom's fixed-line margins by incentivizing rapid fiber migration.
  2. Commercial volume discount agreements between major operators have proactively mitigated the impact of formal regulatory price cuts.

Regulatory Pricing Redirection

1.
Imminent tariff restructuring: The Hellenic Telecommunications and Post Commission (EETT) is finalized to execute a significant structural rebalancing of fixed-line wholesale pricing. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 24 April 2026 that the regulator's upcoming proposals align with market expectations, specifically mandating a 10% to 13% reduction in OTE's wholesale charge for Fiber-to-the-Home (FTTH) connectivity alongside a 4% to 5% increase in its wholesale copper tariff. This dual-track strategy is designed by European and national authorities to accelerate the migration of households from legacy copper lines to high-speed fiber. Consequently, this pricing pivot alters the economic incentive structure for alternative retail operators, making copper-based services relatively more expensive while improving the affordability of premium FTTH products. Consultation and execution timelines: The procedural framework underpinning these tariff shifts has progressed through extensive regulatory reviews and public hearings. Vodafone Group's regulatory filings in November 2025 indicated that EETT launched its first public consultation on the Next Generation Access Bottom-Up Long-Run Incremental Cost (NGA BULRIC+) model update in January 2024, followed by a second consultative draft phase running from July to October 2025. This draft model codified the expected 5% copper fee increase and 10% FTTH price decrease, while keeping the pre-approved commercial discount agreements unaffected. Because these regulatory changes establish a predictable pricing envelope, OTE can confidently project its infrastructure yields as it works toward its target of passing 2.4 million premises with fiber by the end of 2026.
2.
Contrary evidence: Underlying wholesale volumes remain highly resilient despite the downward regulatory price pressure, preventing severe revenue erosion. Hellenic Telecom's Q2 2026 earnings transcript revealed that the company registered a new record of 48,000 wholesale FTTH additions in the second quarter of 2026 alone. This strong volume momentum indicates that competitor migrations are occurring fast enough to offset unitprice declines. Consequently, OTE's aggregate national wholesale profitability remains insulated from severe shocks because unprecedented physical fiber adoption compensates for the mandated tariff reductions.

OTE Positioning and Bilateral Agreements

3.
Strategic volume discounts: Hellenic Telecom has successfully insulated its wholesale business from regulatory volatility by signing long-term bilateral volume agreements with its main competitors. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 20 September 2024 that Vodafone Greece and Nova officially signed up to OTE's FTTH wholesale pricing framework, which offers progressive unit-price discounts of up to 28% in exchange for binding commitment thresholds. These commercial agreements received formal approval from both the European Commission and EETT in mid-2024. By proactively lowering commercial wholesale rates below the nominal regulated benchmarks, OTE secured competitor volumes early and guaranteed that Vodafone and Nova would continue routing their retail subscribers through OTE's high-capacity physical infrastructure. - Earnings Calls Erosion mitigation metrics: While OTE's legacy national wholesale revenue continues to decline due to competitors expanding their own physical footprints, these strategic bilateral agreements provide a powerful buffer. Hellenic Telecom's chief financial officer noted during the Q2 2025 earnings call that the annual erosion of national wholesale revenue has stabilized at approximately €15 million. However, migrating copper wholesale customers to FTTH under the volume framework generates a highly favorable mix shift. Management confirmed that transitioning a wholesale access line from legacy copper to FTTH yields a substantial 10% higher average revenue per user (ARPU) for OTE. - Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls Broadband infrastructure comparison: Hellenic Telecom's dominant market position in physical infrastructure acts as a primary defensive moat against competing wholesale platforms. Deutsche Bank Research calculated that OTE passed over 2.1 million homes in the first quarter of 2026, representing more than 70% of the active FTTH lines in Greece. By comparison, Vodafone Greece targeted 500,000 homes passed in 2025, and Nova's independent fiber rollout remains highly localized. This vast geographical disparity means that competitors must rely heavily on OTE's wholesale access to service their retail broadband subscribers outside their limited proprietary footprints, supporting OTE's long-term wholesale revenue stability and cash flows.

Fiber Migration and Competitive Stances

4.
Government subsidy acceleration: The pace of wholesale fiber migration has been heavily catalyzed by targeted state-sponsored funding mechanisms. In November 2024, the Greek government initiated €180 million in subsidy programs, including the Gigabit Voucher scheme, which provides retail customers with up to €200 to cover connection costs and monthly fees for speeds of at least 250 Mbps. Deutsche Bank Research reported that these subsidies, combined with OTE's volume discounts, drove total wholesale FTTH lines in service up by over 50% in 2025. These state programs effectively transfer customers off low-ARPU copper lines onto high-speed OTE fiber networks, improving retail margins across the sector while boosting OTE's high-margin national wholesale infrastructure utilization rates. Competitor resistance and alternative networks: While OTE's wholesale pricing framework has successfully captured the majority of the market, alternative infrastructure initiatives continue to pose mid-term threats. Public Power Corporation (PPC), Greece's incumbent electricity utility, is executing a €640 million investment plan to build an alternative aerial FTTH network over its legacy distribution poles. However, PPC has struggled to attract wholesale volume because OTE's pre-approved 28% volume discounts make wholesaling from OTE substantially cheaper for Vodafone and Nova. United Group, the parent company of Nova, publicly described PPC's initial wholesale pricing proposals as unacceptable, cementing Nova's operational focus on OTE's network. Margins and financial rebalancing: The transition from copper to fiber wholesale pricing is structurally margin- accretive for Hellenic Telecom over a multi-year horizon. Morgan Stanley outlined in October 2025 that while wholesale revenues face near-term pressure as competitors build out their own networks, OTE's fixed-line retail recovery and high-margin national wholesale stabilization will offset these headwinds. The structural shift reduces OTE's localized maintenance costs, as fiber networks require significantly less ongoing operational expenditure compared to legacy copper infrastructure. This operational transition ensures that OTE can sustain its strong EBITDA margins and support its premium.

shareholder capital return targets over the next 18 months. Other side of the story: Despite OTE's current success in locking in competitors, aggressive infrastructure overbuilding by Vodafone Greece in metropolitan areas could accelerate future wholesale revenue erosion. Hellenic Telecom's management admitted in July 2026 that while Vodafone's migration pace remains strong, some long-term pressure on wholesale revenue will inevitably occur as Vodafone overbuilds OTE's footprint. If competitors successfully expand their proprietary networks to pass a larger portion of their own retail bases, OTE's wholesale utilization rates will fall. This would restrict OTE's capacity to monetize its extensive capital expenditure on rural and semi-urban fiber rollouts, ultimately squeezing long-term earnings per share.

Section 5 – Peer Competitor Actions and Retail Consumer Dynamics

  1. Retail price increases by peer operators have provided robust air cover for sector-wide tariff adjustments in Greece.
  2. Greek consumers continue to prioritize premium network quality and service bundling over low-cost standalone alternatives.
  3. Severe customer churn and regulatory pushback have remained immaterial across the competitive landscape in Greece.

Competitor Pricing Strategies

1.
Standardizing retail indexation: Peer operators Nova and Vodafone Greece successfully implemented automatic retail price adjustments in line with inflation for two consecutive years. This consistent pricing practice supported sector-wide mobile margins against rising energy and operating expenses. The coordinated strategy suggests that Greek operators will likely maintain inflation-linked tariff adjustments during 2027 to offset persistent operational headwinds.
2.
Bullish pricing air cover: Vodafone Greece provided strategic pricing air cover for its competitors by raising its retail tariffs in early 2026. The price increases targeted fixed wireless access services, mobile data sharing, and entry-level pay-TV offerings. This pricing action allowed rival operators to implement supportive retail price adjustments without experiencing severe competitive market share erosion.
3.
Rational competitive environment: Competitive dynamics in the Greek telecom sector remain highly rational because major operators prioritize value over destructive price wars. Nova attempted to compete aggressively on price in early 2023 but quickly course-corrected toward premium services. This shift confirms that Greek telecom consumers consistently prioritize high network quality over low-cost standalone alternatives.
4.
Contrary evidence: Consumer organizations in Germany launched a major class action lawsuit challenging retail price indexation practices. The Federation of German Consumer Organisations initiated litigation in November 2023 to invalidate Vodafone Germany's price hikes.
