1. The judgement in brief

JudgementThe first-half numbers are mostly known; tomorrow's share reaction will be set by what is not yet known. Four things are genuinely new on the day: first-half operating profit and margin (the trading update gave none), what management says about data centre revenue in the second half and into financial year 2028, cash and working capital, and the wording of the full-year profit outlook.

  • The bar on profit: the market expects full-year underlying operating profit of about $153m, the top of the $145.9m to $153.3m range the company quoted on 16 October.
    • No first-half operating profit consensus is published that I could find. Our own memo's estimate is $75m to $77m, a margin of 10.6% to 10.8%, against $57.2m and 9.8% a year earlier.
    • Why it matters: last year's interims (12 November 2025) beat a $52m profit expectation by about 10% and the shares rose 13.3%, although the October update had already given revenue. Profit is the one headline the update did not pre-announce, so it can still surprise either way.
  • The bar on the second half: revenue consensus for financial year 2027 is $1,411m, which leaves about $701m for the second half after the first half's "at least $710m".
    • That is exactly what the company guided: second-half revenue "broadly similar to the first half, after allowing for the revenue brought forward" (about $10m).
    • Why it matters: consensus revenue has no slack in it. Any hint that the second half runs below the first half is a downgrade; any hint that it runs above (Volex's second half has historically been 10% to 13% bigger than its first) is an upgrade.
  • "In line" has stopped being safe. The shares rose 8% to 17% on every beat-and-raise update from April 2025 to August 2026, but fell 5.0% on the full-year results of 25 June and 11.6% on the "towards the upper end" update of 19 October.
    • Why it matters: a result that merely confirms guidance has fallen on each of the last two occasions. The market wants evidence the data centre line has stopped falling, not confirmation of a year it already believes.
  • The single biggest fragility is one customer. The largest customer was 18.7% of financial year 2026 revenue (8.0% a year earlier), named by Jefferies as Amazon, and is about the size of the data centre line.
    • Volex is a secondary supplier, priced 5% to 8% below the first-tier suppliers, going into a 2026 to 2028 window when hyperscale designs are re-awarded (higher power, faster links, optics), with no optical capability (our memo of 4 November).
    • Why it matters: the company does not report data centre as a segment, so tomorrow's tone on this line is the only read the market gets until June 2027.
  • Threats the results cannot answer sit either side of the print.
    • The US data centre policy shock: Texas's governor-elect pledged on 4 November to halt new data centre approvals, and the Nasdaq fell 5.5% on 5 November; Volex fell 4.6% that day on 3.3 times its 50-day average volume.
    • Nvidia reports on Tuesday 17 November after the US close, so Wednesday 18 November is the first Volex session to react.
    • Our memo measured Volex's beta to the European data centre theme at 1.77, so sector news moves it more than its own news.
  • The positives the market may be under-weighting.
    • The supply chain read-across is strong: Amphenol's IT datacom sales rose 117% in its third quarter, TE Connectivity's data network orders rose about 70% in its year, Vertiv's orders more than doubled, and BizLink's October sales rose 80% year on year.
    • Management has not missed an earnings forecast since 2016 and guides conservatively by habit.
    • The shares trade on about 11 times two-year-forward earnings at 540p (our memo's 12.3 times at 590p, scaled), against a mean broker target of about 729p.
  • Sell-side: six Buys, one Hold, no Sell. Estimates rose strongly from June to mid-October and have not moved since 20 October; the brokers need no change to stay at Buy, so the near-term risk is drift and tone, not a wave of downgrades.

ContextWhere the shares stand going in. The close on 10 November was 540p, 22.7% below the 6 October closing high of 699p. Sharecast's price box showed 560p, up 3.9%, at 09:14 UK today. The 200-day moving average is about 560p. The stock is below its other main moving averages, and its relative strength percentile fell from 97 on 9 October to 50 on 10 November; its Industry relative strength is 26.

  • Upgrade cadence has slowed: January 2026 raised, March 2026 "significantly" ahead, August 2026 "ahead", October 2026 only "towards the upper end". Our memo reads this as a maturing cycle: expect a 1% to 3% beat on financial year 2027 profit, not financial year 2026's 4% to 11%.

The policy shock of 4 and 5 November

  • What happened: after the 3 November midterms, Texas's governor-elect Gina Hinojosa pledged an immediate moratorium on new data centre approvals until the legislature passes new rules. Texas holds about a quarter of planned US data centre capacity (Goldman Sachs, cited by Reuters). New York had already imposed a statewide moratorium, and Governor Abbott had paused Texas approvals from August.
  • The market reaction: on 5 November the Nasdaq fell 5.5% and the S&P 500 4.4%; semiconductor indices fell about 12% and Nvidia about 9%. Neocloud operators fell about 21% in four sessions, and CoreWeave's 2031 bonds traded at a 16.3% yield.
  • The partial recovery: chips rebounded on 6 November and the Nasdaq 100 rose 3.3% on 9 November, although Nvidia fell that day.
  • Why it matters for Volex (my judgement): a moratorium hits new projects, not the hyperscale campuses already under way, so the first-order effect on Volex's 2026 to 2027 revenue is small. The second-order effect is the multiple: the market is now pricing a shorter or lumpier build cycle, and Volex trades as a high-beta expression of that theme.

What comes next for the theme

  • Nvidia reports on Tuesday 17 November after the US close; consensus revenue is about $108.8bn and earnings per share $2.47. Its tone on 2027 demand and power architecture will move Volex on Wednesday 18 November.
  • The technology question: Nvidia's 800-volt direct-current rack architecture reduces copper at row level by about 45% from 2027 (Maybank, via our Key Question Analysis). Experts expect copper to stay dominant for short links for a few hardware generations, with co-packaged optics a threat after about 2030.

10. The bull and the bear reading of an in-line result

Suppose first-half operating profit lands at about $76m, the outlook stays "towards the upper end", and data centre is described as "normalising". Both readings below are plausible, and the share reaction will depend on which one the call reinforces.

The bull reading

  • Financial year 2027 is effectively locked by a management that has beaten every forecast since 2016; the profit guide implies the second half is no weaker than the first.
  • Margin progress is real (about 10.7% against 9.8%), driven by mix and Kepler SignalTek, not by the data centre one-off.
  • Earnings per share now grow faster than operating profit because interest, tax and share count stopped leaking (our memo: about 13% to 15% a year).
  • At about 11 times two-year-forward earnings, the shares already price a normalised data centre line; the supply chain (BizLink October +80%, Vertiv orders doubling) says demand is not falling.
  • A working-capital release would hand the market a "cash coming back" story.

The bear reading

  • "Normalising" with no number means the data centre line is still falling and management will not say how far.
  • Consensus already assumes the second half equals the first; with the pull-forward reversing, any slippage is a downgrade.
  • Financial year 2028 has no pulse to annualise, organic growth of about 6.5% does not justify a growth multiple, and the largest customer's renewal is the key event.
  • If cash conversion stays weak while profit beats, the debate turns to the quality of earnings and the hub inventory.
  • Whatever the print, Nvidia and US data centre politics set the price next week.
