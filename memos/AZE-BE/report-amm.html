APM Mini-Memo: Azelis

The one-line view. Azelis is a sticky specialty-chemicals distributor whose recovery is now in the numbers, and the case depends on leverage falling far enough to restart acquisitions and earn back the multiple.

What the company does

Azelis sells specialty chemicals and food ingredients on behalf of about 2,800 manufacturers to some 65,000 customers in 64 countries, with more than 70 laboratories that help customers formulate their products. Life sciences (pharmaceuticals, food, personal and home care) is about 63 percent of sales and industrial chemicals 37 percent; Europe, the Middle East and Africa is 45 percent of revenue, the Americas 35 percent and Asia-Pacific 20 percent. Revenue in 2025 was 4.1 billion euros at an adjusted operating margin of 10 percent, which makes Azelis the third largest listed European distributor behind Brenntag and IMCD, in a market where the top ten hold under 15 percent.

The lock is in the formulation. Once an ingredient is specified into a customer's recipe, and validated by regulators in pharmaceuticals, replacing the distributor is hard. Logistics are outsourced and capital spending is below half a percent of sales, which historically funded a buy-and-build engine of 64 deals in eight and a half years.

A plausible investment thesis

A recovery the market has half paid for. Three things went wrong at once: organic sales fell for three years to the first quarter of 2026, net debt overshot to 3.4 times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation against a 2.5 to 3.0 times target and froze acquisitions, and 2025 brought a third year of downgrades. The shares fell to 6.72 euros and an all-time low multiple in January 2026.

The turn is visible and broadening. Customers finished destocking, and organic growth went from minus 3.9 percent in the first quarter of 2026 to plus 3.9 percent in the second and plus 8.2 percent in the third, reported on 22 October, with every region growing and adjusted operating profit beating consensus in both quarters. A 20 million euro cost programme landed early and a March 2026 refinancing saves 6.5 million euros a year of interest, so profit grows faster than sales.

The route to earnings per share has three legs. Organic growth of 3 to 4 percent and a held gross margin give operating profit growth of about 5 percent a year, and falling interest adds more: the Investment Analyst's path is 0.90 euros in 2026, 1.01 in 2027 and 1.08 in 2028, about 9 percent a year. The third leg is the re-rating: once leverage is back inside 3.0 times the market restores the multiple it pays a consolidator that can consolidate, and management already speaks of 10 to 15 bolt-on deals from the first half of 2027. The drivers to track are the volume-versus-price split of organic growth, year-end 2026 leverage (toward 3.1 times, or above 3.4), and 2027 earnings.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Major CfC Clearing in MQ non-cyclical or better business on Q1 multiple, leading to reversion to normal'. The Investment Analyst's memo of 29 September rated Setups' Fit/Crispness C: the mechanism fits exactly and all five conditions pass, but two pass only at the boundary, because the multiple sat at the 25th to 29th percentile rather than below it and the sell-side never capitulated. At 12.3 times 24-month-forward earnings today, the 24th percentile of the stock's history, the shares still sit just inside the bottom quarter. A trough-on-trough reading of the same facts is the secondary fit.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Investment Analyst's memo of 29 September rated Case Riskiness C: average riskiness, poor simplicity for a 64-country distributor without quantitative guidance, but a case that turns on three dated things. The main risk is that the recovery is a false friend: price without volume, and cash that does not reach the balance sheet. The third quarter's 8.2 percent organic growth leaned on industrial chemicals up 21 percent and on pricing lifted by Middle East disruption, which Deutsche Bank expects to fade, while life sciences grew only 1.5 percent. Meanwhile free cash flow halved, cash conversion fell to 59 percent, leverage rose to 3.46 times and a 350 million euro goodwill write-down landed in the Americas, and the shares fell 13 percent in a fortnight.

What needs to go right is simple to state. Volume-led growth holding above 4 percent, leverage falling toward 3.0 times by year-end, and no top-twenty principal leaving, which would re-open the whole case. The downside is bounded: the covenant is 4.5 times, liquidity is 745 million euros, no bond matures before September 2029, and the memo's mark-to-market downside to a trough multiple is 18 to 36 percent.

Business quality

Solid rather than strong. The supplier franchise is the exceptional fact: thirty years' average tenure with the top ten principals, zero attrition among the top twenty over ten years, and at least 85 percent of top-ten revenue exclusive, which is why the business came through three years of decline intact. Cash conversion averaged 93 percent from 2019 to 2025 and return on tangible capital is about 63 percent. Against that, reported return on invested capital is 7.3 percent after acquisition goodwill, leverage is the highest in the peer set, and IMCD out-executes on gross margin (25.1 against 23.4 percent) and on European growth. The Investment Analyst's memo of 29 September rated Foundations' Robustness C and Thesis's Change Forces' Strength C.

Plausible returns and valuation

About a B return, resting on the re-rating. At 12.05 euros the shares trade at 12.3 times 24-month-forward earnings of 0.98 euros, the 24th percentile of their own history, and at a 21 to 25 percent discount to IMCD on enterprise value to operating profit. The Investment Analyst's modal return, rebased to the 27 November price, is 23.2 percent a year over eighteen months and 15.5 percent over three years, averaging 19.4 percent, and the TSR/Valuation rating in our store is B. The memo's own figure of 20.2 percent had 17 points of the eighteen-month return from the re-rating and 11 from earnings; at the consensus earnings path the return falls to 7.9 percent a year, and the memo put the price at which the case clears both horizons at about 10.4 euros. The market is confirming the case: the weekly Technical Momentum rating of 27 November is A with three grade-A chart setups, and the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating rose to B on 25 November, with a median target of 14.70 euros.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Europe's chemical producers are shrinking and handing small customers to specialty distributors, adding up to a point a year of organic growth; the restock with inflation is the one phase when a distributor wins on volume, price and its own shelves at once; and a newly lit acquisition engine is a stream of upgrades, because consensus counts only completed deals. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 27 November is B.

Cons. The restock story lasts six to eighteen months, and the chemical price spike from panic-buying is expected to reverse. Chinese oversupply shrinks the spread at the semi-specialty end through 2027. In a risk-off market this group pays for cash conversion and balance sheet first, which is where Azelis is weakest, and Industry relative strength for industrials read 26 on 27 November, below the Sell Criterion line of 30 for a second Friday.

In short

  • Interesting because a sticky franchise is recovering in plain sight: organic growth went from minus 3.9 to plus 8.2 percent in two quarters while the shares sit at the 24th percentile of their own multiple
  • The return is about a B: 19.4 percent a year averaged across horizons, but 17 points of it is a re-rating that waits on leverage
  • The hinge is the balance sheet: leverage must fall from 3.46 times toward 3.0 by year-end 2026 for bolt-ons to resume in 2027
  • Watch the volume-versus-price split and year-end leverage; a top-twenty principal loss or leverage above 3.8 times re-opens the case
  • The Investment Analyst's memo of 1 October said park at 11.90 euros; the Lists judgement asks for a full memo now that momentum has turned A
