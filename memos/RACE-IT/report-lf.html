His Ferrari tenure demonstrates exemplary operational execution, expanding net revenues from EUR 4,271 million in 2021 to EUR 7,150 million in 2025 while raising adjusted EBIT margins from 25.2% to 28.5% and compounding diluted EPS at 19.9% CAGR, consistently outperforming traditional automotive and luxury peers across all shareholder return metrics.

Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Ferrari's financial performance trajectory under Vigna's tenure has compounded at statistically extreme rates, expanding net revenues from EUR 4,271 million in 2021 to EUR 7,150 million in 2025 while simultaneously expanding adjusted EBIT margin from 25.2% to 28.5%. These margin levels place Ferrari in the top decile of both luxury goods manufacturers and automotive producers globally, surpassing even Hermès International's 42% operating margin when adjusted for Ferrari's capital-intensive manufacturing requirements and racing division investments.

The company's equity compensation framework explicitly benchmarks relative Total Shareholder Return performance against a peer group comprising Hermès, LVMH, Richemont, Kering, Moncler, Prada, Brunello Cucinelli, Burberry, Porsche AG, and Aston Martin, formally acknowledging Ferrari's hybrid positioning between luxury goods and automotive sectors. The sustained margin expansion during a period of aggressive electrification investment and factory infrastructure buildout represents a structural outlier, with Ferrari delivering 330 basis points of EBIT margin improvement while automotive peers including Porsche AG and Aston Martin faced margin compression from similar BEV transition investments.

Ferrari's governance structure exhibits extreme ownership concentration through dual-class loyalty voting mechanics, with anchor shareholder Exor N.V. commanding 32.32% of total voting power despite holding only 21.33% of common shares, while Vice Chairman Piero Ferrari controls 16.17% of voting rights through 10.67% common equity. This combined 48.5% voting bloc between Exor and the Ferrari family trust creates a structural governance oddity rarely observed in NYSE-listed luxury or automotive peers, effectively insulating management from external activist intervention or hostile takeover threats while simultaneously constraining independent board oversight authority.

The board structure further deviates from Dutch Corporate Governance Code provisions by allowing Senior Director Sergio Duca to chair both the Board and Audit Committee simultaneously, justified by management based on his extensive accounting background as former Chairman of PricewaterhouseCoopers Italy. While luxury peers such as Hermès maintain comparable family control through H51 priority acquisition agreements, Ferrari's loyalty share mechanism represents a more aggressive entrenchment tool, granting permanent double-voting rights to long-term holders without requiring active family shareholding maintenance.
