Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The timing of the European permanent recruitment recovery determines the opening of the two-year window for rapid earnings per share growth starting in 2027.
  2. Consultant capacity management and client mix dynamics will decide whether PageGroup or Adecco serves as the purest recovery play.

Core investment inquiry

1.
Critical inquiry: "What do the third-quarter 2026 results and trading updates of PageGroup, Hays, Adecco and Randstad, and broker models since July 2026, show on four points: permanent placement fees and offer-to-placement conversion in Germany, France, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom; growth from small and medium-sized clients; the share of each extra unit of gross profit that brokers expect to reach operating profit in 2027 after consultants are rehired; and when brokers expect permanent placement in Europe to grow again year on year?"

Significance of recovery timing

2.
Delayed recovery: The lagging recovery of permanent recruitment compared to temporary volumes creates near-term pressure on gross margins across the sector. Deutsche Bank's note of 9 September 2026 highlights that gross margins have declined at Adecco and Randstad because larger, lower-margin enterprise clients and regions recovered first. Unless permanent placements in Germany and France recover, near-term gross profit margins will remain depressed. This mix headwind delays the rapid expansion of earnings per share that investors expect from operating leverage during the initial recovery phase.
3.
Leveraged growth: Operating margins will rise faster once permanent placement fees inflect because professional staffing carries higher margins. Morgan Stanley's report of 12 October 2026 indicates that Adecco's EBITA margin is expected to expand from 3.1% in FY26 to 3.2% in FY27 and 3.4% in FY28. Investors must monitor this progression because elevated leverage and restructuring costs could otherwise drag down growth. A faster recovery of high-margin professional segments would immediately accelerate earnings per share upside.

Capacity management and client dynamics

4.
Consultant capacity: The extent to which recruiters can support fee growth without immediately rehiring determines the initial conversion of gross profit to operating profit. Deutsche Bank's note of 9 September 2026 reports that Robert Walters has already resumed headcount investment in the US and Japan after consultant headcounts fell 33% from peak to trough. If peer companies must aggressively rehire consultants, operating leverage will be severely diluted in 2027. This dynamic directly impacts how much incremental gross profit drops through to support earnings per share growth.
5.
Optimistic leverage: Staffing platforms can deliver dramatic margin expansion if top-line growth accelerates without a corresponding increase in operational expenses. Bernstein's report of 27 August 2026 illustrates a bluesky scenario where Adecco achieves 6% organic growth and a 4% EBITA margin in FY27. This outcome implies a 60% incremental conversion of gross profit to operating profit. Such high drop-through would dramatically lift earnings per share unless extensive consultant hiring offsets these gains.
6.
Other side of the story: Sector recovery remains highly vulnerable to structural headwinds and macroeconomic drag that could prevent traditional cyclical margin expansion. Jefferies argues in its note of 29 September 2026 that structural challenges and AI threats will maintain severe pressure on recruiter profits over the longer term. This means that any cyclical volume recovery may fail to drive substantial operating leverage. If candidate shortages or technological displacement permanently alter hiring patterns, then historical earnings per share peaks may remain entirely out of reach.

Section 3 – Growth Dynamics in Small and Medium-Sized Clients

Section summary
  1. Early recovery volumes are concentrated among large enterprise clients, which compresses gross profit margins across the recruitment sector.
  2. While green shoots are emerging in the higher-margin small and medium-sized segment, active gross profit mix improvements remain limited.
  3. Pure-play professional recruiters benefit from higher-margin SME spot transactions but remain vulnerable to subdued candidate decision-making.

Enterprise volume concentration

1.
Concentrated volume growth: Early recruitment recovery in Europe is driven primarily by large enterprise clients who demand flexible temporary volumes. Deutsche Bank's report of 9 September 2026 notes that large clients recovered first in logistics and manufacturing, which carries lower gross profit margins. This enterprise concentration compresses gross margins because these high-volume contracts carry lower fee rates compared to smaller accounts. Consequently, earnings per share will remain under pressure until hiring expands into the broader economy.
2.
Entrenched margin pressure: Randstad experiences significant gross margin contraction due to its high mix of large enterprise placements in industrial sectors. Deutsche Bank's note of 22 July 2026 reports that Randstad's gross margin fell 70 basis points year-on-year to 18.2% in second-quarter results due to large-client temping. This margin deterioration matches the 30 basis points decline seen at Adecco during the same period. This mix headwind will restrict operating profit growth unless permanent staffing volumes pick up. Randstad Q2 gross margin bridge showing 70 basis point decline driven by temporary staffing mix.
3.
Balanced cost structures: Large enterprise clients offer lower gross margins but provide recruitment firms with superior scalability and lower costs to serve. Morgan Stanley's note of 7 August 2026 highlights that large accounts benefit from materially better productivity and SG&A efficiency than smaller accounts. This structural advantage means that large accounts remain critical for driving near-term operating leverage. Therefore, this enterprise volume supports earnings per share stability even during periods of weak permanent placement fees.
4.
Sector manufacturing drag: The concentration of large clients in struggling industrial sectors has delayed recovery in core European staffing hubs. Deutsche Bank's report of 12 October 2026 highlights that Hays had to rightsize its German temp workforce from 4,500 down to 2,300 following the automotive downturn. This downsizing reflects the extreme cyclical sensitivity of large-client industrial volumes in Northern Europe. Thus, earnings per share recovery remains dependent on a broader macroeconomic stabilization in Germany.

SME green shoots and margin mix

5.
Resilient spot pricing: Small and medium-sized clients typically pay higher fee rates but demand highly specialized professional recruitment. Hays' annual report of 17 September 2026 states that spot transactions carry above-average fees but require highly skilled consultants. These transactional fees are more lucrative than long-term enterprise contracts but are highly vulnerable to macro uncertainty. Consequently, a revival of SME hiring will quickly expand gross profit margins and support earnings per share.
6.
Emerging localized recovery: Initial recovery signals are emerging from smaller clients in less regulated staffing markets outside of Europe. Adecco disclosed in its second-quarter 2026 earnings release that SME revenues grew 23% organically in North America. This rapid acceleration from the 7% growth recorded in the first quarter suggests that smaller companies are starting to hire again. This regional growth provides a positive template for a potential margin recovery in Europe during 2027.
7.
Fragile client confidence: European small and medium-sized enterprises remain cautious about expanding their permanent headcounts under current macro conditions. Deutsche Bank's report of 9 September 2026 notes that SME demand remains highly subdued in specialized sectors like information technology. This ongoing caution contrasts with the stronger hiring resilience shown by larger corporate clients. This hesitation means that high-margin permanent placements are unlikely to drive significant gross profit improvements before mid-2027.
8.
Contrary evidence: Adecco reported in its second-quarter 2026 earnings release that its SME business grew 23% organically in North America. This high-double-digit acceleration suggests that small and medium-sized hiring is leading the recovery in certain major regions, which contradicts the view that early-stage growth is confined solely to low-margin enterprise clients. If this SME momentum spreads to European professional segments, staffing firms will experience rapid gross margin expansion ahead of broker expectations.

Comparative peer exposures

9.

Jefferies' report of 29 September 2026 outlines that PageGroup maintains an approximate 20 to 80 temp-to-perm split of gross profit. This deep exposure to permanent staffing makes PageGroup highly sensitive to SME sentiment shifts. Consequently, any inflection in SME permanent hiring will immediately boost PageGroup's earnings per share relative to generalist peers.
10.
Balanced contract exposure: Hays is less dependent on pure permanent placements than PageGroup but remains highly geared to professional SME business. Jefferies' report of 29 September 2026 notes that Hays has a 60 to 40 temp-to-perm split of net fees. This balanced portfolio provides Hays with stable temporary revenue while preserving substantial operating leverage for when SME permanent placements return. Therefore, this diversified mix helps to protect Hays' earnings per share during the early stages of recovery.
11.
Targeted SME penetration: Adecco is actively attempting to increase its penetration of the higher-margin SME segment to offset large-client margin drag. Morgan Stanley's note of 7 August 2026 explains that Adecco is using agentic AI and digital tools to lower its cost to serve smaller accounts. If Adecco can successfully scale this model, the profitability of individual SME placements will rise dramatically. This structural shift is crucial because it would allow Adecco to expand its operating margins even without a macro recovery.
12.
Specialized unit steering: Randstad relies on its specialized branch model to maintain strong relationships with high-margin small and medium-sized enterprises. Randstad's second-quarter 2026 interim report indicates that professional services revenues fell 2% year-on-year while operational enterprise volumes rose. This divergence underscores how Randstad's near-term growth remains heavily tilted toward lower-margin enterprise staffing. Consequently, Randstad's gross margin will remain under pressure until its professional branch business rebounds.
13.
Subdued professional demand: Similar margin and volume dynamics are visible across other professional staffing peers that specialize in white-collar sectors. Deutsche Bank's note of 9 September 2026 highlights that SThree's management saw subdued IT demand among smaller companies despite resilient fill rates at larger corporations. This trend reinforces the finding that smaller clients remain highly sensitive to macroeconomic headwinds. Consequently, professional staffing providers will face ongoing gross margin dilution until smaller corporate budgets loosen.
14.
Other side of the story: The persistent weakness in high-margin SME permanent recruitment may reflect a structural shift rather than a cyclical delay. Jefferies argues in its report of 29 September 2026 that structural challenges and AI disruption will permanently depress professional recruiter margins. This means that SME permanent placement fees may never recover to their historical cyclical peaks. If clients permanently adopt in-house hiring platforms, the expected gross profit margin inflection will fail to support earnings per share.

Section 4 – Operating Leverage and Gross Profit Drop-Through

Section summary
  1. Recruiting firms expect substantial operating leverage as gross profit recovers, but immediate drop- through is diluted by restructuring costs and consultant retention.
  2. Structural cost-saving programs across major staffers support a higher conversion floor than seen in previous cyclical recoveries.
  3. Brokers remain divided on whether near-term top-line growth will translate into sustained operating profit expansion before consultant hiring accelerates.

Peer target models and drop-through

1.
High flow-through target: PageGroup expects a high conversion rate once fees recover because its consultants are highly productive. Morgan Stanley's note of 13 July 2026 reports that PageGroup management typically expects a 70% flow-through from incremental gross profit to operating profit. This target means that thirty percent of incremental fees is shared with consultants through variable compensation, which is lower than the fifty percent drop-through expected at Randstad, so it should rapidly lift earnings per share unless recruitment volumes contract.
2.
Stretched medium-term goal: Hays is aiming for a substantial conversion margin recovery through self-help efficiency initiatives. Hays' annual report of 20 August 2025 states that management remains confident that under normal market conditions all business lines can achieve a 25% conversion rate before central costs. This target is highly ambitious compared to its historical low conversion rate of 4.7% in fiscal year 2025 and is higher than the sector average, so achieving it would significantly accelerate earnings per share growth.
3.
Randstad drop-through model: Randstad expects stable incremental margins because of its activity-based steering model. Morgan Stanley's report of 8 September 2026 indicates that Randstad management expects a 40% to 50% drop-through from gross profit to EBIT. This structured framework supports the company's mid-term target of a 5% to 6% EBITA margin, which is below the 19.2% gross margin floor but above historical lows, which means earnings per share will rise steadily if permanent staffing stabilizes.
4.
Blue-sky drop-through potential: Some brokers believe Adecco can deliver rapid margin expansion under favorable macroeconomic conditions. Bernstein's report of 27 August 2026 highlights a blue-sky scenario where Adecco delivers a 60% gross profit drop-through to operating profit in 2027. This optimistic model assumes organic revenue growth reaches 6% and EBITA margins expand to 4%, which is double the 2.2% operating profit margin seen at Randstad in fiscal year 2025, which would drive massive earnings per share upside.

Headcount containment and productivity offsets

5.
Forensic resource allocation: Hays is managing its fee-earner capacity tightly to protect profits during the early recovery. Morgan Stanley's note of 10 July 2026 reports that Hays expects its consultant headcount to remain stable in early 2027. Because the company is committed to structurally reducing its cost base, this controlled capacity will allow net fee growth to drop directly to the bottom line, which is more efficient than PageGroup's current transition, supporting near-term earnings per share.
6.
Diluted operating gearing: PageGroup is currently unable to capture full operating leverage due to nonoperational expenses. Morgan Stanley's note of 13 July 2026 reports that PageGroup's operating leverage is being largely offset by one-off restructuring and transformation costs. This means that near-term earnings per share will not immediately reflect the recovery in gross profit, unlike Randstad which faces fewer non-operational transformation drag factors, but this lag should clear by late 2027 as transformation programs conclude.
7.
Staff capacity management: Recruitment firms must balance consultant headcounts to prevent productivity dilution during soft demand. Michael Page's annual report of 23 March 2026 states that the group reduced its fee earner headcount by 7.5% during the 2025 downturn. This defensive action allowed consultant productivity to remain flat despite falling fee volumes, whereas Hays' productivity rose slightly, which suggests subsequent top-line stabilization will immediately boost conversion rates.
8.
Technology efficiency gains: Generalist recruiters are deploying technology to increase consultant capacity and delay the need for rehiring. Morgan Stanley's report of 22 July 2026 notes that Randstad's productivity initiatives deliver growing candidate support per employee as volumes recover. This rising efficiency suggests that Randstad can accommodate initial revenue growth without expanding its headcount, unlike historical cycles where staffing firms rehired immediately, which helps to protect operating margins from immediate payroll inflation.

Structural cost savings and conversion floors

9.
German restructuring benefits: Hays is restructuring its German operations to establish a more profitable operating model. Deutsche Bank's report of 12 October 2026 notes that structural cost savings of £80 million could lift Hays' conversion ratio by 10 percentage points. This cost-led adjustment helps to raise the conversion floor from its low 7% base, which is higher than Hays' historical conversion margin of 4.7% in fiscal year 2025, which means that any recovery in German professional fees will drive substantial earnings per share growth.
10.
Restructuring cost headwinds: Adecco continues to incur exceptional expenses that dilute its reported operating margins in the short term. Morgan Stanley's report of 8 September 2026 notes that restructuring costs in Germany and France are expected to persist. This persistent investment in restructuring means that reported EBITA will lag behind Randstad's performance, which has lower leverage and fewer exceptional items, but the benefits should support a stronger earnings per share progression starting in 2027.
11.
Limited near-term leverage: Generalist staffers face structural challenges that restrict rapid margin expansion during the initial upturn. Jefferies' report of 17 September 2026 predicts that Randstad's operating profit will rise only 2% year-on-year in the third quarter. This sluggish growth highlights very limited operating leverage because persistent gross margin pressure offsets temporary volume gains, which is lower than the double-digit growth seen in other professional services sectors, which means that earnings per share upgrades will remain capped until permanent recruitment recovers.

Comparative peer conversion expectations

This marginal increase from 3.1% in fiscal year 2026 is slightly higher than the 0.9% EBIT margin expected for Hays, which reflects the ongoing drag of gross margin pressure and rising administrative expenses, suggesting that earnings per share expansion will be backend loaded.

This exceptionally high flow-through contradicts the narrative that near-term operating leverage is severely restricted by restructuring costs and consultant rehiring. If this level of conversion is sustained, then operating margins will expand much faster than the modest 10 basis points expansion currently modeled by other brokers.
15.
Other side of the story: Persistent structural headwinds and technological displacement could permanently cap the cyclical recovery of operating margins. Jefferies' report of 29 September 2026 argues that structural challenges and AI threats will maintain severe pressure on sector profits. Historical recovery precedents

9.
