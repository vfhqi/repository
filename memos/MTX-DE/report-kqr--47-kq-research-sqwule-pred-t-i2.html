Section 2 – FX Hedging Policy, Dollar Rates and the Currency Basis of 2030 Targets

Section summary
  1. MTU Aero Engines faces significant transactional currency risk due to its high exposure to dollar- denominated sales and its short-duration hedging book.
  2. The step-up in hedged rates through 2029 will act as an earnings headwind unless mitigated by underlying dollar-denominated spare parts growth.

MTU Hedging Profile and Rates

1.
Elevated sensitivity: MTU Aero Engines displays a highly volatile operating income profile relative to peers because it maintains a flexible currency hedging program. Jefferies Research indicates that MTU secures average hedge rates of 1.10 to 1.16 over the 2025 to 2028 timeframe. Hedging closer to the transaction window allows the group to adapt, but it also increases short-term exposure, leading to more frequent earnings revisions than its civil aerospace peers.
2.
Strategic exposure: The German engine module manufacturer operates with a significantly smaller derivative cushion than other major European aerospace defense companies. Rothschild & Co Redburn reports that MTU has only hedged approximately thirty percent of its estimated net dollar exposure for the 2025 to 2028 period. This positioning leaves the company heavily reliant on spot market movements, effectively making it the most sensitive covered stock to ongoing currency volatility.
3.
Short duration: The duration of MTU's foreign exchange derivative book is remarkably brief compared to its closest industry competitors. Rothschild & Co Redburn reports that the company’s four billion dollars of outstanding currency hedges cover only about 2.1 years of exposure as of mid-2025. This strategic decision explains why MTU’s operating results react so quickly to swings in the global macro environment, presenting both upside potential and downside risk to mid-term earnings per share.
4.
Stepping rates: MTU Aero Engines reports that its rolling hedging model utilizes declining coverage targets to protect short-term margins while leaving outer years open. Financial presentations from September 2026 show that the group's achieved hedge rate steps up from 1.14 in 2026 to 1.19 in 2027, before returning to 1.16 in 2028 and 1.18 in 2029. These moving targets will introduce notable margin fluctuations as the underlying series production ramps up.
5.
Natural hedging: MTU Aero Engines attempts to mitigate its transaction risk through structural matching of foreign currency revenues and operating costs. Presentation materials from Q3 2026 indicate that approximately seventy-five percent of the company's dollar-denominated revenues are naturally hedged by purchasing materials in US dollars. The remaining unhedged net exposure must be managed through financial derivatives to protect the predictability of adjusted operating EBIT and net income.
