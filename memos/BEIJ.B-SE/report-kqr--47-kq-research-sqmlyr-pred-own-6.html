Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The Swedish Riksbank is expected to implement a gradual, short tightening cycle peaking at 2.25% in early 2027.
  2. The European Central Bank is projected to anchor near a 2.50% terminal rate through 2027.
  3. Geopolitical energy shocks present persistent upside risks that could prolong elevated borrowing costs.

Macroeconomic Sensitivity and Segments

1.
Key Question: "Beijer Ref AB (BEIJ.B-SE), a Swedish-listed global wholesale distributor of refrigeration, air- conditioning and heating (HVAC-R) parts and equipment to professional installers, with its own manufacturing of natural-refrigerant systems (SCM Frigo, Fenagy, Refra); it reports three regions (EMEA, North America through the Heritage Distribution platform, Asia-Pacific) and three product segments (comfort cooling and heating, commercial and industrial refrigeration, own OEM manufacturing). What do Swedish and euro-area banks and the Riksbank and ECB themselves currently forecast for EUR/SEK, USD/SEK and AUD/SEK and for Swedish and euroarea policy rates through 2027 and 2028, and what do those forecasts imply for Beijer Ref's currency translation (an EBITA headwind of SEK 209m in 2025 and SEK 72m in H1 2026; about 98% of sales outside Sweden, unhedged) and for its net financial costs, given its debt structure at end-2025 and in 2026?"
2.
Elevated exposure: Beijer Ref exhibits high operational sensitivity to macroeconomic interest rates and currency fluctuations due to its geographically diversified business. Specifically, the comfort cooling and heating segment, which represents 58% of sales, relies heavily on housing activity and consumer demand that are constrained by central bank policy rates. These interest rate pressures can temporarily delay volume recoveries in North America, ultimately impacting consolidated earnings.
3.
Substantial translation: Beijer Ref generates about 98% of its sales outside of Sweden, leaving its consolidated financial results highly vulnerable to currency translation swings when converting foreign earnings back into Swedish Kronor. The EMEA region accounts for approximately 60% of group sales, meaning that any strengthening of the Swedish Krona against the Euro acts as a major headwind for translated EBITA. This translation risk remains largely unhedged for most operations, making the group's earnings per share highly dependent on forecasted exchange rates.
4.
Direct interest: Beijer Ref faces notable interest rate risks because a major portion of its debt consists of variable- rate credit facilities. Valuation multiples and funding costs are sensitive to interest rate cycles, which directly affects the company's financial planning through 2028. Consequently, elevated policy rates set by the Riksbank and the European Central Bank can put downward pressure on valuation and raise net financial costs.
5.
Contrary evidence: Beijer Ref highlights that some parts of its business are highly resilient to macroeconomic cycles, as roughly 80% of residential HVAC demand is driven by urgent repair and replace activity. This high share of defensive replacement demand helps to sustain operational cash flows and mitigates the negative impacts of high interest rates. Consequently, the operational earnings impact from elevated borrowing costs may be smaller than a pure top-line analysis would suggest.

Related Financial Market Dynamics

6.
Slower compounding: Beijer Ref relies heavily on a decentralized, acquisition-led compounder strategy to drive long-term earnings growth. A closely related question is how elevated policy rates affect the valuation multiples and availability of credit needed to execute its robust bolt-on pipeline. If borrowing costs remain elevated through 2028, the company may face lower pricing discipline or slower M&A deal execution.
7.
Higher debt: Beijer Ref's debt profile and interest sensitivities contrast with its larger peer Watsco, which maintains a much lower leverage ratio. While both distributors benefit from structural replacement demand, Watsco's conservative financing structure insulates its earnings per share from sudden central bank rate hikes. Thus, Beijer Ref's reliance on variable debt makes its net financial costs significantly more sensitive to global monetary policy.
8.
Other side of the story: Beijer Ref remains highly confident that structural tailwinds, such as tightening F-gas regulations and the transition to natural-refrigerant systems, will easily outpace currency and interest rate headwinds. These regulatory drivers create mandatory replacement cycles that allow the company to expand its high-margin OEM business segment regardless of short-term macroeconomic shifts. Therefore, robust organic momentum and product mix improvements could entirely neutralize the projected financial and translation headwinds.

Swedish Interest Rate Normalisation

1.
Gradual tightening: The Swedish Riksbank unanimously raised its policy rate by 25 basis points to 2% in November 2026, ending a period of holding rates steady. Strategists at Morgan Stanley forecast that strong macroeconomic data will support a second 25 basis point hike in February 2027, bringing the repo rate to 2.25%. This upward trajectory is expected to raise borrowing costs for Swedish companies through 2028.
2.
Expected hikes: Swedish commercial bank Swedbank forecasts that the Riksbank will follow its November 2026 rate hike with a subsequent increase in February 2027 to 2.25%. Swedbank anticipates that structural spending on national defense and domestic infrastructure projects will support Swedish economic growth. This economic resilience means that interest rates will remain structurally higher than in previous cycles, directly increasing Beijer Ref's domestic interest liabilities.
3.
Moderate pricing: Nordea economists forecast that the Riksbank will keep the policy rate unchanged at its December 2026 meeting before delivering a final 25 basis point hike in February 2027. Nordea projects that the repo rate will peak at 2.25% in this cycle. This normalisation path will likely stabilise short-term financing costs for Nordic corporate borrowers by mid-2027.

Contrary evidence: The Swedish October 2026 flash inflation data surprised significantly to the downside, with CPIF inflation dropping to 1.1% and core CPIF excluding energy collapsing to just 0.1% year-on-year. Nordea analysts suggest that this broad-based weakness demonstrates that domestic inflationary pressures remain extremely modest. If this low inflation trend persists, the Riksbank may refrain from further rate hikes in early 2027, providing relief for interestsensitive corporate operations.

Section 4 – Beijer Ref Currency Translation and EBITA Impact Analysis

Section summary
  1. Unhedged currency translation represents a major source of earnings volatility for Beijer Ref due to its minimal Swedish sales footprint.
  2. Gradual strengthening of the Swedish Krona against the US Dollar is projected to create ongoing translation headwinds for North American earnings.
  3. Conservative hedging practices of international peers contrast sharply with Beijer Ref's open translation exposure.

Historical Translation Vulnerability

1.
Unhedged profile: Beijer Ref generates about 98% of its sales outside of Sweden, creating a highly sensitive translation profile. The company reported a significant EBITA currency translation headwind of SEK 209m for the full year of 2025. This massive currency headwind represents a clear risk to earnings per share because the group leaves translation exposures generally unhedged as a core financial policy.
2.
Persistent headwinds: Beijer Ref experienced a continuation of these severe translation pressures into the first half of 2026. Currency translation headwinds reduced consolidated EBITA by SEK 72m during this period, but these negative effects began to normalise in the second quarter of 2026. Consequently, the group's reported EBITA was highly sensitive to krona volatility before these pressures subsided.
3.
Geographic concentration: Beijer Ref's currency translation mechanics are driven primarily by its three core geographic operating regions. The EMEA region remains the largest contributor, accounting for 59% of group net sales in late 2025. This extreme geographic concentration means that any fluctuations in the Euro against the Swedish Krona will directly dictate consolidated profit margins and translated earnings.
4.
North American exposure: Beijer Ref's geographic expansion has rapidly increased its exposure to North American currency movements. The North America segment grew to represent 21% of total sales by the end of 2025 following the acquisition of Heritage Distribution. Because the group translates these US Dollar revenues back into Swedish Kronor, fluctuations in the USD/SEK rate exert substantial influence over reported EBITA.
5.
Australian exposure: Beijer Ref's Asia-Pacific division contributes the remaining 20% of group sales, introducing exposure to the Australian Dollar. The APAC segment grew by 8% in reported terms during the third quarter of 2026, but this was reduced to just 1% growth when adjusted for constant currency. This divergence demonstrates how currency translation can mask the true organic performance of international divisions.

Outstanding Debt and Capital Structure

1.
Substantial bank facilities support Beijer Ref's short-term liquidity and ongoing acquisition strategy. At the end of 2025, the group's short-term financing consists of bilateral bank agreements and outstanding commercial paper totalling SEK 9,706 mm. By the end of Q1 2026, the company's approved credit facilities reached SEK 19,442 mm. This flexible funding arrangement allows the group to efficiently execute its rapid M&A pipeline across EMEA and North America.
2.
Increasing capital market access has diversified Beijer Ref's funding base away from pure bank relationship lending. The company established a SEK 7 bn MTN programme to broaden its long-term financial flexibility. Under this programme, Beijer Ref successfully issued SEK 2,500 mm in corporate bonds on March 4, 2026. Half of these bonds were fixed at attractive interest rates of 2.843% and 3.365% to hedge against rising rate environments.
3.
Growing lease commitments represent a material component of Beijer Ref's total net debt obligations. The group reported lease liabilities of SEK 2,473 mm at the end of 2025 under IFRS 16 rules. These lease liabilities climbed further to SEK 2,719 mm by the third quarter of 2026. This upward trend reflects the continuous branch expansion and physical footprint growth associated with recent acquisitions.
