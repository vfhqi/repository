Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: "What has Hexatronic's management said in public, and what has it declined to say or left open, on each of the main drivers, risks and sources of optionality in the business over the last twelve months, and what is the latest dated status of each?"

Hexatronic Group AB's transition from a pure-play Fiber-to-the-Home (FTTH) provider to a diversified digital infrastructure company has significant implications for its long-term earnings per share (EPS) trajectory. The company's ability to reach its 2028 financial targets–SEK 11 bn in net sales and a 12% EBITA margin–hinges on the successful execution of its M&A strategy and the normalization of the European FTTH market. For an investor, understanding the precise status of commitments like the Clinton plant expansion and the subsea cable partnership with NKT is critical, as these represent the physical capacity required to meet growing demand in North America and global digital infrastructure. The recent reversal of an impairment at the South Carolina facility indicates a sharp pivot in management's outlook for the U.S. market, shifting from defensive cost-cutting to aggressive growth.

Closely related questions involve the durability of Data Center margins, which reached 16.9% in Q3 2026, significantly above the Group's long-term target of 15% for that segment. Investors must weigh management's qualitative steer on "normalizing" growth against the secular tailwinds of AI-driven infrastructure spending. Furthermore, the supply of critical inputs like glass fiber and connectors has emerged as a bottleneck, with management admitting these constraints now limit the ability to grow faster than current levels. The timing of new supply coming online in 2027 will determine if Hexatronic can capture market share during the anticipated BEAD program ramp-up in late 2026.

The disclosure practices of peers like Prysmian and Corning provide a benchmark for transparency. For instance, Corning's disclosure of multibillion-dollar long-term customer agreements and specific fiber diameter innovations contrasts with Hexatronic's more guarded commentary on customer concentration and pricing splits. By identifying what management refuses to say–such as specific order intake by segment or exact capacity

Hexatronic has delivered on its primary Fiber Solutions restructuring and equity raise commitments, but the 12% Group EBITA margin remains a medium-term target dependent on 2027 supply normalization. Confidence in these statuses is High, supported by the Q3 2026 report and subsequent broker analysis.

Delivered and Outstanding Financial Targets Hexatronic’s primary financial target is reaching an

EBITA margin of 12% by 2028, with segment-specific targets of 10% for Fiber Solutions and 15% each for

Data Center and Harsh Environment. As of Q3 2026, the Group reported an adjusted EBITA margin of 11.3%, showing significant progress from the 7.7% reported in the prior-year period. The Fiber Solutions segment reached 9.4% in Q3, nearing its 10% target, while Data Center exceeded its 15% target at 16.9%.

The proceeds were used to fund the Superior Fiber & Data Services acquisition and provide headroom for further deals. Management has also delivered on its Fiber Solutions restructuring program, which was expanded in late 2025 and reached a full run rate of SEK 120 mm in annual savings by the end of Q1 2026.

Management’s goal is to reach SEK 3 bn in Data Center sales by 2028, and the Q3 2026 rolling 12-month sales already exceeded SEK 2 bn.
