EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 11 findings

Change Materiality and Strategic Inflection

Kering stands at a profound strategic inflection point following the appointment of Luca de Meo as Group CEO on September 15, 2025, bringing three decades of automotive turnaround success from Renault to a luxury sector that typically favors internal promotion. This leadership transition represents much-needed industrial discipline but simultaneously elevates brand-level integration risk and cultural disruption across the organization. The company has pivoted from its historical decentralized holding model–which showed severe limitations as Gucci underperformed peers over five years–to an integrated industrial platform through the five-hub ReconKering initiative spanning industry, client, technology, sustainability, and support functions. Management targets to more than double the 11.1% FY25 recurring operating margin by mid-term, reaching above 22%, while executing a mid-single-digit percentage decline in overall retail square footage for 2026. However, expert interviews reveal an internal culture prone to accelerated firing to cover responsibility during performance downturns, and the creation of industry and client divisions introduces systematic dual reporting that risks operational friction across brand silos.

The magnitude of change extends beyond governance to fundamental business model redesign. Kering accelerated its store rationalization program with 84 net closures in H1 2026, targeting at least 100 closures for full-year 2026 and 250 net closures by 2030, with approximately 130 planned for China representing half of the total target. Strategic ecosystem partnerships include a fifty-year exclusive beauty license with L'Oréal commencing July 2027, with Coty receiving USD 400 million for early redemption. The external environment presents severe headwinds, with a distinct divergence between struggling middle-class aspirational cohorts and resilient high-net-worth consumers, where aspirational buyers historically represent up to 60% to 65% of total luxury personal goods consumption.

Recent Delivery and Operational Momentum

Group comparable revenue returned to positive 2% growth in Q2 2026, a sequential acceleration from flat performance in Q1 2026, while Gucci retail comp sales improved to negative 2% in Q2 2026 vs negative 8% in Q1, beating consensus expectations of negative 4.7%. Recurring operating margin reached 12.8% in H1 2026, up 40 basis points year-on-year and outperforming consensus expectations of 12.0%, with corporate opex declining by 5% primarily driven by structural cost-saving actions and accelerated store network optimization.

However, historical performance reveals profound challenges: Kering experienced a major revenue decline from EUR 19,566 million in FY23 to EUR 14,675 million in FY25, with consolidated EBIT collapsing from EUR 4,746 million to EUR 1,631 million, and adjusted EPS plummeting by 82% over two years from EUR 25.01 to EUR 4.35, as operating margins fell from 24.3% to 11.1%.

Management's historical guidance repeatedly fell short as EBIT collapsed 36% YoY in FY25 despite stable outlooks, with annual sales contracting from EUR 19,566 million in FY23 to EUR 14,675 million in FY25, while severe opex deleverage occurred because fixed luxury opex represented 46% of group sales during revenue contractions, and group margins fell by 1,250 basis points over the 2024-2025 cycle.

Forward Guidance Credibility

Management has embraced a challenger status under CEO Luca de Meo to rebuild financial foundations, though it previously guided to stable FY25 EBIT while actual performance collapsed by 36% year-on-year; current guidance has shifted to fixed-cost rightsizing and store network optimization to support operating margins. Kering delivered FY24 recurring operating income of €2.554 billion, beating its revised late-year target of €2.5 billion, demonstrating some credibility on revised expectations.

James Grzinic at Jefferies expects FY26/FY27 EPS of €6.75/€10.79 with group EBIT margins reaching 12.9%/16.0% in the base case, while the upside scenario targets FY26/FY27 EBIT margins of 14.0%/19.2% supporting a €340 target price, and the downside model projects depressed FY26/FY27 EBIT margins of 11.8%/15.0% risking a €210 price target. The guidance framework is highly sensitive to operating leverage, with high operating leverage exposure from unproductive retail space and fixed opex, though the ReconKering strategy targets more than doubling the 11.1% FY25 margin by 2030.

Sell-Side Positioning and Consensus

The consensus rating distribution per Bloomberg as of June 2026 is 15% Overweight, 70% Equal-weight, and 15% Underweight, with price targets exhibiting wide dispersion ranging from a low of EUR 175 to a high of EUR 360, and the mean consensus target price clustering around EUR 273 reflecting the prevailing cautious hold narrative. Bernstein's Luca Solca raised the price target to EUR 270 from EUR 220 following the H1 2026 beat, and boosted FY26 EPS estimates by 8% to reflect positive opex cuts and cost control.

Jefferies believes that achieving a Gucci operating margin above the high-20s is highly unlikely in the mid-term, while Bernstein's Luca Solca warns that aggressively downsizing Gucci's China retail network could leave lasting scars on brand equity. Redburn's Emily Cooledge argues that doubling group profit by 2028 is extremely demanding in a challenging macro environment, maintaining that Kering remains priced for a profound recovery that fails to reflect massive execution risks.

Fundamental Peer Positioning

Kering's fundamental position within the luxury peer group is deeply depressed. Kering Group Q2 organic growth reached 2%, compared to Hermès at 10.5% and Richemont at 24%, while Gucci is priced at an average 30% discount to peers in core categories, reflecting limited pricing power as it recovers from pushed-too-high levels. The competitive landscape reveals stark disparities: competitors like Louis Vuitton and Hermès leverage unmatched scale and operational agility to capture global market share, Prada and Miu Miu successfully implement smart pricing strategies whereas Gucci remains highly exposed to price elasticity, and peers maintain highly diversified models that insulate their earnings while Kering remains dangerously dependent on Gucci's turnaround.

Operationally, Kering utilizes 4,600 Italian suppliers representing a highly complex network compared to more consolidated luxury peers, and the vast number of small subcontractors exposes Kering to heightened supply chain audit risks and logistical inefficiencies.

Activist Presence

No active activist presence is documented in the AS library for Kering, indicating the absence of external shareholder pressure campaigns or public open letters demanding strategic changes. This lack of activist engagement may reflect either confidence in management's self-help initiatives under de Meo or alternatively a view that the turnaround timeline is too uncertain to warrant activist intervention at current valuation multiples.
