Section 2 – Expected Return on Equity from 2029

Section summary
  1. Germany's transition to a cost-plus, weighted average cost of capital framework for the fifth regulatory period starting in 2029 is expected to lift allowed returns.
  2. Major brokers expect a nominal post-tax return on equity of approximately 6.5% for electricity transmission system operators to support grid investment.
  3. Operators target an all-in return on equity of around 8% by combining base regulatory rates with operational performance incentives.

Transition to WACC-Based Model

1.
Regulatory regime overhaul: Germany is planning a fundamental redesign of its electricity network tariff system starting in 2029. The German legislator has decided to repeal the current electricity network tariff regulation effective on 31 December 2028. This redesign is the sole responsibility of the German Federal Network Agency, also known as the Bundesnetzagentur or BNetzA, following a European Court of Justice ruling on regulatory independence. Consequently, the regulator is transitioning electricity transmission operators from the fourth regulatory period cost-of-equity model to a weighted average cost of capital framework.
2.
Standardized operational expense adjustments: The fifth regulatory period, or RP5, will introduce a standardized operational expenditure adjustment mechanism to better reflect the energy transition. According to an E.ON annual results presentation from 25 February 2026, this mechanism will adjust the revenue cap for energy transition-related opex increases occurring after the base year. Additionally, benchmarking requirements under the new framework will explicitly incorporate redispatch costs. This shift aims to reduce the traditional regulatory lag and protect operators from inflation.

Broker and Operator Expectations

3.
Jefferies return projections: Regulatory visibility is set to improve as BNetzA develops the RP5 framework. In a report dated 3 August 2026, Jefferies assumes a base nominal post-tax return on equity of 6.5% for German transmission operators during RP5. Jefferies argues that grid planning updates will provide greater visibility on the funding roadmap and regulatory asset base growth beyond 2028. This expected return represents a notable improvement over historical legacy benchmarks. Bernstein return projections: Financial parameters for the electricity transmission settlement are expected to be published in 2027. In a sector note from 14 September 2026, Bernstein Research reports that the German regulatory returns are moving toward a cost-plus framework. Bernstein Research indicates that the expected composition could combine an underlying return in the 6% range with added outperformance and incentives to reach an all-in return on equity of approximately 8%. This structure closely mirrors the new Belgian regulatory framework which targets a similar 8% cash return on equity. Operator targets: Regulated network operators are actively engaging with the regulator to secure viable investment conditions. According to E.ON's presentation from 14 September 2026, the company is confident that the final shape of the RP5 regulatory settlement will be acceptable. E.ON's management notes that the gas proposal was potentially favorable because the risk-free rate is based on a longer tenor and the market risk premium increased to 4.95%. Meanwhile, Elia Group argues that competitive returns are essential to sustain its German grid capital expenditure.

  3. Industrial grid congestion and complex offshore wind integration projects justify a superior risk premium for power transmission assets.

This means the regulator could rapidly finalize unfavorable base parameters before operators can mount legal appeals. Such rapid action would compress the timeline for corporate adjustments and negatively impact expected earnings per share.

Financial and Capital Expenditure Impact

1.
Severe capital constraints: If BNetzA sets returns near the draft gas network level of 4.9% post-tax, Elia Group would face material financial headwinds. Under this downside scenario, Morgan Stanley estimates that Elia's 2029 earnings per share would face an unsupportive 8% downgrade. Such a severe regulatory reduction would completely undermine the economic assumptions supporting Elia's massive onshore expansion. Consequently, the group would be forced to restrict its capital expenditure plans to protect its balance sheet.
2.
Impaired asset growth: A restrictive regulatory outcome would disrupt E.ON's capital growth trajectory because network returns directly drive valuation. Bernstein Research warns that the absence of regulatory framework improvements starting in 2029 represents a major downside risk for Elia. E.ON's massive 48 billion euro network investment program is highly dependent on achieving competitive regulatory remuneration. Unless returns are set near the expected 6.5% level, operators cannot sustain their grid expansion targets up to 2030.
3.
Valuation discount pressure: Grid operators face severe valuation de-ratings if the final determination falls short of market expectations. Jefferies argues that Elia's sector-leading growth profile currently justifies its premium valuation. However, a lower allowed return would eliminate the positive catalysts that investors expect from the upcoming RP5 draft. This would severely compound funding gap risks and turn high leverage into the main issue for shareholders.

Supply Chain and Financing Pressures

4.
Constrained supply chain: A major reduction in network capital budgets would immediately impact key equipment suppliers like Siemens Energy. A former Siemens Energy global sales executive explained that the grid sector is currently characterized by extremely tight manufacturing capacity and long lead times. Siemens Energy has achieved a massive 162 billion euro order backlog by securing long-term volume commitments from utilities. If German operators defer projects due to low returns, this sold-out backlog could face cancellations or delays.
5.
Elevated debt costs: Lower allowed returns would fail to cover the rising cost of debt for heavily leveraged operators. Morgan Stanley warns that higher bond yields present a significant downside risk that could trigger sector-wide de-ratings. Elia's high-voltage subsidiary Eurogrid is highly exposed because its net financial debt rose to 10.69 billion euros in mid-2026. If regulatory yields undershoot financing costs, Elia's credit rating would face severe pressure as debt costs rise.
6.
Operational execution risks: Tight supply chains are already inflating the cost of physical grid delivery for major operators. According to 50Hertz, the German market for engineering, route planning, and administrative services is extremely tight due to parallel infrastructure projects. These resource bottlenecks create significant project execution risks and potential delays. If regulatory returns are reduced, operators cannot absorb these rising execution and procurement costs within their revenue caps.

Political Drivers and Early Warnings

7.
Intense political pressure: German political figures and industrial lobbies have strong incentives to push for lower network charges to protect industrial competitiveness. Chancellor Friedrich Merz and Economy Minister Katherina Reiche have prioritized measures to reduce electricity costs for energy-intensive sectors. Indeed, the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs (BMWE) has previously supported temporary subsidies to halve grid fees for industrial consumers. In a stagnating economy, these government entities may pressure BNetzA to enforce stricter tariff caps on regional monopolies.
8.
Rising industrial discontent: Large industrial consumers are actively lobbying against rising network charges that impair their global competitiveness. The German non-ferrous metals producer Aurubis explained that mounting burdens from grid charges represent a significant risk to its domestic operations. Aurubis is actively collaborating with the Association of German Industries (BDI) to lobby for internationally competitive energy prices. This intense industrial pressure could compel BNetzA to limit allowed network operator profits to protect German manufacturing.
9.
Legislative warning signs: Early warning signs of a shifting regulatory environment can be found in recent legislative amendments and court disputes. The German legislature's amendment to the Energy Industry Act (EnWG) awarded BNetzA complete operational independence from executive influence. This shift has allowed the regulator to ignore political directives and prioritize consumer affordability over operator returns. Furthermore, operators like OGE have repeatedly appealed return determinations in the Düsseldorf Higher Regional Court, signaling deepening legal friction over regulatory parameters ahead of RP5.
10.
Other side of the story: While a lower base return is possible, the physical reality of Germany's energy transition prevents a severe regulatory markdown. German grid capital expenditure has significantly lagged the buildout of renewable generation, driving massive grid congestion and redispatch costs. E.ON argues that there is limited direct read-across from gas to electricity because the regulator must incentivize grid investment. Consequently, BNetzA President Klaus Müller has reiterated that investments in electricity networks must remain attractive to secure Germany's long-term energy supply.
