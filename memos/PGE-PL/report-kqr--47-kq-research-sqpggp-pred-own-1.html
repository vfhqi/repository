Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The resolution of PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna's post-2028 coal capacity revenues and state aid negotiations is critical to safeguarding future earnings per share and preventing severe capital transition deficits.
  2. PGE's state-controlled structure forces it to balance public interest grid security with decarbonization, making regulatory approvals vital for EPS stability.

What is the status of Poland's negotiations with the European Commission on support for coal units above 550 g CO2/kWh after 2028, the strategic-reserve option, and statutory compensation for units kept open in the public interest?"
2.

This ownership structure ensures that PGE plays a dual role as both a commercial enterprise and a guarantor of national energy security. Consequently, the group must manage unprofitable legacy assets to support the domestic grid while advancing green projects.

Section 2 – Coal Capacity Market Revenues and Contract Analysis (2026-2030)

Section summary
  1. PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna secures substantial near-term cash flows through 2028 under existing five-year capacity contracts and supplementary auctions.
  2. A sharp revenue contraction is expected after 2028 as European emission rules phase out support for high-emission coal plants.

Current contract revenues and auction results

1.
Strong revenues: PGE's legacy coal fleet continues to generate vital financial support from its existing five-year capacity contracts. According to a Bernstein Research daily note dated May 12, 2025, the company's conventional generation division earned PLN 2.25 bn in capacity revenue in FY24. These lucrative fiveyear contracts, which PGE secured in 2024, will continue to provide highly predictable cash flows to help stabilize segment earnings through the end of fiscal year 2028.
2.
Supplemental awards: PGE successfully expanded its near-term contracted revenue stream during the 2027 supplementary capacity auction. In an official PGE investor presentation published on September 16, 2026, management disclosed that the group contracted 1,859 MW of capacity in this supplementary auction. This award ensures that PGE's aging assets continue to receive stable funding while maintaining grid stability across the Polish national power system.
3.
High clearing: The clearing price of the 2027 supplementary capacity auction represents a major boost for the group's conventional power segment. According to a PGE corporate presentation dated September 16, 2026, the auction cleared at PLN 325.55/kW/year for domestic suppliers. This specific pricing level translates into approximately PLN 600 mm in total capacity-market revenue for PGE's Coal Energy division during the 2027 delivery year.
4.
Aggregated backlog: PGE has secured a vast volume of total contracted capacity to defend its merchant generation business from market volatility. PGE's management reported in an interim presentation dated September 16, 2026, that the group has contracted 11.1 GW of capacity in total for the 2027 delivery year. These aggregated capacity contracts are highly supportive of the group's financial metrics and are deemed essential for maintaining its corporate credit ratings.

5.

A Bernstein Research report published on September 4, 2026, showed that Tauron contracted 1,410 MW of capacity in the supplementary auction compared to PGE's 1,859 MW. This smaller contracted capacity indicates that PGE retains a much larger share of state-supported revenues to offset the high fixed costs of its coal fleet.
6.
Lower revenues: Tauron's projected capacity revenues from the supplementary auction fall below the total expected revenues of PGE. In its interim condensed financial statements dated September 9, 2026, Tauron projected that its revenues range from PLN 525 to 559 million for the delivery year. By comparison, PGE expects c. PLN 600 mm in revenue from its 1,859 MW supplementary allocation, highlighting a superior yield per MW.
7.
Moderate success: Enea achieved moderate success in the supplementary capacity auction but remains highly exposed to post-2028 regulatory changes. Bernstein Research reported on September 4, 2026, that Enea won one-year contracts for 1,595 MW of capacity in the 2027 supplementary auction. This volume is comparable to PGE's 1,859 MW award, but Enea's lack of a large five-year contract backlog makes its near-term conventional EBITDA far more vulnerable.
8.
Superior runway: PGE has secured a far superior revenue runway compared to its principal domestic competitors. According to a Bernstein Research daily note dated May 12, 2025, PGE outsmarted its peers by putting coal units on five-year contracts starting in 2024. In contrast, most of Enea's and Tauron's coal-fired generation was placed on five-year contracts that began in 2021, which leaves them without main capacity revenues after 2025.

Past precedents and structural transition cases

9.
Historic extension: PGE's extension of the Rybnik power plant provides an instructive past precedent for managing uneconomic coal assets. According to a Platts article cited by Bernstein Research on July 27, 2026, PGE decided to extend 920 MW Rybnik operations to 2028 despite having planned to decommission the units in 2023. The plant's continued operation was made financially viable because the units receive robust support from capacity-market contracts that run until 2028.
10.
Critical security: The Rybnik extension illustrates how Polish utilities must balance financial performance with public security obligations. Bernstein Research noted on July 27, 2026, that PGE's Rybnik coal plant was deemed uneconomic due to high operational costs. However, because of capacity payments and grid tightness, PGE adjusted the decommissioning schedule to keep three of the four units running through mid-2028, demonstrating that capacity revenues act as a critical safety net.
11.
Contrary evidence: PGE faces a severe and sudden drop-off in capacity market revenues after 2028 as strict European environmental rules phase out coal support. In its half-year interim report dated September 16, 2026, PGE noted that the European 550 g CO2/kWh emission limit applies to all contracts after 2025, with a temporary derogation allowing supplementary auctions only until 2028. This means that without a newly negotiated EU state-aid package, PGE's coal capacity revenues will effectively drop to zero starting in 2029, severely impacting group EPS.

Section 3 – Reconciliation of Bernstein and Fitch Capacity Estimates

Section summary
  1. The capacity revenue divergence between Bernstein and Fitch is fully reconciled by differing segmental scopes and gas revenue inclusions.
  2. Fitch's estimates focus strictly on legacy coal assets subject to derating, while Bernstein's projections incorporate highly profitable, inflation-indexed gas plants.
  3. PGE's strategic transition and supplementary auction successes protect its conventional earnings from the impending post-2028 coal contract cliff.

Segmental scope and definitions

1.
Segmental differences: PGE's reported conventional generation segment represents a broader operational scope than the pure coal units evaluated by some rating agencies. A Bernstein Research report published on May 8, 2025, showed that PGE's conventional generation division earned PLN 2.25 bn on capacity contracts in FY24. This figure includes both coal-fired units and gas-fired generation, which explains why it is higher than pure coal estimates. This broad scope protects overall group earnings per share from being entirely reliant on coal-fired revenues.
2.
Incremental gas revenues: PGE is actively expanding its gas-fired fleet with modern assets that receive long-term, inflation-indexed capacity payments. According to a Bernstein Research analysis dated May 12, 2026, the newly commissioned Gryfino CCGT plant provides PGE with PLN 350 m in annual capacity revenues. Furthermore, the under-construction Rybnik CCGT project is expected to add another PLN 0.3 bn annually starting in 2027. These highly visible, 17-year indexed cash flows are included in conventional division forecasts but are excluded from narrow coal segment definitions. This segment diversification supports future earnings per share as coal assets phase out. Coal-specific projections: Fitch's published projections on September 28, 2026, estimate that PGE's pure coal capacity revenues will average PLN 914 m annually in 2026-2027. This estimate aligns closely with PGE's reported transition because several major coal-fired plants are being decommissioned. According to PGE's 2025 annual report, Coal Energy segment capacity revenues were PLN 2.66 bn in FY25, indicating a rapid decline is expected. The rating agency's lower estimates reflect a strict focus on coal assets while excluding expanding gas revenues. This narrow definition makes the segment's outlook appear more financially constrained than the overall conventional division.
3.
Decommissioning impacts: PGE is executing scheduled retirements of legacy coal units which materially reduces the capacity base eligible for payments. A Bernstein Research report dated May 8, 2025, noted that PGE is shutting down 908 MW of Dolna Odra hard coal units and 460 MW of Rybnik coal capacities. These retirements explain the sharp reduction in coal-specific capacity revenues projected by rating agencies for the 2026 to 2027 period. The loss of these revenues will drag on coal segment earnings unless offset by cost reductions. This operational contraction underscores the urgency of transition capital spending.

Macro factors and inflation indexation

4.
Inflation adjustments: PGE's long-term capacity contracts benefit from inflation indexation which helps preserve the real value of conventional revenues. According to a Bernstein Research report published on May 8, 2025, new gas-fired and battery storage capacity contracts are fully indexed to inflation. However, legacy coal contracts do not enjoy the same comprehensive indexation mechanisms, leading to real-term revenue erosion over time. This discrepancy explains why some long-term forecasts show lower coal revenues than near-term nominal receipts. This means that inflation will erode coal-specific earnings margins while gas-fired earnings maintain their real value.
5.
Regulatory de-rating: PGE's contracted capacity is subject to strict regulatory de-rating factors that reduce the actual paid capacity below installed capacity. According to a Bernstein Research report dated January 24, 2025, PGE's planned CCGT acquisition features a 17-year contract for 493 MW of de-rated capacity despite having 600 MW of installed capacity. These regulatory de-rating rules are applied heavily to coal-fired units to reflect their lower availability during peak hours. This systematic de-rating explains why financial analysts project much lower capacity revenues than a simple multiplication of installed coal capacity would suggest. This derating directly limits the segment's potential earnings per share.
6.
Auction pricing boosts: PGE secured strong short-term revenues in the supplementary capacity auctions that help bridge the gap before the 2028 coal contract cliff. A Bernstein Research utility update published on September 4, 2026, showed that the 2027 supplementary capacity market tender cleared at Zloty 325.66 to 372.04 per kW per year. PGE was awarded contracts for 1,859 MW of capacity in this auction, providing a substantial near-term revenue boost. These high clearing prices will temporarily support PGE's cash flows through 2027. This short-term success mitigates the near-term risk of earnings per share dilution. Comparative yields: PGE's capacity revenues compare favorably against domestic peers due to its superior bidding strategy and contract timeline. A Bernstein Research report dated September 4, 2026, revealed that Tauron secured supplementary capacity contracts for 1,410 MW, estimating revenues between Zloty 459 m and 525 m. In comparison, PGE's 1,859 MW supplementary allocation yields approximately Zloty 600 m, demonstrating a higher revenue density. This superior contracted volume ensures that PGE maintains stronger cash flows than its peers. This competitive advantage supports PGE's relative earnings per share performance in the Polish utility sector.

Decommissioning schedules and contract cliff

7.
Cliff effects: PGE faces a severe contraction in coal capacity revenues after 2028 due to strict European Union carbon emission regulations. According to a Bernstein Research report from May 8, 2025, PGE secured fiveyear contracts in 2024 that run through the end of 2028. However, the European 550 g CO2/kWh emission limit will prevent any further coal capacity support starting in 2029. This regulatory deadline explains Fitch's low projections of PLN 534 m annually for the 2028-2030 period. This drop-off represents a major headwind for PGE's legacy earnings per share.
8.
Strategic modeling: PGE's updated corporate strategy reflects a conservative view of conventional power cash flows as capacity market support expires. A Bernstein Research report dated October 19, 2026, highlighted that PGE's management expects thermal coal generation to generate a negative EBITDA of PLN 3.3 bn in FY30. This conservative internal target is more pessimistic than Bernstein's own estimate of negative PLN 1.8 bn for the same period. This divergence shows that while rating agencies like Fitch project low coal revenues, PGE's internal models assume even greater operational losses. This strategic realism suggests that future earnings per share will depend entirely on non-coal segments. Contrary evidence: PGE's coal-fired units might remain operational far longer than scheduled due to system- critical grid stability concerns. According to a Bernstein Research report dated May 8, 2025, the Polish government is highly likely to provide additional regulated revenues to keep system-critical coal plants open. Furthermore, analysts expect that a potential power generation gap would be created if these coal capacities are decommissioned too rapidly. This suggests that actual coal capacity revenues after 2028 could exceed Fitch's conservative projections of PLN 534 m per year if new compensation mechanisms are approved. Such regulatory relief would prevent severe earnings per share write-downs for PGE. Historic precedents: PGE's historical management of the Rybnik power plant provides a precedent for extending uneconomic coal assets to ensure grid reliability. According to a Bernstein Research report dated May 8, 2025, PGE previously extended operations of the 920 MW Rybnik plant to support the grid despite its uneconomic nature. This extension demonstrated that capacity payments and grid tightness can lead to adjustments in the.

decommissioning schedule. This precedent indicates that PGE and Polish regulators are willing to delay closures when grid stability is threatened, which could keep additional coal capacity online past 2028. This potential for extensions represents an upside risk to Fitch's capacity revenue assumptions.

Synthesis and analytical reconciliation

9.
Analytical reconciliation: The apparent conflict between Bernstein's PLN 2.25 bn figure and Fitch's lower projections is fully reconciled by segmental definitions and timeline differences. The Bernstein figure represents the historical conventional generation segment's total capacity revenue, which includes profitable gas plants and inflation indexation. In contrast, Fitch's estimates focus exclusively on high-emission coal units undergoing rapid decommissioning and subjected to de-rating factors. Once gas revenues like Gryfino and Rybnik are added, the underlying operational cash flows align. This structural reconciliation shows that PGE's earnings power is transition-oriented rather than solely coal-dependent. Other side of the story: While segment definitions reconcile the numerical divergence, the rapid erosion of coal capacity payments represents an unmitigated threat to PGE's capital transition. According to PGE's 2035 strategy update reported by Bernstein Research on October 19, 2026, the group must fund a massive PLN 210 bn capital investment program while facing negative coal EBITDA. If the Polish government fails to secure EU state aid approvals, PGE will have to absorb massive fixed-cost overheads without any capacity revenues after 2028. This cash drain would severely restrict the capital available for networks and offshore wind, ultimately suppressing long-term earnings per share growth.

Section 5 – Strategic Reserves and Public Interest Compensation Mechanisms

Section summary
  1. Poland's proposed strategic reserve and public interest mechanisms aim to safeguard grid stability and cover fixed costs for system-critical coal plants after capacity market contracts expire.
  2. European precedents like Germany's security reserves demonstrate that structured, off-market reserve payments can successfully preserve dispatchable capacities without distorting commercial power markets.

Structural mechanics of reserve options

1.
Critical transition: PGE's long-term operational framework requires newly designed regulatory mechanisms to support system-critical coal plants after the current capacity market framework expires. According to PGE's updated Strategy 2035 published on October 19, 2026, the company assumes a growing need for additional regulatory mechanisms to cover the operational costs of coal units needed for national energy security. Management anticipates that these off-market reserve structures will provide essential fixed cost compensation packages to bridge the post-2028 funding gap. This specialized funding is vital to maintain positive earnings per share as high carbon costs depress merchant margins.
2.
Targeted support: Polish energy regulators are planning a strategic reserve mechanism that operates outside commercial electricity markets to support units unable to meet the 550 g CO₂/kWh limit. An ING Wholesale Banking report published on July 15, 2014, noted that power plants failing to enter the commercial merit order become the primary beneficiaries of reserve systems such as the operating reserve or cold reserve. These units are kept on standby to be dispatched in times of forecast power deficits rather than competing in daily auctions. 135 136 these reserve contracts will protect PGE's bottom-line earnings from the extreme cash drains associated with uncompensated standby readiness.
3.
Predictable compensation: PGE's historical operating reserve payments show how structured compensation can successfully cover cash costs for less profitable plants. Morgan Stanley research published on November 18, 2014, explained that the Polish grid operator offered a reference price of Zloty 37.13 per megawatt-hour to reserve units, which was designed to cover basic operating costs excluding depreciation and administrative overhead. This mechanism helped stabilize wholesale power prices while providing an essential financial incentive to keep aging units in the national grid. 139 140 A similar cost-plus scheme for post-2028 strategic reserves would insulate PGE's operational EPS from merchant market volatility.
4.
Operational stability: Poland's grid operator Polskie Sieci Elektroenergetyczne continues to emphasize that keeping legacy conventional units online is necessary to prevent severe power shortages. An ING Wholesale Banking article published on February 10, 2025, confirmed that while Poland's long-term energy transition targets a rapid move away from coal, conventional units remain the backbone of system reliability until nuclear and hydrogen technologies mature in the 2030s. The grid operator's investment plans require dispatchable backup to handle peak winter loads. 143, PGE can leverage its system-critical role to negotiate favorable statutory compensation that preserves group earnings stability.

Financial impacts and cost coverage

5.
Severe deterioration: PGE's legacy coal portfolio faces a dramatic profitability decline if it is forced to operate on a pure merchant basis without capacity support. Bernstein Research reported on October 19, 2026, that PGE's management expects its thermal coal generation to produce a negative EBITDA of PLN 3.3 bn in fiscal year 2030. This negative guidance reflects the complete expiration of capacity market contracts and the lack of alternative compensation. Securing a statutory cold-reserve contract would partially offset this massive deficit by covering the fixed maintenance costs of the legacy fleet, thereby protecting group EPS from dilutive write-downs.
6.
Unfavorable spreads: PGE's merchant power profitability is under severe pressure due to high carbon costs and rising renewable penetration. Bernstein Research noted on May 8, 2025, that implied forward baseload clean dark spreads are deeply negative in Poland, which heavily penalizes unhedged coal-fired generation. While capacity payments have historically stabilized conventional revenues, their phase-out after 2028 exposes PGE's 12.4 GW coal fleet to severe market risks. Without a designated public interest compensation mechanism, these negative spreads will severely erode operating cash flows and drag down future earnings.
7.
Contrary evidence: PGE's financial exposure to coal phase-out costs might be smaller than anticipated because the group has already completed massive conventional asset write-offs.[previous_sections] In its half-year interim report published on September 16, 2026, PGE disclosed a PLN 9.1 bn impairment charge on its coal assets, which significantly lowers the book value of the legacy fleet.[previous_sections] Some analysts argue that this massive balance-sheet clean-up means future write-downs will have a minimal impact on reported book value, even if merchant profitability remains depressed.[previous_sections] Therefore, the lack of immediate reserve compensation might not trigger further asset impairments, although it still represents a substantial free cash flow threat.
8.
Significant relief: PGE's ability to resume dividends relies heavily on finding a regulatory solution for its legacy coal segment. Bernstein Research highlighted on October 19, 2026, that PGE intends to resume dividend payments from fiscal year 2026 earnings, targeting a dividend yield up to 5% subject to coal business developments. If the Polish state implements a statutory compensation mechanism to cover the coal segment's fixed costs, PGE can protect its cash flow and maintain this dividend commitment. This regulatory resolution is critical to unlocking a sustained share re-rating and supporting future EPS growth.

European reserve precedents and comparisons

9.
Structured reserves: Germany's established energy framework provides a highly relevant precedent for Poland, utilizing a strategic reserve model that operates completely outside the commercial market. This robust clearing price provides substantial fixed-cost coverage for standby assets without requiring active market dispatch. If Poland adopts a comparable auction-based reserve mechanism, PGE could secure high-margin capacity revenues that bolster its conventional segment cash flows.

Operational restrictions: German transmission system operators utilize a specialized security standby mechanism to maintain mothballed coal plants for extreme emergencies. A grid development report published on July 11, 2025, explained that this reserve consists of coal-fired plants that are temporarily reactivated only as a last resort in extreme situations. This structured standby model offers a viable operational blueprint for Poland to maintain PGE's oldest coal blocks past their commercial viability.
