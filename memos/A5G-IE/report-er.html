The group provides a full suite of personal, business, and corporate banking services across its core operating segments, which include Retail Banking, Capital Markets, Climate and Infrastructure Capital, and AIB UK. Following the 2008 global financial crisis, the Irish State recapitalised AIB to protect the financial system, resulting in 99.8% government ownership of the bank's ordinary shares. Since then, the group has executed an orderly recovery and simplified its business model to facilitate a staged state sell-down. The Irish government completed its orderly exit from AIB's share register on June 17, 2025, returning the bank entirely to public markets

Over the past three years, AIB's financial fundamentals experienced a dramatic turnaround, peaking alongside the European Central Bank interest rate hike cycle. The group's profit after tax rose from €765 million in 2022 to €2,351 million in 2024, before settling slightly at €2,139 million in 2025. This exceptional delivery supported a return on tangible equity of 25.0% in 2025, comfortably exceeding the company's long-term target of 15%
5.
AIB has consistently delivered superior return metrics relative to its primary domestic rival, Bank of Ireland. For the fiscal year 2025, Bank of Ireland reported a statutory return on tangible equity of 10.9% and an adjusted return on tangible equity of 13.9%, which sits significantly below AIB's 25.0% performance. This return premium has allowed AIB to trade at a valuation premium relative to Bank of Ireland, which has historically traded at a wider discount
6.
The group's net interest margin has begun to contract from its peak of 3.16% in 2024 down to 2.73% in 2025, driven by falling rates and deposit migration. Consequently, absolute net interest income fell 9% year-onyear to €3,748 million in 2025 from €4,129 million in 2024. Despite this contraction, net interest income has passed its cyclical trough and is expected to return to modest positive growth in 2026, with updated guidance indicating net interest income will exceed €3.8 billion
7.
Bank of Ireland has also experienced net interest margin compression, with its margin falling to 2.68% in 2025 compared to 2.91% in 2024. However, because Bank of Ireland holds larger structural hedge maturities at lower historical yields, it expects its net interest income to accelerate more rapidly out to 2028 as those hedges reprice. While AIB benefited early from rising rates, Bank of Ireland's lagging hedge roll is expected to drive stronger medium-term NII outperformance relative to AIB

This represents highly disciplined cost management amid elevated inflationary pressures
9.

Although Bank of Ireland aims to reduce its cost-to-income ratio to the mid-40s by 2028, AIB's current operating efficiency of 44% remains superior. This efficiency gap underscores AIB's structural advantage in extracting higher profitability from its asset base than its closest competitor
10.

The bank's proforma CET1 ratio adjusted for the retirement of state warrants was 15.9%, which still exceeds its medium-term target capital requirements. This earnings print confirms that AIB's 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory remains fundamentally intact, underpinned by net interest income that has passed its cyclical trough and is expected to return to modest positive growth in 2026, with updated guidance indicating net interest income will exceed €3.8 billion.

Management upgraded its fullyear 2026 net interest income outlook from "circa €3.8 billion" to "greater than €3.8 billion" due to higher interest rate curves, with the ECB deposit rate now expected to stabilize at 2.5% by December 2026 instead of the previous 2% estimate.

The 25%+ IRR investment case appears achievable but increasingly mature, primarily driven by AIB's exceptional capital generation rather than sustained profitability expansion. AIB's capital generation has remained a key pillar of the investment thesis, with its fully loaded CET1 ratio ending 2025 at 16.2%, well ahead of its 14% target. The fully loaded CET1 ratio ended the H1 2026 period at 16.1% excluding interim profit accruals, and including profits, the CET1 ratio reaches an outstanding 17.8%, representing huge excess capital.

Deutsche Bank Research projected that AIB would maintain a payout ratio exceeding 100% over the next three years to gradually reduce its excess capital stack, translating into a highly attractive capital return yield of approximately 11% per annum. This extraordinary capital distribution capacity forms the primary engine of total shareholder return over the next three years, rather than multiple expansion or dramatic earnings growth.

The single most important change from the latest earnings is the definitive confirmation of net interest margin resilience despite the commencement of the ECB rate-cutting cycle. The group's net interest margin compressed to 2.68% for the half-year period, representing a decline of 10 basis points compared to the 2.78% recorded in the first half of 2025, but the sequential trajectory remains supportive as net interest income expanded by 3.4% quarter-on-quarter in the second quarter of 2026.

AIB's addition of a €10 billion hedge at an average yield of 2.3% in early 2026 was unfortunately timed relative to subsequent market movements, drawing analytical scrutiny from the sell-side. However, this defensive shift reduced annualized net interest income sensitivity to a 100 basis point rate cut down to €262 million, compared to €387 million at the end of 2025, demonstrating that management has successfully insulated the earnings base from catastrophic margin compression scenarios that plagued earlier interest rate normalization cycles.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish investment case centers on Bank of Ireland's structural hedge advantage, as Bank of Ireland holds larger structural hedge maturities at lower historical yields and expects its net interest income to accelerate more rapidly out to 2028 as those hedges reprice, with Bank of Ireland's lagging hedge roll expected to drive stronger medium-term NII outperformance relative to AIB.

Morgan Stanley's Serrano noted that AIB's high rate sensitivity still exposes the bank to margin compression if ECB rate cuts accelerate, while Deutsche Bank's Noble highlighted that Bank of Ireland will generate stronger medium-term NII growth than AIB as those hedges roll over. This structural disadvantage suggests that AIB's current valuation premium versus Bank of Ireland may narrow significantly over the next 24 to 36 months as the relative NII growth trajectories converge and eventually reverse in Bank of Ireland's favor.

A transparent 'WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND' framework requires monitoring four critical tripwires. First, if digital-first challenger banks or aggressive new entrants like Bankinter significantly ramp up deposit rates, retail deposit migration could accelerate and AIB's asset-sensitive balance sheet will face rapid margin compression and lower NII than currently modeled. Second, if Revolut accelerates its delayed mortgage rollout with aggressive pricing, a low-friction digital mortgage could trigger unprecedented customer primary switching, forcing AIB to either concede highly profitable market share or aggressively raise deposit rates.

Third, if ECB rate cuts accelerate beyond the current 2.5% terminal rate assumption to reach 2.0% or lower by end-2026, the €10 billion structural hedge completed in March 2026 will prove to have locked in uncompetitively low yields. Fourth, the unexpected resignation of Chief Financial Officer Donal Galvin on May 27, 2026, creates execution risk if the Irish government's salary caps prevent the bank from attracting high-calibre external candidates, potentially weakening market confidence in financial oversight just as the bank navigates a complex interest rate normalization cycle.

Any two of these four scenarios materializing simultaneously would fundamentally undermine the 25%+ IRR bull case. (Ireland) An outlier consensus call-out concerns the market's excessive focus on near-term NII resilience while underestimating the profound competitive threat from the Bawag-PTSB combination. The structural landscape has transformed into a highly concentrated duopoly following the complete market exit of Ulster Bank and KBC, but Bawag Group's announced €1.6 billion cash offer to acquire 100% of Permanent TSB introduces a highly capitalized and operationally efficient competitor with a proven integration playbook.

Permanent TSB currently controls approximately 19% of the Irish mortgage market and 14% of retail deposits, and Morgan Stanley's Alvaro Serrano expects Bawag to leverage its retail platform and deepen customer penetration in consumer lending and fee products, challenging AIB's dominant direct-to-consumer retail position. The top two things the reader must check next are, first, the trajectory of deposit beta evolution over the next two quarterly reporting cycles.

Management reassured investors that deposit pricing remains highly disciplined, with the overall customer deposit beta remaining stable at approximately 20% during the first half, meeting sell-side expectations and proving more resilient than historical cycles where pass-through rates shifted rapidly. If this 20% deposit beta begins to drift toward 30% or higher, it would signal that competitive deposit dynamics are deteriorating faster than management's public messaging suggests.

Second, readers must closely monitor the CFO succession process and whether AIB can attract an external candidate of sufficient caliber given the legacy remuneration constraints. This high-profile departure underscores the severe operational risk posed by the legacy Irish government salary caps of €500,000, with Chairman Jim Pettigrew continuing to engage with the Minister for Finance to advocate for lifting executive remuneration restrictions, warning that AIB remains at a severe competitive disadvantage in retaining top talent compared to non-bailed-out European peers.

The quality and timing of this appointment will serve as a critical litmus test for whether AIB can sustain institutional excellence as it transitions from state-owned recovery story to privately held dividend compounder.
