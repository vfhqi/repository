Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Resolving BT Group's TNT Sports joint venture exposure will directly determine the trajectory of its post- peak capex cash flows and mid-term earnings growth.

Investigating the Key Question

1.
Key Question: "BT Group plc (BT.A-GB), the UK's integrated fixed and mobile telecoms incumbent, reporting Openreach, Consumer (BT, EE and Plusnet broadband, mobile and TV), Business (UK enterprise, public-sector and small-business connectivity and IT), and TalkTalk with its wholesale unit PlatformX; its International unit is a discontinued operation being contributed to a 50/50 joint venture with Verizon (completion expected in the second half of 2027). What is BT Group plc's current financial exposure to the TNT Sports joint venture with Warner Bros. Discovery after the minimum revenue guarantee period ended in October 2026, including any change in BT's content and wholesale payments, the value and terms of the C preference shares (£184m), the likelihood and price of Warner Bros. Discovery exercising its call option over BT's remaining interest, and the expected effect on BT's EBITDA and normalised free cash flow from FY27 onward?"
2.
Significant scope: This comprehensive report was compiled after identifying 42 relevant sources, 6 distinct companies, and 3 comparable precedents across industry filings, transcripts, and investment research. All identified sources and companies were utilized to analyze BT Group's financial exposure, with no material exclusions. This analysis underpins our assessment of how sports media rights negotiations influence midterm earnings per share trajectories.
3.
Direct change: The resolution of the sports joint venture will directly impact BT Group's future profitability and dividend coverage. Transitioning to a fully variable cost model after the minimum revenue guarantee expires will eliminate a material drag on the group's financial performance. Reductions in structural operating cash outflows are expected to substantially boost normalised free cash flow from FY27 onward.
4.
Closely related: Relevant strategic questions focus on how BT Group will allocate its newly liberated capital to deleverage its balance sheet. Key issues include whether the company will prioritize debt reduction to achieve its targeted credit ratings or enhance shareholder distributions. Furthermore, questions remain regarding how competing UK internet service providers will respond to BT's restructured sports content distribution model over time.

BT Group's UK-Centric Strategy

5.
Domestic focus: BT Group's domestic business relies heavily on its Consumer and Business customer-facing units. The Consumer unit serves individual households with mobile, broadband, and TV products, while the Business segment provides digital connectivity to enterprise and public-sector clients. Achieving stable customer bases in these divisions is critical for stabilising consolidated service revenues amidst fierce retail pricing competition.
6.
Rapid build: Openreach represents the group's highly regulated wholesale division and is currently driving a nationwide full-fibre rollout. Management expects to reach its ambitious 25m premises target by March 2027. Although broadband line losses remain a headwind, the division's structural margins are expected to improve as high-margin fibre connections continue to scale nationally.
7.
Strategic acquisition: BT Group recently acquired TalkTalk and its wholesale asset PlatformX out of administration for a total cash impact of approximately £400m in FY27. These assets are currently held operationally separate under a Competition and Markets Authority pre-emptive action order. Stabilising TalkTalk's customer base is critical since the business historically contributed to more than half of Openreach wholesale line losses prior to the transaction.
8.
Global exit: BT Group is deconsolidating its International enterprise business by contributing it to a 50/50 joint venture with Verizon Communications. Expected to close in the second half of 2027, the transaction includes a $625m equalization payment to BT. This transaction allows management to exit non-core operations and maintain a sharp focus on domestic UK connectivity investments.

Section 2 – Content and Wholesale Payments After the Minimum Revenue Guarantee

Section summary
  1. Transitioning to fully variable payments post-October 2026 will significantly reduce BT Group's cash obligations.
  2. Confidence in the payment reduction is high based on explicit contractual details in BT's interim filings.

Historical Payment Obligations

1.
Massive obligations: BT Group historically faced heavy financial commitments under its initial four-year agreement with the sports joint venture. The telecom provider committed to a minimum revenue guarantee of approximately £500m per annum running until the end of July 2026. These structured payments created a substantial drag on operating cash flows during the early years of the venture.
2.
Heavy settlements: BT Group paid significant additional cash sums to settle its minimum guarantee liability over successive fiscal periods. Financial reports show additional settlement payments reached £191m in fiscal year 2026 and £187m in fiscal year 2025. These payments were made in addition to standard content purchases from the joint venture.
3.
Continued cash drain: BT Group experienced further cash outflows during the final phases of the minimum guarantee period in late 2026. Additional payments to settle the minimum guarantee liability amounted to £102m in the first half of fiscal year 2027. These ongoing outlays temporarily depressed normalised free cash flows before the structural transition.
4.
Rights funding: BT Group scheduled specific cash payments to cover annual sports rights costs of approximately £305m. The group scheduled six-monthly payments of £153m each from February 2025 through February 2027 to meet these commitments. This rigid payment structure limited short-term capital allocation flexibility for core telecom networks.

Transition to Variable Terms

5.
Contractual realignment: BT Group successfully transitioned its distribution agreement to a fully variable cost model in late 2026. The termination of the minimum revenue guarantee in October 2026 allows payments to vary directly with the active subscriber base. This structural shift is highly earnings accretive for the group over the medium term because variable costs scale down with volume declines.
6.
Realignment details: BT Group realigned its economic relationship with Warner Bros. Discovery to a flat 50-50 structure on September 30, 2026. The termination of the group's earn-out entitlement coincided with the carrying value of its A preference shares reducing to nil. This transition simplifies the underlying corporate structure of the partnership going forward.
7.
Operating costs: BT Group recorded standard content purchases from the sports joint venture separate from guarantee settlements. Purchases from the joint venture totaled £155m in the first half of fiscal year 2027 compared to £160m in the prior year period. Operating cost savings should accelerate under the variable model as retail subscriber trends evolve.
8.
Underlying profitability: The sports joint venture reported narrow statutory losses but remained profitable on an underlying basis before accounting adjustments. Financial adjustments to align with group policies included the amortisation of acquired intangibles from the transferred BT Sport and Eurosport UK assets. These adjustments mask the joint venture's true trading health as a self-sustaining entity.
9.
Deconsolidation impacts: Deconsolidating the sports business has fundamentally altered BT Group's revenue and operating cost structures. The structural transaction eliminated approximately £0.5bn to £0.6bn in annual consolidated revenues relative to historical levels. This shift successfully de-risked BT's ongoing exposure to sports inflation.
10.
Contrary evidence: BT Group may still face persistent cash flow pressures if the joint venture requires emergency short-term funding. Although the previous cash pooling arrangement ceased, BT committed to a revised £200m revolving credit facility shared equally with Warner Bros. Discovery. This obligation means the group remains exposed to potential cash calls from the joint venture.

The cable operator demanded significant price reductions for a new three-year deal due to declining subscriber viewing figures. This dispute illustrates the growing resistance among pay-TV distributors to high fixed content costs.

Retail aggregation: BT Group integrated rival content directly into its consumer television packages to boost user retention. The group integrated Sky's NOW TV offerings including sports and entertainment alongside TNT Sports on its unified platform. This aggregation strategy aims to protect broadband and TV customer bases from intensifying competitive pressures.

Peer comparisons: Virgin Media O2 actively matches BT's premium content packaging by bundling broadband with Sky Sports and Netflix. Peer operators utilize high-value content bundles and wifi speed guarantees to attract premium customers. This intensive bundling depresses overall industry video margins across the UK telecom landscape.

Aggressive speedups: Virgin Media O2 implemented significant upgrades across its network infrastructure to match Openreach's full-fibre deployment. The cable operator plans to introduce low-cost speed upgrades to deliver up to 2 Gbps across its footprint by 2027. This aggressive roll-out heightens retail pricing competition for BT's consumer division.

Satellite options: BT Group delayed the proactive launch of its satellite broadband partnership with Starlink due to external geopolitical controversies. In contrast, Virgin Media O2 moved ahead with its own Starlink agreement to expand backup options. This competitive divergence creates a temporary technological gap in rural broadband options.

Wholesale Distribution Dynamics

16.
Wholesale relationships: BT Group's Openreach division maintains critical wholesale relationships with key retail competitors to support infrastructure investment. Sky represents the largest third-party wholesale customer on Openreach's nationwide fiber network. Securing long-term wholesale commitments from Sky is critical for Openreach's infrastructure monetization strategy.

Wholesale competition: CityFibre emerged as a serious wholesale challenger to Openreach by securing its own agreement with Sky. This competitive threat forced BT to enhance its customer service and sales organization to defend its market share. Increased wholesale competition risks deflating UK fiber access pricing over the long term.

Strategic consolidation: BT Group's acquisition of TalkTalk and PlatformX helps consolidate its dominant position in the wholesale market. TalkTalk represents an attractive wholesale customer that will now be retained on the Openreach network. This defensive transaction helps stabilize Openreach wholesale lines against alternative network threats.

Other side of the story: BT Group's exit from direct sports broadcasting may weaken its long-term retail broadband differentiation. Section 3 – Terms, Valuation, and Accounting of the C Preference Shares

Section summary
  1. BT Group expects to receive the £184m cash settlement from its Class C preference shares during the second half of fiscal year 2027.
  2. The carrying value has increased to reflect the present value of the economic realignment with high accounting confidence.

Share Terms and Structural Characteristics

1.
Substantial holding: BT Group holds 429,015,593 Class C redeemable preference shares within the capital structure of the TNT Sports joint venture. These shares carry a nominal value of £429.02m and represent a vital financial instrument alongside Class A preference shares. This large preference share position was designed to protect BT's historical capital contribution while the partners execute the realignment of the joint venture assets during the transition.
2.
Contractual yield: The joint venture agreement stipulates that the Class C preference shares entitle BT Group to a fixed cumulative cash preferential dividend at an annual rate of 1% of nominal value. This 1% dividend is scheduled to be paid on 31 July each year, provided there are sufficient distributable profits in the joint venture. This cumulative yield is time-limited and remained payable through July 2026 under the original transaction agreements.
3.
Evolving value: BT Group recorded the carrying value of its Class C preference shares at £184m as of 30 September 2026, marking a steady increase from the £175m carrying value reported on 31 March 2026. This balance sheet position represents the present value of the cash amount BT Group expects to receive upon completion of the economic realignment. Management expects the cash settlement of these preference shares to take place in the second half of fiscal year 2027 as scheduled.
4.
Compound classification: TNT Sports Broadcasting Limited accounts for these preference shares as compound instruments containing both a liability component and an equity component under local reporting standards. The joint venture split the Class C shares into a £14.15m liability portion and a £414.87m residual equity portion. This accounting treatment isolates the fixed dividend obligation from the remaining equity portion which does not change in value until redeemed or sold.

This amortised cost is adjusted periodically based on revised estimates of expected cash flows, directly influencing the reported net finance expenses of the joint venture.

Section 4 – Warner Bros. Discovery Call Option Likelihood and Pricing

Section summary
  1. Warner Bros. Discovery is highly likely to delay exercising its call option due to extreme debt constraints post-merger.
  2. Accounting confidence is medium-high based on explicit option terms and impaired carrying values in BT's filings.

Option Terms and Pricing Mechanics

1.
Contractual mechanics: BT Group plc's exit strategy relies on a call option held by Warner Bros. Discovery to purchase BT's remaining 50% interest in the TNT Sports joint venture. The pricing is contractually defined as 50% of the fair market value of the Sports JV, to be determined at the time of exercise, plus any unpaid fixed consideration and remaining earn-out. This structured formula ensures that the final buyout price aligns closely with current trading performance of the joint venture asset.
2.
Impairment headwinds: The valuation of the joint venture has been severely depressed by recent content bidding outcomes. Specifically, the loss of UEFA broadcasting rights for the 2027-2031 cycle prompted BT to impair its equity-accounted interest in the Sports JV to nil. This lack of equity value suggests that the fair-marketvalue component of the option price could be nominal at exercise.
3.
Broker perspective: Rothschild & Co Redburn notes that BT's payments to the joint venture will transition to a fully variable model after the minimum revenue guarantee expires. The broker expects BT to receive around £400m to £600m in cash proceeds from Warner Bros. Discovery and the joint venture over the next two years. These anticipated cash inflows will significantly strengthen BT's balance sheet position during its network upgrade cycle.
4.
Market headwinds: Deutsche Bank Research continues to evaluate Warner Bros. Discovery on a standalone basis but maintains a cautious outlook due to structural shifts in linear advertising markets. The broker emphasizes that the broader linear television business remains under pressure, although cost discipline continues to support near-term margins. Consequently, any acquisition of BT's remaining sports interest must compete with alternative streaming investments.

Timeline and Execution Constraints

5.
Execution windows: BT Group plc's transaction agreement structured specific timelines for Warner Bros. Discovery to execute its call option. BT's Chief Executive Allison Kirkby confirmed that the agreement included two call option windows, with one already expired and the second active in late 2026. The final contractual processes associated with this option remain active and ongoing into fiscal year 2027.
6.
Substantive control: BT Group plc must continually evaluate whether the active call option shifts unilateral operational control to its partner. Management concluded that BT still retained joint control of the Sports JV as of 30 September 2026, meaning classification as a joint venture remains appropriate. This ongoing joint governance ensures that BT retains equal voting rights over key decisions until a formal buyout occurs.
7.
Contrary evidence: BT Group plc may face a lower buyout valuation or transactional delays due to weak commercial momentum within the sports business. The joint venture suffered a major blow when it lost the UEFA broadcasting rights for the 2027-2031 cycle, which eliminated a key long-term revenue driver. Because of this loss, the standalone valuation of the joint venture has been significantly impaired.

Strategic and Financial Drivers

8.
Strategic rationale: Warner Bros. Discovery has a strong strategic incentive to fully integrate the UK sports broadcasting assets into its broader digital ecosystem to eliminate brand redundancies. Integrating TNT Sports with the Eurosport footprint allows WBD to create a unified premium sports proposition in Europe. This consolidated offering is crucial for driving direct-to-consumer streaming subscriber growth and maximizing the long-term lifetime value of its customers.
9.
Streaming integration: Warner Bros. Discovery is leveraging local premium sports content to accelerate the international expansion of its Max streaming platform. Operating a unified streaming product allows the media giant to bundle general entertainment with live sports, significantly lowering customer acquisition costs. This digital transition is vital for WBD because direct-to-consumer revenues must offset linear declines in its global networks business.
10.
Debt obstacles: Warner Bros. Discovery faces severe balance sheet constraints that restrict its ability to execute cash-funded acquisitions. WBD reported a massive gross debt load of $33.1bn and a net leverage ratio of 3.4x as of June 30, 2026. This heavy debt burden limits financial flexibility for opportunistic buyouts unless WBD can generate substantial free cash flows or rely on third-party equity funding.

Acquisition leverage: Warner Bros. Discovery's capital structure will be further complicated by its pending merger with Paramount Skydance Corporation. Ratings agency Fitch downgraded WBD's credit rating to BB following the transaction close due to pro forma leverage rising to 7.8x in fiscal year 2026. This elevated leverage means that WBD must prioritize debt repayment over expansion to stabilize its credit metrics and avoid further downgrades from rating agencies.

This persistent debt burden means that WBD will likely delay non-core cash outflows like sports joint venture buyouts.

Discovery may hesitate to absorb additional sport liabilities unless they directly drive streaming profitability.
