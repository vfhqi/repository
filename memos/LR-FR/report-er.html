Section 16 – Conclusion

Legrand's H1 2026 earnings fundamentally altered the investment case by confirming that unprecedented datacenter hypergrowth can entirely offset persistent European building weakness, transforming the company from a cyclical construction play into a structural digital infrastructure beneficiary. The results delivered organic growth above 30% in datacenters, which now represent 32% of group revenues, prompting management to raise full-year datacenter organic growth guidance to an aggressive 25% to 30% range.

This exceptional momentum enabled the company to upgrade its overall FY 2026 sales growth guidance to 16% to 19%, with organic growth lifted to 8% to 10% from the prior 4% to 7% projection. The H1 results decisively validated the multi-year M&A transformation that repositioned Legrand's historical building-heavy portfolio toward highmargin whitespace physical infrastructure, establishing the group as a pure-play specialist capable of delivering double-digit compounding through the end of the decade.

The core investment thesis now rests on three critical pillars that were empirically confirmed by the H1 2026 results. First, Legrand's accelerating M&A velocity –completing seven value-creating acquisitions in H1 2026 that added approximately €450 million in annual revenue –demonstrates disciplined capital allocation with post-tax returns on capital employed expected to reach twice the weighted average cost of capital by 2025. Second, the group remains firmly on track toward its Ambition 2030 target of €12 billion to €15 billion in total sales, having already delivered over half of its incremental €2.5 billion revenue goal through outstanding datacenter demand. Third, management's defensive positioning on the 800V DC architectural transition– confirming that 100% of current orders remain on traditional AC architectures while hybrid sidecar systems represent only 10% to 20% of new IT loads by 2030–alleviates near-term substitution risks. These three forces combine to support a structural growth acceleration that narrows Legrand's historical organic growth discount relative to diversified electrical peers.

However, the strongest counter-hypothesis challenges whether this datacenter-driven transformation can sustainably offset the group's persistent building exposure vulnerability. Legrand remains highly exposed to construction markets with around 60% of group revenues tied directly to non-residential and residential buildings, preventing immediate relative margin expansion compared to grid-exposed capital goods competitors and leaving investors concerned that residential building softness will drag down near-term earnings growth.

The H1 results validated this concern as adjusted EBITA margin of 20.9% missed consensus by 55 basis points due to weak European margins, driven by a substantial profitability shortfall in Europe that offset North American gains. Europe's organic sales contracted by -2.4% in H1 2026 as persistent volume demand depression across Germany and France reflected a broader building construction slowdown. If high interest rates persistently freeze residential permitting activities well into 2027, Legrand's core buildings segment could deteriorate further, sparking intense margin pressure from underutilized local plants and proving that the company remains a cyclical building component play rather than a pure-play datacenter compounder.
