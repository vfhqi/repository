Massive inventory accumulation dominates the asset base of Rémy Cointreau due to the long maturation periods required for premium cognacs. This high concentration of non-liquid strategic assets creates a substantial working capital drag during periods of soft consumer demand.

Historical cost accounting dictates that Rémy Cointreau values its aging spirit inventories at purchase cost plus directly attributable aging expenses like cask depreciation. This valuation methodology excludes interest expenses and does not adjust for the time value of money, meaning the balance sheet carrying value remains conservative.

Over time, the commercial value of these inventories rises significantly relative to cost as the liquids mature into premium categories like VSOP and XO. However, this intrinsic valuation support only benefits the company if depletions pick up significant top-line momentum in key global markets. Extreme balance sheet expansion is highlighted by the ratio of aging inventory to annual sales, which has reached historic highs.
