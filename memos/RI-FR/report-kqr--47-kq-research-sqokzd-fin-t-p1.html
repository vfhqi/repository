Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding fixed-cost operating leverage and cost elimination scope is crucial for investors as it directly dictates Pernod Ricard's earnings trajectory and valuation recovery amidst severe US and China headwinds.

Core Investment Question

1.
Core inquiry: "Pernod Ricard SA (RI-FR), the world's second-largest premium spirits group (Jameson, Absolut, Chivas Regal, Martell, Ballantine's, Malibu, Royal Stag, Blenders Pride, plus Mumm and Perrier-Jouët champagne), organised since January 2026 into two brand units (aged spirits and champagne; clear spirits and ready-to-drink) and ten market management entities, with the US, India, China, Europe and Global Travel Retail its key markets.
2.
Key question: how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
3.
operating leverage from weak US and China sales continues to depress margins. Morgan Stanley on September 29, 2026, projected an organic operating profit decline of 2.3% for fiscal year 2027, driven by adverse geographic mix and high structural overheads. This dynamic demonstrates how fixed cost operational leverage amplifies revenue declines, directly translating top-line pressure into compressed earnings per share unless cost savings are accelerated.
4.
Elevated balance sheet: Pernod Ricard carries high debt that amplifies valuation risks during periods of negative operating leverage. Rothschild & Co Redburn on October 19, 2026, highlighted that the group's leverage is elevated at 3.6x net debt to EBITDA for fiscal year 2027. High interest expenses combined with declining cash flows risk constraining capital allocation decisions and dividend coverage. This financial strain highlights why executing the EUR 1 bn efficiency plan is vital to protect the group's credit profile and earnings per share.
5.
Comparative margin sensitivity: Pernod Ricard's premium spirits peers exhibit similar margin vulnerability when high fixed costs collide with volume downturns. Jefferies on September 29, 2026, reported that Pernod's peer Diageo faces a comparable task of implementing a new USD 1 bn cost-cutting targets to defend organic margins. This peer comparison shows that the entire premium spirits sector has turned to aggressive self-help programs because pricing power alone can no longer offset inflation and negative leverage, leaving sector earnings highly sensitive to execution timing.

Valuation and Capital Impact

6.
Severe multiple contraction: Pernod Ricard's valuation has collapsed to historical lows as negative operating leverage and high debt discourage investors. Deutsche Bank on October 15, 2026, reported that the company's shares trade at a cheap forward price-to-earnings multiple of 11.5x, representing a significant discount to the wider European beverages sector at 15.7x. This deep discount reflects an uncomfortable mixture of depressed volume growth expectations and leverage anxieties. Consequently, a re-rating remains unlikely unless the group proves it can expand earnings per share through structural cost-cutting.
7.
Contrary evidence: Pernod Ricard's valuation multiple is ultimately dictated by organic sales growth rather than self-help efficiency programs. Morgan Stanley on September 14, 2026, noted that while the group has driven higher savings, it is group organic sales growth that determines the multiple at which it trades. This analysis suggests that cost cuts alone cannot support multiple expansion if underlying volumes decline and brands remain underinvested. Therefore, focusing solely on cost-cutting overstates the potential benefit to earnings per share if core volume remains weak.
8.
Other side of the story: Pernod Ricard might achieve a more rapid earnings per share recovery if a partial initial public offering of its Indian business is approved. Bernstein on October 16, 2026, reported that a potential listing of a 20% stake in India could significantly reduce group balance sheet leverage and be modestly accretive to earnings. This strategic transaction would immediately lower finance costs, taking pressure off the dividend and improving cash flow. Such a structural catalyst could expand valuation multiples even if core operating margins remain under pressure from near-term destocking.

Section 2 – Fixed versus Variable Cost Structure

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard maintains a highly rigid operating cost structure where fixed structure costs and aged inventory dynamics limit near-term flexibility.
  2. High fixed overheads from extensive route-to-market distribution networks in emerging markets elevate the group's operating leverage compared to peers.

Cost of Sales Dissection

1.
Elevated production inputs: Pernod Ricard reported cost of sales of €3,909 million for fiscal year 2026 alongside a gross profit of €5,495 million. This cost base is heavily driven by liquid maturation and packaging. Analyst estimates from Jefferies on January 27, 2026, split these cost of sales into wet goods at 33% and dry goods at 24%. This high exposure to agricultural commodities makes gross margins highly sensitive to raw material inflation.
2.
Massive operating footprint: Pernod Ricard operates with a total cost of sales exceeding €3.9 billion alongside a gross profit margin of 58.4% for fiscal year 2026. This profit profile is supported by premium brand positioning but remains highly vulnerable to input cost swings. While dry goods and packaging expenses can be hedged, agricultural liquid inputs are subject to long-term structural inflation. Therefore, the absolute size of this cost base remains the primary area for structural savings under current efficiency targets.
3.
Rigid processing operations: Pernod Ricard faces substantial fixed conversion costs that limit immediate supply chain adjustment during volume downturns. The group's cost of sales contains manufacturing conversion of 14% and logistics and warehousing of 14% based on Jefferies estimates from January 27, 2026. These elements carry high fixed components due to distillery maintenance and long-term storage facility leases. Consequently, volume declines lead to negative fixed-cost absorption that immediately compresses gross margins.
4.
Taxation and auxiliary expenses: Pernod Ricard faces unavoidable geographic cost friction through import duties which account for 9% of its cost of sales. According to Jefferies estimates on January 27, 2026, other production costs represent the remaining 6% of the group's cost of sales. These regulatory and operational charges are highly variable but offer virtually no scope for discretionary cost elimination. This rigid breakdown leaves the group highly exposed to tariff increases such as those recently witnessed in key markets.
5.
Maturing inventory drag: Pernod Ricard maintains a substantial stock of aging spirits that introduces a multi-year cost lag to the profit and loss statement. Jefferies noted on January 27, 2026, that handling costs and natural evaporation losses of these maturing liquids are continuously capitalized into inventory values. This accounting treatment means high-inflation liquids laid down in prior years will eventually pass through cost of sales irrespective of current demand. Consequently, near-term cost-cutting efforts in procurement cannot immediately offset historical production inflation.

Operating Overhead Structure

6.
Substantial structural burden: Pernod Ricard reported structure costs of €1,663 million and advertising and promotion expenses of €1,409 million for fiscal year 2026. These structural outlays represent 17.7% and 15.0% of net sales respectively, reflecting high fixed organizational overheads. Jefferies on January 27, 2026, estimated that structure costs represent 53% of the group's total operating expenses, while advertising and promotion accounts for 47%. This split demonstrates that more than half of operating expenses are locked in administrative and personnel functions.
7.
Discretionary marketing levers: Pernod Ricard utilizes its massive advertising and promotion budget as a key operational safety valve during revenue contractions. The group reported marketing outlays of €1,409 million for fiscal year 2026, representing 15.0% of net sales, which was at the lower end of its historical 16% target. Management was able to scale back this spend during Covid-19 lockdowns, prioritizing immediate variable cost conservation. This tactical flexibility demonstrates that while structural overheads remain fixed, marketing outlays represent a highly cuttable variable cost line during sudden shocks.
8.
Expensive distribution networks: Pernod Ricard carries higher organizational overheads than peers due to its extensive direct distribution networks in emerging regions. Jefferies observed on January 27, 2026, that the group maintains sizable sales teams and runs proprietary distribution networks in India, China, and Africa. While these route-to-market investments provide excellent long-term local execution, they also create a high fixed cost base. This high structural density reduces operating margin flexibility when regional volumes face cyclical pressure.
9.
Contrary evidence: Pernod Ricard can quickly lower its operational cost base by implementing aggressive reorganization frameworks to eliminate structural roles. The company reported on November 20, 2026, that its Fit for Future program achieved an 18% cumulative headcount reduction since June 2023, representing approximately 3,600 roles. This aggressive restructuring supported an organic decline in structure costs of 8.0% during fiscal year 2026. This evidence suggests that structure costs are far more flexible and cuttable than historical patterns indicate.
10.
Persistent depreciation charge: Pernod Ricard faces non-cash operating charges that remain highly rigid due to past heavy capital investments. Bernstein financial forecasts on October 16, 2026, projected group depreciation of fixed assets at €413 million for fiscal year 2026, consistent with historical levels. This persistent charge is expected to remain stable at around €412 million in fiscal year 2027 and €418 million in fiscal year 2028. Because depreciation is dictated by historical asset capitalisation, it remains entirely fixed and insensitive to short-term volume fluctuations.

Peer Expense Comparisons

11.
Peer operational variance: Diageo displays a different operational cost profile where excise duties represent a significantly larger portion of the total cost base. Jefferies estimates on January 27, 2026, showed that excise duties represent 31% of Diageo's operating expenses, while raw materials account for 21% and marketing represents 15%. In comparison, Diageo's staff costs represent 10% and depreciation stands at 7% of its total expense structure. This lighter personnel exposure allows Diageo to leverage its massive global scale more efficiently during market recoveries.
12.
Peer overhead comparison: Davide Campari-Milano and United Spirits maintain highly distinct structural profiles that highlight Pernod's elevated emerging market distribution burden. Jefferies on January 27, 2026, indicated that Pernod's overhead is structurally higher than Diageo's because of these extensive local distribution channels. This heavy sales team infrastructure raises the group's selling, general, and administrative expenses relative to the wider industry. Consequently, Pernod's local operating profitability remains highly sensitive to volume swings within these specific regional markets.
13.
Elevated regional exposure: Pernod Ricard carries distinct margin profiles across its core geographies that dictate overall group operating leverage. Rothschild & Co Redburn on October 19, 2026, noted that while the group is highly diversified, regional headwinds in the US and China continue to weigh on performance. Because domestic platforms like United Spirits in India face local excise duty pressures, structural costs remain high across emerging territories. These persistent regional headwinds prevent the group from easily expanding its structural margins back toward past peak levels.
14.
80 81 & Co Redburn Other side of the story: Pernod Ricard's high structural fixed costs actually position the group to deliver explosive margin expansion once sales volumes recover. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted on January 30, 2026, that after periods of heavy capacity investment, positive operating leverage returns rapidly with top-line growth. Section 3 – Margin Sensitivity and Historical Volume Cycles

Section summary
  1. Premium spirits groups exhibit extreme margin volatility across demand cycles due to high operating leverage and rigid matured inventory cost structures.
  2. Structural market interventions such as antiextravagance campaigns and tariff shocks historically trigger severe margin compression that outweighs standard cost self-help programs.

Historical Downturn Cycles

1.
Severe gross compression: Brown-Forman experienced significant margin headwinds during the 2008–2009 global financial crisis when distributors cut inventories to conserve cash. The company saw its gross margins contract by 270 basis points in the second quarter of fiscal year 2009 as foreign exchange and value-pack promotions pressured results. Gross profit declined further by 200 basis points in the subsequent quarter due to geographic and portfolio mix shifts. This shows how macroeconomic downturns quickly compromise high-margin premium spirit portfolios unless cost-control measures are initiated immediately.
2.
Historical cyclical patterns: Rémy Cointreau has navigated six distinct earnings downgrade cycles over the past three decades, showcasing its vulnerability to global shocks. The group experienced severe demand destruction during the Asian financial crisis in the late 1990s, the post-dotcom slowdown in 2001, and the global financial crisis in 2008. Each downturn demonstrates that luxury spirits cannot easily bypass macro headwinds despite carrying strong brand equity. Consequently, these historical patterns indicate that operating margins remain highly sensitive to external trade and consumer environments.
3.
Policy driven distress: Pernod Ricard faced significant top-line and margin pressure in Asia during the fiscal year 2013–14 campaign against conspicuous consumption. The anti-extravagance campaign in China forced lowsingle digit organic operating profit declines for the regional business. Management was unable to offset this contraction through pricing due to sudden distributor panic. This historical precedent highlights how regional regulatory interventions disrupt premium spirits profit margins rapidly.
4.
Cognac demand collapse: Rémy Cointreau suffered a massive blow from the Chinese anti-extravagance campaign, with Rémy Martin organic sales plunging 32% in the third quarter of fiscal year 2014. During the first half of fiscal year 2014, group net sales fell 3.6% organically, driving an organic current operating profit decline of €10.3 million. The group defended its operating margin near 23% by adjusting advertising outlays. This collapse demonstrates that high inventory weight creates severe asymmetry when shipments fall out of line with depletions.
5.
Contrary evidence: LVMH demonstrated superior relative resilience during the 2013–16 Chinese luxury downturn because of its diversified product portfolio. While the Wines & Spirits division faced a negative impact from gifting, the group maintained mid-single digit growth in other luxury segments. This suggests that diversified luxury conglomerates can withstand spirits-specific cyclical shocks better than pure-play distillers. Therefore, the margin sensitivity of a premium portfolio is not uniform across different corporate structures.

Pandemic Shifts and Rebounds

6.
Sudden margin distortion: Rémy Cointreau saw its organic sales fall 11% in fiscal year 2020 as global lockdowns shut on-premise venues. Despite this top-line decline, the group's gross margin expanded by 290 basis points due to a deliberate exit from low-margin Partner Brands. However, because structural costs were not adjusted to compensate for the lost sales, operating margins fell 250 basis points to 21.0%. This indicates that inflexible structural overheads limit profitability during sudden volume contractions.
7.
Volatile division shifts: LVMH Wines & Spirits experienced severe regional disruption during the pandemic, with Champagne bearing the brunt of early COVID-19 restrictions. Conversely, Cognac & Spirits peaked at 65% of divisional EBIT in 2020 as home consumption supported Hennessy sales. But the subsequent unwinding of these trends shows that divisional margin performance remains volatile over the full cycle. This shows that near-term consumer shifts do not guarantee long-term margin stability.
8.
Post pandemic expansion: Pernod Ricard captured explosive positive operating leverage during the post- pandemic recovery in fiscal year 2023. The group reported a 12% organic sales increase in the first half of fiscal year 2023, while organic profit from recurring operations rose 12%. Operating margins expanded by 65 basis points on an organic basis as price increases offset input costs. This period proves that positive operating leverage drives explosive growth when volumes and pricing align.

Modern Destocking Cycle

10.
Severe modern squeeze: Pernod Ricard faced modern volume pressure as organic sales declined 3.9% in fiscal year 2026. The group's organic operating margin fell 35 basis points as negative operating leverage from key markets took hold. Management responded by lowering organic structure costs by 8.0% and A&P spend by 10.5% in fiscal year 2026. This modern cycle shows that extreme cost discipline is required to defend margins during cyclical volume down-cycles.
11.
Cognac inventory correction: Rémy Cointreau suffered double-digit organic sales and EBIT declines in fiscal year 2024 as destocking hit the United States. Group sales fell 15.9% organically in the first half of fiscal year 2025, forcing current operating profit down 17.6%. Management expects full-year organic operating margins to land between 21% and 22% as destocking persists. This demonstrates how destocking cycles amplify margin degradation when high-margin regions underperform.
12.
Divisional margin contraction: LVMH Wines & Spirits reported an 8% organic sales decline in the fourth quarter of calendar year 2024, highlighting ongoing industry weakness. The division's operating margin plummeted to 23.1% in fiscal year 2024, representing its lowest level in over 25 years. This severe contraction was driven by a painful mixture of lower volume, falling prices, and negative product mix. This historical low confirms that even dominant luxury portfolios experience deleverage during category downturns.
13.
Whiskey capacity pressure: Brown-Forman reported flat organic sales and a 10% decrease in operating income for fiscal year 2026 due to higher SG&A and impairments. For fiscal year 2027, the company guided for flat sales and a 3% to 5% decline in organic operating profit. Gross margins face severe pressure from expensive barreled whiskey laid down at peak inflation in 2021–22. This shows that aged-liquid cost lag delays recovery during modern spirits down-cycles.
14.
Agave and wood cost pressure: Brown-Forman's modern operating margin is highly sensitive to input costs such as wood for barrels and natural gas. The massive build-up of barreled whiskey inventories, which grew 33% since late 2022, creates structural risk. Although production has been reduced to rebalance supply, higher costs of historic fills continue to weigh on the P&L. This proves that long production cycles limit immediate flexibility when consumer demand cools down.
15.
- Conference Other side of the story: Premium spirits producers can protect their structural margins by implementing strict cost-efficiency programs and pivoting to growing emerging markets. United Spirits in India grew its sales by 7% in late 2024, offering a structural hedge against wider Western destocking. This geographic diversification combined with targeted self-help can mitigate negative operating leverage. Consequently, companies that manage regional mix effectively will achieve faster margin stabilization than peers locked in binary US- China distribution channels.

Section 5 – Advertising, Promotion, and Aged-Liquid Inflation Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard maintains flexible short-term control over its marketing outlays but remains constrained by long maturation cycles of inventory.
  2. Multi-year lag effects from high-inflation aged inventory will continue to pressure gross margins before cheaper production runs provide financial relief.

Brand Spend and Peer Intensity

1.
Optimized marketing allocation: Pernod Ricard reported advertising and promotion expenses of €1,409 million for fiscal year 2026, representing 15.0% of net sales. This spend represents a substantial 10.5% organic reduction from the prior year as management optimized non-working spend. While this cut helped defend short-term profitability, management maintains a medium-term target to keep brand investment at 16% of net sales.
2.
Elevated premium investment: Rémy Cointreau maintains a structurally higher marketing intensity than Pernod Ricard with its advertising and promotion ratio reaching 19.7% of net sales. Despite this premium positioning, the cognac house implemented significant reductions over fiscal years 2024 and 2025, capturing €70 million in structural savings. These deep reductions demonstrate that even the most prestigious spirits portfolios must sacrifice marketing outlays when faced with severe volume downturns.
3.
Aggressive brand building: Davide Campari-Milano has chosen a contrasting strategy by upweighting its brand outlays to defend market relevance. Campari reported advertising and promotional expenses at 17.4% of net sales for the first half of fiscal year 2026, up from 16.6% in the prior year period. This aggressive spending pattern represents a deliberate effort to fund marketing using structural overhead savings from its ongoing administrative cost containment program.
4.
Severe marketing retrenchment: Diageo executed a substantial 13% organic marketing reduction during fiscal year 2026 to defend its operating profit. This retrenchment included a massive $210 million cut to advertising and promotion, which alone provided a 208 basis points organic benefit to the group's operating margin. However, analysts warn that such aggressive marketing expenditure cuts could make it highly challenging for Diageo to reverse ongoing market share losses in North America.
5.
Contrary evidence: Pernod Ricard cannot easily restore its marketing spend to the targeted 16% of net sales without causing severe operating margin compression. Analysts at Morgan Stanley on July 13, 2026, argued that intense competitive pricing from Diageo and aggressive marketing from Rémy Cointreau will limit Pernod's ability to cut or optimize its A&P spend further. This suggests that rather than being a flexible variable cost lever, marketing outlays must remain high, pressure profitability, and restrict earnings growth.

Aged Inventory and Inflation Peaks

6.
Lagged liquid pressure: Pernod Ricard's gross margins face multi-year pressure because the high inflation of aged liquid laid down in 2021–23 is now passing through the cost of sales. Bernstein on October 1, 2026, highlighted that this expensive inventory has begun flowing through the profit and loss statement. Consequently, these elevated input costs will continue to squeeze profitability into fiscal year 2027 as historical production expenses are recognized.
7.
Delayed cost relief: The financial benefit of cheaper liquid fills created after distillation reductions at Midleton and across the industry will take years to reach Pernod Ricard's P&L. Because Scotch and Irish whiskey require multi-year maturation, these recent operational adjustments cannot provide immediate margin relief. Instead, the cheaper production batches will only improve gross margins over the long term once maturation cycles are complete.
8.
Persistent inventory headwinds: Brown-Forman experiences similar inventory-related margin headwinds as its expensive barreled whiskey laid down during the peak inflation of 2021–22 enters its cost of sales. The company's large stock of maturing liquid introduces a significant cost lag that limits immediate margin recovery. This structural delay confirms that premium distillers face prolonged profitability pressure even after they implement active distillation cuts to balance supply.
9.
Other side of the story: Pernod Ricard may achieve a faster gross margin recovery if premium pricing and digital promotional tools offset aged-liquid cost inflation. Management's deployment of advanced media catalysts and targeted non-working A&P reductions has already driven significant marketing efficiencies. If these marketing optimizations are combined with favorable geographic mix shifts in emerging markets, the group can expand its recurring operating margin well ahead of current broker expectations.

Section 6 – Operating Leverage from Market Destocking

Section summary
  1. The stabilization of wholesaler inventories in the United States and China represents a massive margin catalyst for Pernod Ricard.
  2. High structural profit pools in these key regions ensure that positive operating leverage will disproportionately expand group profitability as shipments reconnect with depletions.

High Margin Regions

1.
Elevated regional profitability: Pernod Ricard maintains a highly lucrative operation in the United States that remains a vital contributor to its overall earnings. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on October 9, 2026, that US operating margins are expected to stabilize at approximately 36%. This regional profitability is materially above the group average of 25.8% for fiscal year 2026. Consequently, any improvement in US volume will immediately release powerful positive operating leverage across the entire business model.
2.
Exceptional China margins: Pernod Ricard generates even higher profitability from its brand portfolio in China, driven by the premium positioning of Martell Cognac. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted on October 9, 2026, that strong currency exchange rates added 230 basis points to regional estimates. This favorable tailwind allows the group to report China operating margins of approximately 48% in fiscal year 2027. Such dense profitability ensures that stabilizing Chinese shipments will disproportionately lift group recurring operating profit over the next three years.
3.
Easing destocking headwinds: Wholesaler inventory corrections have begun to moderate in key markets, signaling an impending normalization of shipments. Jefferies on October 15, 2026, reported that US sales fell 10% against retail sell-out of negative 6% in the first quarter. This narrowing gap indicates that the heaviest destocking phase occurred in early fiscal year 2027. As shipments begin to converge with underlying depletions, the removal of this drag will support stable organic sales.

Operational Gearing Precedents

4.
Contrary evidence: Wholesaler destocking may persist longer than anticipated and continue to suppress shipment growth in key regions. Jefferies cautioned on October 15, 2026, that US distributor adjustments will continue throughout fiscal year 2027 to bring absolute inventories down. This ongoing correction implies that a rapid shipments recovery is unlikely in the near term. Consequently, the expected release of positive operating leverage could be delayed.
5.
Severe deleverage precedents: Historical spirits downturns demonstrate how negative operating leverage rapidly destroys margins when shipments fall below consumption. Morgan Stanley on April 20, 2020, noted that a 12% sales drop historically cuts Rémy Cointreau's operating profit by 25% to 30%. This asymmetrical profit drop occurs because spirits groups carry significant fixed structural and maturation expenses. Conversely, this high operational gearing means margins expand rapidly during positive rebounds as shipment volumes normalize.
6.
Asymmetrical regional deleverage: Major spirits peers have faced similar severe profit contractions when regional demand softens. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on February 2, 2024, that a 21% organic sales decline for Diageo in Latin America caused a 42% collapse in operating profit. This massive 2x margin sensitivity highlights the strict reality of high fixed-cost distributions. This precedent suggests that Pernod Ricard faces high margin volatility in both directions based on its shipment trajectory.

Recovery Horizons

7.
Stable shipment outlook: Pernod Ricard's financial trajectory is expected to find solid footing as inventory adjustments run their course. Rothschild & Co Redburn modeled on October 9, 2026, that organic shipments in the United States will become broadly stable from fiscal year 2028. This stabilization is expected to occur as the level of wholesaler destocking narrows and turns into a net benefit to sales growth. This structural shift will provide a clean baseline for margins to recover.
8.
Positive depletion signals: Underlying consumer demand in China has shown early signs of improvement despite ongoing trade and macro headwinds. Jefferies on October 15, 2026, observed that Pernod Ricard's China sales fell 25% even as underlying depletions returned to positive growth. This disconnect shows that trade destocking was the primary driver of reported sales declines. Once shipments realign with this positive consumption, Pernod Ricard's highly profitable China segment will accelerate overall group earnings.
9.
Other side of the story: Pernod Ricard may fail to unlock substantial operating leverage if weak underlying consumer sentiment persists in both the US and China. Jefferies on October 15, 2026, warned that consumer confidence remains historically low and spirits volumes are still correcting in the United States. If depletion rates deteriorate further, wholesalers will likely keep reducing absolute stocks rather than resuming shipments. This persistent weakness would prevent any meaningful margin expansion regardless of cost-cutting programs.

  3. Discipline in capital allocation and stable depreciation charges prevent non-cash operating pressures from weighing on earnings.

Organizational Headcount and Structure Costs

Successful personnel reduction: Pernod Ricard streamlined its corporate structure by removing regional reporting layers and creating ten market management entities. These changes resulted in an eighteen percent reduction in headcount since June 2023, representing approximately three thousand six hundred roles. This headcount rationalization helped the company achieve an eight percent organic structure cost decrease in fiscal year 2026. Consequently, lower personnel expenses will help defend recurring operating profit during the current period of weak volume growth.

These cost reductions supported a seventy-seven basis point positive contribution to the operating margin in fiscal year 2026. In comparison, Pernod Ricard has focused on front-loading its structural savings to protect its operating margins. This divergent pacing shows that Pernod Ricard has moved faster than its largest peer to align its cost base with lower demand.

Portfolio Rationalization and Stranded Overheads

5.
Positive margin accretion: Pernod Ricard completed the sale of its Imperial Blue division in India to Tilaknagar Industries on November 30, 2025, for approximately INR 3,442 crores. Management pursued this divestment to exit the low-margin Admix Value segment and redirect focus toward premium brands. This strategic transaction is expected to be immediately accretive to regional operating margins and organic growth. Consequently, the disposal helps eliminate lower-margin volumes while mitigating the risk of stranded overheads in India.
6.
Adverse perimeter impacts: Bernstein reported that the disposals of Imperial Blue and the global wine business will create short-term top-line dilution. These transactions carry a negative perimeter impact of two hundred million euros on sales and thirty million euros on current operating profit. This perimeter contraction will modestly dilute earnings per share in the near term despite improving the group's core margin profile. This trade-off suggests that portfolio rationalization serves long-term premiumization at the expense of absolute short-term profit.
7.
Selective brand divestments: Pernod Ricard sold its American sparkling wine brands, including Mumm Napa, to Trinchero Family Wine and Spirits in April 2026. The group also divested the Kenwood winery operations and associated vineyards to focus its resources on premium international spirits. Additionally, affiliate Corby Spirit and Wine sold its Lamb's rum brand to Dandurand and La Martiniquaise-Bardinet for CAD 39.2 million. These concurrent transactions eliminate non-core asset overheads and reduce the group's operational complexity across North America.
8.
Contrary evidence: Contrary evidence: Pernod Ricard faces lingering stranded overheads from its recent brand disposals because regional sales offices cannot be closed immediately. Bernstein reported that the dilutive scope impact of wine divestments and the Imperial Blue sale would lower fiscal year 2026 earnings per share by six percent. These transaction costs and residual administrative expenses are expected to persist throughout fiscal year 2027. This slow cost elimination shows that portfolio exits do not immediately translate into clean overhead reductions.

Capacity Utilization and Depreciation Trajectory

9.
Decreased distillery production: Pernod Ricard operated eighty-one self-owned production sites across twenty countries during fiscal year 2026. The group decommissioned four industrial sites during the year and experienced a slowdown in distillation activities. This active supply management caused finished product production volumes to fall to 869,597M litres from 996,683M litres in the prior year. Consequently, lower fixed-cost absorption from this reduced utilization will continue to pressure the group's gross margins.
10.
Lower capital allocation: Pernod Ricard capped its annual strategic investments at approximately seven hundred million euros to preserve cash and support deleveraging. This revised target represents a significant reduction from the previous peak of nine hundred million euros. The lower spending cap will help the company achieve a ninety percent cash conversion rate through fiscal year 2029. Consequently, this disciplined capital allocation protects the balance sheet during a period of lower organic sales.
11.
Stable depreciation charges: Pernod Ricard reported depreciation of fixed assets at four hundred thirteen million euros for fiscal year 2026. This charge declined slightly from four hundred twenty-two million euros in fiscal year 2025 due to active portfolio management. Because the group has capped strategic capital expenditures going forward, annual depreciation is expected to remain highly stable. This stable trajectory prevents unexpected non-cash operating expenses from further weighing on earnings per share.
12.
Peer capacity shutdowns: Suntory Global Spirits addressed oversupply in the American whiskey industry by pausing production at its flagship Jim Beam distillery in Clermont, Kentucky. This initial one-year suspension has been indefinitely extended through the better part of 2027 to align stock levels with demand. This aggressive peer action illustrates how prolonged demand weakness forces production cuts across the premium spirits sector. This precedent suggests that Pernod Ricard may need to maintain lower distillation rates at Midleton to prevent inventory gluts.
13.
Historical capacity adjustment: Pernod Ricard faced similar capacity adjustments in past downturns by temporarily slowing its whiskey and cognac production to conserve cash. During the fiscal year 2020 pandemic shock, the group optimized its industrial footprint and tightly controlled capital outlays. This historical capacity management proved that distillery utilization can be dialed down without damaging long-term brand equity. This precedent confirms that modern distillation cuts at Midleton represent a rational operational response to temporary market softness.
14.
Other side of the story: Other side of the story: Pernod Ricard might see its structural margins expand rapidly if the newly streamlined organization captures strong positive operating leverage. The elimination of regional management layers and the establishment of two brand units have created a highly agile corporate structure. If volumes in the United States and China recover sooner than expected, this leaner overhead structure will immediately accelerate earnings per share growth for shareholders. Consequently, the current fixed- cost containment efforts have prepared the group to leverage the next cyclical upturn.
