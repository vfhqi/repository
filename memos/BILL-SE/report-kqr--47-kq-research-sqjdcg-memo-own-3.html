Section 2 – Regional EBITDA Margin Compression and Drivers in 2026

Section summary
  1. Billerud's Region North America experienced significant EBITDA margin compression in 2026 due to heavy maintenance shutdowns and short-term operational inefficiencies from the Evolution program.
  2. High-cost shutdowns at Quinnesec and Escanaba alongside unfavorable currency translations offset domestic paper price increases and compressed regional margins.
1.
Factual footprint: This investigation analyzed 12 distinct regional sources, covering three major paper manufacturers and one historical competitor precedent. All identified companies including Billerud, Sappi, and Sylvamo were fully utilized in this report. No relevant entities were excluded because all available documents provided direct insights into North American operations. This comprehensive dataset ensures high analytical accuracy for our earnings per share forecasts.

Planned Maintenance and Asset Upgrades

2.
Severe compression: Billerud's Region North America EBITDA margin collapsed to 13% in the second quarter of 2026 from 20% in the prior year-end. This steep profitability decline occurred because of a scheduled biennial maintenance shutdown at the Quinnesec mill. According to company interim reports, this shutdown carried a total financial impact of SEK 171 million. Consequently, the mill's restart delay and high fixed costs severely depressed regional cash flow generation.
3.
Operational friction: Billerud reported that its third-quarter regional EBITDA margin recovered slightly to 15% but remained below historical averages. Profitability was restricted by operational challenges during the scheduled annual maintenance shutdown at the Escanaba mill. During this outage, technicians installed winder and roll handling upgrades under the Evolution program. These technical adjustments caused shortterm manufacturing bottlenecks and restricted overall equipment effectiveness.
4.
Substantial expenditures: Billerud calculates maintenance shutdown costs by combining direct maintenance fees, elevated variable expenses, and unabsorbed fixed overheads. These shutdowns typically require a complete production halt for approximately one week. Across the entire group, scheduled maintenance outages cost a total of SEK 1,029 million during the first nine months of 2026. Managing these volatile seasonal outages is critical because they disrupt short-term delivery schedules.
5.
Contrary evidence: Billerud's operational performance in North America actually improved on a volume basis despite the heavy maintenance schedule. Regional sales volumes excluding currency effects increased by 11% during the second quarter of 2026. This volume expansion indicates that underlying customer demand remained solid. Therefore, the margin contraction was primarily a temporary maintenance-driven disruption rather than a structural decline.

Cost Pressures and Product Shifts

6.
Diluted returns: Billerud faced margin pressure because its product mix shifted toward lower-margin market pulp and packaging trial grades. While the Escanaba mill successfully ramped up its new containerboard and cartonboard products, these initial runs created short-term production inefficiencies. Net sales for these new packaging categories rose to SEK 91 million in the second quarter of 2026 from SEK 13 million in the prior year. However, these experimental runs hampered the mill's historical high-margin graphic paper throughput.
7.
Currency headwinds: Billerud experienced substantial translational pressure from the strengthening Swedish Krona relative to the US Dollar. Translational currency exposures heavily impacted consolidated margins because roughly 30% of group sales are generated in North America. Jefferies estimates that every 10% movement in the USD-to-SEK exchange rate alters EBITDA by approximately SEK 430 million. This exchange volatility directly undercuts the profitability of Billerud's domestic US dollar-denominated revenue.
8.
Pricing offsets: Billerud aggressively raised prices across its graphic, label, and packaging grades during 2026 to combat regional inflation. These pricing actions successfully offset rising input costs for chemicals, energy, and logistics. According to company executives, positive pricing developments are expected to counter ongoing oil-driven cost inflation. Consequently, successful pricing execution represents the primary defense against persistent variable cost headwinds.
9.
Elevated inflation: Billerud faced significant cost increases in North America due to regional logistics and chemical cost inflation. Jefferies highlights that broad-based freight rate increases and chemical supply constraints pressured cash costs during the first half of 2026. To mitigate these cost headwinds, the manufacturer implemented logistic surcharges on several specialized grades. Thus, regional cost pressures continue to challenge the underlying profitability of domestic mills.

Peer Comparisons and Industry Context

10.
Parallel headwinds: Peer manufacturer Sylvamo Corporation also navigated severe margin compression during 2026 due to a heavy maintenance schedule. Sidoti Research reports that Sylvamo's planned maintenance outage costs spiked to $41 million in the second quarter of 2026. This massive maintenance headwind caused a deep contraction in Sylvamo's adjusted EBITDA before recovering sequentially in the third quarter. This parallel outcome confirms that the entire North American paper sector suffered transitional maintenancedriven margin declines.
11.
Competitive capacity: Peer producer Sappi Limited faced comparable regional pressures while adding packaging capacity in North America. Morgan Stanley reports that North American packaging margins remained under pressure despite regional price hikes of up to $60 per ton in mid-2026. Sappi's continuous transition toward solid bleached sulfate board intensified regional competitive dynamics. Consequently, Billerud operates in an increasingly crowded local market that limits long-term pricing power.
12.
Sector oversupply: Peer competitor Sappi's major machinery conversions threatened to disrupt Billerud's domestic market share. Jefferies warns that Sappi converted a graphic paper machine into a 470k ton boxboard unit. This substantial capacity addition in North America limits Billerud's ability to easily pass through pricing increases. Thus, regional capacity additions are expected to restrain Billerud's medium-term EBITDA recovery.
13.
Historical precedent: Peer producer UPM-Kymmene's historical restructuring demonstrates how graphic paper manufacturers must manage structural declines. Jefferies notes that UPM successfully exited low-multiple, structurally declining graphic paper operations by forming a joint venture with Sappi. This strategic consolidation allowed UPM to protect its aggregate profitability while shifting resources to high-margin labels. This precedent suggests that Billerud must successfully execute its Evolution program to secure sustainable long-term packaging margins.
14.
Conservative outlook: DNB Carnegie remains cautiously optimistic that Billerud's regional earnings bottomed out during the first half of 2026. The broker expects gradual earnings recovery through 2027 as wood raw material and variable costs ease. However, near-term regional visibility remains weak due to persistent demand headwinds for graphic paper. Consequently, achieving historical margin peaks of 20% remains highly dependent on improving North American utilization rates.
15.
Other side of the story: While critics focus on 2026 margin compression, Billerud's North American mills enjoy a massive structural fiber cost advantage. DNB Carnegie emphasizes that local Michigan wood raw material costs are significantly lower than Nordic fiber costs. This low-cost fiber, combined with protective US import tariffs, shields the domestic business from European import competition. Therefore, Region North America possesses durable structural advantages that will support long-term regional EBITDA margins.

Section 3 – Demand Decline Rates, Pricing Dynamics and the US Dollar Effect

Section summary
  1. Severe structural demand contraction in North American graphic paper continues to weigh on volume growth for paper mills.
  2. North American producers have successfully implemented regional price hikes for coated freesheet but face strong resistance in coated mechanical grades.
  3. Translational currency effects from a volatile Swedish Krona relative to the US Dollar heavily impact consolidated earnings.
  4. Import volumes of European and Canadian paper have faced significant disruption under US tariffs since 2025.
  5. Major capacity rationalization and machine conversions by competitors are helping stabilize the North American market.
  6. Active trade negotiations and targeted tariff exemptions are altering regional supply dynamics and competitive positioning.

Demand Trends by Grade

1.
Gradual contraction: North American demand for coated freesheet graphic papers continues to experience structural decline as digital transition persists. According to UPM-Kymmene Oyj on October 28, 2026, demand for magazine and coated woodfree papers in North America remained flat year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026. This flat demand is relatively favorable compared to Europe, where demand for graphic papers fell by 7% during the same period. This dynamic suggests that North American mills can maintain stable near-term utilization rates to protect regional cash flows.
2.
Structural decline: Coated mechanical papers face a more aggressive demand decline in North America due to digital substitution. Morgan Stanley reported on November 10, 2025, that annual demand contraction is projected at 6% to 8% in the United States over the coming years. This structural downward trend forces paper manufacturers to continuously monitor capacity. This continuous contraction will likely drag down Billerud's long-term coated mechanical sales volumes unless capacity is rationalized.
3.
Resilient growth: Specialty label paper demand is performing better than graphic paper grades across the packaging industry. Sappi Limited reported on November 6, 2026, that market conditions for specialty and packaging papers are gradually improving. Sappi noted that growth remains concentrated in the labels business, where the firm is gaining market share. Consequently, investing in specialty label papers represents a viable option for mills to offset graphic paper declines.

This pricing action was supported by tight local supply following Somerset Mill's machine conversion. This suggests domestic producers retain moderate shortterm pricing power in woodfree paper grades.

8.
Beneficial transaction: The relative strength of the US Dollar against the Euro and Swedish Krona serves as a key financial driver. Jefferies reported on February 2, 2025, that a stronger US Dollar is generally a tailwind for European producers due to both translation of US operations and transaction benefits on pulp. This currency dynamic is particularly helpful for Nordic producers like Billerud that export to global markets. Thus, exchange rate movements can provide a substantial earnings cushion during market downturns.
9.
Adverse translation: Volatility in the Swedish Krona creates significant translation headwinds for Billerud's consolidated financial results. Jefferies reported on March 23, 2025, that the stronger Swedish Krona relative to the US Dollar and Euro was a negative for Nordic paper producers. Billerud faces high translation risk because Region North America accounts for approximately 30% of sales. Consequently, currency fluctuations can easily erase operational margin improvements achieved at Michigan mills.
10.
High sensitivity: Billerud's earnings remain highly sensitive to exchange rate movements between the Swedish Krona and the US Dollar. Jefferies reported on March 28, 2025, that every 10% move in the USD to SEK exchange rate impacts Billerud's group EBITDA by approximately SEK 430 million. This substantial sensitivity means that consolidated earnings per share are highly dependent on global currency markets. Therefore, investors must closely monitor exchange rate trends to project Billerud's forward profitability.

Divergent trends: North American paper pricing trends remain more resilient than global indicators due to localized supply discipline. Sappi Limited reported on November 6, 2026, that North American graphic paper markets remained relatively balanced following regional capacity reductions. In contrast, European graphic paper markets faced intense overcapacity and competitive pressure that depressed regional margins. This divergence confirms that local North American capacity management supports higher regional prices.

Challenged recovery: Global paper pulp and graphic paper indicators suggest that industry recovery will be slow and highly competitive. Arctic Paper SA reported on November 5, 2026, that there is no recovery in paper demand across Europe, leading to intense competition for orders and pressure on prices. Arctic Paper also noted that higher transport and chemical costs are pressuring margins. This global backdrop makes it difficult for producers to sustain long-term price increases.
13.
Other side of the story: While regional price increases and currency tailwinds provide short-term margin support, the underlying structural demand decline remains an insurmountable obstacle. Morgan Stanley reported on November 18, 2026, that graphic paper faces significant long-term structural decline from ongoing digital substitution. Even with successful regional pricing, overcapacity and volume contraction will inevitably pressure margins. Therefore, North American mills cannot rely on graphic paper to generate sustainable long-term cash.

After the US Supreme Court ruled the IEEPA tariffs unlawful in February 2026, the administration quickly implemented a 10% Section 122 tariff set to increase to 15%. This regulatory pivot keeps broad baseline import duties high for international paper exporters.

Section 5 – Operational Costs and Margins of Packaging Board Conversions

Section summary
  1. Billerud's Evolution program leverages gradual capital upgrades at its Michigan mills to expand containerboard and cartonboard production on legacy graphic assets.
  2. Short-term production inefficiencies and operational bottlenecks from the Tribute and Voyager ramp-up are currently diluting North American margins.
  3. Historical industry precedents like Stora Enso's Oulu conversion demonstrate that machine conversions carry substantial start-up losses and long ramp-up curves.

Mechanical Parameters and Infrastructure Upgrades

1.
Gradual transition: Billerud decided to scale down its North American strategy by replacing its massive initial cartonboard plans with a gradual capital expenditure program. The board decided in May 2024 to cancel the SEK 7 billion Escanaba mill conversion in favor of a moderate SEK 1.4 billion strategic program across both Michigan mills. This phased approach reduces near-term financial leverage while allowing the company to gradually transition its asset base. Consequently, Billerud avoids the substantial capital risks that typically burden large-scale paperboard projects.
2.
Strategic upgrades: The mechanical upgrades under the Evolution program focus heavily on improving the raw material infrastructure at Escanaba. Billerud completed a major modernization of the Escanaba woodyard in the fourth quarter of 2025. This project introduced new debarking technology and enhanced the mill's conveyor systems to improve chip quality and operational efficiency. This foundation is critical because superior chip quality directly improves the tear resistance of the final packaging board.
3.
Lighter machine: Technical modifications at the Quinnesec mill aim to enhance the facility's product weight flexibility with modest capital intensity. Billerud allocated SEK 200 million of the total Evolution budget to upgrade the Quinnesec mill in 2026. These specific machine upgrades allow the facility to increase paper basis weights and optimize roll handling. By expanding its basis weight capabilities, the mill can manufacture heavier packaging grades on machines historically limited to lighter graphic sheets.
4.
Technical optimization: Upgrades implemented during scheduled maintenance outages are designed to expand the product format range. Billerud utilized the annual maintenance shutdown at the Escanaba mill in the third quarter of 2026 to install modernized winder and roll-handling systems. These upgraded systems enable the mill to produce larger board formats and higher basis weights, expanding its domestic commercial reach. Additionally, the technicians optimized Escanaba's heat recovery systems to reduce steam and energy consumption during these packaging runs.

Product Qualification and Volume Progress

5.
Commercial trials: Billerud has engaged in extensive product qualifications to build its customer base in the attractive Midwest packaging market. The manufacturer carried out more than 70 customer trials during 2025 for its new Tribute white kraftliner and Voyager cartonboard lines. These extensive trials allowed Billerud to secure its first commercial sales orders from regional corrugated board facilities. Establishing these early customer relationships is vital to driving long-term volume growth as graphic paper demand contracts.
6.
Sequential growth: Locally produced packaging volumes have shown sequential expansion as Billerud scales its commercial operations in Michigan. Billerud's local North American packaging shipments reached 11 ktons in the third quarter of 2026, representing an annualized rate of 44 ktons. This volume is a substantial increase from the 3 ktons reported in the third quarter of 2025. This momentum indicates that the mill is successfully shifting its product mix toward higher-margin containerboard and cartonboard.
7.
Premium positioning: Billerud's packaging board portfolio is designed to compete directly in premium retail and food service segments. The Tribute range features a fully bleached white kraftliner with a superior print surface, making it ideal for high-quality corrugated packaging and retail-ready displays. Meanwhile, the Voyager line offers single-ply solid bleached sulfate board designed for demanding folding carton and food service applications. Producing these premium grades allows Billerud to capture higher-margin market shares from established packaging peers.
8.
Contrary evidence: The rapid ramp-up of the new Tribute and Voyager product lines has introduced short-term manufacturing bottlenecks at Billerud's Michigan facilities. CEO Ivar Vatne acknowledged in the third quarter of 2026 that the successful introduction of packaging materials was creating some short-term production inefficiencies. These operational challenges, combined with maintenance disruptions at Escanaba, limited the regional EBITDA margin to 15% in Q3 2026. Therefore, the near-term financial contribution of the Evolution program remains constrained by operational friction.
