Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence supports near-term plausibility of Helios Towers' expected growth, because delivery to date and contracted protections are real, but the Key Question cannot be fully answered today, because master lease renewals, rural lease-up speed and post-capex-peak demand remain untested, leaving overall confidence at medium. Management guides to approximately 4,000 tenancy additions in 2026 and more than 3,000 in 2027, about 35% above the prior analyst consensus of 2,200. 1 Morgan Stanley's USD 0.13 EPS for fiscal 2027 assumes capital expenditure falling to 19% of sales by 2030, while the same broker flags slower network rollouts or customer consolidation as major downside risks. Jefferies' consensus-style view assumes rising tenancy ratios support mid-teens cash flow compounding, and its downside case has lower tenancy growth keeping leverage elevated and depressing earnings. Airtel Africa's USD 657 million half-year spend, up 106% year-on-year sits within a heavily front-loaded USD 1.1 billion full-year plan, and this phasing suggests tenancy additions could slow in the second half of 2027.

On that conflict, the company-stated capex phasing is favoured for 2027 timing, while Airtel's data traffic growth of over 50% is favoured only as support for multiyear demand rather than for the pace of additions. Helios Towers states that recent build-to-suit vintages add a second tenant in an average of 2.5 years, versus five years for 2010–2015 towers, but it does not disclose lease-up velocities separately for rural and urban sites, and permitting for new towers can take up to two years.

The site mix is currently tilted toward capital-intensive builds, since new sites were 418 of 924 additions in Q3 2026, against a long-run target of about 75% colocation, which matters because a single-tenant tower earns about 12% on day one versus 25% with a second tenant.

Peer precedent favours caution on how fast the mix reverts, as Tower Bersama added 1,421 net build-to-suit sites but only 78 net colocations in fiscal 2024, compressing EBITDA margins to 84.2%. Management's expectation that fiscal 2027 adjusted EBITDA will grow by at least 10% is not matched by peer evidence, since Indus Towers is projected to deliver only mid-single-digit EBITDA growth, although IHS's Sub- Saharan Africa capex fell 34.5% year-on-year in Q3 2026 and Cellnex expects a near trebling of free cash flow, showing capex cuts can protect cash flow.

The IMPACT 2030 targets look attainable on demonstrated strengths: they require more than 42,000 tenancies from a base of 35,379 and a tenancy ratio above 2.5x from 2.26x, backed by a USD 6.2 billion contracted backlog with 6.4 years average remaining life, 71% of adjusted EBITDA in hard currency and build times cut from 167 to 102 days.

The most important contrary evidence is that none of the master lease agreements has yet been renewed at the end of its initial term and expert interviews suggest tower pricing typically declines at renewal, so this report treats the favourable company framing as unproven until renewals occur. Financing and disruption risks remain, namely USD 850 million of senior notes due December 2029, the Vodacom–Orange Esengo Towers venture building 2,000 solar sites in the DRC and Airtel Africa and Vodacom together contributing more than half of leasing revenues.

The principal limitations of this assessment are that there is a lack of historical data on master lease renewals and that rural lease-up timing is undisclosed. Closing them would require observing the first renewal outcomes in key markets, expert calls with local RF planners, and tracking Airtel Africa's second-half capex phasing.

Section 4 – Economics of Single-Tenant Additions and Site-Mix Shifts

Section summary
  1. Helios Towers faces near-term margin and return dilution when building new single-tenant sites rather than high-margin colocations.
  2. Historical precedents from international peer tower companies show that capital-heavy coverage cycles require multiyear lease-up periods to recover initial returns.

Site-Mix Shift Economics

1.
Declining proportion: Helios Towers recorded a significant tilt toward capital-intensive new sites in its recent commercial deployment cycle. During the third quarter of 2026, new build-to-suit sites accounted for 418 of the 924 total additions. This heavy build activity represents a deviation from the company's long-run stated target mix of about 75% colocation additions.
2.
Dilutive margins: Building new single-tenant sites initially compresses overall portfolio profitability because anchor tenants carry lower margins. A single-tenant tower generates an initial site return of only 12% for the operator. In contrast, adding subsequent colocations requires very little incremental expenditure and allows revenues to fall 100% to EBITDA.
3.
Accretive economics: Attracting additional operators to existing infrastructure remains the primary engine for return on capital expansion. Disclosures from Helios Towers show that a second tenant increases site returns to 25%, while a third tenant pushes returns above 34%. Securing colocations remains vital because incremental tenant returns reach 35% to 50% on existing towers.

This persistent singletenant concentration could permanently depress group return ratios if customer leasing demand stagnates.

Precedents from Global Peers

5.
Compressed returns: SBA Communications demonstrates the margin and return pressures associated with expanding single-tenant footprints in international coverage markets. The company's international expansion and build cycle lowered its returns relative to domestic U.S. towers. This capital intensity is expected to compress margins, with international builds dragging projections 80 basis points lower.

Marginal pressure: PT Sarana Menara Nusantara faced structural headwinds after an operator capex peak led to customer consolidation and footprint rationalization. Mobile industry consolidation lengthened the path to achieving a target 2.0x tenancy ratio. Slower tenant additions resulted in severe margin pressure and a one percentage point EBITDA contraction.
7.
Dilutive acquisitions: Indus Towers experienced severe portfolio dilution when it altered its asset mix by acquiring single-tenant properties. The operator acquired 12,000 single-tenancy towers from Bharti Airtel, which immediately compressed group metrics. This shift toward lower-rent legacy assets and single tenancies caused the consolidated tenancy ratio to dip to 1.62 times.
8.
Operational recovery: Indus Towers eventually recovered its operating leverage as secondary carriers initiated network expansion and added colocations. The company saw its tenancy ratio expand to 1.63x as a result of Vodafone Idea's catch-up capex. This shift towards asset utilization is expected to support a 31% post-tax free cash flow CAGR.

Reversion and Lease-Up Dynamics

Tenant additions: Indus Towers experienced near-term tenancy additions volatility due to supply chain disruptions and delayed customer rollouts. The company added 3,100 macro towers but only 4,200 colocations in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. This narrow gap compressed core margins, indicating that slower colocation growth limits upside potential.

The operator added 1,421 net built-to-suit sites but only 78 net colocations in fiscal 2024. This reliance on built-to-suit towers and poor colocation lease-up ultimately compressed EBITDA margins to 84.2%.

The cost of strengthening an existing tower to support a second tenant is only 20% of building a new site. This low incremental cost allows operators to drive rapid tenancy ratio improvements.
13.
Leasing margins: SBA Communications benefits from exceptionally high incremental margins when adding new tenants to its existing towers. The operator incurs minimal maintenance capital expenditure, representing less than 2.5% of total site leasing revenue. This operating leverage has supported a 6% annual growth in AFFO per share.

Other side of the story: Helios Towers is uniquely positioned to achieve faster lease-up and mitigate dilution because of high demand in under-served African markets. Rapid population growth and expanding digital usage insulate the region against the tenant churn seen in Europe.
