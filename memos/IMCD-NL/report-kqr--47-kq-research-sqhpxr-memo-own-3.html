Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

  2. Since its listing, IMCD's valuation has transitioned through steady compounding, an inflation-driven peak, a severe cyclical de-rating, and a subsequent recovery in 2026.

Growth and compounding dynamics

4.
Accretive compounding: IMCD N.V. relies on its equity and debt valuation multiples to maintain a low weighted average cost of capital, which directly determines the returns on its roll-up strategy. When the company's trading multiples compress, as observed during the de-rating cycle of 2023 to 2025, its acquisition capacity is hindered because equity-funded M&A becomes highly dilutive. Conversely, high historical forward multiples allow the distributor to buy smaller, private competitors at low single-digit multiples while utilizing highly valued shares. A higher multiple expands leverage headroom and debt capacity, which is vital for keeping net debt to EBITDA stable near historical levels while funding growth.
5.

The broker Jefferies reported in October 2026 that IMCD's organic gross profit grew at an average of 9% annually over the last decade, or 7% excluding the COVID-19 surge, reflecting solid fundamental expansion. This organic expansion is augmented by bolt-on deals, with Jefferies noting in July 2026 that the group remains well positioned as a leading consolidator in a fragmented industry to drive market share gains. Sustained organic gross profit growth remains critical to support the roll-up model, as any organic slowdown de-rates the stock and prevents further accretive acquisitions.
