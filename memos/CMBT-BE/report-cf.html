Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive analysis of CMB.TECH reveals five material statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies that warrant heightened scrutiny. The first and most striking anomaly concerns the extraordinary magnitude of capital gains from vessel disposals, which reached USD 127.5 million in the second quarter of 2026 alone, representing an atypically high proportion of reported net profit that masks underlying operational earnings volatility.

When combined with the first quarter's USD 267.4 million in net gains from six older VLCCs and two Capesize bulk carriers, the total first-half 2026 vessel sale gains of approximately USD 395 million constitute a material anomaly that significantly distorts year-over-year earnings comparability and raises questions about the sustainability of reported profitability absent continued aggressive asset recycling (cross-reference: Section 5 Historical Earnings Trajectory, Section 6 Most Recent Earnings Delivery).

The second outlier concerns the initial net asset value dilution that occurred when CMB.TECH acquired its Golden Ocean stake in early 2025 at a premium valuation, creating an unexplained disconnect between the strategic rationale for fleet diversification and the immediate shareholder value destruction. This NAV dilution represents a rare instance where management prioritized long-term strategic positioning over near-term financial optimization, yet the delayed consolidation of Golden Ocean financials into sell-side models led to clouded peer EBITDA comparisons during 2025, further complicating the assessment of whether the acquisition ultimately created or destroyed value (cross-reference: Section 2 Strategy Structure and Ambition Change, Section 5 Historical Earnings Trajectory).

The third statistical outlier emerges from the extreme sensitivity of the company's financial performance to spot rate fluctuations. The analysis reveals that average spot TCE rates for remaining VLCCs soared to USD 126,790 per day during the second quarter of 2026, representing a rate level that sits in the top decile of historical tanker spot rates and creates an unsustainable baseline for forward earnings projections.

This extreme spot rate realization, when combined with the company's high operational leverage structure where fully 44,000 of 53,000 shipping days operate on spot rates in 2026, produces an earnings profile with unprecedented cyclical amplitude that dwarfs typical shipping sector volatility patterns (cross-reference: Section 3 External Change Value- Chain Forces, Section 9 Key Drivers Synthesis, Section 10 Forward Guidance Credibility).
