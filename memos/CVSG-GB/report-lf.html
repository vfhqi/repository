EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Chief Executive Quality and Delivery Record

Richard Fairman possesses a deep financial pedigree established across multiple corporate finance and advisory roles, with a career demonstrating strong adaptability despite lacking direct early experience in veterinary medicine.

While his professional history shows exceptional financial alignment with CVS Group, his pre-CVS career was concentrated in functional support roles lacking profit-and-loss responsibility.

Under Fairman's stewardship, CVS Group revenue grew from £327.30 million in fiscal year 2018 to £712.80 million in fiscal year 2026, nearly tripling adjusted EBITDA during his tenure.

Group like-for-like sales growth fell from a robust 7.30% in fiscal year 2023 to a flat 0.20% in fiscal year 2025, before recovering slightly to 2.10% in fiscal year 2026, reflecting weaker companion animal footfall and depressed consumer spending.

A significant portion of early growth was propelled by the post-pandemic pet boom and systemic industry inflation, with subsequent deceleration in like-for-like sales growth suggesting robust early performance was more a product of favorable industry cycles than sustainable operational differentiation.

Leadership Qualities Against Exceptional Standards

Fairman maintains a plain-spoken and highly transparent communication style when presenting CVS Group financial results, consistently addressing macroeconomic challenges and regulatory headwinds without relying on polished or evasive executive language.

Fairman demonstrates rapid operational decision-making, migrating practices to cloud-based systems immediately after the March 2024 cyber security incident and executing bold entry into the Australian companion animal market in July 2023.

He maintains a highly disciplined and conservative appetite for balance sheet risk, committing to keeping group leverage below 2.0x EBITDA despite banking covenants of up to 3.25x.

Mixed delivery against guidance shows FY24 revenue growth met expectations at 9.9%, but like-for-like sales growth fell to 2.9% due to cyber security disruptions and cost of living pressures, demonstrating that macroeconomic factors severely disrupted underlying volume momentum.

Fairman met core margin and cash conversion commitments over three years, but this resilient financial performance was achieved during a structural deceleration in organic customer volumes, with his leadership style potentially relying too heavily on defensive pricing actions and capital recycling rather than generating sustainable organic footfall growth.

Governance Robustness and Board Oversight

CVS Group maintains a clean governance structure with a simple capital structure consisting entirely of one class of ordinary shares carrying one vote each, with zero gap between voting control and economic ownership.

The company maintains a board of nine directors providing a strong mix of clinical, financial, and retail expertise, expanded from seven directors in early 2026 to strengthen corporate governance capabilities during the planned chief executive retirement transition.

CVS Group maintains a pristine financial reporting record with completely unqualified external audit opinions over the past several fiscal years, with auditors confirming financial statements give a true and fair view containing no material exceptions.

The company has established a robust internal control framework based on a three lines of assurance model featuring close executive involvement, clearly defined authority limits, and an independent internal audit function outsourced to KPMG.

However, rapid departure of long-standing non-executives has concentrated corporate memory in a few hands, with high turnover of experienced directors potentially weakening board oversight continuity during the critical chief executive succession search.

Insider Share Dealing Signals

CVS Group directors demonstrated strong personal conviction through open-market share purchases since the end of the 2026 fiscal year, with Chief Financial Officer Robin Alfonso leading by acquiring 768 additional shares, signaling growing insider confidence in post-regulatory recovery and long-term earnings potential.

Long-term share awards are subject to rigorous performance conditions split equally between relative total shareholder return and adjusted earnings per share growth, with full vesting requiring compound annual adjusted earnings growth of more than six percent alongside top-quartile shareholder returns.

Strict performance hurdles resulted in the total lapse of the three-year long-term incentive awards granted in 2023, with zero vesting proving that the remuneration framework enforces high standards of financial accountability.

While recent open market purchases by directors signal positive sentiment, the actual size of these transactions remains extremely small relative to their annual compensation, with modest transactions potentially functioning as routine public relations signaling rather than reflecting deep personal conviction in future earnings acceleration.

Expert Views Against Management's Story

Industry experts reveal that price hikes of 30% to 40% since 2022 have masked underlying volume declines in the veterinary sector, with this pricing power becoming highly fragile because customers are growing increasingly price-sensitive and delaying routine clinical treatments, implying CVS Group cannot rely solely on price increases to drive long-term organic growth without risking severe volume erosion.

Veterinarians heavily resist exclusivity deals that restrict clinical freedom, and because of the chronic veterinary shortage, clinicians will easily leave practices that attempt to dictate drug choices or pricing protocols, making it highly difficult for corporate consolidators to enforce centralized procurement plans without triggering severe veterinarian turnover.

Former CVS practice director warned that the corporate salaried model is less effective than joint-venture structures, with competitors offering shared ownership achieving much higher veterinarian retention and local profitability, suggesting CVS Group will continue to face high recruitment costs and locum reliance.

In Australia, a severe shortage of mid-tier experienced veterinarians has triggered intense wage inflation and constrained clinical capacity, which could restrict CVS Group from realizing anticipated post-acquisition margins unless it successfully builds a reliable local graduate pipeline.

Single Largest Credibility Risk

Management maintained its long-term financial ambition to double adjusted EBITDA by full year 2028 supported by a medium-term target of achieving nineteen to twenty-three percent margins, with meeting these efficiency targets critical to deliver projected earnings per share expansion.

This ambitious doubling of EBITDA represents the single largest risk to management's credibility because it depends on simultaneous execution of multiple highly uncertain factors.

CVS Group experienced persistent pressure on companion animal footfall particularly across routine preventative care visits, with management remaining highly cautious about volume recovery times and this volume pressure potentially constraining short-term organic earnings growth.

The forecast is most likely to prove wrong because it assumes organic volume recovery will materialize in the UK market despite entrenched consumer caution, that Australian integration will proceed smoothly despite clinical wage inflation and cultural challenges, that the incoming chief executive will maintain strategic continuity without delays, and that regulatory prescription caps will not permanently restrict clinical pricing power–any one of these assumptions failing would make the doubling target unattainable within the stated timeframe.
