[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Xvivo is a demand-inflection case with one proven engine and three unproven ones: the lung consumables business is real and accelerating, the heart system is the prize, and the regulator holds the key. At 240 SEK the shares already pay for the prize on the sell-side's timetable, so on our slower path the modal return reaches the 20 percent bar only at three years and only if the multiple holds.

  • The change is external, structural and sector-wide.
    • Machine perfusion is being pulled into transplant practice by forces that do not depend on Xvivo.
    • Circulatory-death donors reached a near 50/50 split with brain-death donors in the United States in 2025, growing at over 7 percent a year, and such organs need perfusion to be assessed
    • Federal pressure on organ procurement organisations to lift utilisation, with decertification of underperformers possible by 2026
    • Every player in the sector is growing: TransMedics +37 percent in 2025, OrganOx +50 percent under Terumo, Paragonix +65 percent organic under Getinge
    • Transplant procedures themselves grow only 5 to 7 percent a year, so the inflection is in perfusion penetration (US lung EVLP at about 10 percent; machine perfusion at 4 percent of the market), not in the procedure count
  • The company's own change is an execution programme riding that demand, not a transformation.
    • There is no chief-executive change and no numerical guidance to raise.
    • The service-led strategy (centralised lung-perfusion hubs; 175 perfusionists through the Perfusion Solution Inc partnership) is producing output in lung and failing so far in organ recovery services
    • The heart system is the largest product entry in the company's history: live in Europe under derogation and in the United States under a continued-access protocol
    • A tenfold disposables capacity project and the manufacturing consolidation to Gothenburg finish in Q4 2026, with unit-cost benefits from 2027
  • The outputs have turned in lung and reached the financial statements.
    • Organic growth was 36 percent in Q2 2026 against -15 percent in Q2 2025
    • lung disposables grew 69 percent
    • heart sales were 24 SEKm against 3 SEKm
    • operating cash flow was 129 SEKm in H1 2026 against -6 SEKm.
    • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has reached layer 5 for lung
      • for heart in Europe it is at layers 4 to 5 under derogation
      • for heart, liver and kidney in the United States it is at layer 3, actions taken and approvals pending, which is where the sell-side's FY2028 numbers live
  • The sell-side is holding revenue and cutting margins.
    • The 'Demand Inflection' is producing revenue upgrades and margin downgrades at the same time.
    • FY2026 consensus earnings per share fell 28.3 percent over twelve months and FY2027 fell 17.2 percent, while FY2028 rose 10.4 percent
      • the cuts after the Q2 report were about margins and operating expenses, not demand
    • Consensus has lung fading to 13 to 15 percent growth a year and heart reaching 347 SEKm of revenue by FY2028 from a product with 24 SEKm in its best quarter so far
  • What we think it earns.
    • Earnings per share of 1.03 SEK in FY2025 rising to 3.1 SEK, 4.8 SEK, 7.2 SEK and 9.5 SEK from FY2026 to FY2029 on our path, 20 to 35 percent below the sell-side's 3.47, 6.27, 9.61 and 15.15 SEK.
    • The difference is the US heart launch in 2028 rather than H2 2027, because every regulatory date in this company's record has slipped, and an EBITDA margin two to three points below consensus, because the abdominal gross margin recovers to the low 60s and not to 70 percent by 2027 and services keep diluting
    • Even on our path earnings per share triple in three years, at 45 percent a year, which is above the framework's 40 percent over-extrapolation warning
  • What it costs. 240 SEK is 28.2x the sell-side's 24-month-forward earnings of 8.52 SEK, the 9th percentile of the stock's own five-year range
    • on our path the same window gives 6.4 SEK and the multiple is 37.5x, the second-highest in a comparison set whose median is 14.9x.
    • So the price already assumes most of the consensus heart ramp, at a multiple that has already compressed to the low end of the stock's history
      • there is no premium multiple on top
  • The return is at the bar, not above it.
    • On our path, at 30x the earnings two years beyond each horizon and with no cash leg, the 'Modal return' is 16.7 percent a year over eighteen months and 19.6 percent a year over three years.
    • The case clears the bar on both horizons below about 230 SEK, 4 percent below today's price
  • Where the chain breaks.
    • The below-EBITDA drop-through is the swing the market does not model: 800.7 SEKm of capitalised development will amortise once the heart is approved, the currency is unhedged into a strengthening krona and the tax rate has had no stable level.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through takes the three-year return to 13.8 percent a year and the 'Modal return' Element from C to D
      • on that path this Building Block would be a C
  • The pattern.
    • 'Demand Inflection' fits at B on the framework's own test, with 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' as a secondary at C because two named transient concerns (the CE-mark delay; the 2025 destocking) sit behind an oversold multiple on the stock's own history.

  • The case therefore is compelling on the change and marginal on the price.

    • The risk is not that the company fails
    • it is that the prize takes a year longer at a lower margin, which is enough to make a marginal return a poor one.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material on every layer that has reported: the demand forces are sector-wide and structural, the company's own outputs have turned hard, and even on a path 20 to 35 percent below the sell-side's the earnings per share multiply three times in three years, so that the strength of the input forces and the outputs carries a return leg that is itself only a C.

    • Not A: the return is at the bar, not above it, with no cash leg and no re-rating leg we would count on, and the eighteen-month return is short of it.
    • Not C: the materiality of the change is not in doubt
    • only the price's share of it is.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against is that Xvivo is a false friend: the revenue acceleration is real but masks structural margin deterioration and regulatory friction, so the outputs look better than the business.
  • The case against, at its strongest. 152.8 SEKm of development was capitalised in 2025, 19 percent of sales, against 20.1 SEKm amortised, so the 20 percent EBITDA margin is flattered and free cash flow was 7.3 SEKm in FY2025 and -4 SEKm in Q2 2026.
    • The abdominal gross margin fell from 68 to 57 percent in a year on the kidney and emerging-market mix the growth plan requires.
    • Services fell 25 percent organic with a gross margin of 10 percent, down from 34 percent.
    • The CE mark is six to twelve months late and the liver trial is paused.
    • A consensus with 100 percent Buy ratings has cut earnings per share 17 to 28 percent in a year and still sits 20 to 35 percent above our path.
    • About 90 percent of revenue is outside SEK and unhedged, into a krona that consensus macro expects to strengthen.
    • If this reading is right, the 30 percent EBITDA margin target is missed by a wide margin, the multiple compresses toward the mature peers' 15x on earnings that do not grow, and the shares halve: RedEye's bear case is 145 SEK and the risks report sizes the downside at 35 to 50 percent.
  • What we concede.
    • The capitalisation point is right, so we treat free cash flow rather than EBITDA as the test of earnings quality until the heart programme's spending ends, and we carry it into the drop-through sensitivity.
    • The abdominal and services margins are worse than any broker modelled, and our path assumes only partial recovery.
    • The regulatory record is one of slippage, and our path adds a year to the US heart.
  • Why we still hold the view.
    • The lung consumables business, 63 percent of revenue at 83 to 85 percent gross margin, growing 53 percent organic in Q2 2026 with operating cash flow of 129 SEKm in H1 2026, is not a false friend: it is the cash engine that funds the rest, and its growth is acceleration (69 percent after 56 percent in disposables) on a sector growing 37 to 65 percent across every competitor, not a rebound.
    • The capitalised development is a known, finite sum tied to trials that end with approvals.
    • The price at 28x consensus, or 37x ours, does not assume flawless execution in the way it did at 45x to 60x in 2023 and 2024.
  • Where the competing hypothesis would win.
    • If lung disposables decelerate below 20 percent while services and abdominal fail to recover.
    • The first sign would be the Q3 2026 report on 22 October 2026.

External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because three external forces are tailwinds of sector scale with two-plus years of visibility (the circulatory-death donor shift; the procurement-organisation reform pressure; the adoption of machine perfusion across every competitor), against two headwinds (competition tightening; currency).
  • Donor mix: circulatory-death donors reached a near 50/50 split with brain-death donors in the United States in 2025, growing at over 7 percent a year, and such organs require machine perfusion to assess ischaemic damage.
    • The proportion of circulatory-death donors undergoing normothermic regional perfusion in the United States reached 55.8 percent in Q1 2026.
  • Procurement reform: underperforming organ procurement organisations face potential decertification by 2026 and administrative actions have begun.
    • The federal review by the Health Resources and Services Administration introduced near-term uncertainty that temporarily weighed on US lung and kidney sales.
  • Sector adoption: OrganOx grew revenue 50 percent year on year in late 2025 under Terumo
    • Paragonix recorded 65 percent organic growth in Q4 2024
    • TransMedics grew 37 percent in 2025 to USD 605.5m.
    • Demand for transplantable organs is ten times the supply.
  • Not A: transplant volumes themselves grow at 5 to 7 percent a year, so the inflection is in perfusion penetration (US lung EVLP at about 10 percent; overall machine perfusion at 4 percent of the market), not in the underlying procedure count, and the two headwinds are real.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a demand-inflection case in a company with one proven engine and three unproven ones, priced for the unproven ones to arrive on the sell-side's timetable.
  • The change is the adoption of machine perfusion in organ transplantation, driven from outside by the rise of circulatory-death donors to about half of United States deceased donors, by federal pressure on procurement organisations to lift utilisation, and by a sector in which every player (TransMedics, Terumo's OrganOx, Getinge's Paragonix) is growing 37 to 65 percent
    • and from inside by Xvivo's centralised lung-perfusion hubs, its heart system reaching patients in Europe under derogation and in the United States under a continued-access protocol, and a tenfold capacity project completing in Q4 2026.
  • The change has reached the financial statements for lung (organic growth 36 percent in Q2 2026 against -15 percent a year earlier; lung disposables +69 percent; operating cash flow 129 SEKm in H1 2026) and is at the actions layer for heart, liver and kidney in the United States, whose revenues wait on the FDA.

  • Our modal return is 17 percent a year over eighteen months and 20 percent a year over three years, all from earnings growth, with no cash leg and a multiple held at 30x.
  • The case clears the bar on our path at prices below about 230 SEK.
  • It fails on our path if the lung engine slows or if the below-EBITDA chain (the amortisation of 800 SEKm of capitalised development; unhedged currency) drops through worse than we assume, which alone takes the three-year return to 14 percent.
  • The lung business is real and accelerating, the heart is the prize and the regulator holds the key, the price pays for the prize already, and the risk is not that the company fails but that it takes a year longer at a lower margin, which is enough to make a marginal return a poor one.
