Legal Frameworks and RPT Safeguards

5.
Strict statutory guidelines: Spanish listed companies must navigate a highly structured legal framework under the Capital Companies Act when executing transactions with related entities. Under Article 529 unvicies, the Board of Directors holds the exclusive, non-delegable authority to review and approve related-party transactions to defend corporate interests. These transactions are assessed by comparing pricing and terms with prevailing market conditions to ensure equal treatment of all shareholders.
6.
133 topic-specific reports Mandatory market disclosures: Spanish regulations require listed issuers to make immediate public announcements to the market when related-party dealings exceed specific statutory thresholds. Under Article 529 unvicies of the Capital Companies Act, companies must submit an announcement to the CNMV when transactions exceed 2.5% of annual turnover in a 12-month period. This regulatory safeguard ensures that major transactions involving controlling family shareholders are subjected to timely and complete public scrutiny.

135 136 topic-specific reports Compulsory director recusal: Spanish company law enforces strict conflict-of-interest rules that restrict the participation of interested parties in boardroom decision-making. Section summary
  1. Spanish family-controlled peers utilize dual-class structures or consolidated majorities but maintain standardized related-party transaction safeguards that contrast with Grifols' parallel consolidation of Scranton.
  2. Precedents such as the ACS-led minority buyout of CIMIC highlight how public market simplifications are structured to eliminate governance discounts and capture cash flows.

Spanish Peers

Shareholding Structures and

1.
Voting Rights Concentrated ownership: Inditex combines family control with an institutional investor base through its single-class voting structure. The founding shareholder Amancio Ortega Gaona holds a combined 59.29% voting interest through his investment vehicles Pontegadea Inversiones and Partler Participaciones. All issued shares carry equal voting and economic rights, which minimizes voting disparities. This single-class structure contrasts with Grifols' dual-class setup where Class A shares command a significant premium over Class B shares due to voting rights.
2.
Dual-class frameworks: Puig Brands utilizes a multi-class share structure that separates voting power from economic ownership to maintain family control. Class A shares command 5 votes per share and are unlisted, while listed Class B shares command only 1 vote per share. This disproportionate voting framework resembles other European family firms where insiders retain voting control despite lower economic interests.
3.
Equal voting: Ferrovial displays a highly concentrated ownership structure that is dominated by its founding family and major institutional investors. The company is controlled by the Del Pino family, which holds an estimated 33% stake in the consolidated entity. Despite this substantial concentration of ownership, each Ferrovial share gives the right to cast one vote at the General Meetings. This equal voting setup ensures all public shareholders retain proportional voting rights.

Related-Party Transaction Safeguards

4.
Rigorous reviews: Inditex implements rigorous independent reviews to manage commercial related-party transactions with its controlling shareholder's property vehicles. The board approved commercial leases with Pontegadea Group subsidiaries after receiving independent valuations from international consulting firms. The Audit Committee verified that the transactions occurred on an arm's-length basis to ensure the equal treatment of public shareholders. This reliance on external real estate evaluations helps prevent the pricing disputes that have historically plagued Grifols' Scranton transactions.

Formal recusals: Puig Brands enforces strict board recusal protocols to manage potential conflicts of interest during related-party deliberations. Affected directors, such as Manuel Puig Rocha, left the meeting room during votes on loans and artwork leases with related entities. This recusal mechanism complies with statutory board regulations by depriving conflicted members of their voting rights. Applying similar physical recusal standards at Grifols is essential to restore independent committee oversight during the Scranton buyout process.
6.
Statutory approvals: Acciona utilizes a formalized board approval mechanism that is designed to supervise material transactions with related parties. Under Article 45 of its regulations, transactions with significant shareholders must be authorized by the board following an Audit and Sustainability Committee report. Shareholders must vote on any related-party transaction whose value exceeds 10% of total corporate assets. This dual board-and-shareholder approval process acts as a statutory shield for minority investors against unilateral family decisions.
