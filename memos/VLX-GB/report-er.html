It would also weaken if the ex-data-centre growth rate of 19.5% faded or EV share-of-wallet gains stalled.

Further triggers would be a copper pass-through lag that compresses gross margin (formula-based agreements operate with a short time lag ), or evidence of design wins for next-generation optical or orthogonal architectures that bypass Volex's copper content.

Non-obvious blind spots. First, the pull-forward and the data centre normalisation sit in the same segment and the same half, so they should be modelled together rather than as separate risks. Second, the medical "recovery" is largely acquired. The division delivered 1.8% organic growth alongside an 11.3 million USD contribution from Kepler SignalTek, and revenue rose by 13.0 million USD in total.

Third, ROCE is already 23.3% against a target of approximately 20%. The target therefore implies expected dilution from capex, goodwill and integration, rather than further improvement. Fourth, the 2 billion USD target relies partly on M&A. Management targets 300 million USD of acquired revenue, while available headroom is about 274 million USD.

Kepler cost up to 89.4 million USD for revenue of 51.8 million USD. At a similar price-to-sales relationship, the target would need more capital than current headroom. Cash generation is therefore the gating variable. Fifth, there are governance subtleties.

Chairman Dave Webster also became CEO of Jennmar in June 2026, and Lord Rothschild's dual status as chief executive and largest shareholder gives him immense control over strategic direction. Sixth, a mitigant is often overlooked. Even if orthogonal designs disrupt data interconnects, copper demand for power delivery remains solid. The technology threat is therefore not uniformly negative across Volex's product set.
