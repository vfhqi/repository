Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Volati's transition to a pure-play five-platform industrial compounder post-Salix spin-off was overshadowed by severe cyclical end-market headwinds and elevated leverage before the Q1 2026 report.
1.
Long-term industrial compounding: Volati was founded in 2003 by Karl Perlhagen and Patrik Wahlén with a simple focus on local entrepreneurship, patience, and a high return on invested capital. Since inception, the company has operated as a Swedish industrial group that acquires well-run, cash-generative, mediumsized companies at reasonable valuations and supports their organic growth.
2.
Impending structural separation: On April 21, 2026, Volati announced a revised segment reporting structure in preparation for the proposed separate listing of Salix Group, its market-leading distribution business. Following the spin-off, Volati's remaining operations were reorganized from three business areas into five distinct operating platforms to enable clearer resource allocation.
3.
Diversified multi-platform operations: Volati's remaining five platforms comprise Ettiketto Group, Communication, Corroventa, S:t Eriks Group, and Tornum Group, which operate across diverse niches such as self-adhesive labels, telecom towers, moisture restoration, concrete products, and grain handling turnkey solutions. This structural setup provides the company with built-in diversification across markets to help mitigate single-industry cycles.
4.
Solid historical growth: In its historical financial performance up to 2025, Volati demonstrated a strong track record as a compounder, growing its annual sales to SEK 8,419m and EBITDA to SEK 1,018m in 2025. DNB Carnegie analyst Korsheden highlighted that the company had achieved an adjusted EBITA CAGR of approximately 12% since its initial public offering in 2016 through 2025 without raising public equity.
5.
Elevated group gearing: Volati's leverage target is to maintain a net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio of 2 to 3 times, not exceeding 3.5 times over a business cycle. However, extensive acquisitions and end-market weakness pushed its leverage to 2.5x net debt to adjusted EBITDA by the end of 2025. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden characterized the balance sheet as stretched at 2.9x net debt in early 2026, including lease debt and preference shares.

6.
Cyclical end-market headwind: Leading up to the Q1 2026 earnings, Volati's business areas were heavily impacted by the deepest construction and agricultural downturns in the Nordics since the 1990s. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden noted that about half of Volati's EBITA was derived from these struggling, cyclical industries, which severely depressed near-term earnings power.
7.
Depressed share price: Reflecting these operating headwinds, Volati's share price suffered a massive contraction, falling approximately 60% from its post-IPO highs by early 2026. In the 12 months preceding the Q1 2026 earnings, the stock performed poorly against the broader market, trading near a depressed four-year share price low as investor sentiment soured.
8.
Deep valuation discount: Volati's weak operational delivery resulted in a historically depressed valuation multiple compared to its sector peers. Going into 2026, the company traded at approximately 11x EV/EBITA, representing a 30% discount to less-proven compounders and a 40% discount to the wider Swedish compounder peer group.
9.
Premium peer valuation: Volati's peer group is characterized by high-quality M&A compounders, which historically trade at premium valuation multiples. For example, key peers Addtech and Lagercrantz traded at 2027e EV/EBITA multiples of 20x or higher due to their superior organic growth, high returns, and longstanding self-financed track records.
10.
Progressive price-target cuts: In the six months leading up to the 2026-Q1 earnings, DNB Carnegie's Korsheden systematically lowered his estimates and price target for Volati. Korsheden reduced the price target from SEK 132 in January 2026 to SEK 120 in February 2026 following a weak Q4 report. He then cut the target further to SEK 111 target price cut in April 2026 to reflect a sum-of-the-parts valuation model ahead of the Salix spin-off.
11.
Unchanged buy rating: Despite cutting the price target and trimming adjusted EPS estimates for 2026-2028 by 1-2%, DNB Carnegie's Korsheden maintained a Buy rating on Volati before the Q1 earnings. The broker argued that the impending separate listing of Salix would act as a powerful positive catalyst to close the deep sum-of-the-parts discount.
12.
Sudden advisory withdrawal: Shortly after the Q1 earnings, on May 27, 2026, DNB Carnegie's Korsheden withdrew his recommendation and target price of SEK 113 for Volati. This action was necessary because DNB Carnegie acted as the financial advisor to the company for the Salix Group separate Nasdaq Stockholm listing.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Sell-side attention before the Q1 2026 report was centered on the value-unlocking Salix Group spin- off as the primary positive catalyst
  2. Analysts remained highly concerned about the underperforming agricultural and construction segments dragging down RemainCo organic growth
  3. Volati's elevated group balance sheet leverage restricted near-term programmatic M&A capacity compared to premium Nordic compounder peers

Separation catalyst and SOTP valuation

1.
Anticipation of corporate separation: Sell-side analysts at DNB Carnegie were intensely focused on the upcoming annual general meeting in April 2026 to approve the separate listing of Salix Group on Nasdaq Stockholm. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden argued that this separation would act as a powerful catalyst to unlock deep sum-of-the-parts value for existing shareholders.
2.
Methodological valuation transition: Due to the high probability of the Salix Group divestment, brokers abandoned traditional consolidated compounding multiples in favor of a sum-of-the-parts model. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden subsequently reduced his price target from SEK 120 to SEK 111 to reflect this structural shift, though he maintained a Buy rating on improving visibility in key end-markets. 62 Volati (Buy): BUY on separation anxiety – Q1 review DNB Carnegie Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP) valuation model for Volati by segment.
3.
Post-separation volatility risks: Sell-side analysts expressed concerns that the remaining business, referred to as RemainCo, would exhibit materially higher earnings volatility post-separation. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden highlighted that removing the stable trading division Salix would leave Volati highly exposed to the more cyclical and lumpy Industry business platforms.
4.
Liquidity and scale concerns: The market was highly focused on how the spin-off of Volati's largest business area by EBITA would impact the company's stock liquidity. DNB Carnegie's Redin warned that creating two smaller public entities out of a single small-cap firm could escalate long-term liquidity concerns for institutional investors.

Segmental performance and end-market recovery

5.
Delayed renovation market recovery: The sell-side closely monitored Swedish renovation and construction indicators to gauge when the downturn would bottom out. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden observed that although Swedish renovation market data suggested a 2026 turnaround, the recovery speed was tracking slower than earlier broker estimates.
6.
Industry segment cyclical headwind: The market's main concern was the prolonged operational slump in S:t Eriks, Tornum, and Corroventa. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden modeled an organic sales decline of 6% for the Industry segment in Q1 2026, indicating that the deeply depressed construction and agricultural niche markets remained a massive earnings drag.
7.
Agricultural visibility and orders: Analysts sought comfort in Volati's secured backlogs to offset agricultural market weakness. DNB Carnegie's Redin pointed to a major agricultural order with Lantmännen as a key buffer that provided important H1 2026 earnings visibility for the struggling Tornum platform.
8.
Easy year-over-year comparables: Despite near-term weakness, the market focused on the potential for easy operating comparisons in the back half of 2026. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden noted that the Industry division had experienced an exceptionally weak period in 2025, which positioned Volati for easy comparable organic growth figures later in the year.

Balance sheet constraints and leverage targets

9.
Stretched leverage ratios: The sell-side was highly critical of Volati's rising debt levels relative to its historical performance. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden pointed out that the combination of continuous acquisitions and weak Industry earnings had pushed the net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio to a highly stretched level of 3.3x on a RemainCo basis.
10.
Debt definition discrepancies: The market closely analyzed the difference between Volati's self-reported leverage and the sell-side's stricter definitions. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden emphasized that his calculated leverage ratio of 2.9x in early 2026 included lease liabilities and preference shares, which presented a more leveraged financial profile than the company's internal targets.
11.
Stalled inorganic capital allocation: Prior to the earnings release, the market focused on how balance sheet constraints had shifted capital away from the Industry platforms. DNB Carnegie's Redin noted that the M&A pace within Industry had stalled as Volati almost exclusively allocated capital to Salix and Ettiketto.
12.
Interest rate sensitivity risks: High leverage made Volati's cash conversion a major focus area in a high- interest-rate environment. DNB Carnegie's Redin cautioned that if interest rates remained elevated, Volati's debt servicing costs could severely limit future bolt-on acquisition capacity.

Peer valuation and compounder discount

13.
Deep relative valuation discount: The market was focused on the persistent valuation gap between Volati and other Swedish serial acquirers. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden highlighted that Volati traded at roughly 11x EV/EBITA, representing a 30% discount to less-proven compounders and a 35% discount to the wider Swedish compounder peer group.
14.
Peer multiple benchmarking: Analysts contrasted Volati's valuation with premium sector peers who operated with superior organic growth. DNB Carnegie's Korsheden noted that high-quality, proven Nordic compounders like Addtech, Lagercrantz, Indutrade, and Lifco traded at a premium median 2026e EV/EBITA of 22x .
15.
Modeling of future acquisitions: Analysts differed on whether to include unannounced M&A in their valuations. RedEye's Binder noted that while some sell-side analysts do not model future M&A for serial acquirers like Lifco, accounting for programmatic deal pipelines remains highly critical for longterm compounder valuations. side of the story: Sceptical investors argued that Volati's lower multiple was fully justified because its return on capital was lower, its leverage was higher, and it possessed greater historical earnings volatility than its high-performing compounder peers.
