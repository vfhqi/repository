Although initial ramp-up phases are slow, local managers are actively transferring advanced technology to improve processing efficiencies. This technical learning suggests that yield enhancements will minimize scrap losses, which will support faster EPS expansion.
9.
Resource recovery gains: AT&S is actively implementing advanced circular economy processes to recover raw materials and lower scrap expenses. The company built a state-of-the-art copper recycling plant at its Leoben facility to reuse valuable metals. Additionally, the Indian facility in Nanjangud successfully recovers up to 35 kilograms of copper per hour from used etchants. These localized recovery efforts mean that raw material waste is actively minimized, directly lowering variable manufacturing costs.
10.
Elevated inventory write-downs: Inventory management and write-down risks remain elevated during the initial commercial scaling phases of new substrate lines. Peer firm Mengniu Dairy faced massive nonrecurring impairments of RMB 2,320 million in its associates due to goodwill and asset write-downs. For AT&S, inventory valuation policies require stating assets at the lower of cost and net realisable value, making them sensitive to sudden price shifts. This sensitivity implies that pricing drops trigger immediate writedowns, directly impacting operating margins.

Trademark agreements and structural savings

11.
Misattributed trademark savings: The premise that AT&S will save costs from the end of a Siemens trademark agreement in 2030 is contradicted by the research findings. The document database shows that the 1 percentage point margin saving from the expiration of the Siemens trademark license in 2030 actually belongs to Siemens Energy AG. Siemens Energy AG pays royalties under this agreement, which terminates in 2030. Consequently, AT&S will achieve zero savings from this specific contractual expiration.
12.
Contrary evidence: The view that AT&S's cost optimization programs can entirely offset pricing pressures is challenged by persistent market realities. Despite achieving massive savings of €170 million, AT&S still reported a consolidated loss for the period of €39.30 million in the first three quarters of fiscal year 2025/26. This shows that cost-cutting cannot fully neutralize the severe price erosion caused by industry oversupply.
13.
Other side of the story: While near-term losses highlight the limitations of self-help cost programs, a successful yield learning curve at Kulim remains a highly durable margin driver. If AT&S can achieve high production yields for AMD's advanced server processors, unit costs will drop rapidly. This operational progress would allow the company to benefit from robust high-performance computing demand. This potential implies that yield mastery will drive profitability even if external price pressures persist.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A sudden transition to overcapacity in 2027 or 2028 would collapse the high-margin substrate pricing structure, reversing mechanical margin gains.
  2. Downstream packaging shifts and supplier price pass-throughs could heavily erode the expected operating leverage of AT&S and its regional peers.

Structural risks of industry overcapacity

1.
Severe capacity expansion risks: Massive capital expenditure plans across the substrate industry could trigger a structural downturn if artificial intelligence demand decelerates. Unimicron Technology raised its 2026 capital budget to NT$53.7 billion, while Ibiden announced a ¥500 billion investment plan through fiscal year 2028. If actual market demand falls short of these aggressive projections, elevated depreciation charges will severely depress corporate operating margins. This means that the expected operating leverage would work in reverse, turning projected profitability into heavy operating losses.
2.
Margin expansion vulnerability: The rapid margin recovery seen in early fiscal year 2026/27 may prove temporary if industry overcapacity returns. AT&S raised its fiscal year 2026/27 EBITDA margin guidance to a range of 32% to 37% on the back of expanded capacity. However, historic precedents show that pricing declines can quickly erase these mechanical gains during capacity waves. If substrate prices fall due to oversupply, margins will contract sharply because heavy pre-operating overheads and fixed depreciation charges cannot be deferred.
3.
Diverging consensus estimates: Broker forecasts remain highly optimistic about the sustainability of the current high-end packaging cycle. Bernstein Research projected that Unimicron's corporate gross margin would return to 35% by 2028, driven by rising packaging complexity. This upbeat outlook assumes that demand continuously outpaces physical supply additions. If client bookings decelerate, actual earnings will miss consensus because specialists cannot easily repurpose advanced lines for lower-end applications.
4.
Pricing power erosion: High operating leverage will amplify the negative impact of any average selling price erosion across the sector. Masterlink Securities highlighted that high-end substrate pricing power remains strong, with Unimicron targeting a long-term gross margin of 50% to 60%. However, this assumption is highly sensitive to competitive capacity additions from new entrants. If overcapacity forces price concessions, operating profits will decline exponentially since price drops flow directly to the bottom line.

Material supply and customer bottlenecks

5.
Monopolistic supplier pressures: Raw material monopolies represent a critical cost risk that could prevent substrate makers from achieving projected cost-out targets. Ajinomoto holds an estimated 95% market share in packaging insulation film and recently initiated like-for-like price increases. This high market concentration leaves buyers with very limited bargaining power over major variable inputs. If raw material costs rise while substrate prices soften, manufacturers will face a margin squeeze because they cannot pass these costs to customers.
6.
Specialty glass constraints: Shortages of specialty T-glass from Nittobo could cap the near-term shipping volumes of leading substrate vendors. Jefferies Research reported that T-glass supply and demand will remain highly constrained through 2027, with a structural gap of 20% to 30%. This material bottleneck directly limits the usable output of high-density substrates. 7.
Customer substitution threats: Large chip designers have the leverage to push the packaging ecosystem toward alternative technologies if substrate costs remain elevated. Morgan Stanley highlighted that tech giants are exploring alternative packaging solutions that do not require standard organic substrates. Furthermore, customers are already actively negotiating long-term agreements to secure capacity on their own terms. If these alternative architectures gain rapid adoption, legacy substrate lines face obsolescence, forcing massive asset write-downs.

Alternative pathways and early warning signs

8.
Ignored component mismatches: Early signs of supply chain disruptions are already emerging at downstream server assembly levels. Jefferies Research identified that components mismatch at server OEMs, particularly in CPU and memory lines, caused slower PCB pull-ins in late 2026. This downstream delay demonstrates that chip-level demand does not translate immediately into factory loadings. If these logistical mismatches persist, substrate suppliers will build excess inventory, leading to sudden valuation write-downs.
9.
Contrary evidence: Persistent market tightness: Contrary evidence: The threat of near-term overcapacity is dismissed by some industry participants who see sustained shortages through the decade. A former director at Intel Corporation stated that substrate shortages will persist through at least 2028 due to surging chip sizes. This suggests that prolonged supply tightness will sustain high average selling prices. If demand continues to grow at this pace, capacity additions will be easily absorbed.
10.
Glass core transition: The mid-term transition toward next-generation glass core substrates could reshape the competitive hierarchy. Jefferies Research reported that Kinsus and Unimicron are developing glass core technologies, but mass production is not expected until 2029. This lengthy timeline is due to persistent manufacturing defects like micro-cracks. If a competitor masters this technology ahead of schedule, traditional organic lines will lose value, ruining the long-term cash flows of late adopters.
11.
Other side of the story: Customer prepayments as a cushion: Other side of the story: Even if industry-wide overcapacity returns, major substrate manufacturers are structurally insulated by customer-funded capital agreements. Morgan Stanley highlighted that Unimicron and Ibiden have secured substantial customer prepayments and capacity reservation commitments from key clients like Nvidia. These collaborative models de-risks multi-billion dollar expansions by guaranteeing minimum utilization rates. Consequently, secured customer commitments will protect corporate earnings per share from the full impact of cyclical pricing downturns.
