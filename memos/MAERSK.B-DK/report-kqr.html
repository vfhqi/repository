Suez Canal Return and Capacity Re-entry

1.
Accelerating network normalisation: Morgan Stanley reported in October 2026 that containership transits through the Suez Canal surged 61.9% week-on-week to 752k TEU, representing approximately 54% of pre-disruption levels. This rapid volume recovery is driven by actual service returns as Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd route multiple Gemini Cooperation services through the canal. Easing Red Sea routing inefficiencies will release significant vessel capacity and exert severe downward rate pressure on global shipping networks.
2.
Broadening competitor returns: Linerlytica observed that private carriers such as MSC and CMA CGM are also selectively returning East-West loops to the Red Sea route as of late 2026. Regulatory news confirms that COSCO has adjusted its vessel deployment on India-Europe trade lanes to align with CMA CGM's Suez-based rotations. Shorter transit distances will reduce voyage times by 7 to 8 days compared to Cape of Good Hope diversions. Easing routing constraints will inevitably trigger overcapacity across the sector.
3.
Gradual capacity re-entry: An expert call with a former Chief Commercial Officer at CMA CGM suggested that restoring full Suez transits will not occur overnight because carriers must navigate insurance and security complexities. Once the route is fully normalised, it will inject an estimated 10% of theoretical capacity back into the global fleet. This sudden influx of capacity will severely depress freight rates unless carriers cooperate on blank sailings.
4.
Declining ocean freight rates: Deutsche Bank Research notes that a potential Red Sea reopening in 2027 will heavily pressurise container rates, especially off current elevated peak levels. Shorter routing options will reduce demand measured in TEU miles by 0.7% in 2027, creating a soft market. This capacity release will likely trigger a cash burn for major shipping lines as pricing converges on historical lows.

Private giant MSC leads with a massive 2.9 million TEU orderbook, followed closely by CMA CGM and COSCO. This unprecedented wave of newbuild deliveries threatens to saturate major trade lanes and trigger aggressive market share battles.
