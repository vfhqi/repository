EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The investment case for Kaspi.kz centers on a stable, co-founder-led management team executing an ambitious 100 million user super app strategy across Kazakhstan and Turkey, absent any activist pressure, though recent margin compression from elevated domestic funding costs and deliberate Turkish investment drag reflects material strategic transition risks. Core domestic operations maintain exceptionally robust 31% revenue growth driven by marketplace expansion and e-commerce platform effects, while consolidated net income margins compressed from 30.9% to 23.3% in Q1 2026 as Hepsiburada integration and 220 basis point deposit cost increases offset top-line momentum.

Strategic expansion into Turkey via the $1.3 billion Hepsiburada acquisition and pending Rabobank A.Ş. banking license represents the single largest execution risk and opportunity, requiring management to replicate Kazakhstan's super app engagement in a competitive, lowermargin Turkish e-commerce market. Kaspi's historical 60%+ return on equity vastly outperforms regional banking peer Halyk Bank's 25.9% and positions the platform at the top tier of digital ecosystem peers, though near-term profitability faces structural headwinds from Kazakhstan's corporate tax increase to 25% and the National Bank's 18% base rate driving 14.3% deposit funding costs.

Sell-side consensus maintains 60% Overweight and 40% Equal-weight ratings with zero Underweight recommendations, with lead Morgan Stanley analyst Luke Holbrook holding an Equal-weight rating on concerns that core Payments and Fintech segments are mature while Turkey expansion remains unproven and capital-intensive. Management operates as an honest-tracker signaling archetype, delivering or exceeding upgraded net income targets from FY22 through FY24 before regulatory and supply-chain headwinds decelerated FY25 growth to 10%, and Q1 2026 net income of -1% year-over-year beat Morgan Stanley's -9% forecast while Adjusted EBITDA growth of 9% paced ahead of fullyear 5% guidance.

The one-line investment verdict positions Kaspi as a high-quality domestic franchise executing an ambitious but unproven international replication strategy where success in closing Turkey's massive transaction frequency gap could unlock significant long-term value, but permanent margin dilution from low-return Turkish assets and elevated domestic funding costs represents the primary downside risk to consolidated returns.

Materiality of Strategic Change

Management pursues an ambitious 100 million user company target absent any activist pressure, with CEO Mikheil Lomtadze anchoring every product launch, international investment, and dividend policy decision to achieving this long-term scale objective. The $1.3 billion Hepsiburada acquisition closed in January 2025, with subsequent share purchases increasing Kaspi's ownership to 85.66% by March 2026, representing approximately half of total platform transaction volumes and fundamentally reshaping the consolidated financial profile.

Kaspi signed a share purchase agreement to acquire Rabobank's Turkish subsidiary in March 2025, receiving official Turkish banking regulator approval on June 24, 2026, with transaction close expected in July 2026, enabling the launch of deposit products, payments, and enhanced HepsiPay services through a fully licensed bank without legacy borrowing clients or physical branches. Regulatory materiality surfaces through Kazakhstan's corporate bank tax increase to 25% from 20% starting January 2026, raising the consolidated tax rate by 200 basis points, combined with burdensome reserve requirement step-ups locking capital with zero interest earned.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Consolidated net profit margin compressed to 23.3% in Q1 2026 from 30.9% in Q1 2025, reflecting margin deterioration of over 750 basis points year on year. This compression significantly underperforms the financial services peer group and signals that elevated deposit funding costs rising 220 basis points to 14.3% are persistently eroding bottom-line returns. The margin compression trajectory is further exacerbated by management deliberately operating Hepsiburada near Adjusted EBITDA breakeven, which temporarily limits consolidated cash conversion and dilutes overall profitability.

Hepsiburada's annual purchase frequency of 6.7 trails Kaspi's domestic 24.8 purchases per customer, representing a massive 3.7x engagement gap that must close for the Turkish expansion to deliver acceptable returns. This frequency deficit underscores highly unproven integration and regulatory execution in Türkiye, introducing substantial capital deployment risks as the company commits approximately $300 million to the fintech strategy.

The execution risk concentration is magnified by Hepsiburada operations being deliberately managed around EBITDA breakeven, dragging down consolidated margins while offering no nearterm profit contribution. The National Bank of Kazakhstan maintained its base rate at 18% into early 2026, driving deposit funding costs up 220 basis points year on year to 14.3% and continuing to compress net interest margins in the Fintech segment.

This monetary policy headwind represents a persistent cyclical drag that will only reverse upon future rate cuts, creating near-term earnings pressure relative to peer banks operating in easing monetary environments. Additionally, high central bank rates led to a temporary 2% decline in total financing volumes in Q1 2026, signaling volume contraction risk in the core lending business.

Local banking peer Halyk Bank reported a 14.6% year-on-year decline in Q1 2026 net income, demonstrating that peer group profitability is deteriorating across the Kazakh banking sector due to shared macro headwinds. While Kaspi's historical return on equity exceeds 60% compared to Halyk Bank's Q1 2026 return on equity of 25.9%, the convergence risk remains elevated if margin compression persists and Turkish integration costs fail to generate acceptable returns. The sector-wide pressure suggests that Kaspi's outperformance is narrowing rather than widening relative to regional banking peers.

Fintech delinquency rate stood at 2.2% in Q1 2026 against 2.1% in late 2025, representing a rising credit risk trajectory that could accelerate if domestic monetary conditions remain tight or macroeconomic conditions deteriorate. Although Fintech cost of risk remains controlled at 0.7%, the directional deterioration in delinquency rates signals potential asset quality stress that bears close monitoring against peer group base rates.

The Plausible problems to monitor synthesis reveals that Kaspi faces simultaneous margin pressure from higher Kazakh bank taxes, elevated domestic funding costs, Turkish integration losses, and intensifying competition–all occurring while the peer group experiences sector-wide profitability declines. These compounding structural and cyclical headwinds create meaningful downside risk to the company's historically exceptional return profile and raise questions about whether the Turkish expansion can offset domestic margin erosion without permanently diluting consolidated returns below peer group averages.
