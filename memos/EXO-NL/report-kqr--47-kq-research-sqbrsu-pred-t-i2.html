Section 4 – Recurring Dividend Pressures and the Capital Allocation Update

Section summary
  1. The suspension of Stellantis's dividend creates a severe recurring income gap that non-recurring extraordinary distributions cannot permanently offset.

Projected Dividend Income Pressures

1.
Severe contraction: Exor's recurring dividend income faces immediate pressure due to the total loss of cash flows from its largest industrial holding. According to Exor's H1 2026 Interim Report published on September 22, 2026, Stellantis distributed nil dividends in the first half of 2026 compared to €306 million in the prior period. This complete suspension of distributions forces a substantial recurring income gap that impairs Exor's longterm organic cash generation capacity unless automotive operations recover.
2.
Temporary cushion: Exor's total dividend income temporarily rose in the short term due to a massive non- recurring extraordinary distribution from one of its recent spin-offs. The Exor H1 2026 Interim Report disclosed that Iveco Group distributed an extraordinary dividend of €427 million in the first half of 2026 compared to just €24 million in H1 2025. This large distribution partially masks the underlying erosion of Exor's recurring cash flows, as the Iveco windfall represents a one-off event that cannot be relied upon in subsequent fiscal years.
3.
Automotive distress: The underlying driver of Exor's recurring dividend pressure is the deep operational distress and strategic write-offs at its key automotive subsidiary. A Morgan Stanley broker report on February 26, 2026, detailed that Stellantis incurred a massive €22 billion strategic write-off that led to a complete cancellation of its dividend. This severe financial adjustment drastically reduces the near-term probability of any meaningful dividend recovery from Stellantis, directly limiting the holding company's look-through earnings per share contribution over the next 18 months.

Contrary evidence: Exor's total dividend inflow actually increased to €907 million in the first half of 2026 from €700 million in the first half of 2025, driven by higher distributions from Ferrari and Lingotto. Investors are closely watching whether Exor will pivot toward a multi-billion-euro acquisition of at least €2 billion or initiate an aggressive tender offer to exploit the record NAV discount.

If Exor chooses a major €2 billion acquisition instead of a capital return, it could further widen the holding company discount if the transaction increases structural complexity.
