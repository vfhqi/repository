Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary

Section 3 – Pricing Dynamics in EUV and High-NA Product Transitions

Section summary
  1. ASML's low-NA EUV average selling prices scale proportionally with wafer-per-hour throughput gains to deliver structural margin expansion.
  2. The pricing ceiling for High-NA systems is constrained by customer adoption delays, pushing mass-volume manufacturing deployments to the end of the decade.

Low-NA throughput and ASP scaling

1.
Low-NA throughput scaling: Low-NA EUV systems exhibit a tight historical correlation between system throughput and average selling price. Bernstein's note on September 1, 2026, highlights that ASML consistently improves low-NA technology year after year to support rising average selling prices. This valuebased pricing strategy ensures that customers pay higher premiums only when they receive proportional productivity gains. Consequently, this scaling mechanism secures robust average selling price expansion as new system generations transition.
2.
Historical pricing benchmark: The transition from legacy low-NA models established the baseline for productivity-linked pricing. Jefferies' estimates from February 17, 2022, show that the legacy NXE:3400B was priced at 110 million euros in 2017. The subsequent NXE:3600C model saw its price rise to 130 million euros in 2019 as throughput stepped up. This established the precedent that ASML can consistently extract higher unit prices as performance matures.
3.
Throughput progression values: Throughput specifications rose substantially across the low-NA platform to lower the customer's cost per exposure. 109 - Earnings Calls Bernstein's analysis from September 1, 2026, shows low-NA throughput progressed from 137 wafers per hour on the NXE:3400C to 160 wafers per hour on the NXE:3600D. ASML's 2025 annual report indicates that the newer NXE:3800E delivered 220 wafers per hour, representing a massive 37 percent throughput improvement over the 3600D final configuration. This continuous performance curve helps justify rising system capital costs for leading-edge chipmakers.
4.
Value enhancement packages: ASML utilizes software upgrades to squeeze incremental capacity and value out of its existing low-NA fleet. ASML's presentation from April 15, 2026, outlines the release of the Productivity Enhancement Package PEP-E, which raised the NXE:3800E throughput from 220 to 230 wafers per hour. These field-installable upgrades are highly lucrative and carry excellent profitability for the service segment. Consequently, software modifications allow ASML to capture high-margin recurring revenues between major tool transitions.

Section 4 – Export Controls and DUV Lithography Competition

Section summary
  1. ASML maintains strong pricing power over DUV parts and service contracts despite rising mature-node competition from Japanese and Chinese players.
  2. Stricter DUV export restrictions in China speed up domestic substitution, threatening long-term mature-node terminal values.
  3. Japanese suppliers helped by yen weakness set a price floor, but their mature systems do not threaten ASML's leading-edge dominance.
  4. The accepted 10 percent service parts price hike in South Korea demonstrates ASML's robust margin capture capabilities across its fleet.

Export controls and Chinese stockpiling

1.
Extensive database coverage: Geopolitical lithography dynamics under tightening export controls were evaluated across 14 distinct sources, 6 equipment manufacturers, and 2 historical process precedents. All identified organizations, including Nikon, Canon, SMEE, and Shanghai Aishengna, were analyzed, and none were left out as each provides critical competitive pricing benchmarks. This extensive evidence base ensures that DUV market share transitions can be quantified with high statistical confidence across future cycles.
2.
Protective equipment stockpiling: Chinese semiconductor fabs have accumulated a massive reserve of lithography tools to insulate their operations from tightening Western trade restrictions. The Centre for Technology and Statecraft estimated that mainland Chinese facilities held 343 ASML immersion DUV tools by early 2026, with 270 of those systems being the NXT:1980i model. This massive installed base provides a substantial buffer against immediate sales bans, which means that corporate service and maintenance revenue will remain highly resilient. Drastic legislative threats: The introduction of stricter export-control frameworks in Western legislatures represents a significant terminal value threat to ASML's business in mainland China. Bipartisan lawmakers in the United States Congress are promoting the MATCH Act to impose a complete ban on immersion DUV sales and servicing to Chinese chipmakers. If enacted, this legislation would disrupt maintenance for the massive installed base, which would severely impact long-term service earnings.
3.
Accelerating domestic substitution: Dutch restrictions on high-end tool shipments risk backfiring by giving the Chinese government an urgent incentive to fund alternative technology programs. Management at ASML publicly argued that US Congress proposals to restrict mature DUV tool servicing are counterproductive because they speed up China's domestic development of competitive lithography systems. Consequently, tightening restrictions may ultimately compress ASML's long-term market share once domestic alternatives mature.

Trendforce reports that ASML is utilizing this momentum to discuss equipment price increases with major customers for newly ordered lithography tools. Accelerated margin expansion: Higher service pricing and software-heavy productivity upgrades are set to provide a substantial boost to ASML's near-term earnings power. Comparative peer pricing behavior

1.
