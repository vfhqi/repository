Section 2 – New Contract Vehicles and Negotiations

Section summary
  1. Bavarian Nordic is actively negotiating a replacement BARDA contract expected in 2027 to transition from its completed 11.5 million dose JYNNEOS order.
  2. While company management targets a broader global multi-year framework, broker models reflect more conservative biodefense stockpiling baselines subject to US budget reviews.
  3. Confidence in the transition timeline is medium, supported by active BARDA renewal talks that are balanced against near-term administrative and political spending reviews.
  4. Federal funding for the Strategic National Stockpile remains stabilized at one billion dollars for fiscal year 2027.
  5. Federal leadership is prioritizing domestic supply chain reshoring and the creation of an active pharmaceutical ingredient reserve.
  6. While vaccine skepticism introduces administrative debates, bipartisan support for national biodefense countermeasures is expected to endure.

Post-May 2026 Contract Negotiations

1.
Active negotiations: Bavarian Nordic is currently negotiating a new contract renewal with BARDA to follow the completion of its historic JYNNEOS order. Management expects to finalize this contract vehicle in 2027E to stabilize long-term biodefense earnings. This timing is critical because the primary 11.5 million dose order was fully completed by the option exercised in May 2026. The outcome will determine if the public preparedness segment can avoid a prolonged gap in US government revenue.
2.
Strategic option: The US government exercised a USD 97 million option in May 2026 to procure freeze-dried formulations of JYNNEOS to replace liquid-frozen doses consumed during the prior mpox outbreak. Bulk vaccine associated with this option will be invoiced in 2026, while final drug product manufacturing is scheduled for delivery during 2027. This tactical bridging contract temporarily stabilizes manufacturing utilization at Kvistgaard. Analysts note that this funding represents largely one-off upgrades for the year.
3.
Improving visibility: Bavarian Nordic management is seeking to establish an underlying public preparedness revenue framework of DKK 1.5 to 2.0 billion over the long term. The company is urging other international governments to adopt multi-year preparedness agreements similar to those utilized by military customers. Securing these international frameworks would help sharpen the baseline stockpiling run rate and reduce the historic valuation discount. This strategy seeks to transition Public Preparedness from a volatile, transaction-based segment to a predictable annuity.
4.
Additional contracts: Bavarian Nordic secured a substantial contract valued at over DKK 700 million with an undisclosed sovereign nation in June 2026. Deliveries under this non-US contract will commence in 2026, but the majority of the associated revenue will be recognized during 2027. This award demonstrates that international stockpiling demand remains robust independently of US biodefense decisions. This geographic diversification helps insulate the firm's operating margins from binary US procurement outcomes.

JYNNEOS Pricing Structures

1.
Elevated list pricing: Bavarian Nordic launched JYNNEOS in the US private market in April 2024 at a premium list price. This commercial launch established a list price of approximately USD 250 per dose, or USD 500 for the complete two-shot vaccination schedule. This retail structure represents a significant premium over traditional stockpiling contracts. Consequently, this commercial transition provides a highly lucrative private revenue stream for the firm's portfolio.
2.
Substantial bulk components: Jefferies observed in May 2026 that historical JYNNEOS biodefense contracts allocate a massive portion of total value directly to bulk vaccine. This allocation traditionally dedicates 90% of contract revenues to bulk products and 10% to subsequent services like storage. These high-value bulk shipments are invoiced immediately upon manufacture to optimize near-term cash flow. Consequently, the heavy bulk allocation helps accelerate early-stage cash collections during manufacturing campaigns.
3.
Profitable contract incentives: Bavarian Nordic's freeze-dried smallpox contract with BARDA incorporates structured financial bonuses for product stability. The agreement provides supplemental payments for all freeze-dried doses triggered by the successful demonstration of an extended shelf life. These supplemental revenues are invoiced pro rata with deliveries of the freeze-dried vaccines. This mechanism ensures that long-term product stability rewards corporate operating margins.

Slower margin: Bavarian Nordic cannot easily offset a major biodefense revenue deficit through its commercial travel vaccine business due to structural margin differences. DNB Carnegie reported that while the Public Preparedness segment operates with exceptional 50% operating margins, the travel division faces significant gross margin drag. This commercial segment experiences integration expenses and marketing investments that contrast with high-margin biodefense divisions. Consequently, travel vaccines like Rabipur and Encepur generate slower margin progression and lower free cash flow yields than the unparalleled margin leverage of smallpox vaccine stockpiles.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bavarian Nordic could experience substantial valuation upside if the US government decides to execute a larger-than-expected multi-year stockpiling framework. Jefferies indicates that an upside scenario featuring annual biodefense revenues of USD 150 million instead of the USD 90 million base case would add DKK 20 per share to the firm's sum-of-the-parts net present value. This increased procurement rate would optimize bulk manufacturing campaigns and significantly boost corporate profitability. Such an outcome would demonstrate that federal agencies remain committed to upgrading the national reserve to freeze-dried formulations.
5.
Elevated operational: Bavarian Nordic would suffer from severe fixed-cost under-absorption at its bulk manufacturing site if procurement contract negotiations drag past 2027. Bavarian Nordic management previously invested in expanding its internal fill-finish and bulk manufacturing capabilities at the Kvistgaard facility to secure unconstrained vaccine supply. A prolonged pause in federal shipments would leave these specialized lines underutilized and trigger costly write-downs of manufactured inventory. Ultimately, the high fixed overhead of these facility expansions would rapidly compress the firm's operating margins during any prolonged procurement pause.

Competitor Dynamics and Shifting Priorities

6.
Enhanced positioning: Emergent BioSolutions stands to benefit competitively if the US government shifts its biodefense stockpiling priorities away from next-generation non-replicating vaccines. Emergent BioSolutions continues to supply the Strategic National Stockpile with the ACAM2000 live vaccinia smallpox vaccine under structured federal contract agreements. Although ACAM2000 faces clinical safety limitations compared to JYNNEOS, federal agencies frequently maintain both products to ensure comprehensive national biodefense coverage. Therefore, delays in JYNNEOS contract execution would allow Emergent BioSolutions to maintain its historic market share in federal smallpox reserves.
7.
Favorable tailwinds: SIGA Technologies could capture a disproportionate share of federal orthopoxvirus funding if newly appointed health leadership favors therapeutic interventions over prophylactic vaccines. SIGA Technologies actively lobbies federal agencies to adopt a comprehensive biodefense model that pairs traditional smallpox vaccines with antiviral therapeutics. Shifting administrative focus toward therapeutic platforms would accelerate procurement of oral TPOXX at the expense of preventative vaccine contracts. This therapeutic pivot would insulate SIGA Technologies' product revenues while compounding the financial headwinds faced by specialized vaccine manufacturers.
8.
Potential competitive: GeoVax could disrupt Bavarian Nordic's sole-source monopoly in the Strategic National Stockpile over the long term if its vaccine candidate achieves regulatory milestones. GeoVax is actively preparing a single pivotal Phase 3 immunobridging clinical study for its MVA-based candidate, GEO-MVA, to target global orthopoxvirus vaccine demand. Successful trial execution would establish a second domestic supplier and introduce severe pricing pressure during future federal procurement cycles. This emerging threat would eventually dilute Bavarian Nordic's Public Preparedness segment profitability.

Early Indicators and Structural Realities

9.
Underappreciated growth: Bavarian Nordic has implicitly raised its baseline expectations for biodefense sales in a structural shift largely overlooked by the broader market. Bavarian Nordic altered its long-term financial presentations to reflect a Public Preparedness linear sales baseline of DKK 3.0 billion, representing a significant increase over its initial CMD target. This structural adjustment suggests that the sustainable biodefense run rate is significantly higher than historical consensus models assume. If correct, this higher run rate would yield structurally stronger corporate earnings per share.
10.
Elevated administrative: Policy changes and federal oversight bodies represent immediate execution hurdles that could delay follow-on contract approvals. The newly established Department of Government Efficiency introduces rigorous payment-by-payment justifications and mandatory contract reviews across all federal health agencies. While these reviews increase near-term execution risks, the absolute necessity of preserving a non-replicating smallpox stockpile for immunocompromised populations ensures a high probability of eventual renewal. Consequently, administrative shifts represent temporary delivery delays rather than permanent demand destruction for the biodefense franchise.
11.
Other side of the story: Although political transitions and administrative reviews introduce near-term timing risks, the national security status of orthopox countermeasures isolates biodefense funding from domestic spending cuts. DNB Carnegie argues that biodefense programs historically expand under Republican leadership because public preparedness contracts are viewed primarily as essential national security assets. Furthermore, federal law restricts the removal of approved vaccines, which limits the potential for major structural disruption to the US vaccine market. Consequently, the eventual execution of a follow-on BARDA contract in 2027 remains highly probable and will stabilize long-term corporate earnings.
