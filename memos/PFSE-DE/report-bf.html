EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Investment thesis and strategic positioning

1.
Cable-agnostic pure-play positioning: PFISTERER operates as an independent pure-play supplier of electrical connection and insulation solutions compatible with all cable brands, designing critical interfaces up to 1,100 kV across five core product segments including High Voltage Accessories, Overhead Lines, Components, Medium Voltage Accessories, and High Voltage Direct Current.
2.
Mission-critical small budget share: Connection products represent only 5% to 8% of total substation project costs but determine entire grid-connection reliability, creating asymmetric pricing power as utilities remain highly unwilling to risk catastrophic grid failures by switching suppliers.
3.
Structural customer moat: The cable-agnostic model prevents conflict-of-interest friction with major cable manufacturer customers, positioning PFISTERER as the preferred partner for grid operators seeking multi-vendor freedom and avoiding vertical supplier lock-in.

Financial profile and peer-relative performance

4.
Exceptional organic growth delivery: Consolidated revenues rose from €334.10 million in 2023 to €449.88 million in 2025, achieving 17.40% year-on-year revenue expansion and demonstrating strong electrification market tailwinds significantly ahead of integrated cable peers.
5.
Superior margin expansion trajectory: Consolidated gross margin improved to 40.60% in 2025 from 39.60% in 2024, with Q1 2026 reaching 43.60% versus 42% prior year, while adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 17.80% versus peer Prysmian's 12.20% and Nexans' 11.90%.
6.
Dramatic balance sheet de-risking: Net debt improved from €63.71 million in 2024 to a net cash position of €19.20 million in 2025, driven by €95 million gross IPO proceeds in May 2025, compressing unadjusted leverage to negative 0.25x versus peer Nexans at 0.36x and Prysmian at 1.30x.
7.
Industry-leading return metrics: Return on capital employed reached 33.42% in FY 2025 (calculated), substantially outperforming Nexans' ROCE of 21.30% and NKT's 24%, reflecting superior asset rotation and lower capital intensity relative to offshore cable manufacturers.
8.
Disciplined capital intensity: Total FY 2025 capital expenditure of €20.40 million represented only 4.53% of sales (calculated), dramatically below peer NKT's highly intensive 27.32% driven by massive front-loaded investments in Karlskrona and Cologne capacity expansion.

Industry structure and competitive dynamics

9.
Massive addressable market expansion: The global connector market reached €81.90 billion in calendar year 2023, projected to grow at 6.50% CAGR through 2030, while data center capital expenditures are expected to expand at a 20% annual rate through 2030, driving unprecedented grid infrastructure demand.
10.
Concentrated oligopolistic structure: The top five global connector manufacturers accounted for 38.70% of the market in CY23, led by TE Connectivity at 15.60%, while high-voltage grid equipment is dominated by a tight triopoly comprising Siemens Energy, Hitachi Energy, and GE Vernova, supporting disciplined industry-wide pricing.
11.
Robust pricing leverage environment: Power grid equipment manufacturers achieved 30% to 50% pricing increases throughout 2025, with high-voltage accessories and transmission margins continuing to exceed consensus at approximately 20%, contrasting sharply with softening low-voltage construction margins.
12.
Stable share concentration: The top three high-voltage cable manufacturers control approximately 75% of the market, ensuring high structural stability, while tight supply-demand dynamics have allowed pricing on new high-voltage orders to effectively double over three years based on downstream backlog proxies.

Customer relationships and switching barriers

13.
Exceptionally diversified customer base: The single largest customer accounted for less than 4% of consolidated sales in fiscal year 2025, with the top ten customers collectively generating slightly more than 25%, preventing high customer concentration risks inherent in major European power grids.
14.
Extreme asymmetric switching costs: Defective grid accessories cause outages where repair costs and penalties exceed the component price by multiples, with transmission system operators facing massive regulatory fines for unplanned network downtime, rendering supplier switching highly irrational since accessories represent only 5% to 8% of final substation costs.
15.
Multi-year qualification moats: Cable accessories must undergo lengthy multi-year qualification and certification cycles before deployment, requiring electrical, mechanical, and environmental laboratory testing up to 1,100 kV, with public utilities demanding proven multi-decade field records under extreme climatic conditions.

Operator quality and capital allocation discipline

16.
Seasoned co-CEO leadership: Co-CEO Johannes Linden oversees group operations, production, and finance with over 30 years of industrial experience, while Co-CEO Dr. Konstantin Kurfiss directs global sales and technology development with over 20 years of transmission expertise, including past executive leadership at cable peer NKT.
17.
Fully funded growth capital program: Strong demand forced management to increase the investment plan to €270 million through 2030, including €30 million for the state-of-the-art HVDC laboratory in Winterbach and the strategic acquisition of submarine connection partner Power CSL for €9.3 million in April 2025.
18.
Proven operational resilience: Management restored silicone manufacturing within one quarter of the September 2024 Wunsiedel plant fire, completing relocation to Kadaň in the first half of 2025 and capturing lower Czech labor costs at 35% to 45% of German levels.

Strategic risks and vulnerability assessment

19.
Unhedged raw material exposure: PFISTERER is heavily dependent on volatile chemical compounds, silicones, and metals, with sourcing of casting resins and plastics experiencing significant price changes over the last 12 months, while newly developed high-voltage direct current products demand substantially more metals and silicone per component unit.
20.
Limited non-metal inflation pass-through: Customers frequently reject price adjustments under fixed-price contracts for non-commodity inputs like specialized silicones and labor, with any failure to correctly estimate or control material costs under fixed-price agreements materially impacting group profitability.
21.
Vertical integration credibility: Cable manufacturers are developing proprietary terminations, including NKT's new KFEV 245 dry plug-in termination, creating the risk that large cable OEMs could accelerate in-house accessory designs to eliminate third-party margins, bypass independent suppliers, and permanently compress specialized margins.

Long-term structural tailwinds and paradigm alignment

22.
