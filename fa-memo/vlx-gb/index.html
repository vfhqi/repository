The operating drivers behind the inputs

  • Data centre is flat in FY2027 and grows slowly after. About 245m in FY2027 (H1 about 125m, H2 about 120m), then +5%, +7%, +5.5% and +5%. H1 FY2026 was about 85.5m and H2 about 150m, including a 20m to 30m one-off (Jefferies, 19-Oct-26). Share gained during competitors' shortages is normalising, and renewal price-downs are offset by the 1.6T ramp from FY2028. The largest customer (18.7% of FY2026 revenue; named by Jefferies as Amazon) is effectively the line.
  • The rest of the group rebounds in FY2027 and then normalises. Non-data-centre organic growth is about +14.5% in FY2027 (about 3 points of copper pass-through, about 3 points of restocking; EV, Off-Highway defence and Medical), then about 5.5% to 6% a year.
  • KST contributes about 36m in FY2027 (consolidated from July 2026) and about 55m in FY2028, at a margin above the group's.
  • Bolt-on acquisitions: about 60m a year of spend from FY2028, buying about 50m of revenue a year at about a 15% operating margin. Acquired revenue is 25m, 75m, 125m and 175m in FY2028 to FY2031. KST's deferred consideration is 7m in each of FY2028 and FY2029. The no-acquisition EPS is 0.535, 0.607, 0.677, 0.752 and 0.823.
  • Margin. The incremental operating margin is about 14% to 15% on organic growth. FY2027 is 10.8%: +60bp, of which copper dilution is about -25bp to -30bp. After that the margin rises 20bp to 30bp a year to 11.9% by FY2031. Self-help (site consolidation, San Luis Potosi vertical integration, Irvine) is mostly absorbed by data-centre price-downs and wage inflation. The EBITDA margin rises faster than consensus's because we carry D&A at about 2.8% of revenue (consensus 37.7m to 42.6m looks low against capital expenditure of 50m-plus).
  • Phasing. H1 FY2027 is about 712m (+21.9%) at an operating margin of about 10.7%. H2 FY2027 is about 723m (+9.8%; organic about 6%) at about 10.9%: the pull-forward reverses and the data-centre one-off is lapped. H1 FY2028 is about 762m (+7.0%).
  • Below the line.
  • Net interest is about 21m and flat: the floating-rate revolving facility, lease interest of about 2m, and about 6m of fixed finance items.
  • The underlying tax rate is 24.5%, then 24.0% (23.6% in FY2026; Turkey's 12.5% production rate from 2027 is partly capped by minimum-tax rules).
  • Minorities (inYantra) are about 2m to 3m a year.
  • The 3.68m-share buyback (20m GBP, -26.5m USD) completed in October 2026. After that, about 8m a year of market purchases to settle awards, against about 1m shares a year of vesting; January 2026 LTIP vesting adds to FY2031.
  • Cash.
  • Net working capital falls from 28.4% of revenue (March 2026) to 26.5% and then 25.5%. The hub inventory build stops with data centre flat, but the hub model persists.
  • Capital expenditure is 3.2% to 3.5% of revenue; lease payments about 10m to 12m.
  • Free cash flow after leases is 0.53 to 0.66 times underlying net income.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
