Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Stating the core operational and financial trajectory of Grifols will determine its long-term equity valuation and debt refinancing viability.

The Key Question

1.
Investigating feasibility: "Grifols, S.A. (GRF-ES; ADRs on Nasdaq as GRFS), a Spanish vertically integrated producer of plasma-derived medicines (immunoglobulins including subcutaneous Xembify, albumin, alpha-1 antitrypsin Prolastin-C, hyperimmunes and specialty proteins, with majority-owned Biotest), run since July 2026 as Biopharma US and Biopharma Rest of World with over 400 plasma donation centres and a plasma joint venture in Egypt, plus Diagnostic, Bio Supplies and Others segments. How plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"

Impact on Earnings and Valuation

2.
Financial progression: The proposed gross margin improvement of Grifols to 40% to 42% by 2028 to 2029 is critical for its consensus EPS progression. Morgan Stanley estimates show that satisfying these targets is vital to support its base case price target of €11 per share. Failure to elevate margins would restrict the expected recovery of cash flows and delay debt reduction, keeping the company exposed to high leverage risk.
3.
Capital constraints: The cost of capital for Grifols remains heavily dependent on its operational performance and leverage ratio metrics. Morgan Stanley valuations apply an 8.0% WACC because of the risks related to its high corporate leverage. Successful execution of the operational turnaround is therefore essential to reduce its WACC and achieve a positive valuation re-rating.
4.
Deleveraging goals: The management team at Grifols targets a leverage ratio of 3.0x to 3.5x under the credit agreement definition by 2029. According to Morgan Stanley, reaching this leverage milestone is highly dependent on improving cash flow conversion rates from 15% to 40% over the next few years. Resolving this key question determines whether the equity story successfully shifts from a special situation to a fundamental turnaround.

Credit Ratings and Refinancing Costs

5.
Refinancing dynamics: Debt maturity management is a key risk factor for Grifols because of its high outstanding debt burden. Company reports show that the successful refinancing of 2027 maturities in April 2026 has extended its debt profile to 2032 and 2033. However, subsequent refinancing of other obligations at potentially higher coupon rates will directly squeeze net earnings unless cash generation accelerates.
6.
Contrary evidence: The refinancing of the 2027 maturities has already successfully closed, meaning Grifols faces no significant debt maturities until the fourth quarter of 2028. This successful execution by management, reported in November 2026, reduces immediate liquidity pressure and grants the company more time to deliver on its margins.
7.
Credit ratings: The progression of Grifols' credit ratings across major agencies is closely linked to its leverage reduction path. Deutsche Bank research notes that rating agency discussions focus heavily on margin progression and fundamental cash flow improvements. Rebuilding credit rating status to investment grade is a critical milestone that depends on sustaining this margin profile.
8.
Other side of the story: Investors may overstate the risk of operational execution because Grifols has already demonstrated robust organic deleveraging capabilities since 2024. Credit agreement leverage fell from 5.5x in early 2024 to 3.98x by late 2026, showcasing strong underlying business performance. This deleveraging record suggests that the company possesses the necessary operational strengths to achieve its margin recovery plan.

Section 2 – Plausibility of Gross Margin Recovery vs. Operating Cost Cuts

Section summary
  1. Grifols' planned gross margin recovery towards 40% to 42% by 2028 is plausible but faces structural constraints as operating cost cuts reach their practical limits.
  2. Industry headwinds and a slower inventory cycle mean that near-term gross margin gains will remain gradual despite falling donor fees.

Margin Targets and Cost Structure Limits

1.
Significant progression: Grifols has reported a consolidated gross margin of 38.0% for the first nine months of fiscal year 2026. This represented a solid recovery from the 37.1% margin reported in the first half of 2026. However, achieving the company's targets will require elevating gross margins towards 40% to 42% over the next three years. This margin recovery is critical to support the consensus estimates for long-term earnings per share.
2.
Efficient operations: Grifols' operating expenses stood at 21% of revenues by the end of 2024, indicating that overheads are already highly optimized. Management has publicly stated that these operating expense levels are already highly efficient compared to historical averages. Morgan Stanley research highlights that future cost-cutting initiatives will focus more heavily on SG&A efficiency rather than cutting core R&D investments. Consequently, future earnings growth must rely on gross margin improvements because operating expense cuts are reaching their structural floors.
3.
Alternative operating paths: Morgan Stanley analysts reported that Grifols achieved its first-quarter 2026 adjusted EBITDA targets despite gross margins falling well below consensus expectations. This EBITDA resilience was driven primarily by aggressive operating expense containment which offset a 36.5% reported gross margin. Such cost control mitigated the immediate impact of high US donor center restructuring costs and lower diagnostic revenue. However, relying on expense reductions to sustain profitability highlights the vulnerability of earnings if gross margins remain depressed.
4.
Slower margin expansion: Deutsche Bank updates indicate that Grifols prioritized profit margins over aggressive revenue volume growth in 2026. This strategy involves balancing immunoglobulin sales volumes against weak Chinese albumin pricing to protect the profitability of each liter of plasma. Management expects this selective volume optimization to steadily enhance the consolidated EBITDA margin without compromising supply. This operational trade-off suggests that the trajectory towards a 42% gross margin will be gradual.

Comparative Analysis of Sector Peers

5.
Moderate peer benchmarks: CSL Behring serves as the primary industry benchmark with a reported gross margin of 50.7% in 2025. Jefferies research models a gradual recovery in CSL Behring's gross margin, projecting it to reach 53.3% by 2027. This peer comparison indicates that Grifols' current 38.0% gross margin remains significantly below the industry standard. Reaching a 42% margin would close this competitive gap, but it requires superior collection yield improvements.
6.
Divergent yield strategies: CSL Behring has actively upgraded its collection network by transitioning its entire US fleet to Haemonetics' NexSys devices and Persona Plus technology. Morgan Stanley projects that a 5% yield benefit from this transition could improve CSL's gross margin by approximately 75 basis points. In contrast, Grifols has historically relied on footprint consolidation and ex-US collection centers to lower its cost base. This divergence suggests that Grifols may struggle to match peer margins unless it implements comparable software-driven yield upgrades.
7.
Challenged industry dynamics: Takeda has also experienced sales moderation across its plasma-derived therapies division in recent quarters. Jefferies reports show that Takeda's Chinese albumin revenue declined by 10% in the first half of 2026, illustrating sector-wide pricing pressures. Grifols faces similar headwinds, with its Chinese albumin revenue declining by 13% during the same period. These synchronized revenue declines indicate that external pricing pressures remain a major obstacle to gross margin expansion for all integrated fractionators.

Macroeconomic and Headwind Pressures

9.

This persistent cost pressure will continue to inflate cost of goods sold unless center productivity increases.
10.
Adverse currency translations: Grifols' reported earnings remain highly vulnerable to foreign exchange fluctuations, particularly the relative strength of the US dollar against the euro. Grifols' management reported that a weaker US dollar resulted in a 23 million euro translation headwind to adjusted EBITDA in the first quarter of 2026. A weaker dollar dilutes the translated value of US revenues while the company's cost base remains denominated in expensive euros. This exchange-rate volatility will directly depress gross margin recovery efforts if the euro remains strong.
11.
Lagged inventory effects: The plasma industry operates on a long manufacturing cycle of seven to twelve months from collection to final product sale. Grifols' cost of goods sold in 2026 continues to reflect the high-cost plasma collected during previous inflationary periods. This timing mismatch means that recent donor-fee reductions will only impact the income statement with a significant delay. Consequently, near-term gross margins will remain depressed by older, expensive inventory even as current collection costs fall.
12.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley forecasts show that Grifols' gross margins could recover faster than expected to reach 42.2% by 2028 if US plasma economics improve rapidly. Furthermore, Morgan Stanley forecasts indicate that improving US plasma economics will drive steady margin gains through 2027. This operational efficiency suggests that Grifols has multiple software and sourcing levers to sustain its earnings turnaround.

Section 3 – Efficacy of Plasma-Centre Closures and Improvement Plans

Section summary
  1. Closures of underperforming plasma centres deliver substantial long-term cost reductions but require significant upfront restructuring cash outflows and face a nine-to-twelve-month inventory lag before improving gross margins.
  2. Grifols and CSL have consolidated their footprints to eliminate operational inefficiencies, while ADMA Biologics has shifted to a capital-efficient third-party sourcing model to boost profitability.

CSL Plasma Footprint Optimization

1.
Substantial restructuring charges: CSL announced the closure of 22 underperforming plasma centres representing 7% of its US footprint in August 2025. To evaluate these restructuring dynamics, this analysis examined three distinct companies, three comparable past cases, and over ten relevant documents. These network optimization initiatives were accompanied by one-off pre-tax restructuring costs estimated at $700 mm to $770 mm.
2.
Underperforming center drag: CSL suffered from high fixed-cost overheads due to maintaining several low-volume centers during the pandemic to secure scarce plasma. These locations created unfavorable fixed-cost absorption across CSL's manufacturing network, which depressed historical gross margins after donor collections stabilized. Once the RIKA and iNomi yield platforms matured, CSL was able to retire these inefficient assets to streamline its cost structure.

While cash cost savings were realized throughout 2023, the bulk of these margin benefits only began to impact the reported P&L during fiscal year 2024. This manufacturing lead time means gross margin gains from operational plans are heavily back-ended.

Upfront restructuring headwinds: Grifols recognized a one-off restructuring charge of EUR 140 million in the first quarter of 2023 to execute its cost-cutting program. This diversified supply strategy minimizes capital expenditure risks while ensuring sufficient raw materials to meet its production targets.

This yield technology extracts 20% more immunoglobulin from the same volume of plasma, proving far more accretive to gross margins than physical center expansions.

Section 4 – Scalability and Economics of the Egyptian Plasma Platform

Section summary
  1. Grifols' joint venture in Egypt is expected to scale to three million liters of plasma by 2029, structurally lowering sourcing costs and reducing reliance on the United States.
  2. While precedent ex-US platforms demonstrate strong cost advantages, geopolitical risks and a long inventory cycle will delay the margin and EPS benefits until 2027.

Egyptian Platform Sourcing Targets

1.
Strategic expansion: Grifols has established a strategic public-private partnership with the Egyptian government to build a vertically integrated plasma platform. To evaluate these ex-US sourcing dynamics, this analysis examined four distinct companies, three comparable past cases, and over ten relevant documents. Company reports published in November 2026 indicate the joint venture is on track to collect approximately one million liters of plasma in 2026 and targets up to three million liters annually by 2029. Reaching these milestones is expected to diversify Grifols' global supply network and significantly improve its long-term corporate profitability.
2.

This regulatory milestone makes Egypt the first country in Africa and the Middle East to operate a fully integrated, European Union-compliant plasma ecosystem. According to company presentations in February 2026, the approval allows Grifols to export surplus Egyptian plasma directly to European manufacturing facilities. This unique regulatory access strengthens supply resilience and supports margin expansion by replacing expensive US imports.

Promising certification Accelerated buildout: Grifols Egypt currently operates 18 donation centers and expects to expand its footprint to 20 active centers by the first quarter of 2027. Sourcing Costs and Recognition Lag

4.
Favorable economics: Egypt provides highly attractive plasma collection economics because its per capita GDP is ten times lower than Spain's. A news report in October 2026 highlights that paying donor compensation is legal in Egypt, allowing incentives that exceed 50% of the national minimum wage. This economic backdrop enables Grifols to achieve a structurally lower cost per liter compared to its US operations. Lower raw material costs will directly improve Grifols' consolidated gross margins as Egyptian volumes ramp up.
5.
Cost advantages: Global plasma collection costs remain historically high, with post-pandemic US collected costs exceeding $200 per liter. Jefferies research from November 2026 notes that US donor compensation alone averages approximately $70 per liter. By comparison, the Egyptian platform provides Grifols with a highly competitive ex-US cost structure because of lower regional wages and donor fee requirements. This labor arbitrage is critical for lowering the average cost of goods sold and driving EPS growth.
6.
Prolonged cycle: Plasma-derived therapies carry a long manufacturing lead time of seven to twelve months from collection to finished product sale. An expert call transcript from November 2021 indicates that these long cycle times delay the financial impact of cost-reduction initiatives. Grifols management has confirmed that its plasma cost savings face a typical nine-month accounting lag before appearing in results. This means the low-cost Egyptian plasma collected in 2026 will not materially expand gross margins until late 2027.
7.
Positive updates: Bernstein Research reported in July 2026 that it raised its long-term adjusted EBITDA margin forecasts for Grifols by 30 to 50 basis points. This upgrade reflects the expectation that Egyptian plasma donations will become a larger percentage of ex-US supply over the next decade. Bernstein analysts believe that sourcing cheaper ex-US proteins represents a game-changing competitive advantage. These improved margins are expected to boost cumulative free cash flow, accelerating the company's deleveraging path.

8.
Strong precedents: China represents a key historical precedent where local fractionators successfully built low- cost ex-US plasma networks at scale. Morgan Stanley research shows that China's plasma collection surged to approximately 14,000 tonnes in CY25, driven by a strict regulatory ban on foreign immunoglobulin imports. Leading domestic producers like Hualan Biological Engineering and China Biologic Products leveraged government-approved collection stations to secure high-margin local supply and meet soaring domestic demand. This precedent suggests that regional self-sufficiency platforms can successfully operate under favorable state support.
9.
High productivity: Chinese fractionators have optimized their regional stations, with average plasma collection per center rising to 60 tonnes in fiscal year 2025. Jefferies reports that companies like Hualan achieve superior margins by keeping donor nutrition fees aligned with local economic conditions. However, because the maximum donation frequency in China is capped at once every two weeks, productivity gains rely heavily on high donor retention and advanced extraction software. This comparison indicates that Egypt's more flexible collection rules could yield even better unit economics.
10.
Targeted programs: Takeda Pharmaceutical launched a groundbreaking collaboration with the Indonesian government in July 2026 to strengthen its local plasma ecosystem. Under this agreement, Takeda will invest up to 30 million US dollars in a two-year pilot program to establish feasibility before expanding into a national collection network. This program is designed to secure local self-sufficiency in therapies while potentially integrating Indonesia into Takeda's global supply chain. This initiative highlights how major competitors are increasingly looking ex-US to expand their sourcing footprints.
11.
Broad footprints: CSL Behring has historically maintained the industry's benchmark global collection footprint to support its high gross margins. Morgan Stanley research indicates that CSL has opened and acquired numerous ex-US centers across Germany, Austria, and Hungary to diversify its raw material sourcing. This global supply network reduces CSL's dependence on the US market while optimizing its regional price-cost structures. Grifols' Egyptian platform mirrors this strategy, allowing it to compete directly with CSL on sourcing flexibility.

Risks and Global Optimization

12.

Company press releases highlight that this reallocation will reduce reliance on US exports, which currently account for 25% of US collections. By aligning local collection costs with local market prices, Grifols expects to achieve structurally higher profitability.
