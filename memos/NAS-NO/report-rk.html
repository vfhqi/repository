Outliers worth noting

The pre-restructuring leverage profile of Norwegian Air Shuttle represented a statistical outlier among European low-cost carriers, with adjusted net debt reaching NOK 74 billion before the 2020 default, a figure that exceeded the entire market capitalization of several peer airlines and highlighted the extreme balance-sheet pressure that ultimately validated bearish insolvency warnings. The scale of the fleet and capital commitment downsizing constituted a bottompercentile restructuring event, with the airline reducing its operating fleet from 156 to just 51 aircraft and terminating outstanding orders representing NOK 85 billion in future capital expenditure, a contraction ratio that placed Norwegian among the most severe airline distressed restructurings in European aviation history. An unexplained anomaly emerged in the carrier's post-restructuring accounting treatment, where recurring nonrecurring aircraft purchase gains totaling NOK 900 million in 2025 and NOK 858 million across multiple quarters were systematically credited against lease and depreciation expenses to artificially depress standalone unit cost metrics, creating a persistent earnings quality distortion that obscured underlying operational cost inflation trends and represented an outlier practice relative to peer cost reporting transparency standards.
