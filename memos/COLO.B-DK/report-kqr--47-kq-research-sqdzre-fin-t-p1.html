Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The business model of Coloplast carries significant operating leverage due to its highly centralized manufacturing footprint, but persistent inflation and reinvestment needs constrain near-term margin expansion.
  2. Understanding the exact split between fixed and variable costs is vital for investors because even minor shifts in growth or cost execution will heavily impact earnings per share.

The Central Problem Defined

1.
Central question: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"

Deutsche Bank estimates on 6 November 2026 that Coloplast will achieve flat adjusted EPS growth of DKK 24.59 in FY27 compared to DKK 24.70 in FY26. Investors focus closely on whether operating leverage can offset persistent Hungarian labor inflation to reignite double-digit EPS expansion. Consequently, resolving the key question determines whether the stock can rerate to historical premium valuations.
3.

Deutsche Bank reports on 6 November 2026 that free cash flow reached DKK 6.3bn in FY26, with capex expected to stay at 5% of sales in FY27 before declining toward 4% by FY30. High capital expenditure is necessary to support the Portuguese footprint transfer and Costa Rican ramp-ups, which limits near-term cash conversion. Investors must understand if improving asset productivity can accelerate free cash flow conversion to enable faster deleveraging.

Coloplast Cost Disclosures

1.
Significant material exposure: Coloplast's manufacturing cost disclosures for fiscal year 2025/26 show that raw materials and semi-finished goods constitute 51% of its total cost of goods sold of DKK 9,695m. Direct salaries account for 8% of cost of goods sold, which matches earlier broker expectations. This high exposure to materials means that raw material inflation directly affects gross margins.
2.
Detailed operational splits: Coloplast reports that indirect salaries represent 14% of cost of goods sold, while depreciations and amortisations account for 9% of production expenses. Freight and energy costs are relatively small, accounting for 4% and 2% of cost of goods sold, respectively. Understanding these components helps investors see that depreciation charges will rise with Portuguese plant investments.
3.
Elevated commercial overheads: Coloplast maintains a high selling cost model, with its distribution-to-sales ratio holding steady at 33% of revenues. Administrative expenses and research and development costs both stand at 4% of total sales. The high commercial cost structure means that selling overheads are mostly fixed, creating substantial operational leverage if revenue growth accelerates.
4.
Atypical operating dynamics: Coloplast's seasonal profitability shows that first-half margins are traditionally lower than second-half margins. Deutsche Bank notes on 6 November 2026 that Coloplast's margin progression in fiscal year 2026/27 will be heavily back-end weighted because of Biologics recovery. This back-end weighting demonstrates that short-term operating leverage fluctuates significantly based on product mix and commercial timing.
5.
Contrary evidence: Coloplast's operating leverage is far more constrained than traditional medtech models because of deliberate reinvestment choices. Deutsche Bank reports on 6 November 2026 that Coloplast's new strategy reinvests 80% of its DKK 500m cost savings back into innovation and commercial expansion. This strategic reinvestment prevents the cost savings from dropping to the operating profit line. Consequently, the business model exhibits limited near-term margin expansion despite solid volume growth.

Section 3 – Historical Operating Leverage and Margin Drivers (2015/16 – 2025/26)

Section summary
  1. Coloplast's adjusted operating margin has compressed from its historical peak of 33% due to the compounding impact of post-pandemic inflation and large dilutive acquisitions.
  2. The business model requires organic growth to exceed a threshold of 5% to unlock positive operating leverage and cover fixed distribution and reinvestment needs.

Decadal Margin Compression

1.
Downward trend: Historical performance at Coloplast shows a long-term decline in profitability from peak levels of about 33% in fiscal year 2015/16. Morgan Stanley indicates on 8 November 2022 that the company delivered a solid average of 7% organic growth and 33% operating margins from 2012 to 2022. This stable performance historically supported a high valuation premium that has since compressed due to operational challenges and major acquisitions. Investors now focus heavily on whether historical margin baselines can be restored under the current strategic period.
2.
Severe dilution: Strategic acquisitions have structurally shifted the cost profile and diluted the historically high operating margins of the business. Jefferies reports on 30 November 2023 that Coloplast's EBIT margin dropped from 32.7% in FY21 to below 28% in FY23 mainly due to inflation and the Atos acquisition. This structural step-down represents a departure from the company's historical financial performance. Consequently, the company has had to reframe its long-term operating margin targets below the historical peak levels.
3.
Stable baseline: Prior to the major post-pandemic inflation shock, Coloplast maintained exceptionally stable and high operating margins. Jefferies indicates on 30 November 2023 that Coloplast consistently delivered EBIT margins north of 30% for a decade prior to the recent dilution. This consistent performance was supported by strong market shares and pricing power in chronic care. However, the subsequent combination of inflation and M&A broke this predictable execution pattern.
4.
Peer comparison: Legacy margins at major medical consumables competitors highlight different operational trajectories and structural shifts. This comparison shows that peers are achieving meaningful operating margin expansion while Coloplast faces ongoing pressure.

Section 4 – Performance of Historical and New Cost Programs

Section summary
  1. Coloplast has successfully completed major historical cost-saving plans, but persistent labor inflation has continuously absorbed these structural savings.
  2. The newly announced Funding the Journey initiative primarily serves as a resource reallocation tool rather than a net driver of corporate operating margin expansion.
  3. Peer comparisons indicate that substantial restructuring programs require large upfront investments that can restrict near-term financial flexibility before delivering benefits.

Legacy Efficiency Initiatives

1.
Successful execution: Coloplast finalized its Global Operations Plan 5 in the 2023/24 financial year to offset rising Hungarian labor costs. This plan relied heavily on manufacturing automation to successfully release around 1,000 blue-collar workers and maintain headcount neutrality. The group subsequently transitioned to Global Operations Plan 6 to diversify its production footprint and build a massive new site in Portugal. However, persistent inflation and labor shortages in Hungary continued to pressure the group's aggregate financial results.
2.
Predictable integration: Coloplast has successfully achieved its target of delivering up to DKK 100m in run-rate operational synergies from the Atos Medical acquisition. This integration was finalized in the 2025/26 financial year by utilizing the group's global infrastructure. These structural savings helped support administrative expense leverage and offset commercial investments. Consequently, the acquired voice and respiratory care business continues to perform consistently in line with expectations since consolidation.
3.
Decisive restructuring: Coloplast completed a major right-sizing of its commercial organization in China at the end of the 2025/26 fiscal year. This operational reset was required because China's organic growth collapsed from double-digit projections to low-single digits. The restructuring successfully lowered local selling overheads and helped to protect the group's distribution-to-sales ratio during the transition. Management expects the benefits of this right-sizing to support margin recovery from the 2026/27 financial year onward.
4.
Strategic pruning: Coloplast completed the divestment of its core Skin Care portfolio in December 2024 to simplify business operations. This portfolio transaction reduced reported revenue by approximately DKK 350m for the 2024/25 financial year. The divestment also generated a positive cash inflow of DKK 192m in the first quarter of that year. Removing this low-margin business delivered an immediate operating margin benefit of 30 basis points for the group.

5.

This initiative is designed to free up around DKK 500m annually with full operational impact expected by the 2027/28 financial year. A substantial portion of the resource reallocation will focus on reducing distribution costs and simplifying administrative structures. To implement these structural changes, the company expects to incur special items expenses of DKK 200m during the implementation.

Coloplast plans to reinvest approximately 80% of the freed-up resources into innovation and commercial expansion. The remaining 20% of the savings will help to offset gross margin headwinds from raw material and freight inflation. Consequently, the program is unlikely to drive significant near-term net operating margin expansion.
8.
Commercial focus: Coloplast is directing its reallocated resources toward chronic care innovation and expanding its commercial capabilities. Key commercial investments will target the lucrative United States chronic care and urology markets. The group is also investing in technology platforms and artificial intelligence to support company-wide efficiency enhancement and scalability.

Section 5 – Manufacturing Footprint Transfers and Ramp-Up Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Coloplast is diversifying its production network away from Hungary through major capital investments in Portugal and Costa Rica to secure low-cost capacity.
  2. Geographic transitions create near-term gross margin headwinds from factory underutilization and elevated dual-running costs before structural savings can be realized.
  3. Historical precedents across the medical consumables sector confirm that footprint transfers initially diluting margins require several years to achieve cost parity.

Coloplast Expansion Timing and Costs

1.
Inaugural plant operations: Coloplast completed the construction of its largest manufacturing facility in Felgueiras, Portugal, which became operational in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025/26. The new 50,000 square meter factory represents a total investment of approximately EUR 100 million. This facility will initially focus on high-volume production for continence care products to meet growing global demand. This expansion is designed to diversify European manufacturing footprint from Hungary.
2.
Prolonged ramp-up headwinds: Coloplast management expects the headwind from Portuguese and Costa Rican plant ramp-up costs to persist through the 2026/27 financial year. The physical transfer of production lines and the validation of machinery limit initial factory utilization. Coloplast reports that initial staffing and training of over 300 employees at the Felgueiras facility will drive elevated overhead expenses. Consequently, near-term gross margins will remain under severe pressure during this transition.
3.
Steady volume growth: Coloplast expects the Portuguese and Costa Rican facilities to gradually increase their share of global production volumes over the next decade. Under the Global Operations Plan, Costa Rica's volume share grew from 7% to 13% in fiscal year 2023/24. Management targets both Portugal and Costa Rica to each account for 20% to 25% of total volumes by 2029/30. Reaching these targets will remove the need for additional factories until 2030.

Labor Cost Differentials and Inflation

4.
Attractive cost structures: Coloplast selected Portugal and Costa Rica because both countries provide highly qualified labor forces at competitive cost levels. Prior to these investments, Coloplast concentrated 74% of its manufacturing capacity in Hungary, where the labor market became highly pressured. Expert insights from a former Coloplast executive on 20 October 2026 indicate that automation was necessary to offset the rising wage bill in Hungary. Portugal offers a stable alternative that reduces European geographic concentration risks.
5.
Persistent wage disparities: Hungarian wage inflation remains a key risk for Coloplast because the country still handles around 70% of total production. Coloplast reports that Eastern European labor shortages have continuously driven up manufacturing expenses over the last several years. Jefferies highlights on 4 November 2026 that Hungarian salary inflation is expected to rise by high-single digits from January 2027. This inflation forces Coloplast to seek lower salary environments like Costa Rica.
6.
Beneficial wage differentials: Salary inflation in Costa Rica remains in the low-single digit range, offering a significant structural advantage over Hungary. This labor cost differential helps to offset the higher freight costs of shipping raw materials to the Americas. However, the actual unit-labor-cost benefits will be realized slowly as utilization rates build up in the new sites. Therefore, the near-term margin recovery remains highly dependent on stabilizing global input costs.

Financial Depreciations and Capex Impacts

7.
Elevated capital intensity: Coloplast maintained a capital expenditure-to-sales ratio of 5% in the 2025/26 financial year to complete the Portuguese factory. Capitalized construction costs in Portugal reached DKK 195 million in FY26 compared to DKK 450 million in FY25. This heavy investment program is expected to increase annual depreciation charges across the manufacturing network.
