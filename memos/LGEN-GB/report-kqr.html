Section 2 – Market size, growth targets, and capital-light mix dynamics

Section summary
  1. Structural transition to capital-light fee businesses is accelerating across European life insurers, driven by ambitious multi-year targets and the rapid growth of workplace pension platforms
  2. However, the shift is unevenly executed, as massive legacy spread-based books and volatile real estate fee streams continue to dominate the earnings mix for several major players
  3. Insurers face a tightening window of pricing power in bulk annuities as intense pricing competition and regulatory updates squeeze margins, though workplace pensions benefit from legislated consolidation tailwinds

Section 8 – Data gaps

Section summary
  1. Material disclosure gaps around standardized longevity stresses, platform margin definitions, and capital-light metrics prevent fully transparent cross-peer comparisons of the European life insurance fee transition.

Structural reporting discrepancies

1.
Inconsistent longevity assumptions: Regulators and credit analysts face major hurdles because life insurers apply non-standardized longevity stresses, with Standard Life applying a five percent longevity stress on an IFRS basis compared to only one percent at Legal & General and two percent at Aviva. This variance means that disclosed contract service margin sensitivities remain highly subjective and limit direct comparability across peers.
2.
Divergent capital-light definitions: Valuation specialists struggle to evaluate business mixes because firms use vastly different parameters for capital-light reporting, such as Aviva including General Insurance in its metric and M&G relying heavily on its With-Profits Fund. This lack of standardization can be resolved by a detailed regulatory filing review to align definitions to a common baseline.
3.
Opaque Swiss profitability sources: Comparing Swiss Life and Baloise remains challenging due to different local disclosure methodologies for biometric risk profits. Analysts can resolve this metric gap through targeted expert calls with former corporate actuaries to disaggregate savings and risk profits.
4.
Aggregated capital generation metrics: Insurers do not provide granular disclosures showing the precise components of Operating Capital Generation, such as separate new business capital strain and back-book release values. This opacity prevents investors from understanding the true underlying run-rate of capital generation, which can only be resolved if analysts wait and monitor for enhanced disclosures.

Granular operational and asset disclosure gaps

5.
Unclear intra-group cash flows: Transparency remains low regarding the movement of liquidity between principal operating subsidiaries and the top-tier holding company. Investors are forced to wait and monitor statutory Solvency and Financial Condition Reports to extract intra-group dividend approvals.
6.
Opaque platform margin details: While Aviva Investors solutions benefit from combined platform administration fees and fund management fees, the company does not disclose granular cost-to-income ratios for individual digital platform cohorts. This can be addressed through field visits to wealth management distributors to estimate average platform pricing power.
7.
Unstandardized private asset data: The ongoing integration of private assets into unit-linked insurance wrappers is hindered by highly confidential and unaudited private market data. General partners and insurers must address these operational and administrative challenges through industry-wide data aggregation partnerships.

side of the story: – The argument that data gaps prevent robust investment analysis is weakened by the wealth of raw, standardized regulatory data published annually in Solvency and Financial Condition Reports.

Specialized analysts argue that any lack of transparency is actually an analytical failure to thoroughly extract and reverse-engineer the extensive Quantitative Reporting Templates already available in public regulatory filings.
