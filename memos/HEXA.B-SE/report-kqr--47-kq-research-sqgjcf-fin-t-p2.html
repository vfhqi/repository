Section 2 – Tariff Pass-Through and Currency Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Hexagon managed its European and Swiss tariff exposures through strategic surcharges, but experienced a clear lag in margin realization due to order backlog delivery timelines.
  2. The group maintains a strict policy against hedging currency transaction exposures, relying instead on natural regional matches to offset volatile foreign exchange movements.

Surcharge Dynamics and Backlog Lags

1.
Strategic pass-through: Hexagon estimated a direct quarterly US tariff headwind of up to EUR 15m starting in 2025. Management quickly implemented strategic pricing surcharges, logistics routing adjustments, and end-assembly shifts to insulate its EBIT. These rapid interventions successfully restricted the direct EBIT damage to just EUR 2m in the second quarter of 2025. Thus, Hexagon showed that strategic surcharges can mitigate trade barriers effectively.
2.
Delayed realization: Gross margin pressures from tariffs persisted into 2026 because price increases were implemented with a delay. Hexagon reported a 60 basis point like-for-like gross margin decline in the first quarter of 2026 due to full tariff effects. Management announced price adjustments at the end of the quarter, noting that backlog delivery times delayed economic benefits until the third quarter. This lag proves pricing actions require several months to restore profitability.
3.
Temporary relief: Hexagon eventually experienced margin support in the second half of 2026 as pricing actions took full effect. During the third quarter of 2026, the group's EBITAC margin benefited from certain US tariff refunds. However, these positive developments were mostly offset by rising transport costs from geopolitical conflicts. Therefore, underlying tariff surcharges remain vulnerable to inflation elsewhere.
4.
Surcharge stickiness: Sector analysis indicates that European capital goods companies generally hold sufficient pricing power to offset US tariffs. Bernstein Research concluded that Hexagon's gross margin expansion of 305 basis points over five years demonstrated its premium position. Consequently, a 20% US tariff scenario was modeled to add growth with no margin dilution, assuming full pass-through. This analysis reinforces the expectation that surcharges can remain highly sticky over time.

Currency Exposures and Hedging Rules

5.
Disproportionate costs: Hexagon faces a structural profitability mismatch because its manufacturing and R&D costs are heavily weighted toward Europe. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that 19% of the group's employee salaries are paid in Swiss francs. Consequently, any strengthening of the Swiss franc hurts operating earnings by raising the local cost base. This geographic asset concentration creates a recurring transactional risk for the group.
6.
Negative translations: Foreign exchange movements significantly altered Hexagon's consolidated financial results in fiscal year 2025. The strengthening of the Swiss franc and weakening of the US dollar generated a negative currency translation effect of EUR 75m. Furthermore, an isolated 5% strengthening of the euro was estimated to reduce group equity by millions upon consolidation. These translation risks directly affect reported earnings per share.
7.
Strict policies: Hexagon manages these foreign exchange transaction exposures through a distinctive internal capital structure. According to the Group Treasury Policy, transaction exposure should not be hedged using derivatives because most customer orders clear rapidly. Management also avoids hedging translation exposure relating to actual and forecasted earnings. This policy means earnings remain directly exposed to global exchange rate volatility.
8.
Favorable offsets: Positive exchange rate movements can occasionally provide material tailwinds to Hexagon's reported operating margins. In the third quarter of 2026, currency translation dynamics shifted favorably to add EUR 7.9m to group EBITAC. Stronger US dollar and Chinese renminbi rates positive for revenue offset the negative Swiss franc cost impact. However, these fluctuations highlight that foreign exchange impacts remain highly cyclical and volatile.

Peer Pass-Through and Pushback

9.
Peer hedging: Trimble manages its transactional foreign exchange risks differently by utilizing active hedging contracts to protect operating income. During the second quarter of 2026, foreign exchange fluctuations favorably impacted Trimble's operating income by $4.7m. Unlike Hexagon's unhedged policy, Trimble's active hedging approach helps smooth quarterly earnings volatility. This difference highlights that peers employ more active currency hedging than Hexagon's natural matching framework.
