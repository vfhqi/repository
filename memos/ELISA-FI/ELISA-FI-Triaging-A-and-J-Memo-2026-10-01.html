[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Elisa is a high-quality, low-growth Finnish operator that has lost its growth premium, not a company in the middle of a change. A late-2025 domestic price war put mobile service revenue into decline and the software growth engine stalled, so at EUR 36.46 the shares pay a 6.6 percent dividend yield to wait for a mechanical Q4 2026 contract roll-off that the sell-side already assumes. On our modal path that is a return of about 8 to 9 percent a year, and the case clears the framework's 20 percent bar only if growth above 3 percent comes back, which we give three-in-ten odds.

  • The change inside the company is real, dated and defensive.
    • A new chief executive (in office 1-Mar-24) and finance director (1-Nov-25), a EUR 40 million cost programme with 357 job cuts, campaign price rises of about EUR 10 in early 2026 that DNA followed, and a software division put under new leadership are all on the record, because the company is repairing a base rather than growing one.
    • The first full-year output of the new leadership was two component guidance cuts on 15-Jul-26: telecom service revenue growth to 0 to 2 percent from 1 to 3 percent, and software organic growth to 5 to 10 percent from over 10 percent.
      • That is the opposite of the guidance rise the framework's screen looks for.
    • The screen's own rule (three strong internal forces, or a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase) is not met: two forces are strong (the chief-executive change; cost and standards) and the rest are medium.
  • The external forces run against the company.
    • Five of the seven are headwinds now (competition, macro, disruption, regulation, hardware inflation).
    • The one tailwind, demand for speed tiers, is fading because 58 percent of Finnish subscribers already sit on high-speed plans.
    • The company's own cycle is at a trough that has not yet turned.
    • Mobile service revenue growth went from +4.8 percent (Q3 2024) to -1.7 percent (Q2 2026), and the turn the company dates to Q4 2026 has already slipped once, from H2 2026, because management under-modelled the volume of 12-month discounted contracts.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are not improving; the one that is, the margin, improves from cost.
    • Revenue fell 0.3 percent in Q2 2026 after +3.0 percent in FY2025
    • comparable earnings per share are flat (EUR 2.36 in FY2025 against EUR 2.35 in FY2024)
    • comparable cash flow fell 37.2 percent to EUR 71 million in Q2 2026
    • consensus FY2027 earnings per share fell 8.5 percent over the twelve months to 25-Sep-26.
    • The comparable EBITDA margin rose to 36.5 percent in Q2 2026, but comparable EBIT fell 0.9 percent to EUR 123 million in the same quarter, because depreciation rises with fibre and software amortisation.
  • The change has travelled three of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and part of the fourth.
    • Resources, choices and actions are in place.
    • The key operating metrics that lead have moved: churn is back to 16.7 percent from 23.0 percent, front-book prices for new sales are above the prior year, personnel expenses fell to EUR 111 million from EUR 118 million in a quarter.
    • The metric that decides, mobile service revenue, has not moved
    • the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates still run the wrong way.
  • What we think it earns: comparable earnings per share of EUR 2.36 in FY2025 rising to about EUR 2.51 in FY2028, about 2 percent a year, 1 to 2 percent below consensus, because we do not give the software division the EBITDA recovery consensus models and we hold depreciation rising.
    • Half of that growth is margin from cost, about a third is revenue growth at about 1 percent a year, and none of it is buybacks.
  • What it costs on the valuation: EUR 36.46, or 14.2 times consensus earnings 24 months forward of EUR 2.5657, the 2nd percentile of the stock's own ten-year range and the 10th percentile of five years against a five-year median of 17.27x, because the premium that growth above peers once earned has been withdrawn.
    • Against the comparison set the multiple is at the median, not cheap: the broad European mobile operators sit at 10 to 13x and the Nordic cohort above (Telia 16.6x, Tele2 18.8x).
      • So the valuation asks nothing extra to enter, and it offers no re-rating that growth does not first earn.
  • The return does not clear the bar.
    • Hold the multiple at 14.5x and the 'Modal return' is about 8.6 percent a year over eighteen months and 8.2 percent a year over three years, an F on the framework's anchors
    • the 6.6 percent yield carries nearly all of it, earnings growth adds under 1 point over the eighteen months and the re-rating 2 points.
    • A blend that gives the cleared branch (mobile service revenue growth back above 3 percent, software EBITDA positive, the multiple at 16.5x) three-in-ten odds gives 11.3 percent a year, a D.
      • Only the cleared branch itself, at 17 to 22 percent a year, reaches the bar.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (higher depreciation, dearer debt, a higher tax rate) takes FY2028 earnings per share to EUR 2.34 and the eighteen-month return to 3.7 percent a year
      • the rating does not move, because it already rests on a return that fails.
  • The pattern it fits is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', at a C fit.
    • A high-quality operator marked to the floor of its own range on a named, dated concern, with the quality carrying the yield while the concern clears.
    • The fit is not crisp, because the second concern, software, is of the least transient kind and the hard clearing evidence does not exist before 21-Oct-26.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the inputs to the leading operating metrics, and it holds on the dividend through the horizon, because the balance sheet at 1.8x net debt to EBITDA can fund a shortfall for two to three years.
    • It breaks at mobile service revenue, which must turn before the estimates stop falling
    • it breaks at software, which is not lagged but stalled
    • and it breaks at the return, which needs both a re-rating and growth the modal path does not have.

  • The case therefore is a good bond with an equity's downside.

    • A neutral park until the Q4 2026 print shows whether the back book re-prices, with a 6.6 percent yield paid to wait and a mark-to-market downside of 10 to 14 percent if the dividend's cover fails.

  • Rating = D, because on materiality the expected change is small: the input forces are real but defensive and partly offset by external headwinds ('Change Forces' at D), the outputs they drive are flat earnings with a cost-led margin ('Financial Outputs' at D), and the return they deliver is a 6.6 percent yield plus 2 percent growth, a 'Modal return' of 8 to 9 percent a year on the modal branch and 11 percent on the blend (the 'Modal return' Element at F, with D live).

    • The parts bind, so no Element carries the others.
    • Not C, because nothing in the chain reaches the 20 percent bar even on the blend
    • not F, because the yield is real, the balance sheet holds and a Q4 2026 turn is a dated, mechanical event with probable odds.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case for the other side, at its strongest: Q2 2026 is an artificial trough made by the mechanical lag of 12-month discounted contracts signed in the H2 2025 price war, so the recovery is arithmetic, not hope.
    • Front-book prices for new sales are already above the prior year
      • churn is back at its ten-year Q2 average of 16.7 percent
      • DNA followed the EUR 10 rise, Telia co-operated and says it has stopped accepting share loss.
    • When the contracts expire in Q4 2026 the back book re-prices upward, mobile service revenue turns to +1 percent or more, the EUR 40 million programme reaches full run-rate, the software backlog converts as deferred deals close, FY2026 comparable EBITDA beats the EUR 830 million midpoint, the sell-side upgrades, and the multiple recovers toward its two-year median of 16.25x, a 12 percent re-rating, or further.
  • What we concede: the lag is mechanical and the leading indicators are in place.
    • We put the odds that mobile service revenue is positive year on year by Q1 2027 at probable, about six in ten.
  • Why we still hold the D, first: the sell-side already assumes that turn (consensus telecom service revenue growth of about +1 percent for FY2026; FY2027 earnings per share +2.9 percent), so a turn that merely happens delivers no upgrade, and a re-rating needs a turn that beats.
  • Second: even the cleared branch, with mobile service revenue growth back above 3 percent and software EBITDA positive, gives an eighteen-month return of 17 to 22 percent a year, at the bar only with both the higher multiple and the higher earnings, and we give that branch three-in-ten odds.
  • Third: the second engine, software, is not lagged but broken (organic growth +0.6 percent, EBITDA EUR -5 million in Q2 2026, guidance cut), and the third, the dividend, is paid at a 102 percent payout.
    • A thesis that clears 20 percent needs all three to work at once, and their joint odds are low.
  • What it comes to: the competing hypothesis is the cleared branch of our own case.
    • We price it at its odds rather than adopt it.
