Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Seplat Energy's operational and financial profile reveals several statistical outliers that distinguish the company from both its indigenous Nigerian peers and the broader emerging markets E&P sector. These anomalies reflect a combination of structural advantages, unique fiscal positioning, and operational vulnerabilities that merit investor attention. The most pronounced outlier is the company's 743% year-on-year NGL segment revenue expansion to $83.4 million in 6M 2026, driven by the completed EAP upgrade offshore [Cross-ref: Section 6].

This growth rate dramatically exceeds both historical base rates for the sector and peer performance trajectories. Indigenous peer Tullow Oil, by comparison, posted 20.68% actual revenue growth in 1H 2026 [Cross-ref: Section 7], highlighting the exceptional nature of Seplat's NGL acceleration. The magnitude of this expansion suggests a structural shift in the company's revenue composition rather than cyclical pricing tailwinds, as the 300 MMscfd ANOH gas plant currently operates at only 60% processing capacity [Cross-ref: Section 9], implying further upside potential as utilization normalizes.

A fourth outlier is embedded in Seplat's working capital dynamics, specifically the scale and persistence of overlift and underlift adjustments. Reported revenues in 1Q 2026 included a substantial $92.0 million overlift, while 6M 2026 featured a crude oil overlift of $67.0 million [Cross-ref: Section 5]. These non-cash revenue adjustments represent 5.0-7.3% of reported quarterly revenues, a magnitude that exceeds typical E&P sector base rates of 1- 3%. In FY2025, other income was supported by a $26.9 million underlift adjustment [Cross-ref: Section 5], demonstrating that these swings are bidirectional and recurring. The volatility of unlifted inventory valuations introduces earnings quality concerns, as reported top-line growth can be artificially inflated or depressed by timing mismatches between production, lifting schedules, and buyer allocations.

This pattern is structurally distinct from integrated peers with more predictable offtake logistics. Finally, Seplat's employee engagement metrics present a positive outlier within the broader energy sector. Employee Net Promoter Score improved from 40% to 65% in 2025, reflecting outstanding internal culture progress
