Section 2 – Regulatory framework and wholesale pricing mechanics 13 findings

Section summary
  1. EETT's wholesale pricing framework enforces strict Significant Market Power obligations while enabling accelerated FTTH migration via FTTC stop-selling rules and margin squeeze controls.
  2. The dual dynamic of proposed FTTH wholesale rate cuts alongside copper price adjustments creates a regulated transition pathway toward full fiber adoption.

SMP designation and market control scope

1.
Regulatory authority and operator designation: EETT maintains regulatory oversight over Hellenic Telecom under Significant Market Power obligations across relevant wholesale and retail fixed access markets
2.
Wholesale rate ceilings: Significant Market Power obligations require Hellenic Telecom to submit its wholesale reference offers for regulatory cost-orientation approval, governing both bitstream and local loop unbundling access
3.
Retail margin squeeze framework: Regulatory price controls mandate that Hellenic Telecom maintain retail prices high enough above wholesale input costs to ensure competing alt-nets can replicate retail offers profitably
4.
Targeted retail flexibility: Recent regulatory modifications by EETT have relaxed retail price restrictions on specific FTTH tiers, allowing Hellenic Telecom to price retail fiber services more competitively against alternative infrastructure providers

FTTH pricing adjustments and commercial discounts

5.
Regulatory rate cut proposal: EETT has flagged a 10-13% reduction in regulated wholesale FTTH access tariffs aimed at lowering national fiber entry barriers
6.
Commercial discount insulation: Hellenic Telecom signed long-term commercial wholesale contracts with key alt- nets Nova and Vodafone offering volume discounts up to 28%, effectively reducing prevailing commercial rates below proposed regulatory ceilings
7.
Wholesale volume growth: Enhanced wholesale FTTH volume adoption led to record quarterly wholesale net additions in mid-2026, increasing infrastructure utilization across third-party operators
8.
Competitor infrastructure reliance: By mid-2026, 48% of competing operators' total FTTH subscribers relied on Hellenic Telecom's physical network infrastructure, up from 46% in mid-2025

Copper tariff adjustments and FTTC stop-sell framework

9.
