EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Core business model and diversification strategy

1.
Premium-focused model: The Navigator Company dominates the premium uncoated woodfree (UWF) paper segment, leveraging strong proprietary brands.
2.
Paper segment leadership: Its mill brands account for 66% of UWF sales compared to an industry average of 20-25%.
3.
Structural demand hedge: To offset the 1-2% annual decline in fine paper, NVG-PT is diversifying into tissue and sustainable packaging.
4.
Rapid tissue expansion: The tissue segment has scaled from 5% to approximately a quarter of total turnover inside ten years.
5.
Inorganic growth driver: This growth was accelerated by strategic acquisitions, including the recent integration of Navigator Tissue UK in 2024.

Financial profile and capital allocation

6.
Softened margin profile: Performance in 2025 was impacted by lower pulp prices, resulting in an EBITDA of €376 million.
7.
Margin contraction trend: The EBITDA margin compressed by 7 percentage points year-on-year to 19% in 2025.
8.
Dilutive tissue impact: Integrating the lower-margin UK Tissue converting business diluted the consolidated EBITDA margin by 1.2 percentage points.
9.
Resilient cash generation: FCF generation remains robust, supporting €210 million of capex and €175 million of dividends in 2025.
10.
Moderate debt leverage: Its net debt to EBITDA ratio stood at 1.87x at end-2025, representing a healthy balance sheet.

Value chain economics and resource moat

11.
Vertical integration advantage: The vertically integrated model spans from proprietary eucalyptus forest management to paper and tissue converting.
12.
High-quality wood base: Eucalyptus globulus provides superior yield and quality advantages over competitors like Klabin, reinforcing the cost moat.
13.
Pulp self-sufficiency: NVG-PT integrates most of its Bleached Eucalyptus Kraft Pulp (BEKP) production directly into downstream paper.
14.
Structural cost insulation: Industrial scale and co-generation assets mitigate global market volatility, maintaining below-average cash costs across segments.

Customer dynamics and supply risks

15.
Low customer concentration: The Group maintains low concentration by selling UWF paper to roughly 1,000 customers globally.
16.
Strategic digital channel: The NVG Hub e-commerce platform generated €300 million in online orders during 2025.
17.
Extreme weather vulnerability: Operations in Q1 2026 faced severe weather disruptions, causing lower output and higher energy costs.
18.
Margin pressure signals: Temporary operational limitations and lower pulp prices compressed the Q1 2026 EBITDA margin to 15.2%.

Operator quality and strategic foundation

19.
Strong majority control: Parent holding company Semapa enforces a highly conservative financial risk profile and disciplined capital allocation.
20.
Clear management alignment: Executive variable remuneration is tied mathematically to EBITDA, net profit, cash flow, and ESG indicators.
21.
High foundation quality: The overall BB#2 confidence rating is HIGH, backed by unmatched cost competitiveness and asset flexibility.
22.
Critical structural risk: The single largest long-term risk remains the accelerating decline of global fine paper demand.

Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. Low-cost South American hardwood capacity additions dominate global supply, while high-cost European assets face structural graphic paper demand declines.

Global pulp market size and demand drivers

1.
Massive global scale: The global pulp and paper market size was valued at USD 344.74 billion in 2024.
2.
Subdued growth forecast: The industry is projected to reach USD 416.56 billion by 2035, growing at a CAGR of 1.7% from 2025.
3.
Positive demand drivers: Long-term consumption growth is supported by population expansion, rising living standards, and plastic-to-fiber substitution trends.
4.
Growing Asian engine: Emerging markets drive demand, with China accounting for approximately 35% of global Bleached Softwood Kraft demand.
5.
Severe market decline: In contrast, mature Western economies face stagnating or contracting demand for graphic papers.

Segment-level demand divergence and structural shifts

6.
Shifting application mix: Market pulp usage is expanding primarily in tissue, hygiene products, packaging, and specialty papers.
7.
Dominant tissue segment: Tissue is the largest consumer application, absorbing approximately 45% of global market pulp deliveries.
8.
Severe graphic decline: Over ten years, the graphic paper share of market pulp fell by more than half to 18%.
9.
Shrinking European consumption: The European graphic paper market continued to shrink in 2025, with consumption declining by 7.9% year-on-year.
10.
Declining French market: France's stationery market fell 2.9% in 2025, with manufactured paper dropping 4.4%.
11.
Robust Indian growth: Indian packaging and paperboard consumption continues to grow rapidly, expanding at 8-9% per annum.

Regional cost curves and Latin American dominance

12.
Superior regional advantage: Southern hemisphere producers (specifically Brazil, Uruguay, and Chile) possess unsurpassed wood cost and yield advantages.
13.
Unmatched cost competitiveness: Delivered wood costs in Latin America are highly competitive at USD 220-240 per tonne.
14.
Severe margin compression: High-cost European assets face severe margin pressure from elevated wood and logistics costs.
15.
Spiking wood inflation: Nordic pulpwood prices spiked 60% for sawlogs and 80% for pulpwood between 2019 and 2025.
16.
Inflated Czech costs: Central European wood costs remain highly inflated, rising 14-15% in the Czech Republic.
17.
Vast South American capacity: South American producers are executing mega-projects, including Arauco's 3.5 million tonne Sucuriú project.
18.
Unfavourable mill displacement: Highly competitive South American hardwood pulp continues to displace high-cost Northern hemisphere production.

Market concentration and major producer shares

19.
Leading global market-pulp: The market pulp industry features major South American consolidators, with Suzano holding a dominant 29% market share.
20.
Vast land assets: Suzano operates 13.4 million tonnes of annual pulp capacity and manages 2.9 million hectares.
21.
Heavy BHKP concentration: The top five global market pulp producers control well over 50% of global BHKP capacity.
22.
Highly consolidated packaging: Niche European sack paper is consolidated, with Mondi holding over 40% capacity share.
23.
Extreme paperboard fragmentation: In contrast, the European containerboard market remains highly fragmented, with the top five players holding under 40%.

Competitor operating rates and capacity curtailments

24.
Depressed operating rates: Excess capacity and weak demand depressed European paper machine operating rates to 81% in 2025.
25.
Unviable asset margins: High-cost European mills face unsustainable margins, requiring permanent closures to rebalance supply.
26.
Aggressive graphic closures: Industry players eliminated approximately 1.2 million tonnes of European graphic paper capacity in 2025.
27.
Permanent softwood shutdown: Stora Enso is permanently closing 100 thousand tonnes of softwood paper pulp capacity at Skutskär.
28.
Widespread defensive downtime: Major global pulp producers, including Suzano, are taking market- related downtime to defend pricing.
29.
Temporary Nordic curtailments: UPM implemented temporary production curtailments at its 700 thousand tonne Kaukas mill.

Key success factors and entry barriers

30.
Imposing capital barriers: Building a globally competitive greenfield pulp mill requires capital investments of USD 1-3 billion.
31.
Essential upstream integration: Over 95% of competitive packaging producers are fully integrated into upstream wood fiber.
32.
Protracted construction timelines: Greenfield pulp projects require two to three years of construction and several years to ramp.
33.
Critical fiber access: The single most vital success factor is securing sustainable, low-cost wood fiber concessions.
34.
Vulnerable yield risks: Eucalyptus wood yields are highly sensitive to climate shifts, dropping up to 14% per 1°C increase.
35.
Other side of the story: European capacity rationalisation is moving too slowly; if low-cost Latin American producers oversupply the market, European integrated margins will permanently compress.
