Helios Towers (HTWS-GB): what the Q3 2026 update of 5 November 2026 does to the case

These results strengthen the operating half of our case modestly, leave the earnings-per-share half exactly where it was, and arrive with the shares about 12 percent above the price the case was struck at; the net is a small positive for the thesis and a thinner margin on the entry.

What changed

  • Volume. Tenancy additions were 924 in the third quarter (to 30 September 2026) against consensus of 662, and 3,435 for the nine months. The case's own test was "above 900 means a raise"; its model had about 850.
  • FY2026 guidance. Additions approximately 4,000 (was 3,500 to 4,000). Adjusted EBITDA approximately 535 USDm (was 520 to 535 USDm). Recurring free cash flow above 235 USDm (was 220 to 235 USDm). Discretionary growth capex 230 to 245 USDm (was 215 to 245 USDm). The case carries about 4,100 additions and 531 USDm of EBITDA.
  • FY2027, four months earlier than the case expected it. Adjusted EBITDA growth of at least 10 percent and over 3,000 additions. Brokers put the prior consensus for 2027 additions at about 2,200 to 2,500. The case's model is 3,000 additions and 12.6 percent EBITDA growth, and it expected the March 2027 guidance to open at 2,000 to 3,000 additions and to read as a deceleration.
  • The quarter. Revenue 252.5 USDm, up 17 percent year on year and 4.69 percent above consensus; Adjusted EBITDA 135.2 USDm, up 13 percent and 0.37 percent above consensus. The revenue beat is mostly power: escalations in the DRC added 11 USDm of pass-through revenue at nil EBITDA, and the Adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 53.5 percent from 54.7 percent in Q2 2026 and 55.5 percent in Q1 2026.
  • Balance sheet and returns. Net leverage 3.4x; 48 USDm of buybacks completed in the year to date; return on invested capital 14.2 percent on the last twelve months; the 2030 targets unchanged.
  • What was not published. Nothing below EBITDA: no tax, finance or currency line.
  • Sell-side. Jefferies to 315p from 300p, estimates up 2 to 5 percent, expecting consensus EBITDA to rise 1 to 3 percent. Deutsche Bank to 290p from 285p, FY2027 EBITDA up about 3 percent, implying about 14 percent growth. Morgan Stanley unchanged at 310p, with 3,400 additions for 2027. All three keep their positive ratings.
  • The price. 229.6p on 10 November 2026, against 205.2p when the case was written.

The challenge

Reading one: the case's edge has been spent, and what is left of it is the bad news. This is the reading the case does not hold. The case was built on a sequence: unfavourable surprises first (a sequential fall in additions on 5 November, an FY2026 EPS print about a quarter below consensus in March 2027), favourable ones after (FY2027 guidance at or above consensus, the buyback in the share count, a refresh of the 2030 targets). The first unfavourable event passed as a beat. The largest favourable event the case had dated to March 2027 is now public. The case's volume figures, which sat above consensus, are now the company's stated floor and so are probably about to become consensus. One broker has already added about 3 percent to FY2027 EBITDA in a single event, against the 2 to 4 percent the case budgeted for the whole period to mid-2027. What the case still knows that the market has not priced is now weighted to the negative and to the near term: the March 2027 EPS print arrives without the guidance news that was meant to sit beside it, and management did not use this update to pre-announce the tax rate or finance charge, which the case named as the thing that would defuse March. The entry is also dearer. Against the case's own values, 229.6p leaves about 22 percent to its unchanged-multiple value of 281p and about 47 percent to the 337p at its 20x fair multiple, and puts its 145p to 180p trough 22 to 37 percent below. For this reading to be right, two things have to be true: the buyers since 5 November are paying for the operating upgrade and are likely to mark the shares on EPS in March; and brokers lift rather than cut FY2026 EPS in the meantime, widening the gap the case calls near-certain. Against it stands the case's own argument that the market values this company on EBITDA and recurring cash, in which case the March EPS line is noise and a guidance floor set this early leaves room to raise again.

Reading two: the cycle is prolonged, not front-loaded. The case named this reading, deferred it to this update, and kept the middle path (about 4,100, 3,000, 2,400). The evidence it waited for leans against the side it took: management put a floor of over 3,000 on 2027 where the case expected an opening range no higher than 2,500 to 3,000, and one broker now carries 3,400. If the company's habit of guiding below the run-rate holds, a floor of 3,000 is plausibly an outcome of 3,300 to 3,500. For the reading to be right, Airtel Africa's second-half capex must not fall by the third its own guidance implies, and FY2028 additions must hold near 3,000 instead of the case's 2,400. But the results fit the case's 3,000 as well as they fit 3,500. Additions fell for a second quarter running (1,406, 1,105, 924), "approximately 4,000" implies about 565 in Q4 2026 against the roughly 665 the case's 4,100 needs, and over 3,000 is still a quarter below 2026. What the results do remove is most of the lower half of the case's FY2027 volume cone. They say almost nothing about FY2028, which is where the front-loading judgement is really tested.

An obvious alternative that fails. A 40 percent beat on tenancies with a 0.37 percent beat on EBITDA invites the reading that volume is converting badly, which the case flagged through a rising share of amendments. The arithmetic does not support it. On the case's own rule (27.7k USD a tenancy at an 80 percent incremental margin), 262 tenancies above consensus are worth about 5.8 USDm of annualised EBITDA and about 0.7 USDm inside the quarter they arrive in; the beat was 0.5 USDm. The margin fall is the power pass-through. The case's margin percentage for FY2026 (56.0 percent) is now unlikely to be met, but its EBITDA in dollars is being beaten.

One piece of arithmetic we cannot resolve. Recurring free cash flow is given as 133.7 USDm for the nine months, up 10 percent, against guidance of above 235 USDm for the year. Taken at face value that needs more than about 101 USDm in the fourth quarter alone, over twice the nine-month quarterly average. The case put what little risk of a guidance cut it saw on the cash line. Either the phasing is lumpy for reasons not disclosed here, or the nine-month figure is on a different basis; it is worth checking before it is trusted either way.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry the re-based consensus: FY2027 additions and EBITDA, the fourth house's response and target, and, most usefully, whether consensus FY2026 and FY2027 EPS has moved up on the volume news. A rise in FY2026 EPS consensus would confirm the first reading's mechanism. It cannot carry anything below EBITDA from the company, because nothing was published, nor anything on FY2028.
  • Airtel Africa's December-quarter capex, early 2027. The case's thresholds stand: above about 330 USDm lifts FY2027 additions toward 3,500; below about 200 USDm cuts toward 2,000. This is the first real test of reading two.
  • FY2026 results, March 2027. The EPS print, the tax reconciliation, the finance note, the fourth-quarter additions, full-year recurring free cash flow against the 235 USDm floor, the formal FY2027 ranges and the contracted-revenue schedule. Whether the shares are marked on EPS or on cash that day is the test of reading one.
  • Not committed by the company, so open for a different reason. Any guidance below EBITDA; whether the 2027 volume floor rests on signed agreements or on pipeline; the terms of the DRC renewals. Nothing scheduled answers these.
