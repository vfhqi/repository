Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The key question regarding medium-to-long-term earnings trajectories for European cable makers is fully resolvable due to unprecedented visibility from multi-year high-voltage order backlogs extending through 2030, though near-term execution risks and regulatory policy shifts introduce moderate variability in precise margin timing. The European power and telecom cable landscape is undergoing a structural transformation as legacy pre-2022 low-margin contracts are successfully washing out of backlogs, allowing modern high-margin awards to drive rapid operational margin expansion across European cable makers through 2030. Record-high transmission backlogs now provide extraordinary revenue visibility, with NKT expanding its high-voltage transmission backlog to €13.0bn as of mid-2026, Prysmian maintaining approximately €18bn providing visibility through 2030, Nexans increasing its adjusted backlog to €6.1bn by late 2023, and Cenergy's Hellenic Cables securing a landmark €1.15bn framework agreement with IPTO for Greek island interconnections in June 2026. This backlog accumulation represents a fundamental shift in industry economics, underpinned by structural capacity constraints and accelerating regulatory support for offshore wind auctions, cross-border interconnectors, and comprehensive onshore transmission grid reinforcements.

The margin inflection is already materializing across the sector, with NKT experiencing a clear improvement in 2026 as legacy pre-2022 contracts finished executing and higher-priced awards entered the revenue recognition phase, Prysmian Renewable Transmission segment margins reaching 20.3% in second quarter 2026 as complex subsea projects began execution, and Nexans PWR-Transmission segment recording a strong EBITDA margin of 15.4% in first half 2026, driven by high-value solutions.

These operational improvements validate the thesis that manufacturers have successfully translated unprecedented demand into immense negotiating leverage, securing extensive price indexation, cash down-payments, and robust contractual frameworks that insulate margins by passing copper and aluminum price fluctuations directly to public utility customers. Capacity expansion initiatives are progressing rapidly to resolve critical high-voltage transmission bottlenecks before 2030, with NKT on track to commence commercial operations at its Swedish Karlskrona facility in second quarter 2027 slightly ahead of the original schedule, Prysmian completing its Finland Pikkala plant expansion featuring a 185-meter vertical tower designed to manufacture daily one kilometer of 525kV submarine cables, and LS Cable beginning vertical construction on its $681m Virginia plant featuring a 660-foot tower slated to become the tallest industrial structure in the state, while Sumitomo broke ground on a £350m Scottish plant.

Subsea installation capacity is similarly expanding, with Prysmian's deep-sea installation capacity projected to be more than twice that of its key competitors by 2028, NKT launching its second high-end vessel NKT Eleonora for final outfitting in Norway to provide installation capacity of 23,000 tonnes of cable, and Nexans commissioning its third state-of-the-art vessel Nexans Electra in mid-2026. These manufacturing and maritime investments create formidable barriers to entry that reinforce the oligopolistic structure of the high-voltage subsea cable market.

The long-term demand outlook is underpinned by massive regulatory support, with over 37GW of offshore wind capacity scheduled for auction over the next 12 months across Europe and the Asia-Pacific region, new wind power installations in Europe projected to reach 151GW during the 2026-2030 period, and the cumulative number of European high-voltage direct current projects expanding at an 8% CAGR from 2023 through 2030. Onshore grid modernization is accelerating in parallel, with annual electricity network capital expenditure across major European nations estimated to more than triple 2022 levels by 2027, E.ON's upcoming Network Development Plan update in October 2026 expected to drive double-digit capex growth, Terna accelerating grid investments to facilitate the integration of southern renewable capacity into northern industrial load centers, Redeia ramping up transmission grid investments to nearly €13bn under its development plan running to 2030, and SSE initiating a £33bn five-year investment plan to 2029/30 with 80% dedicated to regulated electricity networks. This comprehensive infrastructure push reflects the structural imperative to decarbonize electricity systems while accommodating surging data center power requirements and high-density optical fiber upgrades that create highly profitable adjacent demand channels for diversified cable producers.

Several structural investment stories offer multi-year earnings visibility beyond the base-case backlog execution scenario. The German grid regulatory uplift expected in 2027 will unlock substantial utility capex, with grid operators anticipating €360bn to €390bn in capex running from 2027 through 2045, translating directly into higher regulated asset base growth and equipment procurement rates. Hyperscale optical fiber integration represents another transformative opportunity, with Prysmian announcing massive long-term agreements with hyperscalers expected to generate €5bn+ in cumulative revenue over several years, potentially driving Digital Solutions margins toward 20% in the coming quarters.

Hellenic Cables' successful certification of its 525 kV HVDC XLPE cable systems breaks the technology monopoly historically held by the three largest European manufacturers, allowing Hellenic to bid on multi-billion euro subsea interconnections previously closed to smaller regional players and expand operating margins toward 20% plus over the next four years. Proposed revisions to the EU Public Procurement Act and the Industrial Accelerator Act will allow member states to block non-reciprocal foreign bidders from grid tenders, covering a procurement sector valued at approximately €2.5 trillion annually across the European Union and preventing margin erosion from Chinese overcapacity in low-voltage and medium-voltage grids. Analytical confidence varies across dimensions, with high visibility in backlog dynamics and capacity constraints anchored by robust contract value momentum and tracked manufacturing capacity additions that support longterm earnings targets through 2030.

Moderate confidence characterizes short-cycle distribution margins due to macro sensitivities where subdued low voltage markets introduce near-term execution uncertainties. Material data gaps persist around opaque project-level profitability, unconfirmed framework agreement commitments excluded from reported backlogs, and undisclosed raw material indexation formulas, requiring targeted expert calls with former executives, monitoring of public utility procurement filings, and quarterly report analysis to decrypt structural profitability.

These analytical limitations prevent precise mid-term earnings forecasting but do not fundamentally challenge the high-level investment thesis. - Earnings Calls Second and third-order implications suggest that structural margin expansion from grid upgrades and data centers will supercharge downstream engineering, procurement, and specialty metals supply chains as the highvoltage cable oligopoly solidifies, with high copper-to-aluminum price ratios driving accelerated industrial-scale aluminum conductor substitution across global distribution grids, technology giants providing massive upfront capital advances to establish exclusive multi-year optical fiber supply agreements and lock out smaller cloud competitors, and major European utilities enforcing strict local-sourcing procurement criteria to bypass cheaper foreign bids and guarantee grid reliability under politically protected capital budgets.

Risks to the bullish scenario include a sudden slowdown in European offshore wind auctions or severe project delays that could trigger destructive price competition and erode the estimated profitability of the industry's record-high backlogs, rapid industry capacity additions that might eventually upset the favorable supply demand balance and trigger severe subsea margin erosion, previous delays in offshore wind auction awards and project permitting creating a temporary softer demand period that could dampen near-term supply-chain growth, and unanticipated project implementation delays or a sudden reduction in offshore wind policy support that could rapidly degrade assumed backlog margins.

Despite these execution and policy uncertainties, the combination of unprecedented backlog visibility, structural capacity constraints, and accelerating regulatory support for grid decarbonization positions European cable makers for sustained earnings growth through the end of the decade.
