EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Net Signal on Materiality of Change

The 2i Rete Gas acquisition represents a transformative €5.3 billion transaction that closed on April 1, 2025, expanding Italgas's Regulated Asset Base by 50% to approximately €13.3 billion and establishing a European gas distribution leader. This strategic consolidation elevates Italgas's Italian gas market share to approximately 50% from its pre-merger level of 28%, fundamentally resetting the earnings trajectory.

The group now serves nearly 13 million active gas customers across Italy and Greece over 156,655 kilometers of network, marking a structural shift from incremental growth to scale-driven efficiency extraction. The integration execution has been rapid and credible, with legal merger completion in only 90 days and successful migration of 1 petabyte of data without operational problems. Management raised its 2031 EBITDA synergy target from €200 million to €250 million compared to the 2023 baseline, and has already captured €91.4 million of cumulative synergies by March 31, 2026, representing 36.6% of the 2031 target.

This material change transitions Italgas from a steady-state utility to a self-help consolidation story anchored by aggressive cost rationalization and digital transformation initiatives.

Ranked Key Drivers of 18M-3Y EPS Trajectory

The forward EPS trajectory is governed by five critical drivers with differentiated plausibility profiles. First, 2i Rete Gas synergy realization (estimated +15% to EBITDA, HIGH plausibility) represents the primary engine, targeting €250 million in total EBITDA synergies by 2031, including €70 million in savings specifically driven by artificial intelligence initiatives. Management has demonstrated credible execution, achieving €91.4 million in cumulative synergies by March 31, 2026, representing 36.6% of its long-term goal.

However, sell-side analysts flag that the target implies a 6.2% annual opex decline, exceeding Italgas's historical cost-cutting pace of 5.5%, introducing marginal execution risk.

Second, digitalization and network upgrade capex catch-up (estimated +5% to EBITDA, HIGH plausibility) underpins long-term competitiveness. Italgas allocated €3.1 billion to digitizing its network across Italy and Greece over the FY25-FY31 period, targeting the installation of 6 million Nimbus smart meters by 2031 to replace obsolete GPRS devices. Network digitalization is projected to generate over €100 million in incremental revenues by the end of the plan.

This driver exhibits strong interdependency with synergy capture, as digital tools enable the AI-driven predictive maintenance that unlocks operational efficiencies. Third, regulatory WACC resets (estimated -30 bps WACC, MEDIUM plausibility) represent a material margin headwind beyond FY27. While ARERA confirmed the pre-tax, real WACC for gas distribution at 5.9% for 2026 and set the X-factor opex reduction rate at 0% for all Italian gas distribution utilities, Deutsche Bank warns that the regulator may claw back significant cost outperformance, expecting a €50 million efficiency clawback in 2028 and another €100 million in 2030.

Bernstein Research cautions that moving to full cost-of-debt indexation from FY28 could cut WACC by 30 bps, directly offsetting synergy gains.

Fourth, net financing cost headwinds (estimated +50 bps debt cost, HIGH plausibility) will progressively erode financial outperformance. While gross financial debt remains 79.8% fixed-rate, protecting net interest expenses from rising central bank rates, Bernstein Research warns that financial outperformance will diminish as older lowyield debt rolls off for Italian utilities. Italgas recently issued a €750 million fixed-rate bond on April 9, 2026, to pre-fund financial needs, with this 2032 bond carrying a 3.625% annual coupon, substantially higher than legacy rates.

Fifth, ATEM gas distribution tender wins (estimated +8% to EBITDA, LOW plausibility) offer upside optionality but face persistent execution delays. Italgas allocated €1.5 billion in planned capex for Italian ATEM gas distribution tenders through 2031, yet administrative bottlenecks have postponed several ATEM tenders, continuing a multiyear historical lag in Italian market reorganization, leading analysts to exclude them from base cases. This driver carries the lowest confidence level and exhibits high binary risk, with successful execution offering material upside that is not priced into consensus forecasts.

Recent Delivery Signal (Q1 2026)

Q1 2026 results validated the integration thesis with solid in-line delivery. Italgas delivered its 37th consecutive quarter of growth in the first quarter of 2026, with adjusted group revenues reaching €661.70 million (representing a 44.10% year-on-year increase) and adjusted EBITDA growing by 52.60% to €526.80 million. This performance was in line with Morgan Stanley's €190 million estimate for adjusted net profit, demonstrating precise execution against elevated post-merger expectations. The operating margin expansion was particularly impressive, with adjusted EBITDA margin reaching 79.60% (up from 75.20% in the previous year), as management rapidly aligned 2i Rete Gas's lower-margin assets with Italgas's standards. 13 14 - Earnings Calls Cash flow generation exceeded expectations, with operating cash flow reaching €643 million, more than doubling previous year metrics, supported by a positive net working capital movement of €277.80 million.

Importantly, net financial debt excluding lease liabilities dropped by €527 million to €10,206.80 million, demonstrating rapid deleveraging progress. Integration momentum accelerated, with Italgas achieving 36.60% of the €250 million synergy target for 2031 by March 31, 2026, and management expecting to capture 50% of the long-term synergy goal by end-2026. 17 197 18 - Earnings Calls The post-earnings analyst response was stable and constructive, with Morgan Stanley maintaining their Equalweight rating and €10 price target post results, and Deutsche Bank expecting no significant shifts in consensus following the solid Q1 delivery.

Management reconfirmed full-year 2026 guidance of €740 million to €760 million net profit, signaling confidence in the full-year trajectory despite the elevated Q1 base.

Forward Credibility and Management Posture

Forward guidance credibility is exceptionally high, anchored by a proven multi-year track record. Italgas targets a predictable 10% EPS CAGR through FY31, starting from a 2024 IAS 33 adjusted EPS base of €0.59, with the group projecting net income to exceed €1 billion in FY29, which represents a key operational checkpoint. Historical delivery validates this outlook, as actual FY25 adjusted EBITDA reached €1,883.40 million, beating the company's upgraded guidance range of €1.87 billion, while actual adjusted EBITDA of €1,350.90 million matched company guidance of €1.35 billion in FY24. - Earnings Calls Management exhibits a clear honest-tracker posture characterized by conservative baseline plans and consistent outperformance.

Italgas historically maintains a conservative, low-ball forecasting style across successive business plans, and behaves as an honest-tracker that tends to over-deliver through strategic moves such as the Greek expansion. This posture is validated by a 9% annual EPS CAGR from 2017 through 2025, confirming multi-year earnings visibility. Importantly, the 10% EPS CAGR target remains highly credible because it excludes uncommitted ATEM tenders, providing inherent conservatism and upside optionality.

Sensitivity analysis reveals moderate resilience to isolated shocks but high vulnerability to compounded regulatory resets. A 30 bps cut in the allowed WACC would reduce EBITDA by €45M (translating to a -2% impact), while every €50M of missed cost synergies reduces FY30 EPS by 4%. The most severe downside scenario emerges from regulatory efficiency clawbacks, where a complete clawback of opex efficiencies would reduce valuation to €8 per share from €10. This tail risk is partially mitigated by Italgas's high fixed-rate debt mix of 79.80% protecting interest expenses, though Fitch's BBB+ stable rating relies on Net Debt/RAB remaining below 63%, creating a tight warning threshold.

Fundamental Peer Position

Italgas occupies the top peer position on return metrics but exhibits mixed organic growth dynamics. The company ranks at the top of Italian regulated peers with a projected average 15.2% post-tax nominal RoE on actual equity over FY26e-FY30e, outstripping Terna's average actual RoE of 9.9% and Snam's 9.8% over the same forecast period. This superior profitability extends to asset returns, where pre-tax nominal return on Average Calendar RAB reaches 7.3% in FY26e, rising to 7.7% in FY30e, outpacing Snam's 5.6% return in FY26e and Terna's 5.8% return as cost efficiencies accumulate.

Operating efficiency leadership is demonstrated through margin performance, with EBITDA margin reaching 79.6% in Q1 2026, outpacing the peer group average. Cash generation quality is similarly superior, as cash flow from operating activities reached €1,625.1 million in FY25 (rising by €526.4 million year-on-year), with free cash flow before M&A reaching €518.9 million and fully covering €329.5 million in dividend distributions. This compares favorably to Snam's 77% dividend payout target by maintaining comfortable coverage levels.

However, organic growth trajectories lag peers materially. Italgas's organic RAB CAGR through FY31 is projected at a mediocre 3.1% to 3.3%, trailing behind its peers, while Snam exhibits stronger organic trajectories, with Snam targeting a 5.7% organic RAB CAGR through FY30. Terna demonstrates even superior expansion potential, with long-term capex supporting a 9.3% RAB CAGR over FY25-FY30e, offering superior organic expansion compared to Italgas.

The valuation positioning is demanding relative to growth prospects. Italgas trades at a 30% premium to end- FY25e RAB, and on the premium to RAB vs RAB CAGR matrix, Italgas represents the least attractive Italian network name. This valuation tension is partially offset by dividend competitiveness, as Italgas's dividend yield is expected to rise from 4.5% in FY26e to 5.7% by FY29e under the updated policy, though Snam offers a competitive dividend yield of 4.9% in FY26e, ranking as the third highest in Bernstein's coverage.

Balance sheet leverage is elevated versus peers, creating limited financial flexibility. Italgas's high combined leverage ratio stands above Snam's 57% Net Debt/RAB benchmark reported in FY25 and is also elevated relative to Terna's 50% Net Debt/RAB ratio over the same period. Critically, Fitch's rating downgrade trigger is 63% Net Debt/RAB over a sustained period, creating tight balance sheet headroom, while Italgas aims to keep its Net Debt/RAB ratio constantly below 70% across its strategic plan horizon.
