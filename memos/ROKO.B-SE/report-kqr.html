Section 2 – Minority Accounting and Option Liabilities

Section summary
  1. Röko fully consolidates its subsidiaries at 100% and recognizes put and call option liabilities in equity rather than deducting minority interest from its income statement.
  2. The exercise of these options

reclassifies liabilities without altering consolidated operating earnings but requires refinancing that could increase interest expenses.

Minority Accounting Mechanics

1.
Consistent consolidation: Röko AB fully consolidates 100% of its subsidiaries' financial statements rather than deducting non-controlling interests on its income statement. This presentation is enabled by contractual put and call option agreements held with minority shareholders across its niche businesses. Consequently, the entire net profit for the year is presented as attributable directly to the Parent Company's shareholders. This accounting treatment means that consolidated operating results already reflect full ownership, leaving reported EPS unaffected by minority profit deductions.
2.
Equity revaluations: Röko AB books any changes in the valuation of its put and call option liabilities directly within equity as transactions with owners. These revaluations amounted to a debit of SEK 150m in 2025 and SEK 239m in 2024. Because these adjustments are kept out of the income statement, they do not affect reported net income or earnings per share. However, these revaluations directly reduce the group's total equity over time as the subsidiaries grow.
3.
Minority distributions: Röko AB includes declared dividends to minority shareholders directly within its put and call option liabilities on the balance sheet. Total transfers to non-controlling interests reached SEK 298m in 2025, which included SEK 148m of dividends to minority holders alongside the SEK 150m valuation changes. These distributions are classified as financing activities in the cash flow statement instead of operating outflows. Thus, the reported cash flow from operations remains unaffected by minority dividend payments.
4.
Option exercises: Röko AB experiences a balance sheet reclassification rather than an earnings statement impact when minority put or call options are exercised. DNB Carnegie explained on May 5, 2025, that exercising options simply shifts liabilities from put/call debt to interest-bearing bank debt. This refinancing leaves the group's total net debt intact but can increase interest-bearing net debt and raise cash interest expenses. Ultimately, reported EPS would only decline if the new bank interest exceeds the non-cash option revaluations bypassed under IFRS.

Option Liability Valuation and Schedule

5.
Formulaic valuations: Röko AB calculates its put and call option liabilities using a contractually agreed valuation model for each subsidiary. The group multiplies the subsidiary's average EBITA before option exercise by a contract multiple and deducts subsidiary net debt. This formula implies that strong operating performance at subsidiaries directly expands the group's liabilities. Consequently, successful acquisitions create a larger liability on the balance sheet that eventually requires cash settlement.
6.
Growing obligations: Röko AB reported that its total put and call option liabilities rose to SEK 2,779m as of September 30, 2026. This liability has grown from SEK 2,600m at the end of 2025, driven by SEK 172m from new acquisitions and SEK 135m of revaluations. The expansion of these obligations highlights the ongoing funding commitments for minorities as the group continues to expand. This substantial liability represents a significant claim on future group cash flows as options mature.
7.
Settlement timeline: Röko AB faces a structured exercise schedule where SEK 449m of option liabilities are exercisable within 12 months from September 30, 2026. An additional SEK 1,105m of liabilities mature between one and three years, while SEK 1,224m mature after three years. Managing this timeline is crucial because the group must secure refinancing to fund upcoming option redemptions without overstretching its balance sheet. If the group cannot fund these maturities through cash flow, acquisitions must slow down.
8.
Ownership targets: Röko AB aims to raise its average ownership in subsidiaries from approximately 75% to about 85% over the medium term. Achieving this target requires exercising outstanding put and call options with subsidiary founders and management. However, raising ownership levels reduces the financial alignment of local managers who currently retain meaningful equity stakes. Thus, pursuing the 85% target could dilute entrepreneurial operational incentives unless alternative long-term incentive models are introduced.
