Squeezed consumer budgets are driving intense promotional dynamics, voucher schemes, and rapid private label expansion, which is restricting retail operating margin growth. Additionally, late-2026 external shocks (such as a massive cybersecurity incident at Ahold Delhaize and civil unrest in France impacting Carrefour) have temporarily depressed retail profitability. 6. Tobacco (British American Tobacco, Imperial Brands): The sub-sector remains structurally resilient, supported by immense pricing power and narrowing losses in Next Generation Products (NGP). However, the combustibles model is facing a material tax shock due to front-loaded multi-year excise tax increases in Germany (set for January 2027), which has prompted brokers to downgrade volume and earnings assumptions for exposed players. Sector-Wide Macro Themes & Global Forces Input Costs and 2027 Gross Margin Implications The input cost landscape for 2027 is highly fragmented. Packaged food producers are seeing late-2026 upward pressure on key soft commodities like coffee and cocoa, forcing them to maintain high list prices at the risk of losing market share. In dairy, liquid milk remains abundant in Europe, but whey protein and lactose inflation present headwinds to Specialized Nutrition. For brewers and spirit distillers, agricultural softs like barley and sugar remain favorable, but aluminum, glass packaging, and fuel logistics continue to drive COGS inflation. Spirit players are particularly exposed to glass packaging, which accounts for 20% to 30% of their total COGS. For food retailers, a severe El Niño weather pattern threatens global agricultural yields of cocoa, coffee, sugar, grains, and rice, which may trigger a renewed wave of supply-side food inflation into mid-2027. Pricing Power against Retailers and Private Labels Pricing power is under severe strain, particularly in Europe. Retailer buying groups are aggressively pushing back on brand price increases, leading to highly publicized de-listings (e.g., Nestlé in Europe, Campari in Germany, and CCEP in Germany).

Furthermore, private label penetration is reaching a structural tipping point in European food and household products as value-seeking consumer behavior intensifies. Private labels now offer high-quality alternatives at average discounts of 30% versus national brands, forcing multinational consumer goods companies to step up promotional spending and, in some cases (e.g., Haleon), plan outright price cuts to restore competitiveness. - Guidance Calls Restructuring Programs and Marketing (A&P) Spend Rather than allowing gross margin expansion to flow entirely to the bottom line, staples giants are systematically reinvesting their savings back into Advertising & Promotion (A&P) to defend brand equity and support volume recovery.
