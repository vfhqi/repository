Plausible problems to monitor

The comprehensive analysis across bear case evidence, pre-mortem failure scenarios, and foreign exchange exposures reveals five critical areas where EQT AB demonstrably underperforms peer medians or industry base rates, creating compound risk pathways that merit heightened monitoring. The first and most diagnostic plausible problem to monitor centers on lagging distributed-to-paid-in capital conversion across EQT's flagship Private Equity vintages, which falls materially behind core peer benchmarks including ICG and Bridgepoint.

This underperformance is particularly concerning because it directly constrains the velocity of institutional capital recycling, creating a self-reinforcing loop where limited partners lack the liquidity necessary to commit to successor flagship funds. The practical manifestation emerged in deferred fund activations, with EQT XI's launch postponed from August to mid-Q4 2026 specifically because LPs demanded realized cash distributions before committing to new vintages.

This pattern sits materially worse than the peer median where comparable European alternative managers maintained more consistent activation cadences throughout the same period. The second structural weakness manifests in exceptionally low board independence metrics, with independent directors comprising only 14.29% of EQT's board composition in late 2024. This figure places EQT at the extreme bottom of the peer distribution, falling approximately 60 basis points below the European alternative asset manager median of roughly 35-40% independent representation.

The governance vulnerability is compounded by public minority shareholders holding negligible voting power because partners and Investor AB control 75% of total share capital, creating a material misalignment risk during decisions that might favor insider liquidity over public shareholder value optimization. The non-independent chair transition, with EQT Asia head Jean Eric Salata succeeding founder Conni Jonsson in May 2026, further entrenches partner dominance over corporate governance processes at a time when peer firms are moving toward enhanced independence standards.

The third identifiable plausible problem to monitor concerns unhedged translation exposure which generated a negative EUR 53 million revenue impact and depressed adjusted EBITDA by EUR 39 million during fiscal year 2025. EQT AB Group maintains a non-hedging mandate for translation risk, a policy judged as low-discipline that exposes capital to persistent other comprehensive income volatility. This stance contrasts sharply with peer practices where Bridgepoint employs rolling forward contracts providing almost full hedging coverage for its EUR management fees and Partners Group actively increased balance-sheet and equity hedges to protect capital against exchange

The fourth area of relative underperformance involves the widening adjusted-to-reported EBITDA gap, which expanded to EUR 261 million in 2025 as reported EBITDA of EUR 1,382 million sat materially below the adjusted EBITDA figure of EUR 1,642 million. While alternative asset managers routinely present adjusted metrics, EQT's gap magnitude exceeds peer medians by approximately 40-50% on a percentage basis, raising questions about the sustainability of reported earnings quality.

The primary driver stems from acquisition considerations subject to lock-up being treated as personnel expenses but adjusted out of segment metrics, a practice that becomes increasingly material as EQT pursues aggressive M&A expansion across secondaries and wealth channels. This accounting treatment sits at the aggressive end of peer disclosure practices and complicates like-for-like margin comparisons with more conservatively reported competitors.

Outliers worth noting

The second outlier centers on the adjusted-to-reported EBITDA gap, which expanded to EUR 261 million in 2025, representing approximately 16% of reported EBITDA. This material divergence stems from acquisition considerations subject to lock-up being treated as personnel expenses but adjusted out of segment metrics, creating significant opacity regarding underlying operational profitability. The magnitude of this adjustment substantially exceeds peer norms, where such reconciliation items typically remain within single-digit percentage ranges of reported earnings.

The volatility is further amplified by IFRS 9 adoption reflecting short-term fund valuation changes without a portfolio valuation buffer, exposing reported metrics to extreme quarterly fluctuations that obscure fundamental earning power. This accounting outlier creates unusually high analytical complexity for investors attempting to assess normalized profitability relative to industry benchmarks.

The third outlier involves unhedged foreign exchange translation exposure, which generated a negative EUR 53 million revenue impact and reduced adjusted EBITDA by EUR 39 million during fiscal 2025. This translation headwind culminated in a severe EUR 585 million equity loss recorded in other comprehensive income, representing approximately 25% of total equity at prior period values. EQT maintains a non-hedging mandate for translation risk and strictly prohibits any speculative currency trading, diverging sharply from peers like Partners Group and Bridgepoint who actively hedge substantial portions of their currency exposures.

The structural mismatch is exacerbated by direct SEK-denominated revenues being absent while local Swedish costs remain high, creating an asymmetric risk profile where operational margins compress during dollar weakness without corresponding revenue offsets. This unhedged posture places EQT in the extreme tail of currency risk management discipline within the European alternative asset management sector, exposing shareholders to persistent translation volatility that compounds during periods of synchronized central bank policy divergence.
