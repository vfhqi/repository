Section 2 – Changes in Company Guidance and Management's Justifications

First Half Downgrades

1.
Significant reduction: Coloplast lowered its full-year organic revenue growth guidance for the 2025/26 financial year on April 23, 2026. The company reduced its top-line target to a range of 5% to 6%, down from its previous expectation of around 7% organic growth. Management attributed this revision to a slower recovery in the outpatient setting for skin substitutes. This reduction highlights that the company was forced to reset expectations due to severe commercial disruption in its biologics division.
2.
Lowered profitability: The company also revised its constant-currency EBIT growth guidance on April 23, 2026. Coloplast lowered its full-year operating profit growth target to around 5% before special items, down from its initial target of around 7%. Chief Financial Officer Anders Lonning-Skovgaard explained that Kerecis alone accounted for 1.80% of this operating profit growth cut. This downward adjustment means that the acquired business became a significant drag on consolidated group profitability.
3.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts believed that the April 2026 guidance cuts were actually less conservative than they should have been. DNB Carnegie reported on May 13, 2026, that the profitability targets remained demanding because Kerecis was expected to deliver double-digit margins in the second half. The broker argued that management should have taken a deeper, cleaner cut to its targets ahead of the new Chief Executive Officer's arrival. This indicates that the initial reset left too little margin for error in subsequent quarters.
4.
Subdued returns: Coloplast simultaneously downgraded its return on invested capital guidance during the April 2026 reset. The company lowered its expected return on invested capital after tax before special items to around 15%, down from its previous target of 16%. CFO Anders Lonning-Skovgaard noted during the first-quarter earnings call on February 6, 2026, that the initial 16% target was already an improvement of 1 percentage point. The subsequent cut highlights that capital efficiency was materially diluted by the integration of Kerecis.

The Outpatient Reimbursement Overhaul

15.
Severe restructuring: Medicare implemented a major pricing reset for skin substitute products used in outpatient settings effective January 1, 2026. This regulatory overhaul reclassified these cellular products as incident-to supplies under the physician fee schedule rather than separately paid biologics. Consequently, this shift aims to reduce federal healthcare spending on biologics by nearly 90 percent compared to historical runaway levels.
16.
& Knight topic-specific reports 215 10Q Drastic transition: The Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services implemented a standard reimbursement model in the physician office setting to address runaway clinical spending. Prior to this reform, manufacturers benefited from separate average sales price billing with a six percent add-on fee. Under the revised framework, Medicare pays a single site-neutral flat rate for sheet-form products across outpatient clinics. This change severely compresses developer margins and reduces the financial incentives that previously encouraged overutilization.
