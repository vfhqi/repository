Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: "How plausible is the change now expected for Coca-Cola HBC AG (CCH-GB), given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"

The plausibility of Coca-Cola HBC’s (CCH) targets for the Coca-Cola Beverages Africa (CCBA) acquisition is critical to its earnings trajectory because the deal materially alters the group’s margin profile and capital requirements. CCBA's reported FY2024 operating margin of approximately 7.4% sits roughly 330 basis points below CCH's legacy business, meaning the group's ability to "bridge the gap" through its established Revenue Growth Management (RGM) and digital playbooks will decide whether the acquisition dilutes or enhances overall return on invested capital (ROIC).

CCH management has signaled that while ROIC progress might slow in the short term due to the $2.6 billion purchase price, the long-term growth opportunity across 14 African markets justifies the temporary dilution.

Failure to execute the integration effectively would directly impair EPS growth, particularly in 2027 when CCH expects the deal to be low-single-digit accretive. This transition occurs at a time when the legacy business faces its own pressures, including a significant drop in commodity hedge cover from nearly 100% in 2026 to approximately 40% in 2027. If CCH cannot transfer its execution excellence–demonstrated by its high-singledigit volume growth in Egypt despite devaluations–to the CCBA footprint, the group may struggle to maintain its 6% to 7% organic revenue and 20 to 40 basis point margin expansion algorithm.

The question also addresses whether CCH's success is portable across diverse regulatory and competitive landscapes, such as South Africa's Broad-Based Black Economic Empowerment (BEE) requirements and the aggressive price-led competition from Varun Beverages. Understanding if CCH's "bespoke capabilities" like Promo360 and AI-driven micro-segmentation are sufficient for these new challenges, or if they require new, unproven skills, will determine the risk premium investors apply to the stock. Closely related questions include the impact of continued emerging-market devaluations on dollar-denominated costs and the speed at which CCH can modernize CCBA's aging cooler fleets and production lines to drive single-serve mix.

This report used 118 relevant sources covering 12 companies and 8 comparable past cases. No companies named in the query were left out, as all peers provided material evidence on integration playbooks or African operating conditions.

Section 2 – Margin Recovery at CCBA

Section summary
  1. Lifting CCBA's 7.4% operating margin towards legacy levels is highly plausible over a 36-month horizon based on CCH's RGM toolkit and peer precedents. Confidence is high for the plausibility of recovery because CCH has a documented history of margin expansion in similar emerging markets like Nigeria and Egypt.

Plausibility and Timing of Recovery

1.
Coca-Cola HBC management expects the CCBA acquisition to be low-single-digit EPS accretive from 2027, the first full year of ownership, despite CCBA's current 7.4% operating margin. Bernstein Research notes that CCBA's margin is currently 330 basis points below the legacy business, primarily due to lower revenue per case and a less optimized package mix. CCH intends to "transfer Hellenic way of working" to CCBA over a couple of years, focusing on premiumization and digital tools that have already proven successful in other emerging territories. This strategy aims to bridge the profitability gap by shifting the volume-heavy CCBA business toward higher-margin single-serve packs and more targeted pricing. Earnings will likely see a gradual uplift through 2028 as these commercial levers take hold across the 14 new markets.
2.
Comparable past cases suggest that major bottler integrations typically require 24 to 36 months to show material margin improvement in under-invested territories. Coca-Cola Europacific Partners (CCEP), for example, saw its Philippines operating margin start at roughly 7.8% in 2024 with a roadmap to recover toward group parity over the subsequent decade & Co Redburn Similarly, the 2016 merger that created CCEP eventually delivered €330 mm in synergies, which represented 86% of the group's growth in profit before tax between 2015 and 2019. These precedents indicate that while the initial margin dilutes the group, the "roll-up" strategy consistently yields strong earnings growth through the consolidation of similar bottling operations 12 Success depends on applying "cost-efficiency know-how" to sweat the existing asset base harder.

In the first half of 2026, CCH already stepped up its group capex by over €100 mm to fund new lines in Nigeria and Egypt, signaling the scale of investment likely needed for the broader African expansion. This front-loading of capital will likely constrain free cash flow in the initial integration years before the operational leverage from increased capacity begins to flow through to EPS.

Competitive and Regulatory Constraints

5.
The margin recovery in South Africa, which accounts for 60% of CCBA's volumes, faces a unique headwind from strict competition-authority conditions and BEE obligations These obligations often include employment protection and black ownership targets, which can limit the "people-related savings" that peers like Carlsberg leveraged for 80% of their Britvic synergies Furthermore, the presence of a price-led challenger in Varun Beverages forces CCH to balance its margin goals with the need to defend market share. While CCH has navigated devaluations in Nigeria, the South African market's structural labor and empowerment costs create a floor on cost reduction that was less prevalent in its Egypt acquisition.
6.
Other side of the story: While CCH’s playbook is proven, the simultaneous integration of 14 markets is significantly more complex than the single-market Egypt deal, potentially stretching management bandwidth and delaying margin gains. Peer Arca Continental (AC) has shown that route-to-market programs can succeed in South America, but only through localized, multi-year investments in cooler coverage and customer development. If CCH attempts to apply a "one-size-fits-all" digital strategy across 14 diverse African markets too quickly, it risks disrupting local distributor relationships and missing its 2027 accretion targets.

Section 3 – Egypt Acquisition Playbook

Section summary
  1. The Egypt playbook's focus on RGM and digital tools is highly transferable to CCBA markets, having already delivered high-single-digit growth despite macro shocks. Confidence is medium-high because CCH has already started applying these specific tools in Egypt with positive volume results, though the capital intensity of the full rollout across 14 markets remains a variable.

Capital Spending and Margin Timeline

1.
The Egypt acquisition in 2022 required consistent, elevated capital spending to modernize a business that CCH described as having "unlimited source of growth" but needing an "elevated level of capability". In July 2024, CCH secured a $130 mm loan from the EBRD specifically to fund capex and working capital for its Egyptian subsidiary, following a period where it had already substantially expanded the cooler network and production facilities. It took approximately three years to fully integrate the business and reach a "next phase of growth," during which CCH recorded strong market share gains despite two devaluations and high inflation. This suggests that CCBA investors should expect a three-year investment cycle before the African markets reach peak operational efficiency.

Transferability of Commercial Tools

2.
The "Egypt playbook" relies heavily on the Revenue Growth Management (RGM) framework, which uses advanced analytics to tailor pricing and package mix to local dynamics. In Egypt, this involved overhauling the route-tomarket and launching new digital tools like the "Promo360" integrated planning platform and AI-driven microsegmentation. Coran Bogdanovic stated that CCH is already working with CCBA teams to assess where to build from their existing base, with a focus on "upskilling and training people" to work with the RGM framework. These tools allow CCH to drive positive mix through targeted initiatives, such as the 180-basis-point improvement in single-serve mix seen in Q3 2026 across the group.

Local Sourcing and Infrastructure

3.
Local sourcing and infrastructure modernization are central to the playbook's goal of mitigating currency volatility and improving service frequency. In South Africa, peer Arca Continental (AC) has demonstrated that sustained investment in expanding the presence of coolers and improving product availability can drive volume growth even in challenging periods. CCH’s experience in Egypt, where it introduced the Energy category and saw "phenomenal growth," suggests it will likely prioritize similar category expansions in Kenya and Ethiopia to boost overall revenue per case. The ability to manufacture locally–supported by a $85 mm loan facility for production equipment in Nigeria–will be critical to maintaining affordability in markets like Ethiopia and Uganda where hard currency for imports is often scarce.
4.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research points out that while South Africa offers a "Cape of Good Hope" for growth, other markets like Uganda, Kenya, and Ethiopia present significantly different channel dynamics and may not respond as uniformly to the Egypt RGM playbook Specifically, the lower per-capita consumption and higher prevalence of small outlets in East Africa might require a more "boots-on-the-ground" traditional distribution model rather than the high-tech digital ordering systems that worked in Cairo. If the digital tools fail to gain traction with small-scale traders, CCH may see a slower-than-anticipated return on its tech investments in these regions.

Digital Ordering and AI

5.
The deployment of digital and data solutions is a key differentiator that CCH intends to move into CCBA "as quickly as possible". These tools, including the "Digital Hub" in Cairo, help the company design the most effective promotional targeting to expand penetration across NARTD categories. CCH believes that its "bespoke capabilities" and 24/7 portfolio give it a competitive edge in capturing growth opportunities even in a dynamic macroeconomic backdrop. By leveraging AI for micro-segmentation, CCH can deploy assets more effectively than the traditional "one size fits all" approach, potentially accelerating the volume-to-value transition in South Africa and Tanzania.
6.
Other side of the story: The success in Egypt was aided by the fact that CCH was a challenger closing the share gap to a leader, whereas in many CCBA markets, it will be the incumbent defending against aggressive new entrants. In South Africa, Varun Beverages' acquisition of Twizza provides it with a low-cost production base to challenge Coca-Cola's pricing, a dynamic that the Egypt playbook did not have to prioritize to the same extent. Therefore, the playbook may need to be adapted with defensive pricing levers that could temporarily weigh on the 20 to 40 basis point margin expansion target.

Section 4 – Legacy Business Algorithm

Section summary
  1. The 6% to 7% revenue algorithm is sustainable for 2027 through pricing and productivity, even as the company faces lower hedge cover and tough 2026 comparisons. Confidence is medium because while CCH's pricing power is proven, the 60% drop in commodity hedge cover for 2027 introduces a significant variable that will test its operational leverage.

2027 Cost and Hedge Outlook

1.
Coca-Cola HBC enters 2027 with roughly 40% commodity hedge cover, a significant decrease from the near-100% cover it maintained through 2026. CFO Anastasios Stamoulis noted that cost of goods sold (COGS) inflation is expected to be "slightly higher" next year, though the 40% level is considered a "normal" position for this stage of the cycle. Management remains confident that the group's "robust governance" and overall hedging approach will allow it to mitigate increased costs through a combination of pricing and productivity initiatives. If oil and gas prices stabilize, the 2027 cost pressure may prove manageable without breaking the EBIT algorithm, as CCH continues to pull internal levers like "discretionary cost mix" and "promo optimization" first.

Section 5 – Pricing Power

Section summary
  1. CCH demonstrably possesses the pricing power to run at or above unit-cost inflation, supported by its RGM toolkit and successful navigation of recent 2022-2023 volatility.

Confidence is high for CCH's pricing capability, as current results show revenue-per-case growth accelerating in the second half of 2026 even as some inflationary pressures moderated.

Pricing vs. Unit-Cost Inflation

1.
Coca-Cola HBC has shown a "strong RGM toolkit" that allowed it to navigate the 2022-2023 input cost crisis effectively, with a focus on affordability through smaller entry packs while taking price in less sensitive channels In Q3 2026, organic revenue per case grew by 3.4%, an acceleration from the 2.2% growth recorded in the first half of the year This pricing momentum comfortably exceeded the low-single-digit COGS-per-case increase experienced in 2026. For 2027-2028, CCH expects its pricing initiatives to be "flexible," allowing it to adjust to volatile energy and commodity prices while driving mix improvements through targeted pack launches.

Packaging and Mix as Pricing Levers

5.

This shift toward higher-value packaging provides a structural tailwind to revenue per case, independent of headline price increases. Jefferies Research believes that CCH’s ability to "sweat its existing asset base harder" through premiumization in Energy and Coffee will be a key driver of returns through 2028.
6.

While CCH has been "front-footed" in its pricing, the Egyptian pound’s 14% decline and the Ugandan shilling’s 18% depreciation in late 2026 highlight the risk of imported inflation pricing out mainstream consumers. If CCH is forced to revert to large, low-priced "multi-serve" packs to maintain volume in these markets, the mix-driven pricing power it demonstrably has in Europe may not fully translate to the new CCBA footprint.

Section 6 – Integration Capability

Section summary
  1. CCH has a proven track record of integrating complex, under-invested markets, though the simultaneous 14-country CCBA deal is a significant step up in scale. Confidence is medium-high regarding CCH's functional capability, supported by its "repeatable execution model" and the success of the 2022 Egypt integration.

Record of Delivery and Scale

1.
CCH’s "bespoke capabilities" and 24/7 portfolio are the foundation of its confidence in managing the 43 markets it will serve following the CCBA completion. The company has a "proven track record" in Africa, spanning 75 years in Nigeria and the recent transformation of the Egypt business. Following completion, CCH will represent two-thirds of the Coca-Cola system volume in Africa, making it the second-largest bottler globally. This scale allows for "sharing of best practices" across the supply chain, similar to how CCEP leveraged its European shared service centers to improve back-office efficiency for its Australian acquisition.

Functional Integration Planning

2.
Functional teams from CCH have been collaborating with CCBA counterparts since the deal announcement in October 2025 to prepare for Day 1. CCH aims to implement its "revenue growth management framework" and digital platforms across all CCBA markets, acknowledging that while CCBA already has "solid systems," there is significant room for upskilling to Hellenic standards. The company has already successfully rolled out its new visual identity for Coca-Cola Zero Sugar across the majority of its existing markets, demonstrating its ability to execute group-wide strategic initiatives effectively.

Peer Integration Precedents

3.
Comparable cases like AB InBev's acquisition of SABMiller's African businesses show that harmonizing data across hundreds of plants is a monumental task that can take months just for the "Digital Twin" setup. SABMiller’s footprint, covering 15 countries, was seen as a "growth engine" for AB InBev, though it required managing "high growth potential volatility" and local excise duties. Similarly, Carlsberg's Britvic integration is progressing "ahead of plan," with 80% of synergies expected to be delivered by the end of 2026, one year ahead of the original schedule. These cases suggest that with a "razor-sharp focus on business continuity," multimarket integrations can unlock significant value faster than conservative early guidance assumes.
4.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley notes that CCEP was created through the merger of three bottlers that already shared very similar businesses and geographies, whereas CCH’s African expansion involves noncontiguous territories with vastly different regulatory environments. Managing "non-contiguous territories" presents risks that the legacy European business has not fully faced, particularly regarding supply chain logistics and time zone differences. If CCH underestimates the unique "boots-on-the-ground" requirements of 14 separate African regulators, it may face integration delays that were not present in its domestic European roll-ups.

Governance and Talent

CCH's approach to integration emphasizes building the "best teams in the industry" and cultivating local talent in the markets where it operates. The company intends to pursue a secondary listing on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE) to underpin its commitment to South Africa and attract local institutional investors. This localized governance model, combined with CCH's "robust governance" in procurement and hedging, is designed to ensure that the acquisition creates long-term structural advantages rather than just short-term volume gains

aligning incentives through the Coca-Cola system's "incidence-based" model, CCH ensures that its execution excellence is directly linked to shareholder value.
6.
Other side of the story: The "capability gap" CCH identifies in CCBA may be partly structural rather than operational, meaning that no amount of training or digital tools can fully overcome local infrastructure deficits. Unlike Egypt, where CCH could invest in a relatively centralized population, the CCBA markets are highly fragmented, and the "Route-to-Market" overhaul may require significantly more low-tech investment in trucks and warehouses than in AI platforms. If the cost of basic logistics in markets like Ethiopia and Mozambique remains high, the "Digital Hub" in Cairo may find its effectiveness limited by the physical realities of African distribution.
