Primary Drivers of Spot Tanker Earnings

1.

Operating days, vessel operating expenses (OPEX), general and administrative (G&A) expenses, depreciation, interest expenses, and share count play a secondary role in year-to-year variance but define baseline profitability. Small shifts in daily spot TCE dwarf moderate fluctuations in unit operating costs across multiyear cycles. Consequently, spot freight rate volatility translates almost directly into net income and dividend distribution shifts.
2.
Secondary factors: Cost items like vessel OPEX and drydocking expenses remain relatively stable on a per-vessel per-day basis across the peer group. Depreciation and amortization remain fixed relative to vessel purchase accounting and fleet expansion schedules. Meanwhile, interest expense variations track corporate debt reduction cycles and prevailing benchmark interest rates. Share count changes primarily reflect equity-financed fleet acquisitions or share repurchase programs, which alter per-share sensitivity over longer horizons.
3.
Leverage effect: Financial leverage amplifies the impact of TCE rate movements on earnings per share during cyclical peaks and troughs. Tanker owners with higher debt burdens experience sharper EPS swings per USD 1,000/day TCE shift due to fixed debt service costs. Conversely, debt reduction lowers corporate cash break-even levels and insulates earnings during market downturns.

Divergent Earnings Scenarios

Structurally elevated rates: Scorpio Tankers would experience structurally elevated earnings if trade inefficiencies persist through 2029 rather than normalizing rapidly. Morgan Stanley models an upside scenario of ninety dollars per share for Scorpio under continued geopolitical disruptions. This non-consensus outcome would yield immense free cash flow for spot-exposed product tanker owners. These sustained high freight rates would easily support elevated dividends and aggressive share repurchases.
2.
Severe demand recession: International Seaways would face severe cash flow compression if a global economic slowdown triggers widespread refined product demand destruction. Deutsche Bank warns that a prolonged Middle East closure could eventually result in underlying demand destruction and economic contraction. Under these conditions, rapid inventory drawdowns would cease and force product tanker spot rates to normalize immediately. Consequently, dividend yields would compress significantly as spot earnings fall toward baseline cash break-even levels.

Conservative consensus modeling: TORM and its peer group continue to be valued under conservative consensus models that assume a rapid cyclical fade through 2027. Jefferies argues that this common view ignores structural shifts such as longer voyages, a massive sanctioned fleet, and a persistent inventory deficit. These elevated estimates assume that route inefficiencies will outlast the shortterm geopolitical headlines.

Contrary evidence: Some industry experts believe that product tanker rates will inevitably enter a multi-year decline regardless of persistent disruptions. Ultimately, these experts foresee a return to traditional trade routes within six months, drawing parallels to past maritime disruptions.

Downstream Knock-On Effects

5.
Severe refinery squeeze: Global refiners face intense margin pressure as soaring marine transport costs inflate the delivered price of crude feedstock. Morgan Stanley reports that shipping crude oil from the Middle East to China on a VLCC costs thirty-four dollars per barrel compared to a historical average of up to three dollars. These extreme freight rates dislocate standard arbitrage economics and force refiners to prioritize nearby shorter-haul crude sourcing. Over time, these elevated logistics costs will erode refining profitability unless product cracks widen proportionally.
6.
Elevated crude rates: Scorpio Tankers has shifted approximately sixty-six percent of its Long Range 2 fleet into crude service to capture the widening crude tanker rate premium. This aggressive trade migration directly contracts the clean product capacity and supports higher product tanker spot rates. Consequently, clean product tanker capacity remains constrained as long as crude tanker rates outperform. This structural clean-dirty crossover demonstrates how fluid fleet allocation can alter product tanker supply economics.
7.
Shipyard backlog expansion: Leading shipyards like Yangzijiang Shipbuilding are securing record high-price backlogs that extend their revenue visibility through 2029. Maybank reports that the ongoing maritime upcycle keeps yard utilization high and prevents customers from deferring vessel deliveries. While Chinese shipbuilding capacity is expanding, newer yards compete primarily in simpler bulk carriers and tankers, leaving premium eco-vessel construction concentrated among top-tier builders. This technical barrier helps Yangzijiang preserve its competitive advantage and pricing power.

Other side of the story: Although shipyard pricing remains highly resilient, an over-expansion of global shipbuilding capacity poses a long-term threat to asset values. Consequently, vessel values would face downward pressure and impair corporate balance sheets.
