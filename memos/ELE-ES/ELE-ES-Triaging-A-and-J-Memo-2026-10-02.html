[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Endesa is a regulated-growth re-rating that has already happened, in a yield stock. At EUR 41.34 the shares pay 16.6x consensus earnings 24 months forward, above the stock's own ten-year 'mania' band, for a business whose earnings per share grow about 4 percent a year after 2026 and whose dividend yields about 4 percent; on our modal path that returns 1.8 percent a year over eighteen months and 4.7 percent a year over three years, against a 20 percent bar.

  • The change is real, external and dated, and it is mostly tailwind.
    • Spain gave Endesa permission in 2026 to invest more in its distribution grid at a higher return, and extended the nuclear plant that anchors its low-cost generation
    • The Royal Decree lifts the distribution investment cap to EUR 10.2bn for 2027-2030, from EUR 7.7bn in earlier drafts
    • The CNMC's new network return is 6.58 percent nominal pre-tax for 2026-2031, from 5.58 percent, but below the 7.5 percent the industry asked for and fixed for six years with no indexation
    • Almaraz is extended to 2030
      • the 7 percent generation tax is suspended, worth EUR 104m in H1 2026
    • Against these, three external forces are headwinds: retail competition and churn above 25 percent, solar cannibalisation of merchant prices, and a political history of intervention in 2021, 2022 and 2023 with a general election expected in 2027
  • The change inside the company is a parent's rotation and a plan refresh, not a transformation.
    • A grids specialist from Enel became chief executive on 28 April 2026 and confirmed the strategy unchanged
    • The 2026-2028 plan puts EUR 5.5bn gross of EUR 10.6bn of capex into networks, cuts renewables capex 20 percent to EUR 3.0bn and targets a RAB of EUR 13.0bn by 2028 from EUR 11.5bn
    • Guidance for FY2026 was raised on 29 July 2026 to above EUR 2.4bn of net ordinary income, after a half-year that delivered EUR 1,480m
    • Fixed costs fell 8 percent in H1 2026 and leverage is being taken up from 1.6x toward about 2.3x to fund the grid
    • The one internal force that has turned negative is shareholder returns: the EUR 2.0bn buyback has EUR 0.9bn to run to mid-2027 and management has no current plans to extend it
  • 'Financial Outputs' turned up sharply once, and then flatten.
    • H1 2026 EBITDA rose 19.5 percent to EUR 3,240m and net ordinary profit 42.2 percent to EUR 1,480m
    • About EUR 200m net of one-offs sit in that half-year
      • underlying growth was about 21 percent, not 42 percent, and one-offs were about 22 percent of attributable net income against 2 to 4 percent at peers
    • Consensus FY2026 earnings per share rose 19.7 percent in twelve months to EUR 2.42
      • consensus FY2027 is EUR 2.41, flat, and FY2028 EUR 2.50
    • Our modal path is EUR 2.45 for FY2026, EUR 2.45 for FY2027 and EUR 2.55 for FY2028, about 4 percent a year after this year, because the regulated growth compounds at a fixed 6.58 percent on a RAB growing 5.7 percent a year and the integrated supply margin is at a record EUR 56/MWh it is unlikely to exceed
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and into the price.
    • It shows in the new resources and choices, in capex and margins, in the half-year statements, in the sell-side's estimates, and in a share price up 55.8 percent in twelve months
    • Every series the sell-side revises has risen over three and six months
      • targets rose 7 and 13 percent
      • yet the buy share is 9 percent and the consensus target of EUR 36.88 is 11 percent below the price.
      • The sell-side has raised its numbers and not its view
    • There is nothing left in the chain for the price to discover: the next dated things are a flat FY2027, the end of the buyback and an optical second-half decline as the one-offs drop out
  • What it costs is the whole case. 16.6x consensus earnings 24 months forward is the 93rd percentile of the stock's own ten years and above its ten-year 90th-percentile 'mania' band of 16.20x
    • Against twenty-seven sector peers with a multiple the median is about 12.6x
      • Endesa sits above EDP at 15.0x, Naturgy at 13.7x and its parent Enel at 11.0x, below Iberdrola at 18.1x
    • We give the mix shift to nearly 50 percent regulated EBITDA its credit and move our fair anchor from the ten-year median of 13.3x to the 75th-percentile 'defendable' band of 14.9x.
      • We concede that much and no more
  • The return does not clear the bar on any branch we can evidence.
    • On the modal path the shares are worth EUR 42.46 including dividends at eighteen months and EUR 47.39 at three years: 1.8 percent and 4.7 percent a year
    • The cash return carries the whole of the positive return, about 4.2 percent a year
      • earnings growth adds 6 to 12 percent over the horizons
      • the re-rating to 14.9x subtracts 9 percent
    • Even with the current 16.6x held, the return is 8.9 percent a year over eighteen months and 8.1 percent a year over three years, an F on the framework's anchors.
      • The bull branch does not reach a D, which is the finding that decides the case
    • The case clears the bar only from about EUR 27 to EUR 32, a price last seen in the first half of 2026
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', loosely.
    • The shape is there (new chief executive and finance director, a refreshed plan, a cost programme, a raised guide, upgrades of 20 to 33 percent in a year)
    • the substance is a subsidiary executing its parent's priorities, and the maturity is full output with the payoff already collected by the market

  • What the case therefore is: a mature, fully propagated regulated-growth story at a peak multiple.

    • The business is sound
    • the price is the problem.
    • The same facts would make this a C at about EUR 33 and a B near EUR 27

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is not material enough at this price: the input forces are real but moderate ('Change Forces' at C), the outputs improved sharply once and then flatten ('Financial Outputs' at C, with 'Goldilocks growth' at F), and the modal return fails the 20 percent bar by about 15 points a year ('Modal return' at F).

    • The parts combine by binding: the return leg decides whether the change matters to an investor, and it says no, whatever the quality of the inputs.
    • Not C, which was live: a C would need the return to be within reach of the bar on a plausible multiple, and it is not within reach even with the current multiple held.
    • Not F: the change forces, the upgrade record and the dividend are real and measured, and a lower price would make the same facts a C.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the 2026 regulatory changes are net positive but each falls short of what was asked and each is reversible by politics.
  • Network return: the CNMC's 6.58 percent nominal pre-tax return for 2026-2031 is 100 basis points above 5.58 percent, below the 7.5 percent requested, and fixed for six years with no indexation
    • it is equivalent to 4.94 percent post-tax at a 25 percent tax rate.
  • Investment cap: the Royal Decree lifts the distribution cap to EUR 10.2bn for 2027-2030 from EUR 7.7bn in earlier drafts, within EUR 17.9bn of grid spending to 2030.
  • Generation tax: the 7 percent generation tax is suspended, a EUR 104m benefit in H1 2026, with the suspension phasing down from Q3 2026.
  • Almaraz: a formal ministerial order extends the plant to June 2030 and the nuclear safety council has given a favourable opinion
    • the latest research still lists the final ministerial decision as a data gap, so we treat extension to 2030 as probable and extension beyond 2030 as possible.
  • Intervention record: Spain's intervention proposal of June 2021 took the shares to about EUR 20
    • a 1.2 percent windfall tax and a gas cap followed in 2022
    • interventions cut 2023 operating earnings by about 20 percent.
    • A general election is expected in 2027 and the risks research names nuclear approvals and the network cap as the two most diagnostic variables.
  • TOTEX model: the new remuneration model under Circular 8/2025 includes profit-sharing and benchmarks that could depress near-term cash returns if calibrated aggressively.
  • Not B: the return is below the ask and fixed in nominal terms against Spanish inflation of 4.5 percent in August 2026 and an ECB deposit rate of 2.25 percent in June 2026.
    • Not D: the direction of the 2026 changes is unambiguously favourable.
  • This is the force the thesis rests on, and it is the one most exposed to a single political decision
    • that combination recurs in 'Riskiness'.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity in liberalised retail is tightening: Endesa's electricity commercialisation share fell to 27.0 percent at H1 2026 from 28.4 percent at end-2025 while entrants keep adding customers.
  • Entrants: Octopus Energy exceeded 500,000 customers by end-2025 and adds 170,000 to 250,000 a year
    • Repsol reached 3 million power and gas clients by December 2025, a four-fold rise since 2018
    • independents target about 1.0 million customers by 2026-2027
    • over 300 retailers are active.
  • Churn and switching: system churn exceeds 25 percent
    • Endesa's liberalised base fell from 6.9 million in 2023 to 6.2 to 6.3 million in 2025
    • switching time was cut from 21 days to 10 working days and exit penalties were capped at 5 percent.
  • Pricing discipline: the incumbents have limited incentive to start a price war because no new inframarginal capacity has arrived
    • Octopus raised its retail pricing in summer 2026
    • households are accepting higher margins for price stability.
    • The integrated free power margin rose 6 percent to EUR 56/MWh in H1 2026 while the volume leaked.
  • Not C: share loss, falling customer numbers and a rising entrant count is tightening by any reading.
    • Not F: the incumbents' pricing discipline is holding.
  • For the case: the margin is being earned on a shrinking base, which is the fact behind the doubt on the 2028 supply targets.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because end-market electricity demand is growing but slowly: Spanish mainland demand rose 1.7 percent in H1 2026, 1.2 percent adjusted, while gas demand is falling (Endesa's gas sales -17.6 percent to 25,591 GWh in H1 2026).
  • Direction: tailwind on electricity, headwind on gas.
    • Spanish consumption grew 3.0 percent in 2025
    • August 2026 was +5 percent, the strongest August since 2018
    • the business-foundations research sees demand +18 percent by 2030 from 2024.
  • Data centres: 3.0 GW of connection applications sit with the company, but industry observers expect only 2 to 3 GW of digital demand to deploy in Spain by 2030 because of transmission constraints.
  • Connection constraint: 94 percent of Endesa's network nodes are saturated and only 18 percent of connection requests were approved at the Capital Markets Day of 24 February 2026, so demand growth does not reach Endesa's revenue until the grid is built.
  • Not B: a swing with two-plus years' visibility would need demand growth above about 3 percent a year or a booked order book
    • the adjusted H1 2026 figure is 1.2 percent.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the disruption force is strengthening on the merchant side and on volumes, but for an integrated and structurally short supplier low midday prices are partly a tailwind, and the company has responded by pivoting to storage.
  • Solar cannibalisation: Iberian spot prices fell 48 percent to EUR 44.20/MWh in Q1 2026, solar capture was EUR 25.86/MWh, and there were 1,223 negative-price hours in Q1 2026
    • the H1 2026 average pool price was EUR 49.8/MWh, down 19.4 percent.
  • Self-consumption: customers may consume up to 50 percent of on-site output
    • electricity distributed fell to 70 TWh from 73 TWh in H1 2026.
  • Direction: headwind for renewables (segment EBITDA flat at EUR 429m in H1 2026) and for hydro's 15 to 20 percent peaking premium over five to eight years as 7.9 GW of four-hour batteries arrive
    • tailwind for the short position, which contributes up to EUR 300m of EBITDA in rapid spot declines.
  • Response: 400 MW of storage committed
    • renewables capex cut 20 percent to EUR 3.0bn.
  • Not D: the company's own exposure is hedged (98 percent of 2027 power, 60 percent of 2028) and the capex plan has already been cut away from solar.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because fuel sourcing is a strength while grid equipment is a tightening headwind that bears directly on the thesis.
  • Fuel: 3.0 bcm of Henry Hub-linked gas
    • 90 percent hedged for 2027
    • the ten largest suppliers were only 18.7 percent of procurement in 2025.
  • Grid equipment: transformer and switchgear lead times of five to six years
    • wind turbine prices up 40 to 45 percent since 2020
    • 63 percent of planned Spanish substations missing their deadlines with an average seven-year delay.
    • Jefferies calls the EUR 5.5bn network programme "structurally threatened" by lead times
    • Spanish project execution takes six to seven years, double the theoretical timeline.
  • Cost items: nuclear fuel EUR 65m in H1 2026 from EUR 56m
    • an LNG supplier's arbitration seeks about USD 215m retroactively
    • TTF above EUR 75/MWh in September 2026 with Morgan Stanley at EUR 88/MWh for Q4 2026, which helps the gas spread (over EUR 100m of incremental EBITDA possible) but raises collateral calls (EUR 425m at the extreme).
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = C

  • Rating = C, because the company's own cycle has turned up in profit but not in revenue: revenue fell from EUR 32,545m in FY2022 to EUR 21,031m in FY2025 on commodity normalisation and was +0.5 percent in FY2025 and +1.1 percent in H1 2026, while EBITDA on the brokers' basis rose from EUR 3,777m in FY2023 to EUR 5,756m in FY2025 and to EUR 3,240m in H1 2026, up 19.5 percent.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Endesa is a regulated-growth re-rating that has already happened, in a yield stock.
  • The company, 71.2 percent owned by Enel, was given permission in 2026 to invest more in its Spanish distribution grid at a higher return (the Royal Decree's EUR 10.2bn cap for 2027-2030; the CNMC's 6.58 percent for 2026-2031), had its Almaraz nuclear plant extended to 2030, put a grids specialist in charge, raised 2026 guidance to above EUR 2.4bn after a half-year in which net ordinary profit rose 42 percent to EUR 1,480m, and saw consensus FY2026 earnings per share rise 20 percent in a year to EUR 2.39 to 2.42.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, from the new chief executive and the February 2026 plan through capex, margins and the half-year statements into the sell-side's numbers, and from there into a share price up 55.8 percent in twelve months to EUR 41.34.
  • What remains is a business whose earnings per share grow about 4 percent a year after 2026 (FY2026 EUR 2.45 to FY2028 EUR 2.55 on our path; consensus EUR 2.42 to EUR 2.50), because the regulated growth compounds at a fixed 6.58 percent on a RAB growing 5.7 percent a year and the supply margin is at a record of EUR 56/MWh.

  • The risk sits in the price and the politics, not the operations: a mark-to-market downside of 20 percent to the ten-year median multiple, and a regulator that has intervened three times in five years with an election in 2027.
  • The case clears the bar only from about EUR 27 to EUR 32, a price last seen in the first half of 2026.
