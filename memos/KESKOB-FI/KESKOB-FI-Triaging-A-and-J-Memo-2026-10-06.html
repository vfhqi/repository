[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Kesko is a 'Major Corporate Change' case laid over a slow Nordic construction recovery, and at EUR 22.74 it is not compelling: the company's case is sound, the shares have already paid for the part of it that can be proved, and the next per-share event is an equity issue of EUR 500-700m. On our modal path the total return from here is 1.7 percent a year over eighteen months and 5.5 percent a year over three years, against a 20 percent bar, and the case clears that bar only below about EUR 18.

  • The change is internal, large and real.
    • Management is making the Nordic technical and building trade distributor the larger half of the company: the EUR 1.2bn Dahl acquisition (signed 15 June 2026, cleared unconditionally by the European Commission on 24 September 2026, closing by year-end) takes technical trade to 65 percent of divisional sales and the international share of that division from 58 to 71 percent, funded by a bridge loan and EUR 500-700m of new shares.
    • Six of the twelve internal change forces are strong: a refreshed leadership team, a resourced strategy to make Building and Technical Trade the largest division, a visible lift in operating standards (the EUR 250m Onnela logistics centre delivered under budget and fully operational from spring 2026), a geographic step into Sweden, Norway and Denmark, the acquisition step-up, and a balance sheet levered to 2.8x pro forma against a 2.5x ceiling.
    • The chief executive change (Jorma Rauhala, 1 February 2024) is 32 months old and chosen for continuity
      • Guidance has been narrowed, not raised.
      • The screen's prized pair, chief executive change plus guidance increase, is half present.
  • The external force that sets the earnings of that division is a headwind that is easing slowly outside Finland and not at all inside it.
    • Finnish housing starts are projected at about 15,000 units in both 2026 and 2027 against a long-term need of 31,000 to 36,000 (Danske Bank), Norwegian residential permits sit 27 percent below their ten-year average, and Nordea expects Norges Bank to hold 4.5 percent through 2027 with no cuts until 2028.
    • The professional, infrastructure and renovation leg is growing: Onninen Finland's sales rose 16.1 percent in Q2 2026 against a 0.6 percent competitor average, comparable building and technical trade sales rose 12.9 percent in August 2026, Sweden's building-start indicator was +8 percent year-on-year in July 2026 and Denmark is described as good.
    • The grocery half has stabilised after the 2025 price war: K Group gained 50 and 80 basis points of share in the last two reported quarters at a 6.8 percent operating margin in Q2 2026.
  • The change has reached the outputs unevenly along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The grocery share recovery has travelled all six layers: share and margin are in the statements since late 2025 and DNB Carnegie upgraded to Buy in October 2025.
    • The Finnish technical trade recovery is at the operating metrics and the statements (divisional EBIT +EUR 20.3m in Q2 2026; a beat of about 5 percent against Vara consensus) and only partly at the sell-side's estimates (small raises).
    • Dahl is at the choices-and-actions layers (signed, cleared, not closed) and sits in the FY2027 numbers of only two of the four named brokers.
    • Group comparable EBIT fell from EUR 815.1m (2022) to EUR 650.1m (2024), stabilised at EUR 654.9m (2025) and is guided to EUR 670-730m for 2026 with the midpoint unchanged at EUR 700m
      • The Q2 2026 gain of EUR 17.3m to EUR 194.0m was the strongest quarterly improvement since Q1 2022.
  • Per share, the outputs improve at a single-digit pace, with the dilution in the middle.
    • On our path EPS compounds at about 7.9 percent a year from EUR 1.07 (FY2025) to EUR 1.35 (FY2028), below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band on every horizon: about 9 percent over the next six months, 7.2 percent a year over eighteen months and 7.9 percent a year over three years.
    • The issue takes 6 to 7 percent off the per-share figure in FY2027 (27m new shares on a EUR 600m issue at about EUR 22, the share count from 398.1m to about 425m)
      • Dahl is neutral to EPS in FY2027 (about EUR 70m of EBIT against EUR 33m of extra financial items) and modestly accretive in FY2028 (about EUR 90m).
    • Our path sits level with consensus in FY2026 (EUR 1.16) and about 6 percent below it in FY2027 and FY2028 (EUR 1.23 against EUR 1.31; EUR 1.35 against EUR 1.44), because we count the dilution in full from FY2027, give Finnish residential no recovery before 2028 and note that consensus is not on one basis: two brokers consolidate Dahl and two do not.
  • The shares have already paid for the recovery.
    • The price is up 32.4 percent in twelve months to EUR 22.74, at 16.12x 24-month-forward earnings, the 80th percentile of the stock's own five-year range and its ten-year median, the third-highest multiple in a food-retail set whose median is 12.1x, above three of the four named brokers' targets, and ten weeks before the issue.
    • On our own earnings the market is paying 17.2x the current 24-month-forward figure of EUR 1.32.
    • What is priced: the grocery recovery at margin, the professional recovery in technical trade, the Dahl closing and DNB Carnegie's modest 2027 accretion.
      • What is not priced: the issue's terms, a Finnish residential recovery (which consensus does not assume either) and a repeat of S Group's 2025 price move.
  • The return does not clear the bar, and the multiple is the swing factor.
    • At our 15.0x exit multiple on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the eighteen-month value including cash is EUR 23.33 (1.7 percent a year) and the three-year value EUR 26.70 (5.5 percent a year).
    • Holding today's 16.1x gives 6.3 and 7.7 percent a year
    • The ten-year median of 16.6x gives 8.4 and 8.7 percent.
    • None reaches the framework's D anchor of about 12 percent.
    • The cash return (about 4.2 percent a year, policy-backed) carries the return
      • The earnings leg is probable in its professional-recovery part and plausible in its Dahl part
      • The re-rating leg is negative in our modal case, because grocery falls from about 64 percent of comparable EBIT to about 50 percent by FY2028, moving the company from a food-retail profile toward a distribution profile in a sector that is de-rating.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through (the issue at EUR 700m and EUR 20 a share, financial items EUR 15m higher, tax at 22.5 percent) takes EPS about 5 percent lower and the eighteen-month return to -1.4 percent a year
      • The rating does not move.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' at a C.
    • Crisp on the inputs, early on the outputs, and without the pattern's defining feature so far, major EPS trajectory upgrades: consensus FY2026 to FY2028 EPS is down 1 to 7 percent over twelve months and up 1 to 5 percent over six.
    • The pattern that fits the demand side, 'Trough on Trough', fails every price-and-expectations condition: the trough was 2024 and the shares no longer trade at it.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the company's resources, choices and actions into the operating metrics of grocery and Finnish technical trade and into the statements.
    • It breaks at the per-share line (the issue) and at the price (a multiple that assumes the recovery continues and the mix is still a grocer's).
    • The case is therefore a sound company case and a weak investment case from this price: to own the shares at EUR 22.74 we would be betting on a Finnish housing recovery and a distributor premium for a Kesko that is two-thirds technical trade, before the issue has been priced.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material to the company and not to the return at this price: the forces are real ('Change Forces' C, with B live), the outputs they drive improve at a single-digit per-share pace with a dilutive issue in the middle ('Financial Outputs' D), and the modal return from EUR 22.74 is under 2 percent a year over eighteen months ('Modal return' F)

    • The parts combine so that the strongest Element, the inputs, is cancelled per share by the weakest, the price already paid and the shares to be issued.
    • Not C, because a C would need the return at least in the framework's D band of about 12 percent a year, which needs a multiple of about 17.5x on our earnings or consensus earnings at a held 16.1x, and neither is our modal case.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: Kesko is a mis-modelled Nordic distribution compounder at the bottom of its cycle.
      • Dahl makes it the scale buyer in Nordic heating, plumbing and air-conditioning with automated warehouses and 35 percent digital sales
      • The division's margin went from 2.1 to 5.1 percent in the last upcycle, and the 6 to 8 percent target on about EUR 7bn of sales is EUR 420-560m of divisional EBIT against about EUR 250m today
      • Consensus is held down by two brokers who have not consolidated Dahl and by a Finnish residential floor that cannot get lower
      • Grocery is a bounded-downside asset
      • And 16x is the ten-year median, a 10 percent discount to DNB Carnegie's long-run average.
      • On that reading the next two years bring the trajectory upgrades the 'Major Corporate Change' pattern promises and a multiple of 17.5 to 19x, and the shares reach EUR 30 or more by April 2028.
    • What we concede.
      • The operating leverage is real and the division's own 2017-2023 record is the precedent
      • A Finnish recovery from 15,000 starts toward 25,000 would fall on a right-sized cost base
      • The buying scale argues for more than EUR 10m of synergy
      • And if both arrive the fair multiple is higher than our 15.0x, which is why we name a 17.5x anchor and show its return (12.0 percent a year on our earnings).
      • On the bull earnings (a 24-month-forward figure of about EUR 1.65 at eighteen months, divisional EBIT of about EUR 1,050m by FY2029 with grocery at EUR 450m), 17.5x gives EUR 30.31 with cash, 21.1 percent a year, and 19.1x gives EUR 32.95, 28.0 percent a year
      • At our 15.0x the same bull earnings give only EUR 26.18, 9.8 percent a year.
      • So the bull case needs both legs.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • The bull earnings need the Finnish recovery (possible, about 30 percent) and the Dahl accretion (plausible, about 65 percent), nearly independent, so about 20 percent together
      • The multiple at 17.5x or better given those earnings we put at even, because the sector is de-rating and the issue sits in between
      • The joint odds are about 10 percent, unlikely.
      • The two brokers who have consolidated Dahl (EUR 1.31 and EUR 1.28 for FY2027) sit below the one who has not (EUR 1.39), so consolidation alone is not an upgrade
      • The residential floor being a floor does not make it a turn, and the forecasters have it flat through 2027
      • And three of the four named brokers' targets are below the price, which is not what a mis-modelled compounder looks like.
    • The bounded-downside leg, shown like for like.
      • Grocery comparable EBIT in FY2025 was about EUR 418m (6.5 percent on EUR 6,432m of divisional sales), about EUR 328m after tax at 21.5 percent with no interest allocated.
      • At the set's lowest multiple, Carrefour's 8.0x, grocery is worth about EUR 2.6bn, 29 percent of the EUR 9,005m market capitalisation
      • At the set's median 12.1x, EUR 4.0bn, 44 percent
      • At Kesko's own 16.0x, EUR 5.2bn, 58 percent
      • At DNB Carnegie's preferred Axfood-plus-15-percent, about 21x, EUR 6.9bn, 76 percent.
      • Grocery does not cover the market capitalisation at any multiple the set offers, so the downside is bounded by the 4.2 percent yield and by wherever the multiple's floor turns out to be, not by a sum of parts.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = B

  • The grocery value chain is a two-player structure whose economics favour the incumbent that has the data and the sites.
    • S Group holds about 49 percent and Kesko about 34 percent of Finnish grocery, with Lidl at 10 percent and nothing else of size

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)

  • This is a 'Major Corporate Change' case laid over a slow cyclical recovery, and it is not compelling at this price.
  • Kesko is a Finnish grocery duopolist (34 percent share, a 6.5 to 6.8 percent operating margin, a loyalty scheme in 90 percent of households) bolted to a Nordic technical and building trade distributor whose margin fell from 5.1 percent in 2023 to 3.8 percent in 2025 as residential construction halved.

    • In Finnish technical trade, operating metrics far ahead of the market (Onninen +16.1 percent in Q2 2026 against 0.6 percent) and a divisional EBIT gain of EUR 20.3m that reached the statements and produced small estimate raises
    • In Dahl, nothing yet beyond the signing, with only two of four brokers having it in their numbers.
