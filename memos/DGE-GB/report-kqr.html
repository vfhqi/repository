Section summary
  1. European beverage stocks' 2026 recovery was primarily driven by multiple expansion rather than fundamental earnings growth, so it is better read as a valuation rebound and defensive rotation than a broad earnings turn, and it is only selectively sustainable, depending on which companies deliver real volume and earnings delivery. What the outperformance was. The median share in the group outperformed the Stoxx Europe 600 by about 15 percentage points over the six months to mid-November 2026, after falling by about a quarter over the three years to May 2026. Bernstein reported that the sector multiple rose by 5.2% on a relative basis during the year. Deutsche Bank also attributed the rally to exogenous factors and multiple expansion. Because consumer staples valuations remain at a 5% discount to their 10-year average, future performance is expected to depend increasingly on earnings growth rather than further multiple expansion. Volumes were supportive but weather-aided and uneven. Soft drink bottlers led. Coca-Cola HBC's organic volume grew 5.4% in the third quarter, and CCEP delivered 3.6% volume growth. In beer, Heineken's Vietnam volumes grew in the twenties, while AB InBev's US volumes declined 2.7% and its China volumes fell 16.8%. Bernstein cautioned that Europe's 0.6% consolidated volume growth was achieved against a very soft prior-year comparison. This suggests the recovery may reflect temporary summer weather benefits rather than a structural demand turnaround.

Spirits destocking is easing but has not clearly ended. In the US, Pernod Ricard's organic sales fell 10%, with 4 percentage points from distributor destocking. Bernstein said Diageo's US result was slightly helped by favourable shipment phasing rather than a genuine demand inflection. In China, Remy Cointreau's shipments ran ahead of depletions, which will require active destocking in the third quarter. The divergence between companies matters.
