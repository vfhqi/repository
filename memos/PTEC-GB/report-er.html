Section summary

Playtech's H1 2026 results represent a transformational inflection point in its pure-play B2B investment case, delivering extraordinary EBITDA expansion and upgraded cash conversion that places the group materially ahead of its medium-term targets by year two, though the equity remains structurally capped by persistent Evolution litigation uncertainty and severe customer concentration risks that leave the risk- reward profile finely balanced despite exceptional operational momentum. and lower B2B costs, demonstrating the structural operating leverage inherent in the simplified pure-play model. Free cash flow reached €101.0 million in H1 2026, propelled by robust associate dividend inflows from Caliente Interactive, already achieving the top end of the group's medium-term FCF guidance range of €70 million to €100 million in only year two.

Playtech closed the period with a net cash position of €39.2 million, with the balance sheet highly resilient with significant flexibility for future investment, protected by a fully undrawn €225 million revolving credit facility.

The strategic partnership model continues to deliver substantial equity appreciation, with the Hard Rock Digital stake being revalued from €179 million to €247 million, having more than tripled relative to the initial €80 million investment made in 2023. However, under the revised Caliente Interactive agreement, Playtech exchanged its previous option to convert to a 49% stake for a 30.8% direct equity holding, which will cease standard B2B fees and instead recognize lower near-term cash flows, creating a €30 million to €40 million FY25 headwind that was fully anticipated by the market.

The Past Motor Racing product rollout with Hard Rock Digital in Florida gave Playtech a highly lucrative first-to-market advantage, though management acknowledges that H2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA will decline sequentially from H1 levels as exceptionally high initial revenues from the Hard Rock Digital partnership begin to normalize to lower sustainable levels. Playtech successfully launched with multiple tier-one US operators during the half, including Fanatics in several states, FanDuel in West Virginia, and Bet365 in Michigan, expanding Playtech's regulated iGaming presence.

Latin American performance remained robust despite restructuring friction, with revenues increasing 14% YoY to €99.9 million, driven by Mexico and Colombia during the 2026 FIFA World Cup. However, management acknowledged that election-related sensitivities delayed the Brazil launch timetable, with discussions ongoing to secure a formal contract in late 2026 and launch operations by early 2027, pushing material EBITDA contributions into FY27.

The UK regulatory environment inflicted severe margin pressure, with B2B revenue falling 8% to €59.0 million, impacted by customer changes and higher Remote Gaming Duty, which will continue to affect H2 performance.

The UK Remote Gaming Duty rate hike to 40% in April 2026 forced a full €52.9 million balance sheet impairment of Playtech's Sun Bingo minimum guarantee prepayment, representing a material consensus deviation as the actual fiscal drag exceeded the anticipated high-teens million EBITDA impact. Tightening UK Gambling Commission affordability checks severely restricted player activity, with B2C revenues falling by 22% to €32.0 million, exceeding consensus expectations of a mild single-digit decline. Operational efficiency has been structurally upgraded, with total B2B costs declining by 3% to €266.7 million as decisive run-rate optimization measures successfully removed €20 million of annualized expenses from the business through administrative duplication elimination and R&D rationalization.

The HAPPYBET wind-down progressed substantially with revenue plunging by 96% to just €0.3 million, narrowing Adjusted EBITDA losses by 43% to €1.3 million as the unit prepares for a full shutdown by late 2026. Within the Live Casino vertical, table optimization measures combined with narrowing US losses drove a 550 basis point improvement in the Live Adjusted EBITDA margin to 26.6% in H1 2026. The SaaS segment continues to scale as a high-margin diversifier, with revenues growing by 20% YoY to €69.0 million, representing 17% of total B2B revenues and supporting over 2,000 unique brands across approximately 80 licensees.

This low-friction model allows Playtech to efficiently target smaller operators without requiring complex PAM+ integrations, though management is shifting focus to extending horizontally within existing clients by cross-selling Live Casino, poker, and core platform capabilities once an operator adopts the basic SaaS infrastructure. - Earnings Calls Competitive dynamics remain challenging in core European markets, as Evolution continues to expand its dominant position with a 60% market share compared to Playtech's estimated 25%, driven by aggressive exclusive contracting and high-quality game show innovation.

Proprietary player tracker data reveals that Playtech's daily average player count has trended downwards, while Pragmatic Play has emerged as a rapidly growing challenger, highlighting a highly negative structural trend for Playtech's European Live Casino segment. However, Playtech's core PAM+ platform continues to secure major operators due to the unusually high switching costs associated with migrating legacy player account databases, combined with valuable strategic equity stakes in its fastest-growing licensees.

The Evolution litigation overhang remains the primary valuation cap, with Morgan Stanley maintaining that the ongoing defamation case is the primary driver of Playtech's depressed equity valuation due to the prolonged litigation timeline and legal uncertainty. Flutter Entertainment's €2.3 billion acquisition of Snaitech represents a severe long-term platform migration threat as Flutter is incentivised to migrate Snaitech's casino content onto its own proprietary Sisal platform. Furthermore, the group remains exposed to significant regional execution risks and partner credit friction, highlighted by the €29 million provision taken against the unpaid NorthStar loan balance in H1 2026.

Macroeconomic pressures are intensifying across the value chain, with elevated wage inflation and rising operational expenses significantly pressuring gambling operators, limiting their capacity for discretionary thirdparty technology investments. Licensing software is increasingly viewed as giving away distribution control and player data, with operators migrating toward self-publishing platforms to bypass revenue-share fees as they mature.

However, consumer demand has proven highly resilient in core online markets, with Entain's H1 2026 Online Net Gaming Revenue growing 7% on a constant-currency basis, suggesting that strong volume growth can absorb short-term macroeconomic cost inflation.

The 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory has been fundamentally upgraded, with management now pacing to exceed the €250 million to €300 million Adjusted EBITDA target by 2029 materially ahead of schedule, driven by accelerating Americas momentum and structural margin expansion.

The primary investment case conclusion is that Playtech's pure-play B2B pivot has successfully unlocked exceptional cash generation and margin expansion, but the equity remains structurally undervalued due to the persistent Evolution litigation cap and illiquid minority stakes representing nearly half of total corporate value, creating a complex risk-reward profile that requires either a favorable litigation resolution or a successful Caliplay US listing to unlock the full B2B valuation.
