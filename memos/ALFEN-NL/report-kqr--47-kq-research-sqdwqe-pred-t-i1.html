Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Determining Alfen guidance feasibility and consensus revisions over the next 18 months is critical to resolving its steep valuation discount.
  2. New leadership execution and operational visibility will decide whether Alfen can escape its history of guidance cuts and reignite earnings growth.

Defining the Key Research Objective

1.
Core question: "how likely is company guidance to be raised, met or reduced at the earnings events over the next 18 months, and how likely are sell-side consensus forecasts, and the net breadth of revisions, to move higher or lower over the same period?"
2.
Significant risk: Fluctuations in Alfen guidance directly impact investor confidence and equity valuation because the business has historically suffered from repeated mid-year revenue downgrades. The resolution of whether Alfen can meet its targets over the next 18 months will determine whether its earnings per share can recover from recent lows. This trajectory is highly dependent on whether the company successfully manages its ongoing business transformation.
3.
Severe discount: Alfen current valuation trades at a steep discount relative to its historical multiples, reflecting deep market skepticism. Jefferies reported on 4 November 2026 that Alfen shares trade at 12.5x FY26E EV/EBITDA, representing a 50% discount to historical valuation. Limited visibility: Alfen's medium-term recovery trajectory remains highly dependent on resolving grid congestion and pricing pressures. Although the company possesses a strong structural backlog of EUR 86 mm in Energy Storage for FY27, near-term profitability is hindered by elevated personnel expenses. Establishing a clear operational runway is crucial for reigniting consistent profitable growth and restoring investor trust.

Having previously reduced the export VAT rebate on lithium-ion batteries from 13% to 9% in December 2024, and further to 6% in April 2026, the Chinese government is scheduled to fully eliminate the remaining 6% rebate on 1 January 2027.
3.
Supply chain impact: Major cell manufacturers like Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Limited (CATL), which serves as Alfen's primary battery supplier, are actively negotiating cost-sharing agreements with customers to absorb this incremental tax burden. Morgan Stanley reported on 16 October 2026 that Chinese battery suppliers are attempting to pass through the rebate reduction to protect their unit margins, although success depends on the pricing power of specific premium battery chemistries.
4.
Margin pressure: Alfen's utility-scale storage systems rely heavily on importing cells from CATL, meaning any failure to fully offset the 6% rebate elimination will directly increase product bill-of-materials costs. While Alfen's gross margin reached an exceptional 37.1% in Q3 2026, management clarified that this result was inflated by EUR 2.5 million of one-off contingency releases and does not represent the segment's underlying run-rate margin.

Section 6 – EV Charging: Pricing Pressures, Subsidy Removals, and Product Refresh

Section summary
  1. Alfen’s EV Charging unit is navigating a challenging transition marked by steep pricing competition in the residential segment and the phase-out of legacy products.
  2. Streamlining the hardware portfolio and expanding geographically into Southern Europe and the United Kingdom represent Alfen's core turnaround strategy.
  3. Peer benchmarking reveals contrasting execution pathways, with Zaptec and Easee demonstrating strong growth while Kempower and Wallbox face market headwinds.

Performance Dynamics and Pricing Pressures

1.
Declining segment volumes: Alfen EV Charging revenue dropped to 26.8 million euros in the third quarter of 2026, down from 28.7 million euros in the prior year period. The Netherlands private market was heavily impacted after the government terminated its SEPP private-buyer subsidy of 2,950 euros in January 2026. Delivering 25,850 charge points represented a 10.6% year-on-year volume decline, but average selling prices rose 4% due to a stronger business-charger mix. This volume contraction shows that subsidy removals continue to depress near-term residential charging demand.
2.
Q Alfen EV Charging Q3 revenue trend showing a 6.7% year-over-year decline Fragmented regional support: Alfen's operations in Belgium faced direct headwinds following the elimination of regional purchase subsidies and the discontinuation of corporate charge point tax benefits. The Belgian market is heavily driven by fleet purchases, making it highly sensitive to the upcoming tapering of the 100% company-car tax deductibility starting after 2026. To counteract these shifts, Alfen partnered with regional wholesalers like Cebeo to roll out 520 charge points in semi-public locations. Consequently, these commercial agreements are vital to offset declining residential segment volumes across the Benelux region.
3.
Resilient gross margins: Alfen maintained a healthy gross margin of 40.9% for its EV Charging segment during the third quarter of 2026. Although this represented a slight compression from the 43.1% margin recorded in the prior year period, the result remained above the midpoint of company guidance. Management attributed this performance to lower component costs and the phased introduction of premium business chargers. This margin stability indicates that Alfen's disciplined component sourcing partially shields its profitability from intense market price competition.
4.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies reported on 4 November 2026 that Alfen's EV Charging gross margins declined by 220 basis points to 40.9% due to aggressive pricing campaigns in the residential segment. Squeezed by low-cost manufacturers, Alfen has been forced to implement promotional price cuts to clear legacy inventories. This margin compression suggests that Alfen's underlying pricing power remains highly vulnerable to Far East competitors.

Portfolio Streamlining and Southern Expansion

5.
Streamlined product architectures: Alfen is actively consolidating its electric vehicle charging portfolio from five down to three core AC charger types to reduce manufacturing complexity. The company launched its V2Gready Eve Single Plus and Eve Double Plus models in late 2025 to re-establish technological leadership. A dedicated new home charger is on schedule to launch by the end of 2026, aiming to address the highly competitive residential segment head-on. This standardized hardware approach is intended to maximize cost efficiencies and profitability from fiscal year 2027 onward.
6.
Expanding digital software: Alfen launched its EVE control digital asset management platform alongside the Eve Install application to optimize remote service delivery and reduce installation times. The digital ecosystem allows technicians to resolve initial customer service cases remotely rather than performing costly on-site visits. These software-enabled features are critical as Western European installers prioritize reduced time-to-install metrics when selecting hardware brands. Transitioning toward high-margin digital services will enhance long-term customer lock-in and support Alfen's recovery trajectory.
7.
Pivoting to Southern Europe: Alfen generated 61% of its third-quarter EV Charging revenue outside the Netherlands, highlighting its expanding European footprint. The company is targeting core growth markets in Italy, Spain, and Portugal, alongside a planned re-entry into the United Kingdom. Jefferies confirmed on 21 May 2026 that Alfen's geographical diversification is designed to capture rapid charging infrastructure expansion in Southern European nations. Successfully establishing local distributor relationships will determine whether Alfen can recover lost European market share over the next 18 months.

This initial shift illustrated how rapidly EV charging market headwinds could undermine management projections and erode investor confidence. This downgrade established a negative precedent that would repeat in subsequent years.

Compounded struggles: Alfen further damaged its forecasting credibility in June 2024 by revising its revenue guidance down to EUR 485-520m from its original original EUR 590-660m range. This steep revision was driven by delayed storage orders from falling battery prices and Pacto moisture issues. Jefferies observed that the resulting lack of fixed cost coverage triggered covenant breaches and forced a strategic reorientation.
3.
Deutsche Bank reported that this third consecutive cut arose from labor shortages and nitrogen permitting bottlenecks that slowed grid operators. These persistent bottlenecks prevented Alfen from meeting its mediumterm targets and depressed EPS expectations.

This indexation delay directly impacts the gross margin of transformer substations.
2.
Compressed margins: Private grid project profitability is another key blind spot because these contracts do not benefit from the same indexation protections as public operator framework agreements. Alfen reported that segment margins were dragged down in early 2025 due to a shifting product mix and localized technical adjustments. Conducting dedicated expert interviews with Dutch grid installers would help clarify whether private market developers can demand price cuts that squeeze Alfen's historical pricing premium. This pricing pressure represents a severe downside threat to the smart grid division.
3.
Delayed savings: Redundant headcount reduction remains highly uncertain because the company has not published a concrete timeline for phasing out its overlapping restructuring costs. Although management indicates that transformation efforts and duplicate staff expenses will begin to subside in early 2027, the regional labor shortages in the Netherlands could delay this process. Reviewing the next full-year results on 17 February 2027 will confirm whether the restructuring provision has successfully lowered the personnel expense runrate. If employee costs fail to normalise, sell-side earnings estimates will face severe downward revisions.
4.
Restrictive regulations: Internal structural changes face significant execution hurdles due to the powerful role of Dutch employee labor representation. Under local regulations, Alfen must negotiate extensive employee restructuring plans with the works council, which has historically limited the speed of headcount reductions. Cautious analysts have emphasized that executing permanent job cuts remains extremely difficult without triggering costly disputes and legal liabilities. Accessing future works council arbitration reports would provide investors with essential visibility on the actual feasibility of these strategic cost-cutting programs.
