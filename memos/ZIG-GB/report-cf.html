EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

ZIGUP plc has executed a material strategic transformation from isolated vertical operations to a fully integrated mobility solutions platform, unified under the One Road simplification programme that consolidated Northgate Mobility and FMG divisions effective 1 May 2026, while simultaneously exiting two non-core loss-making markets including NewLaw legal services and ChargedEV charging infrastructure to improve future group profitability and returns.

The corporate rebranding from Redde Northgate to ZIGUP plc in May 2024 following 99% shareholder approval represents a unifying inflection point that aims to transition the company to a cohesive platform where customers can rent across all brands via a single account. This strategic overhaul is underpinned by substantial cost-saving commitments of £10 million in FY2027 and a £20 million annualised runrate target by FY2028, positioning the business for structural margin expansion independent of cyclical vehicle disposal tailwinds that have historically distorted earnings quality.

The forward earnings trajectory is supported by five to eight ranked key drivers with differentiated plausibility profiles. First, UK&I simplification savings carry HIGH plausibility with an estimated +10% to +15% EPS impact, driven by £10 million in FY2027 savings expanding to £20 million annualised by FY2028, representing a self-help margin pathway that management has repeatedly reconfirmed with granular divisional margin guidance.

Second, Spanish fleet expansion demonstrates HIGH plausibility with an estimated +8% to +12% EPS impact, capitalizing on 16.2% vehicle hire revenue growth in FY2026 and rising flexible rental penetration from a low 3% base versus the wider European market. Third, disposal profit normalisation presents HIGH plausibility but carries a negative -5% to -10% EPS impact as used vehicle disposal profits fell 30.4% to £36.5 million in FY2026, reflecting the unwinding of pandemic-era residual value spikes.

Fourth, Claims & Services contract wins offer MEDIUM plausibility with +3% to +5% EPS impact, supported by new business from Howden Insurance and Tesco Insurance alongside the 10-year National Highways contract renewal. Fifth, replacement cycle normalisation carries HIGH plausibility with +5% to +8% EPS impact as normalising fleet replacement lowers net replacement capex and drives steady-state cash generation inflection to £95.7 million in FY2026 from £16.7 million.

Sixth, EV transition cost risk presents MEDIUM plausibility with -2% to -4% negative EPS impact due to real-world commercial vehicle usability constraints from battery range and payload limitations.

Material risks to the forward thesis include three primary concerns. These downside scenarios are amplified by material cost inflation from OEMs increasing fleet capital intensity and ongoing supply chain parts inflation squeezing transactional service margins, which could compress profitability faster than the simplification programme can deliver offsetting savings.

Plausible problems to monitor

The company's historical outperformance has been partially dependent on elevated used vehicle residual values during pandemic-era supply shortages, and the normalisation of these cyclical tailwinds exposes the business to margin compression if replacement capex escalates or disposal profits collapse below depreciated book values.

Simultaneously, operational execution risks embedded in the aggressive UK&I simplification program, elevated leverage positioning, and secular EV transition uncertainties create compounding downside scenarios that could overwhelm the £200 million steady-state cash target for FY2028. The most immediate plausible problem to monitor concerns the normalisation of used vehicle disposal profits, which declined by 30.4% to £36.5 million in FY2026 from pandemic-era peaks.

Used LCV residual values have remained stable since late 2024 following extreme pandemic-era volatility, but this stabilisation masks downside risk if European fleet replacement rates accelerate or regulatory ZEV mandates force premature de-fleeting. Jefferies expects used vehicle residual values to continue moderating in the near term, and any sudden systemic collapse below the £1.0k UK and £1.5k Spain depreciated book value thresholds would eliminate disposal profits entirely while simultaneously elevating replacement capex.

This dynamic is particularly concerning given that ZIGUP's ROCE declined to 11.2% in FY2026 from 12.6% in the prior year, with historical returns partially supported by disposal profits. Peer comparison reveals that rental peer Ashtead demonstrates superior structural cash conversion due to longer average asset lifespans, suggesting ZIGUP's disposal-dependent model carries embedded cyclical sensitivity. A second critical plausible problem to monitor emerges from elevated balance sheet leverage approaching the upper bound of management's target range.

Leverage rose to 1.9x net debt to EBITDA, sitting at the top end of the 1-2x target range, while absolute net debt grew by £162 million to £999 million in FY2026 to fund fleet growth. Free cash flow fell to a negative £74.7 million versus a negative £58.1 million in FY2025, reflecting the capital intensity of fleet expansion and de-ageing cycles. While steady-state cash generation inflected strongly to £95.7 million in FY2026 from £16.7 million in FY2025, the sustainability of this inflection remains contingent on benign usedvehicle markets and stable replacement capex.

Higher floating interest rates directly increase financing costs on unhedged variable debt, and any margin compression from operational integration friction or parts inflation could push leverage above the 2.0x ceiling. This leverage positioning is particularly vulnerable given that normalised supply is balanced by material cost inflation from OEMs, increasing fleet capital intensity. The third plausible problem to monitor concerns execution risk embedded in the UK&I simplification program, which management positions as a cornerstone of the forward earnings bridge.

UK&I simplification is on track to deliver £10 million savings in FY2027 and £20 million of annualised savings by the start of FY2028, but aggressive operational rebranding and integration may trigger customer friction, while the NewLaw run-off remains exposed to regulatory cost escalation and collection risks. NewLaw run-off generated exceptional costs of £22.3 million in FY2026, following £12.8 million in FY2025, demonstrating that non-core exits can materially exceed initial expectations.

The integration of Northgate Mobility and FMG under the One Road program introduces coordination complexity across previously siloed operating divisions, and any execution shortfall would directly compromise the 11% to 13% medium-term EBIT margin target for the combined rental division. Net working capital remains elevated as claims and services cash conversion cycles normalize post-pandemic, suggesting operational friction may already be manifesting in slower collections.

A fourth critical plausible problem to monitor stems from margin pressure in the Claims & Services division driven by persistent supply chain inflation and competitive intensity. Claims and services face ongoing supply chain parts inflation, squeezing transactional service margins, while the UK bodyshop market is consolidating as single garages fail to fund complex repair technology. Although Claims & Services EBIT margin accelerated to 4.6% for the full year, benefiting from H2 operational improvements, this margin remains structurally lower than peer benchmarks and vulnerable to volume compression if subdued UK private demand and cost-of-living pressures restrict consumer discretionary claim volumes.

The division's H2 margin acceleration may reflect temporary contract phasing benefits rather than sustainable improvement, and any reversion to historical run-rates would dilute group-level profitability given the segment's growing contribution to consolidated EBIT. The fifth plausible problem to monitor relates to prohibitive EV transition capital costs and residual value uncertainty in commercial vehicle fleets. Real-world commercial vehicle usability is constrained by payload and range limitations of current e-LCV models, while commercial EV adoption faces unsustainable margins and infrastructure deficits, forcing the closure of ChargedEV.

The EU relaxed the 2035 combustion engine ban from 100% to 90% reduction in late 2025, but rapid vehicle regulatory iteration creates severe residual value risks for early EV fleet adopters. Tightening regulatory mandates force European OEMs to meet strict fleet emissions standards of 93.6 g CO2/km, with massive EU non-compliance fines of €4,250 per gram of excess CO2 in 2025, creating disorderly policy incentives that could force premature de-fleeting of ICE assets.

If ZEV mandates accelerate faster than residual value depreciation schedules, ZIGUP could face write-downs on its £2.8 billion fleet book value while simultaneously incurring elevated capex to replace diesel vehicles ahead of their economic life. Industry experts expect fleet electrification remains at least ten years out for small operators, suggesting the transition window may compress suddenly if regulatory enforcement tightens.

A sixth plausible problem to monitor emerges from intensifying competitive pressure in fragmented rental markets where scale advantages may erode through unlisted competitor aggression. The LCV rental and accident management markets remain extremely fragmented with low local barriers to entry, while unlisted competitors operate with higher debt driving aggressive price competition in national markets. Although nationwide scale creates a strong competitive moat against unlisted peers, this advantage is vulnerable if well-capitalised private equitybacked competitors deploy capital to undercut pricing in high-margin corporate accounts.

Leasing competitors face margin compression from lower-maintenance EVs, prompting rising competitive intensity, which could spill over into rental pricing if leasing firms pivot aggressively into flexible hire to defend utilisation. The Spanish market, which currently generates 16.2% revenue growth and 19.3% EBIT margins, may face heightened competitive entry given its exceptional profitability relative to European peers, potentially compressing returns toward more normalised industry levels.

The seventh plausible problem to monitor concerns sell-side consensus polarisation and the risk that cautious analysts prove prescient if multiple downside scenarios materialise concurrently. The rating mix per Bloomberg consensus as of July 2026 stands at 50% Buy and 50% Hold, with price targets showing wide dispersion with a high of 525p and a low of 410p. Jefferies highlights that normalising vehicle disposal profits continue to weigh heavily on EPS, with elevated fleet capital expenditure keeping leverage at 1.9x and lower disposal gains per unit capping near-term rerating potential.

The cautious hold thesis reflects legitimate concern that the structural cash inflection story may prove ephemeral if used-vehicle values decline faster than replacement capex normalises, creating a negative cash flow scissors effect. If the compound probability of negative scenarios exceeds sell-side expectations, the 28% average valuation discount to rental peers could widen materially as investors price in elevated execution risk and cyclical exposure. These seven plausible problem to monitor interact in non-linear fashion, such that the materialisation of any two or three simultaneously could overwhelm management's £20 million cost-saving program and compromise the FY2028 cash generation target.

The report's sensitivity analysis demonstrates that if used residual values collapse rapidly while opex inflation rises, forward EPS could compress by approximately 17%, which would push ZIGUP's alreadyelevated leverage above prudent thresholds and force dividend cuts to preserve balance sheet capacity. While the base case assumes benign execution across these dimensions, investors should monitor the operational tracking metrics outlined in Section 10 for early evidence that plausible problem to monitor are crystallising into material headwinds.

The company's historical delivery credibility provides some cushion, but the structural composition of forward earnings has shifted materially from disposal-supported profits toward operational self-help, elevating the importance of flawless execution during a period of simultaneous strategic integration, fleet transition, and macroeconomic uncertainty.
