Kesko Oyj (KESKOB-FI): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results strengthen the company half of the case materially and leave the return half, which is what the park rests on, where it was or slightly worse: the business did more than the case assumed, and the price did more still.

What changed

Kesko reported Q3 2026 on 29 October 2026. Group net sales EUR 3,394.5m, +5.2 percent (+4.7 percent comparable). Comparable operating profit EUR 231.4m, +11.2 percent, about 3.3 percent above the Vara consensus of about EUR 224m. Group comparable margin 6.8 percent against 6.4 percent a year earlier.

The beat was one division. Building and Technical Trade: net sales EUR 1,328.1m, +7.7 percent; comparable operating profit EUR 93.8m, +30.8 percent, 10 percent above consensus; divisional comparable margin 7.1 percent against 5.8 percent in Q3 2025. Within it, technical trade (Onninen) sales +11.3 percent to EUR 664.3m and profit +24.5 percent to EUR 41.7m; building and home improvement profit +20.6 percent to EUR 38.6m on B2B strength in Sweden and Norway. Onninen Finland grew 12 percent against a 1.4 percent combined competitor growth rate. Management attributed the growth to data centres, industrial projects and grid infrastructure, with Finnish new housing still depressed and consumer DIY still falling.

The other two divisions missed. Grocery: sales EUR 1,689.9m, +2.7 percent; comparable operating profit EUR 118.9m, +1.2 percent, about 1 percent below consensus; margin 7.04 percent against 7.1 percent; Finnish grocery market share +40 basis points on DNB Carnegie's reading; Kespro foodservice sales -1.0 percent and profit -8.9 percent to EUR 20.5m. Car Trade: sales EUR 384.2m, +8.1 percent; comparable operating profit EUR 23.5m against EUR 22.7m, about 8 percent below consensus on a higher used-car mix; new-car order book up about 70 percent year-on-year. Kesko Senukai's share of result rose to EUR 14.1m from EUR 7.4m, partly on supplier-bonus timing that DNB Carnegie says leaves Q4 weaker.

Cash: operating cash flow EUR 331.4m, +15.2 percent; free cash flow about EUR 236.4m; capex EUR 95.0m, -32.5 percent; net debt excluding leases EUR 1,317m at 30 September 2026, 1.5x EBITDA before Dahl.

Guidance: FY2026 comparable operating profit narrowed upward to EUR 690-730m from EUR 670-730m, midpoint EUR 710m; the sell-side had looked for a midpoint of about EUR 715m. Dahl is excluded. October group sales -0.2 percent, -0.8 percent comparable, on one fewer delivery day; Building and Technical Trade +0.3 percent comparable.

Since the quarter: Dahl completed on 2 November 2026 at EUR 1.2bn debt-free; a rights issue of B shares launched on 5 November for gross proceeds of EUR 651.8m through up to 36.21m new shares, which DNB Carnegie puts at about 8 percent dilution.

Sell-side: DNB Carnegie on 30 October kept Buy, raised its target to EUR 27.0 from EUR 26.5, held FY2026 comparable EBIT at EUR 719m, lifted FY2027 adjusted EPS 1 percent to EUR 1.43 and FY2028 2 percent to EUR 1.60, and raised its sum-of-the-parts premium on Building and Technical Trade to 20 percent over peers from 10 percent. The shares were EUR 25.3 on 29 October.

Against the case: the case's modal comparable EBIT for FY2026 was EUR 700m; the guidance midpoint is now EUR 710m and DNB sits at EUR 719m. The case's path had the division's margin reaching about 5.5 percent in FY2028, and only if Finnish permits recovered; the division printed 7.1 percent in one quarter with Finnish residential on its floor. The case's dilution range was EUR 500-700m of new shares, 27m to 35m of them; the actual is EUR 651.8m through up to 36.21m.

The challenge

Three readings the case does not hold, or holds at odds the quarter has moved.

First: the residential leg is not the gate the case made it. The case's mechanism was that volume returning to a right-sized cost base lifts a 3.8 percent divisional margin toward 6 percent, and it put the Finnish residential recovery (possible, about 30 percent on the case's odds) as the thing that moves the FY2028 margin toward 5.5 percent. The division has just printed 7.1 percent, inside its 6 to 8 percent target, with Finnish housing starts unchanged on their floor and consumer DIY still falling. On this reading the professional, infrastructure and data-centre leg plus the Onnela cost base is sufficient on its own, and the residential recovery is upside the case was treating as a precondition. If right, the case's odds on its conceded 17.5x branch (about 10 percent, needing the division at a 6 percent margin on about EUR 7bn of sales) are too low, and its modal EBIT path (EUR 815m FY2027, EUR 875m FY2028) is too low with it.

What has to be true: that 7.1 percent is not mostly season. Q3 is the construction high season; the review gives Q3 2025 at 5.8 percent against 3.8 percent for all of 2025, a gap of about two points, and the same gap would put this year's run-rate nearer 5 percent than 7. That is still the case's FY2028 landing point reached two years early, so the reading survives the seasonal correction in weakened form. It also has to be true that Onninen's 12 percent against competitors' 1.4 percent is share and not price (the review notes rising prices of cables, pipes and insulation supported nominal growth), and that Dahl, whose Danish business the review describes as historically near break-even, does not pull the reported divisional margin back below the corridor from November in a way the market reads as structural.

Second: the market is paying the distributor premium now, and the case's de-rating branch has its sign wrong. The case's central valuation judgement was a 60 percent weight on a de-rating to 14.0x as grocery falls from about 64 to about 50 percent of profit, with the issue in between. What has happened is the reverse of the case's sequence. The issue has been priced and launched; the shares were EUR 25.3 on results day and EUR 25.7 on 26 November, 17.2x consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 1.50, the 57th percentile of the stock's own ten years; at the memo they were EUR 22.74 at 16.0x on EUR 1.42. More of the move since the memo is multiple than earnings. DNB Carnegie's 20 percent divisional premium, up from 10 percent, is the sell-side saying the same thing. The case's own test for this reading was "the shares holding 16x or better through the issue and the first consolidated half"; the first part of that test is passing three weeks into the issue.

What has to be true: that this is a multiple the market keeps paying for a Kesko that is half distribution through a seasonally weak Q4 (Senukai reversing, October comparable -0.8 percent) and through the first consolidated Dahl quarter at Dahl's lower margin. If it does, the case's hold branch (16.5x) or its 17.5x anchor becomes modal, and the 'Modal return' on the case's earnings moves from 1.7 percent a year toward 8 to 12 percent. We note that none of those cleared the 20 percent bar from EUR 22.74 and all of them are lower from EUR 25.7; the case's own bull earnings at 17.5x gave EUR 30.31 with cash at eighteen months, which from today's price is about 12 percent a year. The reading moves the odds; it does not move the park.

Third, and the one that runs the other way: the beat is narrower than the headline. The group beat was about EUR 7m against consensus. Senukai's share of result rose EUR 6.7m, partly on timing that reverses in Q4. Two of three divisions missed consensus, Car Trade for the second quarter running (13 percent below in Q2 on the case's record, 8 percent in Q3), and Kespro's profit fell 8.9 percent after falling through the summer. The guidance midpoint landed at EUR 710m against a sell-side hope of EUR 715m, and DNB's EUR 719m sits above it, which is the "consensus above the guidance midpoint" condition the case already rated D. The case flagged a cockroach pattern "partly present" on three small actuals, Car Trade, Kespro and Senukai; all three recurred. On this reading the recurring two-thirds of the profit base is flat, the beat is one high-season distributor quarter plus a timing effect, and Q4 is set up to disappoint against an estimate that did not move.

What has to be true: Q4 comparable operating profit at or below Q4 2025 and FY2026 landing at or below the EUR 710m midpoint. The case accommodates most of this; what it does not hold is that the second consecutive technical trade beat is partly flattered. We put the reading up because it argues that the share price, not the case, is what this quarter should be challenging.

We do not find a strong reading that these results weaken the foundations. Grocery share rose 40 basis points at a 7.04 percent margin, above the case's 6.5 percent alarm; Onninen took share at a widening gap; cash conversion improved sharply. The obvious bearish alternative, that the divisional margin is an input-price one-off, is weakened by the breadth of the gain across Finland, Sweden and Norway.

What would settle it

The second review, four working days on, carries what has landed since 30 October: the other brokers' FY2027 and FY2028 EPS and target revisions, the twelve-month revision direction on which the 'Major Corporate Change' fit turns, and the explanation of a buy share the dashboard shows at 33 percent after a 33-point fall in three months while estimates rose, which looks like valuation downgrades and bears on the second reading. It carries the rights issue's result if it has completed, and so the final share count. It does not carry a Q4 figure, any consolidated Dahl number, a synergy update, or any Finnish permit data; it cannot decide the first reading.

The first reading is settled by the division's Q4 and full-year margin on 3 February 2027: a FY2026 divisional comparable margin at or above about 5 percent against 3.8 percent in 2025, in a year with no residential recovery, says the lift is structural; a Q4 back at the prior-year level says season. The monthly sales releases for November (mid-December) and December (mid-January) test the case's 5 percent divisional growth alarm after October's +0.3 percent; two months below it is the alarm.

The third reading is settled on the same day: Q4 comparable operating profit against Q4 2025 with Senukai's timing reversed, and FY2026 against the EUR 710m midpoint and DNB's EUR 719m.

The second reading is settled by price, not disclosure: the case's own test is the multiple holding 16x or better through the issue and through the first consolidated Dahl quarter, which is Q1 2027, reported around late April 2027. Dahl's first consolidated numbers at a lower margin are the moment the distributor premium is tested.

Two things nothing scheduled answers. The synergy disclosure the case wants (above EUR 20m against a EUR 10m target) has no committed date. And a Finnish permit recovery above 20,000 annualised for two consecutive quarters, the case's trigger for the residential leg, is external data on which the review shows no movement and the forecasters it cites show none through 2027.
