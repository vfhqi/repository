Section 4 – Financial delivery

Section summary
  1. Reply's latest second quarter and first half results for fiscal year 2026 surpassed market expectations, driving upward consensus estimate revisions despite localized margin dilution from aggressive startup creation.

Consolidated Revenue and Organic Growth Outperformance

1.
Explicit reporting period: Reply reported its interim half-year financial results for the period ended June 30, 2026, on July 30, 2026, demonstrating strong operational execution. Consolidated turnover reached €1,311.9 million, representing a robust 7.4% year-on-year increase compared to the €1,221.3 million recorded in the first half of 2025. This top-line delivery confirmed that the group remains on a highly solid growth trajectory amid a generally sluggish spending environment.
2.
Accelerated organic growth: The group delivered an acceleration in its organic revenue momentum during the second quarter of 2026, which pleased investors. Specifically, Reply achieved 8.0% like-for-like organic growth in the second quarter, up from the 6.6% like-for-like rate recorded in the first quarter of 2026. This acceleration brought the first-half organic growth rate to 7.3%, representing a best-in-class organic revenue performance relative to international IT services peers.
3.
Peer performance comparison: According to analysis by Deutsche Bank's Tintin Stormont, Reply's organic growth rate of high single digit percentage growth is at the upper end of global peer performance in the IT services sector. This makes the group's performance highly resilient compared to international competitors who are experiencing a much slower recovery.
4.
Positive regional divergence: Chief Executive Officer Tatiana Rizzante highlighted that all three operating regions generated top-line expansion in the first half of 2026, though growth remained geographically uneven. Region 1 led with revenues of nearly €850 million, up 8.0% year-on-year, while Region 3 grew by 9.2% year-on-year to almost €260 million. Region 2, which includes Germany, generated a little over €240 million, representing a more modest regional growth rate of 3.1%.
5.
German business recovery: Deutsche Bank's Stormont pointed out that Region 2 witnessed an encouraging organic turnaround during the second quarter. Like-for-like sales growth in Germany rebounded to 5.5% in the second quarter compared to a sluggish 0.8% in the first quarter. This sequential improvement indicates that the company's investments in new localized initiatives are beginning to drive steady incremental turnaround in Central Europe.

Operational comparison: Bernstein's Derric Marcon maintained that Reply continues to post strong profitability relative to the broader IT services industry, even with the startup creation headwind. 10.
Earnings estimate upgrades: Following the solid second-quarter results, Deutsche Bank upgraded its adjusted EBITDA forecasts for Reply by 3% for fiscal year 2026, 8% for fiscal year 2027, and 9% for fiscal year 2028. The single most important thing that changed during this reporting period was Reply's successful demonstration that its aggressive startup incubation strategy is translating into tangible commercial momentum rather than merely representing a margin-dilutive distraction.

The company maintained an incredibly rapid pace of startup creation, launching 30 to 40 new entities since the beginning of 2026, with approximately 95% dedicated entirely to artificial intelligence initiatives. While this aggressive pace introduced a dilutive drag on operating margins, with EBITDA margins in Region 1 and Region 3 showing declines in the second quarter, the corresponding revenue acceleration validated management's thesis that these front-loaded investments secure long-term technological competitiveness.

Critically, Region 2 witnessed an encouraging organic turnaround with like-for-like sales growth rebounding to 5.5% in the second quarter compared to a sluggish 0.8% in the first quarter, indicating that investments in new localized initiatives are beginning to drive steady incremental turnaround in Central Europe. The strongest counter-view to Reply's bullish positioning centers on whether its decentralized network model can sustainably defend pricing power as generative AI productivity tools become commoditized.

Expert interviews with systems integration managing directors warn that clients will eventually demand lower contract prices as generative AI productivity tools become commoditized, and if these deflationary pressures intensify, Reply may be forced to pass more software development savings directly to clients to preserve key accounts. This concern is particularly acute because approximately 30% of Reply's regional revenue remains tied to time-andmaterials pricing, especially in Germany and the UK, exposing these segments to potential margin compression if clients aggressively demand pass-through of AI efficiency gains.

Furthermore, the decentralized network of 300 boutiques introduces chronic internal friction and competition, with individual business units frequently hiding commercial opportunities from sister companies, which hinders seamless organic cross-selling and limits resource mobility during regional downturns.

The critical 'WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND' trigger that would fundamentally undermine Reply's investment case would be evidence that its aggressive startup creation is permanently cannibalizing consolidated margins without delivering corresponding organic growth acceleration across underperforming European regions. If the group fails to offset its aggressive startup investments with immediate organic sales acceleration across Germany and France, the massive volume of new startups launched since the second half of 2025 represents an ongoing structural margin drag that could limit EPS expansion if demand softens.

Specifically, if Region 2 organic growth rates fail to sustain the second quarter's sequential improvement and revert below 4% while EBITDA margins in Region 1 continue declining below 21%, this would indicate that Reply's decentralized model is becoming structurally inefficient rather than merely experiencing temporary investment-phase dilution. A non-obvious consensus blindspot lies in the market's implicit assumption that Reply's specialized positioning in physical AI, agentic architectures, and sovereign cloud solutions provides durable moat protection against hyperscaler disintermediation.

Major cloud hyperscalers are building forward-deployed engineering teams to deploy AI solutions directly for enterprise customers, and this direct delivery model threatens to bypass traditional systems integrators, with the relationship between hyperscalers and consulting partners increasingly resembling partner in public, compete in private. Reply's heavy dependence on strategic alliances with Microsoft, AWS, and Google Cloud creates vulnerability if these vendors decide to fully internalize high-margin AI consulting work.

Microsoft has grown to become Reply's second-largest customer by revenue, and while this relationship was validated in April 2026 when Microsoft chose Reply as an exclusive launch partner for its enterprise-grade Agent 365 platform, this concentration risk remains underappreciated by the sell-side. Another consensus blindspot concerns Reply's capital allocation pivot toward shareholder returns. The group paid a dividend of €1.35 per share for fiscal year 2025, up from €1.15 in fiscal year 2024, and the board's massive €550 million share buyback program initiated in April 2026 represents a transformative pivot in capital allocation toward shareholder returns.

While the market has celebrated this shift, it implicitly signals that management perceives diminishing returns on incremental M&A opportunities or believes its shares are significantly undervalued. If Reply truly possessed a robust pipeline of accretive acquisition targets capable of sustaining highsingle-digit organic growth, the rational capital allocation choice would prioritize M&A over buybacks, suggesting potential strategic caution that contradicts the bullish AI industrialization narrative.
