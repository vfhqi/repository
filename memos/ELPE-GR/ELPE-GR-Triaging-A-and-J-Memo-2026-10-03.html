[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. HELLENiQ Energy is a well-run Greek duopoly refiner riding the strongest external force in the universe this year, the middle-distillate margin spike that followed the Strait of Hormuz closure of late February 2026, and that force has already travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain into the sell-side's estimates. At EUR 18.18 the shares pay for the margin to last into 2028 and for a peak multiple to hold while it does, so the modal return from here is negative over both horizons. It is a compelling business story and not an investment case at this price.

  • The change is external, supply-driven and cyclical, and it is at full strength now.
    • The Hormuz closure, Russian refinery outages and two decades of European refining underinvestment took the realised refining margin from USD 13.80 per barrel in Q2 2025 to USD 22.50 in Q2 2026, the highest since Q4 2022.
    • The margin moves about 65 percent of estimated forward earnings per share
      • The internal forces together move about a tenth.
    • Every Mediterranean refiner captured the same margin, which is why the pattern below is sector-level and why the multiple below is industry-driven.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's choices and actions (the Geneva desk's crude substitution, the decision to keep Thessaloniki running until 2027), in its key operating metrics (103 percent utilisation, a 56 percent middle-distillate yield, exports up 35 percent), in its financial statements (adjusted EBITDA doubled to EUR 442m in Q2 2026, adjusted net income EUR 253m against a consensus of EUR 210m, net debt down EUR 0.7bn to EUR 1.97bn) and in the sell-side's estimates (consensus FY2027 earnings per share up 118 percent in six months).
  • The internal change is real, prudent and small.
    • Management is using the cash to build a second pillar in power and renewables: Enerwave consolidated, 800 MW operating by Q3 2026, 1.5 GW by 2028, 2 GW by 2030, with 10 to 12 percent returns sought in Romania and Bulgaria.
    • It is three years behind its original plan (1 GW by 2026 became 1.5 GW by 2028) and it moves perhaps a tenth of earnings where the margin moves two-thirds, so it does not change the shape of the case within the horizon.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up and the modal path has them turning down.
    • Upgrades are likely for six more months, because Q3 2026 ran on record European diesel premiums
    • But the sell-side's own median path has adjusted earnings per share falling 22 percent in FY2027, and ours has it falling 36 percent from the FY2026 peak to FY2028.
    • On our path adjusted earnings per share goes 1.65 EUR FY2025, about 2.40 EUR FY2026, 1.90 EUR FY2027, 1.50 EUR FY2028 and 1.55 EUR FY2029, as the realised margin normalises toward USD 13 to 14 per barrel, partly offset by the power platform.
  • What it costs on the valuation is the whole case.
    • At EUR 18.18 the shares trade at 9.5x consensus 24-month-forward earnings per share, the 95th percentile of their own ten-year history, and at 11.4x our 24-month-forward figure, after a 126 percent rise in twelve months.
    • The ten-year median is 7.50x and the 75th percentile 8.40x
      • The consensus median target of 13.85 EUR sits 24 percent below the price.
  • The return against the bar is negative on both horizons.
    • Valuing the shares at 8.0x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, and adding the dividends, gives 13.40 EUR at April 2028 and 15.65 EUR at October 2029 against EUR 18.18 today: about -18 percent a year and -5 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar.
    • The cash return of 4 to 5 percent a year is the only positive leg
      • The earnings leg and the re-rating leg are both negative.
      • Even holding today's multiple on the sell-side's own path gives a negative eighteen-month return.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', and it fits loosely.
    • A sector-wide, cyclical inflection has lifted the earnings trajectory of a dominant, fixed-cost-levered refiner inside a benign duopoly
    • But the inflection is in the margin rather than in demand, a refinery is not one-to-many, and the pattern's point, major earnings-per-share trajectory upgrades, has already happened and is in the price.

  • The chain holds from the force to the estimates and breaks at the return.

    • Every link from the external force to the sell-side's numbers is proven in the accounts
    • The last link, from earnings to a return for a buyer at EUR 18.18, is where the case fails, because the price capitalises a margin at its peak on a multiple at its peak.
    • The case is therefore a strong, well-executed cyclical upswing that is fully priced.

  • Rating = D, because the change is material in the near term but at EUR 18.18 the chain breaks at the return: the forces ('Change Forces' B) have driven the outputs ('Financial Outputs' C) but the 'Modal return' (F) is below zero over both horizons, and the parts bind at the weakest link.

    • Not F, because the forces and the outputs are real and the upgrade cycle has about six months to run
    • Not C, because a thesis whose modal return is negative cannot be material to a capital-deploying investor.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The refining golden age is structural, not cyclical.
    • Europe imports 35 percent of its jet fuel and 20 percent of its diesel, Western European capacity keeps closing, the Mediterranean sulphur emission control area added about 100,000 barrels a day of marine gasoil demand from May 2025, and new Atlantic-basin plants are slow to start
      • On this view margins hold near USD 20 per barrel into 2028, FY2029 adjusted earnings per share is 2.5 to 3.0 EUR, and at 8.5x the shares are worth 21 to 25 EUR plus dividends, clearing the bar.
    • We concede that the deficit is real and structural in origin, and that the chief executive's own view (margins elevated through 2027, potentially 2028) sits above the dominant broker's base case and that he has been right about his market so far.
    • We hold our view for three reasons.
    • Global refining capacity is projected to add a net 1,000 thousand barrels a day in 2026, and supply dislocations end: Hormuz traffic partly resumed in June 2026 before hostilities renewed in July, and Chinese exports and Russian restarts are the sell-side's own named reversers.
    • The bull branch needs a geopolitical bottleneck to persist for three years, which is possible, not probable
      • We put it at about one in three.
    • And even the sell-side median already has earnings per share falling 22 percent in FY2027 beneath a median target 24 percent below the price, so the market's own analysts do not believe what the price assumes.
    • Where the hypothesis would win: two consecutive quarters of realised margin above USD 18 per barrel after the Q4 2026 print with no sign of Hormuz normalising, and FY2027 consensus rising rather than falling.
