Section 2 – Amortisation of Acquired Intangibles

Section summary
  1. Addtech's EBIT is substantially lower than adjusted EBITA because purchase-price accounting requires large non-cash amortisation of acquired relationships.
  2. Non-cash amortisation charges scale directly with the pace of acquisitions and typically persist over useful asset lives of five to thirty-three years.
  3. Brokers consistently add back acquisition amortisation to compute adjusted earnings per share, but some warn this practice understates actual capital consumption.

EBITA versus Adjusted EBIT

1.
Significant divergence: Addtech's reported EBIT is significantly lower than its adjusted EBITA due to substantial non-cash amortisation charges. In the 2023/2024 fiscal year, the group reported an EBITA of SEK 2,860m alongside an acquisition-related amortisation of SEK 434m. This amortisation expense resulted in a reported operating profit of SEK 2,426m. Consequently, amortisation represents a major portion of the company's operating profit bridge and obscures reported earnings.
2.
Scaling effects: Addtech's amortisation charges scale directly with the pace of its acquisitions and the size of its acquired intangible assets. For the acquisitions completed during the 2025/2026 fiscal year, the group recognized a significant volume of acquired supplier and customer relationships. The calculated annual amortisation from these new acquisitions alone was estimated at approximately SEK 105 million. Therefore, escalating transaction volumes will accelerate the growth of future amortisation and reported profit headwinds.
3.
Protracted lives: Addtech assigns specific useful lives to different categories of acquired intangible non-current assets based on historical experience. Supplier relationships are typically amortised over a broad range of five to thirty-three years. Customer relationships generally have shorter estimated useful lives of five to ten years. These assumptions mean that amortisation charges persist for decades after acquisitions close and impact net profit.
4.
Favourable division: The division of acquisition purchase price between goodwill and amortisable intangible assets significantly impacts Addtech's subsequent reported earnings. Addtech disclosures state that other estimates regarding fair value affect the distribution between intangible assets with determinable useful lives and non-amortisable goodwill. Since goodwill is only tested annually for impairment rather than amortised, this classification choice has immediate consequences for the reported profit lines. Thus, reclassifying intangible assets preserves reported operating margins.

Scaling with M&A Pace and Asset Lives

5.
Active adjustments: Broker analysts actively adjust Addtech's earnings metrics to reflect the cash-generative nature of the business model. DNB Carnegie adds back acquisition-related amortisation when computing adjusted EBITA and earnings per share to evaluate operational performance. For example, the broker deducted SEK 63m from its 2027/2028 operating projections for non-PPA intangible assets to align with underlying cash generation. This adjustment helps investors assess true operational cash flow without accounting noise.
6.
Disciplined pricing: Addtech's disciplined acquisition strategy focuses on paying reasonable multiples which helps control the initial scale of intangible assets. DNB Carnegie observed that Addtech has remained highly disciplined in its M&A transaction multiples since the 1960s. This valuation discipline helps the group avoid extreme asset revaluations and subsequent massive amortisation heads on the income statement. As a result, disciplined purchase prices limit growth in non-cash amortisation expenses.
7.
Historical precedents: Historical financial results of Addtech confirm that the gap between EBIT and EBITA has widened as the acquisition engine has expanded. In the 2012/2013 fiscal year, Addtech's operating margin before amortisation of intangible assets was 9.2 percent compared to a reported operating margin of 8.1 percent.
