Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Roko AB faces critical pricing power tests across its B2B and B2C segments over the next 18 months to 3 years as inflation, tariffs, and weak construction demand threaten future earnings per share growth.

Financial impact on earnings

1.
Essential inquiry: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: raw-material and electronic-component inflation (2021 to 2022, and again since mid-2025 in memory chips, capacitors, copper and steel); energy price shocks (2022, and 2026 with Dutch TTF gas around EUR 100 per MWh); a European and UK construction downturn (European construction confidence at -5.9 in October 2026, the lowest since December 2020, Morgan Stanley); a UK and Nordic consumer discretionary downturn with weak consumer confidence; a strong Swedish krona against the euro and sterling; US tariffs on China-made goods (2025); rising UK and Dutch minimum wages and employer costs; high interest rates delaying industrial customers' capital spending; artificial-intelligence-driven memory shortages and their reversal; selling to concentrated supermarket and retail buyers (Fri-Jado)?"
2.
Strong profitability expansion: Roko AB experienced a notable gross margin expansion from 40.5% in 2023 to 44.7% in 2025. According to DNB Carnegie research on 26 October 2026, this gross margin improvement has historically supported the group's decentralized business model. The broker expects the resolution of subsidiarylevel pricing power to directly impact adjusted earnings per share, which is currently forecasted to rise from SEK 85.0 in 2026e to SEK 103.4 by 2028e under stable conditions.
3.
Positive pricing contribution: Roko AB's management stated in the Q2 2026 earnings release that organic profit growth was predominantly driven by successful pricing initiatives. In their Q2 2025 earnings call on 17 July 2025, the company emphasized that they continuously work with subsidiaries to protect and improve operating margins through price increases. If pricing power weakens in 2027, the group's ability to offset reaccelerating input costs will diminish, which could restrict future earnings per share growth below consensus estimates.
4.
Stable leverage cushion: Roko AB maintained its leverage profile at 2.6 Net Debt to EBITDA in Q3 2026, which remains below its long-term target of 3.0. According to the interim report on 23 October 2026, this healthy leverage provides about 60% of the leverage covenant limit. Maintaining pricing power is vital because any margin compression would reduce cash flows, thereby restricting the capital available for self-funded acquisitions and eroding the company's long-term earnings per share expansion runway.

Operational risks and headwinds

5.
Divergent segment performance: Roko AB reported total net sales of MSEK 6,078 for the first nine months of 2026, representing a significant increase from MSEK 4,776 in the prior year period. The interim report published on 23 October 2026 showed Segment B2B net sales reached MSEK 4,055 and Segment B2C sales reached MSEK 2,023. Assessing pricing durability is essential because the weak UK construction industry and soft Nordic consumer discretionary spending could severely impact volume growth if prices are raised too aggressively.
6.
Severe macro pressures: Roko AB's various subsidiaries face re-accelerating raw material costs, high energy prices, and rising minimum wages across Europe and the UK. Management noted in the Q3 2026 interim report that company-specific actions, particularly increasing prices on niche products, have successfully mitigated these headwinds in the past. If inflation accelerates further in 2027 while consumer demand softens, the subsidiaries may struggle to pass through these rising costs, which will directly squeeze the group's future earnings per share.
7.
Compelling compounder model: Roko AB relies on a decentralized, sector-agnostic model that has successfully integrated 35 European niche businesses as of Q3 2026. An expert interview with a former head of acquisition at Lifco AB on 1 December 2025 highlighted that the risk-reward ratio in Roko should be very good for the next three to five years. However, the ultimate trajectory of earnings per share depends on whether these acquired units possess true niche pricing power or if they will be forced to give back price increases as competitive pressures intensify.
