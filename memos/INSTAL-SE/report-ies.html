Instalco (INSTAL-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results leave the case where it was, because the case was written on them; what is new is the sell-side's first response and a fresh reading of the same quarter, and those run the memo's way on earnings per share and against it on cash, roughly cancelling.

What changed

The company reported Q3 2026 on 23 October 2026. Net sales SEK 3,526m, +16.4% year on year; organic growth 8.2% against a Modular Finance consensus of 7.8%. EBITA SEK 254m against SEK 180m a year earlier and a consensus of SEK 225m, a 12.9% beat; margin 7.2% against 5.6% and a Bloomberg consensus of 6.5%. The beat was Sweden: Swedish EBITA SEK 179m against consensus SEK 141m, a 7.4% margin, on 15% organic growth. Sweden's beat (about SEK 38m) was larger than the group's (about SEK 29m), so the rest of the group was net below consensus. Norway's EBITA fell to SEK 20m from SEK 28m against a SEK 33m forecast, a 3.5% margin with project delays and credit losses, and management does not expect improvement until delayed projects start in early 2027. Finland and Germany missed on sales by 7.3% and beat on margin, 10.4% against 9.5%.

Reported EPS of SEK 0.69 against consensus SEK 0.40 included a SEK 102m non-cash Fabri AG revaluation worth about SEK 0.38 a share; on simple arithmetic underlying EPS was about SEK 0.31, below consensus, though we cannot confirm whether consensus excluded the gain. Operating cash flow SEK -66m against SEK 133m; rolling cash conversion 74% against 112% and a 100% target; net debt/EBITDA 3.3x, flat year on year, above the 2.5x ceiling and against the lead broker's pre-print model of 2.3x for full-year 2026. Backlog SEK 11,746m, +30.1%, 14.2% of it organic.

The sell-side response post-dates the memo. DNB Carnegie reiterated Buy and held its SEK 47 target (13x 2027 EV/EBITA) on 26 October, called the report "solid" and "another step in the right direction", and trimmed 2027e adjusted EPS by 1.7% (SEK 2.99 to SEK 2.94) and 2026e from SEK 2.30 to SEK 2.28, balancing higher Swedish margin assumptions against lower Norwegian ones. It argued the shares deserve a premium at 11.8x 2027e EV/EBITA against about 12x for its small-cap index. Its margin path is 7.2%, 7.6% and 7.9% for 2026 to 2028: the 8% target approached, not clearly exceeded.

The case's thresholds against today's price: the memo was priced at SEK 42.52 on 21 October. The share is SEK 40.96 (26 November), having closed at SEK 39.58 the day before. That is below the 50-day average (SEK 41.75), 8.2% above the 200-day, above the case's SEK 36 entry, and at the SEK 40 level at which the case says its 14x path clears the bar on consensus earnings. Since the memo the sell-side earnings momentum pin has moved from A to B and the valuation pin from C to B.

The challenge

The memo already holds most of what the quarter said: the operating beat, cash broken, the leverage gate out a year, the Fabri gain stripped, an EPS cut coming. Three readings of the same evidence are not in it, or are in it at the wrong weight.

First: the margin step is the Swedish cycle, and the case has credited the system for it. The memo gives internal change a 66 and says the method "works" because the margin has risen for five quarters through a reorganisation and a cash squeeze. The self-help and cyclical contributions cannot be separated on the evidence, and the quarter's geography argues for the cycle: Instalco 2.0 runs in Norway and Finland too, yet the margin rose only where volume did. Sweden grew 15% organically on recovering activity and pricing, pricing the memo itself calls two-thirds borrowed from rivals' data-centre capacity. Outside Sweden the group was below consensus. Norway, under the same controls, took credit losses at a 3.5% margin. In its strongest form this reading says the Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (70) is a cyclical tailwind wearing an internal-change label, and the margin would follow Swedish volume down from Q1 2027, when the memo itself has organic growth halving, rather than hold near the 7.2% the memo models for Sweden in FY2027. For it to be right, the Swedish margin has to track Swedish volume rather than programme milestones. The Q1 2027 test the memo already names (above 7.5% with organic growth under 5%) is the right test; this reading says it is more likely to fail than the memo allows. The productivity data cut the other way and belong here: revenue per employee is 11% below its Q3 2023 peak, and Bravida Sweden grew it at 3.7% a year against Instalco Sweden's 0.5% over 2018 to 2024. If the margin is utilisation, the runway is longer than the +60 basis points of utilisation in the memo's bridge to FY2028; the memo's answer is that headcount is being rebuilt, which is the start of the fade. Either way the margin's owner is the cycle, not the programme, and that is a different case from the one rated.

Second: the market prices this company on EBITA, and the EPS cut the memo is waiting for may land without moving the price. The memo's central six-to-nine-month call is "operating beats, EPS cuts, guidance unchanged", a 60% probability of a FY2027 consensus cut of more than 5%, and a return at 14x two-year-forward EPS of about 5% a year, with SEK 36 as the entry. The lead broker's response is the first datum: it absorbed the cash miss, trimmed EPS, and held a target set on 13x EV/EBITA while arguing for a premium. The memo knows targets are set on EV/EBITA and says so; what it does not do is follow that through to the price. If the register capitalises operating earnings and the operating line is being upgraded, the memo can be right that FY2027 EPS consensus falls 10 to 15% and wrong that it costs the holder anything, and the SEK 36 entry may not arrive. For this to be right, the shares and targets would have to track EBITA revisions through the next two reports while EPS is cut. The evidence so far is mixed: the share is down about 4% in five weeks after a 13% EBITA beat, which is the "market-catch-up" pattern the memo named as its alarm, yet the lead broker's target sits about 15% above the price. This reading presses on the fair-multiple element (55) and the return element (35) from the side the memo did not defend: its fair multiple is a P/E argument against a market that is not using P/E.

Third: 2025's cash was the anomaly, and 74% is nearer normal for this book. The memo reads cash as moved from timing to structure, carries conversion below 100% through 2027 at about one in two, and still runs its base at 92% in FY2026 and 100% in FY2027, with leverage 2.4x at end-2027. The counter-case sharpens the odds: FY2025's 108% was driven by intense working-capital reduction programmes that are one-off in nature, so the Q4 2026 release the memo's base needs (of the order of Q4 2025's SEK 451m) would be a repeat of a one-off. The backlog that supports volume is also the mechanism of the drag: it is 30% larger, 14% of that organic, more industrial, and converting it absorbs working capital. Norway's credit losses sit uneasily beside the assurance that overdue receivables have not risen. If this is right, the memo's "plausible miss" on cash (80% through 2027, the gate to 2028, 20 to 25% off the price through the multiple band) is the base, not the miss, and the forecast-financials element (90, "A at its floor" on a 15.5% EPS compound) would be at or below the band's edge. We note that the balance of the evidence, and the lead broker, still read the shortfall as timing and mix, with seasonal Q4 collection as the test; the memo's structural reading is the minority one, and this reading is more bearish still. Both cannot be right.

Net: the same, with the composition shifting toward cycle.
  • Foundations' Robustness (38): the quality element (40) is the same. The guidance-and-delivery element (40) is the same: the lead broker's 1.7% trim is smaller than the memo expects, but the memo dated the cut to the Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 reports, so nothing yet contradicts it.
  • Setups' Fit/Crispness (56): the same; underlying EPS below consensus sharpens the "crisp at EBITA, blurred at EPS" description rather than changing it.
  • Case Riskiness (27): more pressure, from the cash-baseline reading and from Norwegian credit losses beside the receivables assurance; already at the bottom grade, so the pressure has nowhere to go in letter terms.
  • Scope for upgrades (24): slightly less pressure. The first sell-side response ran the memo's way on EPS, and the fall in the sell-side momentum letter the memo expected by mid-2027 has arrived early.
  • Current momentum (88): more pressure. The memo placed it at the top of B because both measures and the technicals were A-grade; the price is now below the 50-day average, 8.2% rather than 18.7% above the 200-day, and the sell-side pin is B. Of the twelve, this is the nearest to moving a grade, though on what we can see it probably holds.
  • Fair multiple (55) and return (35): pressure from both sides, the EBITA-pricing reading and the cash-baseline reading, which roughly offset; the same.
  • The price thresholds: SEK 40.96 is above the SEK 36 entry and at the SEK 40 consensus-earnings level; the previous close was fractionally below it.

Nothing here would move a Headline Rating a full grade. We do not re-rate.

What would settle it

The attribution reading is settled by the Q1 2027 report (28 April 2027): a Swedish margin above 7.5% against a hard comparative with organic growth under 5% would say system; a margin falling with volume would say cycle. Norway's margin once delayed projects start in early 2027 is the second half of the same test.

The cash reading is settled by the Q4 2026 report on 11 February 2027: rolling conversion above 90%, a Q4 release of SEK 400m or more and pro forma leverage at or below 2.7x are the case's own gates. Receivables ageing and unbilled work against sales are the disclosures that would separate timing from structure, and the company has not committed to either; no independent receivables disclosure exists. That part of the question is likely to stay open on management's assurance alone.

The EBITA-pricing reading is settled by whether targets and the share track EBITA or EPS revisions through the February and April reports, and by whether a second broker follows Nordea to Hold.

The second Earnings Review, four working days on, carries the broker estimates and targets that have landed since 26 October and the consensus shape they make: whether the 2027 EPS cut is 1.7% or nearer 10%, and whether EBITDA is held. It is also the place to reconcile a figure that is currently unreconciled: the lead broker's 2027 adjusted EPS appears both as SEK 2.94 after the trim and as SEK 2.46 in its forecast table. If the lower figure is the comparable one, part of the memo's 21% gap to consensus is a basis difference rather than a disagreement, which would change the memo's most important insight. The second review does not carry the February cash print, the Q1 2027 margin or any receivables disclosure, so it cannot settle the first or third readings.
