Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Bavarian Nordic carries substantial financial sensitivity to fixed-cost operating leverage due to its highly specialized manufacturing footprint and volatile public preparedness orders.

Financial sensitivity and valuation implications

1.
Critical question: "Bavarian Nordic A/S (BAVA-DK), a Danish specialty vaccine maker with two segments: Public Preparedness (the smallpox and mpox vaccine JYNNEOS / Imvanex / Imvamune and related work, sold mainly to governments such as US BARDA, France and the European Commission's HERA) and Travel Health (Rabipur/RabAvert rabies, Encepur tick-borne encephalitis, Vimkunya chikungunya, Vivotif typhoid and Vaxchora cholera, sold mainly out of pocket in the US and Europe), with bulk manufacturing at Kvistgaard (Denmark) and a second site in Bern (Switzerland). Key question: how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Significant value: Bavarian Nordic's valuation remains heavily tied to sum-of-the-parts calculations that reflect substantial underlying risk from binary preparedness contracts. Analysts at Jefferies noted on October 8, 2026, that the stock suffers a persistent historical discount relative to its asset-based net present value due to extreme cash flow unpredictability. Successfully managing its high-margin biodefense base while securing additive mergers and acquisitions could reduce this historical valuation discount over the long term. Consequently, the resolution of operating leverage represents a critical driver for the equity's long-term rerating.
3.
Volatile projections: Bavarian Nordic's earnings per share expectations show massive fluctuations as public preparedness revenue moves past historic peak levels. Financial estimates from DNB Carnegie on November 17, 2025, projected adjusted earnings per share would drop from DKK 20.30 in 2025 to DKK 8.60 in 2026 before recovering slightly to DKK 10.50 in 2027. This steep earnings compression demonstrates how fixed costs pressure net margins when high-margin preparedness volumes subside. These dynamics suggest that achieving sustained bottom-line growth over the next four years will depend heavily on travel health volume expansion.

Volatility drivers and transition risks

4.
Fluctuating demand: Bavarian Nordic experienced a substantial shift in product sales as its Public Preparedness segment returned to baseline levels. Company filings show that preparedness revenue peaked at DKK 5,027 million in 2023 but declined to DKK 3,105 million by 2025 as stockpiling subsided. This rapid drop in highmargin sales directly impacted profitability, highlighting why government procurement cycles introduce risk to the manufacturing base. Sustaining plant utilization at Kvistgaard remains essential to offset these volatile government order patterns.
5.
Internalized operations: Bavarian Nordic is transitioning its commercial strategy by terminating distribution partnerships to reclaim complete marketing rights. Management stated in the 2025 Annual Report that agreements with Valneva and Dynavax expired in December 2025 and April 2026, respectively, eliminating partnership revenue. Moving these sales functions in-house creates a more fixed internal commercial cost structure. The change increases operational leverage because the company must now support its own direct marketing infrastructure regardless of regional volume variations.
6.
Contrary evidence: Bavarian Nordic's financial performance in late 2026 demonstrates that cost of goods sold did not expand, but actually contracted relative to sales during a period of rising volume. Production costs for the first nine months of 2026 fell to DKK 2,255 million from DKK 2,306 million in the prior-year period despite total revenue expanding. This cost reduction suggests that operating leverage can expand margins favorably during volume upswings rather than acting as a structural drag.
7.
Other side of the story: Bavarian Nordic's exposure to volatile government contracts is balanced by rapid growth in Travel Health, which could de-risk the operating model. Analysts at Jefferies reported that the company's upgraded 2026 revenue guidance to DKK 6.1 billion was driven by rabies vaccine outperformance rather than preparedness orders. This structural shift towards travel vaccines could dilute the unpredictable biodefense reliance over the next four years. Consequently, the fixed commercial base may become highly profitable as global travel demand accelerates.

Section 2 – Cost Structure and Operating Leverage of Bavarian Nordic

Section summary
  1. Bavarian Nordic possesses a highly fixed cost structure where manufacturing facilities and amortization create substantial operating leverage.
  2. High margin sensitivity to public preparedness volume fluctuations exposes the company to severe earnings deleverage during demand downturns.

Cost structure and fixed assets

1.
High fixed absorption: Bavarian Nordic maintains a highly asset-heavy cost structure centered around its Kvistgaard bulk production facility in Denmark and the newly integrated Bern manufacturing site in Switzerland. Annual depreciation and product rights amortization heavily weigh on the production cost line, including DKK 285 million for Rabipur and Encepur product rights alone in 2025. Furthermore, the cost of idle manufacturing capacity at the Swiss facility reached approximately DKK 93 million in 2025. This massive overhead means that a vast portion of production expenses is completely insensitive to volume shifts, magnifying high fixedcost operating leverage across both business segments.
2.
Elevating labor expenses: Bavarian Nordic experiences substantial payroll commitments that further solidify its fixed overhead base across operations. Staff costs totaling DKK 693 million were distributed across the P&L with DKK 259 million allocated directly to production. Commercial activities and direct sales forces in Germany and other expanding markets added DKK 70 million in personnel overhead. Because these payroll expenses do not adjust with manufacturing output, they contribute directly to fixed operating cost deleverage when production volume contracts.
3.
Sticky commercial overhead: Bavarian Nordic expanded its direct sales footprint following the termination of distribution agreements with Valneva and Dynavax. Analysts at Jefferies noted on October 23, 2026, that moving sales functions in-house increases control but adds to the fixed commercial infrastructure. This structural shift towards direct distribution converts variable commission-based costs into permanent commercial fixed overhead that remains sticky during periods of low regional demand.

Volatility and gross margin response

4.
Severe margin compression: Bavarian Nordic faced a steep contraction in profitability when public preparedness revenue declined from DKK 5,027 million in 2023 to DKK 3,105 million in 2025. The decline in these highmargin government contracts directly reduced the company's overall EBITDA margin from its historical peak. Broker forecasts from DNB Carnegie on March 13, 2026, pointed out that the EBITDA margin compressed to 28% in 2025 when excluding the Priority Review Voucher sale. This margin drop proves that lower volumes across the preparedness segment create negative operating leverage impacts on the bottom line.
5.
Extreme gross volatility: Bavarian Nordic recorded massive quarterly margin swings in 2026, with gross margins rising from 45% in the first quarter to 61% in the second quarter. Management explained in their secondquarter interim report that this surge was driven by a favorable product mix and high manufacturing cost absorption. Analysts at DNB Carnegie confirmed on August 21, 2026, that gross profit expanded to DKK 1,231 million in the second quarter compared to only DKK 475 million in the prior quarter. These swings show how sensitive gross profitability is to manufacturing campaign scheduling and mix on a quarter-to-quarter basis.
6.
Variable factory absorption: Bavarian Nordic remains highly vulnerable to underutilization charges when manufacturing campaigns face unexpected interruptions. A temporary shutdown of Kvistgaard bulk production due to water damage in early 2024 restricted indirect cost absorption, directly hitting the P&L. Financial reports show that these idle capacity costs and unsuccessful production runs are recorded directly under other production costs, which totaled DKK 823 million in 2025. This reporting method highlights that any manufacturing delay instantly destroys gross margin by preventing depreciation and overhead capitalization into inventory.

Projections for operating leverage

7.
Declining preparedness volume: Bavarian Nordic faces a projected softening of public preparedness revenues over the 2027 to 2028 period as pandemic stockpiling orders normalize. Analysts at DNB Carnegie projected on August 21, 2026, that public preparedness revenue would decline to DKK 2,072 million in 2027 and DKK 1,927 million in 2028. This anticipated downward trend will test the cost structure as the high-margin biodefense segment contracts to its baseline level. Consequently, the fixed cost base must be supported by other areas to prevent a severe drop in operating profit over the next three years.
8.
Superior incremental returns: Bavarian Nordic exhibits exceptionally high profitability on incremental sales because of its established manufacturing footprint. Financial estimates from DNB Carnegie on October 26, 2026, highlighted an implied EBITDA margin of 68% on upgraded Travel Health revenues. Similarly, the broker noted on May 13, 2026, that an upgrade in public preparedness guidance yielded an incremental EBITDA margin of approximately 58%. These high incremental margins demonstrate that once fixed costs are covered, extra volume delivers rapid bottom-line earnings acceleration for the business model.
9.
Contrary evidence: In certain down-cycles, cost of goods sold did not expand, but actually contracted relative to sales during a period of rising volume, which shows that management can actively mitigate deleverage risks. Specifically, production costs for the first nine months of 2026 fell to DKK 2,255 million from DKK 2,306 million in the prior-year period despite total revenue expanding. This cost reduction shows that operating leverage can expand margins favorably during volume upswings rather than acting as a structural drag.
10.
Stable structural floor: Bavarian Nordic's underlying profitability remains supported by a growing Travel Health division that offsets biodefense volatility. Analysts at DNB Carnegie reported on October 26, 2026, that upgraded Travel Health guidance to DKK 3.4 billion reflects high demand. This expansion in Travel Health provides a more predictable and sustainable revenue stream to absorb fixed plant costs. Consequently, this growing business mitigates the downside from unpredictable government procurement cycles over the next four years.
11.
Other side of the story: Bavarian Nordic's operating model might not carry severe deleverage risk if its base business in public preparedness stabilizes at a higher floor than historic levels. Management highlighted in October 2026 that securing multi-year government contracts will significantly reduce the volatility of future biodefense shipments. Analysts at Jefferies noted on May 19, 2026, that strategic stockpile maintenance provides a strong valuation floor for the franchise. Therefore, the company's high fixed-cost footprint could become a source of sustained high margins rather than a structural risk if global biodefense procurement remains elevated.

Section 4 – Operational Waste, Batch Failures, and Egg-to-Cell Transitions

Section summary
  1. Bavarian Nordic carries high margin volatility due to elevated annual inventory write-downs, batch failures, and Swiss idle capacity charges.
  2. Shifting from egg-based manufacturing to a proprietary cell line is designed to structurally expand bulk capacity and reduce production waste.

Operational write-downs and peer comparisons

1.
Extensive operational write-downs: Bavarian Nordic experienced a substantial inventory hit when total write- downs and scrap reached DKK 450 million in 2025. According to the company's 2025 annual report, this figure includes DKK 325 million in net inventory write-downs and DKK 125 million in non-provisioned scrap. The write-downs were primarily driven by provisions for Encepur and MVA-BN batches that failed final testing. These persistent losses suggest that manufacturing yield volatility directly depresses the company's annual earnings per share.
2.
Persistent product scrap: Bavarian Nordic continues to struggle with product obsolescence and batch failures across its core Travel Health vaccine lines. The company's 2025 financial disclosures revealed that Encepur write-downs alone accounted for DKK 149 million of the total inventory adjustments. Furthermore, the company used DKK 204 million of prior write-downs in 2025, which included scrapping DKK 100 million of expired Encepur finished drug product. These elevated write-downs show that poor inventory planning restricts gross profitability when demand falls short.

Egg-to-cell manufacturing transition

8.
Scaled manufacturing efficiency: Bavarian Nordic is actively transitioning its production processes to improve long-term margins and eliminate raw material dependencies. In its 2025 annual report, the company explained that moving from primary cells to a continuous cell line will significantly increase doses per batch and lower contamination risks. Management expects this proprietary cell line transition to significantly expand bulk manufacturing capacity to meet future demand. Ultimately, this shift is critical because reducing reliance on egg supply chains will structurally lower cost of goods sold.
9.

Regulatory registry updates on November 24, 2026, confirmed that the Phase 2b clinical trial, registered as NCT07199569, is now officially completed. This double-blind comparability study evaluated the safety, immunogenicity, and reactogenicity of the MVA-BN vaccine manufactured using a Quail cell line against the traditional egg-based Jynneos vaccine. Successful validation of this trial is crucial to enable the transition to cell-line commercial manufacturing.

According to its third-quarter 2026 SEC filing, the company is advancing GEO-MVA using the AGE1.CR.pIX continuous cell line to bypass traditional chicken embryo fibroblast methods. GeoVax has successfully demonstrated upstream proof-ofconcept and is developing downstream processes to support production at a 200-liter scale. This technical parallel demonstrates that cell-line platforms represent the future of scalable biodefense vaccine supply.
11.
Contrary evidence: Bavarian Nordic's manufacturing process improvements did not prevent elevated operational costs during the active transition phases. In its interim report published on November 15, 2024, the company noted that delays in rabies bulk production combined with water damage at Kvistgaard severely reduced cost absorption. These disruptions drove other production costs up to DKK 596 million in the first nine months of 2024 from DKK 172 million in the prior-year period. This demonstrates that operational disruptions can temporarily erase the expected savings from manufacturing technology upgrades.
12.
Robust preclinical outcomes: Transgene SA has also validated the feasibility of continuous cell-line platforms for Modified Vaccinia Ankara vaccines. In its first-half 2026 presentation, the company highlighted that its TG- MVA candidate achieved preclinical safety and immunogenicity comparable to the reference egg-based vaccine. The company plans to leverage cell-line manufacturing to provide a cost-effective, high-volume solution that is easy to automate and industrialize. These successful outcomes suggest that cell-line technology preserves vaccine potency while delivering superior scalability.
13.
Growing competitive urgency: Bavarian Nordic faces intensifying competitive pressure to scale its manufacturing and secure its market position. Management stated during its third-quarter 2025 earnings call that the cell line transition makes the company much more robust against potential future market competition. This transition is urgent because peer developers are rapidly advancing their own cell-line vaccine platforms. Consequently, successful execution of the cell-line project is critical to maintain Bavarian Nordic's market leadership in the global biodefense space.
14.
173 174 - Earnings Calls Other side of the story: Bavarian Nordic's transition from egg-based to cell-line production could face prolonged regulatory review timelines that delay commercial cost-saving realization. While the NCT07199569 comparability study is completed, validating new cell lines across international jurisdictions remains highly complex. Precedents from peer vaccine developers indicate that manufacturing process changes require extensive regulatory oversight and immune-bridging validation. Consequently, the company's expected gross margin improvements may take longer to materialize than expected if health authorities request additional safety cohorts.
