EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Galenica AG operates as Switzerland's leading integrated pharmacy, wholesale, and diagnostics provider, demonstrating consistent execution credibility through its conservative forecasting archetype and strategic pivot from low-margin manufacturing toward high-value clinical services and laboratory diagnostics. The investment thesis centers on sustained defensive growth driven by structural healthcare demand, GLP-1 therapy expansion, and digital pharmacy service monetization, though the company faces moderate margin compression risks from regulatory laboratory tariff cuts, prolonged ERP system transitions, and eventual Swiss online OTC deregulation expected around 2029.

Recent earnings delivery in H1 2026 met revenue consensus at CHF 2,136.5 million with 7.1% growth but missed adjusted EBIT expectations by 2% and net profit by 7% due to elevated financing costs, prompting Deutsche Bank analyst Jan Koch to trim outer-year EPS forecasts by 2% while maintaining a Hold rating and CHF 85 price target anchored to a demanding 19x 2027E P/E valuation. The company delivered consecutive top-line expansions reaching CHF 4,135.6 million in FY25 compared to CHF 3,921.1 million in FY24, with adjusted EBIT rising to CHF 234.8 million from CHF 211.0 million, yet Products and Care margins were diluted by higher-volume, lower-margin GLP-1 weight-loss medications.

Management guidance remains highly credible with an honest-tracker profile exhibiting conservative initial forecasts, consistent revenue outperformance exceeding the 3.5% to 5.0% corridor, and adjusted EBIT growth of 11.3% in FY2025 beating initial

4.0% to 6.0% guidance, positioning the updated 2027 EBIT target of CHF 270 million as achievable despite execution dependencies on the critical Niederbipp SAP migration scheduled for late 2026 and Labor Team diagnostic integration capturing synergies.

The materiality of organizational change reflects strong internal continuity paired with substantial legacy knowledge loss, as CFO Julian Fiessinger succeeded Felix Burkhard after his 28 years of service on January 1, 2025, while Marc Werner remains Group CEO since 2020, leveraging 25 years of industry experience, and employee motivation scores reached 78 out of 100 in 2025 with the Employee Net Promoter Score rising significantly to 27 from 20 in 2024, though employee turnover rose to 12.7% in 2025 compared to 10.8% in 2024.

Strategic restructuring demonstrates decisive capital reallocation, as Galenica decided in March 2026 to discontinue loss-making pharmaceutical production at Bichsel by end-2026 while acquiring Swiss laboratory provider Labor Team in September 2025, generating CHF 62.2 million in H1 2026 sales and contributing 3.1% to group revenue growth, alongside a joint venture with Redcare Pharmacy combining MediService with online platform redcare-apotheke.ch to defend against digital erosion.

External value-chain forces show favorable structural tailwinds offset by cyclical headwinds, as demand for GLP-1 weight-loss medication grew approximately 30% in the Swiss market during H1 2026, representing approximately 4% of total pharmacy sales and contributing roughly 1% to group sales growth, though Swiss consumer healthcare demand remains weak at only 0.2% growth in H1 2026 with Verfora's consumer market sales falling 2.6% due to lower cough and cold product demand, while Federal Office of Public Health price reviews impose continuous pricing pressure with cost-containment introducing a CHF 5 handling fee cut and 10% price reduction on major laboratory tests.

Fundamental peer positioning reveals Galenica's defensive margin superiority but growth underperformance relative to pure-play digital competitors, as Galenica's 5.5% adjusted EBIT margin remains the highest in its peer group while Galenica's H1 2026 sales growth of 7.1% lags behind Redcare Pharmacy's 21% net sales growth in Q2 2026, and Galenica achieved a high generic substitution rate of 79.6% in H1 2026 supporting regulatory pricing resilience.

Sell-side consensus remains cautiously neutral with limited near-term catalysts, as coverage comprises six named analysts from Deutsche Bank and Jefferies exhibiting a predominantly hold distribution anchored to demanding local valuations, with Jan Koch maintaining his CHF 85 price target after trimming 2026 and outer-year adjusted EPS forecasts by 2% due to elevated finance costs offset by Redcare valuation recovery.

The absence of activist pressure indicates no active shareholder campaigns identified in available library sources, leaving organic strategic execution as the primary value creation pathway without external governance catalysts to accelerate transformation timelines or capital allocation discipline. Five ranked key drivers shape forward EPS trajectory with differentiated plausibility and execution dependencies. First, GLP-1 therapy expansion fuels sustained high volume growth as weight loss treatments account for approximately 4% of total pharmacy sales and contributed roughly 1% to overall group sales growth, demonstrating high plausibility anchored to structural patient demand though high-volume prescription medicines dilute gross margin percentages despite increasing absolute gross profit CHF.

Second, Consultation Plus pharmacy services aim to double paid consultation volume within three years as basic Swiss health insurance will cover specific pharmacy vaccinations starting January 2027, with each consultation generating approximately

CHF 80 in high-margin revenue, carrying high plausibility contingent on basic insurance reimbursement implementation. Third, Labor Team integration supports the Products and Care ROS target exceeding 10% in 2027 with management raising the 2027 adjusted EBIT goal to CHF 270 million, combining pharmaceutical wholesale with laboratory services offering medical practices a coordinated solution generating CHF 62.2 million in H1 2026, though medium plausibility reflects execution risks from FOPH cost-containment imposing continuous pricing pressures with a 10% price cut on high-volume laboratory tests.

Fourth, SAP modern ERP rollout projects efficiency gains raising Logistics and IT ROS up to 2% by 2028 with Niederbipp distribution center migration scheduled for September 2026 following successful Lausanne-Ecublens completion reaching automation levels above 70%, carrying high impact but medium plausibility due to operational transition fragility. Fifth, Swiss online OTC liberalization delayed until no earlier than 2029 affects only 13% of Galenica's pharmacy-related sales, representing low near-term plausibility but high strategic importance as eventual online OTC opening could trigger fierce price wars impacting stationary retail margins.

Two material cross-cutting risks threaten the base-case earnings trajectory with compounding negative mechanisms. First, prolonged ERP migration execution failures could sustain elevated dual-system costs and warehouse inefficiencies, as if the Niederbipp SAP transition in late 2026 suffers delays, parallel system runtimes will pressure Logistics margins while simultaneous regulatory lab price-cuts and delayed ERP cost savings could depress 2027 EBIT by over 5%, cross-referenced to Section 9 on key drivers and Section 10 on forward guidance sensitivity.

Second, compounding regulatory margin erosion from laboratory tariff cuts and eventual online OTC deregulation presents structural downside, as compounding price-cuts and digital deregulation could trigger price wars eroding Galenica's high-margin local pharmacy business and high brick-and-mortar pricing dependency leaves Galenica highly exposed to margin contraction if Swiss digital pharmacy penetration replicates broader European trends, cross-referenced to Section 3 on external value-chain forces and Section 7 on peer-relative positioning, amplified by the reality that almost complete reliance on the Swiss healthcare market leaves Galenica highly exposed to local regulatory tariff revisions.

Forward guidance credibility remains exceptionally strong despite moderate valuation headroom, as Galenica operates as an honest tracker with execution bias frequently leading to raised outlooks during fiscal years, with net sales growth consistently landing at the upper end or exceeding initial projections and adjusted EBIT targets met or surpassed despite parallel system transition costs, supported by Galenica meeting or exceeding consolidated financial targets in every reporting period since the 2017 public listing.

Recent H1 2026 delivery validated operational momentum despite earnings misses, as organic sales expansion of 4.0% and local pharmacy sales growth of 6.2% outpaced the broader pharmaceutical market, though net profit compression from financing costs signals balance sheet pressures from diagnostic acquisitions elevating seasonal leverage. The overall investment stance reflects a high-quality defensive compounder trading at full valuation with limited nearterm re-rating catalysts, positioned for mid-single-digit organic growth augmented by strategic diagnostics entry and clinical service monetization, yet facing execution dependencies on complex system migrations and regulatory margin headwinds that could compress the 2027 EBIT target if multiple risks materialize concurrently, warranting cautious monitoring of Niederbipp SAP completion timelines and Labor Team synergy capture milestones as definitive forward credibility validation checkpoints.
