Section 1 – Key question and brief 9 findings

Section summary
  1. The key question surrounding AI's structural impact on Kainos's services business is partially resolvable through analysis of its rapid pricing-model transition and high-margin product growth.

Defining the Verbatim Key Question

1.
Verbatim question scope: The core inquiry asks: "what are the risks and opportunities, for revenue and for cost to serve, for consulting and implementation businesses paid on a time and materials basis (by the hour or day, so revenue follows effort), such as parts of Kainos's Digital Services and Workday Services, in a world where artificial intelligence (AI) tools cut the effort needed to deliver?"
2.
Investor strategic intent: The higher intent of a concentrated, long-only investor is to test whether Kainos can sustain a predictable 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory that supports a 25%+ internal rate of return
3.
Conservative financial framework: This query aligns with a UK and Europe-focused mandate targeting businesses with a £1.20bn market cap and robust cash positions, where earnings predictability over a 24- month hold is a key priority for concentrated capital allocation
4.
Digital services context: The public sector digital transformation division faces structural changes as the UK government modernises its systems under a £10bn NHS digitisation plan while demanding greater efficiency and faster delivery through embedded automation
5.
Workday implementation dynamics: Within Workday Services, standard deployments face deflationary risks, making it critical to transition toward higher-value, complex integrations that protect operating margins from price erosion

Adjacent Questions and Resolvability

6.
Adjacent commercial queries: A secondary, critical path of inquiry is whether clients will choose to insource their development capabilities as AI tools lower technical barriers, or if they will continue to rely on external specialist integrators
7.
Product growth buffer: The expansion of Workday Products, which is targeting £200m ARR by 2030, serves as a high-margin hedge that could offset potential services revenue contraction caused by reduced implementation hours
8.
Resolvability through evidence: The overall question is partially resolvable over 12 to 36 months, as near-term margin impacts will be clear when Kainos normalises its contractor mix and proves its fixed-price contracting execution capability
9.
Other side of the story: If the deflationary pressures of AI on time-and-materials day rates are fundamentally unresolvable, Kainos may face permanent structural margin compression that renders its mid-term financial targets unachievable

Section 2 – Pricing Model Mechanics and Revenue Impact 12 findings

Section summary
  1. Kainos is mitigating AI day-rate deflation by aggressively shifting its contract mix from time-and- materials to fixed-price structures.

Mapping the Pricing Spectrum

1.
Deflationary T&M pricing: Traditional time and materials models couple revenue directly to manual delivery hours, exposing consultancies to substantial revenue contraction as AI tools reduce the total headcount hours required for delivery
2.
Rigid day-rate frameworks: Public sector day rates are historically constrained by frameworks like G-Cloud and DOS, where Centrum Broking notes highly structured rate cards limit the ability of midsized vendors to manually adjust pricing upward
3.
Outcome-based pricing models: Outcome-focused arrangements tie vendor compensation directly to achieved business metrics, but they remain highly complex to structure because defining and measuring specific digital deliverables presents mutual friction
4.
Managed services predictability: Managed services subscriptions provide reliable recurring streams, enabling Kainos to stabilise operational visibility while optimizing internal resource orchestration through advanced software platform integration
5.
High-margin software subscriptions: The Workday Products division uses SaaS subscriptions billed annually in advance, allowing Kainos to bypass delivery effort links and capture structuring power over userbased pricing
6.
Evolving SaaS metrics: To counter headwind risks from AI reducing customer headcounts, Kainos is evaluating a pivot from seat-based pricing to flexible usage-based consumption models to ensure long-term subscription growth

Transition Mechanics and Value Retention

7.
Accelerating fixed-price pivot: Kainos is actively moving toward fixed-price engagements, which grew 44% in value for Digital Services to capture the internal productivity gains yielded by automated delivery tools
8.
Workday Services milestone: Fixed-fee arrangements in Workday Services expanded from 30% to 37% of divisional revenue, providing increased revenue predictability while allowing Kainos to retain efficiency savings internally
9.
Retaining productivity gains: By utilizing generative AI to deliver projects up to 20% faster, Kainos can compress a standard 10-year public sector program into seven or eight years to achieve faster customer value while maintaining stable contract values
10.
Project delivery complexity: The transition to fixed-fee contracts shifts execution risk entirely to Kainos, requiring rigorous project estimation since technical hurdles or miscalculated stage completion metrics can directly compress margins
11.
Broker margin expectations: Deutsche Bank highlights that while the pricing shift supports long-term predictability, near-term services margins face temporary cyclical limitations due to a reliance on high-cost delivery contractors
12.
Other side of the story: If sophisticated procurement departments in the UK government and enterprise sectors demanded transparent day-rate audits, Kainos would be forced to pass savings back to clients, neutralizing its margin-retention strategy

Section 3 – Cost to Serve and Delivery Margin Dynamics 17 findings

Section summary
  1. Kainos is defending its high-teens services margins through intensive junior-led pyramid restructuring and a rapid reduction of high-cost external contractors.

Delivery Margin Baseline and Segment Dynamics

1.
Stable overall margin profile: Kainos delivered an overall Group gross margin of 46.1% in FY26, down from 47.9% in FY25, reflecting near-term operational investments and a shifting mix of delivery resources
2.
Digital Services margin compression: The Digital Services gross margin fell slightly to 35.6% in FY26 compared to 36.4% in FY25, driven by an increased volume of lower-margin collaborative delivery partnerships and temporary contractor reliance
3.
Workday Services structural decline: Workday Services gross margins contracted by 6 percentage points to 45.8% in FY26, down from 51.7% in FY25, impacted by historical wage inflation and the transition of lowermargin Employee Document Management services into the division
4.
Underlying Workday Services strength: Excluding the lower-margin Employee Document Management services, the underlying Workday Services gross margin would have reached a stronger 47.7% in FY26
5.
Workday Products gross margin superiority: The high-margin Workday Products software segment maintained stable gross margins of 77.8% in FY26, which is aligned with the underlying FY25 margin of 77.5%
6.
Resilient operating margin performance: Proactive cost controls enabled Kainos to protect an adjusted profit before tax margin of 15.6% in FY26, maintaining its position as one of the highest-margin IT services models in the global peer landscape

Internal AI Adoption and Resource Optimization

8.

9.
Active headcount reallocation: Demonstrating delivery flexibility, Kainos successfully redeployed 83 delivery personnel from its Services segments into the fast-growing, high-margin Products division to optimize bench utilization

Contractor Dynamics and Margin Normalization

Direct margin headwinds: The high cost of external contractor networks created a direct -1.1% margin drag on the overall services division in FY26

Total contractor spending metrics: In absolute terms, contractor costs escalated to £18.5m in FY26, up from £4.5m in FY25, as the group utilized external support to meet short-term delivery obligations
