Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Pricing power is the primary determinant of Asker Healthcare's long-term profitability because it governs how effectively the company can pass through inflation and maintain its gross margins across highly regulated European markets.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Key Question: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: The 2021 to 2023 input-cost and freight inflation and its pass-through to public healthcare customers; a public healthcare budget squeeze with tenders weighted to price (the company states 55% of European procurement processes focus mainly on price); the Nordic 2+2-year public tender renewal cycle and the 88% re-win rate Asker reported for Sweden, Norway and Finland over 2019 to 2024; centralisation of procurement across Nordic regions and borders (Sykehusinnkjøp in Norway, cross-border Nordic procurement pacts); the Dutch insurer-contracted channel where five insurers (Zilveren Kruis, CZ, VGZ, Menzis, DSW) set terms for diabetes and care products; German reimbursement reform and purchasingorganisation dominance; a strengthening or weakening Swedish krona against the euro (the company reports a 1% move changes group earnings by about SEK 3.7m, 2025 annual report); a period of rising interest rates in which customers extend payment terms? Answer for the company itself, for the business model in general, for these named peers: AddLife AB (ALIF.B-SE), which discloses that distributors absorb typical annual like-for-like price declines of 2 to 3% offset by mix, prices above competitors on service in several tenders, and has 21% of 2025 sales under currency indexation clauses; Uniphar PLC (UPR-IE), for Irish and United Kingdom wholesale and medtech pricing under reimbursement frameworks; Mediq (private, Netherlands), the direct competitor in Dutch insurer-contracted homecare supply; Oriola Corporation and Tamro (Finland), for duopoly pricing behaviour in Nordic healthcare wholesale; Coloplast, Convatec and Mölnlycke as upstream manufacturers whose pricing to distributors and direct-to-provider channels sets the distributor's margin; Cardinal Health and Owens & Minor for the United States precedent on med-surg distribution pricing through inflation and group-purchasing consolidation, and for the sector."

Dutch Insurer Contract Dynamics

Unlike traditional tender-based systems, this market relies on individual agreements with health insurance companies to secure product placements. Specifically, five major health insurance companies collectively dominate the sector by setting reimbursement terms. These dominant payors include Zilveren Kruis, CZ, VGZ, Menzis, and DSW. This consolidated payer base prevents distributors from easily raising prices without risking contract exclusion.
