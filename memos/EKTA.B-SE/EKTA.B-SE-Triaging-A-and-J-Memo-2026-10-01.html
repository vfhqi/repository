[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Elekta is a margin-led self-help recovery in a duopoly runner-up, priced at the bottom of its own range. At SEK 52.55 the shares pay for the margin the new management has already printed and for nothing else; the return clears the 20 percent bar only if the top line stops shrinking, which we put at plausible, not probable.

  • The change is internal, multi-dimensional and already acting.
    • An outside chief executive (August 2025) and an outside chief financial officer (March 2026) have within twelve months taken more than SEK 500m out of the cost base, put floor pricing and double-digit service price rises in place, cancelled SEK 2.2bn of poor orders, written off SEK 1.4bn of failed development, cut development capitalisation from about 60 percent to about 40 percent, reorganised into five regions with full profit-and-loss accountability, and set a 14 to 16 percent adjusted EBIT margin target for FY28/29 against 12.3 percent delivered.
    • The screen's rule is met twice over: a chief-executive change plus a target increase, and eight of the twelve internal forces strong.
    • The false-positive checks all pass: the chief executive bought SEK 48m of shares on joining, the SEK 500m run-rate is in the Q1 FY26/27 accounts, no crown jewel has been sold and there is no buyback funded by debt.
  • The external forces subtract at the moment the case needs the top line to turn.
    • Varian, owned by Siemens Healthineers, unveils its next-generation platform at ASTRO from 28 September 2026, into the window Elekta needs for Evo
    • United Imaging has taken China's domestic radiotherapy share from under 10 percent to over 30 percent in five years
    • Tariffs, component inflation and currency are a guided 100 to 200 basis point headwind at the gross margin.
    • The structural demand tailwind runs at 6 to 8 percent a year, and the chief executive said on 27 August 2026 that Elekta "is still not growing with the market", calling it "highly unsatisfactory".
    • So the case cannot claim the external forces
      • It rests on what the company does to itself.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on the margin line and not on the revenue line.
    • Adjusted gross margin rose to 42.6 percent in Q1 FY26/27 from 37.0 percent (41.1 percent excluding a one-off SEK 53m tariff refund)
    • The adjusted EBIT margin rose to 11.2 percent from 6.5 percent
    • Adjusted EBIT rose 68 percent.
    • Organic sales fell 2 percent in the same quarter, the twelfth-plus quarter of top-line disappointment, and revenue fell 7.2 percent reported in FY25/26.
    • The order line has turned: gross orders up 3 percent in constant currency, a book-to-bill of 1.11x, US solutions orders up at a double-digit rate with over 25 percent of Evo orders competitive flips, China orders up for three consecutive quarters.
    • Cash conversion improved to 96 percent on a rolling twelve-month basis and the dividend was covered by cash flow after investments for the first time in five years
      • Net debt to EBITDA is 1.24x on the company's measure.
  • The change has travelled about two-thirds of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's resources, its choices, its actions, its key operating metrics (gross margin, opex run-rate, book-to-bill, US orders) and its financial statements (adjusted EBIT), and faintly in sell-side estimates (FY26/27 EPS consensus up 4.9 percent over three months to 25 September 2026).
    • The layer it has not reached is revenue, and the sell-side earnings-momentum pin is still D because the outer years were cut over twelve months.
    • That last third is the one the market pays for.
  • What we think it earns: EPS of about SEK 3.90 in FY26/27 (a 60 percent rebound from a depressed SEK 2.43 on the brokers' basis) rising to about SEK 5.05 in FY29/30, about 9 percent a year after the rebound.
    • Revenue contributes about 3.8 percent a year of that, the margin about 1.5 percent a year at the EBITDA line, and falling interest and a normalised tax charge about 3.4 percent a year
      • There is no share-count help.
    • The growth investors can extrapolate, revenue and margin, is about 5.4 percent a year, below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
    • Our path sits 3 to 5 percent below the consensus median (SEK 4.12, 4.48, 4.82 and 5.20 for FY26/27 to FY29/30) because we hold the FY26/27 margin at 13.2 percent, below the 13.4 percent consensus, for the mix that flattered the first quarter.
  • What it costs: SEK 52.55, or 11.6 times consensus 24-month-forward earnings of SEK 4.52 (11.7 times on our own path), a 4.6 percent dividend yield.
    • That is the 7th percentile of the stock's own five-year range of 9.61x to 23.70x, against a median of 13.81x, and the lowest current multiple in the twelve-stock comparison set.
    • Jefferies calls the one-year-forward multiple an all-time low and a 30 percent discount to European medtech
      • The discount is mostly company-driven, an execution history, and the two relevant peers are themselves at their 5th and 16th percentiles, so part of it is the sector's and not ours to earn.
    • What is priced is the failure branch: a margin in the 12 to 13 percent range, a flat top line, a competitor winning the replacement cycle.
      • What is not priced is the 14 percent margin, any sustained sales growth or the first year of delivered guidance.
  • The 'Modal return' on our path is about 15 percent a year over eighteen months and about 13 percent a year over three years: a C, below the 20 percent bar.
    • We value at 12.5x the earnings two years beyond each horizon
    • At eighteen months that is SEK 62.75 plus a SEK 2.40 dividend, a 24.0 percent total return.
    • Earnings growth carries 12.1 of the 24.0 points, the re-rating from 11.7x to 12.5x carries 6.6 points, and the cash return carries 4.6 points.
    • The re-rating is the swing factor: it runs from -6 percent at 11.0x to +15 percent at 13.5x on the same earnings, and behind it sits the sales inflection that decides which multiple applies.
    • The cleared branch, in which the margin holds and sales inflect, returns about 25 percent a year at eighteen months, an A
      • The uncleared branch, in which the margin holds and sales stay flat, returns about zero over eighteen months and 6 percent a year over three.
      • We put the cleared branch at about 45 percent, plausible, not modal.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (tax at 25 percent, net interest at SEK 380m, amortisation SEK 100m higher) takes the eighteen-month return to 10.6 percent a year on the milder case and 7.6 percent on the fuller, a D and an F
      • The below-EBIT lines are where a margin story leaks, which is why cash backing is a kill condition.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major Corporate Change', crisply on the inputs and early on the outputs, at "input plus early output" on the maturity scale.
    • The secondary description, 'Major CfC Clearing', explains the price (a 7th percentile multiple after deep estimate cuts) and not the guidance, which was raised, not rebased.
  • Where the chain breaks: at the revenue line, and the break is dated.
    • Varian's platform launch this week could pause US orders
    • The Q2 FY26/27 print on 25 November 2026 against a soft -4 percent organic comparison is the first chance for a positive organic print in five quarters.

  • The case therefore is a C case with an A branch, decided in the next two quarters: compelling in its margin leg and in its price, not compelling in its growth leg, which is the leg the re-rating needs.

  • Rating = C, because the expected change is material at the input and the margin line but not yet at the return: the internal change forces are strong and acting (E 1 at B), the outputs are improving on margin and not on revenue (E 2 at C), and the 'Modal return' on our path is about 15 percent a year over eighteen months and 13 percent over three, inside the C band and below the 20 percent bar (E 3 at C).

    • The parts combine with the return leg binding: the change forces would carry a B if the outputs had reached the top line, because the return would then include the re-rating to the five-year median and clear the bar
    • They have not, so the return sets the level.
    • Not B: B needs the modal return at or near 20 percent a year, which on the evidence needs the cleared branch (sales inflect, multiple to 13.5x) that we put at plausible, about 45 percent, not modal.
    • Not D: the margin change is delivered and measurable, the cash return is 4.6 percent and covered, and the multiple has room
    • A D would say the case does not exist, and it does.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The hypothesis, at its strongest: Elekta is a value trap wearing a turnaround.
      • The margin gain is a one-off from headcount and a flattering mix, not a structural change
      • The company has lost share for a decade to a competitor with twice its installed base and the software clinicians prefer
      • Varian's platform this week removes Evo's brief technological edge
      • United Imaging takes the Chinese volume the negative working capital depends on
      • Revenue stays flat, the margin sags back to 10 to 12 percent once the mix normalises and the pricing desk costs tenders, the outer-year estimates keep falling, and the multiple has no reason to leave its trough.
      • Organic growth of about 1 percent over three years against Varian's high single digits
        • US share from above 20 percent to below 15 percent
        • United Imaging from under 10 percent to over 30 percent of China.
      • Expert after expert calls the software closed and clunky and Unity a commercial flop
        • Q1 FY26/27 printed -2 percent organic against +0.8 percent expected with the solutions line down 9 percent
        • Twelve quarters of misses.
      • The risks research's own reading is "a deep value trap masquerading as a turnaround story", with order growth without cash the most dangerous deception
        • Four of eleven brokers are at Sell with targets down to SEK 41.
    • What we concede.
      • The top line has not turned and the burden of proof is on the company, not on the sceptic.
      • The Q1 gross margin was flattered by mix, which is why we hold the FY26/27 margin at 13.2 percent, below consensus.
      • ASTRO is a real risk and we put the sales inflection at no better than plausible.
      • The China cash profile is a dependency.
      • The odds we give the cleared branch, about 45 percent, are this hypothesis's odds turned round.
    • Why we still hold our view, as far as we hold it.
      • Margin evidence: the SEK 500m is headcount that has left (over 80 percent of departures finalised by the spring), the service price rises are contractual and recur, the software mix is structural, and the EBIT cash margin of 11.4 percent above the adjusted EBIT margin of 11.2 percent in Q1 FY26/27 refutes the capitalisation false friend for that quarter
        • The ratio to watch is named.
      • Price: at 11.6x, the 7th percentile of five years, with a 4.6 percent yield covered by cash flow, the uncleared branch on our arithmetic returns about zero over eighteen months and 6 percent a year over three, not a loss of 25 to 60 percent as the pre-Q1 scenarios in the risks research had it, because the multiple has already fallen and the margin has already printed.
      • Evo evidence: the first product-cycle evidence in a decade that runs the other way, double-digit US solutions orders with over 25 percent competitive flips, and a professor and chair in California rating Iris imaging above Varian's Halcyon on 22 September 2026.
      • Operator: the chief executive has done in twelve months what the previous three did not do in ten years, and has SEK 48m of his own money in it.
    • Where the hypothesis would win: a negative organic print on 25 November 2026, or US solutions order growth below 5 percent in Q2 or Q3, would show the sales line is not turning under the margin cover
      • Two quarters of adjusted gross margin below 40 percent excluding one-offs, or an EBIT cash margin below the adjusted margin, would show the margin is the mirage the hypothesis says it is.
      • So we do not reject the hypothesis
      • We price it at about 55 percent and find that the modal return is a C and the downside is bounded, which is a neutral verdict with a bullish tilt, not a bearish one.
