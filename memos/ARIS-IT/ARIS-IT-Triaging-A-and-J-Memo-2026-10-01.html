[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Ariston is a self-help recovery inside a European residential heating industry whose structure is getting worse. At 4.218 EUR (2026-09-29) the shares already pay for the part of the recovery the company controls and price the part it does not control at even odds, so the modal return is about 5 percent a year over eighteen months against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case clears that bar only below about 3.40 EUR, where the shares stood between April and June 2026.

  • The change is internal, and it is real.
    • The company is doing five dated, funded things at once: the Fit-2-Win cost programme (50 EURm of permanent savings by 2027, about 20 EURm delivered by late 2025, after 80 EURm of opex and capex cuts in 2024)
    • the Riello acquisition (completed 1-Jul-26 at an enterprise value of 289 EURm, with 25 EURm of run-rate synergies targeted by 2030)
    • the Niš cylinder-insourcing plant in Serbia (inaugurated 11-Dec-25)
    • the Lennox water-heater joint venture in North America (launched March 2026, ahead of its internal first-year targets)
    • and a chief executive three years in post, reappointed on 5-May-26.
    • Only one of the twelve internal forces is strong on the screen's standard, cost discipline.
      • Guidance for FY2026 was confirmed after the Q2 beat, not raised.
      • The screen's decision rule (three strong forces, or a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase) is not met, which is why the internal forces rate C rather than B.
  • The external forces run the other way, and they are larger than the internal ones.
    • Six of the seven external categories are headwinds now.
    • East Asian competition is the largest: Chinese heat-pump exports to Europe rose 45 percent in Q1 2026
      • Chinese units are 20 to 50 percent cheaper
      • Italian air-to-water heat-pump pricing fell about 10 percent in Q1 2026 and about 12 percent in Q2 2026
      • German unit pricing fell 14 percent year on year in Q4 2025.
    • German policy has retreated: the 65 percent renewable-heating mandate was removed on 10-Jul-26 and the BEG cost cap was cut from 30,000 EUR to 28,000 EUR from 21-Jul-26.
      • Italy's Superbonus is at 65 percent from 1-Jan-25 and France's MaPrimeRénov' reopened narrower.
    • Input costs reversed hard: copper up 38 percent, aluminium 34 percent, polypropylene 29 percent and steel 11 percent year to date at 30-Jun-26
      • transport costs up 10.8 EURm in the half.
    • The supports are two: the electricity-to-gas price ratio has improved to 2.4x to 2.5x from above 3x, and German heat-pump subsidy approvals averaged 27,000 a month in Q1 2026, up 28 percent year on year.
      • The sell-side reads the second as a pull-forward ahead of the 21-Jul-26 step-down.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned, once, from a trough toward a level below the framework's band.
    • Q2 2026 organic growth was +5.2 percent and the adjusted EBIT margin 5.7 percent (up 0.9 points), beating consensus by 350 to 430 basis points on revenue and 11 percent on EBIT
    • Q2 EBIT rose 28.3 percent on revenue up 7.4 percent, so the operating leverage is real.
    • The trough was FY2024: organic revenue -12.7 percent and an adjusted EBIT margin of 6.1 percent.
      • FY2025 recovered to +3.0 percent organic and 7.1 percent.
      • Q1 2026 was still -0.3 percent organic.
    • On our modal path earnings per share (brokers' basis) go from 0.331 EUR in FY2025 to 0.34 EUR in FY2026, 0.39 EUR in FY2027 and 0.44 EUR in FY2028: about 10 percent a year, below consensus (0.342, 0.410, 0.463 EUR) and below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
      • The FY2027 step of +15 percent (consensus +20 percent) is a one-year catch-up from Riello's full year and Fit-2-Win's last tranche, not a rate.
    • The margin settles near 8 percent on our path (7.5 percent by FY2028) rather than the 10 percent the company aims at, because Italian heat-pump pricing, copper and aluminium and a normalising tax rate are more likely than not to take about 5 percent off the FY2027 consensus step.
  • The change has travelled four of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's resources (the chief executive, the finance director, the new executive layer), its choices (Destination 2030, Riello, Lennox, Niš), its actions (all complete or under way) and one quarter of its key operating metrics.
    • It has not carried the financial statements for a full year: Riello is consolidated only from Q3 2026 and cash conversion and leverage are worsening by choice (net debt to EBITDA 2.27x at H1 2026 before the about 370 EURm Riello outflow).
    • The sell-side's estimates have moved for one quarter on a flat base: FY2027 consensus EPS +10.5 percent over three months but +1.0 percent over twelve
      • FY2026 -5.9 percent and FY2028 -6.6 percent over twelve months.
  • What it costs. 4.218 EUR is 9.35x 24-month-forward consensus earnings of 0.4512 EUR (2026-09-29): below the stock's ten-year median of 10.1x, above its 10th percentile of 8.2x, at the 41st percentile of ten years, and 37 percent above the 52-week low of 3.084 EUR.
    • The five-year percentile of 21 flatters, because those five years include the 2021 to 2022 heat-pump enthusiasm at up to 20.17x, a level the industry's structure no longer supports.
    • The market has already moved with the case on the first evidence: +16.0 percent over three months while FY2027 consensus rose 10.5 percent.
      • What is not yet priced is a confirmed German recovery with stable pricing
      • nor is the uncleared branch at the 10th percentile.
  • The return does not clear the bar on any footing we can defend.
    • The framework's convention values the shares at each horizon on the earnings two years beyond it
    • we apply a modal multiple of 9.0x, a blend of a recovery-confirmed branch at the ten-year median of 10.1x (odds two in five) and an unconfirmed branch at 8.3x (odds three in five).
    • Over eighteen months: +7.2 percent in total, 4.7 percent a year.
      • Over three years: +26.4 percent, 8.1 percent a year.
      • Earnings growth carries the return, cash adds 2.6 to 3.3 percent a year, and the multiple is the swing.
    • At an unchanged 9.35x the figures are 7.3 and 9.4 percent a year
      • at the cleared 10.1x alone, 12.9 and 12.1 percent, a D.
      • The bar is met over eighteen months only on the consensus-high earnings path and the median multiple together, a combination we put at one in five.
    • The one-year-forward footing gives 10.9 and 11.4 percent a year, because it credits the FY2027 step between now and the horizon
      • the convention is right to strip that step, because it is a catch-up.
      • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings (a 25 percent tax rate and 10 EURm more interest) takes about 10 percent off every year of the path and turns the eighteen-month return negative (-3.5 percent)
      • the rating is F either way.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', and it fits only in its first half.
    • The inputs are broad
    • the pattern's defining output, major earnings-trajectory upgrades, has not arrived.
    • 'Trough on Trough' was the crisper fit in the spring of 2026 at 7.17x and 3.084 EUR, and it fails three of its four mandatory conditions today.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the company's choices through its actions into one quarter of operating metrics, and the balance sheet is sound enough to carry the programme.
    • It breaks at two places: at the value chain, where every other actor is moving the margin down while management moves it up, so the case is a race between 80 EURm of named self-help and an unquantified amount of price erosion
    • and at the price, which already calls that race even.

  • The case therefore is a park at 4.218 EUR, not an invalidation: it becomes a case near 3.40 EUR on unchanged evidence, or at the current price once German subsidy applications, German heat-pump pricing and the Q3 2026 margin (4-Nov-26) have each passed the tests set in the 'Case/SP Key Drivers and Risks' Element.

  • Rating = D, because the input forces are real but mixed (several internal forces, only one of them strong; six of seven external forces against), the outputs are improving from a trough toward a level that stays below the framework's bands (a margin near 8 percent; earnings growth of about 10 percent a year), and the modal return at 4.218 EUR is 4.7 percent a year over eighteen months and 8.1 percent over three years against the 20 percent bar.

    • Not C, because a C needs a modal return near 13 to 17 percent a year, and it is not there even at an unchanged multiple (7.3 and 9.4 percent).
    • Not F, because the return failure is partly a matter of price rather than of change (the case cleared the bar at the spring low of 3.084 EUR), and materiality is judged on the change as well as the price.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The H1 2026 beat shows that the operating leverage is real (Q2 2026 EBIT up 28.3 percent on revenue up 7.4 percent): consensus FY2027 EPS of 0.410 EUR (+20 percent on FY2026) is achievable, Riello is accretive by high teens once its run-rate synergies arrive, the Lennox joint venture is ahead of plan in a 10 USDbn market, and so the recovery is under-modelled and the thesis is material.
  • Our response: we concede the operating leverage, and we concede that the first half of 2026 was better than the sell-side's models
    • our own FY2027 figure (0.39 EUR) could prove too low if the Q3 margin holds.
  • But even on the consensus earnings path and an unchanged 9.35x multiple the return over eighteen months is about 7 percent a year, because the framework's convention values the shares on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, and the FY2027 step is already inside today's 24-month-forward figure.
  • Materiality at this price needs a re-rating, and the 'Foundations' do not support one: the value chain is moving against the company, the business is sub-scale in its growth category, and the operator and governance profile is one the market has discounted for reasons that have not changed.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because end-market demand is recovering from a deep trough on subsidy-driven and possibly temporary German heat-pump volume, while new-build and retrofit economics have worsened.
  • German approvals: heat-pump subsidy approvals improved strongly through H1 2026, averaging 27,000 a month in Q1 2026, up 28 percent year on year
    • monthly applications had fallen from 37,500 in December 2025 to 14,200 in January 2026, so the series is subsidy-timed.
  • Housing activity: German completions fell 18 percent in 2025 to 206,600 units, the lowest since 2012
    • permits for the first five months of 2026 were 104,700 apartments, up 15.4 percent year on year
    • French housing starts were up 32 percent year to date at June 2026.
  • Payback economics: for a middle-income German household the modelled payback lengthens from 26 years to 37 years by 2028 and 46 years by 2030 under the new BEG model.
  • Product mix: gas boilers, about 30 percent of sales, face a projected -10 percent compound annual decline
    • water heating, about 50 percent of sales, is described as resilient.
  • US water heating: the 2029 Department of Energy rule pushes electric storage water heaters above 35 gallons to heat-pump technology, a market projected to grow about 25 percent a year
    • a real structural force, but small for Ariston before 2029.
  • Not C, because the visibility is under two years: the sell-side reads the German approval surge as a pull-forward ahead of the 21-Jul-26 step-down.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the regulatory framework that was expected to drive heat-pump demand has been rolled back in Germany, Italy and France within eighteen months, and the only firm regulatory tailwind (the US 2029 water-heater rule) is distant.
  • Germany: the Building Modernization Act approved on 10-Jul-26 removes the 65 percent renewable-heating mandate
    • the BEG cost cap was cut from 30,000 EUR to 28,000 EUR from 21-Jul-26 with the climate-speed bonus phased down
    • the reform saves 2.1 EURbn by moving to an income-targeted model.
  • Italy: the Superbonus fell from 110 percent to 70 percent in 2024 and 65 percent from 1-Jan-25.
  • France and the US: MaPrimeRénov' reopened on 23-Feb-26 with a 3.6 EURbn budget but narrower eligibility

  • Tailwinds: the EU F-gas rules (an R290 capability barrier Ariston has met)
    • the US 2029 rule
    • the recast Energy Performance of Buildings Directive.
  • Not F, because BEG funding is legally intact to 2029 at up to 70 to 80 percent reimbursement, and the electricity-to-gas ratio, which policy shapes, has improved to 2.4x to 2.5x.
