Section summary
  1. extreme capital intensity and spectrum licensing rules protect the incumbent triopoly, though regulatory intervention on low-band sharing aims to lower barriers
  2. technological disruptions like satellite broadband are emerging as complementary adjacencies rather than direct substitution threats to terrestrial 5G

Barriers to Entry and Regulatory Moats

1.
High capital barrier: Building a physical mobile network in Germany requires billions in upfront capital outlays, protecting the three established incumbents.
2.
Substantial financial outlays: 1&1's 2026 cash capex guidance of €500 million to €550 million highlights the massive cost of entry.
3.
Incumbent scale advantages: Deutsche Telekom's massive commitment of €30 billion in network infrastructure through 2030 demonstrates the scale gap.
4.
Sunk civil engineering costs: Building physical fiber backhaul routes requires high upfront civil construction costs with long payback periods.
5.
Strict spectrum limits: Access to essential radio frequencies is highly restricted by the federal regulator BNetzA, creating a natural oligopoly.
6.
Prolonged usage rights: Extending existing 800 MHz and 1.8 GHz frequency allocations to 2030 eased the immediate auction cost burden for incumbents.
7.
Shared spectrum mandates: BNetzA has obliged established operators to negotiate sharing low-band spectrum below 1 GHz to support 1&1's network build.
8.
Failed voluntary talks: The voluntary negotiations for sub-1GHz frequencies failed to secure long-term sharing agreements between 1&1 and incumbents.
9.
Historic auction litigation: German courts ruled BNetzA's 2019 spectrum conditions unlawful, forcing a formal regulatory reassessment of past allocations.
10.
Heavy deployment regulations: Strict rural coverage obligations are attached to spectrum rights, imposing onerous physical rollout requirements on all network operators.
11.
Rural build partnerships: To mitigate high costs, incumbents have agreed to share 6,000 passive infrastructure sites in rural grey spots.
12.
Greenfield exclusion risks: 1&1 was initially excluded from early passive infrastructure partnerships, increasing its standalone deployment friction.
13.
Strict coverage thresholds: Operators must cover at least 98% of households in each state, requiring massive physical site density.
