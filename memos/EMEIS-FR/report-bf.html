Section 3 – Industry Structure, Concentration and Where Profits Are Made

Section summary
  1. Demographic shifts drive structural growth as the elderly population expands rapidly across core European markets.
  2. French private care is highly concentrated among five major players, while other European markets remain highly fragmented.
  3. Care operators have shifted from aggressive volume expansion to return-driven strategies focused on operational efficiency.
  4. Strict regulatory tariff limits and severe wage inflation pose persistent risks to sector margins.

Demographic Drivers and

1.
Market Size Stable demand: Emeis operates in a European elderly care sector characterized by powerful demographic tailwinds. The proportion of people over 85 years old in France is projected to grow from 3.4% in 2025 to 6.4% in 2050. Germany's population aged 85 and older is expected to grow to 5.3 million in 2050 from 3 million in 2024. This structural expansion ensures long-term volume visibility for care services across the region.
2.
Accelerating need: Belgium's population over 85 years old is projected to grow by 46% in the next decade. This expansion represents an increase to 550,000 people in 2040 from 380,000 in 2030. These rising dependency ratios will intensify pressure on healthcare system capacity over the next two decades. Rising chronic long-term conditions like dementia further increase the demand for specialized medical and social care.
3.
Capacity deficits: Private operators are increasingly responsible for adding nursing home capacity to meet growing Scandinavian demand. For example, private care providers account for 40% to 50% of new elderly care capacity in Sweden compared with only 20% private provision overall. Swedish industry experts estimate that more than 400 new nursing home facilities will be needed by 2032. This rapid privatization helps efficient private providers capture market share as municipal capacity declines.
4.
Structural shortages: The supply of nursing home beds fails to keep pace with the aging European population due to limited recent development. Real estate research shows a potential gap of 34% in Germany, 53% in France, and 55% in Spain between current supply and 2040 requirements. This growing imbalance between supply and demand supports high occupancy rates for established operators. These capacity constraints will reinforce long-term pricing power and support operating profitability.

Market Concentration and

5.
Rivalry High concentration: The French private nursing home sector is one of the most consolidated in Europe, with the top five operators representing 55.4% (calculated) of private beds. Within this private market, Clariane represents 15.9%, Emeis represents 13.8%, Domus Vi represents 12.6%, Colisée represents 8.4%, and Domidep represents 4.7%. The combined market share of Clariane and Emeis represents almost thirty percent of private beds in France. Large pan- European operators leverage this high concentration to achieve cost efficiencies and operational scale.
6.
Pervasive fragmentation: Other European markets remain highly fragmented despite the presence of pan- European leaders. In Germany, the private sector represents 41% of the total market, but is highly fragmented and led by Alloheim. In Belgium, the top five private operators represent 65% of private capacity, but private commercial operators make up only 36% of the total beds. In the Netherlands, the private sector is extremely small at 7% of total beds, though the top five private operators represent eighty-four percent of private beds.
7.
Stable ranking: The market shares among the top players have remained highly stable because of significant regulatory barriers to entry and capacity inertia. Capacity inertia prevents rapid adjustment to demand fluctuations, protecting existing operators from sudden market changes. The four largest pan- European operators, Clariane, Emeis, Domus Vi, and Colisée, are all based in France and have historically driven growth through acquisitions. This consolidated leadership provides a strong barrier against disruptive competitors.
8.
Competitive pressure: The public and non-profit sectors dominate the overall care home landscape in Europe. In France, public operators control 48% of total beds and non-profit operators hold 29%, leaving only 23% for the private sector. In Germany, the public sector is 5%, non-profits represent 54% (calculated), and the private sector is 41%. This dual structure means private commercial operators must consistently outperform public alternatives in operational efficiency to defend their margins.

9.

Emeis historically pursued aggressive international M&A, but has pivoted to operational quality and portfolio optimization under its current recovery plan. This operational turnaround is underpinned by improving occupancy rates and pricing discipline. Focusing on existing assets rather than greenfield expansion protects capital and drives near-term earnings per share.

These real estate transactions substantially reduce leverage and interest expenses for the operators.
11.
Capacity restraint: Operators have slowed down their development pipelines to prioritize profitability and cash flow generation. Attendo previously focused on restoring profitability and slow bed expansion, but is now gradually rebuilding its pipeline. Emeis reduced its development capital expenditure to 33 million euros in the first half of 2026 from 43 million euros in the first half of 2025. This structural shift ensures that capital is allocated only to high-return projects.
12.
Operational leverage: Occupancy gains serve as the main driver of operating profitability because of high fixed staffing and rental costs. At Emeis, more than 50% of the revenue increase in 2025 flowed directly to EBITDA, demonstrating high operational leverage. Group occupancy rate rose by 1.8 percentage points in 2025 to 87.6%, helping EBITDA excluding IFRS 16 reach 376 million euros. Reaching high occupancy levels is critical to offset persistent labor inflation and rental costs.
