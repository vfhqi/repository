[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. DiscoverIE is a cyclical demand recovery inside a steady acquisitive compounder, and at 895.0p the recovery has already been priced. The 18-month destocking cycle has ended with the strongest order data in the comparison set, and adjusted earnings per share compound about 10 percent a year on our modal path; but the shares rose 29 percent in the eleven weeks after the update that proved it, so the modal total return from here is about 8 to 9 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case is operationally compelling and financially not; it would clear the bar below about 765p.

  • The change is external and cyclical, and it is real.
    • An 18-month customer destocking cycle has ended, and the evidence is hard rather than hoped for.
    • Organic orders rose 31 percent year on year in the three months to 30 June 2026 (reported 24 July 2026), from 15 percent in the quarter before
      • Organic revenue rose 6 percent from 5 percent
      • Book-to-bill was 1.15 times.
    • The orders are non-cancellable and over 90 percent fall due within twelve months, so the next two halves of revenue are largely written.
    • The turn is sector-wide: book-to-bill above 1.1 times across several European connectivity makers, distribution channels destocked, and the European manufacturing PMI at 52.7 in August 2026, its highest since May 2022.
    • The last unit to recover, Controls, whose six large North American customers destocked for twice as long as expected, returned to organic growth in the quarter to 31 March 2026.
  • The change inside the company is continuity under more pressure, not transformation.
    • The same chief executive since January 2009 and finance director since July 2010 run the same strategy
    • What has changed is the pace and the funding of it.
    • £95m was deployed on three acquisitions in six months at an average 9 times EBIT (Storm Interface, December 2025; Trival Antene, April 2026; 3Gmetalworx, May 2026), after a quieter two years.
    • Gearing of 1.2 times at 31 March 2026 becomes 2.2 times pro forma, above the 1.5 to 2.0 times target, with a path back to 1.8 times by March 2027.
    • A Bangalore plant with three times the prior Indian capacity opened in August 2026, and the margin ambition was raised to 17 percent by FY30 in June 2025, the tenth upward revision in fifteen years.
    • None of the screen's decisive triggers is present: no chief-executive change, no numeric guidance increase.
      • The internal forces are real but are C, not B.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned in direction but not yet in size.
    • Every output that matters is improving against the last three years
    • The core metric still compounds below the 12 to 20 percent target band.
    • Revenue growth has moved from a three-year reported compound rate of about minus 0.4 percent to plus 10 percent at constant currency in the quarter to 30 June 2026 (plus 6 percent organic), and consensus FY27 revenue of £481m is plus 8.5 percent.
    • Adjusted operating margin (13.8 percent in FY26) is set to rise on mix, because Trival and 3Gmetalworx earn margins above 30 percent and Sensing and Connectivity already runs at 17.8 percent, net of a £2m capacity-cost drag that persists through FY27.
    • Our modal path takes adjusted EPS from 40.3p in FY26 to 45.0p, 49.6p and 53.7p in FY27 to FY29, about 10 percent a year and 3 to 8 percent above consensus
      • The first year is at the band's foot, the next two below it as the acquired contribution annualises.
    • Sell-side estimates have moved 1 to 2 percent
      • Upgrades of a few percent are likely after the half-year, because Deutsche Bank's own 5.2 percent full-year organic assumption sits against 6 percent delivered and 31 percent order growth.
  • The change has travelled five of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the company's actions, its key operating metrics, its first-quarter revenue and, just, in the sell-side's estimates.
    • The layer not yet proven is a second consecutive quarter of 6 percent-plus organic growth after the easy comparatives pass
    • The half-year results (1 December 2026, or 8 December 2026 on the financials block's date) are that test.
  • What it costs is the problem.
    • The shares were 695p on 24 July 2026 when the update was published and 895.0p on 2026-10-12, a 29 percent rise with no further earnings news.
    • That puts the 24-month-forward multiple at 18.5 times consensus earnings of 48.31p: the 99th percentile of the last two years, the 53rd percentile of five years, and above the ten-year median of 15.90 times.
    • It is above the comparison set's median of about 15.2 times and above the two closest United Kingdom cohort names (TT Electronics 10.0 times, Volex 14.5 times).
    • The market has paid for the turn before the half-year proves it, so the re-rating component of the return has already been earned by someone else.
  • The return does not clear the bar.
    • Valuing the shares at each horizon at 16.9 times the earnings two years beyond it, the framework's convention, gives 1,007p including dividends at eighteen months and 1,146p at three years: 8.2 percent and 8.6 percent a year.
    • The earnings component alone, at a held multiple of 18.5 times, gives 14.7 percent a year at eighteen months
      • The cash return adds about 1.5 percent a year
      • The re-rating component is modestly negative in our blend, because the branch in which today's multiple holds is about 40 percent.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from operating profit to earnings per share (interest held at £11.6m, tax at 27 percent, the £2m capacity drag persisting) takes FY28 EPS to 46.4p from 49.6p and the returns to 3.9 percent and 6.8 percent a year
      • The rating does not move.
    • The price below which the eighteen-month modal path clears 20 percent a year is about 766p
      • On the three-year path about 663p.
  • The pattern has rotated under the price.
    • This case fitted 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' crisply at 600p to 695p between December 2025 and July 2026: a transient destocking concern clearing in an oversold quality business.
    • The concern has cleared with hard evidence, and the oversold condition has gone with it.
    • What is left fits 'Compounder' loosely, at C: a conservatively modelled quality acquirer whose consensus numbers look low, but on a judgement about restocking rather than on a mechanical error.
    • The second pattern pays less than the first.

  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.

    • It holds from the demand turn through orders, revenue and margin mix to about 10 percent a year of earnings growth
    • The operator has never, in the record before us, converted an order surge into a profit warning.
    • It breaks at the price: the competing hypothesis (that the 31 percent is safety restocking) does not need to be right to cost two or three points of multiple, and at the 99th percentile of two years it is plausible enough to do so.
    • The case is therefore a good business whose shares have run ahead of the proof, to be bought at a lower price or after a half-year that confirms the turn at a price that still pays.

  • Rating = D, because the Building Block measures materiality including the return, and at 895.0p the modal total return of about 8 to 9 percent a year fails the 20 percent-plus test even though the 'Change Forces' (B) and the 'Financial Outputs' (C) are genuine

    • The three Elements do not combine evenly, and the 'Modal return' at F binds the whole because the price has taken the return the change would otherwise have produced.
    • Not C, because only the branch in which today's 18.5 times holds reaches the C band (about 15 percent a year at eighteen months), and that branch is possible, not modal.
    • Not F, because the operating change is genuine, measurable and sector-confirmed, and the earnings component alone compounds about 10 percent a year.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • In its strongest form: the 31 percent order surge is safety restocking, not demand.
    • Customers are lengthening their order horizons "to secure supply safety" as supplier delivery times lengthen, which is the company's own description and the PMI's lengthening-delivery-times reading, and it is exactly how the 2021 to 2022 inventory build began before the 2024 to 2025 destock.
    • The comparison base was the weakest quarter of the cycle.
    • The continental European industrial base is flat: German industrial production fell 1.6 percent month on month in July 2026, machinery output is forecast flat for 2026, France is weaker still, and the company's correlation with the composite PMI is 0.8.
    • When the restock passes, organic growth settles at 2 to 4 percent
      • The margin path then depends entirely on deals bought at rising multiples (9 times on the last three, 11 to 12 times EBITA for 3Gmetalworx) with money at 5 to 6 percent, so EPS compounds 5 to 7 percent a year
      • And the market, which paid about 14 times for this business for most of 2024 and 2025, goes back there.
    • At 14.0 times on 52p of 24-month-forward earnings the shares are worth about 730p plus dividends by April 2028, a loss of about 16 percent.
    • This is the Risks report's "false friend" path of June 2026 with the flattering indicator now being orders rather than cash conversion, and the environment this week is its friend: the Assistant Portfolio Manager holds Fragility at Double Underweight, European breadth is at three-year lows, and the market pays for existing cash and refuses leverage.

    • The evidence for it is the weak comparative, the longer-dated orders, the German data, the restock warnings from both covering brokers on 24 July 2026, Volex's medical segment still shrinking 10.5 percent organically on destocking, and the 2.2 times gearing that pauses the deal engine for a year.

    • What we concede.

      • Part of the 31 percent is restocking.
      • We have put only 45 percent on the durable branch and have let the multiple de-rate in our blend for that reason.
      • The comparatives will take reported order growth down sharply in the second half whatever underlying demand does.
      • The deal engine is paused by leverage until mid-2027.
    • Why we still hold the operating view.
      • The orders are non-cancellable and the business does not accept cancellations, so the restock, if that is what it is, is already revenue for the next two halves.
      • The turn is visible across the sector and in distribution, not only in this company.
      • Design wins, which are not restocking, rose 8 percent to an estimated £385m of lifetime value in FY26.
      • The Controls unit's problem was customer-specific inventory, now cleared.
      • The cost base is variable, so a shallower recovery costs growth, not margin.
      • And the company has never, in the record before us, turned an order surge into a profit warning.
    • Why we do not hold the investment view at this price.
      • The competing hypothesis does not need to be right for the shares to disappoint
      • It needs only to be plausible enough to cost two or three points of multiple, and at 18.5 times and the 99th percentile of two years it is.
      • It is the reason the modal multiple is 16.9 times rather than 18.5, and the reason the recommendation is neutral and parked rather than bullish.

  • Resources, choices, actions: the people are unchanged (chief executive since January 2009, finance director since July 2010)
    • The choices are an upgraded margin ambition (17 percent by FY30, set June 2025) and a fifth target market (security, added September 2024)
    • The actions are three acquisitions, a tripling of Indian capacity (Bangalore, opened August 2026) and a leverage step-up.
  • External against internal: the two main change forces that drive the thesis are external
    • The internal, management-driven change is secondary, but real.
  • Not A, because the demand force is a cyclical restock with about twelve months of order visibility, not the two-plus years the 'Demand Inflection' test asks for, and the internal forces lack any of the screen's decisive triggers (no chief-executive change, no numeric guidance increase).
    • Not C, because a 31 percent organic order rise against a 15 percent rise the quarter before, with sector evidence of book-to-bill above 1.1 times across several European connectivity makers (AlphaSense's view of 10 October 2026, unchecked by us), is a top-quartile external change in this comparison set.

Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because two macro forces act on this company more than on the universe and in opposite directions: European manufacturing activity (turning up, with a lag) and sterling and interest rates (headwinds to translated earnings and to acquisition accretion).
  • European activity: the company's correlation with the composite PMI has been 0.8 since November 2021
    • The European manufacturing PMI was 52.7 in August 2026 and euro-area new orders grew at their fastest since mid-2022
    • Against that, German industrial production fell 1.6 percent in July 2026 and machinery output is forecast flat for 2026.
  • Currency: 79 percent of revenue is non-sterling
    • A 1 percent rise in sterling cuts revenue by £3.5m and EBIT by £0.6m
    • Translation is unhedged by policy and transaction hedges run twelve months or less.
    • Deutsche Bank (24 July 2026) names sterling appreciation as the key downside to FY27 consensus.
  • Interest rates: borrowing costs of 5 to 6 percent erode the accretion of debt-funded deals
    • The Assistant Portfolio Manager's reading for the week of 2026-10-09 has December 2026 rate rises priced at both the Federal Reserve and the European Central Bank, which argues against the "declining interest rates" tailwind management guided to.
  • Not D, because the demand-side macro reading is improving and the company's variable cost base held margins up 1.2 points through a 7 percent organic decline in FY25.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the regulatory forces net to a mild tailwind: direct tariff exposure is contained, two pieces of legislation add demand, and the indirect trade risk is the unhedged residual.
  • Tariffs: Section 232 metals tariffs (April 2026) put finished electrical equipment at 15 percent rather than 25 percent
    • Seven of 38 sites are in the United States and make about half of United States customer demand locally
    • United States sales are about 22 percent of the group and China-to-United States imports about £4m a year.
  • Legislation: the UK-India Free Trade Agreement entered into force on 15 July 2026, timed with the Bangalore expansion
    • The European Accessibility Act drives Storm Interface's audio-guidance demand.
  • Defence: procurement adds order-backed demand, but bookings run ahead of invoicing so quarters are lumpy
    • This applies to DiscoverIE only in proportion to its 9 percent security share (FY24) plus Trival and 3Gmetalworx.
  • Energy: below 1 percent of revenue, so carbon and energy regulation is immaterial.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because supply conditions have eased and pass-through works, with a three-to-six-month lag that costs margin after a metal spike.
  • Availability: supplier lead times eased across steel and standard components with pockets of tightness in specialist power components (expert calls, 6 October 2026)

  • Metals: copper above $14,000 a tonne in May 2026 and steel up 36.7 percent year on year (sector evidence from AQ Group, not this company's) are passed through with a lag of a quarter or two
    • DiscoverIE prices custom products at the material cost of the time, and gross margin reached a record of about 40.5 percent in FY26, up 0.5 points.
  • Not B, because the Magnetics and Controls division carries copper and aluminium exposure and the lag is real.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity in the product niches is stable and benign while intensity in the market for acquisition targets is tightening, and the two offset.
  • Product niches: components are 3 to 5 percent of the customer's system cost, switching needs redesign and re-certification, and the company rarely trades on price.
  • Acquisition market: Hubbell agreed to buy NSI Industries for $3bn in cash (Deutsche Bank, 4 May 2026), and Deutsche Bank reads rising competition for quality assets as a threat to a model that buys at 10 times EBIT or less
    • The 3Gmetalworx price was 11 to 12 times EBITA (Jefferies, 19 May 2026).
  • Expert read-across: post-pandemic multi-sourcing by OEMs and tightly matched pricing once a part is approved come from utility hardware (a former MacLean Power sales manager, 23 January 2026; a Sediver manager, 25 May 2026), not this company's markets
    • We weight them lightly.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
