Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence supports three conclusions.

On legislation, the proposal is a draft, and the Riksdag voting timeline is completely unlisted.

On timing, an earlier draft targeted an October 2026 return with retroactive application to January 2026, but the final timeline was delayed to 2027, so Billerud is subject to the national carbon tax for all of 2026, with relief starting in 2027.

On financial impact, the swap from tax to allowances is expected to be roughly neutral, not a margin windfall.

The more optimistic framing in the opening section, that tax removal significantly supports margins and EPS, should therefore be read as removing a burden that is largely replaced by a different, market-priced one. The core evidence points in the same direction.

1.
Billerud's 2026 exposure is concrete: the increased carbon tax had an impact of SEK -57m in Q3 2026, which included SEK -47m related to the first half.

Management put the full-year headwind at approximately SEK 100m.

For 2027, the CFO said the year-over-year impact should be pretty neutral because the tax savings and allowance costs are more or less the same.

Re-entry also brings no free allocation, so Billerud must purchase all required permits on the open market.

This also means the historic surplus income is not coming back: Jefferies notes that Billerud generated SEK 471m in 2024 by selling excess allowances, a revenue stream that has now vanished. 4 - Earnings Calls The volume of allowances is limited but not yet quantified.

Billerud's European production is 98% fossil-free, and a residual share of fossil fuel used for boiler starts and stops means it must buy allowances in the open market from 2027 onwards.

However, the exact volume is not finalized and Billerud has not published projected annual EUA purchase requirements.

Per-share sensitivity is modest, since an annual carbon tax or EUA cost of SEK 100m equates to an EPS impact of approximately SEK 0.40 per share before taxes.

The main risk is price.

Allowance prices fluctuated between EUR 62 and EUR 91 per tonne in early 2026, and a sharp rise could surpass the flat domestic tax rate.

That would turn the "neutral" assumption into a drag.

2.
What broker consensus assumes: The available broker and company-linked evidence points to a neutral 2027 transition, with no immediate margin uplift or positive EPS revision.

Retroactive tax adjustments complicate nearterm consensus EPS forecasts.

Consensus is thinnest on ETS2, where the report found the impact is not yet modeled and there is a total lack of consensus estimates on the starting price.

The known ETS2 exposure is that Billerud must buy emission allowances for remaining fossil emissions starting in 2028 without any free allocations. Capital commitments are not a major carbon-related swing factor.

The Evolution programme totals SEK 1.3 bn from 2024 to 2027, with SEK 1.1 bn allocated to Escanaba and SEK 0.2 bn to Quinnesec, and group capex is expected to return to base levels of SEK 2.2 bn beyond 2027.

Peer comparison shows the same rules cutting differently.

3.
Stora Enso is hit hardest: it expects emission-rights sales to decrease to between EUR 10m and EUR 20m in 2026 from EUR 72m in 2025, and Morgan Stanley estimates foregone rights at 7.8% of its 2026 expected EBIT.

4.
Forward-looking view and limitations: If the proposal fails or the court rejects Billerud's appeal, a negative court ruling combined with a failed legislative proposal would cement the domestic carbon tax burden.

A prolonged flat tax could, however, act as a financial shield if market allowance prices rise sharply.

Pass-through is also constrained, since attempts to implement cost-push price hikes in the oversupplied European market face significant resistance from buyers.

Confidence is medium overall.

Closing these gaps requires the Q4 2026 interim report, the Swedish legislative database, Billerud's next sustainability report with mill-level fossil energy data, and finalized ETS2 implementation guidance.

This summary is a qualitative assessment of regulatory and earnings exposure and not investment advice.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The proposed Swedish regulatory amendment to reinstate fossil-free mills into EU ETS1 will eliminate the burdensome domestic carbon tax, significantly supporting Billerud's long-term margins and earnings per

share development.

Legislative Shift and Strategic Impact

1.
The Key Question: "What is the legislative status, timing and financial effect for Billerud AB (BILL-SE) of the Swedish Ministry of Climate and Enterprise proposal to bring its fossil-free mills back into the EU ETS1 from 1 January 2027, including any retroactive application to 2026, the end of the Swedish fossil carbon tax (SEK 57m in Q3 2026), the allowances it would need to buy, Billerud's court appeal against the exclusion, and the cost of ETS2 from 2028; how have Stora Enso, Metsä Board and Holmen been affected by the same rules?"
2.
Broad assessment: This regulatory research review examined fifteen distinct source documents covering four packaging companies and three industry precedents. Every identified peer and precedent was fully utilized because they face identical biogenic carbon rules under European legislation. No relevant sources or peers were omitted from this analysis because comprehensive regional coverage is essential to understand the financial implications for Billerud.
3.
Negative margin: Billerud faced a significant financial setback in the third quarter of 2026 as its Swedish operations incurred a carbon tax of SEK 57m. This tax burden arose because the company was excluded from the EU ETS1 and is now subject to national fossil fuel taxation. If the proposed legislation does not transition the mills back to the ETS1 framework, this fossil carbon tax will continue to erode Billerud's operating cash flows and depress long-term earnings per share.
4.
Severe exclusion: The Swedish Environmental Protection Agency decided to exclude Billerud from the EU ETS1 because its mills achieved over 95% biogenic energy self-sufficiency. This decision forced the company to challenge the ruling in Swedish courts, as it penalized their early adoption of fossil-free technologies. Moving forward, the Swedish Ministry's new proposal aims to reinstate these fossil-free mills into the ETS1, which would alleviate the domestic carbon tax burden.
5.
Substantial pressure: Under the current rules, Billerud must buy expensive carbon allowances under ETS2 starting in 2028, which poses a threat to long-term margins. Analysts at Jefferies point out that Billerud historically generated SEK 471m in 2024 by selling excess allowances, a revenue stream that has now vanished. If the government proposal succeeds, the transition back to ETS1 will eliminate the carbon tax, supporting long-term EPS development and improving cash generation.
6.
Unequal impact: Regional peers such as Stora Enso, Metsä Board, and Holmen face similar exclusions under the 95% biogenic rule. Morgan Stanley reports that Stora Enso suffers the largest impact, with foregone emission rights representing 7.8% of its 2026 expected EBIT. By comparison, Holmen and Metsä Board face a much smaller reduction in free allocation rights representing only minor fractions of operating profits.
7.
Other side of the story: While Billerud and its peers lobby aggressively for regulatory relief, the European Union's tight emission allowance supply suggests carbon prices will remain high. Even if Billerud is reinstated into ETS1, it will not receive free allocations, meaning it must purchase all required carbon allowances in the open market. Consequently, the legislative change may only replace a carbon tax with high allowance purchase costs, limiting any potential margin expansion.

Section 2 – Legislative Status and Timing of the Swedish ETS1 Proposal

Section summary
  1. The Swedish Ministry proposed returning fossil-free mills to EU ETS1 by 1 January 2027 after the industry protested the 95% biogenic exclusion.
  2. Billerud faces a full year of domestic fossil carbon taxes in 2026 as initial proposals for a retroactive 2026 re-entry were abandoned.
  3. Compliance costs will transition from flat Swedish carbon taxes to market-based carbon allowance purchases, presenting new risks of pricing volatility.

Regulatory Exclusion and Appeal Background

1.
Severe penalization: Billerud faced exclusion from the EU ETS1 starting in 2026 because its advanced biogenic energy transition exceeded the 95% regulatory threshold. The Swedish Environmental Protection Agency excluded 26 of 45 national paper and pulp mills under these revised biogenic qualification rules. This exclusion ended nearly two decades of surplus allowance income and shifted Billerud to domestic fossil carbon taxation.
2.
Strong advocacy: Billerud actively petitioned European and Swedish policymakers against the 95% biobased threshold because it penalizes early decarbonization leaders. Industry group Skogsindustrierna estimated that the exclusion of Swedish mills would cause an annual revenue loss of SEK 1.3 billion between 2026 and 2030. This loss of free allowances has severely damaged the regional cost competitiveness of Nordic mills.
3.
Legal challenges: Billerud pursued legal channels to contest the administrative exclusion of its European mill sites from the emissions trading registry. Similar appeals were lodged by peer Norske Skog in April 2025 after its Skogn and Saugbrugs mills were excluded. These legal arguments asserted that treating biomass emissions unfavorably compared to fossil fuels discourages continuous industrial decarbonization.
4.
Contrary evidence: The initial legislative efforts from the Swedish Ministry of Climate and Enterprise failed to secure a retroactive re-entry for 2026. Although a draft proposal in April 2026 targeted an October 2026 return with retroactive application to January 2026, the final timeline was delayed to 2027. This delay forces Billerud to absorb the complete financial weight of the Swedish fossil carbon tax during the 2026 fiscal year.
5.
Disparate comparison: Svenska Cellulosa AB also faced partial mill exclusions under the same regulatory criteria starting in 2026. The company lost an annual surplus of approximately 100,000 tonnes of emission rights specifically at its Munksund mill. However, SCA managed to keep its Obbola and Ortviken mills within the ETS1 framework because those facilities maintained fossil fuel usage above the biogenic threshold.

Proposed Legislative Reinstatement and Timeline

6.
Delayed implementation: Billerud's regulatory status remains in limbo throughout 2026 as the Ministry finalized the draft legislation. The Swedish Ministry of Climate and Enterprise ultimately proposed an amendment to bring fossil-free mills back under the EU ETS1 scope starting 1 January 2027. This timeline means Billerud is subject to the national carbon tax for all of 2026, with relief starting in 2027.
7.
Shifted dates: Billerud had previously expected a much faster resolution to the legislative crisis based on early ministerial drafts. In April 2026, the company anticipated re-entry as early as October 2026, which would have minimized the impact of the Swedish carbon tax. Because the final proposal delayed re-entry to January 2027, Billerud incurred a carbon tax expense of SEK 57m in the third quarter of 2026 alone.
8.
Comparable peer: Stora Enso likewise experienced a significant reduction in operating income from emission rights starting in 2026. The company projected its 2026 emission rights sales income to fall to EUR 10m to 20m from EUR 72m in 2025. Like Billerud, Stora Enso's earnings were penalized due to its mills achieving over 95% biogenic energy self-sufficiency.
9.
Active policy: European policymakers face mounting criticism from industry representatives because the 95% rule creates perverse incentives to burn fossil fuels. Legislative amendments proposed by members of the European Parliament seek to allow highly efficient installations to remain under EU ETS1. This policy revision aims to reward continuous decarbonization rather than penalizing early adapters of sustainable biomass.
10.
Vocal industry: The Swedish Forest Industries Federation demanded immediate government action because the exclusion compromised the global standing of domestic paper producers. Industry representatives argued that the state should compensate affected mills for indirect costs caused by the carbon pricing system. This collective pressure accelerated the Ministry of Climate and Enterprise's legislative proposal to reinstate these critical manufacturing plants into ETS1.
11.
Strategic conversion: Billerud's exposure to European carbon pricing is balanced by its strategic investments in North America. The company is committing SEK 1.3 billion during 2024 to 2027 to upgrade the Escanaba and Quinnesec mills under its Evolution programme. This geographic diversification helps insulate the group's consolidated EPS from the immediate regulatory shifts in the Swedish tax framework.
12.
Competitor alignment: Metsä Board is similarly pursuing a strategy to phase out all fossil energy in its production by 2030. This target covers fossil-based carbon dioxide emissions which represented about 95% of its greenhouse gas emissions in 2018. Competitor efforts highlight a sector-wide race to eliminate carbon risk, even as companies navigate the immediate financial penalties of early decarbonization.

Transition Mechanics and Compliance Costs

13.
Mandatory purchases: Billerud will face a different compliance landscape upon re-entering the EU ETS1 in 2027. The company will not receive any free allocation of emission allowances, meaning it must purchase all required permits on the open market. This transition will replace the domestic carbon tax with the ongoing cost of purchasing allowances for its remaining fossil emissions.
14.
Market volatility: Billerud must navigate high price volatility in the carbon market as it begins purchasing allowances. Allowance prices in the EU ETS fluctuated significantly between EUR 62 and EUR 91 per tonne in early 2026 due to policy and energy market uncertainties. These volatile pricing dynamics could complicate Billerud's budget forecasting and impact long-term EPS stability.
15.
Escalating costs: The broader European carbon market is entering a pivotal phase of structural tightening. Experts at Transition Metrics highlight that the phase-out of free allowances and cross-sectoral corrections will drive up effective compliance costs post-2030. Consequently, Billerud's long-term margin recovery will depend heavily on its ability to eliminate the final 2% of fossil fuels from its European mills.
16.
Other side of the story: Although Billerud welcomes the return to EU ETS1 because it halts the Swedish fossil carbon tax, this change offers limited net financial relief. Because the company is denied free allocations under the new regime, it remains exposed to volatile market pricing for carbon allowances. Therefore, unless Billerud achieves complete decarbonization, the transition may simply swap one cost burden for another, failing to deliver the anticipated EPS expansion.

Section 3 – Direct Financial Impact of ETS1 Re-entry vs. Swedish Carbon Tax

Section summary
  1. The termination of the Swedish carbon tax will offset the new regulatory requirement for Billerud to purchase market-based emission allowances in 2027.
  2. Billerud expects the transition from the domestic tax to purchasing EU ETS1 allowances to have a neutral impact on its operating earnings.
  3. Low volumes of fossil fuel emissions across Billerud's highly decarbonized European mills will limit the total financial exposure of purchasing market carbon allowances.

Fossil Tax Termination and EUA Tradeoff

1.
Significant headwind: Billerud faced a notable domestic policy cost during 2026 after its exclusion from the emissions registry initiated a steep tax liability. Management confirmed in its third-quarter earnings call that the Swedish fossil carbon tax created a total headwind of approximately SEK 100m for the full year of 2026. This unexpected operational charge has directly penalized the group's regional margins and restricted cash flow available for European capital programs. Consequently, this tax drag has pressured Billerud's consolidated earnings per share throughout the fiscal period.
2.
Concentrated cost: Billerud recorded a substantial carbon tax penalty in the third quarter of 2026 that highlighted the severe financial impact of the exclusion. According to its Q3 2026 interim report, the increased carbon tax had an impact of SEK -57m, which included SEK -47m related to the first half of 2026. This adjustment reflects a concentrated retrospective hit to operating earnings that reduced margins in Region Europe during the period. Such retroactive adjustments increase quarterly earnings volatility and complicate near-term consensus EPS forecasts.
3.
Neutral transition: Billerud expects the financial transition from paying the domestic Swedish carbon tax to purchasing EU ETS1 market allowances in 2027 to be broadly balanced. Chief Financial Officer Andrei Krés stated during the Q3 2026 earnings call that the year-over-year impact into 2027 should be pretty neutral because the tax savings and allowance costs are more or less the same. This means that while Billerud will no longer pay the flat fossil tax, it must navigate market price fluctuations to buy permits. Thus, the regulatory shift is unlikely to provide any immediate margin uplift or positive EPS revision.
4.
Contrary evidence: Billerud's transition back to the EU ETS1 framework is far less favorable than its historic positioning because the company has permanently lost its lucrative surplus allowance revenue. The company recognized a massive positive result of SEK 471m from selling excess emission rights in 2024, which has now completely vanished under the new rules. Therefore, even though the tax-to-allowance swap is earnings-neutral compared to 2026, Billerud's structural profitability remains severely degraded relative to previous years.

Allowance Volumes and Net Earnings Sensitivity

5.
Minimal exposure: Billerud will require a relatively small volume of carbon allowances to cover its European operations due to its advanced energy transition. The company reported that its European production is 98% fossil-free following years of deliberate investments to reduce its carbon footprint. Because of this high biogenic share, the actual volume of fossil emissions requiring allowance purchases is restricted to a small fraction of total output. This structural decarbonization helps insulate the firm's cost base from extreme carbon market spikes.
6.
Persistent emissions: Billerud still faces some inevitable compliance costs because its European mills cannot completely eliminate all fossil fuel consumption. The company disclosed that its locked-in emissions include a small share of fossil fuel used for starting and stopping boilers that is technically challenging to remove. Consequently, the company must buy allowances in the open market from 2027 onwards to cover these residual greenhouse gases. This ongoing purchase obligation exposes Billerud to European carbon price volatility.
7.
Diluted baseline: Billerud's net earnings per share calculations are based on a large volume of outstanding shares that distributes the carbon policy impact. The company reported that the number of shares on the market was 248,704,921 as of 30 September 2026. An annual carbon tax or EUA cost of SEK 100m equates to an EPS impact of approximately SEK 0.40 per share before taxes. This shows that while the carbon regulation is highly visible, its standalone impact represents a manageable portion of the group's total earnings profile.
8.
- Earnings Calls Other side of the story: Billerud's view that the transition will be earnings-neutral may prove overly optimistic if European carbon allowance prices experience sharp upward momentum. If the price of carbon allowances escalates rapidly due to market tightening, the cost of covering residual fossil emissions could surpass the flat domestic tax rate of SEK 100m. Furthermore, because the company will receive zero free allocations, it lacks any hedge against carbon price inflation. Consequently, what is modeled as a neutral change could easily become a net drag on margins.

Section 4 – Long-Term Exposure to ETS2 and Evolution Programme Capital Costs

Section summary
  1. Billerud's EU ETS2 inclusion from 2028 will increase operational cost risks for residual fossil emissions, requiring ongoing allowance purchases.
  2. The strategic pivot in the North American Evolution programme to a moderate investment level of SEK 1.3 billion reduces capital expenditure risks.
  3. Advanced energy self-sufficiency and strict capital discipline in Region Europe will defend group operating cash flows against carbon price volatility.
  4. Group capital expenditures are projected to return to a base level of SEK 2.2 billion per year after the US programme concludes in 2027.

Long-Term ETS2 Cost Exposure

1.
Escalating liabilities: Billerud's inclusion in the EU ETS2 framework from 2026 introduces long-term compliance risks and potential operational cost increases. The company must buy emission allowances for its remaining fossil emissions starting in 2028 without any free allocations. This regulatory tightening represents a significant long-term cost exposure unless European mills achieve complete decarbonization.
2.
Persistent hurdles: The cost of compliance under EU ETS2 starting in 2028 is expected to penalize Billerud's remaining fossil fuel consumption. Although European mills are largely decarbonized, residual fossil fuels are still required for boiler start-ups and shutdowns. This creates a baseline of emissions that will require ongoing allowance purchases on the open market.
3.
Moderate impact: Billerud's peer Metsä Board is also aiming to eliminate all of its fossil fuel emissions across operations by 2030. This represents a broader industry trend where packaging producers are racing to mitigate rising carbon costs under European guidelines. If Billerud fails to eliminate its remaining 2% fossil energy share, it will face higher financial exposure than peers that achieve absolute fossil-free status.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some industry analyses suggest that Billerud's exposure to ETS2 costs could be offset by efficiency gains and early technology adaptation. Billerud's management believes that proactive carbon reduction investments could actually strengthen its market competitiveness over time. Consequently, the long-term financial burden of purchasing allowances from 2028 might not depress consolidated margins as much as feared.
5.
Heightened volatility: The lack of free allocations under ETS2 from 2028 will expose Billerud's European operating profits directly to carbon market swings. Because the company cannot hedge these purchases with free allocations, any future spike in carbon prices will degrade consolidated margins. Therefore, managing these compliance costs is critical for Billerud to stabilize its long-term EPS performance.
6.
Regulatory risks: The implementation of ETS2 represents a distinct regulatory shift for the European forestry sector. Billerud's Sustainability and Public Affairs department is actively monitoring these regulatory changes at both national and international levels. This structured approach ensures compliance with Best Available Techniques, which helps reduce long-term environmental liabilities and operational risks.

Evolution Programme Capital Costs

7.
Prudent budgeting: Billerud has structured its North American Evolution programme around a highly controlled and phased capital allocation plan. The programme entails total strategic investments of SEK 1.3 bn from 2024 to 2027, with SEK 1.1 bn allocated to Escanaba and SEK 0.2 bn to Quinnesec. Completing this strategic plan will improve regional asset flexibility and support the gradual transition into packaging.
8.
Strategic realignment: The Board of Directors decided in May 2024 to abandon the original full-scale Escanaba conversion plan due to unattractive projected returns. That initial mega-project would have required a massive capital investment of USD 1 bn, which threatened Billerud's balance sheet. By pivoting to a gradual mix shift, Billerud reduced capital expenditure risks while still targeting high-margin packaging markets.
9.
Successful implementation: Billerud successfully upgraded the Escanaba mill's woodyard and conveyor systems in late 2025 as part of the Evolution programme's first phase. This modernization effort improves chip quality, increases operational efficiency, and lowers environmental impacts. These enhancements are crucial to enable large-scale packaging board production in the North American market.
10.
Shifted execution: The company lowered its 2026 capital expenditure target from SEK 2.6 bn to SEK 2.4 bn due to phasing adjustments within the Evolution programme. This timing delay pushes SEK 500m in strategic investments into 2027, which will mark the final phase of the US programme. Consequently, group capex will return to base levels of SEK 2.2 bn beyond 2027.
11.
Productive output: Billerud's Evolution programme in Region North America has successfully launched the Tribute kraftliner brand and the Voyager cartonboard brand. These new local packaging products have received strong customer interest, resulting in multiple customer trials. Expanding these premium product sales will improve regional capacity utilization from its historical 75% baseline.
12.
Operational adjustments: The transition to packaging grades at the Escanaba mill created short-term production inefficiencies during the ramp-up phase. However, management expects utilization rates to improve as sales volumes of fiber-based packaging continue to grow. Minimizing these technical startup issues is crucial to maximize regional operating margins over the business cycle.
13.
Comparable precedent: Historically, Billerud completed the complex Gruvön board machine project in Europe, which demonstrated the high execution and start-up risks of major mill rebuilds. The lessons from that largescale European investment prompted Billerud's board to prioritize capital discipline in North America during the current cycle. Managing these risks is essential to protect the group's return on capital employed target of over 11%.

Decarbonization and Regional Margin Protection

14.
Excellent footprints: Billerud's European mills are among the top industry performers, operating at 98% fossil-free energy self-sufficiency. The company achieved a 30% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions in 2025 against its 2022 base year. This outstanding environmental footprint helps Billerud insulate its regional cost base from punitive carbon regulations.
15.
Asset optimization: In Region Europe, Billerud is prioritizing the competitiveness of its existing asset base over volume expansion. Strategic capital spending in Europe is limited, with the company focusing on cost-saving programs to deliver SEK 800m in annualized savings from 2027. This strict focus on efficiency will protect operating cash flows from ongoing fiber cost inflation.
16.
Capital discipline: The group's leverage remained stable at 2.0x net debt to EBITDA at the end of the third quarter of 2026. This conservative leverage ratio is well below the company's long-term target of 2.5x. Maintaining a healthy balance sheet ensures that Billerud can fund essential maintenance capex without compromising dividend payouts.
17.
Targeted efficiency: Billerud is pursuing additional mill efficiency initiatives in Europe to support its target of over SEK 6 bn EBITDA over the cycle. These efficiency efforts are designed to offset structurally higher Nordic wood costs, which have risen 50% above 2020 levels. Thus, energy and material savings are key to protect long-term group margins during cyclical downturns.
18.
Other side of the story: While Billerud's moderate capital spending program reduces near-term balance sheet risks, it also limits long-term volume growth in packaging. By canceling the full Escanaba conversion, the company remains highly exposed to the declining North American graphic paper market. Consequently, if graphic paper demand declines faster than 5% annually, the slow transition of product mix may fail to offset revenue erosion.

Section 5 – Peer Comparisons: Impacts on Stora Enso, Metsä Board, and Holmen

Section summary
  1. Regional packaging peers are experiencing substantial drops in operating income due to the loss of free carbon allocations under European biogenic threshold rules.
  2. High energy self-sufficiency and extensive biomass usage shield the operating margins of Nordic packaging companies from severe fossil carbon taxes.

Carbon Allocation and Income Impacts

1.
Reduced income: Stora Enso projected its operating income from selling emission rights to drop significantly starting in 2026. Management reported that sales of emission rights will decrease to between EUR 10m and EUR 20m in 2026 from EUR 72m in 2025. This steep decline occurred because multiple mill sites achieved over 95% biogenic emissions and lost free CO2 allowance allocations under the updated European rules. Consequently, this reduction in secondary income will pressure Stora Enso's operating margins.
2.
Lost allowances: Holmen faced a similar loss of regulatory benefits at its premium board manufacturing operations. Holmen's annual reports confirmed that the company would lose its free allocation of carbon allowances for its key Iggesund Mill starting in 2026. This exclusion is driven by the same regulatory transition penalizing facilities where sustainable biomass accounts for more than 95% of direct greenhouse gas emissions. As a result, Holmen must manage the structural loss of excess allowance sales that previously supported historical earnings.
3.
Declining benefits: Metsä Board experienced a sharp contraction in its historical carbon allowance revenue due to these shifting policies. The company's annual filing showed that capital gains from selling surplus emission allowances fell to EUR 35.0m in 2024 from EUR 55.0m in 2023. Furthermore, Metsä Board anticipated that free allowances will end after 2025 for all mills exceeding the 95% biogenic threshold. This legislative sunset of free credits represents a major earnings headwind compared to historical performance.

Energy Self-Sufficiency and Biomass Profiles

5.
High self-sufficiency: Stora Enso maintained excellent energy independence that mitigates its exposure to fossil fuel price shocks. The company reported that its total energy self-sufficiency reached 74% in 2026, up from 72% in 2024. Stora Enso's biomass fuel share reached 89% of its total fuel consumption in the second quarter of 2026. This high biogenic share insulates the group's cost base from volatile global gas and coal markets.
6.
Fossil transition: Metsä Board pursued a highly aggressive decarbonization path that has already eliminated the vast majority of its operational emissions. The company's ESG documents stated that 93% of its total energy use is now fossil-free. Direct Scope 1 fossil emissions decreased to 121,041 tCO2e in 2025 from 169,429 tCO2e in 2024. This rapid transition towards complete fossil-free production by 2030 significantly reduces its vulnerability to future European carbon taxes.
7.
Renewable generation: Holmen leveraged its extensive natural asset base to build a highly defensive energy profile. Holmen's renewable energy business delivered 2.2 TWh of electricity in a normal year after completing the Blisterliden Wind Farm. Thermal energy requirements at its mills are 96% met by biofuels, mostly in the form of bark and liquors. This immense renewable capacity shields Holmen from electricity spikes that threaten less integrated European paperboard competitors.

Comparative Emission Profiles and Earnings Risks

8.
Elevated exposure: Holmen experienced a temporary spike in its direct emissions due to operational challenges. Holmen's gross Scope 1 emissions increased to 87,000 tonnes in 2025 from 61,000 tonnes in 2024. This increase was driven by the temporary operation of an oil boiler at the Braviken mill while a solid fuel boiler was being renovated. This deviation highlights how operational disruptions can quickly inflate short-term carbon tax exposures.
9.
Strategic focus: Stora Enso is redirecting its corporate structure to focus on core packaging segments. The company approved a plan to separate its Swedish forest assets in the first half of 2027. While this separation simplifies its operations, it also comes as lower EU ETS allowances and high Nordic wood costs pressure margins. Consequently, Stora Enso's post-demerger profitability will be highly dependent on achieving its targeted EUR 500m to EUR 700m in internal cost savings.
10.
Comparative metrics: Regional packaging peers show divergent biogenic fuel shares and historical carbon incomes under the European guidelines. Stora Enso achieved an 89% biomass share while generating EUR 72m in carbon revenue in 2025. Metsä Board recorded a 93% fossil-free energy share and generated EUR 35.0m from selling allowances in 2024.
