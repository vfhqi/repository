Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. VERBUND AG has committed to an intensive capital expenditure program exceeding €6.7 bn from 2026 to 2028 to fund grid modernization and renewable growth.
  2. The utility aims to expand its transmission grid and reach 4.8 GW of wind and solar capacity by 2030 while managing construction and operational risks.

Defining the Funding Challenge and Financial Risks

1.
Crucial inquiry: "How will VERBUND AG (VER-AT) fund Austrian Power Grid's investment plan of about \u20ac9bn to 2034 to 2035, its 4.8 GW wind and solar target for 2030 and its pumped-storage projects while keeping an Arange rating and net debt below 3.0 times EBITDA; what do rating agencies' published thresholds and commentary on Verbund say, and what precedents (RWE and Apollo at Amprion, Elia, TenneT, E.ON, hybrid bonds) show for funding grid growth without new equity?"
2.
Significant pressure: VERBUND AG faces potential earnings per share erosion over the next 18 months to 3 years if it cannot efficiently fund its capital expenditure without issuing highly dilutive common equity. Bernstein Research reported on November 6, 2026, that the utility's capital expenditure is expected to expand, causing net debt to rise from €2.81 bn in 2025 to €5.55 bn by 2028. This rising debt load and associated interest expenses could severely drag on recurring earnings per share, which Bernstein forecasts to drop from €4.24 in 2025 to €3.04 in 2026, before partially recovering.
3.
Stricter thresholds: Credit rating agencies place tight boundaries on the group's balance sheet that limit its financial room for maneuver. Morgan Stanley reported on November 5, 2026, that VERBUND AG must maintain its net debt below 3x EBITDA to preserve its solid A-range rating, which is currently supported by its very low leverage of approximately 1x net debt to EBITDA. If capital expenditures push leverage past this threshold, a subsequent rating downgrade would escalate borrowing costs and further damage earnings per share.
4.
Volatile hydrology: The primary risk to VERBUND AG's internal funding capacity remains its extreme exposure to volatile river flows. Deutsche Bank Research noted on November 5, 2026, that unprecedented dry conditions in late 2026 led to a weak hydro coefficient of 0.61, forcing the company to spend €120 mm on power buybacks. These recurring cash drains reduce the operating cash flow available to fund Austrian Power Grid's expansions, magnifying the company's reliance on external debt markets.
5.
Contrary evidence: Regulated grid investments could actually reduce VERBUND AG's overall risk profile and stabilize its highly volatile merchant earnings over the longer term. Bernstein Research highlighted on June 1, 2026, that expanding Austrian Power Grid's regulatory asset base will increase the regulated share of group EBITDA from 14% in 2025 to 22% by 2030. This shift will progressively shield the utility from volatile wholesale electricity prices, meaning that the massive grid expansion plan represents a strategic risk-mitigation tool rather than a pure financial burden.
6.
Related disputes: The ultimate funding outcome is also closely tied to legal and regulatory developments surrounding state-imposed levies. Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 5, 2026, that VERBUND AG is currently contesting the validity of the Austrian generation profit levy in the Constitutional Court, with a final ruling expected by the end of 2026. If the court strikes down the levy, it would immediately unlock substantial cash reserves to support the capital program, directly impacting future earnings per share.
7.
Other side of the story: While rating agencies impose tight leverage thresholds, VERBUND AG's exceptionally under-leveraged balance sheet and strong sovereign backing from the Republic of Austria provide an elite credit profile that may withstand temporary investment spikes. Morgan Stanley highlighted on December 3, 2025, that the utility's low net debt to EBITDA ratio of approximately 1x reflects an under-leveraged position that easily supports its rating. This substantial headroom suggests that fears of imminent rating downgrades or dilutive equity issuances may be overstated, as the company possesses ample capacity to absorb incremental debt without crossing critical rating thresholds.

Section 4 – Non-Equity Funding Precedents for Grid Growth

Structured Equity Partnerships at Amprion

1.
Structured partnership: RWE AG successfully co-funded its massive electricity transmission grid expansion using a structured partnership with private equity firm Apollo Global Management. The German utility transferred its 25.1% stake in Amprion to an intermediate holding company in November 2025 in exchange for 3.2 bn euros of funding from Apollo. RWE used this structure to co-finance capital increases without issuing new common shares, supporting earnings per share preservation during high growth cycles.
2.
Credit positive: Amprion benefited from rating agency equity treatment on the Apollo joint venture transaction, which strengthened the parent company's balance sheet. Bernstein Research reported on June 19, 2026, that the 3.2 bn euro proceeds were treated as equity by rating agencies, temporarily reducing net debt. This structure provided a non-dilutive, balance sheet-efficient financing solution while allowing RWE to maintain strategic operational control of the transmission asset.
3.
Capped options: RWE AG protected its long-term financial upside by structuring the Apollo joint venture with a built-in repurchase option. RWE retains the right to repurchase Apollo's stake at a pre-determined return, allowing the utility to monetize the full asset value later when the regulated asset base expands. This feature ensures that temporary private capital does not dilute long-term earnings per share, unless future regulated returns fall below the agreed private equity hurdle.
4.
Contrary evidence: RWE AG unexpectedly reversed its grid funding strategy in June 2026 by re-acquiring an indirect stake in Amprion and funding it via public equity markets. Morgan Stanley reported on June 23, 2026, that RWE launched a 4 bn euro accelerated equity raise to finance the acquisition of a 35% stake in Amprion. This sudden pivot suggests that private infrastructure partnerships may only act as temporary funding bridges, rather than permanent capital solutions for long-term grid growth.

Grid Funding Models at Elia and TenneT

5.
Strategic partnerships: Elia Group SA/NV relies on diverse partnerships with major institutional investors to fund its international transmission grid expansions. Elia Group expanded its relationship with Canada Pension Plan Investment Board in July 2026 to co-develop the Tarchon interconnector project. By utilizing a 25% minority equity participation, Elia limits its required capital commitment while maintaining structural flexibility.
6.
Separated funding: TenneT Holding B.V. successfully restructured its capital framework by separating its Dutch and German transmission operations into two independently financed corporate entities. Bernstein Research reported on April 22, 2025, that this structural shift was designed to prevent structural subordination of senior debt holders. This separation allowed TenneT Germany to establish its own independent capital structure, proving that sovereign-backed utilities can isolate national grid risks to optimize debt pricing.
7.
Private placements: TenneT Germany secured massive non-equity capital by bringing large pension and sovereign wealth funds directly into its subsidiary. TenneT Holding B.V. announced in September 2025 that GIC, Norges Bank, and ABP agreed to invest up to 9.5 bn euros of equity directly into TenneT Germany by 2029. This model demonstrates how a state-owned utility can attract private infrastructure capital at the subsidiary level to fund grid expansion without diluting the parent company's public owners.
8.
Sovereign backing: TenneT Germany further strengthened its capital base by securing direct equity investment from state-backed financial institutions. Kreditanstalt fuer Wiederaufbau completed a transaction to acquire a 25.1% equity stake in TenneT Germany on behalf of the German state. This dual model of private institutional equity and state participation reduces the parent's financial burden, helping to preserve stable credit metrics.

Hybrid Debt and E.ON Funding Efficiency

9.
Diversified financing: E.ON SE continues to rely heavily on capital market diversification and green financing to support its extensive network capital expenditure. E.ON SE confirmed in November 2026 that it targets annual debt issuances of 3.5 bn to 5 bn euros, using taxonomy-aligned green bonds for over 50% of its requirements. By utilizing green Schuldschein and private placements, E.ON funds its grids efficiently, ensuring that earnings per share are insulated from high financing costs.
10.
Equity treatment: Hybrid bonds represent a powerful financial instrument for electricity grid operators seeking to bolster credit metrics without issuing dilutive common equity. Amprion issued its inaugural 1.0 bn euro green dual-tranche hybrid bond in April 2026, which rating agencies recognized as 50% equity for leverage calculations. Because hybrid coupon payments run through equity rather than interest expense under IFRS, this tool allows utilities to support ratings while preserving headline EPS.
11.
Legal precedents: VERBUND AG possesses established structural and regulatory precedents that could allow it to execute similar non-equity grid funding strategies. Deutsche Bank Research noted on January 26, 2018, that Verbund could theoretically sell up to 49% of Austrian Power Grid to pension funds and infrastructure investors. Selling a minority stake in Austrian Power Grid would mirror the Amprion and TenneT precedents, unlocking billions in non-dilutive capital to fund the 9 bn euro network expansion.
12.
Other side of the story: While structured equity joint ventures and hybrid bonds offer elegant solutions to avoid common equity dilution, these instruments carry high implicit costs that can ultimately erode earnings per share. Bernstein Research highlighted on June 19, 2026, that RWE was forced to forego a substantial portion of Amprion dividends to Apollo under their joint venture agreement. Additionally, hybrid bonds carry significantly higher coupon rates than senior green debt, meaning that over-reliance on complex financial structuring can create a long-term drag on utility net income.
