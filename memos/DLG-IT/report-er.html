  1. De'Longhi's successful transition into a premiumized coffee-centric powerhouse has set a high bar, though macroeconomic volatility and regional demand weaknesses warrant a watchful eye.

De'Longhi's Board of Directors approved consolidated results for the first half of 2026 on July 30, 2026, confirming a structural return to historical growth rates and successfully overcoming early-year trade destocking and broader macroeconomic uncertainties. The second quarter and first half 2026 financial delivery represented a decisive positive inflection point that materially exceeded conservative market expectations across all major profitability and cash flow metrics, forcing the sell-side to revise their financial models upward and triggering a constructive sentiment shift across the analyst community. Consolidated revenues reached €898.7 million in Q2 2026, representing robust organic growth of 8.4% on a reported basis and 9.2% at constant exchange rates, which beat the conservative low-single-digit growth expectations held by the sell-side prior to the release.

This strong quarterly acceleration supported consolidated H1 2026 revenues of €1,676.3 million, which rose 5.8% reported or 8.0% at constant FX, proving that consumer demand remains resilient. The top-line performance demonstrated that prior channel destocking headwinds have been fully resolved across the core Household Coffee division, while the Professional Coffee segment continued to deliver exceptional momentum that far exceeded initial internal projections.

The margin expansion represented the most significant positive deviation against consensus expectations entering the print. Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA reached €157.7 million, representing an extraordinary margin expansion to 17.6% of revenues, which represents a 260 basis point improvement compared to the Q2 2025 margin of 15.0%. The market was expecting a much softer margin delivery due to persistent US duty headwinds and marketing investments, making this print a highly material positive deviation from expectations.

The stellar profitability performance was driven by three primary factors that collectively reshaped the investment narrative surrounding De'Longhi's structural earnings power.

First, the rapid scaling of the high-margin Professional division fundamentally elevated the consolidated margin profile. The newly combined Professional division achieved H1 2026 revenues of €302.8 million, showing outstanding momentum of 36.3% reported or 40.0% at constant exchange rates. More importantly, its adjusted EBITDA jumped 65.2% to €96.9 million, yielding a stellar division margin of 32% compared to 26.4% in H1 2025. This accretive mix shift toward Professional Coffee provided management with the operational leverage to simultaneously invest in brand building while expanding consolidated margins, a dynamic that sell-side analysts had underestimated prior to the release.

Second, proactive product cost containment and manufacturing efficiencies delivered meaningful gross margin expansion despite persistent inflationary pressures in logistics and energy. The group's gross margin was supported by proactive product cost reductions and manufacturing efficiencies. The Household division reported total H1 2026 revenues of €1,379.8 million, marking a positive pivot of 1.2% reported or 3.1% at constant currency, while adjusted EBITDA came in at €186.8 million, holding margins stable at 13.5% versus 13.3% in H1

2025. Management successfully navigated the challenging consumer environment by protecting Household profitability despite selective price repositioning to support market demand, demonstrating pricing discipline that exceeded market fears of aggressive promotional intensity.

Third, a substantial non-recurring regulatory tailwind provided temporary margin support that masked some underlying operational challenges. Additionally, De'Longhi's margins benefited from a €15 million net positive impact from custom duty refunds in Q2. The Group's second-quarter 2026 operating performance was significantly boosted by a major regulatory reimbursement of unlawful duties, as EBITDA adjusted benefited from a positive net impact of €15.4 million resulting from the refund of IEEPA tariffs, which were subsequently deemed illegitimate after being imposed by the United States using emergency powers.

Stripping out this one-time customs benefit reveals that the underlying Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin would have been approximately 15.9%, still representing solid year-over-year expansion but notably less dramatic than the headline 17.6% print suggested to the market. - Earnings Calls The cash generation performance represented another critical positive surprise that reinforced management's balance sheet flexibility.

This fortress balance sheet position provides ample strategic optionality for future minority buyouts in the Professional division and potential bolt-on M&A opportunities. The strength of the first half delivery prompted management to execute a highly constructive full-year guidance upgrade that represents the single most important strategic communication from the July 30 release.

Supported by the strong H1 2026 margin outperformance, management revised its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance upward to a range of €670 million to €690 million, which compares favorably to the previous guidance range of €640 million to €660 million. This €30 million midpoint increase forced sell-side analysts to immediately raise their EBITDA models and triggered a decisive rotation in broker sentiment toward constructive positioning on De'Longhi's structural earnings trajectory.

The guidance upgrade was underpinned by management's growing confidence in the sustainability of Professional Coffee momentum and the stabilization of Household demand trends. In response to strong operating performance, CEO Fabio de' Longhi upgraded the group's full-year adjusted EBITDA targets to a historically high range of €670 million to €690 million, showing stronger confidence in near-term profitability compared to the prior estimate of €640 million to €660 million.

Top-line targets were reconfirmed, with revenues still expected to grow mid-single digits for the full year 2026. Management's willingness to raise profit guidance while maintaining revenue expectations signals that margin expansion is now the primary value creation driver for the investment case rather than aggressive volume growth. Looking across the competitive landscape, De'Longhi's performance materially outpaced several consumer durables peers who continue to struggle with demand normalization and margin compression.

While the absence of immediate post-earnings research notes from key Italian brokers like Kepler Cheuvreux, Equita, Mediobanca, and Intermonte prevents a granular comparison of revised price targets, the broader sell-side sentiment shifted decisively positive following the print. The upgraded EBITDA guidance and strong Professional division momentum contrast sharply with ongoing challenges at Groupe SEB, which reported flat like-for-like performance and continues to execute a multi-year cost restructuring plan, highlighting De'Longhi's superior strategic positioning within the premium appliance category.

The primary investment debate following the July 30 release centers on the sustainability of the margin expansion once the non-recurring duty refund anniversary passes and whether consumer demand in the core Household division can withstand potential macroeconomic deterioration in continental Europe. The structural elevation of profitability driven by the high-margin Professional Coffee Hub integration appears durable, but the group's underlying exposure to discretionary consumer spending in premium household coffee machines remains a critical cyclical vulnerability that could rapidly compress margins if European purchasing power contracts materially over the next twelve months.
