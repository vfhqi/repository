[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. LSEG is a high-quality compounder whose shares are priced for a fear the numbers are disproving. At 8,200p the market pays 13.9 times earnings two years out, the bottom of the stock's own ten-year range, for the desktop terminal business to be disintermediated by generative AI; the subscription metrics say it is not being, and the other four-fifths of the group compounds earnings at about 12% a year while the question is settled.

  • The change we expect is in the evidence about the business, not in the business.
    • The group already grows organically 7% to 8% a year, expands its EBITDA margin by about 100bps a year and returns its surplus cash.
    • What changes is that the numbers reported in October 2026 and March 2027 either keep disproving the near-term form of the AI fear or do not, and the price already assumes the wrong answer.
    • The fear: Workflows, the desktop business, is about 21% of revenue and, on the bear's estimate, about 30% of EBITDA
      • it grew 2.8% organic in H1 2026 and 2.7% in Q2 2026, the slowest since the Refinitiv acquisition, and Workspace seat growth slowed to 2.9% in Q1 2026 from 3.1% in 2025.
    • The subscription metrics: annual subscription value growth 6.1% in H1 2026 from 5.9% at end-2025
      • retention 92.8% from 92.4%
      • enterprise data agreements 18% of Data & Analytics revenue from 16%
      • Data & Feeds +7.5% organic in H1 2026 and on track to overtake Workflows as the largest revenue line
      • sales cycles not lengthening while S&P Global's are.
    • Machine consumption: real-time network traffic +70% year on year in June 2026
      • 202 institutions on the MCP server in July 2026, from 154 in April and 60 at end-2025
      • revenue from the AI commercial framework "minimal" in 2026.
  • The internal change forces are broad, and they are already in the outputs.
    • Guidance was raised twice in 2026 on both lines
    • engineering was insourced to 77%
    • the SwapClear partner-bank revenue share was cut from 30% to 10% and extended ten years
    • a three-part AI commercial framework was set out in July 2026
    • an activist arrived in February 2026 and a £3bn buyback followed within weeks.
    • None of these is a break with the past, which is why the fit is to a clearing concern and not to 'Major Corporate Change'.
    • What is absent: there is no chief-executive change, David Schwimmer having led since August 2018, and the strategy is a continuation from integration to distribution rather than a new direction.
  • The external force the market fears is a headwind that is weakening, not a tailwind.
    • AI disruption is being disproved in its strongest form, the commoditisation of the data, and confirmed in its weakest, flat terminal seats.
    • Demand is shifting structurally from human screens to machine-readable feeds, which LSEG owns
    • regulation has settled in the company's favour to 2028
    • sterling and bank consolidation remain headwinds.
    • For the disproof: 90% of datasets rest on real-time market data and proprietary intellectual property
      • the aggregators the experts call vulnerable to AI are FactSet and Capital IQ, not LSEG.
    • For the confirmation: a fifth of revenue sits in user-facing terminals, and the shares fell 13% in one day in February 2026 on news of a new AI model.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving on every line the framework names.
    • Organic total-income growth stepped from 7.1% in FY2025 to 8.4% in H1 2026
    • the adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 50.3% to 52.7%, with 120bps of the 260bps pure operating leverage
    • equity free cash flow was £2.4bn in FY2025, 111% of attributable profit, and £1.2bn in H1 2026, +29%
    • £2.6bn was returned to shareholders in the half-year.
    • The sell-side has raised FY2026 EPS by 2% at the results and FY2027 by 9.9% over twelve months.
    • The caveat: the H1 2026 beat was cost-driven (operating expenses £1,882m against consensus £1,919m) while revenue only matched consensus
      • the H2 2026 margin is guided about 50bps lower year on year as the SwapClear benefit laps
      • the revision momentum has flattened to 0.0% over one month and +0.2% over three.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for the cost and margin story, and four for the AI story.
    • For cost and margin it shows in the company's choices (insourcing, the SwapClear restructure), its actions, its operating metrics, its financial statements (H1 2026 EPS +17%) and the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 raised 2%).
    • For the AI story it shows in choices and actions and in one operating metric, the MCP count, and not yet in revenue or estimates.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted EPS of £4.18 in FY2025 rising to £5.92 in FY2028 on our modal path, 12.3% a year, inside the framework's 12% to 20% band at its floor
    • consensus is £6.07, 13.2% a year.
    • The components: revenue growth about 7.0% a year
      • margin from 50.3% to 52.0% adding about 1.1% a year
      • the below-EBITDA lines taking about 0.5% a year away
      • the share count falling about 4% a year adding about 4.2%
      • the product 12.2% a year, reconciled to the claim within rounding.
    • Against consensus: our path is below consensus on revenue (7.0% against 7.7% a year) and level on the FY2028 margin, and above it on the buyback beyond FY2027, netting to FY2028 EPS 2.5% lower
      • the brokers' FY2028 range is £5.86 to £6.41 and our £5.92 sits at its lower end.
  • What it costs on the valuation is nothing yet.
    • The shares trade at 13.88x 24-month-forward consensus earnings on £82.30, and 14.2x on our own path, the 0th percentile of the stock's five-year weekly series (13.88x to 25.73x, median 20.59x) and of its ten-year bands (10th percentile 17.90x, median 21.20x).
    • That is below both exchange peers in the dashboard (Deutsche Börse 19.3x at the 77th percentile of its own history, Euronext 16.3x at the 74th) and below every data peer on the sell-side's table (S&P Global 20.6x, MSCI 25.9x, ICE 17.7x on 2027 earnings), level only with FactSet at 14.4x, the one data peer the experts call more exposed to AI than LSEG.
    • What is priced: the bear's reading, a permanent ceiling on the desktop and a conglomerate discount.
    • What is not priced: any re-rating, the continuation of the buyback beyond February 2027, or any revenue from the AI framework.
      • The price has moved against the park: it fell 8% to 12% in April to May 2026 and is 2.4% lower over twelve months while FY2027 consensus EPS rose 9.9%.
  • The return is bimodal, and the blend sits on the bar at eighteen months and below it at three years.
    • At each horizon we value the shares at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it, the framework's convention
    • the multiple is a blend of a cleared branch at 18x and an uncleared branch at 14x, at odds we put at about even over eighteen months and 55 to 45 over three years.
    • Eighteen months: the earnings the multiple is applied to grow from £5.78 to £6.65 (+15.1%)
      • the multiple moves from 14.2x to 16.0x (+12.8%)
      • dividends add £1.77
      • value £106.40 plus dividends, 131.9% of 8,200p, 20.3% a year, a B on the framework's anchors.
    • Three years: earnings to £7.55, multiple to 16.2x, dividends £5.95
      • 156.4% of the price, 16.1% a year, a C.
    • The branches: the cleared branch alone gives 29.9% a year over eighteen months and 20.0% over three, an A and a B
      • the uncleared branch gives 10.2% and 10.8%, a D at both horizons
      • at our own current multiple with no re-rating, 11.2% and 11.3%.
    • Which carries the return: at eighteen months the earnings growth carries about half (15 points of the 32) and the re-rating the other half (13 points)
      • the cash return is small (2 points).
      • The re-rating is the swing factor.
    • On a one-year-forward footing the same path reads: one-year-forward EPS £5.21 now at 15.7x
      • £6.07 at eighteen months
      • at a constant 15.7x the value is £95.2 and the return 11.8% a year with dividends
      • with the same 1.8-turn re-rating, to 17.5x, £106.1 and 20.1% a year.
      • The convention changes the label on the multiple, not the return, because the growth is steady.
  • The chain holds from the inputs to the sell-side's estimates on cost and margin; it breaks, or rather has not yet been tested, at the revenue line the market is watching.
    • The evidence that the concern is clearing sits at the operating-metric layer (subscription value, retention, enterprise agreements, machine traffic) and not yet at the revenue line, where Workflows still decelerates
    • the hardest evidence, physical seat counts, is undisclosed.
    • The drop-through sensitivity: the earnings path also rests on the ratio that turns EBITDA into EPS.
      • If the FY2026 step in net finance expense (£149m in H1 2026 against £66m) repeats as legacy low-coupon debt refinances, the Tradeweb minority grows faster than the group, the tax rate drifts up and the buyback slows to 3% of the count a year, the below-EBITDA contribution is -2.0% a year instead of -0.5% and the share-count contribution +3.1% instead of +4.2%: EPS growth 9.3% a year, FY2028 EPS £5.46 instead of £5.92, the out-year figures 8% lower.
      • At the same blended multiples the eighteen-month return falls to 14.0% a year and the three-year to 13.1%, both C, and the eighteen-month case clears the bar only below about 7,570p.
      • On that path 'Modal return' would be C, this Building Block C with it, and the recommendation neutral rather than bullish.
      • The earnings leg alone cannot carry the case through a worse drop-through, which is the second reason the re-rating is the swing.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing'.
    • A high-quality business, sold down to the 0th percentile of its own multiple on one identifiable external concern, disruption to the earnings-growth profile of the terminal layer
    • the 'Foundations' are what make the concern transient rather than structural, because the value customers pay for is the proprietary real-time data and the netting pool, not the interface, and the contracts are being rewritten to decouple revenue from seats.
    • The fit is crisp on three of five conditions and passable on two: the clearing evidence is early and indirect, and the concerns are two, not one.

  • The case therefore is a cause-for-concern-clearing case in a high-quality compounder, not a demand inflection and not a turnaround: compelling in its asymmetry and its price, adequate in its blended return, and worth progressing because the question it turns on is one number, Data & Analytics subscription growth, reported on dated occasions, and the price already assumes the wrong answer to it.

  • Rating = B, because the internal change forces are broad and already in the outputs, the modal earnings growth sits inside the target band and the blended eighteen-month return reaches the bar at today's price

    • not A because the return clears the bar only on a re-rating at about even odds and the clearing evidence has not yet reached the revenue line
    • not C, though C was live, because the blended eighteen-month return sits at the bar (20.3% a year), the modal branch sits well above it (about 30%) and the price sits at the bottom of the stock's own history.
    • The return rests on the re-rating for roughly half of itself, which is why the re-rating is the swing factor and why C was live
    • the drop-through sensitivity is the argument for C and it is one refinancing year from being right.
    • The parts combine this way: 'Change Forces' B, 'Financial Outputs' B and 'Modal return' B, the last binding the whole because the case is a valuation case as much as a change case.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: LSEG is a "false friend", in the risks report's phrase.
    • The margin beat is cost-driven and partly bought by under-investment, the growth in the desktop is price not seats, the enterprise renewals ahead run into capped procurement with AI-native alternatives to price against, the AI monetisation is a framework without a contract, and the EPS growth is bought with buybacks funded by leverage
    • so the multiple is capped until the AI question is settled, which cannot be done in the near term.
    • The beat: H1 2026 operating expenses were £1,882m against consensus £1,919m while revenue only matched consensus
      • capex fell to 8.9% of revenue from 10.2%, which the risks report's ninth failure mode names as a margin beat stored up as future share loss.
    • The desktop: Workflows organic growth 2.7% in Q2 2026, the slowest since the Refinitiv acquisition
      • Workspace seat growth 2.9% and slowing
      • growth from 3.5% price ratchets, not seats.
    • The renewals: Refinitiv-era five- to seven-year enterprise deals renew over the next two to three years into procurement teams that cap rises at 0% to 3%.
    • The monetisation: the MCP server and the AI framework produce no revenue in 2026.
    • The leverage: 1.8x in December 2025 to 2.1x in June 2026 while buying back, an outlier against peers below 1.5x.
    • The multiple: Redburn expects it to stay range-bound (Neutral, 10,600p)
      • Jefferies concedes AI disruption is hard to disprove in the near term.
      • On this reading Morgan Stanley's bear case of 7,538p (a 9% earnings compound rate, divisional multiples five turns lower, Workflows at 17x) is the modal outcome and Jefferies' £65 (zero for Workflows and Risk Intelligence) the tail.
  • What we concede.
    • The H1 beat was cost-driven.
    • Workflows is a 2% to 3% grower and possibly worse.
    • The buyback is now partly debt-funded at the margin.
    • The AI framework has no revenue attached.
    • The multiple may not move on the first clean print.
    • The risks report's "park" was a reasonable recommendation on 17 July 2026, thirteen days before the results.
  • Why we hold our view: first, the price.
    • At 13.9x the market already pays for the false friend
    • the question is not whether the desktop deserves a premium but whether the other 79% of revenue (clearing at a 56% segment margin and over 90% share; indices at 67%; feeds growing 7.5%) deserves 14x when its peers trade at 17x to 26x.
    • We make no "covers the enterprise value" claim of our own, because the research does not give the divisional profit lines on a basis peer multiples could be applied to
    • we cite instead Jefferies' sum of the parts, £65 a share with zero for Workflows and Risk Intelligence, 21% below today's price, which says the disputed businesses are about a fifth of the value, in line with their fifth of revenue, and that the price carries no premium for them.
  • Second, the evidence since 17 July runs against the false friend at every operating metric the bear named as an early warning.
    • Subscription growth accelerated to 5.2%, retention rose to 92.8%, enterprise agreements rose to 18% of the division, sales cycles did not lengthen, and machine consumption grew 70%.
    • The bear's own invalidation trigger, Data & Analytics subscription growth below 5.0%, is not close.
  • Third, the cash.
    • Equity free cash flow of £2.4bn in FY2025 after £919m of capex, 111% of profit, is the fact that turns the capital-intensity and non-GAAP items from a fatal combination into an accounting note.
  • Where we would abandon the view.
    • A half-year print with subscription growth below 5% or retention below 92%
    • or two clean prints with no movement in the multiple, which would say the discount is structural, the conglomerate rather than the AI question, and the case a 'Compounder' at C, not a clearing concern at B.
