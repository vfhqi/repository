Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The competitive challenge from Varun Beverages and strict South African regulatory conditions directly threaten Coca-Cola HBC's post-acquisition profitability, making the resolution of these operational dynamics critical for long-term earnings per share growth.

Defining the Core Challenge

1.
Critical inquiry: "How is competition in South African soft drinks changing as Varun Beverages integrates BevCo and Twizza (Emkay, 22-Dec-25, expects about 20% volume share by CY27E), what are current shelf- price gaps (Bernstein, 26-Jun-26: a 2-litre Coca-Cola at R28.99 against Pepsi at R19.99), promotional intensity in national key accounts and cooler coverage, how have Coca-Cola Beverages Africa's volume and value shares moved in 2025-26, and what do the Competition Tribunal's employment conditions and black economic empowerment commitments allow Coca-Cola HBC to change in the South African cost base?"
2.
Strategic transformation: Coca-Cola HBC's landmark purchase of a 75% stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa for $2.6 billion represents a massive geographic shift. The company's pro-forma volume contribution from African operations is expected to rise to approximately 48% post-acquisition. This transaction represents a strategically sound but slow-burning investment for long-term growth. The structural growth upgrade from these markets must justify the incremental execution complexity to expand EPS.
3.
Positive targets: Coca-Cola HBC management announced the acquisition of the majority stake in Coca-Cola Beverages Africa on October 21, 2025. The company expects the transaction to be low-single digit EPS accretive in the first full year after completion. Management is confident that their established expertise in emerging markets will help unlock further profitability. This transaction will create the second-largest Coca- Cola bottler globally by volume.
4.
Escalating rivalry: The rapid integration of BevCo and Twizza by Varun Beverages threatens to erode Coca-Cola's dominant carbonated soft drink position in South Africa. Varun Beverages is aggressively targeting a 20% volume share by CY27E. This competitor plans to leverage Twizza's backward-integrated capacity to support massive volume expansion. Consequently, this intense market-share battle could severely restrict Coca-Cola Beverages Africa's pricing power and future EPS growth.

Operational Risks and Constraints

5.
Pronounced discounting: The wide price gap between a 2-litre Coca-Cola at R28.99 and Pepsi at R19.99 highlights severe affordability challenges in the South African market. Competitors have historically achieved significant market-share gains by prioritizing cheaper pricing and general trade expansion. To defend its volume share, Coca-Cola Beverages Africa may be forced to increase its own discount intensity. Such aggressive pricing actions would directly compress margins and reduce the acquisition's immediate contribution to EPS.
6.
Regulatory friction: Public-interest conditions imposed by the South African Competition Tribunal legally restrict Coca-Cola HBC from optimizing its cost structure. The regulator mandated strict restrictions on retrenchments alongside extensive black economic empowerment commitments. These employment and procurement guarantees prevent the company from realizing immediate administrative and operational synergies. Consequently, the rigidity of the South African cost base poses a direct downside risk to projected earnings growth.
7.
Interconnected inquiries: Closely related questions involve whether Coca-Cola HBC can successfully roll out its commercial excellence playbook in non-South African territories. These international markets, including Kenya and Ethiopia, are considered highly exciting but are currently not yet at scale. Furthermore, the group must manage significant margin dilution in the initial years post-acquisition. Resolving these international execution challenges will determine if the acquisition can support the group's mid-term EPS targets.
8.
Other side of the story: While aggressive competition and regulatory constraints present headwinds, Coca-Cola HBC has a strong track record of turnarounds in macro-volatile regions. Evidence from its Egypt acquisition shows that embedding standardized execution can drive an 800 basis point market share improvement. Standardizing operations at Coca-Cola Beverages Africa could add up to 100 basis points to the group's embedded organic growth rate. These commercial improvements are expected to deliver long-term EPS accretion despite near-term integration friction.

Section 2 – Competitive Dynamics and Varun's Integration of BevCo and Twizza

Section summary
  1. Our confidence is medium that Varun will reach a twenty percent volume share by the end of calendar year 2027 based on its rapid integration of Twizza and extensive capacity expansion.

Varun's South African Expansion

1.
Aggressive expansion: Varun Beverages entered the South African market by acquiring the PepsiCo bottler BevCo in March 2024. This transaction was executed for a cash consideration of ZAR 3 billion. Prior to the acquisition, PepsiCo held a low single digit volume share of approximately two percent in the country.
2.
Strategic scaling: Varun Beverages announced the acquisition of South African beverage player Twizza to deepen its market presence. The transaction carried an enterprise value of approximately ZAR 2.053 billion. This target adds three backward-integrated manufacturing facilities to Varun's regional footprint.
3.
Portfolio diversification: Varun Beverages is also utilizing its newly acquired South African network to test adjacent FMCG categories. The company has entered into an agreement with Carlsberg to test-market beer in its African territories. This entry leverages Varun's established route-to-market, where beer and soft drinks can be efficiently loaded onto the same trucks.
4.
Stalled diversification: Varun Beverages also sought to diversify its product portfolio into value-added dairy in South Africa. The company signed an agreement to acquire Crickley Dairy for ZAR two-hundred-thirty-eight million in March 2026. However, this transaction was not consummated due to non-fulfillment of conditions and was subsequently referred to arbitration.
5.
Massive expansion: The acquisition of Twizza provides Varun Beverages with immediate access to established regional production capacity. This transaction is projected to boost South African capacity by seventy to eighty percent. Consequently, Varun expects to completely bypass the time and capital required to build facilities organically.

Volume Share Claims and Competition

6.

The broker expects Varun to achieve about twenty percent volume share by calendar year 2027 compared to roughly ten percent previously. Coca-Cola's internal estimates show a massive eighty-three percent value share of the total sparkling soft drinks market. Pronounced discount: Varun Beverages operates with a substantial pricing discount compared to the market incumbent. Emkay Research highlights that Varun has fifty percent lower realization compared to the Coca-Cola bottler Coca-Cola Beverages Africa. This lower price point helps Varun attract highly price-sensitive consumers in a mature region.
9.
Healthy composition: Varun Beverages' product portfolio in South Africa aligns with global healthy consumption trends. Approximately ninety percent of Varun's South African portfolio comprises low-sugar or nosugar products. This healthy mix protects the company against potential regulatory hurdles like local sugar taxes.

Integration and Commercial Playbook

10.
Commercial restructuring: Varun Beverages is executing a dramatic shift in its South African go-to-market model. The company is working to reduce reliance on modern trade from the historical level of sixty-five percent of revenues. Varun plans to aggressively expand its general trade reach and deploy thousands of new chilling units.

Operational integration: Varun Beverages completed the merger of Twizza into its BevCo subsidiary on August 1, 2026. This corporate consolidation is expected to unlock substantial operational synergies over the coming quarters. The integration will protect margins by leveraging Twizza's extensive backward-integration facilities.

Synergistic manufacturing: Backward integration is a central pillar of Varun's international margin protection strategy. Twizza brings advanced backward integration at all three facilities, featuring five preform lines and one closure line. These in-house capabilities shield Varun's South African business from volatile global packaging material costs.

Successful precedent: Varun Beverages boasts a powerful track record of scaling underperforming international territories. In Zimbabwe, Varun expanded the overall soft drinks market from thirty-five million cases to seventy-five million cases. This previous success suggests that Varun's execution playbook can drive double-digit volume growth in South Africa.

Section 3 – Shelf-Price Gaps, Promotional Intensity, and Cooler Coverage

Section summary
  1. Incumbent pricing premiums on core carbonated soft drink brands face direct pressure as competitors leverage aggressive discounting and historic chilling infrastructure deployments to capture South African consumers.
  2. High promotional participation across major national supermarkets restricts the ability of bottlers to raise list prices without risking immediate volume losses.

South African Shelf Pricing

1.
Pronounced discount: Bernstein Research reported on June 26, 2026, that a 2-litre bottle of original Coca-Cola was priced at R28.99 at Pick n Pay and Checkers. In contrast, Pepsi Cola of the same size was priced at R19.99 across Shoprite, Pick n Pay, and Checkers. This massive gap represents a thirty-one percent price premium for the Coca-Cola brand over its primary competitor.
2.
Segmented pricing: Bernstein Research detailed on June 26, 2026, that Coca-Cola Zero Sugar in a 2-litre bottle was priced at R19.99 in Shoprite. During the same period, Pepsi Max of the same size was priced at R15.99 across major retailers. Bottlers are utilizing reduced-sugar architectures to offer improved consumer affordability points in highly volatile emerging channels.
3.
Sustained premium: Bernstein Research reported on June 26, 2026, that a 300ml bottle of original Coca-Cola was priced at R9.99 in Shoprite and Pick n Pay. Conversely, Pepsi offered its 330ml alternative at R7.99 in Shoprite. This pricing architecture demonstrates that the market incumbent maintains high double digit premiums across both single-serve and multi-serve formats.
4.
Elevated elasticity: Bernstein Research highlighted on June 26, 2026, that South Africa's high inequality makes consumers exceptionally sensitive to macroeconomic fluctuations. The broker warns that these extreme shelf-price gaps will force Coca-Cola Beverages Africa to work extra hard on affordability to sustain volume growth. Consequently, these dynamics may prevent Coca-Cola HBC from easily expanding its regional gross margins.
5.
Contrary evidence: An expert call transcript dated November 19, 2025, featuring a former Director at PepsiCo, suggests that PepsiCo is actually priced lower than Coca-Cola by a gap of about one-thousand points. The expert notes that Coca-Cola has exhibited a fair amount of pricing power through the inflationary environment. This indicates that the brand premium remains highly resilient to competitor actions despite the ongoing volume declines across the category.

This extreme promotional reliance highlights the cash-strapped state of the South African consumer.

Section 5 – Regulatory Commitments and South African Cost Base Flexibility

Section summary
  1. Strict employment and empowerment conditions imposed by the South African Competition Tribunal legally restrict Coca-Cola HBC from pursuing immediate cost-reduction initiatives or headcount rationalization.
  2. Management has pivoted its strategic focus toward top-line distribution expansion and revenue growth management because near-term labor and operational synergies remain highly constrained by regulatory mandates.

Regulatory Restrictions on Cost Optimization

1.
Strict regulations: Regulatory approval of the transaction came with highly restrictive public-interest conditions that directly limit immediate operational cost restructuring. The South African Competition Tribunal approved the transaction in September 2026 subject to clear compliance mandates. These specific conditions impose strict restrictions on employee retrenchments to protect local workers. This legal hurdle prevents the company from consolidating duplicate back-office or administrative positions across its regional footprint.
2.
Prolonged timeline: The final consolidation of these regional beverage operations remains a multi-year effort that postpones immediate cost-containment benefits. The transaction is valued at about two point six billion US dollars and is expected to be completed by the end of 2026. This acquisition brings a massive employee base of over fourteen thousand workers under the group's umbrella. Managing such an extensive geographic and human footprint naturally delays the implementation of standardized cost policies.
3.
Mandated funding: The regulatory mandates also force Coca-Cola HBC to commit substantial capital to local market development rather than corporate downsizing. The Competition Tribunal required the company to make clear commitments to invest in distribution and retail networks. This condition diverts free cash flow toward local retail infrastructure and commercial assets rather than margin-enhancing cost cuts. Consequently, the required network funding prevents immediate extraction of cash synergies from the South African operations.
4.
Incurred expenses: Under the regulatory agreement, the company must also complete a complex corporate listing process that adds to administrative expenses. The Swiss-based bottler is legally pursuing a secondary listing on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange. This fast-tracked listing is scheduled to take effect on November 23, 2026, which is just prior to the final transaction completion. Managing dual-listing regulatory compliance will likely elevate administrative expenses and legal fees in the near term.

Empowerment Commitments and Operational Impact

5.
