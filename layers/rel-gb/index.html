To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

D4022nd of 33

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement. Competitive intensity is rising, and fastest exactly where RELX is growing fastest: the Legal workflow layer. So far this has happened inside an expanding market, so it does not yet show in RELX's numbers. Intensity is broadly unchanged in Risk and in STM content, and rising modestly in Exhibitions. The direction is against RELX at the margin: new customers, small and mid-sized law firms, the drafting layer and, above all, ownership of the user interface.

Reasoning.

  • Legal entrants have scaled and are moving towards RELX's core: Harvey's annualised recurring revenue rose from $190m (December 2025) to more than $300m (May 2026) and more than $350m (late 2026), with an $11bn valuation (March 2026). Legora went from $100m to $200m (September 2026) at a $5.5bn valuation, raised $600m (April 2026), made five acquisitions in 2026 (including Qura for a primary-law corpus and Wexler), launched US Primary Legal Research (August 2026) and consumption pricing (June 2026). Google launched Gemini Enterprise for Legal (August 2026) with Thomson Reuters, iManage, Harvey and Legora as partners, and LexisNexis absent. Anthropic's Claude legal plug-in (3 February 2026) cut RELX's share price 14% in a day.
  • Customer-level evidence: startups win a double-digit share of new customers among small and mid-sized law firms; DLA Piper deployed Harvey firm-wide and judged Protégé less intuitive; some large firms piloted Protégé and did not buy full licences.
  • Incumbents are investing harder: Thomson Reuters plans consumption-based pricing options from 2027; Wolters Kluwer is raising product development to 12% to 13% of revenue in 2026 from 11%.
  • Offsets: the US legal research duopoly is intact (Thomson Reuters about 55%, RELX 30% to 35%); Morgan Stanley puts the probability of Legora becoming a meaningful research substitute at 20%; the startups integrate incumbent content rather than replace it (LexisNexis and Shepard's are integrated into Harvey, in which RELX holds a minority investment).
  • STM: OpenAI's Prism and Anthropic's Claude for Science target literature synthesis; Springer Nature grew published articles 13% in H1 26 against Elsevier's deliberately managed 7%.

Interplay. The economic transmission of rising intensity is not price-cutting on the current product; it is pressure to change the pricing architecture (from seats to consumption or outcomes), which is the variable the second set should model as a risk to pricing at renewal.

Reader's guide: the ten judgements that matter most for the model, and the data traps

The ten judgements

  1. The earnings algorithm is intact and unusually predictable. Underlying revenue growth was 8% (FY23), 7% (FY24), 7% (FY25) and 7% (H1 26). Adjusted operating profit grows about two points faster (9% underlying in FY25 and H1 26) because cost growth is held below revenue growth, so the adjusted operating margin rises about 70 to 90 basis points a year (33.1% FY23, 33.9% FY24, 34.8% FY25, 35.5% H1 26). Buybacks retire 3% to 4% of shares a year; cash conversion is 97% to 100%. Constant-currency adjusted EPS grew 10% in FY25 and 11% in H1 26. Reported figures lag the underlying ones because of sterling translation and the run-off of print.
  2. The mix of growth has rotated, not the total. Legal accelerated to 10% (a record) and STM to 6% in H1 26; Risk is steady at 8%; Exhibitions slowed from 9% (H1 25) to 6% (H1 26) to 4% year to date at nine months (Middle East). Legal's acceleration is substantially a price-and-upgrade migration onto Lexis+ AI and Protégé packages sold on three-year contracts: 90% of new sales value and about three-quarters of US renewal value are already on AI-enabled platforms. The rate of uplift should therefore fade in 2027 and 2028 unless a further paid tier (agentic workflows) lands. FactSet segment consensus already implies Legal at roughly 8.5% to 9% organic in FY27 and FY28 (after stripping the Doctrine acquisition).
  3. The AI threat is real but sits at the workflow interface and in the pricing architecture, not in the content. Harvey went from $190m of annualised recurring revenue (December 2025) to more than $350m (late 2026); Legora doubled from $100m to $200m in under six months, launched US primary legal research in August 2026 and introduced consumption pricing in June 2026; Google launched Gemini Enterprise for Legal in August 2026 without LexisNexis as a launch partner. Proof or disproof arrives at the second renewal cycle of the AI-tier contracts (2027 to 2029), and RELX's whole-number, half-yearly disclosure means it will be seen only after the event.
  4. Read the Risks report's "False Friend" verdict as "least falsifiable", not "most probable". The hypothesis that near-term AI upsell masks later seat contraction wins the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses by registering the fewest hard inconsistencies, but a vague, delayed hypothesis contradicts little evidence by construction (the Wisdom Library entry "The Vaguest Explanation Wins A Fewest-Contradictions Contest"). It is the right thing to monitor; it is not the base case.
  5. Consensus margins look conservative. Company-basis consensus EBIT implies an adjusted operating margin of 35.4% (FY26), 35.8% (FY27) and 35.8% (FY28): +60, +40 and 0 basis points, against +70 to +90 delivered each year and 35.5% already achieved in H1 26. Legal (23.0% margin in FY25, 21.7% in H1 26 after +80 basis points) is the main margin lever.
  6. Consensus share count looks slightly generous. Consensus net income divided by consensus EPS implies the average share count falling about 3.2% (FY26), 4.0% (FY27) and 3.3% (FY28), consistent with roughly £2bn a year of buybacks. The FY26 programme of £2.25bn was front-loaded (£1.75bn in H1) and partly funded by leverage (2.0x to 2.3x net debt to EBITDA); Bernstein models £1.5bn a year for 2027 and 2028. The difference is worth about 0.3% to 1% of EPS: small, but one-directional.
  7. Currency is the largest single swing factor in reported EPS. About 60% of revenue is North American and translation is unhedged; a 10% move in all foreign currencies moves net profit by £170m (about 7% of FY25 adjusted net profit of £2,358m). Currency cut reported revenue by £291m (FY24), £196m (FY25) and 2% (H1 26).
  8. Finance costs are rising. Consensus EBIT less consensus pre-tax profit implies net finance costs of about £334m (FY26) and £405m (FY27) against £273m in FY25, as debt rises with buybacks and refinancing happens at higher yields. The adjusted tax rate is about 22% to 23% (22.8% in H1 26).
  9. Valuation and sentiment are depressed, estimates are not. The 24-month-forward P/E is 17.7x, the 15th percentile of five years (trough 12.7x FY27 earnings at 2,013p in early 2026). Consensus EPS revisions are flat over one, three and six months; the buy share is 63% and falling; target prices were cut in February 2026 on multiples, not on earnings. About half the sector's de-rating is bond yields (US 10-year near 5.1%).

The data traps (check before modelling)

  • FactSet's history line "EBIT (adjusted)" (£3,037m, 31.7% in FY25) is not RELX's adjusted operating profit (£3,342m, 34.8%). Consensus EBIT (£3,558m FY26) is on the company and brokers' basis.
