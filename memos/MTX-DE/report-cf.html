Appendix

Outliers worth noting

The financial analysis of MTU Aero Engines reveals several statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies that warrant particular attention when evaluating the company's forward trajectory and risk profile. The compression of commercial MRO margins to 8.0% in H1 2026 represents a significant outlier, placing MTU in the bottom quintile of peer performance when compared against Safran's propulsion operating margin of 24.50% and Rolls-Royce's civil margin of 20.50%.

This 1,600 basis point margin discount is particularly striking given MTU's status as the world's largest independent MRO provider, suggesting structural rather than cyclical pressures. The dilution stems from a 60-70% share of zero-margin spare parts pass-through bought from OEMs, creating a business model constraint that appears unique among aerospace peers [Cross-ref: Section 7]. A second notable outlier emerges in the accumulated EUR 1.1 billion receivables balance from pre-financed GTF shop visits, projected to grow by EUR 200 million annually through fiscal year 2028.

This working capital distortion places MTU's net working capital days significantly above peer medians, with the company historically trading at a lower FCF yield of 3.50% compared to a peer average of 4%. The magnitude and duration of this prefinancing requirement appear unprecedented in aerospace maintenance history, raising questions about contract negotiation dynamics and structural bargaining power [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 10].

The company's remarkably stable 4% employee turnover rate in both 2024 and 2025 represents a top-decile outlier when benchmarked against industrial sector averages, which typically range from 12-15% annually. This exceptional retention during a period of complete executive board turnover suggests either highly effective internal culture mechanisms or potentially limited external employment mobility in specialized aerospace engineering roles [Cross-ref: Section 1].

MTU's 37% discount to its peak 12-month forward EV/EBIT multiple stands as a valuation anomaly, particularly when contrasted with peers Safran and Airbus trading within 4% and 7% of their peaks. This valuation dislocation of over 30 percentage points appears disproportionate to the underlying operational delivery track record, which has consistently exceeded guidance across multiple periods [Cross-ref: Section 7, Section 11]. Finally, the spare engine delivery ratio elevated at 20% versus a normalized 12-14% represents a statistical outlier in fleet management dynamics.

This 67% premium to historical base rates has artificially inflated OEM segment profitability through emergency provisioning, creating a temporary margin structure that masks underlying structural economics. The anticipated normalization poses a significant mean-reversion risk that consensus models may inadequately capture [Cross-ref: Section 9, Section 10].
