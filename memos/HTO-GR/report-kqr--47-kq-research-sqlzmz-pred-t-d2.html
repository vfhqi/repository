Uncommitted strategy: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization has not finalized the implementation of its planned early-2027 retail price step covering two million postpaid contracts. This pricing hesitation suggests that OTE's retail revenue growth may remain flat if the early-2027 indexation is entirely abandoned.
2.
Regulatory hurdles: The execution of unilateral tariff increases face tighter scrutiny across European telecommunications markets. Regulatory updates suggest that authorities are demanding clearer price transparency at point of sale to protect consumers. Consequently, OTE must ensure any automatic price steps are explicitly detailed in subscriber contracts to avoid regulatory pushback. This compliance burden might delay the cash flow benefits expected from postpaid tariff adjustments.
3.
Diluted growth: The absence of a structured pricing step would directly impact OTE's mobile service revenues over the next eighteen months. Capital market analysts at Morgan Stanley noted on 30 October 2026 that OTE relies on strong mobile service revenue trends to meet earnings targets. Without the postpaid contract price step, OTE's ability to offset rising operational costs will weaken. This structural deficit could ultimately depress projected earnings per share figures.

Deutsche Bank Research reported on 8 May 2026 that OTE's national wholesale revenue fell by EUR 15m to EUR 239m in fiscal year 2025. This reduction reflects the growing maturity of competing infrastructure and the gradual migration of retail customers to proprietary networks. Consequently, OTE must offset these domestic wholesale losses with retail broadband gains to protect its overall operating margin profile.

Industry reports on 10 October 2026 showed that its subsidiary PPC FiberGrid has expanded its network to 2.3 million premises passed.
