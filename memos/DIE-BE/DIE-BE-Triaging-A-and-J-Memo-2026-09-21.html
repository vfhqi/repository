[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. D'Ieteren is a valuation-event case wrapped around a compounder: a family-controlled holding trading at 9.61 times 24-month-forward earnings, the 10th percentile of its own five years, whose value is a 50.3 percent stake in Belron, the world's largest vehicle-glass repairer, which is compounding through a mix shift to calibration and deleveraging at about a turn a year. At EUR 164.20 the shares pay for the part of Belron's margin path that consensus already holds and nothing for the formal review of a Belron listing opened on 9 September 2026, so the case is compelling in its centre and mediocre in its wrapper. On our arithmetic the modal return is about 16 percent a year over eighteen months, a C against the 20 percent bar; it clears the bar only at EUR 156 or below, or once the Belron event has terms.

  • The change that makes this a case now is internal, dated and new, and it was forced from outside.
    • On 9 September 2026, with the H1 2026 results, the group opened a formal evaluation of liquidity options for the Belron minorities, naming an Amsterdam listing
    • announced that Eric Machiels, a private-equity and infrastructure investor, succeeds Francis Deprez as chief executive in December 2026
    • and put its Belgian car-distribution arm into a costed restructuring (344 roles, site closures).
    • The force behind the review is external: the private-equity co-shareholders in Belron (about 40 percent) need to monetise, and their need has turned the previously communicated "hold forever" stance into a process.
    • A EUR 100m buyback is running beside it.
    • The screen's decision rule is met with room: at least four of the twelve internal forces are strong (chief-executive change; strategy and structure change at the main asset; a standards programme at Belron with a restructuring at Automotive; real price rises at Belron), and the false-positive checks mostly clear.
    • The largest change is a choice announced, not an action taken: a review is not a decision, and guidance has not been raised.
  • The external forces split by segment, and the split favours the case because of where the profit sits.
    • Belron was 56 percent of FY2025 operating profit and about 77 percent of sum-of-the-parts value
    • D'Ieteren Automotive fell to EUR 36.4m of adjusted profit before tax in H1 2026 from EUR 108.8m, about 6 percent of group profit on the half-year run-rate.
    • At Belron the tailwind is structural: windscreen recalibration reached 52.1 percent of jobs in H1 2026 from 45.9 percent a year earlier, adding EUR 150 to 300 per job, with a target above 60 percent by FY2028
      • camera mandates make calibration a required step
      • US claims avoidance after 40 percent insurance-premium inflation from 2022 to 2024 is easing, so volume turned to plus 3.9 percent in H1 2026 after a flat FY2025.
    • At Automotive the headwind is structural and the company says so: Belgian registrations fell 7.5 percent in 2025 and management now expects the market to plateau at 400,000 units a year, 20 percent below the pre-pandemic level
      • the segment's adjusted operating margin fell to 2.1 percent from 4.5 percent.
    • Currency is the one force acting on this company more than on the universe: 55 percent of Belron's revenue is North American, largely unhedged in translation, and the H1 2026 results carried a 2.2 percent negative translation effect.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up at Belron and have not yet reached the group's estimates.
    • Belron's organic growth rose to 7.3 percent in H1 2026 (3.9 percent volume, 3.4 percent price and mix) from 5.9 percent in FY2025
    • its margin rose 160 basis points to 23.0 percent against the 21.8 percent the sell-side expected
    • trading cash conversion was 89 percent
    • leverage fell to 4.3x from 5.3x a year earlier.
    • Group free cash flow was EUR 485.1m against EUR 256.8m
    • adjusted profit before tax, group share, rose 6.6 percent to EUR 482.4m.
    • The line the market prices is flat and being trimmed: FY2026 consensus earnings per share (EUR 13.38) has come down 2.7 percent in a month and 10.4 percent in twelve
      • the cuts are Automotive-driven and small, while targets rise (mean EUR 237.46) and nine of nine visible brokers are at Buy or Overweight.
    • Guidance was confirmed, not raised: low-to-mid-single-digit growth in adjusted profit before tax, group share, at constant currency for FY2026, held through a 66.6 percent fall in Automotive's profit.
  • What we think it earns.
    • On our path, group adjusted earnings per share (brokers' basis) go from EUR 12.95 in FY2025 to EUR 13.4 in FY2026, EUR 15.4 in FY2027 and EUR 17.6 in FY2028: about 11 percent a year, a flat year then two years near 15 percent, fading to EUR 19.3, 20.8 and 22.2 in FY2029 to FY2031 as calibration penetration approaches its plateau.
    • Our path sits 1 to 3 percent below consensus in FY2027 and FY2028 because we take Belron's price and mix at about 2 percent as insurer pushback bites, Automotive recovering only to about 2.8 percent margin, and the euro at 1.18.
    • The composition is the weakness: about 40 percent of the growth is finance-cost relief from Belron's deleveraging (about 4.4 percent a year) and buybacks (about 1.2 percent a year), which investors do not extrapolate, against about 2.1 percent a year from revenue and 2.7 percent from margin.
  • What it costs on the valuation, which is the strongest single support. 9.61 times 24-month-forward earnings of EUR 17.1 against a five-year range of 8.19x to 18.65x and a median of 11.23x
    • the 10th percentile of five years, the 14th of two and the 15th of ten
    • a 37 to 45 percent discount to estimated net asset value that the research attributes to family control, a minority stake, minimal disclosure and Belron's leverage, none of which is an industry multiple.
    • The event is not priced: the shares fell from EUR 170.40 to EUR 164.20 through the announcement week.
  • The return against the bar is a C, and the re-rating is the swing.
    • At each horizon we value the shares at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it: at March 2028 those earnings are about EUR 19.7 and the multiple we apply is a blend of the re-rated branch at 11.1x (the stock's ten-year median, at odds of 0.45: a 60 percent chance the event is announced with terms times a 75 percent chance the market re-rates on it) and the un-rerated branch at 9.6x (today's multiple, at 0.55), which is 10.3x
    • the value is EUR 202.9, plus EUR 2.10 of dividends, EUR 205.0, a return of 24.8 percent over eighteen months or 15.9 percent a year.
    • At three years the blend is 10.4x on about EUR 21.9 of earnings, EUR 235.1 in total, 12.7 percent a year.
    • Earnings growth carries 15 of the 25 points at eighteen months
    • the cash return is 1.3 percent a year and does not move the answer.
    • The one-year-forward footing beside it: 11.0 times twelve-month-forward earnings of about EUR 14.9 now
      • held at 11.0 times on about EUR 18.0 at March 2028 the shares are worth EUR 198, EUR 200 with the dividend, 14.1 percent a year, within two points of the convention.
    • The swing: at 9.6x the eighteen-month return is 10.7 percent a year (a D), at 11.1x 21.8 percent (a B), at the 75th percentile band of 12.1x 29.0 percent (an A), at the 10th percentile band of 9.2x 7.6 percent (an F).
      • On the consensus path at the modal 10.3x it is 18.3 percent a year, the bottom of the B band
      • the difference between a C and a B at the modal multiple is the 3 percent by which our path sits below consensus.
    • The drop-through sensitivity: Belron's debt refinanced 100 basis points dearer (about EUR 0.6 per share, 3.4 percent of FY2028 earnings), the dollar 5 percent weaker (minus EUR 0.27, 2.0 percent) and an adjusted tax rate of 30 percent rather than 28 (2.8 percent) together take FY2028 earnings per share to about EUR 16.2 from 17.6 and the eighteen-month earnings to about EUR 18.1
      • at 10.3x the return falls to 9.6 percent a year, a C to an F, and on the re-rated branch to 15.2 percent, a B to a C.
      • That is the size of the leverage in the structure, and it is why the finance-cost leg, although it adds to growth, also caps the ratings.
  • The chain holds at Belron and breaks at the group's last link.
    • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, Belron's internal change has travelled all the way to the sell-side's upgrades
    • the group's has reached its financial statements (adjusted profit before tax up 6.6 percent) but its consensus is still being cut for Automotive
    • the listing is at the second layer, a choice announced.
  • The pattern fits only in part.
    • The best fit is 'Major Corporate Change' at a C: the inputs are broad and the main asset's own change is producing output, but the pattern expects major earnings-trajectory upgrades at the group and the group's estimates are being trimmed, because the payoff of the largest change is a multiple, not an estimate.
    • None of the six 'Target Setups' is built for a holding-discount catalyst.

  • What the case therefore is.

    • A bet that Belron's calibration-led margin and cash keep compounding at 57 percent and rising of the group's profit while a formal process gives the market a price for it, so that a stock at the 10th percentile of its own multiple earns its median back.
    • The bet is worth taking at EUR 156 or below, where the modal path clears 20 percent a year over eighteen months, or at today's price once the event has terms, which lifts the eighteen-month return to about 22 percent a year.

  • Rating = C, because the Building Block measures materiality and the chain breaks at the last link: the change forces are strong (B) and the outputs are improving at the asset that carries the value, but at the group level earnings growth is about 11 percent a year and the modal total return of about 16 percent a year over eighteen months and 13 percent over three falls short of the 20 percent bar on our arithmetic

    • the parts combine so that the return leg binds.

  • Not B, because a B requires the modal return to be about 20 percent, which here needs either the re-rated branch taken as modal (a 45 percent possibility) or a higher re-rated anchor than the stock's own median, and we take neither

    • not D, because the inputs are real, dated and already producing outputs at Belron, and the entry multiple leaves the return's downside bounded.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against is that the thesis is a holding-discount bet dressed as a compounder: group earnings per share on the reported basis were EUR 8.04 in FY2025 against EUR 12.95 adjusted
    • the FY2026 consensus has been cut 10.4 percent in twelve months
    • the segment that just collapsed missed by 51 percent
    • and the entire return above a C depends on an event that private-equity holders and market windows control, announced the same day the chief executive resigned.
  • We concede the composition of the growth (finance-cost relief is 40 percent of it), the flat FY2026, and that our modal return is a C.
  • We hold the thesis because the operating facts at 57 percent of profit and three-quarters of value are unusually good (margin up 160 basis points on 7.3 percent organic growth with 89 percent cash conversion), the deleveraging is arithmetic, the entry multiple is at its 10th percentile so the price already agrees with the bear about the wrapper, and the event has a formal process behind it with motivated sellers holding 40 percent.
  • Where the case against would win: calibration penetration stalling below 50 percent, Belron's margin flat for two halves, or the review closing without a transaction
    • each is a measured series we can watch.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a valuation-event case wrapped around a compounder: a family-controlled holding trading at 9.61 times 24-month-forward earnings, the 10th percentile of its own five years, whose value is a 50.3 percent stake in Belron, the world's largest vehicle-glass repairer, which grew organically 7.3 percent in the first half of 2026 while lifting its margin 160 basis points to 23.0 percent on a mix shift to calibration (52.1 percent of jobs from 45.9 percent) and cutting its leverage a full turn to 4.3x.
  • The change that makes it a case now is corporate: on 9 September 2026 the group opened a formal review of a Belron listing driven by private-equity co-owners of 40 percent, announced a new chief executive from private equity for December 2026, and put its shrinking Belgian car-distribution arm, whose profit fell 66.6 percent in the half, into a restructuring.
  • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, Belron's internal change has travelled all the way to the sell-side's upgrades
    • the group's has reached its financial statements but its consensus is still being trimmed for Automotive
    • the listing is a choice announced, not an action taken.
  • On our path group adjusted earnings per share grow about 11 percent a year to FY2028, flat this year then two years near 15 percent, with about 40 percent of that growth coming from finance-cost relief rather than operations.
  • The shares pay nothing for the event, having fallen through the week it was announced, and the multiple is company-driven, so the re-rating is ours to earn through a named process.
  • The modal return is about 16 percent a year over eighteen months and about 13 percent over three years, a C against the 20 percent bar
    • the branch in which the event happens and the multiple returns to its median gives about 22 percent
    • a plausibly worse drop-through takes the modal return to an F.
  • The case is compelling in its centre and mediocre in its wrapper, and it is compelling enough to progress only at a lower price or on the event.

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Insurer routing to the scaled operator

    • It matters because the calibration premium is the margin engine of the whole case; it threatens both the case being right and the share price.
