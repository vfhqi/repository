Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Indutrade entered 2026-Q1 as a high-quality but temporarily stalled serial acquirer, with a resilient decentralized portfolio and strong cash generation offsetting two years of weak organic profit growth, sub-target returns and a valuation discount to Lifco, Addtech and Lagercrantz.
1.
Niche industrial compounder: Indutrade develops, manufactures and sells technically advanced components, systems and services through more than 220 companies in over 30 countries, primarily across Western and Northern Europe, rather than operating as a single centralized industrial manufacturer.
2.
Decentralized operating model: Management’s model leaves subsidiary managing directors responsible for their own income statements, balance sheets, products and customer relationships, while Indutrade provides financing, industrial expertise, leadership development, digitalization and governance support. - Analyst/Investor Diversified portfolio: The group is organized into Industrial & Engineering, Infrastructure & Construction, Life Science, Process, Energy & Water, and Technology & Systems Solutions, with 35 business segments designed to improve knowledge-sharing and make acquisition sourcing more scalable.
3.
Balanced business mix: Approximately 60% of the portfolio consists of technical-trading companies and 40% of companies with proprietary products, a mix that gives Indutrade recurring distribution revenues while retaining exposure to higher-margin product businesses. - Analyst/Investor Reduced concentration risk: The ten largest companies represented 19% of group sales at the November 2025 Capital Markets Day, versus 30% ten years earlier, so the portfolio had become less dependent on any individual subsidiary before Q1 2026. - Analyst/Investor Acquisition-led history: Since its 2005 listing, Indutrade has compounded earnings per share at approximately 15%, while DNB Carnegie said acquisitions historically contributed about 7–8% of annual group EBITA growth over the prior five and ten years.
4.
Three-year operating reset: Revenue rose 18% to SEK31.8 billion in 2023, then increased only 2% in 2024 to SEK32.5 billion and declined 1% in 2025 to SEK32.2 billion, showing that the high-growth history had not translated into uninterrupted recent expansion.
5.
Margin normalization: EBITA increased 16% to SEK4.8 billion at a 15.0% margin in 2023, fell to SEK4.6 billion at 14.4% in 2024, and reached SEK4.6 billion at a reported 13.8% margin in 2025, or 14.1% excluding extraordinary items, leaving the group below its former earnings trajectory.
6.
Organic growth shortfall: Comparable sales grew 6% in 2023, declined 1% in 2024 and declined 2% in 2025, against the company’s ambition of at least 10% average annual sales growth through the cycle, meaning acquisitions and currency effects were doing more of the work than underlying demand.
7.
Cash-flow strength: Operating cash flow was SEK4.5 billion in 2023, SEK4.1 billion in 2024 and SEK4.0 billion in 2025, while 2025 free operating cash flow was SEK3.5 billion, giving Indutrade capacity to keep acquiring despite stagnant reported profit.
8.
Returns below ambition: Return on capital employed fell from 21% in 2023 to 19% in 2024 and remained below the company’s 20% target in the period before Q1 2026, weakening the case for valuing Indutrade like the highest-return serial acquirers.
9.
Leverage remained controlled: Interest-bearing net debt was SEK7.7 billion at the end of 2023 and approximately SEK8.1 billion at the end of 2025, while net debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization was 1.4 times in both years, preserving acquisition capacity without the balance-sheet stress seen at more leveraged consolidators.
10.
Acquisition pace recovered late: Indutrade completed nine acquisitions in 2023, 16 in 2024 and 13 in 2025, with 10 of the 2025 transactions completed in the second half, but DNB Carnegie estimated that acquisitions added only 4–5% to EBITA in 2024 and 2025 versus approximately 8% historically.
11.
Shareholder returns remained progressive: The proposed dividend increased from SEK2.85 per share for 2023 to SEK3 for 2024 and SEK3.10 for 2025, consistent with the stated 30–50% payout objective but not sufficient on its own to offset the recent EPS stagnation. - Earnings Calls Peer-relative expectations were lower: DNB Carnegie regarded Addtech, Lagercrantz and Lifco as proven compounder peers with stronger recent growth and margins, and therefore applied an approximately 15–20% valuation discount to Indutrade before Q1 2026 rather than assuming convergence to peer multiples. Six-month estimate direction was cautious: DNB Carnegie moved from a Buy rating and SEK290 target after the April 2025 Q1 miss to Hold and SEK250 by October 2025, then lowered the target to SEK240 before Q4 and SEK235 after Q4, while its adjusted 2026 EPS estimate settled around SEK9.8 before the Q1 2026 result.
12.
Other broker perspective: Redeye’s May 2025 initiation was more constructive than DNB Carnegie, assigning a SEK270 base case because it believed the new decentralized acquisition structure could support approximately 9% long-term sales growth and an EBITA margin rising from about 14% to 17% by 2033.
13.
Market-performance data gap: Exact three-, six- and twelve-month share-price returns immediately before Q1 2026 were not retrieved from the structured market-data source, although DNB Carnegie reported a 2025 share-price range of SEK182.7–274.6 and a negative 3-, 6- and 12-month performance snapshot in May 2025. Other side of the story: The apparent earnings stagnation may prove cyclical rather than structural if the strengthened segment organization restores organic growth and the acquisition pipeline returns toward historical contribution levels, but the 2023–25 record and the persistent discount to Lifco, Addtech and Lagercrantz meant that this remained an investment case requiring renewed execution rather than a proven continuation of the old compounder formula.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. The pre-Q1 bar was deliberately modest on reported growth but demanding on proof: investors expected order recovery, margin protection and renewed acquisitions to convert into EPS growth, while DNB Carnegie remained sceptical and Redeye saw greater operating leverage
1.
Recovery bar, cautious: Indutrade’s Q4 message implied that organic order growth of 3%, stable organic sales, a slightly larger order book and easier comparisons should produce gradual improvement in 2026, but the absence of quantitative guidance meant the market expected evidence of recovery rather than a formal forecast. - Earnings Calls Revenue conversion, central: The main pre-Q1 question was whether improving orders would convert into sales quickly enough to lift earnings, because Q4 order intake grew organically by 3% while organic sales were unchanged, leaving investors expecting only a gradual rather than immediate top-line recovery.
2.
Order intake, constructive: Management highlighted positive order development in most companies, customer segments and geographies, with particularly strong demand in energy, water and wastewater, infrastructure and construction, so the market expected Q1 orders to remain ahead of sales and gradually rebuild the backlog. - Earnings Calls Organic growth, disputed: DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden expected Indutrade to remain fairly slow- growing for the next 6–12 months, because the organic and inorganic engines would take time to restart, whereas management’s improving-order narrative implied a more visible recovery during 2026.
3.
Business-area mix, uneven: The market expected Industrial & Engineering to lead the recovery, while Infrastructure & Construction was expected to improve from depressed levels and Technology & Systems Solutions to remain the main drag, reflecting Q4 organic sales growth of 4% in Industrial & Engineering against declines in Life Science and Technology & Systems Solutions.
4.
Life Science, key test: DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden identified stronger organic growth in Life Science as a requirement for becoming more positive on Indutrade, so the pre-Q1 bar was not merely stable demand but evidence that the business area could accelerate despite difficult comparisons related to Novo Nordisk.
5.
Gross margin, protected: Management argued that stable gross margins reflected pricing power and the discipline of subsidiary managing directors, leading the market to expect gross-margin resilience even if volumes remained weak, rather than a major margin recovery from mix alone. - Earnings Calls Cost control, unresolved: The positive Q4 underlying EBITA margin of 14.9% was expected to continue only if expenses remained controlled, but DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden noted that costs had been high in earlier quarters and treated operating expenses as a central risk to the recovery case.
6.
Operating leverage, asymmetric: Redeye’s Knutsson expected strong operating leverage once sales recovered because gross margin remained around 35–36%, while DNB Carnegie expected the benefit to arrive slowly because weak organic growth had not yet absorbed the cost base, creating materially different expectations for Q1 EBITA.
7.
EPS, recovery assumed: DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden forecast adjusted EPS of SEK9.8 for 2026 and SEK10.6 for 2027 before Q1, implying recovery from the 2025 earnings stagnation, but they expected growth to remain slower than the company’s earlier double-digit compounder record.
8.
EPS, long-term credibility: DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden still expected Indutrade to preserve more than 10% long-term EPS growth, but only if EBITA began growing organically again, so Q1 was expected to test whether the historical EPS model remained credible rather than merely whether the company could acquire growth.
9.
Cash conversion, supportive: Management entered Q1 with operating cash flow of SEK1.6 billion in Q4 and rolling cash conversion above 130% for nine consecutive quarters, so investors expected cash generation and inventory reduction to protect acquisition capacity even if profit growth remained subdued. - Earnings Calls Working capital, positive evidence: The market expected working-capital efficiency to remain a support for EPS and M&A because inventories had continued to decline and were described as close to historically low levels, reducing the likelihood that a sales recovery would consume disproportionate cash. - Earnings Calls Acquisition pace, principal swing factor: DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden judged that acquisitions would be the main driver of earnings revisions over the following 6–12 months, because historical acquisitions had added 7–8% to group EBITA annually but only 4% in 2025 and 5% in 2024.
10.
Acquisition quality, scrutinised: Indutrade’s Capital Markets Day presented a standardized valuation process, a target acquisition range of 5–8 times enterprise value to EBITA and a requirement that deals be EPS accretive, so the market expected evidence that a faster deal pace would not require weaker pricing or lowerquality targets.
11.
M&A capacity, constructive counterpoint: Redeye’s Knutsson viewed the strengthened business-area and segment structure as capable of scaling acquisition sourcing, while assuming approximately 7–9 times EBITA for future deals, so his pre-Q1 view gave greater weight to acquired EPS growth than DNB Carnegie’s slower recovery case.
12.
Leverage, comfortable but conditional: Management described the financial position as strong and suitable for a gradual increase in acquisitions, while the 2025 Capital Markets Day reaffirmed a net debt-to-equity objective below 100%, so investors expected leverage to remain manageable but monitored whether faster M&A would weaken financial flexibility.
13.
Project exposure, negative overhang: Redeye’s Knutsson considered the SEK125 million of project write- downs in two United Kingdom Technology & Systems Solutions companies a negative signal even after adjustment, so Q1 was expected to show whether project-control problems were genuinely isolated or evidence of broader execution risk. Geography and tariffs, downside risk: Management said Technology & Systems Solutions had been hurt by weaker United States demand linked to tariffs and by China’s buy-local policy, while expecting gradual improvement during 2026, so the market expected North American and Asian exposure to remain a drag rather than a near-term growth engine. - Earnings Calls Macro sensitivity, material: DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden estimated that approximately two-thirds of Indutrade’s sales were exposed to early- or late-cyclical end markets, so weak European industrial production and investment appetite were expected to limit the speed of organic recovery even if defensive Life Science and recurring-need products provided some cushioning.
14.
Peer bar, difficult comparison: DNB Carnegie valued Indutrade at roughly a 15–20% discount to Addtech, Lagercrantz and Lifco because those peers had stronger recent growth, margins or returns on capital, meaning Indutrade’s Q1 needed to demonstrate improving execution before the market would assume peerlevel valuation convergence.
15.
Peer expectations, higher: DNB Carnegie described Addtech, Lagercrantz and Lifco as proven compounders with similar decentralized, acquisition-led models but stronger track records, so Indutrade faced a higher credibility burden than a smaller or less-established serial acquirer entering Q1.
16.
Targets, credibility gap: Indutrade’s Capital Markets Day reconfirmed minimum annual sales growth of 10%, an EBITA margin of at least 14% and return on capital employed of at least 20%, but DNB Carnegie’s Redin and Korsheden noted that recent profit growth had stagnated, leaving the market to treat the targets as mediumterm ambitions rather than Q1 expectations. - Analyst/Investor Other side of the story: The cautious pre-Q1 bar could have understated the upside because Q4 order growth, strong gross margin, cash conversion and renewed acquisition activity created operating leverage, but Redeye’s Knutsson still acknowledged that organic sales remained soft and that the recovery depended on orders eventually converting into revenue.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. Acquisition capacity is the key swing factor for Indutrade’s three-year earnings path, while organic recovery, cost absorption and leadership succession determine whether the enlarged platform converts deal activity into sustainable earnings per share growth.
1.
Platform expansion, deliberate: The November 2025 Capital Markets Day changed the operating architecture from five business areas into more than 30 business segments, adding segment leaders and acquisition specialists to improve both organic support and deal sourcing; management said the platform was designed to enable another doubling of Indutrade’s size rather than a short-term restructuring.
2.
Acquisition sourcing, broadened: Indutrade moved from relying mainly on brokers and external opportunities toward structured internal lead generation through business-segment leaders, country specialists, employees, customers and suppliers, which should increase exclusivity and reduce dependence on competitive auction processes over the next three years.
3.
Deal discipline, retained: The Capital Markets Day preserved Indutrade’s preference for technically specialized business-to-business companies with stable earnings, healthy gross margins and sustainable returns on working capital, while its standardized valuation framework generally targeted enterprise value to EBITA multiples of 5–8 times and required acquisitions to be earnings per share accretive.
4.
Acquisition pace, accelerated: Indutrade completed 13 acquisitions in 2025, with activity weighted toward the second half of the year, reversing the earlier slowdown and creating a higher acquired-growth contribution base for 2026 even though DNB Carnegie estimated that acquisitions had contributed only 4–5% to EBITA growth in 2024 and 2025.
5.
2026 M&A, materially stronger: Six acquisitions with combined annual sales of SEK 1.25 billion were completed or announced in the first half of 2026, versus 13 acquisitions during the whole of 2025, while Redeye identified nine year-to-date acquisitions by late July and estimated approximately SEK 2.5 billion of acquired annual sales over the preceding twelve months.
6.
Pipeline, supportive but uncommitted: Management described the acquisition pipeline as strong and said many business areas were involved in projects, but the company does not include unannounced transactions in its formal estimates, leaving future earnings per share dependent on deal timing, availability and continued pricing discipline rather than on the pipeline alone.
7.
Life Science, increasingly central: Acquisitions such as Axotan, PHACOTEC, Albiox and later Polish targets Sinmed and Euromed are increasing exposure to medical technology, pharmaceutical packaging, cleanroom products, consumables and diagnostic imaging, shifting the portfolio toward markets with structural demand drivers and potentially higher resilience than industrial construction.
8.
Energy exposure, reinforced: Controlin added Dutch expertise in energy management, power management and industrial automation components serving marine, infrastructure, utilities, data centres and solar parks, strengthening exposure to electrification and grid investment while adding another route for medium-term acquired growth.
9.
Organic growth, still the missing leg: At the start of 2026, sales remained flat organically and full-year 2025 comparable sales had declined 2%, so the new structure had not yet demonstrated that it could restore the company’s 10% through-cycle sales-growth ambition without relying primarily on acquisitions.
10.
Order recovery, improving unevenly: First-quarter 2026 order intake rose 2% year on year and exceeded sales by 7%, with stronger demand in medical technology, pharmaceuticals, energy and process industry, but infrastructure and construction weakened and engineering remained broadly stable, implying delayed rather than universal operating leverage.
11.
Business-area divergence, persistent: Technology & Systems Solutions continued to face cautious industrial customers and weaker United Kingdom operations, while Process, Energy & Water built a record order book but suffered delayed conversion because energy and process projects have longer lead times, making grouplevel recovery dependent on mix and timing.
12.
United Kingdom projects, unresolved risk: Two United Kingdom companies in Technology & Systems Solutions generated SEK 125 million of project write-downs in late 2025, and the subsequent Q1 weakness in the area indicated that the issue was not fully neutralized by the accounting charge, leaving execution and project-control risk relevant to future margins. [#r] Cost base, positioned for leverage: Selling, general and administrative expenses remained too high relative to sales in Q1 2026, but management said cost initiatives were underway and DNB Carnegie expected the cost position to become more favourable in Q2 and Q3 as volumes recovered, making expense absorption the immediate margin test.
13.
Margin ambition, not yet delivered: Indutrade retained its target of at least a 14% EBITA margin, but the 2025 reported margin was 13.8% and Q1 2026 delivered 13.3%, so reaching the target over the next three years requires both organic volume recovery and margin-accretive acquisitions rather than merely maintaining current execution.
14.
EPS path, increasingly acquisition-dependent: DNB Carnegie expected more than 10% long-term EPS growth only if EBITA resumed organic growth, while acquisitions had historically supplied more than half of earnings growth; therefore, persistent organic stagnation would leave the three-year EPS case exposed to a higherthan-normal requirement for successful M&A. [#r] Cash flow and leverage, still enabling: Operating cash flow remained strong enough to support increased acquisition spending, while first-half 2026 free operating cash flow reached SEK 1.30 billion and net debt to EBITDA was 1.7 times at Q2, up from 1.5 times a year earlier but still below the company’s stated financialstability ceiling.
15.
Capital allocation, unchanged: Indutrade reaffirmed a 30–50% dividend payout objective and net debt to equity below 100%, indicating that incremental cash generation is intended to fund acquisitions while preserving progressive dividends and balance-sheet flexibility rather than being directed toward buybacks.
16.
Leadership succession, material: Per-Olow Jansson’s planned retirement from the Process, Energy & Water leadership role was followed by Per Lidström’s appointment from April 2026, while Håkan Svensson became Senior Vice President for Infrastructure & Construction and Anna Vilogorac was appointed CFO from no later than January 2027, adding succession depth but also execution risk during an expansion phase.
17.
Investor-relations continuity, retained: Mårten Svanberg remained Head of Investor Relations and External Communications through the Q3 2026 reporting invitation, so the principal communication channel was unchanged even as finance and business-area leadership evolved.
18.
Macro and competitive setting, mixed: Indutrade’s approximately two-thirds cyclical sales exposure leaves earnings sensitive to industrial production, construction, interest rates, tariffs and geopolitical uncertainty, while peers are also benefiting from thematic demand in electrification, grids and data centres rather than from a broad European industrial upcycle.
19.
Other side of the story: The stronger platform and 2026 acquisition run-rate could restore Indutrade’s historical compounding model faster than current estimates assume, but the opposite outcome remains plausible if the enlarged segment structure becomes bureaucratic, organic demand stays weak or acquisition quality deteriorates as the company attempts to sustain a higher deal pace.
