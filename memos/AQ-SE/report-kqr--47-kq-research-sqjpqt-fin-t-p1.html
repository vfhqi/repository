Operating Leverage and Cost Structure Analysis of AQ Group and Peers

AQ Group (AQ-SE). Question: AQ Group AB (AQ-SE, Nasdaq Stockholm), a decentralised Swedish contract manufacturer of components and systems for industrial OEMs across 17 countries; Component segment (inductive components and transformers including medium-voltage units for data centres, wiring harnesses, sheet metal, injection moulding) and System segment (electrical cabinets and complete system assembly) serving commercial vehicles, construction and agricultural machinery, rail, defence, grid and data-centre power customers. Key question: how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts? Answer for the company itself, for the business model in general, for these named peers: Kitron (Nordic contract manufacturer whose 2026 growth is defence-led), Scanfil (Nordic contract manufacturer expanding capacity into electrification), Note (Swedish contract manufacturer with slow 2026 organic growth), HANZA (Swedish contract manufacturer and acquirer with a history of low margins), Incap (Finnish contract manufacturer under Indian price pressure in 2026), Inission (Swedish contract manufacturer, a direct peer), Hammond Power Solutions (listed North American dry-type transformer maker exposed to data-centre demand), Tamura (Japanese transformer maker adding Mexican capacity), KB Components (Swedish component roll-up), SFS Group (Swiss component maker running a five-site network-streamlining programme), Bekaert (Belgian wire processor that closed 13 plants since 2019 to lift margins), Daetwyler (Swiss component maker running the ForwardNow cost programme), and for the sector. For each: the split of fixed and variable costs as far as it can be established; how margins have moved with volume in past up-cycles and down-cycles (the precedents, dated, with figures); cost programmes announced and what they actually delivered, against what was promised and how long it took; which cost lines proved cuttable and which did not, and why. Test these points: What share of AQ Group's cost base is pass-through material, how much of personnel cost in its Bulgarian, Polish, Lithuanian and Estonian plants flexes with volume, and how large and fixed are site and head-office overheads? How did AQ Group's operating profit and margin move against revenue in 2020 (when Q2 2020 organic sales fell about 21 percent), in 2022 (input inflation), in 2023 and in the 2024 heavy-truck downturn, and why did profit hold when revenue fell? What explains the Q3 2026 result, and how much did high utilisation in the US and China during European holidays contribute, as management stated?

What does underutilisation cost at the Mexico and Plattsburgh, New York wiring plants and at the India inductive plant, and what actions and timelines exist to fill or resize them? How fast and at what cost have acquired units reached group margins (mdexx Magnetronics from about minus 7 to plus 7 percent EBT after a personnel-cost reduction; Michael Riedel Transformatorenbau; Time 24, acquired May 2026 and below expectations in Q3 2026; Norlake, acquired September 2026), and what does the Monitor ERP roll-out save?

What do express freight, overtime and temporary labour cost when delivery precision falls (company KPI 91.1 percent in Q3 2026 against a 98 percent target), using the 2017, 2021 and 2022 episodes? Have contract manufacturers sustained EBIT margins near 10 percent through a full cycle, and what happened to the named peers' margins in the 2008-09, 2020 and 2023-24 downturns? What did SFS's network streamlining, Bekaert's plant closures and Daetwyler's ForwardNow programme deliver against what was promised, and over what time?

What margin do medium-voltage data-centre transformers and custom inductive systems earn compared with wiring harnesses and sheet metal at AQ Group or comparable makers?. What most needs answering: Whether AQ Group's record of holding operating profit through revenue declines survives a volume-led European industrial recession now that more profit comes from capacity-constrained data-centre and defence lines, and how much of the 2026 margin step is temporary utilisation versus durable mix and cost.
