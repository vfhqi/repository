Section 1 – Key question, brief, and competing hypotheses 10 findings

Section summary
  1. The multi-year wave hypothesis is the most plausible because staggered employer size reporting thresholds and uneven national transposition extend the software compliance purchasing window into fiscal year 2028. The core of this analysis addresses the verbatim Key Question: is the EU Pay Transparency Directive creating durable demand for the compliance products and services that Kainos sells, over the next 12 to 24 months, or only a short burst of demand around its June 2026 start date? To evaluate this question, we test four mutuallyexclusive and collectively-exhaustive hypotheses: Hypothesis 1 (multi-year wave of sustained demand), Hypothesis 2 (pull-forward spike and near-term fade), Hypothesis 3 (competitor or partner displacement), and Hypothesis 4 (regulatory delay or dilution). The Kainos offering relies on its Pay Transparency Analyzer, which is resold directly by Workday's sales force, alongside consulting services for data clean-up and job architecture.

Regulatory Deadlines and Headcount Thresholds

1.
Regulatory tiers: The Directive structures compliance by employer size, requiring organizations with 250 or more workers to submit their initial gender pay reports by June 7, 2027, and continue reporting annually thereafter
2.
Medium employers: Mid-sized employers with 150 to 249 workers must also report by June 7, 2027, but are permitted to submit subsequent disclosures every three years
3.
Small employers: Smaller entities with 100 to 149 employees face a significantly delayed timeline, with their first mandatory reports not due until June 7, 2031
4.
Lowered thresholds: Several countries like Spain and Portugal are gold-plating the rules by proposing to extend mandatory reporting to employers with 50 or more workers
5.
Right to information: The Article 7 information right applies to all employees irrespective of company size, meaning even small employers face administrative burdens

Fragmented Member State Transposition

6.
Uneven implementation: By the June 7, 2026, deadline, only four Member States–Italy, Lithuania, Malta, and Slovakia–had fully completed transposition of the Directive
7.
Active laggards: Major European economies like Germany missed the deadline, and the responsible ministry does not expect reporting obligations to apply before June 2028
8.
Extended opportunity: Due to these legislative delays, Broker Deutsche Bank Research notes that the compliance opportunity window has extended to FY28
9.
French delay: In France, parliamentary voting on transposition is delayed, with the government confirming that the existing professional equality index will continue through 2027
10.
Belgian gaps: Belgium has only partially transposed the public sector portion, leaving private-sector employers without comprehensive federal legislation
