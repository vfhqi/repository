Polenergia's FY 2025 earnings print delivered a harsh operational reality check beneath the triumphant offshore wind narrative, as depressed onshore wind performance and severe profitability compression exposed the utility's acute near-term vulnerability while its EUR 5.8 billion Baltic offshore construction campaign remained on schedule but left minimal financial buffer for execution errors. Polenergia reported its full-year 2025 financial results on March 12, 2026, revealing consolidated sales revenue declined to PLN 4,225.8 million, representing a 2.2% drop from PLN 4,320.5 million in FY 2024, driven by a PLN 202.8 million slump in the onshore wind power segment due to depressed power prices and unfavorable wind conditions. The earnings print deviated materially from sell-side expectations that had focused on offshore execution milestones rather than the deteriorating cash generation profile of legacy assets. The statutory bottom line collapse represented the most significant consensus deviation, as the group recorded a statutory net loss of PLN 124.6 million for FY 2025, down from a net profit of PLN 301.2 million in the prior year, severely impacted by PLN 176.4 million of non-operating impairment charges recognized in the fourth quarter. This dramatic PLN 425.8 million swing in statutory profitability highlighted management's aggressive portfolio rationalization and strategic pivot away from speculative hydrogen and retail solar ventures.

Adjusted net profit plummeted to PLN 47.3 million, representing a dramatic PLN 260.1 million decline against PLN 307.4 million achieved in FY 2024, primarily driven by rising financial expenses including higher debt servicing costs and derivative valuation impacts.

The balance sheet transformation proved the most striking strategic shift, as gearing rose rapidly with net debt reaching PLN 1,553 million by December 31, 2025, up from PLN 615 million at end-2024, representing a significant escalation in capital intensity that pushed the consolidated leverage ratio to 26% from negative 1% in the prior year. This 152.5% surge in net debt reflected the group's aggressive funding of its offshore wind equity contributions and the drawdown of project-level facilities.

The offshore wind joint venture execution remained the primary source of management optimism and market focus. Management focused heavily on confirming that the flagship MFW Bałtyk II and MFW Bałtyk III projects, totaling 1.44 GW, are progressing fully on schedule and within budget following their final investment decisions, targeting first power in 2027 and full commercial operations by 2028. The partnership with Equinor successfully closed a non-recourse project finance package exceeding EUR 6 billion, insulating the parent utility's balance sheet from direct project-level default risk while simultaneously constraining liquidity through extensive corporate guarantees.

To secure the financing agreements, Polenergia issued parent company guarantees capping additional contributions at EUR 83.97 million for Bałtyk II and EUR 100.41 million for Bałtyk III, with outstanding PCGs standing at EUR 72.96 million for Bałtyk II and EUR 83.09 million for Bałtyk III as of December 31, 2025. This EUR 156 million outstanding contingent liability represents a material near-term capital risk if Baltic seabed geotechnical challenges or supply chain disruptions trigger cost overruns.

Management's strategic repositioning centered on portfolio purification through aggressive divestments. The company finalized the sale of its 116 MW gas-fired combined heat and power plant in Nowa Sarzyna for PLN 139.7 million in January 2026, and the sale of its prosumer solar subsidiary Polenergia Fotowoltaika was finalized in the second quarter of 2026 to exit the struggling retail solar market. These structural exits cemented Polenergia's transformation into a pure-play renewable utility focused exclusively on wind generation and gridscale battery storage.

The EPS trajectory faces prolonged near-term suppression as capital intensity peaks during the 2026-2028 construction window. Sell-side expectations for near-term EPS are highly constrained as interest expenses and high capital intensity will depress cash conversion over the next 12 to 18 months, with analysts anticipating EPS will remain highly volatile until first power from the Baltic offshore joint venture starts contributing in late 2027. Management's projection of PLN 1.6 billion EBITDA by 2030 implies a more than threefold expansion from FY 2025 levels, contingent entirely on flawless offshore execution.

Risk management through systematic hedging programs partially mitigated macroeconomic volatility. Polenergia entered 2026 with 92% of its generation target hedged at a weighted average net price of PLN 410/MWh, protecting its portfolio against a downward trend in forward power prices and shielding the group's near-term revenue from immediate spot market volatility. At the project level, the Bałtyk II and Bałtyk III project companies entered into interest rate swap transactions covering 90% of their planned EURIBOR-based interest rate volatility, providing crucial macro protection for the capital-intensive Baltic builds. Concurrently, the company converted its own funds deposited on escrow accounts into euros at an average exchange rate of 4.25, hedging EUR 123.79 million in capital exposure and protecting Polenergia's capital reserves from volatile EUR/PLN foreign exchange swings during construction.
