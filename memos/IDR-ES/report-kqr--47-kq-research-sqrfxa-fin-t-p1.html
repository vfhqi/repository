Section 2 – Cost Structure and Fixed-Variable Splits

Section summary
  1. Indra's high structural fixed costs generate significant operating leverage as defense volumes scale.
  2. Spanish salary indexation and specialized engineering talent scarcity create rigid cost floors across divisions.

Personnel Cost Splits

1.
Rigid labor components: Indra's personnel costs reached EUR 2,823.3 million in FY25, split into EUR 2,131.3 million in wages, EUR 654.7 million in social security, and EUR 37.3 million in termination benefits, which represent a highly fixed baseline per the company's annual report of 25 February 2026. This rigid cost base represents approximately 56% of total revenue, meaning that operating margins are highly sensitive to wage movements. Consequently, any failure to expand sales will severely depress EPS because these fixed commitments cannot be quickly reduced.
2.
Unequal workforce distribution: Indra's business divisions carry highly divergent headcount profiles, with Minsait employing 46,564 staff compared to only 8,325 in Defence as of December 2025 per the company's October 2026 presentation. This massive IT labor force means that Minsait's margins are structurally exposed to service delivery inflation. However, Defence's smaller headcount recently expanded by 36% to capture the Spanish Special Modernization Programs, which means capacity costs are front-loaded ahead of actual program execution.
3.
Rigid salary indexation: Spanish collective bargaining agreements create severe salary indexation constraints for Indra. According to the company's earnings call from 27 July 2018, every one percentage point wage increase adds approximately EUR 17 million in recurring operating costs. Because Minsait is bound by the 18th Consulting Collective Agreement and Defence operates under Madrid's Metal Association guidelines, salary floors are structurally protected. This means that unless Indra matches inflation with higher price pass-throughs, underlying operating margins will contract and degrade EPS.
4.
Scarce engineering resources: Defence engineering talent scarcity severely limits Indra's cost flexibility while escalating competitive hiring pressures. Bernstein Research noted on 7 October 2026 that European defense firms compete intensely with civilian tech sectors, meaning recruitment and retention costs remain high. Because defense specialists require years of highly technical training, capacity is difficult to scale quickly in response to procurement cycles. This structural constraint means that operating margins will suffer unless headcount additions are immediately absorbed by rapid project execution.
