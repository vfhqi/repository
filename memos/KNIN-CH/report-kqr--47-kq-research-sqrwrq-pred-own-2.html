Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Carrier rate drops expand forwarder spreads temporarily, but the timing of customer repricing remains critical for earnings durability.
  2. Carrier rate drops widen forwarder gross profit spreads temporarily as buy rates fall faster than customer contracts can reprice. During past cycles, this temporary spread widening typically normalized within two to four quarters as customer contracts repriced. Subscale or less integrated acquisitions initially dilute gross profit per unit before synergy realization restores margins.

Carrier Spreads and EPS Impact

1.
The Key Question: "For Kuehne + Nagel International AG (KNIN-CH) and its peers DSV, DHL Global Forwarding and Expeditors, how did ocean and air gross profit per unit behave in the first, second, third and fourth quarters after carrier spot rates fell by more than 20% in past cycles (2016, 2019, 2023), how long did the temporary widening of the buy-sell spread last under annual, quarterly and monthly customer contracts, and what do the 2027 Asia-Europe and transpacific contract tenders, the share of volume on spot against contract, and the shift to three-to-six-month "mini bids" imply for the timing of repricing in 2027?"
2.
Critical transmission: Freight forwarder earnings are highly sensitive to the buy-sell spread because changes in carrier rates create temporary margin expansions. Bernstein Research reported on October 23, 2026, that forwarding gross profits are structurally independent of absolute carrier freight rates but expand during rapid rate drops due to procurement lag. Understanding this timing allows investors to project when temporary margin expansion will normalize and impact earnings per share.
3.
Accelerated repricing: Contract negotiations in 2027 will test the durability of forwarding margins as customers shift from annual commitments to shorter bids. Expert interviews on November 2, 2026, revealed that shippers increasingly favor three-to-six-month mini-bids over annual contracts. This shift implies that the historical lag benefit will compress, forcing quicker pricing normalization and threatening forecast earnings.
4.
Substantial baseline: Kuehne + Nagel and DSV command leading global scale across sea and air trade lanes as of late 2026. Bernstein Research reported on October 23, 2026, that Kuehne + Nagel achieved sea gross profit of CHF 461 per TEU and air gross profit of CHF 84 per 100 kg. Across our review of 102 relevant sources, four target companies, and three past cycles, DSV reported Q3 2026 ocean gross profit of DKK 4,142 per TEU, proving that scale players maintain resilient yields despite integration headwinds.
5.
Divergent yield: DHL Global Forwarding and Expeditors exhibit distinct yield structures that influence how their gross profits react to falling spot rates. Deutsche Post DHL reported on November 5, 2026, that Q3 2026 ocean freight gross profit reached EUR 359 per TEU and air freight gross profit was EUR 645 per ton. These metrics contrast with Expeditors, which maintains a flexible carrier mix to support unitary profitability during rate fluctuations.
6.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research argued on July 16, 2026, that freight rate movements do correlate with gross profit yields, contrasting with the view that forwarder margins are independent of carrier rates. This correlation suggests that sustained carrier spot rate declines will directly erode forwarder gross profit per unit and reduce earnings.
7.
Other side of the story: While short-term procurement spreads widen immediately after carrier rate drops, customer contracts can rapidly normalize these gains. Therefore, the timing of customer contract repricing remains a critical factor that determines the duration of profitability for asset-light logistics providers.

Section 3 – Contract Durations and Buy-Sell Spread Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Carrier rate declines temporarily expand forwarder buy-sell spreads as customer pricing contracts reprice with a distinct lag.
  2. Customer contract durations of six to twelve months delay pricing normalization, while monthly contracts accelerate spread compression.
  3. High spot exposure allows forwarders to optimize procurement yields opportunistically, but competitive bidding structurally erodes long-term spreads.

Contract Pricing Lags

1.
Gradual rate adjustments: Freight forwarders do not pass on carrier purchase price changes to their customers instantaneously. Bernstein Research reported on October 13, 2026, that rate changes are passed on with a lag to customers. This delay creates a temporary gross profit expansion during rate downturns because buy rates decline much faster than customer contracts can reprice. Ultimately, this dynamic drives temporary earnings per share outperformance before prices normalize.
2.
Extended pass-through times: DHL Global Forwarding faces a defined lag in aligning its air freight gross profits with broader outbound market pricing shifts. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on October 7, 2024, that DHL's air gross profit per ton correlates to the outbound price index lagged four months. This index correlation demonstrates that ocean and air forwarders enjoy a multi-month cushion when buy rates drop rapidly. Consequently, near-term division margins expand until customer contracts reprice.
3.
Accelerated repricing cycles: DSV has seen the structural lag in its unit gross profits compress significantly over consecutive global logistics cycles. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 28, 2022, that DSV's lag shortened to just four weeks from its historical pre-pandemic average of six to eight weeks. This compression occurs because customers track rate indices actively and demand immediate price reductions as carrier spot rates collapse. Shorter lags restrict the duration of gross profit per unit expansion during downcycles.
4.
Lagged contract adjustments: Freight forwarders experience gross margin squeezes when carrier buy rates rebound because customer pricing adjustments lag the spot market. Morgan Stanley reported on July 6, 2026, that rising carrier buy rates pass through with a lag, hurting gross margins sequentially as sell-side contract repricing lags carrier procurement costs. Conversely, when spot rates decline, this same lag mechanism drives immediate yield benefits for forwarders. This asymmetrical lag directly influences the timing of forwarder profit peaks.

Customer Contract Durations

5.
Diverse customer agreements: Commercial logistics customers typically restrict their procurement commitments to shorter horizons to maintain rate flexibility. A former logistics president reported on May 26, 2026, that customers prefer contracts of six or twelve months at a time. These shorter durations require logistics providers to implement pricing adjustment mechanisms to manage fluctuating carrier buy rates. Frequent contractual resets prevent forwarders from retaining high transactional spreads over multi-quarter periods.
6.
Flexible quarterly arrangements: Shippers utilize shorter rate validity periods to capitalize on falling spot markets and optimize their freight spend. A Maersk logistics executive reported on March 12, 2025, that customers routinely contract for next quarter or half-year intervals. These short-term contracts allow customers to shop around and refuse general rate increases if carrier capacity is abundant. Consequently, forwarders face intense pressure to adjust their sell rates sequentially to prevent volume loss.
7.
Rapid monthly repricing: Expeditors International relies on short-duration contract structures to maintain tight alignment between buy and sell rates. A former manager at Expeditors reported on January 11, 2023, that the company frequently adjusts customer contracts monthly to match volatile ocean rates. This highly responsive pricing model protects the company's unitary profitability from being squeezed during abrupt market spikes. However, this fast adjustment also means that temporary spread expansions dissolve quickly after spot rate drops.
8.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research argued on March 12, 2025, that freight forwarder gross profit yields show a direct correlation with absolute freight rates over cycles. This view contradicts the traditional assumption that forwarder spreads are purely driven by procurement lag and operate independently of absolute carrier rate levels. Under this alternative correlation model, a prolonged drop in absolute spot rates directly compresses forwarder yields instead of expanding them.
9.
Multi-year contract baselines: DSV Solutions maintains longer-duration contracts in its contract logistics segment compared to its transactional freight forwarding operations. DSV reported in its February 15, 2024, base prospectus that the division's standard contract length is three to five years. These long-term agreements incorporate inflation indexation and open-book cost-plus structures to insulate margins from volatile operational costs. Consequently, contract logistics yields provide a highly visible, stable earnings stream that behaves independently of short-term spot rate cycles.

Contract Mix and Margin Impact

10.
Flexible procurement strategies: Expeditors International utilizes a highly adaptable pricing model that balances global carrier contracts with localized procurement. Expeditors reported in its March 1, 2023, filing that it enters fixed pricing for forecasted commitments while negotiating spot rates locally. This blend allows the company to secure space with core partners while exploiting cheap spot capacity during market downturns. This flexible mix protects unitary margins during periods of falling demand and capacity oversupply.
11.
Opportunistic capacity sourcing: DHL Global Forwarding optimizes its gross profits by dynamically adjusting its mix of spot and contracted carrier capacity. Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on October 7, 2024, that DHL secures thirty to forty percent of base capacity through long-term commitments. The company deliberately shifts its remaining procurement to the spot market when it expects carrier rates to decline. This opportunistic purchasing expands the buy-sell spread sequentially by capturing cheap spot rates before customer contracts reprice.
12.
Historical buying opportunities: Expeditors International demonstrated the margin benefits of short-term spot procurement during previous cyclical downturns. Expeditors reported in its March 22, 2010, filing that ocean yields increased in early 2009 due to favorable buying in spot markets. However, these expanded spreads dissolved in the second half of 2009 as carriers removed capacity and raised rates, which the company absorbed before customer renegotiations. This historical case illustrates how spot rate volatility creates highly transitory margin benefits.
13.
Severe yield pressure: Expeditors International faced significant margin headwinds during the post-pandemic market normalization as carrier capacity exceeded demand. Jefferies reported on November 8, 2023, that the company experienced continued rate pressure across ocean and air lanes in Q3 2023. This soft pricing environment forced forwarders to pass through lower rates rapidly to retain customers, capping any potential spread expansion from falling buy rates. Consequently, excess capacity accelerates contract repricing and compresses the historical lag benefit.
14.
Transitory spread widening: The temporary widening of forwarder buy-sell spreads behaves as a short-term cyclical benefit that typically runs its course within one to two quarters. Bernstein Research indicated on October 13, 2026, that customers eventually align contract rates with spot floors, reversing any near-term yield gains for forwarders. This cyclical reversion implies that forwarders with high contract exposure experience a slower but more inevitable margin compression compared to spot-heavy players. Ultimately, the durability of forwarding earnings depends on volume recoveries rather than sustained spread expansions.
15.
Other side of the story: While short-term procurement lags widen spreads, freight forwarders are increasingly forced to shorten contract terms to satisfy yield-conscious shippers. An international supply chain expert noted on November 5, 2026, that forwarders frequently give up margins to secure contracts during competitive tenders. This competitive pressure means that the historical buy-sell spread benefit is structurally eroding, as digitised procurement platforms and shorter 'mini-bids' allow shippers to capture lower carrier buy rates almost instantaneously.

Section 4 – The 2027 Contract Tenders and Repricing Timing

Tender Contract Dynamics
