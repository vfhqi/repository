[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Beijer Alma is a self-help case inside a serial-acquirer transition, and the self-help is the part that has been proved: since April 2025 an acting chief executive with a thirteen-year Indutrade record has cut the loss-making tail of Beijer Components, taken its adjusted EBITA margin to 20.0 percent, pointed the group's surplus cash at Beijer Tech and had the board set formal targets, and the sell-side has raised its numbers for six quarters. At 308.5 SEK and 17.8x 24-month-forward earnings the shares already pay for the company's own target delivered in full at today's multiple, so what is left to earn is the part not yet proved: organic growth and a permanent chief executive. The case is material at the inputs and at the outputs, and not material at the price.

  • The change is internal, multi-dimensional and real.
    • Five of the twelve internal change forces are strong and two more are live, which clears the screen's rule of three strong forces twice over
    • The one false-positive flag that fits is the caretaker chief executive.
    • Chief executive: Henrik Perbeck left on 1 April 2025 after seven years
      • Johnny Alvarsson, a director since 2017 and Indutrade's chief executive for thirteen years to 2017, became acting chief executive
      • The board's permanent appointee, Oscar Fredell, took office on 3 November 2025 and resigned on 26 January 2026 after twelve weeks
      • Alvarsson was re-appointed acting chief executive the same day and also runs Beijer Components.
    • Targets: on 6 February 2026 the board set EBITA growth above 15 percent a year over a cycle (10 to 12 percent acquired, 3 to 5 percent organic) and capital efficiency, EBITA over working capital, above 50 percent, against a five-year EBITA growth rate of 11 percent a year and a ten-year rate of 9 percent.
    • Actions: the June 2025 Lesjöfors action plan (MSEK -120, completed in Q4 2025)
      • Alcomex's US door-spring business wound down
      • MSEK 50 of low-margin contracts discontinued
      • Turkish contracts exited
      • A factory closed
      • Five acquisitions in 17 days in Q2 2026 adding about MSEK 415 of annualised sales.
  • The external forces are a mild net headwind, which the internal forces have so far out-run.
    • European industrial demand is flat (group organic sales 0 percent and order bookings down 3 percent organically in Q2 2026), the krona and interest rates are headwinds, and the only tailwinds are on the supply side (gross margin 32.6 percent in Q2 2026 from 30.7 percent a year earlier) and in the thinning competition for acquisition targets.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on margin, cash and estimates, and not on organic revenue.
    • The group adjusted EBITA margin was 16.2 percent in Q2 2026 from 15.1 percent
    • Beijer Components' was 20.0 percent from 17.8 percent
    • Free cash flow was 448 MSEK in FY2025, 78.9 percent of reported net income, from 205 MSEK and 28.7 percent in FY2024
    • Consensus adjusted earnings per share grow 17.8 percent in FY2026 and 17.1 percent in FY2027 from the FY2025 base of 11.92 SEK
    • FY2027 estimates rose 9.2 percent in the six months to 2026-10-07.
    • Revenue: reported growth of 5.3 percent in Q2 2026 was almost all acquired
      • Beijer Components' organic sales fell 2 percent.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It shows in the resources (two chief-executive changes), the choices (the targets, the capital rule, the rename to Beijer Components), the actions (the tail cut, the deals), the key operating metrics (margin; capital efficiency 48.7 percent in Q2 2026 from 43.0 percent), the financial statements (two quarters of beats) and the sell-side's estimates (four brokers at Buy with rising targets).
    • What it has not produced: organic growth, and a permanent chief executive
      • The acting chief executive is 76, on one month's notice, and the vacancy had been open nine months as at our latest research.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share on the brokers' basis of 11.92 SEK in FY2025 rising to about 17.4 SEK in FY2028, 13.4 percent a year, and about 10 percent a year beyond, because organic growth stays near flat, the krona bites on three quarters of sales and consensus already carries the announced deals.
    • Against the company's target: our path sits below the 15 percent EBITA growth target on the acquired-growth line, about 6 percent a year against 10 to 12 percent.
  • What it costs: 308.5 SEK, or 17.8x 24-month-forward consensus earnings of 17.30 SEK, the 79th percentile of the stock's own five-year and ten-year history (five-year median 15.89x), and above the ten-year 'defendable' band of 17.50x.
    • What the price assumes: on the company's own target path with the multiple held at 17.8x, the eighteen-month return is about 20 percent a year
      • The market has priced the target, delivered, at today's multiple.
    • Against peers: a discount to the Swedish compounders (Lifco at a price-to-earnings ratio of 26.5x, Röko 22.2x), explained by the record: 9 percent return on incremental capital over ten years, leverage of 2.30x in 2024, two chief-executive exits.
  • The return on our modal path does not reach the bar.
    • With the multiple drifting to a 16.7x blend, the 'Modal return' is 5.8 percent a year over eighteen months and 8.8 percent a year over three, against the framework's 20 percent bar
    • Holding today's multiple gives 10.3 percent and 10.7 percent, which is a D and is not our modal.
    • Where the chain holds: from the inputs through to layer six, the sell-side's estimates.
    • Where it breaks: at the price
      • The re-rating leg is negative on our blend and positive only in the one-in-five branch where a permanent chief executive is in post, FY2027 EBITA grows 12 percent or more with organic growth positive, and the market pays compounder multiples again.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major Corporate Change', at B: crisp on the inputs and the outputs, blurred on the resource layer and on the one output the pattern wants most, organic growth.
    • 'Compounder' is a secondary fit at C, because the mis-modelled line is named but the upgrade record is six quarters, not years.
  • What breaks the case: Beijer Components' margin below 17 percent, or its organic growth below -3 percent after the tail-cutting ends in late 2026, which would confirm that the margin was the arithmetic of cutting volume
    • A failed or further-delayed appointment
    • Or a de-rating of the cohort.
  • What would make it compelling: a price near the ten-year median multiple (about 273 SEK on current consensus earnings; about 255 SEK for a B return on our modal path), or, at today's price, a permanent appointment of a decentralised-model operator together with two consecutive quarters of positive organic growth in Beijer Components.

  • The case therefore: a good company story that the shares have already paid for.

    • We park it on price, not on quality.

  • Rating = C, because the input forces (B) and the outputs (B) are real and have propagated to the sell-side, but the return they buy at 17.8x is below the framework's D band on our modal path (F), and in this Building Block the parts bind: materiality is measured through to the return.

    • Not B, because a B needs the modal return at or near the bar and it is not
    • Not D, because the forces and the outputs are the strongest current prints in the cohort and the chain has reached layer six.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against is the "false friend": that the margin gains are the arithmetic of cutting volume, not of demand or pricing.
    • The case against, at its strongest: Beijer Components' margin rose because MSEK 50 of contracts, SEK 35 million a quarter of Alcomex sales and the Turkish business were cut, while its organic volume contracted 2 percent in Q2 2026
      • The ten-year record is a 9 percent return on incremental capital
      • The 15 percent EBITA growth target sits against an 11 percent five-year record
      • Leverage rose from 0.60x in 2018 to 2.30x in 2024
      • And the board mis-hired a chief executive who left after twelve weeks.
      • On this reading the recovery is subtraction mistaken for operating leverage, and when the subtraction ends the organic line shows what is underneath.
    • What we concede: the volume point, the capital-return record and the leadership risk, in full.
      • Beijer Components has not grown organically, and we cannot show from the printed accounts that it will.
    • Why we hold our view: the margin is nonetheless real money: group adjusted EBITA was 346 MSEK in Q2 2026, up 13.5 percent, and gross margin rose to 32.6 percent from 30.7 percent, so pricing as well as subtraction is in it.
      • Beijer Tech's 19 percent return on incremental capital and its five-deal quarter are a different engine from the Components record.
      • And the price, not the company, is where the case fails: the false-friend hypothesis and our modal path agree that the shares do not earn the bar from 308.5 SEK, so the hypothesis changes the reason for the park, not the park.
    • Where the case against would win: if Beijer Components' organic growth is still negative two quarters after the tail-cutting ends in late 2026.
      • That is the one print that separates the two readings, and it is why the 'Change Forces' rating would fall to C if it arrives.
