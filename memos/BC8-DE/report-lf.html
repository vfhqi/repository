EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Conclusions on the CEO, Governance and Credibility

Thomas Olemotz is best read as a disciplined, finance-led steward who clearly shows decisiveness, capital allocation, communication and standards, but shows adaptability and transformational drive only partly, because he describes Bechtle as "always more or less a fast follower" and his conservative style could be interpreted as a caretaker approach. - Earnings Calls His delivery record is resilient but not a clean upward line: revenue moved from €6,422.70 mm to €6,305.80 mm to €6,405.90 mm while EBT margin slid from 5.80% to 5.10% and EPS from €2.13 to €1.82 over FY2023– FY2025, before Q3 2026 earnings before tax rose 13.90% to €91.70 mm. Bechtle's governance is formally robust but concentrated: each of the 126 million ordinary shares carries one vote, Deloitte issued unqualified opinions and no material legal or regulatory actions were reported as pending, yet Karin Schick holds a dominant 34.03 percent stake.

Director and officer share dealing signals alignment rather than discretionary conviction, because directors must immediately invest earmarked pay in ordinary shares, a mandatory programme that ensures continuous insider buying, so the transactions may overstate actual executive optimism.

The single largest credibility risk is the Vision 2030 margin promise: a 5 percent EBT margin and over €500 mm of EBT by 2030 against FY2025 EBT of €324.20 mm, which must be delivered by successor Konstantin Ebert from January 1, 2027 despite an estimated three percent EBT headwind from the SAP S/4HANA migration in FY2026, a primarily hardware-focused, lower-value service mix and an expert view that services margins are under pressure and likely to decline.
