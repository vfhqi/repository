Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Corporate actions across European chemicals will determine whether companies can successfully transition to high-value specialties and deleverage. Consolidation and structural splits could materially alter EPS trajectories over the next 18 months.

Restructuring and strategic direction

1.
Key Question: "Which buyers are bidding for Syensqo's Performance and Care segment and its Novecare and Technology Solutions units, at what EBITDA multiples, and when is a signing expected? Since Evonik reportedly rejected BASF's first approach in late September 2026, where do BASF, Evonik and the RAG-Stiftung stand, and do brokers see a purchase followed by a split of Evonik? Is BASF's mid-2027 minority listing of Agricultural Solutions on track, at what valuation? Do brokers or Henkel's management see the new separate legal entities for its adhesives and household brands businesses as a step toward a separation? What do brokers expect Lanxess to receive for its Envalior stake before or at the April 2028 backstop?."
2.
Deleveraging imperative: Balance sheet constraints are driving urgent portfolio decisions across European chemicals as refinancing risks rise. For instance, Lanxess is facing sustained leverage pressures of approximately 4.1x EBITDA pre-exceptionals through fiscal year 2026. Successfully monetizing non-core stakes is crucial to reduce net debt and restore leverage back to target levels below 2.5x.
3.
Portfolio high-grading: Strategic divestments are helping companies transition into high-growth specialty pure- plays to unlock sum-of-the-parts value. Syensqo completed the sale of its Oil and Gas unit for €135 million, which represents a 7x EBITDA multiple. This transaction helps sharpen the strategic focus on specialty materials while improving the group's margin profile by shedding lower-margin commodity businesses.

Valuation and EPS trajectories

4.
Capital allocation: Chemical consolidators are seeking value-accretive acquisitions at depressed valuations to future-proof their operations. BASF is exploring a potential takeover of Evonik to downstream its portfolio, although the deal faces severe market skepticism. This transaction could significantly alter future earnings trajectories and buybacks if cash is diverted from promised shareholder returns.
5.
Operational separation: Separating industrial and consumer operations into distinct legal entities can act as a crucial precursor to full corporate splits. Henkel's operational separation of Adhesives and Consumer Brands aims to build strong standalone businesses. This step could eventually pave the way for separation or joint ventures to unlock latent valuation multiples.
6.
Dividend sustainability: Pure-play holdings are highly dependent on the cash flows and dividend pass-throughs of their underlying operations. Solvac holds a 31.39% stake in Syensqo and is highly exposed to its dividend policy. Any pressure on cash flows from prolonged industrial restructuring or expensive decarbonization capital expenditure could jeopardize future dividend support for reference shareholders.
7.
Industry consolidation: Broader mergers in the coatings sector indicate that consolidation is rapidly shifting from theory to reality. Akzo Nobel is progressing its proposed merger with Axalta to form a combined entity named Axanzo. This combination aims to capture significant run-rate cost synergies of $600 million within three years post-close.
8.
Refinancing risks: Refinancing sensitivity is a major concern for chemical firms as more than half of the sector's debt matures shortly. Many companies are managing interest expenses amidst temporarily elevated energy costs. High refinancing costs could create a substantial earnings headwind equivalent to more than 1% of EBITDA for many European producers.

Syensqo's planned divestments could suffer from complex overheads and lower liquidity if the market fails to re-rate the remaining core business. Furthermore, high interest costs and raw material headwinds could dilute the expected EPS accretion from these structural moves.

Section 2 – Syensqo Performance & Care Strategic Review and Bidding Activity

Section summary
  1. Syensqo's strategic review of its Performance & Care segment could unlock significant valuation upside if a transaction is signed at target multiples.
  2. Our confidence in this segment's divestment timeline remains medium because strategic buyers face near-term balance sheet constraints.

Strategic review and segment structure

1.
Strategic divestments: Syensqo is conducting a strategic assessment of its Performance and Care division to transition into a pure-play specialty chemical firm. This division consists of the Novecare and Technology Solutions business units that specialize in surface chemistry and reagents. Slicing off these assets will allow the remaining core to command higher materials-focused multiples. Consequently, the successful execution of this sale is critical to accelerate Specialty Materials margin growth over the next 18 months.
2.
Portfolio focus: Syensqo’s management is shifting its corporate focus away from upstream commodity chemicals to focus on high-value aerospace and electronics markets. Berenberg notes that divesting the Performance and Care segment is highly desired by investors who view it as dilutive to the core polymers growth story. Slicing off these dilutive segments will align the company's valuation with high-growth pure-play peers. This strategic separation should narrow the persistent valuation gap with specialty material competitors.
3.
Segment valuation: Bernstein Research values the Performance and Care segment at a sum-of-the-parts trading multiple of 7.2x EV/EBITDA. This composite multiple consists of Novecare valued at 6.0x and Technology Solutions valued at 8.4x on a trading basis. But if a full sale is completed, the segment could achieve a 9.2x transaction multiple after factoring in takeover premiums. A final transaction at this elevated level would lift group earnings per share through cash redeployment.
4.
Unit performance: Operational divergence continues to define the Performance and Care division as raw material dynamics shift. Technology Solutions is experiencing strong copper-related reagents demand while the Novecare surfactant business remains temporarily subdued. This mixed performance makes the combined segment difficult to sell as a single package to strategic consolidators. Therefore, management may be forced to accept a lower valuation multiple unless market volumes recover.

Prospective buyers and market interest

5.
Evonik interest: Evonik represents a highly credible strategic buyer for the Performance and Care segment because Novecare would strengthen its specialty surfactants business. However, Evonik is currently prioritizing balance sheet deleveraging and is unlikely to pursue major mergers and acquisitions until 2027. This delay means a near-term deal is unlikely unless Syensqo accepts an all-share transaction. Consequently, the expected signing timeline could slip beyond the next 12 months.
6.
Clariant constraints: Clariant would possess a strong strategic rationale to acquire Novecare to build out its care chemicals franchise. But Clariant is currently constrained by an expensive litigation process that is estimated to cost approximately CHF 2 billion. This massive legal headwind leaves Clariant without the financial capacity to engage in a highly competitive bidding process. As a result, Clariant is highly unlikely to submit a bid for Syensqo's assets.
7.
Arkema strategy: Arkema has historically acquired businesses at rich valuation multiples but has recently shifted its strategic focus toward organic growth and deleveraging. Bernstein Research believes a bid from Arkema would reinforce investor skepticism regarding its transaction track record. Furthermore, acquiring all of Performance and Care would push Arkema's leverage ratio above its comfortable limit of 3.0x. Thus, Syensqo cannot rely on Arkema acting as an anchor bidder during this process.

Section 3 – BASF, Evonik, and RAG-Stiftung Takeover and Split Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Exploratory talks between BASF and Evonik face substantial structural and political hurdles.
  2. We have low confidence in a near-term transaction because Evonik has already rejected the initial approach.

Takeover approach and initial responses

1.
Unsolicited takeover talks: BASF has confirmed it initiated exploratory discussions with specialty chemicals rival Evonik and its major shareholder regarding a potential corporate combination. Evonik separate announcements confirmed receipt of this non-binding proposal but clarified that no formal negotiations are currently taking place. If a transaction eventually succeeds, it would consolidate the highly fragmented European sector to build a more resilient regional champion. This tie-up would significantly broaden BASF downstream specialty earnings over the next three years.
2.
Immediate valuation impasse: Evonik quickly rejected BASF's initial unsolicited proposal of €22.15 per share as being inadequate. This initial offer valued the company at an equity value of approximately €10.3 billion. Rothschild & Co Redburn's report of 29 September 2026 notes that this offer represents a 22.58% premium (calculated) over the undisturbed pre-announcement share price of €18.07. Consequently, BASF must improve its offer valuation terms to re-engage Evonik's board and progress talks.
3.
Elevated cyclical margins: Deutsche Bank Research's note of 28 September 2026 argues that Evonik's recent financial performance makes a low-premium deal highly unlikely. The broker highlights that Evonik’s 2026 EBITDA has been heavily supported by temporary methionine price spikes following the Middle East conflict. Therefore, any acquirer would face distorted peak-cycle earnings metrics rather than normalized operating performance. This cyclical distortion will dilute BASF's pro forma earnings per share if the methionine cycle rapidly rolls over.

RAG-Stiftung and political headwinds

4.
Decisive foundation ownership: Bernstein Research's report of 28 September 2026 emphasizes that the RAG- Stiftung holds the ultimate key to unlocking any German consolidation. The civil-law foundation owns a controlling 43.8% stake in Evonik and manages the perpetual environmental liabilities of Germany's historical coal industry. Consequently, any transaction must guarantee stable dividend streams to cover RAG's annual liabilities. Bernstein calculates that RAG would require an ongoing dividend of €1.59 per share from the merged entity to preserve its funding model.
5.
Perpetual funding liabilities: Morgan Stanley's note of 29 September 2026 highlights that the RAG-Stiftung spent €309 million on its coal-related obligations in 2025 alone. This massive capital requirement means the foundation cannot accept any transaction that jeopardizes its near-term cash inflows. Therefore, a stockheavy merger would only work if the resulting entity maintains reliable dividend distributions over the long term. If cash yields fall, RAG will block the deal to prevent a critical funding shortfall.
6.
Political resistance: Political leaders in North Rhine-Westphalia are raising significant opposition to defend domestic manufacturing jobs and regional industrial sites. Trade unions are vocally criticizing BASF's approach over concerns regarding labor rights and potential plant closures. To neutralize these pressures, BASF is reportedly willing to guarantee German headcount until 2032 in any binding merger agreement. This heavy political compromise will restrict potential headcount cost-savings and dilute post-merger earnings per share.

Feasibility of buy and split

7.
Verbund compatibility test: Rothschild & Co Redburn's report of 29 September 2026 argues that any successful merger requires a highly disciplined buy-and-split plan. The broker's assessment is that only 60% to 65% of Evonik's EBITDA stems from divisions that fit BASF's internal supply chains. These core businesses include specialty additives, performance polymers, crosslinkers, and care chemicals. Separating the remaining 35% to 40% of non-core EBITDA is essential to improve pro forma cash flows and protect BASF's credit rating.

Historical precedents and strategic outlook

11.
Defensive consolidation risks: Deutsche Bank Research's note of 28 September 2026 compares the potential transaction to past large-scale chemical consolidations that historically failed to create value. The broker highlights that previous sector acquisitions, such as BASF's purchase of Ciba, destroyed value because buyers paid premium multiples. By contrast, acquiring Evonik at depressed, near-historical low multiples relies heavily on achieving operational value creation rather than multiple expansion. This lower entry valuation mitigates downside risks and protects long-term earnings per share.
12.
Capital deployment tension: Bernstein Research's report of 14 October 2026 notes that North American investors have reacted almost universally negatively to the potential transaction. Investors are concerned that a cashheavy deal will compete with promised share buybacks and the planned Agricultural Solutions initial public offering. Consequently, management must clarify how it will fund this acquisition without compromising its massive €12 billion shareholder return program.
13.
Other side of the story: While a disciplined buy-and-split transaction offers clear industrial logic, the combination faces severe execution risks and deep investor skepticism. Rothschild & Co Redburn's report of 29 September 2026 notes that purchase price amortization could create a long-term financial burden. Furthermore, the transaction fails to address structurally high European energy and feedstock costs. Therefore, investors may face persistent valuation compression unless BASF secures major concessions from German labor unions and completes non-core asset divestments rapidly.

Section 5 – Henkel Legal Restructuring and Adhesives-Household Brands Separation

Section summary
  1. Henkel's operational carve-out of its adhesives and consumer divisions into separate legal entities provides enhanced strategic and corporate flexibility.
  2. Our confidence in a near-term full separation remains low because management continues to emphasize group integration and digital IT alignment.

Corporate restructuring and legal carve-outs

1.
Corporate reorganization: Henkel's management has implemented a significant structural reorganization to divide its German operations into two separate subsidiaries. According to Henkel's Joint Hive-Down and Contract Report of 11 March 2026, the company transferred its global business units to Henkel Consumer Brands GmbH and Henkel Adhesive Technologies GmbH. This universal succession under German tax law establishes a highly formalized separate legal structure for both segments. This legal division allows the parent company to isolate liabilities while maintaining complete ownership.
