Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Diageo's Moët Hennessy stake critically impacts its earnings per share, cash flow, and leverage ratios.

Importance of the Associate Income Stream

1.
Essential inquiry: "What will Diageo plc (DGE-GB) earn from its 34% stake in Moët Hennessy over FY2027-30: how has its share of associates' profit and the dividends received moved since FY2022; what do LVMH's Wines and Spirits results for 2026 (shipments, pricing, operating margin, Chinese minimum import price arrangements for cognac, US tariffs) imply for Diageo's associate income; how do sell-side analysts model the line; and what are the terms and likelihood of Diageo exercising the put option at 80% of fair value?"
2.
Significant profits: Diageo's consolidated earnings per share depend heavily on the financial health of its principal associate Moët Hennessy. In fiscal year 2026, the group's share of after-tax profit from Moët Hennessy reached $227 million, representing the vast majority of its overall associate income. If this income stream declines, it directly reduces consolidated net profit and dilutes earnings per share. Thus, the performance of the associate directly determines Diageo's bottom-line EPS results.
3.
Direct cash: Diageo receives substantial cash distributions from Moët Hennessy that support its annual capital allocation. In fiscal year 2026, Diageo received $108 million in dividends from Moët Hennessy, down from $169 million in fiscal year 2025. Free cash flow is also impacted by structural changes, such as the cash payments made to terminate joint distribution agreements in multiple countries. A permanent reduction in these cash flows constrains Diageo's financial flexibility.
4.
Elevated leverage: Diageo's consolidated leverage ratios are highly sensitive to Moët Hennessy's operational performance. The company's net debt to EBITDA ratio, which reached 3.4x at the end of fiscal year 2025, incorporates the earnings contribution from the Moët Hennessy stake. Lower earnings from this associate magnify the impact of operating profit cuts on leverage ratios. Consequently, maintaining a healthy financial leverage ratio is challenging without a recovery in the associate's contribution.

Section 2 – Historical Associate Profit and Dividends Since FY2022

Section summary
  1. Diageo's Moët Hennessy stake generated volatile earnings and cash distributions during the post- pandemic macro cycle. Peer luxury spirits companies experienced identical trends as peak demand in fiscal years 2022 and 2023 reversed into destocking and margin compression.

Post-Pandemic Rise and Normalization of Moët Hennessy

1.
Rapid expansion: Moët Hennessy experienced exceptional growth during the post-pandemic period as global consumer demand spiked. LVMH's Wines and Spirits division capitalized on strong pricing power and massive volume gains in both cognac and champagne. This unprecedented surge represented a peak in the luxury spirits market before cyclical normalization began.
2.
Severe contraction: Moët Hennessy's financial performance deteriorated significantly from its high point as macroeconomic headwinds intensified. Standalone sales for the associate fell from $6,691 million in fiscal year 2024 to $6,100 million in fiscal year 2025 before a modest recovery to $6,199 million in fiscal year 2026. This downturn demonstrated the sharp reversal of post-pandemic demand across key international channels.
3.
Collapsed profits: Moët Hennessy's net profits halved as operating deleverage and inventory adjustments severely impacted margins. Standalone annual profit fell from $1,299 million in fiscal year 2024 to $644 million in fiscal year 2025. Although net income recovered slightly to $668 million in fiscal year 2026, these results highlight the severity of the cyclical downturn on the associate's bottom line.

Performance Downturn and Dividend Trends

4.
Reduced income: Diageo's share of profits from the 34% Moët Hennessy holding reflected the wider cyclical pressures. Diageo reported a share of associate after-tax profit from Moët Hennessy of $227 million in fiscal year 2026. This contribution represents a moderate recovery from prior lows but remains well below peak historical levels.
5.
Shrinking cash: Diageo's cash distributions from Moët Hennessy declined in tandem with the associate's profitability. Dividends received by Diageo from Moët Hennessy dropped to $108 million in fiscal year 2026. This reduction in cash flow constrained Diageo's overall free cash flow generation during the period.
6.
Declining payouts: Diageo's total dividend receipts from all associate investments have fallen continuously since the post-pandemic high. Total dividends received from associates dropped from $269 million in fiscal year 2024 to $175 million in fiscal year 2025 and finally to $116 million in fiscal year 2026. This trend underscores the ongoing pressure on associate cash flows across joint ventures.

Comparative Peer Group Performance

7.
Parallel downturn: Rémy Cointreau experienced a highly correlated boom and bust cycle that mirrors Moët Hennessy's performance. Rémy Cointreau's sales grew remarkably in fiscal years 2021 through 2023 but slumped heavily between fiscal years 2024 and 2026. This parallel trajectory confirms that the entire premium cognac segment suffered from identical cyclical headwinds.
8.
Volatile results: Rémy Cointreau's cognac division suffered massive sales declines as destocking took hold in the United States and China. Sales in Rémy's cognac division fell by 25.1% organically in fiscal year 2024. Revenue declined another 21.9% in fiscal year 2025, highlighting the extreme volatility of premium cognac during destocking phases.
9.
Depressed margins: Rémy Cointreau's profitability contracted sharply as a result of rising production costs and adverse mix. Current operating profit in Rémy's cognac division fell by 32.4% organically in fiscal year 2025, lowering its operating margin to 30.2%. This decline emphasizes how fixed cost deleverage hurts margins when shipment volumes plummet.
10.
Weakened performance: Pernod Ricard also reported negative organic trends as inventory adjustments hit its major spirits brands. Organic net sales for Pernod Ricard declined by 3.9% in fiscal year 2026. This drop shows that channel inventory corrections were widespread across the luxury spirits industry.
11.
Halved revenue: Pernod Ricard's cognac-heavy business in China experienced a severe multi-year sales contraction. Pernod Ricard China net sales hit a peak of €1.4 billion in fiscal year 2023 but halved to €0.7 billion by fiscal year 2026. This collapse illustrates the impact of government consumption crackdowns on premium international spirits.
12.
Strained margins: Brown-Forman faced similar margin headwinds due to elevated input costs and channel inventory fluctuations. Brown-Forman's gross margin contracted 150 basis points to 58.9% in fiscal year 2025. These pressures confirm that commodity inflation and lower production impacted even established whiskey portfolios.

Section 3 – LVMH Wines and Spirits 2026 Read-Through and Macro Headwinds

Section summary
  1. LVMH's Wines and Spirits division saw solid organic growth in 2026 but faced ongoing profit pressure.

The Chinese minimum import price deal helped Hennessy avoid steep tariffs but significantly increased cost of goods sold. New United States tariffs and structural shifts in cognac consumption present serious headwinds for Diageo's associate income.

Operational Results and Momentum

1.
Solid growth: LVMH reported that its Wines and Spirits division recorded organic revenue growth of 5% in the first nine months of 2026. This performance was driven by a solid recovery in European champagne shipments and positive momentum for Hennessy in Asia. Consequently, this operational resilience supports a stable short-term outlook for associate profit before accounting for tariff impacts.
2.
Champagne momentum: LVMH's Champagne and Wines division delivered strong organic sales growth of 7% in the first half of 2026. According to Morgan Stanley reports from November 11, 2026, this segment benefited from positive volume and pricing gains led by prestige brands like Krug. However, the broker warned that champagne growth may decelerate in the fourth quarter as prestige brand mix benefits normalise globally.
3.
Cognac recovery: LVMH's Cognac and Spirits division saw organic sales rise by 6% in the third quarter of 2026, outperforming market expectations. The Hennessy brand experienced improved demand in China and European travel retail alongside resilient momentum for VSOP. This recovery suggests that cognac destocking has bottomed out across major international retail distribution networks.
4.
Improved margins: LVMH reported that its Wines and Spirits recurring operating margin reached 22.4% in the first half of 2026, up from 20.3% in the prior-year period. This profit growth of 11% reflected rigorous cost control measures and favorable pricing dynamics. However, Morgan Stanley estimated on May 18, 2026, that full-year margins could still hit a multi-decade operating low of 18% due to transactional currency headwinds and a poor harvest.

Chinese Trade Protectionism and Price Undertakings

5.
Definitive duties: China's Ministry of Commerce announced definitive anti-dumping duties on European Union brandy ranging from 27.7% to 34.9% effective July 5, 2025. This trade barrier was introduced as a retaliatory measure following the European Union's implementation of countervailing tariffs on Chinese electric vehicles. The imposition of these duties threatened to severely damage French cognac exports to their most profitable global market.
6.
Tariff circumvention: Hennessy and other major French cognac producers negotiated a minimum import price agreement with Chinese authorities to avoid the steep 34.9% tariff. Under this price undertaking, cooperative companies are permitted to import brandy exempt from tariffs as long as they sell at or above agreed thresholds. This mechanism represents a substantially less punitive operational alternative than facing full duties at the border.
7.
Elevated costs: LVMH's minimum price commitment in China requires raising the transfer price of cognac shipped from France to its domestic distribution subsidiaries. Bernstein Research reported on July 7, 2025, that this higher import price incurs incrementally higher value added and consumption taxes. Consequently, these higher taxes significantly elevate cost of goods sold for Hennessy, leaving operators with limited room to raise retail prices.
8.
Duty-free restrictions: Cognac producers also faced severe channel disruption in China due to the temporary suspension of duty-free distribution channels. The Bureau National Interprofessionnel du Cognac noted that cognac was excluded from Chinese duty-free shops since December 2024, a channel representing nearly 20% of its national sales. Jefferies reported on July 22, 2025, that while the price undertaking has prompted the reopening of travel retail, historical channel inventory remains elevated.

United States Tariffs and Peer Comparisons

9.
Tariff pressure: LVMH's Wines and Spirits division faces increased profit pressure in the United States following the implementation of a 15% tariff rate effective August 1, 2025. This tariff rate was lower than the initially proposed 30% rate but still represents a significant headwind for imported spirits. To mitigate this, importers accelerated shipments ahead of the tariff deadline to build up low-cost inventory.
10.
Peer exposure: Remy Cointreau has a higher exposure to United States tariff risks than its peers because its American business is almost entirely imported from the European Union. Remy Cointreau reported in July 2025 that it expects a €35 million hit from US tariffs compared to a smaller €10 million hit in China. By comparison, Diageo has broadly diversified import exposure across multiple countries including Mexico, Canada, and the United Kingdom.
11.
Past precedent: China's anti-dumping tariffs on Australian wine in 2020 provide a clear historical example of how punitive tariffs can devastate demand. Deutsche Bank reported on March 21, 2024, that those duties of 107% to 212% caused Australian wine exports to Mainland China to decline by 99% over the subsequent two years. This precedent suggests that Hennessy's negotiation of a price floor was essential to avoid complete market exclusion.
12.
Associate impact: Diageo's consolidated earnings per share remain highly sensitive to these combined Chinese and United States macro headwinds. Morgan Stanley reported on June 23, 2025, that it reduced its forecasts for Moet Hennessy's contribution due to weak underlying demand for cognac. Consequently, any structural compression of Moet Hennessy's operating margin directly translates into downward revisions for Diageo's forecasted associate income stream.
13.
Segment contribution: Diageo owns 34% of Moet Hennessy, which accounts for a notable portion of the group's overall profitability. Jefferies reported on October 12, 2026, that the associate stake represents approximately 6% of Diageo's consolidated net profit. This high concentration means that any profit recovery in luxury spirits remains crucial for Diageo to achieve its mid-term earnings targets.

Structural Consumption Shifts and Contrary Evidence

14.
Contrary evidence: Some regional indicators show that near-term cognac shipment recoveries may overlook deeper structural consumption declines. Morgan Stanley reported on May 18, 2026, that US spirits consumers are actively trading down from premium cognac to tequila. This shifting consumer taste, driven by health trends and younger demographic behaviors, suggests that volume recoveries may remain temporary unless underlying brand desirability improves.
15.
Structural headwind: LVMH's long-term cognac margins face further pressure from rising production costs and unfavorable generational demographic shifts. Hennessy CEO Charles Delapalme noted on January 6, 2026, that the brand is highly culturally relevant but faces declining consumption rates among younger demographics. These long-term demand shifts could prevent margins from returning to peak historical levels above 30% over the next five years.

Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on November 4, 2026, that luxury spirits houses retain high barriers to entry and valuable aging inventories. Consequently, any improvement in global consumer confidence will rapidly restore operating margins as fixed cost coverage expands.

Section 5 – Put Option Mechanics and Likelihood of Exercise

Section summary
  1. Diageo holds a contractually defined put option to sell its thirty-four percent stake in Moët Hennessy to LVMH at twenty percent below fair value. Highly elevated leverage constraints and recent selective asset disposals suggest that while monetization is financially dilutive, it remains a strategic balance sheet remedy.

Contractual Terms of the Put Option

1.
Unfavorable valuation: LVMH confirmed in its half-year financial report published on July 27, 2026, that Diageo holds a contractually granted put option to sell its 34% stake in Moët Hennessy at 80% of its fair value. Under these specific terms, Diageo can exercise this option at any time subject to a strict six-month notice period. The fair value is calculated using a discounted future cash flows methodology as detailed in LVMH's annual accounts.
2.
Significant liabilities: LVMH carries this purchase commitment within its other non-current liabilities, which excludes Diageo's stake from the reported minority interests on its balance sheet. Bernstein Research calculated on January 24, 2025, that this purchase commitment implied an overall valuation of approximately 32 billion euros for 100% of Moët Hennessy. This valuation is highly sensitive to the operating cash flows of Moët Hennessy and is revalued every six months.
3.
Protective covenants: Diageo's annual report published on August 6, 2026, details that if a direct competitor acquires more than 34% of Diageo's voting rights, LVMH can force a sale of the stake. This specific change of control trigger is designed to protect Moët Hennessy from hostile industry competitors. This clause highlights that the joint venture terms contain strict boundaries regarding ownership and competitor control.

Leverage Targets and Strategic Disposals

4.
Strained leverage: Morgan Stanley reported on March 25, 2026, that Diageo's consolidated net debt to EBITDA ratio stood at 3.4x as of December 31, 2025. This elevated debt level is well above Diageo's long-term target leverage ratio of 2.5x to 3.0x. To accelerate balance sheet repair, Diageo has already implemented selective asset disposals and halved its dividend payout.
5.
Progressive deleveraging: Diageo expects to reduce its leverage pro forma to approximately 3.0x through the completed sale of EABL and the RCB cricket team. Bernstein Research reported on December 22, 2025, that the sale of the 65% stake in EABL to Asahi for $2.3 billion would reduce leverage by 0.28x. Meanwhile, United Spirits' sale of the RCB cricket franchise for $1.8 billion is expected to provide an additional 0.2x leverage reduction.
6.
Strong resistance: Diageo's management has consistently rejected speculation about a potential sale of its Moët Hennessy holding. Morgan Stanley reported on January 29, 2025, that the company issued an explicit statement confirming it has no intention to sell either Guinness or its associate stake. This indicates that management intends to retain its highly profitable luxury associate despite ongoing leverage constraints.

Comparative Past Precedents

7.
Punitive precedents: Deutsche Bank Research recalled on February 3, 2015, that Heineken successfully terminated its Amstel brand joint venture with SABMiller in 2007. This termination was triggered by a change of control clause after SABMiller acquired Bavaria in Latin America. This precedent shows how change of control clauses are strictly enforced in the global beverages industry to remove competitors.
