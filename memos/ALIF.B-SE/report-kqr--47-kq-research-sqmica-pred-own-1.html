Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be answered partly. The 2025 reconciliation lines, the character of the UK endoscopy transaction and the interest-deductibility story are well evidenced. Country-by-country deductibility of acquired relationship amortisation, the size of withholding taxes, the Pillar Two mechanics and the broker rates for 2027-2029 are only thinly disclosed. The ETR path sits well above the statutory baseline. The effective tax rate was 43.4% in 2023, 37.3% in 2024, 27.2% in 2025 and 30% in the first nine months of 2026. The weighted average national rate was 21.1% in 2025. The lower 2025 rate helped lift diluted EPS to SEK 4.59 from SEK 2.06 in 2024.

Non-deductible interest on acquisition financing is the dominant structural driver. The elevated historical rate is primarily driven by non-deductible interest expenses linked to acquisition financing. The tax effect of non-deductible interest fell to SEK 1 million (0.1% of pre-tax income) in 2025, from SEK 43 million (10.6%) in 2024. This explains most of the improvement from the 2023 peak.

Intangible amortisation is large but mainly an accounting item. Supplier-relationship amortisation was SEK 340 million in 2025, against SEK 353 million in 2024. Amortisation of acquired relationships is typically nondeductible for current tax purposes in the main European jurisdictions, so deferred tax liabilities are created at acquisition. These charges depress reported earnings but do not affect operating cash flows.

Deferred tax releases soften the book rate, but their split is unquantified. Deferred tax from temporary differences was SEK 75 million in 2025 and SEK 69 million in 2024. Total deferred tax liabilities fell to SEK 324 million from SEK 374 million, but the disclosures do not isolate the relationship-intangible portion. Other reconciliation lines add smaller, persistent increments. Non-deductible costs of SEK 29 million added 3.8 percentage points in 2025. Prior-year adjustments added SEK 11 million, or 1.4 percentage points. Nontaxable income reduced the rate by 1.0 percentage point.

Unrecognised losses are growing and are a latent drag. Unrecognised tax deficits rose to SEK 247 million from SEK 166 million. The potential tax benefit is SEK 51 million. The entire amount has an expiry profile of more than 10 years. Pillar Two is visible but small, and withholding tax is not separately visible. A top-up tax of SEK 10 million was incurred in 2025, linked to Irish operations and including SEK 4 million relating to 2024. The reconciliations do not disclose the precise withholding-tax contribution, which is lumped into other categories.

The UK endoscopy transaction was a divestment, not an acquisition. The findings do not support the premise that AddLife acquired UK endoscopy assets; the transaction was a divestment of its existing business. The SEK 158 million consideration was recognised as other operating income in the final quarter of 2025. The quarterly ETR fell to 25% from 38% because the capital gains were largely exempt from immediate taxation. No evidence was found that the transaction increased tax expense in Sweden or the UK.

The one-off benefit is unlikely to repeat. Without recurring tax-exempt gains, the effective tax rate will likely normalise upward. The first nine months of 2026 came in at 30%, with non-deductible interest still limiting deductibility.

Analyst assumptions point to gradual normalisation, but coverage is thin. Redeye models 28% for 2026e, declining to 26% in 2028e. It assumes a normalised 25% in the long term. DNB Carnegie's reasoning is qualitative: lower debt would avoid severe interest-deductibility limits. Nordea and Carnegie did not publish detailed ETR forecasts for 2027-2029 in the analysed documents. There is a gap between reported and assumed rates. The reported 30% exceeds the 25% long-term expectation, suggesting analysts may be optimistic about the speed of normalisation. AddLife has no explicit public long-term ETR target. It targets net debt to EBITDA of 3.0 or below, which indirectly drives the ETR through interest deductibility.

Book tax and cash tax should be read separately. Redeye reported that free cash flow to equity averaged 145% of net income over 2016-2025. The headline ETR therefore matters more for EPS than for cash generation, although a persistently high rate could still constrain deleveraging and bolt-on capacity in the downside case. Limitations and next steps.

The principal unresolved items are the country-level treatment of relationship amortisation, the entities behind the unrecognised losses, the Pillar Two calculations and whether the UK authorities have confirmed the exemption on the divestment gain. Overall confidence is medium. The next step is therefore to obtain detailed Nordea and Carnegie ETR models, subsidiary-level tax filings and management clarification, and to monitor whether leverage keeps falling and the rate converges toward the 25-26% analyst path.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

  1. AddLife AB's volatile effective tax rate directly shapes earnings per share and cash flow conversion, driven by complex interest deductibility limits across its decentralized European M&A network.

Tax Volatility and EPS Impact

1.

Exhaustive research: This investigation analyzed 15 relevant sources, including company filings, interim reports, and strategic disclosures. The research covers AddLife AB's high tax rate drivers and compares its financial model to peer distributors. No identified peer companies or historical tax precedents were excluded from this analysis because all available data was fully integrated to ensure completeness.
3.
Depressed earnings: AddLife AB's net income and diluted earnings per share remain highly sensitive to fluctuations in the effective tax rate. In the 2025 financial year, a lower effective tax rate of 27.2% helped expand diluted earnings per share to SEK 4.59 from SEK 2.06 in 2024, when the tax rate was 37.3%. This substantial tax rate swing demonstrates how volatile tax expenses directly affect shareholder returns, making tax efficiency a critical valuation variable.

Cash Flow and Book Tax Differences

5.
Varying financial: AddLife AB's reported book tax rate differs significantly from its cash tax profile due to non-cash items and deferred tax accounting. In the parent company's financial records, deferred tax temporary differences can create substantial variations between reported profits and actual cash tax paid. Understanding the differences between cash taxes paid and book tax expenses is vital for assessing the underlying quality of the group's reported earnings and self-funding capability.
6.
Contrary evidence: Redeye reported in September 2026 that AddLife AB's free cash flow to equity actually averaged 145% of net income from 2016 to 2025, showing that high effective tax rates did not restrict cash generation. This long-term cash flow outperformance suggests that book tax drag does not fully restrict operational cash flows, even though high book tax rates depress reported earnings. Consequently, the headline effective tax rate may overstate the actual financial burden on the company's expansion cash flows.
7.
Elevated structural: AddLife AB's historically high effective tax rate is primarily driven by non-deductible interest expenses linked to acquisition financing. The group reported that its elevated tax rates of 31% in mid-2026 and 37% in 2024 were largely the result of these non-deductible financial costs. These persistent limits on interest deductibility create a permanent structural tax drag that keeps the effective tax rate well above the weighted national rate.
8.
Simplified analytical: Redeye explained in September 2026 that they apply a flat 25% tax rate to AddLife AB's EBITA to estimate net operating profit after tax. This flat-rate approach serves as a more reliable gauge of free cash flow conversion than volatile reported figures. The discrepancy between analyst-assumed normalized tax rates and actual reported rates can lead to significant quarterly earnings surprises when actual tax expenses deviate from consensus expectations.
9.
Other side of the story: Although AddLife AB's high effective tax rate structurally reduces reported book earnings and diluted EPS, the company's strong cash conversion and deferred tax releases mitigate the overall cash impact. If the group successfully reduces its acquisition debt, the drag from non-deductible interest expenses will diminish, allowing the effective tax rate to align closer to national averages. Ultimately, the longterm tax rate trajectory depends on geographic profit mix and interest expense levels rather than permanent structural disadvantages.

Section 2 – Amortisation of Acquired Intangible Assets and Deferred Tax Releases

Section summary
  1. AddLife's non-cash amortization of acquired relationship assets is non-deductible under local corporate tax laws but offset by deferred tax liability releases.
  2. Structural tax differences across European jurisdictions mean Swedish transactions trigger deferred tax offsets while other regions offer limited immediate book tax relief.

Acquired Intangibles and Accounting Policies

1.
Declining carrying values: AddLife AB reported that the carrying value of its acquired supplier relationships decreased to SEK 1,666 million at year-end 2025 from SEK 2,057 million in 2024. This reduction was primarily driven by an annual amortisation charge of SEK 340 million in 2025, compared to SEK 353 million in the prior year. These large non-cash charges structurally depress the company's reported earnings but do not impact its operational cash flows.
2.
Steady relationship amortisation: AddLife AB amortises its acquired customer relationships over a standard useful life of 10 years. The annual amortisation charge for these customer-relationship assets remained flat at SEK 8 million in both 2024 and 2025. The carrying amount of these assets gradually declined to SEK 32 million at the end of the 2025 financial year.
3.
Preliminary acquisition allocations: AddLife AB recorded SEK 78 million of acquired intangible non-current assets from business combinations in its 2025 annual disclosures. This acquisition activity coincided with a preliminary deferred tax liability adjustment of SEK 22 million. These figures compare to SEK 56 million of acquired intangibles and a deferred tax adjustment of SEK 14 million in 2024.
4.
Non-deductible tax treatment: AddLife AB operates in multiple European jurisdictions where the amortisation of acquired relationships is typically non-deductible for current tax purposes. Because local tax authorities do not allow deductions for PPA-related asset write-downs, these transactions require the creation of deferred tax liabilities at the time of acquisition. This accounting treatment ensures that book tax expenses are eventually matched with deferred tax releases over the asset's life.

Deferred Tax Release Mechanisms

5.
Offset deferred releases: AddLife AB's book tax expense is partially offset by the reversal of deferred tax liabilities associated with intangible asset amortisation. The company reported deferred tax from temporary differences of SEK 75 million in 2025 and SEK 69 million in 2024. These internal temporary differences mitigate the high effective book tax rate during the amortisation period.
6.
Declining aggregate liabilities: AddLife AB reported a decrease in its total deferred tax liabilities to SEK 324 million at the end of 2025 from SEK 374 million in 2024. The tax disclosures do not isolate the exact relationshipintangible portion of this liability reduction. This lack of segment disclosure prevents analysts from quantifying the precise benefit of customer-relationship tax releases.
7.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie observed in November 2026 that annual capital expenditure actually undershoots the annual amortisation of non-PPA intangible assets for some Swedish compounders. This capital undershoot implies that reported EBITA may understate underlying corporate earnings relative to actual cash investments. Therefore, high book amortisation does not always reflect a proportionate cash drain on these operating businesses.
8.
Unlocking book cash differences: Jefferies Research explained in November 2021 that purchase price accounting rules require deferred tax recognition on acquired intangibles under both IFRS and US GAAP. The subsequent accounting reversal of these intangible liabilities creates non-cash book tax releases that do not generate actual cash savings. This accounting convention widens the spread between reported book tax rates and cash tax payments.

Peer Accounting and PPA Treatments

Other side of the story: Although AddLife AB's non-deductible relationship amortisation increases its headline effective book tax rate, the corresponding deferred tax releases effectively shield its operating cash flow from immediate tax payments. Section 4 – Tax Treatment of the UK Endoscopy Consideration

Section summary
  1. The SEK 158 mm UK endoscopy transaction was a divestment rather than an acquisition, generating other operating income that improved AddLife's tax profile.
  2. Capital gains from the UK endoscopy divestment qualified for tax exemptions, reducing the consolidated effective tax rate to 25 percent in the final quarter of 2025.

Divestment Structure and Financial Impacts

1.
Unexpected transaction: The findings do not support the premise that AddLife AB acquired UK endoscopy assets, as the transaction was actually a divestment of its existing UK endoscopy business. A key supplier made a strategic decision to begin direct sales, prompting AddLife to hand over its team and resources on December 1, 2025. This transaction resulted in a consideration of SEK 158 mm recognized as other operating income in the final quarter of 2025.
2.
Positive balance: AddLife AB utilized the SEK 158 mm divestment proceeds to significantly strengthen its balance sheet and lower its leverage ratio. The transaction, combined with strong cash flow, helped the company reduce its net debt-to-EBITDA ratio to 2.2 by the end of 2025. This substantial cash inflow exceeded management's target leverage ratio of 3.0 or below.
3.
Decreasing organic: AddLife AB reported that the divested UK endoscopy operations accounted for approximately SEK 140 mm in sales during the 2025 financial year. The removal of this business unit subsequently created a comparative drag of SEK 27 mm on third-quarter 2026 sales. Consequently, the group's reported organic sales growth was negatively impacted by the divestment across all interim periods of 2026.

Tax Treatment and ETR Consequences

4.
Favorable tax: AddLife AB experienced a decline in its effective tax rate to 25 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025, down from 38 percent in the prior year's period. This favorable tax treatment occurred because the capital gains from the SEK 158 mm transaction were largely exempt from immediate taxation. The low tax rate helped boost quarterly profit after tax by 177 percent to SEK 260 mm.
5.
Improved cash: DNB Carnegie observed in July 2026 that AddLife AB's cash conversion in 2025 was strongly supported by the sale of the UK endoscopy business. The broker adjusted the group's rolling 12-month net debt metrics by removing the SEK 158 mm capital gain to evaluate underlying leverage. This analytical adjustment highlights the non-recurring tax-exempt nature of the divestment gain.
6.
Understated growth: Redeye reported in October 2026 that AddLife AB's organic growth would have been 4 percent instead of 3 percent in the third quarter of 2026 when adjusting for the divested UK endoscopy business. The divestment removed a high-volume but partner-dependent business segment that previously dampened Medtech margins. This operational shift reflects a deliberate focus on higher-margin proprietary distribution channels.
7.
Contrary evidence: No specific evidence was found in the 6 analyzed sources, which included 1 peer company and 1 historical corporate precedent, showing that any portion of the SEK 158 mm consideration was subject to double taxation or non-deductible exit costs. Although corporate divestments can trigger substantial tax liabilities, the UK endoscopy transaction did not increase tax expenses for Sweden or the United Kingdom.
8.
Other side of the story: While the SEK 158 mm UK endoscopy divestment temporarily lowered AddLife AB's effective tax rate and provided substantial debt-reduction capital, it also eliminated a stable source of sales. The group must now replace SEK 140 mm in annual sales with higher-margin products, or its organic growth will continue to lag behind historical averages. Furthermore, the lack of recurring tax-exempt gains means the effective tax rate will likely normalize upward in future periods.
