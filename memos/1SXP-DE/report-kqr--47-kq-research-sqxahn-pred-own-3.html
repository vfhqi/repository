Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: "SCHOTT Pharma AG & Co. KGaA (1SXP-DE), a German maker of primary packaging for injectable drugs, 77% owned by SCHOTT AG (Carl Zeiss Foundation), fiscal year ending 30 September. It reports two segments: Drug Containment Solutions (glass vials, ampoules and cartridges, increasingly as sterile ready-to-use formats) and Drug Delivery Systems (glass and polymer prefillable syringes). What is the utilisation of SCHOTT Pharma's polymer prefillable syringe lines at Müllheim, Germany, after mRNA vaccine work fell to about 5% of group sales in FY2026 (Jefferies, August 2026)? Which non-mRNA customers and applications (mental health, aesthetics, surgery, intravenous, long-acting injectables, large-volume syringes) have qualified or are in qualification, on what timelines, and what is the risk of an impairment or line closure? How does Stevanato's Nexa Flex polymer syringe affect SCHOTT Pharma's polymer share?"

The answer to this question is critical for investors because SCHOTT Pharma's Drug Delivery Systems (DDS) segment is the company's highest-margin business, and polymer syringes represent a core pillar of its High-Value Solutions (HVS) strategy. According to Deutsche Bank research on November 7, 2023, the company planned to triple its polymer syringe capacity at the Müllheim site by 2027, a project that was initiated when mRNA vaccine demand was at its peak. If utilization fails to recover as this new capacity comes online, the company faces significant negative operating leverage, which would directly compress EBITDA margins and earnings per share (EPS). Management noted in their Q4 2025 earnings call that temporary underutilization of DDS capacities would already be a drag on the 27% EBITDA margin target for fiscal year 2026. - Earnings Calls A shift from a pandemic-driven "order-taker" environment to a competitive commercial landscape forces SCHOTT Pharma to rely on the qualification of a broader pipeline of therapies.

Research has identified over 100 relevant sources for this report, including company filings from SCHOTT Pharma and its peers Stevanato and Gerresheimer, broker notes from Jefferies and Deutsche Bank, and expert transcripts from former heads of process engineering. These sources collectively suggest that while the transition is underway, the "stickiness" of the pharmaceutical industry means that switching from mRNA to other modalities like mental health or aesthetics is a multi-year process.

For an investor, the primary risk is that the "bridging year" of 2026 extends into a structural slowdown if Stevanato's Nexa Flex launch successfully captures the emerging dual-sourcing trend among large biopharma companies. Closely related questions include the durability of the GLP-1 growth wave, which currently provides a "partial offset" to polymer weakness by driving demand for prefillable glass syringes and cartridges., the success of SCHOTT's large-volume 50 mL polymer syringes in the intravenous (IV) and hospital market is a critical variable in diversifying away from the volatile vaccine market.

This report used findings from all major peers–Stevanato, Gerresheimer, and Becton Dickinson–and examined precedents such as the post-COVID destocking cycles in the broader life sciences tools sector to judge the severity of the current utilization trough. No major companies or precedents were left out, as the search covered the top four global players that control the majority of the prefillable syringe market.

Section 2 – Utilization Trends at Müllheim and the mRNA Cliff

Section summary
  1. Medium confidence that Müllheim utilization has stabilized at lower levels, supported by management's confirmation that mRNA exposure has reached a 5% floor.

The mRNA normalization and capacity impact The Müllheim facility serves as SCHOTT Pharma's

primary hub for high-tech polymer solutions, and its utilization has been directly tied to the "dampened momentum" in mRNA vaccination programs. Management confirmed in December 2025 that mRNA exposure, which previously exceeded 10% of group sales, would fall to approximately 5% for FY2026 and beyond. This decline was a primary driver of the "temporary underutilization of DDS capacities" cited by the CFO as a factor impacting FY2026 profitability. Broker research from RBC on December 3, 2025, estimated that demand for polymer syringes fell by about 30% in 2025, creating a low-to-mid single-digit sales headwind that could take years to replace. 18 - Earnings Calls underutilization remains the most immediate threat to the operational leverage gains investors expected from the Müllheim expansion. SCHOTT Pharma's ongoing investment program aimed to triple polymer syringe capacity by 2027, yet Deutsche Bank warned on July 6, 2026, that if this newly commissioned capacity is not increasingly utilized, it will limit margin progression.

The DDS segment, which includes polymer syringes, saw its organic revenue growth pick up to 5% in Q3 2026 after a weaker first half, suggesting that the "trough" in utilization may have passed. However, the segment's EBITDA margin of 29.4% in Q3 2026 remained significantly below the 36.9% reported in the prior year quarter, reflecting the persistent cost of carrying unutilized capacity.

Impairment risks and facility viability Contrary evidence regarding the risk of facility closure was found

in SCHOTT Pharma's 2025 Annual Report, which states that almost all capital expenditure on property, plant, and equipment continues to be focused on growth projects and capacity expansion at Müllheim.

This commitment to the site’s future suggests that management views the current underutilization as a phase in a longer-term transition rather than a signal for impairment or closure. Jefferies research on August 12, 2026, noted that the impairment loss recorded in H1 was specifically related to "customer-specific glass syringes," not a broader write-down of the Müllheim polymer lines. The resolution of a long-term agreement with a major customer in July 2026 further reduced the uncertainty surrounding volume outlooks and potential structural impairments.

The decision-making process for an impairment charge in this sector is highly dependent on long-term cash flow forecasts for validated drug programs. A former Head of Process Engineering at SCHOTT Pharma explained in an expert call on January 23, 2026, that while the COVID phase provided a "lucky" boost, the market is now challenging because big pharma companies are not proceeding as aggressively with mRNA developments.

If new vaccine launches (e.g., for flu or RSV) fail to gain traction in a polymer format, the risk of a "stranded asset" scenario at Müllheim would increase, although currently, the site is being pivoted toward specialty vials for antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs) to mitigate this.

Comparative performance and past cases The utilization struggles at Müllheim reflect a broader

industry pattern where companies that over-expanded during the pandemic now face a "recovery trend" from a bottomed-out position. A comparable past case is found in the broader packaging sector with O-I Glass, which in October 2026 decided to initiate the permanent closure of a European facility to reduce redundant production capacity, recording charges of $100 mm to $120 mm. Unlike O-I Glass, SCHOTT Pharma’s Müllheim site produces HVS products with high entry barriers, which typically discourages closures unless a technological shift makes the entire format obsolete. Peer Stevanato Group also reported a "temporary headwind" in 2026 due to lower volumes from one major customer, but similarly focused on "backfilling" that capacity with new biologic programs rather than closing lines.

If the expected 5% floor for mRNA fails to hold due to further failures in mRNA pipelines (such as Moderna's CMV vaccine), the tripling of capacity by 2027 could lead to a structural oversupply. This would force more aggressive price competition or potentially lead to the impairment charges that brokers like Jefferies have started to monitor closely in their downside scenarios. Section 3 – Pipeline Qualifications and Non-mRNA Applications

Section summary
  1. High confidence that SCHOTT Pharma has a robust non-mRNA application pipeline, but medium confidence on the timing of these converting to high-volume commercial revenue.

Mental health, aesthetics, and long-acting injectables SCHOTT Pharma is aggressively targeting non-

mRNA applications to diversify its polymer portfolio, identifying mental health and aesthetics as key drivers for long-term growth. During the Q1 2026 earnings call, management highlighted that underlying growth in these segments, alongside surgery rooms and animal health, is expected to bring the polymer syringe business to a "flattish" performance for the full year despite mRNA declines. The company's TOPPAC polymer technology is marketed as particularly suitable for these fields because of its dimensional precision and breakage resistance, which are valued in high-stress surgical environments and for viscous aesthetic fillers. 32 - Earnings Calls Qualification for long-acting injectables (LAIs) is a primary focus because these drugs often require the specialized containment properties that high-tech polymers like Cyclic Olefin Copolymer (COC) provide.

SCHOTT Pharma's 2025 Annual Report notes that polymer syringes are increasingly used for highly viscous drugs and deep-cold medications where glass syringes may not meet specific requirements. Jefferies confirmed on August 12, 2026, that they see a "strong recovery in ex-mRNA polymer syringes," indicating that these non-vaccine applications are beginning to move from the qualification phase into commercial orders.

Timelines and the "Stickiness" of Primary Packaging The timeline for a customer to qualify a new

syringe at Müllheim is typically 2 to 3 years due to the necessity of stability trials. An expert and former Head of Process Engineering at SCHOTT Pharma stated that it is "very hard" to get a customer to switch suppliers for an existing biologic drug; instead, the focus must be on entering at the new medicine and development stage. This reality means that the utilization gap created by the mRNA "cliff" cannot be filled overnight. Deutsche Bank research on November 7, 2023, noted that it would take new entrants "over 10 years" to be considered as a supplier for blockbuster drugs, emphasizing the long-dated nature of these relationships. Contrary evidence found in a January 2026 expert call suggests that some big pharma companies are actually not proceeding in mRNA development for non-COVID applications due to safety or efficacy issues, which could shrink the addressable market SCHOTT originally planned to serve. This could push qualification timelines for other modalities even further out as companies re-evaluate their delivery strategies. However, SCHOTT's partnership with SHL Medical for a large-volume sterile polymer cartridge, launched in early 2026, provides a new growth leg that leverages the trend toward homecare and self-administration.

Large-volume syringes and IV applications The intravenous (IV) and hospital market for large-volume

syringes (20 mL to 50 mL) is a segment where SCHOTT Pharma maintains a dominant market position. According to an expert from Aptar Pharma on November 4, 2025, SCHOTT was "quite smart" to go after the 20 mL and 50 mL market where there is not a strong glass presence, allowing them to maintain high market share. These large-volume solutions are critical for hospital processes where they reduce drug waste and improve efficiency compared to traditional vial-and-disposable-syringe combinations.

SCHOTT Pharma claims that 94% of hospital healthcare professionals prefer polymer syringes over glass for IV administrations due to their break-resistance and reduced risk of contamination. This preference provides a steady "core" of demand for the Müllheim lines that is less volatile than the vaccine-related business. Furthermore, the launch of the SCHOTT TOPPAC infuse system, specifically designed for safety and efficiency in hospital settings, won the CPHI Pharma Award in 2025, reinforcing the company's innovation leadership in this niche. - Earnings Calls Other side of the story: While management points to a broad pipeline, the high cost of polymer syringes (2.5x to 3x that of glass) remains a significant barrier to adoption in non-critical applications. An expert noted on January 23, 2026, that unless there is a technical reason like deep-cold storage, many pharmaceutical companies will stick with glass for its lower price point. If the premium for polymer does not compress through scale, the "broad-based" growth in aesthetics and mental health may remain restricted to a few high-end niche products rather than providing the volume needed to fully fill Müllheim's tripling capacity.

Section 4 – Competitive Dynamics: Stevanato Nexa Flex vs. SCHOTT TOPPAC

Section summary
  1. High confidence that Stevanato’s Nexa Flex is entering the market as a primary challenger to SCHOTT’s share, with medium confidence on the rate of share loss.

The launch of Nexa Flex and dual-sourcing Stevanato Group’s entry into the polymer market with the

Nexa Flex syringe marks a significant shift in the competitive landscape for SCHOTT Pharma. Jefferies research on January 27, 2025, warned that SCHOTT, as the category leader in polymer syringes, is "most at risk" from the launch of Nexa Flex. The Nexa Flex, offered in both COC and COP (Cyclic Olefin Polymer) formats, is designed to give pharmaceutical companies a credible alternative to SCHOTT’s TOPPAC, which previously held an estimated 60-70% market share.

The primary driver for customers to adopt Stevanato’s solution is the post-pandemic trend toward dualsourcing of critical components. Industry conversations at the 2025 Pharmapack conference suggested that customers are increasingly looking for second sources due to the supply chain challenges and long lead times experienced during COVID-19. While SCHOTT Pharma’s polymer business is described as "highly profitable" and "very secretive," the lack of a strong number-two player in the past gave the company significant pricing power that is now being tested.

Comparative technology and positioning Stevanato is positioning Nexa Flex as part of a "one-stop

shop" offering that includes equipment, devices, and packaging, an approach that contrasts with SCHOTT's specialized material focus. In an expert call on October 27, 2026, a VP from Gerresheimer noted that in terms of syringes, Stevanato and Gerresheimer are "almost on the same level" in terms of overall capacity (roughly 1 bn syringes each), though Becton Dickinson remains the dominant player in glass PFS. SCHOTT Pharma’s edge in polymer has traditionally been its deep-cold storage capabilities (-100°C), a technical barrier that Stevanato must prove it has fully cleared with Nexa Flex to win mRNA-like modalities. Contrary evidence found in a February 2026 expert call suggests that Stevanato was delayed by about two years in its polymer ramp-up, which may have given SCHOTT a longer window to lock in new biologics programs. Furthermore, the "stickiness" of the business means that even with a viable alternative, customers are unlikely to switch existing, validated drug programs to Stevanato unless there is a major supply failure from SCHOTT. This gives SCHOTT a "structural advantage" in its current installed base that will take years for a new competitor to erode.

Peer benchmarking and market share While SCHOTT dominates the polymer niche, it trails peers in the

much larger glass syringe market, which impacts its overall DDS segment growth relative to Stevanato. Becton Dickinson (BD) remains the market leader in PFS with an estimated share of over 60%, followed by Stevanato as the number two player. In glass syringes specifically, Stevanato is estimated to be selling "twice as many" units as SCHOTT (800 mm vs. 400 mm annually), providing Stevanato with a larger commercial footprint from which to cross-sell its new Nexa Flex polymer products. Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. If polymer demand fails to recover as expected, SCHOTT Pharma faces a structural margin reset that would likely lead to a "downward revision" cycle for its mid-term 30% EBITDA target. If the Key Question is resolved differently from the "temporary headwind" view held by most sources, the knock-on effects on EPS would be severe. Most analysts currently underwrite a return to growth in FY2027, but a failure of the non-mRNA pipeline to commercialize would force the company to rethink its tripling of capacity at Müllheim. This could lead to a permanent lower utilization rate, which would structurally depress margins and could eventually force a $100 mm+ impairment charge similar to the facility closures seen in the broader glass sector. 62 10Q Competitors like Stevanato and Becton Dickinson have both the reason and the power to push the outcome away from SCHOTT's favor by aggressively pricing their own HVS offerings to win share in the new biologics and GLP-1 markets. If Stevanato uses its leadership in cartridges and glass syringes to bundle its new Nexa Flex polymer products at a discount, SCHOTT could be forced into a price war to defend its 60-70% polymer share. This would be particularly damaging to earnings since polymer syringes currently carry a 10% higher margin than the group average. One early sign ignored by most sources is the "unreliable delivery on targets" previously noted by some analysts, which could suggest that management's current "flattish" outlook for polymer in 2026 is overly optimistic. If the 5% mRNA floor is actually a "sliding floor" that falls toward zero as pandemic-era stockpiles are fully exhausted, the "bridge year" of 2026 could become a multi-year downturn. This would impact not only SCHOTT but also its suppliers of high-tech polymer resins (e.g., COC manufacturers), who would see a significant reduction in their most profitable pharmaceutical-grade orders. Ultimately, a different outcome would benefit pharmaceutical customers by providing them with more leverage in pricing negotiations through dual-sourcing. The "stickiness" of the industry has historically protected SCHOTT, but the post-pandemic shift in procurement strategy toward resilience over pure vendor loyalty is a fundamental change that could permanently erode the high-margin "category leader" status of the TOPPAC brand. For an investor, the key indicator to watch is the DDS segment's EBITDA margin in FY2027; if it fails to rebound toward the mid-30s%, it will confirm that the utilization and competitive issues are structural rather than temporary.
