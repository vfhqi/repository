Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Swiss franc strength presents a double-edged sword for SKAN, threatening export competitiveness while testing its robust operational mitigation and natural hedging mechanisms.

Core Questions and Transmission Risks

1.
Key Question: "SKAN Group AG (SKAN-CH), a Swiss maker of isolators and aseptic process systems for sterile drug manufacturing, reporting two segments: Equipment & Solutions (custom and standard isolators, integrated fill-finish process systems built with partner filling-machine makers, eBeam and H2O2 decontamination, laboratory, sterility-testing and cell-therapy isolators) and Services & Consumables (requalification, maintenance, retrofits and spare parts for the installed base; Aseptic Technologies closed vials; ABC Transfer sterile transfer bags and ports; Metronik manufacturing execution software; and the Pre-Approved Services pay-per-use filling business run through Maeven AG). How does Swiss franc strength against the euro and the US dollar affect SKAN Group AG's (SKAN-CH) competitiveness and margins, given that it prices most capital projects in Swiss francs, hedges foreign-currency contracts from signature and has about half its workforce in Switzerland: what evidence exists from tenders, customers or competitors (Syntegon, Optima, Groninger, Getinge) of price pressure attributable to the franc, what are SKAN's currency sensitivities by currency for sales, costs and EBIT and its hedge horizon, and how did SKAN and other Swiss engineering exporters such as Tecan, VAT Group and INFICON fare in the franc appreciation episodes of 2011, 2015 and 2023?"
2.
Vulnerable earnings: Currency fluctuations significantly impact SKAN Group AG's earnings per share (EPS) over the medium term because a substantial portion of its operating costs are in Swiss francs while sales are global. The company faces margin contraction when the Swiss franc strengthens against the euro and US dollar, as it prices most large capital projects in its home currency. Ultimately, resolving these currency sensitivities is essential for investors to assess whether SKAN's medium-term EPS growth targets remain achievable.
3.
Divergent margins: The transmission mechanism of currency risk varies greatly between SKAN's two primary reporting segments. The low-margin Equipment & Solutions segment is highly exposed during project delivery phases where local Swiss personnel costs remain fixed in francs. Conversely, the high-margin Services & Consumables segment acts as a protective buffer, buoyed by the global expansion of its installed base and recurring revenues. This product mix shift helps insulate overall operating profitability from severe exchange rate shocks.
4.
Intense competition: Price pressure from European rivals like Syntegon, Optima, and Groninger intensifies when the Swiss franc appreciates. Customers making selective capital investments can exploit the stronger franc to demand discounts or push for euro-denominated billing. While SKAN relies on its unrivaled technology leadership to justify premium pricing, sustained currency headwind tests customer price tolerance. Consequently, the competitive landscape represents a key risk to SKAN's order conversion and backlog execution.

Macro Precedents and Hedging Policy

5.
Historical resilience: Analyzing how Swiss engineering peers like VAT Group, Tecan, and INFICON navigated past appreciation episodes in 2011, 2015, and 2023 provides vital context for SKAN's defensive capabilities. For example, VAT Group successfully preserved its margins by expanding its global manufacturing footprint to lowercost regions like Malaysia. Understanding these historical precedents helps investors evaluate if SKAN's current decentralized production strategy can shield its EPS.
6.
Active hedging: SKAN mitigates transactional currency risk by hedging foreign-currency contracts from the moment of signature. The company's hedging policy and natural hedges, such as sourcing components in euros, help stabilize project margins during execution. However, these financial derivatives only delay the competitive impact of a structurally stronger Swiss franc over a longer horizon. Investors must monitor these hedging boundaries to anticipate future margin drawdowns.
7.
Contrary evidence: Management reported that the extreme strengthening of the Swiss franc in mid-2022 had virtually no negative impact on the company's record order intake or competitive situation. This suggests that SKAN's technological dominance in custom aseptic systems creates high pricing power that overrides immediate currency concerns.
8.
Other side of the story: While SKAN's technological superiority and robust hedging policies offer significant short-term protection, a permanently strong Swiss franc poses a severe long-term threat to its market share. If European competitors leverage their lower cost bases to underbid SKAN on standardized projects, the company's long-term sales growth could slow. Therefore, defensive hedging cannot fully replace the necessity of shifting production outside of Switzerland to maintain competitiveness.

Section 2 – SKAN's Currency Sensitivities, Costs, and Hedging Mechanics

Section summary
  1. Strong operational hedging and targeted geographical decentralization enable SKAN to defend its margins against Swiss franc appreciation.

Labor Distribution and Sourcing Footprint

1.
High headcount: SKAN Group AG expanded its global workforce by 284 employees to 1,755 in 2025, driven by integrating Metronik in Slovenia and ABC Transfer in France. Approximately 977 employees are based in Switzerland, meaning that about half of its total workforce remains subject to direct Swiss salary expenses. This concentrated labor presence in the home country exposes SKAN's margins to immediate upward pressure when the Swiss franc strengthens.
2.
Geographical leverage: SKAN's Swiss-based labor ratio of roughly 55% compares unfavorably with Swiss engineering peer Inficon, which maintains only 15% of its headcount in Switzerland. While Inficon reports its financial figures in US dollars to limit top-line currency exposures, it still bears substantial cost-side currency risk outside its reporting currency. For SKAN, expanding subsidiaries in Germany and Belgium is a critical operational lever to balance its high Swiss cost base.
3.
Decentralized production: SKAN actively counters Swiss franc strength by expanding its manufacturing capacity in Görlitz, Germany, and Gembloux, Belgium. Shifting manufacturing roles to its German site, which has over 350 employees, allows SKAN to neutralize Eurozone tariff and currency exposures for international projects. This production model demonstrates how structural geographical diversification helps protect the group's medium-term earnings per share.
4.
Strategic insourcing: SKAN's purchase of a 76% stake in Slovenian software maker Metronik added over 160 highly qualified employees to the Services segment in 2025. This geographic expansion immediately diversified its payroll currency footprint into the euro currency zone. By growing its service workforce outside Switzerland, the company gradually dilutes its structural personnel intensity, which reached 50.2% of net sales in 2025.

Project Pricing and Currency Sensitivities

5.
Premium billing: SKAN prices the majority of its capital isolator projects in Swiss francs, shifting the currency risk directly to its international pharmaceutical customers. The company avoids maintaining rigid, public list prices because every sterile project requires individual cost calculations and price adaptations. While this pricing model shields gross margins, it can diminish SKAN's market competitiveness when the franc appreciates.
6.
Direct sensitivity: Management reports that if the euro depreciates by 10% against the Swiss franc, the profit impact to SKAN is approximately 3% to 4%. This precise EBIT sensitivity is driven by fixed salary costs for the local engineering staff based in Switzerland. This means that persistent franc strength directly threatens the operating margins of custom projects unless offset by higher prices.
7.
Dollar translation: Skan's financial statements reflect significant US dollar exposure because the Americas region historically generated 38.5% of net sales. When the US dollar depreciates against the Swiss franc, SKAN suffers direct foreign exchange losses from the revaluation of balance sheet assets. This translation exposure became evident in 2025 when a temporary suspension of a major US GLP-1 project dragged down regional sales.

Hedging Policies and Contract Mechanics

10.
Active derivatives: SKAN utilizes derivative financial instruments, primarily forward exchange contracts, to manage currency volatility on non-franc customer contracts. The risk policy requires hedging foreign-currency contracts from the exact date of signature to lock in planned project margins. While these derivatives insulate project cash flows during the execution cycle, they cannot neutralize long-term structural currency changes.
11.
Natural protection: Skan builds a natural hedge within its cost structure by procurement of raw materials and complex technical components in euros and US dollars. Matching its foreign-currency project revenues with local-currency sourcing expenses reduces the group's dependence on bank-facilitated forward contracts. However, this operational hedge remains partial because Swiss personnel expenses cannot be paid in foreign currencies.
12.
Extended horizons: Skan's transaction hedging practices are similar to peer VAT Group, which actively hedges its broad foreign currency exposures over rolling periods of one to three years. However, peer Stadler Rail highlights that while well-planned hedging concepts can stabilize project-level operating margins, they offer no protection at the top line against currency translation headwinds. This operational reality confirms that derivatives only buy time for exporters to adjust their underlying physical cost structures.
