Section 2 – Capitalised Labour Dynamics by Division

Section summary

  2. Slowing network investments will force BT to expense a larger portion of its labor costs unless headcount is reduced aggressively.

Baseline labor capitalization trends

1.
Accurate verification: BT Group's official financial statements confirm that the company capitalised £1,388m of direct labor during the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026. This level of capitalised direct labor represents a modest decline from the £1,412m recorded in the previous fiscal year. Analysts monitor these capitalised direct labor movements closely because any reduction in capitalised amounts directly increases expensed staff costs and places immediate downward pressure on reported earnings.
2.
Stable capitalisation: BT Group reported £807m of capitalised indirect labor in the same fiscal year. This figure remained flat compared to the £806m of capitalised indirect labor recorded in the prior year. This extensive capitalisation has historically helped the telecom incumbent shield its operating margins from inflation during intensive infrastructure rollouts.
3.
Divisional concentration: BT Group does not publish a precise divisional breakdown of its capitalised labor within its segment reporting notes. However, Redburn Atlantic analysts estimate that the vast majority of capitalised labor resides within the Openreach network division due to its extensive fiber-to-the-premises deployment. This high concentration means any future slowdown in the network build will disproportionately impact Openreach's cost structure and operational profitability.
4.
Incremental overheads: BT Group's acquisition of TalkTalk and PlatformX out of administration on October 5, 2026, introduces a new, separate reporting segment for the group. While the transaction protects wholesale revenues, TalkTalk was historically loss-making and is expected to generate a trading loss of approximately £60m for the remainder of the fiscal year. This retail integration could complicate the group's ongoing capitalised labor reduction strategy and drag down consolidated margins.
5.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research suggests that the financial benefits of acquiring TalkTalk to protect Openreach lines may be competitively unwound. The broker highlights that competitor Virgin Media O2 is expanding its off-net retail presence, which poses an active competitive threat to Openreach's wholesale business. Consequently, stabilizing capitalised labor dynamics through headcount cuts may not prevent line losses from degrading BT Group's long-term earnings.

Section 3 – Fibre Build Slowdown and Engineering Headcount De-escalation

Section summary
  1. BT Group's post-milestone fibre slowdown will trigger a substantial reduction in capital expenditure and capitalized labor.
  2. We have high confidence that workforce reductions can offset the margin pressure of expensing previously capitalized engineering costs, based on peer transition models.

Network build transition

1.
Delayed milestone: BT Group has shifted its target for passing 25 million premises with full-fibre to March 2027 from the previous timeline of December 2026. This strategic adjustment allows Openreach to prioritize customer connections as the footprint reached 24.1 million premises at the end of September 2026. Consequently, the group expects capital expenditure to decline significantly as the peak investment cycle reaches its major milestone in March 2027.
2.
Slowing capitalization: Rothschild & Co Redburn expects that a £1bn step-down in BT's capital expenditure will cause own work capitalized to fall by approximately £600m. This decline in capitalized labor requires aggressive workforce reductions to prevent these engineering costs from expensing into the income statement. However, the broker notes that the planned headcount cuts should generate sufficient operating savings to support medium-term EBITDA goals.
3.
Aggressive downsizing: Morgan Stanley highlights that BT Group is on track to reduce its headcount from 98k in FY26 to 65-70k by the end of the decade. The reduction is largely driven by fewer operational roles needed at Openreach as the peak fibre build cycle concludes. Analysts remain confident that these labor savings will successfully insulate operating profit margins from the capitalization wind-down.
4.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research warns that the next phase of Openreach's fibre deployment beyond 25 million homes will be more difficult and expensive than previous builds. The broker notes that multidwelling units represent 40% of the post-milestone footprint and carry higher upfront unit construction costs. Consequently, engineering headcount reductions may be slower than anticipated if Ofcom and government policies require continued aggressive expansion.

Peer precedents and comparisons

Normalized spending: Orange SA has demonstrated a successful transition into a mature network phase by maintaining its capital expenditure to sales ratio at a stable target of fifteen percent. 6.
Migrating costs: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted an accounting change at Orange SA that shifted €50m from capital expenditure directly into operating expenses in fiscal year 2022. This cost migration occurred because of fixed customer churn and the accounting treatment of co-investment capital allocations. The precedent suggests that BT Group faces similar balance-sheet-to-income-statement risks as Openreach customer lines transition.

Structural separation: Telecom Italia undertook a radical corporate restructuring by agreeing to divest its regulated NetCo fixed-line network assets to Kohlberg Kravis Roberts & Co. The transaction was valued at an enterprise value of approximately €20bn to help the group pay down its elevated debt pile. Additionally, the network transition was supported by intensive voluntary labor exits, achieving substantial workforce reductions through early retirements.

Flat investments: Iliad SA reported nearly flat capital expenditure in its French operations during fiscal year 2023 as network rollout matured. The company successfully offset rising equipment investments by achieving a substantial decline in fibre-to-the-home capital spending. Section 4 – Labor Agreements and Restructuring Cost per Leaver

Section summary
  1. BT Group's restructuring costs per paid leaver surged in fiscal year 2026 as voluntary workforce exits slowed following the peak deployment of full-fibre infrastructure.
  2. While negotiated union agreements help protect operational margins, the financial burden of individual exit packages remains elevated compared to historical baselines.

Restructuring efficiency metrics

1.
Substantial cost: BT Group experienced a sharp increase in its restructuring expenditure per leaver during the fiscal year ending March 31, 2026. The group recorded total restructuring charges of £336m in fiscal year 2026 while reporting that 2,119 employees departed under paid leaver schemes. This equates to a calculated restructuring cost of approximately £158.57k per paid leaver in fiscal year 2026. This elevated unit cost reflects the growing complexity of late-stage network transformation.
2.
Moderate historical: BT Group maintained a much lower restructuring cost profile during the previous fiscal year due to a higher volume of natural attrition. The company reported total restructuring charges of £448m in fiscal year 2025 alongside 6,661 employees leaving under paid schemes. This yields a calculated restructuring cost of approximately £67.26k per paid leaver in fiscal year 2025. This lower cost structure suggests that early-stage corporate restructuring programs achieved greater operational efficiency than current initiatives.
3.
Refined measures: BT Group's dedicated leaver payments present a narrower view of the immediate cash impact of employee departures. The company reported specific leaver-related restructuring costs of £262m in fiscal year 2026 and £278m in fiscal year 2025. These figures generate calculated specific leaver costs of approximately £123.64k in fiscal year 2026 and £41.74k in fiscal year 2025 per departed staff member. These rising specific costs will continue to pressure near-term cash reserves unless exit packages are renegotiated.
4.
Slowing momentum: BT Group's half-year results for fiscal year 2027 indicate that the pace of restructuring outflows is beginning to moderate. The group recorded restructuring charges of £85m for the half year ending September 30, 2026, which represents a decline from the £121m reported in the prior year's first half. This downward trajectory is expected to reduce immediate pressure on cash flows as the company transitions its workforce.

Union negotiations and agreements

5.
Limited visibility: BT Group has not publicly disclosed any definitive agreements with the CWU or Prospect unions regarding specific pay rates or voluntary exit terms for the calendar years 2027 and 2028. Corporate disclosures confirm that the company is pursuing a multi-year cost reduction plan aiming for £3.5bn in gross annualised savings by fiscal year 2030. Analysts must rely on further union negotiations to confirm the exact financial parameters of future exit packages.
6.
Potential friction: BT Group's historical labor relations suggest that implementing major headcount reductions will face active resistance from employee unions. Industry analyses note that Prospect has previously pushed back on corporate restructuring plans, citing concerns over extreme culture changes. If unions reject future voluntary severance proposals, the group may struggle to achieve its planned headcount reduction without experiencing costly labor disputes.
7.
Ambitious reduction: BT Group is executing an aggressive workforce downsizing strategy that aims to shrink the employee base to between 65,000 and 70,000 by fiscal year 2030. This plan represents a substantial decline from the 98,000 employees recorded in fiscal year 2026 and the 90,000 reported in the first half of fiscal year 2027. Achieving these milestones is highly dependent on securing cooperative voluntary departure frameworks with major labor organizations.
8.
Contrary evidence: BT Group's ability to smoothly execute voluntary exits is contradicted by its history of active disputes and union resistance during major restructuring events. Morgan Stanley research highlighted in May 2023 that the group's workforce is heavily unionized and has recently lost productive work days due to engineers going on strike over pay disputes. This history suggests that union cooperation on headcount reductions may be more fragile than management's positive long-term projections imply.
