Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Brokers remain highly skeptical that cost savings can offset mounting pricing pressures across the medtech capital goods sector.
  2. Understanding this margin dynamic determines whether consensus forecasts for 2027 and 2028 are too optimistic or easily beatable.

Cost program implications

1.
Defined question: "Pointed follow-up question for a stock picker's sector note, Sector B.6. Medtech, capital goods, with Carl Zeiss Meditec (AFX-DE) as one worked example only. Our first draft cannot yet judge this: How much of the cost programmes now running in this Sector is already in consensus forecasts for 2027 and 2028? For Carl Zeiss Meditec (ProfitUp, 160 million euros of net savings by the 2028/29 financial year), Elekta (savings above 500 million Swedish kronor), Tecan (Rewired, 25 to 35 million Swiss francs by 2028) and Philips (Project Synchronize, 1.5 billion euros), what margin do brokers forecast against each company's own target, what share of the savings has been delivered so far, how much is used up by price falls (for example Chinese tenders for eye lenses), and what do expert calls and former employees say about execution under the chief executives appointed since August 2025?."
2.
Critical margin drivers: Structural cost-out initiatives are the primary mechanism for these capital goods players to restore operational momentum. While Philips targets 1.5 billion euros in productivity savings, execution on margin improvement remains a show-me story. Consequently, whether these savings flow directly to the bottom line or are absorbed by inflation is the central debate. This dynamic will ultimately dictate sector earnings.
3.
Accelerated earnings risk: If cost programs fail to deliver, the downside to consensus EPS is material across the cohort. For example, Morgan Stanley warns that failing to reset expectations in China cataract and refractive volumes poses substantial threat to Carl Zeiss Meditec's earnings. Thus, the execution of programs like ProfitUp directly determines whether current valuation multiples are sustainable. Investors must therefore monitor these programs closely to protect capital.

Margins versus sales debate

6.
Sales volume dependency: A closely related question is whether medtech companies can expand margins faster than top-line sales growth. Historically, capital goods companies have struggled to expand margins without volume leverage. For example, Elekta's guidance relies heavily on backlog conversion amid soft order trends. If pricing erosion accelerates, margin targets will become virtually impossible to hit.

For example, Elekta's strong first-quarter earnings were significantly supported by tariff refunds rather than pure cost-out initiatives. Section 2 – Carl Zeiss Meditec: ProfitUp Delivery and Pricing Headwinds

Section summary
  1. Broker estimates remain significantly below Carl Zeiss Meditec's midterm adjusted EBITA target of over 15% due to backend-loaded savings.
  2. Severe pricing headwinds from Chinese tenders and a delayed leadership transition constrain near-term operating leverage and earnings growth.

ProfitUp target and broker forecasts

1.
Ambitious margin targets: Carl Zeiss Meditec announced in May 2026 its ProfitUp program targeting 160 million euros in net annual savings by the 2028/29 financial year. Management expects this initiative to restore profitability and lift the adjusted EBITA margin above 15% in the midterm. However, these self-help measures are highly backend-loaded, which means that near-term margins will remain under pressure as restructuring costs of up to 150 million euros are incurred. This structural transition directly delays the recovery of earnings per share.
2.
Cautious broker forecasts: Morgan Stanley's report of 20 October 2026 projects that Carl Zeiss Meditec's adjusted EBITA margin will only reach 8.1% in fiscal year 2026/27. The broker's model further estimates that the operating margin will recover to 9.9% in fiscal year 2027/28. These estimates sit substantially below the company's targets, because slow refractive market recovery and ProfitUp implementation costs limit operating leverage. This conservative trajectory indicates that consensus expectations are still exposed to downward revisions.
3.
Peer margin comparison: Deutsche Bank Research's note of 15 October 2026 forecasts a 9.0% adjusted EBITA margin for Carl Zeiss Meditec in fiscal year 2026/27. This operating profitability is low compared to European medtech peers, such as Coloplast's high-teens operating margins. Underwhelming operating leverage means that Carl Zeiss Meditec will continue to trade at a discount unless cost savings materialize faster than anticipated. Consequently, the company's valuation remains highly dependent on successful restructuring execution.
4.
Delayed savings benefit: Bernstein Research's thesis of 23 September 2026 highlights that initial ProfitUp benefits will remain very small during fiscal year 2026/27. The report notes that the company's restructuring plan of 200 million euros in gross savings is ambitious and carries meaningful execution risks. This slow pacing implies that operating margins will not show linear progress toward the midterm target. - Prepared Remarks & Follow Ups Consequently, near-term earnings per share will likely disappoint investors expecting a rapid operational turnaround.

These early steps include merging the anterior and posterior ophthalmic surgery units and planning a production site in India. Therefore, operational cash flows will not benefit from these structural changes until at least late 2027.

This handpiece manufacturing is being shifted to Chesterfield to improve scaling and cost efficiency. These rationalizations aim to eliminate low-margin operations but will temporarily depress sales volumes.

However, the company must aggressively mitigate high-single-digit input cost inflation to realize these bottom-line gains.
