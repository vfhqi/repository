Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. Greek infrastructure is undergoing a multi-year funding super-cycle driven by unprecedented EU fund absorption and massive pipeline visibility
  2. The market is consolidating around four dominant contractor groups holding the highest technical degrees, creating a steep share curve
  3. Robust bidding discipline and a shift toward risk-sharing contracts are stabilizing margins across the key infrastructure players

Infrastructure market size and funding drivers

1.
Long-term market pipeline: Greek infrastructure pipeline is estimated at EUR 40 billion to EUR 50 billion over the long term.
2.
Relative RRF size: Greece is a top beneficiary of the EU Recovery and Resilience Fund, with an allocation worth 16% of GDP versus the 5% EU average.
3.
Total capital envelope: The RRF allocates EUR 35.95 billion in total capital to Greece, including EUR 17.7 billion in loans and EUR 18.2 billion in grants.
4.
Macroeconomic growth decoupling: Real GDP growth in Greece is projected to reach 2% in both 2026 and 2027, which runs practically double the pace of the wider Euro Area.
5.
Capital investment trajectory: Greek gross fixed capital formation rose 8.9% in 2025 and is projected to expand by an additional 8.8% in 2026, showing structural outperformance.
6.
Broad sector expansion: Construction gross value added reached EUR 3.9 billion in 2023, marking a 14% annual increase.

Competitor tiers and consolidation dynamics

7.
Steep concentration curve: The elite tier of Greek contractors is limited to only four groups holding the seventh-class license required for large-scale public tenders.
8.
Sector tier list: The dominant domestic EPC competitors bidding for these major infrastructure works are GEK Terna, AVAX, Aktor, and Intrakat.
9.
Dominant leader backlog: GEK Terna leads the sector with a total backlog of EUR 9.1 billion as of May 2026, which includes EUR 6.6 billion in signed contracts.
10.
Scale runner backlog: Aktor maintains a highly competitive position with a secured backlog standing at EUR 4.7 billion as of December 2025, reflecting a 30% average market share in Greece over five years.
11.
Peer group backlogs: Competitors AVAX and Metlen boast construction backlogs of EUR 2.6 billion and EUR 2 billion respectively, cementing the oligopolistic structure.
12.
New entry barriers: Mid-tier contractors like Ekter are attempting to scale up, having submitted applications in 2025 and 2026 to obtain the highest seventh-class degree.

Value chain economics and bidding discipline

13.
Sector margin benchmarks: AVAX recorded a stable pro-forma construction EBITDA margin of 10.7% in Q1 2026 compared to 10.8% for the full year 2025.
14.
Leader construction profitability: GEK Terna reported a healthy adjusted construction EBITDA margin of 11.1% in 2025, up from 9.8% in 2024.
15.
Peer margin profiles: Metlen achieved an Infrastructure and Concessions EBITDA margin of 17.6% in 2025, down slightly from 19.7% in 2024 due to project execution mix.
16.
De-risked bidding behavior: Major European contractors are executing a selective and de-risked approach to new orders, utilizing flexible project structures to minimize cost inflation.
17.
Underlined regulatory criteria: Greek law requires seventh-class contractors to maintain minimum equity of EUR 44 million, three-year turnover of EUR 88 million, and fixed assets of EUR 15 million.
18.
Additional technical thresholds: Qualifying criteria also mandate at least seven executives with a fourth- class MEK degree, five years of experience totaling EUR 36.6 million, and machinery exceeding EUR 3 million.
19.
Post-RRF investment continuation: if the government successfully accelerates alternative EU programs like the Cohesion Policy, infrastructure spending levels will remain elevated post-2026.
20.
Other side of the story: the anticipated funding super-cycle relies on strict absorption timelines; if bureaucratic delays prevent the timely execution of public tenders, the transition from RRF to other EU programs will create a fiscal cliff that compresses constructor margins.

Outliers worth noting

Aktor's revenue trajectory exhibits an extreme growth outlier, with Group turnover expanding from a legacy baseline of EUR 412 million in fiscal year 2023 to EUR 1,395 million in 2025, representing a 238% cumulative increase over two years. This growth rate substantially exceeds peer AVAX Group, which expanded revenues by 47.1% to EUR 958 million in 2025 from EUR 652 million in 2024, and dramatically lags diversified peer Metlen's scale of EUR 7,107 million in 2025. The velocity of this expansion is statistically unusual for a pure-play infrastructure contractor operating in a market constrained by strict regulatory certification requirements and limited to four dominant players. While management attributes the revenue step-change to a secured construction backlog of EUR 4.7 billion as of December 2025 and the acquisition of Aktor Concessions completed on 29 September 2025, the three-fold revenue multiplication within 24 months represents an execution pace that materially outstrips organic construction delivery capacity and raises questions about the sustainability of this growth trajectory once the current backlog normalizes.

The pro-forma leverage position presents a significant outlier, with pro-forma net debt standing at EUR 861 million in 2025, representing 4.2x pro-forma EBITDA. This leverage multiple substantially exceeds peer AVAX Group's net debt of EUR 201 million in 2025 and contrasts sharply with Metlen's projected deleveraging trajectory to 1.7x by 2028. The 4.2x multiple positions Aktor in the top 5% of regional infrastructure leverage at a time when the sector is undergoing consolidation and margin normalization.

Management's proposed EUR 650 million share capital increase standby underwritten by major international banks and the EUR 3.0 billion growth capital program over five years provide strategic context for the elevated debt load. However, the leverage multiple remains an extreme statistical outlier relative to the domestic peer set, particularly when considering that pure-play construction segments face heavy cash outflows from working capital movements during initial phases, which could amplify refinancing pressure if execution timelines extend or public sector certification delays worsen.

Contract asset accumulation represents a working capital outlier, with delays in public work certifications causing Group contract assets to increase by EUR 97 million in late 2025. This contrasts with peer AVAX, which reduced its outstanding trade receivable days to 101 days from 156 days and shortened its cash conversion cycle to 125 days in 2025 from 190 days in 2023, demonstrating peer-wide working capital normalization that Aktor has not yet achieved.

Metlen reported a calculated days sales outstanding of 60 days in 2024, further highlighting Aktor's relative underperformance in converting project execution into cash receipts. With projects implemented on behalf of the Greek State constituting 50.89% of Aktor's total turnover for fiscal year 2025 and the Group deriving more than 10% of total sales individually from both Greek and Romanian public sectors, the EUR 97 million contract asset increase suggests that certification approvals from public sector clients in Greece and Romania suffer prolonged execution and payment delays.

Management's narrative emphasizes backlog execution velocity and operational scale, but the contract asset build-up represents a bottom 5% cash conversion performance relative to the immediate peer group, signaling that public sector payment frictions are materially impacting Aktor's ability to translate revenues into operating liquidity despite the massive secured backlog.
