Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary

1.
High prestige: Intercos is a leading business to business global manufacturer that was founded in 1972 by Dario Ferrari. The company operates as a global cosmetic contract manufacturer specializing in makeup, skincare, and hair products. This outsourced innovation partner serves multinationals, emerging brands, and major retailers across three continents. The business model relies heavily on proprietary formulation and research capabilities to maintain high barriers to entry.

What changed in market and broker expectations.

Before the print, brokers had been softening. Jefferies trimmed its FY26 sales estimate by 40 basis points in May 2026. Morgan Stanley moved its FY26 adjusted EBITDA margin from 15.1% to 15.0% and lowered FY26 EPS by 1%. The bar for Q3 was consensus organic growth of 10.2% and a consensus adjusted EBITDA margin of 15.3%.

The result was a split verdict. Organic growth of 12.0% comfortably surpassed expectations of 10.3%. Brokers still reacted positively. 90% of covering analysts held an overweight rating.

Earnings estimates barely moved: Morgan Stanley trimmed FY26 EPS from €0.73 to €0.72 and left FY27 unchanged at €0.84, while Jefferies raised FY26 EPS slightly from €0.72 to €0.73. Target-price increases came from valuation inputs rather than earnings upgrades. Jefferies cited improving medium-term beauty market growth and a lower US 10-year treasury yield, and Morgan Stanley cited peer group re-rating and a higher multiple assumption.

The sell-side therefore absorbed a mix-dilutive beat as if it were unambiguously good news for earnings. Where the quality of the beat is weaker than it looks. The divisional detail shows where growth came from. Hair and body grew 35.9%, makeup grew 11.0%, and skincare declined 9.72%.

Underabsorption of fixed costs in skincare plants dragged down consolidated margins. Jefferies' divisional revisions make the same point. It raised its FY26 hair and body organic growth estimate from 7.5% to 14.8% while lowering makeup from 6.7% to 5.6%. Even the more constructive estimate changes are therefore being driven by the lowest-margin division.

The strongest counter-argument. The best case against this verdict is that the margin drag is cyclical and reversible. Skincare underabsorption is the identified cause of the margin dilution, so a skincare stabilisation would mechanically release operating leverage. Management expressed high confidence in a skincare recovery during the fourth quarter, and Jefferies kept its FY27 skincare growth expectation at 5.0%. Demand support is strong: the makeup and skincare order book grew by more than 20% year on year as of November

If all of this lands, EPS upside would come from both revenue and margin. I rate that scenario as plausible but not the base case, because it requires a Western skincare turnaround that has not yet appeared in reported numbers.
