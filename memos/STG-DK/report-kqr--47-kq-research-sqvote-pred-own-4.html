Section summary
  1. The development of the US premium handmade cigar market heading into 2027 will directly determine Scandinavian Tobacco Group's ability to stabilize its operating margins and expand its earnings per share. Net read: Potential regulatory exemptions, foreign exchange stability, and strategic capital allocation post-divestment represent critical related variables that will shape the company's long-term financial health and shareholder returns.

1.
Key Question: "Scandinavian Tobacco Group A/S (STG-DK, Copenhagen), the world's largest maker of cigars and pipe tobacco, reporting three divisions: Europe Branded (machine-rolled cigars, smoking and pipe tobacco, XQS nicotine pouches, and contract manufacturing of the BREAK and Moro fine-cut brands for Japan Tobacco), North America Branded & Rest of World (premium handmade cigars such as Macanudo, CAO, Cohiba in the US and Alec Bradley), and North America Online & Retail (Cigars International online and catalogue sales and US cigar superstores). Section 4 – Competitor Pricing and the 2026 US Tariff Refunds

Section summary
  1. The landmark invalidation of federal emergency tariffs in early 2026 sparked widespread duty refunds that are significantly boosting the operating margins of major tobacco manufacturers.
  2. Most industry participants are choosing to retain these refunds to offset persistent cost inflation rather than executing aggressive price cuts or promotional reinvestments.
  3. Scandinavian Tobacco Group has successfully leveraged these payouts to upgrade its full-year earnings outlook while preparing for more balanced price competition heading into 2027.

Federal Tariff Invalidation

1.
Legal precedent: The United States Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that emergency tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act were invalid. Following this decision, the United States Court of International Trade directed federal customs agencies to refund these unlawful duties with interest starting in April 2026. This regulatory reversal unlocked massive cash inflows across multiple importreliant consumer goods sectors.
2.
Corporate accounting: Corporate accounting departments across the consumer products sector have processed these multi-million dollar windfalls as direct operational cost relief. Importers like Jewett-Cameron recorded their received federal customs payments directly as a reduction in the cost of goods sold. This treatment directly lifted gross profitability during the quarters of disbursement. Tobacco manufacturers have adopted similar methods to strengthen reported operating profits during the 2026 fiscal year.
3.
Elevated earnings: Scandinavian Tobacco Group raised its full-year financial expectations in late 2026 due to stronger profitability and the positive impact of trade duties. Management revised its expected adjusted earnings per share range to DKK 9.5 to DKK 11.5, up from the previous range of DKK 9.0 to DKK 11.0. This upgrade reflects both resilient operational performance and higher than expected tariff refunds received during the first three quarters of the year. These cash inflows directly buffer the group's bottom-line results against volume declines.

Section 5 – State Remote-Seller Excise Tax Collection on Online Retailers

Section summary
  1. State remote-seller excise tax mandates present a highly likely headwind that will permanently narrow the price advantage of online premium cigar retailers.
  2. Our confidence in this regulatory shift is high, supported by historical growth deceleration at Cigars International and expanding state-level tax compliance complexity.
  3. Scandinavian Tobacco Group's Focus2030 strategy successfully drives market share gains in the US premium cigar sector through aggressive concentration on its four core power brands.
  4. Private premium competitors like Oettinger Davidoff and Arturo Fuente maintain highly resilient luxury positioning that continues to challenge the pricing power of STG's high-end portfolios.

Legal Foundations and Economic Nexus

1.
Gradual implementation: The regulatory landscape for out-of-state retailers underwent a fundamental shift following a landmark United States Supreme Court decision. The high court ruled in South Dakota v. Wayfair that states can compel remote sellers to collect taxes despite lacking physical assets in that state. This ruling effectively overturned decades of legal precedent that shielded mail-order catalog operators from local tax compliance. Consequently, this landmark decision laid the legal groundwork for states to aggressively target online sales platforms.
2.
Negative growth impacts: Scandinavian Tobacco Group experienced immediate headwinds in its direct-to- consumer operations following the implementation of new state laws. Management reported that the gradual introduction of internet sales taxes in several key states began to impact online revenue growth. Consumers temporarily altered their shopping habits by seeking out alternative retail websites that had not yet compliant tax collection systems. This transitional consumer behavior created short-term friction for Cigars International during the initial rollout phase.
3.

Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported that the overall online handmade cigar category flattened significantly during the 2019 fiscal year compared to prior years of rapid growth. This sudden slowdown was primarily caused by the unfavorable reduction in price benefits for digital buyers. Remote Tobacco Excise Tax Mandates

4.
