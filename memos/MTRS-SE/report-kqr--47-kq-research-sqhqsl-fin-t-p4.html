Section 2 – Tax Rate Evolution, US Growth, and the FoodTech Divestment Impact

Section summary
  1. Munters' effective tax rate remains elevated due to geographic shift and tax asset write-downs. Net read: High confidence exists that the tax rate will remain above the Swedish statutory rate of 20.6% through 2026.
  2. Belimo and Carel exhibit far greater tax efficiency than Munters due to local incentives and tax planning.

Historical Tax Drivers

1.
Elevated tax rates: Munters' reported tax rate reached an exceptional 37% for the full year in 2023. According to corporate reports, this elevated rate was primarily driven by a SEK 80 million deferred tax asset impairment on loss carry-forwards. This non-cash adjustment severely depressed the reported net income in the final quarter of 2023. This shows how accounting adjustments can distort EPS.
2.
Unrecognized tax losses: Munters continued to face tax headwinds as its effective tax rate stood at 31% in both 2024 and 2025. Management noted that tax losses not recognized in Sweden and Germany negatively impacted the group tax rate. If these losses remain unrecognized, they will continue to prevent the tax rate from falling toward the Swedish statutory level. This structural drag limits near-term earnings expansion.
3.
Swedish statutory gap: Munters' actual tax rate of 31% is significantly higher than its home statutory rate. For comparison, the statutory corporate tax rate in Sweden is only 20.6%. This substantial gap indicates that Munters fails to optimize its global tax structure compared to domestic peers. This gap means that earnings growth is less tax-efficient than investors might expect.

US Expansion Drag

4.
US geographic shift: Rapid earnings growth in the United States has structurally increased Munters' overall tax burden. The US federal statutory corporate tax rate is 21%, which is higher than the Swedish rate. Because USbased Data Center Technologies expansion drives a larger share of profit, Munters faces higher local tax rates. This geographical shift represents a permanent upward pressure on the effective tax rate.
5.
State tax addition: The tax burden from US operations is further compounded by state and local taxes. These local taxes typically add an extra 1.8% to the statutory federal rate. This high-tax geographic mix means that US earnings growth dilutes group margin on a net basis. Unless Munters implements aggressive tax planning, US expansion will keep the group ETR elevated.
6.
Valuation allowance comparison: Vertiv provides a comparable case of US tax dynamics where US profitability allowed for the reversal of valuation allowances. Vertiv released USD 103.4 million in deferred tax asset valuation allowances in 2023. This shows that sustained US profit can unlock significant tax benefits over time. Munters could experience similar write-backs if its US operations achieve long-term profitability.

FoodTech Divestment Relief

7.
Divestment tax impact: The potential divestment of the FoodTech business segment will directly impact Munters' future tax rate. Selling this business area would eliminate agricultural earnings that are subject to diverse global tax jurisdictions. This divestment would simplify the group's corporate structure and tax profile. The transaction's tax footprint will depend on how the sale is legally structured.
8.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies notes that Munters' 2026 effective tax rate is expected to remain high, which leads to a 6% reduction in their 2026 EPS forecast. This suggests that near-term operational challenges and tariff headwinds will prevent any immediate tax rate relief from materializing. Consequently, investors should not expect rapid ETR normalization before 2027.
9.
Divestment historical precedent: Systemair's commercial air conditioning divestment in 2023 serves as a key peer precedent. Systemair divested its air conditioning business for an enterprise value of EUR 100 million. This strategic portfolio move allowed the company to lower debt and focus on core ventilation. This indicates that divestments can improve overall corporate efficiency despite removing some revenue.

Peer Tax Efficiency

10.
Belimo tax efficiency: Belimo exhibits exceptional tax efficiency compared to Munters. Belimo reported an effective tax rate of 16.7% in 2024 and 16.8% in 2022. This efficiency is driven by Zurich cantonal tax reform benefits such as patent box and R&D deductions. This contrast highlights Munters' lack of localized tax incentive maximization.
11.
Alfa Laval tax comparison: Alfa Laval represents a more aligned Swedish peer with a stable tax profile. Alfa Laval reported a stable tax rate of 26% in both 2024 and 2025. The group consistently guides its tax rate within 24% to 26% over the long term. This suggests that Swedish multinationals can maintain stable tax rates despite global exposure.
12.
Systemair tax volatility: Systemair provides a contrast with a higher and more volatile tax rate. Systemair's ETR was 27.2% in 2024/25 and rose to 30.7% in Q3 2025/26. This increase arose from abolishing hyperinflation tax accounting in Turkey. This indicates how geographic exposures in volatile markets can negatively impact tax rates.

Long-Term Outlook

13.
Carel tax stability: Carel Industries represents another peer with a highly stable tax rate. Carel reported a stable tax rate of 22.6% in 2025 and 23.1% in late 2024. This stability is supported by favorable high-tech company tax status in China. This highlights how international tax agreements can secure long-term ETR predictability.
14.
Post-2026 ETR normalization: Munters' effective tax rate should normalize after 2026 if several factors align. A successful FoodTech divestment and the utilization of unrecognized tax losses in Sweden would support this trend. This means that long-term reported EPS growth will accelerate as the ETR drops. This improvement makes the post-2026 period critical for valuation upside.
15.
Other side of the story: While ETR normalization is expected post-2026, persistent US geographic mix shift could prevent the tax rate from falling below 30%. Continued rapid growth in the high-tax US data center segment will offset any tax relief from a FoodTech sale. Therefore, investors must recognize that the tax rate might remain permanently elevated if US expansion continues to dominate.

Section 3 – Debt Structure, Interest-Rate Exposure, and Covenant Headroom

Section summary
  1. High confidence exists that Munters' leverage will remain elevated above three times adjusted EBITDA through 2026.
  2. This high confidence is based on Q3 2026 reported results and persistent negative operational cash flows.
  3. Floating-rate benchmark exposures leave Munters highly sensitive to interest rate hikes without derivative mitigation.
  4. Peer comparisons with Systemair and Vertiv demonstrate alternative capital models featuring significantly lower leverage.

Munters Debt Portfolio

1.
Elevated group leverage: Munters' leverage has trended upward due to persistent cash flow pressures. The net leverage ratio rose to 3.4x adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 3.2x in June. This leverage increase was mainly driven by negative cash flows and higher lease liabilities. DNB Carnegie notes this elevated ratio means that Munters' near-term financial flexibility will remain severely constrained unless cash generation improves.
2.
Shrinking debt maturity: Munters' debt maturity profile has contracted over the past year. The average maturity of its interest-bearing liabilities stood at 2.5 years at the end of September 2026. This average maturity represents a decline from the 2.9 years recorded in June 2026. This shorter maturity profile means that Munters faces elevated near-term refinancing risks as its obligations mature.
3.
Diversified funding structures: Munters relies on its medium-term note program and green bond issuances to diversify funding. Under this SEK 5 billion programme, Munters established inaugural green bonds in June 2025, including a SEK 200 million three-year bond and a SEK 800 million five-year bond. In September 2025, the company further issued a SEK 1 billion four-year senior green bond. These structured programs ensure that Munters secures stable long-term capital channels to support its ongoing green initiatives.
4.
Increasing lease obligations: Munters' short-term debt and lease commitments have expanded rapidly. In the fourth quarter of 2025, Munters issued a SEK 400 million bond and raised outstanding commercial papers to a nominal SEK 800 million. Concurrently, lease liabilities have risen, driven by major facilities like the new plant in Amesbury, USA. This heavy lease burden means that Munters' lease commitments directly inflate its net debt and pressure reported leverage figures.

Interest Exposure and Covenants

5.
High interest sensitivity: Munters remains highly exposed to fluctuations in benchmark rates due to its floating- rate debt structure. Based on unhedged borrowings at the end of December 2025, a one percent interest rate increase globally would reduce net financial income by SEK 49 million. This sensitivity was calculated at SEK 52 million at the end of 2024. This vulnerability means that rising benchmark interest rates directly depress Munters' earnings per share.
6.
Stable covenant cushions: Munters' core banking agreements are governed by consolidated net debt-to-EBITDA covenants. Although the reported leverage reached 3.4x in Q3 2026, the covenant definition excludes lease liabilities under IFRS 16 and net pension liabilities. The group confirms that covenants were successfully met as of December 31, 2024, despite rising absolute debt. This structure guarantees substantial covenant headroom for operational funding unless EBITDA deteriorates severely.
7.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie notes that Munters' elevated leverage ratio of 3.4x is significantly higher than historical levels. The broker highlights that net leverage was only 1.4x in late 2025 before the recent wave of debt-financed acquisitions and working capital build-up. This rapid escalation means that Munters' M&A optionality is completely restricted unless it completes major divestments. Unhedged rate exposures: Munters maintains a policy of not utilizing interest rate derivatives to hedge its outstanding floating-rate borrowings. According to the 2024 annual report, the majority of borrowings are centrally managed by Group Treasury to monitor limits closely. Because its loan facilities carry variable Stibor and Euribor benchmark rates, Munters remains directly exposed to global central bank actions. This unhedged approach means that interest expenses will fluctuate directly with macro rates.

Financing Peer Comparisons

8.
Conservative peer leverage: Systemair operates a highly conservative financing strategy compared to Munters. Systemair reported a net leverage ratio of only 0.54x EBITDA at the end of the 2025/26 financial year. This low leverage stood even lower at 0.27x when adjusted to exclude lease liabilities. This pristine capital structure means that Systemair retains immense capacity for accretive acquisitions without compromising its financial stability.
9.
Flexible peer refinancing: Systemair refinanced its existing bilateral loans on April 29, 2026, transitioning to unsecured debt. The company established two unsecured revolving credit facilities of EUR 65 million each with Nordea and Handelsbanken. This unsecured structure replaces previous secured financing and reduces both financing costs and operational constraints. This flexibility shows how low leverage enables superior bank loan terms compared to highly leveraged peers.
10.
Rapid peer deleveraging: Vertiv Holdings provides a notable past case of aggressive deleveraging achieved through massive operational growth rather than capital injections. Vertiv's EBITDA leverage was elevated at 5.0x to 6.0x during the 2020 to 2022 period. However, Vertiv reduced its net leverage ratio to approximately 0.5x by the end of 2025. This deleveraging demonstrates how rapid EBITDA expansion can swiftly repair credit metrics and unlock strategic capital flexibility. 125 - Analyst/Investor Strong peer cushions: Systemair maintains substantial cushions above its minimum covenant thresholds. Systemair's financing agreements stipulate a minimum interest coverage ratio of 3.50x, but the company achieved a stellar 20.33x in the 2025/26 fiscal year. Similarly, its net leverage covenant is capped at 3.50x, compared to its actual net leverage of 0.54x. This significant headroom means that Systemair faces virtually no risk of covenant breach even during severe cyclical downturns.

Capital Structure Pathways

11.
Ambitious target ratios: Munters' management prioritizes deleveraging its balance sheet back to historical comfort zones. Historically, management highlighted that reducing leverage to a mid-term target of 1.5x to 2.5x net debt-to-EBITDA remains the top priority. Although strategic M&A has driven leverage to 3.4x in late 2026, the company continues to focus on structural deleveraging. This focus suggests that Munters will pause large-scale acquisitions until leverage returns to guided levels.
12.
Underutilized acquisition firepowers: Systemair's strong balance sheet provides extensive financial capacity to pursue consolidations. Jefferies estimates that Systemair could comfortably re-lever to 2.0x or 2.5x EBITDA without breaching its internal 3.5x leverage ceiling. Re-leveraging to these levels would release between SEK 2.9 billion and SEK 3.8 billion in capital. This immense dry powder means that Systemair can accelerate its high-margin M&A pipeline whenever attractive targets emerge. Strong cash conversions: Vertiv's rapid cash generation enables flexible capital allocation despite low leverage targets. Vertiv generated USD 1.0 billion in operating cash flow and USD 910 million in adjusted free cash flow during the fourth quarter of 2025. This strong conversion allowed the company to deploy USD 1.0 billion for strategic acquisitions while maintaining its 0.5x leverage ratio. This performance indicates that rapid operational scaling enables non-dilutive asset purchases without increasing leverage. Other side of the story: While Munters' reported net leverage of 3.4x seems elevated, its financial risk is mitigated by strong covenant exemptions and structured long-term debt. Because covenant calculations completely exclude IFRS 16 lease liabilities, the operational default risk remains extremely low. Furthermore, the successful establishment of the SEK 5 billion MTN program ensures continuous liquidity access. Therefore, Munters' current leverage should not trigger banking defaults or impair core credit facilities in the near future.

Munters Amortisation Profile

According to Munters' Q1 2026 report, the company recorded total quarterly amortisation of MSEK 78, of which MSEK 18 was directly related to acquisitions. 4.
Historic purchase allocations: The baseline for Munters' current amortisation schedule was established through major transactions in 2024. Munters' 2024 annual report shows that acquisition-related amortisation was MSEK 61, reflecting preliminary purchase price allocations for Geoclima, Hotraco, and Airprotech. This long-term amortisation profile remains a key adjustment point for sell-side models.

Segmented asset additions: The expansion of Munters' intangible asset base has been driven by strategic technological additions. Munters' 2025 annual report indicates that the group added customer relationships valued at MSEK 191 during the year. These additions were primarily tied to the finalisation of purchase price allocations for acquisitions completed in late 2024. This massive asset accumulation ensures that amortisation will remain elevated post-2026.

Option Liabilities and Settlements

6.
MTech option resolution: Munters successfully resolved its largest outstanding option liability in early 2026. According to Munters' Q3 2026 report, a contingent consideration of MSEK 167 was paid in January 2026 to the previous owners of MTech Systems. This final payment followed an initial 80% transaction settlement of USD 80.7 million completed in March 2025. This resolution eliminates further MTech balance sheet volatility but reduced short-term cash reserves.
7.
InoBram minority buyout: Munters moved to secure full ownership of its Brazilian operations by accelerating option exercises. Munters' 2025 annual report discloses that the company agreed in late 2025 to buy out the remaining 40% of InoBram for BRL 83.3 million, equivalent to MSEK 148. This transaction was executed well ahead of the original 2027 option schedule. Consequently, Munters has consolidated full ownership, avoiding potential fair value escalation risks.
8.
Automated Environments commitment: The acquisition of Automated Environments includes a structured path to full ownership. Munters' 2024 annual report states that the company acquired an initial 80% stake in AEI, with the remaining 20% scheduled to be acquired by the end of 2026. Because 100% of AEI has been consolidated from the outset, the final purchase will not alter operating margins. However, this cash outflow will pressure Munters' leverage.

Peer Earn-Out Comparisons

9.
Carel Kiona liability: Carel Industries exhibits a highly comparable structure regarding its outstanding put and call options. Carel's Q2 2026 interim report highlights a current liability of EUR 50.33 million related to the remaining 17.6% minority stake in Kiona. This liability was reclassified to current in 2026 because the option exercise period is scheduled for 2027. This imminent cash outflow means Carel faces similar deleveraging priorities as Munters.
10.
Carel Senva settlement: Carel's recent transaction history illustrates the cash requirements of managing complex earn-out structures. Carel's Q2 2026 interim report notes that the group settled the Senva earn-out during the first half of 2026 for a total of EUR 17.37 million. This settlement represents a reduction from the EUR 23.01 million liability recognized at the end of 2024. This successful negotiation shows that active liability management can deliver tailwinds.
11.
LU-VE US restating: LU-VE SpA provides a critical precedent for how reviews of useful lives can impact amortisation. LU-VE's 2025 annual report notes that the company reviewed the residual useful life of its US customer list in 2023, restating it from 16 years to five years. This adjustment led to an annual increase in amortisation of EUR 303 thousand. This case suggests that underperforming acquisitions can trigger sudden amortisation.
12.
LU-VE Zyklus earn-out: LU-VE's historical acquisition of Zyklus Heat Transfer in 2018 represents a successful precedent of structured multi-year earn-outs. According to LU-VE's H1 2026 presentation, the transaction incorporated a three-year earn-out scheme based on EBITDA targets. This structure allowed LU-VE to align the final acquisition cost with actual US market performance. This suggests that well-designed earn-out terms mitigate early-stage risks for buyers.

Future IAC Trajectory

13.
