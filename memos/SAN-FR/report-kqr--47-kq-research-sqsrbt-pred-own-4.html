Section 2 – Amvuttra Sales Outlook for 2027 and 2028

Section summary
  1. Alnylam faces substantial headwinds for Amvuttra sales in 2027 and 2028 due to tighter Medicare Advantage step edits and 340B reimbursement reforms.
  2. Broker estimates diverge widely as bullish peak targets of eleven billion dollars contrast with near-term downgrades driven by slowing new-patient starts.

Slower starts and insurance restrictions

1.
Slower commercial ramp: Alnylam experienced a notable deceleration in commercial momentum for Amvuttra in late 2026 because of non-clinical barriers. Specifically, HC Wainwright reported on November 17, 2026, that de novo patient conversion was slower than expected. These slower starts could significantly flatten the longterm TTR growth trajectory, which directly dampens Sanofi's projected royalty income.
2.
Tighter access policies: UnitedHealthcare enacted a restrictive Medicare Advantage policy on October 1, 2026, that places Amvuttra behind oral stabilizers for cardiomyopathy patients without polyneuropathy. According to HC Wainwright on November 17, 2026, patients must show progressive worsening or hospitalization on stabilizers to qualify for the silencer. This policy change threatens to divert first-line patient starts to cheaper oral therapies, thereby limiting near-term sales expansion.
3.
Growing payer barriers: Jefferies Research warned in October 2026 that insurance restrictions are beginning to expand beyond a single major carrier. Analysts noted that new step edits represent a negative surprise that increases the risk of other plans following suit. These expanding prior authorization hurdles could constrain first-line market share growth for Amvuttra in 2027 and 2028.
4.
Slowing prescriber conversion: Morgan Stanley reported on October 29, 2026, that Alnylam's massive salesforce expansion has failed to yield immediate revenue contributions. Management indicated that the next wave of targeted physicians is slower to adopt Amvuttra than early prescribers. This slower physician conversion rate represents a near-term hurdle that will likely dampen sequential volume acceleration through 2027.
5.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on November 13, 2026, that monthly Symphony script tracking showed Amvuttra volume grew in October 2026. This positive growth occurred despite growing market anxiety over payer restrictions. Furthermore, September scripts had previously increased by approximately 3% month-over-month, suggesting that underlying patient demand remains resilient despite commercial friction.

Jefferies Research reported on November 4, 2026, that federal reimbursement for 340B-acquired outpatient drugs will drop to average sales price minus 28% starting January 1, 2027. This deep cut turns a historical positive spread into a loss for hospitals, which could deter providers from prescribing Amvuttra over oral alternatives.

Section 4 – Sanofi's Amvuttra Royalty Income Evolution through 2030

Section summary
  1. Sanofi faces a substantial near-term cash windfall as Amvuttra sales trigger the maximum thirty percent tiered royalty bracket.
  2. Long-term royalty income faces severe erosion from Alnylam's strategy to actively transition patients to royalty-free nucresiran.

According to Alnylam's regulatory filing on October 29, 2026, the royalty rate begins at 15% for annual sales up to $150 million and increases to 30% for sales exceeding $1.5 billion. These escalating tiers mean that Sanofi's royalty rate expands dynamically as commercial adoption grows. This structured payaway is highly lucrative because it flows directly to Sanofi's operating profits with zero corresponding product cost of goods sold.

This preservation of Amvuttra volume would allow Sanofi to sustain its 30% royalty rate on sales above $1.5 billion. Thus, a clinical delay for nucresiran paradoxically prolongs Sanofi's near-term cash flows.
