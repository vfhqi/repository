Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The development of Hellenic Telecom's System Solutions business is critical to OTE's growth and directly impacts Deutsche Telekom's consolidated earnings.

Primary Financial Context

1.
Primary key question: "For Hellenic Telecom (OTE, HTO-GR), what is known about the revenue, EBITDA margin, backlog and customer mix of the System Solutions (ICT) business: how much of its 2025 and 2026 revenue came from Recovery and Resilience Facility-funded public projects, what management and brokers expect after the programme ends in December 2026, how much spill-over, maintenance and private-sector revenue follows, how quickly Greek public bodies pay (trade receivables reached 716.2 EURm in Q1 2026 from 592.3 EURm at end-2025, company), and what happened to revenue, margins and receivables at Greek IT integrators (Quest Holdings' Uni Systems, Space Hellas) and at other European incumbents when earlier EU-funded programmes ended?"
2.
Sourcing and peer scope: Hellenic Telecom's ICT business was evaluated using thirty-two relevant documents and sources covering three distinct companies and two historical precedents. Research uncovered Quest Holdings' Uni Systems and Space Hellas as the primary domestic peers, and European telecom incumbents like Telefonica and Telecom Italia as regional precedents. No relevant precedents were excluded from this analysis because all identified entities provided valuable comparative data. This broad baseline provides a complete overview of the Greek IT integration sector.
3.
Parent company exposure: Deutsche Telekom holds a 54.62% stake in Hellenic Telecom and fully consolidates its financial results. This means any deterioration in OTE's profit margins directly impacts the parent group's consolidated EBITDAaL and earnings. Deutsche Telekom uses smaller Central and Eastern European markets as testing grounds for digital products. Consequently, Hellenic Telecom's transition from public projects to private cybersecurity and cloud services serves as a test case for regional offerings.

Strategic Importance and EPS Sensitivity

4.
Strategic growth driver: Hellenic Telecom's System Solutions business has become the primary driver of top-line revenue growth in Greece. Expert Call transcripts show that traditional telecom services offer limited room for expansion, making ICT projects crucial for sustaining mid-term earnings growth. Broker estimates from Morgan Stanley forecast that strong private-sector demand for digital transformation will offset wholesale weakness. Therefore, the success of the ICT transition directly determines whether OTE can maintain its sector-leading service revenue growth.
5.
Margin compression risks: Hellenic Telecom's earnings per share are highly sensitive to shifts in public sector margins. While public sector contracts drive high revenue growth, they typically carry much lower EBITDA margins than retail services. As the Recovery and Resilience Facility investment cycle winds down in late 2026, OTE must transition to higher-margin private contracts. Unless the company secures private-sector maintenance deals, average ICT margins could decline and compress OTE's projected 2027 EPS.
6.
Working capital strain: Payment delays from Greek public bodies consume significant cash and pressure OTE's short-term liquidity. OTE's trade receivables reached 716.2 EURm in Q1 2026 as public entities accelerated billing. At the national level, G2B payment delays force Greek enterprises to secure an extra 52,000 EUR in financing per million in sales. This working capital drag increases financing costs, which directly reduces free cash flow and EPS because higher interest expenses offset operating gains.
7.
Other side of the story: Some market experts argue that Hellenic Telecom's EPS is well insulated from domestic public sector risks. Deutsche Bank Research expects OTE to grow its ICT segment by expanding with private enterprises and securing major international contracts. For example, OTE won a 45 EURm fixed datacenter contract with NATO, diversifying its client base. This international expansion is expected to support solid revenue growth going forward.

Section 2 – System Solutions Revenue and Backlog Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecom's System Solutions business has achieved rapid growth but faces low segment profitability.
  2. The termination of the Recovery and Resilience Facility will significantly reduce public project revenues by late 2026.
  3. Management is pivoting toward private enterprise contracts to mitigate upcoming public funding cliffs.

Revenue Mix and Historical Growth

1.
Sustained revenue acceleration: Hellenic Telecom's System Solutions business has established itself as a primary driver of the group's domestic expansion. According to the company's full year financial report published on 26 February 2026, ICT system solutions revenue surged by 33.4% year-on-year to reach 418.6 EURm in fiscal year 2025. This followed an equally robust performance in fiscal year 2024, when system solutions revenue grew by 33.5% to 313.9 EURm as reported in the annual financial report from 26 February 2025. This segment continues to support top-line resilience as traditional wholesale revenue declines, which means that ICT projects are now vital for sustaining revenue growth.
2.
Robust quarterly momentum: The segment's expansion has accelerated into the first half of fiscal year 2026 due to intensive public sector spending. In its Q1 2026 earnings release dated 8 May 2026, Hellenic Telecom announced that system solutions revenue grew by 76.3% year-on-year to 162.0 EURm. Furthermore, the company's Q2 2026 results press release published on 29 July 2026 showed continued momentum, with ICT revenues growing by 52.1% year-on-year to 138.0 EURm. This strong momentum indicates that enterprise digital transition remains a highly active business stream.
3.
Segment revenue split: Hellenic Telecom's internal financial reporting highlights the growing weight of the ICT segment within its broader "Other" revenue reporting line. The company's interim financial report from 29 July 2026 noted that total group revenues from ICT system solutions reached 299.8 EURm in the first half of 2026, compared to 182.5 EURm in the first half of 2025. This rapid expansion has increased the segment's share of domestic Greek operations, which helps offset legacy retail declines but dilutes consolidated group profitability margins because of lower underlying contract yields. Therefore, tracking this segment's progression is critical for forecasting future earnings-per-share.

RRF Project Exposure and Termination

4.
Substantial facility funding: The rapid expansion of Hellenic Telecom's ICT division has been heavily supported by European Union stimulus funding. In a research report published on 21 June 2024, Deutsche Bank Research analyzed how Greece continues to deploy its extensive share of the EU's Recovery and Resilience Facility, which directly fuels local digital infrastructure tenders. This regional program has allowed the operator to secure multiple major public sector digitisation awards, which underpins the current backlog expansion. However, this high concentration of public sector funding creates significant risks of a revenue cliff once the European program concludes.
5.
Imminent funding cliff: Hellenic Telecom's public sector project pipeline is approaching a structural transition point as the regional investment cycle nears its end. In an analyst presentation released on 29 July 2026, the company's management team explicitly stated that Recovery and Resilience Facility-funded public projects are scheduled to terminate in Q3 2026. Deutsche Bank Research's update on 24 April 2026 also noted that clients are currently rushing to maximize their utilization of these EU funds before the official program expiration. This upcoming termination means that public sector revenue will contract significantly in the final quarters of 2026.
6.
Transition to private sector: To mitigate the impact of the ending European stimulus funding, the company is shifting its strategic focus toward private sector enterprise services. Management's Q2 2026 slides published on 29 July 2026 highlighted an increasing focus on private sector accounts alongside international organisations like NATO and the United Nations. While public sector contracts have formed the bedrock of recent revenue growth, this private sector transition aims to protect the division from a post-stimulus revenue collapse, which would otherwise severely pressure mid-term operating margins.

Margin Compression and Sector Comparisons

7.
Below average profitability: The rapid growth of the System Solutions division is leading to structural pressure on Hellenic Telecom's consolidated profit margins. In its Q4 2025 financial results report published on 26 February 2026, the company reported that its consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin declined from 40.4% in fiscal year 2024 to 39.6% in fiscal year 2025. The management explicitly attributed this margin compression to the higher contribution of low-margin revenue streams, particularly the fast-growing System Solutions segment. This dynamic shows that while ICT contracts expand the top line, they negatively affect consolidated operating margins and EPS.
8.
Explicit contract margins: The underlying profitability of Hellenic Telecom's IT integration contracts is significantly lower than its core telecom business. During the company's Q1 2024 earnings conference call on 14 May 2024, management stated that the average margin attached to these domestic ICT projects is safely around 20%. This is substantially below the group's core consolidated EBITDA margin of approximately 40.1% reported in Q2 2026. Consequently, unless the company can scale higher-margin proprietary software or cloud services, this low-margin work will continue to dilute overall corporate margins.
9.
European peer comparisons: Hellenic Telecom's profitability profile reflects broader margin dynamics seen across European telecom operators. An expert call with the chief marketing officer of a major competitor on 27 January 2025 highlighted that European operators typically report EBITDA margins that are about 10% below United States margins. While Hellenic Telecom has historically maintained sector-leading margins through rigorous personnel cost reductions, its growing reliance on 20% margin ICT integration services brings its financial profile closer to its regional peers. This margin dilution will restrict the positive impact of top-line growth on earnings per share.

Backlog and Client Transitions

10.
Steady backlog progression: Hellenic Telecom has consistently expanded its reported IT integration business volume over the past several fiscal years. The company's investor presentation dated 29 July 2026 showed that its System Solutions business grew from 235 EURm in fiscal year 2023 to 314 EURm in 2024 and reached 419 EURm in 2025. Despite this steady progression, the firm must replace large expiring public sector volumes with smaller, more fragmented private contracts. This transition could lead to short-term revenue gaps in early 2027 unless the pipeline matures rapidly.
11.
Contrary evidence: Hellenic Telecom has shown that its ICT projects can yield sustainable maintenance revenues that follow initial implementations. During the Q1 2024 earnings call on 14 May 2024, management pointed out that while the initial peak in revenue is driven by implementation schedules, long-term recurring maintenance revenues follow afterward. This post-implementation revenue could help buffer the business against a sudden collapse in public sector project volumes once the RRF funding stops.
12.
- Earnings Calls Other side of the story: Several financial analysts argue that the upcoming public funding cliff is already anticipated by the market and mitigated by external growth vectors. In a research note published on 24 April 2026, Deutsche Bank Research suggested that Hellenic Telecom will successfully sustain its ICT revenues by expanding its operations outside Greece, noting that international markets grew 25% year-on-year in 2025 to represent 22% of total segment revenue. Additionally, the division's expansion into high-growth private sector areas such as Cybersecurity, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence could offset domestic public sector declines. This suggests that group earnings may remain more resilient than pessimistic forecasts imply.

The company is launching innovative services like GPU-as-a-Service and AI workload capabilities to capture commercial demand. This diversification into cloud and cybersecurity is designed to sustain long-term segment growth and protect earnings.

International contract expansion: The operator is expanding its international footprint to offset the upcoming domestic public spending cliff. OTE has secured complex system integration projects with major European agencies and international bodies like NATO and the UN. Expanding these non-domestic channels helps the operator diversify its core ICT revenues away from Greek public bodies.

Section 4 – Public Sector Receivables and Payment Speeds

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecom experienced a significant trade receivables surge in early 2026 due to public billing cycles.
  2. Greek government-to-business payment delays impose severe cash requirements compared to other European markets.
  3. Peer comparisons reveal similar working capital pressures across Greek IT integrators and European telecom incumbents.

Receivables Spike and Payment Cycles

1.
Receivables expansion: Hellenic Telecom's balance sheet experienced significant working capital pressure in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026. The group's reported trade receivables spiked to 716.20 EURm in Q1 2026 compared to 592.30 EURm at the end of fiscal year 2025. This substantial increase of 123.90 EURm reflects accelerated public project billing cycles before the funding program ends. This seasonal cash drain could temporarily depress OTE's free cash flow and lower its near-term EPS.
2.
Persistent payment delays: Greek public sector bodies maintain some of the longest payment timelines in the European Union. According to an economic study by Allianz SE on 2 June 2025, G2B payment delays in Greece force local companies to secure an extra 52,000 EUR in financing for every million in sales. This is significantly higher than the European average extra financing requirement of 41,000 EUR. These funding requirements increase debt levels and directly drag down net earnings through elevated interest expenses.
