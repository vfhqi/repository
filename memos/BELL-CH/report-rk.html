EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive financial analysis evaluates Bell Food Group's operational resilience and vulnerability to potential earnings disappointment over the next three years, examining bearish evidence quality, failure scenario probabilities, and foreign currency impacts across its Swiss and European operating segments. Bear Case Credibility Assessment The credibility of negative claims against Bell Food Group emerges as moderate but not overwhelming when measured against available evidence, primarily because the company maintains a concentrated ownership structure that naturally limits public scrutiny and short-seller interest.

Regulatory disclosures confirm that the Coop Group Cooperative maintains a dominant 72 percent majority stake, and concentrated ownership reduces the public float and explains why public short-seller interest is completely non-existent. A systematic review reveals that no substantive findings regarding sell-side analyst coverage or bearish broker reports are currently available, and this absence of analyst reports makes it impossible to evaluate specific broker concerns or estimate revisions.

The limited institutional coverage creates an informational vacuum where operational headwinds become the primary concern rather than acute structural failures. Direct evidence from regulatory filings shows concentrated ownership, which means minority investors face a clear risk that strategic decisions favor the majority owner over other shareholders. Segment data highlights that organic growth in the core international charcuterie business has slowed to a crawl, with Bell International reporting an organic revenue growth rate of only 0.5 percent in the first half of 2026, and sluggish performance supports the bearish inference that international expansion remains highly challenging without continuous acquisitions.

Most Credible Failure Scenarios The most probable path to disappointing earnings centers on capital-intensive modernization programs producing insufficient returns while raw material inflation compounds margin pressure across core Swiss operations. The overall probability of earnings badly disappointing over the next three years is judged to be medium, as heavy capital commitments under the CHF 850 million Swiss modernization program are expected to raise annual operating depreciation by CHF 30 million by 2029, and if these facilities do not achieve full capacity utilization, the rising fixed cost burden will severely compress operating profit margins and dilute future EPS.

Strategic investments under the modernization program will trigger a massive increase in non-cash charges, with operating depreciation expected to rise by around CHF 30 million by 2028 compared to the 2024 baseline of CHF 188.9 million, and unamortized capital spend will flow directly through the income statement, ultimately depressing annual earnings per share by approximately CHF 4.80 unless offset by immediate volume gains.

A secondary but equally damaging scenario involves structural livestock supply shortages driving procurement costs beyond sustainable levels. A structural decline in Swiss domestic herds continues to limit the availability of key slaughter animals, sourcing alternative animal raw materials from third parties exposes Bell Switzerland to volatile inflated prices, and raw material costs account for more than 60 percent of group net revenue, meaning unhedged cost increases directly compress core operating margins and EPS. Primary Failure Triggers

Three distinct operational triggers could precipitate severe margin contraction and earnings disappointment for the company. First, delayed commissioning of modernized facilities would extend double-run operating expenses while preventing anticipated efficiency gains from materializing. The progressive commissioning of Bell Food Group's advanced infrastructure in Oensingen represents a major cost headwind, as management expects these complex facility transitions to generate up to CHF 20 million in start-up expenses between 2025 and 2027, and unanticipated start-up outlays will directly inflate personnel and overhead costs, ultimately reducing operating margins and EPS in the upcoming quarters.

Second, sustained consumer downtrading in Swiss retail channels would accelerate product mix deterioration toward lower-margin offerings. Depressed retail consumer sentiment across European markets is driving a massive shift in purchasing behavior, budget-conscious shoppers are increasingly trading down from premium brands to cheaper entry-level meat and convenience ranges, and unfavorable sales mix adjustments toward lower-priced items dilutes overall gross operating margins and restricts earnings per share.

Third, regulatory disruptions to South American meat imports would force expensive European sourcing alternatives. The scheduled European regulatory ban on Brazilian meat imports will eliminate a major source of low-cost inputs, procuring alternative meat supplies within Europe will force procurement prices to escalate dramatically, and regulatory-driven meat substitutions will increase cost of goods sold, ultimately squeezing gross operating margins and reducing earnings per share.

Largest Integrated Risk Assessment The single most dangerous integrated threat involves concurrent failure of Swiss modernization projects and European procurement disruptions during a period of persistent currency translation headwinds. Multiple failure scenarios are likely to occur together, delays in completing modernization projects could overlap with rising startup costs projected to reach up to CHF 20 million during 2026 and 2027, and if these elevated start-up expenses coincide with delayed pass-through of raw material price hikes, the combined headwinds will depress near-term operating cash flows significantly. This compounding scenario becomes especially damaging because the Swiss core business generates nearly half of total revenue and funds all international expansion initiatives. Debunking the stable Swiss core business argument would cause the most severe damage to the bull case, Bell Switzerland generated 49.8 percent of total group net revenue in the first half of 2026, any margin deterioration in Switzerland would immediately undermine the cash flows needed to fund international expansion, and Swiss market disruptions represent the single most damaging risk to the investor's earnings projections.

Bell Food Group annual reports confirm that commissioning advanced facilities in Oensingen and Schaan generates high start-up expenses and depreciation, if these facility transitions coincide with domestic raw animal supply shortages the company cannot achieve planned automated efficiencies, and unabsorbed facility overheads and high double-run costs would occur together to severely suppress core operating cash flows below expectations.

Currency Impact Conclusion Bell Food Group faces a persistent structural headwind from currency translation that will continue suppressing reported financial results regardless of underlying operational performance improvements. Bell Food Group faces a continuous consolidation headwind because its foreign revenues are primarily Euro-denominated while its reporting currency is the Swiss Franc, the average EUR/CHF exchange rate depreciated from 0.9720 in 2023 to 0.9371 in 2025 representing a steady decline, and ongoing Euro depreciation is projected to lower translated revenues and EBITDA over the next 18 months.

Foreign exchange movements have historically acted as a consistent headwind for Bell Food Group's reported net revenue, in the first half of 2026 foreign exchange fluctuations shaved off CHF 19.6 million from revenue representing a negative 0.8 percent impact, and continued strengthening of the Swiss Franc against the Euro will likely suppress translated international sales and pressure reported EPS. While the company maintains effective natural hedges at the operational level, translation effects dominate reported results.

This currency matching keeps direct transaction exposures small allowing management to limit active transaction risk management to exposures exceeding a CHF 5 million EBIT threshold, Bell Food Group faces much larger currency translation impacts on its reported financial results than direct transaction cash flow losses, and translation effects remain the primary driver of reported earnings fluctuations for the group.

Bell Food Group faces amplified risk when negative translation effects coincide with high start-up costs and rising depreciation from its Swiss modernization projects, the group is managing elevated annual operating depreciation of around CHF 30 million alongside Oensingen start-up costs of up to CHF 20 million, and severe Euro translation losses would further depress reported earnings during this capital-intensive transition phase.

Overall Risk Probability Verdict The comprehensive evidence suggests a low-to-medium overall probability of severe earnings disappointment materializing over the next eighteen to thirty-six months, primarily because operational execution has remained robust and the Swiss core business provides substantial defensive capabilities. The overall likelihood of the bear case materializing is judged to be low to medium, strong operational execution and robust Swiss core operations continue to provide stable cash flows, and stable demand in retail markets is highly likely to offset international restructuring headwinds in the short term.

Bell Food Group faces a low overall likelihood of severe earnings disappointment over the next 18 to 36 months, the company demonstrated strong operational resilience in the first half of 2026 by delivering a 15.4 percent increase in operating profit, and positive operational momentum suggests that core Swiss profit engines remain highly stable despite general consumer headwinds. However, this baseline assessment depends critically on successful facility commissioning and sustained pricing power in domestic markets. The overall probability of Bell Food Group's earnings disappointing is low due to robust cost pass-through capabilities, operating investment requirements are scheduled to decline significantly after peaking in 2026, and successful facility modernizations and automated efficiency gains represent highly effective operational protections for investors against downside risks.

The primary vulnerability remains the concentration of profitability within Switzerland, where any sustained margin compression would immediately threaten the entire corporate earnings trajectory and dividend sustainability over the medium term.
