[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Avanza is a top-decile Swedish savings franchise whose transient, mostly external causes for concern have cleared before its multiple has. At SEK 390 the shares pay us about 14 percent a year for that without any re-rating and about 18 percent a year with a less-than-even chance of one, against a 20 percent bar; the case is compelling on substance and not yet on price, and it clears the bar only below about SEK 360.

  • The change is external, and it has turned from a two-year headwind into a set of tailwinds.
    • Three things de-rated the stock from about 22 times earnings to 17.6 times between 2024 and early 2026, and each has cleared or paused.
    • The Riksbank cut its policy rate from 4.0 percent to 1.75 percent through 2025 and compressed net interest income
      • it held in Q2 2026 and the forward curve moved up about 20 basis points after the quarter.
    • The supervisor's late-2024 deposit rules forced the wind-down of the external Sparkonto+ product, a SEK 9 billion outflow in Q4 2024
      • the wind-down completed on 13 May 2026 with 60 percent of the money retained on Avanza's own balance sheet at roughly ten times the spread (about 50 basis points against about 5).
    • The threat of an EU ban on fund commissions, which are 17 percent of 2025 income, was removed in December 2025, and the Swedish FSA announced it will not investigate a local ban.
    • On top of these, the tax-free threshold on investment savings accounts doubled to SEK 300,000 in January 2026, a structural tailwind to inflows.
  • Retail risk appetite has returned, and that is both the strongest force and the cyclical top of the case.
    • Q2 2026 brought record net fund purchases of SEK 17 billion, foreign trading at 28 percent of turnover and currency income up 72 percent year on year to SEK 224 million.
    • Transaction-related income was 44 percent of Q1 2026 income, so nearly half the revenue line is at a high, not a base
      • this force has a structural floor (Swedish household saving rates of 8 to 12 percent; 44 percent of Nordic household financial assets in equities and funds) and a cyclical top with less than two years' visibility.
  • The internal change is real but secondary: a set of adjacencies to the model, not a re-basing.
    • A leadership team almost wholly renewed within eighteen months, a codified cost cap (9 percent in 2026, 8 percent average to 2030, 5 percent by 2030), a simplified perimeter, a heavier balance sheet, and a first foreign market (Denmark, H2 2027, the first in 27 years) that is input-only today and dilutes earnings by about 2 percent a year until it adds.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on every line but margin, and the sell-side has followed.
    • Q2 2026 earnings per share were SEK 4.97 against SEK 3.81 a year earlier
    • net interest income reached a record SEK 486 million
    • H1 2026 operating income rose about 18 percent and Q2 26 percent
    • the operating margin is flat at 68 to 70 percent.
    • Consensus FY2026 earnings per share has risen 22 percent in twelve months to SEK 19.39 (18 September 2026), every horizon is up over one, three, six and twelve months, and the last month's change of +0.2 percent says the Q2 upgrade round is done.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for the deposit and re-risking legs, and is input-only for Denmark.
    • The deposit internalisation shows in the company's actions, its operating metrics (own savings accounts doubled from SEK 33 billion to 66 billion in eighteen months), its statements (record net interest income) and the sell-side's estimates
    • Denmark shows only in choices and the first SEK 3 million of expense.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of SEK 16.57 in FY2025 rising to about SEK 19.6, 21.3 and 23.2 in FY2026 to FY2028, about 12 percent a year, and about 10 percent a year to FY2031.
    • The growth is revenue-led (savings capital compounding about 11 percent a year on a 7 percent market plus 4 to 5 percent net inflow, with the revenue margin drifting down about 1 basis point a year from 45), the margin is flat because Denmark absorbs the operating leverage, nothing changes below the operating line at a 14.5 percent tax rate, and the share count drifts up about 0.4 percent a year on warrants.
    • Our path sits 1, 3.5 and 5.5 percent above consensus of SEK 19.39, 20.58 and 22.00
      • consensus FY2027 revenue implies average savings capital only 3 to 4 percent above the SEK 1,208 billion held at 30 June 2026, which is where the price is conservative.
  • What it costs: SEK 390, or 17.6 times consensus earnings 24 months forward of SEK 22.13, which is cheap against the stock's own history and not cheap against its peers.
    • That is the 18th percentile of the stock's ten-year range, below the two-year median of 18.7 times and the ten-year median of 21.2 times
    • it is above four of the five peers (AJ Bell 15.3, Fineco 16.4, Flatex 11.3, Quilter 11.9) and a 20 percent discount to Nordnet at 19.1 times
    • the peer median is 15.3 times.
    • Over the three months to 22 September 2026 consensus FY2027 earnings per share rose 5.2 percent and the price rose 0.7 percent, so the upgrades were absorbed by a falling multiple: the price has moved sideways against a rising earnings base, the reverse of "already priced".
  • The return: about 14 percent a year with the multiple held, about 18 percent a year at a blend that gives a re-rating to 20 times 45 percent odds, against a 20 percent bar.
    • On the framework's convention we value the shares at each horizon on the earnings two years beyond it: SEK 26.1 at March 2028 and SEK 30.5 at September 2029 on our path.
    • Eighteen months: 13.9 percent a year at 17.6 times held, 23.7 percent at 20 times, 18.4 percent at the 18.7 times blend, with SEK 14.6 of dividends.
      • Three years: 14.4, 19.0 and 16.5 percent a year, with SEK 46.5 of dividends.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through (a 17 percent tax rate and the full 1.6 percent of warrant dilution) takes 4.4 percent off earnings per share at every horizon and the blended return to 14.8 percent a year on both horizons, 10.5 percent on the no-re-rating branch: the same rating, and very little cushion.
    • The branch in which the case clears 20 percent a year at SEK 390 needs both our earnings path (probable, about 60 percent) and the re-rating (45 percent)
      • the two are positively related, so the joint odds sit at about 35 percent, possible rather than probable.
  • The pattern it fits: 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' at B, a quality compounder whose named, external concern has cleared on hard evidence.
    • Two soft edges: the shares are oversold on valuation (the 18th percentile) and not on price (4.6 percent below the 52-week high of SEK 409.0, up 17.6 percent over twelve months), and a third concern, Denmark's investment phase, opens as the external ones close.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the forces to the outputs to the estimates: the recurring base has stepped up permanently and the estimate cycle is up.
    • It breaks at the price: the multiple is industry-driven, the sector sits in the lower half of its own history, and the market in this sector has paid for growth (Nordnet +28.1 percent over twelve months) rather than quality (Avanza +17.6 percent), so the re-rating leg is only partly ours to earn.
  • What breaks it.
    • Riksbank cuts (a parallel 1 percentage point cut takes SEK 420 million off full-year net interest income, about 12 percent of consensus FY2026 pre-tax profit)
    • a global retail-trading bust that hits the foreign 28 percent of turnover and the currency line together
    • the multiple following the sector down to the peer median of 15.3 times, which is SEK 339 with earnings unchanged and 4 percent a year over eighteen months on our own path.

  • The case, therefore, is a quality compounder whose concern cleared before its multiple, priced at a C.

    • The same thesis is a B below about SEK 360, where the blended multiple gives 25 percent a year over eighteen months and 20 percent over three
    • with no re-rating at all the bar is cleared at about SEK 340.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and has reached the outputs ('Change Forces' B, 'Financial Outputs' B), but the Building Block asks whether it drives a 20 percent a year return in the modal case, and at SEK 390 it does not: 14 to 18 percent a year over eighteen months and 14 to 16.5 percent over three years, so the return part binds the whole

    • the parts combine as a chain in which the last link is the weakest.
    • Not B, because the bar is the bar and the same thesis clears it below about SEK 360, which is the price at which the rating would be B
    • not D, because the forces and the outputs are of B quality and the shortfall is a matter of price, not of substance.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: Avanza is a quality trap at a cyclical earnings peak.
    • Transaction-related income was 44 percent of Q1 2026 revenue and Q2 delivered record fund purchases, foreign trading at 28 percent of turnover and currency income up 72 percent
    • those are the marks of a top, not a base.
    • Net interest income is at a record only because the Riksbank paused at 1.75 percent, and each further point of cuts takes SEK 420 million off it.
    • Costs are guided up 9 percent in 2026 and rise 11 to 12 percent in 2027 with Denmark, a venture Jefferies calls a bigger chance on a smaller prize against an incumbent, Nordnet, with 7 percent of Danish assets and the price-setter's seat.
    • The growth engine at home is structurally slower than the peer's (customers +8 percent against Nordnet's +13; savings capital +22 percent against +29), penetration is already 21 percent of the population, and the market has said which it prefers: Nordnet +28.1 percent over twelve months against Avanza +17.6.
    • So consensus growth of 6 to 7 percent a year for FY2027 and FY2028 is right, and the multiple belongs at the peer median of 15.3 times, which on consensus FY2027 earnings of SEK 20.58 is SEK 315, 19 percent below the price.
      • Jefferies (Underperform, SEK 335) and Morgan Stanley (Underweight) hold versions of this view.
  • What we concede.
    • The growth gap to Nordnet is real and has widened
    • Denmark cannot close it before 2028 and dilutes until then
    • 44 percent of revenue is cyclical
    • and the peer median multiple is a live floor that no argument about quality removes.
    • At 15.3 times our own path returns 4 percent a year over eighteen months and 10 percent over three years, an F and a D
    • it is the number the bear case turns on and it is shown at 'Modal return' below.
  • Why we still hold our view.
    • First, the recurring base has stepped up permanently, not cyclically: own savings accounts doubled to SEK 66 billion, savings capital is SEK 1,208 billion (up 22 percent) and 37 percent of it is in funds, so fund commissions and net interest income, 57 percent of 2025 revenue between them, compound from a higher base whatever trading does.
    • Second, the rate lever is now asymmetric: from 1.75 percent a point of cuts costs SEK 420 million but a point of rises adds SEK 1,170 million, and the forward curve has moved up.
    • Third, the cost cap has been kept two years running (9.2 percent against 9.5 guided in 2024; 10.4 against 11.0 in 2025) and Denmark's cost is bounded and disclosed: SEK 120 to 150 million pre-launch, about SEK 140 million a year after.
    • Fourth, the multiple at 17.6 times already prices most of the bear case: it is the 18th percentile of the stock's own decade, a 20 percent discount to Nordnet, and the estimate cycle is up.
    • Fifth, without any re-rating at all our path still returns about 14 percent a year with a 3.8 percent yield, which is why we call the case a C at this price rather than a bearish one.
  • Where the bear case is right is on price.
    • It is why we set the level at which the case is compelling at about SEK 360, not SEK 390.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening at the margin: Nordnet is out-growing Avanza in customers and savings capital, fintech niches (SAVR, Montrose) target the most profitable cohorts, and brokerage pricing has drifted down, though no price war has started and the incumbent banks' digital response is ineffective.

  • Pricing: both platforms offer commission-free trading up to SEK 50,000 for new customers and comparable volume-based fees

  • Not D, because Avanza's share of the Swedish savings market rose to 8.4 percent in June 2026 from 8.3 percent in March and 7.8 percent in Q4 2024: it is losing the growth race to Nordnet, not losing share at home.
