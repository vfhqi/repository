Regional Growth Dynamics

1.
Accelerated expansion: Medartis has entered a critical phase of geographic scaling where its top-line trajectory is becoming highly dependent on select growth engines. This report utilizes evidence from 28 relevant sources, 10 medical technology companies, and 2 comparable historical expansion cases to evaluate regional concentration. In the first half of 2026, the company reported consolidated core sales of CHF 160.8 million, driven primarily by strong performance in North America and EMEA. This regional divergence raises critical questions about whether near-term earnings per share expansion is concentrated in too few product lines.
2.
Dynamic progression: The US rollout of the TOUCH thumb carpometacarpal prosthesis represents the single largest commercial catalyst for Medartis in the near term. Management announced in August 2026 that the US average selling price for the device is approximately USD 6,500. On this pricing, the upgraded target of 3,000 procedures by end-2026 will generate over USD 19.5 million in high-margin revenue. This concentrated momentum means TOUCH will deliver the majority of incremental US sales growth over the next 18 months.
3.
Unbalanced momentum: Medartis recorded highly uneven organic growth rates across its geographic segments during the first half of 2026. The US market led with 27.4% organic sales growth, whereas EMEA grew by 17.7%, LATAM by 9.7%, and APAC lagged significantly at just 5.5% organic growth. This performance shows that North America is rapidly outpacing mature European markets. Consequently, any deceleration in US surgeon adoption would disproportionately impact consolidated top-line growth and subsequent operating leverage.

This massive base provides essential funding for the ongoing commercialization of KeriMedical. This temporary climatic tailwind suggests that the European growth baseline may normalize downward in subsequent periods.

This price disparity confirms that APAC cannot substitute for the financial contribution of the North American market.

This slow transition confirms that value brands require significant time to scale before contributing meaningfully to consolidated earnings per share.

Section 3 – US TOUCH Pricing, Volume Volatility, and Pass-Through Status

Section summary

  2. Institutional administrative barriers and clinical training requirements present notable hurdles to rapid surgeon adoption in the United States.
  3. Historical precedents from comparable medical devices underscore that long-term volume expansion is contingent on sustained clinical data generation.
  4. Specialist medtech companies frequently struggle to sustain margin expansion in the year immediately following a revenue acceleration.
  5. Medartis's underutilized six-plant capacity and high distribution costs present significant headwinds to achieving its high-teens core EBITDA target.

This rapid expansion illustrates that the US market provides substantial near-term growth compared to mature European segments.

Management confirmed that the average selling price in the United States reaches approximately USD 6,500. This premium price point stands in sharp contrast to the regulated Australian reimbursement of CHF 1,300. This significant pricing disparity highlights how US volume dictates overall product profitability and subsequent operating margins.

Bureaucratic friction: Medartis faces prolonged administrative hurdles that can delay immediate commercial access to hospital operating rooms. Management noted in March 2026 that registering devices through hospital Value Analysis Committees takes up to four months. Consequently, administrative bottlenecks remain a primary constraint on rapid surgeon onboarding and near-term volume expansion.
