The separation of Electrification will transform TKH Group into a high-margin standalone Automation company, with reallocating transaction proceeds toward share buybacks representing the primary driver for long-term earnings per share accretion. The core investment question examines the specific inputs that will drive TKH's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, including normalised tax rates, OECD Pillar Two effects, net interest on the €625 million sustainability-linked facility, separation one-off costs, amortisation of acquired intangibles, the research capitalisation policy of 51.6% of 2025 spend, the share count of approximately 39.9 million since 2023, the 10% buyback authorisation approved in May 2026, currency sensitivity, divestment gains, associates and joint ventures, and capital expenditure guidance.

The evidence base provides robust answers across most critical EPS drivers, though several strategic execution variables remain outstanding. TKH Group's normalised tax rates will remain between 23% and 25% due to ongoing R&D tax facilities despite the loss of certain tax-exempt divestment gains, while potential tax leakage from the Electrification separation is expected to be minimal under the Dutch participation exemption, with confidence in these tax inputs rated as high based on detailed historical filings and clear precedents.

The strategic divestment of Electrification will generate significant cash proceeds to eliminate outstanding debt, with standalone Automation operating with a robust net cash position and structurally lower interest expenses, though confidence in this cash path is medium because final transaction values and buyback timing depend on market execution. Operating one-off expenses and Eemshaven start-up losses will remain elevated until the Electrification separation is finalized, while incoming chief executive Guido Grandi is highly likely to kitchen-sink TKH's base to establish a clean operational baseline, with confidence in these separation and cleanup costs rated as medium because transaction timing depends heavily on market execution. Standalone Automation will benefit from structurally lower capital intensity and more stable R&D accounting post-separation, with completion of the strategic capex programme significantly unlocking cash flow for shareholder returns, and confidence in these capital and R&D inputs rated as high due to clear company guidance and consistent peer benchmarks. TKH Group maintains a stable share count of approximately 39.9 million shares through disciplined treasury stock management, with shareholders approving a 10% buyback authorisation in May 2026 that will facilitate significant share count reduction, while broker models assume that proceeds from the Electrification sale will fund massive buybacks to drive immediate earnings accretion.

If TKH Group deploys half of the estimated €0.8 billion proceeds to buy back shares at Jefferies' €48.90 share price, it would cancel approximately 8.18 million shares, representing a 20.5% reduction in the outstanding share count from 39.9 million shares, with such a massive reduction driving substantial EPS accretion and translating into an immediate 25.8% boost in earnings per share.

Normalised and reported tax rate dynamics

1.
Normalised rate stability: TKH's normalized tax rate is expected to remain stable. TKH Group's interim report on 1 September 2026 reported a normalized tax rate of 24.2% in the first half of 2026 compared to 25.6% in the first half of 2025. This indicates a consistent tax rate range, which means future earnings per share will remain protected from sudden tax shocks unless local statutory rates increase.
2.
Reported rate distortion: The reported tax rate in 2025 was distorted by a tax-exempt disposal gain. TKH Group's annual report published on 11 March 2026 showed a reported effective tax rate of 15.1% in 2025 because of an untaxed book profit of €35.8 million from the Dewetron divestment. Under the Dutch participation exemption, this gain was completely tax-free, which means that the reported tax rate was artificially depressed.

Tax facility advantages: R&D tax facilities are the main driver lowering TKH's normalised effective tax rate. These facilities are also active in Austria, Belgium, Canada, and China, which means they will continue to support standalone Automation EPS unless R&D policies are tightened.

OECD Pillar Two and global tax operations

4.
Transitional safe harbours: TKH is actively monitoring OECD Pillar Two across its 34 operating countries. TKH Group's annual report of 11 March 2026 explained that the group is using the Transitional CbCR Safe Harbour rules to exclude operations in lower-risk countries. During 2024, two jurisdictions fell out of the safe harbour because of tax benefits from recognizing loss compensation, which means that TKH had to conduct more detailed assessments.
5.
Top-up tax insulation: The safe harbour fall-outs are not expected to trigger material top-up taxes. TKH Group's annual report of 11 March 2026 noted that five jurisdictions in total were not able to invoke the safe harbour in 2025 under the once-out-always-out rule. Despite these fall-outs, detailed assessments showed no material top-up tax liability, which means that consolidated EPS will remain insulated from Pillar Two penalties.
6.
Transfer pricing compliance: TKH's decentralized model increases local transfer pricing and tax audit risks. TKH Group's annual report of 7 July 2026 highlighted exposure to tax risks that could result in double taxation. The group manages this by periodically monitoring transfer pricing documentation in accordance with OECD guidelines, which means that standalone entities must maintain robust local compliance to protect earnings.

Separation tax leakage and Dutch rules

7.
Participation exemption protection: The separation of the Electrification business is highly likely to be tax-exempt under Dutch rules. Under the Dutch participation exemption, capital gains realized on the sale of qualifying subsidiaries are completely tax-free, as demonstrated by the Dewetron divestment in 2025. Therefore, a private sale of Electrification would avoid direct Dutch tax costs, which means that the entire cash proceeds can be utilized for share buybacks.
8.
Restructuring cost leakages: A spin-off or carve-out could still trigger transitional restructuring tax leakage. TKH Group's EGM circular on 22 July 2026 indicated that the dual-track process involves significant restructuring and carve-out expenses. These internal assets and contract transfers can lead to localized tax leakage or stranded tax losses, which means that the separation could temporarily depress near-term consolidated EPS until the transaction is finalized.

Since cable manufacturing is capital-intensive, peers like Nexans and NKT also face higher average corporate tax rates, which means that Electrification's standalone tax rate will likely increase post-separation.

This suggests that standalone vision companies like Basler and Jenoptik operate in jurisdictions with higher tax densities, which means that TKH's standalone Automation segment might see its tax rate trend upward if it expands its German and North American footprint.

European corporate restructuring precedents

Jefferies Research's note of 26 February 2026 on Aalberts highlighted that portfolio rebalancing progress often incurs substantial unallocated and M&A costs, with unallocated costs rising by €10.8 million after M&A expenses exceeded €15 million. Contrary evidence: TKH's historical tax rate reduction shows that tax facilities can sometimes overperform and lower the tax burden below expectations. TKH Group's annual report of 11 March 2026 revealed that the normalized effective tax rate decreased to 22.1% in 2025 from 24.4% in 2024, primarily due to increased benefits on R&D investments and innovations. Debt facilities and interest expense

1.
Rising debt costs: TKH Group reported net interest expenses of €13.0 million in the first half of 2026, compared to €29.7 million for the full year of 2025. This cost escalation was driven by higher drawings on variable-rate credit lines, which means that earnings per share remain sensitive to monetary policy. Average interest rates on outstanding bank loans reached 4.4% by the end of 2025, which represents a significant increase from prior periods.
2.
Structured loan agreements: TKH's financing is anchored by a €625 million sustainability-linked credit facility consisting of a €500 million revolving credit line. In 2025, the group signed a new €200 million term loan maturing in mid-February 2027 to replace its previous €125 million term loan. This restructured facility operates with a variable Euribor-linked margin that averaged 1.6% during 2025. These floating rate arrangements expose the group to near-term rate volatility unless debt levels are rapidly reduced after the separation.
3.
Volatile reference rates: TKH Group relies heavily on variable-rate bank loans linked directly to Euribor or SOFR. The group's annual report shows that monetary assets and liabilities are netted where possible to mitigate rate volatility under central management. However, unhedged floating debt leaves the company exposed to rate spikes, which means that near-term interest expense remains volatile until debt is completely retired.
4.

Although the corporate treasury policy targets fixing 40% to 70% of long-term debt, actual hedging has recently run below this bandwidth due to rapid rate shifts.
