Morgan Stanley noted on April 8, 2022, that high donor fee inflation and lower volumes led to a severe drop in gross profitability that consensus failed to anticipate early in the cycle. CSL Behring took multiple years to rebuild its plasma volumes back to pre-pandemic levels after the 2020 disruption. Jefferies reported on February 11, 2025, that while CSL's collection volumes grew, elevated donor fees persistently pressured gross margins, which delayed earnings per share recovery across the sector. This trial represents a major milestone because positive outcomes could demonstrate complement therapy as an alternative to standard plasma treatments.
2.
Delayed clinical trials: Argenx has scheduled the primary readouts for its registrational trials of empasiprubart in chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy for the second half of 2027. The EMVIGORATE study will randomize 218 patients on stable maintenance therapy to evaluate the efficacy of C2 inhibition compared with direct immunoglobulin infusions, as documented in clinical trial updates on November 16, 2026. Successful readouts would increase the long-term risk of volume substitution in core indications for plasma fractionators.
3.
Alternative pipeline candidates: Johnson and Johnson is actively advancing its own neonatal Fc receptor inhibitor, nipocalimab, as a potential competitor to standard immunoglobulin treatments. A Morgan Stanley report on July 15, 2026, highlighted that J&J expects key clinical data from its Phase 2/3 ARISE study in demyelinating neuropathy in late 2026. This candidate represents a significant threat because J&J possesses substantial financial resources to expand market awareness and drive adoption upon approval.
4.
Comparable clinical failures: Sanofi terminated its late-stage clinical development of a competing complement inhibitor in patients with chronic demyelinating polyneuropathy. This historical failure suggests that targeting single upstream pathways may not match the broad therapeutic benefit of polyvalent immunoglobulins.

A Morgan Stanley analysis on April 20, 2026, noted that nearly all major plans mandate a trial of immunoglobulin before patients can transition to Vyvgart. These structural administrative barriers prevent rapid market penetration because payers strictly enforce first-line immunoglobulin step-therapy rules to manage their specialty drug expenditures.

This conservative prescribing behavior suggests that competitive volume erosion will remain gradual and manageable for established fractionators.
