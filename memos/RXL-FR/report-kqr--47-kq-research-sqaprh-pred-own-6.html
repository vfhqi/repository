Section 3 – European Energy Renovation and Electrification Subsidies

Section summary
  1. European electrification subsidies face severe budget tightening and political dilution heading into 2027, tempering near-term volume expectations for electrical distributors.
  2. Germany's new technologyneutral Building Modernisation Act has removed immediate boiler replacement mandates while preserving core subsidy funding.
  3. All EU member states missed the May 2026 transposition deadline for the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive, delaying regulatory clarity.

French budget constraints and social leasing

1.
Ambitious funding targets: French electrification plans aim to inject significant capital into energy efficiency to reduce oil and gas reliance. The government announced a plan to double annual public funding for electric technologies to €10 billion by 2030. This massive funding increase focuses on accelerating the deployment of heat pumps and electric vehicle infrastructure. Higher public spending should structurally support Rexel's long-term sales, unless local administrative hurdles prevent execution.
2.
Sluggish disbursement metrics: French subsidy execution remains slow because administrative backlogs prevent funds from reaching the market. Reports show that as of December 2024, disbursement across the €10 billion portfolio stood at less than 5 percent. Most investment instruments under this package remain in selection or early contracting. This sluggish deployment suggests that distributors will see limited nearterm revenue benefits from these funds.
3.
French production commitments: French industrial commitments are shaping a long-term domestic supply chain for green heating technologies. Industrial players signed a formal commitment with the French government to produce one million heat pumps annually by 2030. This localized manufacturing push aims to secure domestic supply while reducing reliance on foreign fossil fuels. Higher local production will ensure steady product availability for distributors like Rexel, supporting margin expansion.
4.
Income-targeted social leasing: French authorities are introducing focused social programs to support residential heating upgrades. The government launched a social leasing scheme targeting 25,000 low-income households for heat pump installations by 2028. Funding for this specific program is expected to be provided through France's Energy Savings Certificates mechanism. This program represents a gradual opportunity for distributors to supply energy transition products.
5.
National budget tightening: French public spending dynamics face severe constraints heading into the 2027 fiscal year. The 2027 French budget contains meaningful tightening, with about €43 billion in new measures needed to reach deficit targets. This fiscal squeeze raises concerns that green renovation budgets could face cuts. Any reduction in state aid would impair distributor sales volumes in the residential renovation segment.

German Building Modernisation Act pivot

6.
Technology-neutral regulatory pivot: German legislators have shifted toward a more pragmatic approach to building energy standards. The Bundestag passed the Building Modernisation Act in July 2026 to make regulations more technology-neutral. Homeowners are permitted to continue installing oil and gas boilers as long as they meet a gradual renewable fuel blending staircase. Under these rules, newly installed gas systems must blend 10% climate-friendly fuels starting in 2029.
7.
Preserved financial incentives: German heat pump subsidy funding will be maintained despite the removal of rigid boiler bans. The current program, set to remain active through 2029, offers basic grants and bonus payments capped at 70% of costs. These preserved incentives are crucial to support heat pump adoption because these systems cost up to four times more than traditional boilers. Consistent subsidy support will help electrical distributors maintain stable volume demand for HVAC products.
8.
Regressive subsidy adjustments: German building support rules under the KfW program became more restrictive in July 2026. Under these amended rules, the cap on eligible costs fell to €28,000 and declines by €750 every six months starting in February 2027. Additionally, the climate speed bonus will step down by four percentage points every six months. These regressive adjustments will reduce funding for wealthier households, likely preserving the status quo and dampening potential demand acceleration.
9.
Gradual boiler phase-out: German regulations implement a long-term horizon for transitioning away from carbon- intensive heating systems. The Building Modernisation Act requires that all heating systems must be powered entirely by renewable energies by 2045. This extended timeline avoids sudden market disruptions while securing a multi-decade backlog of renovation works. For electrical distributors, this longterm policy clarity ensures sustained demand for electrification products over the next two decades.
10.
Prolonged payback dynamics: German residential heat pump adoption remains highly sensitive to installation economics and energy prices. Payback periods for air-to-water heat pumps can reach 17 years in Germany even under special tariffs. Current natural gas and electricity price ratios are insufficient to create a strong economic incentive for widespread conversion. Without substantial subsidy support, prolonged payback periods will discourage discretionary consumer replacement demand.

European building directive delays and targets

11.
Delayed regulatory implementation: European Union member states have struggled to align central decarbonization mandates with local laws. The deadline for transposing the 2024 recast Energy Performance of Buildings Directive passed in May 2026 with no member state complying. Although transposition by late 2026 is likely to be tolerated, the delays create a challenging regulatory vacuum for property developers. This political inertia postponed the anticipated 2027 demand rebound for energy-efficient building materials.
12.
Mandatory refurbishment obligations: European directives establish strict minimum standards to force the renovation of the worst-performing properties. The building directive requires member states to mandate the renovation of worst-performing buildings, targeting 16% of non-residential stock by 2030. This target expands to require the refurbishment of the least efficient 26% of non-residential buildings by 2033. These binding requirements will generate a massive backlog of commercial electrical installation projects.
13.
National residential trajectories: Residential real estate portfolios face tightening energy performance rules that vary across individual countries. Under the revised directive, national governments must ensure average residential primary energy use declines by 16% by 2030. This residential energy-saving target deepens further to a reduction of 20% to 22% by 2035. While member states retain flexibility in their pathways, these milestones will support steady demand for Rexel's low-voltage offerings.
14.
Severe grid congestion: Infrastructure constraints represent the most immediate threat to the execution of electrification projects. Widespread adoption of electric vehicles and heat pumps is placing immense stress on distribution networks. Germany and Spain face severe constraints, with new grid connection queues frequently taking two to five years. Unless grid reinforcements accelerate rapidly, these network bottlenecks will delay the installation of energy efficiency products.

Contrary evidence and strategic outlook

15.
Contrary evidence: Heat pump adoption is highly sensitive to policy changes, meaning that regulatory modifications can trigger severe market downturns. Germany's heat pump market experienced a 46% decline in 2024 because consumer uncertainty over new regulations and complex subsidies paralyzed purchasing decisions. This severe contraction demonstrates that subsidy reductions or elimination pose the largest near-term risk to demand. If governments restructure or reduce aid programs, distributor sales of high-value heating systems will fall sharply.
16.
Other side of the story: Electrification demand may become increasingly structural because geopolitical shocks and energy volatility force consumers to seek independence. Customers increasingly view investments in solar, batteries, and heat pumps as long-term risk management actions. This shift suggests that electrification is transitioning into a structural trend independent of short-term subsidy shifts. Even if state budgets tighten, distributors can rely on resilient private demand to support steady volume growth.

Section 4 – US Non-Residential Construction and Reshoring Outside Data Centres

Section summary
  1. US non-residential construction remains deeply bifurcated as massive data center gains mask persistent stagnation and double-digit declines across traditional commercial, lodging, and private manufacturing segments.
  2. Leading design indicators confirm a prolonged contraction with the Architecture Billings Index falling to 44.7, its lowest level in recent months.
  3. Rexel's pending acquisition of GCG strategically pivots the distributor toward high-margin, less-cyclical utility grid and defense markets, reducing its vulnerability to traditional building downturns.

Leading architectural and planning indicators

1.
Architecture Billings Index contraction: The US Architecture Billings Index (ABI) has signaled a prolonged downturn in the design pipeline, which typically leads actual non-residential construction spending by nine to twelve months. to Morgan Stanley's report on 25 October 2026, the national ABI fell to 44.7 in September 2026 from 47.2 in August 2026, marking the largest share of architectural firms reporting declining billings since May 2026. This contraction is part of a historic, multi-year slowdown. Deutsche Bank's report on 16 August 2026 notes that architectural billings had been in contractionary territory for 34 of the past 35 months. While project inquiries firmed up modestly to 53.2 in September 2026, the design contracts index fell by 4.6 points to 43.7, representing its lowest reading since January 2026. This persistent weakness in design contracts suggests that broader commercial and institutional starts will remain highly constrained heading into 2027. Divergent planning pipelines: The Dodge Momentum Index (DMI), which serves as a leading indicator for non- residential building projects entering the planning phase, exhibits a stark divergence between institutional and commercial segments. Morgan Stanley reported on 11 October 2026 that the DMI increased 2.4% month-onmonth in September 2026 to 288.0, driven entirely by a 9.4% surge in institutional planning. Conversely, commercial planning declined 1.3% month-on-month, heavily weighed down by a significant pause in data center and warehouse planning pipelines.

Jefferies Research noted on 19 October 2026 that commercial planning excluding data centers was down approximately 15% year-on-year, exposing very weak underlying demand in traditional private non-residential markets. This indicates that non-data center commercial activity will face a severe volume air pocket in 2027 as current planning backlogs fail to convert to active job sites.

Non-residential spending and reshoring dynamics

2.
Bifurcated spending metrics: Total US non- residential construction spending reached an annualized rate of $1.28 trillion as of June 2026, but nominal growth continues to be heavily flattered by inflation. Morgan Stanley's construction report on 8 September 2026 showed that private non-residential spending put-inplace was down 1.3% year-on-year. Excluding data centers, monthly sequential non-residential spending declined 0.2% in July 2026. The contraction was highly concentrated in traditional private verticals, with lodging down 9.9% year-on-year, office ex-data centers down 10.6% year-on-year, and private manufacturing down 21.7% year-on-year. This steep decline in traditional commercial categories means that electrical distributors cannot rely on broadbased market expansion to drive organic volumes in 2027.
3.
Concentrated manufacturing reshoring: While the long-term US reindustrialization story remains structurally intact, reshoring investments have normalized after the initial post-pandemic boom. Jefferies Research reported on 8 October 2026 that manufacturing construction spending grew over 50% annually in 2022 and 2023, but subsequently turned negative in 2025 and declined in the low-20% range for ten consecutive quarters through late 2026. Furthermore, the active reshoring spend is highly concentrated in advanced technology sectors rather than traditional heavy manufacturing. Since 2025, semiconductor and advanced electronics projects have accounted for approximately 70% of tracked US manufacturing investment, followed by pharmaceuticals and biotechnology at 18%, leaving traditional industrial infrastructure segments relatively flat. This highly skewed spending profile means that distributors without direct exposure to advanced cleanrooms and complex instrumentation will miss the bulk of reshoring volume pull-through.

Rexel's portfolio transformation via

GCG Strategic GCG acquisition: On 25 September 2026, Rexel announced an agreement to acquire GCG, a US-based specialty infrastructure and engineered-products distributor, for an enterprise value of $1.4 billion. GCG generates over 75% of its sales from custom assemblies, engineered wire and cable, connectivity, and advanced kitting services. These proprietary and high-touch offerings allow GCG to participate further upstream in project design, helping industrial customers bypass labor shortages and reduce installation times. This high-touch service model historically insulates GCG's profitability from commodity fluctuations and traditional construction cycles. New grid and defense exposures: GCG opens entirely new growth frontiers for Rexel that lie outside cyclical commercial building. Approximately 18% of GCG's sales are in power and utilities, and 11% are in defense, which are completely new markets for Rexel. This exposure is highly strategic because utility grid modernization, grid automation, and defense spending are driven by structural regulations and national budgets rather than short-term interest rate moves. GCG's pre-engineered solutions are also highly geared to the utility "gray space" of power substations and grid connections. This new exposure will help Rexel sustain midsingle-digit volume growth in 2027 even if the broader non-residential construction market experiences a mild recession.

This transaction increases Ferguson's addressable market to $400 billion and deepens its dual-trade capabilities. Contrary and counter-view assessments

Under these accommodative conditions, traditional commercial demand would inflect positively, allowing Rexel to enjoy double-barreled growth across both its newly acquired GCG specialty platform and its highly profitable legacy branch network.

Section 5 – Historical Lead-Lag Relationships and Volume Implications

Section summary
  1. Historical cycles confirm that electrical distributor volumes lag building permits and PMIs by three to twelve months.
  2. Structural post-pandemic changes have compressed electrical permit lag times from twelve months to just three months.
  3. Rexel's record $3 billion North American backlog provides a solid volume cushion heading into the 2027 fiscal year.

Macroeconomic lead indicators

1.
Architectural billing leads: Leading design indicators like the US Architecture Billings Index historically dictate non-residential construction spending with a significant lag. Morgan Stanley's report on 24 August 2012 states that the index typically leads actual building activity and distributor volumes by nine to twelve months. This relationship means that the current 2026 billings contraction will depress non-residential volumes through mid-2027, as builders delay pulling the trigger on new projects. Consequently, electrical distributors will experience a delayed demand inflection point, which will compress early 2027 organic growth before design backlogs finally convert into active job sites.
2.
