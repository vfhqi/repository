While CEO Johan Steene maintains exceptional personal ownership at 5.02% of outstanding shares, independent directors Helena Nathhorst and Boel Sjöstrand own zero shares as of early 2026, falling materially below peer-group alignment averages. This misalignment is detailed in Section 7 Board Composition, Section 8 Executive Compensation, and Section 13 Insider Transactions, suggesting potential oversight motivation gaps during periods of capital stress or operational turbulence.

These five Plausible problems to monitor do not undermine the core investment thesis around Johan Steene's operational competence or Teqnion's long-term compounding potential, but they collectively represent subtle structural fragilities that could amplify downside risk during future macroeconomic stress, rapid international expansion phases, or unexpected leadership transitions. Investors should monitor quarterly disclosures for evidence of formal internal control implementations, succession planning visibility enhancements, UK integration margin trajectories, board equity accumulation patterns, and alignment between management rhetoric and restructuring actions.

Outliers worth noting

The second critical outlier concerns governance scaffolding gaps, specifically that Teqnion operates without a formal internal control function, relying instead on an informal, non-audited matrix, and the board does not engage auditors to review quarterly reports. This represents an extreme deviation from prudent governance practices for a public company managing over SEK 1.8 billion in annual revenue across 30+ decentralized subsidiaries spanning multiple jurisdictions.

The material consequences of this structural weakness became visible when underperforming subsidiaries Reward Catering and Hem1 generated unacceptable losses that depressed 2024 and 2025 profitability, culminating in a Goodwill impairment of SEK 73m in Q3 2025 and the Reward Catering liquidation process in Q4 2025. The absence of systematic internal audit procedures allowed fraudulent activities and operational deterioration to accumulate undetected until catastrophic write-downs materialized.

When contrasted against peer practices where audit committees conduct quarterly reviews and maintain dedicated compliance functions, Teqnion's governance infrastructure appears dangerously thin relative to its operational complexity. The reliance on informal monitoring by a seven-person corporate team managing international operations represents a statistical outlier that increases the probability of future material impairments.

The third notable outlier involves executive compensation asymmetry, where warrant subsidies of 120% substantially reduce downside investment risks for the parent management team. This subsidy structure creates an options-like payoff profile that transfers substantial downside risk from executives to shareholders while preserving full upside participation. While CEO Johan Steene's direct equity ownership of 861,471 shares representing 5.02% of Teqnion provides deep alignment with shareholders, the warrant subsidy mechanism dilutes the personal capital commitment required from other senior executives.

Independent directors Lena Almefelt and Henrik Joelsson hold very small positions of 3,000 and 2,750 shares respectively, while Directors Helena Nathhorst and Boel Sjöstrand own zero shares, creating a bifurcated alignment structure where founder-level commitment contrasts sharply with professional director detachment. This compensation architecture represents a statistical outlier when measured against peer compensation structures that typically require executives to fund warrant purchases at full market prices without subsidies.

The asymmetric risk profile may encourage excessive risk-taking in acquisition underwriting decisions, as executives face limited personal downside from integration failures while retaining substantial upside optionality. Combined with the governance control gaps identified above, this compensation outlier amplifies concerns about management discipline during periods of aggressive international expansion.
