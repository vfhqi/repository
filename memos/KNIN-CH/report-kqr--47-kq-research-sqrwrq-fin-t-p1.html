Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary

1.
Crucial inquiry: "Kuehne + Nagel International AG, KNIN-CH (SIX Swiss Exchange; reports in CHF): an asset-light global logistics group, one of the two largest sea and air freight forwarders, with four segments: Sea Logistics, Air Logistics (including the Apex Logistics air-charter brand), Road Logistics and Contract Logistics. Key question: how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"

Operating Leverage Mechanics in Logistics

2.
Significant variability: Financial performance in asset-light logistics is analyzed through gross profit rather than net turnover because most revenue consists of volatile subcontracted transportation costs passed through to customers. The true margin is the EBIT to gross profit conversion ratio which reflects the value retained after paying production costs. Since forwarders do not own ships or aircraft, their asset base is low and cash conversion is high. This structural asset-light flexibility insulates earnings from severe capital losses when shipping demand declines.
3.
Personnel focus: Operating expenses below the gross profit line are heavily dominated by personnel costs which represent roughly 70% of a freight forwarder's total expenses. Managing headcount productivity is therefore the ultimate determinant of whether incremental volumes translate into higher earnings per share. When volume falls, companies must rapidly right-size staff or risk severe decremental operating leverage on their P&L. Thus, structural cost elimination programs are vital to defend margins during freight recessions.

Strategic Implications for Kuehne + Nagel

4.
Organic execution: Kuehne + Nagel pursues an organic growth strategy and high dividend payouts while aiming for sustainable operating leverage through technology initiatives. The group has historically maintained an Air and Sea conversion margin around 30% which is lower than DSV's industry-leading levels. Structurally, marginal shipments should arrive at higher economics than average ones to drive increased returns to scale. Volume and profit expansion at large forwarders remains supported because global trade continues to grow roughly in line with GDP.

Section 2 – Short-Run Cost Structures and Fixed-Variable Splits

Section summary
  1. Freight forwarding is a labor-intensive, asset-light industry where personnel expenses typically account for 60% to 70% of operating expenses, creating significant short-run cost rigidity.
  2. Rigorous lease structures and capitalized warehouse commitments represent significant fixed liabilities, though back-to-back contract matching offers some operational protection during demand downturns.

Fixed-Variable Splits in Logistics

1.
High staff representation: Kuehne + Nagel reports personnel expenses of CHF 5,148 million for the fiscal year 2025. This massive expense represents approximately 70.25% of total operating expenses (calculated) when combined with selling, general, and administrative expenses of CHF 1,522 million and right-of-use depreciation of CHF 658 million. This direct breakdown validates the industry assessment that the forwarding business is highly labor-intensive. Managing white-collar headcount productivity remains the primary lever to control decremental operating leverage.
2.
Structured opex details: Kuehne + Nagel further details its FY2025 personnel expenses as salaries and wages of CHF 4,005 million and social benefits of CHF 1,010 million. Outside of staff, administrative overhead is distributed across facilities at CHF 473 million and administration at CHF 453 million. Because these lines do not scale with shipment counts, they act as rigid fixed costs over short-run operational cycles. This rigidity explains why volume drops compress conversion margins before headcount is adjusted.
3.
Variable peer structures: Expeditors maintains the highest staff cost concentration with personnel representing 73% of its Air and Sea operating expenses in 2025. This is slightly higher than DHL Global Forwarding and Freight where staff costs are 69% of operating expenses, and DSV which holds the lowest share at 63%. These differences highlight how DSV has successfully leveraged its standardized IT infrastructure to run more cost-effectively than its direct competitors. Thus, asset-light peers with higher labor concentrations face more severe operating leverage during sudden market downturns.
4.
Profitability and personnel: Morgan Stanley reports that efficiency savings through automation represent a major margin lever because staff costs are such a high percentage of gross profit. Specifically, a 15% reduction in staff costs could step up the group conversion margin by as much as 4.5% points under optimal conditions. However, it remains too early to pinpoint the precise implementation costs of these digitized workflows. Therefore, the near-term net impact on earnings remains subject to deployment execution.
5.
Heavy profit consumption: Morgan Stanley reports that staff costs consume approximately 45% of gross profit at DSV. This compares to a higher burden of more than 50% of gross profit consumed by staff expenses at both Kuehne + Nagel and DHL. This higher baseline for staff costs suggests that Kuehne + Nagel has a larger runway to shed costs through administrative automation.

Lease and Facilities Commitments

6.
Significant lease liabilities: Kuehne + Nagel reported right-of-use asset depreciation of CHF 658 million and total lease cash outflows of CHF 849 million in its FY2025 annual report. The group cash flow statement also shows CHF 643 million of lease liability repayments and CHF 38 million of interest paid on lease liabilities. These multi-year commitments cannot be easily liquidated when demand turns, representing a highly rigid short-run fixed cost. Consequently, these capitalized leases form a substantial portion of the forwarder's balance sheet, particularly within contract logistics.
7.
Strategic contract matching: Kuehne + Nagel seeks to mitigate lease rigidity by ensuring approximately 80% of its warehouse leases are back-to-back with contracts. This matching of lease durations protects the operating margin from vacant space when a contract terminates. However, if a warehouse is underutilized before the lease expires, operators face pressure to accept low-margin business just to cover facilities expenses. Therefore, the short-run flexibility of logistics space remains constrained by the remaining duration of the customer contracts.
8.
Asset-heavy road logistics: DSV operates a road logistics division where opex contains a higher portion of fixed costs than its air and sea segments. Road logistics is relatively asset-heavy with depreciation running at 2% to 3% of sales compared to only 1% in Air and Sea. This higher share of fixed costs creates significant negative operating leverage when groupage volumes decline, as seen during the Q2 2026 road margin compression. Managing these terminal networks requires constant capacity alignment to avoid road logistics is relatively asset-heavy persistent margin drag.

Technology and Operational Rigidity

9.
Rigid operational overheads: DHL global supply chain operations face high overhead costs which make smaller logistics contracts less profitable. These heavy structures and expensive IT integrations mean that smaller, niche deals cannot absorb the fixed administrative overhead. Consequently, DHL tends to avoid smaller transactions and focuses instead on large-scale enterprise contracts where fixed costs are easily diluted. This dynamic demonstrates that administrative scale can become a structural barrier for smaller regional freight forwarders.
10.
Flexible blue-collar staffing: Contract logistics operators utilize temporary labor to manage volume. This strategy prevents firms from overstaffing during quiet periods and allows them to rapidly scale down costs by reducing temporary shifts. In past recessions like 2009, this flexible labor base allowed major operators to defend positive EBIT margins despite sharp volume drops. Therefore, blue-collar operations are highly variable in the short run compared to white-collar management staff.
11.
Technology-driven savings potential: Morgan Stanley notes that digitized tools can automate manual workflows for the smallest logistics customers, where labor-intensive functions like quotations still dominate. This automated customer experience should free up valuable human capital to focus on higher-yielding customer prospects across the network. While these productivity-enhancing technologies provide a route to margin upside, they depend on driving change in processes across all work streams. Therefore, the ultimate size of the retained savings remains highly dependent on deployment execution.

Volume versus Yield Sensitivity

12.
Shifted profit drivers: Jefferies reported a structural shift in the drivers of freight forwarding profitability. This yield-centric sensitivity suggests that forwarders' earnings remain highly geared to capacity disruptions rather than baseline demand.
13.
Consistent air dynamics: Jefferies observed that air freight profitability behaves differently than ocean freight across cycles. Air unit economics consistently accounted for more than 50 percent of the segment gross profit delta between 2018 and 2024. This persistent yield sensitivity means that air freight pricing remains more critical than volume shifts for earnings per share. Consequently, forwarders must focus heavily on premium product mixes to defend margins during air freight downturns.

14.
Contrary evidence: Kuehne + Nagel and its closest asset-light peers have demonstrated remarkable margin resilience during severe global demand contractions. During the 2009 financial crisis, the best-performing freight forwarders experienced only a modest 3 percentage point conversion margin compression from prior levels. This historical precedent suggests that the highly flexible, variable cost structures of labor and IT allow forwarders to preserve positive operating profit even when trade volumes plunge.
15.
Other side of the story: While the asset-light forwarding model provides significant variable cost flexibility, the extreme sensitivity to gross profit yields exposes earnings to rapid decremental leverage. When freight rates and yields collapse simultaneously, forwarders struggle to reduce white-collar headcount fast enough to defend conversion margins. Furthermore, aggressive volume expansion strategies often dilute unit economics, meaning that EBIT remains far more sensitive to yield shifts than to marginal shipment additions.
