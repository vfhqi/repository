National Grid PLC – FY31 Investment Strategy, RIIO-ET3 Allowed Returns, and Interconnector Leadership Regulated Asset Base (RAB) Growth to 2030: National Grid has established a massive five-year capital investment framework of at least £70 billion running to the end of fiscal year 2031 (FY31), which represents a substantial scale-up from its previous £60 billion plan. At the Group level, this massive investment program is projected to drive a Regulated Asset Base (RAB) compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of approximately 10% (or 10.60% on certain broker forecasts) over the FY26A to FY31E period, growing the asset base from £59.50 billion in FY25A to approximately £115 billion by the end of FY31.

The principal engine of this asset expansion is the UK electricity transmission (UKET) business, which is forecast to deliver a real RAB CAGR of approximately 14% over the same period. UK electricity distribution (UKED) is projected to grow at a more moderate but steady RAB CAGR of ~5.50%, while the US regulated rate base is guided to grow at an ~8% CAGR (with broker estimates pointing to a ~9% CAGR in USD) 2 4 Regulated Asset Base (RAB) Growth to 2030 (cont.):.

This upgraded spending is projected to drive Terna's RAB to €31.80 billion by 2028, reflecting a solid 9% CAGR. Allowed Returns and Regulatory Resets: Terna's regulated returns are overseen by ARERA, with the pre-tax allowed WACC set at 5.50% real for the 2025-2027 regulatory period. This framework incorporates a market-driven trigger mechanism where a recalculation is activated if parameters shift by at least 30 basis points.

Looking beyond 2027, the WACC methodology for the 2028-2030 period will undergo a full recalibration, with Terna advocating for a revision of the cost of debt calculation to better reflect actual refinancing costs, since currently only 15% of the allowed cost of debt reflects prevailing market funding costs Allowed Returns and Regulatory Resets (cont.):. Terna has a strong track record of outperforming allowed returns, delivering a Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) of 7.50-8.20% and a positive spread of 240 to 250 basis points over the blended regulated WACC. This structural outperformance is primarily driven by Terna's high actual gearing (70% net debt to RAB compared to the regulated assumption of 50%), regulatory incentives, and operational efficiencies.

Under the trigger mechanism, the allowed return resets if marketdriven parameters change by 30 basis points, down from 50 basis points previously. Hydrogen and CO₂ Network Exposure: As a pure-play electricity transmission system operator, Terna does not have direct exposure to hydrogen or CO₂ pipelines, which are the focus of gas transmission networks like Snam.

Allowed Returns & Regulatory Resets: Transelectrica operates under multi-year regulatory tariff cycles (typically 5-year periods) overseen by the National Energy Regulatory Authority (ANRE).
