Section 3 – The Foods Reverse Morris Trust with McCormick and Consensus EPS

Section summary
  1. The Reverse Morris Trust combining Unilever's Foods business with McCormick by mid-2027 will reshape pro-forma sales and trigger substantial near-term dilution of up to 32%.
  2. Unilever is expected to deploy a protective share consolidation model, similar to the 2025 Magnum Ice Cream demerger, to neutralize EPS dilution and stabilize consensus estimates.

Adjustments

Transaction Modeling and

1.
Dilution Impact Structural combination: Unilever and McCormick announced a transaction to combine Unilever's Foods business with McCormick & Co. in an all-stock, tax-free Reverse Morris Trust (RMT) scheduled to close by mid-2027. Under the agreed terms of the deal, Unilever shareholders will maintain a 65% majority ownership stake in the combined JointCo entity, while existing McCormick shareholders will hold the remaining 35% stake. This specific structural threshold of keeping Unilever shareholder ownership above 50% is required to preserve the tax-free status of the spinoff under RMT rules.
2.
Portfolio restructuring: The corporate perimeter change will dramatically scale down Unilever's pro-forma revenue profile as it transitions into a focused home and personal care business. Calculations by Rothschild & Co. Redburn estimate that the Foods group represents approximately 25% of Unilever's total sales and 32% of its operating profits. Based on their pro-forma 2026 modeling, removing Foods' €12.16 bn sales contribution reduces Unilever's overall group turnover from €51.44 bn to €39.28 bn, while pro-forma EBITDA drops from €11.98 bn to €8.20 bn. 50 51 & Co Redburn Near-term dilution: This massive asset carve-out creates a substantial immediate earnings headwind prior to any capital adjustments or cost-synergy realizations. According to Rothschild & Co. Redburn, Unilever's pro-forma 2026 earnings per share (EPS) would drop by 32% to €2.20, down from a baseline of €3.24, due to the loss of Foods earnings. When adjusting for the elimination of €1.05 bn in gross stranded corporate costs, the net EPS dilution is still modeled at a significant 21%.

To ensure operational continuity, the companies are establishing Transition Services Agreements (TSAs) covering IT, marketing, and distribution, some of which are expected to remain active for up to two years post-closing. Consensus adjustments: Sell-side analysts have maintained Foods inside their primary Unilever models because the transaction is not expected to close for several quarters. This delayed modeling approach creates a "consensus in limbo" effect, where headline consensus figures like the FactSet revenue estimates of €55.0 bn for FY2027 and €56.5 bn for FY2028 still reflect the legacy conglomerate perimeter.

Refining estimates: Brokers are beginning to assess how the post-Foods transition will impact consensus revision breadth as the closing date approaches. Analysts at Jefferies project that because core Unilever will trade at over 18x its 2027 earnings once the McCormick stake is marked to market, the post-Foods valuation multiple will appear demanding to investors. Consequently, revision breadth is likely to face downward pressure as analysts systematically strip out Foods' sales and model the €250 mm in net food-exit stranded costs.

Share Consolidation and Ice Cream Precedents Ice

5.
Cream precedent: To evaluate how Unilever will manage the earnings dilution, investors are pointing directly to the demerger of its Ice Cream business, completed on December 6, 2025, as The Magnum Ice Cream Company. In that transaction, Unilever distributed 80.15% of its holding in the Ice Cream business to its shareholders and retained a 19.85% equity investment. To prevent an artificial drop in its historical per-share metrics, Unilever implemented a concurrent share consolidation program.
6.
Consolidation mechanics: The December 2025 share consolidation was executed on an 8-for-9 basis, reducing Unilever's outstanding ordinary share count by 272.4 million shares. Shareholders received 8 new ordinary shares with a nominal value of 3 1/2 pence for every 9 existing shares held. Unilever retrospectively adjusted its historical basic, diluted, and underlying EPS figures in subsequent financial statements to reflect this lower share count, ensuring consistent year-over-year comparability.
7.
Accretion benefits: The share consolidation successfully offset the structural earnings dilution caused by losing the Ice Cream division. Jefferies reported that the share consolidation alone provided a 2.5% retrospective accretion effect on Unilever's FY2026 starting share count, bringing basic shares down from 2.45 bn to 2.18 bn. This share reduction fully neutralized the €6.60 bn market capitalization contribution of the exited Ice Cream assets, maintaining a flat EPS trajectory.
8.
Future expectations: Analysts expect Unilever to deploy a highly similar share consolidation mechanism to accompany the McCormick Foods RMT closing in 2027. Rothschild & Co. Redburn expects that Unilever will address the pro-forma 21% net EPS dilution by reducing its outstanding share count proportionally at completion. This capital adjustment will protect headline per-share consensus figures and prevent forced selling from index-tracking funds.

This structural overhang would drag pro-forma margins down, particularly if the remaining Home Care business cannot deliver expected efficiency gains.

Contrary evidence: Unilever's structural margin expansion is highly likely to persist even under severe inflationary conditions due to its massive self-help program. Deutsche Bank Research indicated in November 2026 that the company's €1 bn targeted productivity gains will easily offset Foods-related stranded costs and fuel brand reinvestment. Furthermore, Deutsche Bank notes that recent commodity shifts point to lower cost growth than feared, which will support pro-forma operating margins. Therefore, these strong efficiency levers make a severe margin reset highly unlikely.

Commodity Volatility and Integration Vulnerability

6.
Accelerated margin pressure: McCormick and Unilever could see pro-forma joint-venture profitability suffer if raw material cost inflation escalates beyond guided levels. Deutsche Bank noted in October 2026 that Unilever Foods is facing a challenging cost backdrop as it seeks to drive volume recovery in mature markets. In parallel, Deutsche Bank trimmed its FY2027 EPS estimates for McCormick to $3.13 due to tougher inflationary headwinds and weaker performance inside the acquired Foods business. If these material costs remain elevated, pro-forma Foods earnings will underperform, dragging down Unilever's retained economic interest.
7.
Severe commodity headwinds: Unilever faces heightened cost volatility because its remaining portfolio remains heavily exposed to agriculture and chemical input fluctuations. Bernstein reported in November 2026 that projected 2027 net material inflation of €700 mm to €1 bn represents about 2.5% to 3.5% of sales, driven by rising agricultural costs like soy. If these commodity pressures remain elevated, the company will be forced to implement selective price increases. This cost escalation could pressure volume growth if consumer elasticities deteriorate across developed markets.
8.
Delayed integration progress: McCormick faces significant execution complexity in carving out Unilever's Foods business, which could delay anticipated synergy realization. Jefferies reported in May 2026 that Unilever Foods' margin progression may have been artificially funded by brand under-investment over the past two years. Reversing this under-investment inside the merged entity could require substantial capital reinvestment, putting the pro-forma $600 mm net integration synergy targets at risk. Consequently, any delay in operational stabilization would depress joint-venture earnings and weigh heavily on Unilever's post-closing equity value.

Customer Retail Friction and Private Label Threat

9.
Unprecedented retail resistance: Unilever faces intense pushback from major retailers if the company attempts to pass through incremental material inflation in 2027. Expert insights from a former Vice President at Reckitt Benckiser in January 2026 show that powerful distributors like Tesco and Walmart are simply pushing back price increase requests, leading to lost shelf space. If Unilever insists on price increases, it risks losing the on-shelf competitive battle to private label alternatives. This structural shift in retailer power severely limits Unilever's pricing agility compared to historic cycles where cost fluctuations could be easily passed to consumers.
10.
Structurally expanding private label: Unilever's core Home and Personal Care brands face severe market share threats from sophisticated private label architectures in Europe. Bernstein reported in October 2026 that European retailers have built multitiered private label offerings that compete directly with branded products on both quality and functionality. Furthermore, discount retailers with a heavy private label mix are rapidly gaining market share in Western Europe, outperforming the wider grocery market. If consumers continue to trade down, Unilever's European volume growth will remain negative, undercutting pro-forma EPS recovery.
11.
Forced price adjustments: Unilever and Nestlé may be forced into dramatic and uncompetitive price cuts if cumulative inflation drives persistent consumer downtrading. According to Jefferies analysis in January 2026, aggressive pricing strategies over the last few years have created a dangerous momentum of downtrading toward cheaper private label alternatives. Jefferies warns that this pricing misalignment might eventually force leading brands into painful price resets, similar to Gillette's across-the-board cuts in 2017. Such forced price cuts would decimate gross margins and wipe out guided pro-forma EPS growth.

Governance Transitions and Early Warning Signs

12.
Unresolved board friction: Unilever's sudden reorganizational shifts raise critical questions regarding the strategic alignment and unity of its board of directors. Jefferies highlighted in November 2026 that returning to regional P&L units represents a major U-turn from the divisional operating model implemented in 2022. Furthermore, Jefferies notes that this transition coincided with activist fund Trian reducing its stake and the sudden resignation of non-executive director Ruby Lu. If these board movements indicate underlying disagreement over the corporate strategy, executive execution could falter, triggering downward revision cycles.
13.
Vulnerable domestic demand: Unilever's organic growth trajectory remains highly vulnerable to localized decelerations within its core North American and European markets. Deutsche Bank highlighted in October 2026 that while emerging markets posted 9.5% growth, developed markets declined 0.9% with both North America and Europe experiencing negative volumes. Furthermore, the key high-margin Wellbeing business faces headwinds from private label pressure in the club channel and reduced distribution of Liquid I.V. and Nutrafol. If these premium brands experience a prolonged volume slowdown, Unilever's ability to reinvest behind its core portfolio will be severely compromised.
14.
Cautious structural outlook: Unilever's strategic transition to a pure-play home and personal care business is highly dependent on flawless operational execution. Based on the gathered evidence, the company's pro-forma valuation rating remains highly sensitive to its ability to sustain volume-led growth above the guided 2% range during localized inflationary periods. Unless regional presidents quickly improve market share trends in developed markets, the core business will face downward consensus revisions. Therefore, the pro-forma EPS framework is at risk of compression if competitive pressures and retail price resistance intensify during 2027.
15.
Other side of the story: While short-term execution complexites and retailer frictions could weigh on pro-forma metrics, Unilever's simplified pure-play structure offers a highly attractive compounding story. Deutsche Bank Research indicated in November 2026 that portfolio focus on 80% of turnover from 25 power brands will drive faster decisionmaking and structurally accelerate organic sales growth. Additionally, Deutsche Bank expects that advanced AI-enabled content creation and marketing efficiencies will allow Unilever to defend and reclaim market share from smaller peers. Thus, these positive structural dynamics should ultimately support a substantial valuation premium and protect the company from severe competitive downside.
