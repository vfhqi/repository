Arm Architecture Market Penetration

1.
Methodological parameters: A comprehensive analysis of this architectural shift was conducted using 54 distinct industry sources covering 12 key semiconductor companies and 3 historical capacity precedents. Bernstein Research reported on June 17, 2026, that the rapid adoption of custom chips is redefining data center hardware requirements. These structural changes directly dictate the future advanced packaging substrate volume allocation and capacity utilization of AT&S.
2.
Accelerating deployment: Cloud service providers are aggressively deploying custom Arm-based server processors to optimize their internal data center operations. Bernstein Research reported on June 17, 2026, that Arm's share of global server CPU value is projected to rise to 55% by 2030, compared to x86 retaining an 80% unit shipment share. This value penetration is driven by the superior power efficiency and lower total cost of ownership that custom silicon offers over standard merchant silicon. This shift creates a growing pipeline of custom chips that absorb advanced substrate capacity globally.
3.
Sustained processor scaling: Amazon Web Services continues to drive high-volume deployments of its proprietary Graviton processor family across its massive cloud infrastructure. Bernstein Research reported on May 29, 2026, that AWS Graviton core counts have scaled 12x across five generations, expanding from 16 cores in Graviton 1 to 192 cores in Graviton 5. This dramatic scaling of processing cores increases the physical die size and packaging complexity of individual server chips. Consequently, these massive multi-core architectures require larger organic substrates to support high-density signal routing.
4.
Broadening custom adoption: Google and Microsoft are rapidly scaling their own custom Arm-based server central processing units to reduce their reliance on traditional x86 merchant silicon. Morgan Stanley reported on April 27, 2026, that Google's Axion processor demand could reach over 7 million units from 2026 through 2028, generating up to $5 billion in custom turnkey revenue. These highvolume proprietary chip programs significantly increase the aggregate demand for high-end ABF substrates.

Jefferies Research reported on October 12, 2026, that the traditional GPU-to-CPU ratio is moving from an eight-to-one relationship toward a balanced one-to-one configuration as workloads shift from training to inference. This architectural evolution occurs because autonomous agentic workflows require heavy sequential task orchestration, database preparation, and memory access coordination. This shift is expected to trigger widespread multiple-fold growth in server CPU volumes.

Expanding market size: The emerging computational demands of agentic artificial intelligence are driving a massive expansion in the total addressable market for server processors. Bernstein Research reported on June 17, 2026, that the global server CPU total addressable market is projected to expand from $39 billion in 2025 to $223 billion by 2030. This expansion is highly positive for advanced packaging manufacturers because it creates a persistent supply deficit for high-end organic substrates.

Substrate Complexity Comparison

9.
Escalating physical specifications: High-performance server central processing units require significantly larger substrate dimensions and higher layer counts than standard client chips. Jefferies Research reported on May 8, 2026, that the packaging substrate dimension for Intel's Granite Rapids processor has increased by 70% compared to Sapphire Rapids, with layer counts reaching 20 or more. This physical expansion is necessary to manage the complex signal routing, power delivery, and thermal dissipation of multi-die architectures. These demanding specifications drag down manufacturing yields and intensify the high-end ABF supply shortage.
10.
Divergent area requirements: The individual substrate area demand for specialized artificial intelligence accelerators is expanding much faster than for standard server processors. Jefferies Research reported on September 15, 2026, that accelerators require substantially more package-level interconnects and memory interfaces to optimize throughput and memory bandwidth. Consequently, high-end accelerators require substrates larger than 100x100mm with over 15 to 20 build-up layers to manage extreme data routing. Although server central processing units are smaller, their higher unit volumes absorb massive capacity across the industry.
11.
Contrasting routing architectures: Standard server processors utilize conventional flip-chip ball grid array packaging, whereas custom accelerators rely on highly complex multi-die advanced packaging. Morgan Stanley explained on February 22, 2026, that advanced two-point-five-dimensional and three-dimensional stacking architectures require substrates to manage massive power delivery demands and maintain signal integrity over larger areas. This routing complexity requires advanced organic substrates to be co-designed with the underlying silicon to prevent electrical failures. For substrate manufacturers like AT&S, these complex codesigns provide substantial pricing power and margin expansion.
12.
Strategic packaging qualifications: AT&S has established a highly differentiated competitive position by qualifying for Intel's proprietary Embedded Multi-Die Interconnect Bridge packaging. Deutsche Bank Research reported on July 21, 2026, that AT&S is one of only four global substrate suppliers qualified for this advanced packaging platform. This advanced architecture requires embedding silicon bridges within the substrate, which involves a highly complex cavity building process that reduces usable output. Consequently, AT&S's unique qualification in this complex manufacturing technology supports sustained substrate volume growth through 2028.
13.
Contrary evidence: Some industry analysts suggest that the ongoing transition to alternative packaging architectures could eventually eliminate the need for organic Ajinomoto build-up film substrates. Deutsche Bank Research reported on February 2, 2026, that emerging packaging technologies like chip-on-wafer-on-printedcircuit-board could potentially bypass the substrate layer entirely. If these alternative designs achieve widespread adoption in high-volume AI accelerators, the long-term demand for high-end substrates could decline faster than market expectations.
14.
Other side of the story: While the rapid growth of custom Arm-based silicon and agentic AI workloads creates a highly favorable demand environment, substrate manufacturers remain structurally exposed to execution risks and high customer concentration. Furthermore, any near-term slowdown in the broader personal computer market could offset the financial gains from server CPU growth, thereby negatively impacting future earnings per share.
