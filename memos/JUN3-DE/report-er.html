Section 16 – Conclusion

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jungheinrich reported its first half and second quarter fiscal year 2026 results on August 11, 2026, following a preliminary ad-hoc trading update released on July 23, 2026. The earnings release revealed a company navigating through a structural inflection point between strong automation-led order momentum and compressed profitability driven by operational disruptions and unhedged cost inflation. The group secured H1 2026 incoming orders of €2,954 million, representing a strong year-on-year increase of 7.7% from the €2,743 million recorded in H1 2025, with Q2 orders of €1,419 million beating the Visible Alpha consensus of €1,344 million by 6%.

However, this top-line resilience contrasted sharply with severe margin compression at the bottom line. Reported EBIT for the first half of 2026 fell by 31.2% to €144.9 million compared to €210.5 million in H1 2025, dragging the EBIT margin down to a weak 5.4%, negatively impacted by unhedged input cost inflation and ongoing factory underutilization.

The earnings print exposed diverging segment dynamics under the newly implemented reporting structure. Incoming orders in the Automation & Warehouse Equipment segment rose 22.5% to €588 million during the first half of 2026, driven by intensive sales in high-density warehouse automation, contrasting with a cyclical decline in forklift volumes. Meanwhile, revenue in the Industrial Trucks & Services segment declined by 1.9% to €2,241 million, as customer service increases only partially offset volume drops in new forklift sales and rentals.

The most significant deviation from consensus expectations centered on an unprecedented tax burden . The group's H1 2026 effective tax rate soared to 45% compared to just 29% in the prior year, driven by nondeductible losses from the Russian divestment and a €13 million tax settlement for historical tax periods spanning 2017 to 2023. This tax shock, combined with free cash flow collapsing to negative €99 million for H1 2026 from positive €57 million during H1 2025, driven primarily by strategic acquisitions including cash outflows of €89 million for EP Equipment and €4 million for Navflex, caught buy-side investors off-guard despite the prior guidance revision.

Management's strategic narrative emphasized accelerating long-term transformation despite near-term margin pressure. CEO Dr. Lars Brzoska defended the downward revision of full-year Group EBIT to between €340 million and €400 million by citing an accumulation of short-term external shocks, arguing that while top-line volumes remained healthy, unhedged input cost inflation and intense pricing pressure necessitated a temporary recalibration of profit expectations.

The company isolated €33.8 million in negative one-off effects including €20.5 million from the sale of the Russian subsidiary, €7.4 million from the Lüneburg strike, and €5.9 million in transformation expenses, with adjusted EBIT reaching €178.7 million and a more stable margin of 6.7% in H1

2026.

The sell-side response reflected cautious optimism rather than a fundamental re-rating. Bernstein's analyst Lorrain increased his target price on Jungheinrich to €47 from €45 while maintaining an Outperform rating, stating that the company remains highly appealing because of its exposure to material-handling megatrends and its discounted valuation relative to historical multiples. Jefferies analyst Lucas Ferhani maintained his Buy rating on the stock with a target price of €33, stating that the current share price represents an attractive risk-reward profile with a 34% upside to price target.

Both brokers viewed the earnings miss as a temporary operational wobble driven by non-recurring headwinds rather than a structural deterioration in the long-term investment thesis. The critical question emerging from this earnings cycle centers on whether near-term margin recovery is achievable amid persistent European industrial stagnation. Official regional data shows German manufacturing turnover was flat year-on-year in late 2025, which fell short of initial sell-side expectations for a rapid post-stimulus recovery, meaning Jungheinrich operates with suboptimal core factory utilization levels compared to historical peaks.

While Jefferies noted that the confirmed full-year 2026 guidance, which was adjusted upward for orders and revenues but reduced for EBIT, points to a much improved operational picture in the second half driven by pricing tailwinds and sequential volumes returning to the core factories, the sustainability of this recovery remains highly dependent on macroeconomic momentum that has yet to materialize consistently.
