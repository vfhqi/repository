MTU Aero Engines (MTX-DE): what the Q3 2026 results of 29 October 2026 do to the case

These results strengthen the case on the leg it turns on, cash, weaken it on the legs it already rated lowest, earnings and the OEM mix, and the share price has done the rest: at €345.3 the shares sit about 1 percent above the level at which the case said it would re-look, so the net effect is positive and large enough to matter, with one condition of that re-look now in dispute.

The challenge

Three readings the case does not hold, each at full strength, and one it does hold that the evidence now favours.

First: the legacy annuity is thinning by utilisation, and the case is watching the wrong gauge. The case made the V2500 cliff "a 2029 question, not a 2027 one" because retirements run at 0.4 percent a quarter, and it set the spares driver's invalidation at a retirement rate above 2 percent a year. The retirement rate is 1.6 percent, so that gauge reads clear. But V2500 flight hours fell 14.2 percent year on year in October, storage sits at 13 to 13.5 percent of the surviving fleet, more than twice the CFM56-7B's, and lease rates have fallen from $90,000 to $80,000. Airlines facing jet fuel up 72.6 percent year on year appear to be parking old aircraft, not scrapping them, and a parked engine consumes no spares while, if eventually torn down, it becomes the used material that competes with new ones. Jefferies has already taken a point out of spares growth in every year from 2027 to 2030, to 6, 9, 8 and 6 percent, from a mid-teens rate today. The case's own earnings path sits 0 to 3 percent above consensus partly because it reads the V2500 stream as lasting into 2029. For this reading to be right, V2500 hours would have to stay down double digits into the first half of 2027 as fuel moderates, rather than recover with it, and spare parts organic growth would have to slip below the guided mid-teens, which the brokers name as the reversal trigger for the cash thesis. Against it: inductions are steady at about 800 a year with heavier workscopes, forward fuel curves point lower into 2027, and a fuel-driven parking wave is the kind that reverses. We cannot knock it down, because the one datum that would, a recovery in V2500 hours, does not yet exist.

Second: the cash beat borrows from 2027. The case's thesis is that conversion rises because compensation is dated and ending, and the results confirm that arithmetic. But Deutsche Bank attributes the Q3 beat to two things: lower compensation, which the case already had, and military prepayments, which it did not. Prepayments are cash received ahead of work and they reverse as the work is delivered. Beside them, OEM revenue grew 1.65 percent in nine months because suppliers could not deliver, and a new-build engine not shipped is working capital not consumed on a product the case's own foundations note is sold at a loss by design, so the bottleneck flatters cash now and releases the demand later. Nine-month conversion of 62 percent sits above the top of a 55 to 60 percent full-year guide, which reads as either conservatism or a weaker fourth quarter; on arithmetic it means a fourth quarter converting well below the nine-month rate. The case accommodates the outcome (its invalidation is an FY2027 conversion guide not above 60 percent in February 2027) but not the mechanism: it treats 2026 cash as the base from which 2027 rises, when part of it may be 2027 cash arriving early. For this to be right, the prepayments would have to be a material share of the €172m, receivables would have to still be rising, and the OE catch-up would have to land in 2027 as bottlenecks ease. None of the three has been disclosed, and that is the point: none can be checked.

Third: the mix shift the case priced for 2030 is arriving now. The case carries the MRO mix as a 0.7x haircut to the median multiple and models the group margin drifting to 15.0 percent by FY2028 from 15.5 percent in FY2025. The nine-month margin is already 14.6 percent, Morgan Stanley has the full year at about 14.6 percent, and the OEM growth guide was more than halved in one quarter, on bottlenecks industry experts expect to persist into the next fiscal year with lead times of 18 to 24 months. If OE stays supply-capped through 2027 while MRO grows mid-teens, MRO's share of divisional profit reaches the case's 2030 proportion years early and the group margin sits nearer 14.5 percent than 15.0, which takes the case's 9 percent a year earnings growth toward the consensus 8 percent or below. The case's own plausibly-worse branch, a 14.3 percent margin and FY2028 EPS of about €21.2, is no longer a bear construction; it is close to the path the current guide implies. Against it: the shortfall is on programmes outside the A320neo, deferred OE revenue is deferred not lost, and supply-capped OE is better for cash than for margin. For this reading to be right, FY2027 consensus EBIT (€1,562m at the memo) has to come down and the 2030 operating profit target has to be held rather than raised on 30 November.

The reading the case holds, and the evidence now favours: the clearing is clearing, and the price has come to it. The cash line beat every broker, the MRO margin passed the case's Q1 2027 check a quarter early at 8.3 percent, the cash floor was raised twice in a year, and management says compensation ends on schedule. At €345.3 the shares are 1 percent from the case's eighteen-month entry level and sit on 15.4x, which happens to be the multiple the case assigned to its own "not cleared" branch. The case's modal path, unchanged and re-based to today's price, would return about 19 percent a year over eighteen months and about 14 percent over three (our arithmetic on the case's €449.9 and €510.1 values), against 14.3 and 11.5 percent at €368.1. On this reading the market has priced the three challenges above and left the cash story for free. The obvious bull alternative, 25 percent a year on a return to the 75th-percentile multiple, still fails for the reason the case gave: it needs a multiple the mix shift argues against, and these results made the mix shift faster, not slower.

What we did not find is a reading in which these results are quiet. The OE guide cut, the utilisation data and the cash beat each press on a named side of the case.

What would settle it

  • The second Earnings Review, four working days on. It is likely to carry the post-print broker estimate changes and targets, several of which have already landed (Jefferies' cuts of 29 October, Redburn's model update of 2 November, Morgan Stanley's €395), and the dashboard revision series as it then stands. It will not carry the Capital Markets Day of 30 November, which falls after it; nor the fourth quarter; nor any receivables figure, which the company reports half-yearly. A memo update commissioned on it would be written days before the event most likely to move the 2030 targets, the dividend policy and the buyback question.
  • The Capital Markets Day, 30 November 2026. Two-sided on the brokers' reading. It settles whether the 2030 operating profit target of €1.9bn to €2.2bn is held or raised (the third challenge), whether the 2030 conversion range narrows to its upper end, and whether the 40 percent payout returns and a buyback is named (the case's 'dividend restoration' driver). It is probable that the €13bn to €14bn revenue target is reiterated, which settles little.
  • The monthly trackers. Jefferies' GTF aircraft-on-ground series: whether 531 moves toward 200 by Q1 2027, or the case's level is restated against the series it meant. Bernstein's flight-hour series: whether V2500 hours are still down double digits in January to March 2027 as fuel moderates. That is the test of the first challenge, and the one most likely to be settled before any memo update.
  • The Q4 2026 results in February 2027. The FY2027 conversion guide (the case's test: at or above 60 percent); year-end receivables against the case's alarm of another €300m in a half; the Q4 OEM margin (a second quarter below 28 percent is the alarm); Q4 spare parts organic growth against mid-teens; the FY2026 dividend against the case's €4.00 alarm.
  • What nothing is committed to answer. The segment the bankruptcy charge sits in, the size of the Q3 military prepayments, and the quarterly receivables balance. Without them the second challenge cannot be closed either way, which is a different kind of open question: the February results may settle the outcome without ever explaining the mechanism.
