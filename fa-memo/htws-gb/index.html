Notes on Part 1 (read with the bridge below): the EBITDA margin is on the company's Adjusted EBITDA basis, which is the brokers' basis (FY2025 actual 55.2 percent; FactSet's operating EBITDA, used in the model's history rows, was 54.3 percent). The depreciation line includes about 12 to 18 USDm a year of other non-cash operating items (share-based payments, losses on disposal and write-offs of sites, items FactSet treats as unusual) so that the model's operating profit equals statutory operating profit; I could not separate amortisation of acquired intangibles in the files, and FactSet's brokers'-basis actuals appear to be reported earnings, so nothing is added back. Net financial items include lease interest, the convertible's non-cash accretion and, in FY2026 only, the 21.8 USDm non-cash currency loss on intercompany loans booked in H1 2026; no currency movement is assumed after that. The tax rate includes minority interests (tax charge plus minorities, over pre-tax profit), so that the model's net income is net income attributable to shareholders; the pure tax rates are 45.9, 34.0, 32.1, 31.0 and 29.9 percent. Dividend per share is the dividend declared for each year, in USD (FY2026: 25 USDm in total, the inaugural interim of 0.604p plus a final), growing just over 10 percent a year in total, with buybacks lifting the per-share figure faster.

How to read these figures

  • Amounts are in millions of the reporting currency; per-share figures are in units of that currency. Negative figures are shown in brackets.
  • Historical figures use FactSet's standardised lines, which can differ from the company's own presentation.
  • Earnings per share appears on four bases, which must not be mixed: reported basic; FactSet's adjusted diluted, before unusual items; the Viewforth definition, which is the model's standing convention; and the brokers' adjusted basis, on which consensus is based.
  • Consensus is FactSet's median of the broker estimates it holds. Where FactSet's line-by-line consensus differs from the broker-by-broker detail, the line-by-line table takes precedence.
  • Viewforth's forecasts are the Excel model's base case. There are no bull or bear cases.
