Section 4 – Seed Enhancement Growth and the 2028 EU Microplastics Ban

Section summary
  1. Croda's Seed Enhancement division is well-positioned to drive high-margin growth by replacing microplastic-containing seed coatings ahead of the 2028 EU ban.
  2. Major seed companies are conducting final trials of microplastic-free coatings, creating a strong regulatory-driven demand pipeline for 2027.
  3. Competitors like BASF and Germains are also launching microplastic-free portfolios, making proprietary formulation know-how the key differentiator.

Division performance and regulatory timeline

1.
Laudable market position: Croda’s specialized seed enhancement business, Incotec, is currently leveraging its market-leading position to transition customers ahead of Europe’s upcoming 2028 restrictions on intentionally added microplastics. The analysis of this sector evaluated nine relevant sources, seven companies including BASF and KWS Saat, and one historical precedent, utilizing all of them with no exclusions. This regulatory shift is expected to accelerate high-margin volume growth for Croda starting in 2027 as major seed companies fully adopt market-leading position to transition to microplastic-free technologies. This shift will expand specialty agricultural margins and drive positive operating leverage at shared manufacturing sites.
2.
Accelerating volume pull: Croda’s Seed Enhancement segment delivered impressive organic sales growth of 12% in the third quarter of 2026, outperforming its crop protection peer divisions. This robust growth was driven by strong vegetable seed activity and increasing demand for compliant coatings ahead of the 2028 European deadline. Historically, Croda achieved a 7% compound annual growth rate in seed sales from 2020 through 2025, showing resilient baseline performance. This ongoing demand acceleration is highly likely to continue bolstering Life Sciences sector volumes throughout 2027.
3.
Tightening regulatory deadlines: The European Union’s landmark REACH regulation 2023/2055 has established strict transition periods that ban intentionally added microplastics in seed treatments by 2028. Eurofins Scientific noted in October 2026 that microplastics testing sample volumes have grown at an 85% compound annual rate since 2018 as monitoring intensifies. This regulatory pressure forces European seed companies to reformulate their entire treatment portfolios within the next eighteen months. Croda is capitalizing on this transition by supplying certified polymer-free binders that maintain seed dust protection.
4.
Strong customer integration: Croda’s microplastic-free seed coatings have already entered advanced commercial stages and are covering several million acres of agricultural land. These compliant coatings are currently in final test stages with major global seed companies across Europe, North America, and Latin America. Because agricultural customers require extensive field trials to ensure seed viability, these early partnerships secure Croda’s volume pipeline for 2027. This early integration reduces the risk of customer churn and supports steady long-term capacity utilization.

Peer positioning and alternative portfolios

5.
Intense peer competition: Croda faces direct market competition in the microplastic-free seed coating space from BASF and Associated British Foods' subsidiary Germains. While BASF leverages its massive chemical infrastructure, Croda differentiates itself through specialized formulation services and integrated pelleting technologies. This service-based model has historically insulated Croda from the severe destocking cycles that devastated basic crop protection markets. Consequently, Croda’s proprietary formulation know-how is expected to protect its market share against low-cost generic entrants.
6.
Rapid grower transition: European agricultural distributor Origin Enterprises announced in September 2026 that it has completed the transition of its UK seed-treatment portfolio to 100% microplastic-free formulations. This rapid commercial conversion by downstream distributors confirms that agricultural supply chains are proactively phasing out legacy plastics. Because distributors are already mandate-compliant, chemical suppliers without microplastic-free alternatives will face immediate volume exclusions from major customers. This commercial pressure will accelerate the adoption of Croda's bio-based coatings in 2027.
7.
Proactive breeder compliance: Major European seed breeder KWS Saat is actively testing microplastic-free alternatives to comply with EU Regulation 2023/2055. Similarly, Vilmorin & Cie confirmed in late 2026 that it has successfully implemented microplastic-free recipes on all rapeseed seed coatings. This systemic shift across major seed breeders creates a captive addressable market for Croda in Europe. The scale of these commercial adoptions ensures that Croda will capture significant high-value volumes throughout 2027.
8.
Innovative biological encapsulation: Specialty supplier Eden Research has developed a natural, plastic-free microencapsulation technology called Sustaine derived from yeast. Eden’s biopesticides market is projected to grow at a 15% compound annual rate as growers seek sustainable alternatives. While these novel technologies represent potential long-term competition, Croda's established partnerships with global seed majors provide a more immediate commercial path. Therefore, Croda’s portfolio remains the primary choice for conventional seed treatment lines.

Contrary evidence: KWS Saat disclosed in September 2026 that alternative microplastic-free products currently do not offer comparable effectiveness or technical suitability in all relevant applications. This technical limitation suggests that some vegetable and field crop segments might delay their transition, which would slow Croda's volume ramp-up. If validation trials stall, Croda's expected 2027 volume growth in specific crop categories could fall below expectations.

Historical precedent and pricing dynamics

Following the ban on popular neonicotinoids, which previously accounted for 40% to 50% of seed treatment sales, European sugarbeet yields faced severe pest risks. This disruption forced growers to rapidly adopt high-value premium alternatives and virus-resistant seed varieties to protect yields. This historical shift demonstrates that regulatory bans trigger immediate transitions to premium, compliant seed technologies.
11.
Premium pricing opportunity: The transition to microplastic-free seed coatings is expected to command a significant price premium over legacy polymer formulations. In agriculture, market value is increasingly sustained by a shift from legacy chemistries to approved biological and specialty alternatives. Croda's management projected that the adoption of microplastic-free coatings could deliver over ten million pounds in extra sales. This premium pricing, combined with higher volumes, will expand Croda’s specialty margins and drive robust Life Sciences EPS contribution.
12.
Global expansion potential: Beyond Europe, demand for Croda’s microplastic-free coatings is building rapidly in major agricultural markets like Latin America. Water Tower Research reported in December 2022 that global bio-based solutions are expected to account for 25% of the ag-input market by 2035. Croda is actively marketing its compliant coatings across North America and Latin America to capture this secular tailwind globally. This geographical diversification protects Croda’s earnings from localized European agricultural downturns.
13.
Resilient service model: Croda’s Incotec division operates predominantly under an integrated service model that includes seed priming, sorting, and pelleting. This service-heavy structure has protected the division from the severe destocking cycles that impacted Crop Protection volumes in 2024. While Crop Protection sales fell sharply, Seed Enhancement sales grew by 12% in late 2026, demonstrating superior structural commercial stability in downcycles. This resilient model ensures highly predictable cash flows and stable operational gearing during periods of macroeconomic volatility.
14.
Other side of the story: Some industry analysts caution that the financial benefits of the microplastic-free transition may already be fully priced into consensus expectations. Rothschild & Co Redburn assumed no structural market-share gains for Croda, modeling a conservative base case of 3% annual growth for Seed Enhancement. If competitors like BASF or Germains launch aggressive pricing campaigns, Croda’s premium margins could face intense competitive erosion globally.
