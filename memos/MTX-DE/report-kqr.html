Analyst Q&A on Cash Conversion

1.
Positive development: Sebastian Growe of Berenberg questioned MTU Aero Engines on the structural cash conversion outlook beyond the year 2030 and the impact of the Fort Worth facility. Katja Garcia Vila declined to guide beyond the 2030 mid-term targets but noted that Fort Worth represents a 100 million euros ramp-up cash headwind. This indicates that capital spending remains high as the company expands its network capacity. This headwind will limit the near-term accrual of free cash flow for earnings per share growth.
2.
Rising concern: Benjamin Heelan of Bank of America asked about the buildup of pre-financed geared turbofan maintenance receivables and how this balance would develop. Katja Garcia Vila explained that these prefinanced shop-visit receivables grew by 64 million euros compared with the prior period. She steered that this balance would continue increasing until late 2028 or early 2029 before turning positive. This buildup represents a significant working capital drag on cash conversion.
3.
Stable operations: Robert Stallard of Vertical Research asked if retirements of older aircraft had negatively impacted maintenance bookings. Johannes Bussmann answered that MTU Aero Engines had seen almost no retirements and that shops remained fully loaded. He steered that customer demand remained robust and that slots could easily be filled. This suggests that mature engine aftermarket revenues will remain resilient.
4.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on October 29, 2026, that the OEM margin decline of MTU Aero Engines to 28.6% in the third quarter was a key driver of stock underperformance. They noted that a fasterthan-expected normalization of the high-margin spare engine ratio would represent a significant downside risk to earnings per share. This challenges management's optimistic view that the transition to more installed deliveries will not dilute margins.

This highlights how unplanned customer insolvencies can drag on segment margins.
