[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Games Workshop is a compounder case at the wrong price. The business is an extreme positive outlier and the edition year, new capacity and a dense licensing pipeline should lift earnings per share about 10 percent a year against a consensus that models two flat years; but at 17,430p the shares already pay 26.5 times consensus earnings for that, so the modal return is 13 to 14 percent a year against the 20 percent bar, and the case clears the bar only below about 16,200p.

  • The change we expect is real, specific and dated, and it is modest.
    • It is the edition-year re-acceleration of a business that grew core revenue 10.9 percent in a fallow year while paying £12 million of new tariffs.
    • The 11th Edition of Warhammer 40,000 has been in the market since June 2026, with the fastest-selling individual launch in the company's history inside FY2026.
    • Factory 4, 49,500 square feet handed over in July 2026 with machines installed through FY2027, doubles the capacity that has rationed a £405.3 million trade channel
      • a first Chief Operating Officer (31 May 2026) unifies design and manufacture to attack the same bottleneck.
    • Dawn of War IV (17 September 2026) and Total War: Warhammer 40,000 (mid-2027) keep the licensing line growing from its re-based £32.9 million.
    • The external forces net to a mild cost headwind, not a demand inflection: tariffs of £13 million in FY2027 against £12 million in FY2026, about £2 million of plastic cost, a stronger pound
      • hobby demand grows 5 percent a year as it has for years.
  • The change has travelled through the company and into its accounts, and has stopped at the sell-side's estimates.
    • It shows in the resources (the Chief Operating Officer, a home-grown Group Finance Director, country managers in Shanghai and Seoul), the choices (Factory 4, the Sawley logistics lease, a £120 million cash buffer), the actions (the launch, the October 2025 price rise, about 30 new stores planned for FY2027), the key operating metrics (trade accounts +12 percent to about 9,100; trade revenue +17.2 percent) and the financial statements (core operating profit +15.7 percent to £245.1 million; core gross margin +160 basis points to 71.1 percent; profit before tax £275.7 million against a £265 million floor).
    • The sell-side's estimates have not moved with it: consensus earnings per share of £6.37 for both FY2027 and FY2028 against £6.22 in FY2026 on the brokers' basis, and consensus EBIT flat at £281 million against £277 million.
  • Our earnings path is about 10 percent a year, and almost all of it is revenue with drop-through.
    • Earnings per share of £6.24 in FY2026 rising to £6.81 in FY2027, £7.51 in FY2028 and £8.28 in FY2029, a 9.9 percent compound rate, of which 9.0 points are revenue growth, 0.6 margin, 0.5 below-the-line and −0.3 share count.
    • That is below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band at every horizon, which is why 'Financial Outputs' are not the strength of the case and 'Goldilocks growth' is a C.
    • The claim is a claim about core volume and price.
      • If volume disappoints, neither the margin nor the below-the-line lines can rescue it.
  • What it costs on the valuation is the whole problem.
    • At 17,430p the shares stand at 26.5x consensus 24-month-forward earnings of £6.59, the 72nd to 73rd percentile of the stock's own history, above the ten-year median of 22.00x and at the ten-year 'defendable' band of 26.90x
    • and the highest multiple of the fourteen names in the dashboard sector.
    • On our earnings path the same price is 22.4x our 24-month-forward earnings of £7.77, which is the ten-year median.
      • So the return in our case comes from consensus moving to our numbers with the multiple roughly held, not from a re-rating.
  • At the multiple we can defend the return is a C, made of a 35 percent chance of an A and a 42 percent chance of an F.
    • We apply 22.8x, the blend of three branches: 26.9x if the upgrade arrives and the market keeps paying the two-year level (35 percent)
    • 22.0x, the ten-year median (23 percent)
    • 19.9x, the ten-year 'conservative' band, if the upgrade does not come (42 percent).
    • At 22.8x on the earnings two years beyond each horizon the return is 14.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 13.3 percent a year over three years, including a 3 percent cash yield.
    • On the cleared branch alone it is 26.9 and 19.3 percent
      • on the uncleared branch 4.6 and 8.6 percent.
      • The multiple is the swing, and each branch alone moves the rate by more than the earnings do.
    • The plausibly worse drop-through, the £13 million tariff unabsorbed (a core operating margin 200 basis points below our path in every year), takes the earnings path to about 8 percent a year and, because the upgrade then does not come, the multiple to 19.9x: 1.0 percent a year over eighteen months and 6.8 percent over three years, an F, and the 'Investment Thesis' to D.
  • The pattern is 'Compounder', a mechanically mis-modelled proven upgrader, at a B fit.
    • The mis-modelled line is the core operating margin and, through it, operating profit: consensus treats the FY2027 tariff and wage lines as unabsorbed and the edition year as a 4 to 5 percent year, for a business that absorbed £12 million with a 160 basis point gross-margin gain and has beaten every floor it has set for four periods.
    • The fit is not crisp enough for an A because the mis-modelling sits in a three-broker median, one of which already models close to our path, and because the pattern is being bought at a 73rd-percentile multiple.

  • The case therefore is: a compelling business and an uncompelling purchase at 17,430p.

    • The chain holds at every link from the inputs to the accounts and breaks at the price
    • the case clears the eighteen-month bar below about 16,200p and the three-year bar below about 14,700p at the blended multiple, and we are not betting on the multiple.

  • Rating = C, because the expected change is real but not material enough for the bar at the price: the forces are incremental ('Change Forces' at C), the outputs improve to 10 percent earnings growth with upgrades probable ('Financial Outputs' at B), and the 'Modal return' at the multiple we can defend is 13 to 14 percent a year (C)

    • the parts combine as a chain that holds at each link but carries too little current, into a price that already discounts most of it.
    • 'Financial Outputs' at B, the upgrade record and the staircase, is what makes the C safe rather than a D
    • the D on re-rating inside the 'Modal return' is what keeps it from a B
    • 'Change Forces' is neither strong enough to lift nor weak enough to sink.
    • Not B, because B needs a return near 20 percent in the modal case and ours clears 20 percent only on the branch we give 35 percent odds
    • not D, because the earnings path is above consensus with a demonstrated mechanism and the cash return is certain.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against, in its strongest form: this is a fully valued compounder whose growth is normalising, and the market has begun to say so.
  • FY2026 earnings per share grew 4.9 percent
    • the second half's £3.04 was below the prior year's £3.06
    • second-half core revenue growth was about 5 percent and December 2025 was only slightly ahead
    • the licensing line has re-based to £32.9 million and the next games run to the licensees' timetables, not the company's
    • consensus models flat earnings for two years and two of the three visible brokers model FY2027 total revenue growth of 3 to 5 percent.
  • Every FY2027 cost line is adverse: tariffs £13 million, plastic £2 million, a stronger pound, 30 store openings, a £28.0 million Sawley lease from FY2026/27, an ERP cut-over in North America in summer 2027
    • and management itself holds a £120 million buffer against its going-concern stress scenarios.
  • At 26.5x 24-month-forward consensus earnings, the 72nd percentile of a ten-year history whose median is 22.0x, the shares carry a base-rate expectation of de-rating: a move to the median on unchanged earnings is −17 percent, to the 35th percentile −25 percent, and the market has already delivered −20.5 percent in three months with relative strength in the 23rd percentile.

  • The bear's sharpest form is that reported growth of 9 to 11 percent is 3.5 to 5 percent price and the balance is not volume, which the company's disclosure cannot disprove because it publishes no unit series.

  • What we concede, on the price, is all of it.

    • The modal return at the multiple we can defend is 13 to 14 percent a year, not 20
    • the mark-to-market risk is the case's largest and it is company-driven
    • the exit rate into FY2027 was about 5 percent
    • the sell-side's FY2027 number is not soft once Jefferies is counted
    • the shares are not a buy at 17,430p for a 20 percent bar.
    • No division covers the enterprise value at any peer multiple, so the downside is bounded by the multiple's floor alone.
  • Why we still hold our view on the business and the path.
    • The FY2026 result answered the cost half of the hypothesis: core gross margin +160 basis points with £12 million of tariffs paid, core operating profit +15.7 percent on +10.9 percent revenue.
    • The volume half is contradicted by the operating series the bear would need to see fail: trade accounts +12 percent to about 9,100
    • MyWarhammer active accounts +14.00 percent in H1 FY2026
    • Asia trade revenue +38.3 percent
    • the fastest-selling launch in company history
    • the Jefferies Retail Trends Barometer at +22 percent in March 2026 after +9 percent in January.
    • Flat earnings for two years would be the first such period since the 2004 to 2016 stagnation, in an edition year, with new capacity, from an operator who has beaten every floor.
  • Where the hypothesis wins, it wins on the price, and our answer is an entry level, not a counter-argument.
    • The probable path is 10 percent earnings growth
    • the case clears the bar only at a lower entry, and the recommendation says where.

The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a compounder case, not a recovery and not a demand inflection: Games Workshop is a vertically integrated owner of the two largest miniature-wargaming franchises, earning a 39 percent core operating margin and a 191 percent post-tax return on capital employed (FY2025) with no debt, and the change we expect is the edition-year re-acceleration of a business that grew core revenue 10.9 percent in a fallow year with £12 million of new tariffs.
  • The change is specific and dated: the 11th Edition of Warhammer 40,000 in the market since June 2026
    • Factory 4 handed over in July 2026 to remove the capacity constraint that rationed a £405.3 million trade channel
    • Dawn of War IV in September 2026 and Total War: Warhammer 40,000 in mid-2027 to keep the licensing line growing from its £32.9 million base.
  • So far the change has travelled through the company's resources (a first Chief Operating Officer, a home-grown finance director, country managers in Shanghai and Seoul), its choices (Factory 4, Sawley, a £120 million buffer) and its actions (the launch, the October price rise, 30 new stores) into the key operating metrics (trade accounts +12 percent to about 9,100; trade revenue +17.2 percent) and the financial statements (core operating profit +15.7 percent to £245.1 million; core gross margin +160 basis points to 71.1 percent), and has stopped at the sell-side's estimates, which model earnings per share of £6.37 for both FY2027 and FY2028 against £6.22 in FY2026.

  • We are betting that a company which absorbed £12 million of tariffs while expanding its gross margin in a fallow year will grow core revenue 9 percent and hold a 39 percent margin in its edition year with new capacity, and that a three-broker consensus modelling two flat years is corrected by the January 2027 floor
    • we are not betting on the multiple, which is why we do not buy at 17,430p.
