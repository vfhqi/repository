The split is on the return's own basis: the two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple and two-year-forward earnings per share, today against each horizon. The multiple and earnings parts multiply (1 + one, times 1 + the other, less 1, is the share price's change); dividends add to that.

The reasoning behind the inputs

Part 4 is the second set's reasoning as written. Where a later set changed an input, that input's figure in the tables above is the changed one and its reasons are in the changed-inputs file named above; Part 4 is not rewritten.

Conventions used in every line above. EBITDA is the company's adjusted EBIT (before items affecting comparability) plus total depreciation and amortisation; it is not FactSet's operating EBITDA history line (SEK 3,412m in FY26, on a different basis) and not the brokers' FY26 EBITDA actual (SEK 2,244m, which appears to be after items affecting comparability). Depreciation and amortisation includes the amortisation of capitalised development (about SEK 714m in FY27 on DNB Carnegie's estimate, rising to about SEK 818m by FY29) and the depreciation of right-of-use assets (about SEK 280m); Elekta's adjusted EBIT does not add back amortisation of acquired intangibles, which is small because its acquisitions are old, so no separate adjustment is made. Lease payments are the principal element only (FY26 total lease cash outflow SEK 303m, of which about SEK 56m is interest that sits in net financial items). Working capital is on the block's FactSet basis (receivables plus inventories less payables: 39.5 percent of revenue at April 2026), which excludes customer advances of about SEK 4.1bn to 4.4bn; the declining ratio is a deliberate proxy that holds the FactSet-basis balance roughly flat in kronor (SEK 6,600m to 6,800m), because on the company's own measure (about -13 percent of sales, including advances) growth with book-to-bill above 1 is roughly cash-neutral. If the model's working-capital line includes customer advances, replace the path with about -13 percent in every year. The dividend per share is the dividend declared for each fiscal year (paid in September of the following fiscal year); the FY27 cash payment is the SEK 2.40 declared for FY26 (SEK 917m, paid September 2026).
