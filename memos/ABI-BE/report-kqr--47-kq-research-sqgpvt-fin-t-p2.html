Section 2 – Input-Cost Inflation and Hedging Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Persistent input-cost inflation challenges brewer margins before rolling derivative hedges absorb the initial commodity price shocks.
  2. Structured hedging programs delay transactional headwinds but subsequently prolong the timeline required to realize raw material deflation.

Volatile Commodity Pressures

1.
Severe headwinds: Persistent inflation in packaging materials and energy continues to challenge the cost structures of global brewers. On March 27, 2023, Jefferies Research reported that prices for aluminum cans increased substantially during the recent inflationary cycle. These elevated costs force brewers to rely on aggressive pricing to offset margins. Consequently, persistent commodity headwinds can restrict a company's near-term profitability.
2.
Significant exposure: Packaging materials represent the largest single cost component for major brewers. On November 19, 2026, Rothschild and Co Redburn estimated that packaging materials represent approximately 43% of Molson Coors' total cost of sales. This high concentration means that unhedged commodity inflation will heavily pressure profitability. Thus, raw material price movements remain a critical determinant of global gross margins.

Hedging Horizons and Lags

5.
Structured delays: Brewers utilize rolling derivative programs to smooth out short-term commodity price spikes. On July 26, 2021, Jefferies Research reported that major beverage companies typically hedge exposures 12 to 18 months in advance to protect their income statements. This risk management strategy prevents sudden operational shocks. However, it also delays the positive impact of falling spot prices.
6.
Tight alignment: Anheuser-Busch InBev manages commodity volatility through a standardized rolling program. On October 29, 2026, Jefferies Research reported that the company manages almost all its exposure under a strict 12-month policy. This structured methodology provides clear cost visibility for the next fiscal year during periods of high inflation. However, the rolling structure means that spot price deflation will not benefit earnings until earlier contracts expire.
7.
Systematic hedging: Carlsberg coordinates its procurement centrally to secure predictable packaging and ingredient pricing. In its 2023 annual report published on February 6, 2024, Carlsberg stated its policy is to have at least 70% of its barley and aluminum hedged by the third quarter of the prior year. This systematic hedging provides strong near-term budget protection. Nevertheless, it prevents the company from immediately capturing favorable spot market shifts.
8.
Elevated pressures: Commodity price spikes force regional subsidiaries to adapt their long-term currency management frameworks. On March 12, 2025, Ambev reported that its foreign exchange hedging program relies on ten to fourteen month contracts to smooth out transaction volatility. This defensive approach allows the brewer to delay the impact of sudden cost shocks. Ultimately, currency hedging remains a critical tool to preserve operating margins in volatile emerging markets.
9.
Contrary evidence: Some rolling programs fail to protect margins when currency devaluations amplify local packaging costs. On March 17, 2023, Ambev disclosed that in 2023 its agricultural and metal hedges were locked in at significantly higher dollar values than the prior year. This currency misalignment drove up local cost of goods sold despite falling global commodity prices. Consequently, standard hedging strategies can occasionally exacerbate cost pressures rather than mitigating them.

This specific physical delivery charge remains highly volatile and difficult to hedge compared to underlying aluminum. Consequently, regional logistics and trade policies create persistent cost headwinds.

These multi-year programs mean that elevated packaging expenses will carry over into future periods. Therefore, extended hedging horizons can prolong margin recovery times.

Section 4 – Emerging-Market Currency Devaluations and Hyperinflation

Section summary
  1. Aggressive emerging-market currency devaluations trigger immediate translational adjustments that distort short-term reported margins before transactional hedges cycle.
  2. Hyperinflationary pricing power requires continuous local currency adjustments to match soaring consumer price indices despite severe volume pressure.
  3. Multi-year hedging programs delay the immediate impact of devaluations but subsequently prolong the timeline required to restore historical profitability.

Bolivian Currency Regimes

1.
Severe depreciation: The central bank of Bolivia abandoned its fixed currency peg on June 29, 2026, transitioning to a flexible regime where the official rate adjusted to 9.73 Bolivianos per dollar. During the third quarter of 2026, the local currency traded down to a range of 9.76 to 12.64 against the US dollar. This transition officially narrowed the parallel currency premium that had persisted throughout the year.
2.
Translational benefits: Ambev recorded positive non-derivative foreign exchange cash gains of R$ 151.7 mm during the third quarter of 2026 due to hard-currency balances held in Bolivia. This positive financial print surprised analysts who anticipated immediate macro headwind from the currency float. However, these nonderivative translation benefits are expected to deteriorate sequentially into the fourth quarter.
3.
Sustained pressure: Ambev faces long-term structural margin pressure because approximately half of its operating costs in Bolivia are dollar-denominated. Analysts estimate that the Bolivian currency devaluation will contract the Latin America South division EBITDA by 10% to 15% going forward. This regional headwind represents a two to three percent hit to group-wide consolidated operating profit.

Contrary evidence: Ambev achieved high-single-digit volume growth in Bolivia in the third quarter of 2026 despite the severe currency float and persistent social unrest. This commercial recovery indicates that underlying demand remained highly resilient even as local prices adjusted to the currency devaluation. Consequently, consumer price tolerance held up better than expected across the region.

Brazil Sin Tax Transitions

1.
Neutral transition: Morgan Stanley reported on November 5, 2026, that the newly published provisional measure sets Brazil's beer excise tax at 4.2% for 2027, replacing the current IPI tax in a neutral manner. The transition is expected to have limited financial impact on Ambev and Coca-Cola Femsa in the short term as the tax burden remains unchanged. However, the government plans to submit a higher, permanent excise tax scheme by May 2027 that will introduce greater margin pressure starting in 2028. Consequently, near-term pricing remains stable under the temporary transition framework before the final rate-setting bill is introduced.
2.
Favorable conversion: Jefferies Research highlighted on November 12, 2026, that beer received the lowest conversion factor of any alcohol category at 1.07 to yield a 4.2% rate in 2027. This provisional rate replicates the previous federal excise burden and effectively de-risks the first year of reform for Ambev and Heineken. The presidency expects stable retail beer prices throughout 2027 because the new selective tax is a direct swap rather than a tax hike. This initial stability will keep near-term pricing in brewers' hands before the next political cycle begins.
3.
Postponed battles: Jefferies Research noted on November 12, 2026, that the critical policy milestone is the upcoming bill due by May 31, 2027, which will establish tax rates for 2028. The returning administration of President Lula da Silva is expected to re-introduce a formula that taxes drinks based on alcohol strength rather than total volume. If Brazil moves to this strength-linked system, mainstream beer and non-alcoholic variants will benefit while spirits face severe tax penalties. Thus, the regulatory environment is set for a significant structural shift in 2028 depending on congressional approval.
4.
Divergent reactions: Industry representatives expressed relief over the deferred implementation of the strength- linked tax according to Jefferies Research on November 12, 2026. While the joint beer association Sindicerv welcomed the stable 4.2% rate for 2027, public-health advocates criticized the delay of punitive health taxes. Although spirits producers continue to lobby for equal treatment per unit of alcohol, brewers are currently defending a highly favorable tax differential in South America. Ultimately, this regulatory dispute will decide whether premium spirits or mainstream beers capture the future share of consumer spending.

Global Pass-Through Behaviors

5.
High elasticity: Historical analysis by Morgan Stanley on December 8, 2023, shows that beverage companies typically pass excise tax increases through to consumers at rates between 1.2 and 2.4 times the tax change. Specifically, the average pass-through rate for beer was calculated at 1.24, compared to 2.40 for wine and 1.71 for cognac. This aggressive pricing response is designed to fully offset the margin compression associated with higher tax expenses. However, such price adjustments often trigger consumer volume losses, particularly in highly price-sensitive emerging markets where disposable income is constrained.
6.
Direct pass-through: Bernstein Research reported on October 2, 2026, that Japanese brewers fully passed the October 2026 excise tax equalization directly through to consumers. This pricing action narrowed the consumer price discount of low-malt beer relative to mainstream full-malt beer from 18% to 11%. As a result of this narrowing, mainstream beer is projected to achieve 8% volume growth in 2027, while discount beer declines by 12%. This historical precedent suggests that equalizing excise taxes across product tiers can structurally accelerate premiumization.
7.
Policy interventions: Ambit Capital reported on November 20, 2026, that the Indian beer industry grew by more than 15% in the second quarter, heavily aided by positive policy interventions. Specifically, the state of Karnataka introduced an alcohol-in-beverage strength-based excise model, replacing bulk-liter taxes and lowering consumer prices. This regulatory shift significantly improved affordability and boosted consumer demand for low-alcohol beer. Consequently, local state interventions highlight that rationalizing excise structures remains essential to drive healthy category volume growth.
