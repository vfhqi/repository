EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Melia Hotels International operates 383 hotels comprising 94,912 rooms across 37 countries, positioning itself as the largest resort hotel company globally and the leading hotel group within Spain. The company maintains a unique model with 60% of room capacity in resort destinations and 40% in city locations, distinguishing it from urban-focused global peers.

Strategic positioning and operating model

1.
Operating structure: Melia utilizes a hybrid operating model with 46% managed, 26% leased, 16% owned, and 12% franchised properties.
2.
Asset-light pivot: Growth pipeline is strictly asset-light, with 89% of pipeline rooms under management or franchise contracts.
3.
Profitability concentration: Despite asset-light shift, owned and leased hotels still generate 63% of group EBIT, creating operational vulnerability.
4.
Geographic exposure: Spain accounts for 38% of rooms and 44% of EBIT, while the Americas represent 28% of rooms and 30% of EBIT.
5.
Customer loyalty: Approximately 80% of clients are leisure travelers, supported by MeliaRewards' 19 million members who drive 86% of direct channel sales.

Financial profile and leverage dynamics

6.
Revenue trajectory: Total revenues reached €2,096.5M in FY25 compared to €2,012.8M in FY24, representing 4.16% growth.
7.
Lease accounting impact: Total net debt stood at €2,228.5M in Q1 2026, with IFRS 16 lease liabilities of €1,279.5M representing over 57% of total debt.
8.
Return metrics: Melia's ROIC of 17.2% trails Marriott's 27.1% but exceeds Accor's 11.5%, reflecting mixed asset intensity.

Industry structure and competitive landscape

9.
Market fragmentation: The global €500bn lodging market features low brand penetration where only 30% of European hotels are branded versus over 70% in the US.
10.
Consolidation opportunity: The top four global hotel chains command only 8.7% share of total global rooms, providing years of secular unit growth through conversions.
11.
Competitive threat: Global Big Four chains (Marriott, Hilton, IHG, Accor) increasingly target leisure resorts, with Marriott Bonvoy's 180 million members dwarfing Melia's 19 million.

Value chain position and margin dynamics

12.
Margin disparity: Pure-play franchising captures EBIT margins of 70% to 80%, while lease operations yield only 3% to 6%.
13.
Direct channel strength: Over 48% of centralized bookings flow through Melia.com, reaching 48.5% of centralized sales in 2025.
14.
Distribution pressure: Online Travel Agencies extract commission rates of 15% to 20% of room revenues, maintaining oligopolistic channel control.

Operator quality and strategic risks

15.
Leadership continuity: CEO Gabriel Escarrer Jaume brings decades of institutional knowledge, successfully navigating multiple volatile macroeconomic cycles.
16.
Real estate backing: CBRE appraised Melia's real estate portfolio at €4.7bn, yet the stock trades at over 50% discount to NAV.
17.
Activist catalyst: Real estate fund Stoneshield Capital acquired a 9.5% stake and board seat, potentially catalyzing accelerated real estate asset disposals.
18.
Leverage vulnerability: High operational gearing from leased hotels risks rapid margin collapse, with €1,279.5M lease liabilities exposing cash flows to indexation.
19.
Succession friction: Three grandchildren publicly criticized the 2018 shareholder pact at the May 2026 annual meeting, arguing it shields the CEO's position.

Growth vectors and disruption forces

20.
Expansion pipeline: Melia entered branded residences with 28 active projects and targets operating 3,000 rooms across Tunisia by 2030.
21.
Technology disruption: Generative AI tools could bypass traditional OTAs, yet hotel brands currently capture only 0.7% of total traffic from AI referrals.
