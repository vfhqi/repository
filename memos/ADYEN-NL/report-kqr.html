Section 2 – Market structure and physical retail penetration

Section summary
  1. Digital-native acquirers are gradually capturing physical market share but face heavily entrenched legacy players.
  2. Europe remains highly consolidated under legacy acquirers holding over two trillion dollars in offline transactions.

Global enterprise POS market structure

1.
Massive addressable runway: The global large-enterprise physical point-of-sale market represents a twelve trillion euro total addressable market. This massive scale is distributed regionally with five trillion euros in North America, two and a half trillion euros in Europe, three and a half trillion euros in Asia-Pacific, and one trillion euros in Latin America.
2.

This compares to Stripe at one point-one percent and Checkout.com at essentially nil share in offline environments during fiscal year twenty-five.
3.
Consolidated legacy dominance: Legacy players continue to command the European point-of-sale market, where the top seven merchant acquirers collectively processed two point-three trillion dollars in volume during twenty-five. Within this landscape, Adyen ranks only eighth in Europe with two hundred and seven billion dollars in point-of-sale volume.
4.
Divergent growth channels: Jefferies' twelve October twenty-six analysis of the Nilson Report shows that while European web-based acquiring grew at a fast pace, in-store volumes grew seven percent year-overyear in twenty-five. This expansion was outpaced by Adyen's total European acquiring growth of sixteen percent, whereas legacy champions Worldline and Nexi registered more modest single-digit growth rates of six percent and four percent respectively.

Comparative POS volume and terminal metrics

5.
Surging terminal base: Adyen's half-year twenty-six interim report highlights that transacting physical terminals across its platform grew twenty-seven percent year-over-year to reach eight hundred and thirtyeight thousand devices. This footprint successfully drove H1 twenty-six in-person volume up twenty-eight percent year-over-year to one hundred and seventy-five point-seven billion euros.
6.
Entrenched merchant networks: Worldline maintains a massive physical footprint in Europe, processing six hundred billion euros in annual merchant services volume, of which its commercial acquiring division directly processes approximately two hundred billion euros. This extensive reach means Worldline serves as the core infrastructure processor for one in three European in-store commerce transactions.
7.
Widespread payment points: Nexi's twenty-five annual report indicates the legacy giant commands a dominant South-European retail market position, serving approximately three million terminal devices across its merchant networks. This infrastructure processed a significant portion of the nine hundred billion euros in pro-forma transaction volume managed by the group during the first half of fiscal twenty-six.
8.
Closing structural gaps: Deutsche Bank's thirteen September twenty-six report highlights that Checkout.com is turning up competitive pressure in North America by launching direct United States acquiring services. This new capability, powered by its Georgia merchant acquirer limited purpose bank charter, represents a major step to compete directly against Adyen's established omnichannel footprint.

Non-consensus dynamics in legacy defense

9.
Alternative routing defense: Legacy processors are defending their territory by supporting non-card alternatives, as Worldline's half-year twenty-six interim report outlines its active support for domestic Wero payment acceptance. This strategy attempts to bypass traditional card scheme rails, potentially altering future checkout economics across core European retail geographies.
10.
Entrenched vertical integrations: Nexi is actively defending its Italian and Nordic leadership by deeply embedding its software and payments via direct integrations with independent software vendors. This verticalization strategy, combined with the rollout of SmartPOS Cassa and mobile SoftPOS solutions, creates high operational switching costs for domestic merchant networks.
11.
Infrastructural replacement barriers: A non-consensus perspective from Morgan Stanley's twenty-nine September twenty-six report suggests that incumbent merchant stickiness remains highly resilient due to the complexity of hardware replacement cycles. Large retailers face steep operational friction and high upfront costs when attempting to migrate legacy in-store terminal estates onto modern unified commerce platforms.
12.
Other side of the story: Investors must consider that the long-term compounding of digital-native POS volumes faces structural limits, as Morgan Stanley's twenty-nine September twenty-six note warns that in-store growth sustainability is heavily debated given the inherently slow replacement cycle of legacy terminals.

  2. Ancillary value-added software products serve as the primary commercial strategy to stabilize transaction yields across modern ecosystems.
