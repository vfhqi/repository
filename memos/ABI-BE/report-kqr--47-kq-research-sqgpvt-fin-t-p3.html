Section 2 – The Deleveraging-to-Buyback Loop

Section summary
  1. Consistent debt reduction allows global brewers to unlock massive financial flexibility and accelerate share repurchases.
  2. High-profile acquisitions or macro rate hikes represent key risks that can disrupt the compounding deleveraging loop.

Trajectory of AB InBev

1.
Steady deleveraging: AB InBev has successfully reduced its balance sheet risk by paying down substantial debt over the past several years. The company reported that its net debt to EBITDA ratio fell from 3.96x in fiscal year 2021 to 2.87x at the end of fiscal year 2025. This leverage ratio further improved to 2.86x as of June 2026 due to strong organic cash generation. Achieving these targets helps the brewer rebuild investor confidence after its expensive historical mergers.
2.
Accelerating returns: AB InBev is utilizing its enhanced cash flow to execute a massive share repurchase program. The company announced a 24-month share buyback program of 6 billion USD on October 30 2025. Management disclosed that they completed 2.6 billion USD of this program as of October 2026. p AB InBev's net debt breakdown for fiscal years 2024 and 2025 This active program directly drives earnings per share accretion by steadily reducing the overall outstanding share count.
3.
Lower interest: AB InBev benefits from highly predictable debt servicing costs because nearly its entire debt portfolio is insulated from rate hikes. The company reported that 98% of its debt is held at fixed rates with an average coupon of approximately 4% in late 2026. This favorable structure shields the brewer's cash flows as it targets a long-term capital structure of 2.0x leverage. Consequently, lower absolute interest expenses will expand future free cash flow for additional capital returns.
4.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 27 2026 that higher sector refinancing costs could encourage brewers to reduce future share buybacks. Elevated global bond yields create downside risks for beverage companies as they refinance maturing debt. This pressure could slow the compounding leverage flywheel if interest expenses absorb cash otherwise earmarked for share repurchases.

Historical Peer Precedents

5.
Successful turnaround: Carlsberg serves as a key historical precedent for running a highly effective deleveraging- to-buyback program. The company reported that its leverage ratio dropped from 1.96x in 2016 to 1.25x by end of 2019 through disciplined cost execution. This deep balance sheet repair allowed the supervisory board to launch multiple share repurchases. Investors rewarded this operational discipline, which closed the historical valuation discount against global beverage peers.
6.
Reinvesting cash: Carlsberg capitalized on its low leverage to return substantial capital to its shareholders before global macro disruptions hit. The company repurchased 4.1 billion DKK of shares under its 12-month program in 2019. This capital return was paired with a 17% dividend increase to maximize total shareholder returns. This historical loop demonstrates how hitting leverage targets immediately shifts cash deployment toward direct equity accretion.
7.
Temporary interruption: Carlsberg's post-2019 capital returns loop was put on hold due to a major non-beer acquisition. The group terminated its share buybacks in July 2024 to fund the acquisition of Britvic plc for 28.8 billion DKK. This transaction caused leverage to jump to 3.28x by the end of fiscal year 2025. However, the company expects to resume repurchases once leverage falls below its 2.5x target.
8.
Steady deleveraging: Heineken provides another comparable case where reducing debt below a specific target unlocked significant corporate flexibility. The company achieved its long-term net debt to EBITDA target of 2.5x at end of 2014 despite currency headwinds. This milestone provided the executive board with the flexibility to consider larger capital returns. Consequently, Heineken successfully deployed part of its asset sale proceeds for a 750 million EUR share buyback.
9.
Renewed buybacks: Heineken resumed its capital returns loop in 2025 as its leverage moderated back below its long-term threshold. The brewer announced a two-year, 1.5 billion EUR share buyback in February 2025 after leverage fell to 2.2x. However, subsequent acquisitions and working capital shifts pushed leverage back to 2.6x by June 2026.
10.
This leverage shift highlights how balancing capital allocation between organic growth and shareholder returns remains a dynamic challenge.

Loop Breakers and Risks

11.
Large acquisitions: Large-scale mergers and acquisitions represent the most immediate threat to any compounding share buyback program. Bernstein Research reported on March 12 2026 that acquiring an 80% stake in Castel Afrique would cost approximately 11.6 billion USD in equity value. Paying cash for this transaction would increase AB InBev's leverage ratio from 2.5x to 3.1x. Such a substantial deal would effectively break the deleveraging loop by consuming the excess cash flow required for buybacks.
12.
Negative translation: Currency volatility represents another major loop breaker that can artificially inflate leverage ratios despite solid operational performance. Bernstein Research reported in May 2026 that a strong euro against the US dollar increased the dollar-value of debt for AB InBev. Because roughly 40% of the brewer's debt is denominated in euros, translation effects can offset underlying debt paydowns. Therefore, sudden exchange rate swings can delay reaching the target leverage ratio.
13.
Relative resilience: AB InBev is expected to maintain its buyback momentum because its massive free cash flow covers upcoming bond maturities. Jefferies Research estimated in October 2026 that AB InBev's annual free cash flow exceeds any scheduled annual bond redemption. This operational strength reduces refinancing risks and allows the board to maintain its progressive capital distribution strategy. Consequently, the deleveraging flywheel is highly likely to continue compounding EPS growth through 2027.
14.
Comparative returns: AB InBev offers superior capital return optionality compared to its primary global brewing peers. Morgan Stanley Research noted in February 2025 that Heineken's free cash flow profile appears more expensive than AB InBev and Carlsberg. This structural valuation difference means AB InBev can generate higher relative yield from its ongoing share repurchases. Therefore, AB InBev remains highly competitive in converting excess operational cash into direct equity accretion.
15.
Other side of the story: Bernstein Research argued on January 2 2026 that large capital allocation adjustments modestly reduce the long-term buyback scale and delay debt targets. For example, repurchasing minority stakes in packaging plants consumes cash that would otherwise fuel share repurchases. Consequently, unexpected operational investments or minor regional acquisitions represent persistent leaks that can stall the compounding EPS feedback loop.

Section summary
  1. Budweiser APAC faces a challenging distribution loop in China where volume declines trigger inventory destocking and margin-diluting promotional spend.
  2. Structural channel shifts and rapid growth from premium peers like Heineken continue to pressure Budweiser's historical premium leadership.

Bernstein Research noted on 8 January 2026 that China Resources Beer is best positioned to capitalize on instant retail due to its broad-based traditional trade route. Section 4 – The US Mainstream Loop

Section summary
  1. Mainstream US beer brands face a challenging loop where price increases and macro headwinds trigger volume losses and shelf-space erosion.
  2. Promotional spend and retail concessions fail to halt the shift toward imported premium beers and ready-to-drink options.

Mainstream Loop Dynamics

1.
Substantial volume contraction: The overall United States beer market faces persistent pressure as affordability concerns and structural headwinds drive consumers away from traditional offerings. Rothschild and Co Redburn estimated in October 2026 that the domestic beer category would decline by approximately 4% during the calendar year. This downward pressure makes it increasingly difficult for mainstream brands to maintain their historical volumes. Consequently, brewers face a self-reinforcing cycle where declining volumes limit operational leverage and squeeze profitability.
2.
Severe volume losses: Anheuser-Busch InBev continues to experience significant volume declines for its core Bud Light brand following historical consumer boycotts. Bernstein Research reported in November 2026 that Bud Light volumes fell 11.4% during the latest tracked twelve-week period. Although the brand showed slight sequential improvements, these declines continue to drag down overall domestic premium performance. This persistent weakness forces the company to rely on other brands to stabilize its North American volume footprint.
3.
Persistent brand declines: The flagship Budweiser brand also faces sustained volume pressure across major retail channels in the United States. Bernstein Research documented that Budweiser volumes declined by 7.0% in the twelve weeks ending October 31 2026. This ongoing contraction suggests that the brand struggles to retain its core consumer base amidst changing tastes. Unless Anheuser-Busch InBev can arrest the brand's volume erosion, domestic operations will remain fundamentally challenged.
4.
Shared segment challenges: Molson Coors is experiencing similar volume contractions as its premium light portfolio faces structural industry declines. Bernstein Research reported in late November 2026 that Coors Light and Miller Lite volumes fell by 5.8% and 5.1% respectively. This performance marks Molson Coors as a significant driver of absolute volume decline alongside Anheuser-Busch InBev. Such parallel losses emphasize that both major domestic brewers are struggling to find a stable volume floor.
5.
Contrary evidence: Anheuser-Busch InBev reported that its overall North American volume performance has outperformed the broader market in recent quarters. The company disclosed in October 2026 that its thirdquarter domestic revenue declined by only 1.2% due to positive price-mix. This resilient top-line execution suggests that strategic revenue management can offset underlying mainstream volume declines. Consequently, the negative operational feedback loop may be less severe than purely volume-based metrics imply.

Shelf Space and Retail Realities

6.
Structural space reallocation: Traditional beer brands are steadily losing valuable retail shelf space to faster- growing alternative beverage categories. An industry expert from Lagunitas Brewing highlighted that beer itself is losing physical footprint to ready-to-drink cocktails. This transition is particularly acute in refrigerated sections where retailers are swapping out poor performing brands for premium innovations. Consequently, legacy mainstream brands face a reinforcing loop of volume declines leading to immediate shelf space reductions.
7.
Temporary market benefits: Molson Coors previously captured significant retail footprint gains as a direct result of competitor shelf space losses in 2023. Management reported that they successfully held these unprecedented space allocations for their core light beer portfolio throughout 2024. However, these historical gains have largely failed to sustain long-term growth as consumer interest shifted elsewhere. This temporary stabilization highlights how difficult it is for mainstream brands to maintain shelf wins without organic demand.
8.
Accelerating share losses: Molson Coors has experienced a rapid reversal of its recent market share gains as competitor dynamics normalized. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that the company's US brand volumes declined by 5.6% during the third quarter. This performance fell below expectations and underperformed Anheuser-Busch InBev's domestic declines of 3.1% in the same period. These losses demonstrate that the temporary benefits from competitor disruptions have fully unwound, leaving the core portfolio exposed.
9.
Defensive commercial spending: Brewers are increasingly forced to utilize intense promotional activities to defend their remaining shelf positions in a soft market. Deutsche Bank Research reported that Molson Coors intends to continue investing in core brand improvements rather than maximizing short-term margin protection. This defensive strategy requires sustained commercial and marketing investments that heavily weigh on segment profitability. Consequently, the need to support underperforming brands prevents any meaningful recovery of operating margins.

Macro Drivers and Pricing Pressure

10.
Restrained pricing power: Consumers are struggling to absorb successive years of price increases, which limits the industry's future pricing flexibility. Jefferies Research noted in November 2026 that brewers are expected to keep pricing near the low end of their long-term range. Attempting to push aggressive price hikes would likely accelerate mainstream light beer declines as affordability pressures mount. Therefore, volume growth must take priority over pricing if brewers hope to stabilize their domestic operations.
11.
Cyclical economic pressures: Elevated fuel costs have emerged as a primary cyclical driver of weakness across the domestic beer category. Bernstein Research reported in late 2026 that higher gas prices have significantly weighed on discretionary retail purchases. This correlation is particularly strong in the convenience channel where budget-conscious consumers are most active. Consequently, rising pump prices trigger immediate convenience store contractions for major mainstream brands.
12.
Disproportionate brand sensitivity: Different mainstream brands exhibit varying levels of vulnerability to fuel-driven consumer budget pressures. Bernstein Research calculated a 53% R-squared correlation between Bud Light convenience store volumes and United States gas prices in 2026. Other value-oriented brands like Natural Light and Busch Light also showed high sensitivity with correlations of 40% and 34% respectively. This high sensitivity proves that mainstream light portfolios are highly vulnerable to macroeconomic shocks that pinch lower-income consumers.
13.
Widespread margin compression: Rising material and logistics costs are severely compounding the operational pressures caused by volume declines. Molson Coors disclosed that cost inflation in materials and manufacturing negatively impacted its third-quarter results. 160 161 162 163 164 165 166 168 reported that the company's gross margin contracted by 376 basis points due to Midwest Premium and spot freight inflation. These escalating operational costs directly depress underlying earnings performance as volume trends remain weak.
14.
Other side of the story: Some distribution partners argue that mainstream brands are showing signs of stabilization as promotional pricing and marketing activations gain traction. A regional distributor manager reported a volume increase in late 2026 driven by Labor Day sales and football season promotions. This operational feedback suggests that targeted local activations can break the downward mainstream loop and restore channel health. 172 173, this positive view is likely temporary because broader structural pressures and cost inflation continue to challenge long-term segment margins.

Section 5 – The BEES B2B Digital Platform Loop

Section summary
  1. The BEES digital platform is a key growth driver that captures more than seventy percent of AB InBev's global revenues.
  2. Structural regulatory limits in the United States and peer platform scaling represent the main limits to this digital ecosystem.

B2B Operational Scale

1.
Steady digital: AB InBev has successfully scaled its digital platform across its global footprint. In the third quarter of 2026, the company captured 73% of group revenue through B2B digital platforms across 30 live markets. Overall gross merchandise value reached 15.9 billion USD, which represents a solid 12% increase compared to the prior year period. This digital adoption strengthens the company's regional route-to-market while laying the groundwork for high-margin monetization.
2.
Marketplace growth: Rapid expansion of the third-party marketplace allows the brewer to monetize its existing distribution network. Third-party marketplace gross merchandise value grew by 45% year-over-year in the third quarter of 2026 to reach 1.4 billion USD from thirdparty products. This asset-light model has scaled rapidly from 3.5 billion USD for the full year of 2025. Consequently, third-party sales are emerging as a major growth engine that improves overall asset utilization.
3.
Compounding data: Digital transaction loops create a compounding data asset that optimizes retailer purchasing patterns. Real-time transaction data enables AI-driven recommendations that increase average SKUs per buyer. Personalized rewards programs like Club B build customer stickiness and incentivize retailers to concentrate their purchases. This loop drives higher volume sales per outlet and structurally reduces churn.
4.
Operational efficiencies: Physical distribution improvements directly translate into expanded operating margins for the core business. AB InBev reported that digitized markets achieved a 33% reduction in frontline costs as a percentage of net revenue. Digital tools also increased sales representative store visits by 15% and boosted logistics labor hours productivity. These operational savings help offset inflationary headwinds and support corporate profitability.

Structural Hurdles and Competition

5.
Contrary evidence: Unclear standalone profitability reporting means the actual financial contribution remains difficult to verify. Jefferies Research noted on September 14 2026 that AB InBev does not disclose standalone BEES profit margins or transaction take-rates. Without these specific data points, any sum-of-theparts valuation remains highly speculative for investors.
6.
Regulatory limits: Legal restrictions in the United States prevent the direct implementation of the standard B2B digital flywheel. The three-tier alcohol distribution system limits direct brewer-to-retailer relationships due to strict state franchise laws. Federal regulations also prohibit brewers from providing anything of value to retailers or holding ownership interests. 188 To adapt, the company utilizes BEES Link to integrate with independent wholesalers.
7.
Competitive systems: Competitive pressure is intensifying as other global brewers deploy their own proprietary digital ecosystems. Heineken reported that its B2B platform eazle captured 13.4 billion EUR gross merchandise value across 32 operating companies in 2025. This rapid scale allows Heineken to deepen its own traditional trade relationships and improve sales force productivity.
