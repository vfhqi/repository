Market Fragmentation and Reallocation

1.
Fragmented capture: European apparel markets are highly fragmented, which spreads Shein's lost market share across too many players to benefit any single retailer. Morgan Stanley reported on 15 October 2026 that the apparel market remains highly fragmented, meaning any market share relinquished by Shein as a consequence of policy changes may be spread across numerous players. Consequently, this fragmentation prevents domestic retailers from seeing a clear-cut, quantifiable sales boost from the July 2026 reform. Therefore, near-term earnings estimates for individual listed entities have not been upgraded solely due to Shein's share loss.
2.
Divergent control: Continental European markets have experienced a sharp reversal in Shein's spending share while the UK market remains a useful regulatory control. Consumer Edge reported on 12 October 2026 that Shein's share of European apparel spend fell from about 7% to under 5% in September. In contrast, Shein's share of UK apparel spend held roughly steady through the summer because no comparable customs levy was active there. This divergence suggests that fixed customs fees are the primary driver of Shein's European volume decline.
3.
Contrary evidence: Shein has maintained high pricing flexibility and continues to offer substantial discounts that limit direct spending reallocation to domestic retailers. Morgan Stanley reported on 15 October 2026 that Shein has responded to the tariff reform by passing on incremental duty costs through targeted price adjustments. Despite estimated price increases of approximately 20% in Europe, Shein continues to offer a substantial price discount to traditional fashion retailers. Consequently, Shein's underlying value proposition remains competitive enough to retain price-sensitive shoppers.

4.
Regional expansion: Polish fashion champion LPP is capturing regional market share through its aggressive roll- out of the value-tier Sinsay brand in Eastern Europe. This physical expansion helps LPP offset regional digital competition and capture budget-conscious shoppers. Therefore, LPP represents one of the purest ways to own the European value retail recovery story.

Cautious trading: Swedish retailer H&M has welcomed the de minimis reform but remains unable to isolate any positive trading impacts on its business. However, H&M cautioned on its September 2026 results call that attributing direct market-share gains to the de minimis changes remains difficult. Consequently, H&M's near-term trading improvement remains highly speculative and unconfirmed.

Promotional relief: British fashion retailer Next has experienced a reduction in digital customer acquisition pressures as cross-border advertising intensity eases. Next reported in late 2026 that the digital competitive landscape has softened as Chinese platforms moderate their marketing. This softening has reduced Next's paid online marketing cost per order and supported its platform economics. Resilient margins: Inditex maintains an outperforming, highly resilient margin profile that protects its earnings against volatile external trade headwinds. Because Inditex primarily operates in a mid-price-point range, it appeals to a more affluent consumer demographic than ultra-value players. Section 3 – Spending Reallocated to Digital Platforms and Second-Hand Resale

Section summary
  1. Budget-conscious European consumers are migrating to peer-to-peer secondhand resale platforms rather than traditional online aggregators.
  2. Resale leader Vinted is capturing significant market share from listed platforms like Zalando and Modivo.

Structural Migration to Resale

1.
Softening market: Zalando is experiencing persistent structural pressure as price-sensitive European consumers shift their discretionary apparel budgets to peer-to-peer secondhand alternatives. The digital aggregator observed its share of the European online apparel market decline from 16% in 2022 to 13% in 2025, highlighting intense competition. This shift indicates that Zalando is losing market share to nimbler, circular platforms that address the ongoing consumer budget squeeze.
2.
Rapid displacement: Vinted has achieved superior customer engagement and transactional metrics compared to traditional fashion platforms across key continental markets. The peer-to-peer marketplace has successfully expanded its presence to command three times more daily active app users than Zalando in Germany. This rapid user migration demonstrates that modern shoppers increasingly prefer peer-to-peer secondhand transactions over the full-price inventory offered by traditional online aggregators.
3.
Stellar expansion: Vinted is demonstrating explosive operational growth that validates the strong consumer appetite for affordable, pre-loved fashion categories. The platform achieved a secondary share transaction valuation of 8 billion euros in early 2026, marking an exciting lead indicator. This robust valuation follows a massive 47% gross merchandise volume expansion in 2025, confirming that recommerce is expanding rapidly across the European continent.
4.
Historical precedent: Vinted demonstrated a powerful capacity to capture low-income household demand during the historical 2022 cost-of-living crisis. The platform grew its active monthly UK visitor base from 4.8 million in 2022 to 11.4 million by 2024, proving that macro pressures accelerate circular reallocation. This structural precedent suggests that the current customs duties on low-value imports will continue to accelerate peer-topeer reallocation over the next three years.

Subdued Aggregator Performance

5.
Disappointing performance: Modivo is facing significant e-commerce trading challenges that stand in stark contrast to the rapid expansion of physical value discounters. The group's digital Modivo.com segment recorded an 11% year-on-year revenue decline to PLN 1,524.4 million for the six months ending July 31, 2026. This double-digit e-commerce contraction confirms that traditional online aggregators face pressure as consumers realign their discretionary outlays.
6.
Sluggish trajectory: Zalando has reported subdued organic top-line momentum that reflects a cautious and highly price-sensitive European consumer environment. The platform registered pro-forma revenue growth of just 2.2% in the first half of 2026, trailing regional physical retailers. Because of this softer top-line development, management refined its full-year guidance to the lower end, proving that topline growth remains constrained across core apparel lines.
7.
Mixed profitability: Zalando is navigating divergent margin trends between its core consumer retail business and its highly profitable digital partner services. The B2B segment grew its revenue by 14.6% in 2025, driven by software layers like Tradebyte and SCAYLE. However, elevated logistics transition costs and warehouse investments continue to squeeze core consumer profit margins in the near term.
8.
Physical pivot: Modivo's parent group CCC is aggressively scaling its brick-and-mortar retail footprint to bypass online performance marketing inflation. The group expanded its offline off-price HalfPrice segment, achieving a spectacular 47% revenue increase in the first half of 2026. This shift suggests that budgetconscious shoppers are capturing price-sensitive fashion spending by returning to physical outlets rather than using digital aggregators.

Operational Headwinds and Shifts

9.
Softening demand: Zalando's near-term sales trajectory is being weighed down by a sharp industry-wide correction in the lifestyle sneaker category. The lifestyle footwear segment represents a low double-digit share of Zalando's total gross merchandise volume. Rather than clearing excess inventory with heavy promotional discounts, the platform has consciously avoided heavy promotional discounting to protect its brand image.
10.
Wavering allegiance: Apparel marketplaces are coping with low consumer loyalty as price-sensitive shoppers search across multiple platforms for cheap deals. The emergence of specialized vertical resale platforms has successfully reduced the social stigma of thrifting for younger demographics. Consequently, legacy department stores face a shrinking customer runway unless they can successfully integrate circular value propositions directly into their platforms.
11.
Efficient architecture: Vinted operates an asset-light, peer-to-peer business model that bypasses the inventory risk and warehousing costs of traditional online retailers. The platform grew its group revenue by 38% to 1.1 billion euros in 2025, driven by transaction-based fees. Because Vinted does not own the clothing listed by its members, it maintains a structurally superior cost profile compared to listed digital aggregators.
12.
Defensive transformation: Modivo is aggressively redesigning its e-commerce operating model to reduce its heavy reliance on expensive online performance marketing. The platform's unified MODIVO Club loyalty program grew its membership base to over 20 million users by mid-2026. By fostering organic traffic, Modivo aims to reduce paid online marketing costs per order and restore e-commerce operating margins.

These upcoming frameworks could impose long-term compliance costs on multi-brand aggregators that are already struggling with high operational costs.

Global disruption: Vinted is aggressively expanding its low-cost transactional marketplace model globally to capture market share from traditional e-commerce aggregators. The company launched its preloved apparel services in Australia in August 2026, offering zero seller fees to drive liquidity. This continuous geographical expansion indicates that Vinted will capture market share on a global scale as resale becomes a primary retail choice.

This flexible manufacturing model allows the group to maximize full-price sell-through and mitigate raw material cost headwinds without relying on markdown campaigns. Morgan Stanley reported on 15 October 2026 that product differentiation remains a key driver of market share gains.
