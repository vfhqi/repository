Section summary
  1. Brockhaus Technologies has successfully transitioned into a highly focused, pure-play security and defense technology group with a streamlined corporate structure concentrated on the Security Technologies

segment (IHSE) and central holding functions, yet this transformation has created a fundamentally altered investment proposition that balances substantial shareholder returns against execution risks in an untested standalone operating model. The H1 2026 earnings delivered a critical validation of management's post-divestment strategy. Continuing operations revenue declined 7.2% to €13.4 million, aligning with slow project expectations, while adjusted EBITDA improved substantially to €-0.5 million from a €-1.7 million deficit in the prior-year period.

The segment-level profitability expansion represents the most important operational delta, with IHSE's adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 14.8% from 10.4% in H1 2025, demonstrating that comprehensive fixed-cost reduction measures initiated in Q3 2025 are delivering structural margin improvements despite revenue headwinds.

The €180 million capital return roadmap stands as the defining financial event, with the company committing to return an aggregate representing an exceptional money multiple of 2.5x on the total invested equity of €120 million across both Palas and Bikeleasing divestments, receiving clear AGM approval with over 83% shareholder support on August 19, 2026. Critically, the public tender buyback offer was resolved at €25 per share rather than the initially anticipated €24, capped at 3,592,000 shares representing up to €90 million, establishing a concrete valuation floor that significantly exceeded prevailing market prices prior to announcement.

The major consensus deviation centers on the sustainability of the remaining business model. While management emphasizes defense sector momentum with the defense business now accounting for approximately 50% of IHSE revenue, the underlying commercial weakness is severe. Regional demand divergence shows marked investment restraint due to US tariff policy and Middle East conflicts, with Americas revenue declining to €1.8 million from €5.2 million in the prior-year period. This commercial cyclicality contradicts broker assumptions of stable organic growth trajectories.

The single most important strategic delta is the completed management transition at IHSE, where Dr. Thomas Niessen joined as Managing Director and Chief Executive Officer in May 2026, assuming responsibility for sales, research and development, and project management to accelerate commercial momentum and establish continuity for IHSE's next growth phase. This operational handover occurs precisely as the subsidiary transitions from being part of a diversified platform to serving as the sole revenue-generating asset, creating concentrated execution risk on unproven leadership during a demanding market environment.

The strongest counter-hypothesis challenges the entire investment case structure. While the divestment of Bikeleasing unlocked an unprecedented cash balance, it deprived the group of its primary organic growth engine, and the remaining Security Technologies segment lacks the scale and growth trajectory of the disposed financial technology business, risking valuation as a low-growth defense technology holding company if management fails to quickly identify and execute accretive acquisitions.

The concentration risk is existential, with the structural transition creating extreme dependence on a single asset (IHSE), meaning that if IHSE struggles to recover from its organic revenue decline or if new management fails to execute, the holding company's valuation will suffer, while the absence of cash pooling prevents TopCo from easily accessing subsidiary cash to fund holding-level operations.

Unpredictable trade policy measures under President Donald Trump and federal budget freezes have severely disrupted IHSE's highly secure communications business in the United States, causing Americas revenue to significantly decline year-on-year to €1.8 million in H1 2026 from €5.2 million in the prior-year period. This geopolitical volatility creates binary outcome scenarios where defense budget allocations could either accelerate dramatically or freeze entirely based on political decisions beyond management control. Capital allocation discipline represents a clear positive, with the group targeting technology champions with enterprise values of €20 million to €200 million that strictly meet the demanding Rule of 50 criteria requiring average annual revenue growth of 20% and an EBITDA margin of at least 30%, ensuring only highly profitable, asset-light B2B models are added to the portfolio. Management's insider alignment reinforces confidence, as founder and CEO Marco Brockhaus and the board hold a substantial equity stake of approximately 24% in the company, ensuring management's interest is strictly aligned with public shareholders.
