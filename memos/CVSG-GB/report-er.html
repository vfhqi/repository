Section 16 – Conclusion

CVS Group's fiscal year 2026 results, reported on 24 September 2026, represent a watershed moment in the investment case, primarily driven by the conclusion of the Competition and Markets Authority's market investigation with legally binding orders published on 22 September 2026. The financial delivery itself was precisely in line with pre-results consensus expectations, with revenue of £712.8m growing 5.9% year-on-year and adjusted EBITDA of £141.5m matching the company-compiled consensus average of £141.6m, demonstrating stable profitability execution despite persistent UK volume headwinds.

The single most important delta that these earnings changed about the investment case is the removal of the regulatory overhang and the immediate restart of UK acquisitions. Following the final CMA decision, CVS officially restarted its UK acquisition program in September 2026, exchanging contracts to acquire a two-site, nine-vet companion animal practice for an initial cash consideration of £15m. This regulatory certainty allows the board to redirect its strategic focus back toward growing the core business after more than two years of intense scrutiny, unlocking the group's ability to restart UK acquisitions which had been frozen for over two years.

Deutsche Bank's Kane Slutzkin highlighted that the CMA resolution marked a final de-risking event with no new remedies imposed beyond the provisional report. When combined with the group's successful Australian expansion to 57 sites and the ongoing geographical diversification strategy, this regulatory clarity fundamentally transforms the investment thesis from a defensive holding pattern to an active growth platform capable of deploying circa £50m per annum into accretive acquisitions across two high-quality markets.

However, the strongest counter-hypothesis that challenges the bull case is the persistent weakness in UK likefor-like organic growth and companion animal footfall. Like-for-like sales growth reached 2.1% for the full year, which implies that like-for-like growth slowed to approximately 1.5% in the second half of the fiscal year compared to the 2.7% recorded in the first half. The UK companion animal veterinary market witnessed continued weakness in customer footfall throughout the financial year, which management attributed to depressed consumer confidence, an unusually wet fourth quarter, and the demographic reality that the large pandemic pet cohort has reached young healthy adult years with lower clinical visit frequency, representing a temporary structural drag on volume that should reverse as these pets age.

The total number of Healthy Pet Club members experienced a marginal reduction of 2.1% over the year to 508,000 members, undershooting initial sell-side expectations and directly caused by weaker consumer confidence protecting discretionary spend and resulting in weaker footfall. This organic volume weakness is compounded by substantial fiscal headwinds, with Jefferies' James Vane-Tempest reporting that changes to National Insurance contributions imposed an additional £8m annualized cost on CVS Group's UK operations, and the combined £11m headwind from NIC and minimum wage could impact the group's adjusted EBITDA by approximately 7.6% if left entirely unmitigated.

What would change the analyst's mind requires observable evidence of sustained UK like-for-like growth acceleration back toward the 4% to 8% medium-term target range. Management expressed high confidence in returning to the historic 4% to 8% like-for-like growth range in the medium term, with this trajectory expected to be underpinned by the humanization of pets and ongoing clinical advancements across veterinary services.

The next observable date is the first-half fiscal year 2027 trading update expected in February or March 2027, which will provide the first clean read on organic momentum following the launch of Healthy Pet Club Advanced on 1 July 2026 and the implementation of post-CMA pricing adjustments during summer 2026. Specifically, evidence would need to show that Healthy Pet Club membership has stabilized or returned to growth beyond the early 14,500 Advanced tier sign-ups, that UK companion animal like-for-like growth has re-accelerated above 3% on a sustained basis, and that adjusted EBITDA margins have been defended within the 19% to 23% corridor despite the full annualization of National Insurance headwinds.

CVS Group officially launched its premium Healthy Pet Club Advanced tier on 1 July 2026 providing unlimited consultations in exchange for a higher monthly fee, with early market response highly positive as approximately 14,500 clients joined within the first three months, demonstrating strong consumer demand for high-value preventative care bundles. Additionally, confirming that the group has successfully deployed its targeted £50m annual acquisition budget across both territories at disciplined multiples while maintaining leverage below the 2.0x ceiling would validate that the inorganic growth engine is fully operational. Failure to demonstrate this organic recovery by mid-fiscal year 2027 would suggest that the structural volume weakness is more persistent than management's optimistic COVID pet aging narrative implies.
