Margin Target Debate and CMD Strategy

4.
Margin target refusal: Management's conservative approach to financial targets has sparked debate among sell-side commentators. At the November 2025 Capital Markets Day, Indutrade broke tradition by refusing to raise its 14% EBITA margin target. Instead, the executive team reconfirmed the existing target, emphasizing that absolute profit growth is their core focus. This decision indicates a clear preference for scaling absolute cash flows rather than optimizing margin percentages. Strategy framing: Commentators evaluate Indutrade's choice differently when contrasted against its high-margin peers. For example, Lagercrantz raised its EBITA margin target to 20% in February 2026. Meanwhile, peer Lifco consistently delivers margins of 22% to 23%. This means Indutrade has a lower margin profile, which justifies its EV/EBITA multiple discount to peers. Nonetheless, absolute growth remains a viable compounding strategy if transaction volume is high. Hurdles for deals: During the CMD, management admitted that raising the target would negatively affect their flexible acquisition strategy. They frequently buy solid companies with 12% margins if they see a clear path to improvement. Thus, keeping a lower target ensures the deal pipeline remains broad, allowing the compounding engine to operate without unnecessary restrictions.

Macro Sensitivity Historical multiples: The valuation of the serial-acquirer sector is highly sensitive to macroeconomic conditions and interest rates. Redeye notes that between 2006 and 2012, average EV/EBIT multiples for Lagercrantz, Addtech, and Indutrade were only around 10x. This was significantly lower than the elevated multiples of approximately 15x seen in recent years. Other side of the story: While some analysts fear multiple compression, others believe Indutrade's decentralization offsets size constraints. Redeye argues that delegating deal execution to independent business areas effectively allows Indutrade to act as multiple smaller serial acquirers. This structural agility means the group can maintain high transaction volumes without overpaying for larger targets. Therefore, the acquisition flywheel remains highly sustainable over the next three years despite the group's larger absolute size.

2.
