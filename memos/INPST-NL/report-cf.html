EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

InPost SA stands at a critical strategic inflection point, executing a wholesale transformation from Polish domestic champion to pan-European out-of-home logistics platform while simultaneously navigating a pending €15.60 per share cash takeover by a consortium led by Advent International, PPF Group, and FedEx. The strategic pivot is highly capital intensive, with the company deploying PLN 2.4 billion in capex during FY26 to install approximately 20,000 new automated parcel machines across Europe, deliberately sacrificing near-term free cash flow to capture long-term market share.

This aggressive land-grab strategy is supported by explosive volume growth of 32% in Q1 2026, but faces severe execution risks from the integration of loss-making UK assets Yodel and Menzies, which registered a combined EBITDA loss of PLN 48.2 million in the most recent quarter. Meanwhile, the domestic Polish business–historically the company's profit engine with EBITDA margins of 47.1% –faces structural margin compression as management deliberately invests in pricing initiatives and new services while confronting insourcing threats from its largest customer Allegro, which operates a competitive locker network exceeding 4,500 units.

The governance structure is undergoing wholesale disruption, with four Supervisory Board members resigning ahead of the takeover, including Chairman Hein Pretorius, to be replaced by consortium-appointed non- independent directors. This dilutes independent board representation to just two members out of seven, creating elevated governance risks around capital allocation discipline post-transaction. However, founder and CEO Rafal Brzoska remains firmly in control of operations, maintaining extreme personal loyalty from the Polish management team but creating high key-man dependency risk if he were to depart. The executive bench has been strengthened internationally with the appointment of Francisco Javier van Engelen Sousa as Group CFO, bringing multinational FMCG credentials from Jerónimo Martins and Signify, alongside salary adjustments for International CEO Michael Rouse reflecting rapid UK expansion responsibilities.

Employee engagement metrics are improving, with Group Employee NPS rising and female representation in senior management reaching 40.9%, exceeding the company's 30% diversity target set for 2026. The strategic architecture is shifting fundamentally, with international operations now contributing 53% of group revenue in Q1 2026, up from a historically Polish-dominated profile. Management is aggressively pursuing merchant checkout integration, making out-of-home the default delivery option through strategic partnerships including eBay UK's Simple Delivery and the Bloq.it manufacturing alliance to ensure elastic APM production capacity of 30,000 to 40,000 units annually.

The acquisition of a 95.5% stake in Yodel in April 2025 by converting £106 million of loans into equity immediately provided InPost with 8% UK parcel market share but brought severe operational complexity, as the UK business registered a Q4 2025 EBITDA loss of PLN 99.3 million due to peak-season quality investments. The integration of Mondial Relay in the Eurozone is progressing more successfully, transitioning the French network from legacy PUDO points to a higher-margin locker-based model that delivered Eurozone EBITDA margins of 13.5% with stable year-on-year performance.

Critically, no active activist presence has been detected in the AlphaSense library, leaving minority shareholders dependent on the recommended consortium bid to extract value. The external environment presents a complex mosaic of tailwinds and structural headwinds. Consumer preference for out-of-home delivery in Europe accelerated from 13% to 14% penetration in 2023, while InPost achieved explosive UK volume growth of 220% and Eurozone volume expansion of 28% in Q1 2026, dramatically outpacing e-commerce market growth rates of approximately 8%.

The company maintains dominant competitive positioning in Poland with 70% of locker capacity and a 47% B2C parcel market share, creating formidable scale barriers against rivals including Allegro One Box and traditional carriers like Royal Mail, DHL, and CTT Correios. However, competitive intensity is rising sharply as Royal Mail targets deployment of more than 20,000 lockers in the UK under new ownership, Allegro expanded its network to over 4,500 APMs, and Quadient operates 27,700 open European locker units.

Regulatory dynamics are broadly supportive, with municipal authorities endorsing locker networks that deliver 75% CO2 emissions reductions versus door-to-door delivery, though local planning bottlenecks in France and Spain can delay site deployments. The supply chain is secured through strategic equity investment in Bloq.it, guaranteeing elastic locker manufacturing, while macroeconomic headwinds from wage inflation and blue-collar labor shortages are mitigated by InPost's highly automated locker model requiring less labor-intensive courier transport than traditional postal networks.

The financial profile exhibits severe bifurcation between domestic strength and international dilution. Group revenue expanded 30.8% year-on-year to PLN 3,862.4 million in Q1 2026, vastly outperforming PostNL's flattish growth, but group EBITDA margins contracted 850 basis points to 23.4% as lower-margin UK operations offset Polish profitability. Poland delivered resilient EBITDA margins of 47.1% on revenue growth of 9.2%, driven by pricing power that offset flat domestic APM volumes declining 1% year-on-year due to Allegro insourcing effects. The UK business remains deeply unprofitable with a negative PLN 48.2 million EBITDA in Q1 2026, though management guides to mid-single-digit margin recovery during FY26 as Yodel integration stabilizes. Leverage has increased materially to 2.4x net debt/EBITDA from 1.9x in the prior year, driven by net debt of PLN 9,875 million to fund acquisitions and locker deployments, though this remains well below the historical peak of 2.7x in H1 2023. Most critically, free cash flow has turned sharply negative at PLN -409.9 million in Q1 2026 due to a massive PLN 214.2 million working capital drain and accelerated capex intensity reaching 9.3% of revenue, compared to PostNL's 3.58% capex intensity, rendering near-term cash generation neutral to negative for FY26.

The historical earnings trajectory over the last three years demonstrates consistent high-double-digit revenue momentum but increasing margin volatility from international expansion. Group revenue grew from approximately PLN 2,425.5 million in Q1 2024 to PLN 3,862.4 million in Q1 2026, representing a 59.2% cumulative increase, while adjusted EBITDA margins contracted from 31.3% in Q1 2024 to 23.4% in Q1 2026 due to dilutive acquisitions.

The company exhibits systematic sandbagging behavior in Poland, with Q1 2026 Polish EBITDA beating consensus by 7%, but faces persistent UK execution misses including a Q4 2025 EBITDA miss of PLN 99.3 million from peak-season cost overruns. Management lowered FY25 EBITDA growth guidance from 20%-25% to mid-teens in November 2025 to absorb UK integration headwinds, demonstrating a sandbagger archetype that resets expectations ahead of operational investments.

Forward guidance for FY26 calls for flat year-on-year EBITDA as Polish margins compress to the mid-40s level from 49% in FY25, reflecting deliberate investments in AI capabilities, pricing initiatives, and new merchant services. The most recent Q1 2026 earnings delivery showed a solid 5.4% EBITDA beat versus consensus at PLN 902.2 million, driven entirely by better-than-expected Polish execution, while UK losses persisted as anticipated.

Group parcel volumes reached 359.2 million units, up 32% year-on-year, with UK volumes tripling to 76.9 million via Yodel consolidation and Eurozone volumes rising 28% to 94.2 million. However, adjusted net profit collapsed 78.7% year-on-year to PLN 71.9 million due to higher depreciation, interest expense, and integration costs. Cash flow conversion deteriorated sharply, with domestic Poland generating robust FCF of PLN 275.8 million but international operations draining PLN -637.5 million, pushing group FCF deep into negative territory.

Postearnings, sell-side analysts maintained flat EBITDA expectations for FY26, with primary analyst David Kerstens of Jefferies downgrading InPost from Buy to Hold and cutting the price target to €15.60 in line with the consortium bid, citing concerns over Polish margin compression and elevated capital intensity. InPost's peer-relative momentum ranking positions the company at the top of the fundamental matrix across European delivery operators.

The company delivered actual revenue growth of 30.8% versus peer medians in the low single digits, with DHL declining -1.9%, Royal Mail UK Parcels falling -1%, and PostNL domestic parcels flat at 0%. InPost's EBITDA margin of 23.4% significantly exceeds PostNL's calculated 7.15% normalized margin and matches CTT Correios at 23.39%, though it trails the company's own historical Polish margins above 47%. The company trades at a premium FY26E P/E multiple of 33x versus the European logistics median of 14.3x, reflecting growth expectations but also elevated execution risk.

InPost maintains formidable competitive moats including 70% of Polish locker capacity, a 47% B2C market share domestically, and 50% of UK out-of-home volume, creating structural scale advantages. However, pricing power dynamics are mixed, with Poland demonstrating 9.2% revenue growth on flat volumes via repricing, while UK and Eurozone markets face intense competitive pressure from traditional carriers and e-commerce platforms insourcing logistics.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

InPost's group adjusted EBITDA margin fell 850 basis points year-on-year to 23.4% in Q1 2026, driven by severe dilution from lower-margin international acquisitions, representing a structural headwind as the company sacrifices near-term profitability for geographic expansion. The UK operations generated an adjusted EBITDA loss of PLN 48.2 million in Q1 2026, with peak-season costs reaching PLN 99.3 million in Q4 2025, indicating that integration execution remains materially behind schedule despite aggressive capital deployment.

Polish APM volumes declined 1.0% year-on-year to 141.6 million units in Q1 2026, marking the first contraction in the core domestic locker business and signaling that Allegro's active diversification of fulfillment away from InPost presents a critical long-term risk to the high-margin Polish profit pool that funds international expansion. The domestic EBITDA margin is modeled to drop by 400 basis points to the lower mid-40s range, reflecting management's deliberate choice to invest in pricing initiatives and new services rather than defend absolute profitability levels.

Adjusted net profit collapsed 78.7% year-on-year to PLN 71.9 million in Q1 2026, demonstrating that elevated interest expenses and depreciation charges from the expanded asset base are eroding bottom-line earnings despite resilient operational performance. Net working capital experienced a massive PLN 214.2 million drain versus a PLN 8.1 million outflow in Q1 2025, pushing group free cash flow to negative PLN 409.9 million, confirming that the accelerated international rollout is temporarily destroying cash generation.

Net leverage increased to 2.4x adjusted EBITDA from 1.9x in the prior year period, approaching the upper bounds of management's comfort zone and limiting financial flexibility for additional acquisitions or shareholder distributions. InPost's capex as a percentage of revenue reached 9.3% in Q1 2026, whereas PostNL's capex stood at 3.58%, highlighting the company's significantly higher capital intensity relative to established postal peers and underscoring the reinvestment burden required to sustain competitive positioning.

Sell-side analysts previously cut FY26E EBITDA estimates by 16.0% following management's margin warning, indicating that external observers view the guidance reset as credible rather than conservative sandbagging. InPost trades at a premium FY26E P/E multiple of 33.0x compared to the European logistics median of 14.3x, leaving limited valuation cushion if execution stumbles or if international markets fail to converge toward Polish locker penetration rates over the medium term.

Outliers worth noting

InPost exhibits several extreme statistical deviations and qualitative anomalies that place the company in either the top or bottom quintile of European logistics and parcel delivery operators, with limited or incomplete explanations from management regarding the sustainability or normalization trajectory of these outliers. The most striking outlier is InPost's blended group EBITDA margin of 23.40% in Q1 2026, which represents a premium of over 16 percentage points compared to PostNL's calculated normalized EBITDA margin of approximately 7.15%.

This profitability differential persists despite InPost delivering revenue growth of 30.80% while traditional postal peers like PostNL experienced flat domestic parcel volume growth of 0.00% in the same period. Management attributes this structural margin advantage to automated locker economics and fixedcost leverage, yet the magnitude of the gap– placing InPost in the top 5% of European transport and logistics operators –suggests either temporary competitive advantages or unsustainable pricing dynamics that warrant closer scrutiny.

The company has not disclosed detailed unit economics comparisons or provided sensitivity analysis explaining how this margin premium would compress under scenarios of intensified competitive locker deployment by well-capitalized rivals or aggressive pricing by traditional carriers seeking to defend market share.

The fifth and final outlier concerns UK seasonal execution volatility, where UK EBITDA registered a loss of PLN 99.3 million in Q4 2025 and the target for Yodel EBITDA breakeven was pushed back by one to two quarters. Management subsequently restarted the Yodel transformation program in January 2026 after pausing integration efforts during the peak season, highlighting execution complexity that appears materially higher than disclosed at the time of the acquisition.

Despite this profitability challenge, UK parcel volumes surged 220% to 76.9 million parcels in Q1 2026, creating an unusual outlier scenario where top-line momentum dramatically outpaces operational capability and profitability realization. This pattern of explosive volume growth paired with persistent losses places InPost's UK operations in the bottom 5% of acquisition integration performance among European logistics consolidators, with management providing limited forensic detail on the root causes of the peak-season service quality investments or the specific milestones required to achieve midsingle-digit EBITDA margin targets.

The recurring pattern of delayed breakeven timelines and operational resets suggests either systematic underestimation of integration complexity or structural challenges in adapting InPost's Polish operational playbook to the more fragmented and labor-intensive UK logistics landscape, neither of which has been comprehensively addressed in public disclosures.
