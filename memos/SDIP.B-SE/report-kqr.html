1.

What has Sdiptech's management said in public, and what has it declined to say or left open, on each of the main drivers, risks and sources of optionality in the business over the last twelve months, and what is the latest dated status of each?"

This strategic shift aims to deliver a sustainable twenty percent EBITA margin and annual profit growth. Investors focus heavily on how this streamlined mix will drive long-term earnings per share expansion through high-pace acquisitions. The success of this transition determines whether the company can maintain its historical double-digit compound growth rate.

This compounding engine depends on maintaining a sustainable cash conversion rate of fifty-five percent and incremental returns on capital. Sustaining this pace through 2032 would significantly expand Sdiptech's financial footprint. Consequently, the exact trajectory of this capital deployment is the primary driver of future earnings growth.

Capital Allocation and Valuation Drivers

5.
Capital optimization: Sdiptech completed the redemption of all outstanding preference shares in the first quarter of 2026. The total redemption proceeds amounted to one hundred eighty-four million Swedish kronor to clean up the equity base. Cheaper financing: Sdiptech successfully refinanced its debt in September 2026 by issuing SEK 400 million in senior secured floating rate bonds. These bonds carry an interest rate of three-month Stibor plus two percent and mature in September 2030. Leverage constraints: Sdiptech reported a total net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio of 2.95 times at the end of the third quarter of 2026. This level remains barely below the leverage ceiling of three times established by management. Acquisition restart: Sdiptech restarted its acquisition engine by completing five transactions over the last twelve months. These deals included the acquisitions of STORR, Rail Safety Systems, JLM Teknik, AUT Wheels and Castors, and Vale Engineering. The board proposes to carry forward the entire profit of over SEK 2.25 billion for reinvestment. Retaining these cash flows is crucial because it funds high-pace acquisitions without equity dilution.
