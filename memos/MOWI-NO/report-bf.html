EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 17 findings

Mowi operates the world's most integrated salmon farming platform, commanding approximately 21% global market share across seven nations while targeting 605,000 GWT harvest in 2026 and exceeding 650,000 GWT by 2029. The company's accelerating volume trajectory delivers 8.3% annualized growth versus 1% projected industry supply growth, positioning Mowi as the dominant global leader. Group operational revenue reached EUR 5,729 million in 2025, driven by harvest volumes expanding from 375,000 GWT in 2018 at a 6.2% CAGR versus 3.7% for the industry.

Sea farming captures the largest value pool in the chain, generating operational EBIT margins between EUR 1.50/kg and EUR 2.50/kg, while downstream Consumer Products achieved a 24.9% ROCE in 2025, generating EUR 197.3 million EBIT as lower raw fish costs expanded secondary margins. This executive summary synthesizes critical findings across business model architecture, financial performance, industry structure, competitive positioning, and foundation durability, weighting heavily toward financial benchmarking and operator quality assessment.

Dominant industry structure with severe biological supply ceilings

4.
Global salmon farming displays highly concentrated leader-tier market structure operating behind strict regulatory licensing frameworks and biological capacity constraints. Global supply of farmed Atlantic salmon reached approximately 3.10 to 3.22 million tonnes WFE, representing over EUR 21 billion market value, while global salmon market value grew at ~8% CAGR over the past decade as supply volume expanded at only 2-3% annually. The top 20 global producers control over 80% of total Atlantic salmon supply, with Mowi holding global #1 position at ~21% market share, followed by SalMar (~10%), Cermaq (~10%), and Lerøy (~7%). Norwegian sea farming is strictly capped by Maximum Allowable Biomass regulations limiting commercial grow-out licenses to 1,195 total permits, with production capacity across Norway's 13 farming zones reassessed biennially based on environmental sea lice impacts on wild salmon stocks. This regulatory architecture creates permanent barriers to new entrant capacity additions while reinforcing incumbent license asset values

Upstream feed concentration and benign competitor conduct stabilize value chain economics and margin distribution patterns. The global salmon aquafeed market is tightly consolidated, with Skretting (25%), Cargill (25%), and BioMar (19%) controlling ~69-70% of non-captive supply. Leading producers focus on biological control, fish welfare, and value-added product processing rather than aggressive price-cutting or volume flooding. Integrated operators with prime northern site locations and feed integration maintain an operational EBIT margin advantage exceeding EUR 1/kg over fragmented peers.
