Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Confidence in Alfen's near-term operational trajectory is only medium, so the Key Question can be answered only partially today. The drivers are concentrated in three variables: Dutch grid-operator call-offs, storage milestone margins and charger pricing. The width and skew of the 18-month outcome range cannot yet be measured with confidence.
1.
Smart Grid anchor: Segment revenue grew 10.1% to 54.0m EUR in Q3 2026, yet gross margin slipped to 23.7% as lower-margin grid-operator products reached 70% of sales.
2.
Customer concentration: Alliander and Enexis supplied 34.8% of FY2025 revenue. Operator call-off cuts drove a 10.2% Smart Grid revenue decline that year.
3.
Storage margin volatility: Storage gross margin was 32.5% in Q1 2025 and 21.1% in Q1 2026. It reached 37.1% in Q3 2026, helped by one-off contingency releases of about 2.5m EUR.
4.
Storage phasing: Q3 storage revenue fell 30.4% after an 87.8% first-half surge, so milestone timing dominates quarterly results. Backlog of 109m EUR includes 86m EUR for 2027.
5.
EV charging divergence: Q3 revenue fell 6.7% and deliveries 10.6% while October BEV registrations rose 50%, because public installations added only 6k chargers.
6.
Cost base: Personnel expenses rose 6.1% to 40.1m EUR in H1 2026, with overlapping transformation costs expected to normalise in fiscal 2027. Amortisation of capitalised development runs near 10m EUR annually.
7.
Broker consensus assumptions: Jefferies' FY2027 EBITDA of 29.2m EUR on 512.7m EUR revenue exceeds the 27.3m EUR consensus. Its adjusted EPS fell to 0.20 EUR from 0.35 EUR. Recovery-speed assumptions across the three business lines explain the dispersion.
8.
Forecast reliability: Fluence missed revenue consensus in 8 of 15 quarters and Kempower issued a profit warning, so lumpy backlog conversion has historically undermined forecasts built on few drivers.
9.
Liquidity and valuation: Alfen renewed its 50m EUR revolver and 80m EUR guarantee facility for three years, yet shares are trading at a deep historical discount, which suggests the market prices transition risk.
10.
Reconciliation, concentration: The 34.8% figure names only Alliander and Enexis, so Stedin's framework, which gives Alfen half its planned volume, is not included as stated. The 54% share in Section 7 and 54.0m EUR in Section 2 appear to be different measures of the same Smart Grid quarter.
11.
Reconciliation, earnings and liquidity: Q3 adjusted EBITDA fell 43% to 4.0m EUR yet beat the 2.3m EUR consensus through a one-off release, so neither reading is a clean signal. Alfen breached a leverage covenant in Q3 2024, so "fully mitigated" liquidity overstates the position. Deutsche Bank's 30.7m EUR FY2027 estimate dates from May 2025 and is stale against Jefferies' November 2026 figures. The FactSet 22.1m–29.7m EUR range is not evidenced in the body text.
12.
Evidence gaps: Enexis' three-year framework tender, starting April 2027, is unresolved, CATL pricing formulas remain confidential and sodium-ion installations are not due before H2 2027. These critical visibility gaps explain the partial answer. The balance of evidence points to downside risk in EV charger pricing and storage order intake. Upside optionality remains if EU-driven grid investment lifts Smart Grid volumes. This analysis relies on broker and company disclosures that are partly dated and, in places, conflicting. The Enexis tender result, the next quarterly reports and disclosure of the CATL terms are the most useful next steps for narrowing the range.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Alfen's near-term earnings recovery is heavily dependent on a few volatile drivers like Smart Grid volumes and Energy Storage milestones.
  2. The company's immediate liquidity risks are fully mitigated by a renewed credit facility, though valuation remains depressed.

Core drivers and strategic transition

1.
Critical focus: "Key question: how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Unfavourable mix shifts: Structural changes in Alfen's business segments are expected to weigh on near-term gross profitability as the company navigates its organizational restructuring. Deutsche Bank reported on 11 February 2026 that lower-margin smart grid and storage businesses are currently offsetting the higher-margin charging segment. This structural change suggests that near-term earnings per share will remain under pressure until volume-driven growth resumes in fiscal year 2027.
3.
15 16 p Q p, y p Alfen financial performance and segment breakdown through Q3 2026 Escalating personnel expenses: Rising organizational costs at Alfen are temporarily eroding adjusted EBITDA margins despite recent operational restructuring campaigns. Jefferies reported on 4 November 2026 that third-quarter adjusted EBITDA fell by 43% to 4.0m EUR due to rising transformation costs. This cost inflation restricts near-term earnings per share expansion unless operating leverage improves alongside a recovery in the EV charging market.

Credit constraints and valuation discount

4.
Severe covenant pressures: Sluggish market demand previously threatened Alfen's operational liquidity position before a critical bank refinancing agreement was finalized. Alfen's 2024 annual report showed the company breached its leverage ratio covenant in the third quarter of 2024 before securing bank waivers and more flexible calculation terms. This restructuring was essential to avoid default risks and preserve the capital structure.
5.
Successful credit renewal: Robust financing agreements have significantly de-risked Alfen's near-term operational funding and guaranteed liquidity through late 2029 under stable terms. Alfen's third-quarter 2026 report confirmed the renewal of its 50m EUR revolving credit facility and 80m EUR guarantee facility for three years. This funding stability supports ongoing project execution and protects earnings per share from sudden capital shortages.
6.
Depressed market valuations: Skeptical investor sentiment continues to weigh on Alfen's public share price despite the company's strategic attempts to reignite growth. Jefferies reported on 4 November 2026 that Alfen shares are trading at a deep historical discount of 50% relative to historical multiples. This deep discount indicates that public markets are heavily pricing in transition risks and will limit share price appreciation until clear earnings per share momentum is established.
7.
Contrary evidence: Strong front-loaded revenue growth in the first half of 2026 suggests Alfen's operational momentum remains healthy despite persistent margin headwinds. Jefferies reported on 18 August 2026 that Alfen's first-half adjusted EBITDA rose by 26% to 16.4m EUR, driven by a 24% surge in revenues. This temporary outperformance suggests that near-term earnings per share could exceed conservative consensus forecasts if storage execution maintains its accelerated pace.
8.
Other side of the story: Persistent revenue phasing in the lumpy energy storage division and pricing pressure in EV charging could completely offset Alfen's grid-led recovery. Deutsche Bank argued on 4 November 2026 that one-off contingency releases inflated third-quarter margins, leaving a very soft outlook for the fourth quarter. These ongoing structural challenges suggest that actual earnings per share could fall far short of consensus if grid operator call-offs fail to accelerate.

Section 2 – Smart Grid Solutions and Dutch Grid Bottlenecks

Section summary
  1. Alfen's Smart Grid Solutions segment remains a resilient revenue anchor despite experiencing severe installation bottlenecks and call-off cuts from major Dutch grid operators.
  2. Structural margin pressure persists as high-volume grid operator products dominate the sales mix over more profitable private network projects.
  3. Strategic positioning and contract indexation clauses help mitigate raw material inflation, though capacity constraints and permitting delays limit short-term volume expansion.

Segment performance and customer concentration

1.
Sustained volume growth: Alfen expanded its Smart Grid Solutions revenue by 10.1% to 54.0m EUR in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 49.1m EUR in the third quarter of 2025. This expansion was primarily driven by high-volume transformer substation sales to Dutch grid operators and record revenues in the Finnish subsidiary Alfen Elkamo. This solid performance is crucial because the grid segment represents a stable revenue anchor that supports overall earnings per share during periods of volatility in other divisions.

37 39 Q g p Alfen Smart Grid Solutions Q3 revenue comparison showing 10.1% year-over-year growth

2.
Declining segment profitability: Alfen experienced a gross margin contraction in its Smart Grid Solutions segment to 23.7% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 25.9% in the third quarter of 2025. Management reported that this decrease was driven by an unfavorable segment mix where lower-margin products for grid operators represented 70% of sales. This ongoing margin compression will weigh on consolidated earnings per share unless Alfen successfully scales its higher-margin private network projects.
3.
Elevated customer concentration: Alfen exhibits significant revenue concentration with two major grid operators Alliander and Enexis accounting for 151.4m EUR or 34.8% of consolidated revenue in fiscal year 2025. This high level of concentration exposes Alfen to sudden strategic shifts or capital expenditure cuts by these utilities. Such concentration means that any reduction in purchasing from either operator directly threatens near-term earnings per share stability.
4.
Long-term volume security: Alfen secured a multi-year framework contract with Stedin, the third-largest Dutch regional grid operator, to supply compact and walk-in substations over four years with options to extend to eight years. Under this agreement, Alfen is allocated half of Stedin's planned volume of substations. This contract provides revenue visibility and supports long-term earnings per share, but its positive impact depends on Stedin maintaining its planned rollout schedules.

Grid bottlenecks and operator call-offs

5.
Severe call-off cuts: Alfen faced a 10.2% decline in Smart Grid Solutions revenue in fiscal year 2025 because two of the three main Dutch grid operators scaled down order quantities. Deutsche Bank reported on 19 May 2025 that operators slashed substation installation plans by 20% to 30% relative to initial projections. This volume contraction severely reduced operating leverage and historical earnings per share as the grid operator segment proved highly sensitive to execution delays.
6.
Persistent installation bottlenecks: Dutch distribution grid operators continue to face severe labor shortages and nitrogen-related permitting constraints that delay substation installations. Alfen noted that while underlying demand for grid reinforcement is strong, these non-equipment constraints limit physical rollout speed. This deployment lag means that Alfen cannot translate its massive manufacturing capacity into immediate earnings per share growth until regulatory approvals accelerate.
7.
Costly product defects: Alfen temporarily halted deliveries of its Pacto substations to Liander in early 2024 after discovering low levels of moisture inside several units. This production halt and subsequent refurbishment campaign resulted in a 5.0m EUR warranty provision that severely impacted the segment's 2024 profitability. This past quality issue illustrates how technical defects in mission-critical equipment can disrupt delivery schedules and suppress near-term earnings per share.
8.
Innovative standardized products: Alfen introduced standardized transport distribution stations for Enexis, serving as an end-to-end contractor to reduce on-site construction times from over a year to four months. This modular approach helps grid operators accelerate installations and allows Alfen to capture a higher average selling price per unit. This contractor role increases segment revenue and supports earnings per share, although it also introduces higher execution and project-management risks.

Competitive dynamics and peer benchmarking

9.
Protective contract indexation: Alfen utilizes indexation clauses in its framework agreements with major grid operators to pass on the rising costs of raw materials. Jeroen van Rossen stated in an earnings call on 11 November 2021 that these discussions are not tied to standard automatic indexes but involve bilateral negotiations of parameters like labor and material costs. This negotiated pricing mechanism protects the segment's gross margin from sudden inflation, thereby shielding near-term earnings per share from supply chain shocks.
10.
Peer margin superiority: Pfisterer achieved strong gross margins of 39.0% in its medium-voltage segment and 42.4% in high-voltage accessories during the first half of 2026, which are significantly higher than Alfen's grid operator gross margins. Bernstein reported on 20 November 2026 that Pfisterer's headcount expanded by 17% YoY ahead of volume delivery, which restricted operating leverage. This peer comparison indicates that specialized grid hardware manufacturers command higher gross profitability than integrated substation assemblers like Alfen.
11.
Scale-led competitive threat: Ormazabal represents a major private substation competitor in Europe with annual revenues exceeding 800m EUR and a workforce of over 2,700 professionals. The company invested 20m EUR to expand SF6-free switchgear production at its Igorre facility to meet growing demand for sustainable grid equipment. This massive scale and dedicated capacity expansion by a direct competitor threaten Alfen's market share and place downward pressure on pricing, dampening long-term earnings per share.
12.
Long-term contract stability: Landis+Gyr manages long utility deployments with a massive backlog of 3.7 bn USD and raised its fiscal year 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin guidance to 15.0% to 16.0%. This stable margin profile reflects resilience under long-term frameworks, which Alfen strives to replicate through its multi-year Dutch contracts. However, no comparable data was found on R&S Group or Arteche to enable direct margin comparisons in this analysis.

Contrary evidence and structural threats

13.
Contrary evidence: Grid prioritization and installation bottlenecks show signs of easing in northern Europe as some project cancellations create temporary capacity headroom. An expert from a European utility stated on 10 September 2026 that consumption-side cancellations in the Nordics could soon free up grid capacity. If this capacity-easing trend spreads to the Netherlands, grid operators may accelerate substation call-offs, allowing Alfen to beat conservative near-term volume forecasts and upgrade its earnings per share outlook.
14.
Strategic geographic pivot: Alfen initiated an organizational restructuring in late 2024 that reduced its workforce by approximately 15% to lower operational expenses and streamline decision-making. Jefferies reported on 18 February 2026 that Alfen's new strategy emphasizes geographic expansion in Europe while targeting five high-growth smart grid segments. This geographic expansion is intended to diversify Alfen's customer base and protect long-term earnings per share from Dutch grid volatility.
15.
Other side of the story: Severe distribution grid congestion and persistent permit queues in the Netherlands could permanently cap Alfen's Smart Grid Solutions volume growth below the double-digit historical trend. Even though Dutch grid operators expect record investment, local labor shortages and nitrogen emissions constraints may restrict actual substation installations through 2027. This persistent structural lag would lead to ongoing operational deleverage, keeping segment gross margins depressed at the lower end of Alfen's 20% to 30% target range and stalling earnings per share recovery.

Section 3 – Energy Storage Systems and Integration Margins

Section summary
  1. Alfen experiences severe revenue and gross margin volatility in its Energy Storage Systems segment due to lumpy project milestones and milestone-based revenue recognition.
  2. Gross margins are heavily driven by the timing of project completions and subsequent releases of one-off contingencies.
  3. Chinese turnkey competition and tariff policies compress system integration margins across Europe, forcing developers to prioritize localized partnerships.

Segment volatility and revenue front-loading

1.
Significant revenue reduction: Alfen's Energy Storage Systems revenue fell by 30.4% to 18.3m EUR in the third quarter of 2026 compared with 26.3m EUR in the third quarter of 2025. This decrease occurred because revenues were heavily front-loaded in the first half of 2026, during which Alfen achieved progress milestones on two of its largest projects. Consequently, third-quarter revenue represents an anticipated transition into a period of lighter project construction rather than a structural loss of segment demand.
2.
Accelerating early volume: Alfen reported a substantial 87.8% increase in Energy Storage Systems revenue to 98.8m EUR in the first half of 2026 compared to 52.6m EUR in the first half of 2025. This strong front-loaded growth was primarily driven by the NOP Agrowind and Return Antares projects reaching major execution milestones. This early phasing indicates that Alfen's near-term earnings per share remain sensitive to lumpy construction schedules and contract-specific billing timelines.
3.
Volatile backlog development: Alfen's energy storage backlog reached 109m EUR as of 30 September 2026, which represents a healthy expansion from 93m EUR at the end of the first half. The company's backlog is split into 23m EUR for delivery in the remainder of 2026 and 86m EUR scheduled for fiscal year 2027. This pipeline recovery supports the management's target to surpass the 127m EUR backlog level recorded at the end of 2025, which underpins expected profitability normalisation in 2027.
4.
Lumpy project phasing: Deutsche Bank reported on 4 November 2026 that Alfen's energy storage segment continues to suffer from erratic revenue patterns. The broker noted that storage revenues were highly backend weighted in 2025 but shifted entirely to a front-loaded pattern in fiscal year 2026. These structural shifts complicate quarterly earnings per share forecasting because revenue recognition depends on milestone signoffs rather than steady production.

Gross margin swings and contingency releases

5.
Elevated margin outperformance: Alfen recorded an exceptional gross margin of 37.1% in its Energy Storage Systems segment during the third quarter of 2026 compared to 18.2% in the third quarter of 2025. This result was significantly higher than Alfen's historical expected segment margin range of 15% to 25%. The outperformance was primarily driven by project contingency releases totaling approximately 2.5m EUR upon the completion of several massive utility-scale installations.
6.
Contrary evidence: Slow execution in previous years shows that Alfen's storage division can face sudden profitability headwinds when battery asset write-downs occur. Alfen's 2025 annual report revealed that its 2024 storage gross margins were suppressed by a 1.6m EUR impairment of older battery inventories. This historical precedent demonstrates that segment margins can fall below the targeted double-digit levels if rapid technological transitions render existing inventory obsolete.
7.
Sequential margin compression: Alfen reported a gross margin of 21.1% in the first quarter of 2026, which represents a steep decline from the 32.5% gross margin achieved in the first quarter of 2025. Management stated that the Q1 2025 gross margin was artificially inflated by similar one-off releases of project contingencies. This ongoing volatility shows that Alfen's gross profitability will continue to swing based on riskassessment revisions as large projects near completion.
8.
Lacking operating leverage: Deutsche Bank reported on 13 May 2026 that a lower storage contribution in the second half of 2026 will lead to a higher group gross margin but will not boost absolute profits. The broker explained that the lower volume in the segment will lead to declining operating leverage within storage assembly operations. This deleveraging effect means that near-term earnings per share will remain constrained until Alfen can secure more continuous order volumes.

European tender dynamics and supplier partnerships

9.
Strategic battery supply: Alfen secured a landmark partnership with Contemporary Amperex Technology to deploy 5 GWh of advanced sodium-ion battery energy storage systems across Europe starting in 2027. This supply deal allows Alfen to diversify its product offering and mitigate raw material cost volatility associated with traditional lithium-ion chemistries. Utilizing sodium-ion cells should protect Alfen's integrated margins from sudden battery market price increases, thereby stabilizing long-term earnings per share.
10.
Intensified pricing pressure: Jefferies reported on 4 November 2026 that utility-scale storage projects are increasingly subject to competitive pricing from turnkey suppliers entering the European market. This competitive dynamic is worsened by a rapid 40% decline in battery system prices, which reduces the absolute contract value of projects. These factors mean Alfen must continuously improve its execution efficiency to prevent these price declines from eroding its consolidated earnings per share.
11.
Shifting procurement preferences: A senior European procurement executive stated on 10 September 2026 that developers are increasingly demanding fully integrated grid-forming storage solutions to meet stringent grid code requirements. This technical shift benefits suppliers with deep local grid connection expertise and advanced software capabilities. Alfen's established position in the Dutch grid market provides a critical advantage in capturing these hybrid project tenders, which should support future margin recovery.

Competitive benchmarking and integration margins

12.
Peer margin contraction: Fluence Energy expects to achieve gross margins at the lower end of its 10% to 12% guidance range due to intense competition and tariff uncertainties. Jefferies reported on 15 May 2025 that proposed tariffs on Chinese battery components unilaterally push pricing upward across the battery integration sector. This industry-wide pressure suggests that Alfen's 15% to 25% gross margin target represents a relatively resilient and high-margin profile compared to purely US-focused integrators.
13.
Integrated margin premium: Wärtsilä Energy Storage targets a moderate EBIT margin of 3% to 5% in the medium term, demonstrating the thin-margin nature of pure hardware integration. A senior director at Fluence stated on 15 March 2024 that Alfen's ability to generate double-digit margins rests on delivering proprietary mediumvoltage substations alongside storage units. By bundling grid connection hardware with battery packs, Alfen commands a premium that pure integrators cannot easily replicate, supporting more resilient earnings per share.
14.
Scale-led funding limits: Merus Power faces working capital financing bottlenecks that restrict its ability to compete in large-scale energy storage projects exceeding 100 MWh. Inderes reported on 23 February 2026 that Merus Power's energy storage segment is low-margin and ties up substantial operating cash in working capital. This peer constraint highlights Alfen's superior position, as its renewed 50m EUR revolving credit facility provides the liquid headroom necessary to finance large projects without endangering near-term earnings per share.
15.
115 side of the story: Heavy pricing pressure from Chinese turnkey battery manufacturers and rising European import tariffs could permanently compress Alfen's Energy Storage Solutions margins below its targeted 15% to 25% range. Even though Alfen's grid integration capabilities command a premium, Chinese competitors are leveraging immense cost advantages and shorter lead times to undercut European system integrators. This persistent price erosion, combined with lumpy project milestones that trap working capital, may lead to ongoing cash flow volatility and permanently lower the division's contribution to Alfen's earnings per share recovery.

Section 4 – EV Charging Competition and Pricing Pressures

Section summary
  1. Alfen faces intense competition and margin contraction in its EV Charging segment despite a broader acceleration in European battery-electric vehicle registrations.
  2. Slow public charger installations and grid congestion limit segment volume growth as charge point operators constrain capital expenditure under high interest rates.
  3. Competitors like Zaptec and Wallbox expand their market presence through active product transitions, while historical precedents show hardware margins are highly vulnerable to sudden reversals.

Alfen segment volumes and pricing trends

1.
Deteriorated segment revenues: Alfen reported a decline in third-quarter 2026 EV Charging revenue by 6.7% to EUR 26.80 mm compared to EUR 28.70 mm in the third quarter of 2025. Deliveries fell to 25,850 charge points, representing a 10.6% drop from 28,900 units in the prior-year period. This contraction reflects persistent competitive pressures in the residential charging segment. Consequently, the portfolio transition continues to weigh heavily on absolute segment revenues before growth recovers in fiscal year 2027.

Q g p Alfen's Q3 EV Charging revenue trend showing a 6.7% year-over-year decline

2.
Stable gross margins: Alfen maintained a gross margin of 40.9% for its EV Charging segment in the third quarter of 2026, down from 43.1% in the third quarter of 2025. This margin remained above the midpoint of Alfen's guidance range of 35% to 45% despite active sales campaigns. Profitability was supported by a 4% increase in the average sales price, which reflected a stronger business-charger mix. This product shift suggests that commercial deployments can mitigate residential price erosion unless overall B2B demand softens.
3.
Contracted historical performance: Alfen experienced a larger revenue drop of 17.4% to EUR 51.10 mm in the first half of 2026 from EUR 61.80 mm in the first half of 2025. The segment's adjusted gross margin fell to 39.5% during the first half of 2026 compared with 44.1% in the first half of 2025. This reduction was driven by competitive pricing strategies and uneven public purchasing patterns, which offset lower component costs. This historical trend shows that aggressive discounting has impaired the segment's underlying profitability margins.

Divergent market growth and grid barriers

4.
Accelerating vehicle registrations: European battery-electric vehicle registrations grew strongly as October 2026 volumes surged by 50% year-on-year to 334,974 vehicles. Year-to-date registrations increased by 44%, leading to a record-high passenger electric vehicle penetration rate of 27.1% across major European markets. This demand acceleration was particularly visible in Germany where registrations rose by 79% and in France with a 63% increase. This strong registration momentum indicates that underlying consumer demand remains highly resilient as the total vehicle fleet continues to transition.
5.
Severe installation slowdown: Europe's public charging network expansion experienced a sharp deceleration in the third quarter of 2026, adding only 6k incremental chargers. Grid availability, slow connection permitting processes, and lengthy construction times are increasingly acting as primary bottlenecks for infrastructure deployment. Summer holidays and increasing grid congestions in early adopting countries are delaying the physical build-out of new stations. This installation lag means that Alfen cannot capitalize on rising vehicle sales because grid bottlenecks delay equipment procurement.

Peer comparisons and product transitions

6.
Expanding peer revenue: Zaptec achieved strong growth as its first-half 2026 revenues rose by 26% to NOK 506 mm, which represents approximately EUR 47 mm. Zaptec's expansion was driven by its strong position in semi-public applications with the Zaptec Pro charger, although the company remains weak in highly pricesensitive residential segments. This peer outperformance indicates that Alfen has lost significant market share during its legacy product phase-out.
7.
Contrary evidence: Robust business-charger demand allowed Alfen's third-quarter 2026 EV Charging revenue of EUR 26.80 mm to exceed the company-compiled consensus estimate of EUR 25.10 mm. The phased introduction of new platform features and the launch of the Eve Double Plus product drove solid year-on-year growth in the commercial segment. If this business-charger momentum accelerates, Alfen could outpace conservative consensus estimates and restore segment profitability ahead of schedule.
8.
Severe Swedish ban: Easee faced severe operational disruptions following a product ban in Sweden during early 2023, which caused its sales at distributor Elektroimportoren to decline by 90% in the second quarter of 2023. Elektroimportoren was unable to cover this product shortfall due to supply shortages from alternative manufacturers, leading to a 55% decline in its total charger sales. This historical precedent illustrates how regulatory interventions and compliance failures can abruptly halt market momentum.
9.
Strong recovery momentum: Wallbox reported a strong recovery as its second-quarter 2024 revenues grew by 48% year-on-year to EUR 48.80 mm, supported by its cost engineering and strategic sourcing initiatives. This operational efficiency helped Wallbox expand its gross margin by 910 bps to 39.1% compared with a gross margin of 38% for Alfen in fiscal year 2023. This comparison indicates that lean manufacturing is essential to defending gross profitability in a consolidating hardware market.
10.
Highly price-sensitive home charging: Garo noted that the current residential charging market is extremely price- sensitive and requires exceptionally high sales volumes to achieve necessary economies of scale. Macroeconomic uncertainties in Sweden have limited short-term visibility, forcing Garo to rely on more positive markets outside its home country. This peer experience suggests that residential charging hardware has become heavily commoditized unless bundled with advanced load management software.

Margin reversals and site-owner economics

11.
Slowing hardware demand: Kempower reported a 19% year-on-year decline in its third-quarter 2026 order received, driven by a sharp 59% drop in North America as operators delayed investments. Charge point operators are restricting capital expenditure due to rising interest rates and are focusing on improving the utilization of existing stations. This sudden demand contraction indicates that charging hardware margins face severe pressure when high interest rates force developers to halt rapid expansion.
12.
Superior station economics: Fastned achieved a 63% revenue increase to EUR 51.30 mm in the third quarter of 2026, driven by a 43% surge in charging volumes to 67.0 GWh. Fastned maintains a high tender success rate of 85% because its high-traffic stations sell four to five times more electricity than competing sites. This operational performance shows that site-owner economics and high utilization rates remain highly profitable even as hardware manufacturers struggle with declining sales volumes.
13.
Other side of the story: Persistent public charger installation delays and aggressive Chinese hardware entries could permanently prevent Alfen from recovering its historical market share. Even if European vehicle sales continue to rise, severe grid congestion and lengthy permitting procedures may restrict the physical build-out of new charging points. Furthermore, low-cost manufacturers and specialized peers continue to launch aggressive pricing campaigns. These structural headwinds suggest that Alfen's EV Charging revenues may remain depressed, stalling any near-term earnings recovery unless grid connection approvals accelerate.

Section 5 – Cost Structure and Broker Forecast Dispersion

Section summary
  1. Alfen's personnel cost trajectory remains temporarily elevated in the second half of fiscal year 2026 due to dual-operating organizational expenses.
  2. Amortization of capitalized development costs of approximately ten million euros annually represents a significant structural headwind to net earnings.
  3. Sell-side expectations for Alfen's fiscal year 2027 and 2028 profitability exhibit wide dispersion due to differing assumptions on EV charging and smart grid execution.
  4. Historical evidence from peer clean-energy equipment integrators shows that forecasts relying on a limited number of drivers are frequently missed.

Trajectory of internal personnel expenses

1.
Escalating labor costs: Alfen's adjusted personnel expenses increased by 6.1% to 40.1m EUR in the first half of 2026 from 37.8m EUR in the first half of 2025. Management explained in its first-half 2026 report published on 18 August 2026 that this rise was driven by a collective labor agreement indexation of approximately 5% implemented since mid-2025. Rising structural wage inflation across Europe reduces the company's operating leverage unless offset by rapid top-line expansion. Therefore, near-term earnings per share will remain constrained as Alfen implements its internal reorganizations.
2.
Elevated transformation expenditures: Alfen expects personnel expenses to remain elevated throughout the second half of 2026 before normalizing in fiscal year 2027. Management stated on its earnings call on 19 August 2026 that temporary overlapping costs exist as old and new business units operate side-by-side during the strategic shift. Deutsche Bank reported on 19 August 2026 that hiring contract workers and shifting staff across departments create short-term operational frictions. This transient inefficiency is expected to completely phase out by early 2027 as the new organizational structure settles.
3.
168 - Earnings Calls Costly restructuring provisions: Alfen recorded a substantial restructuring provision of 3.5m EUR in the first half of 2026 to execute its organizational alignment. Management confirmed in its semi-annual report on 18 August 2026 that this provision is part of a planned 4.5m EUR total transformation cost. Jefferies reported on 21 August 2026 that the restructuring aims to transition Alfen from a functional to a business-unit-led structure. However, these upfront severance payouts severely depress reported operating income and weigh on fiscal year 2026 earnings per share.
4.
Flexible labor reductions: Alfen can rapidly adjust its external workforce to protect operating profitability during periods of weaker market demand. Deutsche Bank argued on 19 May 2025 that external temporary workers represent roughly 15% of Alfen's total headcount. The broker estimated that reducing temporary headcount by 75% would generate a cost cut of 11% in 2027. This labor flexibility serves as a key defensive tool to shield earnings per share if grid operator call-offs or EV charging demand disappoints.

Capitalized development and amortization impact
