Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The separation of Opella transforms Sanofi into a pure-play, innovation-driven biopharmaceutical company, redirecting capital to high-growth areas but increasing exposure to pipeline and alliance risks.

Shifting Strategic Focus

1.
Critical inquiry: "what does the evidence say about the specific inputs that will drive this company's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, namely: (1) The scale and recurrence of US gross-to-net releases in Sanofi's 2026 quarters and whether any reversal is expected; (2) the Regeneron economics: profitshare expense trend (Regeneron 10-Q, Q3 2026: its share of collaboration profits rose to USD 2,574.9m from USD 1,455.5m), the end of development-cost reimbursement after full repayment of the development balance in Q2 2026, and the terms of the October 2026 alliance expansion (USD 1bn upfront, up to USD 7bn milestones, 50:50 profit split); (3) Amvuttra royalties: the tiered rate reaching 30% on annual sales above USD 1.5bn, Alnylam's Amvuttra sales trajectory, and cannibalisation risk from nucresiran, which carries no royalty to Sanofi; (4) Sobi's US Beyfortus royalty steps from 25% toward 30% to 35% by 2028; (5) equity-accounted income from the retained 48.2% Opella stake and the 26.4% Cheplapharm stake, and the deferred Cheplapharm payments from 2028; (6) the business tax rate after the Opella separation (Morgan Stanley: about 20% from 21%) and any Pillar Two effects; (7) currency: Sanofi's disclosed sensitivity (about EUR 1,069m of sales and EUR 0.23 of business EPS for a 0.05 USD/EUR move) and its hedging horizon; (8) interest cost and leverage under acquisition scenarios consistent with Sanofi's stated capacity and AA rating, and how business EPS treats acquired-intangible amortisation and capitalised upfront payments; (9) share count: buybacks completed in 2025 and 2026 and any new programme; (10) restructuring and amortisation excluded from business EPS, and how sell-side brokers treat them in their forecasts?"
2.
Significant transformation: Sanofi completed the sale of a 50% controlling stake in Opella to Clayton Dubilier & Rice on April 30, 2025. This transaction narrowed the company's focus to innovative prescription medicines and vaccines while removing the steady cash-flow cushion of consumer healthcare. Consequently, Sanofi's post-separation profile features elevated operating margins and faster topline growth but exposes its valuation directly to the volatility of pharmaceutical pipelines.
3.
Inherent discount: The exit from consumer health helps rebase Sanofi's long-term earnings toward innovative biopharma segments. According to Bernstein Research on November 29, 2024, Sanofi traded at a 20% discount to the pharma sector on 2025 estimates, suggesting room for multiple expansion post-separation to close the peer valuation gap. However, this rerating depends on successfully deploying the €10.4 bn in transaction proceeds into value-creating acquisitions rather than dilutive assets.

Valuation and Pipeline Realities

4.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley on November 3, 2026, forecast a slower 4% business EPS CAGR for 2027 to 2030, citing weaker new product launches and intensifying vaccine competition. This perspective suggests that the post-separation pure-play focus may not guarantee superior operating leverage if pipeline execution falters. Thus, structural separation alone cannot offset clinical setbacks or competitive erosion.
5.
Elevated concentration: The biopharma division remains highly dependent on Dupixent, which is co-developed and profit-shared with Regeneron. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted on November 4, 2026, that mitigating Dupixent's upcoming 2031 loss of exclusivity is central to Sanofi's long-term investment case. Because Sanofi profitshares half of its Dupixent revenues, diversifying the late-stage research pipeline is critical to sustaining earnings power.

27 & Co Redburn Other side of the story: While the pure-play pharmaceutical model theoretically commands higher valuation multiples, Sanofi faces unmitigated exposure to generic competition and localized market volatility without consumer health's stability. Therefore, the transition could depress valuation multiples if new oncology or immunology assets fail to achieve blockbuster status over the next four years.

Section 2 – Gross-to-Net Dynamics and Regeneron Alliance Economics

Section summary
  1. Favorable gross-to-net releases provide temporary tailwinds for Sanofi, but accelerating profit-share payments to Regeneron and the end of development-cost reimbursements present significant operating income headwinds over the next four years.

Gross-to-Net Price Adjustments

1.
Favorable adjustments: Sanofi recorded strong US Dupixent sales of €4,376 million in the third quarter of 2026, representing an operational growth rate of 16.1% over the prior year period. This robust expansion was driven by volume gains across multiple indications and was further supported by favourable gross-to-net price adjustments arising from commercial operational improvements. These pricing adjustments boost nearterm business operating income, though management expects underlying demand to continue at more moderate growth rates in subsequent quarters.
2.
Significant pricing: Sanofi reported a substantial 31.2% increase in Toujeo sales to €430 million and an 18.5% rise in Lantus sales to €530 million in the third quarter of 2026. The performance of both diabetes therapies in the US market was heavily augmented by favourable gross-to-net price adjustments alongside local market share gains. However, these positive out-of-period adjustments represent non-recurring tailwinds that are expected to moderate over the next 18 months.
3.
Volatile adjustments: Novartis experienced contrasting gross-to-net volatility for Kisqali in the fourth quarter of 2025, where global sales grew 44% but were depressed by negative out-of-period adjustments. In contrast, the company recorded a positive gross-to-net one-timer in the first quarter of 2025, which created a higher comparative base. This peer precedent demonstrates how pharmaceutical gross-to-net adjustments can cause significant quarterly sales fluctuations that do not reflect underlying volume demand.
4.
Substantial liabilities: GSK carries massive returns and rebates accruals on its balance sheet, with US returns and rebates liabilities totaling £4,891 million at the end of fiscal year 2025 compared to £5,235 million in fiscal year 2024. These gross-to-net adjustments reflect complex commercial and government reimbursement arrangements that create significant estimation uncertainty from one quarter to the next. Such substantial balances indicate that any subsequent reassessment of rebate liabilities can significantly impact reported biopharmaceutical revenues.
5.
Contractual reductions: AstraZeneca reported that its total gross-to-net adjustments for prior-year net US product sales was 0.7% in 2025, showing relative stability compared to 0.6% in 2024. Meanwhile, Roche reported that contractual price reductions in its pharmaceuticals division reached CHF 3,060 million in the first half of 2026, up from CHF 2,858 million in the first half of 2025. These comparisons demonstrate that while peer gross-tonet adjustments remain structurally high across the sector, Sanofi's positive quarterly releases represent a temporary divergence from standard accrual trends.
6.
Variable consideration: Replimune Group reported that gross-to-net adjustments are treated as variable consideration under ASC 606, which are only recognized when significant reversals are highly improbable. These adjustments are updated on a cumulative catch-up basis each reporting period, affecting both product revenue and net income. This accounting standard highlights how Sanofi's US gross-to-net releases remain sensitive to shifting payer channel mixes and historical return experience over the next four years.

Regeneron Alliance Economics

7.
Expanding collaboration: Regeneron reported in its third quarter 2026 Form 10-Q that its share of collaboration profits rose to USD 2,574.9 million from USD 1,455.5 million in the prior year period. This massive 77% profitshare expansion was primarily driven by continued global net product sales of Dupixent, which increased 35% to USD 6.5 billion during the third quarter of 2026. The rising profit-share expense paid to Regeneron represents higher cost of sales for Sanofi, potentially compressing its Biopharma operating margins.
8.
Complete repayment: Regeneron fully repaid the outstanding Sanofi development balance by the end of the second quarter of 2026, which previously represented its obligation to reimburse Sanofi's prior R&D funding. This repayment previously reduced Regeneron's reported collaboration revenues by approximately USD 930 million in fiscal year 2025 and USD 530 million in the first half of 2026. Consequently, the cessation of these development inflows means Sanofi will experience a reduction in other operating income beginning in the third quarter of 2026.

October Alliance Expansion

13.

Section 3 – Royalty Streams: Alnylam's Amvuttra and Sobi's Beyfortus

Section summary
  1. Rising royalties from Alnylam's Amvuttra and Sobi's Beyfortus will provide substantial high-margin cash flows for Sanofi over the next four years, though pipeline transitions and near-term ordering volatility create modeling hurdles.

Amvuttra Royalty Dynamics

1.
Tiered royalties: Alnylam owes Sanofi tiered royalties on global annual net sales of Amvuttra under their exclusive TTR licensing agreement. Sanofi receives 15% on the first USD 150 million, stepping up to 17.5% up to USD 300 million, 20% up to USD 500 million, 25% up to USD 1.5 billion, and 30% on sales exceeding USD 1.5 billion. Because Amvuttra's rapid clinical expansion in cardiomyopathy drove sales past the USD 1.5 billion threshold in 2025, Sanofi has already unlocked this lucrative top tier. This high-margin revenue stream flows directly into Sanofi's business operating income.

2.
Expanding margins: Sanofi anticipates that emerging royalty streams will become an increasingly important driver of its biopharma operating leverage through the end of the decade. Management specifically highlighted the Amvuttra royalty as a significant contributor to its financial outlook, projecting its business operating income impact to rise between 2028 and 2030. Compared to legacy partner models, this royalty carries minimal operational costs for Sanofi, meaning the cash inflows translate almost entirely into pure earnings per share. This royalty stream helps offset Sanofi's heavy research expenditures.
3.
Royalty headwinds: Alnylam's reported product gross margin fell to 72% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 77% in the prior year period. This margin compression was primarily driven by the escalating average royalty rate paid to Sanofi as Amvuttra revenues scaled. While this tiered royalty structure represents a growing financial burden for the partner, it serves as an increasingly profitable asset for Sanofi. Thus, Alnylam's rising cost of goods sold acts as a direct positive driver for Sanofi's business earnings.

Trajectory and Cannibalisation

4.
Decelerating trends: Alnylam reported global third-quarter 2026 Amvuttra sales of USD 1.13 billion, marking 12% quarter-on-quarter and 65% year-on-year growth. However, the partner's gross margin of 72% in the quarter was meaningfully lower than the 75% recorded in the second quarter of 2026. This trend demonstrates how the mounting royalty payment burden directly restricts Alnylam's profitability as the franchise expands. Consequently, Alnylam faces strong corporate incentives to transition patients away from Amvuttra to preserve its own operating margins.
5.
Cannibalisation risk: Alnylam is actively developing nucresiran as a next-generation, wholly owned RNAi therapeutic that carries no royalty obligations to Sanofi. This pipeline asset is highly differentiated by its biannual or annual dosing regimen and deeper TTR knockdown compared to Amvuttra. If approved, Alnylam will likely direct all commercial efforts to shift patients to nucresiran to evade the heavy Sanofi royalties. This potential transition would lead to a rapid erosion of Sanofi's royalty base after the expected 2030 launch.
6.
Contrary evidence: H.C. Wainwright on November 17, 2026, noted that Alnylam expanded its Phase 3 TRITON-CM trial of nucresiran to 2,250 patients from the previous 1,750-patient design. This enrollment expansion was implemented to preserve clinical study power under conservative event-rate assumptions, which risk-adjusts the clinical probability of success down to 65%. This adjustment suggests that nucresiran's market entry faces potential delays and higher execution risks. Consequently, Sanofi's high-yielding Amvuttra royalty stream may enjoy a longer-than-expected commercial runway before facing material cannibalisation.

Beyfortus US Royalty Steps

8.
Royalties structure: Swedish Orphan Biovitrum, also known as Sobi, receives a tiered royalty from Sanofi on US net sales of the RSV prophylactic Beyfortus. Sobi acquired these US rights in 2019 from AstraZeneca and subsequently established a direct royalty agreement with Sanofi in 2023. The contractual royalty rates started at 25% at launch and are structured to increase each year through 2028 to a top tier of 30% to 35% of US net sales. These structured step-ups represent an expanding margin drag for Sanofi's vaccines franchise over the next four years.
9.
Financial liabilities: Sanofi carries a massive balance sheet liability related to the future royalties payable to Sobi on US Beyfortus sales, which stood at €1,647 million as of June 30, 2026. The nominal value of estimated payments due within one to five years is €916 million, while payments expected after five years total €2,150 million. Because this liability is calculated using long-term sales forecasts, any subsequent upgrades to Beyfortus's market trajectory will proportionately increase Sanofi's financial obligations. This compounding liability represents a significant non-operating expense for the biopharma group.
10.
Launch volatility: Sobi recorded substantial quarterly phasing for its Beyfortus royalties, which DNB Carnegie estimated to decline 23% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026. This near-term volatility reflects Sanofi's ongoing challenges in balancing commercial supply schedules. Despite this quarterly noise, DNB Carnegie expects full-year royalties to remain positive as the immunization schedule stabilizes. Thus, the royalty stepups will play out against a highly variable quarterly sales baseline.

However, Sobi's internal surveys of 100 US physicians indicate that over 90% of healthcare providers still prefer Beyfortus over competing RSV prophylactic options. This solid preference suggests that the underperformance is a temporary supply phasing issue rather than a structural loss of demand.

Section 4 – Associate Stakes, Post-Separation Taxes, and Currency Sensitivities

Section summary
  1. Retained associate stakes and structural post-separation tax shifts provide Sanofi with significant high- margin income, though unhedged currency translation risks pose near-term earnings volatility.

Associate Stakes and Deconsolidation

1.
Significant retention: Sanofi holds a 48.2% equity interest in OPAL JV Co, the associate entity created after deconsolidating the Opella consumer health business on April 30, 2025. This transaction yielded net cash proceeds of EUR 10.4 bn and allows Sanofi to retain significant influence under equity accounting. Jefferies estimated on October 21, 2024, that the Opella exclusion causes a 10% dilution to business EPS before factoring in cash deployment. This structural change removes steady consumer cash flows but focuses Sanofi entirely on higher-margin biopharma assets.
2.
Expanding partnerships: Sanofi announced a strategic partnership in September 2026 to transfer 20 mature medicines and three manufacturing sites to Cheplapharm. In exchange for this legacy portfolio, Sanofi will receive a 26.4% equity stake in Cheplapharm SE. Deutsche Bank noted on September 15, 2026, that this transaction materially increases Cheplapharm's scale while adding Sanofi as a strong strategic shareholder. The commercial transfer is scheduled to begin in the first quarter of 2027 and complete by the third quarter.
3.
Deferred considerations: Sanofi is positioned to receive substantial cash flows from Cheplapharm starting in 2028 through a highly structured deferred payment agreement. According to Deutsche Bank on September 18, 2026, Sanofi will receive up to EUR 800m NPV of preferred dividends yielding EUR 202m annually over 2028 to 2032. Additionally, the agreement includes EUR 350m NPV of performance-based earn-outs linked to Lovenox gross profits. These deferred cash inflows will bolster Sanofi's long-term capital allocation capacity.
4.
Deferred capital: Sanofi will also benefit from a deferred, three-year payment arrangement with Cheplapharm covering acquired Lovenox inventory. Deutsche Bank estimated on September 18, 2026, that this inventory absorption is valued at more than EUR 500m. While Cheplapharm's cash flows will be subdued from 2028 due to these payouts, Sanofi secures a highly defensive income stream. This arrangement effectively monetizes Sanofi's declining general medicines without requiring ongoing marketing investments.
5.
Contrary evidence: Sanofi's share of losses from associate investments can create meaningful non-operating headwinds, as evidenced by a substantial equity-method loss recorded for OPAL JV Co in fiscal 2025. This negative impact was driven by the allocation of investment costs and fair value adjustments on Opella's intangible assets. If the newly formed Cheplapharm partnership faces integration hurdles or steeper-thanexpected Lovenox biosimilar erosion, Sanofi's equity-accounted income will suffer. Thus, associate stakes do not represent risk-free cash flows.

Post-Separation Tax Structure

6.
Targeted stability: Sanofi expects its 2026 full-year effective tax rate to remain stable at approximately 21%. This tax rate reflects the non-deductibility of certain pipeline-related intangible asset impairment losses. Morgan Stanley projected on March 31, 2026, that Sanofi's core tax rate will decline from 22.3% in 2026 to 20.2% in 2027. This projected reduction follows pipeline decision adjustments and the deconsolidation of the consumer business.
7.

This sensitivity makes Sanofi's reported earnings highly dependent on macroeconomic shifts and transatlantic monetary policies.
11.
Active policies: Sanofi manages its operating foreign exchange risk by executing regular assessments of its worldwide currency exposures. As of June 30, 2026, Sanofi had forward currency sales with a total notional amount of EUR 16,423m, including EUR 13,361m in US dollars. These derivative contracts mainly hedge material cash flows arising after the reporting period with a maximum expiry of 2027. This hedging horizon helps smooth short-term transaction volatility but does not protect against long-term translation headwinds.
12.
Unhedged exposure: Roche Holding AG reports in Swiss francs but adopts a foreign exchange strategy that focuses strictly on hedging transaction exposures. This policy exposes Roche's reported sales and core operating profits directly to the appreciation of the Swiss franc. For instance, currency headwinds had an adverse net impact of 8 percentage points on Roche's sales in the first half of 2026.
13.
Structural mismatches: Novartis AG experiences a structural currency mismatch because its expenditures in Swiss francs are significantly higher than its Swiss franc revenues. Novartis reports in US dollars, meaning Swiss franc appreciation increases its local operating expenses and dilutes core margins. In fiscal 2025, operating expenses in Swiss francs represented 18% of the company's total expenses, while Swiss franc sales were only 1%. This operational mismatch demonstrates how currency volatility can distort underlying financial performance across the European pharmaceutical sector.
14.
Adverse translation: Roche's sensitivity analysis shows that a 1% change in exchange rates against the Swiss franc impacts US dollar sales by CHF 148m. This same 1% shift impacts Roche's core operating profit by CHF 52m, illustrating the leverage of unhedged translation exposure. Because Sanofi utilizes active hedging programs, its short-term translation risk is more insulated than Roche's. However, persistent euro strength against the US dollar remains a structural headwind for Sanofi's translated US product sales.
