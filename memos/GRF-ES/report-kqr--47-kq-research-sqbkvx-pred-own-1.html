Post-Refinancing Capital Structure Overview

1.
Secured credit refinancing: Grifols completed a comprehensive refinancing of its near-term credit agreements on April 14, 2026, establishing a new Credit Agreement totaling approximately USD 5.300 billion. The transaction successfully retired all outstanding senior secured facilities that were originally scheduled to mature in November 2027. These proactive capital actions postponed the company's next significant maturities to the fourth quarter of 2028, substantially lowering near-term solvency risks and stabilizing forward earnings per share.
2.
Maturity runway extension: The primary focus of the April 2026 refinancing was the absolute elimination of the EUR 740 million senior secured notes and bank tranches due in 2027. By executing this transaction, Grifols has pushed out the maturity profile of its senior bank debt by seven years, securing a final maturity date of April 14, 2033, for the Term Loan B tranches. Consequently, the group is free from material refinancing pressure over the next two years, allowing management to focus on operational margin execution. This extended runway directly supports the potential expansion of earnings per share by insulating the company from immediate high-cost high-yield debt issuance. Debt optimization measures: Grifols followed up its syndicated bank refinancing in May 2026 by completing an early partial redemption of EUR 500 million of its 7.5% senior secured corporate notes due in 2030. Section 3 – Interest Rate Exposure and Hedging Mix

  2. The group currently maintains no interest rate derivatives to hedge its variable-rate Term Loan B exposure.

Fixed and Floating Debt Mix

1.
Elevated floating exposure: Grifols increased its sensitivity to capital market fluctuations following the major refinancing completed in April 2026. The nominal value of variable-rate senior bank debt rose to EUR 3,002 million in the third quarter of 2026. This shift means that floating-rate instruments now represent more than thirtyfive percent of the group's total outstanding funded debt.
2.
Reduced fixed proportion: The fixed-rate portion of Grifols' capital structure declined relative to historical levels after the refinancing process was finalized. At the close of fiscal year 2025, more than 73% of the group's total debt was referenced to fixed interest rates to limit rate volatility. Shifting a larger share of capital into variable-rate term loans reverses this conservative debt mixture and increases the cash interest burden.
3.
Historical variable baseline: Grifols has historically carried a significant baseline of floating-rate liabilities within its capital structure. For instance, in December 2024, variable-rate debt represented 29% of the total outstanding debt principal. The subsequent increase to over thirty-five percent post-refinancing highlights that the group is now more vulnerable to interest rate cycles than in prior years.

Lease liabilities buffer: The consolidated balance sheet includes substantial long-term lease liabilities that act as a fixed financial element. Lease liabilities recognized under IFRS 16 stood at EUR 1,045 million as of June 30, 2026. Derivative Portfolios and Hedging

5.
Missing rate hedges: Grifols has not established any active derivative agreements to protect its newly issued floating-rate credit facilities. The company's H1 2026 interim report reveals that derivative portfolios only consist of foreign exchange forward contracts and energy power purchase agreements. Consequently, the entire EUR 3,002 million in Senior Debt Tranche B remains fully exposed to central bank rate shifts without swap protection.
6.
Historical swap structures: The group previously utilized cross-currency swap agreements to manage its long- term foreign exchange and interest expenses. In October 2021, the company entered into three fixed-for-fixed cross-currency swaps totaling USD 500 million to hedge its dollar unsecured notes into euros. These historical agreements demonstrate that management understands hedging operations but has chosen not to apply them to its new term loans.
7.
General derivative risks: Derivative transactions designed to mitigate macroeconomic rate shocks carry inherent counterparty and index fixing mismatch risks. Broker analysis from Deutsche Bank in November 2025 noted that index fixings may lag or mis-measure the actual movement in underlying interest rate variables. Consequently, even if Grifols utilizes interest rate swaps in the future, these instruments introduce complex execution risks to the capital structure.
8.
Unhedged credit facilities: The newly restructured revolving credit facility remains exposed to volatile interest rates when drawn. Grifols had outstanding draws of EUR 220 million under this facility during the third quarter of 2026. Because this facility is priced over SOFR plus 200 basis points without interest rate caps, any surge in base rates will immediately increase the group's cash interest outflows.

9.

Historical sensitivity analyses performed by the company indicate that a 100 basis point change in interest rates impacts net profit by millions of euros annually. Elevated rate drag: The high base of Euribor and SOFR rates combined with wider margins creates a significant annual interest expense drag. The Euro Term Loan B is priced at Euribor plus 300 basis points, while the U.S. Dollar tranche is priced at SOFR plus 250 basis points. Servicing these variable tranches limits the flowthrough of operational growth to net income and suppresses forward earnings per share.

High central bank interest rates represent a key hurdle that limits the benefits of Grifols' operational deleveraging.
