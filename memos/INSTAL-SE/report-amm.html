APM Mini-Memo: Instalco

Written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, 1 October 2026. Ideas Bench rank 7 in the lists drawn from the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection. Evidence: the Investment Analyst's Triaging memo (written on July data, published 3 August 2026), the research digest (second-quarter 2026 figures), the ratings store (script readings of 29 and 30 September 2026) and FactSet price and valuation of 30 September 2026.

The one-line view. Neutral, medium conviction: a founder-led margin turnaround that is real in the profit and loss account but not yet in the cash, priced at 13.1 times forward earnings with the shares 3% off their high, so the modal return looks short of 25% a year.

What the company does

Instalco is a Nordic technical installation group, a federation of over 150 local subsidiaries that keep their own brands and full profit responsibility under a tiny central team, across electrical, heating and plumbing, ventilation and industrial work. Sweden is 71% of net sales. Revenue in 2025 was 13,598 million Swedish kronor, third in the Nordics behind Bravida and Assemblin Caverion, in a market where the top three hold only 23%. About 35% of sales is service, about 43% fixed-price projects; the market value is 905.2 million euros.

A plausible investment thesis

Bullish on the mechanism. Founder Per Sjöstrand returned as chief executive on 1 August 2025 with a "margin, margin and margin" mandate and a country-based structure, Instalco 2.0. The second quarter of 2026 delivered organic growth of 8.4% after six quarters of contraction, a margin of 7.1% (operating profit before amortisation, against 5.9% for 2025 and Bravida's 5.4%), and backlog up 17.0% to 10,937 million kronor. The Investment Analyst rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength B on 3 August, mapped and not re-judged; the script's Sell-side Momentum reading of 29 September is A, after DNB Carnegie raised its target to 47 kronor from 42.

The mechanism to earnings per share is margin. On the digest's sensitivity, each 50 basis points of margin moves adjusted earnings per share by about 8.9%, so closing the gap from 7.1% to the 8% target is worth roughly 16% (my arithmetic). DNB Carnegie's base case has adjusted earnings per share of 2.47 kronor in 2026 and 3.00 in 2027, and FactSet's 24-month forward figure is 3.28. Fabri AG in Germany, 51% owned and consolidated from 1 August 2026, adds sales at a 12.7% margin and mid-single-digit accretion to 2027 profit.

The drivers to track are the Swedish margin (7.0% in the second quarter), cash conversion above 100%, leverage falling from 2.6 times to the 2.5 times target, and Fabri's financing cost.

The Target Setup

The Investment Analyst's memo named change-from-current clearing in a high-quality compounder as its primary setup and corporate-change-driven earnings step-up as secondary; Setups' Fit/Crispness carries its B, mapped and not re-judged. Translated into the six Case Setups, my read is that the Target Setup is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU': a founder's return, a reorganisation, a named margin programme and a German platform are corporate change, and they are what moves earnings.

The clearing setups fail on quality: a 9.6% return on capital employed, goodwill at 145% of equity and late-cyclical demand do not make a high-quality, low-cyclicality business. 'Delivering - Serial Acquirer - Mechanical EPS Upgrader' does not fit today either: acquired growth was 1.1% in the second quarter against 12.0% in late 2023, and balance-sheet capacity is the binding constraint.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The Investment Analyst's memo of 3 August rated Case Riskiness C. I agree this is not a simple case: three internal changes run at once, the German majority step is financed on credit lines, and the digest judges the joint probability of every driver executing over 18 months as low. The tripwires are clear: leverage above 3.0 times including earn-outs, or a Swedish margin below 6.0%.

Business quality

Mixed. The Investment Analyst rated Foundations' Robustness B on 3 August, mapped and not re-judged, though that memo graded the robust-base question C. The moat is local relationships: repeat customers are 80% of revenue, and margins ran above 8% from 2017 until the contraction, against about 5.5% for peers. Against that sit leverage of 2.6 times against a peer average of 1.1, and a 2025 that missed every target, with a margin of 5.9% against 8%. Cash conversion was 108.1% in 2025 and the founder owns 8.56%.

Plausible returns and valuation

Fair, not cheap. At 42.9 on 30 September, FactSet puts the shares on 13.1 times 24-month forward earnings, the 46th percentile of their history, and the script's TSR/Valuation reading of 30 September is D, a valuation reading only with no Modal return. DNB Carnegie's target is about 10% above the price, and the market has noticed: relative strength of 92 against 55 for the sector, and the script's Technical Momentum reading of 30 September at B. The rerating option is the discount to European peers, which closes only if 8% margins arrive with cash.

My rough modal read: earnings per share growing toward 3.00 kronor in 2027 on a flat multiple gives low to mid teens a year, well short of 25%. More needs 8% margins by late 2026; the bear case of zero growth, 6.5% margins and 9 times enterprise value to operating profit would cost a great deal.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The pros are visible demand: the European Union renovation directive, public work such as a prison partnership in Skövde and a hospital contract in Oslo, a Norwegian backlog up 40.9%, and service rising from 30% of sales. Fabri opens a German market five times the size of the Nordics.

The cons sit in the value chain. Tenders frequently draw 15 bidders against a historical 5, 43% of sales is fixed-price work exposed to wage and material inflation, and the memo graded suitability for oil-price-induced inflation D. The script's Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C, environment only and provisional, and the Portfolio Fitness for Fighting reading of 56.9 reflects the Fragility axis: a mid-cap serial acquirer is what this regime punishes first.

The strongest counter-case

The digest's own competing-hypotheses matrix picks the false friend profile, with one hard inconsistency against five for the thesis holding. Second-quarter operating cash flow fell 48.5% to 104 million kronor while backlog grew 17%, a 75 million kronor receivables impairment came in the first quarter, and the fall in leverage from 3.3 times came mainly from releasing working capital rather than profit growth. Financing absorbs 14% to 20% of operating profit, Fabri adds debt and Instalco has issued shares for deals before. If inflation returns, a fixed-price backlog struck in a crowded bidding market locks in thin margins with the shares near their high.
