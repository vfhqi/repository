Section 2 – The 2027 Household G Tariff Application and Regulatory Approval

Section summary
  1. Regulated G-tariff pricing remains the primary source of retail earnings volatility for PGE and its domestic peers.
  2. Polish utilities have submitted their 2027 tariff proposals under heavy confidentiality, and URE's regulatory approval will determine if onerous provisions can be reversed.

PGE Tariff Submission and Pricing

1.
Uncertain outlook: PGE Obrót recently submitted its household G-tariff application for the year 2027 to the Polish Energy Regulatory Office. During the Q2 2026 earnings conference call, the management board explicitly declined to comment on the specific applied rate due to ongoing negotiations with the regulator. This commercial silence indicates that regulatory pricing remains highly dynamic, which ultimately prevents analysts from making reliable early forecasts on retail supply margins.
2.
Severe margin pressure: The upcoming tariff is negotiated against the approved 2026 G-tariff average of PLN 495.16/MWh, which excludes value-added and excise taxes. PGE Obrót previously reported that this level was insufficient to cover its actual procurement and balancing costs, leading to an identified potential loss of PLN 560 million. Consequently, the supply segment's future profitability relies entirely on whether URE approves full cost coverage in the upcoming 2027 regulatory cycle.
3.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that retail tariff fluctuations have a marginal impact on the utility's long- term valuation. Bernstein Research reported that PGE's strategic focus is on offshore wind and regulated distribution networks, which will generate most of its cash. This shift suggests that retail segment volatility is minor compared to capital-intensive renewable assets.
4.
Onerous liabilities: The company's prior tariff deficit forced it to establish a major balance sheet provision for onerous supply contracts. This substantial accounting recognition directly hit consolidated net income and degraded the segment's operating performance for the fiscal year 2025. Management expects similar provision risks if approved tariffs lag behind the actual procurement costs of electricity.

Section 3 – Government Price Caps and Compensation Schemes for 2027

Section summary
  1. Polish authorities are actively drafting household energy shielding measures for 2027 to mitigate the impact of escalating wholesale procurement costs on end consumers.
  2. Proposed gas sector interventions combine a maximum price cap with a targeted ten percent revenue levy on dominant traders to fund supplier compensation schemes.

Electricity Price Cap Proposals

1.
Pending intervention: Polish authorities are planning to introduce protective household electricity price caps for the first half of 2027 to limit retail pricing shocks. Morgan Stanley reported on October 29, 2026, that the government is highly motivated to protect consumer budgets. Upcoming national elections are driving this administrative willingness to suppress retail electricity tariffs. This state intervention will compress retail revenues unless the government introduces fully offsetting utility compensation payments to shield suppliers.
2.
Growing costs: The state budget faces severe fiscal pressure from the projected funding requirements of the 2027 household energy shielding program. Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 6, 2026, that maintaining these retail caps under rising international fuel prices could exceed the government's current budget estimate of 0.5% of GDP. This rising cost could force the state to seek alternative revenue sources or adjust the compensation scale. Consequently, PGE's future cash flows depend heavily on whether the state maintains sufficient fiscal capacity for compensation throughout 2027.

Gas Cap and Trader Levy

5.
Draft legislation: The Polish Ministry of Energy has prepared a draft bill to guarantee maximum gas prices for protected household consumers throughout 2027. The ministry announced on November 6, 2026, that the draft caps retail natural gas tariffs based on the 2026 tariff approved by Poland's regulator URE. This measure is intended to ensure cost predictability for households and public utilities. For diversified utilities, this statutory cap eliminates uncontrolled retail price escalation but requires a robust compensation mechanism to cover resulting supply losses.
6.
Taxed revenues: The proposed gas compensation mechanism will be financed through a new windfall levy on dominant domestic natural gas trading companies. The Ministry of Energy disclosed on November 6, 2026, that the levy will equal 10% of liable companies' net revenues. This design ensures that the financial burden of shielding households is not funded solely by the state budget. However, this levy directly reduces the consolidated net income of large traders while funding competitor compensation claims in 2027.
7.
Regulatory exemptions: The upcoming 2027 gas levy specifically targets only the largest energy players to protect smaller market participants. The Ministry of Energy stated on November 6, 2026, that gas companies holding less than 10% of total entry-point capacity rights are fully exempt. This design concentrates the tax burden on major state-controlled entities like Orlen while sparing private retail competitors. Consequently, this tax asymmetry shifts market share dynamics in Poland by penalizing the dominant state-owned utility.
8.
Comparable precedent: Poland's historical approach to funding fuel shielding programs reveals a persistent legislative preference for corporate windfall taxes over state funding. Prime Minister Donald Tusk defended a similar corporate energy profit tax bill on July 26, 2026, which aimed to raise PLN 4 billion from corporate profits. This historical bill was structured to collect PLN 3.8 billion in 2026 with the remainder in 2027. This precedent suggests that Polish policymakers will continue utilizing corporate levies to fund consumer subsidies during periods of high wholesale volatility.

This regulatory mechanism prevents domestic utilities from capturing margin windfalls when procurement costs fall. Consequently, PGE can utilize its low-cost domestic generation assets to protect consolidated retail margins even under restrictive price caps. This operational hedge means that consolidated earnings per share remain resilient despite the ongoing parliamentary debate over windfall levies.

  3. Consolidated peer groups utilized diversified asset bases and targeted corporate windfall levies to absorb these regulatory shielding costs without permanent damage to earnings per share.
