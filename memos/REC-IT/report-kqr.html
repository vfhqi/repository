Section 4 – Ownership Structures and Governance Barriers to M&A

Section summary
  1. Restrictive ownership architectures across European mid-cap drugmakers represent formidable barriers to unsolicited takeovers despite highly attractive public market valuations.
  2. High-vote share classes, foundation structures, and family alliances effectively control the voting registers of key targets, forcing bidders to negotiate bilateral deals.
  3. Listed entities like Swedish Orphan Biovitrum and Camurus present the lowest absolute governance hurdles, though active anchor shareholders still command major blocking stakes.

Foundation and Family Controls

1.
Foundation voting blocks: Multi-class share structures and dedicated foundations create robust barriers against hostile bids at ALK-Abelló AS and H. Lundbeck AS. Shareholder disclosures for ALK-Abelló AS as of 31 December 2025 show the Lundbeck Foundation controls 67.20% of the total voting rights through its holding of high-vote A and AA shares carrying ten votes each, compared to one vote for B shares. This absolute voting majority means the Lundbeck Foundation completely controls ALK-Abelló's corporate destiny. Similarly, Lundbeck's corporate governance disclosures from February 2026 show the Lundbeck Foundation holds 69% of the total share capital and approximately 77% of the voting rights in H. Lundbeck AS. This concentration of voting power was further consolidated through a voluntary 1:1 exchange offer of A-shares for B-shares completed in September 2026, which left the Foundation holding approximately 92% of the A-shares and 63% of the B-shares, representing approximately 84% of total voting rights. Consequently, unsolicited takeover attempts at either ALK-Abelló or Lundbeck are impossible without the explicit consent and strategic alignment of the Lundbeck Foundation.
2.
Family concerted actions: Concentrated family ownership and formal voting pacts lock up the voting registers of Almirall SA and Ipsen SA, preventing outside corporate raiders from securing control. Almirall's 2025 annual management report shows that the Gallardo family, acting through Grupo Plafin, S.A.U. and Grupo Corporativo Landon, S.L., holds a combined 59.88% of the share capital and voting rights. This dominant position is regulated by a long-standing concerted action agreement signed by Antonio Gallardo Ballart and Jorge Gallardo Ballart on 28 May 2007. For Ipsen SA, family control is similarly locked, with the founding Beaufour family holding 52% of the equity. French bylaws grant double voting rights to registered shares held for at least two years, giving family vehicles Beech Tree and Highrock a combined 66.10% of gross voting rights as of 31 December 2025. These voting structures mean strategic buyers cannot bypass family-controlled boards to execute take-private transactions.

Voting Caps and Holding Vehicles

3.
Drastic voting caps: Double voting rights and stringent voting restrictions in company bylaws represent major defensive walls for Orion Oyj and UCB SA. Orion's Articles of Association dictate that each Class A share carries twenty votes and each Class B share carries one vote. Crucially, Orion's bylaws impose a voting cap stating that no shareholder can cast more than 1/20 (5%) of the aggregate votes represented at a General Meeting. This voting cap neutralizes any hostile accumulation of shares because a bidder's voting influence remains capped regardless of their economic stake. For UCB SA, ownership is anchored by Financière de Tubize SA, which held 36.28% of UCB's voting rights as of 30 June 2026. Financière de Tubize is itself a listed holding company controlled by a tight family concert (including FEJ SRL and Barnfin SA) holding 53.49% of its voting rights to ensure the long-term stability of UCB. Additionally, UCB's Article 38 restricts voting power by preventing shareholders from voting shares that have not been declared under transparency thresholds at least twenty days prior to a general meeting.
4.
Anchor institutional blocks: Listed mid-caps Swedish Orphan Biovitrum AB (Sobi) and Camurus AB lack multi- class voting structures and defensive voting caps, but still feature highly concentrated anchor shareholders. In Swedish Orphan Biovitrum, Swedish investment vehicle Investor AB stands as the largest single shareholder, holding 34.40% of the share capital and voting rights as of 31 December 2025. Sobi's Articles of Association do not contain any restrictions on voting rights, meaning Sobi remains technically open to bids. Similarly, Camurus AB's share register as of 30 September 2026 shows Sandberg Development AB as the dominant shareholder with 30.20% of capital and votes. Camurus's bylaws do allow for high-vote Class C shares (carrying 1/10 of a vote), but these are temporarily issued only to secure employee incentive programs and are converted back into common shares. While these single-class share structures make Sobi and Camurus the most eligible targets, a buyer must still negotiate directly with Investor AB or Sandberg Development AB to secure a control premium.
