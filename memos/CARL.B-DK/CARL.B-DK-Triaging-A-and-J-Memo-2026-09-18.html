[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Carlsberg is a deleveraging and re-rating case in a low-growth consumer staple, with a self-help component from the Britvic synergies and no demand inflection. At DKK 840.6, or 11.3x earnings two years forward, the shares pay for a 3.7 percent revenue grower with 3.0x leverage and a China problem; they do not yet pay for the buyback that a dated deleveraging path makes probable within about eighteen months, and that buyback is what the return depends on.

  • The change is internal, broad and real, and the external forces subtract from it.
    • Since September 2023 the company has changed its chief executive and finance director, its strategy (Accelerate SAIL, February 2024), its shape (Britvic for DKK 36.4 billion, completed 17 January 2025; a quarter of Southeast Asia sold to Sapporo at 21.3x 2025 EBIT), its capital structure (the EUR 1.8 billion hybrid of May 2026) and its incentives (pay rewired to absolute revenue growth).
    • Nine of the framework's twelve internal forces are under way and at least five are strong.
    • Against that, five of the seven external forces are headwinds: beer demand in Western Europe (-3.9 percent in H1 2026) and China (-6 percent in Q2 2026), mainstream price competition in the UK and Poland, a dense tax and packaging roadmap, input inflation of about 5.4 percent in 2027, and a discretionary squeeze in China and Europe.
    • So the input change is strong where the company decides and weak where the market decides, which is why 'Change Forces' is B and not the A its internal half would earn alone.
  • The change has reached the outputs the company controls and not the ones the market wants.
    • Organic operating profit grew 5.9 percent in H1 2026 against consensus of 4.6 percent
    • the operating margin rose 30 basis points to 15.8 percent
    • free operating cash flow rose from DKK 2,918 million to DKK 3,694 million
    • leverage fell from 3.9x to 3.0x in twelve months on the company's definition.
    • Volume and revenue have not followed: organic volume +1.7 percent (of which 1 to 1.5 points is the Kazakhstan licence takeover that laps out in 2027) and organic revenue +2.7 percent against a 4 to 6 percent algorithm
      • price and mix only +1.0 percent.
    • The sell-side's estimates have not moved: FY2026 consensus is up 0.8 percent over six months, FY2027 is down 2.0 percent and FY2028 is down 1.2 percent, with a 5.0 percent cut to FY2028 over twelve months.
    • Along the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain the change has therefore travelled from the company's resources through its choices and actions into its key operating metrics on profit, cash and leverage, and into its financial statements on operating profit and cash flow
      • it has not reached the operating metrics on volume and price, and it has not reached the sell-side's estimates.
  • What it earns is about 9 percent a year, which is below the framework's band.
    • Our modal adjusted earnings per share run from the company's DKK 60.81 for FY2025 to about DKK 65 for FY2026, DKK 70.7 for FY2027, DKK 77.3 for FY2028 and DKK 84.4 for FY2029, a rate of 9.2 percent a year over FY2026 to FY2029
    • consensus runs 64.78, 69.07, 76.21 and 83.42, or 8.8 percent a year.
    • The parts of that growth are revenue 3.7 points, margin 1.9 points, the lines below EBITDA 2.4 points (lower interest as debt falls, a tax rate near 23 percent) and the share count 1.0 point once buybacks resume
      • a little over half of it is the revenue and margin growth investors extrapolate, the rest is financial help of a benign kind.
    • The 12 to 20 percent band is missed at every horizon: about 6.5 percent over six months, 9.2 percent over eighteen months, 9 to 10 percent over three years.
      • Improving against the last three years (about 5.5 percent a year from 2023 to 2025), but not inside the band.
  • What it costs is nothing on the valuation, which is the strongest single support. 11.33x earnings 24 months forward is the 10th percentile of the stock's own five years (range 9.95x to 18.83x, median 13.90x) and the 6th of ten
    • the six-peer median is 13.25x, with AB InBev at 14.8x and Heineken at 11.7x.
    • The cheapness is partly the sector's: Heineken sits at its 2nd percentile, Royal Unibrew and AG Barr at their 0th, so a company-specific catalyst lifts Carlsberg within a cluster that can itself fall.
    • The consensus share count is flat through FY2028 and the median target of DKK 1,050 is 25 percent above the price and not believed
      • the buyback is not in the price, the volume worry is.
  • The return clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months only with a re-rating, and does not clear it over three years.
    • We value the shares at each horizon on the earnings two years beyond it: FY2029 earnings of DKK 84.4 at eighteen months, a blend of FY2030 and FY2031 of about DKK 98 at three years (an extrapolation of the same 9.2 percent rate, flagged as such).
    • The multiple we apply is a blend of two branches: 13.5x if a buyback has been announced by the FY2027 results (anchored below the five-year median of 13.90x because the growth profile is the slowest of the brewers), at odds of about 65 in 100
    • 11.5x if not (between today's 11.3x and the two-year median of 11.96x), at 35 in 100
    • the blend is 12.8x.
    • Eighteen months: value DKK 1,080 plus dividends of DKK 65 (31.24 in March 2027, 33.66 in March 2028) on DKK 840.6 is 36.2 percent in total, 22.9 percent a year.
      • The parts multiply: earnings growth on the 24-month-forward figure +13.8 percent (74.1 to 84.4), re-rating +12.9 percent (11.33x to 12.8x), cash return 7.7 percent added at the end.
    • Three years: value DKK 1,254 plus dividends of DKK 102 is 61.3 percent in total, 17.3 percent a year, at the top of the framework's C band.
    • Without the re-rating the same path gives 13.9 percent a year over eighteen months and 13.0 percent over three years, both C.
      • On the one-year-forward footing (about 12.4x today on earnings of about DKK 67.9; 12.4x held on about DKK 79.1 at eighteen months) the return is 15.7 percent a year
      • the convention adds about 7 points a year because it applies the multiple to a year two years further out on a 9 percent growth path and lets the multiple rise.
      • The one-year footing with no re-rating is the honest floor of the case.
    • The re-rating is the swing factor and it is a balance-sheet event, not a volume event: the eighteen-month bar is cleared if and only if the buyback is announced, because the cleared branch clears it even on a worse earnings path (20.1 percent a year at 13.5x with earnings growing 6 percent) and the uncleared branch does not clear it even on the modal path (15.0 percent a year at 11.5x).
  • The earnings path is exposed to the lines below EBITDA, and the plausibly worse case takes the case down a rating.
    • If the Sapporo minority takes about a quarter of the Southeast Asia joint venture's EBIT from 2027 (about USD 30 million a year on the price paid), the hybrid coupon of about DKK 475 million a year sits below net income, tax stays at the reported 24.7 percent rather than 23 percent, depreciation rises with capital expenditure at the top of DKK 6 to 7 billion and buybacks slip to 2028, the 2.4 points of below-EBITDA help and the 1.0 point of share count disappear and earnings per share grow at about 6 percent a year (3.7 percent revenue plus 1.8 points of margin) instead of 9.2.
    • On that path FY2029 earnings are about DKK 77.2 and the three-year blend about DKK 85
      • at 12.8x the eighteen-month return is 16.2 percent a year and the three-year 12.4 percent
      • at the uncleared 11.5x, where a disappointing earnings path would more likely leave the multiple, the eighteen-month return is 8.7 percent a year, an F.
    • That sensitivity would take the 'Modal return' to D and this Building Block to D.
      • One of its parts (the minority) is certain
      • the rest is what happens if every below-the-line item goes wrong at once, so we hold it as the adverse case and not the modal one.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', crisp on mechanism and half-formed on outcome.
    • Every input of the pattern is present and dated
    • the first outputs (profit, margin, cash, leverage) have arrived on schedule
    • the output in the pattern's name, earnings upgrades, has not begun.
    • The chain holds from inputs to the profit and cash lines
    • it breaks at volume and at the estimates, and the return depends on the market paying a higher multiple before those two links form.

  • The case is therefore a good company at a low price with a dated catalyst, not a compelling one.

    • It is a C thesis on a D case structure: the modal eighteen-month return is a B only through a probable re-rating, the three-year return is a C, and the earnings growth is below the band at every horizon.

  • Rating = C, because the input change is material (E 1 rated B, the internal forces A) but the outputs it drives are only moderately improving (E 2 rated C, earnings-per-share growth about 9 percent a year against a 12 to 20 percent band) and the return they produce clears 20 percent a year over eighteen months only with a re-rating and falls to about 17 percent a year over three years (E 3 rated C)

    • materiality is what this Building Block measures, and a change that produces 9 percent earnings growth and a partial re-rating is a moderately material change.
    • The Elements bind rather than carry: strong inputs do not make a thesis material if the outputs are middling, the outputs are the binding constraint, and the return follows the outputs.
    • Not B, because B would need either the earnings growth inside the band or the return inside the B band at both horizons, and neither holds.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The change is real but is a defensive reshaping of a low-growth staple, not a growth inflection: the multi-beverage pivot lowers gross margin (45.2 percent in 2025 from 45.8 percent) and returns (return on invested capital 10.1 percent in 2025 from 13.8 percent), the 4 to 6 percent revenue algorithm is unreachable on a footprint whose embedded beer volume growth is 0.3 percent a year, and the 11.3x multiple is the fair price of a 3 to 4 percent grower with 3.0x leverage, so there is no re-rating to earn.
  • Our response: we concede the growth point in full, which is why our path uses 3.7 percent revenue growth and not 4 to 6 percent, and we concede the return-on-capital point.
  • We do not concede the multiple, because the two-year median of 11.96x and the five-year median of 13.90x were both earned by this same business at lower growth expectations than the market now holds, and the deleveraging that removes the leverage overlay is measurable and dated (3.9x to 3.0x in twelve months; the Sapporo cash of USD 643 million by end-2026; a target below 2.5x by end-2027 that the brokers expect earlier).
  • Where the hypothesis would beat us is if China declines structurally at the same time as a 2027 cost squeeze that pricing does not cover
    • that would slow deleveraging, delay the buyback and leave the shares at 11.5x on a lower earnings base, which is the F outcome in the sensitivity above.
  • We put that combination at possible, not probable.

Internal forces: are there sufficiently strong internal change forces under way? (RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because the change is under way on nine of the twelve forces and is strong on at least five (chief executive, strategy, acquisitions, standards, new products), which passes the screen's rule of three strong forces or a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase twice over.
  • False-positive check: the chief executive is not a caretaker
    • the transformation is not a slide deck (Britvic completed 17 January 2025, synergies at about 50 percent in year one)
    • the guidance increase is not trivial or one-off (a record of beats since 2016)
    • no crown jewel was sold (Russia was seized and then sold; Southeast Asia was part-sold at 21.3x EBIT)
    • no empire-building beyond one large deal.
  • Leverage flag: the one screen flag that fits is dangerous, debt-funded leverage (3.28x at end-2025)
    • it is being unwound (3.0x at H1 2026) and is the case's central risk rather than a disqualifier.
  • Not B, because the screen's decision rule is met twice and the one flag that fits is reversing at pace
    • B would be right if the forces were three years old and fading, and instead new ones were added in 2026 (Sapporo, Azerbaijan, the hybrid, the India prospectus).

External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because five of the seven forces read as headwinds (demand in beer, competition, regulation, input costs into 2027, macro in China and Western Europe) and the tailwinds (soft drinks and alcohol-free demand, weather comparatives in 2027) are narrower than the headwinds.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because end-market demand for beer, two-thirds of the company's 148.0 million hectolitres (FY2025), is structurally flat to declining, and the growing categories are the smaller third.
  • Beer: Western Europe beer volumes fell 3.9 percent organically in H1 2026 while soft drinks there grew 3.2 percent.
    • Global beer volumes are forecast at a 0.2 percent compound rate to 2030
    • Jefferies puts Carlsberg's own footprint at a 0.3 percent embedded beer volume rate to 2030, the slowest of the major brewers, with 1.6 million hectolitres of incremental volume against Heineken's 12.2 million and AB InBev's 9.8 million (13 September 2026).
  • China: volumes fell 6 percent in Q2 2026 against buy-side hopes of about -5 percent
    • management says the Chinese market is unlikely to grow in 2026 and expects weakness and de-stocking into Q3.
    • China is about 30 percent of group beer volumes, so a 100 basis point regional swing is about 30 basis points at group level.
  • Growth categories: soft drinks +9 percent organically in H1 2026 (+4 percent excluding Kazakhstan), alcohol-free brews +11 percent
    • the three growth categories (soft drinks, premium beer, alcohol-free) are 51 percent of group volume.
    • A tailwind from a smaller base and at lower margin.
  • Direction: a headwind on the larger part of the business, a tailwind on the smaller
    • the framework's 'Demand Inflection' test (sector-level, structural or two-plus years' visibility) is not met on the tailwind side.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening in three of Carlsberg's important markets at once and the company's response is to concede mainstream share.
  • United Kingdom: Heineken cut the alcohol content of Foster's and recycled the duty saving into price cuts
    • Molson Coors followed on Carling
    • Carlsberg Danish Pilsner lost on-trade share and management chose profit over volume.
  • Poland: Asahi discounted mainstream lager aggressively into a market disrupted by the Deposit Return System
    • Carlsberg's Polish volumes fell low double digits in H1 2026, with share lost to local discounting.
  • China: Heineken's licensed volumes grew close to 30 percent in H1 2026 through China Resources Beer while Carlsberg's fell 6 percent in Q2
    • Heineken Silver grew nearly 30 percent in 2025.
  • Direction: headwind.
    • Not F, because in soft drinks the joint Carlsberg-Britvic UK offer has won share and new on-trade contracts, and in Vietnam the earlier Sabeco-Heineken price war has abated with volumes up more than 20 percent in H1 2026.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because a dense sequence of tax and packaging rules is moving cost and pricing against the company in both categories, and the only offset, a barrier-to-entry benefit for incumbents, is asserted rather than shown.
  • Vietnam: the Special Consumption Tax roadmap raises beer duty from 65 percent to 90 to 100 percent by 2030 (the earlier research had 70 percent in 2027 and 90 percent by 2031)
    • Decree 90 tightens penalties further

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Carlsberg is a deleveraging and re-rating case in a low-growth consumer staple, with a self-help component from the Britvic synergies and a margin rebuild, and no demand inflection.
  • The company changed its leadership in 2023, its strategy in 2024 and its shape in 2025, buying Britvic for DKK 36.4 billion with debt, becoming PepsiCo's European anchor bottler and selling a quarter of Southeast Asia to Sapporo at 21.3x EBIT.
  • The change has travelled through the company's resources, choices and actions into the operating metrics it controls: organic operating profit +5.9 percent in H1 2026 against consensus of +4.6 percent, the operating margin +30 basis points to 15.8 percent, free operating cash flow up from DKK 2.9 billion to DKK 3.7 billion, leverage down from 3.9x to 3.0x in twelve months.
  • It has not travelled into volume or revenue (organic +1.7 percent and +2.7 percent against a 4 to 6 percent algorithm, with China -6 percent in Q2 2026), and not into the sell-side's estimates, which are flat for FY2026 and down 1 to 5 percent for FY2027 and FY2028 over twelve months.
  • The shares trade at 11.3x earnings 24 months forward, the 10th percentile of five years and the 6th of ten, and yield 3.7 percent.
  • On our modal path earnings per share compound at about 9 percent a year, which is what a 3.7 percent revenue grower with two points of margin and two points of financial help delivers, and that is below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band at every horizon.
  • The return clears the framework's 20 percent bar over eighteen months (22.9 percent a year) only if the multiple re-rates from 11.3x to a blended 12.8x as buybacks resume below 2.5x leverage, which is probable at about 65 in 100 and not yet in the price
    • it does not clear the bar over three years (17.3 percent a year), and on a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share it falls to 16.2 percent a year over eighteen months, or 8.7 percent if the buyback does not come.
  • The case is not compelling: it is a good company at a low price with a dated catalyst, and by the framework's tests it is a C thesis on a D case structure.
Setup verdict: The sorting-box verdict: primary 'Target Setup', any secondary, and the mechanism tying Thesis and Foundations to the pattern (Part 2, E 3, BB 4)
  • The primary 'Target Setup' is 'Major Corporate Change', with a B for crispness: every input of the pattern is present and dated (a new chief executive and finance director, a new strategy with numbers, a transformational acquisition, disposals, licences, a rewired incentive scheme), the first outputs have arrived on schedule (organic operating profit, margin, cash conversion, leverage) and the last output in the pattern's name, earnings upgrades, has not.

Congruence: The congruence judgement: does the whole hang together across past, business, value chain and market environment; where ratings disagree, why (Part 4, E 3, BB 4)
  • The whole hangs together on one half and not the other, and the ratings disagree along exactly that seam.
  • With the past and the business the thesis is congruent on profit and cash: the company has delivered organic operating profit at or above the top of its initial guidance every year since 2016, through a -2.0 percent volume year in 2025 and a weather shock in 2026, and the modal path asks it to keep doing what it has always done, at 5 to 7 percent a year, while paying down debt with cash it has always generated.
  • With the past and the business the thesis is incongruent on revenue: the 4 to 6 percent algorithm asks for growth the record has not shown (the 2023 to 2025 plan's revenue part vested at 12.10 of 20.00; organic revenue +2.7 percent in H1 2026), which is why our path uses 3.7 percent and why the operator is B and not A.
  • With the value chain the thesis sits with the forces on pricing and procurement (excise passed through within months, private label at 9 percent, an oligopoly that has mostly prioritised margin) and against them on volume (moderation, a shrinking on-trade, mainstream discounting in the UK and Poland, a tax and packaging roadmap)
    • the deleveraging part is indifferent to the value chain and the revenue part is hostage to it.
  • With the stock-market environment the thesis is not congruent: European beverages have de-rated to decade lows as a group, the shares sit below every moving average with relative strength at the 37th percentile, and momentum and valuation disagree with the thesis for an explained reason (growth doubts, the leverage overlay, China), which is the entry price and not a reason to doubt the analysis.
  • The ratings disagree accordingly.
  • The 'Investment Setup' is B because the pattern's mechanism is clear and its first outputs have arrived
    • the 'Investment Thesis' is C because the outputs are moderate and the return needs a re-rating
    • the 'Foundations' are C because the operator carries the profit half through a value chain that is D
    • and the 'Investment Case' is D because a simple mechanism inside a complicated, exposed company is still a complicated, exposed case at the level of the whole.
  • Where the pieces would come into line is on a buyback announcement, which would confirm the mechanism, remove the leverage overlay and give the market a reason to pay the five-year median multiple
    • where they would come apart is a structural China decline together with a 2027 cost squeeze that pricing does not cover, which would slow deleveraging, delay the buyback and leave the shares at 11.5x on a lower earnings base.
