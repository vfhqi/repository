Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Graubuendner Kantonalbank exhibits three statistically exceptional characteristics that position it at the extreme edges of the Swiss regional banking peer set, each carrying distinct structural drivers and strategic implications. The bank's CET1 capital ratio of 20.0% at the end of fiscal 2025 represents a dramatic outlier, significantly surpassing St.Galler Kantonalbank's CET1 quote of 15.8% in the same period, placing GKB comfortably within the top 5% of cantonal banks for regulatory capital strength.

This exceptional capitalization is structurally anchored by the Canton of Graubünden's statutory liability guarantee covering all parent bank liabilities (excluding subsidiaries and certificates), combined with the canton's dominant 84.6% ownership stake. The sovereign backing creates a gravitational pull effect that enables GKB to maintain substantially higher capital buffers than commercially owned peers, as depositor confidence allows the bank to accumulate earnings without facing shareholder pressure for aggressive capital returns.

The 20.0% ratio sits well within GKB's internal strategic target range of 17.5% to 22.5%, suggesting deliberate management policy rather than accidental over-capitalization. This structural advantage provides exceptional shock-absorption capacity but simultaneously signals that GKB operates with materially lower financial leverage than commercial competitors, potentially constraining return on equity relative to more aggressive regional players.
