Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Sell-side expectations for Scandinavian Tobacco Group were set very low ahead of the Q2 2026 earnings due to persistent volume declines in Europe and elevated leverage.
  2. Analysts prioritized the strategic execution of the Focus2030 cost-savings program and the financial details of the DKK 1.3 billion German fine-cut business divestment.
  3. The market sought clear evidence of stabilization in North American handmade cigars and growth in next-generation products to offset structural legacy headwinds.
1.
Stable positioning: Scandinavian Tobacco Group maintains a resilient global footprint within the niche tobacco industry, deliberately avoiding exposure to cigarettes or moist snuff. The company operates as one of the world's largest manufacturers of cigars and traditional pipe tobacco. Its market leadership is anchored by its massive presence in the United States, where it represents the top player in the high-margin handmade cigar segment.

Carnegie noted that while the low valuation and strong cash flows were attractive, the stock was trading near all-time low earnings multiples for good reason.

Deteriorating expectations: The consensus expectations and broker price targets for the tobacco company had been cut dramatically over the preceding year. Carnegie had previously held a buy rating with a target price of DKK 115.0 in late 2024, which was subsequently lowered. Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary

  2. Rapid expansion of next-generation products, led by the XQS nicotine pouch brand, is crucial to offset legacy volume declines.
  3. Balance sheet deleveraging following recent asset sales is expected to pave the way for a resumption of share buybacks in 2027.
  4. Tightening regulatory environments in key European markets continue to pose long-term volume risks for the core cigar business.
1.

The group's leverage ratio stood significantly above the company's long-term target of remaining below 2.5 times. Carnegie analyst Ekman noted on August 27, 2026, that the elevated debt load was a primary concern that restricted financial flexibility and suppressed the stock's valuation. Carnegie analyst Ekman reported on April 20, 2026, that this structural transition coincided with a substantial increase in trademark amortization. The updated accounting framework was expected to drag the adjusted EBIT margin down by approximately 80 basis points. The current multiple was in line with its historical three-year average but represented a steep discount to global tobacco peers. The company possessed weaker pricing power relative to peers like Altria or British American Tobacco. Section summary
  1. Tightening European regulations on digital sales and packaging pose long-term volume risks to Scandinavian Tobacco Group's core legacy categories.
  2. Sizable near-term tax disputes and delayed federal flavor bans in the United States create regional regulatory uncertainty for the company.
1.
Rapid expansion: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has achieved significant market share gains in the modern oral category through its Swedish brand XQS over the last three years. The brand's Swedish volume market share expanded to an all-time high of 13.8% by September 2026, which was higher than the 12.3% recorded in 2025. Ramping up modern oral volumes represents a critical strategic pillar under the company's Focus2030 strategy.
2.
Deficit margins: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces persistent gross margin pressure within its Next Generation Products division because the company currently outsources the production of most of its nicotine pouch business. Deutsche Bank analyst McNeela reported on April 24, 2026, that outsourced manufacturing contributes to a negative product mix impact on Europe Branded margins. Management has under-communicated potential insourcing timelines, which remains a key variable for investors hoping for an outer-year margin recovery.
3.
Capital-efficient rollout: Scandinavian Tobacco Group is executing an innovative digital distribution strategy in the United Kingdom to bypass high entry barriers in physical retail. The company is adopting an online-first launch strategy to test and refine new pouch strengths and flavours via specialized e-commerce platforms like Haypp. Transitioning to a direct online model allows the manufacturer to optimize retail sell-through and build brand equity before investing in costly physical store listings.
4.
Accelerating growth: The United Kingdom nicotine pouch market is experiencing robust structural growth, with retail scan data showing total sales approaching 230 million pounds in 2026. Deutsche Bank analyst McNeela reported on May 26, 2026, that category volumes surged by 55% year-on-year following a 90% expansion in 2024. However, intense competition from 450 active brands has led to aggressive pricing and promotional strategies that threaten long-term profitability.
5.
Weak brand loyalty: Nicotine pouch consumers continue to exhibit very low single-brand loyalty, which alters the dynamic of e-commerce platforms and DTC channels. Deutsche Bank research from November 25, 2026, revealed that fewer than one-third of survey respondents in either the US or UK report using just one brand regularly. High-frequency pouch users typically rotate across four to five different products each week, which benefits multi-brand online platforms like Haypp.
6.
Restrictive barriers: High-frequency pouch consumers continue to purchase products offline due to persistent friction points associated with online ordering. Deutsche Bank research from November 25, 2026, indicated that delivery times and high shipping costs remain primary barriers to online channel adoption for US and UK users. Purchasing friction points limit the near-term growth of direct-to-consumer models, even though online prices are often discounted by 40% to 50% compared to offline retail.
7.
Severe regulatory hurdles: Direct-to-consumer platforms face significant structural complexity in the tobacco and nicotine industry due to highly fragmented municipal laws and excise structures. Expert insights from March 31, 2026, noted that managing age verification and local municipal taxes creates an extremely challenging online distribution environment for manufacturers. Complex regulatory barriers make it difficult for smaller brands to scale their own DTC portals, leaving established physical and third-party digital networks as the dominant channels.
8.
Shifting preferences: Consumer preferences in the United States continue to gravitate toward vapor and modern oral products rather than heated tobacco alternatives. Deutsche Bank research from April 17, 2026, reported that US nicotine users are migrating heavily toward vaping and pouches because these formats offer ultimate convenience and discretion. Heated tobacco platforms like IQOS require a more controlled consumer ritual, which has limited their immediate market penetration relative to other non-combustible alternatives.
9.
Minor youth usage: Regulators in the United States maintain intense scrutiny over modern oral and e-cigarette categories due to concerns regarding youth uptake. Deutsche Bank analyst McNeela reported that the 2025 National Youth Tobacco Survey showed nicotine pouches ranked second as the most used product among youth at a stable 1.7%. Although overall youth tobacco use fell to 7.2% in 2025, continued monitoring of emerging non-combustible categories remains a priority for federal enforcement agencies.
10.
Impending regulatory headwinds: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces a significant long-term threat in Europe from the upcoming Tobacco Products Directive, which is projected to be implemented between 2028 and 2030. DNB Carnegie analyst Ekman reported on November 12, 2026, that this regulation is expected to focus heavily on oral tobacco and online sales restrictions. Such potential marketing and packaging bans could severely disrupt the group's digital retail footprint and pouch expansion plans.
11.
Intense promotional pressure: Major international tobacco peers are deploying aggressive promotional pricing to capture a dominant share of the expanding modern oral category. Morgan Stanley research from October 16, 2026, highlighted a significant step-up in manufacturer-funded promotions including deep discounts on Zyn, on!, and Velo. Ramping up modern oral investments to counter these pricing moves could severely hamper near-term profits and cash flow for Scandinavian Tobacco Group.
12.
Encouraging digital growth: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has leveraged its strong digital presence to deliver consistent net sales growth in its North American online business. The North America Online & Retail division delivered organic net sales growth of 3% during the third quarter of 2026, supported by robust premium cigar demand. Reliable online retail execution highlights the resilience of the group's proprietary e-commerce platforms despite a highly challenging consumer spending backdrop.
13.
Restructured digital infrastructure: Scandinavian Tobacco Group experienced temporary supply chain and operational disruptions due to the multiyear rollout of its standardized ERP system. Deutsche Bank analyst McNeela reported on February 23, 2026, that European machine-rolled cigar sales suffered from supply instability following the SAP rollout in late 2025. Although management has since resolved these initial supply bottlenecks, the transition highlights the operational risks inherent in consolidating the group's global digital architecture.
14.
Segmented market dynamics: The modern oral category is developing a bifurcated competitive structure that separates compliant, premium offerings from low-cost commoditized products. Expert insights from March 5, 2026, indicated that premium modern oral brands maintain strong market defensibility and pricing power because of high technical and regulatory barriers to entry. Conversely, commoditization is expected to occur primarily in the low-end, non-compliant segments of the market, shielding premium operators from severe margin erosion.
15.
Other side of the story: The prevailing bearish narrative regarding the lower profitability of modern oral products could prove overly conservative if Scandinavian Tobacco Group successfully insources pouch production over the next three years. Ramping up domestic manufacturing capabilities at the group's optimized factories would materially improve gross profit margins for the XQS brand. Deutsche Bank analyst McNeela noted that any corporate commentary surrounding potential pouch production insourcing would act as a major positive catalyst for outer-year earnings per share.

Section 14 – Value chain: political and regulatory

Section summary
  1. Scandinavian Tobacco Group's supply chain is highly consolidated, with key Tier 1 suppliers representing 80% of procurement value.
  2. A rare raw tobacco leaf quality issue disrupted European supply channels during 2026, highlighting the operational volatility of agricultural raw materials.
1.
Favorable deregulation: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has benefited from a federal court ruling that vacated strict Food and Drug Administration rules on premium cigars. The judicial verdict was appealed but has temporarily allowed the tobacco manufacturer to introduce new premium cigar offerings for the first time since 2016. Analysts believe the federal deregulation will support product innovation and protect high-margin North American revenues over the next 18 months. The positive regulatory development serves as a key mitigant to structural declines.
2.
Delayed restrictions: The United States Food and Drug Administration continues to delay its proposed federal ban on non-tobacco characterizing flavors in cigars. Despite these federal delays, local jurisdictions and individual states have implemented regional flavor bans that restrict Scandinavian Tobacco Group's mass-market sales. Investors face ongoing risks that a future nationwide ban could disrupt approximately forty-five percent of group sales derived from handmade cigars.
3.
Accelerating restrictions: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces potential marketing bans and oral product constraints under the European Union's upcoming Tobacco Products Directive. The updated regulatory framework is projected for implementation between 2028 and 2030, with a primary focus on nicotine pouches and online sales channels. Prospective rules are expected to focus heavily on oral tobacco and strict online distribution boundaries, which threatens digital e-commerce retail networks.
4.
Uncertain litigation: Scandinavian Tobacco Group is currently contesting a taxable claim from Belgian customs and excise authorities. The regional audit covers a disputed EUR 7m-9m tax liability stemming from the disposal of tobacco waste without following correct formalities. Management remains highly confident that the waste was destroyed properly, but the legal outcome of the dispute remains unproven under Belgian court practice.
5.
Fragmented policies: Next Generation Products face highly fragmented and restrictive marketing rules across key European jurisdictions. German regulators have leveraged food classifications to remove nicotine pouches from physical store shelves entirely. The Netherlands has proposed a total ban on all pouches, while France is considering regulatory bills that stop short of complete product eradication. Regulatory barriers will continue to hamper modern oral profit margins until production is insourced.
6.
Gradual tax proposals: A draft opinion from the European Parliament suggests a more moderate approach to future tobacco excise overhauls. Proposed legislative amendments would cap inflation-driven rate hikes at 12% and extend transition windows for cigars. Analysts view this draft as a positive signal for market stability because abrupt tax spikes would incentivize illegal cross-border smuggling.

Section 15 – Value chain: supply side

1.
Severe supply disruption: Scandinavian Tobacco Group experienced a rare leaf tobacco quality defect in its French brand Signature during the second quarter of 2026. The quality defect caused cigars to burn unsatisfactorily, which interrupted supply to key European markets and dragged down volume market share from 27.2% to 26.7% in the first half. Although the incident led to a DKK 35 mm obsolete write-down, replacement leaf was secured and supply normalized by the fourth quarter. This temporary disruption will compress near-term earnings per share, but the swift resolution limits multi-year downside.
2.
High supplier concentration: Scandinavian Tobacco Group relies heavily on a consolidated group of raw material partners, with Tier 1 suppliers representing approximately 80% of its total procurement value. To mitigate supply chain vulnerabilities, the company is intensifying its engagement with these partners through the Sustainable Tobacco Program to enforce strict ESG and deforestation-free standards. By securing deforestation-free tobacco and packaging supply, the company protects its long-term brand equity and shields its earnings per share from impending European regulatory compliance penalties.
3.
Elevated agricultural risk: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces rising exposure to chronic and acute weather risks across its diverse global sourcing network, spanning South Asia, the Caribbean, and South America. Extreme weather events, such as cyclones in Central America or floods in Indonesia, directly threaten tobacco crop yields and reduce leaf quality while driving up purchasing costs. These environmental pressures create unpredictable raw material cost inflation that can compress gross margins unless offset by pricing power.
4.
Strategic footprint optimization: Scandinavian Tobacco Group operates a highly consolidated manufacturing footprint, leveraging dual machine-rolled wrapper and binder sites in the Dominican Republic and Indonesia to achieve scale. Under its Focus2030 program, the company aims to optimize OEE across these sites from historical levels of 50% to 65% toward best-in-class benchmarks of 80% through standardized digital processes. Improving production efficiency is critical to offset rising labor costs, directly supporting future earnings per share growth.
5.
Vulnerable logistical transitions: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has previously suffered from severe supply chain instability and market share losses due to temporary production bottlenecks during its global SAP software rollout. These logistical challenges in European factories hindered product shipments during the first half of 2025 and demonstrated the operational risks of system standardization. While these bottlenecks have since been resolved, any future disruptions during digital system integration will immediately threaten product availability and pressure earnings per share.
6.
Other side of the story: The supply chain optimization targets under Focus2030 could be severely compromised if the company fails to successfully insource its rapidly growing next-generation nicotine pouch production. Currently, Scandinavian Tobacco Group largely outsources its XQS pouch manufacturing in Europe, which creates a negative product mix impact that dilutes European Branded margins. If outsourced manufacturing agreements prevent margin expansion through 2030, the company's projected earnings per share recovery will remain constrained despite legacy cost reductions.
