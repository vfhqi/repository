EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

S.N.G.N. Romgaz S.A. stands at an unprecedented strategic inflection point, with governance stability secured until mid-2027 but facing transformative capital projects that will fundamentally reshape its earnings profile over the next three years. The company is accelerating the Neptun Deep offshore development with first gas targeted for the first half of 2027, representing a critical milestone that will dictate future earnings growth, while simultaneously completing the acquisition of SC Azomures SA's fertilizer production activities in September 2026 for €69 million, marking a major structural shift into the chemical industry that will immediately impact consolidated revenue and operating expenses.

The scale of internal transformation is unprecedented, as Romgaz transitions from a pure-play upstream gas producer into a vertically integrated energy and chemicals group, yet this ambitious repositioning carries significant execution risks during a period of peak capital expenditure intensity that is suppressing near-term free cash flow and shareholder returns.

Strategic Transformation and Earnings Drivers

Managing volume growth from the Neptun Deep Black Sea development represents the primary strategic focus, with the offshore block holding an estimated 100 billion cubic meters of recoverable gas that could double the company's output and drive massive expansion in earnings per share. The total capital expenditure budget for the offshore project is approximately €4 billion, with Romgaz financing its 50% participating share through corporate funds and debt issuances, though this high capital intensity will suppress free cash flow until commercial sales commence.

The vertical integration of Azomures will directly impact consolidated operating revenues and expenses starting in the final quarter of 2026, with brokers estimating an initial net profit drop to RON 1.15 billion in 2027 due to rising restart costs, yet this acquisition provides a strong strategic framework to capture higher margins along the entire energy value chain by converting raw methane into higher-value agricultural products.

Completing the delayed 430-megawatt Iernut thermoelectric plant remains essential to restore high-margin electricity sales and boost overall earnings per share by replacing old, inefficient infrastructure, though construction has faced severe delays with the government extending the completion deadline to December 31, 2026 following contract termination. Changes in state-regulated wholesale gas tariffs directly impact Romgaz's core upstream revenues and net profit margins, with pricing restrictions suppressing the company's realized wholesale prices and representing a major headwind that limits short-term earnings per share growth.

Under Government Emergency Ordinance Number 12/2026, the wholesale gas price for household consumers was capped at 110 RON per megawatt-hour starting April 1, 2026, representing a direct reduction from the previous cap of 120 RON per megawatt-hour that immediately compresses realized wholesale revenues. Entering regional gas export markets, particularly Moldova, will directly impact wholesale sales volumes and average realized prices, with management expecting to boost average realized gas prices and drive a 15% expansion in consolidated earnings per share, though this export expansion depends absolutely on the timely completion of Neptun Deep, as Romgaz will lack the surplus gas volumes required to enter international markets if offshore drilling faces technical delays.

Recent Performance and Financial Profile

Romgaz achieved a consolidated net profit of RON 1.74 billion during the first half of 2026, representing a 3.37% increase compared to the same period of 2025, with this bottom-line growth showing that the company can sustain profitability during price corrections, yet consolidated total revenues fell by 8.59% to RON 3.88 billion in the first half of 2026, driven by lower domestic production volumes and the reduction of the regulated price cap, confirming vulnerability to state pricing interventions.

Romgaz ranks at the 83rd percentile for margin trend due to its highly efficient domestic regulatory exemptions, maintaining an exceptionally high EBITDA margin of 53.49% in the first half of 2026 that reflects the company's ability to protect operating profitability despite lower realized wholesale gas prices. Capital expenditures rose to RON 3,833.96 million in 2025, representing massive investment that will suppress near-term free cash flow until commercial gas production commences in 2027, while the company significantly altered its capital structure by issuing EUR 500 million in corporate bonds in late 2025 carrying an annual coupon of 4.625% and maturing on November 4, 2031.

Romgaz distributed a total dividend of RON 605.9 million in 2026, representing a gross dividend of RON 0.1572 per share and a dividend yield of only 1.16%, which limits near-term shareholder returns unless the company raises its payout ratios after the 2027 startup. The Romanian government authorized flexible dividend policies under Government Emergency Ordinance Number 31/2024, allowing Romgaz to lower its payout ratio to 20% to conserve cash for Neptun Deep, which temporarily prioritizes project funding over shareholder yield. Romgaz is battling a steady, natural decline in production volumes across its highly mature onshore gas reservoirs, with management actively managing extraction rates to limit this annual output decline to a maximum of 2.5%, though this natural drop constrains upstream gas sales until major new supply sources are brought online.

Competitive Positioning and External Environment

Romgaz ranks at the 17th percentile for revenue growth among its Central and Eastern European peers during the first half of 2026 because revenues contracted by 8.59% to RON 3.88 billion, meaning the company must rely heavily on upcoming offshore gas volumes to regain top-line momentum. Romgaz maintains its position as the top natural gas producer and main supplier in Romania, accounting for a substantial 53% share of the country's total gas production in 2025, with this massive domestic market share safeguarding its competitive positioning and ensuring it remains the primary beneficiary of domestic gas demand stabilization.

OMV Petrom represents the primary domestic competitor accounting for approximately one-third of Romanian gas production, and while both companies cooperate as equal partners on Neptun Deep, they compete directly for wholesale and industrial customers, with this competitive tension limiting independent price increases. Romania's macroeconomic landscape remains pressured by persistent inflationary forces, with the consumer price index rising by 10.4% in June 2026 driven by higher commodity prices and fiscal adjustments, driving up corporate operating expenses and increasing the risk of margin compression. High central bank interest rates are putting pressure on capital-intensive corporate investments, with the National Bank of Romania maintaining the central bank key rate projected to remain elevated at 6% through 2027, increasing the cost of corporate debt as Romgaz raises loans to fund its share of Neptun Deep.

Romgaz faces significant transition risks as Romania aligns its energy sector with the European Commission's Green Deal, establishing a comprehensive decarbonization strategy running from 2025 through 2050, though implementing these carbon-reduction technologies will demand substantial capital allocation potentially diluting near-term earnings. The European Union's Net Zero Industry Act imposes highly disproportionate CO2 storage obligations on regional producers, with Romgaz facing an individual carbon injection capacity contribution obligation of 4.12 million tonnes per annum by 2030 and filing a direct lawsuit against the European Commission to appeal these uncompetitive mandates.

Primary Structural and Cyclical Risks

The primary structural barrier to Romgaz's upstream profitability is the price cap enforced by the Romanian government, with the regulated wholesale gas price reduced to 110 RON/MWh from 120 RON/MWh starting April 1, 2026, capping the company's potential profit because Romgaz delivered up to 73.12% of its gas volumes under these restricted prices in the first half of 2026. The high concentration of state ownership at 70% exposes Romgaz to severe political interference and sudden governance shifts, with the revocation of non-executive directors in April 2025 highlighting how rapidly majority state control can disrupt established board structures and potentially stall major capital projects if political priorities change.

The success of the regional export strategy and the vertical integration of Azomures are highly dependent on the on-time completion of Neptun Deep, with domestic onshore fields experiencing natural decline of up to 2.5% annually meaning Romgaz lacks surplus gas volumes to supply both the fertilizer plant and regional export markets simultaneously, so a delay in first gas would immediately disrupt downstream and international expansion.

A co-occurring perfect storm of external headwinds could severely depress earnings per share over the next three years if domestic natural gas demand continues to contract due to persistent industrial stagnation while the government simultaneously extends regulated price caps past 2027, combined with regional geopolitical escalation in the Black Sea physically disrupting development drilling, triggering a sharp drop in consolidated EBITDA and contraction of shareholder dividends.

The combination of transformative offshore development, aggressive vertical integration into chemicals, and delayed power plant completion creates a highly correlated set of earnings drivers where execution success on Neptun Deep is the prerequisite for downstream and export strategies, meaning any material delay in the 2027 first gas timeline would immediately cascade into multiple strategic failures and severe earnings per share dilution.
