This indicates that medical equipment builders face a highly resilient contract pricing environment despite softening consumer demand. This contract strength will keep purchase costs high for medical technology companies.
2.
Softening spot: Jefferies Research reported on November 20, 2026, that conventional DRAM spot prices fell by 5% over the preceding month, presenting a sharp contrast to contract markets. This widening gap between spot and contract prices is estimated to exceed 20%, showing that spot indicators fail to capture enterprise demand. This mismatch creates a structural division in memory pricing that complicates standard cost planning. This division means equipment makers must look beyond spot trends to assess actual cost pressure.
3.
Steady escalation: TrendForce forecasts cited by Jefferies Research on November 20, 2026, indicate that the DDR4 8Gb contract price will rise from $29.50 at the beginning of 2027 to $33. This continuous appreciation is supported by the three major DRAM producers shifting capacity to High-Bandwidth Memory. Consequently, medical equipment makers will face a gradual rise in legacy component costs through 2027. This price trend will continue to compress gross margins unless offset by pricing actions.

Severe constraint: Guotai Junan Securities reported on October 9, 2026, that High-Bandwidth Memory consumes two to four times the wafer area of conventional DRAM. This structural shift severely reduces the supply of standard commodity DRAM available for legacy applications. This reallocation will keep industrial-grade memory component prices high.

This pricing reversal should provide some margin relief for storage-intensive medical equipment manufacturers.

This suggests that medical-device makers will experience persistent storage costs despite broader NAND spot market deflation.
