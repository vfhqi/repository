EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Conclusions

1.
CEO performance, early and credible but not yet proven: Markus Asch has stabilised results, and the 2025 earnings recovery was only partly his own doing. Interroll reported stable consolidated sales of CHF 514.2 million in fiscal 2025, and EPS stabilised at CHF 67.45. Skeptics note that he benefited from favorable cyclical recovery momentum and that much of the order acceleration stems from a broader industry rebound.
2.
Alignment, partial: Personal alignment is weak, while the pay design is more constructive. Asch holds only 45 shares, about 0.01% of voting rights. Against that, the long-term incentive is paid exclusively in shares with a four-year blocking period.
3.
Governance, formally robust but entrenched: Audit and compliance controls are clean, but structural protections could delay a leadership change if execution falters. If strategic execution under Asch falters, these protective structures may delay necessary leadership changes.
4.
Single largest credibility risk: The most likely claim to prove wrong is the unchanged mid-term target of roughly CHF 600 million in sales supporting an EBIT margin of 15% or higher. Management reiterated that target, yet Interroll gave no quantitative guidance for 2026. The target also depends on the North American sales restructuring strengthening commercial momentum in the second half of 2026.

5.

Jefferies kept Interroll at Hold while upgrading Kardex to Buy in June 2025. Interroll's North American recovery remains in an earlier phase. It therefore says little about conviction over the 18-month EPS horizon. No individual transaction disclosures are visible since March 2026.

Key-person strain and senior-team churn: Three C-level officers departed within two weeks in early 2026, and Asch took over as COO ad interim in October 2025. No formal successor has been identified for Asch. New CTO Ulrich Engenhardt has worked alongside Asch at Kärcher and Rittal. This may speed execution but also concentrates dependence on one network.
