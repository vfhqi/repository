Mechanics of supply reliability

This aggressive inventory accumulation enabled BETEK to maintain its delivery capacity while smaller, cash-constrained competitors were forced to withdraw. This supply reliability successfully protected the company's customers and will permanently solidify critical customer relationships over the medium term.
2.
Exploding margins: The Materials Solutions segment reaped massive windfall profits in the first half of 2026 because of these pricing adjustments. The segment's adjusted EBITA margin jumped to 22.4% in H1 2026, which is up 14.3 percentage points from 8.1% in the prior year period. Although management expects this extraordinary momentum to subside as material costs rise, the initial inventory positioning has already triggered a massive guidance upgrade for the full year. This pricing power guarantees a higher earnings floor unless customers aggressively push back.
3.
Favorable pricing spread: The extreme divergence between European and Chinese ammonium paratungstate prices has further supported BETEK's regional pricing power. This massive 40% discount reflects tight export controls inside China and intense Western strategic stockpiling campaigns ahead of defense mandates. While Chinese domestic prices fell to USD 1,750 per metric ton unit in September 2026, Rotterdam prices peaked at USD 3,100 in August. This pricing cushion protects European margins unless the export restrictions are suddenly lifted.

Customer resistance: Large original equipment manufacturers are beginning to resist further raw material surcharges as industrial demand weakens. An industry expert noted in July 2026 that while smaller distributors accept price hikes, large automotive clients are pushing back more strongly against surcharges than they did during the initial price spike. This resistance means that passing on incremental tungsten costs will become increasingly difficult for BETEK in late 2026. This capping of pricing flexibility could pressure Materials Solutions margins unless volumes inflect positively.

Materials segment cash drain: The primary driver behind INDUS's capital crunch was the unprecedented inventory buildup in the Materials Solutions segment. M&A pause and restart thresholds

4.
Acquisition restart metrics: To resume active portfolio expansion, INDUS must steer its balance sheet back toward its strict investment-grade parameters. Free cash flow compression: This working capital expansion forced management to dramatically lower its free cash flow outlook for the year. INDUS's press release on 12 May 2026 adjusted its full-year free cash flow guidance from more than EUR 70 million to at least break-even. Projected restart timing: Despite the H1 deal deceleration, management expects to re-engage in acquisitions before the end of the year. In an EQS-News release on 5 October 2026, the company stated that it expects to strengthen the INDUS Group with further acquisitions before the end of 2026. This indicates that working capital pressure is easing enough to allow selective deal execution, although any major transaction remains highly constrained.

Contrary evidence: NuWays AG argued on 8 October 2026 that INDUS's M&A deceleration was purely a temporary precautionary measure that is already under control. The broker expects strategic acquisitions to resume immediately in late 2026, suggesting that the balancing feedback loop is weaker than the peak working capital figures indicate. This swift resolution would protect the company's long-term EPS target of doubling 2024 earnings by 2030 unless inventory liquidations take longer than planned.

Regional succession pressure: The demographic pressure is not distributed evenly across the German industrial heartland, creating localized sourcing opportunities. This regional focus ensures that the company can target highly profitable niche operators facing immediate generational transitions.

This trusted positioning allows the firm to cultivate long-term relationships with founders years before they are ready to transact. This reinforcing referral cycle reduces transaction costs and allows INDUS to maintain exclusive access to high-quality assets.

Emotional transaction barriers: Generational business transfers in the German Mittelstand represent highly emotional life events that require structured personal planning. Jefferies Research noted on 20 August 2020 that about 21% of SME owners who planned to withdraw lacked finalized plans because of the complexity of succession preparations. This administrative bottleneck means that a professional acquirer who offers operational continuity can easily stand out. By positioning itself as a stable, long-term custodian, INDUS can overcome these emotional exit hurdles where traditional private equity fail.

Preserving entrepreneurial identity: Retaining the existing operational structures of acquired businesses is key to winning the trust of local founders.
