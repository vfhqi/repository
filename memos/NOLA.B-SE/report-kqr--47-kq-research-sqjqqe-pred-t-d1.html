Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The expected shift for Nolato relies heavily on ramping newly validated medical capacity and integrating acquisitions to drive higher earnings per share.
  2. Resolving whether Nolato can hit its targets using existing strengths is critical for the medium-term investor debate.

Defining the Key Debate

1.
Defining question: "Key question: how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"

Strategic Growth Targets

2.
Significant leverage: Investor interest in Nolato centers on whether its achieving annual organic growth targets can drive substantial growth in earnings per share over the next three years. The group announced financial targets at its Capital Markets Day in March 2025, which include achieving annual organic growth of more than 8% and an EBITA margin exceeding 12% over a business cycle. Achieving these targets would represent a notable acceleration from the 2% organic growth reported by the company for the full year of 2025.
3.
Expected expansion: Financial estimates from market analysts indicate that Nolato is poised for a significant earnings recovery in the coming years. Broker estimates from DNB Carnegie on 29 October 2026 project adjusted earnings per share of SEK 2.91 for 2026, rising to SEK 3.50 in 2027 and reaching SEK 3.80 by 2028. This growth trajectory depends on a gradual recovery in Medical Solutions margins and stable utilization of newly validated capacity.

Drivers and Execution Risks

4.
Growth drivers: The core of Nolato's growth strategy lies in ramping up its Medical Solutions division, particularly through major contract wins. Analysts at DNB Carnegie on 29 October 2026 highlighted that the SEK 700m obesity and diabetes contract announced in April 2024 should support Medical Solutions growth by 2 to 3 percentage points annually during 2027 and 2028. Ramping this newly validated medical capacity is key to driving higher capacity utilization and improving segment margins.
5.
Acquisition integration: The company is also moving into mechatronics and box-build solutions through recent bolt-on acquisitions. Nolato completed the acquisitions of Unitron and M&M Qualtech in September 2026, which DNB Carnegie estimated on 29 October 2026 will add approximately SEK 600m in annual sales. Integrating these electronics-intensive businesses under a new chief executive represents a critical shift in the company's operating model.
6.
Cost dynamics: The active acquisition strategy has introduced new cost pressures that could weigh on near-term margins. According to estimates from ABG Sundal Collier on 3 May 2026, rising precious metals prices have recently created margin headwinds that company price hikes only offset with a lag. Investors are closely watching whether the step-up in central costs will be permanent or if it can be reversed as the acquisition strategy matures.
7.
Broad evidence: This research report evaluates Nolato's prospects by analyzing a total of nine distinct companies, seven historical precedents, and more than fifteen primary sources. The findings assess whether expected margin recoveries are realistic given historical operating conditions.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some brokers caution that near-term cyclical risks and external cost pressures may delay the expected margin recovery. In a report dated 3 May 2026, ABG Sundal Collier lowered its EBITA estimates for Nolato by 4% for 2026 and 3% for 2027 to 2028 due to temporary margin pressures. These estimate reductions indicate that external headwinds might offset the benefits of Nolato's internal strengths.
9.
Other side of the story: While consensus expectations focus on a smooth recovery driven by GLP-1 contracts and M&A, history shows that filling large capacity can take longer than anticipated and dilute margins. If Nolato struggles with on-time validation or experiences price pressure at renewals, its EPS growth could fall significantly short of current broker forecasts. Unforeseen challenges in improving margins for the Medical Solutions segment due to uneven capacity utilization remain a key risk to the investment case.

Section 2 – Historical Margins and Capacity Utilization Records

Section summary
  1. Nolato has historically reached peak group EBITA margins above 12% and medical margins above 13% only under exceptional volume and product mix conditions.
  2. Previous capacity-filling cycles for China and GW Plastics demonstrate that reaching full utilization and optimal margins typically requires several quarters of operational and contract adjustments.
  3. Footprint optimizations and portfolio restructurings by peers like

GVS and Trelleborg show that structural margin expansion requires significant upfront investment and sustained cost control.

Margin History Analysed

1.
Strong historical performance: Nolato achieved a group EBITA margin above 12% in fiscal year 2015 when it reached 12.1% due to high telecom demand, according to company reports. This peak group margin has not been sustained for a full year since then, although the group hovered close to this level in 2021 when it recorded an 11.8% EBITA margin. This historical threshold shows that exceeding the company's long-term margin target requires ideal volume conditions that have rarely repeated.
2.
Elevated segment profitability: Nolato's Medical Solutions segment has repeatedly achieved and sustained an EBITA margin of 13% or more, reaching 13.1% in 2017 and 13.0% in both 2018 and 2020, as reported by analysts. These high margins were driven by favorable product mixes and high capacity utilization, as shown in company reports. This demonstrates that the medical division has proven its ability to reach high margins under optimal conditions.
3.
Favorable product mix: Nolato Medical first reached these elevated margin levels in 2013 and 2014 when segment EBITA margins stood at 13.0% and 13.6% respectively. These results were achieved through high productivity and favorable product mixes rather than sheer volume. This historical baseline indicates that segment margins are highly sensitive to the types of medical devices being manufactured.
4.
Direct peer comparison: Gerresheimer, which has historically focused on ramping up its medical packaging and autoinjector lines, remains Nolato's closest medical peer. While Nolato Medical has managed to hit margins above 13% historically, Gerresheimer's margins have also been supported by high-value drug delivery systems. This peer context indicates that Nolato's medical division operates at highly competitive profitability levels when capacity is fully utilized.

Capacity Filling Cycles

5.
Prolonged capacity absorption: Nolato's Chinese vaporizer heating product capacity experienced volatile utilization cycles that significantly impacted group profitability before 2022, as shown in company reports. Vaporiser inventory adjustments took more than two quarters to normalize after demand fell in late 2018. This demonstrates that dedicated capacity in consumer electronics is highly vulnerable to rapid customer-side changes.
6.
Slow margin recovery: Nolato's acquisition of GW Plastics in September 2020 initially diluted the Medical Solutions margin because the acquired business operated at a lower 7% EBITA margin. Integrating the US operations and filling its capacity took over eight quarters amid pandemic-related disruptions in the surgical segment. This precedent shows that ramping up medical capacity is a multi-year endeavor.
7.
Operational execution delays: Nolato's integration of GW Plastics required the implementation of the "Medical Excellence" program to align the US plants with group standards. It took several years of strategic price adjustments and contract improvements to stabilize capacity utilization in North America and push margins toward historical levels. This long timeline suggests that newly acquired capacity does not translate into immediate margin gains.
8.
Extreme demand volatility: Nolato's Integrated Solutions segment margin collapsed during the VHP downturn as capacity became underutilized. A sharp decline in vaporiser volumes from a previously significant customer led to severe capacity underutilization and lower margins that took multiple years to address. This highlights that filling dedicated capacity is highly dependent on a single customer's product lifecycle.

Footprint Optimization Precedents

9.
Aggressive footprint consolidation: GVS completed a major three-year industrial transformation in 2025 by closing its Puerto Rico plant and consolidating operations in Suzhou, China. The Suzhou relocation project, which required an investment of 25 million euros, was completed over a multi-year period from 2021 to 2025. This precedent indicates that footprint optimization requires substantial upfront capital and time before delivering efficiency.
10.
Severe restructuring drag: GVS experienced significant non-recurring restructuring charges and tax audit penalties during its multi-year consolidation process in China. The relocation of Suzhou and plant rationalizations weighed on short-term operating profits before the new 30,000 square meter plant became fully operational. This shows that footprint optimization projects often dilute margins during the transition phase.
11.
Systematic portfolio management: Trelleborg has historically improved its growth and margin profile by divesting cyclical operations with limited growth potential, such as its automotive-related lines. Active portfolio management successfully reduced its sensitivity to business cycles and expanded its EBITA margin to 18.0% by fiscal year 2024. This suggests that structural margin improvement is best achieved through active pruning.
12.
Persistent restructuring costs: Trelleborg's continuous cost optimization programs have resulted in consistent items affecting comparability that weigh on its reported earnings. Restructuring costs in Trelleborg's quarterly interim reports averaged 67 to 80 million kronor during the first half of 2026. This comparison shows that even highly successful cost programs require ongoing expenditures to maintain high operational efficiency.

Opposing Margin Perspectives

13.
Contrary evidence: Nolato's historical capacity ramps have occasionally delivered immediate positive results without the expected multi-quarter drag. In Q3 2020, Nolato reported that its Medical Solutions EBITA margin reached a strong 12.9% despite the initial consolidation of the lower-margin GW Plastics business, as high pandemic-driven diagnostics volumes temporarily offset the acquisition dilution.
14.
Other side of the story: While historical precedents suggest that Nolato can achieve margins above 12% at the group level and 13% in Medical Solutions, these peaks occurred under exceptional demand conditions. Previous margin peaks relied on high-volume consumer electronics cycles or pandemic-driven diagnostics booms, whereas the current recovery path relies on ramping highly regulated GLP-1 capacity and integrating complex acquisitions. Consequently, expecting a swift return to peak margins without experiencing significant utilization delays is highly optimistic given past execution timelines.

Section 3 – Medical Solutions Incremental Margins and GLP-1 Capacity Ramps

Section summary
  1. Nolato's Medical Solutions segment has recorded low incremental margins due to ramp-up costs and raw material inflation.
  2. Strong peer performance from Ypsomed and West Pharmaceutical Services demonstrates that high-value product mix shifts can offset capacity ramp-up dilution.
  3. Stevanato's recent single-customer GLP-1 volume cut highlights the high customer concentration risks and utilization headwinds inherent in medical device contract manufacturing.

Quarterly Incremental Margin Analysis

1.
Soft incremental profitability: Nolato's Medical Solutions segment generated soft incremental margins during the recent volume ramps between 2024 and 2026. For example, comparing the third quarter of 2026 with the third quarter of 2025, Nolato Medical added SEK 158 million in quarterly sales but only earned SEK 7 million in additional EBITA, resulting in a low incremental EBITA margin of 4.43% (calculated). This soft incremental performance occurred because heavy project ramp-up costs and raw material inflation weighed on short-term profitability. This pressure indicates that near-term earnings per share will remain constrained until capacity utilization improves.

Operational Realities and Volume Pressures

10.
Extreme customer concentration: Stevanato's sudden November 2026 GLP-1 volume cut illustrates the severe risk of relying on a single large customer. Stevanato reported a year-over-year decline in GLP-1 revenue to 17% of sales in the third quarter of 2026 after a major customer reduced forecasts for 2027. This volume cut caught investors by surprise and led to a sharp stock price decline. This negative precedent proves that medical contract manufacturers are highly vulnerable to originator inventory adjustments and launch delays.
11.
Gradual contract recovery: Stevanato's backfilling strategy demonstrates how contract manufacturers must scramble to reallocate freed capacity. Stevanato plans to backfill its idle Nexa syringe capacity by shifting lines to other biologic customers and securing incremental demand from Eli Lilly. Management expects these replacement biologic programs to be margin-neutral or accretive because they utilize more sophisticated syringe configurations with attractive pricing. However, some capacity will remain open in 2027 due to customer validation and ramp timelines, highlighting that backfilling is a slow process.
12.
Direct pricing pressure: Novo Nordisk's lower US pricing starting January 2027 is expected to intensify price pressure across the GLP-1 supply chain. Novo Nordisk agreed to White House drug pricing terms for GLP-1s, leading to negative group sales growth expectations. A supply chain expert noted that pricing per autoinjector typically ranges from USD 4 to USD 8. The expert also highlighted that high-volume contracts are priced toward the lower end of this range. This volume-tiered pricing means that contract manufacturers will face margin pressure unless they optimize their internal production yields.
13.
Other side of the story: While peer ramps indicate a long and dilutive utilization path, Nolato's Hungarian expansion has been highly structured to minimize start-up losses. DNB Carnegie confirmed that Nolato's SEK 600 million investment in Hungary is backed by a major obesity contract that provides substantial long-term growth of 2 to 3 percentage points annually. Furthermore, the customer's parallel investment in the Hungarian site is roughly twice Nolato's expenditure, providing strong downside protection if capacity is underutilized. Therefore, Nolato's ramp may avoid the severe margin dilution seen at peers because its contract structure is heavily de-risked.

Section 4 – Precedents in Organic Growth and Contract Ramping

Section summary
  1. Achieving Nolato's ambitious 8% organic growth target relies heavily on executing the Hungarian medical contract.
  2. Prior peer contract ramps show that high customer concentration risks can trigger severe organic growth resets.
  3. Nolato's Materials business is experiencing a powerful expansion driven by Trishield deployment in data centers and satellites.

Strategic Organic Growth Targets

1.
Ambitious acceleration: Nolato established a new target at its March 2025 Capital Markets Day to achieve annual organic sales growth of more than 8% over a business cycle. This represents a notable jump from the historical baseline of 2% organic growth recorded for the full year of 2025. Achieving this relies on a major obesity contract in Hungary to add 2 to 4 percentage points to annual growth. This trajectory represents a steep ramp that requires unprecedented contract execution and rapid validation of new capacity.
2.
Muted expectations: Carnegie models organic growth rates that remain below management's long-term targets despite the anticipated ramp of the Hungarian obesity contract. The broker forecasts 6% organic sales growth for the group in 2026, which includes 8% growth in Medical Solutions. This projection assumes that excluding the major obesity project, the medical division's underlying organic growth would be only 5.5% in 2026. This difference shows that financial analysts remain cautious about the company's ability to immediately sustain organic growth over a business cycle.
3.
Sustained peer expansion: Gerresheimer represents a key medical peer that successfully lifted its growth prospects by securing long-term biologic contracts. This strategic shift was designed to transition the business toward high-value solutions and medical systems. Gerresheimer expects its GLP-1 business to reach sales of 200 to 300 million euros by 2027. This precedent suggests that re-rating on organic acceleration is highly dependent on securing multi-year customer commitments.

Contract Ramping and Customer Concentration Risks

4.
Severe growth reset: Stevanato Group represents a cautionary precedent where high customer concentration interrupted a major organic growth ramp. The company expected double-digit organic growth but experienced a sudden moderation in GLP-1 syringe demand from a single customer. This customer reduction created a significant headwind that is expected to limit Stevanato's organic growth to low-single digits in 2027. This case proves that relying on a single massive contract leaves contract manufacturers highly vulnerable to sudden customer-side inventory resets.
5.
Protracted capacity backfilling: Stevanato is attempting to backfill its idled high-value syringe capacity by shifting production lines to other biologic customers. While the company's non-GLP-1 biologics grew by more than 30% in late 2026, fully replacing the lost volume will take several quarters. This slow recovery occurs because new replacement programs require customer validations and regulatory approvals before reaching commercial volumes. Consequently, backfilling idle capacity is a slow and operationally complex process that limits near-term margin recovery.
6.
Contrary evidence: Nolato's major obesity contract in Hungary provides strong contractual safeguards that protect the company against sudden volume cuts. The customer has co-invested twice as much capital as Nolato into the facility, significantly lowering the risk of stranded assets. This structure differs from peers because downside risk is contractually minimized through long-term take-or-pay agreements. Therefore, Nolato's contract ramp is less vulnerable to the sudden volume cancellations seen at Stevanato.

The business area was propelled by lucrative waves in mobile phones and vaporizer heating products. This history demonstrates that highly cyclical consumer and telecom cycles can rapidly dismantle multi-year organic growth achievements.

Network equipment volumes slowed further in 2024, offsetting early growth in automotive applications. While telecom volumes finally stabilized from low levels in late 2024, the downturn significantly weighed on the segment's performance. This cycle shows that Materials cannot easily sustain growth above 15% when its primary end-markets undergo structural corrections.

Adjusted for currency, Materials sales grew by 17% in the second quarter of 2026 and accelerated to 27% in the third quarter. This rapid expansion was fueled by significant shipment volumes for data centers, satellites, and aerospace/defense. Materials is now the largest market area in Engineered Solutions, contributing robust profitability to the segment.

Trishield Technology and Market Exposure

10.
Unique shielding solutions: Nolato's proprietary Trishield technology provides advanced electromagnetic interference shielding for demanding electronics applications. The silver/nickel-filled conductive silicone gaskets are dispensed directly onto components to protect optical modules in server cabinets. This slim and effective solution is highly demanded because it optimizes space and reduces compression force in tight enclosures. This technological advantage positions Materials to capture rapidly growing artificial intelligence opportunities in global data centers.
11.
Expanding satellite deployment: Nolato's Materials division is actively deploying its Trishield technology in low Earth orbit satellites. The materials must withstand vacuum conditions and ensure secure, interference-free communication between ground stations and satellites. Since these satellites are expected to compete with traditional base stations, Nolato has established partnerships with several leading aerospace manufacturers. This satellite exposure provides a durable structural growth avenue that diversifies revenues away from cyclical automotive and consumer markets.
12.
Other side of the story: While Nolato's Materials business is growing rapidly from emerging AI data center demand, its overall impact on group earnings remains limited. Management confirmed that absolute revenue exposure to data centers is still very small from a group perspective. Furthermore, the high-margin segment remains exposed to commodity volatility, as rising silver prices created immediate margin headwinds in 2026 due to lagged pricing pass-through. Therefore, Materials alone cannot offset broader industrial downturns in the automotive and hygiene segments unless the medical ramp-up succeeds.

Section 5 – Bolt-On Acquisitions, Mechatronics and Central Cost Dynamics

Mechatronics Transition Precedents

1.
Strategic expansion: Danish polymer peer SP Group acquired United Kingdom-based OGM Moulding in August 2026 to secure advanced box-build and mechatronics capabilities. The transaction was completed for a total consideration of DKK 209.80 million, representing an attractive entry valuation of approximately 4.80x EBITDA including earn-out payments. This bolt-on acquisition allows SP Group to strengthen its high-margin medtech offerings and initiate extensive cross-selling campaigns to historical customers.
2.
Operational complexity: Component molders moving into medical mechatronics experience a prolonged operational learning curve when integrating acquired electronics facilities. Global contract manufacturer Jabil is pivoting toward these specialized mechatronics capabilities to target long-term gross margins of 9% to 10% while phasing out commoditized consumer accounts. High regulatory barriers in medtech outsourcing support these superior margins but require highly complex cleanroom infrastructures and consistent multi-site standardizations.
3.
Profitability enhancement: Industry precedents demonstrate that copying automation and lean management tools to newly acquired mechatronics factories can significantly lift margin structures. For instance, BYD Electronic successfully optimized operating margins at acquired Jabil plants by implementing in-house automated assembly concepts. This shows that mechatronics acquisitions require active operational synergies to drive higher long-term profitability.

This persistent central cost headwind is expected to dilute short-term earnings per share until larger commercial synergies are achieved.
