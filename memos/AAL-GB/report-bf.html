EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Anglo American is executing a rapid simplification to focus on copper and premium iron ore, exiting non-core diamond, coal, and nickel divisions while pursuing a transformational merger with Teck Resources to form Anglo Teck. The group agreed to sell its Australian steelmaking coal business to Dhilmar for up to US$3.88 billion in cash, with US$2.3 billion upfront on completion, and selected Global Diamond Consortium as the preferred bidder for its 85% stake in De Beers.

The proposed merger of equals with Teck Resources will create a global critical minerals champion, with Anglo shareholders receiving a US$4.5 billion special cash dividend prior to completion and owning 62.4% of the combined group. The transaction targets US$800 million in annual pretax cost synergies by the fourth year and US$1.4 billion in annual pre-tax revenue synergies from integrating adjacent copper mines in Chile.

Anglo American recorded actual revenue of USD 19,325 million in 2025 following its divestment actions, significantly smaller than Rio Tinto's USD 57.6 billion and BHP's USD 51,260 million, though the simplified continuing operations achieved an improved underlying EBITDA margin of 44%, matching Rio Tinto but trailing BHP's sector-leading 58% and Antofagasta's exceptional 60%. BHP achieved a five-year average return on capital employed exceeding 20% due to tier-one iron ore assets, while Anglo American historical 2024 ROCE sat at 12% prior to the launch of the restructuring, improving to 17% return on capital employed across its simplified continuing portfolio. Anglo recorded a USD 2.3 billion pre-tax impairment on De Beers due to structural price declines but reduced its net debt to USD 8.2 billion at the end of the 2025 cycle, managing cash flows to limit net debt below the consensus estimate of USD 9.1 billion. Anglo American delivered 91% cash conversion across its continuing pro-forma asset base, though Anglo American's elevated pro-forma metrics depend on executing high-risk asset disposals; if rough diamond and coal markets remain depressed, delayed transactions will force debt levels higher and erode pro-forma return calculations. The global copper market exhibits extreme supply deficits and low concentration, with the top five producers controlling only approximately 26% of global supply, while rising capital intensity from USD 5,844/t in 2000-09 to a projected USD 18,053/t for 2024-30 and global copper mine grades falling by over 30% between 2000 and 2025 reinforce structural supply constraints. The seaborne iron ore industry remains highly consolidated, with the top five producers controlling approximately 75% of global seaborne supply, though the market is tilting toward structural oversupply as global iron ore supply will absorb 55 million tonnes of incremental output from Simandou and major producers in CY26. Polyhalite remains an unproven niche market representing only 1 million tonnes of production, which is equivalent to approximately 1% of the global potassium-magnesium fertilizer market, constraining Anglo's crop nutrients diversification upside. Anglo American focuses its pro-forma portfolio almost exclusively on high-margin mining and extraction, with the extraction stage capturing the majority of value, yielding typical gross margins of 60.0% (estimated) and EBIT margins of 44.0%. Downstream smelting and refining operate on low margins, typically capturing only 12.0% (estimated) gross and 4.0% (estimated) EBIT, and Anglo operates no custom smelters, shielding its proforma earnings from high capital intensity in downstream refining. Anglo American captures a 44.0% proforma EBITDA margin by concentrating capital in extraction rather than downstream smelting, while low treatment charges shift value upstream to miners, with the 2026 annual benchmark TC/RC falling to zero from USD 21 in 2025, squeezing downstream smelting margins to typical 4.0% (estimated) EBIT. Chief Executive Duncan Wanblad launched a radical restructuring in May 2024 to exit non-core diamond, coal, and nickel businesses, with the group's clear ambition to concentrate capital on world-class copper and premium iron ore operations to maximize shareholder value, accelerated in April 2024 as a defensive measure against BHP's unsolicited takeover bid. The executive team achieved a run rate of USD 1.3 billion in annual operating expenditure savings by late 2024 and reduced group capital expenditure guidance for 2026 to approximately USD 3.2 billion to preserve liquidity. Standalone copper operations are anchored by three tier-one assets in Chile and Peru, representing best-in-class resource bases, with the massive Chilean Collahuasi mine holding 2.6 billion tonnes of sulphide reserves at an exceptional 0.96% copper grade, though a Chilean court ruling in May 2026 overturned Collahuasi's desalination plant environmental permit, introducing near-term water security challenges. The modern Peruvian Quellaveco mine delivered one of the fastest ramp-ups in the industry to full 300ktpa capacity, while the mature Chilean Los Bronces mine faces structural challenges including water scarcity, falling grades, and hard ore from ageing pits. Copper assets delivered an excellent 60% EBITDA margin in the first half of fiscal year 2026, while Premium Iron Ore EBITDA margins fell to 37% in H1 2026 on rising South African logistics costs, though Copper Peru achieved a solid return on capital employed of 32% in the first half of 2026. Anglo Teck will establish a top-five global copper footprint to compete directly with premier pureplay miners, protected by steep permitting complexities, remote geography, and multi-billion dollar capital requirements. Confidence in the foundation quality is medium due to the high regulatory and logistical friction of core mining jurisdictions, with the largest business quality risk being the resolution of Chile's environmental reassessment for Collahuasi's desalination plant, while escalating unit costs at Los Bronces or further Transnet rail derailments would flag operational deterioration before consolidated earnings. Infrastructural and permitting disruptions could erode the copper thesis; if desal permits fail, water scarcity will choke highgrade asset production.

Long-term growth is underpinned by structural copper and premium iron ore demand, with Anglo deploying fullscale Bulk Ore Sorting at Los Bronces to improve feed grades through early waste rejection and embedding Coarse Particle Recovery technology at Quellaveco and El Soldado to recover copper from conventional tailings, while the proprietary nuGen Zero Emission Haulage Solution prototype hydrogen-powered haul truck was successfully launched at Mogalakwena, targeting a 50% to 70% reduction in open-pit emissions. The Second Environmental Tribunal set aside Collahuasi's environmental authorization for its desalination plant in May 2026, though if alternative water sources remain accessible, operators expect no immediate impact on short-term copper production at Collahuasi. Global electricity consumption and copper demand are expected to rise 50% by 2040 on green transition tailwinds, supporting the paradigm fit of Anglo's pro-forma copper concentration, though complex permitting regimes and environmental water litigation could stall development; if desalination permits are permanently denied, production profiles will contract. Foundation Verdict: Duncan Wanblad is executing an impressive portfolio transformation and Teck merger to concentrate on world-class copper and premium iron ore assets, but mature operations face deep operational, logistical, and permitting risks that introduce medium confidence in foundation quality amid persistent infrastructural and environmental bottlenecks.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Anglo American's financial and operational profile reveals five critical areas where the company sits materially below peer medians or industry standards, signaling structural vulnerabilities that may constrain its competitive positioning despite the ongoing strategic transformation. First, revenue scale remains a significant weakness, with Anglo American recording actual revenue of USD 19,325 million in 2025 compared to Rio Tinto's consolidated sales revenue of USD 57.6 billion during the same annual cycle.

This threefold scale disadvantage limits procurement leverage, operational diversification, and absolute free cash flow generation relative to diversified mining majors. The gap becomes even more pronounced when benchmarked against BHP Group, which operates at an even larger consolidated scale. This below-median revenue positioning constrains Anglo's ability to absorb cyclical downturns and invest simultaneously across multiple growth projects without materially impacting leverage ratios.

Second, EBITDA margin performance lags the peer group, with Anglo American achieving an improved underlying EBITDA margin of 44% on its simplified continuing operations, materially below BHP Group's sector-leading underlying EBITDA margin of 58% in H1 FY26. This 14-percentage-point margin deficit reflects structural cost pressures at mature Chilean copper operations and elevated South African logistics expenses that peers with more geographically diversified portfolios avoid.

The gap is particularly concerning when Antofagasta expanded its EBITDA margin by 9 percentage points to 60% in 2025, demonstrating that pure-play copper competitors are capturing superior margins even under similar commodity price environments. Third, return on capital employed sits below the high-barrier threshold established by industry leaders, with Anglo American delivering a 17% return on capital employed across its simplified continuing portfolio in 2025, up from a historically depressed 12% in 2024, yet still trailing BHP's five-year average return on capital employed exceeding 20% due to tier-one iron ore assets.

This persistent ROCE gap indicates that even after aggressive portfolio rationalization, Anglo's capital productivity remains structurally constrained by ageing mine infrastructure, elevated sustaining capital requirements, and higher jurisdictional risk premiums embedded in Chilean and South African operations. The below-median returns profile raises concerns about whether the proposed Teck merger can sustainably elevate ROCE to peer levels or whether integration costs and operational friction will perpetuate the productivity gap. Fourth, net working capital days reveal inefficiency in cash conversion cycles, with Anglo American recording proforma inventory days of 53 days following restructuring, materially above BHP's 12 days and Rio Tinto's 22 days as shown in the peer benchmarking table.

This elevated working capital intensity is exacerbated by South African transport rail bottlenecking that built Kumba finished stockpiles up by 0.4 million tonnes to 7.5 million tonnes, trapping operating cash in unshipped inventory. The above-median working capital burden exposes Anglo to greater liquidity risk during commodity price downturns and reduces the effective free cash flow yield available for shareholder returns compared to peers operating with more efficient logistics networks and lower inventory buffers.

Fifth, customer concentration risk stands out as a structural vulnerability, with standalone operations exhibiting severe concentration where China represents 50% of 2025 continuing revenue at USD 9,612 million, compared to more balanced geographic mixes at diversified peers. This concentration is further amplified through Kumba Iron Ore's regional exposure being highly centralized through China Mineral Resources Group, Beijing's unified purchasing platform, with Anglo entering a previously undisclosed deal making CMRG the exclusive China sales agent for 10 million tonnes of Kumba ore through March 31, 2027.

This above-median buyer concentration materially weakens Anglo's negotiating leverage on pricing terms, exposes the company to unilateral volume reductions by a single state-backed buyer, and creates asymmetric downside risk if Chinese steel demand deteriorates or geopolitical tensions escalate. Peers with more diversified customer bases across multiple end-markets and geographies maintain superior pricing power and revenue stability through commodity cycles.
