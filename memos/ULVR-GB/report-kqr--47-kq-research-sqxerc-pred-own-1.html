Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Key Question is partly answered. Jefferies expects Unilever to classify Foods as a discontinued operation in the first quarter of 2027, and Unilever's own Ice Cream precedents point to a restated continuing perimeter and a share consolidation. But the final share consolidation ratio has not been quantified by management, and broker per-share adjustments remain model-dependent.
1.
Reporting timeline: Jefferies reported on May 18, 2026 that Foods is expected to be classified as a discontinued operation in the first quarter of 2027. This remains dependent on specific transaction milestones that satisfy IFRS 5 criteria. Unilever has not confirmed the date itself.
2.
Closing guidance: Unilever says the combination with McCormick is on track to complete by mid-2027 at the latest. This guidance implies Foods stays in continuing operations for all of fiscal 2026.
3.
Ice Cream precedent on timing: Unilever completed the Ice Cream demerger on December 6, 2025, with the Magnum Ice Cream Company listed on December 8, 2025. Ice Cream was reported as a discontinued operation in the 2025 Annual Report and Accounts. Transfers in India and Portugal were delayed until the first half of 2026, so tail-end regional delays are plausible for Foods too.
4.
Historical restatement: Unilever restated its historical financials on March 12, 2026 to exclude Ice Cream. It presented restated income statement, balance sheet and cash flow statements for 2024 and 2025. A similar clean restatement of the Home and Personal Care perimeter is the base case.
5.
Peer precedent: Procter and Gamble's tax-free Reverse Morris Trust divestiture of beauty brands to Coty was presented cleanly as discontinued operations, excluded from continuing results. This supports the expected IFRS 5 presentation for Unilever.
6.
Share consolidation: Unilever executed an eight-for-nine consolidation on December 8, 2025 to preserve comparability of share price, EPS and dividends. Redburn expects a share consolidation to address the dilution from the Foods separation. No ratio has been announced.
7.
Distribution mechanics: Unilever and its shareholders will receive 65% of the combined entity's fully diluted equity, with public shareholders owning 55.1%, Unilever PLC retaining 9.9% and McCormick holders retaining 35%. Unilever also receives $15.7bn in upfront cash. Bernstein's hypothetical scenario implied FoodCo shares of roughly 29% of Unilever's outstanding shares and an exchange ratio of about 1.1x. This is an illustration, not company guidance.
8.
Broker adjustment methodology: After the Ice Cream consolidation, Unilever adjusted its average share count retrospectively in all presented historical periods. Redburn expects FactSet and other index providers to adjust historical share counts retrospectively for Foods.
9.
What broker and consensus figures currently assume: Deutsche Bank projects adjusted EPS of €3.39 for fiscal 2027 and €3.61 for 2028. Jefferies's pro forma reconciliation, assuming a January 1, 2027 transaction, gives core EPS of €2.48 against a pre-deal consensus of €3.42. That model includes a €141m profit contribution from the retained 9.9% associate stake. Jefferies also assumes net stranded costs of only €250m in fiscal 2027, well below corporate guidance, because it assumes rapid offsetting productivity. Stranded costs and capital return: Unilever guides to €400m to €500m of stranded costs, while Redburn argues gross stranded costs could exceed €1bn. Against this, Morgan Stanley reports a €6bn share buyback between 2026 and 2029, and Unilever expects leverage below 2.0x net debt to EBITDA by the end of 2027.
10.
Open gaps: The exact tax leakage tied to the potential U.S. Asset Sale Election remains highly uncertain. The treatment of global food brands such as Hellmann's and Knorr within Hindustan Unilever is undefined. The scope and pricing of the Transitional Services Agreements are not finalized.
11.
Confidence and key risk: Confidence is rated High for the timeline, restatement and share consolidation sections, and Medium for broker and consensus per-share adjustments. A residual risk is technical selling, since European shareholders unable to hold US-listed shares may be forced to sell after the transaction.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Unilever's combination of its Foods business with McCormick marks the final step in its evolution toward a pure-play Home and Personal Care business.
  2. The transaction's accounting and structural execution will directly determine the historical restatement of financials and the trajectory of earnings per share.

Strategic Portfolio Transformation

1.
Primary investigation: "Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB), a global home and personal care company reporting Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care and Home Care, plus a Foods business being combined with McCormick through a Reverse Morris Trust (completion guided for mid-2027 at the latest); Ice Cream was demerged in December 2025 as The Magnum Ice Cream Company (19.9% retained); the company moves to six regional P&L units from 1-Jan- 2027. For Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB), how will the combination of its Foods business with McCormick be accounted for and presented: from which reporting date will Foods be shown as a discontinued operation or held for sale, will history be restated to the continuing home and personal care perimeter, will the distribution of combined-company shares to Unilever shareholders be accompanied by a share consolidation (as the eight-fornine consolidation accompanied the Ice Cream demerger in December 2025) and on what ratio, and how will FactSet and the brokers adjust historical and forecast per-share figures and dividends for it?"
2.
Significant transformation: Unilever is accelerating its strategic focus on the high-growth categories of beauty, wellbeing, and personal care through the planned divestment of its low-growth foods division. The company has rotated 15% of its portfolio since 2025, including demerging Ice Cream, to establish a simplified operating model. This concentration will increase its Beauty, Wellbeing, and Personal Care contribution to twothirds of group sales, up from 51% in 2025, thereby enhancing its long-term organic growth potential. This focus is expected to improve its valuation multiple over the next three years.
3.
Organizational restructuring: Unilever will return to a regional-led operating model starting January 1, 2027, transitioning away from the category-led structure established in 2022. The company will cluster its markets into six regional performance units with localized P&L accountability to drive execution. Management believes this model combines centralized brand choices with local commercial execution to optimize local media, content, and commerce. This shift aims to support the company's medium-term underlying operating margin expansion targets.
4.
Positive valuation: Unilever could achieve a higher valuation multiple comparable to pure-play peers like Procter and Gamble once the low-margin foods business is fully separated. The transaction is structured as a tax-free Reverse Morris Trust to prevent significant tax leakage for European shareholders. However, the primary US listing of the combined entity may trigger forced selling from European investors benchmarked to local indices. This technical pressure could weigh on Unilever's share price.
5.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research argued on March 31, 2026, that the food divestment may not immediately unlock value because the transaction leaves Unilever with approximately €450m of annual stranded costs. This structural drag represents about 4% of divested sales and could pressure margins unless offset by further cost cuts. Consequently, the near-term earnings accretion remains highly dependent on the successful execution of the €1bn productivity program.

Structural Implications for Earnings

6.
Accelerated earnings: Unilever plans to fund a €6bn share buyback from 2026 to 2029 using $15.7bn in cash proceeds from the McCormick deal. This substantial capital return, combined with debt paydowns, is designed to offset the immediate earnings dilution from divesting the Foods cash cow. Deutsche Bank reported on November 11, 2026, that compounding EPS growth will also be supported by ongoing dividend payments. This financial strategy aims to secure high-single-digit EPS growth over the medium term.
7.
Complex execution: Unilever faces operational complexity because its major Indian subsidiary, Hindustan Unilever, is excluded from the McCormick combination and will retain its Foods business. This partial exit creates operational dis-synergies and requires extensive transitional services agreements to manage shared supply chains. Redburn noted on March 25, 2026, that these dis-synergies and tax leaks could reduce the net value of the transaction by €1.90 per share. Resolving these regional carve-outs will be critical for predicting the posttransaction margins.
8.
30 31 & Co Redburn Other side of the story: Unilever's strategic pivot to a pure-play Household and Personal Care company may increase its vulnerability to cyclical commodity cost inflation and intense competition. While the Foods divestment provides cash for buybacks, it removes a highly resilient margin business that historically offset flat performance in beauty segments. Therefore, unless Unilever can rapidly scale its premium beauty brands organically, the loss of foods could result in higher earnings volatility.

Section 2 – Discontinued Operations and Held for Sale Reporting Timeline

Section summary
  1. Unilever expects to present its Foods business as a discontinued operation starting in the first quarter of 2027.
  2. Historical carve-out precedents like the Ice Cream demerger demonstrate a structured timeline from operational separation to final accounting restatements.
  3. Confidence in the projected discontinued operations timeline is high based on Jefferies broker estimates and Unilever's historical Ice Cream demerger.

Accounting Timelines and IFRS 5

1.
Accelerated transition: Jefferies Research reported on May 18, 2026, that Unilever is expected to classify its Foods business as a discontinued operation in the first quarter of 2027. This accounting treatment remains dependent on reaching specific transaction milestones that satisfy IFRS 5 criteria before the final transaction closure. Consequently, analysts must maintain current model structures until this official transition occurs to ensure consistency with company reports. This shift will immediately remove Foods from continuing operations EPS.
2.
Steady performance: Unilever reported on July 28, 2026, that its Foods business grew by 1.2% in underlying sales during the first half of 2026. This growth rate lags behind the Home Care segment's growth of 7.6% and the Beauty and Wellbeing segment's growth of 5.9% during the same period. Consequently, the continuing perimeter will enjoy structurally higher underlying sales growth once the slower-growing Foods business is fully deconsolidated. This shift supports management's multi-year sales targets.
3.
Planned closure: Unilever announced on July 28, 2026, that the planned combination of its Foods business with McCormick is on track to complete by mid-2027 at the latest. The company has established dedicated crossfunctional project teams comprising more than 200 employees to manage the separation and integration process. Management remains fully focused on driving performance in Foods until the transaction officially closes in 2027. This guidance implies that Foods will remain in continuing operations for the entirety of fiscal year 2026.
4.
Severe friction: Unilever projected on April 30, 2026, that it will face post-separation stranded costs of €400m to €500m following the Foods transaction. This scale of stranded costs compares poorly with the €250m in annual stranded costs left behind after the Spreads divestment in 2017. To mitigate this margin drag, the company plans to deliver offset savings over the 2027 to 2029 period while incurring €500m in one-off restructuring costs. If unaddressed, these stranded expenses will materially dilute continuing operations EPS post-merger.
5.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research calculated on August 11, 2026, that net stranded costs from the Food exit will only be €250m in fiscal year 2027. This assumption is significantly lower than corporate guidance because it assumes rapid execution of offsetting productivity measures. However, if the company fails to execute its productivity program quickly, the full gross €500m stranded cost drag will pressure Core Unilever operating margins. This discrepancy highlights the potential for near-term margin volatility depending on the speed of cost elimination.

Historical Carve-Out Precedents

6.
Structured separation: Unilever completed the demerger of its Ice Cream business on December 6, 2025, which was subsequently listed as The Magnum Ice Cream Company on December 8, 2025. The legal and operational separation of this unit was achieved through a series of sequenced corporate reorganisations initiated on July 1, 2025. The company reported Ice Cream as a discontinued operation in its 2025 Annual Report and Accounts. This historical precedent indicates that Unilever requires approximately six months between operational carve-out and final demerger listing.
7.
Deferred completion: Unilever had to delay the transfer of Ice Cream operations in India and Portugal to the demerged entity until the first half of 2026 due to regional regulatory and structuring complexities. These delayed transactions generated a net loss of €11m for Unilever in 2026 but were offset by a €299m gain on disposal. This case demonstrates that global portfolio separations often require tail-end transitional agreements that extend past the main deal completion date. Investors must expect similar regional delays for the Foods business in complex jurisdictions.
8.
Sluggish execution: Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on November 26, 2025, that Unilever's prior divestments of its Spreads and Tea businesses took far longer to execute and were less clean than the Ice Cream demerger. The Tea carve-out was slowed significantly by its heavy reliance on shared ambient distribution networks and the operational complexity of large retained assets. Because Foods also shares extensive regional distribution channels, the upcoming separation could replicate these past operational delays rather than the clean break of Ice Cream. This history suggests that Foods may require complex transitional service agreements.
9.
Complex exclusions: Hindustan Unilever completed the demerger of its own Ice Cream business into Kwality Wall's India in December 2025 with a separate listing in February 2026. This local separation was required because the global Ice Cream demerger did not automatically incorporate complex Indian legal entities at the initial close. Because the current McCormick deal also excludes the Indian Foods business, Unilever faces a similar multi-stage legal process to separate these regional assets. This complexity will require prolonged transitional service agreements.

Transaction Valuation and Market Multiples

10.
Premium valuation: Unilever agreed on April 1, 2026, to combine its Foods business with McCormick in a transaction implying an enterprise value of $44.8bn. This valuation represents a multiple of approximately 13.8x fiscal year 2025 EBITDA, which matches the implied 13.8x multiple of McCormick itself. This transaction multiple compares favourably with previous divestments and reflects a premium valuation for the cash-generating Foods division. The strong multiple helps support Unilever's capital return plans.
11.
Conservative valuation: Jefferies Research reported on March 31, 2026, that it valued Unilever Foods at a lower multiple of 11x EV/EBITDA based on comparable food sector precedents. The broker assigned an 11.8x multiple to the high-performing Power Brands while applying a lower 8.5x multiple to non-Power Brands. This analytical approach suggests that public markets may undervalue the Foods division relative to the transaction multiple achieved with McCormick. If public markets maintain this discount, Unilever's pro forma share price could experience initial pressure post-transaction.
12.
Other side of the story: Unilever's aggressive timeline for completing the Foods transaction by mid-2027 may result in significant operational dis-synergies that erode post-deal profitability. Bernstein Research reported on June 1, 2026, that some investors believe the rush to complete this deal quickly creates a misalignment between leadership and shareholders. Furthermore, because the major Indian Foods business is excluded from the deal, Unilever must manage a complex parallel operating structure. Consequently, the speed of execution may compromise the long-term margins of both the continuing and divested perimeters.

Section 3 – Restatement of Historical Financials to Continuing Perimeter

Section summary
  1. Unilever will restate its historical accounts to cleanly reflect the continuing household and personal care perimeter.
  2. Historical demerger precedents demonstrate that Unilever will separate its assets and liabilities to support comparability.
  3. Peer transactions like Procter and Gamble's Reverse Morris Trust show that discontinued operations presentation simplifies reporting.

Financial Separation Precedents

1.
Significant adjustments: Unilever will implement an extensive restatement of its historical accounts to reflect the new continuing household and personal care perimeter. This research analyzed ten relevant sources, two key companies, and two historical precedents to evaluate the structural separation of the Foods business. This historical realignment is expected to remove Foods turnover and related operating profit from continuing operations metrics to allow for a direct comparison of the pure-play household and personal care business.
2.
Asset allocation: Unilever had to allocate shared depreciation costs of certain assets during the prior Ice Cream separation process. The company charged these depreciation expenses as part of transitional service agreements to ensure that the standalone entity could reflect its true operating footprint. This prior case indicates that the restated continuing perimeter will initially exclude certain overheads that are temporarily covered by transitional fees.
3.
Historical precedent: Unilever previously restated its historical financials on March 12, 2026, to exclude the demerged Ice Cream business from its continuing operations. The company presented restated historical income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statements for fiscal years 2024 and 2025 to ensure consistency for investors following the demerger of The Magnum Ice Cream Company. This prior adjustment demonstrates that the company will likely publish a standalone restatement package several months before completing the McCormick transaction.
4.
Segment restatement: Unilever reported that the historical demerger of its Ice Cream business required reclassifying €7,924 million of 2023 turnover as discontinued operations. Under the prior restatement, the continuing operations turnover for the first half of 2025 was set at €25,506 million compared to €26,507 million in the first half of 2024. This clean presentation helped insulate the continuing business groups from the volatile seasonal performance of the ice cream category.

Peer Precedents and Discontinued Operations

5.
Comparable transaction: Procter and Gamble completed the divestiture of forty-one beauty brands to Coty on October 1, 2016, utilizing a tax-free Reverse Morris Trust structure. In accordance with GAAP guidance, the company presented the historical results of the Beauty Brands business cleanly as discontinued operations and excluded them from continuing operations. This peer comparison indicates that Unilever will follow a similar reporting method under IFRS 5 for its Foods transaction.
6.
Diluted metrics: Procter and Gamble reported that its diluted earnings per share from continuing operations was $1.06 for the quarter ending September 30, 2017. The company's prior period results from discontinued operations were adjusted to reflect transition costs associated with the Beauty Brands sale to Coty. This historical precedent suggests that Unilever's restated GAAP earnings per share may suffer temporary downward pressure from upfront separation expenses.
7.
Clean restatements: Procter and Gamble excluded the divested beauty business from its reportable segment results for the three months ended September 30, 2016, to maintain comparability. The historical net sales for the divested beauty brands were reported as $1,159 million for that quarter with a corresponding loss of $72 million from discontinued operations before income taxes. This peer precedent suggests that removing the lower-growth foods business will dramatically simplify the remaining corporate reporting structure.

Stranded Cost Accounting Drag

Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research cautioned on August 11, 2026, that Unilever's restated historical margins may look structurally lower if the company cannot quickly eliminate the remaining stranded costs. The broker estimated that gross stranded costs from the Ice Cream demerger were €300 million and noted that Foods stranded costs could reach €500 million. This indicates that the historical restated margins may initially be diluted until productivity programs of €1 billion are fully executed.

Section 4 – Share Consolidation Dynamics and Distribution Ratios

Section summary
  1. Unilever is highly likely to implement a share consolidation to prevent share price and dividend dilution following the Foods transaction.
  2. Mathematical transaction metrics imply a large equity component that will redistribute value to Unilever shareholders via combined-company shares.
  3. High confidence in the share consolidation is supported by the direct precedent of the December 2025 Ice Cream demerger.

Precedents and Share Price Continuity

1.
Expected adjustment: Rothschild and Co Redburn reported on March 25, 2026, that Unilever is expected to address the massive earnings dilution from the Foods separation through a share consolidation. Because the Foods division represents about 25% of sales and 32% of total profits, its separation will immediately create nearterm dilution that would otherwise depress the parent company's stock price. Management will likely use this technical consolidation to maintain historical comparability of per-share metrics and ensure that the dividend-per-share series remains continuous for income-focused investors.
2.
Successful consolidation: Unilever implemented a highly comparable corporate action in December 2025 to manage the demerger of its Ice Cream business. The company executed an eight-for-nine share consolidation on December 8, 2025, to preserve the comparability of Unilever's share price, earnings per share, and dividends. Under this arrangement, shareholders received eight new ordinary shares with a nominal value of 3.5 pence for every nine existing shares previously held. This technical adjustment successfully offset the structural value reduction resulting from the spin-off of The Magnum Ice Cream Company.
3.
Retroactive restatements: Unilever's historical accounting treatment of the Ice Cream demerger provides a clear roadmap for the upcoming Foods transaction. Following the consolidation, the company adjusted its average share count retrospectively in all presented historical periods to ensure consistent comparisons. This restatement allowed the basic and diluted EPS from continuing operations to be presented cleanly as if the separation had already occurred. A similar retrospective restatement will be essential for preventing artificial EPS drops once the Foods transaction completes.

Transaction Math and Exchange Ratios

4.
Diluted ownership: McCormick and Company announced on March 31, 2026, that the combination will be structured using a tax-free Reverse Morris Trust. Under the agreed terms, Unilever and its shareholders will receive a proportionate mix of voting and non-voting McCormick common stock equating to 65% of the combined entity's fully diluted equity. Upon closing, Unilever's public shareholders will directly own 55.1% of the combined company, while Unilever PLC will retain a 9.9% stake, and existing McCormick shareholders will hold the remaining 35% of the combined shares. This structure is designed to maximize tax efficiency for European investors while delivering direct equity upside.
5.
Theoretical value: Unilever's transaction details from March 31, 2026, value the equity consideration at approximately $29.1 bn based on McCormick's volume-weighted average share price of $57.84. In addition to the shares, Unilever will receive $15.7 bn in upfront cash on closing, bringing the implied enterprise value of the separated Foods business to approximately $44.8 bn. This cash inflow will be allocated to fund €6 bn of share buybacks between 2026 and 2029 to further support earnings accretion. These buybacks will help compress Unilever's share count and partially offset the loss of Foods earnings.
6.
Hypothetical dilution: Bernstein Research analyzed a hypothetical Reverse Morris Trust scenario on March 24, 2026, assuming Unilever would distribute Foods shares to its shareholders before merging with McCormick. The broker estimated that Unilever would distribute FoodCo shares representing approximately 29% of Unilever's outstanding shares to its shareholders. In this model, Unilever shareholders exchange their FoodCo shares for combined-company stock at a theoretical exchange ratio of approximately 1.1x. This mathematical exchange demonstrates how value is transferred from the parent perimeter to the new standalone flavor entity.

Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on March 20, 2026, that the transaction's ultimate structure could create inversion risks if the final equity swap results in Unilever shareholders owning more than 60% of the US-listed combined entity. To mitigate this risk, the broker suggested that McCormick might be enticed to inject more cash funded by incremental debt to keep McCormick shareholder ownership above 30%. Other side of the story: Unilever's reliance on a share consolidation to mask the earnings-per-share dilution of the Foods transaction may fail to satisfy institutional investors. While the consolidation will mathematically maintain share price and dividend-per-share continuity, it does not alter the underlying loss of cash flow and operational diversification. If the pure-play household and personal care business faces rising raw material costs or fails to expand its premium beauty margins, the consolidated share price could suffer a severe multiple de-rating regardless of technical accounting adjustments.

Section 5 – Broker and Consensus Adjustments to Per-Share Metrics

Section summary
  1. Sell-side brokers expect the McCormick transaction to initially dilute Unilever's earnings per share by twenty-one to thirty-two percent.
  2. Analysts expect a technical share consolidation and a six billion euro buyback program to successfully mitigate this near-term dilution.

Analyst Consensus and Valuation Adjustments

1.
Upward revision: Deutsche Bank updated its model to project Unilever's adjusted earnings per share of 3.39 euros for fiscal year 2027 and 3.61 euros for 2028. The broker expects the standalone business excluding the Foods division to trade at a premium multiple of 17.5 times forward earnings. This valuation represents a substantial markup over the historical median of the European consumer staples sector. Consequently, sell-side analysts are premium-rating the pure-play household and personal care perimeter.
2.
Technical dilution: Jefferies Research modeled a pro forma reconciliation assuming the transaction occurs on January 1, 2027, which results in a core earnings per share of 2.48 euros. This pro forma figure is substantially lower than the pre-deal consensus estimate of 3.42 euros per share. The model incorporates a 141 million euro incremental profit contribution from the retained 9.9% associate stake. This adjustment illustrates that the transaction initially dilutes parent per-share metrics prior to share consolidations.
3.
Optimistic outlook: Bernstein Research reported that Unilever management expects the McCormick transaction to be mid-to-high single-digit accretive to earnings per share in its first year. This accretion projection is calculated before factoring in any of the anticipated 600 million dollars in cost synergies. However, the broker noted that some investors remain cautious about execution risks. This skepticism could make the stock dead money for at least the next year.

Stranded Cost Drag and Capital Allocation

4.
Contrary evidence: Rothschild and Co Redburn calculated that the gross stranded costs left behind by the Foods exit could exceed 1 billion euros. This estimate is significantly worse than corporate guidance because it reflects the deep integration of Foods within shared shipping networks. Overcoming these structural overheads will require extensive transitional services agreements. Consequently, the historical continuing margins could face severe downward pressure if these costs persist.
5.
Structured mitigation: Morgan Stanley reported that Unilever will implement a transitional services agreement of up to two years to smooth the separation from McCormick. The company plans to spend 500 million euros on restructuring between 2027 and 2029 to eliminate stranded costs. These service agreements are designed to prevent operational dis-synergies during the transition. Consequently, forecast per-share figures will exclude these non-underlying restructuring charges.
6.
Consolidation pressure: Rothschild and Co Redburn reported that Unilever's pro forma earnings per share would drop by 32% if stranded costs are included. The broker expects management to address this major dilution through a technical share consolidation. This action would mirror the successful eight-for-nine consolidation implemented during the prior Ice Cream separation. Consequently, FactSet and other index providers will adjust historical share counts retrospectively.
7.
Capital support: Morgan Stanley reported that Unilever will utilize the cash proceeds to execute a 6 billion euro share buyback program between 2026 and 2029. The company will also focus on paying down outstanding debt to achieve a target leverage ratio of 2.0 times. These structural measures are designed to offset dilution and support compounding per-share growth.
8.
174 175 side of the story: Rothschild and Co Redburn highlights that the remaining business may experience prolonged earnings dilution due to the complexity of European labor laws. The broker warns that these strict regulations could prevent management from quickly eliminating the gross stranded costs. Consequently, the continuing household and personal care division is unlikely to achieve its margin targets as quickly as consensus models assume.
