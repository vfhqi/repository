However, exhaustive database searches across broker research and corporate filings yielded no substantive findings regarding any published short-seller reports targeting Loomis AB, and the available archives do not contain records of historical short-seller attacks or public allegations of fraud against the company.

Loomis AB's earnings could collapse over the next three years if the cash-to-digital transition accelerates dramatically across its core European markets, resulting in a permanent reduction in cash transactions that directly erodes legacy transit volumes and route density, causing the group's fixed transport networks to suffer from rapidly declining capacity utilization.

Additionally, the company faces significant operational hurdles following its SEK 3 billion acquisition of Hermes Transportes Blindados in Peru, as unidentified compliance or licensing issues could cause severe disturbances in the integration process, resulting in delayed financial reporting and unrecoverable administrative expenses. The overall likelihood of the bear case fully materializing over the next 18 months is assessed as medium, as cash in circulation is steadily rising in the United States and the Eurozone, which shields the company from immediate volume collapse and provides a defensive baseline that helps offset mature market transactional declines.

Critical early warning signs that would signal imminent operational distress include rising small-to-medium business customer churn, severe contract losses in core European countries, and sharp increases in net debt to EBITDA ratios. Investors can monitor leading indicators such as rising small-to-medium business customer churn to detect early signs of operational distress, as severe contract losses in core European countries like Sweden and France would signal accelerating network decay, while a sharp rise in net debt to EBITDA above 2.0x would warn of materially weakened credit metrics from underperforming acquisitions.
