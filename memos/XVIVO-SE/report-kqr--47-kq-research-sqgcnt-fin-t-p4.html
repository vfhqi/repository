Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence partly answers the Key Question regarding specific inputs that will drive Xvivo Perfusion AB's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, with strong disclosure on capitalization and depreciation schedules but significant gaps on tax rate normalization, currency hedging decisions, and the precise timing of US regulatory approvals and commercial adoption rates. Xvivo's capitalized development balance reached SEK 800.68 million at end-2025, having grown substantially as the company capitalized SEK 152.80 million during 2025 while only recording SEK 20.10 million of amortization, and then capitalized another SEK 131.70 million in the first nine months of 2026. The European CE mark for the Heart Assist Transport system triggered immediate amortization at a rate of SEK 39 million per year over ten years, with the first monthly charge of SEK 3.20 million appearing in Q3 2026. This massive step-up in non-cash charges will create significant earnings headwinds unless revenue scales rapidly, particularly as US heart and liver launches begin amortizing their own capitalized assets upon commercial approval.

Xvivo's effective tax rate expanded sharply to 35 percent in 2025 from 14 percent in 2024, despite profit before tax dropping to SEK 38.56 million from SEK 199.95 million, driven by permanent tax differences and foreign rate mismatches that severely bloated the consolidated tax liability relative to profits. The primary driver was a SEK 66.60 million net currency loss within financial items in 2025, compared to a gain of SEK 32.70 million in 2024, which artificially drove up the reported rate because these losses occurred at the parent level where tax deductibility was limited. As US profitability rises following the heart launch, the geographic profit mix will shift toward higher-tax jurisdictions, permanently elevating Xvivo's long-term consolidated tax rate unless transfer pricing strategies successfully mitigate this effect.

Currency exposure remains severe and entirely unhedged, with sales in US dollars accounting for approximately 55 percent of total revenue (about SEK 450 million) and the Euro contributing approximately 35 percent of overall revenues. Management has published explicit guidance that a 10 percent depreciation of the US dollar against the Swedish krona would reduce global sales by approximately 5 percent (representing a SEK 45 million reduction ) and translate directly into a decrease of EBITDA margins by approximately 2 percentage points.

This unhedged structure creates massive bottom-line volatility, and the company's refusal to implement derivative hedging instruments leaves earnings per share highly vulnerable to Swedish krona strengthening during the critical US commercial launch phase.

Capital structure remains conservative, with the group utilizing approximately EUR 8 million of its EUR 20 million revolving credit facility as of Q3 2026, leaving an unused credit line of EUR 12 million that provides sufficient short-term financial flexibility. Shareholders authorized the board to resolve new share issues of up to 10 percent of capital, representing a maximum of 3,149,947 shares, although management has not exercised this authority because cash positions remain robust.

Outstanding employee incentive schemes carry a maximum aggregate dilution of only 1.35 percent of capital and votes if fully exercised, meaning that employee share awards will not materially depress earnings per share over the forecast period. However, slower-than-expected US market penetration could deplete cash reserves and trigger a dilutive financing event using the board's issuance authority.

One-off items continue to distort reported profitability, with thoracic gross margin declining to 80 percent from 89 percent in 2025 due to R&D inventory write-downs, demonstrating that operating profits remain highly volatile and difficult to forecast accurately. A sudden accounting transition for machine placements created a substantial topline boost in Q3 2026, with a SEK 8.50 million positive operating profit effect from upfront finance lease recognition for three XPS machines, pulling forward future revenues and creating near-term margin expansion at the expense of long-term recurring lease income.

The Services segment faces severe profitability challenges, with net sales falling 28 percent to SEK 14 million in Q3 2026 while the gross margin collapsed to negative 19 percent, threatening consolidated margins unless new contracts are secured quickly. DNB Carnegie forecasts depreciation as a percentage of sales to reach 8.30 percent in 2026, expanding to 8.80 percent in both 2027 and 2028, reflecting the scaling of Swedish production facilities and the conclusion of the tenfold capacity project, which will limit near-term EBIT margin expansion despite strong sales.

In its longterm DCF valuation, DNB Carnegie applies a constant statutory tax rate assumption of 24 percent, but its operational model projects a paid tax rate of approximately 15 percent through 2028, implying that cash tax shields will persist in the near term. DNB Carnegie modeled net financial items of SEK 27 million for 2026, followed by net financial losses of SEK 2 million in 2027 and SEK 5 million in 2028, assuming currency revaluation volatility will subside to nominal levels in outer years.

RedEye utilizes a flat tax rate assumption of 21 percent within its base-case DCF valuation, yielding a fair value of SEK 360 per share, which is closer to the Swedish statutory rate and suggests highly efficient transfer pricing will offset US tax rate premiums. RedEye's updated estimates incorporate Xvivo's transition to a 510(k) pathway for US liver access, which is considerably faster and lower-cost than the previously planned PMA, meaning capitalized R&D will begin amortizing in early 2028 rather than 2029 and reducing total capitalized assets before amortization begins. DNB Carnegie models Xvivo's outstanding share count to remain constant at 31.50 million shares through 2028, expecting all projected EBITDA growth to flow directly into earnings per share rather than being diluted by capital raises during the US commercial launch. Critical evidence gaps remain regarding the exact scope of usability trials required for the US Liver Assist 510(k) pathway, the precise timing of revenue decline from Xvivo's largest US lung customer as it transitions to its own EVLP platform, and the rate at which Western European centers will adopt the newly CE-marked Heart Assist system.

RedEye adjusted its US heart launch projection from late 2027 to early 2028, demonstrating how easily modeling assumptions can shift, and if FDA approval is delayed beyond early 2028, ongoing clinical trial capitalization will persist longer than expected, severely depressing projected near-term EPS relative to broker forecasts. The absence of any formal hedging policy means that persistent Swedish krona strengthening could materially undermine the company's ability to translate strong operational performance into bottom-line earnings growth.

The evidence favors a cautious interpretation of consensus earnings models because sell-side analysts have not fully incorporated the compounding impact of stepped-up amortization charges, rising effective tax rates from US profit scaling, and the material downside risk from unhedged currency transaction exposures. While Xvivo's clinical superiority and favorable regulatory pathways provide substantial upside potential if commercial adoption accelerates, the company's near-term earnings per share trajectory remains highly sensitive to regulatory timing, customer transition speed, and macroeconomic currency movements that lie outside management's control.

Investors should focus on quarterly disclosures regarding US heart PMA review timelines, European hospital registration rates, Services segment contract wins, and any board decisions to implement formal FX hedging policies as critical signposts for whether the optimistic broker scenarios will materialize or require further downward revision.

Section 2 – Amortization of Capitalized Development and Depreciation

Capitalization and Amortization Timelines

1.
Capitalized development balance: Capitalized development expenditure reached SEK 800.68 million at the end of 2025. This asset balance grew substantially as Xvivo capitalized SEK 152.80 million during 2025 while only recording SEK 20.10 million of amortization. During the first nine months of 2026, Xvivo capitalized another SEK 131.70 million of R&D costs. This deferred recognition means future earnings per share will face steep amortization headwinds once regulatory assets are fully commercialized.
2.
Amortization step-up: The receipt of the European CE mark for the Heart Assist Transport system in late 2026 triggers immediate amortization. Xvivo began amortizing this specific asset over a ten-year useful life at a rate of SEK 39 million per year. The Q3 2026 interim report showed the first monthly charge of SEK 3.20 million. Slower sales scaling in Europe would result in this high fixed charge immediately depressing operating margins and bottom-line EPS.
3.
Heart and liver US timelines: Regulatory timelines in the United States govern when the remaining capitalized R&D starts amortizing. The FDA received Xvivo's Premarket Approval application for the Heart Assist Transport system in September 2026. Xvivo is targeting a US commercial launch for the heart system by the end of 2027. For the liver program, Xvivo planned a 510(k) submission for H1 2027, with a US commercial launch targeted for early 2028 pending approval. Amortization for these major programs will begin upon their respective launches, creating consecutive EPS headwinds.
4.
Capitalization decline: Management suggests that the capitalization of development expenses will eventually fall as regulatory trials conclude. The company raised SEK 440 million of capital dedicated to the US heart and liver trials. Clinical trial capitalization will continue over the next few years until the US launches are finalized. As capitalization declines, more R&D costs will flow directly through the income statement, further pressuring EPS.

Xvivo reported SEK 39.31 million of depreciation and impairment of property, plant, and equipment in 2025, up from SEK 32.44 million in 2024. During the first nine months of 2026, Xvivo invested SEK 30.60 million in property, plant, and equipment, focusing on production facilities in Sweden. This ongoing investment guarantees that depreciation charges will rise continuously over the 18-month to three-year forecast period.

Section 3 – Tax Rate Drivers and Intercompany Structure

Section summary
  1. Unhedged currency losses and non-deductible items drove Xvivo's effective tax rate to 35 percent in 2025.
  2. Rising US profitability as the heart technology launches will shift Xvivo's tax mix toward higher- tax jurisdictions.
  3. Nordic peer Bonesupport demonstrates that scaling US revenue triggers substantial operating leverage and complex deferred tax utilization.

Swedish Statutory Rate and 2025 Drivers

1.
Swedish statutory rate: Swedish corporate tax regulations dictate a baseline statutory rate of 20.6 percent for domestic operations. This rate serves as the starting point for calculating Xvivo's tax expenses before adjusting for international tax rate differences and permanent differences. Consequently, domestic profits are taxed at a lower rate than profits generated in key growth markets like the United States.
2.
Effective tax rate expansion: Xvivo reported a consolidated effective tax rate of 35 percent in 2025, up from 14 percent in 2024. Profit before tax dropped to SEK 38.56 million in 2025 from SEK 199.95 million in 2024, yet tax expense remained high at SEK 13.40 million. This high rate occurred because permanent tax differences and foreign rate mismatches severely bloated the consolidated effective tax liability relative to profits.
3.
Currency loss impacts: Financial items in 2025 were heavily depressed by currency volatility, which directly distorted the tax reconciliation. Xvivo recorded a SEK 66.60 million net currency loss within financial items in 2025, compared to a gain of SEK 32.70 million in 2024. Because these currency losses occurred at the parent level where tax deductibility was limited, they artificially drove up the reported rate for the entire group.
4.
Reconciliation details: Permanent differences played a major role in pushing the 2025 effective rate far above the Swedish statutory rate. Xvivo's statutory tax on 2025 earnings would have been SEK 7.94 million, but nondeductible expenses of SEK 14.20 million and foreign tax differences of SEK 1.28 million increased the burden. These permanent additions mean that non-deductible items heavily penalize EPS during years with depressed pre-tax income.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Xvivo's underlying domestic tax rate remains stable and will normalize once currency volatility subsides. DNB Carnegie notes that adjusting for one-off net financials and positive tax effects in Q3 2026 actually led to an upgrade in near-term EPS estimates. This suggests that unhedged currency swings are temporary and do not reflect structural long-term tax inefficiency.

US Scaling and Deferred Taxes

6.
US launch tax shift: Launching the Heart Assist Transport system in the United States will fundamentally alter Xvivo's geographic profit mix. The company expects the majority of short-to-mid-term growth to be concentrated in the US, which carries a higher corporate tax rate than Sweden. Unless offset by domestic transfer pricing, rising US profits will permanently elevate Xvivo's long-term consolidated tax rate.
7.
Deferred tax assets: Consolidated deferred tax assets shrank significantly as Xvivo utilized its historical Swedish loss carry-forwards. Total deferred tax assets fell to SEK 21.04 million in 2025 from SEK 32.45 million in 2024, primarily driven by a drop in capitalized loss carry-forwards to SEK 12.73 million. This reduction means that tax shield protections are fading, leading to higher cash tax payments in Sweden.
8.
Intercompany transfer structure: Intercompany transactions represent a dominant portion of the parent company's operational flow, raising transfer pricing risks. In 2025, intercompany revenues accounted for 58 percent of the parent company's total transactions, down from 63 percent in 2024. Because internal pricing is based on the arm's length principle, any regulatory audit of these flows could trigger severe retrospective tax adjustments across jurisdictions.

Peer Analysis of US Scaling

9.
Bonesupport scaling comparison: Nordic orthobiologics peer Bonesupport Holding AB provides an excellent case study of tax dynamics during rapid US commercial scaling. Bonesupport's US sales grew by 46 percent in constant exchange rates in 2025, driving total net sales to SEK 1,175 million. This scaling shows that high gross margins of 93 percent can generate rapid profitability, triggering sudden transitions from tax benefits to material tax expenses.
10.
Bonesupport tax transitions: Bonesupport's rapid transition to profitability illustrates how deferred tax assets can distort reported tax lines. Bonesupport recorded a tax expense of SEK 38.90 million in 2024, compared to a massive tax income of SEK 229.30 million in 2023 when it first recognized its deferred tax assets. This shows that DTA recognition creates massive earnings volatility before statutory tax payments actually normalize.
11.
Bonesupport loss carry forwards: Bonesupport's tax strategy relies heavily on Sweden's group contribution rights to recover research deficits. The parent company recognized Swedish tax losses carried forward of SEK 19.53 million in 2024, which are legally recoverable across the group. This indicates that Nordic medtech peers can shield domestic profits even while scaling highly profitable commercial operations in the United States.

Sell-Side Modeling Assumptions

In its long-term DCF valuation, the broker applies a constant statutory tax rate assumption of 24 percent. However, its operational model projects a paid tax rate of approximately 15 percent through 2028, implying that cash tax shields will persist near-term.
13.
RedEye valuation tax rate: RedEye's valuation models incorporate a slightly lower long-term corporate tax rate for Xvivo's discounted cash flows. RedEye utilizes a flat tax rate assumption of 21 percent within its base-case DCF valuation, which yields a fair value of SEK 360 per share. This rate is closer to the Swedish statutory rate, suggesting that RedEye assumes highly efficient transfer pricing will offset US tax rate premiums.

This lack of visible data means that broader consensus tax expectations remain opaque to the public market.
15.
Other side of the story: Although geographic profit shifts and fading tax assets pose headwinds, Xvivo's intercompany transfer structure can be optimized to mitigate tax leakage. By utilizing centralized Swedish IP ownership and charging appropriate service fees to US subsidiaries, the company can keep consolidated effective tax rates near statutory levels of 21 percent. This transfer pricing flexibility means that scaling US revenues will not necessarily lead to a dramatic deterioration in net margins or EPS.

Section 4 – Financial Items and Currency Sensitivities

Section summary
  1. Xvivo does not hedge its foreign exchange revenues, making reported earnings highly sensitive to fluctuations in the US dollar and Euro.
  2. Peer companies Vitrolife and Ambu similarly utilize natural hedging strategies instead of active financial derivatives to manage currency transaction risk.
  3. Sell-side analysts expect currency revaluation volatility to normalize to nominal levels, but models remain exposed to potential Swedish krona strengthening.

Unhedged Assets and Currency Mix

1.
High currency exposure: Xvivo's top-line performance remains heavily dependent on foreign currency rates because the Swedish krona is its reporting currency. Management reported that sales in US dollars accounted for approximately 55 percent of total revenue in 2025, which represents about SEK 450 million. The Euro represents another major currency for Xvivo, contributing approximately 35 percent of overall revenues. Consequently, the business experiences significant transaction exposure as most sales occur in foreign currencies while the parent reporting currency remains in Swedish kronor.
2.
Cash currency holdings: Monetary assets held by Xvivo contain a high proportion of foreign currencies that fluctuate without formal currency hedges. The company reported that its consolidated financial assets valued at amortized cost amounted to SEK 500 million at the end of September 2026. Cash and cash equivalents reached SEK 313.20 million in the same period, of which a substantial portion was denominated in US dollars and Euros. This concentration of unhedged monetary assets means that consolidated net profit fluctuates wildly due to period-end balance sheet revaluations.
3.
Mismatched operational costs: Operating expenses at Xvivo present a structural mismatch relative to its geographically diversified revenue streams. The group's cost base is primarily concentrated in Swedish kronor and Euros, while a growing portion of US commercial launch costs is denominated in US dollars. External sales from its American subsidiary are naturally matched with local dollar outflows, but parent company expenses are mostly settled in Swedish kronor. This cost structure guarantees that operating margins remain highly sensitive to the relative strength of the US dollar.
4.
Unhedged risk profile: Executive management confirms that Xvivo does not utilize derivative financial instruments to manage foreign exchange transaction risks. The company currently leaves its foreign currency revenue streams entirely unhedged, creating direct operational vulnerability to a strengthening Swedish krona. While management has the authorization to implement derivative hedging structures, they continue to rely solely on natural operational offsets. This unhedged approach means currency volatility directly impacts earnings per share during periods of Swedish krona strength.

Published Management Currency Sensitivities

5.
Quantified currency sensitivities: Management has published explicit guidance regarding the operational impact of a weakening US dollar on Xvivo's consolidated results. Assuming a 10 percent depreciation of the US dollar against the Swedish krona, global sales would fall by approximately 5 percent, representing a SEK 45 million reduction. Management estimates that this 10 percent dollar decline would translate directly into a decrease of EBITDA margins by approximately 2 percentage points. Therefore, unhedged dollar depreciation severely compresses the company's operating leverage.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some sell-side analysts believe the company's underlying currency risk is lower than published sensitivities because of strong US commercial scaling. DNB Carnegie upgraded its near-term earnings projections in October 2026 despite persistent currency headwinds. The broker noted that adjusting for one-off net financials and positive tax effects actually revealed strengthened underlying operational profitability. This indicates that rapid commercial volume scaling can effectively neutralize negative transaction impacts over the forecast period.
7.
Swings in financial items: Periodic revaluations of cash balances generate large non-cash gains and losses within Xvivo's net financial items. During the first nine months of 2026, exchange rate differences in cash and cash equivalents contributed a positive SEK 19.20 million to net income, compared to a loss of SEK 32.60 million in 2025. In Q3 2026 alone, cash currency revaluations added SEK 7.10 million to the bottom line. This volatility proves that net financials will remain unpredictable without a formal cash hedging policy.

Peer Practices in Nordic Medtech

8.
Peer natural hedging: Nordic reproductive health peer Vitrolife pursues an identical treasury policy of relying on natural operational currency matches rather than financial derivatives. Vitrolife is highly exposed to currency fluctuations because its sales are global, yet its reporting currency is Swedish kronor. The company continuously seeks to align its net debt across currencies in which it generates revenues, primarily the US dollar and Euro. Vitrolife explicitly avoids using financial derivatives as hedging instruments, mirroring Xvivo's operational strategy.
9.
Peer sensitivity comparisons: Transaction sensitivities at Vitrolife highlight the massive scale of unhedged exposure typical of Nordic medtech companies. Vitrolife reported that a 10 percent change in the Swedish krona against the Euro and US dollar would impact operating income by SEK 47 million and SEK 27 million, respectively. Across all major foreign currencies, a 10 percent fluctuation would impact pre-tax income by +/- SEK 114 million. This peer comparison demonstrates that large unhedged transaction exposures are standard across Nasdaq Stockholm medtech peers.
10.
Peer scope hedging: Single-use endoscopy peer Ambu similarly relies on natural currency matching while facing material translation risk on its Danish krone reporting line. Ambu invoices slightly more than 50 percent of its total revenues in US dollars, while 31 percent is in Euros or Danish kroner. The company's production and capacity costs are settled in US dollars, Danish kroner, Euros, Malaysian ringgit, and Chinese renminbi. Ambu relies on natural hedging of foreign currency transactions, leaving its net income exposed.
