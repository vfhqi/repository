Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The development of volumes versus prices determines if hobby giants can sustain long-term earnings growth.
  2. Understanding revenue mixes and customer conversion channels reveals the safest asset to own in the premium consumer segment.

Core Questions and Sizing the

1.
Opportunity Critical inquiry: "Is Games Workshop's growth now coming mainly from price rather than from more hobbyists? Since the 11th edition of Warhammer 40,000 launched in June 2026, how have its core sales volumes, new trade accounts and like-for-like sales in its own stores moved, and what do brokers say explains the fall of about 16% in its share price between July and October 2026? For tonies, has the 37% rise in United States figurine prices changed how many figurines each Toniebox owner buys? For Asmodee, how much of its growth comes from distributed Pokémon and Magic cards against its own games, and what do brokers expect after the Pokémon anniversary year? Is there evidence that screen adaptations bring in new collectors?"
2.
Significant risk: Games Workshop faces potential earnings volatility if its revenue growth relies primarily on price increases rather than expanding its customer base. Former wargaming employees suggest that the company's growth is increasingly driven by price, raising concerns that the core hobbyist base may be approaching its spending limit. Since miniatures are highly price inelastic, further pricing actions could protect near-term margins but risk accelerating customer churn long term, which would ultimately depress future earnings per share.
3.
High elasticity: Tonies SE relies heavily on its US expansion to drive global growth and scale its high-margin figurine segment. Analysts at NuWays AG note that the 37% price increase for US figurines tests the limits of consumer demand within this highly sensitive category. If this price hike reduces the number of figurines purchased per Toniebox owner, the company's high contribution margins of over 30% could contract, severely impacting expected EPS growth in coming years.
4.
Unbalanced mix: Asmodee Group AB relies heavily on partner-published trading card games, which generate lower gross margins than its proprietary board games. Brokers at DNB Carnegie highlight that the Pokémon franchise alone accounts for 20% to 40% of Asmodee's total sales, representing a massive concentration risk ahead of the 30th anniversary year in 2026. A post- anniversary slowdown in TCG sales, combined with high third-party dependency, could stall margin expansion and disappoint consensus EPS forecasts for the standalone entity.

5.
Consumer Engagement Uncertain conversion: Games Workshop hopes transmedia initiatives like the upcoming Amazon partnership will sustainably expand its customer acquisition pipeline. Expert interviews with former licensing managers suggest that while high-profile media collaborations enhance brand awareness, they face significant challenges converting viewers into active miniature collectors. If entertainment projects fail to generate core hobby volume, high-cost infrastructure investments could lead to underutilized capacity and weigh heavily on future EPS.
6.
Strategic choice: European hobby assets present distinct investment profiles ranging from vertically integrated IP owners to broad physical distribution platforms. While Games Workshop controls its entire value chain to capture high margins, Asmodee is highly dependent on third-party licenses, and tonies remains a specialized audio platform. Determining which company maintains the most sustainable customer volume growth will define the safest entry point for investors seeking exposure to this premium hobby sector.
7.
Other side of the story: Tabletop companies might possess stronger pricing power and brand resilience than skeptics assume, driven by vertical integration and intense community loyalty. Tabletop marketing directors argue that hobbyists exhibit highly completionist behavior and continue purchasing high-quality miniatures even during macroeconomic downturns. Under these conditions, the firms could comfortably maintain premium gross margins of approximately 70%, supporting resilient earnings growth despite price increases.

Section 2 – Games Workshop Growth Drivers and Share Price Correction

Section summary
  1. Games Workshop's volume growth remains robust post-launch of the eleventh edition of Warhammer.
  2. Physical trade expansions and strong cash returns continue to support wargaming demand despite price hikes.

Post-Launch Performance and

1.
Channel Trends Robust performance: Games Workshop achieved record revenues of £659.7 million in the fiscal year ended 31 May 2026, showcasing strong underlying demand. This represents sustained double-digit sales growth for the eighth consecutive year despite being an off-year in the product release cycle. Pretax profit of £275.7 million stood out, coming in 4% ahead of the board's guidance.
2.
Broadening reach: Games Workshop successfully expanded its global footprint by increasing its net trade accounts by 1,000 to reach approximately 9,100. Selling through physical independent outlets remains critical for geographic wargaming expansion, especially where dedicated retail stores are commercially unviable. Independent retailers expressed positive feedback regarding the allocation of the Warhammer 40,000 Armageddon box set launch.
3.
Divergent trends: Games Workshop experienced mixed results across its physical retail network, with North American actual sales declining by 1.5% to £50.9 million. Conversely, retail sales in Continental Europe reached record levels after climbing by 12.3% in actual terms. The wargaming manufacturer plans to open approximately 30 new physical stores to bolster real-world hobby communities.

Share Price Movement and

4.
Investor Sentiment Contrary evidence: The findings did not surface any broker notes directly attributing Games Workshop's 16% share price correction to specific financial misses, suggesting the retreat was merely temporary profit-taking after the launch. Jefferies highlights that the shares tracked back recently despite the business delivering comfortable double-digit underlying progress. Additionally, trading through late August remained in line with board expectations, indicating the Warhammer 40,000 11th Edition launch met its internal sales plan.
5.
Rapid cash return: Games Workshop announced an ad-hoc dividend of 70 pence per share in September 2026, taking the cumulative year-to-date payout to £3. This represents increased cash generation versus prior periods when cumulative dividends stood at £2.25. Sell-side analysts view this step-up as a marginal positive that reflects comfortable double-digit underlying progress.
6.
Other side of the story: Investors could remain cautious because Games Workshop's wargaming volumes could decline if premium wargaming prices test the absolute spending limit of core hobbyists. Jefferies lists wargaming pricing impacting consumer demand and margin erosion among the key risk factors for Games Workshop's valuation. If wargaming customers exhibit price sensitivity, the company's dependency on constant currency trade growth of 18.3% could suffer.

Section 3 – tonies US Price Elasticity and Purchase Volumes

Section summary
  1. Structural US price increases for premium audio figurines have yielded significant revenue expansion without triggering consumer volume pushback.
  2. Deep brand attachment and high customer satisfaction drive highly predictable long-term figurine purchasing cohorts.

US Figurine Pricing and

1.
Margin Optimization Significant pricing: Tonies implemented selective price increases in the United States on May 1, 2025, to partially offset the financial impact of new trade tariffs on imports from China and Vietnam. The company consolidated its complex pricing structure down to three simplified tiers and raised Group 1 figurine prices to a flat $19.99, representing an increase of up to $2 on popular items. This strategic adjustment successfully protected the firm's margins during a volatile macroeconomic period while preserving consumer clarity.
2.
Tactical repositioning: Tonies simultaneously reduced the price of its Creative Tonies in the United States from a previous peak of $12.99 down to a flat $9.99. Management designed this price reduction to drive platform engagement and encourage broader consumer adoption of customizable content, although Creative Tonies comprise only a small fraction of total US revenues. This selective reduction helped the brand maintain an entry-level price point, thereby balancing the overall pricing index.
3.
Elevated average prices: Tonies experienced a substantial expansion in its global average selling price as a direct result of these North American pricing actions. The group's calculated figurine average selling price climbed by 37% year-on-year to reach €11.03 during the third quarter of 2025. This price-driven revenue uplift was supported by favorable currency tailwinds and an improving product mix, which together helped to reinforce the company's regional segment profit margins and offset import costs.
4.
Inelastic demand: Tonies demonstrated remarkable consumer demand resilience following its price increases, reporting only negligible volume adjustments post-implementation. Company management reported two months after the price hike that no substantial negative volume impact was visible across its major retail or direct channels. This structural inelasticity suggests that parents prioritize the platform's educational and entertainment value over marginal price variations, confirming strong brand equity.

Cohort Purchase Velocity and Customer Lifetime Value

5.
Predictable collection curves: Tonies consumers exhibit highly sticky and predictable purchasing habits, expanding their collection sizes steadily over multiple years. Empirical transaction data confirms that the average Toniebox owner purchases approximately 20 figurines within a 4.5-year window following their initial hardware acquisition. This recurring demand profile establishes a highly visible, subscription-like revenue stream that reduces the company's reliance on continuous hardware acquisitions to drive gross margins.
6.
Expanding engagement metrics: Tonies has seen its customer engagement strengthen over time, providing a solid foundation for long-term customer monetization. The average weekly playtime of active households climbed to 282 minutes by late 2025, reflecting deeper ecosystem integration. This growing engagement directly correlates with larger individual figurine collections, as children continuously seek fresh interactive content to use on their Toniebox devices, boosting recurring lifetime value.
7.
Compounding attach rates: Tonies registered accelerating momentum in its high-margin recurring business during the first half of fiscal year 2026. Constant currency figurine revenue expanded by 43% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, outpacing the underlying hardware growth cycle. This accelerating trend confirms that the enlarged installed base is actively buying more content figurines per device, which directly supports mid-term earnings per share goals.
8.
Contrary evidence: Tonies might face a future threat to its premium pricing model from a growing secondhand market that could divert demand away from its primary sales channels. Broker research from NuWays AG in October 2026 listed this secondhand ecosystem as a potential structural weakness for the primary brand, as used audio figurines are acquired at discounted rates. This dynamic could limit the company's ability to raise prices further without risking primary volume erosion.

Comparative Market Elasticity and Channel Strength

9.
Positive retail feedback: Walmart retail experts confirmed that Tonies figurines achieved exceptional sales growth during the critical fourth-quarter holiday season of 2025. The brand recorded figurine sales growth exceeding 30% year-on-year at the major retailer, comfortably outpacing the broader preschool toy category. While average unit retail prices rose due to necessary tariff-mitigation adjustments, underlying unit volume growth remained highly positive and exceeded prior-year results.
10.
Divergent retailer ordering: Walmart adjusted its procurement strategies to favor recurring figurine sales over additional hardware inventory during the peak 2025 trading period. The retail chain increased purchase order volumes specifically for popular figurines, while keeping hardware orders flat compared to the previous year. Figurines represent a reliable, high-volume segment that retail partners prioritize because they drive repeat customer foot traffic and minimize excess stock risk.
11.
Exceptional brand loyalty: Tonies maintains an exceptionally high Net Promoter Score of 77 in the United States, placing the brand ahead of major consumer technology leaders. This extraordinary level of consumer satisfaction exceeds the average scores of globally recognized brands such as Apple, Netflix, and Amazon. This strong consumer advocacy fuels a powerful, word-of-mouth marketing flywheel that reduces customer acquisition costs and supports premium retail pricing models.
12.
Superior category performance: Tonies successfully established itself as the single largest growth property within the entire United States preschool toys market in 2025. This dominant market position contrasts sharply with broader industry stagnation in the consumer discretionary space during the same period. The brand's ability to drive double-digit growth and execute price hikes during macroeconomic headwinds underlines its unique value proposition.
13.
Other side of the story: Consumer price resistance could eventually emerge if macro headwinds worsen or if Tonies introduces further aggressive pricing actions to combat lingering tariff pressures. While the initial $2 US price hike was absorbed smoothly, consecutive increases might test the absolute limit of household entertainment budgets and trigger consumer pushback. If families begin to restrict their discretionary spending, cohort purchase velocity could slow down below the historical twenty-figurine benchmark, impacting long-term high-margin recurring revenues.

Section summary
  1. Asmodee relies heavily on distributed trading card games, which dilute profit margins despite driving substantial organic sales volumes.
  2. Broker forecasts suggest a post-anniversary growth normalization for Asmodee, presenting lower organic top-line momentum.
  3. Coordinated transmedia pipelines and screen adaptations significantly enhance brand awareness and attract new collectors.
  4. Industry experts warn that converting casual viewers into active, high-cost wargaming hobbyists remains a challenging process.
  5. Overall confidence in this transmedia acquisition channel is medium because although media awareness is high, expert calls highlight significant friction in converting casual viewers to physical hobbyists.

Revenue Mix and Partner Game Distribution

1.
Uneven growth: Asmodee relies heavily on third-party partners rather than its own proprietary studios to drive sales growth. The company reported that games published by partners expanded by 27% to reach €331.2 million during the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. Conversely, net sales of games published by Asmodee's own studios increased by only 1.8% to €79.9 million during the same period. This imbalance means that the majority of revenue growth is third-party distributed rather than driven by owned intellectual property.
2.
High concentration: The company's distribution business is deeply exposed to specific mega-franchises like Pokémon and Magic: The Gathering. Trading card games remain the principal growth driver, with category revenues increasing by 23.1% year-on-year to €284.8 million during the first quarter. These figures confirm that distributed cards dominate Asmodee's quarterly top-line expansion, which limits its self-reliance. Analysts highlight that managing this exposure is critical to sustaining long-term earnings because third-party agreements typically carry lower gross margins than owned studios. 99 100 101 106 g ( y) g p g Q p Asmodee interim financial results and forecasts by publisher type and region.
3.
Portfolio diversification: Asmodee is actively expanding its third-party partnerships to reduce its extreme reliance on a single card franchise. The group continues to expand its distributed catalogue by adding new franchises like the KPop Kayou cards to capture broader demographics. Citing upcoming releases and ongoing portfolio diversification, analysts remain confident in the outlook for the trading cards segment. This expanding partner roster should help mitigate franchise concentration risks over the next three years.
4.
Unprotected rights: Analysts worry that Asmodee does not hold exclusive long-term contracts for its most critical distributed properties. The Pokémon IP represents a massive portion of Asmodee's overall sales, with the relationship built entirely on standard commercial business terms. Losing these distribution rights in a growing market would have an immediate, highly detrimental impact on sales and earnings per share. This structural risk makes the distributor vulnerable to unilateral changes by intellectual property owners who might choose to go direct.

Consolidating ATM Gaming from April 2026 is expected to contribute approximately 350 basis points to Asmodee's near-term board game revenue growth. Standalone sales for the acquired company reached €10.5 million, highlighting the immediate value of acquiring niche studios to bolster owned IP. This strategy illustrates how targeted acquisitions can systematically reduce Asmodee's heavy dependence on partner-published card brands.

This forced pause limited Asmodee's ability to acquire high-margin proprietary studios and left it more dependent on its lowermargin card distribution business. Severe deceleration: Underwriters expect a severe growth deceleration once the peak holiday and anniversary year activations conclude. However, any post-anniversary fatigue among collectors remains a prominent downside risk that could halt this positive cycle.
9.
Steady earnings: Despite the expected top-line slowdown, broker models predict that bottom-line earnings will continue to advance. Analysts project adjusted EPS to rise from €0.95 in fiscal year 2027 to €1.01 in fiscal year 2028 and €1.07 in fiscal year 2029. This resilient profitability is projected to be driven by operational leverage and reduced debt expenses, which should cushion the decelerating sales growth. Nevertheless, achieving these targets depends heavily on the company maintaining its tight control over operational expenses.

First, granular customer cohort data remains incomplete across all three companies. While tonies discloses that owners purchase approximately 20 figurines over 4.5 years, there is no public evidence on whether this purchasing velocity has changed following the May 2025 price increases or whether newer cohorts are exhibiting different buying patterns. Closing this gap requires waiting for tonies' full-year fiscal 2026 results expected in March or April 2027, which should include updated cohort analysis and retention metrics across pricing tiers.

Similarly, Games Workshop does not publicly disclose detailed like-for-like sales by store vintage or customer lifetime value metrics by acquisition cohort, making it difficult to assess whether growth is truly driven by price rather than expanding the customer base as former employees suggest. Expert interviews with current Games Workshop regional managers or access to internal CRM analytics would directly address this gap.

Second, post-anniversary demand visibility for Asmodee remains highly speculative. While brokers project severe deceleration from 14% growth in FY2027 to 2% in FY2028, these forecasts lack granular support from retail pointof-sale data or collector surveys. The specific question of whether Pokémon card collectors will maintain engagement after the 30th anniversary concludes in late 2026 cannot be answered without quarterly trading updates from Asmodee through at least Q2 FY2028 (likely August 2027).

Additionally, channel checks with major European hobby distributors and specialty retailers in France, Germany, and the UK would provide early warning signals on restocking patterns and inventory turns for trading card games versus proprietary board games. Third, transmedia conversion rates remain unmeasured and largely anecdotal. While comparable cases like Bandai Namco's Gundam demonstrate that Netflix exposure can drive physical product sales, there is no direct evidence quantifying how many Amazon Prime Video viewers of Warhammer content ultimately become active miniature purchasers.

Expert skepticism about converting casual viewers into high-cost hobbyists cannot be validated or refuted until the Amazon series debuts, which is not expected until late 2026 at the earliest. Closing this gap requires tracking Games Workshop's new customer acquisition metrics for at least two quarters post-launch, ideally through management commentary in earnings calls or investor presentations. Conducting consumer surveys among new Games Workshop customers asking about their discovery channel would provide definitive evidence on whether screen adaptations successfully expand the hobbyist base.

Fourth, competitive dynamics and market share shifts remain opaque, particularly for tonies and Asmodee. Tonies established itself as the largest growth property in the US preschool toys market in 2025, but there is no disclosed data on how its market share has evolved relative to traditional audio alternatives or digital streaming platforms aimed at young children. Access to NPD Group or Circana market share reports for the preschool audio entertainment category would quantify tonies' competitive positioning.

For Asmodee, the absence of exclusive long-term contracts for Pokémon distribution represents a massive structural risk, yet there is no public information on contract renewal timelines or The Pokémon Company's strategic intentions. Direct engagement with The Pokémon Company licensing executives or obtaining confidential distribution agreements would eliminate this uncertainty, though such information is unlikely to become public.

Addressing these evidence gaps would elevate confidence from medium to high and enable a definitive answer to whether these premium hobby companies can sustain earnings growth through volume expansion rather than price alone, and whether they represent the safest entry point for investors seeking exposure to this consumer segment.
