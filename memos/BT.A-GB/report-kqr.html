Section 2 – Depreciation, Amortisation, and Capital Expenditure Peak

Section summary
  1. BT Group's depreciation charges will fall gradually as peak fiber build capital expenditure declines. Net read: Legacy copper network switch-off and asset life adjustments will determine statutory profitability.
  2. Comparable post-build cases suggest capital cliffs structurally lift net cash margins.

Capex Peaks and Consensus Trends

1.
Extensive review: BT Group's post-peak capital expenditure transition was analyzed across 15 relevant sources covering three companies and two comparable past cases. This research shows that BT Group peaked its capital expenditure at £5.1bn in FY26. Management expects accrued capital expenditure to fall to between £4.2bn and £4.3bn in FY27. This substantial capital reduction is expected to improve normalized free cash flow significantly over the next three years.

44 45 g y p p g; g BT Group capital expenditure trend showing decline from FY26 peak through FY30 estimate

2.
Gradual reduction: BT Group's depreciation and amortisation charges are projected by consensus estimates to decline only slightly from peak levels. Morgan Stanley estimates show that group depreciation and amortisation will decrease from £4.67bn in FY26 to £4.47bn in FY27. The broker forecasts that this expense will continue falling to £4.07bn in FY29 and £3.97bn by FY30. This slow decline demonstrates that depreciation lags the capital expenditure drop due to long asset useful economic lives.
3.
Varied durations: BT Group assigns distinct useful economic lives to its core physical infrastructure categories to calculate straight-line depreciation. The BT Group 2026 Annual Report specifies that freehold buildings are depreciated over 14 to 50 years, while short-term leasehold improvements are depreciated over the shorter of 10 years or the lease term. Network transmission ducts are depreciated over 40 years, whereas copper cables range from 3 to 25 years and fiber cables carry useful lives of 5 to 20 years. These accounting policies ensure that long-lived passive infrastructure assets dilute the near-term earnings per share impact of capital additions.
4.
Significant efficiency: BT Group expects the retirement of legacy copper networks to yield substantial operational cost savings. Morgan Stanley noted that switching off legacy copper technology reduces network operating costs because operating expenses for fiber are 50% less than those of copper. Additionally, the copper switch-off helps BT Group decommission power-hungry legacy networks that currently consume half of the company's total energy. These operational opex savings will support adjusted EBITDA growth through the end of the decade.

Asset Lives and Copper Accounting

5.
Lucrative recycling: BT Group has started extracting material financial value from its redundant legacy copper network during the fiber transition. Morgan Stanley reported that BT Group secured a first-of-its-kind £105m payment by selling old copper cables to recycler EMR. The company plans to extract up to 200,000 tonnes of scrap copper through the 2030s under this recovery arrangement. This scrap copper cash inflow will offset the significant extraction costs of legacy copper cable recovery.
6.
Structured migration: BT Group must operate under strict regulatory parameters set by Ofcom to decommission its legacy fixed-line copper infrastructure. Jefferies reported that Ofcom permits copper retirement to commence once consumer copper usage falls below a 10% threshold in a specific exchange area. Once this milestone is reached, Openreach can issue a two-year notice of intention to retire copper services. This structured regulatory process prevents forced premature consumer migration to fiber networks.
7.
Accelerating losses: BT Group's Openreach division is experiencing a high volume of legacy line losses as customers migrate to alternative networks. Deutsche Bank reported in June 2026 that Openreach line losses were guided to slow slightly in FY27 compared to the steep losses recorded in FY26. Approximately half of these line losses reflect internal consolidation where other BT business units drop legacy copper lines in favor of fiber. These migrations shift wholesale revenues but do not impact consolidated BT Group profits because internal payments eliminate upon consolidation.

Comparable Post Build Case Studies

8.
Improving profitability: Chorus provides a comparable case study of financial dynamics following the completion of a nationwide fiber network build. New Zealand regulatory news reported that Chorus returned to net profitability, posting a $15m net gain for the six months ended December 2025 compared to a loss of $5m in the prior year period. This financial turnaround was driven by a 7% increase in fiber broadband revenue alongside tight operating cost control. The completion of its interest-free government debt funding program in 2023 has allowed Chorus to transition toward simplified operations.
9.
Deferred expansion: Royal KPN offers another clear comparable precedent for capital expenditure reductions driving cash flow expansion. Morgan Stanley noted that KPN's free cash flow growth is back-end loaded because capital expenditure remains elevated at €1.2bn annually before dropping below €1bn in 2027. In previous cycles, KPN managed to deliver stable operational free cash flows despite major fiber investments. This historical precedent indicates that capital cliffs can structurally lift net cash margins even when wholesale pricing remains regulated.
10.
Elevated intensity: BT Group's capital expenditure intensity is projected to remain higher than its continental peer group. Morgan Stanley estimates show that BT Group's capital expenditure as a share of sales will decline from 26.2% in FY27 to 21.2% by FY29. By comparison, Royal KPN's capital expenditure has remained stable at €1.2bn annually, which represents approximately 22% of its revenue. This indicates that BT Group will maintain a higher capital reinvestment rate than KPN through the end of the decade.

Contrary Evidence and Alternative Outcomes

Deutsche Bank noted that BT Group's hopes of a meaningful dividend increase remain constrained by its heavy leverage and the necessity of becoming a taxpayer again from FY28. This suggests that lower capital expenditure might not translate directly into higher earnings per share because tax and restructuring cash outflows will offset operational savings.

Deutsche Bank reported that the street may be underestimating the combined impact of pension top-ups, lease renewals, and cash taxes on BT's balance sheet. Consequently, statutory earnings per share could remain pressured by non-cash pension adjustments and restructuring charges.

Section 3 – Net Finance Costs and Debt Refinancing Yields

Section summary
  1. BT Group faces rising net finance costs as low-coupon legacy bonds mature and reprice at higher sterling and euro yields.
  2. A high fixed-rate debt ratio protects short-term earnings but delays rather than eliminates refinancing pressure.
  3. BT Group's refinancing risks compare favorably to highly leveraged rivals like Virgin Media O2 but remain elevated against investment-grade peers.

Bond Maturity Schedule and Yield Pressures

1.
Extensive refinancing schedule: BT Group has a substantial volume of debt maturing over the next three years that will need to be refinanced in a significantly higher interest rate environment. According to BT Group's 2026 Annual Report, the company faces listed bond maturities of £372m in FY27, £1,013m in FY28, and £1,296m in FY29. These upcoming maturities represent a total of £2.68bn in debt requiring refinancing through the end of FY29. This concentration of near-term maturities exposes the company's interest expense to prevailing market rates. Repricing headwinds: The coupons on BT Group's maturing bonds are significantly lower than current market yields for BBB-rated sterling and euro debt. Deutsche Bank Research noted in May 2026 that BT Group spent £932m on cash interest (including lease interest) in FY26, which was a 13% increase year-on-year. This increase was primarily driven by the refinancing of cheaper legacy debt in favor of longer-term maturities. Deutsche Bank estimated that a new €850m 8-year bond issued by BT in May 2026 at a coupon of 3.875% carried an effective cost of approximately 5.4% when swapped back into sterling. Exposure to interest rate hikes: The structural impact of higher interest rates represents a long-term headwind for BT Group's profitability. Bernstein Research reported in October 2026 that BT Group's coupon-derived average interest rate on financial instruments was 4.5%, compared to a marked-to-market rate of 5.3% based on prevailing yields. Bernstein calculated that marking the entire debt stack to market would result in an 8% net pro-forma dilution to BT Group's profit after tax. However, considering only the refinancing requirements until the end of calendar year 2027, the near-term pro-forma impact on profit after tax was estimated at a more manageable 1%. 92 93 Consensus interest projections: Equity analysts expect BT Group's net finance costs to remain elevated or rise slightly over the next few years. Morgan Stanley's November 2026 estimates project that BT Group's net finance costs will be £1.25bn in FY27, before falling slightly to £1.18bn in FY28 and then rising to £1.19bn in FY29 and £1.21bn by FY30. These projections reflect the gradual blending up of BT's weighted average cost of debt as older, cheaper tranches are replaced. Consequently, net finance costs will act as a persistent drag on statutory earnings per share, even as operating capital expenditure declines.

Capital Structure Hedging and Pension Liabilities

2.
High fixed-rate protection: BT Group employs a conservative hedging strategy to insulate its balance sheet from sudden interest rate fluctuations. According to BT Group's Annual Report for the year ended 31 March 2026, the company's treasury policy mandates that at least 70% of ongoing net financial debt must be maintained at fixed rates. At the end of FY26, BT Group's ratio of fixed-rate debt stood at 90%, with £15,999m in fixed-rate sterling debt against £1,848m in floating-rate debt. This high fixed-rate proportion protects BT's short-term earnings, as a parallel 1% increase in sterling interest rates would not materially impact annual net finance expenses. 96 ( ) BT Group's ratio of fixed-to-floating rate debt and average effective interest rates Lease interest burden: Beyond standard bond debt, lease interest represents a substantial recurring financial outflow for the group. In the financial year ended 31 March 2026, BT Group reported lease interest liabilities of £133m, down slightly from £135m in FY25. Total cash outflows for leases, including the principal repayment element, reached £864m in FY26. These lease liabilities are primarily linked to network infrastructure and property, and their eventual roll-over in a high-rate environment represents an additional financing cost risk.
3.
Pension interest accounting: The financial expense associated with BT's retirement obligations is a major below- the-line driver of statutory net income. BT Group's 2026 Annual Report showed that specific items within finance expense included £191m of net interest on retirement benefit obligations, down from £197m in FY25. For the half-year ended 30 September 2026, the company incurred £100m in IAS 19 pension interest costs, compared to £93m in the prior-year period. While these pension interest charges are non-cash specific items, they directly reduce statutory earnings per share.

Section 4 – Tax Dynamics and the Durability of Patent Box Benefits

Section summary
  1. BT Group's adjusted effective tax rate remains below the statutory rate due to persistent Patent Box benefits.
  2. Cash tax payments are projected to step up starting in FY28 as capital allowance incentives taper.
  3. BT Group maintains a substantial tax loss carry-forward that provides a long-term shield compared to European peers.

Tax Reconciliation and Patent Box

1.
Significant divergence: BT Group reported a statutory effective tax rate of 25.0% for the financial year ended 31 March 2026. However, the company's adjusted effective tax rate before specific items was lower at 22.1% in the same period. This divergence reflects the significant tax savings generated by the company's ongoing utilization of the UK Patent Box regime. This tax reduction directly supported adjusted earnings per share expansion.
2.
Durable benefits: BT Group secured a substantial £54m tax benefit from the UK Patent Box regime in the fiscal year ended 31 March 2026. This incentive provides a preferential tax rate on profits from patented inventions, which compares favorably with the standard UK corporation tax rate of 25%. Auditor reports confirm that these patent benefits have remained highly stable, having reached £55m in the prior fiscal year. This ongoing tax relief continues to lower the group's overall tax expense.
3.
Massive shields: BT Group carried forward approximately £11.3bn of tax losses into the financial year ended 31 March 2026. These accumulated losses were generated by pension contributions and enhanced capital deductions under the UK government's super-deduction program. Management expects to utilize these losses to offset taxable profits over the coming decade. This deferred tax asset will underpin future statutory profitability.
4.
Stable interim: BT Group reported a stable effective tax rate on adjusted profit of 22.8% for the half year ended 30 September 2026. This performance represents a slight increase from the 22.1% rate recorded in the prior financial year. Management attributed the below-standard tax rate primarily to the ongoing benefits of the UK Patent Box regime. These low interim rates help protect the short-term earnings profile.

Capital Allowances and Cash Tax Resumption

5.
Tapering incentives: BT Group continues to benefit from the UK capital allowances regime, which provides 100% tax relief on qualifying capital expenditure. This incentive is expected to dramatically reduce the company's current year UK tax liability. However, cash tax payments are guided to pick up as capital expenditure declines from peak levels. This shift will inevitably impact the group's cash conversion.
6.
Stepping payments: Morgan Stanley estimated that BT Group will pay approximately £90m in cash tax during the fiscal year ending March 2027. The broker expects this cash tax obligation to step up significantly to approximately £250m annually from the fiscal year ending March 2028 through March 2030. This escalation is driven by the tapering of capital expenditure tax relief. These rising cash payments will reduce normalized free cash flows.
7.
Extended protection: Rothschild & Co Redburn estimated in September 2024 that BT Group's £11.3bn of recognized tax losses will provide a durable tax shield through 2034. The broker modeled a tax asset value of £3bn, which cushions the company's long-term cash flows. However, the broker also expects some cash tax payments to resume starting in 2028. This suggests that tax liabilities are deferred but inevitable.
