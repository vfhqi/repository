Section 2 – Volvo Cars Torslanda 2027 Production and Model-Change Outlook

Section summary
  1. Volvo Cars' Torslanda plant faces significant 2027 production challenges as it transitions to the next- generation SPA3 electric vehicle platform.
  2. Delays in electric vehicle manufacturing and battery assembly ramp-ups will directly depress Nolato's polymer component supply volumes and segment profitability.

Transition to SPA3 Architecture

1.
Reduced utilization: Volvo Cars' Gothenburg plant in Torslanda represents a significant manufacturing footprint with an installed capacity of roughly 300k units. However, actual production volume has trended downward and remains substantially below this installed capacity level. Consequently, the resulting low utilization rates will pressurize fixed-cost absorption and supplier profitability into 2027.
2.
Delayed recovery: Volvo Cars commenced the production of its fully electric EX60 SUV at Torslanda in April 2026, marking its first model on the new SPA3 electric architecture. The carmaker initially planned an aggressive volume ramp-up to satisfy strong European pre-orders. However, manufacturing complexities with the new automated battery assembly line delayed full scale production into 2027.
3.
Slower ramp: Volvo Cars experienced severe manufacturing problems on its automated assembly line that pushed out the EX60's timeline to full production. Although the EX60 is flagged as margin accretive for the carmaker, this delay creates an immediate volume supply bottleneck for component suppliers. Consequently, the push-out acts as a near-term headwind that prevents Nolato from achieving projected capacity utilization.
4.
Technological shift: Volvo Cars is reducing its future research and development spend after completing the core development of the SPA3 platform. The carmaker already laid its technological foundation with the launch of its HuginCore central computing system. This development completion allows Volvo to lower its corporate overhead and shift capital focus toward manufacturing execution.
5.
High commonality: Volvo Cars designed the SPA3 platform with 95% code commonality between the EX60 and the next scheduled electric vehicle. This software reuse translates to a 60% reduction in industrialization costs for the second vehicle. This rapid platform scaling is expected to improve supplier long-term economics once initial manufacturing bottlenecks are cleared.

Production Estimates and Peer Comparisons

6.
Stagnant output: Volvo Cars expects its overall global production volumes to remain under pressure due to weak retail performance and lack of restocking demand. The carmaker's Q3 2026 wholesale volumes fell 10k units below retail sales as dealer inventory rose to SEK 78 bn. This inventory overhang means Torslanda's 2027 production will likely see prolonged factory schedule adjustments to clear excess vehicle stock.
7.
Inventory accumulation: Volvo Cars recorded a massive cash outflow of SEK 9 bn in Q3 2026, largely driven by inventory build-up at Torslanda. This working capital drag reflects the mismatch between high pre-production of older internal combustion models and slower retail deliveries. Consequently, clearing this inventory will require lower factory run rates in early 2027 to balance supply.
8.
Balanced footprints: Volvo Cars operates Torslanda with an annual capacity of 300k units, which matches its Ghent facility but exceeds the 250k capacity planned for Kosice. This is significantly lower than Ford's Valencia plant which boasts a capacity of 500k units. This moderate plant size allows Torslanda to implement flexible production shifts more effectively than larger European competitors.
9.
Contrary evidence: Volvo Cars reported that early demand for the EX60 was exceptionally strong, leading to initial plans to expand production volumes at the Torslanda facility. The company even negotiated with labor unions to keep the factory open for an extra week during the summer. This initial optimism indicates that underlying consumer demand exists despite the near-term assembly line delays.
10.
Subdued margins: Nolato's Engineered Solutions segment reported a decreased EBITA margin of 10.8% in Q3 2026 due to automotive volume declines. This margin level sits below Nolato's long-term corporate EBITA target of over 12% across a business cycle. This performance highlights how Torslanda's model transitions directly depress supplier profit margins during volume transition phases.

Component Sourcing and Cost Pressures

11.
Sourcing synergies: Volvo Cars is collaborating deeply with Geely to increase the share of common hardware components used in its vehicles. The carmaker aims to source highly competitive parts from Chinese suppliers to lower its bill of materials costs. This procurement strategy will displace traditional domestic suppliers unless they can match Chinese cost benchmarks.
12.
Tariff exposure: Volvo Cars remains highly dependent on its European manufacturing base, which produces approximately 70% of its unit sales. Any slow transition to electric vehicles might force the carmaker to reinvest in legacy multi-energy platforms to maintain volumes. This prolonged reliance on older platforms could stabilize near-term component demand for established regional suppliers.
13.
Footprint regionalization: Volvo Cars is fast-tracking the localized production of long-range plug-in hybrid models in both Europe and the United States. However, Bernstein Research estimates that the carmaker's US capacity utilization will remain very low until high-volume XC90 manufacturing commences in 2029. This persistent low global utilization rate forces the carmaker to demand deeper price concessions from its global polymer suppliers.

Restructuring Precedents and Strategic Response

14.
Leaner operations: Volvo Cars launched a reinforced turnaround plan in late 2026 to reduce indirect spend and variable costs by over SEK 5 bn. The strategy focuses on driving commonality with Geely to achieve 30% parts sharing by 2030. This cost-saving measure will restructure regional supply chains and pressure highcost domestic polymer suppliers in Sweden.
15.
Structural reduction: Volvo Cars announced a workforce reduction of approximately 2,500 jobs, with 2,000 of those positions based in Sweden. The carmaker is actively adapting its blue-collar workforce to current low production volumes through existing flexibility. This restructuring will incur estimated costs of SEK 600 million to be recognized in the late 2026 financial results.
16.
Comparable precedent: Volvo Cars previously optimized its manufacturing footprint in early 2025 by shifting production of the EX30 from China to Ghent. This shift was completed to bypass high European Union tariffs on Chinese-built electric vehicles. This case demonstrates that footprint regionalization takes time but remains a necessary strategy to secure long-term European market profitability.
17.
Management transition: Nolato appointed Cecilia Hellner as the new Head of Engineered Solutions in October 2026 to navigate these segment challenges. Hellner previously spent a large portion of her career holding several senior positions at AB Volvo. Her deep automotive background is expected to accelerate capacity alignment initiatives at Nolato's Gothenburg facility.
18.
Other side of the story: Volvo Cars' long-term Torslanda outlook remains supported by its regionalized SPA3 platform strategy and upcoming BEV launches. If the EX60 battery assembly issues are resolved quickly in early 2027, production volumes could rebound sharply to meet deferred retail orders. Consequently, the negative impact on Nolato's sales would prove temporary, paving the way for rapid supplier earnings recovery in late 2027.
