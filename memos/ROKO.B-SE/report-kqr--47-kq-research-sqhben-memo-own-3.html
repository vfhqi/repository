Performance of Listed Serial Acquirers After High-Leverage Acquisition Phases

Roko (ROKO.B-SE). Question: R\u00f6ko AB (ROKO.B-SE), a Swedish decentralised serial acquirer and perpetual owner of about 35 European niche businesses, listed on Nasdaq Stockholm since 11 March 2025, reporting two segments: Business to Business (about two-thirds of sales:

niche manufacturers and distributors such as Fri-Jado, ETB Technologies, Lambda, ATEMAG, Oppold, Pureoptics, Renovotec, Access Building Products) and Business to Consumer (about one-third: brands and retailers such as AJAT, Golfshopen, Golf Experten, Snowminds, Topa/Brauer, CyTech, Dan-Form). What happened to the earnings estimates, valuation multiples and share prices of listed serial acquirers in the 12 to 36 months after a year in which acquisitions financed mainly with debt lifted earnings strongly and took leverage close to the company's own ceiling, as at R\u00f6ko AB (ROKO.B-SE) in 2026 (five platform acquisitions adding SEK 1,543m of annual sales in nine months; financial net debt including option liabilities at 2.6x adjusted EBITDA against a stated ceiling of 3.0x; Q3 2026 interim report)?

Find every relevant case among Lifco, Addtech, Lagercrantz, Indutrade, Idun Industrier, Teqnion, Sdiptech, Storskogen, Volati, Momentum Group, Bergman & Beving, Beijer Alma, Bufab, AddLife, Judges Scientific, Diploma, Halma and other listed European serial acquirers. For each case give: the year; the leverage reached and on which definition; organic growth and acquired growth in each of the following three years; whether acquisitions paused, for how long and why; how consensus earnings per share for the following two fiscal years were revised over the next 12 and 24 months; the forward P/E or EV/EBITA multiple before the year, at its peak and trough afterwards, and where it settled; and the share price performance relative to the local market.

Also report: the range of forward multiples at which Nordic serial acquirers have traded in different phases (the first three years after listing; periods of estimate upgrades driven by acquisitions; periods when organic growth slowed to about zero; periods after leverage was reduced and acquisitions resumed), with dates and sources; and what brokers identify as distinguishing the companies whose multiples rose from those whose multiples fell.

Key Distinguishing Factors Identified by Brokers Brokers distinguish between compounders whose multiples rise versus those whose multiples fall based on several core criteria: 1. Organic Growth Delivery: DNB Carnegie highlights that long-term multiple expansion requires consistent organic growth (typically c. 4% to 5%) alongside M&A. Serial acquirers without organic growth risk being valued as low-margin distributors, causing long-term multiple compression. 299 2. Portfolio Quality and Return on Capital (ROIC): Jefferies and RedEye note that high-quality compounders are distinguished by a primary focus on ROIC rather than raw EBITA growth Portfolios that successfully shift from low-margin distribution toward proprietary products with high gross margins, high returns, and limited cyclicality enjoy multiple expansion. Conversely, platforms with high accumulated goodwill from expensive acquisitions can drag group-level ROIC down to 6% to 8%, capping multiple expansion. 3. M&A Discipline and Arbitrage: Inderes and DNB Carnegie report that premium compounders focus on acquiring capital-light niche businesses at disciplined low multiples (typically 5x to 7x EV/EBITA) that generate immediate returns on capital above 15% to 20%. Overpaying or taking on high integration risk compresses multiples. 4. Leverage Control and Cash Flow Conversion: Stretched leverage (e.g., above 2.50x–3x EBITDA) restricts further acquisitions and results in substantial valuation discounts Premium compounders maintain strict control of working capital and lease liabilities to maximize free cash flow, allowing them to self-fund acquisitions without dilutive equity raises.
