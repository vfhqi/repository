Section 3 – Multiples Across the Earnings Cycle: Trough, Recovery, and Peak

Section summary
  1. Consumer hardware valuation multiples expand aggressively during the initial phase of earnings recovery as gross margins expand and channel inventories normalize.
  2. Peak multiples are rarely sustained once top-line growth moderates, resulting in multiple compression even as companies expand margins or initiate buybacks.

Logitech Valuation Cycles and Drivers

1.
Cyclical trough: Logitech faced severe headwinds in fiscal 2023 and 2024 as pandemic-driven demand subsided and retail channels aggressively cleared excess inventory. Logitech reported in its January 23, 2023 presentation that third-quarter net sales fell 17% in constant currency to $1.27 billion, while gross margin plummeted to 37.9% due to intense promotions. This deep operational trough compressed valuation multiples as investors struggled to project near-term demand visibility.
2.
Recovery phase: The first year of recovery for Logitech was characterized by rapid gross margin expansion and healthier channel inventory dynamics rather than massive volume growth. Deutsche Bank reported on January 31, 2025, that Logitech's sell-through returned to positive territory, reaching 8% growth in Q3 fiscal 2025, with gross margin expanding 90 basis points to 43.2% as product costs fell. This operational recovery allowed the company to command a premium valuation of 23 times forward earnings by early 2025.
3.
Peak normalization: Logitech struggles to maintain peak valuation multiples once long-term organic growth expectations moderate. Morgan Stanley reported on January 23, 2025, that Logitech's forward price-to-earnings multiple de-rated by four turns from 22 times in May 2024 to 18 times by early 2025 as the market aligned with a lower growth outlook. This de-rating occurred despite consecutive gross margin beats, demonstrating that long-term revenue growth expectations, rather than near-term margin spikes, ultimately dictate peak hardware multiples.

Gaming Peripherals Cycle Case Studies

4.
Corsair trough: Corsair Gaming experienced severe cyclical distress in 2022 as post-pandemic demand normalized and retail channels faced massive inventory builds. Morgan Stanley reported on August 11, 2022, that macroeconomic headwinds affecting consumer spending had caused a buildup of inventory both in Corsair's warehouses and in the retail channel. This inventory overhang and demand contraction compressed Corsair's forward valuation multiples to historic lows as investors feared prolonged gross margin pressure.
5.
Corsair recovery: Corsair's valuation recovery in 2025 and 2026 was propelled by dramatic gross margin expansion and successful niche acquisitions. Corsair reported in its November 5, 2026 interim filing that thirdquarter gross profit grew 5% year-over-year to $97.8 million, with year-to-date gross margin improving to 30.7% from 27.2% in 2025. This margin expansion, driven by premium gamer peripherals and supply chain execution, helped narrow GAAP net losses and support a rebound in operational multiples.
6.
Turtle Beach: Turtle Beach followed a similar cyclical trajectory, suffering from channel inventory reductions in 2022 before initiating a strong recovery in 2026. Morgan Stanley reported on March 3, 2022, that Turtle Beach guided fiscal 2022 performance below expectations due to channel adjustments. By November 5, 2026, Turtle Beach reported that third-quarter net revenue returned to 5.3% growth to reach $84.7 million, with adjusted EBITDA of $8.1 million. This cyclical recovery was driven by new wireless product launches and retail inventory replenishment ahead of key software releases.

Drivers and Contrary Perspectives

7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank argued on January 14, 2025, that Logitech's valuation of 21 times forward earnings was unsustainable because the hardware upgrade cycle was slowing. The broker noted that Dell and HP had warned of a sluggish PC recovery, while global PC volumes returned to year-over-year declines in late 2024. This slower industry backdrop suggests that hardware multiples can contract prematurely during recovery if broader market demand fails to materialize.

Other side of the story: Jefferies Research argued on October 6, 2022, that gaming hardware companies are highly cyclical and should be valued based on average cycle multiples rather than near-term trough or peak figures. The broker noted that while gaming comps had historically traded at a 12-month forward EV/EBITDA of 13.0 times, they contracted to 8.0 times during the 2022 trough. Consequently, utilizing near-term peak or trough multiples to value continuing operations risks mispricing the underlying business over a full multiyear hardware cycle.
