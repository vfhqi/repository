Section 1 – Brief company description and history

1.
Pure-play upstream profile: Vår Energi is a Norwegian independent exploration and production company focused on oil, natural gas and natural gas liquids from the Norwegian Continental Shelf, with operations organised around the Balder, North Sea, Norwegian Sea and Barents Sea hub areas and a stated purpose of supplying reliable energy to Europe while creating shareholder value.
2.
Long operating heritage: Although Vår Energi was established in 2018 through the merger of Eni Norge and HitecVision-backed Point Resources, its asset base incorporates more than 60 years of Norwegian Continental Shelf operating history, including Eni’s Norwegian heritage and Point Resources’ acquisition of ExxonMobil’s Norwegian assets.
3.
Acquisition-led scale-up: The company’s production growth has been driven by the 2019 acquisition of ExxonMobil’s Norwegian portfolio, the 2024 acquisition of Neptune Energy’s Norwegian business, subsequent hub-focused transactions and organic project start-ups, taking production from above 200 kboepd in 2023 to approximately 400 kboepd in the fourth quarter of 2025.
4.
Ownership remains concentrated: Eni is Vår Energi’s strategic majority shareholder and is expected to retain approximately 57.33% after the BlueNord combination, while the transaction is expected to increase free float to approximately 43% by issuing 248.4 million new shares to BlueNord shareholders.
5.
Norwegian asset footprint: Before BlueNord completion, Vår Energi’s portfolio spans approximately 200 licences, around one-third of which it operates, and includes 57 fields across five hub areas, although the fifth hub, Denmark, will be added only when the BlueNord transaction closes. 10 11 21 Oct Third quarter 2026 Results presentation Vår Energi’s Norwegian licence footprint, hub areas and operated fields Balanced production mix: Vår Energi’s production is approximately 65% liquids and 35% gas, giving it more oil exposure than a pure gas producer but materially more European gas sensitivity than oil-heavy Aker BP, while gas also provides direct exposure to European energy-security pricing.
6.
Recent operating trajectory: Vår Energi reported production of 311 kboepd for the first nine months of 2025 and 383 kboepd for the first nine months of 2026, while third-quarter production was 369 kboepd versus 370 kboepd in the third quarter of 2025 and 376 kboepd in the second quarter of 2026. Current production is below the original bar: Management reduced 2026 production guidance from 390-410 kboepd to approximately 390 kboepd after Johan Castberg, the Jotun floating production, storage and offloading vessel and the Åsgard area suffered operational interruptions, although Vår Energi said the issues were not reservoir-related.
7.
Costs remain competitive but deteriorated: Third-quarter production cost was USD 11.1 per barrel of oil equivalent, compared with USD 11.2 in the second quarter and USD 10.6 in the third quarter of 2025, while first-nine-month cost was USD 10.9 per barrel of oil equivalent and full-year guidance was raised from approximately USD 10 to approximately USD 11. Financial profile is highly commodity-sensitive: Third-quarter 2026 petroleum revenue was USD 3.566 billion, EBIT was USD 2.214 billion, cash flow from operations after tax was USD 1.518 billion and free cash flow was USD 758 million, but the company’s cash conversion was reduced by USD 1.1 billion of tax payments and a USD 0.4 billion working-capital outflow. Balance sheet has improved sharply: Net interest-bearing debt fell to USD 3.110 billion from USD 3.388 billion in the second quarter and USD 5.258 billion at year-end 2025, while rolling EBITDAX rose to USD 10.266 billion and leverage declined to 0.3 times from 0.4 times in the second quarter and 0.8 times at year-end 2025. Dividend proposition is unusually prominent: Vår Energi paid USD 1.2 billion in dividends in 2025 and expects total 2026 distributions of USD 1.45 billion, including an extraordinary component, compared with its stated long-term policy of distributing 25-30% of cash flow from operations after tax through the cycle. Dividend sustainability is the central investor debate: Morgan Stanley’s Alice Bergier Winograd and Martijn Rats value Vår Energi on a dividend-discount model, retaining an Overweight rating and NOK 56 target while assuming USD 0.60 and USD 0.50 per share of dividends in 2027 and 2028, respectively. The key bearish interpretation is post-2026 cash-flow decline: DNB Carnegie’s Steffen Evjen and Christian Klingenberg Theodorsen rated Vår Energi Sell at NOK 46, arguing that the company’s approximately 20 tieback opportunities may not fully offset natural production decline and forecasting average 2027-28 free-cashflow yield of approximately zero. BlueNord changes the scale, not yet the current earnings base: The proposed combination adds approximately 45 kboepd of net production, approximately 195 million barrels of oil equivalent of 2P reserves plus near-term 2C resources, and a long-term production target of approximately 450 kboepd, but completion remains subject to regulatory, partner and licence approvals.
8.
The combined portfolio improves longevity: Vår Energi presents the enlarged company as having approximately 2.4 billion barrels of oil equivalent of 2P reserves and 2C resources, around 15 years of reserve and resource life, approximately USD 10-11 per barrel of oil equivalent unit operating costs and post-tax synergies of USD 250-300 million over 2027-2032. Development inventory supports the growth thesis: Vår Energi has 15 projects in execution containing approximately 370 million barrels of oil equivalent of net 2P reserves, around 30 early-phase projects targeting approximately 500 million barrels of oil equivalent of 2C resources and a target of 10-15 exploration wells annually. The portfolio is not yet fully de-risked: The company reports approximately 438 million barrels of oil equivalent of reserves and risked resources scheduled for 2026-2029 at September 2026, down from 527 million barrels at December 2025, while its long-term production target depends on project execution, exploration success, improved recovery and the BlueNord closing. Short-cycle projects are the investment proposition’s differentiator: Vår Energi says sanctioned projects have average breakeven of approximately USD 30 per barrel of oil equivalent and an average internal rate of return around 35%, while Balder Phase VI is presented as having approximately 17 million barrels of oil equivalent of gross reserves, breakeven below USD 30 and less than one year of payback after production begins.
9.
Peer positioning is mixed: Aker BP offers larger visible growth projects and targets production above 525 kboepd by 2028 with dividends growing at least 5% annually, while Vår Energi offers a higher current distribution yield but greater debate over production durability, operating costs and whether its dividend is funded by recurring free cash flow. Share-price performance has benefited from the commodity environment: DNB Carnegie recorded Vår Energi’s share-price gains at 25.73% over three months, 15.74% over six months and 59.09% over twelve months by 12 October 2026, versus Oslo Børs Benchmark Index relative gains of 19.08%, 14.41% and 35.70%, respectively. The six-month pre-results expectation was already demanding: Jefferies’ Mark Wilson rated Vår Energi Buy with a NOK 40 target in March, while Norne’s Nojus Juodžiavičius raised his target to NOK 60 and retained Buy in October after lifting his oil-price assumptions, whereas DNB Carnegie downgraded to Sell at NOK 46 because it expected production below consensus and questioned dividend durability. The earnings bar therefore combined two conflicting expectations: Investors expected Vår Energi to deliver a strong near-term dividend and benefit from high oil and gas prices, but were also testing whether 2026 production would reach the original 390-410 kboepd range and whether post-2026 cash flow could sustain elevated distributions. Other side of the story: The bullish case is that temporary outages have ended, King and Balder Phase VI lift fourth-quarter production, BlueNord adds stable low-capex Danish output and higher European prices support.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary

The pre-results bar was not simply a strong third quarter, but proof that temporary outages would be reversed quickly enough to preserve 2026 production, cash flow and an unusually high dividend while the market tested whether Vår Energi’s post-2026 production plateau was credible. Production bar, below consensus: DNB Carnegie’s Steffen Evjen and Christian Klingenberg Theodorsen expected third-quarter production of 365 kboepd, 5-6% below Visible Alpha and Bloomberg consensus of roughly 385-390 kboepd, because Balder, Johan Castberg and Ormen Lange had all experienced downtime. Their forecast therefore created the most explicit bearish earnings bar, with any result near 369 kboepd likely to beat their estimate while still missing the broader published consensus. Guidance-cut risk, already priced: DNB Carnegie expected the production shortfall to force Vår Energi to reduce 2026 guidance below the original 390-410 kboepd range, potentially reopening the market’s concern about the durability of the long-term production profile and dividend capacity. The issue mattered because the company had repeatedly presented approximately 400 kboepd in 2026 and 350-400 kboepd through 2030 as a de-risked production platform. Recovery case, operational not geological: Vår Energi’s 12 October trading update said Johan Castberg and the Jotun floating production, storage and offloading vessel had returned to expected production levels from early August, while Åsgard irregularities were not reservoir-related. The market therefore expected a sharp fourth-quarter recovery rather than a permanent reserve impairment, but the operational explanation still left execution timing as the key test for earnings estimates. Production, projects and execution Fourth-quarter ramp-up, central catalyst: The company’s pre-results case depended on more than 50 new production wells planned for 2026, with around 70% already producing, plus the King start-up and Balder Phase VI in the fourth quarter. Investors were consequently looking for evidence that the 369 kboepd third quarter was a temporary trough and that the remaining wells could restore the exit rate toward, or above, 400 kboepd rather than merely support the reduced 390 kboepd full-year outcome. King, modest near-term volume but important credibility: King began production during the third quarter, ahead of the expected fourth-quarter volume uplift, and management linked it with completed turnarounds and Balder Phase VI to higher Q4 output. The market expected King to contribute incrementally rather than transform earnings immediately, so its importance was primarily evidential: timely start-up would support the company’s “subsea factory” execution narrative after interruptions at larger hubs.
