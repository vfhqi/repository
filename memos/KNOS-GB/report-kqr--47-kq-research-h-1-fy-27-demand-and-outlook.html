Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: "Kainos (KNOS-GB): Belfast-headquartered digital services and software company listed in London, with three divisions (Digital Services, Workday Services and Workday Products), a financial year ending 31 March, and half-year results for the six months to 30 September 2026 reported on 9 November 2026. What did the halfyear results of 9 November 2026 show about demand for Kainos's services and the credibility of its outlook for the second half and the financial year to March 2028?"

Valuation Impact and Related Questions

2.
Strong divisional performance: Kainos operates three core divisions, where H1 FY27 results show Digital Services grew revenue by 57% to £162.5 million and Workday Products grew 22% to £48.0 million. Workday Services increased by 9% to £58.3 million, proving that demand has rebounded strongly. This divisional expansion is critical because high-margin software subscriptions from Workday Products are expected to structurally elevate the long-term margin profile.

3.
Limited operational gearing: The outstanding revenue growth did not translate into proportional pre-tax profit due to a drop in gross margin to 43.1% from 48.0%. Kainos relies heavily on expensive contractors and partners to scale up major projects, which suppresses near-term earnings per share. Understanding the trajectory of contractor usage is crucial because normalising these costs in H2 FY27 and FY28 represents the main catalyst for margin recovery.
4.
Elevated public exposure: Public sector and healthcare clients account for approximately 90% of revenues in Digital Services, exposing Kainos to UK government spending policy shifts. A key related question is whether the new Labour government's budget priorities will accelerate digital transformation projects or cause procurement delays. Changes in government policy can severely compress future public bookings, directly impacting medium-term revenue visibility.
5.
Deflationary pricing pressures: Investors are increasingly concerned that artificial intelligence tools could introduce deflationary pressure and potential margin erosion over the next 18 months. A closely related question is whether AI will make it cheaper to write Workday modules, thus threatening the pricing power of Kainos's proprietary software. If IT services transition from traditional time-and-materials billable hours to fixed-price delivery, it could permanently alter the sector's profitability.
6.
Contrary evidence: Kainos achieved a 45% year-on-year increase in adjusted diluted earnings per share to 27.4p in H1 FY27, which indicates that profitability can expand even during periods of heavy contractor usage. This outcome contradicts concerns that gross margin compression to 43.1% will inevitably cap near-term earnings growth.
7.
Other side of the story: While the share price fell 12% following the results on November 9, 2026, Panmure Liberum analyst Andrew Ripper argued that the unchanged full-year guidance represents a highly cautious stance from management. If contractor levels normalise faster than anticipated in the second half, the company could deliver significant earnings upgrades for both FY27 and the financial year ending March 2028.
