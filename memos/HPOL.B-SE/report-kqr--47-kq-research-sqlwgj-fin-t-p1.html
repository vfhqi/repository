Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding operating leverage and cost flexibility is critical for assessing Hexpol's earnings resilience.
  2. Evaluating how polymer compounders can manage their fixed cost footprints determines their valuation multiples.

Defining the Central Inquiry

1.
Central challenge: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Structural margins: Polymer compounding companies like Hexpol AB operate with flexible batch production setups that theoretically protect margins during volume downturns. Analysts from DNB Carnegie note that while Hexpol faces declining organic volumes, its highly flexible business model enables it to maintain solid margins. This operational flexibility directly determines whether the company can protect its earnings per share when industrial demand weakens.
3.
Operational risk: Valuation multiples in the specialty chemicals sector are highly sensitive to end-market cyclicality and capacity utilization. For instance, brokers at Rothschild and Co Redburn observe that negative operating leverage during downturns can cause dramatic EBIT margin contractions of up to 750 basis points when capacity utilization falls. Consequently, understanding cost structures is vital because any unplanned drop in production volume can severely penalize investment returns.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some industry studies suggest that compounders can actually run a decentralized cost structure without forcing deep cost synergies. Analysts at DNB Carnegie highlight that many successful Nordic compounders do not implement aggressive centralized cost-cutting because it would counter the decentralized entrepreneurial culture that drives customer relationships. Therefore, material large cost cuts are not always the optimal route to maximize equity value.
5.
Comparative margins: Comparative analysis across the compounding sector reveals wide margin discrepancies between low-cost players and highvalue formulators. An expert interview with a former AirBoss division president noted that Hexpol maintains superior EBITDA margins of 18% to 19% compared to AirBoss due to procurement advantages and a richer product mix. This performance gap shows how product mix drives structural operating leverage independently of volume.
6.
Correlated metrics: Operational dynamics raise closely related questions regarding raw material pass- through lags and pricing power. Analysts at Nuvama Institutional Equities point out that input cost passthroughs typically lag by one to two quarters. This lag creates transient working capital volatility and temporary operating margin compression during rapid raw material price inflation cycles.
7.
Active portfolio: Precedents from diversified industrial groups demonstrate that active portfolio restructuring is a primary driver of margin expansion. Deutsche Bank Research observes that Trelleborg successfully divested its highly cyclical tire and automotive operations to reduce business cycle sensitivity. Consequently, strategic divestments can structurally re-rate a compounder's valuation and mitigate negative operating leverage without requiring incremental plant closures.
8.
Strategic investments: The cost inflation reported by Hexpol during 2026 was largely driven by investments in sales coverage and R&D. Acting CEO Peter Rosén explained that Hexpol's margins were recently compressed by the overhead costs needed to enable long-term volume growth. This shows that fixed costs cannot be easily eliminated if a company wants to capture future market share.
9.
Other side of the story: Aggressive plant footprint rationalization programs at peers like Avient show that restructuring can unlock substantial margin expansion. Jefferies Research reports that Avient has successfully reduced its plant count from 109 to 95 facilities to improve utilization. This suggests that Hexpol might be underutilizing its global footprint by maintaining over 50 local production sites instead of consolidating.

Section 2 – Compounding Cost Structures: Fixed vs Variable Splits

Section summary
  1. High variable raw material costs define compounding cost structures, limiting strict fixed costs to a minority share.
  2. Pricing pass-through formulas protect absolute profit but distort and dilute reported margin percentages during inflation.

Cost Structure Architectures

1.
Dominant variable shares: Polymer compounding cost bases are heavily weighted toward raw materials, which typically represent the largest variable cost component. For Avient Corporation, raw material costs comprise approximately 80% of its cost of goods sold. This high material concentration means that strict fixed expenses like plant depreciation remain a small percentage of total costs, cushioning the business during volume declines.
2.
Flexible operating costs: Direct production expenses at compounding facilities are largely semi-variable as management can adjust operational shifts quickly. At AirBoss of America, managing controllable overhead costs and continuous improvement initiatives helped expand year-to-date gross margins to 17.6% in 2026. While labor is often perceived as fixed, plant managers can scale down direct labor hours over one to four quarters if compounding demand falters.
3.
Sticky overhead burdens: Administrative and corporate selling expenses exhibit less flexibility than direct manufacturing costs over multiple quarters. Avient Corporation experienced a spike in selling, general, and administrative costs from 15% to nearly 20% of sales following its major acquisitions. These overhead costs require targeted synergy extraction programs to reduce and cannot be easily shed dynamically when demand declines.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some highly specialized compounders operate with extremely low and centralized cost models that resist margin erosion without requiring footprint changes. Swiss specialty polyamide producer EMS- Chemie achieved an outstanding EBIT margin of 30.6% in the first half of 2026. By concentrating on high-margin specialties and executing strict cost discipline, the company grew profits despite a weak industrial economy.

Pricing Pass-Through Mechanics

5.
Absolute margin dilution: Hexpol manages input cost volatility by passing on the absolute change in raw material prices directly to its customers. According to CEO Henrik Elmin, passing on absolute price changes means that Hexpol's operating margins will dynamically decline when raw material prices rise. This mechanism protects absolute operating profits but introduces a structural distortion to reported percentage margins.
6.
Delayed contractual adjustments: Formula-linked customer contracts create temporary pricing mismatches because input cost adjustments typically operate with a lag of several weeks. AirBoss of America utilizes pricing formulas to adjust selling prices when raw material costs fluctuate. This time lag narrows gross spreads when raw material costs rise rapidly and temporarily widens them when input costs fall.
7.
Rapid commercial pricing: Dynamic pricing power allows specialized compounders to quickly recover raw material cost increases without contractual indexing. For Avient Corporation, fewer than 5% of customer agreements are contractually indexed to costs. This operational model allows the company to adjust pricing dynamically and maintain margin protection during inflationary quarters.
8.
Severe commodity sensitivity: Unprotected compounding operations face severe earnings pressure when raw material costs spike and cannot be passed forward. AirBoss of America estimates that a 10% increase in natural and synthetic rubber costs would decrease pre-tax earnings by $5.56 mm if the costs are not passed to customers. This sensitivity highlights the vital importance of maintaining active pass-through agreements.

Leverage Comparisons and Vulnerabilities

9.
High-value structural margins: Specialty polyamide compounding generates superior operating leverage compared to standard elastomeric rubber compounding due to higher pricing power. Swiss compounder EMS- Chemie consistently maintains high-margin specialty product portfolios to offset rising energy costs. By focusing on innovative metal-replacement materials, the company has insulated its earnings from the volumedriven leverage that penalizes standard compounders.
10.
Hydrocarbon market dependency: Polymer compounders remain structurally exposed to upstream petrochemical cycles due to their reliance on oil-based feedstocks. Avient Corporation expects mid-single-digit raw material inflation in 2026, with the primary pressure originating from hydrocarbon-based raw material purchases that represent 35% of its basket. This exposure links compounding operating margins directly to volatile global energy markets.
11.
Elevated energy costs: Energy consumption represents a volatile semi-fixed cost component that can squeeze margins during supply disruptions. Swiss group EMS-Chemie spent CHF 33.68 million on energy during its 2025 fiscal year, down from CHF 37.35 million in the prior period. Compounding operations are inherently energy-intensive, and sudden utility price hikes can erode profitability if pricing adjustments lag behind.
12.
Other side of the story: While flexible cost structures and pass-through formulas are designed to shield polymer compounders, prolonged volume downturns inevitably trigger negative operating leverage. When industry capacity utilization is low, intense price competition from aggressive peers can contract EBIT margins significantly regardless of cost flexibility. Under these conditions, the theoretical benefits of variable cost splits fail to protect earnings if absolute volumes drop below fixed plant operating thresholds.

Section 4 – Hexpol Strategic Cost Programmes, Divestments, and Mixing 5.0

Section summary

Footprint Optimization and Closures

1.
Successful consolidation: Hexpol consolidated its California compounding operations from two factories to one to improve supply chain efficiency. Management took the restructuring costs in 2023 when the process initially started. The physical move was completed in summer 2024 after customers approved the transferred chemical recipes. This measure successfully increased local production utilization and delivered meaningful savings starting in 2025.
2.
Efficient rationalization: Hexpol closed its compounding facility in Kennedale, Texas during the second quarter of 2025 to eliminate redundant capacity. The company recognized closure costs of approximately 20 MSEK in the fourth quarter of 2024. Production volumes were transferred to other North American factories, primarily the facility in Burton, Ohio. Management achieved an operational payback of less than one year on this closure project.
3.
Strategic divestment: Hexpol divested its non-core manufacturing facility in Muscle Shoals, Alabama in late 2024 to Karo Ventures. The facility generated about 7 MUSD in annual sales but operated below the average profitability of the group. The divestment resulted in a 40 MSEK negative impact on operating profit but had a minimal net result impact due to tax benefits. This transaction allowed Hexpol to streamline its regional supply chain.
4.
Contrary evidence: Hexpol was unable to quickly reduce operating expenses during previous downturns because plant closures require substantial calendar time. Customers must verify and approve new compounds before production can be transferred, which delays immediate cost savings. This technical constraint limits rapid fixed cost elimination when volumes decline.

Mixing 5.0 Automation Initiative

5.
Advanced automation: Hexpol is streamlining its compounding operations through its proprietary Mixing 5.0 automation project. The initiative combines modern production engineering with artificial intelligence to optimize compound quality and safety. Management expects this new standard of rubber compounding to outperform the prevailing industry standard by far. The project is run cross-functionally with leading machinery suppliers.
6.
Future implementation: Hexpol expects the first automated Mixing 5.0 production line to be fully operational in spring 2027. The specifications for the initial mixing line are almost completed without any equipment delivery delays. The company plans to roll out this automated technology across European and North American facilities over time. This systematic rollout will gradually replace traditional maintenance capital expenditure.
7.
Capital commitments: Hexpol estimates that setting up the first Mixing 5.0 site will require an initial capital expenditure of approximately 10 MEUR. The company made additional investment decisions to fund the project during the final quarter of 2025. This high initial capital outlay is expected to lower manufacturing costs over the long term.
8.
Synergistic investments: Hexpol is combining Mixing 5.0 with other specialized technological projects such as a new devulcanization line in the Czech Republic. The company uses these investments to develop price-neutral sustainable compounds under the HEXGREEN brand. These sustainable offerings strengthen customer retention and volume growth without increasing manufacturing costs.
