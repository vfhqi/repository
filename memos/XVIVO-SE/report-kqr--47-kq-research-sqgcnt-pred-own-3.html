The Traditional Organ Preservation Reimbursement Landscape

1.
Medicare reasonable cost-reporting: Medicare reimbursement for organ transplants involves two distinct payment mechanisms: the inpatient hospital prospective payment system based on MS-DRG codes and a separate reimbursement for organ acquisition costs. Advanced organ-preservation and machine-perfusion technologies like the TransMedics Organ Care System (OCS) are classified under the second mechanism as organ acquisition costs. Rather than receiving a fixed prospective payment, hospitals are reimbursed for these preservation and perfusion costs on a reasonable cost basis through the Medicare Organ Acquisition Cost Center cost report. The Medicare patient ratio bottleneck: While the cost-report pathway avoids the restrictive bundling of traditional MS-DRGs, it does not guarantee dollar-for-dollar commercial reimbursement. Under this framework, Medicare reimburses a transplant center for organ acquisition costs based solely on that specific hospital's Medicare patient ratio. For example, if only 25% of a transplant center's patients are covered by Medicare, the hospital is only reimbursed by the government for 25% of the high-tech perfusion system's costs, leaving the remaining 75% to be negotiated with private insurers or absorbed. Private payor and compliance gaps: Because CMS reimbursement rules were designed for traditional organ procurement rather than high-tech perfusion systems bundled with national logistics, transplant centers face coverage gaps. Private insurance companies cover roughly 60% to 70% of patients needing a transplant, and while they often follow Medicare's lead, they require separate agreements and pre-authorizations. If transplant centers over-report or misclassify these high-tech perfusion and preservation fees on their cost reports, they risk triggering compliance issues and audits.

Precedent Analysis: TransMedics OCS Heart CMS Strategy

2.
No active CMS engagement: Prior to securing formal FDA approval, the reimbursement pathway for TransMedics OCS was established through clinical trials, continued access programs, and post-approval registries. During its Q2 2025 earnings call, TransMedics management confirmed that they were not actively engaged with CMS because the technology was already approved and utilized these established billing precedents. This history of billing precedent has allowed the OCS to bypass the need for a National Coverage Determination (NCD) or Local Coverage Determination (LCD) entirely. - Earnings Calls 197 10K Consistent cost-reporting history: TransMedics has stated that CMS has been highly consistent in its reimbursement methodology for the Organ Acquisition Cost Center cost report. According to company communications, CMS has never denied coverage for the OCS under this cost-reporting system, and reimbursement has generally not been an issue for active transplant centers. This established pathway minimizes commercial friction, as transplant stakeholders consistently prioritize clinical outcomes over procedural costs when organ acquisition costs are reimbursed through Medicare pass-through mechanisms.

The Proposed RAPID Pathway for Breakthrough Devices

3.
A dual-agency accelerated framework: On August 7, 2026, the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) released a proposed procedural notice outlining the Regulatory Alignment for Predictable and Immediate Device (RAPID) coverage pathway. Developed in collaboration with the FDA, RAPID is a voluntary pathway designed to dramatically shorten the period between FDA market authorization and national Medicare coverage for Class II and Class III Breakthrough Devices. The program is intended to replace the previous Transitional Coverage for Emerging Technologies (TCET) pathway, which CMS has paused for new candidates. & Knight & Spalding Immediate NCD issuance: Under the proposed RAPID framework, CMS would release a proposed National Coverage Determination (NCD) on the exact same day that an eligible breakthrough device receives its FDA market authorization. Following a statutorily required 30-day public comment period, CMS aims to issue a final NCD within approximately 60 days of FDA approval for Class II devices and 90 days for Class III devices. This compressional shift represents a significant acceleration compared to traditional CMS NCD processes, which historically require 9 to 12 months. 204 & Spalding into early clinical development: Unlike traditional reimbursement pathways that are negotiated after regulatory clearance, RAPID moves CMS coverage considerations into the premarket clinical development phase. To participate, a manufacturer must engage with CMS and the FDA at the Investigational Device Exemption (IDE) pre-submission stage to design clinical trials. The clinical trials must enroll Medicare beneficiaries and evaluate health outcomes that both the FDA and CMS agree are clinically relevant to the Medicare population.

Contrary Evidence and Other Side of the Story

4.
Contrary evidence: Regulatory approval and breakthrough designation do not guarantee immediate or successful reimbursement, as demonstrated by the historical commercial rollout of the CardioMEMS heart failure monitor. Despite receiving FDA approval in 2014, CardioMEMS faced severe, multi-year commercial setbacks due to localized reimbursement non-coverage decisions by Medicare Administrative Contractors (MACs) First Coast and Novitas in 2015 and 2016. These local non-coverage policies were not retired until July 1, 2020, demonstrating that breakthrough devices can languish in a commercial "valley of death" for years when systemic reimbursement channels fail to align. Other side of the story: While the RAPID pathway is designed to streamline reimbursement, the simultaneous repeal of alternative inpatient prospective payment system (IPPS) pathways creates a much higher barrier for devices that do not qualify. CMS has finalized the repeal of the alternative New Technology Add-on Payment (NTAP) and Outpatient Prospective Payment System (OPPS) Device Pass-through pathways. Consequently, breakthrough technologies that are past the IDE pre-submission phase or fail to qualify for RAPID will face a much more difficult and costly traditional reimbursement pathway with no guaranteed expedited coverage.
