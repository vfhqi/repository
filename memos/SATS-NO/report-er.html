EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

SATS ASA reported its interim financial results for the second quarter of 2026 on August 14, 2026, delivering group revenue of NOK 1,442 million, representing a 3% reported increase and a 7% currency-adjusted increase compared to Q2 2025, which narrowly beat the Modular Finance consensus of NOK 1,437 million. The company achieved exceptional profitability outperformance, with EBITDA before IFRS 16 growing by 16% to NOK 313 million, beating the Modular Finance consensus expectation of NOK 302 million by 4% and DNB Carnegie's forecast of NOK 284 million.

This material earnings beat was driven by Average Revenue Per Member reaching NOK 635, representing a strong currency-adjusted increase of 6% year-on-year compared to DNB Carnegie's expectation of 4% currency-adjusted growth, demonstrating that SATS's December 2025 price increases of 5% to 7% are successfully rolling through the subscription base.

The quarter's most significant deviation from consensus expectations centered on SATS's ability to drive margin expansion despite operating a smaller physical footprint. The total member count increased by 1% year-over-year to 744,000 members in Q2 2026 despite the company operating with three fewer clubs than a year ago, proving that SATS can expand its base within a smaller club network through higher capacity utilization. Critically, club operating opex increased by only 2% on a currency-adjusted basis, demonstrating excellent like-forlike expense management, which created a wide spread between cost and revenue growth that captured exceptional operational leverage.

The transformative change in the investment thesis revolves around SATS's transition from a growth-oriented physical expansion model to a high-yield cash cow prioritizing immediate capital returns. The Board resolved to distribute a cash dividend of NOK 0.72 per share for the first half of 2026, and when combined with NOK 318 million in share buybacks during H1 2026, SATS achieved a total payout ratio of 152%, which sits well ahead of the company's long-term policy of distributing at least 50% of annual net profit.

This aggressive distribution strategy is enabled by free cash flow surging by 164% year-on-year to NOK 100 million and the company reducing its net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio to 1.1x in Q2 2026 from 1.3x in Q2 2025, remaining comfortably below the group's target range of 1.5x to 2.0x.

Denmark's VAT regulatory change represented a critical test of SATS's pricing power and product differentiation. Denmark removed the VAT exemption on group training and personal training effective January 1, 2026, and SATS adjusted Danish list prices by 21% year-on-year for members over 30 to absorb the VAT impact.

Despite this massive pricing adjustment, Danish membership volumes decreased by a manageable 4% year-on-year in Q2 2026, which was better than initially feared, while Danish country EBITDA before IFRS 16 still improved by 13% currency-adjusted to NOK 16 million, aided by higher VAT opex deductions. This low volume drop highlights the highly price-inelastic demand for premium group training in metropolitan Denmark.

The sell-side response was broadly constructive, with DNB Carnegie raising its adjusted EPS estimates for 2026 and 2027 by a substantial 11% post-results and analyst Ole Martin Westgaard raising the 12-month target price for SATS to NOK 47.0 from NOK 43.0 while reiterating a HOLD rating. The rating maintained neutral reflects SATS's projected 2026e EBIT margin of 17.9% leading the entire Nordic retail peer group, but DNB Carnegie expecting SATS's 2025-2027e EPS CAGR of 14.8% to lag behind the Nordic retail median of 20.9%, which restricts immediate re-rating arguments despite the company's best-in-class profitability. 12 Macroeconomic tailwinds are providing structural support to SATS's premium subscription model across its core Nordic markets.

Norwegian household discretionary budgets are experiencing unexpected acceleration as wage growth outpaces non-discretionary inflation, with DNB Carnegie revising the estimated 2026 average household discretionary consumption growth upward to 5.8% year-on-year. Sweden's consumer outlook has improved dramatically, with the Swedish central bank slashing its policy rate from a peak of 4.0% in May 2024 down to 1.75% in mid-2026, combined with real wage growth projections of 3.2-3.4% for 2026-2027, which is set to drive a robust recovery in discretionary outlays.

The most material risk to the investment case remains SATS's deliberate footprint consolidation strategy. The group is actively executing a portfolio consolidation strategy, operating with three fewer clubs than a year ago, though closing underperforming locations boosts efficiency, it may limit total membership expansion capacity. While SATS increased its committed expansion pipeline to 13 clubs through 2028, up from eight previously, including five planned for 2027, this pipeline could mitigate structural headwinds from recent net closures, prevent long-term market share erosion as low-cost peers aggressively capture suburban territory. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish thesis is that operating a consolidated network with fewer gyms could permanently cap long-term membership growth if competitors aggressively capture suburban market share. Additionally, Norway continues to maintain a zero-VAT regime on physical training, with no ongoing political discussions to change this status, however analysts at DNB Carnegie continue to flag the potential introduction of a fitness VAT in Norway as a major structural risk to the investment case. If Norway decides to implement even a partial VAT on fitness memberships, SATS may be unable to execute similar 21% price hikes without triggering a severe contraction in its core membership base.

The ultimate net read is that SATS ASA's Q2 2026 results confirm extraordinary pricing power and capital return acceleration that significantly upgrades the 18-month EPS trajectory, establishing a highly predictable 25%+ IRR pathway for equity investors. The combination of 6% currency-adjusted ARPM growth, disciplined 2% club opex growth, 164% free cash flow expansion, and rapid deleveraging to 1.1x demonstrates that SATS has successfully transitioned from a footprint expansion story to a high-yield, margin-led cash generation model.

The primary investment debate shifts from physical growth velocity to whether SATS can sustain its premium pricing power through late 2026 and 2027 as list price increases approach consumer acceptance thresholds. Investors should monitor Q3 2026 organic membership retention following seasonal campaigns, verify if Sweden's real wage growth continues to accelerate, and watch for any political developments regarding Norway's VAT exemption status, as these metrics will confirm if SATS can sustain its pricing momentum into the upcoming low-season quarters.
