Gross Debt and Interest Mix

1.
Substantial maturities: Diageo faces a structured refinancing schedule over the next three years, with a significant portion of gross borrowings maturing in the near term. The company's annual report published on 17 August 2026 shows that out of 21,511 million dollars in total gross borrowings, 2,449 million dollars are due within one year, while 4,247 million dollars mature between one and three years. Another 3,765 million dollars are scheduled to mature between three and five years, indicating that medium-term refinancing needs will continuously shape cash flows and impact interest expenses.
2.
Favorable positioning: Diageo maintains a highly insulated balance sheet against near-term interest rate volatility through a heavily fixed debt structure. According to the Companies House filing on 16 November 2026, the group held 18,547 million dollars in fixed-rate net borrowings compared to only 1,368 million dollars in floatingrate net borrowings. This highly fixed mix aligns with the board's risk management policy to limit interest expense volatility by keeping fixed-rate debt within 70% to 90% of forecast net borrowings.
3.
Steady improvement: Diageo has demonstrated historical stability in its financing costs, with its effective interest rate showing a downward trajectory in recent years. The group's filings on 16 November 2026 show that the average effective interest rate fell from 4.3% in fiscal 2024 to 4.1% in fiscal 2025, and further to 3.9% in fiscal 2026. While management guides to a slight rate increase to approximately 4.0% for fiscal 2027, this historical trend suggests strong execution in debt markets.

Rolling over these notes in a higher yield environment will gradually drag on capital allocation and put downward pressure on earnings per share.
