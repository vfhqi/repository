EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Vicat S.A. operates a resilient, vertically integrated mineral and building materials model with consolidated revenues of €3,854 million in fiscal year 2025, balancing cash-generative developed markets (60% of sales) with high-growth emerging countries (40% of sales). The dominant Cement segment represents 53.1% of consolidated revenue at €2,047 million, while the strategically integrated Ready-Mixed Concrete and Aggregates divisions comprise 36.9% of revenues at €1,421 million.

Management explicitly pursues downstream concrete integration to reinforce and secure volume outlets for its core Cement business, providing structural protection for upstream clinker utilization during cyclical downturns. The founding Merceron-Vicat family maintains majority control and stable governance since 1853, ensuring long-term strategic continuity and conservative capital allocation discipline.

Business Model and Geographic Structure: Vicat maintains active operations across 12 developed and emerging countries with 10,423 employees globally, recently consolidating from six historical geographic divisions into four simplified regional segments effective January 2026. The new Europe region integrates France, Switzerland, and Italy to leverage common strategies and comparable growth levers, while the Asia-Mediterranean region combines India, Kazakhstan, Turkey, and Egypt under a shared management structure. The multiyear Turin-Lyon high-speed rail link (TELT) provides highly visible volume demand beyond 2030, exemplifying the structural infrastructure tailwinds supporting the European core. The "producing local to build local" approach minimizes costly product transport distances, exploiting the fact that cement is structurally constrained to an inland shipping radius of approximately 150km due to high heavy transport costs.

Financial Performance and Peer Benchmarking: Vicat operates at a materially smaller scale of €3,854 million compared to global heavyside giants such as Heidelberg Materials, which reported €21,460 million in revenues for FY2025. The group achieved a 3-year revenue CAGR of 2.2% from 2022 to 2025, underperforming CRH's 4.8% growth rate but outperforming Cementir's contraction of -0.2% over the same period. The group's reported EBITDA margin compressed slightly by 20 basis points to 20.0% in fiscal year 2025 compared to 20.2% in 2024, which lags peer leader Buzzi Unicem at 27.1% and Cementir Holding at 24.8%.

Vicat achieved €324 million in free cash flow in 2025, marking the third consecutive year of strong cash generation, delivering a free cash flow yield of approximately 9% at the start of 2026, which represents one of the highest cash yields in the building materials industry. However, return on capital employed (ROCE) remained stable but low at 8.1% in 2025, structurally lagging CRH's ROCE of 13.3% in 2025 and Buzzi Unicem's Adjusted ROIC of 16.2%.

20 22 24 26 Industry Structure and Regulatory Transformation: European free ETS carbon allocations will progressively decline starting in 2026, dropping by 48% in 2030 and down to 0% by 2034, while the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) became fully operational on January 1, 2026, levying tariffs on carbon-intensive imports. Phase-out of free allocations transforms cement from a high fixed cost to a high variable cost industry, forcing emitters to adopt a value-over-volume mindset, abandoning destructive price cuts to chase incremental market share during downturns. CBAM carbon tariffs effectively prevent non-EU foreign manufacturers from dumping cheap, carbon-intensive cement into vulnerable coastal European markets. Spot EU ETS carbon prices traded at €75-87/t CO2 in 2025, with Vicat forecasting that average European CO2 prices will reach €140 per ton by 2030, peaking at €250 from 2036 onward. Critically, Vicat holds a significant surplus of 4.9 million free EU ETS carbon allowances valued at approximately €424 million, fully covering Vicat's compliance needs through 2030-2032, providing substantial near-term margin insulation versus peers.

Value Chain Positioning and Margin Distribution: Upstream clinker production captures the highest margin pool in the vertical chain due to high barriers, with typical EBIT margins of 25% to 35% across mature European markets. In contrast, ready-mixed concrete mixing is highly commoditized and dilutes overall corporate margins due to low barriers, with ready-mixed operations typically struggling to exceed EBIT margins of 3% to 8% standalone.

Vicat's core Cement business achieves superior profitability over downstream segments, yielding EBITDA margins of 24.94% on €2,385.86 million operating revenue, while the Concrete & Aggregates segment yields EBITDA margins of only 9.87% on €1,476.88 million. Vicat expands downstream specifically to protect upstream clinker utilization and secure cement sales outlets, with Vicat concrete plants obtaining a substantial portion of cement and aggregates internally.

Customer Dynamics and Pricing Power: No single customer accounts for more than 10% of total revenue, reducing structural reliance on individual accounts. The group maintains strong pricing discipline across major regions to fully transfer cost inflation to customers, implementing a ~10% price ask in France to offset escalating

EDF nuclear electricity contracts and an $8-10 per ton cement price hike in the US to protect margins. Turkish cement prices rose +33.9% like-for-like in 2025 to recover soaring local energy costs. Cement represents only ~1% of residential construction budgets, enabling frictionless pass-through of regulatory carbon costs, demonstrating the highly inelastic nature of builder demand. Supply Chain and Energy Strategy: Total consolidated energy expenses amounted to €513 million in fiscal year 2025, which represents approximately 13% of total group revenues.

The group secured 70/80% of its 2026 European electricity consumption, alongside 100% in California (Lebec) and 80% in Brazil, while Vicat signed a 15-year contract with EDF starting in 2026, securing 70% of French electricity needs. Alternative fuels accounted for 37.4% of cement process thermal energy in 2025 compared to 36.0% in 2024, targeting 50% globally by 2030, with the group targeting 100% alternative fuel use in Europe by 2030, eliminating primary fossil fuels to lower carbon exposure.

Competitive Landscape and Regional Dynamics: Four large groups control over 80% of total French cement volume, including multinationals Holcim, Heidelberg Materials, Eqiom (CRH), and Vicat as the sole French-owned producer. Switzerland exhibits a highly consolidated market structure with only three domestic producers creating high market concentration, while Vicat's subsidiary National Cement ranks as the 12th largest cement manufacturer nationally in the United States with an estimated 3% market share.

Major European producers have abandoned destructive market share price wars, preferring to idle capacity to maintain margins, with listed peers like Heidelberg Materials and Cemex prioritizing positive price-over-cost spreads over simple volume growth.

33 35 Management Quality and Capital Allocation: The founding Merceron-Vicat family directly and indirectly controls 75.45% of total voting rights as of December 2025, representing 63.38% of the company's outstanding share capital. Family-led management focuses on continuous asset modernization rather than near-term volume maximization, supporting a 30-year track record of stable or growing dividends. Net capital expenditure decreased to €299 million in fiscal year 2025, down from €320 million in 2024, with capital allocation split evenly with €176 million for maintenance and €161 million for strategic growth.

Management paused new strategic growth projects, pledging zero expansion capex until net debt/EBITDA drops below 2.0x, successfully reducing net financial debt to €1,151 million, representing 1.49x leverage. Decarbonization Strategy and Disruption Positioning: Management lowered its group clinker factor target to 69% by 2030, down from 76% in 2025, leveraging calcined clay that burns at ~800°C and does not release process CO2, reducing emissions by ~50% versus traditional Portland clinker.

The flagship €700 million VAIA initiative at the Montalieu-Vercieu plant aims to capture 1.20 million tonnes of CO2 annually post- 2030, having secured €340 million in combined funding from the EU Innovation Fund and the French GPID framework. However, Deputy CEO Hugues Chomel notes VAIA requires ETS carbon prices of ~€150/t to justify taking final investment decisions, creating execution uncertainty if carbon markets remain subdued.

Investment Thesis Synthesis: Vicat presents a high-quality, family-controlled regional cement champion protected by concentrated oligopoly structures and massive banked carbon allowances, delivering sector-leading free cash flow yields despite structurally lower returns on capital. The company's vertical integration strategy secures upstream clinker utilization, while long-term nuclear electricity hedging and alternative fuel substitution insulate margins from energy volatility. However, material exposure to fragmented, hyperinflationary emerging markets (Turkey, India, Egypt) introduces significant earnings volatility, while heavy downstream concrete integration dilutes consolidated margins relative to pure-play cement peers like Buzzi Unicem. The investment case hinges on management's ability to execute disciplined deleveraging below 1.0x by 2027 while maintaining pricing discipline in its protected European core markets, balanced against execution risks in capital-intensive carbon capture projects requiring elevated carbon prices and continued emerging market currency devaluations offsetting developed market pricing gains.

Vicat S.A. operates at a structurally lower return profile compared to its European building materials peers, with return on capital employed remaining stable but low at 8.1% in 2025, representing a foundational weakness that constrains the company's ability to compound shareholder value at competitive rates. This return profile lags significantly behind industry leaders such as Buzzi Unicem, which achieved an adjusted ROIC of 16.2%, and CRH, which generated a ROCE of 13.3% in 2025.

The persistent gap reflects Vicat's smaller scale, heavy capital intensity in emerging markets, and extensive downstream integration into lower-margin ready-mixed concrete operations, all of which dilute overall corporate returns as detailed in Section 2.

385 The company's EBITDA margin profile presents a second material weakness relative to peer benchmarks. Vicat's reported EBITDA margin of 20.0% in fiscal year 2025 lags peer leader Buzzi Unicem at 27.1% and Cementir Holding at 24.8%, a gap exceeding 400 to 700 basis points that persists despite management's efforts to optimize pricing and cost structures. This margin disadvantage stems from the company's extensive vertical integration into commoditized downstream concrete, which Section 4 demonstrates yields EBITDA margins of only 9.87% compared to the core Cement segment's 24.94%.

The strategic rationale of securing upstream clinker volumes through downstream outlets creates a structural margin drag that cannot be easily remediated without fundamentally restructuring the business portfolio.

Vicat's modest organic growth trajectory constitutes a third area of relative underperformance that threatens long-term competitive positioning. The company achieved a 3-year revenue CAGR of 2.2% from 2022 to 2025, underperforming CRH's 4.8% growth rate, reflecting both geographic exposure to mature, low-growth European markets and cyclical headwinds in key emerging regions like India and Turkey. Section 5 highlights that while infrastructure projects like the TELT rail link provide multi-year volume visibility, persistent residential construction weakness in developed markets and intense regional price competition in southern India constrain the company's ability to drive meaningful volume expansion.

This tepid growth profile limits Vicat's capacity to leverage its heavy fixed-cost base and improve return metrics through scale economies.

The company faces significant energy cost exposure and inflationary pressures within its supply chain that present ongoing operational risks. Total consolidated energy expenses amounted to €513 million in fiscal year 2025, representing approximately 13% of total group revenues, creating substantial earnings volatility when fuel and power markets experience geopolitical or regulatory shocks. The strategic margin insulation provided by this carbon buffer represents a multiyear competitive moat unavailable to subscale regional competitors and differentiates Vicat's near-term margin trajectory from European peers facing immediate carbon tax exposure. The third noteworthy outlier is the extreme regional margin dispersion within Vicat's vertically integrated business model, where the Cement segment yields EBITDA margins of 24.94% on €2,385.86 million operating revenue, while the Concrete & Aggregates segment yields only 9.87% EBITDA margins on €1,476.88 million.

This 15-percentage-point margin gap between upstream and downstream operations ranks Vicat in the top quartile of vertically integrated European building materials companies by internal margin dispersion. While upstream clinker production captures the highest margin pool with typical EBIT margins of 25% to 35% due to massive capital barriers, ready-mixed concrete mixing is highly commoditized and dilutes overall corporate margins, typically struggling to exceed EBIT margins of 3% to 8% standalone.

This structural margin hierarchy is detailed in Section 4, where Vicat expands downstream specifically to protect upstream clinker utilization and secure cement sales outlets, guaranteeing high kiln utilization and optimizing fixed-cost operating leverage. However, Section 4 also highlights that extensive vertical integration into downstream ready-mixed concrete exposes the Group to severe labor and transport cost inflation, which cannot always be passed through during building downturns, creating an inherent margin volatility asymmetry that distinguishes Vicat from peers pursuing portfolio rotation into higher-margin lightside construction chemicals.
