Implications — Prysmian (PRY-IT) — Q3 2026 results, 29 October 2026

These results, read with what the shares have done since, move the case a long way and in the main upward: the company delivered the earnings side of the case and more, while the price has fallen to, and briefly through, the thresholds the case itself set, so the reason for the park has gone — unless the first of the two readings below is right, in which case the price is telling us that the earnings, not the multiple, are the problem.

What changed

The quarter was a beat and raise, with the beat concentrated in one place.

  • Q3 sales EUR 6,417m, organic growth 13.1% (9.4% in Q2), 8.0% above company-compiled consensus of about EUR 5,942m. Adjusted EBITDA EUR 851m, up 32.1% year on year and 7.31% above the EUR 793m consensus; management called it the best quarter in the company's history.
  • Electrification adjusted EBITDA EUR 369m beat consensus by 20.0%; the Industrial and Construction margin was 17.6% against 14.5% a year earlier, on the review's standard-metal basis; North American Industrial and Construction organic growth 24.0%. Electrification now supplies about 45% of group EBITDA.
  • The misses: group quarterly margin 13.3%, 10 basis points below consensus; Power Grid 13.8% from 14.7%, 40 basis points below, on a five-fold rise in the US Midwest aluminium premium hitting fixed-price overhead-line contracts, with management expecting a return to 15% once the legacy contracts complete by early 2027; Renewable Transmission 20.0% (17.8% a year earlier) against one broker's 20.9%; Digital Solutions EUR 134m, 1.0% light, at a 25.0% margin (19.6% a year earlier). Quarterly free cash flow missed on the timing of the Molex downpayment, and the trailing-twelve-month figure of EUR 1,434m is flattered by it.
  • Guidance: FY2026 adjusted EBITDA raised to EUR 3,075m to 3,125m, a midpoint of EUR 3,100m against the prior EUR 2,850m and 6.06% above prior consensus of EUR 2,923m; the whole range sits above the top of the case's EUR 2,800m to 2,900m, which is the range the case's "small upgrade room" judgement rested on. Free cash flow guidance raised to EUR 1,750m to 1,850m, 9.69% above consensus. Management said the 2028 adjusted EBITDA target of EUR 3.05bn will be beaten two years early. Part of the raise is Atkore, closed 19 October 2026 (about EUR 60m of Q4 EBITDA on one broker's estimate); the organic part is a high-single-digit upgrade to Q4. The CFO put full-year adjusted EPS at about EUR 4.60, which sits below every broker number below; the review does not reconcile the definitions.
  • Net financial debt EUR 2,857m at 30 September 2026 (EUR 4,318m a year earlier), 14.74% better than consensus; year-end leverage projected near 0.8x. Transmission backlog stable at about EUR 16.5bn with a further EUR 2bn secured and not yet booked. Encore synergies USD 110m captured of USD 140m. New long-range targets: Transmission margin 24% to 25% by 2030, about a point above consensus; Digital Solutions 26% or more in 2027, rising to 30% by 2030. Two hyperscaler fibre agreements worth about EUR 5bn combined are expected between December 2026 and January 2027. Eight new medium-voltage lines come on at one a quarter from early 2027.
  • The sell-side raised EPS everywhere, by differing amounts: FY2027 to EUR 6.37 (+7.7%), to EUR 6.69 (from EUR 6.17), and +5.0% at the three houses the review carries; FY2026 to EUR 4.93 to 5.15. Targets went the other way: one cut to EUR 163 from EUR 167 on sector de-rating, one raised to EUR 110 with an Equal-weight held because Electrification lacks a backlog, one held at EUR 145; the median is EUR 145. More than one house reads further near-term upgrade potential as limited and the improvement as already understood.
  • The price: EUR 110.40 on 30 October, EUR 93.12 on 25 November, EUR 99.24 on 26 November. Six-month return -35.6%; 18.8% below the 200-day average of EUR 122.21; relative-strength percentile 19. P/E 12.9x on consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 7.71, the 25th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history. Dashboard pins: technical momentum D, sell-side earnings momentum A, valuation B. EPS revisions +6% over one month and +7% over three; target price -4% and -5%; buy share 65%; consensus target EUR 152.05.

Set against the case: the case priced the shares at EUR 124.0 on 17.5x consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 7.08, the 90th percentile; its earnings path was FY2026 EUR 4.85, FY2027 EUR 6.00, FY2028 EUR 6.90, 2% and 4% below consensus at the time; its modal FY2026 EBITDA was about EUR 2,870m. Since then consensus 24-month-forward EPS has risen about 9% and the multiple has fallen from 17.5x to 12.9x in eight weeks. The case called the de-rated branch modal and gave the multiple-holding branch about one in three; it did not expect the de-rating to arrive inside a quarter, on upgrades, with no estimate cut anywhere.

The challenge

Two readings the case does not hold, each with a real chance of being right.

First: the market is pricing an earnings cut, not a de-rating, and the beat is the top of the US low-voltage cycle. The case modelled its mark-to-market risk as a de-rating with earnings unchanged, and its drop-through adverse case as EPS 11% lower from D&A, interest and tax, not from margins; it said a multiple "needs no mechanism". The strongest alternative is that the mechanism is in this print. Electrification is about 45% of group EBITDA and has no backlog; management itself called North American Industrial and Construction margins unsustainably high and expects pricing to normalise as competitors add capacity; one house puts US low-voltage margins at 27% against a historic 20%; Atkore's own margins halved in FY2025 when supply eased; and Prysmian is adding eight medium-voltage lines of its own. A de-rating of about 38% from the June peak against continuous upgrades, followed by a further fall after a beat and raise, is what a market discounting a future estimate cut looks like, and it is the "wisdom of crowd" and "market catch-up" red flags the case had forming, not formed. In this reading 12.9x is a peak-earnings multiple; the FY2027 and FY2028 numbers (the brokers' EUR 6.37 to 6.69 and the case's own EUR 6.90) carry a record Electrification margin into years in which it will not be there; and the relevant entry is the case's drop-through level of about EUR 88 or lower, not EUR 98. What has to be true: US non-residential construction slows or competitor low-voltage capacity lands through 2027, so that North American Industrial and Construction margins fall back toward last year's 14.5% (the review's own change-of-view line is below 20.0% by mid-2027); the Section 232 shield (a 25% tariff on derivative wire and cable) is weakened; or Atkore's integration slips. None of the three is visible in the quarter; all three are named by the houses that are neutral. This reading also explains the one fact the bullish reading cannot: why target prices fell while EPS rose.

Second: the premium half is going higher, not just holding, and the case's earnings base is too low. The case anchored at 16.0x and declined 17.4x because the premium mix was not rising and two-thirds of the business sat at peak margins; its modal FY2028 EBITDA margin was about 14.3%, with Transmission's Alarm below 18% and Digital Solutions' Alarm below 20%. Management has now set Transmission at 24% to 25% by 2030 and Digital Solutions at 26% or more next year rising to 30%, under take-or-pay structures of the Molex kind; it says the 2028 EBITDA target falls in 2026; the backlog covers Transmission revenue to the end of 2028. In this reading the case's modal path, now roughly 6% to 10% below the brokers for FY2027, is the conservative tail; the top of the brokers' FY2028 range (EUR 7.65 at one house now, EUR 8.50 at the top of the range the case recorded) is nearer the mode; and at 12.9x the return clears the bar with no help from the multiple. The case's mix objection survives, because Electrification's outperformance and Atkore push the premium share down, not up, but mix is the wrong lens if the premium half's margin rises by a fifth or more from here on contracted volume. What has to be true: new high-voltage award pricing holds through NKT's Karlskrona start (Q2 2027), Sumitomo's Scottish plant (opened October 2026) and the Chinese entrants (Zhongtian's RMB 3.2bn subsea backlog with TenneT and Ørsted, which comes close to the case's Alarm of a flagship European tender going to an Asian entrant); the two EUR 5bn fibre agreements land in the December to January window; Digital Solutions holds 25% through 2027. The case put the first of those conditions at about 50%.

We do not think the results admit a third reading of weight. The Power Grid miss is aluminium on legacy contracts, dated and explained; the free cash flow miss is timing. What the two readings share is that both say the case's current numbers are wrong, in opposite directions, and that the price has, for now, chosen the first.

What would settle it

  • The first reading turns on the North American Industrial and Construction margin. The review's own line is below 20.0% by mid-2027, observable at the Q2 2027 results in July 2027; the case's Alarm is any sequential fall and its Invalidate is 8.0% on the case's basis. The two bases differ (the case recorded the group at 12.2% on current metal prices against 14.2% on standard for FY2025), so the review's 17.6% cannot be set against the case's 8.0% directly; the memo update has to put them on one footing before either threshold can be read. Earlier reads: the Q4 2026 exit rate at the FY2026 results in February 2027; US copper wire and cable PPI and distributor inventory, monthly; any formal review of Section 232.
  • The second reading turns on three dated events: the two EUR 5bn hyperscaler fibre agreements, expected December 2026 to January 2027 and not committed; new high-voltage award pricing as Karlskrona starts in Q2 2027; and the Capital Markets Day in H1 2027, where the 2030 margin targets become formal and where the case's own "what would change our mind" sits (Transmission and Digital Solutions above half of EBITDA).
  • The second review, four working days from now, will carry the settled broker set after the 2 to 6 November notes, consensus FY2027 and FY2028 EPS against the case's EUR 6.00 and EUR 6.90, the re-based target consensus (EUR 152.05 on the dashboard against the case's EUR 159.94), and the price against the thresholds. It will not carry any of the evidence that decides between the two readings: no new segment margin, no fibre agreement, no award pricing, no Capital Markets Day. It will say how far the case's numbers are from the street's; it will not say whose are right.
  • The CFO's EUR 4.60 adjusted EPS against the brokers' EUR 4.93 to 5.15 for FY2026 is a definitional question the second review may resolve and the memo update needs resolved, because the case's path is on the brokers' basis.
  • The CFO handover on 1 January 2027 and the Atkore synergy run-rate (USD 150m targeted) are the integration-bandwidth concern made live; no disclosure date for synergy progress is committed before the Capital Markets Day.
