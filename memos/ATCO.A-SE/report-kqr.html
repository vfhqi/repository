Section 2 – Market structure and sub-fab content economics

Section summary
  1. The expansion of leading-edge logic, DRAM, and high-bandwidth memory drives a multi-year sub-fab vacuum upcycle as increasing architectural complexity and extreme ultraviolet lithography exponentially raise process chamber and gas extraction intensity

Sub-fab content economics and tool density

1.
Expanding sub-fab content: DNB Carnegie observes that 85-90% of Atlas Copco's semiconductor equipment is located in the sub-fab floor underneath cleanrooms, where dry-vacuum systems continuously handle hazardous process exhaust.
2.
Critical tool reliability: DNB Carnegie highlights that sub-fab dry pumps and abatement systems are highly critical to process-tool uptime, as any sub-fab vacuum failure will halt the cleanroom equipment above.
3.
Expanding process relevance: Redburn Atlantic reports that Chemical Vapor Deposition, Physical Vapor Deposition, and dry etching require vacuum pumps to create ultra-clean and controlled environments for nanoscale thin-film fabrication.
4.
Rising vacuum intensity: Jefferies Research notes that vacuum content is structurally higher in deposition and etching tools than lithography because the vacuum serves as the process environment itself.
5.
Stringent environment control: Jefferies Research explains that transistor node shrink requires purer vacuum environments and more higher-value vacuum components at each step to prevent microscopic particulate contamination from reducing wafer yields.
6.
Precise atomic deposition: Lam Research indicates its Altus systems combine Chemical Vapor Deposition and Atomic Layer Deposition to deposit highly conformal and selective barrier films for advanced metallization.

Advanced logic and memory physical drivers

7.
Extreme vertical scaling: Applied Materials technologists state that building a single Gate-All-Around device requires more than 500 process steps with five distinct materials, massively multiplying sub-fab processing intensity.
8.
Expanding logic complexity: Redburn Atlantic emphasizes that the transition to 2nm Gate-All-Around architectures shifts the WFE tool mix heavily toward vacuum-dependent deposition and etch steps, raising vacuum valve intensity per capex dollar.
9.
Accelerating physical content: Jefferies Research points out that logic-specific innovations such as Backside Power Delivery and Molybdenum Metallisation are meaningfully increasing physical vacuum content within leading-edge logic fabs.
10.
Expanding vertical demand: Freedom Broker argues that higher-layer 3D NAND and high-bandwidth memory architectures rely heavily on repeated deposition and etching, which increases sub-fab gas delivery demand.
11.
Structural node progression: Jefferies Research expects that the post-2031 transition to 3D DRAM will drive structural growth for vacuum as vertical stacking introduces significantly more deposition and etch steps.
12.
Inflating wafer intensity: A former SK Hynix director explains that HBM packaging yields drop below 80% for 12-high stacks, requiring more gross wafer starts that directly inflate overall vacuum tool utilization.
13.
Increasing cell size: A former SK Hynix director reports that HBM4 wafers consume 30% more cell space than conventional DRAM due to Through-Silicon Via digging, requiring specialized high-vacuum etch steps.

Lithography vacuum intensity and WFE translation

14.
Essential vacuum requirement: Bernstein Research explains that 13.5nm extreme ultraviolet light is absorbed by air, forcing EUV systems to operate in vacuum and replace refractive lenses with reflective mirrors.
15.
Expanding exhaust volume: Edwards patents show that increasingly large quantities of hydrogen gas are used as a curtain to shield optics from tin debris, requiring heavy-duty sub-fab dry pumps for continuous gas extraction.
16.
Elevated power consumption: Edwards patents indicate that exhausting massive quantities of EUV process gases at atmospheric pressure demands substantial sub-fab electrical power, forcing vacuum OEMs to develop sub-atmospheric recovery systems.
17.
Rising lithography intensity: Bernstein Research reports that lithography WFE intensity structurally reset from 17% in 2022 to stabilize at 23% to 24% due to expanding EUV adoption across logic and DRAM nodes.
18.
Accelerating spending forecasts: SEMI forecasts, cited by Freedom Broker, show global WFE spending rising from $150 billion in 2026 to $190 billion in 2027 and approaching $220 billion by 2028, driving substantial vacuum orders.
19.
Surging memory spending: SEMI forecasts, cited by Freedom Broker, estimate that 300mm memory equipment spending will surpass $50 billion for first time in 2026 before reaching $57 billion in 2027.
20.
Other side of the story: Jefferies Research notes that while vertical stacking in 3D NAND continues to advance, overall NAND WFE spending remains highly sluggish as vertical density gains reduce the need for incremental wafer starts, capping overall sub-fab equipment unit expansion.

  2. Atlas Copco leads the sector with rapid capacity expansion, translating record-high memory orders into profitable revenue conversion while private-to-public consolidation optionality provides unique strategic levers

The sub-fab content density dimension carries the highest confidence because it is anchored in verifiable physical engineering requirements rather than cyclical demand forecasts. Gate-All-Around devices require more than 500 process steps with five distinct materials, massively multiplying sub-fab processing intensity, while HBM4 wafers consume 30% more cell space than conventional DRAM due to Through-Silicon Via digging, requiring specialized high-vacuum etch steps.

These architectural complexities are design-locked years in advance and cannot be easily reversed by short-term demand fluctuations. The physical necessity of vacuum environments for Chemical Vapor Deposition, Physical Vapor Deposition, and EUV lithography creates a structural floor beneath equipment demand that transcends cyclical volatility. competitive positioning similarly merits high confidence due to deeply entrenched qualification barriers and aftermarket lock-in effects.

High physical degradation under harsh operating environments requires dry pumps to undergo comprehensive overhauls within six months of installation, creating highly predictable recurring aftermarket service revenue streams. The combination of dominant market share, copy-exact qualification requirements, and service attachment rates creates a formidable competitive moat that is observable across multiple broker reports and expert interviews.

Unlike forward-looking demand forecasts, these structural advantages are present-tense facts that can be verified through field research and customer interviews. The memory cycle volatility dimension carries significantly lower confidence because it depends on unpredictable behavioral responses from oligopolistic memory manufacturers. Morgan Stanley reports DRAM pricing is set to transition to late-cycle by 4Q26, which threatens to severely depress investor sentiment, while rapid DRAM capex increases can trigger oversupply within two years, historically causing sharp pricing collapses.

Historical precedent shows that memory capital expenditure cycles are notoriously unstable, with sudden inventory corrections capable of halting equipment orders within a single quarter. The timing and magnitude of such corrections cannot be predicted with precision using publicly available data. Chinese domestic substitution represents the dimension with the lowest predictive confidence due to opaque qualification timelines and rapidly evolving geopolitical constraints.

The precise displacement speed of European dry pumps by domestic manufacturers in Chinese foundries remains highly uncertain and volatile due to undisclosed qualification timelines at SMIC, while tracking precise market share in harsh process applications is limited by competitive reporting gaps. Expert interviews reveal that Chinese tool makers are aggressively copying foreign hardware, yet the exact timeline for achieving qualification in the most demanding chemical vapor deposition and furnace applications remains genuinely unknowable without direct access to Chinese fab floor data.

This information asymmetry fundamentally limits the resolvability of the Chinese substitution threat. The private-to-public consolidation thesis for Busch and Pfeiffer Vacuum faces severe confidence constraints due to the absence of transparent financial reporting and strategic intent. Busch Group's private status veils combined research spending and prevents analysts from directly comparing consolidated Pfeiffer and Busch operating profit with listed competitors, while the tight integration of Pfeiffer Vacuum under Busch obscures distinct product segment margins, making it difficult to isolate high-vacuum margins from commodity industrial pumps.

Without access to private board discussions or binding term sheets, any consolidation timeline remains speculative regardless of strategic logic.

1.
Other side of the story: The confidence classification itself may embed systematic bias by overweighting nearterm observable metrics while underestimating the speed of structural regime shifts. If Chinese DRAM manufacturers successfully bypass extreme ultraviolet lithography constraints via 3D DRAM architectures, the timing of domestic tooling maturity could accelerate far faster than the consensus expects. Similarly, sudden memory capital expenditure cuts or a rapid reduction in the layer counts of advanced highbandwidth memory stacks could abruptly strand massive cleanroom and power capacity additions, leaving specialized contractors and filtration suppliers exposed to severe utilization and margin de-rating. Highconfidence dimensions may prove brittle if the underlying physical architecture assumptions are invalidated by disruptive process innovations or geopolitical supply chain fractures that are currently invisible to traditional sellside research methodologies.
