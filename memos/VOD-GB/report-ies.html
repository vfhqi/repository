Vodafone (VOD-GB): what the H1 FY27 results do to the existing case

These results strengthen the operating half of our case modestly and leave its central disagreement with the market, the per-share line below EBITDAaL, exactly where it was, because the evidence that would test it is not in what we have.

1. What changed

The case went into 10 November 2026 with its own odds on guidance: 40 percent reiterate at the upper end, 40 percent softer wording, 15 percent cut, 5 percent raise. The 5 percent outcome happened.

  • Guidance raised. FY27 Adjusted EBITDAaL to EUR 13.2bn to 13.4bn (from EUR 13.0bn to 13.3bn); FY27 Adjusted free cash flow to EUR 2.8bn to 3.0bn (from EUR 2.6bn to 2.9bn). The case's own FY27 figures were EUR 13.17bn and EUR 2.85bn, so the new EBITDAaL floor sits above the case's point forecast. European EBITDAaL guidance is unchanged at EUR 7.6bn to 7.9bn: the raise is Turkey and Africa.
  • Top line ahead, profit slightly short. Group organic service revenue growth was 6.4 percent in the second quarter (consensus 5.1 percent) and 5.8 percent for the half. H1 Adjusted EBITDAaL of EUR 6,451m was 0.9 percent below consensus (EUR 6,510m). That is the pattern the case describes: small profit misses against consensus, cash ahead.
  • Germany as the case assumed, with one detail that is new. Service revenue growth went from +1.2 percent in the first quarter to -0.5 percent in the second; mobile service revenue fell 1.9 percent in the second quarter; H1 EBITDAaL fell 3.2 percent to EUR 2,139m (60 basis points below consensus), and only about 1 percent excluding the court-ordered price reversal. Broadband net losses were 98,000 and 100,000 in the two quarters, against the case's about 95,000. New-customer broadband ARPU rose 25 percent; mobile contract net additions turned to +10,000 in the second quarter after -85,000 in the first. The new detail: the losses are reported as concentrated in older DSL lines, not in cable.
  • UK on the case's path. H1 EBITDAaL of EUR 984m (+5.9 percent organic, 1.0 percent below consensus), EUR 50m of gross synergies in the half. Twice the half is close to the case's EUR 1.95bn for FY27.
  • Balance sheet. Net debt EUR 33.5bn and leverage 2.5 times at 30 September 2026, with management expecting the bottom half of the 2.25 to 2.75 times range by the end of FY27. The buyback is paused. A EUR 4.85bn non-cash German impairment was taken. The interim dividend rose 2.5 percent to 2.31 eurocents.
  • Cost. EUR 200m of gross opex savings in the half through about 3,000 role reductions; a further 2,300 German roles to go. The programme's size is unchanged.
  • Sell-side response. Morgan Stanley stayed equal-weight with a 125p target and judged the new EBITDAaL range in line with consensus; the free cash flow midpoint is 2.2 percent above consensus (EUR 2,840m). Deutsche Bank reiterated buy at 160p and suggested consensus EPS upgrades of more than 10 percent for FY30 are possible. No post-results notes from Barclays, Citi, Jefferies or UBS were found. The shares rose 1.5 percent to 118.40p on the release.

Two gaps in the evidence matter more than anything above. First, the case expected this release to show, for the first time, Safaricom's minorities and the post-disposal financing cost. We have neither figure, and no EPS figure. Second, H1 Adjusted free cash flow is given as both minus EUR 0.4bn and minus EUR 845m; against the Deutsche Bank forecast of minus EUR 560m, one is a beat and the other is a miss.

2. The challenge

We see three readings the case does not hold, and one obvious alternative that fails.

Reading one: the German decline is a DSL run-off, not a cable overbuild. The case's frame for Germany is a cable network being overbuilt by fibre, in the largest and wholly owned profit pool, and it models German EBITDAaL falling about 2.5 percent a year. If the broadband losses are concentrated in resold DSL lines, the lines leaving are the low-margin ones and the cable base is holding better than the headline says. That would explain why 198,000 lines left in the half while fixed service revenue grew 1.1 percent organically, and why underlying EBITDAaL fell only about 1 percent, which is the case's own bull branch for Germany (-1 percent a year), not its base. In its strongest form: the volume number the case and the market both watch is the wrong gauge, and the right one, cable lines and cable ARPU, is stable. For this to be right, the technology split has to be as described and durable, the ECJ reversal letters must not trigger cable churn, and mobile, where service revenue is falling 1.9 percent and the gap to Deutsche Telekom is about five percentage points, must not take over as the source of decline. The mobile point is the weak link: the second-quarter fall is the wholesale lap the case already expected, but the gap to the leader is wider than a lap explains.

Reading two: management is a repeat raiser, and the six months of negative skew the case expects do not arrive. The case calls management an honest tracker that raises once, and builds its timing on that: softer events through to May 2027, a plateau that is more likely to resolve down, and an entry near 100p. FY27 has now been raised twice, the second time while absorbing the ECJ cost that the case listed as unabsorbed. If that is the pattern, FY28 guidance in May 2027 would plausibly bracket consensus and not the case's lower EUR 13.9bn, and the reset the case is waiting for is a low-probability state. For this to be right, the second raise has to be operating delivery and not currency or perimeter. The evidence is against a strong version: Europe is unchanged, the raise is emerging markets, Morgan Stanley reads the new range as in line with consensus, and the case itself named a stronger rand as a raise trigger. So the reading is plausible on style and unproven on substance.

Reading three: the market capitalises cash, and our below-the-line EPS disagreement is right but does not move the shares. The largest gap between the case and consensus is FY28 EPS (the case's EUR 0.114 against a consensus of EUR 0.130 when written), through minorities, financing and tax. These results moved the debate to cash: the largest positive surprise is the free cash flow guide, management frames FY27 as the first year of cash expansion, and the only financing evidence we have, interest payments below Deutsche Bank's forecast, leans toward consensus and away from the case's EUR 1,550m FY28 financing cost. If investors anchor on Adjusted free cash flow growing at a double-digit rate, EPS cuts that are largely definitional would not de-rate the stock, and the case's expected route to a lower price closes. For this to be right, the adjusted cash measure has to survive the same leakage the case identifies in EPS. The case's own numbers say it does not fully: its FY27 equity free cash flow after restructuring, spectrum and minorities is EUR 1,075m against EUR 2,850m adjusted. The reading holds only while the market accepts the adjusted measure.

The obvious bear alternative fails as an alternative. "The raise hides a weakening wholly owned core" is not a new reading; it is the case's own ownership asymmetry, and the evidence fits it: both Germany and the UK missed consensus EBITDAaL while minority-shared markets carried the raise. It confirms the case and does not challenge it. A UK-specific bear reading also fails: the half-year EBITDAaL is on the case's FY27 path.

3. What it does to the case

  • Conservative guidance and the record of delivery (C+, 60): more upward pressure. The reasons that held it there, no absorbed ECJ cost and a 15 percent chance of a cut, have been removed for FY27. The offset is that consensus is again level with the new range and the raise is not European.
  • Scope for upgrades over 6 to 12 months (F, 23): less downward pressure, not reversed. Its leading reason, that guidance was more likely softened or cut than raised, was wrong for this event. Its second reason, a 5 to 12 percent cut to consensus FY28 EPS, is untested, and Deutsche Bank is pointing the other way for FY30.
  • Operating environment (F, 41): the same, with the German reading above as the one thing that could lift it. Broadband losses of 100,000 a quarter are slightly worse than the case's 95,000 on volume and possibly better on mix.
  • Internal change forces (B, 71): the same. Synergy and cost delivery are on schedule; nothing is new.
  • Current momentum (C, 70): mixed, net the same. Guidance momentum has turned positive. The price has not followed.
  • Riskiness (C, 46) and Case Riskiness (C, 47): slightly more pressure. Leverage of 2.5 times sits at the level the case names as putting the buyback at risk if it persists to March 2027, the Kenyan stake sale has been declared void and is under appeal, and the per-share chain is no more visible than before.
  • The side the case took on Germany as the structural negative: the same on the reported numbers, under challenge on interpretation.
  • The side the case took against consensus FY28 EPS: untouched. This is the case's most important judgement and these results, as we have them, say nothing about it.
  • The price thresholds the case states: none crossed. At 116.25p on 17 November 2026 the shares are below the 50-day average (122.0p) and 0.5 percent above the 200-day (115.6p). The case's re-look level of about 100p and its 110p break level are both below the price; its upside marker, a close above the 50-day average on the print, did not happen.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry the broker estimates and targets that land in between: the missing notes from Barclays, Citi, Jefferies and UBS, the post-results consensus for FY28 EPS, and therefore the first evidence of whether revisions run down, as the case expects, or up, as Deutsche Bank suggests. It should also resolve which H1 free cash flow figure is right. It will not carry any new company disclosure, and so it cannot test the German reading or the repeat-raiser reading.
  • H1 minorities and net financing cost. These settle the largest gap between the case and the market. The case's marker is minorities below EUR 250m for the half as evidence against its own view. They are half-year disclosures the company has already made; they are simply not in our evidence, and the second review carries them only if brokers report them. We should obtain them directly.
  • The German technology split. The DSL-against-cable reading needs cable line and cable ARPU figures each quarter. The company has not committed to that split, so this is a question that plausibly stays open; the next German figures of any kind come with the third-quarter update, which the case dates to February 2027. The case's own markers remain two quarters of losses below 60,000 (upward) or service revenue below -2 percent in a quarter (downward).
  • Leverage at March 2027 and the May 2027 statement. Leverage back in the bottom half of the range, FY28 guidance and the buyback decision settle the repeat-raiser reading and the capital loop together. Nothing before May 2027 does.
  • The Kenyan appeal. Undated, and outside the company's control.

VERDICT

  • Direction: positive
  • Grade: minor
  • Moves: guidance outcome landed on the case's 5 percent raise branch (FY27 Adjusted EBITDAaL EUR 13.2bn to 13.4bn against the case's EUR 13.17bn); upward pressure on topic 4 guidance (C+, 60) and topic 7 scope for upgrades (F, 23) without a full grade; pinned sell-side earnings momentum B against the D the case recorded; no price threshold crossed
