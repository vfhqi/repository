EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Bavarian Nordic faces operational strategy uncertainty following CEO Paul Chaplin's planned departure by end- 2026 and Chairman Luc Debruyne's November 2025 resignation after a failed private equity takeover attempt, introducing transitional governance risks during a critical commercial expansion phase. Shareholder discontent manifests through consecutive Remuneration Report rejections over executive compensation levels, while 17.4% employee turnover in 2025 signals potential cultural instability beneath apparently strong operational delivery. The investment case pivots on whether management can execute a strategic transition from R&D innovation to profitable commercial scale while navigating extreme biosecurity revenue volatility and integrating complex manufacturing transfers. Strategic direction shifts from high-risk vaccine innovation toward milking existing approved assets and disciplined commercial-stage acquisitions, though the pending CEO transition and absence of active M&A targets raise questions about near-term capital allocation priorities beyond share buybacks. The firm terminated its DKK 1 billion unutilized credit facility and executed DKK 500 million in share buybacks through July 2026, launching an additional DKK 750 million program in Q3 2026, signaling either financial discipline or a lack of mature acquisition opportunities. Commercial expansion accelerates with Vimkunya's European approval and Brazilian submission via Eurofarma partnership, though five-year GSK manufacturing transfers expose protracted operational and regulatory integration risks.

Public Preparedness demand shifts structurally toward freeze-dried JYNNEOS formulations with enhanced shelflife, securing USD 97 million in BARDA options and over DKK 700 million in undisclosed sovereign contracts spanning 2026-2027, though normalized baseline revenues of DKK 1,800-2,000 million annually reflect inherent cyclicality. Travel Health markets face competition from Pfizer's FSME-IMMUN and Valneva's Ixchiq, though Rabipur experienced only 1pp market share losses while Valneva's safety setbacks position Vimkunya favorably as a safer VLP-based alternative.

Next-generation mRNA mpox candidates like CSPC's SYS6037 and continuous cell line platforms from GeoVax present long-term structural disruption threats to traditional livevirus manufacturing, potentially challenging platform sustainability over five-plus years. Financial inflection accelerates with Travel Health achieving 26% organic growth in H1 2026, driving gross margin to 61% in Q2 2026 and EBITDA margin to 45%, though Public Preparedness declined 18% in H1 2026, exposing extreme cyclicality beneath headline profitability.

Cash generation strengthens post-acquisition milestone completion, with cash reserves reaching DKK 2.3 billion and negative net debt of DKK 3,921 million in 2026e, supporting projected free cash flow above DKK 1 billion annually and a 10% FCF yield, though USD 1.2 billion in M&A capacity remains undeployed. Historical trajectory reveals massive earnings volatility, with Public Preparedness surging to DKK 5,027 million in 2023 during the global mpox outbreak before normalizing to DKK 3,105 million in 2025, while Travel Health grew to DKK 2,963 million to cushion biosecurity order swings.

Quarterly phasing remains highly seasonal, with operating losses of DKK -7 million in Q1 2026 reflecting light winter travel demand, though the freeze-dried formulation upgrade improves storage flexibility, with bulk shipments invoiced in 2026 and doses delivered in

2027.

Q2 2026 delivered an extraordinary financial beat with revenue reaching DKK 2,034 million (+23% YoY) and EBITDA of DKK 925 million (45% margin), representing a DKK 522 million revenue beat (+35%) and DKK 471 million EBITDA surprise (+104%) versus consensus, prompting management to raise full-year EBITDA margin guidance to approximately 30%. Travel Health surged 45% YoY to DKK 1,022 million with adjusted growth of 56% excluding discontinued partnerships, driven by Rabipur sales of DKK 649 million (+55% YoY) and Encepur growth of 58% to DKK 268 million as wholesaler inventory constraints normalized.

Market positioning shows RabAvert share slipping slightly to 78% in the US while Encepur maintains 96% German dominance, though delayed ACIP MMWR publication handicapped Vimkunya's US launch, forcing 2026 revenue expectations down to DKK 200 million alongside elevated SG&A costs of DKK 319 million. Bavarian Nordic ranks second on the four-dimensional peer momentum matrix behind ALK-Abello, which delivered 18% organic growth and 31% EBITDA margin in Q2 2026 after upgrading 2026 revenue growth guidance to 14-16%.

Competitive positioning benefits from peer struggles, including BioNTech's 19% revenue guidance cut to €1.6-1.9 billion amid 60% YoY revenue decline and a 34% Q2 consensus miss as COVID-19 vaccine demand weakened. Peer operational challenges intensify with Valneva recording €14.2 million in Ixchiq manufacturing charges and initiating global restructuring to reduce burn, while Emergent BioSolutions reported a $180.2 million net loss after a $191.3 million impairment in Q2 2026.

Sell-side coverage features a highly concentrated bullish pool with ten named analysts led by DNB Carnegie's Rune Majlund Dahl and Jefferies' Benjamin Jackson and Michael Leuchten, with Buy recommendations dominating over half of covering brokers. Target price revisions track structural expansion, with Carnegie raising its target to DKK 265 in August 2026 from DKK 260 in May 2026 while Jefferies maintains DKK 280 using sumof-the-parts NPV, having upgraded from DKK 275 following BARDA option exercise.

Bull narratives emphasize complementary GSK and Emergent acquisitions securing sustainable cash flows and 10% FCF yield postmilestone payments, while bears focus on extreme biosecurity order volatility forcing steep valuation discounts, past Phase III RSV clinical failures, and delayed ACIP publications presenting near-term launch risks. Five core drivers shape forward EPS trajectory: US BARDA freeze-dried contract contributing 40% impact with USD 97 million in exercised options (HIGH plausibility), Travel Health organic growth targeting 10-12% revenue CAGR contributing 25% impact (HIGH plausibility), GSK tech transfer margin gains delivering 15-20pp gross margin expansion contributing 20% impact (HIGH plausibility), Vimkunya launch contributing 10% impact but lowered to DKK 200 million expectations (MEDIUM plausibility), and pipeline execution capping contributing 5% impact via DKK 750 million R&D spending caps (MEDIUM plausibility).

Driver dependencies reveal bulk vaccine invoicing accelerating 2026 cash inflows with freeze-dried doses delivered in 2027, Public Preparedness historically delivering DKK 1.5-2.0 billion baseline, Rabipur momentum with DKK 649 million (+55%) and Encepur rebounding 58%, though pending MMWR publication prevents routine Vimkunya reimbursement while Valneva's Ixchiq safety setbacks highlight Vimkunya's superior profile.

Management demonstrates classic conservative sandbagging behavior, routinely upgrading initially low-ball targets with seven guidance upgrades during fiscal 2022, 2025 revenue beating original midpoint guidance at DKK 6,244 million versus DKK 6,200 million, and preliminary 2025 EBITDA margin of 28% exceeding latest 26% guidance, leaving substantial room for subsequent contract additions annually. Upgraded 2026 guidance targets revenue of approximately DKK 5,700 million with 30% EBITDA margin, backed by DKK 2,300 million in secured Public Preparedness orders supporting DKK 2,500 million segment guidance, though management delayed EBV and Lyme clinical entries to protect margins, revised Vimkunya down to DKK 200 million, and maintains tight R&D caps at DKK 750 million and capex at DKK 250 million with highly sensitive USD/DKK 6.30 FX assumptions.

Delivery track record shows consistent execution, with DKK 6,244 million in 2025 revenue beating expectations and 28% adjusted EBITDA margin exceeding 26% guidance by 200bps, successfully deferring DKK 600 million of orders to manage tight capacity, completing Rabipur and Encepur integration on budget, finalizing complex rabies tech transfer in 2024, and discontinuing high-risk RSV program to save over DKK 500 million annually.

Operational execution reveals Travel Health growing 30% to DKK 2,963 million offsetting Public Preparedness lumpiness at DKK 3,105 million, though performance was burdened by DKK 93 million in Swiss plant idle costs, DKK 450 million in non-provisioned scrap and write-downs revealing yield inefficiencies, and risks that prioritizing emergency smallpox manufacturing jeopardizes travel vaccine delivery timelines during sudden outbreaks.

- Earnings Calls Material risks concentrate in three critical dimensions: first, the biosecurity franchise remains fundamentally dependent on unpredictable government budgets where funding freezes would render the 30% EBITDA margin target unachievable as Travel Health cannot offset high-margin revenue collapses; second, severe single-source Kvistgaard manufacturing exposure creates vulnerability to operational bottlenecks with dual-segment production conflicts potentially delaying tech transfers and bulk invoicing; third, next-generation mRNA competition threatens long-term platform sustainability, with regulatory approval of competitive vaccines potentially dissolving the dominant biosecurity market share currently enjoyed.

The forward thesis assumes joint bull-case probability of approximately 35% across core drivers, conditional on Kvistgaard avoiding capacity bottlenecks, though any single manufacturing failure would unwind projected margin gains and expose the structural fragility beneath apparently strong quarterly delivery.

Bavarian Nordic exhibits multiple operational and governance vulnerabilities that position the company materially below peer standards and industry norms. The most critical plausible problem to monitor emerges in employee turnover, which reached 17.4% in 2025, standing high relative to industry standards and suggesting underlying cultural or compensation issues that could impair execution during the critical CEO transition period.

This elevated attrition occurs alongside consecutive annual shareholder rejections of executive remuneration reports, revealing a fundamental disconnect between management compensation practices and shareholder expectations. The juxtaposition of maximum executive bonuses against rising staff departures signals potential organizational stress that competitors such as ALK-Abello have avoided through more balanced compensation structures.

On the financial side, Public Preparedness revenues declined 18% in H1 2026, exposing the inherent cyclicality of sovereign stockpiling orders that creates structural earnings volatility far exceeding biotech peer medians. Market share erosion presents another concerning trend, with Rabipur experiencing small 1 percentage point market share losses in the US and German markets despite robust underlying growth, suggesting competitive pressure is intensifying in core Travel Health franchises.

This deterioration is quantified in US rabies positioning, where RabAvert market share slipped to 78% in Q2 2026 versus 79% in the prior-year period, marking a reversal in what had been a defensively positioned franchise.

Manufacturing inefficiencies represent a third critical weakness cluster. Financial performance was burdened by DKK 93 million in idle costs associated with Swiss plant operations, while non-provisioned scrap and writedowns totaled DKK 450 million in 2025, revealing operational yield inefficiencies within the bulk vaccine network that are materially worse than specialized biotech manufacturers. The company faces inter-segment capacity conflicts where prioritizing emergency mpox bulk production can jeopardize the timing of tech transfers for travel vaccines Encepur and Rabipur, creating execution risks that vertically integrated peers like CSL or Sanofi can avoid through dedicated production networks.

Communication credibility also trails peer standards, with Carnegie researchers suggesting that management's communication can occasionally appear too bullish during outbreak spikes, contributing to elevated valuation volatility relative to more conservatively managed biotech franchises. The recent governance disruption compounds these concerns, as Chairman Luc Debruyne resigned in November 2025 following a failed private equity takeover attempt by a consortium of institutional investors, forcing rapid board reconstitution that introduces additional uncertainty during the parallel CEO succession process.

These aggregated plausible problem to monitor collectively position Bavarian Nordic in the bottom quartile for organizational stability and operational efficiency among mid-cap European biotech peers, creating material downside risk if the upcoming leadership transition falters or if biosecurity demand normalization accelerates beyond current baseline assumptions.
