Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. TKH Group entered its FY 2025 results with steady share price recovery but faced persistent destocking headwinds across its Smart Vision and Smart Connectivity segments compared to peers.
1.
Innovative business profile: TKH Group operates as an international technology developer focusing on customer-centric solutions in Automation and Electrification. The company organizes its technologies into Smart Vision systems, Smart Manufacturing systems, and Smart Connectivity systems, serving markets across Europe, North America, and Asia. This unique positioning allows the company to develop onestop-shop, plug-and-play innovations by integrating hardware, software, and artificial intelligence.

2.
Distinct segment structure: The group's technology portfolio is diversified across three core segments to address structural megatrends. The Smart Vision segment provides 2D and 3D machine vision and security systems, while Smart Manufacturing delivers tire building systems and automated machinery. Lastly, the Smart Connectivity systems segment develops specialty and energy cables to capture opportunities in the energy transition.
3.
Declining historical turnover: TKH Group's top-line performance showed pressure in the years leading up to the FY 2025 release. Specifically, group turnover decreased from €1,847.5 million in FY 2023 to €1.7 billion in FY 2024. This decline contrasted with its peer Belden, which reported record FY 2025 revenues of $2.7 billion, representing steady recovery and solutions growth over its prior year's level of $2.46 billion.
4.
Squeezed operating margins: The company's profitability has experienced downward pressure from customer destocking and underutilization. While TKH delivered an EBITA of €237.0 million with a 12.8% ROS in FY 2023, its FY 2025 adjusted EBITA fell 7% to €189.5 million. In comparison, peer Jenoptik achieved an improving EBITDA margin profile of 18.4% on revenues of €1,046.0 million in FY 2025.
5.
Robust tire building: The Smart Manufacturing segment served as a key stabilizer during periods of weakness in other divisions. During FY 2023, Smart Manufacturing achieved a strong performance, allowing the group to meet its EBITA forecast. This segment's resilience is comparable to WashTec's record equipment business, which set a new revenue record of €498.6 million in FY 2025, up 4.6% from the prior year.
6.
Lagging peer valuation: TKH Group shares have consistently traded at a severe discount compared to its technology and capital goods peers. Going into the FY 2025 earnings, TKH Group traded at 7.3x FY 2026 EV/EBITDA, which represented a substantial 50% discount to peers. This discount was despite its strong market-leading positions across key electrification and automation niches.
7.
Underestimated secular growth: Deutsche Bank's Scribner initiated coverage on peer Belden with a Buy rating, highlighting that the company should trade at least at historical multiples. The analyst argued that secular tailwinds like the Internet of Things make connectivity players highly attractive long-term investments despite short-term market fluctuations.
8.
Moderate stock performance: The company's share price demonstrated solid but underperforming returns over the longer term before its earnings event. Over the 12 months preceding March 6, 2026, TKH's share price grew by a modest 4.5%. This performance was materially weaker than peers and lagged its own historical share price performance, which experienced a 12% decline over the longer three-year period.
9.
Accelerating short-term recovery: The stock's short-term momentum showed signs of improvement as investors anticipated the Eemshaven facility ramp-up. In the 3 months leading to March 6, 2026, TKH's share price gained 2.7%, while the 6-month performance rose by an impressive 14.6%. This short-term pick-up reflected growing optimism regarding the turnaround in the Smart Connectivity and Electrification operations.
10.
Positive target revision: Analyst sentiment turned increasingly constructive as the group progressed with its electrification expansion plans. Jefferies' analyst David Kerstens raised the company's price target to €50 in March 2026, up from €45 in August 2025. This upgrade was driven by the completion of a €200 million strategic investment program designed to expand higher-margin cable capacity.
11.
Persistent destocking hurdles: Despite optimistic capacity expansions, the business continued to battle severe customer destocking. Management noted that ongoing destocking in Smart Vision and Smart Connectivity acted as a material drag on earnings in late 2025. This matched WashTec's North American segment, which was similarly impacted by lower unit sales and delayed customer installations.
12.
Strategic portfolio pruning: The group has actively reshaped its operations by divesting lower-margin, non- core commodity businesses. In FY 2023, TKH completed two large divestments that delivered a oneoff net profit contribution of €54.8 million. This strategic divestment strategy is aligned with peer Jenoptik, which has targeted portfolio simplification to divest its non-photonic assets.
13.
Other side of the story: While the Eemshaven facility is expected to drive substantial growth, its execution has been plagued by a slower than expected ramp-up of larger cable dimensions that has held back short-term profitability. If these operational delays persist or if macroeconomic headwinds cut European utility grid spending, the anticipated recovery in Smart Connectivity margins may fail to materialize, leaving the company with high underutilization costs on its expanded asset base.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. TKH Group is undergoing a profound corporate transformation through leadership shifts, structural divestments, and the planned separation of its capital-intensive Electrification business.
  2. The strategic pivot toward an asset-light model is designed to eliminate the conglomerate discount and unlock shareholder value.
  3. Operational recoveries in cable production and specialized machine vision are offsetting structural order declines in tire-building machinery.
1.
Upcoming executive transition: Long-time Chief Executive Officer Alexander van der Lof, who has led the company for over 25 years, is stepping down to become the Chief Executive Officer of the Electrification business post-separation. He will be succeeded by Guido Grandi as the new group Chief Executive Officer on January 1, 2027, which marks a major shift in corporate leadership aimed at managing the next phase of focused automation growth.
2.
Planned electrification separation: Under the Capitalise & Execute 2028 strategy launched in September 2025, management is pursuing a dual-track process to execute the carve-out of its Electrification division by late 2026 or early 2027. This separation is highly anticipated by the market because it represents over half of the group's total enterprise value and is expected to narrow the persistent conglomerate discount that currently stands at approximately 46% compared to pure-play peers.
3.
Closing of Dewetron divestment: In October 2025, TKH Group completed the sale of its 100% stake in Dewetron to Anritsu Corporation for a transaction that generated a one-off net profit contribution of €35.8 million. This divestment of non-core test and measurement systems removed €29.4 million of lower-margin turnover from the Smart Manufacturing segment to sharpen the group’s industrial focus.
4.
Ongoing portfolio pruning: In early March 2026, the company divested Alphatronics, a manufacturer of intrusion and access control solutions, to Sweden-based Tenuro. The divestment of this non-core asset within the Vision Technologies segment was executed as part of a wider program to divest non-core activities representing approximately €250 million in total group turnover.
5.
Shift in capital allocation: Despite receiving substantial proceeds from the Dewetron divestment, management opted not to initiate a share buyback program in early 2026. Instead of returning cash to shareholders immediately, the group is prioritizing debt reduction and capex to lower its leverage ratio to 1.9x by year-end 2025 while keeping its bank covenants in check.
6.
Peak capital expenditure decline: Following the completion of a €200 million strategic investment program that tripled subsea cable capacities, the company's capital expenditures are projected to drop significantly. Future capital deployment will transition toward lower levels aligned with depreciation and amortization, which is expected to drive higher free cash flows over the next three years.

Driven by demand in battery and consumer electronics markets, Machine Vision is expected to outperform broader automation peers through its proprietary 2D and 3D technologies.

Unified technology ecosystem: To counteract cost inflation, the group is transitioning its Vision division away from disparate hardware brands toward a unified software and artificial intelligence ecosystem. This integration of 2D and 3D technologies is designed to increase R&D efficiency and drive higher software-driven recurring revenues.

Stable dividend policy: Despite ongoing structural changes, the company maintained its commitment to shareholder returns by declaring a dividend of €1.35 per share for FY 2025. This consistent payout aligns with the company's objective to maintain a dividend yield of at least 3% throughout its transition phase.
14.
Peer financial divergence: While TKH Group is battling destocking headwinds, its peer Jenoptik is exhibiting robust semiconductor-driven order growth that has led to margin expansions. Similarly, peer Belden reported a record revenue performance of approximately $2.7 billion in FY 2025, which underscores the stronger secular demand backdrop enjoyed by pure-play automation and solutions providers compared to diversified conglomerates.

Capitalize and Execute strategy targets: The Capitalise & Execute 2028 strategy has established updated, standalone financial targets for both business units. Section 4 – Financial delivery

Section summary
  1. TKH Group delivered FY 2025 adjusted EBITA of €189.5 million, meeting market expectations and maintaining covenant leverage at 1.9x despite persistent operational headwinds across multiple segments.

Full Year Performance against Consensus

1.
Full year adjusted EBITA performance: Management reported that full year adjusted EBITA decreased by 7.1% to €189.5 million compared to €203.9 million in FY 2024. This delivery was fully in line with consensus expectations of €189.9 million, as compiled by the company, and matched the preview of €190.4 million by Jefferies' analyst David Kerstens. By comparison, peer Jenoptik saw its FY 2025 EBITDA decline 13.1% to €192.5 million in a highly challenging market.
2.
Topline turnover growth: Total turnover for the group reached €1,761.2 million, up 2.8% on a reported basis and up 4.9% organically compared to €1,712.7 million in the prior year. This reported growth was supported by a very strong recovery in Q4 2025, where turnover increased organically by 8.7% to €483.7 million. In comparison, peer Belden reported a record FY 2025 revenue of $2,715 million, reflecting a stronger 10.3% year-on-year expansion.
3.
Compression of gross margin: The company's added value margin, reflecting gross profitability, decreased to 51.0% compared to 51.9% in FY 2024. This slight deterioration was driven by adverse product mix shifts and start-up losses in the new Eemshaven facility. On the other hand, peer Belden reported an expansion in adjusted gross margin to 38.5% in FY 2025, representing a 40 basis point improvement.

Segment Delivery and Deviations

4.
Vision Technologies operational strength: The Smart Vision systems segment showed strong performance with turnover increasing organically by 6.7% to €522.6 million. Driven by demand in machine vision, the segment's adjusted EBITA grew by an impressive 19.6% to €101.2 million compared to €81.1 million in FY 2024. This organic turnaround outperformed peer Jenoptik, which saw a 13.3% decline in semiconductor-related sales.
5.
Automated Machinery volume decline: Automated Machinery segment turnover fell organically by 8.6% to €522.6 million compared to €608.8 million in the prior year. This decline, though severe, was better than the double-digit drop that brokers initially expected due to sluggish tire manufacturer capital expenditure. In contrast, peer WashTec achieved record equipment sales of €268.0 million, up €6.6 million year-on-year.
6.
Electrification progress and teething costs: The Electrification segment saw strong top-line organic growth of 36.9% in Q2 2025 and 29.0% in Q4 2025. However, the full-year profitability of the division was heavily burdened by Eemshaven factory start-up losses where costs exceeded revenues. This margin squeeze contrasted with peer Belden's Automated Solutions segment, which achieved a 21.0% EBITDA margin, up 50 basis points.

Section 16 – Conclusion

TKH Group's FY 2025 results, delivered on March 5, 2026, met consensus expectations with adjusted EBITA of €189.5 million, representing a 7.1% decline from €203.9 million in FY 2024. However, the true significance of these earnings extends far beyond the headline numbers, fundamentally reshaping the company's trajectory over the next 18 months to three years through a combination of strategic corporate restructuring, operational stabilization, and market repositioning.

The single most important change emerging from these earnings is the accelerated execution pathway toward the planned dual-track separation of the Electrification division, designed to eliminate the persistent conglomerate discount currently penalizing the stock. Sell-side estimates valued the Smart Connectivity and Electrification cable activities at approximately €800 million, representing over half of the group's total market capitalization.

This structural transformation represents a profound shift in capital allocation priorities and risk-return profiles. Under the Capitalise & Execute 2028 strategy, TKH is prioritizing its asset-light Automation business over its capital-intensive activities, with the core Automation segment focusing on advanced vision technology and proprietary software, contrasting sharply with the historical strategy where heavy capital expenditures in Electrification effectively constrained Automation's value creation.

The earnings demonstrated that management has successfully navigated the most capital-intensive phase of the Eemshaven facility expansion, with capital expenditures projected to drop significantly following completion of the €200 million strategic investment program, driving higher free cash flows over the next three years. Free cash flow increased significantly to €74.9 million in FY 2025 compared to €48.0 million in FY 2024, leading to an improved EBITDA-to- FCF conversion rate of 32.1% against only 19.1% in the prior year.

This inflection point in cash generation, combined with the planned divestment proceeds, positions TKH to deliver materially higher earnings per share through a combination of debt reduction, targeted buybacks post-separation, and accretive bolt-on acquisitions in high-margin Automation niches. The 18-month to 3-year EPS outlook has fundamentally improved because the company is transitioning from a discount-laden, capital-hungry conglomerate to a focused, asset-light automation specialist with structurally higher returns on invested capital.

The strongest case against this conclusion rests on persistent execution risks at the Eemshaven subsea cable facility and the uncertain valuation discount that may persist even after separation. Management claimed that the main challenges related to the ramp-up of the Eemshaven facility had been solved and that production yields were improving, but this message was slightly contradicted by their admission that the ramp-up of larger cable dimensions took longer than expected in Q4 2025, which limited total output.

Due to persistent start-up costs at the Eemshaven facility, management trimmed the Electrification EBITA margin target to 12% to 15% by 2028, down from the previous target of 15% expected prior to the results period. These margin target reductions signal that underlying operational challenges remain material, and any further technical delays could severely depress the segment's valuation when presented to prospective buyers or public markets.

Additionally, while the onshore energy cable business benefits from strong structural demand supported by long-term framework agreements, the Automated Machinery subsegment experienced a substantial decline in order intake from Tier 1 tire manufacturers, creating a temporary revenue headwind for the division over the next 12 months. Management reported that segment turnover fell organically by 8.6% due to deferred investments by Tier 1 tire manufacturers, and the order book fell by 24.1% to €380.9 million, indicating near-term headwinds. If this order backlog weakness persists beyond 2026 or if the Asian Machine Vision recovery stalls due to macroeconomic volatility, the standalone Automation business may struggle to achieve its 17% to 19% EBITA margin targets, undermining the investment case for the post-separation entity.

Furthermore, TKH Group was trading at 7.3x EV/EBITDA for FY 2026, which was 25% below its historical valuation and represented a substantial 50% discount to peers. There is no guarantee that the market will fully re-rate the separated entities, particularly if broader European industrial weakness persists or if the Electrification business fails to demonstrate sustainable margin expansion post-separation.
