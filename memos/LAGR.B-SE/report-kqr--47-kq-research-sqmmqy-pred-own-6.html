Weather Disruptions and Seasonality

5.
Climatic volatility: The recent acquisitions of Epoke and Friggerakers have introduced a substantial winter weather dependency to the group's consolidated quarterly earnings per share trajectory. These units generate a combined annual revenue of approximately SEK 350 million by manufacturing advanced salt and sand spreaders for municipalities and airports. A succession of mild winter seasons with low snowfall in northern Europe would severely depress municipal replacement cycles for this specialized equipment.
6.
Delayed deliveries: Severe seasonal concentration makes the group's cash flow vulnerable to postponed deliveries which temporarily restricts quarterly group liquidity before peak winter collections. For example, the group's historical summer installation cycles build up significant inventory before autumn deliveries materialize. This operational timing cash lag requires careful modeling of working capital cycles to avoid overstating shortterm margin compression.
7.
Early indicators: Monitoring regional weather patterns and municipal winter-service tenders serves as an essential method to track early organic demand fluctuations before quarterly reports. For example, Tonder Kommune in Denmark recently awarded a DKK 2.64 million winter ice-clearing and snow-removal contract which reflects local preparedness activity. Similarly, Oslo Kommune continues to procure specialized maintenance equipment like electric wheel loaders to handle winter snow clearing.
8.
Favorable shift: The Swedish government has recently approved a major 48% increase in road infrastructure budgets which should provide a robust long-term demand tailwind. This planned 50% funding boost for operations and maintenance from 2026 onwards is designed to address a severe Nordic road maintenance deficit. However, actual quarterly deliveries can still be deferred by regional road contractors if autumn installation schedules are disrupted by bad weather.

Inflation and Margin Pressures

9.
Lagged recovery: Lagercrantz continues to experience a temporary margin compression of approximately 40 basis points due to lagged pricing pass through effects. If these price adjustments take longer to implement than consensus assumes, the underlying margins will remain under pressure. This margin drag would be compounded if rising metal and plastic material costs continue to escalate faster than price hikes can be realized.
10.
Accounting distortion: Lagercrantz's underlying margins are actually weaker than reported figures suggest because of volatile contingent consideration remeasurements that boost paper profits. The group recognized positive net earnout reversals of MSEK 17 in Q4 2025/26 and MSEK 6 in Q2 2026/27 within the income statement. If investors fail to strip out these non-operational adjustments, they will base their future models on inflated operational baselines.
11.
Contrary evidence: Lagercrantz management maintains that the group is fully insulated from seasonal volatility through its highly diversified portfolio of ninety niche businesses. This perspective is contradicted by RedEye's analyst who identified seasonal softness in the acquired units as the primary cause of a three percent EBITA miss in Q1 2026/27. This operational gap proves that the consolidated results are increasingly vulnerable to the distinct seasonal patterns of new subsidiaries.
12.
Other side of the story: Although mild winters present a threat to the sales of road equipment subsidiaries, the broader infrastructure transition continues to protect long term group organic growth. Strong demand across green transition niches and grid electrification investments helps to buffer any temporary weather-driven shortfalls. Analysts at DNB Carnegie continue to project a resilient long-term track record of 15% to 20% earnings per share growth over the cycle.
