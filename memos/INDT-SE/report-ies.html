Indutrade (INDT-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results leave our case close to where it stood, because the case was written on the headline numbers, but the detail that has arrived since weakens it slightly: the margin mechanism and the order lead both look less solid than the case treats them, and the deal engine looks a little better.

1. What changed

The headline figures are the ones the case already holds. For the third quarter of 2026 (July to September, reported 23 October 2026), Indutrade group net sales were SEK 8,871 million, up 13%, with organic growth of 5% and all five business areas positive. EBITA was SEK 1,358 million, up 19%, a margin of 15.3% against 14.6% a year earlier. Organic order intake rose 6%, book-to-bill was 101%, and the gross margin was 35.8%. Thirteen acquisitions in the first nine months added about SEK 2.1 billion of annualised sales.

What is new to the case is the detail and the broker response:

  • The split of the profit growth. Redeye puts acquired companies at SEK 112 million of EBITA in the quarter, at an 18.3% margin, and acquired sales at SEK 628 million against SEK 392 million of organic sales growth.
  • The backlog. The total order backlog was 11% higher than a year earlier, but long-lead European energy projects increasingly dominate it. Management expects these to support organic growth starting in late 2027 and says quarterly conversion is harder to predict.
  • Where orders slowed. Carnegie notes that organic order momentum weakened against the second quarter in Life Science and in Process, Energy and Water.
  • Project margins. Process, Energy and Water's margin fell 0.2 points to 15.9% on lower-margin project deliveries. Technology and Systems Solutions fell 0.8 points to 15.1% on project execution weaknesses in a few UK companies, which management says need substantial time.
  • Deals. A fourteenth acquisition, Acoma, was signed on 12 November 2026. Redeye puts the average multiple paid in 2026 at 7.9x EV/EBITA against 8.1x in 2025. Infrastructure and Construction made its first acquisition in two years.
  • Balance sheet. Net debt was SEK 9,574 million and net debt to EBITDA 1.6x, against 1.4x a year earlier. Return on capital employed was 19%.
  • Sell-side. Carnegie (26 October) kept Hold and a SEK 260 target, and lifted adjusted EPS for 2027 to SEK 11.90 from 11.80 and for 2028 to SEK 12.90 from 12.80. Redeye (1 November) raised its fair value to SEK 278 from 250, 2027 sales by 5% to SEK 39.7 billion and 2027 adjusted EBITA by 3% to SEK 6,153 million, 5% above the FactSet consensus of SEK 5,840 million, citing a stronger deal pipeline and rising peer multiples.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the existing companies delivered about half the leverage the case's rule predicts. The case's margin rule is one-third of a point of EBITA margin for each point of organic growth above 2%, so 5% organic growth should give about one point. The case explained the quarter's 0.7 points as the rule less a project-mix shortfall in one business area. Redeye's split suggests a different account. Take out SEK 112 million of acquired EBITA and SEK 628 million of acquired sales, and the existing estate earned about SEK 1,246 million on about SEK 8,243 million, a margin of about 15.1%. That is roughly 0.5 points of improvement from organic growth, with the other 0.2 points coming from buying higher-margin companies. The two business-area margin declines explain only about 0.15 points of the gap between them. On this reading the rule runs nearer one-sixth of a point than one-third, and the case's 15.0% margin for 2027 leans on the deal count more than on absorbed capacity. For it to be right, Redeye's acquired figures must be on the same basis as group EBITA, and the third quarter must be representative and not a quarter where wage costs happened to land. The review's repeated flag on wage inflation across 225 subsidiaries is consistent with it, though not proof.

Reading two: the order lead is not the lead the case treats it as. The case reads orders running ahead of sales as a short-cycle signal with two to three quarters of visibility, and it treats about 40% of sales as structural and independent of the cycle. The review points the other way on both. The backlog growth is increasingly long-lead energy work that management itself dates to late 2027, which is after the July 2027 print the case names as the test. And the order deceleration Carnegie flags is in Life Science and Process, Energy and Water, which are the structural strands. If both hold, the short-cycle cover for the hard comparison is thinner than a 101% book-to-bill suggests, the dip from the second quarter of 2027 is deeper than the case's 3% organic assumption, and the support beyond it is better than the case assumes. It also moves the mix toward project work, where this company's margins are lower and where its one control failure sat. For this to be right, the project share of orders must be rising materially, which the company does not quantify. Against it, Life Science organic orders still grew 8% in the quarter, the strongest of the five areas, so the deceleration is from a high level.

Reading three: the discount-narrowing leg may already be spent. The case's fair multiple of 24 to 25x needs the discount to peers to narrow a few more points, inside a cohort it describes as de-rating on yields. Carnegie reports the shares have outperformed key peers by about 35% since June 2026. On Redeye's October table Indutrade trades at 19.7x 2026 EV/EBITA against Addtech at 21.7x and Lagercrantz at 23.6x, a discount of about 9% and 17%, which is already at or inside the 15 to 20% the case calls earned. Redeye also describes peer multiples as rising, not falling. Either the case's cohort premise is too gloomy, which helps the multiple leg, or the stock's own narrowing has run ahead of what its returns justify, which hurts it. This rests on one broker's table and we hold it less firmly than the first two.

One alternative we do not put up. The review argues that a hidden shift to proprietary, higher-margin acquired businesses puts 2028 earnings materially above consensus. The arithmetic does not carry it beyond what the case holds: about 6% of sales bought each year at about three points above the group margin is roughly 0.2 points a year, which is the top of the case's own mix allowance, and the case is already above consensus for 2028.

3. What it does to the case

  • Pricing power, operating leverage and cost control (60, C+): more pressure. The rule the case states is the largest mechanism in its forecast, and one quarter's split says it delivered about half.
  • Forecast financials (78, B): slightly more pressure on the margin path, the same on the EPS growth band, because a faster deal engine offsets a weaker rule. Carnegie's 2027 adjusted EPS of SEK 11.90 now sits above the case's figure of about SEK 11.7, so the case's position of slightly below consensus for 18 months has widened a little.
  • Tailwinds in the operating environment (61, C): more pressure on the structural 40% and on visibility into mid-2027; less pressure beyond late 2027.
  • Scope for upgrades (47, C+): more pressure. The case put a 40 to 45% chance on an estimate cut at the July 2027 print. Brokers have raised 2027 since, and the backlog cover arrives after that print, so the pressure runs toward the higher end.
  • Internally-driven change forces (62, D): less pressure. Fourteen deals by mid-November is on the case's pace of 15 to 16 for the year, short of its 18-or-more upgrade trigger, and the average price paid is below the case's 9x warning level. The UK project companies remain the drag the case named. The review's 15.1% margin for Technology and Systems Solutions does not sit easily with the case's recovery path for that area and needs reconciling before it is read as a signal.
  • The fair multiple (62, C) and total shareholder return (83, B): the same, with the direction unresolved by reading three. The share price was SEK 267.8 on 13 November 2026, against the SEK 262 to 267 the case started from. No threshold the case states has been crossed: the price is above the 50-day average (SEK 258.6) that the case uses as its warning line, and the case's central return still sits just below its top band at this price.
  • Riskiness of forecasts (55, C+) and Case Riskiness (58): the same. Lifco's 12% fall on its report day is the cohort shock the case already describes. Leverage of 1.6x is inside the case's range.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (63): marginally more pressure. Foundations' Robustness (73) and Setups' Fit/Crispness (74): the same.
  • The pinned letters stand as they were: technical momentum B, sell-side earnings momentum A, valuation C.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry the estimates and targets of the brokers beyond Carnegie and Redeye, and so show whether consensus for 2027 has risen into the hard comparison and whether revision breadth has turned, which the case names as a trigger. It does not carry any new company data. It cannot settle readings one or two.
  • The fourth-quarter report, which the case dates to 27 January 2027. For reading one: the existing-estate margin once acquired EBITA is taken out, against the easiest comparison of the cycle. If the estate does not deliver close to the rule on that base, the rule is probably wrong. For reading two: order growth in Life Science and Process, Energy and Water, and whether book-to-bill holds above 100%.
  • The July 2027 print. It is the only evidence that settles reading two outright: organic growth against a 5% base before the long-lead backlog converts.
  • What nothing is committed to answer. The company does not disclose the backlog by delivery year or the project share of orders, and the acquired EBITA figure comes from a broker, not the company. Reading two's timing rests on one management statement, and reading one can be tested only through broker splits each quarter. Reading three depends on the yield regime, which the review does not resolve.
