Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. European logistics earnings trajectories are highly resolvable over eighteen months due to structural indexation, but long-term visibility remains constrained by power grid bottlenecks.

The central investment question asks how the eighteen-month EPS trajectories of listed European logistics landlords will evolve as rental indexation normalizes, which is critical because investors now prioritize immediate cash earnings over long-term asset value appreciation.

Short-term earnings trajectories are highly resolvable due to high occupancy levels and transparent rental indexation structures, with landlords such as Montea reporting strong rental growth of nearly three percent that provides immediate earnings visibility and supports full-year guidance targets.

However, grid access remains the primary bottleneck for digital infrastructure expansion, locking up capital-intensive projects in multi-year queues. - Earnings Calls Logistics landlords are rapidly monetizing powered land through data center conversions to capture premium development yields, but the transition accelerates earnings growth while introducing higher execution and power procurement risks.

Segro added 0.5GVA to its strategic power bank in the first half of 2026, successfully reaching 3.0GVA of potential capacity across established European availability zones, while its Slough Trading Estate remains Europe's largest data center cluster, operating with 0.5GVA of installed capacity that generates £58 million in headline rent.

However, grid connection queues of five to ten years in core FLAP-D markets severely restrict speculative developments, and German grid connection timelines can reach seven years, pushing developers to prioritize peripheral locations.

Core European logistics rental growth is bifurcating as prime assets capture resilient indexation while secondary portfolios face rising vacancy pressures.

Tritax Big Box holds a record 29 percent reversionary potential and expects to capture 72 percent over the next three years, while LondonMetric reported that urban open market rent reviews achieved uplifts of 38 percent, driving 4.2 percent like-for-like rental growth across the portfolio.

Conversely, Sagax's like-for-like rental growth turned negative at minus 0.5 percent as negative reversion began offsetting indexation tailwinds in Sweden and Finland, and overall market rents have flattened as indexation tailwinds gradually fade across the continent.

European logistics developers capture superior yield spreads through development rather than acquisitions despite rising financing costs, and capital recycling through joint ventures and asset disposals sustains development pipelines without diluting equity bases.

CTP achieves a highly profitable development pipeline delivering an eleven percent yield on cost across its core Central and Eastern European stronghold, while Segro's development pipeline of £2.9 billion targets attractive yields of seven to eight percent on newly invested capital.

However, the average cost of debt for European landlords will reach 3.4 percent by 2030 as cheap historic bonds gradually face refinancing, and German and UK logistics prime yields expanded by twenty-three to twenty-five basis points in the second quarter of 2026. and Eastern Europe continues to capture resilient nearshoring and industrial localization activity despite localized macroeconomic headwinds, with rapid integration of specialized technology clusters transforming the region from a cost-efficient logistics hub into a high-value manufacturing center.

Poland has overtaken Germany as Europe's largest road freight market, representing 20 percent of European Union volume, while Central and Eastern European investment volumes rose 15% in the first half of 2026, anchored by Poland's 72 percent year-on-year surge.

CTP operates a dominant 3.4 million square meter portfolio in Romania, retaining 89 percent of its clients, while VGP holds 1.68 million square meters of land in Romania to capture accelerating regional manufacturing demand. European logistics real estate is undergoing unprecedented corporate consolidation as wide valuation gaps between public markets and private portfolios prompt major takeover bids and mergers.

LondonMetric completed its sixth corporate acquisition in six years by absorbing Picton Property Income in September 2026, which immediately adds sixteen million pounds of net contracted rent, and this all-share transaction allows LondonMetric to deploy its highly scalable platform, which operates at a sector-leading seven percent cost ratio.

Most significantly, Prologis agreed a recommended fourteen billion pound takeover of Segro in August 2026, representing a fourteen percent premium to Segro's nine hundred two pence NTA, and this blockbuster deal represents one of the largest corporate combinations in European logistics. Resolving European logistics EPS trajectories is hindered by opaque utility grid connection queues and private refinancing structures.

Monitoring grid regulatory filings from Ofgem resolves opaque utility connection wait times that currently complicate development underwriting, while organizing expert calls with utility transmission operators and developers resolves unclear power delivery dates that delay critical pre-let schemes.

Strategic growth stories in European logistics are accelerating through power-first data center conversions and regional manufacturing localization, but structural energy and refinancing constraints introduce material capital expenditure and margin risks across the sector.
