Mechanics of Deleveraging and June 2026 Hybrid Bond

1.
Deleveraging drivers: AT&S reported a significant improvement in its leverage profile, reducing its net debt to EBITDA ratio from 3.2x as of 31 March 2026 to 1.9x as of 30 June 2026. Management attributed this sharp deleveraging to the successful placement of a EUR 400 million hybrid convertible bond in June 2026, alongside continuous EBITDA margin expansion. This capital injection, coupled with positive volume and pricing trends, allowed the company to rebuild its liquidity reserve. Consequently, this positive deleveraging loop frees up operational flexibility to fund ongoing technical qualifications, directly supporting future earnings per share growth. Convertible bond details: The hybrid convertible bond issued on 16 June 2026 carried a favorable annual coupon rate of 2.5% and featured an initial conversion price of EUR 254. Under accounting standards, this subordinated bond is treated as equity, which directly reduced net debt and strengthened the equity ratio by 6.4 percentage points to 29.0%. Operating with lower coupon obligations reduces near-term net finance costs, which historically rose to EUR 100 million in fiscal year 2025/26. This reduction directly improves net margin and support EPS recovery.
2.
Reversal triggers: The deleveraging loop is highly sensitive to capital expenditure overruns and delayed customer ramps. If cash inflows from bilateral customer agreements decline or if start-up costs in Kulim escalate, AT&S's cash burn will accelerate. Rapid cash consumption would force the company to draw on its variable-rate bank borrowings, which constitute 64.3% of its outstanding financial liabilities. Increased debt drawdowns would drive interest expenses higher and reverse the deleveraging trend, negatively impacting EPS.

Hybrid Bond Refinancing Risk and Working Capital Management

3.
Early redemption: AT&S faced a major balance sheet test regarding its EUR 350 million hybrid bond issued in January 2022, which was first callable in January 2027. On 8 October 2026, AT&S announced the early redemption of this bond on 22 October 2026, successfully neutralizing the refinancing risk. The redemption was funded from existing liquidity and the proceeds of the June 2026 hybrid convertible bond. This proactive refinancing optimized the capital structure and protected future earnings from expensive interest penalties. Step-up coupon penalty: Prior to the early redemption, the outstanding 2022 hybrid bond carried an interest rate of 5%. If AT&S had failed to call and redeem the bond in January 2027, the interest rate would have reset to the five-year swap rate plus a massive step-up premium of 9.942 percentage points, leading to a total coupon of approximately 15%. Refinancing this instrument with the new 2026 hybrid convertible bond cut the recurring financing cost by approximately 50%. This interest expense mitigation avoids a major EPS drag that would have occurred under the step-up coupon. Working capital build: While refinancing risk has been mitigated, working capital requirements continue to pressure cash flow. During the first quarter of fiscal year 2026/27, AT&S experienced a EUR 54.1 million increase in inventory due to a higher order backlog, alongside a EUR 129.0 million increase in trade receivables. This substantial working capital build absorbs operational cash, keeping operating free cash flow tight at EUR 5.1 million. If demand continues to accelerate, the associated cash consumption could limit capital allocated to K2 shell installations, slowing overall volume expansion.
4.
Factoring program reliance: To alleviate working capital pressures, AT&S relies on active factoring programs to accelerate cash conversion. In fiscal year 2025/26, cash flow from operating activities was supported by a reduction in trade receivables driven by higher factoring volumes. However, factoring program capacity is capped by bank credit lines and credit market liquidity. Any reduction in factoring facilities would immediately lengthen the cash conversion cycle, expanding working capital and requiring additional bank debt to maintain liquidity.
