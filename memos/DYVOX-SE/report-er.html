Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Dynavox entered the 21 October 2026 results as a high-growth, high-margin assistive-communication leader with an unusually strong structural runway, but expectations already assumed continued execution toward 20% currency-adjusted growth and greater than 15% EBIT margins.
1.
Integrated AAC platform, structurally differentiated: Dynavox operates in augmentative and alternative communication, supplying people with conditions including cerebral palsy, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, autism, Parkinson’s disease and spinal-cord injury, while combining eye-controlled and touchscreen hardware with software, clinically developed language systems, mounting solutions, training, funding expertise and ongoing support. The breadth of this offering is more defensible than a standalone-device model because the company participates in assessment, prescription, reimbursement and user support rather than only selling hardware.

Software and language systems deepen retention: Dynavox’s software allows users to access computers, browse the web, text and develop literacy skills, while its language portfolio includes synthetic voices, symbol libraries and text-based systems that can be customised for individual users. The company also offers personalised voice identities in more than 30 languages, giving its hardware a software and content layer that supports longer product lifecycles and makes simple device substitution less straightforward.
3.
Funding support is a core capability: Most Dynavox solutions are medical-grade devices funded through public or private reimbursement systems, so the company’s funding team helps families and professionals navigate insurance approval and access. This capability is a competitive asset in a market where the principal commercial bottleneck is often not need but awareness, prescription and payment approval, although the same dependence later became the central risk to the 2026 outlook.
4.
Global reach remains heavily United States weighted: Dynavox served customers in more than 65 countries with over 1,000 employees by the third quarter of 2026. Its January-to-September 2026 revenue mix was 74% North America, 20% Europe and 6% other markets, compared with 78% North America, 18% Europe and 4% other markets in the company’s 2023 presentation. The modest diversification helps, but the United States still dominates the earnings baseline.
5.
Market penetration leaves substantial runway: Dynavox and DNB Carnegie describe an addressable population of roughly 50 million people unable to communicate verbally, with only about 2% of newly diagnosed people receiving the support they need. DNB Carnegie also estimated that approximately 50,000 AAC devices were sold globally in 2025, including about 30,000 in the United States, while the number of US speech-language pathologists prescribing more than four Dynavox devices annually increased from roughly 300 in 2022 to around 1,200 in 2025.
6.
Five-year history supports a premium growth narrative: Dynavox reported that revenue had grown at an average annual rate of 25% during its first five years as a standalone listed company, ahead of the current long-term target of 20% average annual currency-adjusted growth including acquisitions. That history explains why investors accepted a premium valuation before the October results, but it also raised the earnings bar above the broader AAC market’s approximately 9% projected compound annual growth rate cited by DNB Carnegie.

North America, recovery required: DNB Carnegie’s pre-results thesis required a return to stronger United States growth after Q1 weather disruption and Q2 prescription delays, with Q3 revenue expected to rise sequentially to SEK700 million at group level; any flat United States performance therefore risked undermining the recovery narrative even if reported group growth remained positive.

Europe, mixed evidence: Investors expected European momentum to provide diversification from the United States, but DNB Carnegie’s pre-results framework still relied on Germany, France and Italy integrations translating into scalable growth, while the company had warned that the full benefit of those acquisitions and the unified enterprise resource planning system was not yet visible. Germany and Australia, positive offsets: Dynavox had identified Germany and Australia as strong contributors and DNB Carnegie expected international expansion to support the growth case, so the market was looking for evidence that these regions could offset a United States slowdown rather than simply add acquisition-driven revenue. Capital allocation, newly relevant: Dynavox’s first proposed dividend of SEK0.50 per share and stated policy to return at least 40% of available net profit made cash generation part of the earnings debate, although the market still expected growth investment and selective acquisitions to take priority over aggressive buybacks.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Section summary
  1. The 21 October 2026 earnings transformed Dynavox's investment narrative from a high-confidence operating-leverage story into a show-me execution case, with revenue missing broker forecasts, reported EBIT quality weakened by earn-out reversals and capitalised research and development, and medium-term EPS expectations cut by 6% to 11% despite unchanged formal targets. The result did not break the long-term structural thesis, but it reset the timeline and probability that 20% growth and greater-than-15% recurring margins can be delivered over the next 18 months to three years, while exposing United States reimbursement friction as a persistent bottleneck rather than a temporary Q2 delay. Dynavox reported Q3 revenue of SEK685 million, below DNB Carnegie's SEK700 million forecast and the SEK709 million consensus, delivering a material top-line miss rather than a clean beat. Reported EBIT of SEK119 million exceeded RedEye's SEK106 million estimate by 12.3% and consensus by 5.3%, but DNB Carnegie calculated that EBIT excluding the earn-out reversal was SEK103 million, 8% below consensus, so the headline profit beat overstated underlying operating improvement. The combination of a revenue shortfall and lowerquality margin beat forced both accessible brokers to reduce 2027 and 2028 earnings forecasts, with DNB Carnegie cutting adjusted EPS to SEK2.91 for 2026, SEK3.60 for 2027 and SEK4.40 for 2028 from SEK3.10, SEK3.90 and SEK5 respectively, while RedEye reduced 2027 EPS to SEK3.4 from SEK3.6 and 2028 EPS to SEK4.1 from SEK4.9. 450 The EPS implications over 18 months to three years are materially worse than the pre-results baseline because Dynavox's increasingly scalable cost base means weaker sales delay operating leverage disproportionately. The cuts extend beyond one weak quarter because United States Medicaid and managed-care friction appears more persistent than the Q2 narrative suggested. Management disclosed that Medicaid and managedcare organisations represented approximately 40% of revenue, and while two major state reimbursement challenges were resolved in Dynavox's favour, the number of cases requiring additional review and the effort needed to resolve them had increased. DNB Carnegie interpreted stable rejection rates and the two resolved state disputes as evidence that some revenue was deferred rather than lost, but its forecast cuts for 2027 and 2028 show that it no longer assumed the delay would have only a short-lived effect on conversion. Margin quality proved weaker than the headline suggested because Dynavox's interim report said other operating gains improved by SEK19 million, with SEK16 million from earn-out reversals, and because the reversal increased reported EBIT without increasing cash earnings, it should not be capitalised into forward EPS or treated as evidence that the underlying cost base has permanently improved. The evidence therefore shows that recurring profitability did not materially improve, despite the reported 17.4% margin exceeding the greater-than-15% target.

Target credibility weakened because Q3 currency-adjusted growth of 15% comprised 13% organic growth and 3% acquisitions, while nine-month currency-adjusted growth also remained 15%, comprising 12% organic growth and 3% acquisitions, versus Dynavox's long-term target of 20% average annual currency-adjusted growth including acquisitions. Management retained its three-to-four-year objective of 20% average annual currencyadjusted revenue growth including acquisitions and declined to provide a specific 2027 forecast, so the target remained an unchanged ambition rather than updated guidance, while nine-month currency-adjusted growth was 15% and DNB Carnegie subsequently forecast only 12% organic growth for 2027, leaving a material execution gap.

The market entered Q3 already sceptical, with DNB Carnegie explicitly warning in June that FactSet consensus appeared high on both organic growth and margins, and its July review recorded 12% organic growth for 2026 and 15% for 2027, materially below the company's 20% target, so the result confirmed rather than created the credibility problem.

Valuation reset through both lower earnings estimates and reduced multiples, with DNB Carnegie's Laséen retaining Buy but reducing the 12-month target price to SEK90 from SEK110, explicitly reflecting lower earnings forecasts and lower valuation multiples after Q3, while RedEye's Grunewald maintained a positive valuation view but lowered its fundamental Base Case to SEK95 from SEK103 and reduced its blended Fair Value to SEK99.

RedEye raised its weighted average cost of capital by 0.5 percentage points to 9.6% because of higher interest rates and reduced its fundamental value from SEK103 to SEK95, with the higher discount rate lowering the present value of the long-duration cash flows required to justify a premium growth multiple and therefore raising the EPS delivery threshold for investors. The post-results broker disagreement was narrow on direction but material on magnitude, because both retained positive stances while cutting targets by SEK15 to SEK20, showing consensus that the investment case remains intact but that the path to value creation has lengthened.

The single most important change is that reimbursement conversion has moved from a Q2 timing issue to a structural earnings variable affecting approximately 40% of revenue, with RedEye describing the problem as recurring state-by-state "whack-a-mole" and saying the situation was unchanged from Q2, contradicting the earlier market expectation that delays would quickly normalise after deferred orders were released. This matters more than the Q3 revenue miss because it implies that future quarters face comparable uncertainty, making clean organic growth and recurring margins harder to prove and reducing confidence in the three-year EPS trajectory even if formal reimbursement policies remain unchanged.

The strongest case against the conclusion is that Dynavox said it had seen no formal change in reimbursement policy in the United States or other major markets, and management continued to argue that underlying demand and denial rates were intact, which is better than a direct reimbursement-rate cut, but it does not remove the macroeconomic risk because administrative review, documentation requirements and payment timing can weaken near-term revenue without changing statutory coverage. If delayed Medicaid cases convert quickly in Q4 and early 2027, the completed platform investments could produce sustained operating leverage without a comparable rise in amortisation, in which case Q3's 15% currency-adjusted growth and 17.4% reported EBIT margin could mark a temporary timing trough rather than the new earnings baseline, but the company must demonstrate this through cash-backed EBIT and clean organic growth because the quarter's accounting adjustments concealed the underlying margin miss.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND requires three observable developments over the next six to nine months: first, United States backlog conversion demonstrated by North American revenue returning to sequential growth and exceeding broker forecasts by at least 3% to 5% in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027, showing that delayed cases are being released rather than permanently deferred; second, European organic growth reaching double digits in Q4 or Q1 after the RehaMedia anniversary, confirming that Germany's strength is genuine post-integration demand rather than acquisition consolidation; and third, recurring EBIT margins above 15% after removing earn-out gains and capitalised research-and-development effects for two consecutive quarters, proving that operating leverage is real rather than accounting-assisted.

These conditions would validate management's "strong underlying demand" claim and justify restoring 2027–2028 EPS estimates closer to pre-Q3 levels, but absent that evidence by mid- 2027, the lower broker forecasts should be treated as the new baseline. The non-obvious reading supported by the evidence is that Dynavox's moat is strongest in clinical relationships and funding expertise but weakest in hardware and software features, because a former chief marketing officer described AAC hardware as commoditized, with quality and supplier relationships still important, while software is becoming more centralised and competitive, weakening the argument that Dynavox can defend share through device design alone and raising the importance of language content, clinical workflow, funding expertise and support quality in determining customer retention.

This implies that United States reimbursement friction is more damaging to Dynavox than to a pure-technology competitor because it delays the exact conversion process where Dynavox's advantage is concentrated, while a former vice president said Smartbox and PRC-Saltillo had already introduced artificial-intelligence-related features while Dynavox had not publicly announced comparable features by March 2026, creating a gap that matters because faster experimentation can improve user experience, attract prescribers and reduce Dynavox's software-led differentiation if it persists.

The two most important questions for the next disclosures are: first, what is the quantified value of United States cases held in administrative review at the end of Q4 2026, and what proportion converted into shipped revenue during the quarter, because management has not provided a backlog figure, conversion timetable or state-bystate revenue bridge, leaving investors unable to distinguish temporary deferral from a lower conversion rate; and second, what is the normalised EBIT margin after adjusting for earn-out reversals, capitalised research and development net of amortisation, and any other non-operational items for both Q4 2026 and the full year, because the market needs clean recurring profitability to test whether the greater-than-15% target has genuinely been achieved or whether Q3's 17.4% reported margin overstated underlying earnings power.

Other side of the story: The regulatory downside could be overstated if the two resolved states release most delayed cases in Q4, denial ratios remain stable, private-insurance and Medicare funding grow, and Dynavox's 120- person reimbursement organisation converts its payer relationships into faster approvals, with RedEye's view being that the state-by-state problem resembles recurring "whack-a-mole" rather than a structural market change, while the company's health-economic evidence supports long-term disability-policy backing; that optimistic case would nevertheless require observable improvement in approval lead times, United States revenue conversion and recurring margins before the current regulatory risk can be removed from 2027–2029 EPS assumptions.

If those conditions are met, the market may have over-extrapolated Q3's weakness, because RedEye argued that after the share-price decline the market was already discounting prolonged reimbursement friction and growth well below the 20% target, with Dynavox trading at approximately 18 times 2027 enterprise value to EBIT against peer multiples around 23 times, while its blended Fair Value of SEK99 implied roughly 30% upside from the SEK76.20 reference price despite the downgrade.
