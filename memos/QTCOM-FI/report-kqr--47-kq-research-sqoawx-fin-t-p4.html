Unhedged exposure and asymmetric structure

1.
Asymmetric exposure: QT Group operates with a significant mismatch between its foreign currency sales and euro-denominated cost base. According to the company's 2025 financial bulletin, the group does not use foreign currency derivatives to hedge its ongoing commercial cash flows. This leaves reported net sales fully exposed to translation risks, particularly from the US dollar. Consequently, any structural strength in the euro dilutes growth and compresses operating margins without derivative offsets.
2.
Revenue distribution: Foreign currencies represent a dominant share of the company's top-line generation compared to its localized expenses. DNB Carnegie reported on February 18, 2026, that the United States accounts for 36% of total revenue, while China represents approximately 13% of sales. In contrast, about 68% of operating costs are denominated in euros, creating a structural operational asymmetry. This structural mismatch means that operating margins are highly sensitive to the relative strength of the euro.
3.
Contrary evidence: The company has selectively utilized derivative instruments in the past to mitigate transaction risks during major corporate events. QT Group's 2025 annual report states that hedge accounting was applied specifically to hedge foreign exchange fluctuations associated with payments for the IAR acquisition. This demonstrates that management is willing to hedge transactional exposures despite leaving regular commercial operations completely unhedged. This selective approach highlights a dual risk profile for the company's earnings per share.

These fluctuations introduce non-operational volatility into quarterly earnings per share figures.
