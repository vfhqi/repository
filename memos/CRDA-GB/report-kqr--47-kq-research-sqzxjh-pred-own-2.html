Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Resolving Croda's capital return strategy is critical because it will determine the company's valuation multiple and earnings per share growth over the next three years.
1.
Key Question: "Croda International Plc (CRDA-GB), a UK-listed specialty ingredients maker that sells direct to more than 17,000 customers from eleven shared multi-purpose manufacturing sites. It reports three sectors: Consumer Care (Beauty Actives, Beauty Care, Fragrances & Flavours, Home Care), Life Sciences (Pharma Ingredients and Pharma Solutions, Crop Protection, Seed Enhancement) and Industrial Specialties. For Croda International Plc (CRDA-GB), what have the board, the chief financial officer Stephen Oxley and the activist shareholder Standard Industries said, publicly or as reported by brokers, about share buybacks, special dividends, the leverage floor (management's stated intention to keep net debt above 1.0 times EBITDA, Morgan Stanley, March 2026) and the dividend payout (111p for FY2025, a 76% payout against a 40% to 50% policy, company)? How did Croda handle earlier periods of low leverage (the £50m buyback in 2011; the £136m special dividend in 2016)? And what do Standard Industries' campaigns at Johnson Matthey and elsewhere suggest about the likely timing and form of a capital return at Croda?"

Capital allocation and EPS impact

2.
Optimised leverage: Morgan Stanley reported in February 2026 that Croda could execute buybacks of 3% to 8% of its market cap if it levers to 1.5 times net debt to EBITDA. This capital return mode is supported by a normalisation of capex to 6% of sales, which boosts free cash flow. Therefore, a formal share repurchase program represents a direct mechanism to accelerate EPS compound growth rates over the next three years.
3.
Strategic distribution: Rothschild Redburn asserted in November 2026 that the company trades at an attractive discount of 12 times forward EV/EBITDA versus the ingredients average of 14 times. They observed that special dividends or share buybacks offer additional upside as the ordinary dividend payout normalises. Resolving the capital return format is critical because it will decide whether the market closes this historical valuation gap or maintains a discount.

Activist pressure and strategic risks

4.
Activist presence: Jefferies Research noted in September 2025 that Standard Industries' 5.5% holding has concentrated investor attention on capital efficiency. The broker noted that key debates focus on how Croda can monetize its expensive research and development pipeline. This activist presence could reduce management's capital allocation leeway and accelerate structural changes.
5.
Structural optionality: Rothschild Redburn reported in March 2026 that Standard Industries' chemical sector track record suggests a push for divestments and capital discipline. The broker argued that activist engagement could lead to selling lower-margin businesses like Seed Enhancement. These strategic options could rebuild Croda's operating margins and free up cash for distribution.
6.
Contrary evidence: In December 2025, CEO Steve Foots stated that Croda is only open to distributing excess cash if there are no viable bolt-on M&A opportunities. This preference means capital returns will remain secondary to strategic expansion despite the low leverage. This strategy could disappoint yield-focused investors who expect near-term payouts.
7.
Other side of the story: Morgan Stanley highlighted in March 2026 that management has no intention of letting leverage drop below 1.0 times EBITDA. However, pushing leverage up to the activist's desired levels could reduce financial flexibility during cyclical downturns in the consumer and agricultural end-markets. Consequently, prioritizing debt-funded returns over capital preservation could compromise Croda's long-term credit rating and increase interest expense.

Section 2 – Management and Activist Views on Capital Return

Section summary
  1. Management and activist views suggest a strong medium-term outlook for capital returns as capital expenditure normalizes and cash flow accelerates.
  2. We have high confidence in this outlook because both company filings and broker analyses confirm that excess capital will be returned once leverage constraints are satisfied.

Stated leverage floor and targets

1.
Prudent leverage targets: Croda's management maintains a strict capital allocation policy designed to optimize balance sheet efficiency and preserve liquidity. Morgan Stanley reported in March 2026 that Chief Financial Officer Stephen Oxley has no intention of letting the net leverage position fall below 1.0 times EBITDA. This stated leverage floor protects the company against downgrades while forcing management to consider returning capital if cash balances grow too high. Ultimately, this policy ensures that leverage remains within target levels of 1.0 to 2.0 times EBITDA.
2.
Favorable cash generation: Croda is entering a free cash flow harvesting phase as its intensive capital expenditure cycle finally comes to an end. The company announced in July 2026 that future capital expenditure will normalize to around 6% of sales compared to previous peak levels. Morgan Stanley reported that this normalization will significantly improve free cash flow and provide optionality for special returns from FY27 onward. This transition to higher cash generation represents a key driver for future earnings per share growth.
3.
Optimized buyback potential: Morgan Stanley analysis in February 2026 showed that Croda has the capacity to implement a substantial share repurchase program if it chooses to optimize its leverage. The broker highlighted that levering up to the 1.5 times EBITDA midpoint would allow the company to fund a buyback of £85m to £240m by FY28. This action would translate to a reduction of three to eight percent of the current market capitalization. This capital return mechanism would provide a direct and meaningful boost to earnings per share over the next three years.
4.
Improving operational discipline: The board is applying its capital allocation framework with significantly greater rigour to improve financial returns and cost discipline. Company filings from July 2026 confirm that the capital allocation priorities remain focused on organic growth, ordinary dividends, and maintaining leverage in the 1-2x EBITDA range. This disciplined approach means that excess capital will be returned once organic investment needs are fully met. Consequently, any lack of viable M&A opportunities will naturally trigger shareholder distributions.
5.
Contrary evidence: In December 2025, Rothschild Redburn reported that Chief Executive Officer Steve Foots prefers bolt-on acquisitions and organic expansion over share buybacks. The company's stated priority is to reinvest cash in the business rather than executing special capital returns if attractive growth opportunities are available. This strategic bias toward expansion means that special distributions could be deferred if the company finds attractive targets.

Elevated dividend payout dynamics

6.
Generous ordinary dividends: Croda's board proposed a full-year ordinary dividend of 111p per share for FY2025, which was a minor increase of one penny from the previous fiscal year. This payment represented an exceptionally high payout ratio of seventy-six percent of adjusted earnings. Despite this being well above the historical 40-50% target policy, the board decided to maintain the payment to show confidence in the group's long-term recovery. This decision demonstrates the company's strong commitment to regular shareholder returns.
7.
Restoring earnings cover: The board's choice to maintain the interim dividend flat at 48p in July 2026 reflects a tactical decision to conserve capital. Company filings show that the board intends to keep the ordinary dividend flat until earnings cover is fully restored. This commitment will help Croda reduce the ordinary dividend payout back toward its target of 40% to 50% of adjusted earnings. In doing so, the company can protect its balance sheet while avoiding a dividend cut.
8.
Rising cash cover: Morgan Stanley reported in August 2025 that Croda is approximately one year away from fully covering its dividend payments with free cash flow. The broker projected that improved profits and lower working capital will allow free cash flow to comfortably cover the ordinary dividend from FY26. This cash cover development is crucial because it removes the risk of debt-funded dividends and improves capital return safety. As cash flow coverage strengthens, the board will have more flexibility to authorize additional returns.

Section 3 – Historical Leverage and Capital Return Precedents at Croda

Section summary
  1. Historical capital returns at Croda demonstrate a consistent policy of returning cash to shareholders via tactical buybacks or special dividends when leverage drops below 1.0 times EBITDA.
  2. The board has historically successfully utilized these balance sheet adjustments to enhance earnings per share growth and maintain optimal capital structures without compromising organic growth.

The 2011 share buyback program

1.
Strategic balance optimization: Croda initiated a fifty million pound share buyback program in 2011 because its leverage ratio dropped below target levels. The company's annual report published in March 2012 stated that this tactical program purchased 2.8m shares at an average price of 1782p. This capital return allowed Croda to manage its balance sheet cash while maintaining capital expenditure and funding a 57.1% increase in its ordinary dividend payout. Consequently, the buyback served as a key mechanism to optimize the group's capital structure during a period of strong organic cash generation.
2.
Accelerated earnings growth: The execution of the 2011 buyback program directly contributed to a substantial expansion of Croda's basic earnings. Company filings in February 2012 reported that continuing earnings per share grew by 28.4% to 122.5p in 2011, supported by a reduced average share count of 135.3m. This EPS performance was superior to the wider UK chemicals sector where earnings growth was more modest. The buyback proved that reducing outstanding equity is highly effective at enhancing shareholder earnings per share when trading profitability is strong.
3.
Low financial leverage: Croda managed to maintain exceptionally low debt levels throughout the 2011 buyback period due to excellent underlying trading. The company's 2011 financial review showed that net debt stood at only £231.1m, representing a leverage ratio of 0.8 times EBITDA. Morgan Stanley noted in May 2012 that this leverage was extremely low compared to historical averages. This low gearing level meant that the board had substantial unused debt capacity to pursue both organic growth and potential acquisitions.
4.
Valuation multiple support: The market rewarded Croda's proactive capital management and strong cash conversion with attractive valuation multiples. Morgan Stanley reported in March 2012 that Croda's forward price-to-earnings ratio stood at 14.6 times, which was lower than peers like Victrex at 15.8 times and Johnson Matthey at 14.9 times. This disciplined approach to returning excess capital prevented the multiple from derating despite a volatile macroeconomic environment. Ultimately, the buyback demonstrated to the market that management was committed to preventing balance sheet cash drag from diluting equity returns.
5.
Contrary evidence: In March 2012, Morgan Stanley reported that Croda's management actually viewed share buybacks as a last resort behind bolt-on acquisitions. The broker highlighted that the company's preference was to deploy up to £60m annually on technology acquisitions. This preference meant that buybacks would remain a secondary tactical tool rather than a permanent capital return strategy.

The 2016 special dividend

6.
Substantive cash distribution: Croda returned £136m to shareholders via a special dividend of 100p per share in June 2016 to address ongoing low leverage. The company's 2015 annual report published in February 2016 stated that this return was combined with a share consolidation to maintain capital discipline. This special payout allowed the company to distribute excess cash while continuing to invest over £100m in capital projects. This distribution successfully demonstrated the board's commitment to returning surplus capital to investors when organic options are exhausted.
7.
Efficient balance sheet: The 2016 special dividend successfully increased Croda's financial leverage toward the middle of its historical target range. The group's 2016 full year announcement reported that net debt rose to £364.1m, which represented a leverage ratio of 1.1 times EBITDA. Deutsche Bank Research welcomed this more efficient use of the balance sheet in February 2016, noting it combined cash returns with accretive technology acquisitions. This recapitalization ensured that Croda maintained an optimal capital structure while avoiding excessive balance sheet de-leveraging.
8.
Strong profit growth: Croda achieved record trading performance alongside the execution of its 2016 capital return program. Company filings from February 2017 showed that adjusted operating profit grew by 12.9% to £298.2m, driving a 15.4% increase in adjusted basic earnings per share to 155.8p. This earnings expansion was supported by a 20% increase in sales of high-value new and protected products. Thus, the capital return occurred during a period where operational momentum remained highly robust across all core business sectors.
9.
Comparable peer precedent: The use of special dividends to return excess cash was a strategy shared by other high-quality specialty chemical peers during this era. Morgan Stanley noted in December 2017 that both Croda and Victrex successfully utilized special dividends as a primary tool to distribute surplus cash. This peer comparison highlights that specialty ingredients makers with high free cash flow yields are uniquely positioned for special returns compared to more cyclical diversified chemical companies.
10.
Sector valuation premiums: The market valued Croda at a premium multiple during the 2016 capital return period due to its high cash conversion. Deutsche Bank Research reported in February 2016 that the stock traded at 19 times forward price-to-earnings, which was justified by its strong profit growth. This valuation was supported by an overall dividend yield of 5.8% including the special dividend. Consequently, returning capital via special dividends helped defend Croda's premium valuation multiple during a period of subdued global economic demand.
11.
Other side of the story: Although Croda's historical returns in 2011 and 2016 successfully optimized leverage without disrupting capital projects, they occurred during periods of exceptionally strong macroeconomic growth and high margins. Under current conditions where operating margins have fallen and leverage has already risen to 1.4 times EBITDA, executing similar large-scale capital returns could risk straining the balance sheet and delaying crucial cost-saving programs. Consequently, the board is likely to prioritize ordinary dividend cover and operational recovery over immediate special returns.
