EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Storskogen operates a highly diversified serial acquisition model managing 114 business units across Services, Trade, and Industry divisions, generating SEK 33,097m in net sales during 2025. The company is transitioning from rapid debt-fueled expansion to disciplined organic-led growth under permanent CEO Christer Hansson, who has successfully reduced interest-bearing net debt by approximately SEK 4,000m over three years to reach a 2.3x net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio.

However, the group exhibits structural weaknesses relative to elite Nordic compounders, including a depressed 10.2% return on capital employed and a 6.4% adjusted return on equity, which fall substantially below peer benchmarks like Lifco's exceptional ROCE and Indutrade's 14% median ROIC. The company's extreme decentralization preserves local entrepreneurship but prevents meaningful synergy extraction and leaves individual business units operating as independent price-takers without vertical integration or centralized purchasing power, creating vulnerability to input cost inflation that cannot be efficiently passed downstream.

DNB Carnegie applies a 30-35% valuation discount to Storskogen relative to established serial acquirer peers due to high leverage and lower profitability targets, with the company targeting only GDP plus 1-2% organic growth compared to elite peer organic expansion rates. The adjusted EBITA margin stabilized at 9.4% in both 2024 and

2025 but compressed to 8.1% in Q1 2026 due to Services division headwinds, reflecting persistent margin pressure in cyclical infrastructure installation segments. The group achieved 74% adjusted cash conversion in 2025, exceeding its 70% corporate target, while the equity-to-assets ratio improved to 50% by December 2025, demonstrating successful balance sheet stabilization. The European SME M&A landscape offers unlimited structural runways due to extreme fragmentation, with the top five Nordic compounders controlling less than 5% of the regional market and consolidation remaining in its early phase compared to North American markets.

Storskogen's business units are positioned downstream in value-added manufacturing, B2B wholesale distribution, and local infrastructure services, operating as independent price-takers with minimal upstream or downstream coordination, which prevents group-wide value chain optimization despite preserving entrepreneurial drive at the subsidiary level. Value-added production stages command margins of 10% to 15%, but Storskogen's consolidated 9.40% EBITA margin underperforms Lifco's premium 22% to 23% target, reflecting a portfolio mix weighted toward lower-margin distribution and installation services rather than proprietary IP-driven products.

The company's fragmented supplier base operates without coordinated grouplevel purchasing synergies, exposing business units to localized input volatility from fluctuating global commodity prices for steel, wood, and freight services, though centralized logistics framework agreements with DHL, PostNord, and Vattenfall partially mitigate downstream distribution costs. Management has executed a major portfolio cleanup by divesting nine loss-making business units in 2024 and reducing Services headcount by approximately 1,000 FTEs, while successfully extending debt maturities to 2027-2029 and alleviating immediate refinancing risks.

The company resumed selective M&A in H2 2025 with platform acquisitions completed at 5x to 8x EV/EBITA multiples, but DNB Carnegie expects only 6% annual acquired growth over 2026-2031, reflecting a disciplined, slower expansion pace compared to the 119 deals completed during 2021-2022, and Storskogen's ability to redeploy cash flows at 25-30% post-tax RONIC remains highly unproven. A Vestum expert flagged that extreme decentralization creates operational integration risks and prevents synergies, while non-integrated trade subsidiaries lack joint purchasing power and suffer raw material cost inflation, and the decentralized corporate structure lacks a unified R&D framework, leaving innovation under-funded at subsidiary levels.

Storskogen's macro regime fit remains constrained by high interest rates and elevated capital costs, with rising rates driving multiple contraction and leaving highly leveraged serial acquirers out of favor, while the 30% peer discount reflects structural concerns about the company's ability to generate elite-level returns in a higher-cost-ofcapital environment. The group aligns with long-term secular trends, including validated net-zero transition targets by the Science Based Targets initiative and strategic focus on acquisitions supporting automation, digitalization, energy infrastructure, and electrification, though achieving these goals requires increased capital expenditures in electrified vehicle fleets and renewable energy systems.

The foundational business quality assessment reveals that while Storskogen has successfully stabilized its balance sheet and demonstrated pricing pass-through capabilities during recent inflation cycles, the company's decentralized foundation lacks structural moats and high-return reinvestment runways characteristic of elite Nordic compounders, with confidence in advantaged business strength rated as LOW due to the absence of proprietary IP, limited pricing power at the subsidiary level, and a structurally incapable capital reinvestment engine that cannot consistently generate the 25-30% post-tax returns on incremental capital required to justify premium valuations in a higher-rate environment.

Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive financial and operational analysis of Storskogen reveals two significant statistical outliers that warrant investor attention due to their material deviation from peer norms and industry base rates. These outliers represent quantitative extremes that management has not fully reconciled in its strategic communications, despite their profound implications for understanding the company's structural positioning and historical trajectory.

The first notable outlier is Storskogen's exceptional working capital return of 62.2% achieved in 2025, which positions the company in the top percentile of working capital efficiency metrics when benchmarked against diversified industrial peers. This performance appears structurally anomalous when considered alongside the company's decentralized operating model, where individual business units independently manage their own supplier relationships and inventory policies.

The sustained elevation of this metric suggests that the group-wide working capital rationalization initiatives implemented during 2023-2024 produced cash release effects that significantly exceeded typical industry optimization programs. However, management has repeatedly cautioned that the exceptional cash conversion rates approaching 100% during the 2023-2024 period were driven by onetime working capital liquidations and are fundamentally unsustainable, with normalization expected toward the 70-80% range.

This disconnect between the reported working capital return metric and management's forward guidance creates analytical ambiguity regarding the true sustainable efficiency baseline of Storskogen's underlying business portfolio.
