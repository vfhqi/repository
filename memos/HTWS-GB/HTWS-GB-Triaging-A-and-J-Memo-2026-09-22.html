[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Helios Towers is a cash-compounding case with a demand accelerant on top: its African mobile operators have stepped up network spending, each added tenancy lands on a fixed site cost base, and the balance-sheet repair that kept the cash from shareholders is finished. At 205.2 pence (2.74 USD) the shares pay about 22 percent a year on the framework's convention at an unchanged multiple and about 16 to 17 percent on a cash-yield footing, so the case is compelling on the operating line and merely adequate on the equity line. It turns on two things: whether the tenancy step-up persists into FY2027, and how much of the EBITDA growth reaches earnings per share through interest, currency and tax.

  • The change is external, structural and now visible in the customers' own spending.
    • Airtel Africa's cash capex was 389 USDm in the June 2026 quarter against 121 USDm a year earlier
    • Vodacom raised its medium-term EBITDA and operating free cash flow targets to early-teens growth
    • the top four African operators raised capex guidance by 33 percent.
    • That spending produced a record 1,406 organic tenancy additions in Q1 2026 and 1,105 in Q2 against a consensus of 487, and a second upgrade of FY2026 tenancy guidance to 3,500 to 4,000.
    • Airtel Africa calls its spending "a deliberate frontloading strategy", and the second-half guidance implies 500 to 750 additions a quarter against 1,255 a quarter in the first half.
      • So the step-up may be partly a pull-forward, which is why the November trading update and the FY2027 guidance are the test.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The customers chose to spend
    • the company built and colocated
    • the tenancy ratio rose to 2.26x and group ROIC to 14.4 percent
    • H1 2026 revenue grew 11 percent to 466.3 USDm and Adjusted EBITDA 14 percent to 257.0 USDm
    • recurring free cash flow rose 52 percent to 105.8 USDm
    • and every named broker raised EBITDA after the 30 July results.
    • Nothing in the chain is still waiting to happen.
  • The internal change is a capital-allocation turn, not a transformation.
    • Four internal forces are strong: guidance raised twice in 2026, shareholder returns started (an inaugural dividend of 0.604p and a buyback now sized at 175 USDm to late 2027), a resourced strategy change (IMPACT 2030, organic lease-up over acquisitions) and rising operating standards.
    • There is no leadership change, no perimeter change and no real price rise, which is what keeps this from being a multi-dimensional corporate change and what makes it a compounder's harvest of its own footprint.
  • The operating outputs are improving on every horizon; the exception is earnings per share, and that exception is the case's weak line.
    • Net leverage has fallen from 5.1x in 2022 to 3.4x, the debt is refinanced at 6.75 percent to 2031, the EBITDA margin rises about a point a year, and cash conversion is up on every measure.
    • But reported profit fell to 21.7 USDm in H1 2026 from 30.9 USDm on non-cash currency movements in finance costs, the reported tax rate was 71 percent in FY2025, and consensus FY2026 EPS has been cut 10.7 percent over three months while EBITDA and targets were raised.
    • The sell-side is upgrading the operations and downgrading the residual.
  • We expect EBITDA to compound at 10 to 13 percent a year and earnings per share much faster, because the bill below EBITDA is fixed.
    • Our modal path takes Adjusted EBITDA from 530 USDm in FY2026 to 700 USDm in FY2029, brokers'-basis EPS from 0.075 USD to 0.205 USD, and recurring free cash flow per share from 0.220 USD to 0.340 USD.
    • About 9 to 10 points a year of the EPS growth come from revenue and margin
    • the rest comes from the drop-through and a share count falling about 2 percent a year on the buyback.
    • We sit on consensus for the operating lines and below it for near-term earnings, because we hold the tax rate at 40 percent through FY2028 against consensus's 39 to 31 percent.
  • The shares cost 17.31 times 24-month-forward consensus earnings, which is the top of the only range that has real earnings in it.
    • That is the 78th percentile of the last two years against a median of 13.87x
    • the five-year and ten-year percentiles rest on years without earnings and say nothing.
    • On EV/EBITDA the shares are at 8.5x consensus FY2026 EBITDA against European towers at 14.2x and US towers at 16.0x, and against IHS and Indus at 6.0x: a frontier discount the sell-side itself calls persistent.
    • The price is 18.5x our own earnings.
  • At an unchanged multiple the return is about 23 percent a year over eighteen months and about 22 percent over three years, carried almost entirely by earnings growth.
    • The cash return is about 1.2 percent over eighteen months
    • the re-rating is nil by assumption
    • earnings growth on the 24-month-forward figure is 36 percent.
    • On the cash-yield footing, at an unchanged 9.05 percent recurring free cash flow yield, the return is 16.5 percent a year.
    • The case needs no re-rating.
    • The swing factor is the drop-through.
      • If the reported tax rate stays at 45 percent and the finance charge runs 15 USDm a year above our path, the eighteen-month return falls to 11 percent a year (D) and the three-year return to 16 percent (C), on two lines management does not control.
  • The pattern is 'Compounder', with 'Demand Inflection' as the secondary fit.
    • Brokers model management's own conservative plan (about 1,700 to 2,100 tenancies a year), management guides below it and beats, each beat lands on a fixed cost base at 80 percent flow-through and on a fixed interest and depreciation bill, and consensus holds the share count flat while 117 USDm of buyback is still to run.
    • The proven record (fourteen consecutive quarterly EBITDA beats, three years of raised midpoints) makes the next beat probable rather than hoped for.
  • The chain holds from the customers' spending to the company's cash, and breaks, if anywhere, between EBITDA and earnings per share and at the border.
    • Five customers are 80 percent of revenue
    • about 104 USDm of tax claims in the DRC, Tanzania and Congo Brazzaville are unprovided
    • the cash reaches London through Mauritius and central banks can restrict convertibility.
    • None of these has broken the chain in ten years of EBITDA growth, and 71 percent of EBITDA is in hard or pegged currency.

  • The case is therefore a material one that clears the bar narrowly and on one lens, with its risks named, dated and mostly monitorable.

    • It does not need the multiple to move.
    • What it cannot survive is a break in customer capex or a sovereign event in the DRC or Tanzania.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material by the framework's test: sufficiently strong input forces, sufficiently improving outputs on every operating line, and a 'Modal return' that clears 20 percent a year on the convention with earnings growth alone.

    • The three Elements combine rather than bind: the demand acceleration feeds the fixed cost base, which feeds cash, which feeds distributions, and none of the three needs another to be better than it is.
    • Not A, because the return clears the bar by a small margin, does so through the below-EBITDA arithmetic, has no re-rating leg and a negligible dividend leg, and the EPS line is being cut while everything above it is raised.
    • Not C, because the operating chain has already travelled to the sell-side's estimates and the return on the convention is above the bar.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against the thesis is the false-friend reading that the risks report's own competing-hypotheses analysis chose: the tenancy beat is customer front-loading (Airtel Africa's own word), the H2 2026 run rate falls to 500 to 750 a quarter, the EBITDA beat is partly power pass-through (10 USDm of Q2 2026 revenue at nil EBITDA), and none of it reaches the equity: H1 2026 profit fell 30 percent, consensus FY2026 EPS has been cut 13 percent in twelve months, the shares fell 23 percent from June 2026, the chief executive sold 24 percent of his stake in March 2026 and two pre-IPO holders are selling down.
  • We concede the EPS fragility, the front-loading risk until the November update, and the overhang.
  • We hold the view because the operating chain is contractual (non-cancellable ten-to-fifteen-year leases, a 1 percent annual churn cap, 5.9 USDbn of contracted revenue with 6.5 years remaining)
    • because tenancies and Adjusted EBITDA move together with a correlation of 0.96
    • because recurring free cash flow rose 52 percent in H1 2026, which pass-through revenue cannot produce
    • because leverage of 3.4x with debt fixed at 6.75 percent to 2031 removes the refinancing lever from the bear case
    • and because the return we claim needs no re-rating, only a continuation of the 10 to 13 percent EBITDA growth that the customers' capex has already funded.
  • What would change our mind is set out in the 'Investment Case' Building Block: a quarter below 600 additions, FY2027 guidance below 3,000, or the FY2026 final dividend not paid from operating cash.
