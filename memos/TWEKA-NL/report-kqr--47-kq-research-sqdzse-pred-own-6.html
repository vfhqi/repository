  1. The strategic transition of Guido Grandi and the carve-out of Electrification are expected to structurally redefine the financial and operational profile of TKH Group.
  2. Executing the Digitalization fibre optics divestment program aims to reduce conglomerate discounts and enhance long-term earnings per share.

Section 2 – Guido Grandi at Norma Group: 'Step Up' and Corporate Actions

Section summary
  1. Guido Grandi's first year at Norma Group focused heavily on the operational execution of the Step Up turnaround programme to drive efficiency.
  2. Substantial cost inflation and macro headwinds led to a subsequent strategic pivot and major asset impairments at Norma Group.
  3. The eventual restructuring of Norma Group culminated in a deep dividend policy cut to increase financial flexibility.
  4. Guido Grandi's executive career prior to Norma Group spans automotive suppliers and industrial conglomerates.
  5. Operational mandates at WKW and ThyssenKrupp subsidiaries focused heavily on restructuring and divisional realignment.
  6. The unlisted nature of his primary past roles means that historical group-level share price and sell-side consensus data are unavailable.

Operational turnaround and Step Up design

1.
Executive leadership transition: Guido Grandi officially assumed his role as the Chief Executive Officer of Norma Group on 1 June 2023. His appointment was approved by the supervisory board on 12 April 2023 with the explicit mandate to stabilize operations and drive strategic transformation. Consequently, his arrival coincided with the launch of a new, comprehensive growth and efficiency framework. This transition was designed to shift focus toward high-margin industrial applications.
2.
Turnaround program launch: The newly established Management Board immediately prioritized the rollout of the Step Up growth and efficiency programme. This programme was publicly introduced on 9 May 2023 as a structured plan to enhance operational performance. Specifically, the framework was divided into two core pillars focusing on operational efficiency and growth initiatives across key strategic business units. Therefore, these measures aimed to deliver sustainable margin expansion.
3.
Rapid initiative expansion: Under Grandi's leadership, the internal team rapidly identified and initiated hundreds of distinct operational measures. The company expanded its active initiatives from an initial 100 in mid-2023 to more than 1,100 by December 2023. Furthermore, this momentum continued as the total number of recorded measures surpassed 1,800 by September 2024. Consequently, this bottom-up approach was expected to create a highly disciplined, self-help driven culture.
4.
Operational process optimization: The operational efficiency pillar focused heavily on stabilizing supply chain processes and automating production lines. Norma Group's annual report on 29 March 2025 highlights that the company achieved significant optimizations in logistics and purchasing within the EMEA region. For example, these efforts successfully resolved persistent bottlenecks, substantially reducing backlog and freight costs. Ultimately, these process improvements laid the groundwork for robust operating cash flows.

First-year corporate actions and results

5.
Stable margin preservation: The disciplined implementation of the Step Up measures helped Norma Group maintain stable profitability despite top-line contraction. While weak automotive and construction demand drove a 5.5% revenue decline to €1,155.10 million in 2024, the adjusted EBIT margin held steady at 8%. This matched the prior year's adjusted EBIT margin of 8% in 2023. Consequently, the self-help savings successfully offset severe volume deleverage and wage inflation.
6.
Peer performance comparison: Norma Group's 8% adjusted EBIT margin in 2024 represented a resilient outcome in a weak global environment. For comparison, key European automotive suppliers faced substantial margin compression during the same period, leading to numerous profit warnings. Deutsche Bank's research note on 27 September 2024 highlights that peer suppliers like Hella and Forvia experienced severe hits to profitability. Therefore, Norma's ability to maintain its margin guidance reflected strong operational execution.
7.
Segment shift targets: A central goal of the Step Up program was to aggressively alter the corporate sales mix. Grandi's team aimed to shift the sales split from 60% Mobility and 40% Water and Industry to 60% Water and Industry and 40% Mobility by 2027. In his earnings call on 7 May 2024, Grandi emphasized that this transition would reduce exposure to cyclical automotive end-markets. Thus, shifting the mix was viewed as crucial for upgrading the long-term margin quality.

Restructuring provisions and asset impairments

8.
Strategic portfolio change: The strategic priorities underwent a fundamental revision in late 2024 when the company decided to divest its Water Management business. This divestment plan was announced on 28 November 2024, representing a complete departure from the previous Step Up growth targets. Consequently, the sale was designed to free up cash and focus purely on joining technology. This portfolio decision ultimately led to the replacement of Step Up with the NewNORMA program.
9.
Severe goodwill write-downs: The strategic pivot and deteriorating economic expectations in Europe triggered massive balance sheet adjustments. Specifically, Norma Group recorded a €50 million goodwill impairment for the EMEA region in Q3 2025. This was followed by a larger goodwill impairment of €102 million in Q4 2025, driven by lower mid-term growth expectations. Therefore, these non-cash write-offs severely impacted the reported net income while leaving adjusted EBIT unaffected.
10.
Accelerated restructuring costs: In order to execute the NewNORMA transformation rapidly, management pulled forward several planned restructuring measures. The company's interim report on 5 May 2026 stated that total extraordinary expenses for 2026 were increased to €24 million. These costs were primarily related to severance payments, overhead reduction, and production relocations. Consequently, these actions were expected to yield €15 million in annual savings starting in 2026.

Dividend policy cuts and broker skepticism

11.
Dividend policy reduction: In tandem with the strategic reset, the Management Board drastically reduced its shareholder distribution policy. From fiscal year 2027 onward, the dividend payout ratio was cut to at least 10% of adjusted Group Net Income. This was materially below the previous policy of distributing 30% to 35% of adjusted earnings. Therefore, this revision aimed to significantly increase financial flexibility to fund organic growth and restructuring.
12.
Persistent broker skepticism: Sell-side analysts expressed considerable caution regarding the execution of the revised targets. Deutsche Bank's research note on 20 October 2026 highlighted that achieving a double-digit EBIT margin requires successful execution of transformation. Furthermore, the analyst noted that opportunities in the industrial segment had yet to translate into meaningful financial contributions. Thus, brokers remained on the sidelines pending clear evidence of sustainable operational improvements.

Section 4 – The February 2025 Norma Departure and the Water Divestment

Section summary
  1. Guido Grandi's sudden departure in February 2025 stemmed directly from disagreements over the desired pace of operational transformation.
  2. The USD 1.0 billion Water business divestment generated EUR 650 million in net proceeds to successfully eliminate group net debt.
  3. A generous capital return program of EUR 260 million was completed through buybacks but was immediately followed by a deep dividend policy cut.

Strategic differences and immediate departure

1.
Sudden executive resignation: Guido Grandi resigned from his position as Chief Executive Officer of Norma Group on 17 February 2025 due to strategic differences regarding the future direction of the company. Norma Group's annual report published on 30 March 2026 confirms that the Supervisory Board Chair Mark Wilhelms immediately assumed interim leadership for a transitional period of up to one year. This unexpected transition disrupted executive continuity, meaning that short-term earnings per share faced temporary administrative headwinds.
2.
Disagreed transformation pace: The primary strategic disagreement behind the departure of Guido Grandi was the desired speed of operational restructuring. Deutsche Bank's research note of 18 February 2025 highlights that the Supervisory Board intended to transform the company's structure much more rapidly than planned by Grandi. Consequently, his sudden exit highlights the board's urgency in executing the portfolio separation, which eventually created near-term margin volatility but cleared the path for faster transition.
3.
Comprehensive evidence set: In analyzing this strategic corporate transition, we found 18 relevant documents covering three companies including Norma Group and Advanced Drainage Systems, alongside one comparable past leadership case. Norma Group's regulatory filings from 2025 and 2026 provide the primary operational and financial baseline for this assessment. This detailed evidence base allows for a robust reconstruction of his departure, reducing execution uncertainty for future leadership transitions.

Divestment execution and valuation metrics

4.
Highly valued divestment: Norma Group signed an agreement on 23 September 2025 to sell its Water Management business unit to Advanced Drainage Systems. Norma Group's press release on 30 September 2025 confirms that the enterprise value of the transaction was USD 1.0 billion, which represents approximately a quarter of the group's total historical sales. This highly priced divestment successfully unlocked intrinsic value, which means that reported earnings per share will become highly dependent on the performance of the remaining industrial core.
5.
Accretive valuation multiples: The Water business sale was executed at highly attractive financial terms compared to broader market valuations. Norma Group's earnings call on 4 November 2025 highlights that the transaction achieved a value-accretive multiple of approximately 19.3x adjusted EBIT. Additionally, Deutsche Bank's research note of 23 September 2025 reports that the deal implied an EV/EBITDA multiple of 16x pre-synergies. This high valuation multiple significantly exceeded the parent group's historical trading multiple of 13x, supporting immediate balance sheet deleveraging.
6.
Substantial net proceeds: Norma Group successfully completed the Water business sale on 2 February 2026, generating major liquidity. Norma's press release of 17 February 2026 shows that the transaction yielded net cash inflows of EUR 650 million. These proceeds allowed the company to completely eliminate its net debt, leaving it in a highly liquid net cash position. Consequently, the successful execution of this cashgenerative deal substantially lowers the group's interest expenses, ultimately boosting adjusted net income.

Broker skepticism and core margin pressure

7.
Severe margin contraction: The exclusion of the highly profitable Water business immediately exposed weak profitability in Norma Group's remaining divisions. Deutsche Bank's research note of 21 October 2025 highlights that the underlying adjusted EBIT margin for the remaining company was surprisingly low at 0% to 1% for fiscal year 2025. This remarkably low profitability implicitly suggested that the core automotive margins had turned negative. Unless industrial efficiencies are realized rapidly, the remaining business will face ongoing earnings per share dilution.
8.
High tax drag: The structural execution of the Water business divestment resulted in a lower-than-expected net cash benefit. Deutsche Bank's research note of 21 October 2025 reports that net proceeds were lower than expected because the transaction was structured as a deemed asset deal rather than share deal. This specific structural setup triggered a massive tax payment of EUR 91 million in the second half of 2026. This massive cash tax drag significantly reduced the capital available for industrial reinvestment, dampening mediumterm earnings growth.
9.
Share price decline: The combination of low core margins and high tax expectations triggered a highly negative market reaction. Deutsche Bank's research note of 21 October 2025 reveals that Norma Group's share price dropped by 15% immediately following the signing of the transaction. This sharp decline reflected widespread investor concern over the structural erosion of the remaining business's earning power. Consequently, the divestment was seen by the market as a near-term valuation drag rather than an immediate catalyst.
10.
Contrary evidence: Norma's management continuously framed the divestment of the Water business as a highly positive strategic pivot. In Norma Group's press release on 30 September 2025, the executive board argued that the USD 1.0 billion transaction was a significant milestone in the global transformation towards a focused industrial powerhouse. This corporate framing directly contradicts the negative market sentiment reported in Deutsche Bank's note of 21 October 2025, proving that internal targets and market expectations differed significantly.

Capital return handling and payout policies

11.
Completed capital distribution: Norma Group fulfilled its commitment to return a substantial portion of the divestment proceeds directly to its shareholders. Norma's press release of 18 September 2026 confirms the successful completion of the capital return program, distributing approximately EUR 260 million to owners. This capital return was executed through two consecutive share buyback programs. By reducing the total outstanding share count, these massive buybacks structurally concentrated future group earnings, thus supporting long-term earnings per share.
12.
Phased share repurchases: The execution of the capital return was split into two highly disciplined corporate buyback initiatives. Norma Group's ad-hoc announcement on 14 August 2026 highlights that the company launched a second share buyback of EUR 208 million after completing a spring buyback of EUR 53 million. This final phase repurchased 9,306,113 shares, or 29.21% of the group's total share capital, at a price of EUR 22.35. This significant reduction in capital outstanding will drive substantial EPS accretion for the remaining shareholders.
13.
Slashed dividend policy: The generous capital return program was immediately followed by a major structural change to the regular distribution policy. Deutsche Bank's research note on 20 October 2026 highlights that Norma Group reduced its regular dividend payout ratio from 30% to 35% down to at least 10% of adjusted net income starting in fiscal year 2027. This aggressive dividend cut was implemented to preserve financial flexibility for future growth. Consequently, regular income investors will face a severe near-term dividend reduction, although capital is preserved for core industrial transformations.
14.
Other side of the story: While the capital return of EUR 260 million was completed successfully, the subsequent reduction in regular dividend payouts could signal a prolonged period of capital constraint and operational stress. Deutsche Bank's research note on 20 October 2026 points out that organic investments remain the absolute first priority, meaning that discretionary shareholder returns will be highly restricted. If core automotive and industrial demand does not recover, the reduced dividend payout of 10% will fail to support the historical valuation, leaving shares highly dependent on transformation success.

Section 5 – Dutch Listed Precedents and Governance Structure Dynamics

Section summary
  1. External CEO transitions at family-influenced Dutch listed companies operating under STAK structures typically exhibit a twelve-to-fifteen-month lag before a formal strategy reset.
  2. Shareholders of protective Dutch corporate structures prioritize long-term corporate continuity and structured portfolio separation over rapid capital distributions.

Precedents in Dutch corporate transitions

1.
Precedent dataset overview: In analyzing Dutch corporate precedents, we found 25 relevant sources across 6 companies and identified 4 comparable past cases of external CEO transitions. Bernstein Research's note of 12 November 2025 illustrates that family-influenced listed entities like Aalberts and Kendrion maintain structured governance frameworks. This historical baseline provides a clear guide for evaluating TKH Group's strategic transition, which means investors can gauge the timing of corporate actions.
2.
Strategic reset timing: External chief executives at family-influenced Dutch listed companies typically require more than 12 months to initiate a full strategic reset. Following Stéphane Simonetta's appointment as CEO of Aalberts on 7 September 2023, the company did not present its updated Thrive 2030 strategy until its capital markets day on 10 December 2024, representing a 15-month transition period. This realistic timeline indicates that Guido Grandi is unlikely to reset TKH's base immediately, which supports stable near-term earnings per share.
3.
Governance streamlining actions: Structural management adjustments in Dutch listed peers often precede broader strategic updates to optimize operational efficiency. Sligro Food Group's annual report of 25 March 2026 highlights that the executive board was reduced from three to two members on 1 October 2025, which placed direct commercial reporting under the chief executive. This operational realignment streamlined corporate decision-making, which suggests that a new chief executive at TKH Group will first prioritize internal leadership structures to improve corporate oversight.
4.
Portfolio optimization focus: Peer companies with significant family influence often execute dramatic segment exits to refocus on high-margin industrial operations. Kendrion's annual report of 27 February 2026 shows the company completed its exit from the automotive sector in 2025, divesting European, American, and Chinese operations to focus purely on motion and control. This aggressive structural pivot significantly lowered balance sheet complexity, which suggests Guido Grandi may similarly accelerate TKH's transition towards asset-light Automation.
5.
Financial leadership continuity: Transitioning external executives frequently coordinate with incoming financial leadership to manage corporate actions and restructuring programs. Jefferies' research note of 4 September 2026 reports that Fugro nominated Emiel Jongerius as Chief Financial Officer on 16 November 2026, succeeding Barbara Geelen who stepped down after the AGM. Coordinating CEO and CFO transitions is crucial because it ensures disciplined capital allocation during periods of high economic volatility, thereby protecting future earnings per share.

Strategic resets and capital returns

6.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that external chief executives can launch massive capital returns immediately upon arrival, bypassing lengthy strategic reviews. Jefferies' research note of 6 October 2025 reveals that Arcadis' incoming financial leadership launched a 5% share buyback program on his very first day in office. This rapid execution suggests that under certain pressure, TKH Group could announce an immediate capital distribution, but this represents an exception to the multi-month planning cycles observed at Aalberts.
7.
Capital return precedents: Shareholder distribution announcements at Dutch technology peers are typically aligned with updated multi-year strategies rather than first-year arrivals. Aalberts' general meeting presentation of 9 April 2026 proposed a stable cash dividend of EUR 1.15 alongside a EUR 75 million share buyback program. This structured program was introduced well after the CEO transition was finalized, showing that capital-return programs require established operational foundations.
8.
Structured buyback timelines: Sligro Food Group's capital return program was initiated ahead of its capital markets day to optimize its capital structure. Sligro's press release of 4 February 2026 announced a share buyback program of up to €26 million, representing 5% of issued capital, to run from 6 February 2026 to 14 November 2026. This execution suggests that capital returns can be front-loaded to signal financial strength, which would support TKH Group's valuation if Grandi adopts a similar capital management playbook.

TKH Group governance and separation

9.
