EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Key Question asks how plausible is the change now expected for Nexans, a French-listed cable maker that completed its exit from non-electrification businesses in July 2026, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire. Nexans's earnings per share are highly sensitive to the execution of its 2028 Capital Markets Day targets, with Deutsche Bank noting on October 26, 2026, that the company compiled consensus expects adjusted EBITDA of €805 million for 2026.

Jefferies forecasts that Nexans's adjusted EBITDA will expand to €1,088 million by 2028, representing a substantial increase from the reported 2025 adjusted EBITDA of €729 million. The evidence gathered across this report suggests that Nexans's expected 2027 and 2028 earnings rest predominantly on demonstrated operational strengths, though execution risks remain material in specific segments where the company has limited historical precedent.

The comprehensive analysis reveals that Nexans possesses proven capabilities in four critical areas: the SHIFT selectivity programme that delivered approximately €300 million of structural EBITDA from 2018 to 2024, the ontime and on-budget delivery of the Electra cable-laying vessel in Q2 2026, the successful qualification and production ramp-up of 525 kV extruded subsea cable technology for TenneT's LanWin2 project in October 2026, and the on-schedule extraction of synergies from the La Triveneta Cavi acquisition.

These accomplishments demonstrate that the company's new leadership team, despite being completely refreshed across all senior roles between October 2025 and November 2026, is successfully executing on inherited targets through demonstrated corporate strengths rather than unproven capabilities. However, the evidence also identifies three material execution gaps that could prevent Nexans from achieving consensus earnings expectations.

First, Nexans's mass-impregnated production line, which represents approximately 20 percent of Transmission revenues, faces utilization risks after mid-2028, with Deutsche Bank reporting on October 26, 2026, that while Nexans is preferred bidder for a Mediterranean replacement project to fill capacity freed by the postponed Great Sea Interconnector, failure to sign final agreements would recreate under-utilization risks. Second, Nexans starts with a very limited operational scale in the highly consolidated United States low-voltage cable market, with Bernstein Research reporting on April 28, 2026, that Republic Wire commands only a 4 percent market share in the United States building wire segment, while dominant integrated incumbents like Southwire and Prysmian collectively control over 60 percent of the market.

Third, European residential construction markets present a highly fragmented picture that threatens the speed of the low-voltage recovery, with Bernstein Research's report of 22 October 2026 highlighting that the Nordic residential region remains exceptionally weak in terms of volume demand, whereas southern European countries like France and Italy show early signs of stabilization. The most important evidence supporting the plausibility of Nexans achieving its 2028 targets includes the following findings.

PWR-Transmission achieved a solid adjusted EBITDA margin of 13.7 percent in the first half of 2026, representing a 21.2 percent year-on-year increase in adjusted EBITDA to €106.5 million despite flat organic sales growth, with management expecting that execution of the remaining €7.8 billion high-quality backlog will drive margins toward the 17 percent target by 2028. Prysmian's reported results show a much higher profitability benchmark, with its Transmission EBITDA margin reaching a stellar 21.2 percent in the second quarter of 2026, representing a substantial increase of 4.1 percentage points compared to the 17.1 percent recorded in the prior year's quarter.

This peer precedent confirms that Nexans's 17 percent Transmission margin target is within historically demonstrated sector ranges.

PWR-Grid reported remarkable 14.8 percent organic growth in the third quarter of 2026, significantly exceeding the company's mid-single-digit guidelines due to seasonal strength and progressive capacity additions, indicating that the company is successfully converting robust underlying demand into solid revenue. Nexans plans to expand its total European grid manufacturing capacity by approximately 40 percent between 2025 and 2028, introduced to support a European market where utility demand consistently outstrips available industrial supply, allowing Nexans to secure larger long-term frame agreements with major distribution system operators.

Grid equipment manufacturers continue to benefit from exceptionally strong pricing power due to persistent supply deficits, with Morgan Stanley reporting that medium-voltage switchgear and transformer prices stabilized at 80 to 90 percent above 2020 levels, supporting strong margin retention during Nexans's capacity ramp-up phase.

- Earnings Calls Nexans completed the acquisition of Republic Wire on June 1, 2026, for an enterprise value of approximately €680 million, with the company expecting to generate c. €23 million in annual run-rate synergies within three years of closing, essential for reducing the implied transaction multiple from 10.3x Adjusted EBITDA before synergies to 7.6x post-synergies. Nexans has a successful track record of executing margin-accretive integrations through its proprietary SHIFT operational program, with management stating in the fourth-quarter 2023 earnings call that Centelsa delivered 20 percent run-rate synergies while Reka Cables achieved 50 percent run-rate synergies, suggesting that Nexans's structured integration methodology will likely deliver targeted savings ahead of schedule.

Prysmian's integration of Encore Wire serves as a major industry benchmark, with Morgan Stanley reporting on July 16, 2024, that Prysmian's €140 million synergy target represents an ambitious 5.9 percent of Encore's acquired sales, while Nexans's synergy target of €23.6 million is highly realistic and conservative because it represents only 4.5 percent of Republic Wire's €520 million revenue base. Nexans reported a significant decline in its cash conversion ratio to 42.7 percent in the first half of 2026 compared to the prior year, though company reports show the actual adjusted EBITDA conversion was an outstanding 64 percent in H1 2025, with this sharp volatility occurring because the previous period was highly elevated by temporary working capital benefits, making management of these cyclical fluctuations critical for meeting medium-term earnings per share targets.

Nexans has committed to achieving an annual free cash flow conversion rate of over 45 percent of adjusted EBITDA by 2028, embedded as a core performance condition for executive share awards carrying a 20 percent weighting, implying generation of over €517 million in annual free cash flow if the company delivers its targeted €1,150 million of adjusted EBITDA, which will significantly accelerate net debt reduction and fund future complementary acquisitions.

The conflicts between sections center primarily on the feasibility of maintaining elevated margins during simultaneous capacity expansions across multiple segments. Peers have historically experienced substantial profitability headwinds during the initial phases of ramping up new distribution capacity, with NKT reporting an impressive 20.1 percent Transmission EBITDA margin in the second quarter of 2026 despite facing a significant 200 basis point headwind from Sweden and Germany, highlighting that initial capacity utilization carries material execution and cost integration risks.

This historical peer precedent conflicts with the assumption embedded in consensus models that Nexans can simultaneously expand PWR-Grid capacity by 40 percent while maintaining or improving segment margins. While Nexans's 40 percent European capacity expansion targets a structurally undersupplied distribution market, a prolonged regional construction slump could severely limit volume uptake, with Jefferies Research noting that persistent construction weakness and higher interest rates could delay any potential demand recovery within European building wire, meaning Nexans's aggressive capital expenditures may result in margin-diluting under-utilization if distribution grid investments flatten across Europe.

Another significant conflict emerges between the projected recovery of PWR-Connect margins and the deteriorating European residential construction backdrop. Nexans is aiming to recover its PWR-Connect adjusted EBITDA margin from 11.8 percent in the first half of 2026 back towards historical heights, with Deutsche Bank's note of 31 July 2026 reporting that management expects sequential margin improvement in the second half of 2026 to push margins progressively above 12 percent, relying on pricing actions and asset optimization, which means that operating margins can expand sequentially even if the European residential building sector remains stagnant.

However, while Nexans's pivot to data centers and structural cost programs supports the Connect segment, a prolonged European construction slump represents a persistent margin headwind, with Jefferies Research's note of 31 August 2026 arguing that Nordics weakness will likely delay any real low-voltage margin rebound, and if residential construction remains depressed for a prolonged period, under-utilization in European factories will dilute the positive margin impact of the Republic Wire synergies and data center capacity additions.

The leadership transition risk presents an unusual dynamic where a completely refreshed executive team must execute aggressive targets set by predecessors. Nexans initiated a profound governance transition when its Board of Directors appointed Julien Hueber as Chief Executive Officer on October 13, 2025, designed to create new operational momentum after parting ways with former chief executive Christopher Guérin, with Julien Hueber bringing over two decades of in-house operational experience, having previously managed the highly successful European Grid and Connect business lines, helping stabilize corporate governance during a critical period of strategic acceleration. Broker feedback indicates that the complete boardroom transition was received with initial caution but quick reassurance, with Deutsche Bank Research noting on October 14, 2025, that the sudden departure of the previous chief executive initially triggered a temporary sell-off, though subsequent management calls successfully reassured the market that the group's core strategy remains completely unchanged, maintaining strong investor confidence in the stock despite the rapid executive changes.

Critically, the new team has already demonstrated execution capability through the on-time Electra delivery, the 525 kV technology qualification, and the on-track La Triveneta Cavi integration. Looking forward, the greatest uncertainty surrounds the mass-impregnated line replacement order and the Republic Wire competitive positioning. Nexans has yet to announce the formal signing of the critical Mediterranean mass-impregnated subsea cable replacement contract, with Bernstein Research reporting on October 22, 2026, that although Nexans was named preferred bidder, the final contract remains unsigned, leaving the Halden plant's MI line utilization uncertain after mid-2028, requiring waiting for the formal award announcement, which is expected by late 2026, or monitoring subsequent regulatory filings because underutilization would directly dilute transmission earnings per share.

Bernstein Research argued on October 22, 2026, that the scope contribution from the Republic Wire acquisition has started softer than the market anticipated, with the broker noting that consensus modeled a 28 percent scope impact in the Connect segment for the third quarter of 2026, but the actual results missed by 3 percentage points, suggesting that Republic Wire may struggle to capitalize fully on data center tailwinds due to intense competitive pressure from integrated incumbents.

The net assessment is that Nexans's 2027 and 2028 earnings targets rest primarily on demonstrated strengths including the proven SHIFT integration methodology, the successful delivery of complex capital projects like the Electra vessel, the technological qualification of advanced 525 kV extruded subsea cables, and the structural margin expansion delivered through operational selectivity programmes from 2018 to 2024.

The company has shown it can execute on inherited strategic plans through operational and industrial excellence rather than requiring acquisition of fundamentally new capabilities. However, three material execution risks could prevent achievement of consensus EBITDA expectations: the unsigned Mediterranean mass-impregnated replacement order creating potential capacity under-utilization after mid-2028, the limited 4 percent starting market share position in US building wire requiring successful disruption of dominant incumbents, and the prolonged Nordic residential construction downturn threatening PWR-Connect margin recovery despite data center demand offsets.

The overall confidence level is high for near-term 2027 targets but medium for full 2028 achievement given the backend-loaded nature of capacity ramp-ups and integration synergies.
