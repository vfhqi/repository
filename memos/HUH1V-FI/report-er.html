EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Huhtamaki's H1 2026 and Q2 2026 results reveal a company navigating a profound operational and strategic transition under new CEO Ralf Wunderlich, delivering a mixed financial performance that significantly beat consensus expectations on adjusted EBIT while grappling with severe currency headwinds, persistent North American operational challenges, and weakening free cash flow generation. The quarter marks a critical inflection point as management pivots from volume-focused expansion to disciplined capital allocation, decentralized segment empowerment, and accelerated sustainable packaging substitution amid intensifying regulatory tailwinds and volatile macroeconomic conditions.

The company reported Q2 2026 adjusted EBIT of EUR 103.70 million, representing a robust 11.27% beat against Bloomberg consensus expectations of EUR 93.20 million, primarily driven by a major operational outperformance in Flexible Packaging. However, reported net sales declined 3% to EUR 1,955.80 million for H1 2026 as favorable comparable growth of 1% was entirely offset by a massive EUR -77.80 million currency translation headwind, predominantly from a 7% average depreciation of the USD against the EUR. This currency drag represents a severe deterioration from prior periods and significantly dilutes the company's underlying operational momentum, with unfavorable foreign exchange movements creating an EBIT headwind of EUR -6.40 million during H1 2026. H1 2026 print exposes a highly polarized segment performance dynamic that fundamentally reshapes the near-term investment thesis.

Flexible Packaging emerged as the key driver of the Q2 beat, delivering EBIT of EUR 37.40 million against consensus of EUR 26.40 million, supported by volume growth and active polymer cost pass-through, while Fiber Packaging sustained strong organic growth with Q2 comparable net sales increasing by 7%, driven by robust underlying demand for egg and fruit packaging across Europe. In stark contrast, North America reported EBIT of EUR 33.30 million, missing consensus expectations of EUR 37.10 million by 10.24% due to operational plant challenges, with comparable net sales falling by 8% in Q2 2026 attributed to operational problems and plant ramp-up issues.

Similarly, Foodservice comparable net sales declined by 1% in Q2 2026, representing a moderate sequential improvement from the 8% decline in Q1 2026, as sluggish volumes from local customers and Middle East shipping disruptions continued to depress segment performance.

The most significant deviation from pre-earnings expectations centers on the company's dramatic free cash flow contraction and persistent North American execution failures. Free cash flow for H1 2026 declined to EUR 33.90 million compared to EUR 63.10 million in H1 2025, driven by lower reported H1 EBITDA of EUR 275 million and working capital pressure from polymer price inflation. This cash conversion bottleneck directly contradicts management's narrative of capital discipline and margin resilience, with reported EBIT of EUR 74.20 million impacted by EUR -29.50 million in restructuring charges in Q2 2026, including an EUR 16 million impairment in the Foodservice segment related to footprint optimization actions.

The magnitude of ongoing North American plant inefficiencies proves particularly concerning, as adjusted EBIT of EUR 33.30 million fell sharply from EUR 44.70 million in Q2 2025, exposing the inadequacy of self-help task forces deployed to address these local market competition and operational bottlenecks. 9 a strategic perspective, the H1 2026 period marks the full operationalization of CEO Ralf Wunderlich's sweeping leadership overhaul and organizational restructuring agenda.

Wunderlich, who formally took office in January 2025, brings wealth of international packaging experience from Amcor and TC Transcontinental and is refocusing the group's playbook on driving organic growth, customer intimacy, and executing smaller bolt-on acquisitions. The company has implemented a decentralized operating model by decentralizing commercial and financial accountability directly to business segments, ensuring that segment-level leaders remain closer to their local customers to drive customer intimacy and win market share, while simultaneously establishing a centralized global procurement organization on April 1, 2025 under EVP Changsheng Wu to maximize purchasing scale, as prior procurement activities were managed separately at the individual segment level.

This dual structural realignment aims to balance segment-level commercial agility with highly centralized procurement efficiencies, though execution friction remains a risk if purchasing mandates clash with local segment cost-recovery needs.

Management has also embedded sustainability directly into executive compensation and strategic priorities, with greenhouse gas emission reduction and safety metrics comprising 10% of the total weight for the CEO's variable earning opportunity under the 2026 short-term incentive framework, while the company committed to reducing absolute Scope 1 and Scope 2 emissions by 50.44% by 2030 compared to its 2022 baseline under a strict 1.5°C trajectory validated by the Science Based Targets initiative, alongside a 25% absolute reduction in Scope 3 emissions across its supply chain.

Capital expenditure discipline represents another pillar of the new strategic playbook, with management slashing 2025 capex by 31% year-on-year to EUR 172 million, enabling net debt to adjusted EBITDA to remain comfortable at 2x at the end of Q2 2026, well within the target range of 2x to 3x.

sell-side response to the H1 2026 results reveals cautious optimism anchored in valuation support rather than near-term operational conviction. DNB Carnegie's analyst Robin Santavirta reiterated a Buy rating and raised the target price to €38 from €37, driven by higher earnings estimates in the Flexible Packaging and Fiber segments, with adjusted EPS estimates for 2026-2028e raised by 1% to 5%, sitting 3% to 5% above the Modular Finance consensus expectations.

In contrast, Deutsche Bank's analyst Kevin Fogarty maintained a Hold rating and kept the target price unchanged at €32, reflecting short-term risks from raw material and energy cost inflation despite the solid Q2 beat, while Jefferies Research analyst Cole Hathorn reiterated a Hold rating and kept the target price unchanged at €31, noting that while the valuation remains undemanding at 11x PE, the shares lack near-term catalysts without volume recovery. The divergence in analyst sentiment underscores the fundamental tension between Huhtamaki's attractive valuation discount to peers and its uncertain operational recovery trajectory, with the company's 2026-2027 forward multiples trading at a 12% to 20% discount to the peer average of listed global packaging converters. Macroeconomic headwinds and supply chain disruptions present severe near-term margin threats that could undermine consensus earnings expectations. Middle East geopolitical escalations drove steep polymer cost increases due to rising oil prices, with polymer-based plastics comprising over 80% of raw materials in the Flexible Packaging segment, while average European natural gas import prices of $12.37/MMBtu were approximately four times higher than the US benchmark of $2.51/MMBtu, with high energy and electricity prices in Europe continuing to act as a primary obstacle to the competitive position of European converters.

These input cost pressures are compounded by hyperinflationary devaluations in emerging markets, with the Turkish Lira suffering a 27% average depreciation against the Euro and the Indian Rupee falling by 16% on an average rate basis. Management's ability to successfully pass through these inflated raw material costs remains uncertain, particularly given that consumers are not willing to pay green premiums for sustainable packaging according to a former strategy manager at Huhtamaki, which restricts commercial pricing power. a competitive dynamics perspective, footprint consolidation and volume recoveries among global packaging peers intensify pressure on Huhtamaki's pricing power.

Primary competitor Amcor reported a positive volume inflection of 0.5% for its legacy and Berry businesses in the June 2026 quarter, with its Global Flexible Packaging segment expanding its adjusted EBIT margin to 15.1% compared to 14.5% in the prior year period, driven by the realization of 285 million dollars in annual integration synergies, while Silgan Holdings achieved a 90 basis point segment margin expansion to 16.4% in its Custom Containers segment, driven entirely by defensive footprint rationalization and the deliberate exit of lower-margin contracts. This peer outperformance suggests that Huhtamaki's operational challenges in North America and delayed polymer passthrough in Flexibles could place the company at a structural competitive disadvantage unless self-help turnaround initiatives accelerate materially in H2 2026.

Regulatory and technological disruption forces present a long-term structural opportunity offset by near-term adoption bottlenecks. The regulatory landscape in North America includes approximately 225 pieces of packaging legislation debated or passed across 30 states, with restrictions on foam-based packaging in seventeen US states providing significant near-term growth opportunities for Huhtamaki's fiber alternatives, exemplified by the completion of a USD 100 million molded fiber egg carton investment in Hammond, Indiana, positioning the North America segment to capture structural demand from state-level bans on polystyrene. However, the pace of sustainable packaging substitution remains constrained by significant economic and technical barriers, as shifting from conventional plastic packaging to sustainable paper-based alternatives is highly cost-inflationary, with paper solutions costing up to five times more than legacy polymers depending on the specific application, while long regulatory food-contact trials and shelf-life testing mean that migrating the division's total sales will take years, with Blueloop representing only about 30% of segment sales. The core investment thesis has fundamentally shifted from a volume-recovery story to a capital-discipline and margin-defense narrative. Prior to H1 2026, the market focused on Huhtamaki's ability to capture volume-driven operating leverage as consumer demand recovered from post-pandemic destocking cycles and inflationary consumer spending headwinds. The H1 2026 results demonstrate that while Flexible Packaging has successfully turned around and Fiber Packaging maintains strong structural momentum, persistent North American operational inefficiencies and Foodservice volume weakness prevent the group from demonstrating full earnings leverage across its optimized asset base.

The dramatic free cash flow contraction and ongoing restructuring charges highlight that management's self-help turnaround initiatives remain incomplete, with significant cash consumption required to stabilize underperforming facilities before the company can sustainably scale earnings. The most important element that changed in this quarter is the revelation that North American operational challenges are far more severe and structurally entrenched than previously disclosed, with the segment's 8% comparable sales decline and 25% EBIT contraction exposing fundamental execution failures that cannot be resolved through incremental self-help task forces alone.

This operational deterioration directly contradicts management's prior guidance and suggests that the Hammond capacity expansion and Paris facility investments may be underutilized for an extended period, delaying the payback on over USD 130 million in recent capital expenditures and undermining the company's return on investment targets of 13% to 15%. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bearish North American view argues that the Flexible Packaging turnaround and Fiber volume momentum demonstrate management's ability to execute operational fixes, suggesting that North American challenges represent a temporary ramp-up issue rather than a structural competitive disadvantage.

Bulls led by DNB Carnegie contend that the company's attractive valuation discount of 12% to 20% to global peers is unjustified given its defensive consumer-facing end markets and validated sustainability leadership, with 2027 earnings recovery driven by gradual volume improvements in European and emerging markets capable of re-rating the equity once North American plants stabilize. This optimistic scenario assumes that self-help initiatives deployed in mid-2026 will deliver visible sequential improvements in Q3 and Q4 2026, enabling management to restore North American EBIT margins to historical levels above 12% by H1 2027. The exact quantitative triggers that would change the investment view in future reporting periods include the following observable metrics: First, North America comparable sales growth must inflect positive to at least

3% by Q4 2026 to confirm that plant ramp-up issues are resolving rather than reflecting structural market share losses to competitors. Second, group free cash flow must recover to at least EUR 150 million for full-year 2026 to demonstrate that working capital pressures and restructuring cash costs are normalizing, validating management's capital discipline narrative. Third, Flexible Packaging adjusted EBIT margins must sustain above 9.5% for two consecutive quarters to prove that polymer cost pass-through mechanisms are functioning effectively and the segment turnaround is durable rather than a one-quarter pricing anomaly.

Fourth, management must provide explicit quantitative guidance on the expected EBIT contribution from the Hammond and Paris capacity expansions by Q1 2027 to enable investors to validate the return profile of recent capital investments. Fifth, emerging market currency translation headwinds must moderate to below EUR 30 million annually to prevent macroeconomic volatility from overwhelming operational improvements. Failure to meet these quantitative milestones would confirm that Huhtamaki's operational turnaround is stalling and that consensus 2027 EPS estimates of 11% growth are too optimistic, warranting further target price cuts and potential rating downgrades across the sell-side coverage universe.
