Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Geopolitical threats from the proposed MATCH Act could disrupt ASML's sales by restricting shipments and servicing to key Chinese entities.

Geopolitical legislative threats to lithography sales

1.
Key question: "For ASML Holding N.V. (ASML-NL), what share of its China revenue and of its China installed- base servicing comes from the entities that the proposed MATCH Act would designate (covered affiliates of SMIC, Hua Hong, Huawei, CXMT and YMTC) as against other Chinese customers, and, if shipments and servicing to those entities were banned while ASML's immersion capacity is short of global demand, how quickly could ASML re-allocate the affected immersion systems to customers in Taiwan, Korea, the United States, Japan and Europe?"
2.
Geopolitical exposure remains a serious concern for ASML as the bipartisan MATCH Act, introduced in April 2026, aims to ban DUV immersion sales and servicing to China. The draft legislation specifically targets chokepoint lithography tools where no local Chinese alternative exists, potentially restricting Dutch exports. Consequently, if the bill passes, ASML could face a material loss of tool sales and service revenue. These restrictions directly threaten the medium-term earnings per share outlook over the next 3 years unless global demand elsewhere compensates.
3.
12 13 Allied export harmonization represents the primary mechanism of the MATCH Act, which mandates a 150-day window for allies to align their export controls. In response, Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten actively engaged with US lawmakers in September 2026 to seek workable exceptions. If diplomatic efforts fail to narrow the servicing ban, ASML will be blocked from maintaining its installed base. This outcome would significantly dilute the service gross margins, which otherwise benefit from strong installed base management growth globally.

Dominance of Dutch lithography systems

4.
Technological monopoly underpins ASML's strategic position because the company is the sole provider of advanced EUV lithography systems. Former employees note that the company maintains a DUV market share exceeding 90%, leaving Nikon and Canon far behind. This extreme concentration means global chipmakers like TSMC and Intel are completely reliant on Dutch machinery to scale their advanced logic. Consequently, any disruption to ASML's shipment licenses directly influences global wafer capacity additions and limits the broader semiconductor capital expenditure cycle.

Strategic importance of Chinese logic and memory

5.
Chinese memory demand has been a massive driver of lithography bookings as local manufacturers expand capacity for self-sufficiency. Huatai Research reported in September 2026 that ChangXin Memory Technologies aims to reach 300k wafers per month of DRAM capacity, representing 13% of global capacity. Similarly, Yangtze Memory Technologies is expanding NAND capacity to bypass foreign restrictions. If the MATCH Act bans DUV shipments, ASML's sales would suffer because China has been stockpiling critical lithography tools for years.
6.
logic manufacturing in China continues to expand despite lithography export controls, driving demand for ASML's mature immersion tools. Bernstein Research reported in September 2026 that Huawei's Kirin 9050 Pro chip demonstrated that Chinese firms can utilize vertical LogicFolding and DUV multi-patterning. This architectural workaround doubles SMIC's logic wafer consumption, fueling an extended DUV immersion investment cycle. However, Chinese foundries rely completely on continuous ASML maintenance and software support to keep these complex multi-patterning lines running.
7.
Other side of the story: The impact of a MATCH Act ban could be far less damaging to ASML's financial results than feared because global demand remains extremely robust. Stated company targets plan for a 30% increase in DUV immersion capacity in 2027, yet supply will still fail to meet global demand through 2028. Jefferies reported in October 2026 that strong AI-driven capex at TSMC and Samsung would easily absorb any reallocated immersion systems. This means ASML can quickly pivot its shipment capacity to non-banned regions, preserving its long-term earnings per share growth.
