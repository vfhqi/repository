Section summary
  1. The Key Question can be answered in direction but not yet in full magnitude. Helios Towers' FY2025 results, with revenue up 7.8% to $854.1 million against a 1.3% rise in cost of sales to $414.2 million, show real site-level fixed-cost operating leverage. The scope for cost reduction sits mainly in discretionary capex and corporate overhead rather than in site operating costs. Overall confidence in the evidence is medium. - Earnings Calls Leverage is visible in the cost splits. Power costs fell from 24.6% of revenue in FY2023 to 21.7% in FY2025. Nonpower site costs fell to 11% of revenue from 12.2%. Together these support the view that the established site platform is operating as designed.

The tenancy rule of thumb holds up as a guide. Management's rule is that every 500 additional tenancies contribute about $5 million of in-year EBITDA. Those additions are said to yield over $10 million of annualized EBITDA in subsequent years. Contract-level detail is not disclosed, so the rule remains a management claim rather than a verified figure. - Earnings Calls Overheads are the main leak in the leverage story.

Administrative expenses rose 14.4% to $155.1 million in FY2025. They also rose to 18.1% of revenue from 17.1%. Corporate cost is therefore not fully fixed, and its growth can offset site-level gains. Consensus broker assumptions are constructive but conditional.

Broker models indicate that a 9% EBITDA CAGR can compound into a 13% recurring levered free cash flow CAGR because financing and site costs remain largely fixed. They also assume the company is transitioning into a capital distribution phase after its major build-out cycles. Support comes from the roughly 70% of Adjusted EBITDA denominated in or pegged to hard currency. It also comes from customer contracts with automatic escalators linked to fuel and inflation.

Helios's average time to add a second tenant has shortened to 2.5 years from five in 2020.

Year-to-date additions of 3,435 tenancies included 942 new build-to-suit sites, a 27.4% share against the 25% long-run target. In Q3 alone, new builds were 45.2% of additions versus 16.6% in FY2025. Inwit owned about 18.9% of the land under its towers by September 2026. Helios's own land buyout yields are rarely reported.

Capex and overhead flexibility is the strongest cost-reduction argument, with caveats. Management says discretionary growth spending can theoretically be switched off at short notice. Section summary
  1. Understanding the operating leverage of the passive tower business model is essential for evaluating medium-term earnings growth.
  2. High fixed-cost exposure and power costs make these companies sensitive to regional macroeconomic shocks and operator capital spending cycles.
