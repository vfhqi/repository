Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. The key question regarding KONE Oyj is partially resolvable from current evidence given the structural regulatory drivers of European modernization, although antitrust approval timelines create mediumterm earnings trajectory uncertainty.

Defining the investment thesis

1.
Defining key question: The deep-dive question is whether the European lift modernization wave and the consolidation from four to three Western makers via the KONE-TKE deal can predictably accelerate KONE’s EPS trajectory.
2.
Aligning investor intent: The analytical objective is to determine if this modernization wave can support a predictable three-year EPS trajectory with twenty-five percent potential annual investment returns.
3.
Assessing adjacent questions: Sequential critical gates include the exact timing of European Commission merger clearance and the realization of seven hundred million Euros in targeted run-rate cost synergies.
4.
Contextualizing business stage: Standalone KONE is transitioning away from the structurally depressed Chinese new equipment market toward high-margin European modernization and service-led revenues.
5.
Tracking industry consolidation: The planned combination with TK Elevator aims to leapfrog competitors by managing a maintenance base of over three million units globally.

Evaluating regulatory triggers: Implementation of the Energy Performance of Buildings Directive serves as a critical driver accelerating existing building refurbishment ratios across Europe.

Section 2 – Market structure and consolidation dynamics

Section summary
  1. The consolidation of KONE and TK Elevator into a dominant three-player Western oligopoly will reshape global service density and pricing power, despite protracted antitrust reviews and mandatory asset divestments.
  2. Realizing the projected seven hundred million Euros in cost synergies hinges on successful manufacturing integration and service route rationalization across highly overlapping European and North American metropolitan markets.

Transaction architecture and governance

1.
Transformative combination: KONE and TK Elevator announced a definitive agreement to merge their global businesses in a cash-and-share transaction representing a total enterprise value of twenty-nine point four billion Euros, which is set to create the world's largest elevator manufacturer by surpassing historical industry leaders Otis and Schindler.
2.
Balanced financing: The cash portion of five billion Euros will be funded through fully committed bank facilities while the equity portion is structured via the issuance of a maximum of two hundred and seventy million newly issued KONE class B shares representing thirty-three point eight percent of post-merger outstanding shares.
3.
Sustained control: Continuity of strategic direction is preserved because Antti Herlin will retain more than fifty percent of the voting rights in the combined entity and has committed to purchase one billion Euros of KONE class B shares from the sellers at completion.
4.
Structured representation: Under the share purchase agreement, private equity owners Advent and Cinven retain the right to nominate two board members as long as their ownership remains above fifteen percent, with representation reducing to one seat and eventually terminating if holding levels fall below ten percent.

Ambitious synergies: Bernstein Research highlights that the total transaction value creation target reaches nine hundred million Euros, combining seven hundred million Euros in pre-tax cost savings with two hundred million Euros in financing synergies

Section 3 – Regulatory landscape and ageing stock

Section summary
  1. Spain's newly implemented Royal Decree 355/2024 and European energy directives are unlocking a multi-billion Euro modernization opportunity across Europe's highly dense, decades-old elevator infrastructure.
  2. China's subsidized equipment renewal program is driving high-volume but lower-margin public upgrades, contrasting sharply with the highly profitable, safety-driven European private residential market.

European safety mandates and Spanish ITC AEM 1

1.
Tightening safety: Spain implemented its stringent ITC AEM 1 safety regulation on July 1, 2024, under Royal Decree 355/2024 to enforce strict, mandatory safety upgrades across its massive elevator fleet.
2.
Enormous market: This new Spanish legislation is estimated by local authorities to affect up to forty percent of Spain's one million elevators, creating a seven hundred million Euro modernization pipeline over the next seven years.
3.
Mandatory compliance: Public administrative tenders in Madrid are already accelerating to meet these regulatory standards, funding extensive mechanical remedies and safety adaptations for state-owned facilities.
4.
Fragmented legislation: Unlike the unified Spanish decree, Europe lacks standard regional maintenance legislation, with Western countries requiring five to ten technician visits annually compared to biweekly visits in China.
5.
Longer lifetimes: European elevators are heavily tilted toward low-rise buildings, meaning lower utilization rates and longer operational lifetimes of thirty years before requiring full modernization.

European ageing stock and energy directives

6.
Severe obsolescence: Europe represents a highly mature market where more than half of the seven million installed elevators are older than twenty years old and ripe for systemic upgrades.
7.
Exceptional density: Europe possesses a highly concentrated infrastructure with twelve elevator units per inhabitant, a ratio significantly higher than three in the United States and nine in Japan.
8.
Energy targets: The European Union Energy Performance of Buildings Directive mandates that existing buildings gradually upgrade their energy performance, driving substantial demand for energy-efficient elevators.
9.
Incremental benefits: Building owners are prioritizing elevator upgrades as part of broader green renovations, as elevator energy management accounts for a growing four point five percent share of building renovation spend.
10.
German aging: Strong demand for replacements and partial modernizations in Germany is driven by high failure rates in rapidly aging residential building stocks.

China's subsidized renewal program

11.
Policy push: China launched an equipment renewal program in July 2024 worth three hundred billion Renminbi, which actively subsidizes large-scale elevator modernization and replacements.
12.
Town expansion: In 2026, China expanded the subsidy coverage to prioritize elevator installations in old residential towns, elderly care institutions, and fire and rescue facilities.
13.
Divergent mindsets: Unlike European customers who prefer cost-effective life extensions, Chinese building owners traditionally focus on expensive complete elevator replacements rather than partial modernization.
14.
Smart integration: Chinese technology firm Taoping is leveraging this policy shift by integrating real-time IoT monitoring with smart elevator insurance pilots to reduce operational costs by twenty percent.
15.
Margin pressure: Despite the high volume of China's twelve million units, Chinese modernization margins are closely following declining installation margins due to severe local competition.

Other side of the story

16.
Other side of the story: If national governments fail to strictly enforce safety penalties or if residential funding dries up, building owners will postpone expensive safety upgrades as they did during historical regulatory transitions.

Section 4 – Modernisation financial mechanics and pricing power

Section summary
  1. Elevators are transitioning from low-margin new installations under five percent to higher-margin modernization and service contracts that expand valuation multiples.
  2. Upward contract repricing is secured through CPI-linked inflation indexing and labor scarcity, extending the pricing window for up to twenty years.

Modernisation revenue and margin transmission

1.
Superior margins: Modernization activities yield operating margins of ten to twenty percent at minimum compared to historically low-single-digit margins for Western new installations.
2.
Equipment comparisons: Industry new installation margins typically hover around five percent or below globally as manufacturers treat initial elevator placements as loss leaders.
3.
KONE profitability: Standalone KONE has successfully scaled its modernization margins to align with its group average of approximately twelve percent.
4.
Otis margin targets: Otis targets modernization margins of ten percent in the medium term as it leverages industrialized manufacturing scale.
5.
Schindler dilutive mix: Schindler accepts modernization margins below its overall group average to strategically win market share and pull units into its maintenance portfolio.
6.
Multiple expansion: The transition of the business mix toward modernization supports higher valuation multiples, with KONE's discounted cash flow model assuming a terminal exit multiple of eighteen point five times.

Quarterly order growth trajectories

7.
KONE quarterly growth: KONE reported modernization order growth exceeding fifteen percent in Q2 twenty-six at comparable exchange rates, confirming its leading position in European building updates.
8.
Otis order acceleration: Otis recorded modernization order growth of nine percent in Q2 twenty-six , exiting the quarter with a massive constant-currency backlog expansion of twenty-six percent.
9.
Otis Q1 momentum: During the first quarter of twenty-six, Otis achieved modernization order growth of eleven percent at constant currency, driven by robust replacement demand.
10.
Schindler H1 acceleration: Schindler's modernization orders grew by twelve point eight percent in local currency during the first half of twenty-six, supported by EMEA market share gains.
11.
Schindler Q1 momentum: Schindler posted strong modernization order growth of fifteen percent in local currency during the first quarter of twenty-six as its standardized upgrade packages gained commercial traction.

Contract structures and pricing durability

12.
Inflation indexing: Service and modernization margins are hedged through escalator clauses directly linked to consumer price indices, ensuring that rising labor costs are passed on.
13.
Annual pricing gains: Elevator service pricing has risen six to eight percent annually to offset material and wage inflation, despite resistance from commercial property buyers.
14.
Micro-pricing execution: Otis is rolling out twenty million dollars in targeted micro-pricing initiatives to manage customer retention and optimize regional margins without triggering churn.
15.
Prolonged repricing window: The pricing window is expected to remain open for twenty years because the vast pool of aging global lifts exceeds immediate technician installation capacity.
16.
Other side of the story: If macroeconomic uncertainty leads building owners to postpone discretionary modernization upgrades, the high-margin backlog execution will slow down and expose manufacturers to fixed-cost under-absorption.
