Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Assessing fixed-cost leverage and capacity dynamics is critical to forecasting Hexatronic's medium- term EPS trajectory.

Defining the core inquiry

1.
Defining the inquiry: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"

Financial impact of leverage and costs

2.
Significant operating leverage: High fixed costs in polymer extrusion and cable assembly mean that small volume changes have outsized effects on operating income and earnings per share. During the 2023-25 destocking phase, lower capacity utilization led to severe margin contraction as fixed plant costs remained unabsorbed. Conversely, a recovery in US FTTH volume in 2026 demonstrates how structural cost-sharing drives rapid profitability improvements as plant utilization increases. 18 y (Q ) g q, Hexatronic historical margins and operating expense ratios showing the 2023-25 contraction and 2026 recovery.

Volume sensitivity precedents: Historical cycles in the fiber sector illustrate how quickly operating leverage can transform a company's profitability. Section 2 – Cost Structures in Polymer Extrusion, Assembly, and Installation

Section summary
  1. Material costs represent the largest expense in polymer extrusion, while installation services are heavily dependent on variable subcontractor labor.
  2. High raw material exposure in duct manufacturing introduces price lag risks, but service segments offer flexible variable cost structures.
  3. Peer comparisons show that physical manufacturing requires high capacity utilization to avoid margin drag, whereas services scale with minimal capital.

Polymer extrusion plant cost economics

1.
Dominant material exposure: Duct and microduct manufacturing plants carry highly variable cost structures because polymer resins constitute the vast majority of total production expenses. Jefferies Research reported on 28 September 2020 that raw materials account for 60% to 65% of sales and 75% to 80% of total costs in plastic pipe extrusion plants. Direct labor represents only about 5% of sales, while power and fuel costs consume another 4% of revenues. This means that operating leverage is primarily driven by polymer purchase prices rather than fixed labor, which protects margins when resin costs fall.
2.
Lagged pricing mechanisms: Polymer extrusion operations face margin pressure during rapid raw material inflation due to index pricing delays with customers. Orbia Advance Corporation's management noted in an earnings call on 29 July 2021 that its Dura-Line conduit business typically experiences a three-month to four-month lag before recovering higher raw material costs through increased pricing. Ethylene and polyethylene prices represent a massive portion of Dura-Line's cost base. 40 41 43 - Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls These pricing delays mean that sudden resin price spikes temporarily depress operating profit margins until contracts reset.
3.
Favorable energy impacts: Direct energy costs represent a meaningful operational expense in polymer processing, especially during regional power crises. DNB Carnegie noted in a research report dated 21 April 2026 that polymer heating, injection, and subsequent cooling are highly energyintensive. This power sensitivity was demonstrated when Hexatronic consolidated its European duct footprint by planning the closure of its Weterings Plastics facility in the Netherlands. This footprint consolidation suggests that concentrating extrusion in lower-cost regions like Sweden and Austria protects the group from volatile European electricity prices.

Fiber cable assembly cost dynamics

4.
Labor intensive connectorization: High-density fiber-cable assembly plants exhibit significantly higher direct labor intensity than capital-intensive raw glass preform facilities. Jefferies Research noted on 14 March 2022 that the fiber cabling and connectivity business is highly labor-intensive, unlike the automated process of drawing raw glass. This labor focus is particularly prominent in connectorization, where workers manually terminate fiber ends with precise plastic ferrules. These dynamics indicate that assembly plants carry lower fixed depreciation overheads but remain highly sensitive to local manufacturing wage inflation.
5.
Low manufacturing margins: Raw component costs in connector assembly are highly optimized, which shifts the profit focus toward specialized high-margin system designs. An expert interview with a former manager at Corning Optical Communications on 5 February 2025 revealed that the direct cost to make a duplex connector in a Shenzhen facility was less than $0.35 landed. However, the finished custom assemblies sell to enterprise and data center customers for several thousand dollars. This discrepancy confirms that system design and integration services generate the majority of operating profits rather than simple component manufacturing.
6.
Restructuring cost programs: Manufacturing connectivity operations utilize corporate restructuring to shift personnel costs to low-cost geographies during industry down-cycles. Jefferies Research reported on 17 February 2021 that CommScope shifted core engineering, research, and corporate roles to India, China, and Ireland. Jefferies Research further noted on 15 December 2021 that these restructuring efforts under the CommScope NEXT program were highly detailed. Shifting these roles confirms that structural cost elimination is highly feasible in assembly segments, which supports medium-term earnings recovery.

Installation services labor cost structures

7.
Highly variable cost structures: Specialty contracting and installation services feature highly variable cost bases due to the widespread utilization of subcontractors. Dycom Industries' annual filing on 9 March 2026 stated that the company utilizes subcontractors to manage volume fluctuations and minimize fixed asset investments. Its costs of earned revenues, which consist of direct labor, subcontractors, and materials, represented 79.4% of contract revenues in fiscal 2026. This variable cost flexibility means that installation margins are highly protected during sudden customer capital expenditure cutbacks.
8.
Precedent deployment economics: Typical fiber-to-the-home rollouts exhibit an extreme imbalance between labor and passive material costs. Hexatronic's 2024 annual report stated that labor costs represent 80% of total fiber deployment project costs, while passive materials represent only 20%. Hexatronic's investor presentation on 18 January 2024 emphasized that investing in high-quality, easy-to-install systems can deliver substantial labor savings. This cost split implies that service providers are highly sensitive to installation speed and labor productivity, which directly impacts project returns.
9.
Service margin dilution: Shifting the corporate revenue mix toward services reduces capital intensity but dilutes the higher operating leverage of manufacturing segments. RedEye reported on 23 October 2026 that Hexatronic is shifting toward service-heavy data center segments to fuel growth. Although services require limited capital expenditure and offer greater subcontractor flexibility, they carry lower baseline operating margins. Consequently, an expanding services mix can dilute overall corporate operating leverage, which limits long-term earnings expansion during cyclical demand peaks.

Peer comparative operational economics

10.
Peer margin comparisons: Comparing peer cost structures reveals distinct margin profiles based on vertical integration and manufacturing capacity utilization. Hexatronic's management noted in its Q3 2025 earnings call that its US duct business did not match the operating margins of market leader Dura-Line. Management attributed this gap to Dura-Line's superior capacity utilization and stronger pricing with key accounts. Historical overcapacity precedents: Previous industry downturns demonstrate how rapid capacity expansion ahead of demand can severely damage corporate operating margins. Loose fiber prices halved in just 1.5 years as a result of underutilized facilities. 12.
Contrary evidence: Top optical preform and fiber producers ran at near-full capacity utilization in 2025, which suggests that capacity investments can yield strong returns despite overcapacity memories. Huatai Research asserted in its report dated 14 July 2026 that highend preform constraints would cause a 35 million fiber-kilometer deficit in 2026. This supply deficit is projected to support sustained price strength and expand gross margins for preform-capable players. This supply shortfall indicates that strategic capacity additions remain profitable during localized demand surges.

Structural risks and margin offsets

13.
Flexible workforce scaling: In contrast to highly capital-intensive manufacturing, specialty contractors can maintain stable margins during revenue shifts by scaling their workforce rapidly. Dycom Industries reported in its fiscal 2025 annual report that it expanded its revenue by 50% and its adjusted EBITDA margin by 450 basis points since fiscal 2022. This margin expansion was achieved by scaling its skilled workforce to meet fiber-to-the-home and data center demands. This flexible scaling demonstrates that contractors can expand operating profitability without incurring the severe operating leverage risks of manufacturing plants.
14.
Other side of the story: While polymer extrusion and cable assembly carry substantial operating leverage, the high variability of installation service costs provides a critical cushion against cyclical downturns. Dycom's ability to adjust subcontractor expenses, which represent the vast majority of its cost base, minimizes its fixed overhead exposure. However, manufacturing entities like Hexatronic remain exposed to unabsorbed plant depreciation and resin price volatility. This operational split means that relying heavily on service revenues stabilizes corporate earnings during severe broadband rollout contractions.
