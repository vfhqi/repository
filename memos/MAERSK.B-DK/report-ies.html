Maersk (MAERSK.B-DK): what the Q3 2026 results do to our case

The results leave the fundamental case where it was, because our case was written three days after them and already contains them; what is new is the market's answer, which modestly strengthens the case's call on timing and shrinks the loss it forecasts from here.

1. What changed

The company reported on 5 November 2026. The figures, all Q3 2026:

  • Group revenue USD 18,095 million (+27.4% year on year), 2.1% below Bloomberg consensus. Group EBITDA USD 4,710 million (+75.4%), 13.7% below consensus of USD 5,456 million. EBIT USD 3,269 million, 10.6% below. Diluted EPS USD 206 against consensus of USD 257.
  • Ocean EBITDA USD 3,699 million, 14% below consensus, at a margin of 29.4% against 34.2% expected. Loaded freight rates rose 46% year on year. Loaded volumes fell 1.0% while the market grew 2% to 4%. Unit cost at constant energy prices rose 5%. Utilisation was 96%.
  • Logistics & Services EBITDA USD 519 million, 6% above consensus, EBIT margin 5.8%. Terminals EBITDA USD 551 million, 8% above, margin 36.3%.
  • Full-year 2026 underlying EBITDA guidance raised to USD 14.0-14.5 billion from USD 10.5-12.5 billion; free cash flow above USD 3.0 billion. The buyback decision was deferred to February 2027. Capital commitments stood at USD 19.5 billion at September 2026, against USD 13.4 billion at the end of 2025.

The case assumed every one of these. Three details in the evidence are not in the case's reasoning and matter below:

  • The volume decline was not even. East-West lanes fell 4%, North-South 0.5%, and intra-regional lanes grew 7%. Volumes had grown 6.5% in the first half, so the swing is about 7.5 percentage points in one quarter.
  • A direct competitor grew through the same congestion: COSCO's volumes rose 4.5% in the quarter.
  • The brokers disagree on the cause of the miss. One attributes it to cost and volume; another says the rate itself fell short, having expected +55% against the +46% delivered, with Ocean EBIT 30% below consensus.

The sell-side response: targets rose but ratings did not turn positive. One house moved from Sell to Hold with a target of DKK 19,600; two others sit at DKK 17,500 with negative ratings; one holds at DKK 19,010. One broker raised 2027 EBITDA by 17% and cut 2028 by 9%. One projects 2027 EPS of USD 110.12 and another negative free cash flow from 2027.

The share price is DKK 19,895 (11 November 2026), against the DKK 22,870 from which the case measured every return. It is below the 50-day average (DKK 23,051) and above the 150-day (DKK 18,916).

2. The challenge

Reading one: the share loss is Maersk's own, so the upside tail is worth less to Maersk than the case assumes. The case treats volume as a rounding error and reads the 1% decline as a physical cap: at 96% utilisation there was nothing left to sell. That explains a flat quarter. It does not explain a 4% fall on exactly the East-West lanes where the Gemini network runs, while intra-regional volumes grew 7% and a competitor grew 4.5% under the same port conditions. The stronger reading is that a hub-and-spoke network, which concentrates cargo through a few hubs, is more fragile under congestion than a direct-call network, and that customers moved. If so, the consequence runs against the case's own upside scenario, not its base. The case gives 20% odds to a disruption-extended year and values it by applying the full rate sensitivity to an unchanged volume and cost base, to reach its USD 14.5 billion and its +28% return. Q3 is the first direct evidence of what disruption does to this network: a record rate, lower volume, higher unit cost and a 14% miss. If that penalty scales with congestion, the tail the case counts as its main risk of being wrong is smaller than stated, and the cone is less two-sided. For this to be right, the East-West loss has to persist into Q4 while peers keep growing, and renewed disruption has to bring congestion with it, which Cape routing plausibly does. Against it: the same network produced large beats in earlier disruption years, before congestion reached this level.

Reading two: the market has reset in one step, and this is a floor forming, not the first leg of a collapse. The case reads the failure to reward the raise as the signature of a top, with the price tipping into collapse through the first half of 2027. It was written without the price reaction. The reaction is now visible: the shares are about 13% below the case's basis, at roughly the sell-side's mean target, and on the case's own book anchor at about 0.7 times, not the 0.8 times it called a price for the upside tail. The alternative reading is that the price has already moved from discounting the upside to discounting something close to the sell-side's fair value, and can stay there. What supports it: one house upgraded on precisely this ground; the nearest estimate changes for 2027 went up, not down; the dividend the case expects is now about 6.5% of the price; the buyback is undecided. For it to be right, February guidance has to land near consensus and the price has to hold its 150-day average. The case puts the first at well under even odds. We do not think this reading is the more probable one, but it is not one the case can knock down, because the case's return arithmetic was never run from this price. Run from DKK 19,895 on the case's own values and odds, the base-case eighteen-month return is roughly -14% and the scenario-weighted figure roughly -9%, against the case's -25% and -20%.

A third reading we considered and set lower. If the miss was mainly rate under-capture and not cost, the case's lesson that congestion-supported earnings are low quality loses some of its evidence. It does not change the case's direction, because the case's transmission runs through the rate either way. It does matter for Q4: under-capture with a one-quarter lag is possible upside; under-capture that is permanent is a lower peak.

  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength (10, F): same. The supply wave and the Suez return are untouched.

  • #8 Current momentum (90, A, placed at the floor of A): more pressure, and enough to move. The case rested the A on both momentum measures being in the top fifth. The pinned sell-side earnings momentum letter is now B, relative strength is at the 82nd percentile against the case's 97th, and the three-month price return is -3.9%.
  • #10 Lifecycle (22, F): the case's call is being confirmed faster than it expected. The first of its two markers for the turn, a break of the 50-day average, has happened; the second, the 150-day, has not.
  • #12 Total shareholder return (9, F): less pressure within the grade. The forecast loss is smaller from the lower price; it is still a loss.
  • #7 Scope for upgrades (15, F): same. The raise in one broker's 2027 number was already in the case.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry the full set of broker estimate and target changes: whether 2027 consensus rose or fell after the print, whether 2028 was cut, and whether the houses settle on cost or on rate as the cause of the miss. It will carry no new company disclosure; the company has published nothing since the results. It cannot settle reading one.
  • Reading one needs Q4 volumes by trade lane and peers' Q4 volumes, in February 2027. The company has not committed to disclosing Gemini's reliability or any price premium earned on it, so that part may stay open.
  • Reading two is settled by the price against its 150-day average of DKK 18,916, by the Asia-Europe contract settlements between December and February, and by the February 2027 results: the 2027 guidance range, the buyback decision the company has deferred to that date, and the Q4 loaded rate against the case's own markers of 3,000 and 2,700 per FFE.
  • The rate question is observable only if spot rates stay elevated through the first half of 2027, which the first-half 2027 results would show.
