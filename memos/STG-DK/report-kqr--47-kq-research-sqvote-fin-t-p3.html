Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence only partly answers the Key Question: the negative European operating loop and the positive buyback loop are both real, but which one dominates Scandinavian Tobacco Group's (STG) EPS over 2027-2030 depends on volume stabilisation and cash conversion that the sources do not yet confirm.
1.
Key Question: The report asks which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model. It tests the operating loop (European cigars, supply disruption, US premium) against the capital loop (divestment, leverage, buybacks).
2.
Operating loop: DNB Carnegie reports that European volumes are declining at three to five percent annually, which pricing actions fail to fully offset. This erodes the operating leverage of the European Branded division. A structural volume drain is therefore the base condition. Fragmented cigar markets leave manufacturers with weaker pricing power than cigarette peers.
3.
Supply-disruption loop: The 2021 Agio integration led to prolonged supply issues and a decline in market share. The 2025 SAP roll-out generated new supply issues that eroded market shares. Q4 2025 free cash flow before acquisitions fell to DKK 147 million from DKK 604 million. Consolidation programmes in shrinking categories thus carry a demonstrated share-loss risk. The pending Mac Baren moves repeat that risk: fine-cut to Holstebro, pipe tobacco to Assens, and exit of all Mac Baren facilities by next summer.
4.
Last man standing: Mac Baren is expected to deliver DKK 150 million of annual cost synergies, with full effect by 2027. Integration also carries DKK 150 million of special costs and DKK 125 million of capex. The report judges that consolidation can offset fixed-cost deleverage for about three to five years. The strategy is a time-limited margin offset, not a durable growth engine.
5.
US premium cigars: The Cigars International loop is the strongest reinforcing element. The customer database holds nearly one million active customers, and mature superstores deliver EBITDA margins above 25% and ROIC above 15%. Against that, the underlying US handmade cigar market is declining by a midsingle-digit percentage. Promotional intensity has also pressured margins. The loop is therefore a share-gain-dependent reinforcing loop.
6.
Capital loop: The German fine-cut divestment reduced leverage to roughly two times and allows buybacks to resume. The loop is also fragile. Leverage rose to three times in late 2025, prompting a dividend cut and a buyback suspension. Capital returns remain highly sensitive to operational leverage. The loop is operationally gated, not self-starting.
7.
What brokers already assume: DNB Carnegie assumes net debt falls to about two times and supports a resumed buyback of roughly DKK 500 million. It also assumes the reduced share count offsets organic operating declines, so the capital loop overrides near-term operational headwinds through 2028. Company guidance centres on a low single-digit EBIT CAGR target. Deutsche Bank's bear case sits against this: a persistent two percent annual organic sales decline from 2027 through 2030. Consensus thus embeds a capital-loop-led base case, with the operating loop as the downside. Conflicting figures resolved: Leverage is the clearest conflict. Reported leverage reached 3.0x after the ERP cash disruption, a buyback pause occurred at 2.6x against a 2.5x target, and the post-divestment estimate is about two times. These figures sit at different dates and bases, and the sources do not fully reconcile them. The most coherent reading is that the two-times figure comes from an asset sale rather than from operating cash. Volume figures conflict too. Q3 2026 Europe Branded organic sales rose five percent, against three to five percent structural declines. The rebound followed resolution of the Signature quality issue, and machine-rolled cigar volumes still fell 3.6% in 9M26. The resolution is that a one-off recovery versus structural decline explains the gap. The capital loop can plausibly dominate through about 2028. Beyond that, sustained buybacks depend on operating cash flow, which the evidence does not yet support.
8.
Excise and downtrading: Germany's parliament voted for combustible excise increases of nine to thirteen percent annually between 2027 and 2030. Illicit trade has captured 41.4% of French consumption. Spain is the contrary case, with industry declines of only 2.3%. The excise-driven downtrading loop is therefore real but uneven across markets.
9.
Next-generation products: XQS reached an all-time high of 13.8% volume share in Sweden in Q3 2026, but UK share stayed at about 1%. DNB Carnegie says the segment is barely profitable and too small to drive growth. Scale rivals add pressure, with Velo holding a 54% volume share outside the US and Haypp controlling about 85% of the US online pouch channel. XQS is a regionally profitable niche brand on a balancing path, not a scale driver through 2030.
10.
Peer benchmarks: Peers show consolidation can be managed more smoothly. Imperial stabilised European net revenues while exiting low-margin volume, and BAT replaced South African production with imports without volume headwinds. Imperial's buyback is expected to drive a five percent annual share reduction. STG's execution record is the differentiator, since its history shows disruption, not seamless transition.
11.
Confidence and gaps: Overall confidence in the dominant-loop reading is medium. Key gaps are the post- 2026 Mac Baren synergy run-rate, XQS profitability and compliance, and German elasticity. Documents that would close them include the Annual Report 2026 in March 2027, the Annual General Meeting in April 2027 for the buyback schedule, and the Q1 2027 interim report in May. Risks beyond consensus, including a Cuba embargo end and FDA flavour restrictions, mean this is a partly answered, not settled conclusion.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The tension between Scandinavian Tobacco Group's negative operating feedback loop and positive capital feedback loop will determine its earnings trajectory through 2030.
  2. Operational stability must be achieved across European machine-rolled cigars and US premium retail channels for share buybacks to successfully drive earnings per share.
1.
Strategic inquiry: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"

Strategic tension in core business

2.
Accelerating decline: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces structural pressure in its European machine-rolled cigar category due to ongoing volume contraction and consumer downtrading. DNB Carnegie reports that volumes in Europe are declining at an annual rate of three to five percent, which pricing actions are failing to fully offset. This persistent decline erodes the operating leverage of the European Branded division, directly threatening the long-term sustainability of earnings per share growth unless cost-reduction measures are significantly accelerated.
3.
Elevated risk: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces potential supply chain disruptions as it consolidates its manufacturing footprint to defend operating margins. Deutsche Bank Research highlights that raw tobacco supply chain normalisation and continuous Focus2030 investments have already contracted margins. Any operational friction during these facility integration moves could result in irreversible market share losses across key European brands.
4.
Favourable returns: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has the opportunity to drive a positive capital allocation feedback loop through aggressive share repurchases. The company completed the divestment of its German fine-cut brands on October first, which reduced leverage to approximately two times. This improved leverage ratio allows the company to resume substantial share buyback programmes that can systematically increase earnings per share despite flat underlying net income.

Slowing momentum: Scandinavian Tobacco Group face a challenging path to profitability in next-generation products as the nicotine pouch market remains highly fragmented. DNB Carnegie reports that while the nicotine pouch segment offers high growth potential, it remains barely profitable and is too small to be a growth driver. Ramping up commercial investments to compete with scale leaders could severely hamper shortterm cash flows and delay any positive impact on consolidated earnings per share.

Superior efficiency: Imperial Brands has demonstrated how a consistent capital return strategy can support valuation multiples even during periods of volume decline. DNB Carnegie notes that Imperial Brands trades at an adjusted price-to-earnings multiple of over eight times, whereas Scandinavian Tobacco Group trades at a meaningful discount of seven point five times. Emulating this evergreen capital allocation approach could unlock significant equity value re-ratings for shareholders once core earnings stabilise.

Section 2 – Cost Programmes and Supply Disruptions in Shrinking Categories

Section summary
  1. Structural supply disruptions during manufacturing consolidations and ERP migrations pose critical operational risks to declining tobacco categories.
  2. Operational missteps during Scandinavian Tobacco Group's historical factory integrations and system migrations have consistently resulted in market share losses.
  3. Consolidating market share in mature tobacco categories allows leading firms to harvest cash flows and offset fixed-cost deleverage.
  4. High integration expenses and persistent volume declines eventually limit the duration of synergy-driven earnings growth. For this analysis, the researcher found three relevant peer companies, two comparable past consolidation cases, and used 14 different source documents.

Scandinavian Tobacco Group operational bottlenecks

1.
Operational friction: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has experienced recurrent supply chain challenges during major restructuring initiatives. In its November 2025 Capital Markets Day presentation, the company acknowledged that historical decisions made during the 2021 Agio integration led to a prolonged period of supply issues. These supply issues prevented the company from adequately supplying its core European markets and directly triggered a decline in market share across key regions. This indicates that integration missteps can severely damage franchise value.
2.
Persistent disruption: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faced further operational bottlenecks in 2025 as it transitioned to a unified IT infrastructure. The company reported in November 2025 that rolling out its global SAP solution in European markets generated new supply issues. This ERP migration, known as One Process, was designed to simplify the IT landscape but instead caused a prolonged period of supply issues that eroded market shares. These operational challenges highlight the risk of consolidating legacy systems in mature business segments.
3.
Financial headwind: Scandinavian Tobacco Group reported a severe contraction in cash generation due to billing and collection delays from the ERP transition. In its full-year 2025 report published in March 2026, the company stated that Q4 2025 free cash flow before acquisitions fell to DKK 147 million from DKK 604 million in the prior year. This significant decline was directly driven by delays in collecting receivables associated with the new European ERP implementation. Consequently, the operational disruption directly depressed short-term cash flows and increased leverage to 3.0 times.
4.
Ambitious integration: Scandinavian Tobacco Group is executing a complex manufacturing consolidation following its acquisition of Mac Baren Tobacco Company. In its 2024 annual report published in March 2025, the company outlined plans to achieve DKK 150 million in annual synergies by consolidating its manufacturing footprint. The integration involves moving fine-cut tobacco production to Holstebro and pipe tobacco to Assens, with plans to exit all Mac Baren facilities by the end of next summer. If executed poorly, this footprint rationalisation could replicate the operational issues seen in previous integrations.
5.
Market consequences: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in November 2026 that Scandinavian Tobacco Group continues to suffer from supply constraints. The broker reported that European machine-rolled cigar performance was severely impacted by category declines and the Signature brand supply disruption. This operational failure has compounded market-share losses in high-margin regions like France and Spain, where recovery efforts under the Focus2030 program are critical. These findings demonstrate that supply chain friction can directly offset tactical pricing benefits.

Manufacturing consolidation at key peers

6.
Peer restructuring: Imperial Brands is carrying out its own manufacturing consolidation by phasing out high-cost European assets. In its H2 2026 financial statement, the company confirmed that production at its Langenhagen factory in Germany will cease entirely by July 2027. The closure was necessitated by steadily declining utilization aligned with long-term reductions in tobacco volumes. To optimize its footprint, the company recognized a closure provision of 220 million pounds to cover decommissioning and people-related costs.
7.
Managed volume: Imperial Brands has intentionally reduced its exposure to low-margin categories to protect profitability during factory closures. In its H2 2026 annual report, the company stated that it reduced manufacturing for the low-margin private label segment in Germany ahead of the Langenhagen exit. This deliberate contraction caused European tobacco volumes to decline by 3.4% overall. By cutting low-margin volume, the company successfully stabilised European net revenues, which grew by 40 basis points on an adjusted basis.
8.
System transition: Imperial Brands has managed its global ERP roll-out with minimal operational disruption by utilizing a phased deployment strategy. In its June 2026 prospectus supplement, the company reported that its new enterprise resource planning platform went live in Poland and Australasia. This system migration is a key step to build a simpler organisation and leverage global scale. Unlike Scandinavian Tobacco Group's system issues, Imperial's transition was supported by a strategic partnership with Capgemini to manage operational risks.
9.
Strategic footprint: British American Tobacco decided to end domestic cigarette production in South Africa due to severe local market pressures. In its 2025 annual report, the company announced the closure of its Heidelberg factory by the end of 2026. The Heidelberg facility, which was operating at just 35% capacity, suffered from unchecked growth of illicit trade that captured 75% of the market. This closure reflects a broader strategy under its Fit2Win program to prune low-utilization manufacturing assets.
10.
Operational transition: British American Tobacco mitigated supply risks during its South African factory closure by transitioning to an import-based model. Jefferies reported in January 2026 that the company will satisfy local demand via imports, preventing any headwinds to volumes. The broker noted that Heidelberg's closure was driven by potential production efficiency gains rather than market exit. This seamless operational transition demonstrates how scale players can consolidate manufacturing without incurring major supply disruptions.
11.
Restructuring costs: British American Tobacco Bangladesh experienced temporary profitability pressure due to restructuring and factory closure costs. The Financial Express reported in October 2025 that the company's nine-month profit dropped 46% year-on-year to Tk 7.20 billion. This bottom-line decline was driven by lower sales, higher excise duties, and additional factory closure costs in Dhaka. Consequently, earnings per share fell to Tk 13.34 from Tk 24.49, illustrating that factory closures can temporarily depress local earnings.
12.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research reported in its Q2 2024 review that Scandinavian Tobacco Group was on track to achieve its full-year financial expectations. The broker expressed confidence that the Mac Baren deal would be accretive to margins and earnings, pointing to the company's strong track record of delivering substantial integration synergies from previous transactions like Royal Agio. This positive outlook suggests that operational risks may be temporary, contrasting with the permanent market-share erosion observed during Imperial Brands' restructuring efforts.
13.
Other side of the story: While supply disruptions from ERP migrations and factory consolidations have historically triggered market share losses, these restructuring programmes are essential to protect long-term margins. DNB Carnegie notes that European cigar volumes are in structural decline at three to five percent annually, making cost reduction unavoidable. Successfully integrating Mac Baren and stabilizing the SAP system will allow Scandinavian Tobacco Group to simplify its operations and realise critical cost savings of DKK 150 million by 2027. Therefore, near-term supply friction is a necessary trade-off to offset fixed-cost deleverage.
