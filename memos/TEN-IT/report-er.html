This comprehensive earnings review for Tenaris reveals that the most recently reported period has fundamentally reshaped the 18-month to 3-year earnings trajectory, though not in the direction many yield-focused investors anticipated. The company's strategic pivot to an all-dividend policy, marked by a doubled interim dividend to $0.59 per share while share buybacks slowed dramatically to only $90 million during the first half of 2026, surprised the market's aggressive return expectations and disappointed several yield-focused investors who were expecting aggressive share repurchases.

This capital allocation shift, combined with temporary but material Middle East shipment deferrals of approximately $130 million due to Strait of Hormuz closures, has introduced near-term earnings volatility that was not fully reflected in pre-earnings consensus models. & Co Redburn The single most important change emerging from this earnings cycle is the structural confirmation that Tenaris can sustain and expand its through-cycle profitability despite severe macroeconomic and geopolitical headwinds, but only by permanently elevating its capital intensity in domestic North American manufacturing. In response to rising US steel import tariffs, the company has maximized local asset utilization by adding extra work shifts at its domestic mills, propelling the state-of-the-art Bay City facility in Texas to produce at record operational levels, while launching a massive US$230 million investment program at its seamless pipe mill in Ontario.

This regionalization strategy, while necessary to bypass punitive 50% Section 232 tariffs, fundamentally alters the company's historical low-cost import model and locks in higher structural operating costs that will compress margins if domestic rig counts fail to recover as anticipated. - Earnings Calls Despite near-term disruptions, the medium-term EPS trajectory remains constructive on the back of an exceptionally robust offshore backlog.

Tenaris expanded its high-value offshore backlog through major pre-salt contracts with Petrobras in Brazil, delivering risers, deepwater flowlines, and thermal insulation coatings for the Búzios 9 and Búzios 11 projects, with these premium pre-salt deliveries scheduled to continue into 2026. Furthermore, the company extended its dedicated casing services for ExxonMobil's massive Stabroek block development in Guyana under a 10-year supply contract signed in late 2021, and management is expanding its Rig Direct footprint in the region to support ExxonMobil's aggressive 35-well exploration campaign.

These longcycle deepwater contracts provide multi-year revenue visibility that significantly de-risks the outer-year earnings profile, even as short-cycle onshore North American activity remains subdued.

The path to a 25%+ IRR over the next three years depends critically on three interconnected catalysts: first, the normalization of Middle East shipping lanes to unlock deferred high-margin deliveries; second, the sustained recovery of US onshore OCTG pricing supported by import tariff protections and inventory destocking; and third, the successful ramp-up of premium offshore deliveries in Brazil, Guyana, and Suriname.

Rothschild & Co Redburn analyst Sebastian Erskine expects the drop-through of price increases will allow Tenaris's group EBITDA margin to exceed 25% by 2027, meeting the prior capital markets day guidance corridor of 20% to 25% set in 2024. However, achieving this margin threshold requires that geopolitical shipping disruptions do not become permanent and that raw material cost inflation can be successfully passed through to customers without demand destruction. & Co Redburn The strongest counter-hypothesis to this constructive medium-term outlook centers on the structural vulnerability of Tenaris's onshore-heavy revenue mix to a sharper-than-expected contraction in US shale drilling activity.

Sales in North America accounted for 49% of tubular sales in FY25, highlighting a substantial geographical concentration of sales, while South American operations contributed 18% of total tubular revenue during the same fiscal year. If global oil prices experience a sustained decline toward the $60 per barrel threshold due to macroeconomic recession or accelerated energy transition policies, US shale operators would aggressively curtail drilling programs, triggering severe volume-led downside that offshore backlogs cannot fully offset.

While structural offshore backlogs and tariff-driven pricing power offer strong near-term support, Tenaris remains highly vulnerable to a broader macroeconomic slowdown, as a significant drop in global oil prices and subsequent contraction in US shale drilling could decimate onshore pipe demand, leaving localized North American mills severely underutilized and preventing Tenaris from sustaining its targeted 25% EBITDA margin.

Additionally, the sudden deceleration in share repurchases fundamentally alters the near-term EPS support mechanism that many investors had embedded in their valuation models. Jefferies analyst Jamie Franklin observed that the company's capital allocation priorities have shifted significantly with the pause in its share buyback program, which came as a major surprise to the market because analysts were modeling a continuous annual repurchase run-rate, and although the company holds a strong net cash cushion, the sudden halt in repurchases dampened equity market sentiment and removed key near-term support for the earnings per share trajectory.

This pivot means that EPS growth over the next 18 months will depend entirely on organic earnings expansion rather than share count reduction, placing greater emphasis on successfully executing the offshore backlog and capturing US onshore pricing momentum. The specific, observable data point that would change this conclusion is a quarterly US land rig count that falls below 550 active rigs for two consecutive quarters while WTI crude remains below $65 per barrel.

This threshold represents a critical inflection point where onshore drilling activity would contract to levels that overwhelm the company's offshore revenue growth, triggering severe underutilization at its newly expanded domestic mills in Bay City and Sault Ste. Marie. At these activity levels, Tenaris's domestic manufacturing regionalization strategy would shift from a defensive tariff mitigation tactic to a structural margin trap, as fixed costs at underutilized North American facilities would erode group profitability faster than offshore premium connections could offset.

The two immediate checks for the portfolio manager are as follows: First, closely monitor the monthly US OCTG import data published by the US International Trade Commission to verify whether Section 232 tariffs continue to suppress foreign competition and support domestic pricing power, as any meaningful rebound in low-cost imports from exempt countries would signal that Tenaris's domestic pricing tailwind is fading faster than management projects.

Second, track Petrobras's quarterly capital expenditure execution in its pre-salt offshore fields, specifically focusing on whether the Búzios 9 and Búzios 11 projects maintain their planned delivery schedules, as any material delay or budget reallocation by Petrobras would directly threaten Tenaris's most visible high-margin revenue stream and challenge the achievability of the 25% EBITDA margin target by 2027. The doubled dividend provides attractive near-term income support with an annualized yield approaching 7%, but the absence of aggressive share buybacks means that total shareholder returns will depend almost entirely on EBITDA margin expansion toward the 25% threshold.
