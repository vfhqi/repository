APM Mini-Memo: Convatec

The one-line view. Convatec is a reliable chronic-care consumables compounder on a bottom-fifth multiple, and its return depends on the market coming to trust a thirteen percent earnings path as the payer worries clear on a dated calendar.

What the company does

Convatec sells recurring consumables for chronic conditions: wound dressings, ostomy bags, catheters and infusion sets. The Investment Analyst's memo of 18 November 2026 splits revenue into Advanced Wound Care at 31 percent, Ostomy 28, Continence 22 (including the US home-delivery distributor 180 Medical) and Infusion 19; over 90 percent of revenue recurs, because patients reorder for years and nurses rarely switch a settled patient. Revenue was about 2.4 billion dollars in 2025, reported in dollars while the shares trade in pence in London. Convatec leads only in infusion sets, where it makes about two-thirds of the world's supply, and in US catheter home delivery; in ostomy it is number three behind Coloplast and Hollister, and number four in wound care.

A plausible investment thesis

Delivery first, then re-rating. The memo's case is a good non-cyclical business doing what it said it would, at a multiple in the bottom quarter of its ten-year range. Earnings per share should compound at 13 to 14 percent a year to 2030, built from revenue growth of about 6 percent, an operating margin rising from 22.3 to about 25 percent, and a share count falling almost 4 percent a year through buybacks. Consensus already sits on that path, so the extra return has to come from the multiple recovering as a stack of worries clears one by one.

The worries are clearing on a calendar. The ten-month update of 18 November raised organic growth guidance, showed growth of about 9 percent in July to October, and held the margin floor of at least 23 percent while absorbing more currency headwind, which the memo reads as a hidden raise: about 225 basis points of underlying margin expansion against 70 as reported. The first half of 2027 laps a depressed first half of 2026, so earnings per share should rise 20 to 25 percent in that half, reported earnings roughly double as the spin-out amortisation ends, and a bipartisan bill of 9 October would keep ostomy and continence out of Medicare competitive bidding for five years. The drivers to track are the full-year 2027 guidance on 23 February 2027 (6 to 8 percent growth, a margin of at least 24 percent), the segment table that shows whether Ostomy accelerated alongside Infusion, Natura Body's US launch in mid-2027, and Medicare's payment amounts in late summer 2027.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple. The Rater rated Case Riskiness A on 18 November, meaning simple and low-risk, sixth of 83. What needs to go right is modest: the margin floors are met (23 percent in 2026, 24 in 2027), organic growth reaches about 6.5 percent led by Infusion, and surplus cash goes to buybacks. Each of the memo's sensitivities is small: a 50 basis point margin miss costs about 2.4 percent of earnings per share, one point less growth 2 to 3 percent, no buyback in 2027 about 1.4 percent. Even at a trough multiple with a miss, the memo puts the three-year return at about nil rather than a loss.

The main risk is the payer. About 9 percent of group revenue goes through Medicare, and competitive bidding from 1 January 2028 is expected to cut retail prices 20 to 30 percent; the forecasts already assume it proceeds at the low end of management's range, so a harsh outcome costs about 5.5 percent of 2028 earnings and a deferral adds about 3.4. The payer has form: one Medicare rule cut InnovaMatrix's price by 85 percent and removed 64 million dollars of revenue in a year, and Germany and France are both tightening reimbursement in 2027. Track Medicare's payment amounts in late summer 2027, committee action on the bill, and adjusted gross margin, where price concessions, polymer inflation and automation slippage would all show first. Two lesser risks: sterling at 1.39 would cut pence earnings about 5 percent, and growth now leans on Infusion, where new capacity is being added at a plant under an FDA warning letter while a new chief financial officer starts on 1 March 2027.

Business quality

Above average, not top quality. The Rater rated Foundations' Robustness B on 18 November. About 70 percent of revenue sits in concentrated oligopolies where rivals compete on evidence and service rather than list price, churn at 180 Medical is under 1 percent, return on invested capital has risen from 7 percent in 2019 to 13 to 16 percent, and the operating margin from 17.7 percent in 2021 to 22.3 in 2025. What holds the grade down is the absence of pricing power, a challenger position in ostomy and wound care, a gross margin seven points below Coloplast's, and 141 million of about 500 million dollars of acquisitions written off. Cash lags earnings this year, with conversion cut to about 90 percent as capital spending peaks near 9 percent of sales, so reported cash flatters the picture for now.
