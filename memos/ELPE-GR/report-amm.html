APM Mini-Memo: HELLENiQ Energy

The one-line view. Greece's dominant refiner is earning record diesel and jet fuel margins, has paid down debt fast and now trades on six times consensus earnings, and the story depends on Mediterranean refining margins holding through 2027 when every published base case has them falling back.

What the company does

A refiner first. HELLENiQ Energy runs three coastal refineries in Greece, at Aspropyrgos, Elefsina and Thessaloniki, with 342 thousand barrels a day of capacity, 65 percent of the country's total. Refining earned 72 percent of the group's adjusted operating profit before depreciation of 1,132 million euros in 2025, and 54 percent of refining sales are exports, so the group is a Mediterranean exporter rather than a Greek utility. Around the refineries sit a 33 percent share of Greek fuel retail through 1,557 stations, a polypropylene plant, and a power and renewables arm with 510 megawatts operating in 2025 against a target of 1.5 gigawatts by 2028.

Closely held. Paneuropean Oil and Industrial Holdings owns 40 percent and the Greek State 31 percent, leaving a free float of 28 percent. The state is also customer, supplier and tax-setter, and a 33 percent solidarity contribution under a 2024 law cost the company 173.5 million euros.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is a windfall being banked. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz in late February 2026, Russian refinery outages and two decades of European underinvestment took HELLENiQ Energy's realised refining margin from 13.80 dollars a barrel in the second quarter of 2025 to 22.50 dollars a year later. Each dollar a barrel is about 106 million euros of pre-tax profit, so adjusted operating profit before depreciation doubled to 442 million euros in the second quarter of 2026 and net debt fell by 700 million euros in one quarter to 1.97 billion euros. Debt repaid in a windfall does not come back when margins normalise, so the equity keeps a larger share of whatever the business earns next.

The route to earnings per share runs through one number. Refining cracks set about 65 percent of forward earnings per share, the euro against the dollar about 15 percent, and the renewables build about 10 percent. Consensus has followed the margin: 24-month-forward earnings per share rose from 1.92 euros on 2 October to 2.70 euros on 27 November while the price fell from 18.18 to 16.22 euros, so the forward multiple dropped from 9.5 times to 6.0 times, by my arithmetic from the card's two readings. That swing moved the valuation reading from F to A and lifted the stock from the Longlist to the Ideas Bench.

Why the market could pay more. The specialists now call the European distillate shortage structural, management expects strong margins through 2027, and the sell-side base case still has the margin falling back to historic levels by 2027; the gap between those views is where further upgrades come from. The drivers to track are the realised margin against 18 dollars a barrel after the fourth-quarter print, the direction of 2027 consensus, Brent against the 85 dollar level that would remove the downstream energy row's reason, and the year-end dividend and buyback decisions.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Demand-driven EPSU', and the Investment Analyst's memo of 3 October rated Setups' Fit/Crispness D because that fit is loose: the inflection is in the margin rather than in demand, Greek fuel demand fell 6 percent in the second quarter while the margin doubled, and the earnings step-up had already happened. The memo named the trough-on-trough setup as the one this stock will fit when the margin normalises, estimates are cut and the multiple sits in its 6.1 to 7.5 times band. My own reading is that the multiple has arrived there first, at 6.0 times, while earnings are still at the peak, so the setup is half-formed: the price is at trough, the earnings are not.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple to state, not low-risk. The Investment Analyst's memo of 3 October rated Case Riskiness D, where A is simple and low-risk, because the one driver that makes the case is the one that unmakes it. The memo's own path has adjusted earnings per share peaking near 2.40 euros in 2026 and falling to 1.50 euros by 2028 as the realised margin returns to 13 to 14 dollars a barrel, and even the sell-side median has 2027 earnings falling 22 percent.

What needs to go right. The Mediterranean middle-distillate margin must stay above about 18 dollars a barrel for two consecutive quarters after the fourth-quarter 2026 print with no sign of Hormuz normalising, and 2027 consensus must rise rather than fall; the Investment Analyst put that branch at about one in three. The dated risks beside the margin are a repeat windfall levy, the Thessaloniki turnaround in 2027, and an unhedged currency exposure where a 5 percent move in the euro shifts earnings per share about 9 percent. The constructive point is that the leading indicators are daily: the margin is published, the arithmetic is public, and a wrong call shows quickly.

Business quality

Excellent operator, ordinary returns. The largest turnaround in the company's history finished on schedule, utilisation reached 103 percent, and the Geneva trading desk replaced the lost Hormuz crude with Norwegian and American grades without a slowdown; that desk's premium of about 7 dollars a barrel over the Mediterranean benchmark has held across cycles. Against that, normalised return on invested capital of 7.4 percent sits below a cost of capital of 7.9 to 8.2 percent, the renewables build has delivered about half of what was promised, and management publishes no guidance. The Investment Analyst's memo of 3 October rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength B and Foundations' Robustness C: the duopoly and the deficit are robust, and the business beneath them does not survive a change of environment.

Plausible returns and valuation

Two readings disagree, and the gap is the case. The weekly routine's plausible return of 19 November is about 31 percent a year on average, an A against the bar, built on the multiple moving from 6.0 to 9.4 times while consensus earnings fall 32 percent a year. The Investment Analyst's memo of 3 October, at 18.18 euros, gave a modal return of about minus 18 percent a year to April 2028 on an 8 times multiple applied to the memo's lower earnings path, with the 4 to 5 percent dividend the only positive leg. My own reading is that at 16.22 euros the answer sits between: the memo's bull branch of 21 to 25 euros plus dividends by 2029 is roughly a C-grade return from here, the memo's central path still gives a negative one, and so the return is a bet on which earnings path is right rather than on the multiple.

Watch the cause of the swing. The Investment Analyst grades the valuation swing below the data until its cause is known, and the consensus mean target of 15.46 euros on 2 October sits below today's price.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. HELLENiQ Energy carries two of the Sector's five live stories at full strength: record diesel and jet margins, and the windfall wiping out debt and buying a utility. The Greek market is a two-player duopoly with over 90 percent of wholesale fuel and no volume war, and the Romanian and Bulgarian renewables yield 10 to 12 percent against lower single digits at home. All four top-down readings are A: the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 25 November, and the weekly Technical Momentum, Thematic Fit/Momentum and TSR/Valuation ratings of 27 November; downstream energy is the row where the target holds Double Overweight and the book holds nothing.

Cons. The Sector digest marks the margin story as fed by the Strait of Hormuz staying shut, and a reopening that coincides with new capacity is the downturn case. The Assistant Portfolio Manager's note of 6 November found no refiner or product tanker yet a capital case while the memos call the upswing fully priced, the state's levy precedent and retail price caps run one way, and polypropylene spreads at record lows are a drag.
