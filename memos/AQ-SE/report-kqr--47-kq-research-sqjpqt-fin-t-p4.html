The Key Question asks what the evidence says about specific inputs driving AQ Group's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, namely OECD Pillar II tax impact, currency translation sensitivity, normalized net working capital, acquisition cadence and margins, depreciation evolution, warrant dilution, interest income, and revenue ramps for Norlake and data-centres, and how management, sell-side, and peers' records differ on these inputs.

The evidence shows that this question can be substantially answered for most financial inputs, though critical gaps remain regarding the precise magnitude of Pillar II top-up taxes in specific jurisdictions and the speed of margin convergence in recently acquired entities. The following bullets synthesize the most important findings on the core drivers of future earnings per share. AQ Group faces its first OECD Pillar II cash payments in 2027 because operations in Bulgaria, Estonia, and Lithuania require full top-up tax calculations after failing transitional safe harbour tests, which will raise the group's effective tax rate from the reported 18.1 percent in FY2025.

Section 2 – OECD Pillar II and the Rise in Effective Tax Rate

Section summary
  1. AQ Group faces an upward tax rate trajectory as OECD Pillar II rules trigger top-up taxes in key European manufacturing hubs starting in 2027.
  2. Sell-side expectations currently model a flat 20% tax rate from 2026 onward, but regional profit mixes and compliance costs pose upside risks.

Pillar II adoption and regional exposures

1.
Tax threshold exceeded: AQ Group became subject to global minimum tax rules after its turnover exceeded the EUR 750 million threshold. According to the AQ Group 2025 Annual Report published on 25 March 2026, the first cash payments under the OECD Pillar II rules will be made in 2027. This transition will require top-up calculations for operations that have historically enjoyed corporate tax rates below 15%. Ultimately, these top-up obligations are expected to raise the group's effective tax rate over the next 18 months.
2.
Unproductive safe harbours: AQ Group identified several lowtax countries that failed the transitional country-by-country safe harbour tests. The AQ Group 2025 Annual Report dated 25 March 2026 specified that Bulgaria, Estonia, and Lithuania require full Pillar II top-up tax calculations. These three jurisdictions represent massive industrial operations for the company because they accounted for a combined SEK 3,054 million in revenues during the first half of 2026. Consequently, failing these safe harbour tests will dilute future earnings per share unless operational efficiencies can offset the increased tax burden.
3.
Elevated baseline rates: AQ Group faces a Swedish statutory tax rate of 20.6% which historically contrasted with its lower foreign tax obligations. The AQ Group Annual Report of 25 March 2026 noted that the actual reported tax rate was 18.1 percent in 2025, supported by tax effects in foreign subsidiaries. This reported rate is higher than historical levels such as the 16% rate recorded in 2023. Thus, as Pillar II closes the gap between low-tax jurisdictions and Sweden, the group's average tax expense will inevitably converge toward the higher Swedish baseline.

Peer tax structures and Pillar II impacts

4.
Estonian tax deviations: HANZA maintains operations in Estonia where corporate profits are only taxed upon distribution. According to the HANZA 2025 Annual Report published on 21 April 2026, the effective tax rate on Estonian operations was set at 0 percent for calculation purposes. This operational setup mirrors AQ Group's historical treatment of its Estonian subsidiaries which were not burdened with corporate income tax. However, both contract manufacturers must now adjust to Pillar II rules, meaning untaxed foreign profits will face a mandatory 15% minimum rate.
5.
Peer tax escalations: Scanfil reported a substantial increase in its tax expenses during the 2026 financial year. The Scanfil plc Interim Report published on 22 October 2026 revealed that its effective tax rate for January-September 2026 rose to 26.0%, up from 22.6% in the prior year period. This rate is significantly higher than AQ Group's reported rate of 19% for the first quarter of 2025. The rise in Scanfil's rate highlights how acquisitions in hightax countries can drive overall effective tax rates upward for contract manufacturers.
6.
Geographical tax exposure: Kitron maintains a diversified Central and Eastern European footprint with manufacturing hubs in Poland and Lithuania. The Kitron Interim Report of 22 October 2026 highlighted that the company has over 3,000 employees across these regions. Like AQ Group, Kitron relies heavily on Lithuania for low-cost assembly where corporate tax rates are below the OECD minimum. This exposure means Kitron will likely face similar Pillar II headwinds, which will restrict net profit margins across the Nordic contract manufacturing sector.
7.
Deferred tax precedents: AQ Group historically accounted for its Estonian earnings by reporting deferred tax only on future estimated dividends. The AQ Group Annual Report of 24 March 2025 noted that this approach complied with Estonia's unique tax regime. This model was also utilized by HANZA as disclosed in its 2020 interim report where its Estonian tax rate was set to 0 percent. However, this historical precedent of avoiding local tax is now obsolete under Pillar II, which forces immediate minimum tax recognition regardless of distribution plans.

Sell-side modeling and financial impact

8.
Broker tax assumptions: DNB Carnegie's research note on 15 October 2026 modeled a long-term tax rate for AQ Group. The broker's model assumed a tax rate of 20% for the years 2026, 2027, and 2028. This projection matches the 20% actual tax rate reported by the group for the fiscal year 2024. Thus, the sell-side has already priced in the Pillar II hike by raising its modeled tax rate from the historical 18.1 percent level.
9.
Cash flow divergence: Rising tax obligations will create a wider gap between AQ Group's reported earnings and its actual free cash flow. The DNB Carnegie report published on 15 October 2026 estimated that AQ Group's paid taxes will rise to SEK 218 million in 2027 from SEK 138 million in 2026. This substantial tax payment increase is projected to reduce free cash flow despite growing operating profits. Consequently, investors must monitor whether this growing cash tax drag will constrain the company's aggressive acquisition agenda.
10.
Contrary evidence: The impact of Pillar II might be less severe than anticipated because some jurisdictions are introducing localized amendments and transitional safe harbours. A regulatory news report from 1 April 2025 indicated that Bulgaria enacted amendments to its Pillar Two legislation that include transitional UTPR safe harbours and anti-hybrid arbitrage rules. These localized safe harbours could allow AQ Group to mitigate early top-up taxes in its major Bulgarian hub where it had SEK 1,308 million in revenue in the first half of 2026. Therefore, these local legal shifts could prevent the group's overall effective tax rate from reaching the modeled 20% baseline.

A regulatory report published on 19 June 2026 stated that approximately 60 jurisdictions have enacted global minimum tax mechanisms, with many mandating registrations by late 2025. For AQ Group, managing these staggered deadlines across 17 operating countries will likely increase administrative and legal expenses. These additional compliance costs represent an indirect headwind to administrative margins over the forecast period.

A DNB Carnegie note dated 14 July 2026 highlighted that the target was raised to above 10%, up from the previous 8%.
