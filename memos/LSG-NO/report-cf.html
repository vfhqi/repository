EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Leroy Seafood Group presents a structural turnaround narrative anchored by exceptional sixteen-year CEO tenure and a newly implemented long-term incentive program linking compensation to sustainability metrics, yet the investment case remains shadowed by extreme executive longevity breeding potential complacency and Austevoll Seafood's 52.7% controlling stake limiting independent governance oversight. The group is transitioning from capital-intensive growth to harvesting returns through structural cost reductions, targeting NOK 1 billion in annual cost-base savings via strategic partnerships including the Cargill feed collaboration expected to deliver NOK 1.4 per kg in structural savings. However, biological volatility, currency headwinds, and persistent peer disadvantages create material execution risk across the 220,000 GWT 2030 volume target.

Strategic change and capital allocation trajectory

The company has clarified its strategic framework around Growth, Cost, Simplify, and Leadership through 2030, implementing the Lerøy Way system across nearly 100% of entities to standardize operations. Management is transitioning from intensive investment cycles in smolts and shielding technology to optimization and disciplined capital allocation, with group CAPEX guided down to NOK 1.7 billion in 2026 from NOK 1.8 billion in 2025.

Approximately half of 2026 volumes are expected to harvest from shielded or submerged sites, leveraging Aquatraz closed-containment systems with first fish anticipated in Q1 2027. The long-term revenue ambition of NOK 50 billion by 2030 represents organic farming volume growth to 220,000 GWT in Norway from 159,620 GWT harvested in 2023, a substantial 37.83% increase. The contrarian perspective warns that scaling down shielding CAPEX may prove premature if biological challenges like string jellyfish require rapid, unbudgeted capital outlays to protect standing biomass.

Top forward drivers ranked by impact and plausibility

Six primary forward drivers will dictate the group's mid-term earnings trajectory, with shielding technology deployment ranking highest at an estimated NOK 4.0 per kg impact with HIGH plausibility despite medium confidence dependent on sea temperatures. The Cargill feed partnership targets NOK 1.4 per kg in structural savings with HIGH plausibility and high confidence, though dependent on sourcing mix flexibility, while the company has already realized NOK 0.3 per kg in verified reductions.

Farming volume growth to 220,000 GWT carries MEDIUM plausibility with medium confidence dependent on smolt quality, while Sjøtroll's biological turnaround targeting NOK 10.1 per kg by 2027 carries MEDIUM plausibility but low confidence due to genetics uncertainty. Wild Catch recovery to NOK 400 million operational EBIT holds HIGH plausibility with high confidence despite severe quota cuts, as record pricing for cod and saithe has offset volume pressure.

Downstream VAPS&D expansion to NOK 2.0 billion in operational EBIT on 420,000 tonnes sold by 2030 carries MEDIUM plausibility with medium confidence dependent on strategic retail contract penetration. The investment thesis matches a shielding technology adoption and structural cost reduction archetype, though if independent probabilities of feed, shielding, and volume targets are 80%, 70%, and 60%, joint probability falls to approximately 33.6%, with salmon spot price realization in NOK remaining the single most critical driver, as a stronger Norwegian krone severely dilutes domestic price realizations and caps cost reduction benefits.

Recent delivery quality and forward guidance credibility

Q1 2026 operational EBIT landed spot on expectations at NOK 858 million, matching consensus but declining year on year, while biological performance improved with average harvest weights reaching 4.8kg GWT despite Sjøtroll trout suffering severe biological maturation downgrades that realized NOK 10 per kg lower prices than standard salmon. Wild Catch operational EBIT surged 54% year on year to NOK 228 million as record cod and saithe prices more than offset a 25% quota drop, though VAPS&D margins contracted to NOK 160 million pressured by escalating transport logistics costs and Norwegian krone strength.

Management behaves as an honest-tracker on volumes, delivering 195.6kt GWT in FY2025 versus 195.0kt guided, but historically overpromises on biological cost curves during warm seawater seasons, evidenced by Q3 2025 operational EBIT registering only NOK 15 million compared to consensus expectations of NOK 100 million. Earnings remain highly sensitive to FX movements, where DNB Carnegie cut FY2026 adjusted EPS by 25% because of a stronger EUR/NOK assumption moving from 11.25 to 10.8, lowering salmon spot price forecasts to NOK 76 per kg.

The group demonstrates highly volatile biological and volume execution upstream while downstream processing shows reliable structural profit delivery, achieving NOK 1.29 billion against the NOK 1.25 billion 2025 target, yet the company significantly missed its 18% ROCE target, reporting pre-tax ROCE of only 8% in 2025, and failed to achieve its stated goal of becoming the number one farming company on EBIT per kg.

Sell-side consensus positioning and contrarian counterweight

The sell-side pool features four active named analysts across DNB Carnegie and Norne Securities maintaining overwhelmingly positive sentiment with dominant BUY ratings, though recent currency shifts forced price target cuts from NOK 60 to NOK 55. DNB Carnegie's Alexander Aukner, Ola Trovatn, and Henrik Andre Larsen hold BUY ratings with NOK 55 targets focusing on margin re-rating potential from shielding technology and the Cargill feed partnership, high free cash flow generation with potential to double dividends as biological risks ease, and long-term supply contraction supporting salmon spot prices.

Norne Securities' Mindaugas Čekanavičius maintains a BUY rating with a NOK 60 target arguing that farming measures linked to smolt genetics and seabased technology will gradually deliver results. DNB Carnegie is 11% below Bloomberg consensus on 2026 EPS because of conservative NOK currency assumptions but sits 2% to 6% ahead of consensus on 2027-2028 EPS modeling long-term recovery, while valuation methodology employs a DCF with 8.6% WACC at two-thirds weight and an SOTP at one-third, deducting NOK 2 per share for potential EU price-fixing fines.

The bearish counterweight argues that the sell-side may suffer from structural groupthink, as persistent biological issues and currency volatility threaten the NOK 55 bull case.

Peer-relative positioning and percentile ranking

Leroy Seafood Group ranks in the lower quartile of peer momentum, lagging market leaders SalMar and Mowi on margins and revisions, with Q1 2026 revenue expansion of just 2% year on year underperforming major peers and delivering an operational EBIT margin of 10.62% versus SalMar's 24%. DNB Carnegie slashed FY26 adjusted EPS by 25% on June 2, 2026, as the stronger Norwegian krone drove material earnings cuts, with sector-wide consensus downgrades averaging 18% across Mowi, SalMar, Bakkafrost, and Leroy.

Traditional pre-tax ROCE of 8% in 2025 fell far below Mowi's long-term average, while NWC-to-sales ratio is modeled at 21.50% for FY26 by DNB Carnegie as Norwegian salmon licensing costs have tripled capital employed across the sector since 2018. SalMar maintains a structural cost advantage of NOK 8 to 9 per kg over Leroy Seafood anchored by a premium portfolio of site locations, while Mowi recorded a strong operational EBIT margin of NOK 23 per kg in Norway during Q2 2025 and achieved a leading 17.5% return on capital employed over the last five years.

Ex-wildcatch EBIT margins are projected to reach NOK 11.5 per kg in 2026, up from NOK 6.7 per kg in 2025, yet Leroy continues to trail SalMar's NOK 8 to 9 per kg structural cost advantage due to superior site locations, while the group is expected to deliver a sector-leading 11.2% FCFE yield in 2026 due to its discounted valuation multiples.

Activist presence and ownership structure

Board Chairman Arne Møgster, elected in 2009, represents majority owner Austevoll Seafood which holds a 52.7% controlling stake, and there is no active activist presence in the AlphaSense library for Leroy Seafood Group or parent Austevoll Seafood. Shareholder-elected directors Drivenes, Munch, Møgster, and Dragesund were reelected in May 2025 for standard two-year terms, while the group established a Restricted Share Unit program on July 1, 2026, halving short-term cash bonuses from 100% to 50% of base salary and shifting the balance to RSUs with adopted guidelines requiring executive targets to integrate at least one sustainability KPI. The governance concern remains that extreme executive longevity breeds complacency while majority owner Austevoll's board control reduces independent oversight and blocks strategic governance renewal.

Material plausible problem to monitor and structural headwinds

Voluntary employee leavers ticked up to 9% in 2025 from 8% in 2024 showing minor retention pressure, while accident frequency registered at 12.0 per million hours in 2025 which remains a primary operational headwind. Heavy capital rollout of submersible pens and onshore post-smolt systems structurally lowers sea lice treatment frequency, yet the traffic-light system forced a 6% capacity reduction in production areas three and four with Lerøy Sjøtroll seeing its capacity temporarily cut by 2,500 tonnes maximum allowable biomass, while the retroactive 25% resource rent tax increases the nominal tax rate to 47% and impairs retained earnings available for technology investments.

Feed costs represent the single largest operating cost component and remain highly sensitive to disruptions in Peruvian anchoveta fisheries, where lower second-season quotas drove steep price spikes for global fishmeal and recommended cuts for 2026 North Atlantic pelagic catch threaten raw material availability. A strengthening Norwegian krone severely dilutes domestic price realizations for exported salmon, with Q1 2026 end-of-period NOK strength against the Euro negatively impacting NOK-denominated sales, while geopolitical conflicts in West Asia disrupted flight patterns and escalated freight costs to Asia.

The cross-cutting risk warns that a combined NOK appreciation and El Nino-driven feed cost spike could entirely wipe out the anticipated margin benefits of shielding technology.
