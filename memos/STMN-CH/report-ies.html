Straumann (STMN-CH): what the 28 October 2026 update does to the existing case

These results modestly strengthen the operating half of our case and materially weaken the return half: growth is at the rate the case needs, nothing else the case depends on has been tested, and the share price has moved through every level at which the case said we were paid.

One limit on everything below. The evidence we hold on the 28 October 2026 update itself is thin. The review is built on the Q1 2026 print and carries the third quarter only in fragments: a headline, a confirmed outlook, a handful of company disclosures and the titles of four broker notes. It gives no Q3 2026 regional split, no North American figure, no China figure and no estimate changes after the print.

1. What changed

  • Growth. The company's release of 28 October 2026 reports group organic growth accelerating to 9.2 percent in Q3 2026, against 7.1 percent in Q1 2026 (CHF 673 million of revenue). The case needs growth only to hold at about 9 percent and sets its alarm at a quarter below 7 percent. This is at the case's modal rate.
  • Outlook. The full-year 2026 outlook was confirmed on 28 October 2026. The review does not restate the margin figure for the third quarter. This was a sales update: it carries no margin, no gross margin and no cash.
  • Brazil. Bernstein's note of 29 October 2026 is titled "healthy growth outlook despite softer Brazil". The case had already recorded Latin America as a cylinder misfiring; it has not recovered. Neodent opened a third factory in Curitiba in September 2026.
  • Digital. The company reported in October 2026 that AXS has more than 20,000 active users, with revenue from active users growing faster than from non-users. The case recorded more than 15,000.
  • China. The Q3 2026 presentation says the rules for the second procurement round have not yet been announced, with implementation anticipated in Q1 2027. Management expects price cuts of 5 to 15 percent (Morgan Stanley, September 2026). Deutsche Bank warned in October 2026 that the lack of finalised rules introduces substantial competitive volatility for early 2027.
  • The market around it. Morgan Stanley's October 2026 survey is titled "Dental Sentiment Hits a New Low" and has practitioners expecting value brands to take share from premium over three to five years. A periodontist reported in October 2026 that implant placements at a specialty practice were down approximately 50 percent year on year in Q3 2026, on vanishing generalist referrals and cheaper systems.
  • Sell-side response. Morgan Stanley (28 October 2026): "Premium brand delivering". Bernstein: "Strong execution continues". Jefferies: "Good Quarter, Tough Crowd, Same Debates". Deutsche Bank: "Limited Scope to Outperform". Consensus EPS is up 1 percent over one month and the consensus target is CHF 116.94 (14 November 2026).
  • The price. CHF 107.2 on 13 November 2026, 14.2 percent above the 200-day average and within 3 percent of the 52-week high of CHF 109.8. The case was written at CHF 92.16 and says the return clears the bar below about CHF 89.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the acceleration is confirming the wrong driver. The case treats faster organic growth as the quarterly reading on premium share gains. The strongest alternative is that 9.2 percent is being earned away from the premium specialist channel on which the moat rests. Specialist sentiment is at a new low, one specialist practice has halved its placements because generalists have stopped referring, practitioners expect value to gain on premium, the softness is in Brazil while Neodent adds capacity, and the fastest-moving disclosed metric is a digital platform. On this reading the growth is real and the mix underneath it is moving toward general practitioners, value brands and equipment. That is the False Friend the case already names, but with a new twist the case does not hold: growth at the case's modal rate would then be evidence for the bear, not against it. For it to be right, the premium-to-value mix must have shifted in the second half, iEXCEL's share of premium volumes must have stalled, and the core gross margin excluding one-offs must come in below the 70 percent at which the case sets its alarm. A sales update cannot distinguish the two readings. We note that the channel evidence is one practice and one survey.

Reading two: the chief executive left over the strategy. The case rests on the departure being unexplained, and on the concern being transient: it clears if the successor confirms the 2030 targets at his first results. A former head of sales said in October 2026 that the departure was motivated by the shift toward the subscription-based AXS platform, which he calls a complete paradigm shift for an implant maker. If that is right, confirmation of the targets is the wrong test. The board would have chosen a change of business model over the person who built the implant record, and hired an external leader to carry it out. The targets could stand in name while their composition, their revenue timing and their margin path change. For it to be right, the successor's first statements must put the service model ahead of the implant core, or change how the platform is charged for. This is one former employee's belief and it is uncorroborated. The AXS figures above are also consistent with a board backing something that works.

Reading three: the clearing has been paid for before it has happened. The case found that the positives were not in the price and the concerns were. That is plausibly no longer true. On the case's own figures, with its earnings unchanged, the eighteen-month value of CHF 116.7 is about 9 percent above CHF 107.2. Its cleared branch (CHF 130.1) is about 21 percent above and its uncleared branch (CHF 105.3) is about 2 percent below, with the case's odds a little short of even on clearing. Neither dated event has occurred. For this reading to be wrong, earnings must be heading above the case's path, which a sales update cannot show.

We do not put up a fourth. The obvious bull alternative, that end-market demand is recovering, fails on the evidence: the surveys worsened.
