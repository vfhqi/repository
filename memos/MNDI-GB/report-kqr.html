Section 2 – European Containerboard Capacity: Start-ups, Conversions, and Closures

Section summary
  1. European containerboard supply will expand significantly in 2027 and 2028 through massive recycled machine start-ups.
  2. Industry analysts maintain medium confidence that this new recycled capacity will end testliner price hikes while leaving fresh-fibre kraftliner premium pricing largely intact.
  3. United States unbleached kraftliner exports to Europe fell below 300,000 tonnes due to massive domestic capacity closures and higher-margin domestic pricing.
  4. Industry experts maintain low confidence that significant US export volumes will return to European shores in 2027 and 2028 because domestic supply remains tight and producers prioritize local markets.

(2027-2028)

Major Capacity Additions

1.
Significant supply expansion: Palm has announced the conversion of its newsprint machine at the King's Lynn site in the United Kingdom. This project will add 700,000 tonnes of recycled containerboard capacity starting in March 2027. The massive scale of this machine represents a major threat because it introduces new recycled containerboard supply into an already oversupplied market.
2.
Impending supply pressure: Eren Paper is progressing with its massive Shotton project in the United Kingdom. This development is scheduled to add 750,000 tonnes of recycled containerboard capacity during the 2026 to 2027 period. Industry experts expect that this open market exposure will put sustained downward pressure on containerboard prices as the volume ramps up.
3.
Delayed capacity arrival: Progroup remains committed to building its new recycled containerboard mill in Stockstadt, Germany. However, the company recently indicated that commissioning will be delayed due to the challenging economic environment. This delay will push the arrival of new recycled capacity toward the end of the decade which offers temporary relief to regional supply balances.
4.
Impending recycled additions: International Paper is completing the installation of a new recycled containerboard machine at its Lucca mill in Italy. This new machine will replace an older 175,000 tonne machine and deliver 450,000 tonnes of annual nameplate capacity. Scheduled to come online in late 2026, this project further swells the recycled containerboard capacity pipeline entering 2027.
5.
External capacity influence: Turkish containerboard capacity expansions are poised to leak into the broader European packaging market. Recent additions include 650,000 tonnes at Kipas Kagit's Söke mill and 640,000 tonnes at Modern Karton's Zonguldak mill. Although these sites sit outside Western Europe, brokers warn that these tonnes will influence broader European pricing dynamics by competing for export markets.

Capacity Rationalization and Closures

6.
Positive rationalization steps: Smurfit Westrock completed the closure of its paper mill in Birmingham, United Kingdom, at the end of July 2026. This closure permanently removed 220,000 tonnes of recycled containerboard capacity from the market. While this action helps to offset some incoming supply, brokers calculate that much more capacity rationalization is required to balance European supply and demand.
7.
Potential capacity mitigation: Palm could offset some of its new United Kingdom production by closing older facilities in Europe. Analysts suggest that the company's 240,000 tonne Descantes mill in France is a prime candidate for closure. Closing this high cost mill would help Palm manage regional operating rates and supply balances post conversion.
8.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on 6 August 2026 that European recycled containerboard capacity ramp ups and conversions could reach 1.72 million tonnes over the next nine months. This massive supply expansion represents approximately 4% of the European installed base and could easily keep the fresh-fibre market in check. Consequently, the influx of recycled board might prevent fresh-fibre producers from maintaining their recent price increases.

Comparable Past Cases

9.
Historical capacity shock: European producers added substantial recycled capacity in 2025 through major graphic paper machine conversions. The primary projects included Norske Skog's 555,000 tonne Golbey mill in France, Heinzel's 470,000 tonne Laakirchen mill in Austria, and Mondi's 420,000 tonne Duino mill in Italy. These massive startups severely depressed regional operating rates and triggered a sharp price retreat in late 2025.
10.
Substandard startup returns: Jefferies estimated that the three new European machines ramping up from 2025 were deeply unprofitable during their initial operations. Together, the Golbey, Laakirchen, and Duino mills were estimated to be 30 million to 40 million euros EBITDA negative in 2025. This historical case suggests that the upcoming 2027 start-ups will likely face prolonged initial cash losses in an oversupplied market.

Broker Expectations on Pricing

11.
Divergent pricing expectations: Jefferies forecasts that the massive new recycled supply from Eren and Palm will severely pressure testliner pricing. The broker expects that the September 2026 recycled price increases of 60 euros per tonne will be completely given back in the second quarter of 2027. Conversely, the virgin containerboard market remains much tighter due to a total lack of major fresh fibre supply additions.
12.
Weakened market balance: Deutsche Bank reported in September 2026 that Western European containerboard capacity additions in 2025 net of closures resulted in 860,000 tonnes of new supply. This structural oversupply means that European operating rates will likely remain depressed around 85% through 2028. Without substantial permanent mill closures, prices are bound to decline when costs ease in 2027.

US Export Volume Contraction

14.
Collapsing trade volumes: US unbleached kraftliner shipments to Europe declined significantly as North American availability tightened. Analysis of USITC trade data shows a steady year-over-year decline beginning in August 2025, with sharp contractions in April and June 2026. Monthly export volumes dropped from over 50,000 metric tonnes in mid-2025 to approximately 29,000 metric tonnes by June 2026. This decline has restricted European supply, helping to support higher regional kraftliner pricing over recycled grades.
15.
Deepening annualized deficits: US containerboard exports to Europe fell by 33% year-to-date through August 2026. This structural shift represents an annualized decline of approximately 150,000 tonnes, or nearly 2% of total European virgin capacity. Integrated producers in Europe benefit from this supply curtailment because it tightens the market and sustains virgin premiums over recycled board. Consequently, European mill operators are insulated from the traditional threat of cheap imports from transatlantic competitors.
16.
Diverted global allocations: US unbleached kraft linerboard exports fell to 282,000 tonnes in late 2024. This massive drop was driven by strong domestic US corrugated box demand, which forced producers to divert volumes away from international export channels. This historic low highlighted the vulnerability of global buyers who rely on consistent US imports. When US domestic consumption rebounds, global export markets quickly experience a sharp reduction in shipped volume that drives price increases.

Domestic US Capacity Constraints

17.
Historic capacity rationalization: North American containerboard producers permanently shut down nearly 4 million short tons of capacity between early 2025 and mid-2026. This massive reduction represents approximately 10% of total regional capacity and is concentrated in unbleached kraft linerboard. Industry giant International Paper accounted for approximately half of these closures, retiring 2.2 million short tons. This supply discipline has structurally tightened the domestic market, leaving little surplus volume available for export to Europe.
18.
Recovering operating rates: US containerboard industry operating rates reached approximately 95% in mid-2026. This high utilization rate reflects a successful rebalancing of the market following unprecedented mill shutdowns rather than a massive surge in box demand. With operating rates hovering near capacity, domestic producers are focusing on meeting local demand rather than exporting. This sustained domestic tightness means that export availability will remain highly constrained unless local demand collapses.
19.
Persistent supply tightness: The restart of International Paper's 870,000 short ton Pine Hill mill in August 2026 failed to ease supply constraints. Lead times for domestic orders remained extended at five to six weeks, while export lead times stretched to over 100 days. This ongoing tightness is exacerbated by the fact that 60% of the permanently closed US capacity historically served export markets. Importers are consequently forced to diversify away from US kraftliner due to extreme local scarcity.

Domestic Pricing and Freight Dynamics

20.
Elevating domestic pricing: US domestic containerboard prices rose by $170 per ton during 2026. This substantial domestic price strength incentivizes producers to prioritize domestic box plants, where profit margins are significantly higher than in export markets. To narrow this price gap, US mills have aggressively raised export prices by $150 per ton to China and 180 euros to Southern Europe. This aggressive export pricing strategy is designed to maximize margins on remaining export tonnes sent abroad.
21.
Growing freight barriers: US containerboard exports face rising shipping costs and logistically complex supply chains. Higher global freight rates and tariffs have increased transit costs, making transatlantic exports less economically viable. Furthermore, European packaging producers face elevated wood and energy costs that hinder global cost-competitiveness. These compounding logistical and operational hurdles prevent a rapid resumption of trade between North America and Europe.
22.
Contrary evidence: US unbleached kraftliner exports for export surged by more than 44% year-over-year in the second quarter of 2024. According to the American Forest and Paper Association, this surge occurred before the massive capacity shutdowns of 2025 and 2026 took effect. This historical surge proves that North American mills can rapidly flood the global market with cheap kraftliner when domestic operating rates soften. Consequently, any future domestic demand downturn in the United States could quickly reignite export pressure on European pricing.

This historical import pressure only eased when US domestic consumption recovered and prolonged dock strikes disrupted shipping logistics. This precedent highlights how quickly US producers can disrupt European pricing power when domestic markets soften. It suggests that European price stability is highly dependent on continuous US economic growth.

Delayed capacity return: Rabobank Economic Research expects that new North American containerboard capacity additions are unlikely to emerge before 2027. Any future supply growth will likely be driven by gradual demand recovery and the conversion of other paper grades into containerboard. This delayed capacity return ensures that the North American market will remain structurally tight for the next 18 months. Therefore, European virgin producers should enjoy prolonged relief from US import threats through 2028.

Persistent export barriers: Metsa Board reported that trade barriers and high transport costs continue to limit transatlantic paperboard trade. US import tariffs and weak underlying economic activity in key markets have further suppressed delivery volumes. These structural trade barriers prevent producers from easily shifting volumes between the US and European markets. This insulation allows European mills to maintain their domestic price increases without fear of immediate arbitrage.

Section 4 – European Gas Price Spike and Recycled Containerboard Dynamics

Section summary
  1. The massive spike in European natural gas prices has shifted and steepened the cost curve for recycled containerboard producers.
  2. While high energy costs force extensive economic downtime at gas-reliant recycled mills, biomass-powered virgin mills face limited headwinds and enjoy wider price premiums.

Natural Gas Price Surge and Testliner Costs

1.
Severe energy inflation: European natural gas prices experienced a dramatic surge as the Dutch TTF benchmark climbed significantly during the second half of 2026. Reports from Rothschild and Company Redburn in October 2026 highlighted that European gas prices in September 2026 were up 133% year-over-year. This massive escalation in energy input costs has directly translated into higher utility bills for manufacturing operations across the continent. Consequently, industrial energy costs have returned to levels not seen since the peak of the 2022 energy crisis.

2.
Escalating cost push: The sharp rise in benchmark gas prices has immediately altered the cash costs of manufacturing recycled paper. Jefferies Research reported on 16 October 2026 that every 10 euros per megawatt-hour increase in Dutch TTF gas prices adds approximately 15 euros per tonne to recycled containerboard production costs. With gas prices climbing to 85 euros per megawatt-hour, energy costs now represent a staggering 24% of the average cash costs for recycled containerboard. This cost-push inflation has severely diminished the profit margins of non-hedged continental recycled paper mills.
3.
Elevated waste feedstocks: Recycled containerboard producers are facing dual cost pressures from both energy and their primary raw material inputs. Jefferies Research reported on 16 October 2026 that European old corrugated container prices hovered around 131 euros per tonne in late 2026. Because producing one tonne of recycled containerboard requires slightly more than one tonne of waste paper, these elevated OCC costs directly shift the base of the recycled cost curve upward. When combined with rising transport and chemical costs, the total cash cost to manufacture testliner has reached unsustainable levels.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some regional recycled producers have successfully insulated their operations from the immediate impact of spot energy inflation through extensive hedging programs. Reports from Jefferies Research in March 2022 showed that DS Smith had hedged 75% of its natural gas consumption for fiscal year 2023. Similarly, Smurfit WestRock was 50% hedged for its 2022 energy usage to mitigate severe margin compression. These financial hedges mean that large integrated players avoid immediate losses during sudden energy price spikes, although smaller, unhedged competitors face immediate cash deficits.
5.
Stable recycled pricing: Despite massive energy cost increases, recycled containerboard index prices in Europe remained flat during October 2026. Jefferies Research confirmed on 15 October 2026 that German recycled testliner prices stayed stable month-over-month. This price stagnation followed a cumulative increase of 160 euros per tonne achieved earlier in the year to recover previous cost inflation. However, the lack of further pricing progress indicates that integrated box makers face severe resistance when trying to pass secondary paper price hikes through to downstream corrugated customers.

Cost Curve Mechanics and Mill Operating Rates

6.
Steepened industry curves: The combination of high gas and raw material costs has dramatically steepened the European recycled containerboard cost curve. Jefferies Research reported on 16 October 2026 that with natural gas at 85 euros per megawatt-hour, approximately 20% of the recycled containerboard cost curve was operating at a cash loss. Earlier in March 2026, when gas spiked to 50 euros per megawatt-hour, Jefferies estimated that nearly 50% of unhedged recycled capacity was cash-negative. This steep curve ensures that marginal high-cost mills set pricing in the oversupplied European packaging market.
7.
Forced economic downtime: High energy expenses have compelled several high-cost European recycled mills to temporarily halt production. Jefferies Research reported on 16 October 2026 that elevated gas costs have driven a high amount of economic downtime to prevent cash losses. These curtailments are particularly common among smaller, non-integrated operations that lack the scale and financial backing of larger peers. Consequently, these operational halts have helped to prevent a massive inventory build across the structurally oversupplied European market.
8.
Persistent structural oversupply: The European containerboard sector continues to struggle with a significant imbalance between installed capacity and actual demand. Jefferies Research noted on 16 October 2026 that Western European containerboard capacity utilization remains depressed at approximately 85%. To restore a healthy supply-and-demand balance and raise operating rates toward 90%, the European industry needs to permanently close more than 2 million tonnes of capacity. Without these permanent closures, any easing of energy costs will likely trigger immediate recycled price declines as mills increase output.
9.
Eroded converting margins: Integrated paper and packaging companies are experiencing near-term profitability pressure because of the lag in pricing pass-through. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 29 October 2026 that Smurfit WestRock's corrugated converting margins fell below 2% as higher paper costs preceded box price increases. Management expects a more material price-cost benefit to emerge in the first half of 2027 as downstream box pricing catches up. Consequently, unexpected freight and energy inflation has delayed the company's expected margin recovery in North America.
10.
Unfavorable earnings revisions: The prolonged impact of energy and logistics inflation has forced major packaging producers to lower their near-term profitability forecasts. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 29 October 2026 that Smurfit WestRock reduced its full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance midpoint to 4.7 billion dollars. This reduction was driven by freight and energy costs representing a massive 700 million dollar yearover-year headwind. These persistent cost pressures demonstrate that energy inflation continues to delay the expected earnings recovery for integrated operators.

Widening Premium and Virgin Producer Advantages

11.
Widened price premium: The price spread between high-performance fresh-fibre kraftliner and recycled testliner has expanded to historic highs. Jefferies Research reported on 16 October 2026 that the virgin-to-recycled price premium reached 230 euros per tonne, driven by a tighter virgin market. Unbleached kraftliner prices reached 1,005 euros per tonne, whereas testliner index prices stood at 775 euros per tonne. This substantial premium reflects the superior strength and moisture resistance of fresh-fibre packaging. Consequently, integrated fresh-fibre mills have maintained elevated pricing power over recycled producers.
12.
Sustained virgin tightness: The European virgin containerboard market remains structurally tighter than the recycled grade due to limited supply additions. Jefferies Research reported on 16 October 2026 that unbleached kraftliner prices have risen by 200 euros per tonne off their 1Q26 lows. This pricing strength was supported by reduced imports from the United States following extensive North American mill closures. Because there are no major new fresh-fibre capacity startups planned in Europe, virgin pricing should remain resilient through early 2027.
13.
Biomass energy insulation: Integrated fresh-fibre producers generate the vast majority of their energy needs internally from renewable wood biomass. Reports from Jefferies Research in October 2026 highlighted that virginbiased players like Mondi, SCA, and Billerud are the relative winners of the gas shock. These producers utilize biomass combined heat and power plants, protecting them from volatile natural gas markets. Consequently, these companies are experiencing significant packaging margin expansion because they capture higher containerboard prices without facing equivalent energy cost headwinds.
14.
Operational energy leverage: Nordic paper producers enjoy highly favorable energy positions relative to their continental European competitors. DNB Carnegie reported on 2 October 2026 that Nordic forestry companies are in a structurally better energy situation. For example, Svenska Cellulosa AB achieved a 25% quarter-overquarter EBITDA increase in its third-quarter 2026 results, supported by improved net energy and lower maintenance. This localized resource advantage ensures that Nordic fresh-fibre mills remain highly profitable even during severe continental energy spikes.
15.
Other side of the story: While virgin-biased mills benefit from biomass-based energy generation, escalating wood costs in Central Europe present a major threat to their profitability. Jefferies Research reported in September 2026 that Mondi faced a 35 million euro half-on-half headwind due to rising Central European wood costs. This inflationary wood cost trend contrasts sharply with the easing timber prices enjoyed by Nordic peers. Consequently, high wood fiber costs could completely erase the margin benefits of elevated kraftliner prices for non-Nordic integrated mills.

Section 5 – Corporate Earnings Exposure: Pure Plays and Strategic Positioning

Section summary
  1. Virgin-biased containerboard producers Mondi, SCA, and Billerud offer high exposure to fresh-fibre pricing, but their pricing power remains constrained by raw material inflation and downstream box price pass- through difficulties.
  2. Industry estimates indicate that Mondi represents the highest earnings sensitivity to kraftliner prices, though lagging contract resets and structural overcapacity will likely test the endurance of recent pricing gains.

Pure Play Fresh Fibre Positioning

1.
Superior virgin exposure: Svenska Cellulosa AB represents a highly integrated Nordic forestry model that focuses almost exclusively on fresh-fibre products such as kraftliner and market pulp. Jefferies reported on October 26, 2026, that this vertical integration significantly reduces cost inflation risks and positions SCA as one of the most levered names to European kraftliner prices.
