Section 4 – Financial Infrastructure: Currency Exposures, Hedging, and Taxation

Section summary
  1. Bavarian Nordic faces notable transaction and translation exposures from its US dollar revenues, which are partially managed through a rolling twelve-month hedging program.
  2. The group's medium-term earnings are supported by non-time-limited Danish tax loss carry-forwards, though the exhaustion of Swiss tax assets increases local tax liabilities.

Currency Sensitivity and Hedging

1.
Significant exposure: Bavarian Nordic's financial performance remains highly sensitive to fluctuations in the US dollar as its current guidance is based on an assumed exchange rate of 6.30 DKK per USD. This company benchmark can be compared with external projections such as Nordea's published currency forecasts of 6.22 within 24 months and 6.07 by late 2028. A declining dollar exchange rate relative to the corporate benchmark will exert substantial downward pressure on EPS unless offset by increased sales volume.
2.
Active protection: Bavarian Nordic utilizes currency forward contracts to execute a rolling twelve-month hedging policy that helps mitigate short-term transaction risks. The company entered into forward contracts to sell USD 80 million at agreed rates between 6.31 and 6.47 DKK per USD to secure future cash flows. These hedges help stabilize highly volatile preparedness revenues but leave the company exposed to long-term structural shifts in exchange rates.
3.
Financial impact: Bavarian Nordic reported net foreign exchange losses of DKK 10 million in 2025, showing a significant shift from the net gain of DKK 67 million recorded in 2024. These fluctuations highlight the ongoing impact of unhedged currency exposures on the group's net financial items. This currency volatility introduces unpredictable swings in net earnings regardless of underlying vaccine demand.
4.
Hedging efficiency: Bavarian Nordic holds cash flow hedges for currency forward contracts of DKK/EUR at a fixed rate of 7.40 DKK per EUR. These contracts are deemed highly effective hedges of future transactions, ensuring stable pricing for European sales. This currency stability protects the European preparedness revenue from negative exchange rate fluctuations.

Swiss Franc Exposure and Swiss Operations

5.
Swiss costs: Bavarian Nordic maintains significant manufacturing operations in Bern, Switzerland, which creates a substantial cost base denominated in Swiss francs. This cost structure remains largely unhedged, making the firm's operating margins vulnerable to any appreciation of the Swiss currency. A strengthening Swiss franc relative to the Danish krone directly inflates manufacturing cost of goods and restricts overall EPS growth.
6.
Tax exhaustion: Bavarian Nordic fully utilized all of its Swiss tax loss carry-forwards in the Swiss tax jurisdiction during 2025. The elimination of these tax assets means that the Bern operations will face higher local tax liabilities going forward. This exhaustion of Swiss tax shields increases the effective tax burden on the group's European manufacturing profits.
7.
Swiss cost impact: Bavarian Nordic's unhedged Swiss franc cost base includes employee and operational expenses incurred by the Berna subsidiary. The group employed 1,609 full-time equivalent staff globally at the end of late 2024, with a notable portion based in Switzerland. These unhedged Swiss labor costs amplify the risk of margin contraction during periods of Danish krone weakness.

8.

Total tax expenses reached DKK 426 million in 2025 compared to a tax income of DKK 17 million in the previous year. This rising tax burden creates a major drag on EPS as profitability normalizes.
9.
Danish shields: Bavarian Nordic holds remaining nonrecognized tax loss carry-forwards in the Danish jurisdiction with a tax value of DKK 202 million as of December 31, 2025. These tax losses are not subject to any time limits under Danish joint taxation rules, ensuring longterm utility. The presence of these shields will keep Danish cash taxes low for the foreseeable future.
10.
Parent taxable: Bavarian Nordic reported parent company taxable income of DKK 377 million for 2025 after the active utilization of Danish tax losses. The parent company recognized no tax expense for the first half of 2024 due to the substantial size of these unrecognized tax assets. This tax mitigation strategy temporarily buffers net profit margins against rising operational costs.
11.
Pillar II Compliance: Bavarian Nordic became subject to the Minimum Tax Act under OECD Pillar II model rules starting in 2025. Management does not anticipate additional tax costs from this framework under the current group structure, but expects a non-insignificant compliance burden. This new regulatory framework adds notable administrative compliance costs that could marginally drag on net margins.
