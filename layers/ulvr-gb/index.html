Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external environment is shifting from favourable to moderately adverse. The single most important external fact for the model is that input costs turn into a material headwind in 2027 (company guide €550m to €750m net inflation) just as the emerging-market demand tailwinds (India GST, easy comparisons) lap, while developed-market volume remains structurally negative. Revenue growth can hold around 4% to 5% through price, but the composition shifts from volume to price, and the gross margin comes under pressure. FX translation remains a persistent 2% to 4% annual drag on reported figures. The Consumer should build the 2027 model with lower volume (about 1.5% to 2%), higher price (about 2.5% to 3%), gross margin flat to down before savings, and FX drag.

C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

F3316th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: competitive intensity is rising, in three distinct ways, and the direction is against margin expansion in developed markets.

  1. Peers are resetting margins to buy volume. Haleon cut guidance to about 2.5% organic growth and reset margins by about 150bps; Reckitt cut to 3.5% to 4.5% and is reinvesting; Beiersdorf reset consumer margin by about 250bps. When three peers simultaneously reinvest, promotional and marketing intensity on shelf rises for everyone. Unilever's own brand and marketing investment (16.1% of turnover in H1 2026) is now matched by peers spending their margin reset.
  2. Private label and retailer alliances strengthen in Europe and the US. Costco's Kirkland electrolyte product directly attacks Liquid I.V.; European buying alliances use private label as leverage; Beiersdorf's Nivea trades at about 6.4x the price of private label equivalents, the sharpest illustration of the price gap at stake.
  3. Emerging-market local competitors return as input costs fell in 2025 to 2026 and will be tested again as costs rise; in India, local players (Godrej, Dabur, Jyothy and the new direct-to-consumer brands) compete hard in detergents and personal care.

Offsetting factor: L'Oréal remains the only peer with clear momentum (Q3 +7.7%), and its strength is concentrated in prestige beauty, where Unilever's exposure (the prestige portfolio, for example K18 and Tatcha, a small part of Beauty & Wellbeing) is limited. In mass household and personal care, Unilever's relative position has improved in 2026 (share gains on its power brands per company commentary) because peers stumbled. The intensity rise is real but Unilever is currently the relative winner within it.

C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

F286th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: a clear and turning headwind. The 2025 to 2026 benign input cost period that funded margin and volume is ending, and 2027 net material inflation is guided at €550m to €750m (Deutsche Bank estimates €700m to €1bn).

  • Magnitude: Morgan Stanley's hedged-inflation estimate for 2025 to 2027 is 310bps of sales for Unilever, the highest in the peer group (Reckitt 170bps, Beiersdorf 190bps, L'Oréal 90bps), because Unilever's basket is heavier in crude-derived chemicals, surfactants, palm oil and packaging. The relevant basket rose about 20% year on year by late 2026. Hindustan Unilever is only about 29% hedged on crude derivatives, so India feels it faster.
  • Transmission: the H1 2026 gross margin already fell 70bps (to 46.8%). On the company guide, €550m to €750m is roughly 1.4% to 1.9% of a pro forma home and personal care turnover of about €39bn. Covering it with price requires price growth near or above 2.5%, which is exactly the threshold above which volume historically falls below 2% (Deutsche Bank).
  • Availability and supplier concentration: no material shortage; supplier concentration is low; Unilever's buying scale is an advantage in a rising cost period.

Implication: the 2027 gross margin should be modelled flat to down unless the €1bn savings programme and capex-led productivity (management: +20 to 30bps per year) offset the inflation. This is the central tension of the 2027 model: price-led recovery against volume elasticity.

C-07

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

F3523rd percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: macro bites hardest at the company level, through currency translation, rather than through demand.

  • Company level: about 86% of revenue is outside the euro area. FX cut 2025 turnover by 5.9%; the company guides about −2% to −3% FX on 2026 turnover and about −30bps on margin. Jefferies estimates about 4.4% annual FX drag medium term. Emerging-market devaluation (Argentina, Turkey, Nigeria, Indonesia, Brazil) is the recurring mechanism: high EM growth in local currency partly reflects inflation that devaluation then removes in euros.
  • Sector level: consumer staples is a defensive sector whose rating moves with bond yields and with the market's appetite for defensives. European staples re-rated about 12% in October 2026 to about 17x forward, still about the 20th percentile relative to the market.

Implication: model reported growth as USG plus a consistent FX drag (2% to 4% per year is the evidence-based range) plus M&A do not let EM USG convert one-for-one into euro profit growth.

C-01

To what extent is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically, and with how much visibility? (A cyclical swing counts when it has two-plus years of visibility.)

F4420th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: demand is bifurcated and the bifurcation widened in 2026; the strong half (emerging markets) is partly cyclical and has low visibility beyond two quarters, while the weak half (developed markets) shows a structural volume problem for branded staples. There is no cyclical swing with two-plus years of visibility.

  • Emerging markets (about 60% of turnover; the Change Forces report gives 62%) accelerated: Q3 2026 underlying sales growth (USG) +9.5% (volume +6.0%, price +3.3%). India (Hindustan Unilever) grew about 15% with volume about 7%. Part of this is cyclical and policy-driven: India's GST rate cuts (September 2025) lifted demand and lap from Q4 2026; Brazil's tax reform causes destocking in Q4 2026; Indonesia and China were easy comparisons after 2024 to 2025 weakness. Strip these out and the underlying EM run rate is plausibly 6% to 7%, not 9.5%.
  • Developed markets (about 40%) contracted in volume: Q3 USG −0.9% (volume −1.7%); North America −0.7% (volume −2.5%); Europe −1.7%. Scanner data show Unilever's Western European volume −4.7% and US volume −2.3% with price +4.5%. This is structural pressure on branded staples (private label gains, value-seeking consumers, retailer resistance, a weak US wellness subsegment), not a short dip: the European household and personal care volume trend has been negative for most of three years across peers (Beiersdorf Nivea −4.9%, Reckitt and Haleon cuts).
  • Visibility is short. Management raised underlying volume growth (UVG) guidance twice in 2026 (to at least 3%), but the Q3 volume (+2.9%) already slowed from Q2 (+5.5%) as price rose to +2.3%. The empirical rule from Deutsche Bank's work on the sector (volume growth below 2% when price exceeds 2.5%) suggests 2027 volume falls back as pricing is pushed to recover input costs.

Implication for the model: model EM and DM separately. EM USG mid-to-high single digit with an explicit fade from Q4 2026 as GST and comparison effects lap; DM USG around zero to +1%, volume negative to flat. Group volume for 2027 nearer 1.5% to 2% than the 3%+ of 2026.

To what extent is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved, and in which direction does it act on the company?

C+5775th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: the main disruption forces (social commerce, creator-led marketing, AI-mediated product discovery, direct-to-consumer brands) are strengthening, and Unilever is harnessing them better than most peers; the exception is US wellness, where the disruption cuts against it.
