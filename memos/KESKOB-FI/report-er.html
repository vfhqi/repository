Section 16 – Conclusion

The H1 2026 earnings event published on 22 July 2026 fundamentally validated Kesko Oyj's strategic pivot toward B2B technical trade consolidation while confirming that the group's comparable EBIT delivered in line with Vara consensus expectations despite mixed divisional performance. Management's decision to narrow full-year 2026 comparable EBIT guidance from €650-750 million to €670-730 million while preserving the unchanged mid-point of €700 million demonstrated measured confidence in the underlying operational trajectory without triggering excessive near-term enthusiasm.

The most material change to the investment case centers on the strategic transformation of Kesko's asset mix and divisional priorities. The confirmed €1.2 billion acquisition of Saint-Gobain's Dahl operations represents the largest transaction in Kesko's history and will establish building and technical trade as the largest segment, with illustrative combined annual sales of approximately €6.8 billion causing BTT to surpass grocery trade in sales for the first time.

This consolidation will sharpen divisional focus on professional contractors by shifting the B2B sales mix from 83% to approximately 88% while dramatically reducing home market reliance by raising the international share of BTT sales from 58% to approximately 71% on an illustrative 2025 basis. This structural reshaping elevates Kesko from a domestically concentrated Finnish grocery retailer into a pan-Nordic B2B technical distribution leader, fundamentally altering the risk-return profile and valuation framework appropriate for the equity. deviations at the divisional level revealed the uneven nature of Kesko's cyclical recovery.

Building and Technical Trade comparable EBIT beat Vara consensus by approximately 5% showing resilient professional customer demand, while Grocery Trade comparable EBIT undershot Vara consensus by 1% demonstrating slight margin softness, and Car Trade comparable EBIT missed Vara consensus by 13% as the division faced delayed order backlog deliveries. The critical positive deviation in BTT performance provided tangible evidence supporting the Dahl acquisition rationale, as professional demand in Onninen Finland surged by 16.1% in Q2, significantly outperforming the local market average growth of 0.6%, allowing Kesko to capture substantial B2B market share while heavy infrastructure and public utility demand offset weak Finnish residential starts.

Conversely, the material negative deviation in car trade reflected persistent consumer discretionary weakness that management expects to reverse through the car trade's 40% higher order backlog expected to drive stronger earnings in H2. The strongest positive driver validating the revised investment thesis emerged from Kesko's sustained grocery market share gains despite maintaining strict margin discipline. K Group's grocery market share continued its strong expansion throughout H1 2026, gaining 0.8 percentage points in Q2 alone according to PTY market data, while Kesko maintained a strong grocery operating margin of 6.8%, well aligned with its long-term target of clearly above 6%. This performance directly contradicts the prior market narrative that aggressive S Group competition and Lidl's discount pressure would force structural margin erosion, instead demonstrating that K Group captured 80 basis points of market share in the Finnish grocery market during Q2 2026, building on a 50 basis points gain from late 2025, confirming that Kesko's strategic pricing campaigns and hypermarket investments have successfully stabilised its position against S Group. The most compelling counter-hypothesis that could undermine the bullish B2B consolidation narrative centers on the prolonged structural stagnation of Nordic residential construction markets.

The domestic residential building sector is experiencing a prolonged depression due to high interest rates, with market-financed housing production failing to recover and housing starts projected to remain stuck at approximately 15,000 units for both 2026 and 2027, severely depressed compared to the estimated long-term annual housing need of 31,000 to 36,000 units in Finland. Central bank policies across the Nordic region continue to sustain elevated interest rates, with the key policy rate in Norway expected to remain high at 4% through 2027 compared to historically low levels of 0% in 2020, acting as a direct drag on the debt-servicing capability of Nordic households and corporate property developers.

If this high-rate environment persists structurally longer than management's base case anticipates, the massive capital deployed into Dahl and Danish acquisitions could generate depressed returns as a prolonged period of high interest rates across the Nordic region could exhaust the backlog of active renovation projects, causing a deeper building trade contraction, with new construction permits remaining frozen if Norges Bank and the Swedish Riksbank maintain restrictive rates through 2028.

What would change the analyst's mind toward a more cautious stance would be evidence of three critical negative inflection points. First, a sustained reversal in grocery market share trends where Kesko loses more than 50 basis points of share over consecutive quarters while simultaneously compressing operating margins below 6% would invalidate the defensive grocery thesis. Second, a failure of Onninen Finland's exceptional market outperformance to sustain into H2 2026, with quarterly growth rates converging back toward the anemic 0.6% local competitor average, would signal that professional B2B demand is exhausting its renovation backlog.

Third, meaningful delays in regulatory approval or post-merger integration milestones for the Dahl transaction extending the completion timeline beyond Q1 2027 would elevate execution risk and dilute the expected accretion profile. Conversely, positive catalysts that would reinforce conviction include tangible evidence of Nordic residential building permits inflecting positively with Finnish monthly housing starts recovering above 2,000 units sustainably, successful realization of the first €5-10 million in Dahl purchasing synergies within six months of closing, and continued grocery margin resilience above 6.5% despite intensifying competitive dynamics from S Group or international discounters.
