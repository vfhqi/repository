Outliers worth noting

1.
Exceptional EBITDA margin performance: Rubis captures an industry-leading 12% average EBITDA margin, representing double the downstream peer average of 6%, positioning the company in the top 5% of global fuel distribution operators. This margin premium is directly attributable to the company's unique concentration in highmargin regulated refining assets and localized niche retail distribution networks, particularly in island markets where logistics barriers limit competitive intensity. The margin structure appears sustainable given that retail pricing structures pass on raw material volatility to preserve underlying unit margins, though sudden oil price shifts trigger temporary inventory valuation swings that can obscure the true underlying operational performance. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 4] Exceptional free cash flow conversion: The group achieved over 100% free cash flow conversion historically between 2011 and 2022, placing Rubis in the top 5% of capital-intensive downstream distribution operators globally. This outlier performance reflects positive cash generation from working capital changes of €34 million in FY2025 and €39 million in FY2024, driven by the company's ability to manage inventory valuations during commodity price cycles and improve receivables cash flows by €13 million in FY2025 as government subsidy backlogs cleared. Peer DCC plc recorded working capital days of only 0.4 days in 2026 due to negative working capital models, establishing the competitive benchmark. However, total capital expenditure jumped 52% to €376 million in FY2025, driven by Renewable division capex increasing 132% to €190 million, which materially compressed recent free cash flow conversion as the company scales its Photosol solar platform. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 8]
2.
Extreme customer diversification: Rubis maintains no single customer representing 10% or more of consolidated revenue, with net customer receivables of €804 million containing €610 million in accounts not yet due and only €193 million in past-due balances, representing a bottom 5% customer concentration profile among midstream energy infrastructure operators. The distribution profile is balanced between B2C accounts at 52% of sales and B2B clients at 48%, fragmenting credit risk across thousands of independent service station operators, road contractors, airlines, and residential LPG customers. This stands in sharp contrast to the U.S. wholesale distribution market where the largest distributor controls only an 8% market share representing 15 billion gallons, yet individual customers within that operator's portfolio exhibit far higher concentration. The extreme diversification provides defensive resilience against individual customer defaults but simultaneously limits pricing leverage and contract negotiation power at the account level, as customer proximity and full logistics chain control prevent competitors from easily intercepting local accounts rather than long-term contractual exclusivity.
