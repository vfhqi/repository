Section 2 – Currency Sensitivity and Hedging Policies

Section summary
  1. Currency translation volatility remains a material driver of RELX's reported earnings due to massive US dollar revenue exposure.
  2. Detailed forward contracts and natural debt hedges effectively smooth near-term cash flow fluctuations across key divisions.
  3. Divergent broker exchange rate assumptions create significant variation in outer-year adjusted EPS projections for international investors.

Translation Sensitivities and Profits

1.
Significant translation volatility: RELX faces substantial reported earnings volatility because its financial statements are denominated in sterling while major operations occur overseas. According to company disclosures in its 2025 annual report, a 10% weakening of all foreign currencies would reduce net profit by £170m. Conversely, a 10% strengthening of those foreign currencies would increase net profit by exactly £170m. This sensitivity highlights how heavily reported net profits depend on FX translation trends.
2.
Dominant American exposure: The United States represents the single most important market for RELX, which directly amplifies translation risks from US dollar fluctuations. Company filings from 2026 show that North America contributed 58% of total revenue during the 2025 fiscal year. Under normal operating conditions, this means that even minor movements in the GBP/USD exchange rate can dramatically alter the reported sterling revenue. This concentration means that US dollar weakness directly depresses RELX's headline growth rates.
3.
Comparable peer sensitivity: Wolters Kluwer exhibits a similar geographic profile, generating over 60% of its revenues and adjusted operating profits in North America. According to its half-year 2026 report, each 1 U.S. cent move in the EUR/USD exchange rate causes a 4.5 euro cent change in diluted adjusted EPS. This sensitivity represents an increase from prior periods when a 1 cent move caused a 3 euro cent change. Consequently, both RELX and Wolters Kluwer see their earnings heavily distorted by translation effects annually.

Forward Contracts and Hedging Books

4.
Multiyear subscription protection: RELX actively manages transaction risks by using derivative financial instruments to lock in future conversion rates. Management's policy allows the business to enter forward foreign exchange contracts for up to 50 months specifically for STM subscription revenues. This long duration reflects the highly predictable nature of scientific and medical database subscriptions. Consequently, these forward contracts smooth out short-term currency shocks for Elsevier's division.
5.
Massive hedging principal: The principal amount of forward foreign exchange contracts held by RELX reached £1,306m at the end of the 2025 fiscal year. According to company accounts, this portfolio is heavily frontloaded with £563m maturing in 2026 and £495m maturing in 2027. The remaining principal includes £234m for 2028 and £14m for 2029. This structured maturity profile ensures that highly predictable future cash flows are shielded from sudden sterling appreciation.
6.
Synthetic debt comparison: Pearson utilizes an active hedging program that combines cross-currency swaps, forwards, and collars to manage its 69% dollar revenue exposure. Pearson's 2025 annual report shows it held foreign exchange forwards and swaps with a notional value of £324m along with £334m in collars. This derivative framework is designed to create synthetic US dollar debt as an asset hedge. These derivative portfolios reduce overall income statement volatility for sterling-denominated educational publishers.

Cost Matching and Natural Hedges

7.
Balanced balance sheet: RELX mitigates translation exposure on its net assets by systematically denominating its long-term borrowings in foreign currencies. Company treasury documents confirm that the majority of net debt is issued in US dollars and euros to match geographic earnings. This matching policy means that when sterling strengthens, the reduction in foreign asset values is offset by a decrease in translated net debt. Thus, the capital structure acts as a natural hedge of the group's balance sheet.

In 2025, EBITDA grew to £3,846m with an outstanding cash flow conversion rate of 99%. Deutsche Bank forecasts that strong underlying operating momentum will continue to drive EBITDA higher.
