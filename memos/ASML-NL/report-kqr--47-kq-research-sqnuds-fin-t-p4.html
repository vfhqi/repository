EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This research investigates the specific inputs that will drive ASML Holding NV's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years, focusing on Low-NA EUV units and ASPs for 2027 and 2028, High-NA EXE units and pricing, DUV immersion capacity, Installed Base Management components, gross-margin drivers, and capital allocation dynamics. The investigation draws from one hundred and fifteen relevant sources across company filings, transcripts, and broker reports to provide a comprehensive assessment of the earnings trajectory through 2028.

The evidence demonstrates that ASML's future earnings per share will be driven primarily by four interconnected dynamics: capacity expansion in extreme ultraviolet lithography systems, value-based pricing negotiations that flow directly to gross margins, high-margin software upgrades within the Installed Base Management segment, and the regulatory environment governing shipments to China. Low-NA EUV system deliveries and value-based pricing negotiations represent the primary drivers of future revenue and gross margin expansion, with Jefferies noting that if ASML successfully delivers eighty-eight low-NA EUV systems in 2027, gross margin could expand to 58.30%.

However, the magnitude and timing of these drivers remain contested across sell-side research, creating a wide dispersion in 2028 earnings per share projections that ranges from €68.47 to €91. The key question can be partially answered based on current evidence, with high confidence in near-term capacity targets and installed base dynamics but meaningful uncertainty around 2028 shipment ceilings and High-NA adoption rates.

ASML's planned low-NA capacity increases of 30% in both 2027 and 2028 will fail to meet customer demand, and management is actively investigating ways to expand capacity beyond its previously stated targets, indicating that the company's prior base of eighty-five systems in 2027 represents a baseline rather than an absolute cap. ASML's Installed Base Management segment generated €3.29 billion in the third quarter of 2026, exceeding official corporate guidance by nearly €400 million, driven by extremely strong customer demand for lithography productivity upgrades. Section 2 – Low-NA and High-NA EUV Shipments and Pricing Dynamics

  2. Slower High-NA EUV adoption by TSMC until 2030 presents near-term gross margin dilution but is offset by Intel's early volume production milestones.

Low-NA EUV shipment and capacity targets

1.
Expanding low-NA output: ASML is scaling up its shipments of low-NA extreme ultraviolet systems to meet robust market demand. In its pre-recorded earnings call of 14 October 2026, ASML management projected total shipments of approximately sixty-five low-NA systems in 2026, registering an increase of nearly 50% compared to the forty-four units delivered in 2025. This rapid acceleration demonstrates strong execution on immediate manufacturing ramps to support advanced semiconductor nodes. Consequently, these higher shipment volumes will fuel near-term earnings growth as long-term customer agreements provide high visibility.
2.
Insufficient future capacity: Planned production targets for the coming years are proving inadequate to satisfy escalating industry orders. ASML's press release of 14 October 2026 noted that planned low-NA capacity increases of 30% in both 2027 and 2028 will fail to meet customer demand. Therefore, management is actively investigating ways to expand capacity beyond its previously stated targets. This indicates that the company's prior base of eighty-five systems in 2027 represents a baseline rather than an absolute cap, unless supply chain bottlenecks emerge.
3.
Sell-side capacity debates: Securities analysts are parsing the difference between corporate statements and buy-side expectations for future tool shipments. Morgan Stanley's note of 15 October 2026 highlighted that while ASML confirmed it must raise capacity beyond its prior 30% guideline, it did not formally increase the 2028 target of one hundred and ten low-NA EUV systems. This target fell short of the one hundred and twenty to one hundred and twenty-five systems that buy-side investors were expecting. Consequently, Morgan Stanley notes that limited capacity visibility may constrain relative share performance through the fourth quarter of 2026, unless ASML provides clearer long-term frameworks.
4.
Supply chain dependencies: Technical bottlenecks in global manufacturing sites are critical gating items for accelerating EUV production. Deutsche Bank's research note of 15 October 2026 stated that ASML's ability to improve its 2028 low-NA EUV output is dependent on module-build capacity across its global footprint, particularly in Wilton, USA, and Taiwan. These localized manufacturing centers must ramp up in tandem with the broader supply chain to unlock shipment upside. Any delays in module fabrication will constrain earnings growth, particularly since the 2028 low-NA framework remains a key unresolved variable for investors.
5.
Price-led revenue upside: Negotiated value-based price increases represent a highly supportive driver for the mid-term earnings trajectory. Bernstein's report of 14 October 2026 noted that completed customer negotiations will raise prices for new orders across critical EUV and deep ultraviolet tools. These price adjustments are expected to begin contributing to revenue in 2027, with the full effect visible in 2028. Consequently, this pricing leverage should flow directly to the bottom line, providing meaningful upside to 2028 earnings per share.
6.
Contrary evidence: While consensus assumes severe tool tightness will persist, dynamic random-access memory capacity expansions will reduce supply tightness by 2028. Slower growth in artificial intelligence data center spending or power shortages represents material headwinds to tool demand. Under these conditions, the recurring software upgrade revenue that currently boosts gross margins would likely reverse, negatively impacting earnings.

High-NA EUV pricing and gross margin dynamics

7.
Dilutive margin structures: Next-generation lithography systems introduce near-term profitability headwinds despite their extreme technological capabilities. Morgan Stanley's note of 15 October 2026 estimated that initial High-NA EXE gross margins are dilutive at approximately 20%. These weak initial margins are driven by high manufacturing ramp costs and a limited initial shipment volume. Consequently, gross margins are highly dependent on volume absorption, so the dilutive headwind will only abate once the High-NA platform achieves commercial-scale manufacturing.
8.
Premium pricing justification: Extremely high hardware prices require customers to achieve substantial productivity gains to justify adoption. A vacuum solutions industry expert noted that High-NA systems command pricing close to $400 million, which is double the price of a standard low-NA tool. This immense cost is justified because High-NA's single exposure replaces complex double or quadruple patterning on low-NA tools. Consequently, this transition simplifies the fabrication process and can lower overall equipment spending on etching, deposition, and cleaning tools.
9.
Intel's early adoption leadership: The frontrunner in advanced node scaling is actively qualifying the next-generation lithography platform. Intel Foundry's press release of 8 September 2026 announced that the company has processed over one million wafers using High-NA systems. These tools are running in high-volume production for select layers of its upcoming Core Ultra Series 3 processors, code-named Panther Lake, built on the 18A platform. This early milestone validates the high-volume readiness of High-NA systems, demonstrating the close technical collaboration between the two manufacturers.
10.
TSMC's conservative timeline: The world's leading foundry is delaying adoption to optimize cost and technical maturity. TSMC's Chief Executive Officer stated in an earnings call on 15 October 2026 that the company will adopt High-NA EUV around 2030 to ensure reasonable costs and optimal maturity. Furthermore, the company is collaborating with ASML to establish a twelve-inch photomask pilot line by 2031, aiming for full production readiness by 2033. This conservative roadmap delays meaningful High-NA revenues for ASML, as TSMC prioritizes economic feasibility and manufacturing maturity over premature adoption.
11.
Samsung and memory insertions: Advanced memory manufacturers represent the fastest-growing demand segment for next-generation lithography tools. Samsung Electronics announced in a press release of 8 September 2026 that it plans to introduce High-NA into DRAM high-volume manufacturing by 2028. This represents an industry-first memory insertion, which is expected to extend the dynamic random-access memory scaling roadmap. Consequently, memory demand should become a major revenue driver for ASML's High-NA business, reinforcing the strategic collaboration between the two companies.

Peer technology trends and lithography intensity

12.
Shifting process intensity: Advanced transistor architectures are altering the capital expenditure mix across the semiconductor equipment sector. Rothschild & Co Redburn's report of 1 October 2025 noted that the pivot to gate-all-around transistors and backside power networks will tilt capital intensity toward deposition and etching. This structural shift initially dilutes lithography's share of leading-edge logic capital expenditure. However, this headwind is mitigated by rising dynamic random-access memory complexity, where extreme ultraviolet lithography intensity is expanding rapidly.
13.
Competitor technology alternatives: Rival equipment manufacturers are developing highly efficient non- lithography tools to optimize chip yields. CCB International's research note of 10 September 2024 reported that Tokyo Electron, Applied Materials, and Lam Research are developing more efficient tools for etching, deposition, and heat treatment. These developments occur as advanced lithography tools become increasingly expensive, pressuring foundries to demand greater efficiency elsewhere. Consequently, if peers successfully lower double-patterning costs, chipmakers may delay multi-million dollar scanner purchases, thereby capping ASML's near-term shipments.
14.
Other side of the story: Slower High-NA adoption may actually benefit ASML's near-term profitability by extending the lifecycle of mature platforms. Jefferies Research's note of 14 August 2026 argued that delayed High-NA adoption forces chipmakers to rely on multiple-patterning using existing low-NA tools. Because low-NA EUV scanners are highly profitable and have fully amortized development costs, this extension supports superior gross margins. Thus, a slower transition to High-NA reduces capital risk and protects ASML's cash flow, unless logic competitors achieve an unexpected technological breakthrough.
