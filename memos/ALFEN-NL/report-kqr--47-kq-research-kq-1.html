Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Alfen's customer concentration risk is heavily structural and dominated by two major Dutch Distribution System Operators that together generated 34.8% (€151.4m) of group revenues in FY25, and uncovering the exact customer identities and contractual terms behind this concentration is essential to establish 18-to-36-month EPS visibility and underwrite a 25%+ IRR thesis. While regulatory disclosure boundaries prevent absolute confirmation of individual counterparty names and revenue splits, the convergence of evidence across segment dynamics, geographic concentration, framework contract structures, and operational characteristics establishes high confidence that the two top customers reside within the Smart Grid Solutions segment and represent multi-year framework agreements with Dutch grid operators rather than lumpy utility-scale Energy Storage projects. The combined top-two customer revenue contribution increased from €118.6m in FY24 to €151.4m in FY25, lifting top-two concentration from 24.3% to 34.8% of total group turnover, while the Netherlands represented €400.7m (92.0%) of consolidated FY25 group revenues by entity domicile, underscoring that both top customers are Dutch entities. Note 84 of Alfen's 2025 Annual Report explicitly identifies Enexis and Liander as representing a significant portion of the Smart Grid Solutions business line, and Alfen maintains long-term framework agreements with all three core Dutch grid operators, including Stedin, which covers the third largest regional territory in the Netherlands. These three operators together control approximately 95% of the Dutch regional grid market, creating a structurally concentrated customer landscape that aligns precisely with the reported revenue concentration dynamics.

Smart Grid Solutions revenue with Dutch grid operators is underpinned by multi-year framework agreements, such as the 4-year contract with Liander that includes optional extensions for up to four additional years, while Alfen was awarded a 4-year framework agreement with Stedin with an option for two 2-year extensions, securing approximately 50% of Stedin's total required volume. Contractual framework agreements incorporate price indexation mechanisms to pass through underlying component and raw material inflation to utility partners, providing structural margin protection while delivering recurring revenue visibility across multi-year horizons. This contractual architecture stands in stark contrast to the lumpy project dynamics observed in Energy Storage, where utility-scale BESS deployments generated €125.6m in FY25 revenue with cost-to-cost progress milestones creating intense quarter-to-quarter revenue swings, and Q1 2026 ESS revenue surged 145% year-on-year to €50.7m as major milestone completions concentrated turnover into the first half of the year. - Earnings Calls The EV Charging segment can be structurally excluded from contributing to top-two customer concentration, as total EV Charging segment revenue fell 21.2% to €120.8m in FY25, rendering it mathematically impossible for any single charging client to account for the top-two concentration total of €151.4m, while the largest individual EV charging counterparties generate low single-digit percentages of consolidated revenue, remaining far below the 10% annual report disclosure threshold.

The segment operates as a highly fragmented pure B2B flow business with revenue distributed across public utilities, charge point operators, system integrators, retailers, and electrical wholesalers across multiple European jurisdictions, making sustained high customer concentration commercially implausible. Despite multi-year frameworks, grid operators scaled down order quantities in 2025 due to severe labor shortages and nitrogen-related project permitting delays, creating temporary mid-term revenue lumpiness that obscures the underlying structural predictability of DSO framework contracts.

However, Dutch distribution operators project substation installations to increase by 13% annually through FY28 to alleviate regional grid constraints, while major Dutch grid operators secured substantial capital backing, including €500m in EIB financing for Enexis and Stedin respectively. Pending legislation under the Dutch Crisis Act and National Action Programme aims to streamline permitting timelines and accelerate grid infrastructure buildouts, positioning framework contract execution for significant volume acceleration once municipal bottlenecks ease.

The strategic positioning of customer concentration within Smart Grid framework contracts carries profound second-order implications for EPS predictability and IRR potential. Grounding top customer concentration in Dutch grid operator framework contracts provides unmatched multi-year revenue visibility that stabilizes baseline earnings, and Jefferies highlights that four-to-eight-year contracts with operators like Enexis and Stedin lock in structural substation demand through 2030.

High-volume substation deliveries under grid frameworks generate predictable cost absorption across Almere manufacturing lines, and ING research emphasizes that volume recovery in Smart Grid Solutions is the primary catalyst required to rebuild group adjusted EBITDA margins toward double-digit targets. This operating leverage dynamic enables a concentrated long-only investor to underwrite earnings recovery with substantially higher conviction than if top customer exposure resided in lumpy utility-scale storage projects.

Critical data gaps remain around exact individual customer revenue splits, guaranteed minimum purchase commitments, and volume call-off caps within framework agreements, but analyzing statutory annual reports and municipal budget filings of Enexis, Liander, and Stedin quantifies historical capital expenditure allocations to Alfen, while formulating direct Q&A requests during post-earnings calls can isolate whether top customer concentration resides entirely within Smart Grid Solutions. Conducting structured expert interviews with former DSO procurement officers reveals specific framework pricing indexation mechanisms and duration extension terms, while reviewing PwC audit sampling procedures for contract progress confirms whether customer-specific project work accounts for the majority of over-time revenue. These targeted resolution pathways provide clear mechanisms to achieve full contractual certainty without requiring direct management disclosure of customer identities.

The investment thesis hinges on whether top customer concentration delivers structural earnings resilience or transient project volatility. Reaching a 25%+ IRR target requires confidence in broker consensus EPS accelerating from €0.21 in FY26E to €0.45 in FY27E and €0.77 in FY28E, and this earnings trajectory is materially more credible under a framework contract concentration scenario than under lumpy Energy Storage project exposure.

High EPS predictability from framework agreements allows long-only investors to underwrite an 18- month earnings recovery with high conviction, and Jefferies projects consensus EPS to compound rapidly from FY26E levels, driving net profit recovery through FY28E. The preponderance of evidence across segment revenue scale, geographic concentration, disclosed customer references, contract structures, and operational execution patterns establishes that Alfen's top-two customer concentration is framework-led rather than project-driven, providing the multi-year earnings visibility required to support concentrated portfolio exposure and target IRR thresholds.
