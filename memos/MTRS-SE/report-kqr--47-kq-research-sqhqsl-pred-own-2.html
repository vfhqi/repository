Section 2 – Structure of Borrowings, Facilities and Leases

Section summary
  1. Munters Group AB maintains a diversified debt structure comprising bank loans, commercial paper, and laddered green bonds.
  2. Capitalized lease obligations from strategic facility expansions have elevated the consolidated leverage ratio to 3.4x.

Bank Facilities and Credit Programs

1.
Diversified bank facilities: Munters refinanced its core financing agreements in 2025 to optimize liquidity and secure long-term capital. The group established sustainability-linked facilities totaling MUSD 200 and MEUR 250 with a consortium of banks. These new credit facilities have a three-year maturity that can be extended by up to two years. This refinanced structure replaced older bilateral agreements that were scheduled to mature in 2026.
2.
Expanding total credit: The overall corporate credit envelope has been systematically expanded to support rapid data center growth. Munters reported that its total loan facilities portfolio, including unutilized credit facilities, grew to approximately MSEK 8,450 by late 2025. This represents a solid increase from the MSEK 8,100 facility portfolio reported at the end of fiscal 2024. Securing these unutilized lines provides the company with crucial operational headroom during capital-intensive periods.
3.
Weighted maturity extension: Strategic debt restructuring has systematically pushed out the average maturity profile of the group's liabilities. Following the 2025 bank refinancing, the weighted average maturity of outstanding loans increased to 2.8 years. This maturity extension is highly favorable compared to the brief average maturity of 1.6 years recorded at the end of 2024. This longer duration insulates Munters from immediate refinancing shocks in volatile credit markets.
4.
Floating rate exposure: The bank debt portfolio remains highly sensitive to macroeconomic interest rate adjustments because of its floating rate structure. Munters utilizes variable interest rates for its primary bank loans and revolving credit lines. Historical disclosures show that the average interest rate was 4.7% for longterm borrowings and 5.3% for short-term debt in 2024. Consequently, the group remains exposed to interest rate increases unless it implements hedging derivatives.

Medium-Term Notes and Green Bonds

5.
Establishing credit programs: Munters established corporate market debt frameworks to reduce its historical dependence on bilateral bank lending. The group finalized a SEK 5bn Medium-Term Note program alongside a Commercial Paper program in 2025. The commercial paper framework has an authorized maximum limit of SEK 2bn to fund short-term needs. This twin-track strategy allows the company to tangibly diversify its capital funding sources away from traditional bank channels.
6.
Green bond framework: Sustainable financing has become a central component of the group's capital structure strategy. Munters established a dedicated Green Bond Framework evaluated by Moody's Ratings to issue notes under the MTN program. This framework is fully aligned with the International Capital Market Association's green principles. Adhering to these principles ensures that proceeds target climate change mitigation projects such as low-emission manufacturing.
7.
Laddered bond maturities: The group successfully issued three tranches of green bonds under its MTN program to fund sustainability projects. Munters has SEK 2bn in total outstanding green bonds laddered across multiple maturities. These bonds consist of a SEK 200m tranche maturing in 2028, a SEK 800m tranche maturing in 2029, and a SEK 1bn tranche maturing in 2030. This laddered debt schedule prevents concentrated refinancing bottlenecks in the medium term.
8.
Capital allocation tracking: The proceeds from green bonds are strictly monitored to ensure compliance with green investment mandates. Group Treasury manages a Green Debt Register to track the allocation of issued funds to green projects. Munters had allocated SEK 1.4bn out of the SEK 2bn outstanding to eligible projects by mid-2026. This stringent tracking is critical because unallocated funds must be held in lowyielding liquidity reserves.

Commercial Paper and Lease Structure

9.
Short-term liquidity tools: Munters actively utilizes its commercial paper program to manage seasonal working capital fluctuations. The company expanded its outstanding commercial paper issues during late 2025 to enhance near-term liquidity. Managing short-term obligations is vital because the group has experienced negative operating cash flow from inventory build-up. This commercial paper program serves as a flexible buffer to absorb cash swings before long-term financing is secured.
10.
Capitalizing lease liabilities: Lease obligations represent a major and growing portion of the company's overall debt profile. Munters leases multiple offices, vehicles, and critical manufacturing facilities globally to support its footprint. Under IFRS 16 rules, the group's capitalized lease liabilities reached MSEK 1,083 by the end of 2024. These non-cancelable lease contracts represent fixed cash drainage obligations that behave identically to interest-bearing borrowings.
11.
Expanding operational footprint: Recent factory expansions have materially driven up the capitalized lease obligations of the group. Capitalized interest-bearing lease liabilities grew significantly by mid-2026 due to the expanded Daleville, Virginia campus. This expansion pushed total lease liabilities to MSEK 1,462 in noncurrent liabilities and MSEK 280 in current liabilities by late 2025. These expansions have elevated the consolidated leverage ratio to 3.4x adjusted EBITDA.
12.
Undiscounted lease exposure: The undiscounted maturity of the group's leasing commitments shows substantial long-term cash requirements. Undiscounted lease obligations at the end of 2024 stood at MSEK 238 due within one year, MSEK 573 within five years, and MSEK 545 thereafter. The total undiscounted leasing commitments reached MSEK 1,356, reflecting long-term factory commitments. This long-term cash exposure remains a persistent drag on future net cash generation.

Covenant Definitions and Calculation Methodology

1.
Core bank covenant definition: Munters' primary credit agreements with its banking consortium dictate a single financial covenant to regulate external borrowings. This structured testing interval ensures that the group's financial risk remains aligned with bank expectations.
2.
Crucial accounting exclusions: The calculated leverage ratio for covenant compliance purposely deviates from standard consolidated balance sheet metrics. The accounting standard for leases, IFRS 16, does not affect the covenant calculation according to the loan agreement definition. Furthermore, net pension liabilities are also explicitly excluded from the consolidated net debt definition. This framework insulates Munters' bank compliance from lease-driven balance sheet inflation.
3.
Leverage monitoring procedures: Group management maintains a rigorous internal protocol to track and forecast covenant compliance. The group's forecasted performance in relation to the limits stipulated by the covenant is closely monitored. This continuous monitoring ensures that the group fulfills its commitments to external lenders and minimizes liquidity and financing risks. Maintaining this financial discipline is vital during capitalintensive manufacturing expansions.
