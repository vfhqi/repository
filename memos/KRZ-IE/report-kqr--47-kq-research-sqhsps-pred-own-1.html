Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group's ability to transfer expected 2027 raw material cost increases through pricing is critical to defending its earnings per share and maintaining its long-term operating margin targets.
  2. Kerry Group utilizes dynamic pricing models covering approximately half of its portfolio with automatic raw material cost pass-through contracts.
  3. The pass-through lag ranges from real-time pricing on commoditized contracts to at least two months for regional adjustments and up to a year for protective foodservice agreements.
  4. Confidence in these pass-through structures is high based on detailed commercial expert insights, company terms of sale, and broker research from Bernstein and Deutsche Bank.

The Key Question: "How will Kerry Group plc (KRZ-IE) pass through the raw material inflation it expects in 2027 in dairy, spices, natural oils and citrus, and in freight and energy after the October 2026 oil price rise?"
2.
Broad research: This comprehensive investigation evaluates 86 relevant documents across corporate filings, expert interviews, and broker reports, focusing on six primary companies. The analysis utilizes all 86 identified sources to assess pricing structures, commercial strategies, and the historical inflation precedent. No peer companies were excluded from this report because comprehensive coverage is required.

Deutsche Bank forecasts Kerry's adjusted EPS to grow by 12.5% in fiscal 2027 to €5.86, which assumes successful margin defense through pricing. If price adjustments lag behind cost increases, the resulting margin compression will directly depress profitability.

Section 3 – EBITDA and Margin Behavior in the 2021-2022 Inflationary Precedent

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group's historical 2021-2022 performance demonstrates that absolute EBITDA can grow during severe inflation despite substantial mathematical margin dilution.
  2. Comparative industry evidence shows that Kerry's pricing pass-through model defended profitability more effectively than Givaudan and Symrise during the peak inflation period.

Reconstructing Kerry's Margin Performance

1.
Significant expansion: Kerry Group successfully increased its absolute group EBITDA by 12.9% in fiscal year 2022 to reach €1.2 billion despite severe macroeconomic cost pressures. This growth was supported by solid volume gains of 6.1% and group pricing actions that rose 11.7% during the year. The group's performance proved that absolute cash generation can expand during high cost inflation even as margins undergo temporary pressure. This absolute earnings defense is critical because it underpins the company's continuous capital deployment capabilities.
2.
Progressive dilution: Kerry Group experienced a steady step-up in quarterly pricing throughout 2022 with price increases starting at 4.6% in the first quarter and accelerating to 11.7% by the fourth quarter. This escalating pricing was required to pass through raw material cost inflation of over 20% during the period. However, recovering these absolute costs caused a reported margin dilution of 120 basis points to 13.9% because of the mathematical denominator effect. Management adjusted short-term incentive targets to exclude this dilutive pricing effect, which reached 160 basis points in internal calculations.
3.
Distinct segmentations: Kerry Group reported a group EBITDA margin of 12.8% in the first half of 2022 before recovering to a stronger relative performance in the second half of the year. The core Taste and Nutrition division proved highly resilient, generating an annual EBITDA margin of 16.5% despite facing 180 basis points of pricing dilution. Meanwhile, the Dairy Ireland division bore the brunt of commodity spikes with pricing up 36.0% and its EBITDA margin compressing to 4.6% for the full year. This performance shows that retail dairy channels remain more vulnerable to severe cost inflation than high-technology formulations.
4.
Contrary evidence: Kerry Group suffered significant volume deterioration and margin collapse in its commodity- exposed consumer segments as pricing actions failed to protect profitability. The Dairy Ireland division saw its fourth-quarter volumes plunge by 4.0% as customers pushed back against escalating prices. Additionally, regional sales directors reported losing 45% of key sugar-reduction deals to competitors because rigid internal plant costs made pricing uncompetitive. This evidence reveals that aggressive pricing can trigger severe customer pushback and volume losses when applied to less differentiated portfolios.
