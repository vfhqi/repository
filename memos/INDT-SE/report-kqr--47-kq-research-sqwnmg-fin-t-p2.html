Section 2 – Gross Margin Drivers and Portfolio Pricing Resilience

Section summary
  1. Indutrade's gross margin expansion is driven by structural pricing power, high-margin acquisitions, and product mix shifts.
  2. Exchange rate fluctuations present a cyclical driver where a strong krona lowers import costs for trading subsidiaries.
  3. Comparative analysis shows Indutrade maintains resilient margins but trades at a structural margin discount to peers like Lifco and Diploma.

Portfolio gross margin progression drivers

1.
Upward margin progression: Indutrade's gross margin climbed to 35.4% in 2025 and reached 35.6% in the second quarter of 2026. This positive trajectory demonstrates that the group successfully defended its profitability despite soft organic volumes. Managing directors maintained strict pricing discipline to ensure that rising input costs were fully passed through to end customers. Consequently, these pricing actions served as the primary driver of gross margin resilience throughout recent cyclical downturns.
2.
Portfolio mix optimization: The shift towards proprietary products has structurally enhanced the group's overall profitability. While standard technical trading historically represents about 58% to 60% of total group sales, own-manufactured goods carry superior pricing power. In business areas like Life Science and Technology & Systems Solutions, own-brand components allow subsidiaries to command premium pricing. This ongoing corporate evolution continues to raise the structural gross margin ceiling over the long term.
3.
Acquisition margin accretion: Systematic bolt-on acquisitions have consistently contributed to the upward margin trend. Newly acquired entities generally carry higher profitability profiles than the historical group average. During the first half of 2026, the group completed nine acquisitions that added high-margin niche manufacturing capabilities. These transactions successfully offset underperforming units to sustain consolidated gross margin expansion across the portfolio.
4.
Krona driven purchasing gains: Fluctuations in the Swedish krona significantly impact the purchasing power of trading subsidiaries. A strong krona lowers the landed import costs of goods purchased in euros or dollars and sold locally in Swedish kronor. This operational leverage provides a temporary margin boost for Scandinavian import-focused subsidiaries. However, these gains are often partially passed back to customers over subsequent pricing cycles.
5.
Decentralized margin protection: The core philosophy of Indutrade's decentralized business model places gross margin protection directly on the individual managing directors. Unlike centralized pricing strategies, subsidiary leaders have full authority to implement tactical price adjustments as local conditions change. This entrepreneurial autonomy ensures rapid responses to localized input inflation. This decentralized responsibility serves as a highly effective local defense mechanism across the group's diverse businesses.
6.
Manufacturing cost absorption: Mix changes between industrial distribution and in-house niche manufacturing affect margins through cost absorption. During periods of high demand, superior volume absorption in manufacturing subsidiaries expands gross margins. Conversely, operating leverage works in reverse when industrial production volumes decline. This dynamic means that manufacturing-heavy segments face greater volatility during volume downturns.

Peer gross margin benchmarking

7.
Peer group comparison: Indutrade's gross margin remains highly resilient but varies significantly compared to its specialized serial acquirer peers. The group's gross margin of 35.6% is lower than Lifco's high average gross margin of approximately 43%. Similarly, UK-based peer Diploma maintains a superior gross margin of 46.5% due to its value-add focus. This indicates that Indutrade's large share of standard technical trading creates a structural margin ceiling relative to pure-play proprietary product peers.
8.
Subsidiary level margin variance: Gross margin performance is highly diversified across Indutrade's five core business areas. For instance, the Life Science segment achieved a record gross margin in late 2025 due to a favorable product mix. Conversely, the Technology & Systems Solutions segment faced EBITA margin declines from negative operational leverage in standard components. These internal variations prove that portfolio diversity mitigates single-market risks but dampens peak gross profitability.

Historical supply shocks: The pass-through speed of massive raw material and freight cost increases was tested in 2021 and 2022. Proactive pricing work successfully neutralized the severe headwind from raw material, component, and freight prices during this period. Subsidiaries demonstrated exceptional flexibility compared to larger integrated industrial groups. Historical precedents and currency cycles

11.
Favorable currency precedent: The historical precedent of 2010 demonstrates how a strong Swedish krona directly benefits the group's trading business. During that period, import-heavy Scandinavian technical traders recorded higher gross margins on foreign purchases. Combined with product mix shifts toward higher-margin products, this currency tailwind supported a stable EBITA margin despite lower sales. This cycle proves that favorable exchange rate movements provide a powerful operational buffer.
12.
Unfavorable currency precedent: In contrast, the prolonged weak krona cycle of 2014 to 2016 caused significant margin compression. Trading subsidiaries faced a dramatic drop in margins because they imported in strong foreign currencies but sold under fixed-price contracts. Price renegotiations suffered from structural lags as customers resisted price hikes. This precedent highlights that lagging price pass-through mechanisms pose a material risk during currency depreciations.
13.
Contrary evidence: The group's gross margin actually improved during previous weak currency cycles because foreign-earned profits offset Swedish import losses. During the weak krona period of 2014, Johnny Alvarsson noted that a weak krona remains positive for Indutrade on a consolidated group level. This means that international geographic diversification effectively insulates the consolidated bottom line from domestic currency shocks.
14.
Other side of the story: While management attributes gross margin expansion to pricing power, the upward trend may be cyclically inflated by temporary currency tailwinds and mix shifts rather than sustainable pricing hikes. Indeed, CFO Patrik Johnson acknowledged that Swedish trading companies benefited heavily from the stronger krona in late 2025. If exchange rates reverse or high-margin Life Science demand softens, Indutrade's gross margin floor could quickly erode back to historical levels.

Contract structures and pass-through velocity

6.
Short-term transaction focus: Indutrade's revenue is predominantly generated from short-term product sales with point-in-time recognition. Most customer contracts have an original expected duration of one year or less, which limits long-term pricing exposure. This short-term contract structure enables subsidiaries to renegotiate prices rapidly with customers as input costs fluctuate. Consequently, the lack of long-term fixed-price commitments prevents prolonged margin compression during inflationary shocks.
7.
Systematic currency clauses: Transaction currency risks are mitigated systematically across Indutrade's operating subsidiaries. Exchange rate effects are eliminated as far as possible by using currency clauses in customer contracts. Additionally, subsidiaries match incoming and outgoing payments by purchasing and selling in the same currency. These contract structures ensure that unexpected foreign exchange movements do not impair subsidiary gross margins.
8.
Regulated framework indexation: Service and maintenance revenues are governed by multi-year framework contracts. These contracts feature annual price escalation caps and cost-indexation clauses to protect profitability. However, lag effects in these indexation mechanisms mean that rapid wage inflation can cause short-term margin pressure. Thus, managing directors must actively monitor these contracts to ensure timely cost passthrough and protect earnings.
9.
Rapid inflationary pass-through: Indutrade's decentralized subsidiaries pass through cost increases with high agility. During the supply disruptions of 2021 and 2022, subsidiaries passed through freight and semiconductor price hikes. This rapid repricing capability successfully limited the net margin impact from rising input costs. The speed of these adjustments proves that decentralized decision-making enhances pricing agility compared to centralized corporate peers.

Verification of automation channel pricing

10.
Varying price-cost dynamics: A Q3 2026 automation- channel expert check suggests some distributors face tightening price-cost dynamics. Specifically, rising freight and material cost inflation is running slightly ahead of realized pricing increases. This pressure means some distributors are experiencing gross margin contraction as supply chains normalize. If this trend intensifies, it could cap group-level gross margins for technical-trading segments.
11.
Contrary evidence: Expert insights from Flowserve and Munters in late 2026 directly contradict the view that suppliers recover only 2% to 3% of cost inflation. Flowserve successfully implemented price increases of 3% to 6% in Q3 2026, with aftermarket prices up 4% to 8%. Similarly, Munters achieved price increases of 3% to 5% in 2026 to offset component cost inflation. This evidence demonstrates that critical component suppliers retain robust pricing power despite normalizing supply chains.
12.
Value-over-price prioritization: Procurement experts confirm that technical suitability and reliability are prioritized over price in critical applications. Sika achieved a 20% year-over-year pricing increase in adhesives and sealants due to raw material inflation. Despite these higher prices, Sika recorded a 20% volume increase as customers prioritized quality. This dynamic shows that highly specified engineered products maintain pricing power even during broader construction market slowdowns.

Peer comparisons and raw material exposure

13.
Specialized pricing advantage: Indutrade maintains superior pricing power and gross margins compared to generalist distributors like Rexel or RS Group. Generalist distributors typically face shorter pricing windows and lower barriers to entry on standard catalogue components. Consequently, they face immediate margin compression when raw material or freight costs inflate ahead of pricing. Indutrade's focus on high-technical-expertise niches helps insulate the group from generalist price erosion and maintain stable EPS.
14.
Peer margin sensitivity: Even high-performing peers experience temporary gross margin pressure from rapid raw-material inflation. Peer Lagercrantz reported a 20 basis point gross margin fall in Q1 2026/27 due to rising input costs. This compression occurred because cost increases could not be passed through immediately across all divisions. This precedent indicates that pricing lags can temporarily depress earnings before price hikes fully take effect.
15.
Other side of the story: While decentralized pricing has defended Indutrade's gross margin floor, a prolonged European construction recession presents severe headwinds. Construction and infrastructure markets remain subdued, limiting the volume base over which costs can be absorbed. If returning customer bargaining power in 2026 forces subsidiaries to offer volume discounts, the group gross margin could contract. Therefore, maintaining the current margin target requires continuous portfolio pruning and high-margin acquisitions.
