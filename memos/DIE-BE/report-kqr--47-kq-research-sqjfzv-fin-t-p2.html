Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. D'Ieteren's valuation hinges on Belron's pricing power, as ADAS recalibration adoption offsets cyclical automotive headwinds.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Key question: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: US motor-insurance premium inflation and consumer claims avoidance through higher deductibles; insurer caps on ADAS recalibration reimbursement (State Farm, GEICO and Progressive in 2026) and documentation rules (USAA); glass input-cost inflation from tariffs and energy prices; a falling miles- driven or recessionary environment; tougher European glass pricing where insurer contracts are tendered country by country; a shrinking Belgian new-car market with Chinese-brand discounting and changes to company-car taxation; European car-parts price deflation (2025) and re-inflation (2026); an industrial and agricultural equipment downturn?"
2.
Dominant operating asset: Belron is the primary engine of D'Ieteren, generating approximately 70% of the group's adjusted operating income despite accounting for only 41% of consolidated revenues. This high concentration means any structural shift in Belron's ability to price windshield replacements directly impacts D'Ieteren's overall consolidated earnings per share expansion path over the next three years. Unless Belron maintains its organic growth algorithm of 5% to 6%, rising financial leverage from recent recapitalizations could compress net margins.
3.
Confirmed group guidance: D'Ieteren management confirmed its FY2026 guidance for low-to-mid-single-digit growth in adjusted pre-tax profit group share, implying a level of approximately EUR 1.0 billion. Jefferies notes that this growth remains stable despite a sharp slowdown in the Automotive segment, which saw adjusted profit before tax fall 66% to EUR 36.4 million in the first half of 2026. Strong performances from Belron, TVH, and PHE are expected to fully offset these cyclical distribution headwinds.
4.
Leverage reduction trajectory: Belron's rapid cash generation allowed its senior secured net leverage ratio to decrease to 4.3x post- IFRS 16 at the end of June 2026 compared to 4.5x in December 2025. 35 Deutsche Bank Research notes that as the leverage cost from Belron's massive EUR 3.8 billion refinancing in late 2024 dissipates, group profit before tax contribution should increase significantly through 2028. Decreasing leverage reduces interest expense, which directly supports the group's targeted double-digit earnings compound annual growth rate.

Valuation and Cash Flow Implications

5.
Narrowing holding discount: D'Ieteren's stock historically trades at a significant holding company discount of 30% to 50% relative to its sum-of-the-parts net asset value. Jefferies argues that an investment in D'Ieteren provides access to Belron, the undisputed global market leader, at a discount of almost 60% to market value. Resolving the pricing power question will determine whether this holding company discount narrows or persists over the next 18 months.
6.
Anticipated liquidity catalyst: D'Ieteren's management formally confirmed in September 2026 that Belron's shareholders are actively evaluating strategic options, including a potential listing in Amsterdam or New York. Bernstein argues that a standalone listing of Belron would serve as a major catalyst to realize the hidden holding company value. This strategic review is expected to enhance valuation transparency and unlock significant capital allocation flexibility for the parent company.
7.
Aftermarket platform compounding: D'Ieteren's newer assets, Parts Holding Europe and TVH Parts, represent high-quality aftermarket platform businesses that are increasingly important to the group's diversified cash generation. Deutsche Bank Research expects PHE and TVH to continue compounding through bolt-on acquisitions in fragmented markets, helping the group achieve a three-year double-digit growth trajectory. Capital allocation success in these segments will diversify D'Ieteren away from its traditional automotive import business.

If major US insurers successfully limit recalibration reimbursement rates, Belron's historical pricing power and D'Ieteren's premium valuation multiple would face severe downward pressure.

Section 2 – Belron and Safelite: US Calibration Billing and Insurer Cap Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Insurer billing regulations threaten Belron's pricing but high scale and strategic partnerships sustain its core market share.
  2. Structural shifts in calibration mix and TPA contract wins provide stable volume offsets to near-term pricing headwinds.

Calibration Pricing and Revenue Dynamics

1.
Sustained price expansion: ADAS calibration services represent a critical growth driver that materially enhances the average transaction value for Safelite. Former Safelite executives reported in January 2026 that dynamic calibrations command $250 to $350 while static procedures range from $250 to $400. These elevated rates mean dual calibrations can reach $450 to $700 depending on vehicle complexity. Consequently, these high-ticket services provide substantial margin support for the consolidated group.
2.
Evolving mix contribution: Calibration penetration has expanded rapidly across Safelite's operating footprint to become a material revenue contributor. Bernstein Research reported in September 2026 that calibration penetration reached 52.1% in the first half of 2026 compared to 47.2% in FY2025. According to Bernstein's analysis, calibration services accounted for approximately 8% of Belron's total revenue mix by August 2026. This rising adoption ensures that mix and complexity continue to outpace underlying volume growth.
3.
Exceptional incremental margins: The financial returns on internalized ADAS services are highly lucrative due to low ongoing variable operating costs. Former Safelite regional managers estimated in January 2026 that calibration yields 80% to 90% incremental gross margins. Once the initial diagnostic software and target equipment are purchased, the primary cost is technician labor. This high-margin profile will continue to support Belron's cash flow unless insurers successfully enforce systemic discounts.

Insurer Regulations and Reimbursement Caps

4.
Mandated diagnostic compliance: Large motor-insurance carriers are implementing strict technological documentation rules to verify calibration necessity and control claim payouts. State Farm enacted a mandatory policy in late 2025 requiring shops to utilize its ADAS Map identification tool to receive full calibration reimbursement. This system forces repair shops to resolve data conflicts between proprietary equipment and insurance-specified tools. These rigid documentation standards could slow down repair cycle times if administrative friction increases.
5.
Reimbursement rate containment: Insurance buyers are increasingly pushing back against the historical wild-west pricing of ADAS services to limit rising claims severity. Former collision executives noted in April 2026 that GEICO and State Farm are leading the industry in reducing calibration reimbursements. Other national insurers, including Liberty Mutual, are actively evaluating similar cost-control changes to implement within 14 months. If these caps spread widely, Belron's ability to hold high-margin pricing will depend entirely on its national scale advantages.
6.
Mechanical rate standardization: Insurers are exploring alternative pricing frameworks to replace flat-rate calibration charges with standardized hourly metrics. A Travelers Insurance director explained in April 2026 that calibrations may transition to mechanical-rate time studies. Under this proposed model, a simple calibration would be capped at one hour of mechanical labor at approximately $200 per hour. This structural shift would compress the gross profit per job relative to historical flat-rate fees.

NAGS Mechanics and Cost Pass-Through

7.
Standardized benchmark indexation: Auto glass insurance reimbursements in North America are fundamentally governed by the National Auto Glass Specifications database. Mitchell International owns and maintains NAGS, which publishes industry-standard benchmark pricing lists. Actual transaction pricing is typically negotiated as a contracted discount or premium relative to these published list prices. This standardized system ensures a consistent baseline for claims processing but limits immediate unilateral price adjustments by individual repair shops.
8.
Quarterly lag constraints: The standard indexation schedule of NAGS creates temporary margin pressure when glass manufacturers pass down rapid input cost increases. Regional auto glass operators reported in December 2025 that NAGS updates prices only quarterly, lagging immediate wholesale price hikes. Windshield costs from suppliers rose 15% in 2025 due to tariff anticipation, causing short-term gross margin contraction. This indexation delay means Safelite must absorb raw material inflation for up to three months before seeing reimbursement relief.
9.
Contracted markup premium: Despite indexation restrictions, Safelite leverages its market leadership to negotiate superior commercial terms compared to regional competitors. Former Safelite directors noted that their agreements secure higher markups over the standard NAGS benchmark lists. This premium pricing is justified by Safelite's extensive geographic coverage and superior customer turnaround times. This structural advantage protects Belron's margins and allows it to maintain a stable pricing premium over smaller operators.

TPA Dynamics and Peer Benchmarking

10.
Dominant routing advantages: Safelite's dual role as a service provider and third-party administrator provides a massive volume steering mechanism. Safelite Solutions secured a major contract to administer State Farm's national program effective July 1, 2025, replacing LYNX Services. State Farm represents the largest US auto insurer with a 17% market share, covering over 49 million vehicles. This administrative mandate provides Safelite AutoGlass shops with a continuous long-term volume tailwind across North America.
11.
Peers internalizing calibration: Comparable operators in the collision and glass sectors are aggressively expanding internal calibration capabilities to capture high-margin revenue. Boyd Group Services increased its calibration internalization to 75% in the fourth quarter of 2025, up from 53% in late 2024. Boyd targets an 80% run-rate internalization within two years to capture margin from third-party sublet services. This competitive focus on internalization highlights the industry-wide rush to defend margins against rising labor and material costs.
12.
Glass segment outperformance: Belron continues to maintain a significant operational and financial lead over its closest listed North American peers. Bernstein Research highlights that Belron achieved an adjusted EBITA of 22.9% in FY2025, outperforming Boyd's 18.3% and Driven Brands' 4.3% margins. Driven Brands' Auto Glass Now segment operates 211 stores and reported FY2025 Adjusted EBITDA of $25.90 million. This massive margin delta confirms that Belron's scale and TPA integration provide superior pricing power.
13.
Contrary evidence: A former regional vice president at Safelite reported in January 2026 that Safelite's higher pricing makes it less competitive in cash-pay and commercial markets. The expert noted that while Safelite dominates the insurer-routed channel, regional players successfully capture local cash-paying customers by offering lower pricing and more flexible terms. This indicates that Safelite's pricing power is highly dependent on insurance routing, leaving it vulnerable if deductibles rise.
14.
Other side of the story: While Belron's scale and exclusive TPA contracts protect its dominant volume routing, aggressive insurer reimbursement caps could compress margins if flat-rate calibration standardizations are widely eliminated. Analysts at Jefferies warn that a rapid shift toward self-calibrating camera systems, such as those utilized by Tesla, could diminish the overall need for traditional physical calibrations. If insurers successfully benchmark ADAS reimbursements to mechanical labor hours, or if technological automation reduces calibration complexity, Belron's high-margin pricing power could face structural erosion over the next three years.

Section 3 – Belron and Cary Group: European Glass Pricing and Contract Structures

Section summary
  1. Tough European insurer contracts keep glass margins structurally aligned with labor inflation.
  2. Belron maintains superior profitability over Cary Group due to higher geographic density and digital claims integration.

European Insurer Dynamics and Country Tendering

1.
Highly competitive tendering: European vehicle glass pricing is strictly regulated by country-specific insurer contracts because tender processes are managed country by country. An expert from Cary Group noted in February 2026 that European pricing models range from unbundled fixed fees in the United Kingdom to less structured list-based pricing in Spain. Consequently, Belron must negotiate separate commercial terms with regional underwriters, which keeps average replacement prices around €300 in key Eurozone countries. This fragmented structure restricts unilateral pricing actions.
2.
Concentrated buyer power: European insurance markets exhibit high concentration because the top three insurance companies control 40% to 75% of industry premiums depending on the country. Bernstein Research reported in September 2026 that this extreme concentration incentivizes major insurers to select large, digitally integrated partners. This means Belron's Carglass and Autoglass brands can capture steady volumes, whereas smaller regional independent shops are excluded from direct routing. Ultimately, high buyer concentration limits Belron's ability to raise prices without facing significant pushback from these powerful institutional clients.
3.
Divergent regional frameworks: The French vehicle glass repair market operates under highly decentralized conditions because consumers can invoice any insurer without structured steering. An expert from Cary Group stated in February 2026 that the French market remains geographically fragmented with numerous regional competitors competing for drivers. This decentralized routing forces operators to attract retail customers through promotional incentives and gifts rather than direct insurer agreements. This unique framework reduces Belron's systemic volume steering advantage in France relative to its highly structured, unbundled model in the UK.

Comparative Profitability and Cary Precedents

4.
Superior scale returns: Belron generates significantly higher profitability than Cary Group because it benefits from massive network density advantages across Europe. Jefferies reported in September 2022 that Belron's EBIT margin of 17.1% in the Nordics was matched by Cary Group's Nordic margin of 17.1% in FY2021. However, Cary Group achieved only a 7.4% EBIT margin in the Rest of Europe where it directly competed with Belron. This margin delta proves that Belron's dominant market share allows it to sustain superior pricing and operating efficiency in overlapping geographies.
5.
Robust volume resiliency: European vehicle glass claims display significant structural resilience during broader macroeconomic slowdowns. Bernstein Research reported in September 2026 that historical evidence from mobility restrictions shows glass claims are more resilient than simple traffic volumes. This occurs because road deterioration, weather events, and rising vehicle complexity exert a far greater influence on windshield breakage rates. Consequently, both Belron and Cary Group maintain highly defensive volume profiles even when recessionary environments reduce total miles driven.
6.
Evolving private valuations: The financial valuation of major European glass repair networks reflects robust long-term consolidation expectations across the continent. Jefferies noted in March 2026 that Cary Group was valued at 22.0x EV/EBIT when it was taken private by CVC and Nordic Capital in 2022. In March 2026, Bloomberg reported that Cary's private equity owners were evaluating an exit that could value the business at at least €3 billion. This rising valuation trend demonstrates strong investor confidence in the long-term cash flow sustainability of European glass consolidation platforms.
7.
Severe volume deleveraging: Historical operating precedents show that vehicle glass networks are highly vulnerable to sudden demand shocks. During a mild winter in 2012, Belron experienced an organic sales decline of 4.4% because the market shrank by 10.5% in key regions. Because Belron was unable to reduce its direct labor costs immediately, its current operating result fell by 24.43% (calculated) from €262 million to €198 million. This historical precedent illustrates that fixed technician labor costs create significant negative operating leverage when volumes decline.
8.
Contrary evidence: Cary Group's contract structures protect it from short-term glass manufacturing cost volatility because cost increases are mirrored to customers. Cary Group's CFO explained in November 2021 that the majority of its customer contracts contain flow-through clauses that pass raw material and energy price increases directly to insurers. Although these price adjustments can be delayed by one or two quarters, they prevent sustained margin compression. This contractual buffer suggests that glass cost inflation does not represent a material long-term threat to European glass operators' profitability.

Inflation Indexation and Cost Pass-Through

9.
Standardized annual indexation: European vehicle glass replacement contracts primarily utilize indexation metrics to pass through labor and material inflation. Jefferies reported in April 2023 that Cary Group's multi-year agreements are indexed to the Consumer Price Index. Cary Group's prospectus noted that this standard indexation generally yields one to two percent price growth across all European markets. This contractual mechanism ensures that rising labor costs are passed to insurance providers on an annual basis, mitigating immediate margin pressure.
10.
Compressed calibration upside: European calibration services provide far less pricing upside than the US market because European pricing tracks actual labor costs. An expert from Cary Group explained in February 2026 that European insurers clearly understand that calibration is a labor-intensive service. This clear cost understanding keeps European calibration prices stable and aligned with inflation, preventing the high premium rates achieved in the US. Consequently, European calibration margins are similar to replacement margins, limiting Belron's ability to drive rapid margin expansion through Eurozone complexity mix alone.
11.
Persistent margin drivers: Underlying Eurozone margin sustainability is heavily supported by the structural growth of specialized aftermarket services. Fitch reported in October 2024 that the requirement for specialized windshield calibration represents the key driver for increasing profitability as ADAS penetration rises. Since this transition is heavily accelerated by European safety regulations, the shift is expected to remain rapid and margin-accretive. This confirms that technological premiumisation, rather than simple pricing increases, remains the primary engine of Eurozone profitability.
12.
Currency and import exposures: European glass networks face direct input cost pressure from macroeconomic and trade disruptions. D'Ieteren management noted in August 2017 that the UK market Autoglass suffered cost headwinds from the devaluation of the sterling because there is no local glass production. Because glass was imported from continental Europe and invoiced in euros, sterling weakness directly compressed gross margins before prices could be renegotiated. This highlights that European glass networks remain exposed to significant import cost volatility when local currency fluctuations disrupt regional supply agreements.
13.
Other side of the story: Although tough insurer negotiations and labor-linked indexing limit European pricing power, Belron achieved significant Eurozone margin expansion due to structural complexity shifts. D'Ieteren reported in September 2026 that Belron's Eurozone organic sales grew by 4.4% in the first half of 2026. Driven by strong operational execution and rising ADAS penetration, Belron's consolidated adjusted operating margin expanded by 160 basis points to 23.0% in the same period. This confirms that operating leverage and rising windshield complexity can offset the pricing constraints imposed by country-by-country European insurance tendering.
