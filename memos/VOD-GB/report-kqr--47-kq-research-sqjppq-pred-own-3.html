EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The evidence regarding Vodafone Germany's national roaming agreement with 1&1 is sufficiently clear to answer the key questions with high confidence on contractual terms, financial contributions, and network rollout progress, though medium confidence remains on merger and acquisition scenarios due to the private nature of ongoing negotiations. Vodafone's trading valuation already reflects substantial downside risk, suggesting the market has priced in potential wholesale revenue loss, while management's conservative planning framework does not rely on permanent 1&1 revenue streams for mid-term group targets.

1.
Contractual structure: The August 2023 national roaming agreement establishes a capacity-based pricing model with an initial five-year term, two unilateral five-year extension options for 1&1, and a subsequent three-year transition period, creating a potential total duration of eighteen years that secures long-term wholesale alignment between the partners.
2.
Minimum payment commitments: The contract guarantees Vodafone fifty million euros per year in minimum payments throughout the initial term, while the capacity-based pricing requires 1&1 to pay a fixed price per percentage point of network used, aligning both parties' interests as data volumes scale across Germany.
3.
Tariff volatility exposure: The capacity model exposed 1&1 to unexpected wholesale cost inflation when Vodafone's network data growth slowed below projections, forcing 1&1 to cut EBITDA guidance from 571 million to 545 million euros in June 2025 due to elevated roaming costs driven by a higher-than-budgeted percentage of network usage.
4.
Vodafone's wholesale revenue: Current broker consensus estimates the agreement delivers approximately 100 million euros in quarterly revenue and EBITDAaL to Vodafone Germany, reaching an annualized 400 million euro run-rate, though this contribution is modeled to fade to approximately 200 million euros annually towards decade-end as 1&1 migrates traffic to its own infrastructure.
5.
Capacity investment burden: Vodafone spends approximately 100 million euros annually on network capacity expansion to support the wholesale agreement, representing a cumulative 300 million euros by fiscal 2027, which renders the deal largely free cash flow neutral in its early years as high-margin EBITDAaL contribution is consumed by capacity upgrades.
6.
Network rollout progress: 1&1 achieved 27 percent household coverage by end-2025, exceeding the regulatory milestone of 25 percent and eliminating near-term fine risk, though the company operated only approximately 1,500 active sites in late 2025 while remaining heavily reliant on national roaming agreements to support its twelve million customer base.
7.
Regulatory and spectrum hurdles: The Federal Network Agency mandates 50 percent household coverage by 2030, requiring approximately 6,000 antenna sites, while 1&1 faces severe economic and physical limitations without access to sub-1GHz low-band spectrum, currently operating only on 2 GHz and 3.6 GHz frequencies that restrict rural penetration.
8.
Merger and acquisition speculation: Spanish press reported in February 2026 that Telefónica began formal negotiations to acquire 1&1 for four point five billion euros, with broker estimates suggesting a combination would address a combined eleven point five billion euro cost base and deliver annual synergies between 700 million and 1.4 billion euros from duplicate network rationalization.
9.
Consolidation impact on Vodafone: A successful Telefónica-1&1 merger would create an entity with approximately fifty-seven point eight million mobile lines and reduce network operators from four to three, likely causing 1&1 to migrate traffic back to Telefónica and resulting in an estimated annual EBITDAaL loss of approximately 300 million euros for Vodafone Germany.
10.
Capital expenditure mitigation: If 1&1 migrates, Vodafone can immediately reduce 100 million euros in annual expansion capex and another 100 million euros in maintenance capex over three years, limiting the net free cash flow hit to just 200 million euros through disciplined capacity investment adjustments and strategic capital flexibility.
11.
Group-level cushion from UK synergies: Vodafone upgraded its VodafoneThree cost and capex synergy target to one billion pounds annually by fiscal 2032, representing a 40 percent increase over prior guidance and providing operating free cash flow 500 million pounds above previous estimates, effectively neutralizing the German wholesale revenue risk at the group level.
12.
Regulatory and founder resistance: High antitrust hurdles and Ralph Dommermuth's firm commitment to building an independent virtualized network create substantial obstacles to any transaction, with 1&1 management strongly rejecting speculation that its physical rollout is merely tactical leverage for wholesale negotiations, suggesting any takeover would require a highly substantial premium to secure founder approval.

Section summary
  1. Understanding Vodafone's German roaming deal with 1&1 is critical because Germany represents Vodafone's largest market, and the agreement directly influences mid-term cash flows and group earnings per share.

The Key Question

1.
Key Question: "Vodafone Group Plc (VOD-GB), a European and African telecommunications operator reporting Germany (cable, fibre wholesale access and mobile, including national roaming for 1&1), the UK (VodafoneThree, wholly owned since July 2026), Other Europe (Portugal, Ireland, Greece, Romania and others) and Türkiye, and Africa (Vodacom, including Vodafone Egypt and, since July 2026, a consolidated Safaricom and M-Pesa), together with Vodafone Business and a Vodafone Investments unit holding non-controlled stakes such as 50 percent of Oak Holdings (Vantage Towers). What are the terms and the outlook of Vodafone Germany's national roaming agreement with 1&1 (the minimum payments, the initial term, the extension options and the capacity-based pricing), how much revenue and EBITDAaL does it contribute, how fast is 1&1 building its own network toward 50 percent household coverage by 2030, what is the status of Telefónica's talks to buy 1&1, and does Vodafone's own planning assume the loss of 1&1 revenue, as Deutsche Bank reported on 15 June 2026?"

This geographic breadth helps stabilize earnings but increases exposure to localized competitive and regulatory pressures.

In addition to these African markets, the group retains valuable infrastructure exposure through a 50 percent stake in Oak Holdings which controls Vantage Towers. These high-growth mobile money and tower investments support group earnings, although their non-controlled nature limits direct operational control and cash extraction.

Section 2 – Terms and Mechanics of the 1&1 National Roaming Agreement

Section summary
  1. The capacity-based pricing model and 18-year term of the Vodafone-1&1 agreement secure long- term structural alignment, though they expose 1&1 to short-term tariff volatility when host network data growth slows.
  2. 1&1's migration from Telefónica's MBA MVNO to Vodafone's 5G roaming represents a major financial shift, replacing capitalized prepayments with fully opex-recognized wholesale costs.

Agreement Structure and Extension Options

1.
Long-term commitment: The national roaming agreement signed between Vodafone Germany and 1&1 in August 2023 establishes an expansive contractual framework that supports all current and future mobile technologies. This landmark cooperative partnership commenced commercial operations in late August 2024 to deliver comprehensive 5G mobile coverage nationwide to 1&1's customer base. The initial contractual term is set for a basic duration of five years from the start date. It secures a long-term network sharing arrangement that allows 1&1 to leverage Vodafone's active radio access infrastructure.
2.
Accelerated subscriber onboarding: The onboarding timeline scheduled the full migration of 1&1's mobile subscribers to Vodafone's network by the second half of the 2026 fiscal year. This migration is planned to transition more than twelve million customer contracts over to the new national roaming grid. Once this process is completed, 1&1 will obtain its nationwide national roaming services exclusively from Vodafone. This exclusive wholesale relationship underpins Vodafone's projected German service revenue recovery.
3.
Flexible extension options: Under the strict terms of the agreement, 1&1 Mobilfunk retains the unilateral right to prolong the partnership beyond the initial timeframe. Specifically, 1&1 can exercise options to extend the contract term up to twice by five additional years each time. These options grant 1&1 a potential total duration of 15 years of continuous national roaming. Furthermore, a subsequent three-year transition period is legally appended to the end of the contract to facilitate a seamless customer migration.
4.
Financial minimum commitments: The long-term agreement contains strict financial obligations that guarantee a baseline revenue stream for the host operator. Specifically, 1&1 is bound by a minimum payment commitment of fifty million euros per contract year. This fixed baseline payment is enforced throughout the entire five-year initial term of the contract. This contractual minimum provides Vodafone Germany with predictable wholesale cash flows while 1&1 migrates its extensive customer base.
5.
Strategic long-term alignment: The national roaming agreement represents a critical milestone for 1&1 as it rolls out its own virtualized network. The long-term nature of this agreement implies that 1&1 may take many years to reach complete network coverage. This allows 1&1 to transition its subscribers gradually without risking service disruption. Thus, the extended longevity of up to 18 years provides a secure safety net for the entrant.

Capacity-Based Pricing Mechanics

6.
Capacity-based pricing model: The financial core of the agreement utilizes an innovative capacity-based pricing model rather than standard usage-based fees. Under this structure, 1&1 pays a fixed price per percentage point of the network used by its customers. This model represents a highly advanced cooperative wholesale structure designed to protect both parties in the long term. It aligns the financial interests of both companies as data volumes escalate across Germany.
7.
Cost sharing mechanics: The pricing formula requires 1&1 to reimburse a share of total mobile network costs. This cost share corresponds directly to the proportion of data used by customers relative to Vodafone's total network volume. Consequently, the final billing fluctuates based on various vertraglichen variables and overall capacity utilization. This calculation ensures that 1&1 participates in the future economics and scale benefits of Vodafone's network.
8.
Shared network efficiency: The capacity-based pricing model aligns the operational incentives of both German operators by maximizing active mast utilization. By sharing the cost burden of the physical radio network, both companies achieve greater long-term investment efficiency in 5G infrastructure. This model is particularly beneficial as operators face intense regulatory coverage requirements across Germany. Consequently, the shared capacity model acts as a cushion against redundant capital expenditure for both parties.
9.
Commercial tariff freedom: Operating under the capacity-based agreement allows 1&1 to offer competitive unlimited 5G data tariffs. These high-volume data offerings are economically viable because the agreement supports unlimited mobile plans without spiraling incremental costs. In contrast, offering unlimited data under 1&1's previous roaming setup would have presented severe commercial problems. This structural pricing advantage enables 1&1 to aggressively target premium mobile subscribers in the German market.
10.
Contrary evidence: 1&1's optimistic data assumptions backfired in 2025 because overall data growth on Vodafone's network was slower than expected. Specifically, while 1&1's own traffic growth was in line with its original plans, Vodafone's overall network volumes slowed down substantially. This divergence forced 1&1 to pay for a higher percentage of network usage than originally budgeted. As a result, 1&1 was hit with unexpected wholesale cost inflation that directly depressed its initial profit margins.

Tariff Volatility and Structural Protections

11.
Material guidance revisions: The unexpected wholesale cost inflation driven by the capacity formula forced 1&1 to revise its financial outlook. In June 2025, 1&1 officially cut its EBITDA guidance from 571 million euros to 545 million euros. This downward revision was entirely attributed to the elevated national roaming costs under the Vodafone contract. However, this financial adjustment was isolated to the Access segment, leaving the standalone mobile network segment unchanged.
12.
Slower host traffic: The structural risk of the capacity model became apparent as Vodafone's mobile data volumes decelerated post-COVID. Vodafone's German data growth slowed from 35% in the fourth quarter of 2024 to 17% in the fourth quarter of 2025. Vodafone management attributed this trend to tariff price-ups, the elimination of cheap promotions, and general tariff simplification. This deliberate slowing of host network traffic directly inflated 1&1's relative share of network costs.
13.
Contractual structural protections: To mitigate such volatility, the national roaming agreement incorporates specific structural mechanisms that protect both partners economically. The model naturally incentivizes the client against running data offers too far ahead of the host network's expansion. If 1&1 runs slower data volume growth in future quarters, its wholesale payments will automatically adjust downward. This balancing mechanism ensures that short-term traffic deviations do not permanently disrupt the commercial logic of the partnership.
14.
Projected cost normalization: Despite the 2025 earnings drag, 1&1 expects its national roaming costs to stabilize over the medium term. The management of 1&1 expects these elevated roaming expenditures to normalize during the 2026 financial year. This stabilization is supported by the completion of customer migrations and ongoing network expansion. Furthermore, once all customers are migrated, 100 million euros in temporary migration and upstream costs will be eliminated.

Comparison with Telefónica MBA MVNO

15.
Prior MVNO comparison: The financial profile of the Vodafone agreement differs fundamentally from 1&1's prior arrangement with Telefónica Deutschland. Under the previous MBA MVNO contract, 1&1 made capitalized prepayments every five years which were then depreciated as scheduled. That structural setup allowed 1&1 to keep wholesale network access costs partially insulated from immediate EBITDA impacts. The expiration of that Telefónica agreement marked the end of this capitalized payment mechanism.
16.
Shift to EBITDA: Under the new national roaming agreement with Vodafone, the financial treatment of wholesale network access has shifted entirely. The capacities used by 1&1 are recognized entirely in operating EBITDA as wholesale costs. This accounting shift causes a decline in reported segment EBITDA while providing a corresponding relief in depreciation and amortization. Consequently, while this commercial transition is EBITDA-negative, it remains completely net income-neutral for 1&1.
17.
Enhanced commercial flexibility: Although the Telefónica MBA MVNO agreement hosted roughly 12 million subscribers, it limited 1&1's long-term product evolution. The former Telefónica roaming arrangement only encompassed the 4G standard during the transition phase. In contrast, the Vodafone partnership delivers immediate access to 5G and future mobile technologies. This access allows 1&1 to market modern highspeed mobile services nationwide while building out its physical Open RAN infrastructure.
18.
Other side of the story: The shift to Vodafone's capacity model ultimately delivers superior long-term economics and competitive independence despite initial tariff volatility. By avoiding the multi-year capitalized prepayments required under the prior Telefónica contract, 1&1 preserves valuable cash liquidity. Furthermore, the capacity-based pricing structure allows 1&1 to fully participate in scale benefits as its customers consume more data. This commercial design ensures that 1&1 can scale its high-margin unlimited 5G subscriber base without facing exponential cost increases.

Section 3 – Revenue and EBITDAaL Contribution to Vodafone

Section summary
  1. The 1&1 roaming agreement secures robust high-margin wholesale streams for Vodafone Germany, contributing approximately 100 million euros in quarterly revenue and EBITDAaL.
  2. Associated network capacity expansion imposes an annual 100 million euro capex drag on Vodafone, although this would be recovered through investment offsets if traffic migrates.

Financial Contributions and Run Rates

1.
Significant wholesale contribution: Vodafone Germany receives substantial financial support from its long-term national roaming agreement with new entrant 1&1. According to Deutsche Bank Research on 21 April 2026, the partnership delivers approximately 100 million euros in quarterly revenues and EBITDAaL. This wholesale contribution reaches an annualized run-rate of 400 million euros, which provides an essential operational cushion during a challenging retail market turnaround.
2.
Fading long-term profile: The initial financial contribution from the wholesale contract is expected to decline as 1&1 slowly builds out its virtualized network. Morgan Stanley argued on 27 January 2025 that the roaming setup would deliver potentially 200 million euros of EBITDAaL for the German segment. This contribution is modeled to fade to around 200 million euros annually towards the end of the decade as 1&1 migrates traffic.
3.
Top-line trajectory impact: The rapid onboarding of wholesale subscribers has historically driven mobile service revenue outperformance. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 30 September 2026 that German mobile service revenue growth slowed to 1.2% in Q1 due to a tougher 1&1 comp as the initial migration phase fully annualized. Consequently, headline growth is expected to slow down in future quarters as these wholesale benefits fully comp out.
4.
Segment margin implications: High-margin wholesale streams play an essential role in stabilizing Vodafone Germany's operational profitability. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 30 September 2026 that these wholesale agreements carry exceptionally high operating profit margins of approximately 70%. These lucrative wholesale flows help offset the ongoing structural drag from the decline of legacy cable television revenues across major housing associations.

Capacity Capex and Opex Drag

5.
Temporary capacity investment: Vodafone Germany faces substantial upfront network costs to accommodate the heavy mobile data traffic from 1&1's subscribers. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 21 April 2026 that the company spends 100 million euros annually on capacity to support this wholesale agreement. This temporary capacity expenditure represents a cumulative cash outflow of 300 million euros by the 2027 fiscal year.
6.
Capital intensity burden: The elevated network capacity demands of the 1&1 contract restrict Vodafone's near- term capital expenditure flexibility. Morgan Stanley noted on 27 August 2025 that Vodafone Germany faces a persistent fixed and cable capex overhang relative to its peers. This heavy investment burden limits the division's ability to drive organic free cash flow growth while capacity remains constrained.
7.
Contrary evidence: Slower data traffic growth on the host network has actually reduced the immediate capacity strain on Vodafone's infrastructure. The annual report of 1&1 AG published on 18 March 2026 noted that the Vodafone network grew slower than 1&1 had anticipated during the initial phase. This slower host data expansion forced 1&1 to pay higher wholesale charges without requiring Vodafone to immediately accelerate its capacity expansion capex.
8.
Network quality preservation: Vodafone continues to prioritize high capital investment to maintain its competitive network positioning against Deutsche Telekom. ING Wholesale Banking reported on 28 November 2024 that the roaming contract successfully commenced with active mobile network quality recognized as a close second in independent German tests. This ongoing network investment ensures that the massive influx of 12 million wholesale users does not degrade retail user experience.

Free Cash Flow Dynamics

Cash flow neutral transition: The financial benefits of the 1&1 wholesale contract are heavily offset by capacity capital expenditure during the initial rollout phase. Morgan Stanley reported on 16 July 2026 that the agreement is expected to be largely free cash flow neutral in its early years. This neutral cash profile occurs because the high-margin EBITDAaL contribution is entirely consumed by the 100 million euros in annual network capacity upgrades.
10.
Capex mitigation offsets: If 1&1 unexpectedly terminates the roaming agreement, Vodafone has structural capital expenditure levers to protect its cash flows. Deutsche Bank Research explained on 21 April 2026 that an immediate 1&1 migration would cause Germany capex to drop by 100 million euros in expansion investment. This capex relief combined with another 100 million euros in long-term maintenance savings would limit the net free cash flow hit to just 200 million euros.
11.
Valuation multiple support: Long-term cash flow predictability is key to driving a rerating of Vodafone's discounted European valuation multiples. Deutsche Bank Research argued on 15 June 2026 that Vodafone Europe's enterprise value to operating free cash flow rises by approximately one turn if 1&1 migrates. This multiple expansion occurs because the market would remove the persistent overhang of a sudden wholesale revenue cliff.
12.
Group cash generation: The cash flow dynamics in Germany remain a primary focus for group-level dividend and deleveraging commitments. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 22 January 2026 that group free cash flow was expected at the upper end of guidance between 2.4 billion euros and 2.6 billion euros. This performance supports Vodafone's ongoing 2 billion euro share buyback program and progressive dividend policy.

Premium wholesale margins: Vodafone's 70% wholesale margin on the 1&1 deal compares favorably to typical European telecom wholesale structures. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 30 September 2026 that newly launched wholesale divisions in Europe strive to secure high-margin revenue to offset retail price competition. Valuation discount comparison: Vodafone's combined European operations continue to trade at a steep valuation discount compared to single-country incumbents. Other side of the story: A sudden loss of the 1&1 roaming agreement would severely dent Vodafone Germany's near-term profitability before any capital expenditure offsets can be realized. Spectrum Hurdles and Dispute Resolution

10.
Vital frequency access: The economic viability of 1&1's standalone network remains highly dependent on acquiring low-band spectrum below 1 GHz. 1&1 currently operates its virtualized network using only 2 GHz and 3.6 GHz frequencies. Without access to low-band spectrum, 1&1 faces severe economic and physical limitations in rural areas, where signal propagation is weak.
11.
Mandated operator sharing: The Federal Network Agency linked its spectrum license extensions to an obligation that the three established operators share part of their low-band spectrum with 1&1. These sharing negotiations between the incumbent operators and 1&1 remained unsuccessful at the end of the year. Consequently, the regulator initiated a formal hearing process in February 2026 to order cooperative frequency use under mandated terms.
12.
Infrastructure access disputes: 1&1 faces ongoing construction delays from its tower partners including Vodafone's spun-off Vantage Towers unit. The Federal Cartel Office launched an investigation into whether Vantage Towers had blocked 1&1's 5G rollout. In a preliminary assessment in April 2025, the regulator sided with 1&1 in this dispute over radio mast access.
13.
High spectrum expenditure: Negotiated solutions or potential auctions could impose substantial financial obligations on 1&1. Redburn Atlantic argued on 30 May 2024 that 1&1 still craves its own low-band frequencies. The broker estimated that 1&1 will spend approximately 800 million euros on spectrum to secure low-band access, representing a major cash outflow.

Comparative Entry Precedents

14.
Italian rollout precedent: The competitive path of Iliad in Italy provides a useful comparative precedent for 1&1's infrastructure-based transition. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 19 May 2023 that Iliad Italy's mobile service revenue grew by 17.9% in the first quarter of 2023. Iliad's network rollout was supported by lower roaming costs and active sharing of physical infrastructure, which improved profitability.
15.
Active sharing strategies: Iliad Italy successfully negotiated an active network-sharing joint venture with WindTre in early 2022. Under this agreement, WindTre moved approximately 7,000 mobile radio sites into a shared entity where Iliad acquired a 50% stake. This sharing arrangement allowed Iliad to reduce its standalone capital intensity and accelerate rural 5G coverage across shared regions.
16.
