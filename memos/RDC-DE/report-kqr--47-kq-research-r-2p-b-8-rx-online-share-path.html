Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Brokers project German online prescription penetration to reach low-to-mid single digits by 2028, supported by peak marketing and AI pricing.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Central investment inquiry: "What online share of the German statutory prescription market do brokers and industry experts now model for 2027 and 2028, and on what assumptions? What do the monthly Gematik data and the Redcare and DocMorris disclosures since mid-2026 show about repeat ordering by chronic patients after their first online prescription? Do brokers or experts expect a dip in online redemptions when CardLink's licence ends on 31 January 2027 and Proof of Patient Presence replaces it, and is Proof of Patient Presence on schedule for the first half of 2027? Finally, after the fixed fee per prescription rose to 9 euros in July 2026 and to 9.50 euros in January 2027, while the pharmacy discount to the statutory insurers rises to 2.07 euros, what net gain per prescription do brokers now model for Redcare?"

Financial Significance and EPS Impact

2.
Widening valuation disconnect: Recent share price divergence has resulted in Redcare trading at a significant discount to its peer DocMorris. According to Jefferies' note of 8 October 2026, Redcare trades at 0.45x EV/Sales for FY27 while DocMorris trades at 0.57x EV/Sales. This valuation mismatch is unexpected because Redcare maintains a clear market-leading position and superior margin and growth profile. Resolving the Key Question will show if this valuation gap is justified or if Redcare's earnings per share trajectory will outperform consensus expectations.
3.
Upgraded guidance momentum: Positive revision of financial targets indicates that the electronic prescription rollout is successfully driving financial performance. On 28 September 2026, Redcare raised its FY26 total revenue growth guidance to a range of 16% to 18%. This growth is primarily driven by German prescription sales, which are now expected to grow between 45% and 51% in FY26. Consequently, verifying the durability of these trends is essential to determine whether Redcare can sustain this accelerated growth rate into 2027 and 2028.
4.
Extended regulatory runway: The unexpected prolongation of digital authorization approvals has removed the immediate threat of an operational cliff-edge. Jefferies reported on 8 October 2026 that gematik extended CardLink approval until 31 March 2028. This decision provides both Redcare and DocMorris with crucial operational visibility during the transition to Proof of Patient Presence. As a result, this regulatory support helps determine how long the growth window remains open for online pharmacies.
5.
Gradual channel shifting: Omnichannel operators are leveraging digital joint ventures to defend physical retail market shares against pure-play online pharmacy competitors. Deutsche Bank reported on 14 August 2026 that Swiss operator Galenica continues to position its physical network as the primary healthcare contact. Galenica management views the potential liberalization of online medicine sales as an opportunity rather than a near-term risk due to its joint venture with Redcare. Consequently, Galenica's stake in Redcare provides valuable exposure to digital growth while shielding its core defensive pharmacy earnings.
6.
Peer growth comparison: International comparable cases show that online pharmacies in other European regions can maintain highly profitable growth trajectories. In a Swedish small cap report on 9 October 2026, DNB Carnegie described Apotea as the gold standard in online retail due to its high defensive earnings growth. Apotea is targeting a sales growth rate of 15% to 19% and an EBIT margin of 7% to 8% in the medium term. This peer performance proves that structural channel shifts drive margin expansion once initial automation investments are fully de-risked.
7.
Contrary evidence: Despite optimistic growth assumptions, the online prescription channel still faces substantial customer adoption barriers. Redcare's management admitted during their Q2 2026 earnings call on 29 July 2026 that the growth of active prescription customers slowed sequentially. Active prescription customers only grew to 1.74 million from 1.50 million in the prior-year period, representing a notable bottleneck in customer acquisition. This slower expansion rate indicates that relying solely on e-prescription mandates might not guarantee rapid market-share gains.
8.
Increasing prescription fees: Structural changes in pharmacy compensation directly impact the net profitability of each digital transaction. DocMorris' presentation on 7 October 2026 shows that the fixed remuneration per medication rose to 9 euros in July 2026 and will rise to 9.50 euros in January 2027. However, the statutory pharmacy discount also increases from 1.77 euros to 2.07 euros on 1 January 2027. Shifting fee dynamics will expand the net gain per prescription, defining whether online players can offset rising transport and delivery costs of approximately 20 cents.
9.
Other side of the story: While online pharmacies are currently experiencing rapid double-digit growth, physical retail advocates continue to lobby for protective legislation. Deutsche Bank's report from 18 September 2026 noted that German brick-and-mortar pharmacy associations are demanding a complete ban on mail-order prescription sales. Although analysts believe the risk of such a ban remains very low, persistent political noise could trigger severe share-price volatility. Ultimately, these legislative disputes could depress valuation multiples for European peers like Apotea and Galenica if regulatory contagion spreads.

Section 5 – Prescription Fee Changes and Redcare Net Gain Modeling

Section summary
  1. Regulatory changes in Germany's pharmacy fees will expand Redcare's net profitability per prescription, despite rising statutory discount headwinds.
  2. Deutsche Bank models Redcare to achieve a superior EBITDA tailwind compared to DocMorris due to higher package volume and better business mix.

Implemented German Pharmacy Fee Reforms

1.
Completed statutory price adjustments: Regulatory adjustments to the German pharmacy pricing framework have restructured the baseline economics for mail-order prescription fulfillment. According to Redcare Pharmacy's Q2 2026 presentation, the third regulation amending the pharmacy price ordinance (Arzneimittelpreisverordnung) was officially adopted, establishing a phased increase in the fixed pharmacy dispensing fee (Fixum). This fixed fee per prescription package rose from EUR 8.35 to EUR 9 on July 1, 2026, and is scheduled for a second statutory increase to EUR 9.50 effective January 1, 2027. These adjustments provide a highly predictable regulatory framework for online pharmacies looking to expand their footprint. Mandatory health insurer discounts: Parallel health insurance reforms introduce cost-containment measures that offset a portion of the gross fee gains for pharmacy operators. Under the adopted Statutory Health Insurance Contribution Rate Stabilization Act (GKV-BStabG), the mandatory pharmacy rebate (Kassenabschlag) paid to statutory health insurers is set to increase. Effective January 1, 2027, this statutory discount will rise from EUR 1.77 to EUR 2.07 per prescription. The statutory discount represents a direct EUR 0.30 regulatory headwind that must be subtracted from the gross Fixum increases. Incremental remote delivery requirements: Regulatory guidelines for mail-order delivery logistics introduce minor cost additions that slightly dilute net transaction economics. In its global markets presentation on October 7, 2026, DocMorris disclosed that new transport and signature-on-delivery requirements impose an additional cost of approximately 20 cents per delivery. For online pharmacy models, these delivery cost increases are typically modeled on a per-shipment basis. Deutsche Bank Research estimated this incremental shipping cost at EUR 0.19 per prescription delivery, assuming a typical delivery contains more than one prescription package.
