[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Vimian is a 'Major Corporate Change' case that the share price has already paid for. A board-forced change of chief executive in July 2025, a rebuilt leadership layer and a reorganised US orthopaedics business have lifted organic growth from 5% to 12% in four quarters and produced two consecutive beats, but at SEK 35.7 the shares sit at the median of their own multiple range after a 36.6% six-month rise, so the modal return is about 5% to 8% a year against the framework's 20% bar. The same case at about SEK 29 clears the bar.

  • The change is internal, broad and real.
    • The board removed the chief executive on 17 July 2025 after eighteen months, made Alireza Tajbakhsh permanent from 24 November 2025, and a new leadership layer followed through 2026: a Head of MedTech from April 2026, a Head of Specialty Pharma from 1 October 2026, a Head of M&A from 5 October 2026.
    • The unit that had been shrinking was reorganised: US orthopaedics moved from inside sales to a field sales force in January 2026, more than 4,000 of 22,000 MedTech SKUs were discontinued, and the annual order programme was ended.
    • A dental platform (iM3, AllAccem, Dental Focus, David AI) was built by acquisition and has offset the US orthopaedic decline, lifting MedTech's adjusted EBITA margin from 23.7% to 25.8%.
    • Six of the framework's twelve internal forces are at B and none at A
      • the screen's rule of three strong forces is met.
      • There is no guidance increase, which is why 'Change Forces' is B and not A.
  • The external forces net to a mild headwind that the niches have outgrown.
    • US clinic visits fell 1.9% year on year in Q2 2026, the 17th consecutive quarter of contraction, yet Specialty Pharma grew 14% organically (about 9% underlying) and MedTech 11% in the same quarter, because their demand is chronic and membership-based rather than elective.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned at the top line and in the segment margins, and not yet where the return is made.
    • Organic growth ran 5%, 5%, 7%, 9%, 12% over the five quarters to Q2 2026
    • MedTech swung from -4% to +11%
    • both 2026 quarters beat consensus by 2% to 4% on revenue and 4% on adjusted EBITA.
    • The group adjusted EBITA margin is flat (24.5% in Q2 2026 against 24.3%), and has fallen from 26.3% in 2023 to 24.8% in 2025.
    • Cash conversion collapsed to 13% in Q2 2026 (operating cash flow EUR 3.8m against adjusted EBITA EUR 29.7m) on a EUR 21.3m working-capital build
      • management says it reverses in Q3 2026.
    • The sell-side has moved from cutting (FY2026 mean earnings per share -11.5% over twelve months) to nudging up (+9.6% over one month, +1.4% over three, at 2026-09-18).
  • The change has travelled through resources, choices and actions into the key operating metrics; it has reached the financial statements only at revenue and segment profit, not the cash-flow statement, and the sell-side's estimates only in the last one to three months.
    • That is the "input plus early output" stage the 'Major Corporate Change' pattern looks for, and it is also why the output the pattern is named for, an upgraded earnings trajectory, is not yet on the page.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share (brokers' adjusted basis) of EUR 0.100 in FY2026 rising to EUR 0.157 in FY2029, about 16% a year, a little below consensus at 17%.
    • Revenue contributes about 10 points a year, margin mix about 1.6 points, and below-EBITA normalisation (financial items, tax, comparability items) about 4.5 points, which is a larger share from the least prized source than the framework wants.
    • The FY2027 step is +22% on our path and +26% on consensus
      • without the below-EBITA relief it is about 12%.
  • What it costs is the problem.
    • At SEK 35.7 the shares are at 21.8x consensus 24-month-forward earnings, the 58th percentile of ten years and the 53rd of five, and 23.3x on our path
    • the price has risen from SEK 27.90 in February 2026, +28%.
    • Valued at the stock's own five-year median of 21.2x on the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the shares are worth about SEK 38.6 in eighteen months and SEK 44.9 in three years, with no dividend: 5.4% and 8.0% a year.
    • Earnings growth carries the whole return
      • the multiple is the swing factor, and on our path the re-rating leg is about -9%.
  • The chain holds at the operating level and breaks at the return.
    • The change forces are B, the outputs are C, and the 'Modal return' is F at this price
    • the case is material in what it is doing to the company and immaterial in what it pays the shareholder from here.
    • The price at which the eighteen-month test clears the 20% bar on our path is about SEK 29.4
      • the February-to-April 2026 range of SEK 27.90 to 30.40 is where this case was a buy.

  • What the case therefore is: good change, real early output, fully priced

    • a case to park until the Q3 2026 cash print or a lower price.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material at the operating level ('Change Forces' B, 'Financial Outputs' improving to C) and the third link, a sufficient return, fails at this price ('Modal return' F)

    • the parts bind at the return, and the whole is held at C rather than D because the same thesis clears the bar at about SEK 29 to 30, so the immateriality belongs to the price, not to the change.
    • Not B: the return is the framework's own test of materiality and it is not met.
    • Not D: a D would say the change forces and outputs were themselves weak, and they are not.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against the materiality of the thesis is that the acceleration is a campaign and a soft comparison, and the rest is arithmetic: Specialty Pharma's 14% is 9% underlying
    • MedTech's +11% sits against a -4% comparison
    • the group margin has not moved in three years (26.3% to 24.5%)
    • the cash-flow statement went the wrong way (13% conversion)
    • the EUR 300m adjusted EBITA target for 2030 needs 23% to 27% a year against about 10% delivered historically
    • and the below-EBITA normalisation that makes FY2027 look like +26% on consensus is tax and interest, not business.
  • We concede the campaign, the flat group margin and the cash quarter, and we concede that a fifth of FY2027's step on our own decomposition sits below EBITA.
  • We hold that the change forces are real and broad (a new leadership layer; a reorganised orthopaedics unit whose organic growth swung fifteen points; a dental platform that lifted the segment margin 210 basis points), that the outputs have moved at the top line and the segment line, which is where a 'Major Corporate Change' moves first, and that the Q3 2026 cash print on 22 October 2026 is a test with a date rather than an unknown.
  • Where we side with the case against is the return: at SEK 35.7 the thesis is material in its inputs and outputs and immaterial in its modal return, which is why the rating is C and not B.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because end-market demand is soft, not inflecting: US weighted clinic traffic -1.9% year on year in Q2 2026, the 17th consecutive quarter of decline
    • Vetsource shows a 2.6% drop in clinic volume.
  • Value of care: holding better than the volume.
    • Pet services spending was USD 79 billion in 2025
    • 81% of veterinarians surveyed report tougher macro conditions
    • care is shifting to acute and emergency rather than routine visits.
  • Elective orthopaedics: the most exposed category.

  • Insulated niches: dental is under-penetrated (80% of adult pets have periodontal disease), and allergy and nutrition demand is largely insulated from traffic.
    • Direction: headwind on volume, tailwind in the company's niches.

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because three disruption forces are strengthening rather than being disproved, each aimed at a Vimian channel or product: online pharmacy (pet owners planning online fulfilment rising to 10%; Chewy's Modern Animal clinics), non-surgical orthopaedics (Zoetis's Librela and Solensia; PetVivo's Spryng) and AI point-of-care diagnostics (Vetscan Imagyst, IDEXX inVue Dx).

  • Mitigant: clinics pushed out of pharmacy revenue lean harder on in-clinic diagnostics and consumables, which is where iM3 and Indical sit.
    • Direction: headwind forming.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because input costs are rising (raw material and merchandise costs EUR 73.2m in H1 2026 against EUR 64.8m, +13%), potential US tariffs touch about half of US sales for a low-single-digit percentage-point risk to EBITA (Jefferies), and about 20% of consolidated cost of goods is linked to Taiwan suppliers.
  • Sensitivity: a combined 10% rise in raw material and external costs would cost about EUR 10m of operating profit on the historical cost structure.
  • Mitigants: right-shoring, renegotiated supplier terms, average price increases of 5% implemented.
    • Direction: headwind, partly passed through.
