EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Jacob Aarup-Andersen, Carlsberg's Chief Executive Officer since September 2023, demonstrates strong strategic vision and personal conviction through persistent open-market share purchases and rapid execution of transformational acquisitions, though his complete lack of prior consumer packaged goods experience presents notable execution risk. His tenure has delivered robust financial performance, with the group consistently beating conservative operating profit guidance and successfully integrating the DKK 29.5 billion Britvic acquisition ahead of schedule.

The launch of the Accelerate SAIL strategy in February 2024 marked a fundamental strategic pivot from cost-cutting to organic growth, raising the medium-term revenue target to 4% to 6% while diversifying into soft drinks to reduce beer volume dependency. The investment community maintains robust coverage across six brokerages and eleven analysts, with sentiment shifting positively over eighteen months as management demonstrated operational credibility through upgraded guidance and solid execution.

Evidence on the Chief Executive and Leadership Qualities

The Chief Executive exhibits relentless drive through persistent personal capital investment, purchasing Carlsberg Class B shares immediately upon taking office and continuing through market drawdowns to demonstrate unwavering commitment to shareholder value creation. He demonstrates exceptional speed in decision-making, executing the transformational acquisition of Britvic within months of taking office and reversing years of strategic stagnation through bold portfolio rebalancing.

His strategic adaptability is outstanding, successfully pivoting Carlsberg toward multi-beverage categories and expanding PepsiCo bottling agreements across seventeen markets to navigate structural beer volume declines. Capital allocation discipline remains partial but improving, as the debt-financed Britvic acquisition temporarily pushed leverage to 3.28 times EBITDA, though management prioritized rapid deleveraging to restore the 2.5 times target ahead of schedule.

His communication style is exceptionally transparent, avoiding evasive corporate language while openly discussing competitive headwinds and volume pressures to build substantial trust with analysts and institutional investors. He enforces exceptionally high performance standards, consistently overdelivering against conservative organic operating profit targets and rewiring incentive programs to demand absolute revenue growth rather than local cost management.

Delivery Record and Operational Execution

Carlsberg established a highly credible guidance track record by setting conservative initial forecasts and subsequently upgrading targets multiple times, ultimately delivering 5.2% organic operating profit growth in 2023 and 6.0% in 2024. The Britvic acquisition integration is proceeding ahead of plan, with management raising annual cost synergy expectations to GBP 110 million and achieving 30% of synergies within the first year.

Strategic partnerships strengthened significantly through expanded PepsiCo bottling rights across the Nordics, Baltics, and Central Asia, including a new state-of-the-art beverage plant in Kazakhstan to double regional business scale. Asian volume performance remains mixed, with severe weather-driven destocking in China causing third-quarter organic volumes to drop almost 10 percent, highlighting persistent geographic concentration risk.

Prior turnaround success at ISS demonstrated strong operational delivery, expanding revenues from DKK 70.75 billion to DKK 78.68 billion while restoring underlying operating margin to 4.60% after severe pandemic disruptions.

Governance Structure and Foundation Control

Carlsberg maintains a well-structured supervisory board comprising twelve members with eight elected by shareholders and four employee representatives, providing a strong majority of independent directors to protect public shareholder interests. The dual-class share structure concentrates absolute voting power with the Carlsberg Foundation, which holds 30% of share capital but controls 78% of total voting rights, providing longterm strategic stability while preventing minority public investors from influencing corporate governance. The foundation's legal charter requires substantial annual dividend payouts to fund scientific and cultural activities, creating a structural constraint that could limit capital allocation flexibility during severe industry downturns and potentially starve high-return brand reinvestment. Executive compensation demonstrates partial alignment with shareholders through demanding three-year performance hurdles on share awards, though individual pay breakdowns are primarily disclosed in aggregated format within consolidated filings. Financial restatements at Carlsberg India highlighted localized control weaknesses regarding unrecorded tax liabilities, demonstrating that decentralized operations carry reporting risks that require the refreshed internal control framework to prevent further errors.

Insider Share Dealing and Alignment Signals

Chief Executive Jacob Aarup-Andersen executed a notable open-market purchase of 2,000 Class B shares on September 14, 2026, for DKK 1,690,200, representing approximately 5.3% of his prior holdings and highlighting strong personal financial commitment to the growth strategy. He demonstrated persistent buying conviction through multiple tranches since taking office, expanding his cumulative Class B holdings by 43.7% in a single year to reach 38,070 shares, signaling insider expectations of sustained earnings per share growth over the next three years.

His track record of open-market purchases during his previous turnaround role at ISS was replicated at Carlsberg, where he initiated ownership just four days into his role by acquiring 11,000 shares for DKK 10.8 million, partly funded by forfeited LTI awards redirected into Carlsberg equity. Executive compensation was restructured to reward absolute revenue expansion over local cost-cutting, aligning short-term incentives with the Accelerate SAIL program to encourage calculated risk-taking and accelerate volume and profit growth globally.

Expert Testimony and Risks to Management Credibility

Former senior executives at competing brewers confirm that Carlsberg's non-beer segments now account for approximately 30% of total group volumes, successfully reducing structural volume risks through strategic diversification into soft drinks and low-alcohol extensions. Industry finance leaders warn that beer price elasticity ranges between 0.80 and 0.87 of consumer price index inflation, meaning aggressive pricing above inflation risks hurting volume progression and depressing future earnings per share through negative operational leverage.

Management's confident growth narrative contrasts with actual declines in core beer volumes, as total beer volumes fell 3.5% despite impressive 8.5% soft drinks expansion, indicating that acquisitions are masking structural declines in core brewing operations. Former Britvic commercial directors state that Carlsberg is actively deprioritizing Britvic's own higher-margin brands to fund PepsiCo franchise brands, creating a negative product mix shift that will make long-term margin expansion more difficult to achieve than management projects.

Former supply chain directors describe a highly decentralized organizational structure with extreme local autonomy that leads to slow decision-making, inconsistent quality control, and weak integration capabilities, suggesting operational reforms may deliver slower results than management publicly projects.

Single Largest Risk to Management Credibility

The single largest risk to management's credibility is the Chief Executive's complete lack of direct consumer packaged goods experience before joining Carlsberg, as his entire career was spent running financial service divisions and facility management firms rather than brand-led consumer marketing operations. Combining beer and soft drink distribution systems under the Britvic acquisition introduces severe operational complexity, with distinct route-to-market channels creating potential friction that could cause projected cost synergies to fail and significantly challenge earnings per share growth targets. Sustaining top-line growth faces structural beer volume declines in mature European markets, macroeconomic slowdowns in China, and heavy promotional competitive pressures in Poland, with the risk that premiumization strategies may prove insufficient to offset mainstream volume losses if consumer spending weakens further.
