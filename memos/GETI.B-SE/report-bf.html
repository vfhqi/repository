Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Operating margin compression represents the most severe weakness, with Getinge's adjusted EBITA margin contracting to a structurally depressed 14.0% in 2025, significantly below Sartorius's 25.0% EBITDA margin and even trailing Siemens Healthineers' 22.0% EBITDA margin. The group's historical margin peak of 21% in 2011 deteriorated to a low of 11% in 2018, and despite recovery efforts, the company remains far from historical norms. This margin deficit stems from multiple compounding factors including SEK 367 million in tariff absorption during 2025, with tariffs and currency delivering a combined 3.3 percentage point negative impact on margins in mid-2025. [Cross-ref: Section 2] 215

Pedestrian organic growth emerges as a chronic weakness, with Getinge achieving a lackluster 2.6% organic sales CAGR over the 2014-2024 period, substantially underperforming peer Ambu's 12.0% three-year revenue CAGR. Post-pandemic demand normalization further exposed this vulnerability, as group sales growth slowed to just 0.6% in 2025 following the emergency ventilator boom. This anemic organic trajectory persists despite management's strategic repositioning toward higher-margin consumables and service revenues.

Hardware margin discount creates a profitability ceiling, as capital equipment manufacturing delivers gross margins around 48%, significantly lagging proprietary consumables that command gross margins exceeding 60%. While management actively shifts the revenue mix toward consumables, the capital equipment base still represents 33% of sales as of Q2 2026, limiting near-term margin expansion potential. [Cross-ref: Section 4] Supply chain concentration risks expose operational fragility, with critical component vulnerability to subcontracted electronic components and semiconductors that can halt production of intensive care systems.

Historical component shortages impacted delivery capacity of Cardiosave and ECMO devices by SEK 400 million, while quality problems with deliveries from a key supplier for HLS and PLS consumables constrained shipment volumes in Q3 2024. [Cross-ref: Section 6]

These aggregated plausible problem to monitor collectively illustrate a company struggling with structural profitability challenges, below-median capital efficiency, geographic vulnerability, and mounting competitive pressures that cannot be dismissed as transitory. The convergence of margin compression from tariffs, anemic organic growth relative to peers, and credible competitive threats in core cardiopulmonary niches raises material questions about whether management's 16-19% EBITA margin target by 2028 represents achievable mechanical improvement or requires heroic operational execution.

Outliers worth noting

Getinge's recurring revenue transition stands out as a structural outlier, with consumables and service rising sharply to 67% of sales in Q2 2026 from 61% in 2023, a rate of portfolio transformation that significantly exceeds the typical gradual evolution observed across capital equipment peers. This shift represents an unusually aggressive rebalancing toward high-margin, sticky revenue streams that most medtech competitors achieve only through decades of organic evolution or transformational M&A.

The company's dominant 40-45% share in the cardiovascular surgery device category globally represents an extreme concentration of market power in a critical clinical niche, positioning Getinge at the very top of the competitive hierarchy in life-support equipment. Such commanding market leadership in a regulatory-protected category is highly unusual and suggests nearmonopolistic competitive dynamics in specific cardiopulmonary applications.

Getinge commands world-leading shares of 65% in ECMO and 70% in IABP hardware, concentration levels that place the company in statistical outlier territory relative to the broader medtech landscape where market fragmentation is more typical. These shares represent extraordinary clinical entrenchment that is difficult to replicate and faces minimal direct competition, yet this positioning has not translated into proportionally superior operating margins compared to more diversified peers.

The company's gross margin profile presents a notable anomaly when benchmarked against its peer set, with broker research estimating the 2025 adjusted gross margin at 50.4% compared to 47.6% reported in company filings, a discrepancy that suggests potential differences in calculation methodologies or one-time adjustments that management has not fully reconciled in public disclosures. Management's expectation to eliminate SEK 200-400 million in annual costs through resolving legacy FDA quality issues represents an unusually large structural cost overhang for a company of Getinge's scale, suggesting that regulatory remediation expenses have been absorbing 1-2% of revenues annually.

The magnitude of this compliance burden is atypical even among medtech peers facing regulatory scrutiny and implies that operational profitability has been materially suppressed by issues that are theoretically transitory. Additionally, the company's working capital days hovered at 88.8 days in 2025, which sits at the higher end of the peer distribution and contrasts sharply with Siemens Healthineers' significantly lower 36.5 days, pointing to structural inefficiencies in inventory management or extended payment terms that have not been adequately explained by management commentary.

The divergence between company-reported and broker-calculated free cash flow figures presents another unexplained oddity, with company reports stating 2025 FCF at SEK 2,652 million whereas brokers report SEK 2,123 million, a SEK 529 million difference attributed to Carnegie's exclusion of acquisitions and divestments.

This material variance in a core financial metric suggests that the treatment of M&A-related cash flows lacks standardized disclosure, creating potential confusion around the true underlying cash generation capability of the operating business. Finally, Bernstein's calculation of 2024 leverage at 1.86x versus company reports showing 1.60x Net Debt/EBITDA represents a meaningful discrepancy in a key credit metric, stemming from differing treatments of lease liabilities within gross debt calculations.

This inconsistency in leverage reporting methodology has not been transparently reconciled by management and could create misleading impressions of the company's true financial flexibility and debt capacity.
