Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

The first material underperformance emerges in EBITDA margin density, where Hera's consolidated 11.2% EBITDA margin in fiscal year 2025 trails the peer average of approximately 12-15% achieved by comparable regional multi-utilities such as Acea (15.0%), A2A (12.0%), and Iren (14.5%). This margin gap reflects Hera's higher exposure to low-margin liberalised retail energy supply activities, which compress grouplevel profitability relative to peers with greater concentration in pure-play regulated infrastructure assets.

The margin disadvantage becomes particularly pronounced when compared against the sector outlier Italgas, whose pure-play gas distribution model delivers a 65.0% EBITDA margin through near-total regulated asset concentration. [Cross-ref: Section 2]

The second identifiable weakness surfaces in gas distribution market share concentration, where Hera commands only a 7% national market share in a sector dominated by Italgas's overwhelming 54% post-merger position. This competitive imbalance is further quantified by the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index reaching approximately 3,300 for Italian gas distribution, signaling extreme market concentration that limits Hera's ability to capture material organic volume growth outside its established northern regional clusters.

The structural reality constrains strategic optionality, forcing the group to pursue inorganic bolt-on acquisitions rather than organic territorial expansion to achieve scale economies comparable to the national monopolist. [Cross-ref: Section 7] The third plausible problem to monitor manifests in retail customer portfolio volatility, where gas customer counts contracted by 165.0 thousand connections (8.4%) and electricity connections fell by 107.2 thousand customers (4.2%) during the first half of 2026.

These losses stem primarily from the managed expiry of low-margin lastresort and transitional protection schemes, yet they expose Hera to heightened churn risk as competitive intensity escalates ahead of the automatic free-market transition scheduled for April 2027. The attrition rates exceed typical sector churn benchmarks and introduce execution risk around the successful commercial retention of the large STG customer cohort under intensifying price competition from national retailers. [Cross-ref: Section 5]

Outliers worth noting

Hera's adjusted return on net invested capital of 9.4% in H1 2026 and historical full-year ROI of 10.4% in fiscal 2024 position the group in the upper performance quartile when benchmarked against Italian multi-utility peers, yet these metrics do not constitute extreme statistical outliers relative to the broader European utility universe. The peer comparison table reveals that Italgas S.p.A. exhibits a substantial margin outlier with an EBITDA margin of 65.0%, driven by its pure-play regulated gas distribution focus with near-zero retail or waste volume exposure across assets.

This performance level sits well above Hera's blended margin profile and represents a top-tier anomaly within the Italian regulated network sector, though it reflects business model specialization rather than operational outperformance within comparable segments.
