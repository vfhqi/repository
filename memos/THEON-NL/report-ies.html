Theon International (THEON-NL): what the Q3 2026 results, and the response to them, do to the existing case

The results leave the operating half of our case a little stronger and give its balance-sheet hinge a first better-than-expected print, but the week since has weakened the half the market prices: net, the case is modestly weaker, and the weakening comes from how a clean beat was received, not from the beat.

1. What changed

  • Operating lines beat, as the case expected. Q3 2026 revenue was €134.1 million (+40.4% year on year) against Deutsche Bank's €130 million; the case expected about 132 EURm. Q3 adjusted EBIT was €35.1 million, a 26.2% margin; the case expected a company-basis margin near 23.5%. Nine-month revenue was €382.84 million against consensus of €379.0 million, and adjusted EBIT €100.2 million against €99.4 million.
  • The beat came from outside night vision. Nine-month night-vision sales were €315.5 million, below consensus of €319.2 million. Thermal was €30.7 million against €23.3 million and platform €26.0 million against €22.0 million. Non-night-vision reached 17.6% of nine-month sales, from 7.1%.
  • Gross profit beat by far more than EBIT. Nine-month gross profit was €139.0 million, 5.1% above consensus; adjusted EBIT was 0.8% above. The mix gain did not drop through.
  • The balance sheet beat, which the case did not expect. Net debt at end Q3 2026 was €251.1 million, 10% below consensus of €279.0 million, at 1.6x LTM EBITDA. Working capital was 42.7% of LTM revenue against Deutsche Bank's 45%; the case also expected about 45%. FY2026 capex guidance fell to about €25 million from about €30 million, with the balance moved into FY2027.
  • The case's own test was missed. The case called a nine-month book-to-bill at or above 1.0x "the number that matters". It was 0.74x, on order intake of €285.2 million (+22.5%), 0.8% below consensus. Deutsche Bank puts the Q4 intake needed for a full-year 1.2x at about €440 million.
  • Germany arrived early. The German parliament approved the DAMON MR contract: up to 60,000 units worth more than €450 million, with an initial call-off of nearly €200 million for 25,000 units, about half of that Q4 requirement. The units use 18mm tubes made by Harder Digital, not bought from Exosens.
  • The medium-term target went up. Organic growth guidance rose to more than 20% a year from more than 15%; FY2026 guidance was reconfirmed. The case expected the medium-term ambitions to be the ones "most likely to be pushed out".
  • The response. Deutsche Bank kept Buy and its €39 target on 10 November 2026, only fine-tuned estimates, and still projects FY2026 net debt of about €573 million and 3.4x. It is the only broker response in hand; Bernstein does not rate the stock. The dashboard shows consensus EPS and target unchanged over one month, the Buy share at 89% from the case's 100%, and the shares at 26.86 EUR on 13 November 2026 against the case's 30.42 EUR on 30 October.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the dispute is no longer the tube cap; it is the case's non-night-vision build. The first-morning reading was that the raised target meant the cap had lifted. On the evidence now in hand that form loses force. Night vision was the one line below consensus, and the company's own FY2029 mix, about 50% night vision in about €1 billion, implies about €500 million of night vision. The case has 588 EURm. The company is not arguing with the cap; it is planning inside it. What it argues with is the other half: about €500 million of non-night-vision in FY2029 against the case's roughly 357 EURm (38% of 945.5 EURm). For this to be right, non-night-vision must grow from €67.3 million in nine months to that level in three years, on products where the company is, in the case's words, a sub-scale entrant with no moat. Thermal and platform running 32% and 18% ahead of consensus says the direction is real. We rate the company's level possible and a level well above the case's plausible. The case holds faster non-night-vision only as a margin counter-case, never as the revenue base.

Reading two: the raise is bad news for the line the market pays for. The case's central positive is that cash "improves mechanically" because growth slows: working-capital absorption falls from 48 to 27 EURm a year as growth drops to 13% and 11%. The company has now said growth does not slow. At a ratio near the reported 42.7%, each extra €100 million of revenue absorbs about €43 million. On our arithmetic the leverage ratio barely suffers, because EBITDA rises too. Free cash flow does: the case's 94 and 120 EURm for FY2028 and FY2029 depend on the slowdown. If the market is paying for cash and not growth, as the case says, a beat-and-raise that defers cash is a reason to sell, and the shares fell. For this to be right, the ratio must stay near 42% (no prepayments, no MilDef-style release) and the target must be delivered. We rate it plausible. The case has no place for it: it treats upgrades and deleveraging as independent goods.

Reading three: the discount is structural, not dated. The case said good operating prints would not re-rate the stock but that a net-debt print "not worse than expected" would start to move the narrative. That print came in 10% better. The shares are about 12% lower, relative strength is at the 9th percentile, and a broker left Buy, which the case expected only after a third bad net-debt print. We cannot attribute all of the fall to the results, because the dates straddle them. But the strongest form is this: the market is not waiting for a print; it is pricing a peak the sell-side puts at 3.4x, against the case's 2.9x and a covenant the case puts at 3.5x or possibly 3.0x. A €28 million beat did not move that projection. For this to be right, the gap must be real (reported, not pro-forma, EBITDA) and not a matter of definition. We rate it possible; it gained force this week.

Dropped: the raise as cover for a weak order quarter. Intake was within 1% of consensus, the quarter was expected to be soft, and nearly €200 million has been approved since. What survives is arithmetic, not motive: about €240 million more is needed in Q4 for 1.2x.

One reading cuts the other way. A frontline NATO buyer has taken Harder tubes, which industry experts called unsuitable for that use. The case holds this as a counter-case that eases supplier dependence. It also makes nearly €200 million of orders dependent on a tube source with about 15,000 units of annual capacity.

3. What it does to the case

  • Current momentum (C, 54): more pressure, and it would probably move. The rating rested on mixed measures. Sell-side earnings momentum is now D from C, technical momentum is F, and the 100% Buy offset has gone.
  • Scope for upgrades to guidance, consensus and the narrative (D, 45): pressure upward on guidance, none on consensus. A five-point raise is not the 1% to 3% the rating called small. Consensus did not follow in the first week.
  • Conservative guidance and the delivery record (B, 76): slightly less pressure. The net-debt record is no longer unbroken. Whether a 20% target is still conservative is untested.
  • Pricing power, operating leverage and the flat margin (D, 30): the same, and supported. Gross profit beat by 5.1% and EBIT by 0.8%.
  • The tube-cap judgement (model 853 EURm for FY2028): less pressure than it first appeared. The pressure moves to the non-night-vision build.
  • Forecast financials (A, 91): the same on EPS; more pressure on the free-cash-flow path.
  • Riskiness (D, 42) and Case Riskiness : slightly more pressure. The broker's 3.4x was reiterated after the beat.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength , Foundations' Robustness , Setups' Fit/Crispness : the same.
  • Price. At 26.86 EUR the shares are above the 23.34 EUR low the case names and 14.5% below the 200-day average it wanted regained; neither threshold is crossed. They are below the case's 29.20 EUR return base; against the case's 32.93 EUR value the 18-month return is about 23%, above the 10% to 20% band its return rating sat in. Valuation stays B.

4. What would settle it

No later review follows this one. Of the questions left open on the results morning:

  • Whether consensus moves toward the raised target: not settled. One broker has responded and did not move; the consensus figures are flat over one month.
  • The Bundestag stage: approval and the call-off are reported; the review gives no date, so whether the committee or the full vote has passed is not confirmed.
  • The HGH closing date: not settled. The review carries both an early-2027 closing and a FY2026 net debt that includes the purchase.

What would settle the open readings:

  • Q4 order announcements through December 2026, and full-year intake with the FY2026 results, which the case dates to 19 March 2027: at or above €440 million ends the order question.
  • The same results: end-2026 net debt, the year-end working-capital ratio against 42.7%, and whether "below 2.5x in 2027" is restated beside more than 20% growth. That pairing is the cleanest test of reading two.
  • First FY2027 guidance and the product split in it, for reading one. Each quarter's non-night-vision share is the running test.
  • An HGH closing announcement, before or after 31 December 2026, for reading three.
  • Exosens' Q4 release for tube volumes, the case's own marker, and first DAMON MR deliveries for Harder's quality.
  • Not committed by the company: the covenant level and its EBITDA definition, the base and years to which "more than 20%" applies, Theon's tube allocation, and any cash or interest guidance. Nothing scheduled answers these, and reading three turns on the first.

VERDICT

  • Direction: negative
  • Grade: semi-material
  • Moves: Current momentum (C, 54) toward D; sell-side earnings momentum letter C to D; Buy share 100% to 89%
  • Reason: The results were a beat on every operating line and on net debt, which modestly helps the case, but the net is negative because the one rating that would move a full grade is momentum, after the market and the sell-side marked the shares and their stance down on exactly the print the case expected to start the turn.
