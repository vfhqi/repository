Section 3 – FX Translation and Transaction Effects on Swedish Exporters

Section summary
  1. Swedish exporters rely primarily on natural hedging and localized production to mitigate the transaction impacts of a strengthening krona.
  2. Industrial and medtech exporters remain highly sensitive to translation headwinds, which immediately dilute reported sales and operating profits when consolidated into Swedish kronor.

Pricing Power and Cost Structures

1.
High pricing power: Sectra possesses strong pricing power because it specializes in highly differentiated, niche medical IT and secure communication solutions. With more than 70% of its sales generated in foreign currencies like the US dollar, euro, and British pound, its revenue is highly sensitive to translation effects. However, its proprietary technology allows the company to maintain high margins and absorb immediate currency fluctuations without losing market share.

Moderate price adjustments: Lindab struggles with low market volumes in Western Europe but utilizes active pricing strategies to defend its profitability. The company faces high raw material and transport inflation, which it attempts to offset by raising prices to customer groups. Consequently, Lindab chooses to rely on local price management rather than financial derivatives because 81% of its sales occur outside Sweden.
3.
Global cost distribution: Trelleborg mitigates transaction risks by establishing manufacturing facilities in emerging geographies to match its regional sales. The company generates the vast majority of its earnings outside Sweden, leaving its consolidated results heavily exposed to translation headwinds when the krona strengthens. Through continuous structural improvements and geographical expansion, Trelleborg has successfully managed to reduce its domestic cost concentration over several business cycles.
4.
Severe structural exposure: Elekta faces complex currency headwinds due to its international supply chain mismatch, where expenses in British pounds and Chinese yuan confront revenues in US dollars. The company manages this structural vulnerability by deepening the localization of its supply chain and product sourcing. These initiatives aim to expand gross margins and mitigate transaction exchange rate risks over the medium term.

Hedging Strategies and Margin Impacts

6.

Consequently, the company does not hedge transaction risks, whereas peers like Elekta actively hedge up to 24 months of foreign currency flows.

Divergent financial hedging: Elekta actively uses forward contracts to hedge between 50% and 100% of its recognized foreign currency assets and liabilities. In contrast, Lindab reports zero outstanding transaction hedges on its balance sheet, choosing instead to rely entirely on natural hedges. This structural variance means Elekta can temporarily smooth operating margins during krona appreciation cycles, while Lindab faces immediate gross profit volatility.
8.
Low volume sensitivity: Morgan Stanley reported that Swedish industrial exporters are structurally insulated from exchange rate moves because their exports consist of highly differentiated, high-value goods. The broker noted that Swedish export volumes exhibit statistically insignificant sensitivity to currency fluctuations due to substantial foreign-currency invoicing. Consequently, krona movements pass through weakly to the prices foreign buyers face, allowing exporters to preserve global market shares during appreciation phases.

Modest margin drag: DNB Carnegie reported that Trelleborg’s foreign exchange headwinds moderated notably from 9% in the first quarter of 2026 to negative 1% by the second quarter. The broker estimated that currency effects would only drag operating earnings by roughly SEK 20 m in the second quarter. Consequently, the stronger krona had a neutral impact on operating margins, as high-value acquisitions and pricing strategies successfully supported profitability.

During the first half of 2015, transaction exposures from a weaker currency had boosted sector EBITA margins by 1.3 percentage points before reversing. This historical cycle indicates that exporters struggle to sustain operating margin expansion when currency tailwinds dissipate and organic growth fails to accelerate.
