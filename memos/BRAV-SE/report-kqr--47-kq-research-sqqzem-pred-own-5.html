Section 2 – The 2027 Swedish Collective Agreements Landscape

Section summary
  1. Negotiations for the spring 2027 Swedish collective agreements will shape installation sector labor costs amid high demands for specialized electricians.
  2. Structural labor shortages and digital infrastructure growth are poised to drive persistent wage drift above negotiated baseline increases.

Bargaining Timetable and Institutional Positions

1.
Timetable and institutional framework: The collective bargaining process for the spring 2027 Swedish wage round, known as Avtalsrörelsen, is structurally anchored by the manufacturing sector's benchmark agreement. The central framework establishes the "mark" (industry standard) which traditionally dictates the baseline for subsequent agreements across the economy. Negotiations between the employer organization Installatörsföretagen and both Svenska Elektrikerförbundet (representing electrical installers) and Byggnads (representing plumbing and ventilation installers) will peak in the spring of 2027 as existing agreements expire. These talks operate under a central decentralisation framework where nationwide industry-level agreements establish binding minimum standards that override conflicting local arrangements. 28 for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) Union demands and labor dynamics: Svenska Elektrikerförbundet and Byggnads enter the 2027 round with strong bargaining leverage due to a severe structural shortage of skilled trade professionals. Historically, the Swedish Trade Union Confederation (LO) has demanded wage increases exceeding Riksbank expectations, citing elevated corporate profit shares. For the 2027 cycle, unions are expected to prioritize substantial increases in agreed minimum wages, enhanced apprentice compensation, and stricter safety and work-life balance provisions. This aggressive positioning reflects a recovering macroeconomic environment where purchasing power demands are reinforced by long-term labor shortages.
2.
Employer counter-positions and pricing pressure: Installatörsföretagen's primary objective is to maintain cost competitiveness and mitigate margin erosion for installation contractors. Employers argue that excessive wage hikes cannot be fully passed on to clients due to intense price competition in certain construction segments. The employer group will advocate for moderate wage increases aligned with stabilizing domestic inflation and the Riksbank's long-term targets. Furthermore, employers are pushing for greater flexibility in local labor allocation and working hours to optimize project execution in a resource-constrained market.

Quantitative Wage Forecasts and Drift Drivers

3.
Minimum wage estimates versus actual forecasts: A clear divergence exists between expected agreed minimum wage increases and forecasted actual pay increases. SEB estimates that agreed minimum wages could rise by about 8% in the 2027 agreements, representing a significant front-loaded adjustment to historical minimum scales. In contrast, Swedbank’s August 2026 economic outlook projects that total wage growth will average around 3.2% per year in 2027–2028, reflecting a normalization compared to the previous two-year agreements. This 3.2% projection aligns closely with the Riksbank's anticipated total wage growth of 3.2% to 3.4% for the period. Meanwhile, Nordea's macroeconomic baseline forecasts actual Swedish wage growth to remain just below 3.0% a year, supported by stabilizing domestic demand.
4.
Structural drivers of installer wage drift: Wage drift, which represents actual paid wage increases exceeding centrally negotiated agreements, is driven by structural imbalances in the Swedish labor market. The technical installation sector faces a compounding supply deficit because vocational school enrollment dropped 15% between 2015 and 2024. Furthermore, approximately one-quarter of the Swedish construction workforce is expected to retire by 2030, reducing the pool of experienced mentors. Consequently, intense local competition for skilled installers to deliver complex high-voltage and data-center projects drives persistent wage drift.
5.
Sustained demand from infrastructure expansion: Rapid industrial and societal electrification projects across Sweden provide a durable demand anchor that fuels wage pressure. Municipalities and private developers are accelerating investments in electrical grids, energy storage, and water supply infrastructure. Veidekke's October 2026 market update forecasts that the Swedish civil engineering market will grow by 6% in 2027, driven largely by public transport and energy projects. This elevated infrastructure spending ensures that specialized installers remain in high demand, allowing local unions to secure wage premiums well above central targets.

Contrary Evidence and Alternatives

6.
Contrary evidence: Although labor shortages are severe, a weaker-than-expected recovery in residential new builds could moderate wage drift and overall salary pressures in the wider installation market. Swedbank warns that the primary downside risk to wage projections is a weaker economic recovery, which would quickly reduce overall hiring needs and ease union leverage. Furthermore, historical data from the Swedish National Mediation Office indicates that wage drift has occasionally flattened during periods of macroeconomic uncertainty. If the broader construction sector remains subdued, the pool of general installers may reallocate to technical projects, dampening wage pressure.
7.
Other side of the story: While a residential construction slowdown may temporarily ease general labor constraints, it will not resolve the acute shortage of highly specialized technicians required for green transition and digital projects. Nimlas Group highlights that the expansion of AI computing infrastructure is expected to triple European demand by 2030, requiring physical, on-site installation of complex electrical, cooling, and fire protection systems. Because these specialized installations cannot be completed remotely, demand for qualified electricians and ventilation specialists remains decoupled from the general construction cycle. Therefore, even if standard building labor costs stabilize, contractors executing highly technical projects will face sustained wage drift and localized personnel cost inflation.

Section 3 – Historical Margin Response to 2023 and 2025 Wage Rounds

Section summary
  1. Historical Swedish wage agreements in 2023 and 2025 created transient margin pressure for both Bravida and Instalco due to legacy fixed-price obligations.
  2. Structural pricing discipline and extensive indexation clauses helped contractors stabilize margins during subsequent periods.
  3. Peer comparisons highlight that decentralized models and active contract management are critical to protect operating margins from sudden labor cost inflation.

1.
Margin deterioration: Bravida encountered short-term margin contraction in its Swedish operations during the 2023 wage round because of high inflation and fixed-price contract lags. Specifically, the company was unable to pass on all immediate cost increases to customers during the high-inflation environment of late 2023. This cost mismatch ultimately led to lower labor productivity and diluted the EBITA margin temporarily. Consequently, the firm adjusted its pricing procedures for new tenders to protect long-term earnings. 57 58 - Earnings Calls Decentralized resilience: Instalco leveraged its highly decentralized operating model to mitigate the inflationary impact of the 2023 Swedish wage negotiations. This flexible structure means that local management can offset negotiated wage hikes by adjusting personnel capacity to match volatile project demands. Sweden profitability: Sweden remained the strongest regional earnings contributor for both contractors despite the mounting wage pressures of the 2023 round. DNB Carnegie highlights that Sweden generally offers the highest EBITA margins in the installation sector, followed by Norway and Finland. Impact of the 2025 Wage Round

2.
Contract selectivity: Bravida shifted its operational focus toward project selectivity during the 2025 wage rounds to protect profitability over volume. This deliberate restriction in order intake caused a temporary decline in net sales but protected the group's bottom line. Consequently, the group successfully lifted its EBITA margin to 5.4% in the second quarter of 2025 from 4.5% in the prior year. This strategic pivot shows that restricting risk-heavy installation projects prevents wage-driven margin erosion. Sweden margin pressure: Instalco faced localized profitability headwinds in Sweden during 2025 as wage inflation intersected with slow market demand. The company's Swedish segment reported a decline in EBITA to SEK 554 million in 2025 from SEK 613 million in 2024. DNB Carnegie noted that weaker Swedish performance was particularly visible in the southern regions of the country. This trend demonstrates that unhedged wage increases in slow regional markets can depress technical service profitability unless local prices are raised. Cost restructuring: Bravida initiated a massive restructuring of its Swedish operations following the 2025 wage round to optimize its long-term cost base. The firm merged its North, Central, and South divisions into a single national organization to eliminate administrative redundancies. This integration incurred a SEK 20 million charge in late 2025 and is expected to cost up to SEK 90 million in 2026. Management anticipates these measures will yield annual cost savings of SEK 65 million to support future earnings.

Contract Indexation and Margin Protection

3.
Indexation insulation: Long-term agreements at Bravida utilize indexation clauses to absorb cost increases and insulate project profitability. The company actively uses the Swedish contracting index to adjust pricing for labor and material inflation on multi-year projects. This contract architecture ensures that unexpected labor rate hikes are transferred to end customers. Consequently, these contractual protections shield long-term project margins from the full brunt of collective bargaining agreements.
4.
Fixed-price exposure: Instalco remains partially exposed to wage rounds because approximately 43% of its projects are delivered under fixed-price contracts. The company acknowledged that fixed-price projects are vulnerable to rising inflation because pricing is locked before wage increases materialize. This vulnerability forced the company to enhance its pricing discipline and introduce value-added pricing under its Instalco 2.0 program. These pricing initiatives are crucial because protecting fixed-price contracts stabilizes consolidated operating margins during sudden labor shocks.
5.
Pricing discipline: Both companies focused heavily on pricing strategies during the 2023 and 2025 wage negotiations to prevent contract backlog dilution. Instalco prioritized its pricing capacity as a core pillar of the Instalco 2.0 efficiency program. This systematic focus is designed to improve project calculation and enforce margin-supportive pricing. This structural alignment means that improving value-added pricing expertise allows the firm to reclaim its long-term group EBITA margin target of 8%.

Peer Performance and Benchmarking

6.
EBITA benchmarking: Instalco has historically maintained a higher margin profile than its peer group through rigorous cost control. DNB Carnegie reports that Instalco achieved an EBITA margin of approximately 8% in 2021, compared to a peer average of 5.5%. This superior profitability was partially supported by Instalco's heavy geographical concentration in the lucrative Swedish market. However, the post-2023 cost inflation caused its margin to fall to 5.8% by 2025, demonstrating that sustained wage pressures affect even highmargin operators.

Peer margin compression: Broader Nordic peers experienced similar margin pressure as labor cost inflation outpaced general contract pricing. Inderes highlights that Nordic infrastructure peer Infrea experienced volatile profitability, with its EBITA margins collapsing from mid-single digits in 2022 to near break-even in 2023 and 2024. This peer performance demonstrates that thin contract margins are vulnerable to labor and material inflation. Therefore, contractors must implement strict project risk screening to preserve earnings per share. Contrary evidence: Although wage agreements present margin risks, both Bravida and Instalco have demonstrated their ability to quickly recover profitability through cost-plus contract flexibility. Instalco reports that approximately 57% of its sales are derived from cost-plus and running bill compensation models. These dynamic compensation frameworks allow contractors to immediately bill clients for actual labor hours worked at updated rates. Profitability sensitivity concerns: Bravida's earnings remain highly sensitive to labor cost developments because a single percentage point increase in payroll expenses reduces its annual profit before tax by SEK 95 million. If negotiated wages settle significantly above historical averages, the firm's cost base will experience immediate upward pressure. Consequently, we judge that unauthorized local wage drift could neutralize the planned savings of SEK 65 million from Bravida's ongoing organizational restructuring. This means that unexpected settlement terms would severely dilute forecasted earnings per share unless pricing models are radically transformed.

Downstream Pressures on Subcontractors

3.
Subcontractor financial distress: Sustained labor inflation and high minimum wage demands will likely intensify financial distress among specialized subcontractors across the Swedish market. Because subcontractors account for 15% of Instalco's total cost base, insolvencies within this segment could trigger widespread project execution delays. We believe that rising labor expenses would accelerate subcontractor bankruptcy rates, which historically spiked during previous construction downturns. This supply chain vulnerability would force primary contractors to absorb expensive replacement labor costs on critical projects.
4.
Industry consolidation shifts: A non-consensus wage round could accelerate consolidation as smaller, financially weaker technical installers fail to navigate rising personnel expenses. Larger players like Bravida can leverage their extensive balance sheets to acquire troubled regional installers at depressed multiples. However, Instalco's elevated leverage including earn-out liabilities of 2.8x might restrict bolt-on acquisition activity in the near term. This financial divergence will likely reshape the competitive landscape by allowing larger peers to gain market share.

Public Developer Leverage and Strategies

5.
Public buyer resistance: The Swedish Transport Administration, Trafikverket, has the power to resist price increases by enforcing strict, price-driven contract procurement policies. Trafikverket's aggressive stance is highlighted by its recent moves to withhold payments and issue billions in recovery claims against inefficient contractors. If public clients refuse to accept inflation adjustments, technical installers will face severe cash flow constraints. Consequently, contractors will struggle to pass on actual wage increases on major public civil engineering projects.
6.
Infrastructure spending buffers: Increased public infrastructure allocations provide a critical buffer that could offset localized commercial and residential installation weaknesses. The Swedish government has increased infrastructure allocations by 27% in its national plan for 2026 to 2037. We expect this funding boost will sustain high demand for specialists in the electrical grid and civil engineering sectors. This sustained public spending should support industry pricing power even if negotiated minimum wage settlements exceed expectations.

Early Signals of Non-Consensus Scenarios

7.
Improving market sentiment: The Economic Sentiment Indicator for Sweden rose further in September 2026, which points to a broadening recovery across the domestic economy. This macroeconomic improvement is driven by positive sentiment in the construction sector and rising household confidence. We analyze that a stronger economic backdrop will strengthen union bargaining leverage heading into the spring 2027 negotiations. This rising economic confidence could trigger aggressive union wage demands that exceed current consensus expectations.
8.
174, FX and Macro Research Capital allocation revisions: A shift toward higher interest rates in Sweden could force property developers to delay private construction investments and reduce installation project volumes. While the Riksbank kept its policy rate at 1.75% in the third quarter of 2026, experts expect rates to rise to 2.25% by early 2027. Continued high financing costs will likely depress new housing starts and keep the residential construction segment subdued. These capital allocation constraints will limit the contractor pricing power required to pass through sudden labor cost shocks.
9.
Specialized vacancy escalation: The persistent shortage of technical professionals in northern Sweden acts as an early warning signal of localized wage pressure. High demand for energy transmission and electricity storage projects is actively drawing skilled talent away from southern urban centers. We argue that this regional resource drain will amplify skilled labor scarcity and drive significant local salary inflation. Consequently, installers working on complex industrial sites will demand unprecedented salary premiums to remain on schedule.
10.
Contrary evidence: A weaker economic recovery in Sweden could suppress overall hiring demand and ease union bargaining leverage during the 2027 negotiations. Swedbank's economic outlook warns that a delayed residential construction recovery would limit the volume of new projects and reduce labor scarcity. Consequently, a subdued macroeconomic environment would prevent excessive wage drift and allow installer salaries to remain within historical ranges. This means that contractors would maintain stable labor cost bases without experiencing margin dilution.
11.
Other side of the story: While a weak construction market could temporarily cap baseline wage settlements, the structural deficit of skilled installers guarantees localized cost inflation. High-growth segments like digital infrastructure and grid electrification will continue to absorb specialized technicians, regardless of broader residential trends. Therefore, we judge that contractors cannot rely on general macroeconomic weakness to protect margins, as the acute scarcity of certified electricians will sustain wage pressure. Ultimately, only companies with robust pricing power can protect their long-term profit margins from severe wage rounds.
