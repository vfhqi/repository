Section 2 – The SwapClear Revenue-Share Change and Retroactive Impact

Section summary
  1. The renegotiated SwapClear partner-bank agreement contributed 140 basis points to adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in H1 2026.
  2. This margin boost was driven by a comparative timing mismatch rather than a retroactive cash payout, which will reverse in late 2026.

The Contractual Renegotiation Details

1.
Significant cost reduction: London Stock Exchange Group plc implemented a revised clearing agreement in late 2025 that significantly restructured its relationship with major partner banks to enhance profitability. The group reduced the revenue-share surplus paid to partner banks from approximately 30% to 15% in 2025, and further down to 10% starting in 2026. This contract renegotiation immediately lowered the cost of sales for the Markets division, which directly supports long-term earnings per share growth unless clearing volumes decline significantly.
2.
Substantial capital deployment: London Stock Exchange Group plc executed this structural change by acquiring the remaining revenue share of the SwapClear business for £1.15 billion in cash. This large-scale transaction was financed through debt and cash reserves while being partially offset by selling a 20% stake in its Post Trade Solutions business for $170 million. This structural realignment ensures that operating margins remain elevated for the next two decades, as the new 10% partner-bank rate is locked in through 2045.
3.
Favorable financial impact: London Stock Exchange Group plc benefited from a substantial reduction in its recurring operating expenses due to the SwapClear restructuring. Rothschild & Co Redburn estimated on October 24, 2025, that the cost of sales impact would reach negative £120 million for the full year 2026. This reduction directly enhances group operating leverage and boosts earnings per share, although comparable partner-bank fee structures for peers like Deutsche Boerse are not publicly available for direct comparison.
4.
High revenue concentration: London Stock Exchange Group plc relies heavily on SwapClear within its broader post-trade clearing operations to drive high-margin clearing volumes. Jefferies Research highlighted on October 5, 2026, that OTC derivatives generate a significant portion of clearing revenue, with SwapClear accounting for approximately 80% of this subsegment. This high concentration explains why renegotiating the revenue share has such a disproportionate impact on the group's total adjusted EBITDA margin compared to smaller business lines.

Of this total expansion, 140 basis points of benefit was directly generated by the modified SwapClear partner-bank revenue-share agreement that reduced cost of sales.

Steady underlying progress: London Stock Exchange Group plc isolated the temporary contract benefits from its core operational performance during the July 30, 2026, earnings call. Aside from the 140 basis points contract gain, the company achieved 120 basis points of underlying constant-currency margin expansion. This organic performance was driven by group operating leverage of 80 basis points and 40 basis points of favorable market transaction flow-through from elevated volumes.
