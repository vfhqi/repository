Judgement: competitive intensity is easing in the United States, where the growth is, and tightening in the United Kingdom and Europe, where most of the profit is. C-04

To what extent is political or regulatory change under way, and how does it bear on the company?

❌D3228th of 30

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: regulation is a live, dated cost headwind with one binary swing, and for a £50m-EBITDA company the swing is material.

Before the answers: the model's cross-check gaps, explained, and what the Financial Analyst should do

The one-page summary flags every forecast year: EPS of 22p, 27p, 32p, 36p and 40p in the model against 26.5p, 33.0p, 39.3p, 43.8p and 48.4p in the second set's cross-check; free cash flow of £35.2m, £42.4m, £44.4m, £42.1m and £45.7m against £30.5m, £48.2m, £50.9m, £48.6m and £52.8m. We traced each gap. None of them shows a forecast input to be wrong; all of them come from one error in how the model applied the inputs, plus one small definitional difference in the cash flow. We therefore change no input and do not write inputs-changed.md; the correction belongs in the model.

The cause: the model grew statutory revenue, not brand revenue. The summary's revenue line starts from FactSet's FY2025 statutory revenue of £325.0m, which books the United States as royalty income plus goods recharged at cost, and applies the second set's growth rates to it: £344.5m, £365.5m, £387.1m, £408.4m, £429.6m. The inputs were written on brand revenue (the company's adjusted revenue, with the United States at Molson Coors' invoiced sales), base £375.3m, as the first convention in Part 4 of the inputs file states; applied to that base they give £397.9m, £422.3m, £447.1m, £471.6m, £495.9m. Every forecast revenue in the model is therefore 13.4% too low, and because the margin input is applied to that revenue, every EBITDA is 13.4% too low: £42.4m against £48.8m (FY2026), £50.4m against £58.3m (FY2027), £57.7m against £66.7m (FY2028), £62.5m against £72.0m (FY2029), £67.0m against £77.5m (FY2030). The lines below EBITDA are applied correctly (the D&A input, the net financial items, the 25% tax rate and the share count all reconcile), so the whole EPS gap is the after-tax EBITDA shortfall over the share count: £6.4m at 75% over about 113m shares is 4.3p in FY2026 (the flagged gap is 4.5p); £7.9m is 5.5p in FY2027 (gap 6p); £9.0m is 6.4p in FY2028 (gap 7p); £9.5m and £10.5m are about 7p and 8p in FY2029-30 (gaps 8p). The residuals are the rounding of the model's two-decimal EPS.

The same error corrupts three other things in the summary. (i) The half-year phasing: the summary says Part 3's growth rates "were moved by −5.9 points to match the year". That adjustment was needed only because the model's H1 2026 actual is FactSet's statutory £165m (+14.4%, an artefact of the licence switch on 1-Feb-25), so forcing a +6.0% year on a statutory base pushes H2 2026 growth to about −0.7%. On brand revenue the half-years reconcile without adjustment: H1 2026 £183.6m (actual) plus H2 2026 £214.3m (£203.7m at +5.2%) equals £397.9m, +6.0%. (ii) The "Viewforth against consensus" lines (revenue −14.0%, −15.8%, −17.6%; EBITDA −15.2%, −21.1%, −25.6%; EPS −24.3%, −30.7%, −34.9%) compare a statutory-based model with a brand-based consensus. The true gaps on our base are revenue −0.7%, −2.7%, −4.9%; EBITDA −2.2%, −8.8%, −13.9%; EPS −8%, −15%, −20%. (iii) The valuation: the summary's "two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple today 30.5x" is 926p over the model's understated forward EPS; on our base it is about 25x and on consensus about 20x.

Free cash flow: the same cause, with the sign reversing after FY2026, plus exceptional cash costs. The working-capital input (13.7%, 11.0%, 9.5% of revenue) was calibrated to the FY2025 balance of £51.4m on brand revenue (13.7% of £375.3m). Applied to statutory revenue, the model's FY2026 working capital is 13.7% of £344.5m, or £47.2m, a spurious £4.2m release against the opening £51.4m, where our base has a £3.1m build (13.7% of £397.9m is £54.5m): £7.3m in the model's favour. Add the £3.0m of exceptional cash costs that the cross-check deducts and the model does not, and subtract the £4.8m after-tax EBITDA shortfall, and the model's FY2026 free cash flow should exceed ours by about £5.5m; it exceeds it by £4.7m. From FY2027 the working-capital release is similar on both bases (the percentage falls on both; the model's release is slightly smaller because its base is smaller: −£1.0m in FY2027, −£0.6m in FY2028, about +£0.3m in FY2029-30), the exceptional costs shrink (£1.5m, £1.0m, £0.5m, £0.5m), and the after-tax EBITDA shortfall dominates (−£5.9m, −£6.8m, −£7.1m, −£7.9m): the predicted gaps are −£5.4m, −£6.4m, −£6.3m and −£7.1m against the summary's −£5.8m, −£6.5m, −£6.5m and −£7.1m. Everything reconciles. The model's own net-cash reconciliation (a £15.1m unexplained difference in FY2026) is an internal problem of the model, not of the inputs; on our base year-end FY2026 net cash is about £44m.

The Financial Analyst should (1) replace the FY2025 revenue base of £325.0m with brand revenue of £375.3m, and the half-year bases with brand revenue of £171.6m (H1 2025), £203.7m (H2 2025) and £183.6m (H1 2026 actual), so that Part 1's growth rates and Part 3's half-year rates apply to the basis they were written on and the −5.9 point phasing adjustment disappears; (2) keep the FY2025 adjusted EBITDA base at £42.4m (11.3% of £375.3m) and the half-year EBITDA bases at £18.4m and £24.0m, so that the margin inputs reproduce guidance (£49.9m) and consensus on the same basis; (3) deduct the exceptional cash costs of £3.0m, £1.5m, £1.0m, £0.5m and £0.5m in the cash flow, or state that the model's free cash flow is before them; and (4) re-run the two-year-forward multiple and the total-shareholder-return split on the corrected EPS, and show the return at a fair multiple in the lower part of the stock's range as well as at the 38.8x guideline (our view of the fair multiple is in D-07 and in the judgement on the fourth set). Until this is done, the forecasts of record understate revenue and EBITDA by 13.4% and EPS by 17-20%, and the summary's comparisons with consensus and its valuation should not be used in the memo; the second set's cross-check figures are the ones to use.

One further basis point, because one of the new reports contradicts it. The report on adjusted EBITDA definitions (file 11) states in one place that Fevertree "retains share-based payments as an operational cost within adjusted EBITDA".
