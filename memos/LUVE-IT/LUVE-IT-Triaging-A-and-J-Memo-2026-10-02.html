[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. LU-VE is a demand-inflection case in a mid-margin component manufacturer: data-centre and power-generation cooling demand has lifted the orders, the revenue, the margin and the sell-side's estimates, and the company built the capacity in the right place in time to take it. At EUR 69.40 the shares already pay for that delivery and for a multiple the stock has never held for long, so the business is worth owning and the price is not; the case clears the framework's 20 percent bar below about EUR 50, not at EUR 69.

  • The change is external, structural and sector-wide.
    • The AI-driven data-centre build-out, through the shift to high-temperature liquid cooling at the rack that rejects more heat outdoors per megawatt, and the power-generation infrastructure that feeds it, have lifted demand for the large dry coolers and air-cooled equipment LU-VE makes
    • Air conditioning applications, which carry the data-centre line, rose 41.9 percent to EUR 92.8m in H1 2026, 28.5 percent of application turnover
      • Industrial cooling, which carries power generation, rose 25.0 percent to EUR 42.1m
    • The same force shows across the peer set: Alfa Laval's organic orders rose 29 percent in Q2 2026, Carel's H1 2026 revenue rose 20.9 percent on data-centre cooling, Kelvion's data-centre business is over half its mix and growing over 50 percent, and industry lead times for heat exchangers are 20 to 30 weeks
    • A second external engine is contractual in its timing: the revised EU F-Gas Regulation bans new units on refrigerants above 150 GWP from January 2027 and cuts the HFC quota to 11 percent in 2027, moving demand towards the natural-refrigerant products that are 58 percent of LU-VE's turnover
  • The company's own change is a capacity choice made in time, not a transformation.
    • The same chief executive, strategy and structure
    • What changed is that the Jacksonville plant was doubled to over 30,000 square metres and inaugurated in May 2026, a month after a framework agreement of over EUR 100m with a global hyperscaler was signed in April 2026
    • The medium-term plan was raised in February 2026 and again on 4 September 2026, to low-double-digit organic volume growth and a 15 to 17 percent adjusted EBITDA margin from high-single-digit and 14 to 15 percent
    • Growth capex was raised to EUR 30m to 35m a year from EUR 20m to 30m, and 200 hires are targeted in Texas within 18 to 24 months
  • 'Financial Outputs' have moved through every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • Plant and plan, then contract and deliveries from May 2026, then the key operating metrics, the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates
    • The order backlog reached a record EUR 333.3m at June 2026, up 48 percent on the year, and US product sales rose 92.1 percent
    • H1 2026 revenue rose 10.3 percent to EUR 325.1m
      • The adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 15.3 percent from 14.6 percent and the adjusted EBIT margin to 10.8 percent from 9.4 percent
      • Net profit rose 60 percent to EUR 27.2m
    • Consensus FY2027 EPS rose 32.7 percent in twelve months to EUR 3.02
      • Five of five brokers raised FY2026 to FY2028 after the print
  • Our modal earnings path compounds at about 21 percent a year, revenue- and margin-led.
    • On the brokers' basis EPS runs from EUR 1.91 (FY2025) to EUR 2.40 (FY2026), EUR 2.95 (FY2027) and EUR 3.38 (FY2028)
    • Revenue growth of about 11.6 percent a year carries about half of it and margin expansion to 16.2 percent about four points
      • The lines below EBITDA (interest and tax) carry about 4.3 points, which is the leg investors do not extrapolate and the one we lean on least
    • A plausibly worse drop-through (tax at 25 percent, interest flat, depreciation EUR 36m) cuts FY2028 EPS to about EUR 2.88, 15 percent lower
  • What the thesis costs on the valuation is the whole case.
    • At EUR 69.40 the shares have risen 94.4 percent in twelve months to 20.8x the consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 3.33, the 97th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history and above its ten-year 'mania' band of 16.90x
    • About two thirds of the twelve-month move is the multiple: the price rose 94.4 percent while FY2027 consensus EPS rose 32.7 percent
    • On our path the price is 23.5x FY2027 EPS and 20.5x FY2028
      • On a one-year-forward footing it is about 24.5x
  • The chain holds from the force to the estimates and breaks at the price.
    • On the framework's convention, which values the shares at each horizon at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it, our 'Modal return' is about minus 3 percent a year over eighteen months and about plus 1 percent a year over three years, because a de-rating from 20.8x to 16.3x and then 15.0x consumes the earnings growth
    • Even with the current 20.8x held throughout the return is about 13 percent a year, a C on the framework's anchors
      • That is the ceiling of the case from this price without a second hyperscaler contract
    • The one-year-forward footing beside it is two to three points a year kinder, because the FY2027 step that the convention already counts in today's denominator is counted again there
  • The pattern is 'Demand Inflection', and it fits at B.
    • The mechanism is capacity in the right place: local US manufacturing and Eurovent-certified natural-refrigerant ranges meet a customer base that buys on lead time, so sector demand becomes backlog, backlog becomes volume on a plant already built, and volume becomes margin
    • The same mechanism limits the crispness: a position held on capacity is held while capacity is short, and one-to-many scalability is absent

  • The case is therefore a good bet at the wrong price.

    • The change is material, the outputs are improving and the pattern fits
    • The return does not clear the bar at this price, and the right response is to park with an entry level of about EUR 50, where the 24-month-forward multiple is the 'defendable' 15x band and the eighteen-month modal return clears 20 percent a year

  • Rating = C, because the change forces are material (B) and the outputs are improving materially (B), but the chain's last link, the return, is F at this price

    • The Building Block measures materiality as a whole and the parts bind at the return, since a thesis whose modal return is below 10 percent a year is not material to a 20 percent-a-year investor at the price offered.
    • B was live on the forces and the outputs
    • Not B because the price has already travelled with the chain.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: LU-VE is a false friend.
      • The backlog and the growth are real, but they are bought with cash the business does not generate
      • Days sales outstanding have risen from 51 (2023) to 63 (2024) to 73 (2025)
        • Days in inventory from 62 to 66 and now 83 in H1 2026
        • The cushion is a payables stretch to 158 days on 120-day terms that a supplier can reverse
        • Operating cash flow was negative EUR 12.1m in Q1 2026
      • The hyperscaler contract was won on lead time in a product the experts call increasingly commoditised
        • The hyperscaler may be over 15 percent of revenue by FY2027, and it leaves when Kelvion, Modine and Vertiv's new capacity arrives
      • The FY2027 step is a one-contract event priced as a franchise, in a family-controlled company where the minority has no lever and the strategic bench is three people
        • The shares at the 97th percentile of their own history are the market making the same mistake in reverse
    • What we concede.
      • The margin structure (adjusted EBIT 10.8 percent, gross margin 20 percent, return on invested capital about 12.5 percent) is that of a component maker, not a franchise
      • The working-capital trend through 2025 was adverse and the H1 2026 inventory build is real
      • The EC motor dependence is unmitigated beyond stock
      • The position in data centres rests on capacity timing
      • And the price is wrong
    • Why we still hold our view on the business.
      • H1 2026 cash flow from operations rose 31 percent to EUR 39.8m and leverage fell to 0.7x, which a false friend does not do
      • The inventory build is a stated choice to secure delivery of a backlog that is now 48 percent larger, and the company has guided mid-teens working capital with the exclusions named
      • Pass-through pricing held absolute EBITDA through a 10 percent copper rise
        • The refrigeration cycle turned in Q2 2026
        • And the one documented stress test, the 2023 to 2024 heat-pump collapse, produced record EBITDA on lower sales
    • Where the case against and our case meet.
      • Its conclusion, hold and watch, is ours as well, reached by the price rather than by the cash
      • Its monitorables (working capital above 18 percent of sales, three negative cash-flow quarters) are in our monitoring table.
      • No part of this record argues that any division or asset covers the enterprise value, so no such cover is shown
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because two external forces (data-centre and power-generation cooling demand; the natural-refrigerant regulatory shift) are strong, sector-level and new or intensifying, and the internal forces (a capacity step-up, a plan upgrade, a new geography and a new product application) are four strong with a guidance increase, which meets the framework's screen rule of three strong or chief-executive change plus a guidance rise.
  • Mix: the largest application, commercial refrigeration at 45 percent of application turnover, is cyclical and only now stabilising, so the strong forces act on just over half the group
  • Position: the data-centre win rests on capacity timing rather than a proprietary position, so the force is strong but the company's grip on it is conditional
  • Not A because of those two limits
    • Not C because the demand force passes the 'Demand Inflection' test outright and the internal forces are resourced, not announced.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand in data-centre and power-generation cooling is changing structurally at sector level with more than two years of visibility, and it is this force that moved the backlog.
  • Company evidence: air conditioning applications (the data-centre line) rose 41.9 percent to EUR 92.8m in H1 2026, 28.5 percent of application turnover
    • Industrial cooling (power generation) rose 25.0 percent to EUR 42.1m
  • Sector evidence: Alfa Laval reported 29 percent organic order growth in Q2 2026
    • Carel's H1 2026 revenue rose 20.9 percent to EUR 370.1m on data-centre cooling
    • Kelvion's data-centre business is over 50 percent of its mix and growing over 50 percent
    • Market lead times for heat exchangers are 20 to 30 weeks
    • Morgan Stanley puts liquid-cooling market growth at over 50 percent a year to 2028
  • Mechanism: high-temperature liquid cooling at the rack rejects heat into secondary loops that need larger outdoor dry coolers, so the outdoor content per megawatt rises rather than falls
    • The direction is a tailwind
  • Not B because the force is sector-wide and structural, not cyclical.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption force the market feared, direct liquid cooling eliminating dry coolers, is being disproved and reversed into a multiplier, while the far disruption is years away.
  • Near disruption: racks past 50 kW make air cooling at the rack impossible and heat rejection outdoors larger, so liquid cooling adds to LU-VE's content rather than removing it
  • Far disruption: in-chip microfluidics tested by Microsoft and Corintis, and radical model-efficiency gains of the kind Bernstein describes, could cut cooling content per unit of compute
    • Neither is dated inside the horizon
  • The direction is a tailwind now, with a dated risk at the far end of the three-year horizon.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the revised EU F-Gas Regulation and the US AIM Act move demand towards the natural-refrigerant products that are 58 percent of LU-VE's turnover, a contractual tailwind, while subsidy rollback is a headwind to the heat-pump component line.
  • F-Gas: a ban on new units with refrigerants above 150 GWP, including R-32, from January 2027
    • The HFC quota falls to 11 percent in 2027 on a fixed timetable
  • Subsidies: Germany's heating law was softened and the UK's 2035 boiler ban stepped back, which weakens the demand for heat-pump components in Europe
  • The net is clearly positive for a company at 58 percent sustainable sales
    • The offset is confined to one product line.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle has turned: revenue fell 4.6 percent in FY2024 and rose 2.8 percent in FY2025, then 13.5 percent in Q1 2026 and 7.6 percent in Q2 2026, 10.3 percent for H1 2026.
  • Refrigeration: the commercial-refrigeration line, which drove the 2023 to 2024 downturn, grew 9.0 percent in Q2 2026 after a flat Q1
    • The refrigeration customers order on a three-year trade-fair cycle, which is the stated timing driver
  • Heat pumps: the heat-pump component line recovered in early 2026 after a 65 percent fall in FY2024
  • The direction is a tailwind, cyclical in kind, which is why the inflection is treated as cyclical until proven structural.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening in European heat pumps and consolidating above LU-VE in data-centre cooling, while in large dry coolers the industry is capacity-constrained and intensity is, for now, easing
    • A headwind on balance.
  • Heat pumps: Midea's EU heat-pump exports rose more than 60 percent year-on-year in H1 2026, targeting 15 percent share from 4 to 5 percent
    • Price and mix deteriorated in Italy and the UK
  • Data-centre cooling: SLB is buying Kelvion for USD 4.1bn (August 2026)
    • Vertiv bought ThermoKey on 12 June 2026
    • The consolidation is happening above LU-VE's stage, among larger and better-capitalised rivals

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = C

The foundations make the expected change plausible but not robust: the demand is real and the regulatory position is sound, but the business that carries them is a competent, well-run, mid-margin component manufacturer whose edge in data centres is capacity and lead time, and whose supply chain has one binary point of failure.

  • The value chain supports the thesis in its direction and undermines it in its fragilities.
    • Customers, regulators and, for now, competitors are supportive
    • The support is conditional at two points
    • Large EC electronic motors come from two global manufacturers, and the mitigation is safety stock and source diversification, nothing structural
    • The hyperscaler customers buy on lead time and local capacity, which LU-VE has today and which Kelvion under SLB, Modine and Vertiv with ThermoKey are adding
      • The largest customer rose to 7.1 percent of H1 2026 sales from 5.1 percent in FY2025
    • Regulators are the most reliable supporter in the chain, on a fixed timetable
      • Politicians are the least, having collapsed heat-pump demand by about 50 percent in H1 2024 and now targeting 4 million installations a year by 2030
  • The industry structure is fragmented at LU-VE's stage and benign only because capacity is short.
    • A few global suppliers and a long tail of local competitors
    • Share is not drifting mechanically to the largest
    • Kelvion's share slipped from 15 to 20 percent to 13 to 15 percent while it was largest
      • The consolidation is happening by acquisition above LU-VE's stage
    • Competitor behaviour is cooperative in dry coolers because lead times are 20 to 30 weeks, and aggressive in European heat pumps where Chinese entrants with a 20 to 35 percent cost advantage price for share
  • The live thematics are at the company's back.
    • AI data-centre infrastructure, power-generation electrification and the natural-refrigerant transition are accelerating the applications that are growing
    • European heat pumps and residential construction, which the base rides, are stable to fading
    • 58 percent of turnover is from sustainable, low-GWP products against a 56 percent target, on a plan path to 64 percent by 2029
  • The operator is good and the bench is thin.
    • A chief executive of thirty-two years' tenure with a decade of growth above guidance, value-disciplined acquisitions at an average 7.0x EBITDA and a sale at 12.5x, deleveraging from 1.18x to 0.7x in eighteen months, and clear, specific communication

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a demand-inflection case in a mid-margin component manufacturer: the AI data-centre build-out, through the shift to high-temperature liquid cooling that rejects more heat outdoors per megawatt, and the power-generation infrastructure that feeds it, have lifted demand for the large dry coolers and air-cooled equipment that LU-VE makes, and LU-VE built the capacity in the right place (Texas, inaugurated May 2026) in time to win a framework agreement of over EUR 100m with a global hyperscaler in April 2026.

    • The case clears the framework's bar below about EUR 50.
