EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Fabio Marasi was appointed Chief Executive Officer of Interpump Group SpA in April 2023, separating operational powers from founder Fulvio Montipò, and represents an operator-promoted executive with deep M&A and financial discipline built through prior leadership roles including Head of M&A (2016-2020), divisional CEO at Walvoil Group, and operational restructuring experience across GS Hydro's 12 international subsidiaries. The CEO brings a business economics degree cum laude from Parma University and early private equity training at Bank of America Equity Partners, though his finance-led origin contrasts with engineer-operator peer norms across European industrial machinery competitors, potentially lacking deep technical engineering instincts during technology inflections.

Operational delivery and margin resilience through downcycle Marasi's three-year operational track

record demonstrates exceptional cash generation discipline alongside uncompromising structural profitability standards. Free cash flow expanded from €205 million in 2024 to a record €220 million in 2025, while EBITDA margin remained anchored at 22.3% despite a 4% organic sales decline, outperforming European fluid power peer cash conversion rates during the post-COVID Hydraulics normalization cycle. Hydraulics experienced a prolonged seven-quarter organic decline falling 14% organically in 2024 before stabilizing in late 2025, yet Water-Jetting EBITDA margin reached 27.2% in 2025, offsetting cyclical margin compression in Hydraulics to 19.6%. Management executed decisive cost adaptation containing organic EBITDA contraction to just 3% during the revenue decline, while Hydraulics organic growth accelerated to 6.2% in second quarter 2026, marking four consecutive quarters of operational expansion.

Capital allocation remains strictly disciplined, with Marasi deploying only €42 million for add-on acquisitions (Padoan, MVV, Teknoice) while improving net financial position to €306 million in 1H 2026 and returning €57 million to shareholders through dividends and buybacks, backing management's commitment to achieve zero net debt by 2028 while targeting €2.5 billion in revenues.

Leadership style and communication directness The CEO exhibits plain-spoken operational directness

and a conservative guidance posture, prioritizing long-term credibility over short-term market narrative management, evidenced by his explicit rejection of transaction rumors stating "we are not participating in the Dana beauty contest" and affirming "we don't want to play with words and we don't want to mislead our stakeholders". Management maintains high decision velocity during downturns, executing rapid operational realignments including shift schedule changes, personnel moves, and production insourcing without administrative drag, while implementing list price surcharges "carefully and without any hesitation" to offset raw material cost inflation.

Marasi demonstrates uncompromised capital discipline, rejecting low-quality acquisitions to artificially meet revenue targets and maintaining strict valuation discipline, stating "we do not regret having refused compromises or easy shortcuts that could have turned out to be detrimental", requiring acquisition targets to meet an uncompromising "perfect fit" standard across technological excellence, strategic alignment, and financial return criteria.

The conservative guidance philosophy routinely incorporates negative macro scenarios, with Marasi refusing to narrow annual ranges prematurely and citing geopolitical uncertainty, though hyper-conservative guidance and strict M&A refusal thresholds risk missing larger strategic consolidation opportunities, leaving market share vulnerable to aggressive capital deployers.

Governance structure and board independence Interpump's board demonstrates strong independent

oversight with seven of ten directors qualifying as independent under the Italian Corporate Governance Code, representing 70% of the board, with women comprising 50% of the administrative body exceeding the 40% statutory threshold. Executive Chairman Fulvio Montipò holds executive powers while CEO Fabio Marasi operates day-to-day operations, separating strategy oversight from daily execution, though Federica Menichetti was reconfirmed as Independent Lead Director despite exceeding nine years of service, with the board citing deep operational knowledge. Major shareholder representation reflects Giovanni Tamburi representing Tamburi Investment Partners (TIP) holding a residual ~3% direct stake, with Gruppo IPG Holding S.p.A. holding 23.4% of share capital and submitting the majority list obtaining 61.34% of votes cast. The Control and Risks Committee and Related Party Transactions Committee are composed entirely of Independent Directors chaired by Federica Menichetti, while CEO Fabio Marasi serves directly on the Sustainability Committee, providing adequate oversight mechanisms, though high concentration of committee seats among a few long-standing independent directors risks rubber-stamping founder-backed initiatives if founder control via Gruppo IPG overrides independent committee recommendations. The governance framework features clean single-class voting structures without loyalty mechanisms or dual-class differentials, with the board explicitly confirming Interpump is not subject to management or coordination by Gruppo IPG Holding S.p.A. under Italian Civil Code, while related-party transactions remain transparent and immaterial relative to group scale, executed on standard market terms, supported by clean PricewaterhouseCoopers statutory audit opinions, zero antitrust actions, and zero material whistleblowing reports during 2025.

Executive compensation and insider alignment disconnect Compensation structure features modest

CEO base salary of €210 thousand in 2025 standing at ~30th percentile against European fluid power peers, contrasting with Executive Chair Fulvio Montipò receiving €1.55 million fixed remuneration reflecting founder influence and strategic oversight. Under the 2025-2027 Incentive Plan, the Board granted 160,000 options to CEO Marasi and 1.14 million options to Executive Chair Montipò at an exercise price of €30.44 per share, though CEO Marasi exercised 60,000 options from the 2022-2024 plan in 2025 and immediately liquidated 58,393 shares on the market.
