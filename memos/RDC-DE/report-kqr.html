Section 2 – Market structure and electronic prescription rollout 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Germany and the Nordics show diverging electronic prescription maturities, but regulatory visibility supports robust online volumes as physical barriers dissolve.

German Telematics and CardLink visibility

1.
Stabilizing electronic volume: Germany has established a mature baseline of 48 to 50 million electronic prescriptions redeemed monthly, which now consistently represents 89% to 94% of GKV scripts
2.
Extended regulatory visibility: The national digital medicine agency Gematik has extended CardLink approval until 31 March 2028, giving specialists greater regulatory and operational visibility during the transition to the new infrastructure
3.
Smoother customer journey: Early technical revisions under the Gematik specification will remove the SMS verification step when attaching physical insurance cards to smartphones, creating a far smoother digital redemption process
4.
Persistent doctor skepticism: While 96% of pharmacies are connected, some German medical practices remain skeptical about digital systems or hesitant to disrupt traditional workflows, creating a temporary bottleneck to full digital penetration

Nordic digital systems and delivery rules

5.
Advanced Nordic maturity: Sweden has established a highly advanced digital pharmacy landscape where online penetration reached 27% during early 2026, driven by mature digital consumer purchasing habits

7.
Tightening delivery regulations: The Swedish Medical Products Agency introduced stricter home delivery rules that restrict unattended package drops to unlocked mailboxes, representing a new structural friction for logistics
8.
Mitigating delivery restrictions: Pure-play specialists should experience minimal volume disruptions from mailbox restrictions because shipping companies are highly incentivized to implement compliant face-to-face signature alternatives

Swiss omnichannel integration and IT modernizations

9.
Integrated Swiss networks: Galenica operates the largest pharmacy network in Switzerland with 384 locations, while utilizing its Prescription Manager tool to consolidate digital and physical pharmacy visits
10.
Cost-neutral service compensation: Swiss regulators introduced the new LOA V service-based remuneration scheme on 1 January 2026, which was implemented without operational disruption and is expected to be earnings neutral

Wholesale logistics automation: To support omnichannel delivery, Swiss operators are modernizing backend systems, such as the Lausanne-Ecublens warehouse automation upgrade which pushed processing automation above 70%

Slower consumer switch: Despite advanced digital platforms, Swiss and German consumers have traditionally been slower to switch prescription orders online compared to over-the-counter products, presenting a prolonged timeline for channel transitions

Section 4 – Prescription economics and gross margin trajectories 14 findings

Section summary
  1. Surging electronic prescription volumes dilute gross margin percentages across pharmacy networks, but highly automated fulfillment centers and superior average basket sizes protect absolute transaction profitability.

Segment gross margins and product mix dilution

1.
Dilutive prescription mix: German specialist Redcare Pharmacy saw its Group gross margin decline to 22.1% in the second quarter of 2026 because of a surging share of prescription revenue which commands lower percentage margins
2.
Higher relative yields: Deutsche Bank reports that Redcare's Rx gross margin remains regulated between 18% and 22%, while its non-prescription and over-the-counter lines consistently yield margins of approximately 30%
3.
Stable Nordic pricing: Sweden-based Apotea maintained a stable gross margin of 27.5% in the second quarter of 2026, successfully balancing a higher Rx mix with improving procurement terms in OTC and traded goods
4.
Supportive basket economics: Carnegie analysts argue that although Rx products carry lower gross margins, they increase average order values and minimize shipping costs per transaction due to their lightweight properties
5.
Pressured Swiss margins: Omnichannel leader Galenica reported a Group gross margin of 28.7% in the first half of 2026, even as pharmacy margins faced pressure from high-priced medications
6.
Accretive diagnostic services: Deutsche Bank notes that Galenica's Products & Care margins improved 120 basis points to 47.5% in the first half of 2026, as accretive diagnostics acquisitions offset the dilutive impact of statutory prescription growth

Fulfillment automation and logistics efficiency gains

Fulfillment cost leverage: Carnegie analysts highlight that Apotea's long-term EBIT margin is set to exceed 5% by 2027 as rising capacity utilization drives operating leverage at its highly automated Varberg hub

Automated Swiss platforms: Swiss distributor Galenica pushed wholesale processing automation above 70% at its Lausanne-Ecublens site, helping to partially offset rising domestic personnel costs across its network

Section 8 – Second and third-order implications 9 findings

Section summary
  1. The electronic prescription transition unlocks massive parallel access infrastructure while dismantling traditional high-street location moats and consolidating the pharmaceutical supply chain.

Downstream channel and drug logistics re-routing

1.
Disrupting location logic: E-prescriptions dissolve the natural advantage of proximity to doctor practices, causing traditional location-based security to fade as physical pharmacy real estate values decline
2.
Pressuring wholesale distribution: Wholesaler consolidation and the growth of online e-pharmacy networks weaken traditional intermediary bargaining power, enabling platforms to bypass wholesalers for direct manufacturer purchasing
3.
Accelerating obesity logistics: Novo Nordisk launching oral Wegovy in Germany utilizes telehealth channels to bypass traditional clinics, enabling rapid digital script fulfillment of weight-loss therapies
4.
Relieving operational costs: Increasing demand for temperature-sensitive injectables forces specialty handling, but the BMG abandoning stricter cold-chain requirements provides near-term margin relief for online players

Strategic actor motives and systemic outliers

5.
Targeting direct access: Pharmaceutical manufacturers utilize means, motive, and opportunity to bypass intermediaries, deploying direct-to-consumer digital channels to establish parallel access infrastructure alongside insurance
6.
Exploiting automated prescribing: Telemedicine platforms leverage the means, motive, and opportunity of remote care, utilizing online questionnaires to route high-margin private prescriptions to epharmacies
7.
Outlier: Cross-border routing: The European Health Data Space will facilitate EU-wide cross-border electronic prescriptions by 2029, allowing online pharmacies to resolve critical national medication shortages dynamically
8.
Outlier: Clinical refill automation: Near-term clinical systems will feature automated refill pilots checking appropriateness, allowing digital platforms to dispense routine therapies with minimal physician involvement
9.
Other side of the story: Hasty digital health acceleration creates digital exclusion for elderly heavy users and triggers severe cybersecurity vulnerabilities, proving that unregulated automation creates systemic barriers to essential care
