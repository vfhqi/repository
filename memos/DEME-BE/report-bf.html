EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 finding

DEME Group operates a highly diversified segment mix led by Offshore Energy contributing 52% of total turnover and Dredging & Infra accounting for 42% of revenue, establishing a balanced operational foundation across capital-intensive marine contracting segments. The geographic footprint is heavily anchored in Europe, which generates 60% of total group turnover and represents 80% of the order book, providing substantial multi-year backlog visibility while limiting exposure to higher-risk emerging markets. Recent fleet integration of Norse Wind and Norse Energi –two next-generation vessels capable of installing XXL monopiles up to 3,000 tons –has structurally enhanced installation capacity and commenced active turbine deployment in Germany, positioning the group to capture accelerating global offshore wind demand.

The group demonstrated strong revenue and margin expansion, achieving a record EBITDA margin of 22.4% in fiscal year 2025, supported by elevated fleet utilization rates and favorable project mix dynamics across both dredging and offshore energy divisions. DEME maintains a conservative debt profile, having deleveraged to net financial debt of EUR 291 million by mid-2026 with a leverage ratio of 0.3x EBITDA, providing substantial financial flexibility despite ongoing heavy shipyard commitments.

Capital intensive requirements are balanced by outstanding free cash flow generation of EUR 231 million in the first half of 2026, rebounding sharply from negative territory following the Havfram acquisition, while negative operating working capital of EUR -834 million provides an additional liquidity buffer through favorable customer payment terms.

The offshore wind installation market is expanding rapidly, with European capacity on track to reach 50GW by late 2027 from 39GW in 2025, representing annual capacity additions forecast to triple to 6.1GW in 2026. The competitive landscape has evolved into a tightening triopoly in foundations, where DEME holds a commanding 39% market share of the European offshore wind foundations market, followed by Van Oord at 32% and Subsea 7 at 12%, creating substantial pricing power and defensive positioning.

Operational scale thresholds are rising mechanically as turbine sizes escalate to 15MW and beyond, with only 20 out of 38 global turbine installation vessels currently capable of deploying next-generation models, further tightening the supply-demand balance in favor of specialized contractors. The marine contracting value chain contains highly profitable manufacturing and installation segments where specialized assets command premium returns, particularly in monopile fabrication yielding gross margins of 35% or more and marine cable operations generating gross margins of 33% to 35%, while site characterization remains fragmented with compressed margins under 10%.

DEME operates as a vertically integrated balanceof-plant contractor managing comprehensive offshore wind scopes from seabed preparation through foundation and turbine installation, supported by specialized fleet dominance and vessel occupancy rates of 85% in strong cycles, enabling the group to capture value across multiple high-margin stages. Installation and capital dredging stages represent the most attractive profit pools, with dredging EBITDA margins rebounding to 22% in mid-2026 on improved fleet occupancy, while cable and foundation installation command premium margins due to strategic vessel scarcity and tightening supply-demand dynamics across European markets.

DEME maintains a highly diversified and sovereign-backed client base with no single customer representing more than 10% of total group turnover in fiscal year 2025, while the United States generated 16.05% and Belgium contributed 14.65% of group revenue in the first half of 2026, demonstrating balanced geographic customer diversification. Contract structures provide strong margin protection via indexation clauses, with a substantial portion of contracts incorporating price-review formulas to adjust for raw material inflation in steel and fuel, complemented by active financial hedging to lock in energy prices during pre-committed project periods.

Switching costs are anchored in multi-year vessel reservation lock-ins, where customers reserve specialized vessel capacity up to 3-4 years before offshore construction begins and service providers undergo rigorous triple-year prequalification cycles covering financial records, insurance, and technical qualifications, creating substantial barriers to customer churn. The group manages its highly specialized heavy equipment supply chain through deep yard partnerships, including co-ownership of specialized assets like Green Jade with Taiwanese shipyard partner CSBC and selection of Royal IHC for split hopper vessel design, though sourcing specialized installation assets requires at least two years from design to vessel commissioning, limiting supply-side flexibility.

Exposure to commodity volatility in steel and fuel prices represents major risk factors for project execution margins, but contractual terms incorporate price-review formulas to pass raw material cost increases directly to clients, while DEME actively utilizes financial forward contracts to manage vessel fuel and LNG price risks across multi-year project horizons. Distribution channels are predominantly direct, serving public hydrographic authorities and private utilities without intermediaries, while the Concessions division acts as an equity partner to secure EPC contracting works internally, and specialized vessel scarcity shifts supply-demand dynamics in favor of contractors over developers.

The offshore marine sector operates as a consolidated Benelux oligopoly where specialized fleet technology dictates market leadership, with the global capital dredging market controlled by four Benelux giants and one Chinese competitor, while DEME matches peer Boskalis with a high 23% market share based on active hopper utilization. Competitive intensity is characterized by rational pricing discipline, as competitors tend to maintain price discipline during high demand cycles to preserve returns, though peer Boskalis experienced a 26% decrease in first-half 2026 EBITDA to EUR 553 million due to Middle East operational disruptions and delayed project awards, temporarily reducing fleet occupancy.

Vertical integration threats from developers are low due to risk mitigation through chartering, as offshore wind developers strongly prefer to charter specialized construction vessels rather than owning them in-house to avoid managing vessel operations and carrying associated marine asset liabilities, while emerging Chinese manufacturing giants integrating logistics and port access represent a nascent downstream competitive threat.

Expert and cross-chain signals strongly cohere with management's positive outlook on tight vessel capacity, as shipbuilders indicate strong demand with highly extended lead times, European developers continue locking in specialized transport capacity several years in advance, customers are accepting balanced contracting forms sharing geological and weather risk, competitors are expanding high-specification turbine installation capacity, and major peer groups show disciplined bidding behavior prioritizing margin preservation.

Working capital requirements remain highly volatile based on individual project payment terms, but operating working capital remained highly negative at EUR -834 million in mid-2026 while total cash flow from changes in working capital rebounded to a EUR 102 million inflow in mid-2026, demonstrating favorable deferred balances and customer advance payment dynamics that support near-term liquidity.

DEME features highly stable corporate governance under CEO Luc Vandenbulcke and CFO Stijn Gaytant, backed by majority shareholder Ackermans & van Haaren NV holding a dominant 62.12% stake, providing long-term governance stability and aligned executive incentives incorporating safety and financial KPIs including EBITDA, net profit, and debt rates. Capital allocation is led by the transformational Havfram acquisition valued at approximately EUR 900 million, which included two next-generation wind turbine installation vessels and implies an initial pre-tax ROIC exceeding 10% according to broker analysis, structurally expanding installation capacity at attractive return profiles despite near-term integration complexity.

Structural moats are anchored in high-tech fleet specialization and leading market shares, as DEME operates one of the most advanced specialized fleets globally including over 100 vessels, commands a leading 39% share of the European offshore wind foundations market, and benefits from multi-year lags between fleet investment and vessel delivery that shield the company from immediate capacity threats. Mid-term financial targets focus on EBITDA expansion with management guiding for a medium-term EBITDA margin range of 16% to 20%, though the group maintains conservative guidance and disclosure policies by refusing to publish explicit ROCE or ROIC targets due to project business complexities, while FactSet consensus projects a modest ROIC of 6.2% by fiscal year 2028 due to asset intensity.

Heavy physical assets and high capital barriers protect the business from digital disruption, as DEME remains highly insulated from artificial intelligence disruption because operations rely on a massive physical fleet of specialized marine assets carrying a book value of EUR 3.03 billion, representing a significant tangible asset hedge against rapid digital paradigm shifts. Evolving environmental policies like the EU ETS extension introduce carbon compliance costs through phased maritime taxation extending compliance obligations to offshore vessels of or above 5,000 gross tonnage from 2027, directly increasing operating expenditures, though cost increases are subject to project-specific contractual pass-through clauses to shelter margins from legislative inflation. Long-term growth is anchored in massive offshore wind targets where the European Union targets 88GW by 2030 and 360GW by 2050, supported by nine North Sea coalition countries committing to a pipeline of 15GW annually from 2031 to 2040, while coastal protection spending represents a highly efficient public investment as flood damage costs are 7x to 10x higher than prevention cost, underpinning multi-decade dredging and marine infrastructure demand.
