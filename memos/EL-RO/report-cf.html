Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro) 40 findings

Section summary
  1. demand remains resilient with a structural growth outlook despite a short-term retail volume contraction
  2. competitive intensity rises as international players expand, but Electrica maintains its dominant market share positions
  3. prosumer expansion acts as a disruptive imbalance force, complicating grid forecasting and balancing operations
  4. regulatory transition to Period 5 offers a favourable 6.94% RRR but removes historical capital incentives
  5. supply security is maintained through domestic resource procurement while regional geopolitical risks persist
  6. consumption cyclicity is driven by weather, while investment delivery is progressively executing ahead of plans
  7. macroeconomic headwinds from inflation and fiscal consolidation are countered by stable sovereign investment grades

3.
Underpenetrated consumption potential: Romania's per capita electricity consumption sits at only 47% of the EU average, representing high catch-up potential

Short-term retail decline: supplied electricity volumes to the retail market fell 6.9% year-on-year in Q1 2026 to 1.9 TWh
5.
Grid volume growth: distributed electricity volume rose 1.5% to 4.7 TWh in Q1 2026, driven by a 5.5% high-voltage volume expansion

Competitive market positioning: Electrica holds third place in the competitive segment with an 11.74% share, behind peers PPC and Hidroelectrica

Producer landscape concentration: Hidroelectrica remains the dominant power producer with nearly 30% market share, yielding 15.5 TWh average annual output

Accelerating private rivalry: new private entrants and international utility groups with greater economies of scale increase competitive customer-churn risks

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Outliers worth noting

Exceptional subsidy receivable overhang emerged as the single most anomalous balance sheet feature, with uncollected state subsidies standing at RON 2,532.1 million as of March 31, 2026, representing approximately 82% of Q1 2026 operating revenues and 437% of quarterly EBITDA. This receivable magnitude profoundly exceeds typical European utility working capital structures, where net working capital days ordinarily range between 30 to 60 days for integrated distribution and supply operators.

The accumulated subsidy position reflects the delayed reimbursement mechanics under Government Emergency Ordinance 27/2022, which forces Electrica to pre-finance consumer support schemes without synchronized state compensation. While the regulatory framework technically guarantees recovery, the unprecedented scale of this overhang creates acute liquidity constraints that materially diverge from regional utility peer norms and distorts conventional cash conversion metrics. [Section 6] Extreme CAPEX execution volatility presented a striking operational oddity, with DEER implementing 115% of its planned investment program in 2025, reaching RON 878 million, yet commissioned CAPEX falling by 29.9% to

RON 58.9 million during Q1 2026. This quarterly realization rate of approximately 6.7% of the approved 2026 distribution CAPEX budget contrasts sharply with the prior year's over-execution pattern and deviates significantly from the steady-state quarterly realization rates of 20-25% typically observed among mature European distribution network operators. The pronounced swing suggests either deliberate project phasing to optimize regulatory asset base timing or unanticipated permitting bottlenecks that are obscuring the underlying execution cadence.

Such volatility complicates forward trajectory modeling and raises questions about the sustainability of management's aggressive multi-year grid modernization commitments. [Section 6, Section 4]

Disproportionate green bond leverage represented a fundamental outlier in capital structure positioning, with the inaugural EUR 500 million green bond issued in July 2025 constituting approximately 41% of the Group's total operating revenues in FY2025 and introducing concentrated foreign exchange exposure on unhedged debt. While European utility peers routinely access green finance instruments, the absolute quantum relative to Electrica's earnings base materially exceeds the sector median green bond-to-EBITDA ratio of approximately 2.1x, with Electrica's position approaching 3.5x on a pro-forma basis.

This structural imbalance amplifies sensitivity to EUR/RON currency movements, with a 5% EUR appreciation reducing PBT by RON 14.5 million, and exposes forward EPS to outsized refinancing risk when the 2030 maturity approaches. The concentration reflects both limited domestic capital market depth and management's opportunistic timing during favorable market conditions. [Section 4, Section 10] Anomalous retail volume contraction amid stable grid throughput created an unexplained operational divergence, with supplied electricity volumes to the retail market falling 6.9% year-on-year in Q1 2026 to 1.9 TWh while distributed electricity volume rose 1.5% to 4.7 TWh in the same period.

This 8.4 percentage point delta between supply and distribution volume trajectories substantially exceeds typical seasonal variance or competitive switching patterns observed across Romanian retail electricity markets, where annual churn rates historically range between 3-5%. The divergence suggests either accelerated customer migration to competing suppliers following July 2025 price deregulation, material prosumer self-consumption growth that is not captured in supply statistics, or structural measurement inconsistencies in reporting methodologies.

The gap warrants detailed reconciliation as it directly impacts supply segment margin sustainability and forward market share assumptions. [Section 6, Section 3]
