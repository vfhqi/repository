Section 14 – Value chain: political and regulatory

Section summary
  1. Geopolitical tariff agreements in China and the United States have resolved severe downside risks but introduced permanent annualized cost burdens.
  2. Complex state-level excise changes and ongoing legal disputes in India continue to disrupt local distribution networks and challenge margin expansion.
  3. Pernod Ricard capped strategic investments at seven hundred million euros annually to support cash generation while managing elevated aging stock requirements.
  4. Rising input costs for glass, grain, and logistics threaten gross margins, forcing the company to accelerate energy-efficiency upgrades and procurement hedges.
1.
Satisfactory tariff resolution: Pernod Ricard resolved the threat of definitive Chinese anti-dumping duties on European grape spirits through a formal minimum price undertaking agreement signed in July 2025. The agreement replaced provisional duties of 38.1% with a strictly enforced minimum import price on containers under 200 litres. Management quantified the annualized cost impact of this transfer price adjustment at €45 million.
2.
Mitigated tariff escalation: Pernod Ricard faced lower-than-feared tariff burdens in the United States after a bilateral agreement established a 15% tariff rate effective August 1, 2025. The negotiated outcome replaced a highly punitive 30% tariff proposal that originally threatened Western spirits. Management reported that these import duties created an annualized profit headwind of thirty-five million Euros.
3.
Adverse tax headwinds: Pernod Ricard experienced localized volume disruption following the introduction of a preferential Maharashtra Made Liquor tax category in July 2026. The policy change forced distributors to frontload spirits purchases before the tax hike, severely depressing subsequent regional sales. State-level excise increases on popular segments in Karnataka further compressed regional sales margins.
4.
569 - Earnings Calls Favorable excise adjustments: Pernod Ricard experienced rapid volume acceleration in Karnataka after the state government cut excise taxes on premium spirits. The local tax cuts enabled distillers to pass savings directly to consumers through a seven to nine percent price reduction on imported spirits. Lower retail prices are expected to accelerate local premiumization trends and support higher regional operating returns.
5.
Ongoing legal disputes: Pernod Ricard India is preparing for a potential trial regarding alleged money laundering violations linked to the National Capital Territory of Delhi route-to-market changes. Although a Special Court discharged the associated criminal case in February 2026, local authorities have appealed the decision. The ongoing lack of a Delhi retail license continues to prevent direct sales of premium brands in this key northern territory.
6.
Favorable tax rulings: Pernod Ricard successfully reduced its Indian tax exposure after the Supreme Court dismissed tax authority appeals regarding the deductibility of advertising and promotion expenses for the 2007 fiscal year. The ruling reduced the company's immediate cash risk by 580 million Indian Rupees. Tax advisors subsequently reclassified the historical advertising tax liability risk to remote from possible.
7.
Persistent customs disputes: Pernod Ricard India faced ongoing audits as customs authorities challenged the transaction values of imported concentrates of alcoholic beverages. A localized customs decision issued on June 29, 2026, attempted to reinstate marketing expenses into the taxable import value of international products. The company filed a formal appeal against this adverse custom valuation adjustment to protect local operating margins.
8.
Bottlenecked trade agreements: Pernod Ricard faced near-term supply chain disruptions as Indian customs officials held dozens of bulk Scotch containers in local ports. The dispute arose following the July 2026 implementation of the India-UK Free Trade Agreement which slashed import tariffs to 75% from 150%. The Ministry of Finance argued that imported bulk Scotch for local blending should still attract the maximum tariff.
9.
Rising excise risks: Pernod Ricard faces potential margin dilution in Latin America due to a proposed Brazilian Sin Tax scheduled to take effect in 2028. The legislation proposes a 24% tax burden on spirits to replace the existing industrial products tax. The tax structure threatens to compress profitability unless the group can successfully pass these higher excise costs onto consumers.
10.
Other side of the story: Geopolitical trade tensions and state-level tax reforms could stimulate local volume demand if they accelerate the removal of protectionist barriers. Progressive tariff reductions under new trade agreements like the India-UK pact could drive significant price cuts. The price reductions would make premium imported spirits highly affordable for middle class consumers, driving rapid organic volume growth.

Section 15 – Value chain: supply side

Cost inflation and logistics pressures

1.
Elevated cost pressures: Pernod Ricard faced persistent gross margin headwinds from inflation in key raw materials like glass, grains, and packaging during the fiscal year 2026. Mauve Croizat stated in the annual presentation on November 20, 2026 that the company's gross profit margin contracted to 58.43% of net sales. Structural inflation in neutral spirits and imported bulk Scotch is expected to continue dragging on consolidated margins. Managing these volatile costs remains critical to prevent further margin dilution and stabilize intermediate gross operating profits over the next eighteen months.
2.
Volatile shipping dynamics: Pernod Ricard's global supply chain experienced significant logistical disruptions and higher freight costs arising from the West Asia geopolitical conflict in late 2026. The company noted in its annual report published on August 27, 2026 that regional tensions continue to cause port congestion and longer transit routes. Systematix Institutional Research reported on November 18, 2026 that these disruptions increased glass and energy expenses for major distillers. Prolonged transportation delays will likely inflate regional shipping and logistics costs and delay product deliveries.

Capex and decarbonization initiatives

3.
Disciplined capital spending: Pernod Ricard capped its future strategic capital expenditures and aging inventory investments at 700 million euros annually over the fiscal year 2027 to fiscal year 2029 period. Management stated in the first-quarter sales release on October 15, 2026 that this represents a major reduction from peak investment levels. Trevor Stirling of Bernstein reported on October 16, 2026 that keeping strategic capex at this level is crucial for achieving the 90% cash conversion target. Capping these capital outlays protects intermediate free cash flow and helps accelerate balance sheet deleveraging targets over the medium term.
4.
Targeted environmental investments: Pernod Ricard is expanding capital expenditure on energy-efficiency upgrades and renewable power purchase agreements to mitigate transition risks and carbon taxes. The company detailed in its annual report on August 27, 2026 that direct fossil fuel consumption is being reduced through mechanical vapor recompression and heat pump solutions. Analyst Stephen Blagg of Jefferies estimated on November 19, 2026 that climate-related refrigeration capex will rise to 115 million US Dollars annually. These investments are critical because increasing carbon taxation under environmental laws will create a material financial drag on European production sites.

Inventory and stock dynamics

5.
Massive aging inventories: Pernod Ricard maintains a substantial financial commitment to its maturing spirit stocks, which are necessary to support the long-term growth of Scotch and Cognac. The company disclosed in its consolidated financial statements on October 28, 2026 that aging inventories represented 85% of its total net inventories of 8,470 million euros. Chris Pitcher of Redburn argued on October 9, 2026 that these maturing stocks exceed 80% of net sales, which sits well above historical averages. Slower sales velocity in premium categories will lock up valuable working capital in wood barrels for several years.
6.
610 & Co Redburn Other side of the story: Pernod Ricard's aggressive reduction of strategic investments below peak levels risks creating inventory shortages if global demand for aged spirits recovers rapidly. A national director at Republic National Distributing Company warned on December 9, 2025 that long-cycle distillers must project sales years in advance because turning on production takes considerable time. If the company under-invests in maturing wet goods today, competitors with more substantial barrel reserves could easily capture market share, ultimately limiting long-term volume and earnings growth when the cycle inflects.

613 %16 – Conclusion The FY26 results confirm that Pernod Ricard is in a reset year, not a recovery year, and that the earnings changed expectations more than they changed the underlying picture. Reported net sales were €9,404 million, a 14.19% contraction. That slightly missed the Vuma consensus of €9,427 million. Organic net sales fell 3.9%, slightly worse than the Bloomberg consensus of a 3.7% decline.

The shortfall was driven by a 14% sales drop in the United States and a 19% decline in China. The earnings did not introduce a new problem. They removed the assumption that the US and China would rebound quickly. The main change was in management's framing and in how far the sell-side had to move towards it.

The key deviations from expectations were mostly about quality and composition rather than the headline. Organic operating profit declined by only 5.2%, ahead of the Bloomberg forecast of a 5.9% decline. The operating margin contracted by just 35 basis points organically to 25.8%. That defence rested heavily on self-help. Advertising and promotion spend was cut to €1,409 million. Deutsche Bank attributed a 109 basis point organic operating margin lift to this reduction. Organic structure costs also fell 8.0%. Cash was the second positive surprise. Cash conversion reached 91%, up 17 percentage points from 74% in FY25. Free cash flow rose 6% to €1,197 million. The negative deviations were the reported-versus-organic gap and the balance sheet. Currency translation took €568 million from net sales and €268 million from profit from recurring operations. Leverage rose to 3.76x from 3.3x. Peer comparison was also unfavourable. Diageo's organic net sales fell 2.0% while its organic operating profit rose 2.0%, against Pernod Ricard's 3.9% organic sales decline.

The guidance reset is what changed the outlook. Management guided to broadly stable organic net sales for FY27. The medium-term framework now projects average organic growth close to the bottom of the 3% to 6% range over FY27 to FY29. Management is no longer assuming rapid recoveries in the United States.

The offsets are cost and cash commitments. About 50% of the €1 billion efficiency program was completed in FY26, with full delivery now targeted for FY28. Strategic capex and ageing inventory investment is capped at about €700 million annually. The target remains net debt to EBITDA below 3.0x by FY29.

The sell-side broadly accepted the reset. Brokers trimmed target prices and earnings estimates to reflect a prolonged recovery timeline. Their models still diverge from management on several points. Redburn cut its FY27 organic sales growth forecast to 0.3% from 3.6%.

Redburn also models leverage staying above 3.0x until FY30, a year later than management's target. Morgan Stanley doubts the achievability of the medium-term margin expansion. Jefferies left its FY27 organic sales forecast unchanged at negative 0.9%. 2 17 - Earnings Calls The value-chain evidence supports this reading. Demand weakness is real, but the gap between shipments and consumption is the central source of both the risk and the potential relief.

US retail depletions fell about seven percent against the fourteen percent organic sales decline, so wholesaler destocking explains much of the shortfall. The sell-out gap to the market narrowed from seven points in FY26 to about two points by the first quarter of FY27. In China, depletions returned to positive growth in the first quarter, but a 25% decline in reported net sales masked the trend. India remains the key offset.

Organic net sales rose 7%, or 9% excluding Imperial Blue. Part of its early FY27 strength is not durable. Morgan Stanley said the 19% first-quarter growth was partly a pipeline fill ahead of a relabelling exercise linked to a potential IPO. The mix effect is also a structural cost.

Consolidated volumes grew six percent while organic sales fell 0.9%, because lower-margin emerging-market shipments outpaced premium sales in the US and China. At brand level, delivery was uneven but encouraging. Jameson grew volume 3% and Malibu 5% in NABCA control states. Código and Jefferson's remained weak.

Ready-to-drink grew 12% organically. Outside the commercial picture, the policy and cost backdrop is manageable but not benign. Import tariffs in the United States and China cost 45 basis points of gross margin. Ageing inventories represent 85% of net inventories.

Leadership continuity is a mitigating factor. Mauve Croizat succeeds Hélène de Tissot as finance chief effective October 1, 2026.
