Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings 16 findings

Section summary
  1. Prior to 1H2026 earnings, the market was bracing for continued volume deleverage while debating when destocking in still wine would bottom

Core closure volumes and destocking dynamics

1.
Core volume expectations – persistent pressure: Sell-side models factored in ongoing top-line weakness following the 8% drop in consolidated sales in the first quarter of 2026. Analysts anticipated that volume contraction would continue to weigh on the business during the second quarter due to sluggish global wine consumption. This headwind was expected to keep organic growth negative across all key operating business units
2.
Destocking timeline – extended uncertainty: Market participants remained highly focused on the duration of winery destocking cycles, which had severely constrained order visibility. Although management previously noted that clients were adopting highly conservative purchasing policies to preserve capital, the sell-side hoped for stabilization. However, analysts remained skeptical about whether destocking would fully run its course by mid-2026
3.
59 Product mix shifts – divergence in demand: Analysts monitored the distinct divergence in consumption patterns, expecting structural declines in still wine to be offset by premium segments. While still wine closures faced pressure from changing consumer habits, the sparkling wine and spirits segments were expected to expand. The sell-side projected that Amorim would leverage its dominant packaging position in champagne and wirehoods to capture high-margin sparkling volumes
4.
61 62 Wine segment outlook – structural headwinds: According to Jefferies research by analyst Mundy, still wine was expected to decline by 1% to 1.5% annually through 2030. The broker highlighted that still wine corks were impacted by a negative product mix and lower recruitment among younger consumers. Consequently, the market expected this core segment to remain a persistent drag on sales volume
5.
64 Spirits and sparkling expansion – structural tailwinds: In contrast to still wine, Jefferies analyst Mundy projected sparkling wine growth of 1% to 1.5% and spirits growth of 2% annually. The broker anticipated that premiumization and mixology trends would favor spirits stoppers. Additionally, Amorim's recent acquisition of wirehood and champagne hood manufacturers was expected to secure market share gains in sparkling segments

The sell-side expected this transaction to partially offset organic closure declines in the North American market

Raw materials and margin drivers

10.
Raw material cork prices – margin protection: A key positive driver going into the earnings was the consumption of lower-priced raw cork harvested in prior years. This favorable pricing had supported a gross margin expansion to 54.9% in the first quarter of 2026. The sell-side expected these inventory dynamics to insulate EBITDA margins from the severe operating deleverage caused by falling volumes

Cork raw material supply – procurement recovery: In the previous years of 2024 and 2025, profitability was severely impacted by elevated raw material procurement costs. However, the 2026 budget assumed a restoration of historical profitability levels as supply-side pressures began to ease. The market focused on whether the harvest yields would continue to support this improving cost trajectory
12.
EBITDA guidance – flat expectations: The company estimated that the Florestal unit's EBITDA for 2026 would remain broadly in line with 2025. This flat performance reflected a planned reduction in the selling price of cork to the Amorim Cork unit, which was offset by higher raw material yields. Sell-side models had fully incorporated this expectation, predicting stable but unexciting operational profits for the forestry segment
