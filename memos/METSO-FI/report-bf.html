Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. the minerals processing market exhibits stable structural expansion driven by grade depletion and high service intensity, outperforming broader economic growth
  2. the competitive landscape is highly consolidated among five key tier-one players, creating a disciplined market focused on margins over volume
  3. scale thresholds define a steep margin ceiling for full-flowsheet providers, while sub-scale products experience substantial profitability dilution

Global scale and structural drivers

1.
Robust market growth: The mining equipment sector is entering a multi-year growth cycle driven by critical electrification metal demand.
2.
Outgrowing global production: Ore grade depletion and deeper mines mechanically increase the volume of material processed per tonne of copper.
3.
Resilient opex footprint: Mine production activity drives a high opex spend that supports equipment consumables independent of capital cycles.
4.
Dominant aftermarket mix: Aftermarket services generate approximately 55% to 65% of total mining equipment supplier revenues.
5.
Compounding margin differentials: Service and replacement part activities generate operating margins two to three times higher than new equipment sales.
6.
Long technology cycles: Equipment lifespans of 10 to 30 years provide a multi-decade aftermarket runway for established flowsheet suppliers.

Competitor tiers and concentration

7.
Established tier structure: The industry is dominated by Metso, Sandvik, Epiroc, Weir, and FLSmidth, who control the primary minerals processing flowsheet.
8.
Deep comminution duopoly: Metso and Sandvik compete fiercely in crushing and screening but successfully defend a high industry price level.
9.
Specialized pump dominance: Weir Group holds a dominant position in slurry pumps with an estimated 45% global market share.
10.
Dynamic portfolio pruning: Tier-one players are actively shedding low-margin non-core businesses to eliminate capital-intensive project risk and raise returns.
11.
Strong pricing discipline: Major manufacturers avoid price wars on capital equipment to lock in lucrative high-margin proprietary consumables contracts.
12.
Rising entry barriers: Long-term customer relationships and local service networks prevent low-cost Asian competitors from easily entering tier-one mine sites.

Value chain economic benchmarks

13.
High margin floors: Tier-one minerals processing divisions maintain through-cycle EBITA margin floors consistently above 15% on strong aftermarket mix.
14.
Premium peer ceiling: Weir Minerals and Sandvik Mining achieve peak operating margins well above 20% by leveraging deep service integration.
15.
Significant sub-scale dilution: Sub-scale products and non-core equipment lines frequently dilute overall margins down to standard construction equipment levels.
16.
Heavy working capital: Maintaining high uptime requires a net working capital commitment of 20% to 30% of annual sales.
17.
Minimum scale threshold: Flowsheet providers require billions in revenue to support the R&D and global service networks essential to qualify for contracts.
18.
High return profile: Asset-light assembly models and high-margin services enable leading operators to generate return on capital employed exceeding 15%
19.
Other side of the story: severe macroeconomic downturns can compress capital margins, and if junior miners defer critical maintenance spend, aftermarket pricing power fails to protect overall margins.
