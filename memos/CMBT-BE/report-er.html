CMB.TECH's Q2 2026 results delivered a material EBITDA beat driven by massive vessel sale gains and unseasonally strong spot rates, though the aggressive dry bulk pivot and heavy green capex split sell-side sentiment between near-term cash optimism and long-term regulatory execution risk. CMB.TECH reported unaudited EBITDA of USD 552.8 million for the second quarter ended June 30, 2026, representing a 27.11% beat against the Bloomberg consensus of USD 434.9 million and marking a substantial improvement over the USD 224.06 million delivered in Q2 2025. This operational outperformance was bolstered by USD 127.4 million in net capital gains from the delivery of three sold vessels, including the VLCCs Ilma and Ingrid alongside the Suezmax Sienna. The company's EBITDA margin expanded dramatically to 78.10% for the first half of 2026 compared to 62.20% in the prior fiscal year 2025, reflecting strong operational leverage and aggressive asset recycling at cyclical highs. Net profit for the quarter reached USD 364.4 million, a significant

turnaround from a net loss of USD 7.58 million in Q2 2025, though this figure fell slightly short of the USD 374.7 million consensus expectation due to higher-than-expected transitional voyage positioning costs. The Supervisory Board proposed a total distribution of USD 0.64 per share for Q2 2026, significantly exceeding the consensus expectation of USD 0.48 per share by 33.33% and matching the USD 0.64 per share distributed in Q1 2026.

This generous payout was structured as a USD 0.21 per share intermediary dividend combined with USD 0.43 per share paid from the share premium reserve, which is exempt from Belgian withholding tax. The distribution level remains highly dependent on discretionary capital gains from selling older tankers, raising questions about sustainability if secondhand vessel pricing cools. Despite these strong payouts, analyst Jørgen Lian of DNB Carnegie cautioned that the company's heavy remaining capital expenditure program of USD 900 million could limit future distributions if organic operating cash flow from the dry bulk fleet fails to support these levels without additional asset sales.

CMB.TECH's Marine division delivered exceptional spot rate performance during the second quarter of 2026, with VLCC spot earnings surging to USD 140,029 per day due to the de-facto closure of the Strait of Hormuz and widespread Middle East geopolitical disruptions. Suezmax spot earnings reached USD 146,567 per day, representing an extraordinary premium over the historical second-quarter average of USD 30,946 per day.

The company's Newcastlemax dry bulk fleet captured an average Time Charter Equivalent of USD 37,960 per day in the first half of 2026, up from USD 20,975 per day in the prior year, driven by strong iron ore, coal, bauxite, and grain demand. However, the company's realized VLCC spot rate of USD 126,790 per day undershot the wider market average due to transitional positioning voyages, representing a minor near-term disappointment against extremely bullish peer benchmarks.

The company's contract backlog remained highly stable at USD 3.26 billion at the end of Q2 2026, matching the USD 3.26 billion reported at the end of Q1 2026, providing strong forward revenue visibility despite volatile spot markets. This backlog was preserved through the addition of two 2-year Commissioning Service Operation Vessel charters and one 1-year VLCC charter during the quarter, successfully offsetting natural attrition.

The group's landmark charter agreement with Fortescue for up to 12 ammonia-powered Newcastlemax bulk carriers represents a major commercial validation of zero-emission shipping, with CEO Alexander Saverys asserting this milestone demonstrates the maritime industry can "decarbonize at scale right now." The company also secured 12-year charter agreements with Mitsui O.S.K. Lines for three ammonia-fitted Newcastlemax bulk carriers and six chemical tankers on seven- to ten-year terms, providing unprecedented long-term revenue visibility compared to typical spot-exposed peers.

CMB.TECH executed a massive block-sale of eight vessels in Q1 2026, including six older VLCCs and two Capesizes, generating USD 267.4 million in net capital gains to help repay the Golden Ocean bridge loan. During Q2 2026, the company delivered the sold VLCCs Ilma and Ingrid for a USD 98.2 million capital gain alongside the Suezmax Sienna for an additional USD 29.2 million gain. The company has scheduled the delivery of two Suezmaxes, Brest and Brugge, in Q3 2026 to generate approximately USD 100.2 million in additional gains, while Q4 2026 is expected to deliver the sold VLCC Donoussa and Suezmax Bristol for combined gains of USD 131.2 million.

This aggressive asset recycling strategy at peak tanker valuations has successfully reduced the company's remaining newbuilding commitments to USD 900 million from USD 1.60 billion at the end of December 2025, marking significant progress in funding the green fleet transition.

The company's adjusted value equity on total assets crossed the through-the-cycle target of 50%, ending Q2 2026 at 51.50%, representing a major balance sheet deleveraging milestone achieved ahead of original schedules. This rapid deleveraging was powered by strong spot cash flows and massive vessel sale proceeds, allowing management to accelerate debt reduction. However, DNB Carnegie's analyst Lian warned that the company operates with high market-adjusted leverage, calculating that a mere 10% change in underlying vessel asset values would impact net asset value over 20%, keeping the broker highly cautious despite near-term strength.

The firm maintained a Hold rating while raising the target price to USD 18.90 from USD 16.10, driven by higher underlying asset values across shipping segments but tempering enthusiasm due to elevated dry bulk exposure and fundamental Chinese demand headwinds. The sell-side response to the Q2 2026 results was mixed, with ING characterizing the print as positive and concluding the share price would rise, highlighting that CMB.TECH made the "right decision to pivot toward dry bulk" while still capturing significant gains from high tanker values.

ING maintained a Hold rating with a EUR 11 target price, expecting strong Q3 freight rates to drive another earnings beat and force upward consensus revisions. In contrast, DNB Carnegie reiterated a Hold rating, arguing the near-term risk-reward remains balanced as the stock trades at a tighter discount to underlying net asset value, reducing the margin of safety. The broker raised concerns over CMB.TECH's increasing dry bulk exposure of approximately 55% of fleet value, noting fundamental demand headwinds surrounding China's sluggish steel sector, where Chinese iron ore imports represent 21% of global dry bulk volumes.

CMB.TECH's structural pivot from tankers to dry bulk has successfully delivered substantial capital recycling gains and lowered outstanding capex commitments, though the company's open dry bulk spot exposure of roughly 60% of fleet days divides sell-side conviction. The diversified fleet on water has reached 206 modern vessels with an average age of 5.9 years, with dry bulk now representing 108 vessels or approximately 60.9% of the marine segment's capital allocation.

The group accelerated its dry bulk fleet expansion in June 2026 by taking delivery of the Newcastlemax carriers Mineral Magyar and Mineral Lietuva, while simultaneously reducing crude tanker exposure through the sale of the modern Suezmaxes Brest and Brugge. This strategic execution reflects management's aggressive bet on long-term dry bulk fundamentals, though it introduces significant cyclical vulnerability if Chinese steel demand weakens further.

The company's decarbonization execution accelerated with the successful completion of testing for the breakthrough WinGD X72DF-A ammonia engine at CSSC Engine Company in June 2026, positioning CMB.TECH to take delivery of its first ammonia-powered vessels in the second half of 2026. Management reaffirmed its commitment to reduce fleet-wide carbon intensity by at least 40% by 2030 compared to its 2008 baseline, maintaining ambitious long-term emissions targets despite the International Maritime Organization's one-year postponement of its Net-Zero Framework vote.

CEO Alexander Saverys expressed deep frustration regarding this regulatory delay, noting that "prolonged regulatory uncertainty is inherently bad for business," though he reiterated the company has "completed its transition homework early" and will not alter its capital allocation timeline. The company has 13 dual-fuel ammonia-fitted vessels on order with first deliveries scheduled for late 2026, targeting 5% to 10% low-carbon fuel uptake across its entire fleet by 2030.

CMB.TECH de-risked its fuel procurement by signing a green ammonia off-take agreement with CEEC Hydrogen Energy guaranteeing 20,000 tons in its first operational year, while acquiring a 25% minority stake in Jiangsu Andefu, a leading Chinese ammonia distributor with 180 tanker trucks and key refrigerated storage terminals.

The company also took 100% control of Cleanergy Solutions Namibia in January 2026, securing captive green molecule supply from the Walvis Bay hub that integrates a 5MW electrolyser with solar generation. These strategic upstream investments provide direct commercial access to green fuel supply chains as the first zeroemission vessels prepare to launch, though they represent highly capital-intensive and speculative commitments that carry high risk if global regulatory adoption of green ammonia slows.

The competitive landscape continues to evolve as CMB.TECH consolidated its position following its blockbuster merger with Golden Ocean Group in August 2025, expanding its fleet to more than 250 vessels and creating an eleven billion dollar diversified maritime power bloc. In stark contrast, peer operator 2020 Bulkers completed the total sale of its six Newcastlemax vessels in April 2026, representing a complete wind-down of active shipping operations and leaving the company with no assets to compete with.

Meanwhile, rival Exmar emerged as a direct technological competitor by taking delivery of the world's first dual-fuel ammonia ocean-going gas carrier in commercial service in June 2026, demonstrating the technology is commercially viable and intensifying the race for green propulsion leadership. Traditional peer Frontline continues to eschew massive alternative fuel commitments, relying heavily on exhaust gas scrubber exposure to comply with current regulations, though this conservative posture leaves the company highly exposed to future carbon pricing mechanisms.

Management executed a rationalization of top-tier executives following Chief Strategy Officer Benoit Timmermans' resignation effective May 1, 2026, with his responsibilities distributed among remaining board members rather than appointing an immediate replacement. This structural simplification marks the transition from merger integration to steady-state operations, though the redistribution of fuel procurement and chemical segment duties risks diluting executive focus on execution as the company enters its peak green newbuilding delivery cycle.

The Supervisory Board underwent succession transitions with Patrick De Brabandere appointed to succeed Marc Saverys as chairman, while independent director Carl Steen was co-opted as Remuneration committee chairman, bolstering governance frameworks during the highly capital-intensive execution phase. The company's operating divisions delivered mixed operational efficiency ratings, with the Bocimar Newcastlemax fleet achieving an average Annual Efficiency Ratio of 2.07 and a Carbon Intensity Indicator rating of B, confirming the dry bulk fleet is already meeting strict 2030 targets.

In contrast, the Euronav VLCC segment achieved a less favorable CII rating of C based on an AER of 2.28 in 2025, illustrating the ongoing necessity of selective divestments of older, less-efficient tankers. The Windcat offshore division captured strong spot margins, with commissioning service operation vessels achieving a Time Charter Equivalent rate of USD 64,451 per day in Q2 2026, though seasonal booking patterns show Q3 2026 pre-booked at the lower rate of USD 50,511 per day for 65% of days, preserving solid cash generation compared to prior year winter troughs.

The ongoing Middle East geopolitical disruptions triggered by the de-facto closure of the Strait of Hormuz beginning in March 2026 unlocked unprecedented spot shipping premiums, with war-risk premiums for Gulfbound vessels surging several-fold. This critical chokepoint represents approximately 26% of global seaborne petroleum trade, making its closure a systemic shock that forced a widespread reconfiguration of global oil trade flows.

The June 2026 Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding between the United States and Iran quickly collapsed by late July, prompting the abrupt re-closure and dashing market hopes for swift normalization. This escalation drove global dry bulk demand into a new expansionary phase supported by emerging long-haul trade routes, including the Simandou iron ore project in West Africa, which commenced operations in late 2025 and recorded accumulated shipments of 7 million tons, substantially expanding ton-mile demand for Capesize and Newcastlemax freight rates. The sell-side coverage pool for CMB.TECH remains concentrated, with active coverage primarily led by DNB Carnegie and ING, while other major global investment banks such as Jefferies officially dropped coverage following the complex restructuring and merger transactions.

This limited sell-side pool creates a lack of consensus clarity on long-term earnings trajectory, though the broker community broadly acknowledges that if a prolonged period of unseasonally high freight rates persists alongside additional massive vessel sale gains, it could trigger a structural re-rating and force analysts to upgrade ratings to buy. The stock delivered a 3-month return of 27.60% through late July 2026, ranking among the top ten best performers in the Benelux region, and rose 25% year-to-date by August 2026, representing a 14% outperformance against the market benchmark, driven by rapid improvement in core shipping operations profitability.
