EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business Model and Structural Position

1.
Dominant capital-light model: OEM International operates a highly efficient decentralized technical-trading model serving over 30,000 industrial clients across Europe, with Sweden accounting for approximately 58% of net sales and 65% of group EBITA.
2.
Above-peer organic momentum: Historical organic growth averaged 6.1% to 6.4% annually over the last decade, outpacing peer stagnation, while operating margins expanded from 10.4% in 2014 to 15.5% in 2024.
3.
Structural switching-cost moat: Technical value-selling positions OEM as the outsourced local sales arm for over 400 manufacturers, with customization and engineering integration creating high switching costs and robust customer retention.
4.
High-margin value-chain stage: OEM captures EBITA margins of 13% to 16% in the technical distribution stage, materially outearning downstream contract assemblers at 6% to 8% margins.

Financial Profile and Return Excellence

5.
Peer-leading return metrics: Return on capital employed reached 40% in 2024 and 30.7% in 2025, with incremental ROCE of 32% to 87% across multi-year periods, substantially exceeding Atlas Copco and Swedish serial acquirers.
6.
Superior cash conversion: Cash conversion averaged 61% over ten years and 66% over five years, with lease-adjusted FCFE representing a median 84% of net income from 2013 to 2023.
7.
Elevated working capital intensity: Net working capital to sales reached 25.1% in 2025 versus peer averages of 18% to 20%, with 92 net working capital days versus Lifco's 65 days, reflecting inventory-heavy operations.
8.
Under-leveraged balance sheet: The group maintained a net cash position of SEK 603 million at year-end 2025, with net debt to EBITDA at -0.8x versus peers at 1.0x-2.4x, leaving significant M&A capacity unutilized.

Customer and Supplier Dynamics

9.
Exceptional customer fragmentation: OEM serves more than 30,000 customers with no single client exceeding 4% of sales and the top five accounting for only 15% of consolidated revenue, materially reducing concentration risk.
10.
Diversified supplier base: The group maintains partnerships with over 400 leading manufacturers, with no single supplier exceeding 3% of consolidated sales, effectively mitigating single-source dependencies.
11.
Strong technical lock-in: Technical value-selling embeds specialists deep within customer product development phases, while customization allows subsidiaries to successfully pass on cost increases and maintain high operating margins through inflationary cycles.

Competitive Landscape and Market Structure

12.
Benign competitive dynamics: OEM competes against Swedish serial acquirers like Addtech and Indutrade, delivering superior organic sales growth of 7.0% over five years versus peer flatlines, while competitors shift focus toward proprietary manufacturing, leaving the pure distribution landscape less crowded.
13.
Formidable entry barriers: Sourcing and stocking regional components requires prohibitive capital investments for subscale competitors, while exclusive local distribution mandates and deep design-in integration create the ultimate barrier to entry by legally preventing brand replication.

Operator Quality and Strategic Transition

14.
CEO succession underway: Managing Director Jörgen Zahlin, who has led since 2000 with over forty years of institutional knowledge, will step down by December 31, 2026, with Henrik Forsberg Schoultz appointed Deputy CEO to accelerate value-accretive M&A.
15.
Private equity ownership shift: EQT Partners acquired 26.19% of share capital and 73.68% of voting rights in February 2025, aiming to materially accelerate acquisitions, but introducing severe cultural risks that could break the decentralized entrepreneurial model.
16.
Exceptional operating discipline: The group maintained an average EBITA margin of 15.2% over 2020-2024 against a 12% target, with return on equity averaging 32.0% versus a 25% target, achieving substantially higher return metrics than listed peer groups.

Secular Tailwinds and Structural Risks

17.
Robust secular growth drivers: Factory automation market expansion of 6.5% annually through 2034, industrial electrification growing 13% annually to 2025, and nearshoring initiatives with 95% of companies planning investments provide strong structural tailwinds.
18.
Material disruption vectors: Apparent nearshoring and automation tailwinds may fail to materialize if European industrial stagnation persists, while global manufacturers could bypass distributors by establishing direct local e-commerce networks inside the Nordics.

Aggregate Verdict

19.
Critical transition juncture: Rapid M&A acceleration under EQT ownership could destroy the historic capital- light profile and depress returns as capital employed scales up, with operator transition and cultural dilution representing the single largest risk, signaled early by organic deceleration paired with rising staff costs.
