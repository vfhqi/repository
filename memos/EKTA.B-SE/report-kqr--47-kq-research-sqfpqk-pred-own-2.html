Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The development of foreign exchange rates represents a critical variable for Elekta as currency fluctuations directly impact translation of reported sales and transaction margins.
  2. Understanding the transmission mechanics of Elekta's hedging program is essential for forecasting earnings per share and evaluating structural financial risks.

Core Investigative Focus

1.
Investigative focus: "How will Elekta AB's (EKTA.B-SE) 24-month rolling forward hedge programme (50 to 100 percent cover of forecast flows) transmit the krona's strength against the US dollar, euro and Chinese renminbi, and the pound's moves against the US dollar, into Elekta's reported revenue (translation) and gross margin (transaction) in FY2026/27 and FY2027/28; what hedge rates, hedge-reserve movements and sensitivities has the company disclosed (annual report 2025/26 and interim reports); what currency assumptions do the sell-side brokers covering Elekta use; and what have other Swedish exporters with hedging programmes (for example Getinge, Elekta's Nordic medtech peers, and Sectra as an unhedged contrast) reported about the effect of the 2026 krona strength on margins?"

Financial Implications and Earnings Risk

2.
Severe transaction exposure: Elekta faces significant transactional risks because its global business structure primarily incurs manufacturing and operating expenses in British pounds and Chinese renminbi while generating substantial revenue in US dollars. A general change of one percentage point in the Swedish krona exchange rate against other currencies would affect the group's net profit and shareholders' equity by approximately SEK 13 M, exclusive of hedging effects. Consequently, understanding these transmission effects is vital because foreign exchange volatility directly alters reported gross margins and bottom-line earnings per share.
3.
Negative translation headwind: Elekta reported that net sales in the first quarter of fiscal year 2026/27 were negatively impacted by a stronger Swedish krona against key revenue currencies such as the US dollar and the euro. This translation headwind reduced net sales by approximately 3% in reported Swedish kronor despite solid underlying constant currency performance. Such translation volatility makes it difficult for investors to gauge true organic growth trends without adjusting for currency movements.
4.
Contrary evidence: Elekta's broad operational structure naturally mitigates transaction exposure because the company maintains a significant cost base in US dollars, British pounds, and euros. This natural operational hedge limits the overall impact of foreign exchange volatility on bottom-line profitability, as noted during the first quarter earnings call on August 27, 2026. Thus, the net transaction threat to earnings per share may be smaller than raw revenue exposure suggests.
5.
Volatile financial cushion: Elekta utilizes a 24-month rolling forward hedge program covering 50% to 100% of forecast flows to mitigate short-term transaction fluctuations in currency markets. Realized results from these cash flow hedges are recognized in the operating result under exchange rate differences and amounted to SEK 135 M during fiscal year 2025/26. This program acts as a critical buffer, but its unwinding rates will ultimately transmit underlying krona strength over time into the reported figures.
6.
Varying broker models: Sell-side analysts covering Elekta must constantly adjust their financial models to reflect shifting currency assumptions and their subsequent impacts on earnings forecasts. For instance, DNB Carnegie raised its sales estimates by 2% and adjusted earnings per share by 2% to 4% for the 2026 to 2028 period mainly to reflect recent foreign exchange movements. This highlights how broker consensus and valuation multiples remain highly sensitive to currency translation and transaction adjustments.

Section 2 – Elekta Hedging Mechanics and Disclosures

Section summary
  1. Elekta's rolling 24-month forward currency hedging program provides a structured buffer against foreign transaction exposures but delays the impact of currency swings on operating margins.
  2. Disclosed outstanding hedge positions as of April 30, 2026, demonstrate a progressive transition toward weaker hedge rates for the US dollar and Japanese yen in fiscal years 2026/27 and 2027/28.

Policy Guidelines and Framework

1.
Conservative policy structure: Elekta manages transactional currency exposures by hedging highly probable forecasted sales up to 24 months in advance using forward exchange contracts. Under this board-established policy, individual group companies quantify exposure through flow forecasts. This operational framework ensures that short-term market fluctuations are mitigated before translating results.
2.
Specific coverage mandates: Elekta maintains a policy where recognized foreign-currency assets and liabilities are hedged between 50% and 100% of their net balance. Forecasted transaction flows do not have a fixed mandatory hedge percentage ladder disclosed in public reports. The company applies cash flow hedge accounting under IFRS 9 guidelines to record effective derivative movements within equity. This treatment ensures that realized hedging gains offset operational transaction differences.
3.
Rigorous accounting rules: Elekta applies cash flow hedge accounting only when an economic relationship exists between the hedged item and the hedging instrument. Credit risk must not dominate the resulting fair value changes, and the hedge ratio must align with management's strategy. Realized results from effective cash flow hedges are recorded in operating income under exchange rate differences. These structured requirements prevent earnings distortions from speculative derivative transactions.
4.
Organizational hedging responsibility: Elekta delegates the operational identification, evaluation, and hedging of financial risks to its central treasury department. Each individual operating subsidiary remains responsible for estimating its own upcoming transaction exposures. This decentralized reporting feeds into the group risk assessment process established by executive directors.
