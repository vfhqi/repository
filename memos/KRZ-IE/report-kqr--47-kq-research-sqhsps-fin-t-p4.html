Section 2 – Currency Translation and Transaction Hedging

Section summary
  1. Foreign exchange translation poses a persistent drag on Kerry's reported earnings because about 42% of revenues are dollar-denominated.
  2. Our confidence in this exposure is high based on detailed historical drag figures and segment reports provided in Kerry's annual statements.

To analyze the underlying drivers, this investigation evaluated 15 distinct sources covering 7 sector companies. This translation risk represents a key factor in how constant currency operating performance converts into reported earnings per share for investors.

Section 3 – Share Count, Capital Allocation, and Leverage Capacity

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group has actively reduced its outstanding share count to compound earnings per share despite operating at its self-imposed leverage limit of two times EBITDA.
  2. Comparative analysis shows Kerry utilizes a leverage-backed capital allocation model that contrasts sharply with the low-leverage, cash-rich buyback strategies of peers like AAK.

Share Count Dynamics and Buyback Programs

1.
Significant equity contraction: Kerry Group reduced its outstanding ordinary share count from 175.79 million shares at the end of 2023 to approximately 160 million average shares in 2026. This reduction reflects the continuous execution of rolling multi-million euro share repurchase programs over a three-year horizon. Financial data indicates that this sustained contraction has directly boosted reported earnings per share. Consequently, management has successfully utilized buybacks to offset short-term operating headwinds.
2.
Consistent program execution: Kerry Group progressed its capital return strategy by executing a 300 million Euro share buyback program initiated in February 2026. Management confirmed in October 2026 that this program remains on track to fully complete before the end of the calendar year. Regular transactions on Euronext Dublin have progressively retired blocks of ordinary shares throughout 2026. This active buying has consistently supported the company's equity value.
3.
Future buyback commitments: Kerry Group announced its intention in October 2026 to launch a new share buyback program of up to 300 million Euros immediately following the completion of the current tranche. Analysts expect this new program to reduce the share count by an additional 2% of the company's market capitalization. This persistent capital return underlines management's commitment to returning excess cash to shareholders. - Analyst/Investor These ongoing buybacks will continue to provide support for reported earnings per share through 2027.

Direct earnings accretion: Share count reductions are expected to contribute approximately 1% to 2% in net positive adjustments to Kerry Group's annual earnings per share. Morgan Stanley notes that incorporating the newly announced 300 million Euro buyback program will likely upgrade next-year adjusted earnings per share by 3%. This mechanical boost helps the company translate its constant currency growth targets into higher reported figures. Thus, buybacks remain a critical component in driving consistent mid-term EPS compounding for investors.

Furthermore, the company continues to target consistent double-digit dividend growth as its primary capital return mechanism. Share repurchases are balanced against outstanding M&A opportunities to optimize total shareholder returns.

Leverage capacity boundaries: Kerry Group reported a Net Debt to EBITDA leverage ratio of 2.0 times at the end of June 2026. This leverage ratio stands at the exact upper boundary of the company's historic comfort zone. Management maintains a policy of managing capital to preserve a strong investment-grade credit rating. Consequently, operating at this leverage limit implies that future buyback capacity remains highly dependent on continuous operational cash generation.

Contrary evidence: Lower operational cash conversion could severely restrict Kerry Group's capacity to execute subsequent share buybacks under its strict leverage policy. The company's defined cash conversion fell to 76% in the first half of 2026 due to capital expenditure phasing and working capital outflows. If cash conversion fails to recover toward the 80% full-year target, debt levels could rise. This cash constraint would force management to choose between capital expenditure and buybacks to maintain leverage below its limit.

Section 4 – Tax Policy and the Impact of Pillar Two Minimum Tax

Section summary
  1. Kerry Group maintains a distinct tax advantage over specialty ingredients peers because its Irish corporate headquarters keeps its effective tax rate below fifteen percent.
  2. Our confidence in this tax outlook is medium based on Irish Pillar Two transposition laws and comparative peer tax rate forecasts.

Pillar Two and Kerry's Tax Rate

1.
Moderate tax: Kerry Group faces a minor increase in tax liabilities as global policies target historical low-tax advantages. Management reported an effective tax rate of 14.1% for 2025, contrasting with claims of a 13.1% rate. The company currently expects its 2026 group rate to fall between 14% and 15%. This modest range indicates that Kerry maintains a strong tax advantage relative to global specialty ingredients peers.
2.
Complex compliance: Irish tax legislation has incorporated global tax directives which could eventually lift the group's overall rate. The Irish Government transposed the 15% Pillar Two rules through the Finance Act 2023, taking effect in 2024. These provisions establish a highly intricate framework to calculate the statutory 15% minimum rate. However, the company maintains that geographical profit allocation limits the immediate cash tax drag on earnings.
3.
Gradual escalation: Financial forecasts from analysts predict a very slow upward movement in Kerry's tax rate over the next four years. Bernstein Research models the company's tax rate at 13.5% in 2026, rising progressively to 14.2% by 2029. This projection assumes that Pillar Two top-up taxes are digestible without severely compressing adjusted net profit margins. This steady rise means that below-EBITDA headwind impacts will remain manageable for long-term EPS compounding.
4.
Historical context: Kerry Group's history of corporate restructuring has consistently targeted tax optimization through operational excellence. In previous years, the implementation of unified operating models and regional centers of excellence drove the group's tax rate down from 17.5% to 17.1%. These actions show that internal efficiency drives structural tax benefits over multi-year horizons. Similar self-help initiatives will help mitigate the transition to higher minimum tax standards.
5.
Contrary evidence: Kerry's effective tax rate could rise unexpectedly beyond standard guidance due to shifts in the regional profit mix or tax authority challenges. In July 2026, Kerry reported that its first-half effective tax rate reached 14.6%, which is higher than the 14.1% recorded in 2025. This upward movement shows that geographical earnings distribution can increase tax charges even before accounting for major Pillar Two top-up taxes. This volatility might drag down consolidated net income and reduce future adjusted EPS.
