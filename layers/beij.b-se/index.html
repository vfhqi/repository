Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external forces support the earnings path more than the multiple. Over the last six months the balance moved in the company's favour: demand turned (organic +8.3% in Q3 2026 after about zero for four quarters), currency stopped subtracting, and the regulatory calendar came closer (the 1 January 2027 quota step). The forces with the longest visibility (regulation, European cooling penetration) are tailwinds; the forces that are present but cyclical (US residential replacement, European construction) are bottoming headwinds; the force that is strongest right now, the discount rate, acts on the share price rather than on the business. Two cautions for the next set: first, about half of the Q3 2026 acceleration was weather, which will reverse into a headwind in the summer of 2027, so the model should not extrapolate the EMEA rate; second, currency is now a tailwind but it is unhedged and two-way, and a Riksbank hike or a risk-on rally could flip it again.

The objective is to produce that judgement, based on the first and second sets' answers and your answers to the third set, for the Investment Analyst who writes the investment memo, so that Richard, who reads the memo, can form his judgement on the investment case.

What the scores mean in this layer

Finish each answer and each judgement on a group or set with a score from 1 to 100; a higher number is more positive for an investment case in the company.

For the third set's questions, the score says how positive the thing the question asks about is for the predictability of the company's financial forecasts over the next 18 months, and so for an investment case in the company: 100 the most positive, 1 the most negative, 50 neither.

For the fourth set's questions, the score says how favourable for an investment case in the company the gap is between our assessment and what current guidance, sell-side estimates and the stock market narrative already reflect: 100 the most favourable, 1 the least, 50 neither.

The Investment Analyst's opening notes

Read this first

The conclusions in six lines.

  1. The earnings path is reasonably predictable; the organic-growth prints and the share price are not. Our FY2027 EPS is SEK 6.44 (the model, without minorities, SEK 6.51). I put eight outcomes in ten between about SEK 6.05 and 6.85, and the outer plausible range at SEK 5.85 to 7.05. A little over half of the 27% EPS growth from FY2025 to FY2027 comes from acquisitions that are already signed.
  2. The risk to the 18-month forecast is concentrated in two drivers the company does not control: the European summer of 2027 against the record summer of 2026, and the krona. Neither can be observed in the next six months, in which every scheduled disclosure faces an easy comparison.
  3. Our FY2027 EPS is about 5% below consensus like for like, but only about 3 of those 5 points will surface as estimate cuts; the other 2 are transaction costs that brokers leave out of adjusted earnings.
  4. Consensus revisions are likely to trace a V: flat to up through April 2027 (new acquisitions, easy comparisons, the refrigerant price step), down by about 3% for FY2027 between July and October 2027, and up again into 2028.
  5. The narrative already holds the positives (organic turn, re-lit acquisitions, regulation, new chief executive, new anchor owner); what it does not hold is mostly negative and mechanical (interest rising, option liabilities outside net debt, the Q2 leverage peak, the size of the Q3 2027 print).
  6. The share price moved on the company's own news over the last six months but around a level set by its cohort. I do not think the evidence supports the guideline fair multiple of 34.26x; I would treat 24x to 26x as fair in a delivery phase (see "The fourth set as a whole").

  • FY2027 to FY2030: the gap is explained to within SEK 20m and shows no input to be wrong. It is a difference of counting. The model's figure is free cash flow after paying for the working capital of the businesses bought; the cross-check is free cash flow of the existing business. Both are valid; the memo should say which it quotes. For the cash the existing business generates (the base for dividend cover and for funding acquisitions) use the cross-check: about SEK 2.7bn in FY2027, SEK 5.3 a share, a 3.4% yield on SEK 156.3.
  • FY2026: a further SEK 368m is unexplained, and here it is the cross-check, not an input, that I judge to be wrong. The year-end working-capital input of 37.5% of revenue (SEK 15.2bn) implies that the existing business absorbs about SEK 0.9bn of working capital in 2026; the cross-check allows about SEK 0.5bn. For the cross-check to hold, working capital would have to fall from SEK 15,752m at Q3 2026 to about SEK 14.8bn (36.6%), an underlying fourth-quarter release of about SEK 1.1bn, against releases of SEK 0.3bn in Q4 2024 and SEK 0.8bn in Q4 2025 and with distributors stocking refrigerant and compliant equipment before the 1 January 2027 quota step. I keep the 37.5% input and read FY2026 free cash flow on the second set's definition as about SEK 1.9bn to 2.0bn, not SEK 2.3bn.
  • EPS: the model is SEK 0.03 to 0.07 above the cross-check in every year because minority interests (SEK 22m to 34m a year) are not modelled, by Richard's ruling of 16-Oct-26. Not material.
  • A second gap, which the summary reports but does not flag as a cross-check difference, matters more for the memo: the model's net debt is overstated by about SEK 2.5bn at the end of 2026 and by about SEK 3bn in every later year. The model shows SEK 13,911m at end-2026 (2.64 times EBITDA); the second set expects SEK 11,410m and I expect SEK 11.3bn to 11.4bn (about 2.2 times EBITDA including leases; about 1.9 times on the company's definition, which excludes leases). The mechanism can be inferred from the model's own reconciliation, and the arithmetic fits to within about SEK 60m. Reported net debt rose by SEK 2,202m in the first nine months of 2026 (from 9,501 to 11,703), but the model's cash flow lines for those nine months sum to an outflow of only SEK 208m, because the reported quarters carry no acquisitions (FactSet holds none) and, it appears, no dividend; that is the SEK 1,994m difference the summary reports. The model then appears to place the whole year's acquisitions input (2,400) and the whole year's dividend (837) in the one forecast quarter, Q4 2026 (its Q4 outflow is SEK 2,208m), on top of a Q3 balance that already contains the nine months of acquisitions actually paid (including SEK 982m of option and earn-out settlements) and the SEK 760m dividend paid in the spring. No input change can repair this without mis-stating the year's acquisitions, so I leave the inputs alone.
  • Why I do not change "Acquisitions less disposals" to remove the double count of acquired working capital. Inside the model, acquired working capital is charged twice (in the working-capital line and inside the acquisition price), which over-states the yearly rise in net debt by SEK 0.9bn to 1.3bn. But the model also leaves out the growth in lease liabilities (about SEK 0.3bn a year; FactSet's net debt includes leases), and the second set's acquisition prices look too low (see D-01, tension 2). These errors roughly cancel: from FY2028 the model's yearly rise in net debt (1,757; 1,345; 1,329) is within about SEK 130m of the second set's own table (1,630; 1,300; 1,330). Changing one of three offsetting items would make the model worse, not better.
  • For the Financial Analyst. The Financial Analyst should (1) stop the full-year FY2026 acquisitions and dividend inputs from falling wholly into the Q4 2026 column, by entering in the reported quarters what was actually paid in them (or by deducting it from the year's input), so that end-2026 net debt starts from the reported SEK 11,703m at Q3 2026 plus only what remains to be paid in Q4; (2) show equity free cash flow both before and after the working capital of acquired businesses, or take that working capital out of the working-capital line when "Acquisitions less disposals" is the full consideration; and (3) show FY2025 on the forecast years' basis (EBITDA SEK 4,772m, EBITA SEK 3,962m, EPS SEK 5.08), so that the first forecast year's growth is like for like.

Three cautions on reading the one-page model summary.

  • "EPS growth +23.9%" for FY2026 is a basis artefact. The FY2025 base of SEK 4.69 is FactSet's line after the SEK 150m restructuring charge and after acquired amortisation; like for like the base is SEK 5.08 and FY2026 growth is about 13% to 14%. The same applies to EBITA (3,458 to 4,389 is +27% as shown; +11% like for like from 3,962).
  • The "EBIT (Viewforth: EBITA) against consensus" row (+5.6%, +1.7%, +3.7%) compares our EBITA, before SEK 250m to 320m of acquired amortisation, with brokers' EBIT after it. Like for like our EBITA is within about 1% to 3% of consensus in each year and below it in FY2027.
  • The EPS row (-2.2% for FY2027, -2.0% for FY2028) understates the like-for-like gap because the FactSet median mixes brokers who add back acquired amortisation with brokers who do not. Broker by broker, on each broker's own basis, our FY2027 EPS is 4.5% to 9% lower (DNB Carnegie 6.76 against our 6.44 on the same basis: -4.7%).

Limits of the evidence. The co-movement table supplied for D-07 is empty (no price history was found), so D-07 rests on returns, multiples and dated episodes, not on measured correlations. The AlphaSense reports hold Beijer Ref research from two houses only (DNB Carnegie and Jefferies); the models of seven other named brokers were not visible, and no investor, press or social-media commentary was found, so "the sell-side" below means two houses plus the FactSet figures, and the investor half of I-03 is inference. Where an AlphaSense report contradicts the company's own figures I use the company's (for example the leverage ceiling is below 2.5 times, not 3 times).
