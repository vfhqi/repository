Structural segment performance and pressures

1.
Severe sales contraction: Siemens Healthineers faced persistent sell-side scrutiny regarding the deep downturn in its diagnostics division ahead of the late 2026 earnings release. Deutsche Bank analyst Friedrichs on August 18, 2026, noted that structural market rebasing in China was dragging down global diagnostic sales, which fell 5.50% on a comparable basis during the third quarter. Underperformance severely threatens the company's mid-term earnings per share target because diagnostic revenue declines exert negative operational leverage on margins.
2.
Persistent revenue dilution: Siemens Healthineers experienced severe top-line erosion from its legacy diagnostic platforms, particularly across North American markets. Bernstein analyst Ludwig on August 4, 2026, reported that legacy product lines, which comprise less than 35% of diagnostic sales, were offsetting growth in Atellica systems. Headwind is expected to depress group margins until the structural transformation program is completed, which could lower adjusted earnings per share by €0.12 in the upcoming fiscal year.
3.
Weak diagnostic demand: Siemens Healthineers encountered significant procurement delays in China as local health authorities implemented laboratory fee reforms. Morgan Stanley analyst Noor on September 9, 2026, highlighted that changes in laboratory testing economics were altering customer purchasing behavior and depressing clinical testing volumes. Operational drag lowers diagnostic profit expectations, which means that near-term earnings per share growth will remain constrained unless regional policy volatility subsides.
4.
Strategic portfolio restructuring: Siemens Healthineers drew intense market focus regarding its planned carve-out and potential exit of the diagnostics business. Deutsche Bank analyst Friedrichs on August 18, 2026, stated that the diagnostics restructuring was progressing, with management actively considering a potential exit to enhance group returns. Strategic divestiture of this underperforming asset would immediately eliminate a massive margin drag, potentially boosting adjusted earnings per share by up to €0.15 over the next three years.
5.
Resilient imaging expansion: Siemens Healthineers maintained a highly favorable market position in its core imaging business despite broader macroeconomic volatility. Jefferies analyst Dormois on September 30, 2026, observed that structural demand for imaging, supported by clinical advancements in computerized tomography, was driving consistent market share gains. High imaging demand is expected to sustain a 6% revenue compound annual growth rate through 2029, which will support stable earnings per share development.
