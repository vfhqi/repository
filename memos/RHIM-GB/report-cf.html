EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

RHI Magnesita presents a structurally resilient but cyclically constrained investment case, demonstrating stable executive leadership under long-tenured directors while navigating elevated leverage at 2.9x Net Debt to EBITDA that significantly exceeds the core 1.0-2.0x target, yet the company delivered resilient adjusted EBITA margins over the last three years despite experiencing the weakest global refractory demand cycle in fifteen years.

The strategic thesis centers on self-help restructuring and North American M&A integration, with the 2035 Strategy targeting €30 million in annual savings by 2027 through plant closures and shared services, while the integration of the Resco acquisition drives local-for-local US production to 65% with plans to reach 80% by 2028, supported by pronounced second-half weighting of earnings that persisted throughout the cycle due to seasonal cement production and sporadic industrial project deliveries.

The company exhibits outstanding cash conversion of 105% providing self-help deleveraging capability and a clear competitive advantage over peers, with RHIM achieving a cash conversion of 105% generating €391 million in adjusted operating cash. However, the external environment remains challenging as global refractory demand faces cyclical headwinds from European steel contraction offset by robust structural growth in India, while competitive intensity is elevated as aggressive Chinese exports and domestic capacity additions trigger widespread price erosion, and trade and environmental regulations are tightening through definitive CBAM enforcement and revised US tariffs. The earnings trajectory shows outstanding cash conversion exceeding 100% supported aggressive strategic acquisitions and highlighted structural earnings quality over the historical period, while RHI Magnesita demonstrated resilient operational execution over the last three years, using self-help to offset weak global demand.

Yet vertical integration margin contribution collapsed due to magnesite oversupply, shifting the earnings burden entirely onto refractory business execution. The company met adjusted EBITA expectations with a 15% year on year adjusted EBITA increase in Q1 2026 driven by self-help measures, though regional performance was highly divergent with North American M&A strength offsetting severe European contraction.

Peer momentum analysis reveals RHI Magnesita dominates peers with a cash conversion of 105% despite nearterm steel cycle headwinds, ranking 2nd overall but 1st in cash generation, though elevated leverage and vertical integration headwinds could compress margins to single digits if steel markets undergo prolonged decline. Sell-side positioning reflects analyst consensus has drifted downward over the last 12 months as macro challenges in Europe offset Indian growth, with lead industrial analysts maintaining Hold ratings citing near-term volume uncertainty despite strong self-help cost delivery, while underlying target price estimates were cut significantly through early 2026 to reflect persistent vertical integration margin weakness.

Notably, there is no active activist presence in the AS library for RHI Magnesita. Key forward drivers include Resco Group Integration delivering +10m EBITA in H2 25 with HIGH plausibility, Network Optimisation Americas contributing +15m EBITA in FY26 with HIGH plausibility, Global Shared Services targeting +20m EBITA by 2029 with MEDIUM plausibility, Captive Mineral Normalisation adding +1.0-1.5pp EBITA margin with MEDIUM plausibility, 4PRO Premium Solutions generating +15m EBITA in H2 25 with HIGH plausibility, and the Steel EAF Transition driving +1.0-2.0% volume growth with HIGH plausibility.

Management guidance exhibits a cautious archetype requiring self-help cost savings to offset structural pricing headwinds globally, with historical guidance tracking revealing periodic downgrades specifically in FY24 and FY25 when macro conditions deteriorated, while margins remain highly sensitive to FX and volume shifts making local onshoring and pricing execution critical. - Sales Revenue Top risks include underlying leverage remaining significantly above Vesuvius with persistent vertical integration weakness that could drag margins below 10% if cash generation stalls, compounded by the reality that rapid customer transition to onshored local production could increase capital intensity and trigger oversupply if global demand fails to expand.

The extreme dependency on self-help savings could expose margins to double-digit declines if plant restructuring faces execution delays, while seasonal first half cash outflows and persistent European volume declines might derail the targeted deleveraging to 2.6x if steel demand fails to recover. The heavy reliance on self-help cost measures could fail to defend margins if prolonged European steel declines trigger fixed-cost under-absorption, creating execution risk around the ambitious transformation agenda.

The forward outlook balances structural resilience through superior cash generation and strategic M&A against cyclical volume pressure and margin compression risk from depressed vertical integration returns. Success hinges on flawless execution of restructuring programs and normalization of captive mineral margins back toward the 2.0-2.5% range, while maintaining pricing discipline in an environment of aggressive Chinese competition. The investment case ultimately rests on management's ability to deliver promised synergies and cost savings while navigating a multi-year steel cycle trough, with deleveraging progress serving as the critical near-term credibility test for the broader transformation narrative.

Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro)

Section summary
  1. global refractory demand faces cyclical headwinds from European steel contraction offset by robust structural growth in India.
  2. competitive intensity is elevated as aggressive Chinese exports and domestic capacity additions trigger widespread price erosion.
  3. technological disruption is accelerating with rapid customer adoption of electric arc furnaces and circular recycling platforms. Net read: trade and environmental regulations are tightening through definitive CBAM enforcement and revised US tariffs.
  4. raw material security remains critical as Chinese magnesite oversupply depresses global prices while alumina input costs remain volatile.
  5. global industrial production cycles are highly divergent, showing deep property-led softness in China against Indian infrastructure expansion.
  6. macroeconomic pressures continue to weigh on margins due to geopolitical logistics bottlenecks and localized energy crises.

Demand

1.
Highly correlated demand: Refractory consumption is intrinsically linked to industrial output cycles of steel, cement, copper, and glass.
2.
High steel intensity: Manufacturing one ton of steel demands 10 to 15 kg of refractories, compared to only 1 kg for cement.
3.
Divergent global consumption: Global finished steel demand fell 1.9% in 2025, driven by a 7.1% contraction in China.
4.
Robust Indian growth: Indian finished steel demand is expected to expand 9% to 10% in FY2026-27, supporting domestic refractory sales.
5.
Delayed maintenance cycles: Customers are actively extending furnace shutdown intervals to manage short- term costs, temporarily reducing immediate refractory volumes.
6.
Soft industrial demand: Industrial demand softened slightly in early 2026, particularly across glass and cement project pipelines, as reported by Jefferies.

Competition

7.
Aggressive Chinese exports: Chinese producers are exporting surplus refractories at low prices, creating severe downward pricing pressure globally.
8.
Destructive price competition: Unintegrated competitors utilize a cost-plus pricing model, competing aggressively on price when raw material costs drop.
9.
Indian capacity additions: Excessive local capacity expansion in India has eroded pricing discipline in commodity-grade refractories.
10.
Regional margin contraction: Intensified competition from Chinese imports has compressed gross margins to 22.5% in regional steel markets.
11.
Commoditized market risk: Customers facing financial strain increasingly shift toward low-cost local suppliers in less-differentiated segments.

Disruption

12.
Rapid EAF shift: Steelmakers are accelerating the transition from basic oxygen furnaces to low-carbon electric arc furnaces.
13.
Specialized product requirements: Electric arc furnace steelmaking requires highly technical refractories, including EAF hearth ramming materials.
14.
Scrap recycling transition: Tata Steel aims to increase site scrap intake from 17% to over 70% under planned EAF facilities.
15.
Differentiated solutions adoption: RHI Magnesita is expanding its integrated 4PRO platform to de- commoditize its business and secure long-term contracts.
16.
Circular economy integration: The group formed a greenfield recycling joint venture with Khemka Refractories to accelerate spent refractory recovery.

Plausible problems to monitor

RHI Magnesita's leverage position remains materially elevated at 2.9x Net Debt to EBITDA, substantially exceeding both its stated target range of 1.0-2.0x and peer Vesuvius' 2.0x ratio, creating heightened financial risk during cyclical downturns referenced in Section 4. The company's vertical integration margin contribution collapsed to only 1.1% in FY25 from 1.7% in FY23, representing the lowest contribution in recent history and significantly underperforming the targeted 2.0-2.5% range, as detailed in Sections 4, 5, and 9. This persistent weakness in backward integration returns directly undermines the structural margin advantage that vertical integration should theoretically provide.

Group revenue declined 3% to €3,366 million in FY25, underperforming peer Vesuvius which experienced a smaller 0.6% revenue decline to £1,809.5 million, indicating RHI Magnesita faced more severe volume or pricing pressures than competitors as discussed in Section 4. Regional margin compression to 22.5% gross margin reflects intensified competitive pressure from Chinese imports, documented in Section 3, highlighting vulnerability to low-cost foreign competition.

The combination of delayed maintenance cycles by customers actively reducing immediate refractory volumes and extended furnace shutdown intervals creates near-term volume uncertainty beyond normal cyclical patterns, as noted in Section 3. executed a material mid-year guidance downgrade for FY25, reducing EBITA targets from above €407 million to €370-390 million, demonstrating forecasting challenges discussed in Section 10.

Historical earnings exhibited pronounced second-half weighting with H1 25 representing only 35% of full-year consensus, creating quarterly volatility that exceeds normal seasonal patterns as documented in Section 5. Sell-side analyst consensus drifted downward over the last 12 months with Jefferies maintaining a Street-low price target, reflecting persistent skepticism about near-term recovery prospects detailed in Section 8. Competitive intensity remains elevated with aggressive Chinese exports creating severe downward pricing pressure globally and excessive Indian capacity expansion eroding pricing discipline in commodity-grade segments, as examined in Section 3. The requirement for cautious mid-year guidance revisions during both FY24 and FY25 suggests structural challenges in forecasting accuracy covered in Section 11.

Extreme dependency on self-help savings to achieve margin targets creates execution risk if plant restructuring faces delays, highlighted in the sensitivity analysis of Section 10.

Outliers worth noting

RHI Magnesita exhibits several statistical and qualitative anomalies that warrant close scrutiny, as they represent deviations from historical norms, peer benchmarks, or management explanations. The most striking outlier is the dramatic collapse in vertical integration margin contribution, which plummeted from 2.50% in FY22 to just 1.10% in FY25, despite management's strategic emphasis on backward integration as a core competitive advantage.

While Chinese magnesite oversupply provides a partial explanation, the magnitude and persistence of this decline appears disproportionate given that the company sources 67% of its basic materials internally. Management's guidance for normalization to 2.0% to 2.5% by 2027 relies heavily on self-help mineral optimizations rather than addressing the structural market dynamics that created the initial compression, suggesting the recovery path remains uncertain and execution-dependent. A second material anomaly is the extreme second-half earnings weighting observed in FY25, where H2 adjusted EBITA represented approximately 63% of full-year results, a substantial deviation from the historical 50-50 split. This distortion was attributed to delayed industrial project deliveries and glass customer deferrals, yet the magnitude of the skew raises questions about the predictability and quality of the earnings stream.

The pattern suggests potential customer behavior changes around furnace maintenance timing or capital budget allocation that management has not fully explained, creating forward visibility challenges for investors attempting to model quarterly cadences. The company's cash conversion performance of 105% in FY25 stands as a positive outlier relative to peer Vesuvius's 75%, yet this exceptional result was achieved partly through €84 million of working capital release following significant inventory reductions.

While management frames this as disciplined execution, the aggressive drawdown raises questions about whether the company sacrificed operational flexibility or customer service levels to generate near-term cash, particularly given the concurrent €391 million Resco acquisition that elevated leverage to 2.9x Net Debt to EBITDA. The sustainability of triple-digit cash conversion appears questionable once working capital normalizes post-integration.

A geographic anomaly emerges in the European revenue contraction of 12% in FY25, driven by a 14% reduction in shipments, which significantly outpaced the modest 1.9% decline in global finished steel demand. Management attributed this to cyclical steel weakness, yet the disproportionate volume loss suggests potential market share erosion or customer-specific losses that have not been transparently disclosed. The severity of the European decline contrasts sharply with North American revenue growth of 22%, creating an unexplained regional performance divergence that extends beyond macro steel production trends.

Finally, the goodwill impairment of INR 5.56 billion recorded by the Indian subsidiary on acquired Dalmia assets represents a significant anomaly given the relatively recent acquisition timing and management's bullish 40% Indian market share target. While management cited margin pressure from Chinese imports and domestic overcapacity, the scale of the write-down suggests fundamental miscalculations in the original acquisition thesis or integration assumptions.

This impairment, combined with persistent pricing pressure in commodity-grade Indian refractories, challenges the strategic coherence of the aggressive Indian expansion narrative presented in concurrent investor communications. These outliers collectively suggest execution risks and structural headwinds that may not be fully reflected in management's forward guidance or strategic positioning.
