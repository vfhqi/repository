Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The separation of Electrification represents a pivotal moment for TKH Group to unlock valuation upside.
  2. Standalone multiples for the high-margin Automation business are expected to rise significantly upon completion.

Strategic separation rationale

1.
Key question: "how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire?"
2.
Strategic separation: TKH Group is separating its Electrification business to focus on its high-margin Automation segment. Management announced in July 2026 that shareholders approved the dual-track separation to unlock long-term conglomerate value. This divestment will allow the company to accelerate its high-margin buy-andbuild strategy in core automation fields. Standing alone, the focused Automation division can achieve cleaner corporate focus because it is no longer constrained by the capital-intensive Electrification unit.
3.
Valuation expansion: TKH Group's standalone valuation multiples are expected to expand significantly following the Electrification carve-out. Jefferies' note of August 12, 2026 argues that TKH shares trade at a 50% discount to SOTP-based NAV of €103.5 per share, implying 14.7x EV/EBITDA, which is 46% below peers. Eliminating the persistent conglomerate discount remains critical for driving multiple re-rating because standalone assets trade at much higher sector valuations. Unless the separation fails, this structural change will likely boost the company's forward earnings per share progression as valuation gaps with peers narrow.

Cost structures and reporting

4.
Cost structures: Standalone Automation must absorb corporate overheads that were previously unallocated at the group level. TKH Group's 2025 annual report outlines that its medium-term Automation adjusted EBITA margin target of 17% to 19% excludes these support costs. Additionally, the July 2026 circular warns of unavoidable stranded separation costs that could burden the remaining business. These corporate additions mean that the standalone Automation operating margins could face downward pressure unless head-office costs are strictly managed.
5.
Reporting transition: TKH Group has adjusted its internal segment reporting ahead of the separation, laying the groundwork for discontinued operations accounting. Since January 2026, non-core assets totaling €250 million in turnover have been regrouped under the Other segment. This intermediate step will transition to formal IFRS 5 discontinued operations presentation once a binding sale agreement is reached. This accounting shift will isolate core Automation earnings from divested cable results, which means investors will see a cleaner view of core earnings per share growth.
6.
Market friction: TKH Group faces a narrow European initial public offering window that complicates its dual-track separation strategy. Deutsche Bank's report of April 15, 2025 highlights that European IPO volumes are declining amidst structural capital market headwinds. Because leveraged buyers face higher financing costs under a 5% US ten-year yield regime, a private trade sale may become the preferred route. financing environment means that a delayed public listing could postpone any anticipated capital returns to shareholders.

Contrary perspectives and risks

7.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that separating Electrification may not eliminate the conglomerate discount if remaining Automation assets face order pressure. Jefferies' note of October 2, 2026 indicates that core Automation activities face near-term pressure due to delayed order intake in tyre building. This structural weakness means that earnings per share progression could stumble shortly after the separation is finalized.
8.
Other side of the story: The proposed separation of Electrification may fail to generate immediate valuation upside if macroeconomic headwinds delay the recovery of core segments. While peer separations like Smiths Group or Sandvik demonstrated long-term margin improvement, they required substantial post-separation restructuring. If TKH Group cannot achieve sustained double-digit organic growth in its remaining Smart Vision technology, the standalone multiples will remain compressed. Consequently, the expected re-rating relies heavily on macro factors rather than simple corporate simplification.

Section 2 – Standalone Automation Margin Viability and Separation Cost Allocation

Section summary
  1. High confidence exists that standalone Automation margins will experience temporary compression below historical levels upon absorbing unallocated corporate overhead and stranded costs.
  2. Structural evidence from European corporate separation precedents demonstrates that post-carve-out holding costs consistently dilute initial operating margins by 100 to 200 basis points.

Baseline Automation Margin Performance and Targets

1.
Baseline profitability: Automation demonstrated strong underlying profitability in the first half of 2026 by delivering an overall return on sales of 18.4%. TKH Group's interim report published on 1 September 2026 shows that Vision Technology achieved a 20.2% margin on sales of €270.0 million while Automated Machinery generated 16.0% on sales of €218.5 million. These figures compare favorably with historical group operating margins of 11.9% recorded in full-year 2024. However, these reported segment results reflect pure operational performance before accounting for corporate headquarters expenses. Consequently, standalone Automation margin sustainability depends heavily on central cost absorption.

2.
Corporate exclusion: Corporate targets for the remaining business fail to incorporate overhead expenses previously shared across the broader conglomerate. TKH Group's 2025 annual report published on 7 July 2026 explicitly states that the medium-term Automation target of 17% to 19% adjusted EBITA margin excludes unallocated support charges. By comparison, group corporate eliminations and unallocated support expenses totaled €12.6 million in the first half of 2026. Transferring even half of these central costs to standalone Automation would immediately reduce reported operating margins. Therefore, medium-term targets appear overly optimistic without explicit cost-reduction programs.
3.
Subsegment margin disparity: Operational dynamics between Vision Technology and Automated Machinery create underlying margin volatility for the standalone entity. TKH Group's half-year financial statement of 1 September 2026 reports that Vision Technology EBITA grew organically by 12.1% to €54.5 million. Conversely, Automated Machinery EBITA contracted by 5.7% organically to €35.0 million due to weak tire-building order intake from Tier 1 customers. Peer machine-vision provider Cognex generated operating margins of approximately 18% during similar cyclical troughs. Unless Automated Machinery demand recovers, subsegment product mix shifts could erode overall Automation returns.

Separation Circular and Stranded Corporate Overheads

4.
Stranded cost burden: Corporate separations inevitably leave behind fixed operational expenses that cannot be immediately eliminated or transferred to the divested unit. TKH Group's shareholder circular dated 22 July 2026 highlights that creating two standalone companies will trigger significant ongoing governance, legal, rebranding, and IT infrastructure overheads. These duplicate corporate structures create stranded costs that the remaining parent entity must absorb during the transition period. Similar industrial spin-offs typically require two to three years to fully rationalise redundant administrative functions. As a result, stranded overheads will temporarily depress standalone Automation earnings.
5.
Transitional service friction: Temporary support agreements between the parent group and the separated business introduce operational complexity and cost friction. The 22 July 2026 circular confirms that TKH Group will maintain transitional service arrangements for software licenses, payroll applications, lease accounting, and corporate guarantees. While these services generate cost recovery, they require administrative oversight that distracts executive focus from core operational efficiency. Industrial group separations studied across European markets indicate that transitional service agreements frequently exceed budgeted expense allowances. Consequently, transitional service fee execution poses near-term margin risks.
6.
Divestment cost impacts: The planned disposal of non-core activities adds further administrative friction to head- office cost structures. TKH Group's presentation of August 2026 details a €250 million divestment program targeting non-core turnover including Digitalization fibre optics. While removing low-margin revenues improves gross margins, the lost revenue base reduces overall overhead absorption across central corporate functions. European engineering groups executing multi-asset divestment strategies often see unallocated holding expenses spike during execution. This structural dynamic implies that corporate cost dilution will linger until portfolio rationalization completes.

Section summary

Market Leadership and Order Resilience

1.

Jefferies' note of May 24, 2024 states that VMI holds an estimated market share of over 70% of the outsourced tire-building machine market. This dominant position ensures that VMI remains the primary partner for major tire manufacturers. Consequently, this strong market share will support long-term earnings per share growth once capital expenditure cycles normalize.

Order book contraction: Automated Machinery experienced a significant decline in its order book during the recent cyclical downturn. TKH Group's interim report of September 1, 2026 states that the segment's order book fell to €259.2 million at June 30, 2026 from €420.2 million in the prior year period. This contraction was driven by delayed order placements by Tier 1 customers due to macroeconomic uncertainties. As a result, nearterm segment earnings will face headwinds until these deferred projects convert into active deliveries.

Jefferies' note of May 24, 2024 explains that the UNIXX platform delivers unprecedented flexibility and lower waste for premium manufacturers. This qualification process is highly time-consuming and can take several quarters to complete. This means that while repeat orders are secured, the positive impact on earnings will be gradual.
