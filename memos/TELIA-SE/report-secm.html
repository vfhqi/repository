Telecoms relative strength: a pullback in a longer uptrend, or structurally impaired?

Richard's question (2-Nov-26, his case file note on Hellenic Telecom): "In parallel, first, we need an IA/APM analysis re. whether the sector/industry SP trends (i.e. RS) is having a pullback temporarily or if its RS is structurally impaired… I had thought it was a pullback in a longer uptrend… But that was 1-2 months ago. That view seems less likely now IMHO, but we need a detailed investigation, analysis and judgement memo produced". And on its purpose (3-Nov-26): "Those are two key questions, but that is not just the purpose of the note. The purpose is what I stated, too". So this memo answers his question for the whole group (sections 1 to 7), and the two key questions for Hellenic Telecom: whether it can be a Probing Bet before the group's relative strength turns, and how much weight Industry RS deserves for a stock that trades largely on its own news (section 8).
Terms used throughout. Stock RS, Sector RS and Industry RS are the readings named in the Position Entry and Exit Rules: a 0 to 99 rank of a weighted 3-to-12-month return relative to the Stoxx Europe 600 (Stock RS ranks each stock across the universe; Sector RS ranks each Sector's median stock across 84 Sectors; Industry RS ranks each Industry's average stock across 16 Industries). The Pool is the Pool of Stocks Eligible for Middle Innings Portfolio Selection, the buying gate for a Middle Innings Core Investment; its tests include Stock RS of 80 or more and Sector RS of 50 or more. Sell Criterion 1 is Industry RS below 30; Sell Criterion 2 a relative price whose 200-day average is flat or falling; Sell Criterion 3 Sector RS below 50 with Stock RS below 70 (all three bind Core Investments; for an Early Innings Probing Bet they are information only). "Relative line" means an equal-weighted basket divided by the Stoxx Europe 600. Artificial intelligence agents are software assistants that compare and switch a customer's contracts for them; terminal growth is the growth rate a discounted cash flow valuation assumes for ever after its forecast years. Grades use the Viewforth scale against the set named beside them: A the top 10%, B the next 15%, C the next 25%, D the next 25%, F the bottom 25%. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers.

1. The judgement

Richard's revised view is the one the prices support: this is no longer a pullback in a longer uptrend. On every measure set before the event (all 32 stocks, the median stock, breadth, and the group without its two satellite operators), the relative strength uptrend that ran into May 2026 has broken. On Barclays' sector note, the strongest structural case in hand, the impairment is structural in Barclays' valuation and unproven in operations. Barclays has written artificial intelligence agents and satellite broadband into lower long-run growth across its coverage and cut its price targets by about 10% on average; on its own numbers the stocks are still about 4.5% above the new targets on average. It expects the damage to come slowly (about 1% of group revenue a year for five years), changes no earnings per share estimates for it, and shows no evidence yet of switching or price cuts caused by agents. The third-quarter results of Proximus and KPN (28 and 30-Oct) cannot test it: the quarter ended about two weeks after the agent narrative began, so they are a baseline, not a verdict. So the structural case now weighing on the group is one broker's long-run valuation view, laid on a fall whose dated drivers were the June satellite shock (about 9 of the 14 points, an effect Barclays does not model) and rates. No single quarter can disprove a long-run assumption, but two or three quarters of steady churn and pricing would erode it; the first full quarter after the agent narrative is reported from 28-Jan-27. Further falls do not need that proof (the 14% so far came without it); a recovery probably needs it, or falling bond yields, or permissive EU merger guidelines. The most likely path is a sideways base: about one in five on a resumed uptrend by mid-February 2027, and Industry RS likely (about three in four) still below 50 at the end of January.

4. The structural case at its strongest: Barclays' sector note

Bottom line: Barclays' "An UnaMUSEing Basket Case?" (completed 19-Oct-26, released 20-Oct-26, 78 pages) gives the structural case a mechanism, a size and a valuation method. It states the market's working hypothesis well and turns it into a lasting valuation input; it does not yet show switching or price cuts caused by agents. Read it as the strongest statement of the hypothesis now weighing on the group, not as evidence of damage.

What it argues

  • Artificial intelligence agents: agents such as Meta's Muse can compare contracts and switch or renegotiate on the customer's behalf. Telecoms are exposed because operators discount to win new customers and charge older, out-of-contract customers more, and because main brands and value brands are priced far apart. Barclays sees the agent risk highest in Belgium and Germany and lowest in France and Italy, and in the United Kingdom higher for fixed broadband than for mobile
  • Defences, and their limits: an excerpt from Barclays' United States colleagues (25-Sep-26) lists barriers already in place (identity checks in person to take a new contract in some markets, voice authentication to leave in others), blocking agents, and discounts kept for in-store sales, which it says may even help growth if done well, while noting that technology often finds a way around such barriers. It adds natural friction (number porting; swapping home equipment and engineer visits to change broadband) and expects operators to deploy their own agents in response. Its conclusion is greater price dispersion, more product complexity and more restrictions, rather than uniformly lower prices
  • Satellites: a real, gradual risk to rural fixed broadband, with 20% or more of households a potential long-run market. Starlink had more than 13 million subscribers worldwide by mid-August 2026, against 8.9 million at end-2025, and ran price promotions in the United Kingdom, France, Italy and Spain in 2026. Satellites serve 0.2% to 0.5% of households in the big European markets (data from early 2025 to mid-2026), against about 2.3% in the United States; Barclays assumes about a point a year of share gain, which gives its 2.5% of revenue from private customers. Direct-to-phone service it treats as a complement and does not model (about 80% of mobile use is indoors; spectral efficiency from space is 3 to 5 times lower). Barclays calls the broadband threat broadly manageable in Europe (high fibre coverage and low prices, especially against the United States). The mobile risk it names is satellite companies signing wholesale access deals with weaker operators; a hybrid satellite-terrestrial network it sees as a remote possibility in the United States and with no path in Europe. SpaceX bought United States mobile spectrum (from Grain, a reported 8 billion dollars, awaiting approval) but holds no European mobile spectrum and, in Barclays' view, has very limited ability to buy enough; the 2027 EU 2 GHz satellite auction offers about 20 MHz (open to non-European bidders and usable across the EU), small against the 200 to 300 MHz an operator typically holds

How big

  • Country inputs: eleven markets are scored; Greece is not one of them. Satellite risk is highest in Belgium, the United Kingdom and the Netherlands and lowest in Spain and Switzerland
  • Company effects over about five years (its charts): revenue -3.8% (Swisscom) to -6.8% (BT), with Bouygues at -0.5% because most of its business is not telecoms; EBITDA -8% (Tele2, Swisscom, KPN) to -13% (Telefonica), computed as lost revenue at a 70% gross margin; equity value -5% (Tele2; Bouygues -4%) to -29% (Telefonica and Proximus), largest where debt is highest (its text gives 5% to 30%)
  • Into prices: terminal growth cut by 0.25 to 0.75 percentage points (0.5 as a starting point); price targets cut by 2% (Bouygues) to 30% (Liberty Global), about 10% on average; after the cuts its peers' average downside to the new targets is about 4.5% (its comparison in the Telefonica paragraph). No earnings per share estimates changed for the thesis (the only changes were Hellenic Telecom, for wholesale, and Telenor, for Bangladesh)
  • Why a slow effect moves prices a lot: Barclays' illustration values a typical operator (1% revenue growth a year for 15 years, net debt 2.5 times EBITDA, 7.5% cost of capital, 1% terminal growth) at about 7.2 times EBITDA; with 0.5% revenue growth about 6.0 times, and with none about 5.0 times. EU incumbents trade at about 7.3 times 2026 EBITDA (challengers 9.8 times; Barclays, at 14-Oct prices) while sector revenue is already slightly falling, two-thirds of its costs are fixed and its debt is 2 to 2.5 times EBITDA for most operators. The sector also lagged the market for about ten years before 2024

What it does not show, and the first test

  • No evidence of switching or price cuts caused by agents: the 5% is assumed (penetration times savings). The note has pricing-structure data (discounts, value-brand gaps, revenue per customer), Starlink's growth and sector revenue, but no data on agent use, switching or agent-driven price cuts; the only market reaction it cites is the share price
  • Most of the fall came first: its own chart of the sector index against the Stoxx Europe 600 shows the largest drop in June, around the SpaceX listing (its marker is approximate), on direct-to-phone fears it does not model; the agent leg is September and October
  • The operating weakness it shows predates the agents: sector revenue growth has been slightly negative since the second quarter of 2025 (about -0.6% year on year in the third quarter of 2025 and about -0.3% in the second quarter of 2026, read from its chart), which it puts down to customers optimising spend and to challengers
  • The first results are a baseline, not yet a test (28 and 30-Oct-26; the quarter ended about two weeks after the agent narrative began): Proximus (Belgium, Barclays' most exposed market) grew Residential customer services revenue 2.9% with its January 2026 price indexation in place and Residential revenue 2.4%, adding mobile contract customers in what it calls a highly promotional market; its domestic underlying revenue grew only 0.3% and Business fell 1.9%. KPN (the Netherlands: average agent risk, high satellite risk; upgraded by Barclays) grew service revenue from private customers 1.8%, mostly from mobile (+5.1%); fixed service revenue +0.2%, fixed revenue per customer +0.3%, broadband -7,000 lines, and churn temporarily higher after it changed its terms and conditions. Pricing held; volumes are under pressure; the first full quarter after the narrative (October to December) reports from 28-Jan-27
  • Testable within six months: churn, existing-customer pricing and moves to value brands in the markets Barclays scores most exposed (agents: Belgium, Germany, United Kingdom fixed; satellites: Belgium, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands) against the least (France and Italy for agents; Spain and Switzerland for satellites), above all in the results from late January (the first full quarter after the agent narrative); whether sector revenue growth worsens; operators' defences against agents; regulators' satellite share data and Starlink's subscriber counts; approval of SpaceX's United States spectrum purchase and any European satellite wholesale deal

6. Base rates, and the Theon comparison

Bottom line: from where telecoms stand today, groups rarely resume their uptrend quickly (a quarter within 26 weeks of reaching this position, and telecoms are already 11 weeks in), but they usually stabilise rather than collapse (median +2% relative over six months; over half back above their 200-day average). Strong stocks in weak Industries do about as well as strong stocks generally, but their upside needs the Industry to turn. Theon followed its group down.

Groups in today's position (our data, Sectors and Industries, 2022 to 2026)

  • The match: relative line 8% to 16% below a 52-week high set 13 to 30 weeks earlier, 0% to 8% below a 200-day average that has flattened (within 0.5% a month); one case per group per six months; 113 cases with 26 weeks of data. Telecoms entered this position on 14-Aug-26
  • After 26 weeks: 25% had made a new relative high; 55% were back above the 200-day average; 71% had a falling 200-day average; median relative return +2%, positive in 61% of cases. After 13 weeks: 9% at a new high, 34% above the 200-day average
  • For context, from the moment a pullback first reaches a still-rising 200-day average (196 cases with 26 weeks of data): 35% made a new relative high within 26 weeks and half saw the average turn down. All 32 telecoms reached that point on 18-Jun-26, and it failed

Industry RS falls (our data, August 2022 to October 2026)

  • Falls from Industry RS 80 or more to 30 or less within six months (14 cases): 29% were back at 50 or more 13 weeks later and 50% after 26 weeks; median relative return over the 26 weeks +6%
  • Those still at 30 or less after 13 weeks, as telecoms were on 30-Oct (7 cases with 26 weeks of data): 2 were back at 50 or more after 26 weeks (Consumer discretionary in 2024; Energy, commodities and metals mining in 2023) and 4 at 30 or more
  • Small samples: read these as orders of magnitude, not probabilities. The telecom episode started on 31-Jul-26, so its 26-week mark is the end of January 2027

Strong stocks in weak Industries (month-end samples, 824 stocks)

  • Stock RS 60 or more with Industry RS 30 or less: median relative return over the next 13 weeks about +1%, the same as for all stocks with Stock RS 60 or more (all stocks: about 0%); 73% still had Stock RS of 50 or more 13 weeks later
  • Where the stock had stayed strong through a long weak spell in its Industry: median +6% when the Industry recovered within 13 weeks, and about 0% when it stayed weak. The weak Industry is not, on average, a headwind for a strong stock, but the upside needs the Industry to turn

Richard's Theon comparison, tested

7. Pullback or structural: the case each way

The judgement in one paragraph. On price, the question is answered: the longer uptrend in telecoms' relative strength has broken on every measure set before the event, and only the absence of a lower low (by a 3% margin) keeps it from being a confirmed downtrend. On the cause, Barclays' note, the strongest structural case in hand, makes it structural in Barclays' valuation (a lower long-run growth view written into terminal value across its coverage; on its numbers the stocks are still about 4.5% above its new targets on average) and unproven in operations (no switching or price cuts caused by agents are shown, and the third-quarter results, from a quarter that ended about two weeks after the narrative began, are only a baseline). It is one broker's view so far, laid on a fall whose dated drivers were the June satellite shock and rates. Further falls can come without operating proof, as the 14% so far did: higher yields, more brokers cutting terminal growth, or a satellite wholesale deal would do it. A recovery more likely needs operating evidence that stays clean through the first full quarter after the narrative (results from 28-Jan to late February), falling bond yields, or permissive EU merger guidelines. Until then base rates say the likeliest path is a sideways base: about one in five for a resumed uptrend by mid-February 2027, about even for Industry RS back above 30 by the end of January, and about three in four that Industry RS is still below 50 then.

The strongest case that it is structural

  • The price trend has already broken, breadth has nearly halved and the median stock lost 14%: markets often de-rate a group before its estimates move
  • A long-run view is now inside sell-side valuations: Barclays has cut terminal growth for its whole coverage, calls the threat a material overhang, and on its numbers its coverage is still about 4.5% above the new targets on average. Such views can hold multiples down for a long time: the 2022 to 2023 de-rating kept telecoms in the bottom fifth for about a year, and the sector lagged the market for about ten years before 2024
  • The sector was already not growing: revenue growth has been slightly negative since mid-2025, before the agents. On Barclays' illustration one point less long-run revenue growth (1% a year to none) takes a typical operator from about 7.2 to 5.0 times EBITDA; Barclays itself values a typical operator at about 7 times and cut terminal growth by only 0.25 to 0.75 points, so it does not argue for the lower figure, but with two-thirds of costs fixed and most operators' debt at 2 to 2.5 times EBITDA, even 1% a year of lost revenue matters
  • Satellites are growing fast from a low base (Starlink 13 million subscribers in mid-August 2026 against 8.9 million at end-2025, with promotions in four big European markets), and defences against agents may be worked around
  • Higher yields for longer: our weekly environment note reads the United States ten-year as anchored above 5%; the yield-stock discount does not reverse until yields do
  • The first operating cracks: Hellenic Telecom's traditional fixed-line revenue falling about 2.5% to 3% from pricing (an analyst's calculation that its chief executive confirmed, 30-Oct); KPN's broadband base -7,000 in the third quarter, with churn temporarily higher; the Public Power Corporation's (PPC, Greece's utility) aggressive broadband pricing spreading (Barclays, 2-Nov); Digi's replenished balance sheet able to fund new price wars (our sector note of 15-Oct)
  • What would have to be true for it to win: November results and February 2027 guidance show rising churn, falling existing-customer pricing or shrinking fixed-line revenue, especially in Belgium, Germany, United Kingdom fixed and the Netherlands

The strongest case that it is a pullback that will recover

  • No lower low yet: the relative line's October low is above its December 2025 low (by a thin margin: the line is 3% above it)
  • The agent effect is assumed, not observed: Barclays shows no agent-driven switching or price cuts and changed no near-term earnings estimates for it; in Belgium, its most exposed market, Proximus held its pricing in the third quarter, though that quarter is too early to be a test
  • The satellite share is still small in Europe: 0.2% to 0.5% of households in the big markets; direct-to-phone a complement; no European mobile spectrum for SpaceX
  • Valuation already pays for some damage: the median telecom trades at about the 25th to 33rd percentile of its own ten-year price-to-earnings range, with group estimates broadly holding
  • Little outperformance is needed: about 2% to 4% lifts Industry RS above 30; about 4% to 7% takes it to 50, and 3% to 8% takes Sector RS to 50
  • From here, groups usually stabilise: median +2% relative over 26 weeks in today's position, and over half back above their 200-day average
  • What would have to be true for it to win: yields fall (the weekly note's trigger is the United States ten-year under 4.5% with the Federal Reserve's expected December hike priced out; euro yields and credit, which squeezed the second leg, would need to ease too), or results through February show stable churn and pricing at the operators Barclays downgraded, or the final merger guidelines are permissive

8. Hellenic Telecom: the two key questions

Bottom line: for Hellenic Telecom the group matters less for which way the stock moves week to week and more for how far it can re-rate. Its weekly moves are largely its own, but since end-July telecoms excluding it lost about half as much as it did against the Stoxx Europe 600, and Barclays cut its target by the sector-average 10% (lower terminal growth plus lower wholesale estimates) without scoring Greece. Whether to hold a Probing Bet before the group turns is Richard's judgement; the evidence on both sides is below, and this memo makes no recommendation.

Hellenic Telecom: below a falling 50-day average since late September, still above a rising 200-day average

Question 1: how much weight does Industry RS deserve for a stock that trades largely on its own news?

  • Little for weekly direction: weekly regressions over the last year explain about a tenth of the stock's moves by the Stoxx Europe 600, telecoms and Greek stocks together, and about 2% by telecoms alone (a fifth by all three in 2022 to 2024, when Greece mattered most). The 29-Oct-26 investment memo found the telecom basket explains about 4% of daily moves
  • More for the level: from 19-May to 31-Jul the stock beat telecoms (excluding itself) by 19% and Greek stocks by 3%. Since 31-Jul it has lost 6% against the Stoxx Europe 600 while telecoms excluding it lost about 3%, so about half of its fall matches the group's (an arithmetic split, not a measured cause); it also lagged Greek stocks by 9%. Against a strong group effect: its rolling two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple rose from 10.3 (May) to 11.1 (September) while the group's fell 12%; it is at the 33rd percentile of its own ten-year range
  • Through the sell side's valuation inputs: Barclays applied its sector-wide terminal growth cut to Hellenic Telecom without scoring Greece (its valuation now uses 0% terminal growth at an 8.5% cost of capital) and cut its wholesale estimates for the PPC-Vodafone venture. On Barclays' own logic the equity effect is largest where debt is highest; Hellenic Telecom's net debt is about 0.2 to 0.5 times EBITDA against 2 to 2.5 times for most of the peers it ranks
  • Its own exposure, contained so far by management's account but not absent: Starlink took customers from OTE's poorest copper lines until fixed wireless arrived in January 2025, and OTE "suffered a bit more" than rivals; the chief executive says Starlink's growth slowed in 2026, and calls fixed wireless a temporary solution until fibre arrives. The pressure it does face now is price competition from PPC: traditional fixed-line revenue down about 2.5% to 3% from pricing (an analyst's calculation the chief executive confirmed), retail fixed revenue excluding Data Com -0.6%, and wired broadband lines down about 23,600 in the quarter, partly OTE moving its own copper customers to fixed wireless
  • Base rates: strong stocks in weak Industries did about as well as other strong stocks over 13 weeks (median about +1%); 73% stayed at Stock RS 50 or more and 27% did not (Theon fell to 32); the bigger gains (median +6%) came only when the Industry recovered within 13 weeks. Hellenic Telecom's Stock RS was 83 at end-July and is 59 now
  • Answer: low weight for whether the stock rises or falls on its own news; real weight for how far it can re-rate. While Industry RS stays low, the stock's upside depends on its own catalysts, and the bull case in the investment case assessment needs a re-rating that the group's weakness makes harder

Question 2: can Hellenic Telecom be a Probing Bet before the group's relative strength turns?

  • What the rules say: as a Middle Innings Core Investment it fails three of the Pool's tests (Stock RS 59 against 80; Sector RS 31 against 50; sell-side sales Net Upgrade Breadth below zero, per the Pool build of 2-Nov) and shows Sell Criteria 1 and 3; it would need about 10% of outperformance by end-January for Stock RS 80. As an Early Innings Probing Bet, entry is Richard's judgement, the Sell Criteria are information only, and the 10-day stop-loss rule on the moving average he chooses governs exit
  • Facts that favour acting before the group turns: strong stocks in weak Industries did about as well as other strong stocks over 13 weeks (median about +1%), and 73% kept Stock RS of 50 or more; its debt is low, so on Barclays' own logic its equity is less exposed than that of the indebted peers; its own chart has not rolled over (above rising 150-day and 200-day averages)
  • Facts that cut both ways: the stock's own dated tests (the price step decision in January to February 2027; the 2027 guidance in late February) come before the group is likely to turn (Industry RS about three in four still below 50 at end-January; one in five on a resumed group uptrend by mid-February). A price step and guidance in line with management's stated intention (growth at about this year's level) would separate it from the group; no price step would count, in the investment case assessment, as a sign that Barclays is right
  • Facts that argue against: Barclays' double downgrade (Overweight to Underweight, its 18 EUR target below the 18.85 EUR close, its 13 EUR downside case); the investment case assessment's probability-weighted return of about +5% to end-2027, below the cash yield, and its bear values of 15.20 and 13.00 EUR (-14% and -26% including the dividend); one in four strong stocks in weak Industries lost Stock RS 50 within 13 weeks; and its own chart: Stock RS down from 83 to 59, and its lead over its relative 200-day average down from 11% at end-July to 2%
  • On its own chart it is topping out, not yet rolled over: close 18.85 EUR (2-Nov); 52-week high 20.40 (3-Aug), 8% below it; below its falling 50-day average (19.53); above its 150-day (18.74) and 200-day (18.04) averages, both rising. A close below about 18 EUR with the relative 200-day average flattening would be rolling over
  • No recommendation: the decision and its size are Richard's
