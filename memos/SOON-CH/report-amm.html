APM Mini-Memo: Sonova

The one-line view. Sonova is a well-run leader of the hearing-aid oligopoly with real self-help on top of a strong product cycle, and the story depends on holding share through the crowded late-2026 launch window and on buying the shares cheaper than today's 288 francs.

What the company does

Sonova is a Swiss hearing-care group: it makes hearing aids under the Phonak, Unitron and Hansaton brands, runs more than 3,000 of its own clinics, and makes cochlear implants through Advanced Bionics. Sales for the year to March 2026 were 3,606 million francs, up 5.9 percent in local currencies, and the market value is about 14.5 billion euros. Wholesale is about half of sales at operating margins above 30 percent, retail is 39 percent at mid-teens margins, and the loss-making Sennheiser consumer business, about 5 percent of sales, is being sold.

An oligopoly leader. Five makers hold over 94 percent of the world's hearing-aid wholesale market, and Sonova leads with 25.3 percent. Platform launches every two years support prices, and prices held when volumes softened in 2025.

A plausible investment thesis

Self-help on top of a product cycle. A completely new leadership team, in place by December 2025, narrowed the company to medical hearing care, moved to a four-region model in April 2026, launched a programme targeting 90 million francs of run-rate savings by year four, and set an algorithm of 5 to 10 percent local-currency sales growth and 7 to 12 percent core operating profit growth towards 6 billion francs of sales by the 2030/31 year. Underneath, the Infinio and Sphere artificial-intelligence platforms are winning share: wholesale grew 10.9 percent in local currency in the second half of the year to March 2026 against a market growing 2 to 4 percent, and Sonova's unit share at the United States Veterans Affairs reached 54 percent in July 2026, up four and a half points in a year.

The route to earnings per share. Sales growth of about 6.5 percent a year and margin gains of about 2 percent a year compound earnings per share at about 9 percent a year in francs on the Investment Analyst's modal path, from 10.34 francs in the year to March 2026 to about 13.40 francs by March 2029. Growth is faster in local currency, where earnings per share rose 16 percent last year but were flat in francs because translation took 221 million francs off sales. The named sources of upgrades are specific: the smaller Sphere device, year-two savings, the consumer exit worth about 200 basis points of group margin, and cheaper components; consensus has risen 4 to 5 percent on every horizon in six months with no cuts.

Three drivers to track. Wholesale share through the launch window, read monthly from the Veterans Affairs series; the drop-through of savings to a margin of about 24 percent; and the franc against the dollar and the euro, which translates both into reported earnings.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU'. The Investment Analyst's memo of 11 October rated Setups' Fit/Crispness B: the leadership change is complete and its four big choices were acted on within a year, but the outputs reported so far belong to the product cycle more than to the corporate change, so the pattern fits crisply on inputs and less crisply on maturity. My own reading adds a 'Demand-driven EPSU' alongside, from the artificial-intelligence upgrade cycle that patients pay for themselves. The memo's secondary pattern, 'CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal', fitted in May 2026 at 193 francs and 16 times earnings and has since been priced out.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple where it counts, exposed on price. The Investment Analyst's memo of 11 October rated Case Riskiness C, on a scale where A is simple and low-risk: one bet, three drivers and monthly indicators, with no red flag, no deceleration and no estimate cuts, but a price-side exposure that is not constrained. What needs to go right is the late-2026 launch window, the most crowded in years, with four of the five majors launching premium platforms at once and Demant's Oticon Zeal already having taken the custom category, where Sonova's value share fell from 61 to 48 percent. Sonova must ship the smaller Sphere in time for the Veterans Affairs procurement window in November and hold wholesale share through it; the trigger to re-run the case is monthly Veterans Affairs unit share below 45 percent, and the reassuring sign would be the compact Sphere recovering custom share.

A miss costs a de-rating. A lost window would take the multiple from about 20 times towards the ten-year tenth percentile of 16.8 times or the sell-side's bear multiple of 14 times, a 17 to 31 percent fall on unchanged earnings. Two headwinds Sonova does not control sit beside this: the franc, where about 15 percent of costs but 1 percent of sales are in francs and hedges run one to six months, so this year's guidance carries a 2 to 3 point profit headwind; and channel consolidation, where Amplifon's purchase of GN Hearing takes about 60 million francs of revenue from late in the 2027 year. The cochlear implant unit is small, fell 17 percent in the second half and awaits a new processor; treat its recovery as an option.

Business quality

High quality, with a lead that must be re-earned every two years. The Investment Analyst rated Foundations' Robustness B on 11 October. Gross margin is 73.7 percent, the normalised operating margin 22.5 percent and 25.4 percent in the second half, return on capital employed 19 percent, cash conversion above 90 percent and leverage 1.1 times, with a record dividend of 4.70 francs proposed. The moat is more than the chip: audiologists' familiarity with the fitting software, custom moulds, the Roger ecosystem in schools, in-house silicon and the clinic network kept total share rising through the very quarter in which the custom category was lost.

Capital allocation is the swing factor. The 6 billion franc ambition needs about 800 million francs of retail acquisitions at mid-teens margins, and the 1.5 billion franc buyback is paused to fund them, so group returns on capital fall even as sales grow. Against the new team's speed stand a 2022 profit warning, the failed consumer acquisition and a product quality rate that worsened last year.

Plausible returns and valuation

The return is an F at today's price. At 264.60 francs the Investment Analyst's memo of 11 October put the modal return at 8.5 percent a year over eighteen months and 9.4 percent over three years, on a 20 times multiple two years beyond each horizon plus dividends. Re-based by the weekly routine to 288 francs on 6 November, the TSR/Valuation rating reads 2.7 percent a year over eighteen months and 6.5 percent over three years, an F against the 20 percent bar, where the memo had pinned B on the multiple alone. The bull branch, with the window won and the market paying the ten-year 75th percentile of 23 times, gives about 26 percent a year at odds of about one in five.

Where the case clears the bar. The shares re-rated from about 16 times to 20.2 times two-year-forward earnings between May and October 2026, rose 48 percent in the six months to October and sit 2 percent below the 52-week high; the current multiple on FactSet is 19 times, the 30th percentile of ten years. The modal path clears the bar below about 227 francs on eighteen months and below about 200 francs on both horizons, where the shares traded between May and August 2026. The consensus target of 245 francs sits below the price and the buy share is 24 percent, so the sell-side has not yet followed the market; the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 5 November is B and the weekly Technical Momentum rating of 6 November is B.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Artificial-intelligence sound processing drives a premium upgrade cycle that the patient pays for, and Sonova owns the silicon, the software and a clinical channel: the Sphere took 52 percent of rechargeable receiver-in-canal aids at the Veterans Affairs within months at a 10 percent premium. Hearing-aid makers were the Sector's strongest cohort, up 41 percent in the twelve months to 22 October, Demant raised its growth guidance on 9 October, and the weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 6 November is B, up from the D pinned in the memo.

Cons. A launch premium lasts as long as the technical lead, which is 12 to 18 months, so the Sector Digest judges the hearing story known and in the price, and better bought after a fall. Retail consolidation is turning Sonova's biggest customers into competitors, with Amplifon absorbing GN Hearing and Demant integrating KIND's 900 German clinics, and part of the Sector's rise may be money leaving technology that can leave again.

In short

  • A quality business with real change: oligopoly leader, 73.7 percent gross margin, new team, perimeter narrowed, savings under way and wholesale growing three times the market
  • The price has run ahead of the proof: the re-based modal return at 288 francs is 2.7 percent a year over eighteen months, an F, because the multiple already assumes the launch window is won
  • One event decides the next year: the smaller Sphere must hold share through the late-2026 launch window, tracked monthly in Veterans Affairs unit share with 45 percent the trigger to re-run the case
  • The entry price is the lever: the modal path clears the 20 percent bar below about 227 francs on eighteen months and 200 francs on three years, so be ready for a fall rather than chase the high
  • Watch the franc and the channel: both sit outside Sonova's control and each can take points off reported earnings
