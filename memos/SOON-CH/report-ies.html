Sonova (SOON-CH): what the H1 2026/27 results do to our case

These results strengthen the business half of our case moderately, because the case's own launch-window test is passed on the first-half numbers, and they leave the price half, which is the whole reason for the park, no better, because the shares have risen about as far as the estimates have.

1. What changed

  • Wholesale. CHF 968.1 million in H1 2026/27 (six months to 30 September 2026, reported 12 November 2026), up 11.0% in constant currency against a consensus of 9.5%, the eleventh consecutive quarter of share gains. The case set a win at 7% or above and an Invalidate level below 5.0%.
  • Group. Sales CHF 1,834.1 million, up 7.5% in constant currency and 6.7% in francs, 1.0% above consensus. Retail CHF 748.6 million, up 6.5% in constant currency (5.2% organic). Cochlear Implants CHF 117.4 million, down 10.2% in local currency.
  • Profit. Core gross margin 74.7% from 72.4%. Core EBIT CHF 384.0 million, up 22.9% in local currency and 10.3% above consensus; margin 20.9% from 18.4%. Core EPS from continuing operations CHF 5.37, up 35.6% in francs and 18.8% above consensus. The case's modal path compounds EPS at 9.0% a year in francs, with CHF 11.10 for FY2027.
  • Currency. Translation cost CHF 13.7 million of sales, 0.8 points, in the half. Management expects the full-year effect to be broadly neutral on sales and core EBIT at early-November 2026 rates. The case assumed 1 to 2 points off sales and 2 to 3 points off core EBIT.
  • Cash. Operating free cash flow CHF 287.0 million from CHF 89.7 million; net debt CHF 1,072.4 million, 1.1x EBITDA at 30 September 2026.
  • Guidance. FY2026/27 sales growth 6% to 8% in local currency (from 5% to 8%); core EBIT growth 10% to 13% (from 7% to 10%); market growth assumption 3% to 5% (from 2% to 4%). A strategic investment step-up of a low double-digit million franc amount falls in the second half. Morgan Stanley reads the midpoint as second-half wholesale growth of about 5%; DNB Carnegie reads it as second-half core EBIT growth of 1% to 6%.
  • Competition. Sonova's VA share was 53.5% in September 2026, flat on the month; Demant's rose 50 basis points to 23.8%. In the rechargeable custom category Virto R held 43% and Demant's Zeal reached 28%. Demant's wholesale division grew 17% organically in the third quarter of 2026.
  • Sell-side. Five brokers moved FY2026/27 EPS to between CHF 11.56 and CHF 12.60, raises of 4% to 10%; the largest, DNB Carnegie's, partly adds back losses from discontinued operations. Targets run from CHF 253 to CHF 312, with a post-results median of CHF 270. No rating changed: four neutral, one Outperform. The common line is a beat and a raise already in the price.
  • Price. CHF 290.6 on 18 November 2026, 9.8% above the CHF 264.60 at which the case was written and 28% above the case's re-entry level of about CHF 227.

2. The challenge

The half that passed the test did not contain the test. The case made the first-half wholesale figure the judge of the crowded launch window. EON launched in August 2026 and the half closed on 30 September. The 11.0% was driven by Virto R, Infinio Ultra, the Costco return and only "early" EON, which are the previous cycle's tailwinds, and the case itself says they annualise in the second half. On this reading the window is still open and the early evidence leans against Sonova: Demant grew wholesale 17% in the quarter the window opened, Virto R's 43% sits below the 45% the case set as its custom-share Alarm level (the category definitions need matching before we lean on this), and the company's own guidance implies about 5% wholesale growth in the second half, below the case's 7% Alarm level, with the Amplifon loss already embedded. For this to be right, the guidance must be an honest estimate and not the conservatism Morgan Stanley and Bernstein assume, and VA share must start to slip. The case holds a "false friend" counter-case, but it tested it with this print. It has no answer to the possibility that its test fired early.

The earnings base has reset for reasons that have nothing to do with the window. The case said the return turns on one fact: the engine runs inside the band in local currency and below it in francs, and it called the franc drag structural. In this half the drag was 0.8 points and the full year is guided neutral, the gross margin rose 230 basis points, and EPS grew 35.6% in francs. If that holds, the case's FY2027 EPS of CHF 11.10 is plausibly 4% to 13% too low on the brokers' new figures, and the binding constraint the case named has loosened whoever wins the window. For this to be right, three things must hold: the gross margin must stay near 74.7% when the comparison no longer benefits from the prior year's footprint costs; the franc must stay where it was in early November, which is a rate assumption and not a hedge; and the second-half investment must stay at the size stated. The case's drop-through sensitivity runs downward only; it never priced this side.

The window may not be a contest at all. The case gives the window about even odds of being won, as if share were the only prize. Industry volume grew an estimated 6% in the third quarter of 2026 with price adding a point, underlying market growth was 5% in the half, and Demant, Sonova and GN all grew at once. A synchronised launch cycle that pulls replacement demand forward lifts every maker and makes Sonova's 11.0% partly market growth, which says less about share than the case's trigger assumes. It also carries a sting the case does not hold: demand pulled forward leaves a gap behind it. For this to be right, market growth must stay at 5% or more beyond the launch quarters, against channel reports of patients trading down and deferring. The case rates demand C, "not a swing", and scores a demand inflection D.

3. What it does to the case

  • 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1), B: less pressure, and enough of it upward that this Element would probably move. Margin, cash conversion (rated C, read as timing) and EPS growth in francs (rated C at about 9%) all improved sharply. This is a close call between a component moving and the Element moving; we judge the Element.
  • 'Change Forces' (E 1, BB 1), B: mixed. The franc (Macroeconomic, D) is under less pressure. Competition is under the same or more. Supply side is supported.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength, B: mild upward pressure. The case's reason for withholding an A stands: these are still mostly the product cycle's outputs.
  • 'Modal return', F, and with it 'Investment Thesis' (BB 1) C and 'Investment Case' (BB 4) C: the same. The earnings leg rose and the price rose with it. On the case's own unrevised figures, its modal value of CHF 299.0 is 2.9% above today's price, and its bull value of CHF 374.6 is 28.9% above, about 18% a year over eighteen months, below the 20% bar. Those figures need re-running upward for the new earnings; that is the memo's job, not ours.
  • Case Riskiness, C: more pressure on the price side. The case's lost-window levels of CHF 220 and CHF 183 are now 24% and 37% below the price, against the 17% to 31% it weighed. The fundamental side is cleaner.
  • 'Foundations' (BB 2) B and 'Investment Setup' (BB 3) B, maturity C: the same. Amplifon is tracking as the case sized it. Cochlear Implants, capital allocation without a published hurdle and rising retail lead costs are unchanged concerns.
  • The launch-window trigger. Met on wholesale; VA share stood above 50% in September, on a share measure that should be matched to the case's unit measure. By the case's own rule the bull branch's odds rise and the return is re-run at the prevailing price. The first challenge above is the reason to be slow about the first half of that sentence.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry the remaining brokers' estimates and targets and a settled consensus, so it can tell us how much of the upgrade is operating and how much is the discontinued-operations add-back. It will not carry anything about the second half, the window, or the margin. It can sharpen the earnings leg of the return; it cannot answer any of the three challenges.
  • The monthly VA series. October and November 2026 data are the first clean read of EON against Reveal and Zeal. The levels are the case's: total share below 50% for a month, custom share below 45%.
  • Demant's capital markets day and its next print, for whether 17% holds.
  • The FY2026/27 results in May 2027. These carry second-half wholesale growth against the implied 5%, the full-year core EBIT margin against the case's 22.0% and 24% levels, the gross margin without the comparison effect, and whether inventory, at 187 days from 163, has normalised as management expects.
  • The franc, daily; no disclosure is needed.
  • Not committed to by the company: the run-rate revenue loss from Amplifon, and retail lead costs or unit economics. Brokers ask for both; nothing obliges an answer, so these stay open in a different way. The US clearance of the Integro processor and any tariff outcome from the Section 232 investigation are outside the company's control and carry no date.
