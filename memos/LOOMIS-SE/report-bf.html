EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Business Model and Strategic Position

Loomis operates a highly decentralized cash handling model across three geographical divisions–Europe and Latin America, USA, and SME/Pay–managing approximately 400 branches spanning more than 25 countries, with Segment USA maintaining 178 branches covering the United States, Canada, and Israel, and Division Europe and Latin America comprising operations in 16 European countries and three emerging Latin American nations.

The company organizes its business into eight lines including Cash in Transit, Cash Management Services, ATM, and Automated Solutions, with core cash handling services generating approximately 60 percent of European segment revenues while Automated Solutions under the SafePoint brand achieved double-digit growth for several consecutive quarters. Loomis contracts typically span three years for standard secure transit and five years for automated technologies, providing high revenue predictability and consistent recurring revenue streams, with price adjustments integrated into contracts to protect underlying operating margins.

The company completed its largest historical acquisition by purchasing Hermes Transportes Blindados in Peru in October 2026 for approximately SEK 4 billion at a 6.6x adjusted EBITDA multiple, employing approximately thirty-four hundred people and serving one thousand clients, establishing a strong platform for growth in Latin America.

Financial Profile and Capital Efficiency

Loomis expanded its group operating margin (EBITA) to 12.7% in 2025 from 12.0% in 2024, with total operating income (EBITDA) reaching SEK 6,530 mm in 2025 compared to SEK 6,262 mm in 2024, while high route density in segment USA pushed regional operating margins to a record 19.2% in Q3 2026. Loomis achieved an improved return on capital employed (ROCE) of 16.3% in 2025 compared to 15.6% in 2024, further increasing to 18.1% as of September 30, 2026, while return on shareholders' equity reached 16% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 14.0% in the prior year.

Operating cash flow conversion remained solid with rolling twelve-month operating cash flow reaching 84% of EBITA in Q3 2026, with net working capital as a percentage of sales at 7.7% in the full year 2025 compared to 7.6% in 2024. Net debt to EBITDA ratio improved to 1.52x in Q3 2026 compared to 1.65x in Q3 2025, with total net debt reaching SEK 11,206 mm as of September 30, 2026, and stated dividend policy committing to distribute 40% to 60% of net income to shareholders, supported by Standard & Poor's reaffirmed BBB investment grade rating.

Industry Structure and Competitive Dynamics

The global cash logistics market was valued at 52.73 billion dollars in 2023 and serves as the foundational backbone of the financial system, with cash in circulation steadily rising in absolute terms in the United States and the Eurozone, ensuring the long-term viability of cash logistics operations globally. The market remains highly fragmented with local and specialized players controlling significant operations, as the top ten competitors globally accounted for only 9.66% of total market share in 2023, enabling larger players to acquire smaller local operators to expand density.

Loomis ranks as the second-largest player with a 1.32% market share, following Brink's at 1.56% and ahead of Prosegur Cash at 1.23%, with the combined market share of the top five global competitors at 6.45% of total market. Typical profit ceilings and floors reflect local market dominance and density, with Loomis recording an EBITA margin of 15.1% in Q3 2026 supported by strong performance in segment USA.

Value Chain Position and Bargaining Power

Loomis operates primarily in the cash-in-transit and cash management services stages of the secure cash value chain, with traditional transit services increasingly treated as commodity while the company positions itself as an integrated provider by expanding upstream into automated teller machine operations and smart safe subscriptions. Operating margins in the traditional cash transit segment are estimated in words to be in the high single digits, while automated retail solutions and smart safe platforms like SafePoint deliver margins estimated in words to exceed twenty percent because software-enabled safes reduce retailer labor costs.

Loomis maintains relatively high bargaining power over its hardware suppliers, with equipment manufacturers like Tidel and CIMA supplying standard smart safe units, while the company's substantial order volumes enable it to negotiate favorable pricing for hardware components. Large banking clients hold significant bargaining power over Loomis during structured request-for-proposal cycles, evaluating secure transit providers every two to three years and prioritizing pricing and service reliability, forcing Loomis to accept lower margins on traditional cash contracts and presenting a constant threat that can squeeze secure transit pricing terms severely.

Customer Concentration and Retention Strength

Loomis maintains a highly diversified client portfolio where no single customer represents more than 10% of total revenue, with financial institutions and retail enterprises comprising the core customer base, stabilizing group earnings because operations do not rely on any single counterparty. Loomis secures its automated solutions revenue through multi-year agreements that typically span five years, while standard cash-in-transit and cash management services agreements usually cover terms of one to three years, ensuring steadily increasing recurring revenues to the group.

Loomis installs SafePoint smart safes on customer store floors while maintaining full ownership of the equipment and controlling specialized software, creating high technical barriers to competitor conversion, as proprietary software is completely removed from machines if retailers terminate contracts. Loomis experienced a substantial contract expiration in Sweden when the shared banking network Bankomat insourced its operations, leading to a twenty-six percent revenue decline in Sweden during the first quarter of 2026, highlighting vulnerability of traditional ATM services to regional banking consortium decisions.

Management Quality and Strategic Execution

Loomis AB maintains clear focus on core market growth and product expansion through targeted transactions under Chief Executive Officer Aritz Larrea, successfully completing six acquisitions in 2025 to expand its geographic footprint and valuables logistics services, allowing the company to strengthen its market leading position in established countries. Loomis AB achieved a strong return on capital employed of 16.3% in fiscal year 2025 compared to 15.6% in 2024, further optimized to 18.1% as of September 30, 2026 through structured efficiency programs, with robust cash flow generation supporting the group's ability to reinvest in high return segments like Segment USA.

Loomis AB secures its primary technological advantage through direct vertical integration of safe manufacturer CIMA, placing proprietary smart safe technology directly onto customer store floors creating substantial barriers to competitor conversion because smart safe subscriptions create high customer switching costs due to training and software integration requirements. Loomis AB successfully exercises pricing power by passing wage and fuel cost increases directly to its customer base through automatic fuel fee matrices incorporated into stated contract terms, enabling the company to protect its underlying operating margins from inflationary pressures.

Disruption Risks and Long-Term Positioning

Secure logistics operations require massive capital investments in specialized armored vehicle fleets and regional branch networks to establish minimum viable scale, with relicensing and fleet costs preventing new competitors from entering the cash-in-transit market at a regional level, protecting Loomis AB from localized competitive threats.

Secure logistics providers face persistent long-term threats from the secular shift toward card and mobile payments, with acceleration of cash-to-digital payment trends representing a key risk to valuation multiples of cash handlers as contactless payments and online grocery shopping drive cash digitization in mature markets. Cash demand remains highly resilient in absolute terms despite rapid adoption of digital alternatives, with banknotes in circulation continuing to rise in the United States and the Eurozone due to economic uncertainty and consumer privacy concerns, as consumers value cash as a reliable fallback during network failures and cyber disruptions. Loomis expands its addressable market by targeting high-security, temperature-controlled logistics for the pharmaceutical sector, with the acquisition of Swiss-based specialist Kipfer-Logistik in 2025 significantly accelerating Loomis Pharma division growth, representing a natural extension of secure logistics capabilities and helping reduce dependency on traditional cash circulation volumes.

Most Important Strength and Weakness

The most important strength found for Loomis AB is its combination of highly diversified customer concentration where no single customer represents more than 10% of revenue, multi-year automated solutions contracts spanning five years that create high switching costs through proprietary SafePoint hardware and software integration, strong return on capital employed reaching 18.1% as of September 30, 2026, and proven pricing power through automatic fuel matrices and inflation indexation that protect operating margins from inflationary pressures.

The most important weakness found for Loomis AB is its dual vulnerability to sizable contract losses from banking consortium insourcing decisions–evidenced by the twenty-six percent revenue decline in Sweden during Q1 2026 when Bankomat terminated its contract–combined with persistent exposure to accelerated cash-todigital payment transitions in mature European markets that threaten traditional ATM and cash handling revenues, as declining cash transactions could push routing density below profitable levels before Automated Solutions can fully scale to offset volume losses.
