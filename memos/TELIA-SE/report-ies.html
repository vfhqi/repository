Implications — Telia (TELIA-SE) — Q3 2026 results, 22 October 2026

These results leave the case where it was, parked on price, but they strengthen its operating half modestly, bring two of the four re-engagement gates closer, and put one reading in front of us that the case does not hold: that the cost lever is repeatable rather than spent.

What changed

Telia reported Q3 2026 revenue of SEK 21.306bn, service revenue of SEK 18.016bn and adjusted EBITDA of SEK 8.872bn against company-compiled consensus of SEK 20.725bn, SEK 17.802bn and SEK 8.792bn: beats of about 2.8%, 1.2% and 0.9%. Like-for-like service revenue growth accelerated to 3.3% from 2.8% in Q2; adjusted EBITDA growth slowed to 2.9% from 3.4%. EPS was SEK 0.69 against SEK 0.65 consensus and SEK 0.60 a year earlier, but continuing-operations net income was SEK 2.703bn against SEK 2.713bn, so the EPS beat is not a recurring-profit beat of the same size. The case carried a management pre-warning that Q3 EBITDA growth would run below 2% because projects phased into Q4; the quarter delivered 2.9%, and the phasing ran the other way.

The composition is the point:

  • Sweden grew service revenue 4.7% (2.6% in Q2), about 2.1% above consensus, with mobile service revenue back to +0.9% from -1.0% in Q2. Management said some business-solutions and other non-connectivity revenue expected in Q4 was pulled into Q3 and put underlying Swedish growth at about 4% excluding that and legacy copper. Swedish broadband ARPU stayed below the prior year, roughly half of the dilution from Bredband2. Converged customers are about 1.2 million, 55% of the base, nearly 60% excluding former Halebop; each extra service cuts household churn by about half and lifts household ARPU by about 3%.
  • Finland: service revenue -0.2%, mobile service revenue -3.9%, adjusted EBITDA +0.9% on lower opex; Bernstein puts the miss at about 0.9% on both lines. Management said competition softened only slightly late in the quarter and declined to call a turnaround. Elisa's Finnish mobile service revenue fell 2.3% with postpaid churn at 18.7%.
  • Norway: service revenue +1.8%, adjusted EBITDA +0.9% after nine quarters of decline, about 10,000 postpaid adds against 2,000 in Q2; Bernstein estimates misses of about 0.7% and 1%. Mobile service revenue fell 0.3% on interconnect.
  • Other operations: adjusted EBITDA +10.0%, about 13% above consensus on lower group-function costs; this and the Baltics supplied the group EBITDA beat. Lithuania grew service revenue and adjusted EBITDA 6.4% each.
  • Opex fell 2.6% year on year; opex to service revenue 26.6% from 28.2%.
  • Free cash flow SEK 3.731bn, about 27% above consensus; cash capex SEK 2.847bn against SEK 3.201bn (about 10% below consensus); working capital +SEK 320m in Q3 but only +SEK 1m for nine months against SEK 1.051bn a year earlier. Rolling twelve-month FCF SEK 10.245bn, SEK 2.61 per share against SEK 2.05 of dividend paid. Net debt SEK 65.614bn; leverage 2.00x from 2.06x.
  • Guidance: 2026 adjusted EBITDA growth 3–4% from around 3%; FCF at least SEK 9bn from around SEK 9bn; service revenue around 2% and capex below SEK 13bn unchanged. Consensus already sat near 4.8% EBITDA growth and SEK 9.4bn FCF. The 2025–2027 targets were reiterated.
  • Announced 12 October: about 900 positions removed, SEK 1bn of annual gross savings, full run-rate from 2028, about SEK 600m of charges in Q4 2026 with the cash effect mainly in 2027; about 220 positions move from central Technology into country units; about 280 of the cuts fall in Finland. No gross-to-net reconciliation; management declined to change the FCF path for it.
  • Norway RAN: 50/50 joint operation with ice; operations early 2027, migration through 2027, build to 2030; regulatory discussions unresolved; negative cash effect in 2027, smaller negative in 2028, NOK 300–400m of annual benefit from 2029. Payment mechanics withheld.
  • Latvia: a second-half 2026 close is now unlikely; binding agreements unsigned. Capital Markets Day planned for the first half of 2027; no special dividend or buyback commitment.

Sell-side: Morgan Stanley called it a modest upside with next-twelve-month EPS modestly higher, Equal-weight and SEK 45 held. Bernstein called it solid but mixed, held Underperform and SEK 39.20 (raised from SEK 30.70 on 24 September) and left 2026 and 2027 adjusted EPS at SEK 2.13 and SEK 2.42. Deutsche Bank holds Buy and SEK 50, an upgrade from Hold and SEK 34 made on 15 October before the print, on 2027–2030 FCF raised 10%, 2027 FCF of SEK 10.7bn and 2028 FCF 4% above consensus; its argument is that consensus assumes less domestic opex reduction for Telia than for Telenor or Tele2. DNB Carnegie (Hold, SEK 51) had not published post-results.

Price: the dashboard holds the pre-results close, SEK 46.05 on 21 October, 1.9% above the 200-day average of SEK 45.18, above the 50-day (SEK 44.76) and just below the 150-day (SEK 46.16). P/E 16.1x at the 70th percentile of its ten-year history. Consensus target SEK 47.37; consensus EPS SEK 2.61. Pinned letters: technical momentum D, sell-side earnings momentum B, valuation D.

The challenge

Three readings: two the case does not hold, and one it rejected.

1. The cost lever is the operating model, not a one-off, and consensus does not carry it. The case's structural judgement is that a cost programme can be executed once, that the levers "have been pulled rather than are being pulled", and that longevity grades D because nothing is published beyond 2027. A second programme of 900 positions and SEK 1bn gross, launched before the first has fully lapped, with run-rate in 2028, reads differently: management describes a shift from rightsizing the centre to making the remaining organisation produce more per employee, and Deutsche Bank's argument is that Telia's workforce is large enough for this to repeat and that consensus assumes less opex reduction for Telia than for its peers. That is a named, mis-modelled consensus line, which the case said did not exist (E8.RA1: no specific consensus line is demonstrably wrong). If this reading is right, Telia is a productivity compounder whose repeatable driver is cost, the 2028 leg restores the ratchet the case says decays after 2027, and parking on price forfeits a leg the price does not yet carry, because management declined to capitalise the programme and the guidance moved only to consensus. For it to be right: gross must convert to at least SEK 700–800m of net recurring EBITDA, the review's bar, where two of three predecessors missed; the 220 localised positions must not recreate the duplication the 2024 programme removed; and the 2027–2028 EPS path must survive the SEK 600m charge and the Norway cash drag. Nothing in the print disproves this. The print does not yet test it.

2. The second programme is a confession, not a lever: cost is defending a revenue base that cannot carry it. This is the reading the case rejected when it adopted the risks report's park and dismissed its false-friend reasoning as an earnings-quality finding. The results re-open it with company-level evidence rather than framework argument. Adjusted EBITDA grew 2.9% while service revenue grew 3.3%: the incremental revenue came in below average margin, because it was business solutions, non-connectivity and television, and management said it rejected large tenders as uneconomic. The deepest cuts, about 280 positions, land in Finland, the market whose mobile revenue is still shrinking 3.9% and where Elisa is shedding postpaid subscribers at 18.7% churn. The Norway venture now costs cash in 2027 and 2028 before saving anything, and Deutsche Bank itself warns it makes ice a stronger rival. On this reading the case's "legible business at the wrong price" is too generous: the earnings are cost-held, and a re-rated memo would move not on price but toward the case's kill condition (organic growth below 1.5% for two quarters). For it to be right: Swedish Q4 growth must fall back toward the 2% guide as the pull-forward reverses; Finland must breach the case's minus 4% threshold; EBITDA growth must stay below service revenue growth; net savings must land well under SEK 700m. The first two are observable in January.

3. Convergence is a volume engine, and the case graded it as a moat. The case holds that there is no secular growth engine (E13 D), no volume engine beyond 2027, and that compounding is "entirely on the cost side". It knew convergence and graded it under moat (E10.RA1 B). The consensus-ignored reading is that 1.2 million converged households, each extra service halving churn and lifting household ARPU about 3%, is a repeatable mid-single-digit Swedish engine, management's roughly 4% underlying figure, and that with Sweden at 46% of Deutsche Bank's 2027 group EBITDA the 2% group guidance is structurally conservative. Two quarters at 2.8% and 3.3%, mobile back to growth, more than 100,000 mobile and 150,000 television additions since the start of 2025, and Bredband2 cross-sell at or slightly ahead of plan support it. If right, the growth half of trigger two is not a fluke and the secular-growth grade is the mis-specified one. For it to be right: Q4 Swedish service revenue growth of at least 2.5–3.0% without pull-forward commentary, the review's bar; mobile staying positive; broadband ARPU dilution stabilising as the Bredband2 mix laps; and Telenor's Bahnhof acquisition, lifting its Swedish broadband share from roughly 15% to 27%, not turning bundling into a price war. The counter is that penetration near 60% leaves a narrowing runway and that Swedish broadband ARPU is still falling.

Readings 1 and 3 are bullish and reading 2 is bearish, and the results admit all three. What discriminates them is the Q4 Swedish number, the Q4 Finnish number, and a gross-to-net disclosure the company has not promised.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry post-results estimate changes: Morgan Stanley's revision quantified, any DNB Carnegie post-results note, and whether Bernstein's SEK 2.13 and SEK 2.42 survive, plus a refreshed consensus target and P/E percentile, which is what the E3 question needs. It will not carry Q4 Swedish growth, Finnish Q4 mobile revenue, the working-capital content of full-year cash, or a gross-to-net savings bridge. None of those exists before January.
  • Q4 2026 print, January 2027. Swedish service revenue at or above 2.5–3.0% without pull-forward commentary (reading 3, trigger two); Finnish mobile revenue against minus 4% (reading 2); full-year FCF at or above SEK 9bn with working capital still near zero (trigger three); the SEK 600m charge booked; Q4 EBITDA growth against a base that now lacks the pulled-forward revenue.
  • Gross-to-net disclosure. The company has not committed to reconcile the SEK 1bn to severance, local hiring or duplication. The review's bar is SEK 700–800m net by Q2 2027. If management declines to give it at Q4 or at the first-half 2027 Capital Markets Day, reading 1 stays untestable and the case's base-rate objection stands by default. This is the question nothing is scheduled to answer.
  • Finland, Q2 2027. Two quarters of flat-or-better mobile service revenue is the review's bar for the Finnish half of trigger two; management's "no major trend shift" makes that unlikely by Q1.
  • Norway. A regulatory statement permitting operations by early 2027 without material remedies, and Lyse's 2026 report, the case's trigger four. Payment mechanics remain the undisclosed series.
  • Capital Markets Day, first half 2027. The shareholder-return framework at 2.00x leverage settles E20.RA1 one way or the other.
