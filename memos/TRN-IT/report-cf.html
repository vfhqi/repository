EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of change

Terna experienced abrupt CEO turnover in May 2026 when Giuseppina Di Foggia was mutually terminated and replaced by Pasqualino Monti, introducing execution risks during critical transition periods. The board was reconstituted with 11 out of 13 independent directors representing 85% independence, significantly exceeding Italian FTSE-Mib utility peer averages. The updated 2024-2028 Industrial Plan targets €16.6 billion in regulated capex through 2028, driving a projected RAB CAGR of 9% to reach €32 billion by 2028. Strategic restructuring consolidated non-regulated operations under Terna Energy Solutions, while completing acquisitions of Rete 2 S.r.l., STE Energy S.r.l., and a stake in Gyala S.r.l. to expand core infrastructure and renewable integration capabilities. Resolution 130/2025/R/com transitioned RAB indexation to Italian HICP, replacing the gross fixed investment deflator and strengthening inflation protection mechanisms.

Top ranked drivers of forward EPS

RAB expansion represents the highest-impact driver with 9% CAGR and high plausibility, contingent on supply chain stability and CAPEX delivery execution. ARERA WACC reset parameters could deliver plus or minus 20 basis points of return impact with medium plausibility, dependent on macro inflation and sovereign bond yield movements. MSD output-based incentives contribute approximately 110 basis points on RAB with high plausibility, subject to regulatory benchmark calibration. The ROSS totex model implementation is expected to generate roughly 2% EBITDA uplift with high plausibility, pending ARERA final implementing resolutions.

Nonregulated business mix expansion targets 11% EBITDA CAGR with high plausibility, dependent on Tamini and Brugg manufacturing capacity constraints. Work-in-progress under-remuneration creates a negative 20 basis points drag on RAB with high certainty, tied directly to commissioning timelines of major offshore projects.

Most recent financial delivery

H1 2026 results delivered consolidated revenue of €2,113.6 million up 11.6% year-on-year, EBITDA of €1,467.0 million up 7.9%, EBIT of €961.4 million up 5.3%, and group net profit of €591.2 million essentially flat versus H1 2025. Q2 2026 beat company-collected consensus by 4.7% on revenue, 4.1% on EBITDA, and 5.0% on net profit. Regulated activities generated revenue of €1,663.1 million representing organic growth of 4.3%, driven by RAB increases and €76 million in output-based incentives.

Non-regulated revenues surged 50.1% to €450.5 million, with energy services expanding to €181.4 million driven by STE Energy consolidation, equipment revenues reaching €235.6 million, and Tamini Group contributing €116.8 million on higher industrial demand. Capital expenditure reached €1,581.0 million in H1 2026 representing 19.8% expansion, with Q2 CapEx growing 41.3% to €1,069.6 million and surpassing €1 billion for the first time in a single quarter.

Forward guidance credibility

Terna expects FY 2026 revenue of €4.41 billion, EBITDA of €2.93 billion, net profit of €1.12 billion, and total capital expenditures of approximately €4.2 billion. Management represents a defensive honest tracker archetype, as Terna consistently exceeds its initial targets by minor controlled margins of 1% to 4% at EBITDA. The group consistently met or exceeded consolidated EBITDA targets from FY23 to FY25 by small controlled margins, with output-based incentives consistently providing high-quality earnings including €76 million in H1 2026.

The new multi-year industrial plan has been postponed from late 2026 into the first half of 2027, with the delay potentially allowing better integration of ROSS totex rules to improve forward visibility.

Sell-side positioning

Terna trades at a 3.0x PEG ratio for 2026E, significantly higher than the European TSO peer median and comparing unfavorably to Elia Group at 1.3x and National Grid at 1.6x. The stock trades at a 20% premium to its 2027E RAB matching the broader European TSO average premium, representing a slight increase over Terna's five-year historical average premium of 17%. Rating mix per Refinitiv consensus as of July 17, 2026 shows 30% Overweight, 55% Equal-weight, and 15% Underweight.

Morgan Stanley increased its price target by 9% to €8.85 but maintained Underweight rating, viewing Terna's valuation as extended at a 25% premium to RAB compared to peers. Jefferies maintained Hold rating with a price target of €9.80 post-results. Deutsche Bank highlighted positive evolution in incentive income, modeling a €10.50 price target with Buy rating.

Fundamental peer positioning

Terna's planned €16.6 billion regulated capex drives RAB CAGR of 9.3% over FY25-FY30E, exceeding Snam's 5.7% and Italgas' 3.3% organic gas asset CAGR. Terna achieves a 2027E RoRE of 13.5% which is well above the European regulated utility median, comparing favorably to National Grid at 12.5% and Elia Group at 7.9%. Terna's projected EPS CAGR of 5% through 2029E lags behind its asset expansion due to rising financing costs, with the stock trading at approximately 16x FY27E P/E which is expensive relative to peers on growth-adjusted terms.

Under-remunerated work-in-progress projects create a 140 basis points return discount relative to completed tariff-active RAB assets, weighing heavily on high-growth networks like Terna compared to mature lowgrowth gas distributors.

Activist presence

No active activist presence was identified in the AlphaSense library for Terna. Major institutional backing is anchored by CDP RETI at 29.851% and BlackRock at 5.041%, with the dominant public stake securing long-term strategic alignment and insulating the Board from short-term market pressures.

Material risks

Terna's Net Debt/EBITDA is projected to increase from 4.9x in 2026E to 5.9x by 2029E, while FFO/Net Debt is modeled to decline sharply from 20% in 2026E to 10% by 2029E, matching the minimum rating agency threshold required to maintain BBB+ credit ratings and creating potential downgrade vulnerability [Cross-ref: Section 4]. Global supply chains face critical shortages in specialized cables and power transformers with extended lead times, as only five to six global companies excluding Chinese players can manufacture and install ultra-highvoltage transmission cables, threatening major interconnection project timelines [Cross-ref: Section 3].

ARERA confirmed the regulatory WACC for transmission service at 5.5% real pre-tax for 2026, but the regulator's 30 basis points annual trigger mechanism recalculates returns if market interest rates or sovereign risks shift significantly, exposing Terna to potential downward WACC adjustments that could compress earnings trajectories [Cross-ref:

Section 3, Section 9].

Investment case verdict

The 18M-3Y EPS trajectory and 25%+ IRR potential remains MIXED, as record RAB expansion at 9% CAGR and robust output-based incentives supporting double-digit regulatory returns are fundamentally offset by rising leverage approaching rating threshold limits, persistent under-remuneration of work-in-progress assets creating 140 basis points of return drag, and stretched valuation premiums trading at 20% above forward RAB with limited margin of safety against potential WACC resets or supply chain disruptions delaying major offshore interconnection commissioning schedules.
