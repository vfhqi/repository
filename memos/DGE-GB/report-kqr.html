Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Evaluating the sub-sector's transitional stories reveals highly plausible growth trajectories across both spirits and nicotine houses despite near-term headwinds.

The fundamental question of whether European spirits and tobacco companies can deliver 25%+ internal rate of return opportunities over the next one to four years is resolvable with medium-to-high confidence across most core narratives, though critical data gaps regarding illicit channel volumes and final regulatory texts prevent absolute certainty.

The analysis targets leading spirits houses including Diageo, Pernod Ricard, Remy Cointreau, and Campari, alongside nicotine pioneers British American Tobacco, Imperial Brands, and high-growth pure-play online retailer Haypp.

The investment thesis centers on identifying predictable 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectories that can unlock compelling shareholder returns as destocking cycles normalize, next-generation nicotine products scale exponentially, and regulatory frameworks clarify. The spirits destocking cycle is gradually resolving as U.S. and China channel inventories normalize, with Remy Cointreau successfully reducing U.S. distributor inventories to four months and Pernod Ricard experiencing a narrowing gap to market sell-out in the United States.

Tariff resolutions are materializing as the abolition of US Scotch tariffs will boost Diageo's profits by approximately one point five percent, while Chinese authorities accepted minimum import price commitments to resolve anti-dumping duties on European cognac imports.

However, negative geographic and price mix remains the primary headwind to gross margin expansion, and lowering retail prices has failed to stimulate volume growth for seventy-two percent of key brands.

The total number of US oral nicotine pouch users is projected to increase from fifteen point eight million in 2025 to over twenty-five million by 2030, while Haypp Group achieved record organic revenue growth of twenty-seven percent in second quarter 2026, driven by tripledigit pouch volume acceleration in US and UK markets.

Nicotine pouches offer retailers up to six times the margin of traditional cigarettes on a percentage basis, creating powerful economic incentives for channel expansion.

However, illicit products have captured fifteen percent of the US oral nicotine pouch market, with organized networks successfully migrating distribution to sales sections of encrypted messaging applications like Telegram and WhatsApp. European regulatory landscape is fracturing as divergent member state positions delay harmonised excise tax and product standards frameworks through 2030, with the July 2025 Tobacco Excise Directive proposal contemplating a January 1, 2028 implementation date with a four-year transitional period extending to 2032, while the current Tobacco Products Directive framework excludes modern oral nicotine pouches entirely and the next revision is expected to impact European business by approximately 2030.

Policy divergence is stark, with Sweden approving a nine percent cigarette tax increase while slashing snus tax by twenty percent in late 2024, and the Czech Republic's Action Plan Addiction Policy advocating for risk-proportionate excise tax settings to motivate smokers to switch to less harmful alternatives.

Conversely, Belgium's outright bans on disposable vapes and nicotine pouches fueled a fifty percent illicit market, France enacted a ban on non-medicinal oral nicotine products starting April 1, 2026, and German regulators classify nicotine pouches as unauthorized novel foods, removing pouches from retail shelves while online supply persists. nicotine sales on encrypted messaging platforms like Telegram and WhatsApp, multi-national operators cannot predict final restrictions on pouch packaging and characterising flavours before draft publications in 2027, and finance ministries face uncertainty over harmonised minimum excise rates due to the required unanimous voting mechanism. 19 20F The forward-looking outlook for European spirits and nicotine houses over the next one to four years hinges on three critical inflection points materializing in sequence.

First, spirits houses must demonstrate sustained margin recovery as destocking completes in major markets by late 2026 to early 2027, with pricing power stabilizing as premium brands defend their positioning against private label encroachment.

Second, next-generation nicotine products must achieve penetration rates exceeding 15 percent of total nicotine spending in pioneer markets like the US and UK, validating the unit economics and demonstrating that modern oral can offset combustible declines without triggering punitive regulatory responses.

Third, regulatory frameworks must crystallize by 2028 to 2030 with risk-proportionate taxation structures that preserve manufacturer margins while channeling smokers toward reduced-risk alternatives rather than illicit products.

The investability of the sector depends critically on whether European policymakers adopt harm-reduction frameworks resembling Sweden and the Czech Republic, or punitive prohibition models like Belgium and France.

If the regulatory trajectory tilts toward prohibition, the thesis collapses as consumers migrate to illicit channels, eroding both legal volumes and pricing power.

The analysis demonstrates that the core investment question is resolvable for spirits destocking timelines and modern oral unit economics, which achieve high confidence based on robust Nielsen scanner data, manufacturer shipment trends, and retail economics.

Regulatory trajectories achieve medium confidence given the visibility into draft legislative timelines and historical precedents, though final texts remain uncertain.

The primary limitation of this analysis stems from the opacity of illicit distribution channels and the lag in private distributor inventory reporting, which prevent precise near-term margin forecasting.

Future research should prioritize expert calls with former distributors, field visits to key US ports, and continuous monitoring of European court rulings to resolve these critical data gaps.

The investment thesis remains directionally sound but requires active monitoring of regulatory developments and channel inventory dynamics to optimize entry timing and position sizing across the spirits and nicotine sub-sectors.
