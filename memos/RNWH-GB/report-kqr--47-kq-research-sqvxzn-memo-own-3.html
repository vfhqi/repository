EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Renew Holdings plc experienced a dramatic valuation journey since January 2024, with its forward price-toearnings multiple expanding to a peak of approximately 16x in late 2024 following the strategic disposal of its Specialist Building division, before collapsing to around 10.5x after severe rail renewal deferrals under Control Period 7 were announced in January 2025. The multiple subsequently stabilized at 12.3x for fiscal year 2027 as other regulated segments like water and power offset ongoing rail headwinds.

This trajectory reveals that Renew's ability to execute accretive acquisitions and compound earnings per share remains directly constrained by its equity valuation, with the compressed rating significantly limiting its traditional acquisition-led growth engine. The strategic portfolio distillation proved to be the primary catalyst driving Renew's late-2024 valuation peak. The completion of its transition to a pure-play engineering services provider through the sale of its Specialist Building division in October 2024 allowed the market to value the group's defensive core without construction drag, successfully driving the forward P/E multiple to peak at approximately 16x.

The concurrent acquisition of Full Circle for £51 million, which secured entry into high-growth onshore wind services boasting a 14% operating margin, further bolstered investor enthusiasm as the market rewarded high-visibility compounding growth in sustainable energy maintenance. This premium rating reflected investor recognition of regulated maintenance visibility over lumpy building projects.

The January 2025 profit warning triggered an immediate and severe derating that fundamentally reset market expectations. Trading momentum reversed sharply when Renew announced that its core Rail business was severely impacted by the slow start of Network Rail's CP7 cycle, resulting in significant delay and deferment of renewal work that dragged trading behind management's expectations. The operational challenges forced a substantial cut to full-year earnings expectations, with Deutsche Bank reducing the group's operating profit forecast by 9% and forward EPS projection from 72.11p to 64.21p. Equity market reaction was extremely severe, with Renew's shares plummeting by approximately 22% on the day of the announcement to close at 744p. This shock demonstrated that even regulated spending remains vulnerable to client mobilization delays.

Renew's relative positioning against UK infrastructure contractors compressed materially during this period.
