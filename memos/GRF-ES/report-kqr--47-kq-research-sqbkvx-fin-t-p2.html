Innovative Biologics and Clinical Disruption

1.
Encroaching competition: Argenx represents a significant long-term threat to the established plasma fractionators as its anti-FcRn therapy Vyvgart secures rapid adoption in autoimmune neuromuscular indications. Broker estimates from Rothschild & Co Redburn on 2 November 2026 project argenx group sales to exceed USD 14 billion by 2031, driven by Vyvgart's expansion into chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyneuropathy (CIDP) and myasthenia gravis. Neurologists are increasingly attracted to this targeted approach because it selectively clears pathogenic autoantibodies without broad immune suppression, which could eventually displace traditional intravenous immunoglobulin therapies in key patient cohorts. This clinical displacement directly threatens Grifols' neurology margins because neurological indications historically command premium net pricing.
2.
Targeted pipeline: Argenx is also actively developing its pipeline to address unmet needs in neurology through mechanism-based complement pathway inhibition. Reports from Rothschild & Co Redburn on 21 October 2026 highlight the blockbuster potential of empasiprubart (ARGX-117), an investigational monoclonal antibody targeting C2 that is undergoing Phase 3 testing for multifocal motor neuropathy (MMN). This asset is modeled to achieve peak global sales of USD 1.1 billion by 2035, serving approximately 13,000 patients globally. If successful, empasiprubart could reduce patient dependence on regular immunoglobulin infusions, thereby limiting long-term volume demand growth for established fractionators.
3.
Emerging pipeline: Dianthus Therapeutics is advancing a competitive subcutaneous complement inhibitor that could further disrupt the neuroimmunology franchise. Analysts from Rothschild & Co Redburn reported on 21 October 2026 that Dianthus is evaluating claseprubart (DNTH103), a long-acting C1s inhibitor designed for infrequent self-administration, in its Phase 2 MoMenTum study. This molecule shows a thirty-eight-fold higher potency in vitro compared to empasiprubart, offering a highly competitive therapeutic alternative. The introduction of such convenient home-based therapies could intensify pressure on hospital infusion revenues and force Grifols to offer larger volume discounts to preserve its accounts.

Contrary evidence: Skeptical academic neurologists are halting their clinical adoption of newer anti-FcRn treatments in CIDP due to suboptimal patient outcomes. This clinical friction suggests that biologics are not silver bullets and may only succeed in a finite subset of patients. This skepticism helps protect Grifols' core revenues because standard therapies remain the trusted clinical baseline.
5.
Pathophysiological divergence: Clinical experts highlight that the highly complex and heterogeneous nature of CIDP makes broad-spectrum immunomodulation superior to highly targeted biologics. A former global marketing executive at Takeda explained on 26 February 2026 that because the exact pathology of CIDP is not fully understood, the polyvalent action of immunoglobulin therapies allows them to regulate multiple immune pathways simultaneously. In contrast, targeted inhibitors like Vyvgart only block specific receptors, which restricts their efficacy to an estimated eighty percent of mild-to-moderate cases. This mechanistic advantage ensures that immunoglobulin remains the uncontested first-line standard of care for the majority of neurological patients.
