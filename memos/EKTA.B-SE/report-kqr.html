Section 3 – Currency Hedging and Development Capitalisation Changes

Section summary
  1. Elekta uses a rolling 24-month currency hedging program to buffer gross margins against krona volatility.
  2. Unlike Elekta, Swedish medtech peers Sectra and RaySearch choose not to hedge transaction exposures.
  3. The transition toward matching R&D capitalization with amortization reduces reported EBIT margins but improves earnings quality.

Hedging Framework and Margins

1.
Systematic hedge coverage: Elekta hedges anticipated sales up to 24 months using forward contracts to reduce short-term exchange rate volatility. The estimated positive impact of these hedges on the income statement drops from SEK 16 M in Q1 of fiscal year 2026/27 to zero by fiscal year 2027/28. This program ensures that short-term currency fluctuations are buffered before translation rates hit gross margins.
2.
Long-term hedging mandates: Elekta conducts currency hedging under a policy defined by the board of directors to mitigate transaction risk. The scope of this program is determined by ongoing currency risk assessments and targets anticipated sales up to 24 months. Consequently, this hedging policy aims to smooth shortterm exchange rate shocks but cannot alter structural currency trends.
3.
Impact of translation exposure: Elekta's translation risk is heavily concentrated in the US dollar, where a one percent weakening of the krona increases operating profit by SEK 18 M. Conversely, a one percent weakening of the Swedish krona against the British pound decreases operating income by SEK 27 M. These divergent sensitivities show that mixed currency exposures can offset the benefits of dollar-denominated contracts.
4.
Weakening translation impact: Elekta faces strong headwinds when the Swedish krona strengthens against the US dollar. During the third quarter of fiscal year 2025/26, adverse foreign exchange movements created a 130 basis points headwind on the gross margin. Thus, currency hedging delays but does not completely prevent exchange rate shifts from compressing reported product profitability.
5.
Positive hedge realization: Elekta reported realized results from cash flow hedges of SEK 135 M in the 2025/26 fiscal year. These gains are recognized in the operating profit under exchange rate differences. However, because the hedge book rolls off, a persistently strong krona will eventually dilute gross margins as older, favorable contracts expire.

Peer Exchange Rate Policies

6.
Immediate currency exposure: Sectra explicitly avoids hedging currency flows because it believes long-term currency gains and losses do not justify the costs. Consequently, any movement in foreign exchange rates has an immediate impact on Sectra's reported profit or loss. This lack of hedging means Sectra's earnings exhibit higher immediate translation volatility than Elekta's results.
7.
Unhedged software translation: RaySearch Laboratories follows a strict policy established by its board of directors to leave currency fluctuations unhedged. RaySearch reports that a ten percentage point shift in the Swedish krona against the US dollar affects its operating profit by SEK 36 M. This unhedged exposure directly exposes RaySearch to instantaneous operating profit shifts during rapid currency moves.
8.
Comparative currency sensitivity: RaySearch's operating profit is also highly sensitive to the euro, where a ten percentage point move impacts earnings by SEK 32 M. This contrasts sharply with Elekta, which seeks to smooth transaction risks through forward contracts. While peer models invite short-term volatility, they avoid the rolling hedging losses that can drag on Elekta's performance.

Capitalisation and Earnings Quality

9.
Deliberate accounting transition: Elekta is actively transitioning its R&D accounting to match capitalization with amortization in order to improve the quality of earnings. Historically, R&D capitalization exceeded amortization, which created an artificial tailwind for reported operating profits. By removing this tailwind, future EBIT improvements must rely on genuine operational gains rather than capital shielding.
10.
Historical capitalization benefits: Elekta historically used high R&D capitalization rates that reached up to 7.7 percent of sales to flatter its earnings. Jefferies Research noted in January 2022 that these accounting choices positively impacted reported EBIT margins by an estimated 400 basis points. This aggressive accounting practice means that prior operating metrics were inflated relative to actual cash generation.
11.
Shrinking capitalization rate: Elekta's high gross R&D spend of 12 percent of sales is expected to stabilize around 10 percent of sales by the 2028/29 fiscal year. The capitalization rate is set to decline gradually as the company enters a more normal phase. Bernstein Research reports that this stabilization will dampen reported EBIT expansion as fewer costs are deferred to the balance sheet.
12.
Drastic margin headwinds: Elekta experienced a combined 190 basis points negative impact on its EBIT margin during the first quarter of fiscal year 2026/27 due to lower capitalization and higher amortization. This headwind follows a similar 150 basis points drag in the third quarter of the prior year. Consequently, this accounting change depresses reported margins temporarily even though underlying operational efficiency is improving.
13.
Converging profit metrics: Elekta expects that matching capitalization and amortization will bring reported EBIT and cash EBIT into alignment. The gap between reported and cash-based EBIT has already shrunk from four percent to just 1.3 percent over recent years. This convergence means that reported EBIT becomes more relevant as a true measure of cash generation.
14.
Underlying profitability acceleration: Elekta's adjusted cash EBIT margin, which excludes capitalization effects, showed a strong sequential improvement of 170 basis points in the third quarter of fiscal year 2025/26. This cash-based metric highlights that the company's core profitability is expanding. Analysts watch cash EBIT to gauge true operational performance during the accounting transition.

Precedents and Opposite Readings

15.
Historical precedent case: RaySearch Laboratories serves as a key comparable case where a reduced effect from capitalized R&D improved earnings quality. RaySearch historically prioritized R&D capitalization, but a subsequent reduction led to a more conservative balance sheet. This transition demonstrates that restoring balance sheet transparency eventually supports a cleaner financial picture once near-term margins normalize.

This risk suggests that the margin recovery path could be longer and more painful than consensus models assume.

Other side of the story: Jefferies Research highlights on May 29, 2026, that the transition to matching capitalization and amortization does not solve Elekta's core issue of high gross R&D spend. Gross R&D remains elevated at 12 percent of revenues, which is high for a medtech company and continues to drain cash flow. Unless Elekta delivers substantial organic sales growth to dilute this expense, reported EBIT margins will struggle to reach the guided 14 to 16 percent range.

Section 4 – Chinese Recovery Dynamics and the Accela Competitive Risk

Section summary
  1. The Chinese procurement recovery represents a volume-driven expansion with increased pricing pressure.
  2. Varian's premium-priced Accela launch poses a significant long-term competitive threat but will not disrupt Elekta's near-term US Evo rollout.
  3. Local Chinese vendors led by United Imaging are rapidly capturing market share in radiotherapy.

Chinese Recovery and Sales Lags

1.
Volume-driven expansion: Deutsche Bank reports that the Chinese radiotherapy market is rebounding back toward pre-campaign levels with over 270 linear accelerator installations projected in the fiscal year 2026/27. It also notes that centralized procurement policies increase pricing pressure while expanding overall market access. To defend its market-leading position, Elekta is deepening its local manufacturing and R&D presence. Consequently, this volume-heavy recovery will expand market access but squeeze margins without localfor-local manufacturing efficiencies.
2.
Elongated delivery cycles: Elekta experiences significant lags between booking Chinese orders and recognizing reported sales due to post-anti-corruption regulatory environments. Morgan Stanley notes that China conversion lead times historically ran from six to 12 months, which was notably faster than the rest of the world. However, conversion processes have slowed down following the healthcare audit campaigns. Thus, despite order intake rising for three consecutive quarters, the extended backlog-to-sales conversion delay restricts near-term top-line acceleration.
3.
Competitive bidding dominance: Elekta maintains high commercial win rates in China's newly established centralized bidding procurement framework despite rising domestic competition. Morgan Stanley reports that approximately 25 percent of the Chinese radiotherapy market was conducted through centralized bidding in the 2025/26 fiscal year. Elekta achieved a win rate of over 50 percent in these centralized tenders. Therefore, the company's localized footprint secures robust public market share as state purchasing expands.
4.
Recovering order momentum: Elekta's performance in China is characterized by a stark divergence between declining current sales and strengthening forward order books. Jefferies outlines that net sales in China declined by 16 percent in the first quarter of the 2026/27 fiscal year due to weak legacy market conditions. However, strengthening order intake is expected to fuel positive sales growth in the second half of the year. Consequently, the Chinese market is transitioning from a headwind to tailwind as channel inventories normalize.

Varian Accela and Evo Competitive Threat

5.
Substantial technical specification: Varian's newly launched Accela platform holds a clear technical advantage in treatment speed over Elekta's newly released Evo CT-Linac. DNB Carnegie notes that Accela delivers a maximum gantry speed of 2.5 RPM and dose rates up to 40 Gy/min compared to Evo's 1 RPM and 22 Gy/min. However, because speed is only one element of hospital purchasing decisions, specifications alone may not alter the competitive field. Hence, Evo remains a viable alternative due to its online adaptive capabilities and lower pricing.
6.
Prohibitive price premiums: Varian's Accela system commands a massive premium that preserves market share opportunities for Elekta's more affordable solutions. DNB Carnegie highlights that Accela is expected to carry a potential price of USD four million to five million. This high price point makes the platform difficult to sell in price-sensitive markets. Consequently, Elekta's Evo can secure market share as a highly competitive lowercost alternative for capital-constrained clinics.
7.
Gradual revenue contribution: Varian's launch of the Accela platform at the ASTRO congress represents a long- term threat rather than an immediate danger to Elekta's US orders. Jefferies points out that Accela's commercial rollout will not fully ramp before the end of calendar year 2026. Due to installation lead times and production bottlenecks, meaningful sales contributions are unlikely before the 2027/28 fiscal year. Thus, Elekta has whitespace to build Evo momentum in the US market.
8.
Resilient customer pipeline: Elekta's US sales funnel has not experienced a pause in purchasing decisions despite Varian's competitive platform announcement. Deutsche Bank notes that customer feedback does not suggest any delay in orders ahead of the Varian launch. While the US market remains intensely competitive, Evo's FDA clearance provides a critical tool to compete. This resilience suggests that Evo orders are insulated from immediate competitive deferrals.

Peer Comparison and Local Challengers

9.
Exploding domestic competition: United Imaging has rapidly emerged as a primary competitor to Elekta's historic dominance in the Chinese radiotherapy sector. United Imaging reports that its new market share in the Chinese radiotherapy business increased by 18.1 percentage points in 2025 to take the top spot. Their radiotherapy business generated 591 million yuan during the period. This rapid rise reshapes the competitive landscape where Elekta was once the uncontested market leader.
10.
Severe scale disadvantages: Accuray struggles to maintain competitive parity against Elekta and Varian in China due to ongoing geopolitical and financial constraints. Accuray notes that its Chinese operations continue to face tariff-driven uncertainties and cash flow pressures. Although their local joint venture received Class B National Medical Products Administration approval in September 2023, P&L contributions remain slow.
