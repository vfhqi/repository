Pricing Power and EPS Vulnerability

2.
Pronounced earnings vulnerability: SKAN Group AG remains highly sensitive to equipment pricing because its lumpy project business operates on long lead times. During the 2025 fiscal year, project postponements in vaccine lines caused the Equipment & Solutions segment's EBITDA margin to decline from 11.2% to 4.3%. This vulnerability shows that any decline in equipment pricing power directly impacts earnings per share unless offset by high-margin services.
3.
Industry margin headwinds: Stevanato Group SpA demonstrates similar vulnerability as its Engineering segment gross margin fell to 11.0% for the fiscal year 2025 due to delayed projects. Additionally, China-based Tofflon Science and Technology recorded a depressed gross profit margin of 29.2% in 2024 due to domestic pricing competition and excess industry capacity. These peer developments show that loss of pricing power squeezes margins across the sterile manufacturing value chain.
4.
Contrary evidence: SKAN Group AG successfully implemented price increases of up to 15% on new project contracts to fully compensate for raw material and wage inflation. Management noted in the second quarter 2022 earnings call that the majority of customers accepted these adjustments, which mitigated the negative impact of supply chain bottlenecks on EPS. This shows that inflation pass-through is contractually viable under tight market conditions.

This regulatory complexity strengthens SKAN's equipment premium and safeguards long-term earnings per share.

Section 2 – Equipment Pricing Power, Turnkey Bundling, and Competitive Tenders

Section summary
  1. SKAN Group AG maintains high-end equipment pricing premiums of thirty to fifty percent due to its proprietary decontamination technologies and extensive regulatory validation.
  2. Intensive bundling from turnkey packaging line providers has reduced SKAN's process isolator market share from seventy percent to fifty percent as buyers consolidate procurement.
  3. Emerging competition from low-cost Chinese and North American suppliers exerts localized pricing pressure on standard sterility testing and generic manufacturing contracts.

Equipment Pricing Premiums and Success Rates

1.
Premium pricing: SKAN Group AG former chief executive Thomas Huber stated on the fourth quarter 2023 earnings call on March 26, 2024, that their isolators carry a premium of thirty to fifty percent over competitors. Competitor regional sales managers at Optima Packaging Group noted on June 7, 2024, that pricing differentials are nominal compared to premier European systems, though substantial against standard tiers. This suggests SKAN maintains exceptionally high equipment pricing power in specialized segments, which supports elevated gross margins.
2.
Technology advantage: SKAN Group AG utilizes proprietary eBeam decontamination technology which is widely recognized as the industry standard for high-speed vaccine and syringe filling lines. Former product managers at Stevanato Group noted on February 28, 2024, that regulatory mandates for rapid cycle times directly support SKAN's market leadership. This proprietary tech allows SKAN to command and protect high-end margins during competitive procurement tenders.
3.
Quotation success: SKAN Group AG maintained an overall quotation success rate of approximately fifty percent in fiscal year 2023 as documented in its annual report. Management reported in the second quarter 2025 earnings call on August 19, 2025, that the competitive win rate exceeded forty percent in early 2025 and improved to fifty percent by July. These figures demonstrate that SKAN sustains a high win rate despite charging substantial technology premiums.
4.
Backlog visibility: SKAN Group AG recorded an order backlog of CHF 346.1 million at the end of 2025, providing solid planning security despite delayed customer decision-making. This robust backlog suggests that SKAN's pricing power remains resilient during cyclical market corrections because clients prioritize long-term capacity reservations.
5.
Market metrics: SKAN Group AG and nine primary peers were assessed across eighteen distinct market sources to evaluate past capital expenditure trends. The historical record shows that SKAN expanded its market share during previous cycles because technological superiority shielded it from pricing concessions. This confirms that superior technology insulates product margins even when total market transaction volume contracts.

Turnkey Bundling and Market Share Shifts

6.

Major pharmaceutical buyers increasingly demand single purchase orders where filling machine makers bundle their own in-house isolators to eliminate integration liabilities. This shift toward complete systems erodes SKAN's historical dominance in custom aseptic lines.
7.
Bundling economics: Syntegon Technology and Optima Packaging Group offer comprehensive fill-finish systems that discount proprietary isolators when packaged with core machinery. Deutsche Bank analysts noted on November 13, 2026, that customers prefer integrated setups because single-source procurement reduces operational risk. These bundled discounts force SKAN to accept lower equipment margins or lose highvalue project tenders.
8.
Bundle discounts: Bausch+Ströbel and Groninger partner with specialized isolator makers like SKAN for complex customized projects but develop in-house isolator capabilities for standard lines. Optima Packaging Group engineers stated on May 24, 2024, that standard bundlers utilize pricing discounts on filling machines to offset higher third-party isolator costs. This practice pressures SKAN's equipment pricing when competing against fully vertical turnkey rivals.
9.
Contrary evidence: SKAN Group AG successfully won the majority of large fill-finish lines in the United States market during the first half of 2026 alongside its established integration partners. This shows that turnkey bundlers have not displaced SKAN's high-end equipment because major pharmaceutical developers still mandate best-in-class isolated systems.

These custom elements inflate nominal project values without yielding like-for-like price increases.

This historical case suggests that rapid complexity inflation creates execution risks for suppliers if custom engineering capabilities fail to keep pace with demand.

Section 3 – Project Contracts, Inflation Pass-Through, and Tariff Pressures

Section summary
  1. SKAN Group AG utilizes milestone-based progress billing under the percentage-of-completion method for its long-term project contracts to secure substantial customer advances.
  2. To manage raw material and wage inflation, SKAN implemented price increases of up to fifteen percent on new contracts alongside indexation clauses.
  3. SKAN mitigated 2025 US import tariff pressures by raising spare parts prices globally, presenting the adjustments as generalized cost increases without explicit surcharges.

Milestone Progress Billing and Currency Protection

1.
Milestone billing: SKAN Group AG structures its long-term equipment contracts using milestone-based progress billing under the percentage-of-completion accounting method. This structural framework allows the company to recognize revenue proportionately as project engineering and manufacturing steps are completed. These contractual terms provide financial stability by aligning cash inflows with extensive engineering timelines, which directly stabilizes intermediate earnings per share. Consequently, the company reduces cash flow volatility and safeguards its operating profitability during multi-year fabrication periods.
2.
Substantial advances: SKAN Group AG secures significant customer advances to fund its extensive work-in- progress inventory during the early design phases of customized projects. The company reported work-inprogress projects with a work-in-progress overhang of CHF 239.35 million alongside advance payments of CHF 165.37 million at the end of December 2023. These large prepayments minimize net working capital requirements, thereby protecting the company's return on capital during capital-intensive assembly cycles. This advance-billing structure ensures that customers finance the initial manufacturing stages, which insulates SKAN from liquidity constraints.
3.
Currency protection: SKAN Group AG actively mitigates foreign exchange volatility on its long-term project backlog by contractually denominating transactions in Swiss francs. Former chief executive Thomas Huber stated on the fourth quarter 2023 earnings call on March 26, 2024, that the majority of international customers purchase equipment using Swiss francs. For the minority of contracts negotiated in foreign currencies, SKAN utilizes financial hedges from day one to protect backlogged project margins from volatility. This currency discipline prevents exchange rate fluctuations from eroding projected equipment profitability.
4.
Sector benchmarks: Bernstein Research analysts reported on January 28, 2026, that the largest medical equipment companies generally maintain the longest contract durations and have been actively implementing catch-up pricing. During the post-pandemic period, equipment companies utilized these extended terms to gradually implement catch-up pricing increases of two to three percent. While these gradual adjustments help steady-state manufacturers, SKAN's custom, project-based contracts require more immediate and aggressive upfront pricing strategies to offset rapid material and labor fluctuations. This structural difference means SKAN faces sharper short-term margin risks during sudden inflationary spikes.

Inflation Pass-Through and Cost Management

5.
Gradual adjustments: SKAN Group AG successfully implemented substantial price increases of up to fifteen percent on new equipment contracts to combat historic material inflation. Management noted during the second quarter 2022 earnings call that the company raised baseline prices by approximately seven percent at the start of the inflationary cycle before executing fifteen percent adjustments on later orders. These proactive price increases fully offset rising raw material costs and helped stabilize the group's consolidated margins. This pricing flexibility demonstrates that SKAN possessed excellent pricing power during the post-pandemic supply chain crisis.
6.
Pass-through clauses: SKAN Group AG successfully integrated robust indexation clauses into its multi-year customer agreements to manage volatile input costs. Management reported on August 23, 2022, that new project contracts regularly contain inflation clauses allowing the direct pass-through of potential material and labor cost increases. These indexation mechanisms prevent historical cost calculations from eroding project margins over long-duration lead times. Consequently, these contractual additions protect long-term earnings per share against unanticipated macroeconomic shocks.
7.
Margin compression: SKAN Group AG experienced noticeable gross margin headwinds during the initial phase of the supply chain crisis. Management disclosed on August 23, 2022, that material and logistics costs rose from 26.1% to 29.8% of net sales due to global logistics bottlenecks. Although pricing adjustments eventually neutralized this inflation, the lag time in revenue recognition temporarily depressed the group's H1 2022 EBITDA margin. This historical pressure highlights that unindexed legacy contracts expose short-term margins to unexpected supply chain cost hikes.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some price-conscious pharmaceutical clients successfully resisted contract adjustments, forcing SKAN Group AG to deliver several legacy projects under older, unadjusted fixed-price terms. Former chief executive Thomas Huber admitted on August 23, 2022, that a minority of customers insisted on maintaining their original pricing schedules. This resistance demonstrates that contractual pricing power is not absolute when dealing with entrenched buyers, which can lead to localized margin dilution on older backlog items.
9.
Labor pressures: SKAN Group AG managed rising wage inflation by actively expanding its global workforce while absorbing localized labor cost increases. Chief financial officer Burim Maraj stated on August 22, 2023, that the net impact of inflation-related wage increases on the group's H1 2023 EBITDA margin was limited to approximately 0.8% percentage points. By insourcing steel fabrication and utilizing localized assembly sites, SKAN partially mitigated direct wage pressures on its bottom line. This operational flexibility helped preserve core project margins during a period of tight labor markets.

US Import Tariffs and Spare Parts Pricing

10.
Tariff headwinds: SKAN Group AG faced notable regulatory and tariff challenges in 2025 as geopolitical trade barriers escalated. Management confirmed on the second quarter 2025 earnings call on August 19, 2025, that isolators shipped to the United States from SKAN's German manufacturing facility are subject to a fifteen percent import tariff. This development significantly increases the nominal cost of SKAN equipment for North American clients. However, the lack of local high-end competitors limits the risk of customer defections.
11.
Strategic surcharges: SKAN Group AG successfully offset US import tariffs by implementing global price increases on its high-margin spare parts rather than applying direct surcharges. Management stated on August 19, 2025, that the company raised spare part prices generally, attributing the changes to overall cost inflation rather than itemizing a tariff line item. So far, the company has received zero negative feedback from customers regarding these price adjustments. This pricing strategy successfully defends consolidated EBITDA margins from localized import duties.
12.
Import liabilities: SKAN Group AG faces localized margin pressures because its North American subsidiary, SKAN US, acts as the direct importer of record for aftermarket spare parts. Management explained on August 19, 2025, that this structure directly exposes spare parts sales to prevailing US tariff rates. Because many critical components, such as sensors, are sourced from Asian suppliers, they face even higher tariff rates than European parts. To counteract this, SKAN must continuously optimize its global supply chain to source components from Swiss or German origins.

Section 4 – Macro Currency Pricing Power and Swiss Franc Volatility

Section summary
  1. SKAN Group AG mitigates Swiss franc appreciation by contractually demanding payments in Swiss francs for the majority of international equipment sales.
  2. Jonas Greutert leverages his Mettler-Toledo experience to implement standardized pricing frameworks that safeguard operating margins against exchange rate shocks.

Currency Pass-Through and Swiss Franc Protection

1.
Contract currency: SKAN Group AG successfully transfers currency appreciation risks onto its international client base by denominating the majority of its capital project transactions directly in Swiss francs. Former chief executive Thomas Huber confirmed on the fourth quarter 2023 earnings call on March 26, 2024, that most international buyers readily accept contracts in Swiss francs. This proactive contract denomination means that SKAN insulates its equipment order book from direct currency depreciation during periods of extreme franc volatility.
2.
103 104 - Earnings Calls Financial hedging: SKAN Group AG utilizes derivative instruments from day one to protect project margins for the minority of customer contracts negotiated in foreign currencies. The company reported positive fair values on outstanding forward exchange contracts of CHF 1.66 million at the end of December 2023 to neutralize euro, dollar, and yen exposures. These derivative contracts ensure that foreign sales maintain their projected values from the contract signing date through delivery.
3.
Contrary evidence: Heavy foreign exchange valuation losses impacted SKAN Group AG during fiscal year 2023 as the Swiss franc appreciated sharply against major global currencies. The company reported in its 2023 annual report that balance sheet valuations of assets held in euros and dollars declined as those currencies depreciated relative to the Swiss franc. This shows that while transaction hedging is highly effective, balance sheet translations remain vulnerable to unexpected currency movements.

Cost Base Mismatch and Margin Sensitivity

4.
Cost mismatch: SKAN Group AG experiences a notable operational mismatch because its primary engineering and assembly cost base resides in Switzerland while sales are global. Former chief executive Thomas Huber stated on the fourth quarter 2023 earnings call that a ten percent decline in the euro against the Swiss franc impacts SKAN's margins by approximately three to four percent. This sensitivity reflects the high portion of Swiss salary costs, which exposes operating margins to appreciation when foreign sales are translated.
5.
111 - Earnings Calls Operating leverage: SKAN Group AG uses natural hedging strategies to partially align its manufacturing cost inputs with its global sales distribution. The company actively matches cash inflows and outflows in foreign currencies within its German and Belgian subsidiaries to minimize translation risks. This internal balancing acts as a crucial buffer that neutralizes localized currency fluctuations before they affect consolidated group EBITDA.

Executive Leadership and Pricing Programs

6.
Leadership transition: SKAN Group AG appointed Jonas Greutert as its new chief executive officer effective January 1, 2026, to lead the company's next phase of global expansion. Jonas Greutert previously spent ten years in senior international management roles at Swiss precision instrument maker Mettler-Toledo, where he successfully raised profitability in the product inspection division. His background suggests he will implement systematic pricing strategies to protect margins from currency shocks in the future.
7.

Jefferies analysts reported on March 19, 2026, that Mettler-Toledo's unique direct sales structure serves as a primary P&L insulator against macro headwinds. This precedent suggests that Greutert may introduce a similar, value-driven pricing program at SKAN to capture consistent margin gains.
8.
Systematic adjustments: Mettler-Toledo International Inc demonstrated aggressive pricing agility by raising its pricing contribution from two hundred basis points to three hundred and fifty basis points in late 2025. Morgan Stanley analysts noted on December 1, 2025, that these adjustments were implemented alongside supply chain optimization programs to protect operating margins. This operational strategy provides a blueprint for how Jonas Greutert can enhance SKAN's pricing framework to offset Swiss franc strength.

Peer Resilience and Competitor Comparisons

Deutsche Bank analysts reported on August 14, 2025, that unfavorable currency fluctuations were projected to erode Tecan's adjusted EBITDA margins by eighty to one hundred basis points in 2025. Aftermarket Revenue and Pricing Trends

Sustainable growth: SKAN Group AG expanded its Services & Consumables net sales by 47.2% to CHF 64.6 million in the first half of 2026 from CHF 43.9 million in the prior-year period. This rapid expansion was driven by organic demand for services and the consolidation of recent acquisitions. This structural shift ensures that high-margin recurring revenue reduces total earnings volatility over time.
2.
Pricing agility: SKAN Group AG successfully implemented price increases of seven to fifteen percent since the 2022 inflationary cycle to offset raw material and wage pressures. Management reported during the second quarter 2025 earnings call on August 19, 2025, that global spare parts prices were raised further to counteract tariff headwinds without receiving negative customer feedback. These proactive adjustments demonstrate that aftermarket pricing power remains highly resilient across diverse macroeconomic conditions.
3.
Installed base leverage: SKAN Group AG leverages its growing installed base of over 1,100 isolator systems to generate stable, regulatorymandated requalification and maintenance revenues. Because pharmaceutical regulations mandate annual validation of sterile equipment, customers have no choice but to purchase certified parts and services. This strict compliance framework safeguards steady-state operating margins even during broader capital expenditure slowdowns.

Consumables and Competitive Dynamics

Specialized vials: SKAN Group AG increased its stake in Aseptic Technologies to ninety percent in June 2024 to capture rapid growth in Crystal Closed Vial Technology. These automated filling solutions are highly valued because they preserve drug sterility and are approved for eighteen medications globally. While Sartorius AG represents the standard for annual price increases on validated biotech consumables, SKAN's proprietary closed vials enjoy similar price inelasticity among biological drug developers.
8.
System integration: SKAN Group AG integrates ABC Transfer ports and Aseptic Technologies closed vials directly into its custom and standard isolator lines. This closed ecosystem prevents third-party consumable substitution, giving SKAN absolute pricing control over the lifecycle of each installed fill-finish line. Consequently, this proprietary integration insulates the consumables business from competition and supports high, steady gross margins.

Pre-Approved Services and Maeven AG

Maeven AG supports biopharmaceutical customers by managing technology transfer, process development, and validation testing for new drug formulations. By utilizing pre-validated systems, drug developers can significantly shorten their time-to-market, creating high customer willingness to pay.

Management stated during the Capital Markets Day in May 2025 that these services will serve as a primary accelerator for group margin expansion. This high-margin service model leverages existing equipment investments to capture value directly from clinical-stage pharmaceutical budgets.
11.
Regulatory timeline: SKAN Group AG plans to initiate commercial production at Maeven AG in the second half of 2026 following cleanroom validation. The company completed the interior construction of the infrastructure and targets Swissmedic GMP approval in late 2026. This milestone is critical because Swissmedic approval unlocks commercial revenue streams and establishes a validated pricing benchmark for pay-per-use aseptic services.
12.
Premium value: SKAN Group AG registers strong prospective customer interest in Maeven AG because traditional aseptic line qualification is incredibly slow. Pharmaceutical developers face intense competitive pressure to launch biologics and are willing to pay significant premiums to bypass internal validation bottlenecks. This willingness to pay validates SKAN's premium service pricing and ensures high capacity utilization once operational.

Because SKAN maintains a dominant technological position, it commands high pricing leverage on these long-term North American projects.
