Section 2 – Financing Structure and Net Interest Expense

Section summary
  1. Bechtle's net interest expense will rise sharply through 2028, driven by the €450m Schuldschein placed on 30 June 2026, which structurally alters its historical low-debt financing model.

The Schuldschein and H2 2026 Impact

1.
Increased interest burden: Bechtle AG successfully placed a €450m Schuldschein loan on 30 June 2026, which was structured across six tranches spanning maturities of three, five, and seven years with both fixed and floating interest rates. This transaction represents Bechtle's second Schuldschein issuance since 2018, with the final volume upsized from an initial target of €250m to €450m due to strong national and international investor demand. The company motivated this funding measure as a strategic tool to strengthen financial flexibility and provide dedicated liquidity for its international M&A strategy.
2.
Validated interest estimates: Broker research from Jefferies on 28 July 2026 estimated that the €450m Schuldschein would generate approximately €5m of incremental net interest expense for Bechtle in the second half of 2026. This projection is strongly supported by Bechtle's actual Q3 2026 financial statements published on 13 November 2026, which revealed that quarterly financial expenses rose to €11.78m in Q3 2026 compared to €6.73m in Q3 2025. This represents an increase of €5.04m in Q3 financial expenses alone, confirming that the Schuldschein's interest drag is materializing rapidly.

Projections for 2027 and 2028

3.
Structurally higher expenses: Bechtle's net interest expense for the full years of 2027 and 2028 will remain elevated as the full-year effect of the Schuldschein tranches is combined with the interest from its €279m convertible bond. The convertible bond, which was placed in December 2023 with a total nominal value of €300m and a carrying value of €279.18m as of 31 December 2025, carries a nominal interest rate of 2.0% and is accounted for using the effective interest method. Consequently, Bechtle's annual interest paid on financing activities, which stood at €14.48m for the first nine months of 2026 compared to €13.19m in the prior-year period, is expected to exceed €25m annually in 2027 and 2028.
4.
Lease interest components: Under IFRS 16, Bechtle's lease liabilities also represent a recurring interest drag, with the interest portion of lease payments recognized directly within financial earnings. Bechtle's total lease liabilities fell due with undiscounted minimum lease payments of €73.69m as of 30 June 2026, down from €77.58m on 31 December 2025. These lease interest expenses combined with the Schuldschein and convertible bond coupon payments will limit Bechtle's net profit margin recovery, even if operating margins improve under the 2030 corporate ambitions.

Section 3 – Tax Rate Drivers and Convertible Bond Dilution

Section summary
  1. Bechtle's effective tax rate is expected to settle between 28% and 30% over the next four years, driven by international profit mix and German trade taxes.
  2. Dilution risks from the €279m convertible bond are low in the near term because the conversion price of €54.99 is significantly above current trading levels.

Tax Rate Trends and Operational Drivers

1.
High tax: Bechtle's effective tax rate has risen steadily over the years, reaching 29.6% in 2025 from 27.9% in 2019. The actual tax expense of €95.87m in 2025 was driven by €4.75m in non-deductible expenses and €1.35m in tax increases from controlled foreign company (CFC) rules. This rising tax burden directly reduces net income and pressures future earnings per share unless operational efficiencies can offset the domestic tax rate.
2.
p Bechtle AG's tax expense reconciliation for fiscal years 2024 and 2025 Rising international: Bechtle faces shifts in its weighted domestic and foreign expected tax rate, which rose to approximately 28.0% in 2025 from 27.7% in 2024. This trend reflects Bechtle's expanding international footprint across 14 European countries, where varying tax jurisdictions alter the consolidated expected tax rate. Unless foreign profits shift toward lower-tax regions, geographical mix will continue to act as an upward driver of Bechtle's effective tax rate.
3.
Contrary evidence: Bechtle's effective tax rate trend could settle lower if deferred tax adjustments or historical tax credits continue to offset operational tax increases, as seen in the 2025 annual report where a negative €3.46m tax rate change for deferred taxes was recorded. If these non-recurring tax benefits recur or foreign tax rates decline, Bechtle's tax rate could settle below 28.0% instead of rising further.
4.
Stable baseline: Bechtle's underlying tax rate is anchored by the domestic corporate tax environment, which has remained stable but elevated. The expected tax rate reflects the blend of German and foreign jurisdictions where Bechtle's 120 decentralised system houses operate. This operational decentralisation means that local tax rates directly influence the consolidated tax rate, making localized tax planning crucial for EPS protection.
5.
Robust regulation: Bechtle's tax rate will not face immediate upward pressure from OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax rules because its effective tax rates in relevant jurisdictions exceed 15%. Management's review of residence jurisdictions in 2025 concluded that safe harbor rules or local top-up taxes were sufficient to avoid any additional minimum tax expense. This regulatory safety protects the group from supplementary tax drags through 2028, securing the baseline EPS forecast.

This illustrates that localized European losses without tax relief can dramatically inflate a competitor's group-wide effective tax rate.

The group's tax rate fell because the geographical profit mix shifted increasingly toward the United States, which has a lower blended federal and state tax rate of 25%. This demonstrates how geographic profit reweighting can rapidly reverse upward tax drift and support diluted EPS.

This blended domestic rate includes corporate income tax, trade tax, and the solidarity surcharge. Management excluded this future rate reduction from deferred tax valuations because its quantitative impact remains small. This tax policy outlook suggests that Bechtle will also see limited immediate benefit from German tax rate changes before 2028.

Convertible Bond Specifications and Terms

10.
Strategic funding: Bechtle's convertible bond issued in 2023 was a low-coupon financing alternative to traditional bank debt before the €450m Schuldschein was placed in 2026. This strategic financing choice allowed Bechtle to lock in a low 2.0% coupon rate, reducing the short-term interest expense relative to market interest rates. However, this low-interest benefit comes with the trade-off of long-term equity dilution once the share price reaches the conversion threshold.
11.
Latent dilution: Bechtle's financing structure contains a €300m nominal convertible bond issued in 2023 with a six-year maturity due in 2029. The carrying value of this financial liability stood at €279.18m at the end of 2025, up from €275.28m in 2024. This unsecured and unsubordinated bond creates a recurring effective interest expense that reduces annual net profit until conversion or redemption.
12.
High premium: Bechtle's convertible bond terms specify an initial conversion price of €54.99, which represents a 30% premium over the €42.30 reference share price. The high conversion premium means that no share dilution can occur unless the market price of Bechtle shares surpasses €54.99. Consequently, the convertible bond remains out-of-the-money at current market levels, protecting short-term EPS from dilution.

Dilution Mechanics and EPS Impact

13.
Diluted expansion: The total share count effect from Bechtle's €300m convertible bond would involve issuing approximately 5.46m new no-par-value shares if fully converted. This potential conversion represents a 4.3% dilution of Bechtle's total outstanding shares, based on a nominal value of €100k per bond. Investors must monitor this latent dilution because it will structurally reduce diluted earnings per share once the share price recovers.
14.
Mitigated dilution: Under IFRS reporting, Bechtle's basic share count remains unaffected by the convertible bond while the share price is below €54.99. However, if the share price exceeds €54.99, diluted EPS will reflect the full share count expansion, partially mitigated by the add-back of saved interest expenses. This double effect means that the EPS dilution will be smaller than the raw 4.3% share count expansion suggests.
15.
Other side of the story: Although Bechtle's high convertible bond price of €54.99 limits short-term dilution risk, the company's rising tax rate trend represents a persistent operational drag. Computacenter's severe 34.1% tax rate spike in 2025 shows that localized profitability declines can rapidly inflate effective tax rates. If Bechtle's international entities experience similar losses or German corporate taxes remain high, the effective tax rate will settle above 29.5%, dragging down EPS.

Estimated profit headwind: Bechtle's intensive investment in its internal IT infrastructure and its ongoing corporate migration to SAP S/4HANA represent a material drag on short-term profitability. This ongoing headwind indicates that the company's guidance for a stable operating margin is not solely a consequence of a difficult macroeconomic demand environment, but rather reflects conscious, heavy reinvestment into its own operational future-readiness. The 2025 Capitalisation Accounting Shift

2.
Stark capitalization drop: Bechtle recorded an abrupt change in the balance sheet treatment of its software development costs during the 2025 fiscal year. The Bechtle Annual Report 2025 stated that capitalized internally generated software additions plunged to just €1.75 million in 2025, representing a massive decline from the €29.97 million capitalized in the 2024 fiscal year. Although the company did not provide an explicit narrative explanation for this steep year-on-year drop in capitalized software within its main financial commentary, the accounting shift fundamentally alters how development costs hit the consolidated income statement.
3.
Surging work in progress: A significant portion of Bechtle's internal development spending was re-routed into alternative balance sheet line items rather than being capitalized as finished internally generated software. The Bechtle Annual Report 2025 revealed that the balance sheet item "advance payments and software under development" surged to €25.51 million at the end of 2025, compared to just €9.03 million at the end of 2024. This shift suggests that while total development activity remained high, a vast majority of the projects had not yet met the strict criteria for completed software capitalization under IAS 38, leaving them parked as work-inprogress assets.
4.
Strict capitalisation criteria: Under Bechtle's established accounting policies, internally generated software is capitalized only when specific criteria are fully satisfied, including technical feasibility, clear marketability, expected future economic benefits, and a defined intent for internal use or sale. The Bechtle Annual Report 2025 explained that any development costs incurred before achieving technical feasibility are immediately recognized as research expenses in the period they occur. Given that Bechtle incurred approximately €20 million of organic development expenditures in 2025 across various internal and customer-facing application solutions, only a small fraction ultimately qualified for formal capitalization during the year.

Impact on Operating Expenses and Margins

5.

The Bechtle Annual Report 2025 noted that administrative expenses increased by 10.4% from €382.90 million in 2024 to €422.70 million in 2025, driven in part by higher expenses for the company's own internal IT systems. This disproportionate cost growth caused Bechtle's administrative expense ratio to climb from 6.1% in 2024 to 6.6% in 2025, directly suppressing the group's consolidated operating margin.
6.
Lagging amortization patterns: The financial headwind from expensing current development is compounded by the fact that historical software capitalization continues to generate substantial amortization. The Bechtle Annual Report 2025 showed that amortization of internally developed software actually rose to €10.76 million in 2025, up from €5.93 million in 2024, despite the record-low capitalization additions in 2025. This means Bechtle is currently absorbing a double negative: it faces higher direct operating expenses from expensing current IT work, while still carrying the amortization burden of past capitalized projects.
7.
Persistent margin pressure: The combination of active S/4HANA migration costs and expensed software development continues to weigh on quarterly results well into 2026. Bechtle's Q3 2026 earnings release highlighted that the company has continued to make heavy, unquantified investments in the future-readiness of its own IT and in productivity improvements. CFO Christian Jehle noted that keeping margins largely stable in Q3 2026 was a major achievement, confirming that internal IT investments remain a persistent hurdle to absolute margin expansion.

  2. The divergence between business volume and IFRS revenue will continue to widen as the corporate sales mix shifts structurally toward software and cloud.

models reveal similar software-to-revenue dynamics, but Bechtle's high hardware exposure provides a partial buffer against industry-wide software take-rate compression.

Accounting Mechanics and Trends

1.
Significant structural divergence: Under the provisions of IFRS 15, Bechtle recognizes its standard software revenue on a net commission basis, whereas hardware sales are recognized on a gross basis. This specific accounting treatment means that the difference between business volume and reported revenue represents the effective pass-through of software products. Consequently, when software demand expands faster than hardware, reported revenue growth lags behind business volume growth. 128 & Co Redburn This accounting gap lowers reported revenue but structurally elevates Bechtle's gross margin percentage.
2.
Pronounced historical gaps: Bechtle's reported business volume grew by a solid 8.1% to reach €8,596.10 million in the 2025 fiscal year. Elevated financing burdens: Bechtle AG's interest expenses could escalate beyond consensus expectations if the floating tranches of its newly issued €450m Schuldschein loan face prolonged high benchmark rates. This persistent drag would directly suppress diluted earnings per share through 2028.

Delayed cash recovery: Bechtle's projected free cash flow recovery remains highly sensitive to the timely unwind of its €667.20m inventories.
