Outliers worth noting 2 findings

Healthcare volume renewal outlier: The Healthcare segment's volume renewal rate of 78% in FY25 represents a material performance outlier that falls well below the group consolidated rate of 83% and significantly trails the Non-Healthcare division's 85% renewal rate, exposing concentrated retention friction within the firm's highestmargin and most strategically valuable business segment that generated 50% EBITDA margins. This Healthcare weakness is particularly concerning given that elite customers spending above £100,000 annually achieve renewal rates of 96%, suggesting the retention pressure concentrates disproportionately among mid-tier pharmaceutical accounts where GlobalData struggles to displace entrenched vertical specialists despite operating as the purported market leader in pharma intelligence. minority stake valuation anomaly: The Inflexion transaction achieved an operational EV/EBITDA multiple of 22x for the 40% Healthcare minority stake sale, representing an extreme premium valuation that sits at the very top end of comparable business information services transactions and substantially exceeds the trading multiples applied to the consolidated group equity. This valuation outlier reflects the Healthcare platform's exceptional 50% EBITDA margin profile compared to the Non-Healthcare segment's depressed 25% margin, confirming a dramatic bifurcation in asset quality and competitive positioning between the firm's life sciences franchise and its generalist consumer, technology, and energy divisions that struggle against dominant vertical incumbents

Working capital deterioration outlier: GlobalData's average trade debtor collection period lengthened severely from 58 days in 2023 to 68 days in 2024 before partially recovering to 63 days in 2025, representing a 10-day extension that signals a meaningful shift in customer payment behavior despite standard contract terms mandating full upfront payment before platform access is granted. This debtor day deterioration persists notwithstanding the subscription-advance payment model, suggesting clients are successfully negotiating extended payment terms or delaying invoice settlements amid macro budget pressures, which directly contradicts management's narrative of strong pricing power and represents a leading indicator of potential downstream margin compression as enterprise customers exploit their growing bargaining leverage during contract renewals and commercial reorganization cycles.
