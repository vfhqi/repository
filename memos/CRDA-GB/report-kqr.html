Section 2 – Currency Translation and Net Finance Costs

Section summary
  1. Unhedged currency translation and floating rate debt exposure will remain the primary macroeconomic drivers of Croda's short-term earnings per share.
  2. Recent central bank interest rate hikes and volatile exchange rate forecasts continue to prompt minor adjustments in sell-side financial models.
  3. Peer comparisons reveal that disciplined hedging and geographical natural hedges provide greater margin predictability than Croda's unhedged structure.

Foreign Exchange Sensitivity

1.
Vulnerable translation: Croda operates with severe exchange rate sensitivity because 97% of its sales originate outside the United Kingdom. The company estimates that every single cent movement in the US Dollar or Euro impacts annual adjusted operating profit by £1m. This unhedged translation risk means that fluctuations in major global currencies directly dictate earnings per share.
2.
Shifting guidance: Croda initialised its fiscal year 2026 foreign exchange guidance in February 2026 with a predicted negative impact of £8m on operating profit. By November 2026, the company adjusted this projected translation headwind down to approximately £4m based on prevailing exchange rates. This demonstrates how volatile exchange rate environments can rapidly alter short-term corporate profitability profiles.
3.
Stable translation: Givaudan faces similar challenges with the strength of the Swiss franc acting as a persistent headwind to revenue growth. However, the Swiss company relies on its wide geographical spread as a natural hedge to protect its EBITDA margins. This contrast shows that geographic diversification mitigates translation risk even when formal financial hedging is not actively deployed.
4.
Contrary evidence: Givaudan actually suffered severe currency headwinds when it overhedged its US Dollar revenue exposure, leading to significant margin downgrades. The company had to change its treasury approach and communicate a clearer hedging policy to reassure investors after these execution errors in September 2022. This shows that active hedging programs can increase risk if executed poorly by corporate treasury departments.
