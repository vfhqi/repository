Section 3 – Automation Segment Margins and Stranded Central Costs

Section summary
  1. Standalone margins in the remaining Automation business face dilution from unallocated corporate costs following the carve-out.
  2. Structural demand drivers for Automated Machinery are supported by an aging global tire-building machine fleet.
  3. Chinese machine vision competitors are aggressively capturing market share in price-sensitive entry-level segments.
  4. High-end software licensing represents the key growth lever for standalone vision technology profitability.

Standalone Costs and Corporate Overhead

1.
Diluted segment profitability: TKH Group's medium-term targets for the remaining Automation segment outline an Adjusted EBITA margin of 17% to 19% by fiscal year 2028. However, a footnote in the TKH Group annual report of 7 July 2026 notes that these segment targets explicitly exclude unallocated support and head-office costs. Consequently, the standalone profitability of the Automation business will likely be lower than reported margins unless central overhead is radically reduced.
2.
Heavy corporate overhead: The reported segment results show a significant gap between divisional profits and group-level earnings. According to the TKH Group interim report of 1 September 2026, unallocated support and eliminations depressed first-half 2026 Adjusted EBITA by €12.96 million. This central cost burden suggests that a standalone Automation entity must absorb a substantial portion of historical group-level overhead expenses.
3.
Elevated transition risks: Separating the corporate functions will require complex transitional service agreements that delay cost-saving execution. The TKH Group circular published on 22 July 2026 reveals that the company will maintain transitional arrangements for software, finance, and logistics for a limited post-separation period. These temporary arrangements could prevent the newly focused Automation business from achieving immediate margin efficiencies.
4.
Accelerating portfolio simplification: Management intends to offset structural cost drags by divesting low-margin activities to focus on highly scalable technologies. Jefferies research of 13 August 2026 highlights that simplifying the portfolio will help narrow the group's historical conglomerate discount. By shedding asset-heavy and low-proprietary divisions, the remaining Automation segment can maximize its return on capital under a streamlined standalone structure.

Automated Machinery and Tire Building Thesis

5.
Severe order declines: The Automated Machinery division experienced an operational slowdown due to delayed investments by major tire manufacturers. As disclosed in the TKH Group corporate presentation of 14 September 2026, Adjusted EBITA for the sub-segment fell organically by 21.1% in H1 2026. This performance reflects temporary capital expenditure delays, which means that standalone earnings will remain highly cyclical and volume-dependent.
6.
Strong replacement drivers: The core long-term investment case for Automated Machinery relies on an extensive wave of equipment replacement. A TKH Group presentation dated 12 August 2026 indicates that replacement of existing tire-building machinery represents 75% to 80% of demand. Furthermore, the company claims that 40% of the non-TKH installed base is older than 20 years, creating excellent visibility for future orders.
7.
Expanding installed base: The company's strong competitive positioning is anchored by its massive global market share in outsourced machinery. A TKH Group investor presentation on 11 August 2026 notes that the group holds a 30% to 35% share of the total global installed base of tire-building machines. Capturing aftermarket services from this fleet provides a high-margin recurring revenue stream that stabilizes standalone operational margins.
8.
Advanced platform commercialization: Commercializing unique technological platforms is vital to overcoming cyclical headwinds in the capital goods sector. The TKH Group annual report of 7 July 2026 states that the company successfully delivered its first full UNIXX system in 2025. This advanced, highly automated platform should drive organic growth by displacing legacy in-house assembly systems.

Vision Technology and Competitor Dynamics

9.
Strong segment growth: The Vision Technology division has demonstrated superior growth dynamics that contrast sharply with the cyclical declines in manufacturing. According to the TKH Group press release of 11 August 2026, first-half Adjusted EBITA for the sub-segment rose by 24.8% organically. This robust growth was driven by factory automation, indicating that advanced vision assets represent the primary engine of standalone profitability.

Growing software monetization: Transitioning from pure hardware manufacturing to software licensing represents the principal margin-expansion lever for the standalone division. In the AGM minutes of 13 August 2026, management stated that over 30% of the technology proposition consists of software. Expanding direct software licensing will drive structurally higher gross margins, allowing the segment to converge with high-margin global peers.

If customer capital expenditure rebounds strongly, the standalone division could comfortably surpass its 17% to 19% Adjusted EBITA margin target.

Section 4 – Accounting Rebasing, Governance, and Corporate Structure

Section summary

Accounting rebasing and modeling risks

1.
Disrupted consensus modeling: The strategic separation of the Electrification division requires extensive financial re-basing under IFRS 5 rules. Management notes that historical comparative figures must be restated to present the Electrification division as discontinued operations. This re-basing will disrupt algorithmic stock screens and distort historical price-to-earnings ratios for remaining assets. Consequently, analysts must extensively rebuild their consensus forecasting models to avoid automated database errors.
2.
Complex presentation changes: Financial reporting structures will face further revisions due to the mandatory implementation of IFRS 18 beginning 1 January 2027. Corporate disclosures show that this standard alters the classification of income statement items into operating, investing, and financing categories. The Audit Committee has initiated detailed impact assessments to manage these presentation adjustments. These changes will complicate multi-year financial comparisons and limit corporate screening visibility for quantitative investors.
3.
Elevated accounting complexity: Restructuring efforts require extensive coordination across corporate legal and tax advisory departments. Management emphasizes that global tax compliance systems are becoming increasingly complex during corporate reorganizations. The division of segment liabilities and transition structures requires significant administrative overhead. This complex setup means that standalone financial databases may misinterpret initial post-separation filings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some sell-side analysts downplay these accounting disruption risks by focusing entirely on normalized operations. Jefferies maintains its forward models using simplified segment adjustments to project a smooth transition path. This research assumes that underlying demand drivers remain intact regardless of temporary accounting classifications. Therefore, professional analysts may experience far fewer modeling hurdles than algorithmic quantitative screening tools.

Earnings quality and revenue recognition

5.
Aggressive development capitalization: TKH Group continues to capitalize a high proportion of its annual technology expenditures. The group's capitalized development costs rose to €114.30 million in 2025 compared with €107.73 million in 2024. This represents 51.60% (calculated) of the €221.70 million total estimated EBITA for 2025. High capitalization rates artificially boost short-term reported earnings, which creates material earnings quality risks if commercialization fails.
6.
Subjective revenue recognition: Deloitte Accountants B.V. identified over-time revenue recognition in Smart Manufacturing as a key audit matter. The auditor emphasizes that customer-specific tire-building projects require complex estimates of completion percentages. Furthermore, estimating the necessary cost to come involves significant management judgment. These subjective estimates represent a presumed risk of management bias or potential reporting errors.
7.
Rigorous audit testing: External auditors have expanded their substantive procedures to mitigate potential revenue recognition fraud. Deloitte conducts extensive back-testing on historical management assessments and cost projections. Audit procedures cover approximately 86% of the group's total over-time revenues. These extensive reviews are designed to prevent material earnings misstatements during cyclical factory slowdowns.

Executive transition and strategic resets

8.
Experienced leadership transition: Guido Grandi will formally succeed Alexander van der Lof as group Chief Executive Officer on 1 January 2027. Executive searches show that Grandi is a German national who previously managed Norma Group. His previous firm oversaw global operations with €1.20 billion in revenues and 8,500 employees. This international background is expected to bring disciplined operational focus to the remaining Automation business.
9.
Strategic base reset: Investors anticipate that the arrival of a new CEO will trigger a strategic clean-up of the balance sheet. Historically, incoming corporate leaders often write down legacy capitalized R&D or adjust medium-term margin targets. Grandi's appointment allows TKH to kitchen-sink older asset allocations before embarking on the Capitalise & Execute 2028 strategy. Such adjustments would lower near-term EPS but establish a cleaner earnings foundation for future growth.
10.
Management split focus: The transition plan leaves legacy leadership in control of the separated Electrification business. Alexander van der Lof will transition to lead the Electrification segment as standalone Chief Executive. This arrangement ensures that historical operational expertise is preserved at Eemshaven. However, splitting executive focus could lead to frictional administrative costs and transitional inefficiencies.

Protective structures and activist defense

14.
Restrictive depositary receipts: TKH Group employs a protective Dutch depositary-receipt trust structure. Ordinary shares are held in trust by Stichting Administratiekantoor TKH Group, which issues depositary receipts. The STAK foundation retains the ultimate voting rights to these underlying shares. This mechanism is designed to protect the company's intellectual property and commercial secrets.
15.
Crucial exchangeability limits: The conversion of depositary receipts back into ordinary shares is severely restricted. Capital terms dictate that depositary receipts can only be exchanged up to a maximum of 1% of total issued capital. This strict limitation prevents hostile accumulators from gaining direct voting control. Consequently, activist shareholders are blocked from initiating aggressive proxy battles or split-up demands.
16.
Robust takeover defense: The STAK foundation has the statutory authority to revoke voting proxies under specific threat scenarios. Proxies may be withdrawn if a hostile public offer is announced or if an activist acquires a 25% stake. Additionally, Stichting Continuïteit TKH holds an option to issue protective preference shares. This option can double the defensive voting share capital to neutralize any hostile takeover attempts.
17.
Other side of the story: The company's comprehensive defensive structure might limit its long-term valuation by deterring constructive activist interventions. While the STAK structure protects TKH's proprietary machine vision and subsea cable designs, it simultaneously isolates the board from market accountability. This lack of external pressure could allow inefficient cost structures to persist post-separation. If Guido Grandi fails to aggressive trim unallocated central overhead, the protective trust will prevent shareholders from forcing necessary operational reforms.
