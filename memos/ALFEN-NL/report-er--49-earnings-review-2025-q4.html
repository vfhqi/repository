Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Alfen entered May 2026 after consecutive years of revenue decline severely depressed its historical valuation, despite its strong grid position.
1.
Core integrated model: Alfen specializes in high-quality electrical infrastructure solutions, operating through three highly synergetic business units that address the grid bottlenecks of Europe’s energy transition. The company designs, engineers, and produces secondary substations, energy storage systems, and electric vehicle chargers. Management historically prioritized integrated solutions to cross-sell offerings and differentiate from single-segment competitors.

2.
Market-leading grid position: The Smart Grid Solutions segment produces distribution substations and medium-voltage transport distribution systems for utility and private customers. In its home market of the Netherlands, Alfen maintains a dominant position with an estimated market share of seventy percent in secondary transformer substations. Growth in this division is structurally secured by long-term framework agreements with major Dutch grid operators like Enexis and Alliander.
3.
Challenging charging segment: The Electric Vehicle Charging segment designs and manufactures alternating current charging equipment for home, business, and public locations. While Alfen holds a substantial thirteen percent European market share in charge points, the segment faced severe headwinds. Intense competition in the home segment and falling public installations resulted in persistent volume and margin pressure leading up to 2026.
4.
Rapidly expanding storage: The Energy Storage Systems segment delivers stationary utility-scale and mobile battery systems like the Mobile-X to independent power producers. Alfen relies on buying third-party battery enclosures and software components, which historically restricted its segment margins compared to larger volume players. Despite margin constraints, the segment grew rapidly with landmark project wins including a 100 MW/200 MWh system for Return Energy.
5.
Volatile financial trajectory: Alfen’s absolute financial performance was volatile over the three years preceding May 2026. Total revenues grew to €504.5m in 2023, but subsequently fell to €487.6m in 2024 and further contracted to €435.6m in 2025. This 2025 revenue level was roughly in line with the company's lowered guidance of €430m to €480m.

6.
Eeroding operational margins: Profitability margins significantly deteriorated from historically high levels. Adjusted EBITDA margins plummeted from mid-to-high teens to a stable 5.8% margin in 2025. Absolute adjusted EBITDA decreased 11% to €25.5m in 2025, supported by cost savings of €13m from a 15% headcount reduction.
7.
Favorable peer dynamics: Peer comparisons highlight Alfen’s relative operational struggles in the commoditized charging space. For fiscal year 2025, Norwegian competitor Zaptec ASA grew its revenues by 21% with a 40% gross margin. In contrast, Alfen's EV charging segment revenue shrank by 21% in 2025 even though it maintained a similar 42.0% gross margin.
8.
Subscale storage profitability: In the energy storage market, Alfen faced comparable high-growth, low- profitability peer dynamics. Finnish peer Merus Power Oyj reported record net sales of €54.6m in 2025 with an EBITDA margin of 3.3%. While Alfen delivered much higher energy storage revenues of €125.6m in 2025, its segment gross margin was limited to 22.0% gross due to project size scaling.
9.
Historical valuation discount: Alfen's share price performance was severely depressed prior to its Q1 2026 earnings release. Over the 12 months leading to March 2026, the stock plunged by 29.3% in value . This followed an absolute decline of 5.9% over 6 months and 14.1% over 3 months, reflecting a steep historic discount of 70% to its historical valuation multiples.
10.
Step-by-step rating adjustments: Broker coverage from Jefferies tracked the bottoming out of the stock's valuation. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens upgraded Alfen from Underperform to Hold in November 2025 with a €10 PT, before lowering it to €9 in February 2026. By May 2026, Kerstens upgraded the stock to Buy with an €18 PT, citing strong grid and storage tailwinds.
11.
Pivoting analyst sentiment: Deutsche Bank's research followed a similarly volatile path. Deutsche Bank analyst Nikita Papaccio upgraded Alfen to Hold in November 2025 with a €10 PT, and later upgraded to Buy in January 2026 with a €30 PT. Papaccio eventually downgraded Alfen back to a Hold rating in June 2026 with a €33 PT.
12.
Subdued market expectations: Going into the Q1 2026 reporting period, broker consensus was highly conservative. Analysts compiled a consensus target for FY2026 revenues of €458m and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 6.2%. This reflected widespread skepticism that Alfen could successfully execute its strategic turnaround under new leadership.
13.
Other side of the story: Although Alfen’s dominant position in the Dutch grid market provides structural support, this business is highly exposed to severe labor shortages and lengthy regulatory permitting delays. If these grid bottlenecks persist, or if European EV adoption continues to stall, Alfen's streamlined product portfolio will fail to drive recovery over the next 18 months, rendering recent broker upgrades premature.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. Alfen’s corporate landscape is undergoing a comprehensive strategic overhaul under its newly appointed executive team.
  2. Operational changes focus on streamlining product lines to defend margins against intense commoditization.
  3. Medium-term growth expectations have been moderated significantly as the company prioritizes organizational stability.
1.
Transitioned executive leadership: Alfen appointed Michael Colijn as its new Chief Executive Officer effective October 1, 2025, to navigate a challenging market. Colijn brings extensive experience in smart energy systems and sustainable mobility to lead the company's next phase of growth. This transition aims to stabilize Alfen's operations after consecutive years of revenue declines. Under Colijn, the company has pivoted toward a decentralized business-unit structure to improve customer execution.
2.
Abrupt financial transition: Chief Financial Officer Onno Krap stepped down on short notice as of July 1, 2026, raising immediate investor concerns about continuity. To maintain long-term stability, Alfen quickly appointed Bart Meussen, a highly experienced finance executive, as his successor. Meussen's background in business transformation is expected to help implement Alfen's renewed commercial strategy. Analysts expect this rapid transition to prevent operational disruptions during the strategic reorganization.
3.
Decentralized business structure: Alfen formally adopted a new business-unit led organizational structure on May 1, 2026, split into three dedicated divisions. This structural shift aims to increase customer focus and execution across Alfen's key markets. By moving away from a centralized functional organization, management wants to improve decision-making speed.
4.
Elevating restructuring expenses: To support its organizational realignment, Alfen planned a four point five million restructuring provision for fiscal year 2026. This charge will be taken primarily during the second quarter of 2026, with a smaller portion scheduled for the third quarter. The restructuring activities focus on reallocating internal resources toward growth-critical areas without further headcount reductions.
5.
Streamlined charger lineup: Alfen began a major rationalization of its Electric Vehicle Charging segment by simplifying its alternating current portfolio. Management decided to reduce its product lineup from five to three alternating current types to minimize manufacturing complexity. Furthermore, the company completely discontinued its direct current fast-charging development to focus resources on more profitable product-market combinations.
6.
Reshaped geographic targets: Alfen's renewed electric vehicle charging strategy targets targeted expansion in Italy, Spain, and Portugal. Crucially, the company reversed its prior decision to exit non-core markets by planning a re-entry into the United Kingdom to capture growth. This geographic expansion is designed to help Alfen regain market share as the European charging network grows.
7.
Low-cost market entry: Deutsche Bank's Papaccio noted that Alfen's expansion into the United Kingdom was prompted by existing clients. Papaccio expects that following current customers will help Alfen achieve low re-entering costs in this highly competitive region. However, ongoing market volatility and lack of clarity on the final commercial structure maintain risks.
8.
Refocused grid segments: Within the Smart Grid Solutions business, Alfen is refocusing its efforts on the five fastest-growing product-market segments. The division is shifting its commercial focus to target private market substation sales across Europe. Alfen aims to leverage its dominant seventy percent Dutch market share to capture broader international projects.
9.
Prioritized storage applications: Alfen's updated Energy Storage Solutions strategy prioritizes high-capacity utility-scale and mobile battery storage systems. The company intends to scale its growth in the commercial and industrial segment to improve asset turnover. This segment experienced positive momentum, including securing its largest two hundred megawatt-hour project in the Netherlands.
10.
Compressed margin targets: Deutsche Bank's Papaccio Glass emphasized that Alfen's revised adjusted EBITDA margin guidance of four to seven percent for fiscal year 2026 was a major disappointment. This revised guidance represented a thirteen percent consensus profitability miss compared to initial sell-side expectations of 6.2%. The downward adjustment reflects a structural shift toward lower-margin segments and near-term organizational friction.
11.
Repositioned financial goals: Under its new strategic plan, Alfen dropped its historical medium-term target of returning to a low double-digit EBITDA margin. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens now projects a slow recovery, with EBITDA margins rising from four percent in fiscal year 2026 to ten percent by 2030. This conservative outlook reflects expectations of persistent headwinds in the electric vehicle charging segment.
12.
Aggressive inventory reduction: Alfen implemented strict working capital discipline, achieving a substantial twenty-three point one million working capital reduction in early 2026. This effort helped the company transition to a net cash position of six million compared to its previous net debt position of twenty point seven million. Analysts believe maintaining this cash discipline is critical to supporting Alfen's overall liquidity during its transitional phase.

This decline was driven by intense competition in the home segment and slower public charger installations. Group Topline and Profitability Delivery

1.
Reporting period and timing: The reporting period under review is the first quarter of fiscal year 2026, which Alfen reported on May 13, 2026. Group revenue and other income increased twenty-five percent to €129.70m, compared with €103.80m in the first quarter of 2025. This strong start was driven primarily by rapid milestone deliveries in Energy Storage Systems and Smart Grid Solutions projects. This delivery significantly outperformed company-compiled consensus expectations of €114.60m by thirteen percent, representing an unexpectedly strong start to the year for the business.
2.
Compressed operational margins: The group gross margin fell to 26.20% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, down from 29.80% in the corresponding period of 2025. This was a direct result of an unfavorable segment mix shift toward the lower-margin Energy Storage Systems division. Deutsche Bank analyst Nikita Papaccio highlighted that this gross margin performance missed consensus expectations of twenty-seven point seven percent by 150 basis points. However, on the back of higher revenues, the absolute gross profit of €34m beat consensus of €31.80m by seven percent.
3.
Operational leverage EBITDA beat: Adjusted EBITDA rose nearly fifty percent to €8.20m, compared with €5.50m in the first quarter of 2025. This represents an adjusted EBITDA margin of 6.30% of revenue, up 100 basis points from the prior year's 5.30%. Deutsche Bank research reported that this surpassed consensus expectations of €6.60m and a 5.80% margin by twenty-four percent, primarily because higher revenues improved fixed cost utilization across manufacturing plants.

Segment Level Performance Breakdown

5.
Smart Grid Solutions execution: Smart Grid Solutions revenue increased three percent to €55.80m, compared with €54.30m in the first quarter of 2025. The division delivered 833 substations during the quarter, with 590 deployed in the Netherlands and 243 in Finland. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens noted that this revenue beat consensus expectations of forty-nine point six million by thirteen percent, supported by strong project delivery in both the regular utility business and transport distribution stations.
6.
EV Charging volume contraction: Electric Vehicle Charging revenue decreased nineteen percent to €23.30m, compared with €28.80m in the first quarter of 2025. The company delivered approximately 25,000 charge points, representing a twelve percent decrease from the 28,400 produced in the prior period. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens reported that segment revenues fell eighteen percent short of consensus of €28.30m, driven by uneven public order patterns and competitive pricing that dragged average selling prices down.
7.
Storage segment revenue surge: Energy Storage Systems revenue surged 145% to €50.70m, compared with €20.70m in the first quarter of 2025. This massive topline increase was primarily driven by reaching payment and progress milestones on two large-scale projects. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens emphasized that this performance beat consensus expectations of thirty-six point six million by thirty-nine percent, validating the segment's strong market momentum.
8.
Storage margin stabilization: Energy Storage Systems gross margin landed at 21.10% in the first quarter of 2026, representing a decrease from the exceptionally high 32.50% reported in the first quarter of 2025. The previous year's margin was artificially boosted by a one-off release of project contingencies. Jefferies research highlighted that the current quarter's gross margin was above the midpoint of expected ranges of fifteen to twenty-five percent, even as larger project proportions put downward pressure on average margins.

Cash Flow and Balance Sheet Evolution

9.
Positive cash generation: Cash flow from operating activities reached €6.30m in the first quarter of 2026, highlighting a solid start to the year. This represents a solid improvement compared with historical cash strains during previous restructuring phases. Deutsche Bank's Papaccio noted that this positive cash flow reflects strong cash conversion on project execution despite the company recording a minor €4.50m restructuring provision starting in the second quarter of 2026.
10.
Strict working capital reduction: Alfen achieved significant working capital improvements, reducing its working capital requirement by €23.10m. Former Chief Financial Officer Onno Krap noted that the company made significant progress in utilizing overstocked electric vehicle charging components from the 2021 and 2022 market boom into finished products to release cash. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens reported that this cash discipline was highly favorable compared to peers and historic levels.
11.
Transition to net cash: Due to strong operating cash flows and inventory liquidation, Alfen's net debt position dramatically improved. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens reported that Alfen transitioned to a net cash position of six point two million at the end of the period, compared to a net debt position of €20.70m at the end of fiscal year 2025.

Forecast Revisions and Future Implications

12.
Front-loaded project dynamics: Management reiterated that fiscal year 2026 revenues for the Energy Storage Systems segment would be highly front-loaded in the first half of the year. Alfen expects the second quarter of 2026 to remain in line with the first quarter, meaning the second half of the year will be substantially weaker. Jefferies research analyst David Kerstens pointed out that this front-loaded profile implies fifty percent lower revenues in the second half of fiscal year 2026 compared to the first half.
13.
Guidance reaffirmation: Despite the mixed segment dynamics, Alfen reiterated its full-year fiscal 2026 financial guidance. The company continues to project revenues between €435m and €475m and an adjusted EBITDA margin of four to seven percent. Jefferies analyst David Kerstens noted that this outlook represents a prolonged transitional period before returning to consistent year-on-year growth in fiscal year twenty-seven and beyond.
14.
Other side of the story: While the first quarter revenue and adjusted EBITDA delivery significantly surpassed market expectations, this outperformance is almost entirely artificial. The beat was driven by the front-loaded execution of two large-scale storage projects, while the core, high-margin Electric Vehicle Charging segment continues to contract rapidly. If Alfen fails to secure significant new storage orders as the 2026 backlog window closes, or if Dutch grid bottlenecks prevent substation installations, the company will suffer severe margin contraction in the second half of the year, rendering the first-quarter optimization shortlived.
