Rexel absorbed a EUR 124 million antitrust fine from the French Competition Authority in October 2024 for historical price-fixing activities, positioning the company between Schneider Electric's heavier EUR 207 million penalty and Legrand's lighter EUR 43 million sanction, with the full fine amount paid in cash during April 2025 despite an active appeal process. The company simultaneously appealed the French pricing decision before the Paris Court of Appeal in December 2024 while posting a EUR 48 million cash guarantee ordered by a French magistrate for parallel investigations, and management recognized a matching EUR 124 million receivable and provision in the 2025 accounts, creating an extraordinary balance sheet artifact where Rexel effectively carries both a liability and an offsetting asset for the same regulatory penalty.

This accounting treatment represents an unusual financial anomaly rarely observed in peer electrical distribution firms, where most competitors either contest fines without payment or accept penalties without maintaining receivable positions, and the dual-entry structure may signal management's unusually high confidence in ultimate appeal success or reflect aggressive financial reporting practices that deviate from conservative European governance norms.
