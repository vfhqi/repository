Outliers worth noting

The analysis of Subsea 7's financial profile and competitive positioning reveals three notable statistical outliers that warrant specific attention across the value chain and peer benchmarking dimensions. First, Saipem operates as a significant margin outlier within the peer group, reporting an adjusted EBITDA margin of only 11.1% in 2025 compared to Subsea 7's 20.9% and the subsea sector's typical mid-to-high teens profitability.

This structural discount stems from Saipem's diversified business model, which includes a low-margin onshore engineering and construction division that dilutes the profitability of its high-end subsea operations. The margin gap is particularly striking given that both companies compete in identical deepwater SURF markets, suggesting that investors have historically assigned Subsea 7 a premium valuation due to its pure-play, high-end subsea installation focus.

This divergence creates a critical valuation tension heading into the proposed Saipem7 merger, as the combined entity will inherit Saipem's margin dilution while attempting to preserve Subsea 7's premium market positioning. Second, the vessel replacement cost environment represents an extreme outlier relative to historical norms, with high-specification pipelaying vessels now requiring capital investments of up to USD 900 million and mandatory cash deposits of 50% compared to just 10% historically.

This dramatic inflation in both absolute capital requirements and financing terms has effectively doubled from previous upcycle baselines and creates a nearinsurmountable barrier to new competitive entry. The magnitude of this shift is unprecedented in the subsea installation sector and fundamentally alters the industry's competitive dynamics by protecting incumbent pricing power and ensuring that no speculative newbuild capacity emerges to challenge the existing oligopoly.

This cost barrier works in tandem with severe shipyard capacity constraints to lock in the current market structure through at least the end of the decade. Third, Subsea 7's return on capital employed trajectory exhibits an extreme recovery pattern, rising from a deeply sub-economic 1% ROCE in 2023 to a current 9% in 2025, with analyst forecasts projecting a dramatic expansion to 23.7% in 2026 and 26.1% by 2028. This represents one of the steepest capital return improvement cycles observed in the offshore services sector and reflects the powerful operating leverage embedded in the company's high fixed-cost vessel fleet during periods of elevated utilization.

The scale of this return inflection– from substantially below the weighted average cost of capital to well above it within a three-year window– underscores both the severity of the prior downcycle trough and the exceptional margin expansion potential available as higher-priced contracts signed since 2024 flow through the profit and loss statement during execution phases.
