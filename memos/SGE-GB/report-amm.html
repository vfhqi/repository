APM Mini-Memo: Sage

The one-line view. Sage is a highly predictable software compounder on a bottom-of-range multiple, and the story depends on margins rising as artificial intelligence pricing spreads.

What the company does

Sage sells accounting and business-management software by subscription to about 3 million small and medium-sized businesses. Annual recurring revenue was £2,721 million at the first half of financial year 2026, growing 11% organically, and 97% of revenue recurs. Sage reaches those customers through 40,000 accountancy practices and 30,000 resellers, a channel that makes the software hard to dislodge.

North America is the growth engine. North America grew 14%, led by the cloud finance product Sage Intacct at 26%, against 10% in the UK and Ireland and 7% in Europe.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is a steady compounder whose earnings grow faster than its sales. Sales grow about 10% because customers renew at 102% of last year's value and because price adds 5.5%, linked to the Copilot assistant (Morgan Stanley, 20 August 2026). Earnings per share then grow faster through a rising margin, 23.9% in the first half and up 0.8 points on the year, and through buybacks, £600 million so far.

Improving now. First-half organic growth of 10.1% beat consensus and management raised guidance to "above 9%", from a team the July memo describes as habitually cautious. Sage has also hired new product, strategy and growth chiefs, from Salesforce and McKinsey among others, to scale artificial-intelligence-powered software. The Investment Analyst's memo of 20 July 2026 rated Thesis's Change Forces' Strength B: the drivers are credible, and 10% growth trails the 17% or more of premium software peers.

Three drivers to track. Organic recurring revenue growth should hold above 10%, with a warning below 9%. North American growth should stay above 12%. The margin should move from 23.9% towards the 25.3% peer median, helped by repeating abroad the 25 to 30% price lifts that the artificial intelligence tiers achieved in the UK.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal'. The Investment Analyst's memo of 20 July rated Setups' Fit/Crispness B, with a concern clearing in a high-quality compounder as the primary fit; matching that wording to your named setup is my own mapping. The cause for concern is that artificial intelligence and stronger rivals squeeze a mid-market vendor, and the multiple of 14.7 times earnings sits in the 1st percentile of its ten-year history.

The clearing has begun. The share price rose from 845.6 pence on 17 July to 1,180 pence on 13 November, 2% below the 52-week high. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of 13 November is B, where the July memo had D, and our chart screen rates a new uptrend as probable. Demand-driven EPSU is a poor fit, because growth is steady rather than inflecting.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Moderate. The Investment Analyst's memo of 20 July rated Case Riskiness C, on a scale where A is simple and low-risk. The core is simple, one recurring revenue engine. Three regions, several product lines and two or three drivers that must all work make the case less simple than a single-product story.

The main risk is a squeezed middle that shows up as a stalled margin. Sage sits between fast small-business platforms such as Intuit and Xero and the large suites of SAP and Oracle. If that squeeze holds the margin near 23%, the bear case at Deutsche Bank, earnings per share grow only as fast as sales and the low multiple is deserved. Net debt at 2.0 times operating profit before depreciation, the top of Sage's own range, would then limit buybacks.

What needs to go right. The margin must keep rising each half, the UK price lifts must repeat in other countries, and Sage Intacct must gain pace in Europe. Track the renewal rate too: 102% moving towards 103% says customers accept the pricing, and a reading below 101% is the warning.

Business quality

High on cash and predictability, upper-middle on growth and margin. Cash conversion was 116% and has exceeded 100% for seven years running, a top-5% record among European software peers. Recurring revenue of 97% and cautious guidance suit your test of predictable earnings per share. The margin is 1.5 points below the peer median.

The quality grade is provisional. The Investment Analyst's memo of 20 July rated Foundations' Robustness C. That memo drew on one research report, on the forces of change, and the report on the business foundations had not been written.

Plausible returns and valuation

A on the reading, unproven. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 13 November is A, set on valuation alone. The weekly routine's plausible return, dated 12 November, is 49.7% a year over eighteen months and 30.0% over three years, against your bar of 25%. Consensus earnings growth of 11.7% a year supplies a D-grade return by itself, so the A comes from the multiple moving from 14.7 to 22.3 times, the routine's fair multiple from the ten-year history.

The multiple needs checking first. Our files show 14.7 times earnings both at 845.6 pence in July and at 1,180 pence now. A 40% price rise is not explained by earnings growing 12% a year, so one of the two readings is stale. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 12 November is C, where the July memo had B: analysts' estimates have not yet followed the chart.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pro: Sage holds the accounting record. Our sector note of 13 October sorts software by how the vendor charges and places Sage among the toll collectors of the mid-market, whose billable activity grows as artificial intelligence automates work. The sector is recovering one company at a time: the median enterprise software share fell 14.5% over twelve months and rose 18.4% over three, to 12 October. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 13 November is B, read from the environment only.

Con: the window has no dated end. The same note says the advantage lasts until artificial intelligence agents bypass the application layer, which nobody dates. Europe, growing 7%, is the weak region.

In short

  • Interesting because rare: 97% recurring revenue and seven years of cash conversion above 100% sit on a multiple at the bottom of its ten-year range
  • Improving now: growth beat, guidance rose and the share price is up 40% since July, so the concern is starting to clear
  • Decisive driver is the margin: a rise from 23.9% towards 25.3% lifts earnings per share faster than sales, and a stall near 23% leaves a deserved low multiple
  • Return is unproven: the A needs a rerating to 22.3 times, and the 14.7 times reading must be re-checked at today's price
  • Next step is the July memo re-read against the full-year results, expected in November 2026 and not in our files
