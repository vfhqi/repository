Outliers worth noting

Solva experienced an extreme profitability collapse with audited net income falling 94.08% year-on-year from 3,700,599 thousand tenge in 2022 to 219,134 thousand tenge in 2023, representing a statistical anomaly far exceeding typical peer performance volatility. This translated into return on equity plummeting from 19.54% to just 1.24%, placing Solva in the bottom percentile of regional microfinance institutions while peers maintained double-digit returns. [Cross-ref: Section 5, Section 7] 204 Solva represents the only participant out of three major MFO applicants that failed to secure banking conversion approval, as regulatory authorities blocked its reorganization from an MFO to a bank holding structure in December 2024 while competitors Bankey Finance and KMF successfully obtained banking licenses, creating a unique competitive disadvantage in funding costs and deposit access.

This regulatory rejection rate of 100% for Solva versus 0% for its direct peers constitutes a true structural outlier. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 4, Section 7, Section 9] Solva issued two-year corporate bonds on the KASE at exceptionally high coupon rates of 20%, significantly above competitor KMF's 17.75% yield and MyCar Finance's 19% rate, positioning Solva at the extreme expensive end of the MFO funding spectrum and suggesting elevated perceived credit risk or weaker negotiating position in capital markets. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7] Following the domestic banking conversion setback, Solva executed an unusually rapid strategic pivot to geographic expansion into Uzbekistan within weeks, announcing plans in January 2025 to enter an entirely new market and targeting an operating license by H1 2025.

This represents an exceptional velocity of strategic redirection rarely observed among regional financial institutions, particularly when the pivot involves complex cross-border regulatory approvals and operational infrastructure deployment in an unfamiliar jurisdiction.
