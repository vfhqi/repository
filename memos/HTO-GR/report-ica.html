1. Bottom line

  • Richard was right on the direction of 2027: the 29-Oct memo's +1.0% is withdrawn, but a full 3% is not yet earned. On the 30-Oct call management said System Solutions should run at about the same level in 2027 and group growth should stay at about 2026's levels (about 3%). Putting System Solutions flat into the memo's own bridge gives about +1.8%, and that already includes a December price step that management has not committed to; without the step it is about zero. I weight management's signal against that bridge and settle at about +2.3% (model update 3 carries +2.4%), close to Barclays' +2.5% and the median broker's +2.7%. Why it matters: the February estimate cut the memo expected now looks unlikely, but growth still rests on price and cost, not volume, as the memo's "spine" said.
  • The revenue cuts are mostly low-margin revenue, not core revenue, but not "purely" transit. Brokers' 2027 revenue forecasts differ by 239 EURm and their EBITDA forecasts by only 56 EURm; in the third quarter revenue rose 1.0% (5.0% without transit) while EBITDAaL rose 3.1%. Transit explains perhaps half to two-thirds of the cut in consensus sales; most of the rest is System Solutions (about a 15% to 20% margin), a real cut with a small profit effect. The one normal-margin cut found is Barclays', and it is small. Why it matters: the Pool's sales-revision test fails on one cut out of eight brokers, so it says little about the core business.
  • The Sell is a call to underperform, built on Greek competition and announced in a sector-wide review. Barclays' target of 18.00 EUR implies a 4.5% price fall, about a flat year with the dividend. Its 2027 and 2028 EBITDAaL (1,450 and 1,468 EURm) are almost the same as model update 3's (about 1,449 and 1,464); the whole disagreement is long-run growth (Barclays about 0.5% a year, the model 1.0% to 1.2% in 2029 to 2030). The evidence for it in the quarter is real: retail fixed revenue without Data Com fell 0.6%, the broadband base lost 8,164 customers (almost all of the year's loss), and the chief executive said competition has increased.
  • On free cash flow and EV/EBITDA, with multiples held, the return is about the cash yield: +8% to +11% to end-2027. The 29-Oct memo's +28.5% needed the P/E to re-rate to 12.5x, which equals about 6.1x EV/EBITDA and a 7.1% free cash flow yield. Against European incumbents OTE is about 15% cheaper on EV/EBITDAaL and about the same on equity free cash flow yield (8% against 7.9%) with far less debt, so it is modestly cheap on risk, not on yield.
  • Risk and reward here and now: roughly balanced, slightly positive. Six scenarios run from 13.00 EUR (Barclays' downside, -26%) to 23.44 EUR (+29%); the probability-weighted return is about +5% to end-2027 (about +4.6% a year), below the cash yield, because a de-rating and Morgan Stanley's bear case (15.20 EUR) are given about as much weight as a re-rating. The 10.8x two-year-forward P/E is already at the 69th percentile of five years and the 81st of two, after OTE re-rated while the telecoms group de-rated 12%.
  • The One Thing: topping out, not rolled over. Profit growth is holding at about 3%, but the engines under it are slowing (mobile service growth 5.5%, 2.3%, 2.0% in the three quarters of 2026; System Solutions +52% to +9%; the broadband base turning down). Sell-side momentum is drifting lower: four Holds and a Sell among 13 ratings, FY2028 EPS consensus -2.2% over six months, Eurobank's target cut 11%. The price is below a falling 50-day average (19.53) and above a rising 200-day average (18.04). What would make it rolling over: a close below about 18 EUR with the 200-day average of its price relative to the Stoxx Europe 600 flattening, a 2027 guide below 2%, or no price step in early 2027.
  • The rules still say no to a Core Investment. Hellenic Telecom fails the Pool (Stock RS 59, Sector RS 31, sales revisions -12.5%) and is flagged on Sell Criteria 1 (Industry RS below 30) and 3 (Sector RS below 50 with Stock RS below 70). A Probing Bet is Richard's judgement, not a rule outcome; nothing in this memo is a recommendation to trade.

3. The 2027 growth path, and the model update

What management said about 2027

  • Group growth: the chief executive said OTE intends to stay within these kinds of growth levels moving into 2027 (call summaries; the KQ report cites the third-quarter presentation and Morgan Stanley for the same point). This matches Richard's reading: "IMHO CEO was clear on conference call expectation of similar growth (3%) next year too".
  • System Solutions: "more or less along the same levels" in 2027 (call summary); NBG Securities reports that management targets stable IT revenues and margins in 2027 from new private-sector and international work. This matches Richard: "CEO/CFO were clear on call re. they think it should be similar levels in 2027".
  • Capital spending: about 600 EURm "for the next year as well, more or less" (chief financial officer, call summary; "a bit over 600" in the KQ report). This matches Richard's "not being higher than this year at 600m EUR"; the model keeps 600 EURm plus about 78 EURm for spectrum in 2027.
  • Price indexation: since July 2026 OTE's contract terms allow a CPI-linked price increase at the start of each year, but management has not committed to applying it in early 2027 (NBG Securities). Greek inflation was 5.0% in September.
  • Competition: the chief executive said competitive intensity has increased since the beginning of the year (call summary).

Caveat on the wording. I have not seen the verbatim transcript Richard read: the Earnings Review and the KQ report paraphrase, and the earlier window used Investing.com call summaries. The sources agree on the substance (growth levels held, System Solutions about flat, capital spending about 600), but they are not fully independent (the KQ brief quoted the call summaries as context to verify), and none states a "3%" figure for 2027.

4. Greek competition: absorbed, or is the damage ahead?

"I am not clear enough on the imact on the case from PPC related competitive intensity #Q"; "is this being successfully absorbed…? Or is the risk ahead… Unclear to me. VERY IMPORTANT KEY DRIVER #J" (Richard, 2-Nov-26)

My judgement: not absorbed. It was held on volume until the third quarter and is not held on price; the larger damage sits in 2027 to 2028, through retail pricing first and wholesale second.

  • Volume held, until this quarter. The broadband base was about flat for a year, but the third quarter brought almost all of the year's loss (-8,164 of about -9,500), despite 52,250 fibre additions. If, as is likely, fixed wireless customers (+15,461) are counted in that base, wired lines fell by about 23,000 in the quarter; that needs confirming with Investor Relations. PPC itself had about 11,000 retail broadband customers at end-2025 (Deutsche Bank) and perhaps 20,000 later (the 29-Oct memo's estimate), under 1% of OTE's lines, so the pressure works through prices more than through PPC's own sales.
  • Not held on price. Retail fixed revenue without Data Com fell 0.6% in the quarter: OTE is keeping customers at a cost in average revenue per customer. PPC prices about 50% below the incumbents. Vodafone and Nova, the challengers, raised prices in line with inflation in early 2026, the second year running, which gives OTE some cover.
  • Why most of the damage is ahead. (1) The Vodafone-PPC fibre venture (a non-binding 50-50 term sheet of June 2026, about 1.5 to 1.85 million homes, about 130 EURm to PPC) targets closing in the first half of 2027 after competition review; Vodafone, and possibly Nova, could then move lines they rent from OTE onto it. (2) PPC keeps building, from about 1.8 million homes in early 2026 towards 2.7 million at end-2026 and 3.8 million by 2028. (3) The regulator has proposed cutting fibre wholesale prices by 10% to 13% while raising copper wholesale prices by 4% to 5% (decision expected early 2027).
  • Size. National wholesale is about 232 EURm of 2026 revenue at an 80% to 85% margin (the model's reconstruction). The model already has it falling 6% to 8% a year, about 40 EURm of EBITDA lost by 2029 and 51 EURm by 2030. A faster migration of Vodafone's lines (30% to 40% of national wholesale gone by 2029) would cost about 55 to 75 EURm in total, so only about 15 to 35 EURm (1% to 2% of EBITDA) more than the model. The larger bear risk is retail pricing spreading from fixed to the whole base, which no source sizes; together with no price step it would take 2027 growth to about zero.
  • Precedents cut against "stabilisation". In Italy and Ireland, pricing pressure lasted as long as the overbuilder was still building, which argues against the KQ report's own "may stabilise into 2027" headline; the challengers' price rises and a venture framed as co-investment argue for it.

Sources: the KQ report on Greek competitive intensity (2-Nov-26; medium confidence, several stale or misattributed citations, no euro figure for revenue at risk); OTE third-quarter figures; the 29-Oct memo's wholesale reconstruction.

5. Revenue cuts: transit or real?

"The company are clear about calling out that revenue cuts from zero-margin wholesale international transit revenues do not have any real profit impact. Risk is that it will keep cutting the SS revenue estimates…? And algos will pick up on that. A negative "sentiment" risk - one I am willing to take. Also, our sell criteria may be distorted by it - unclear to me #J" (Richard, 2-Nov-26)

My judgement: mostly low-margin revenue, not purely transit. Transit (zero margin) explains perhaps half to two-thirds of the cut in consensus sales; most of the rest is System Solutions (about a 15% to 20% margin), a real cut with a small profit effect. The one normal-margin cut found is Barclays', and it is small. The evidence:

  1. Across brokers (27-Oct-26 snapshot): 2027 revenue forecasts range from 3,383 to 3,622 EURm (a spread of 239) while EBITDA forecasts range from 1,453 to 1,509 (a spread of 56); a euro of revenue difference carries about 6 cents of EBITDA, against a group margin of about 42%. That fits a mix of zero-margin transit and low-margin System Solutions, not core telecom revenue.
  2. Over time: since September 2025, consensus FY2027 sales fell 5.4% (about 200 EURm) while FY2027 EBITDA rose 1.0% (FactSet by named year). Transit in 2027 is about 125 to 130 EURm.
  3. Not all transit: Eurobank Equities, which has the lowest 2027 revenue, forecasts revenue falling 97 EURm from 2027 to 2028, after transit has ended, while its EBITDA barely moves; its notes point to a System Solutions "growth cliff". That is a real cut, at a low margin.
  4. The quarter: revenue +1.0% (+5.0% without transit) with EBITDAaL +3.1%. Morgan Stanley then cut its fourth-quarter revenue and kept EBITDAaL (KQ report).
  5. Barclays' cut is normal-margin but small: 2027 revenue -0.7% and EBITDAaL -0.6% together; EPS -2.4% (1.70 to 1.66 EUR), mostly from depreciation and interest. Barclays has no revenue estimates in FactSet, so it does not move the Pool test.

The rules effect. The Pool's SS Sales Net Upgrade Breadth over 100 days is -12.5% because one broker of eight cut its next-year sales estimate and none raised (FactSet, calendar year plus one; the counts are dated 28-Oct-26, before the results). One clean-up is enough to fail the test. Which broker it was is not on file; the likeliest on the evidence is Eurobank (target cut to 17.50 EUR on 14-Sep-26, the lowest revenue, the System Solutions cliff), which would make it a System Solutions cut rather than transit. Why it matters: the failed test is reading low-margin revenue, not the core business; it is not wrong, but it is weak evidence. Whether to treat low-margin revenue cuts differently in the rules is Richard's decision; this memo only reports the distortion.

6. The Sell, investigated

"IA - A "sell" rating has emerged. Investigate in depth the logic for the recommendation. Take this seriously" (Richard, 2-Nov-26)

What the Sell is

  • Barclays moved from Overweight (20 EUR target, 8-May-26, when it called Greek competition "relatively benign") to Underweight (18 EUR) on 19 or 20-Oct-26, inside a sector review that also cut Vodafone, Telefonica and Proximus to Underweight, raised KPN and Telia to Overweight, and named two structural threats to European telecoms: artificial intelligence agents that help customers search for and switch to cheaper plans, and satellite broadband (Barclays' sector note "An UnaMUSEing Basket Case?", cited in its 2-Nov note; neither it nor the downgrade note is in hand). Barclays rates the sector Neutral, so Underweight is a call to underperform the sector.
  • The Greek argument (Barclays, 2-Nov-26, after the results): PPC's aggressive pricing is spreading; retail fixed revenue is already declining as pricing weakens; the PPC-Vodafone venture points to lower wholesale revenue as alternative fibre scales; these limit growth beyond cost efficiencies. It also expects the fixed pressure to extend into the fourth quarter and 2027, against tougher fourth-quarter comparisons.
  • Its numbers: EBITDAaL 1,450 EURm in 2027 (+2.5%) and 1,468 in 2028; shareholder returns "well supported"; a DCF on about 0.5% a year revenue growth to 2030 gives 18 EUR; upside case 25 EUR (Greece growing 1% to 2% a year), downside 13 EUR (more competition in mobile and fixed). It models dividends plus buybacks of 440 to 535 EURm a year against its free cash flow of 573 to 601 EURm, the low end of the company's 70% to 100% policy (about 100% in 2026).
  • What it implies: 18 EUR against 18.85 is a 4.5% price fall; with a dividend yield of about 5% the twelve-month total return is about flat, below the sector's.

The other cautious brokers (13 ratings in FactSet: 7 Buy, 4 Hold, 1 Sell, 1 without a rating)

  • Eurobank Equities, Hold, 17.50 EUR (14-Sep-26; 19.60 EUR in July): little room after the 2026 rise; 5.7x 2026 EV/EBITDA, only modestly below the European average; a System Solutions "growth cliff" in 2027. Management's 2027 signal answers the last point, not the first two.
  • Goldman Sachs, Hold, 20.00 EUR and New Street Research, Hold, 21.20 EUR (both 29-Jul-26); a Restricted broker, Hold, 21.60 EUR. Their reasoning is not in hand.
  • Deutsche Bank is shown "Without" a rating since 23-Oct-26 (probably a restriction, not a view).
  • The positive brokers, for contrast: Morgan Stanley Overweight 23.80 EUR (unchanged after the results; base 6.8x EV/EBITDA; bear case 15.20 EUR at 4.5x); NBG Securities Outperform.

Judgement: how seriously to take it

Seriously on the long run; it is not a near-term earnings call. On almost the same 2027 and 2028 EBITDA as Barclays, model update 3 is worth 19.45 to 20.64 EUR at today's to modestly higher multiples, against Barclays' 18. The whole gap is long-run growth and the multiple: whether Greek telecoms is a 0.5% a year revenue market (Barclays' DCF) or a 1% to 2% one (its own upside case, and the model's 1.0% to 1.2% for 2029 to 2030). The best evidence for the Sell is in this quarter: the first decline in retail fixed revenue without Data Com, the broadband base turning down, and the chief executive's remark that competition has increased. The best evidence against is that EBITDAaL still grew 3.1% with the margin up 0.9 points, and that the revenue cuts are mostly low-margin. What would prove Barclays right within six months: a second quarter of falling retail fixed revenue (February 2027), no price step or a capped one, a binding venture with Nova as a tenant, and a 2027 guide below 2%.

The KQ report on the least positive brokers read no Barclays research (it credits Barclays' rating to a Bernstein note) and covered 4 of 7 houses, so this section rests on Barclays' own notes, FactSet, and press reports of the 20-Oct sector review.

10. Richard's other questions

  • Why FY2028 EBITDA consensus "fell through 1H-26 and ... recovered": the figures I have do not show a fall through the first half. FY2028 consensus was 1,460 EURm in January, 1,533 in June, 1,500 in August and 1,514 at the end of October (1,512 median on 27-Oct; FactSet by named year, as recorded in the earlier window). The drop came between June and August, around the second-quarter results (29-Jul-26: Morgan Stanley's "top-line weakness" from the transit phase-out; mobile slowing; Eurobank's System Solutions cliff). Only five brokers forecast FY2028 (Optima 1,489; Morgan Stanley 1,534; Eurobank 1,492; the Restricted broker 1,543; Goldman Sachs 1,512), so one broker joining or leaving moves the median by 1% to 2%; without broker-by-broker history I cannot separate the two causes. Question for Richard: which chart did you read? If it was a rolling "two years forward" line rather than FY2028 by name, the January step is the roll from FY2027 to FY2028 (Wisdom Library: "a rolling forward consensus jumps at the year switch").
  • "Barclays ... may move to neutral": Barclays had already moved further, from Overweight to Underweight in October, and kept Underweight after the results. A move back would need its long-run growth view to change, not its 2027 numbers.
  • The Theon-like pattern (a strong stock in a weak group): OTE's weekly moves are largely its own (the Stoxx Europe 600, telecoms and Greek stocks together explain about 10% of them), but the group's weakness has still taken about half of its relative fall since July.
  • Interest rates: OTE trades as a yield stock; the telecoms de-rating of 11-Sep to 9-Oct came with United States ten-year yields above 5%, and a fall below about 4.5% is one of the Sector Memo's triggers for the group.
