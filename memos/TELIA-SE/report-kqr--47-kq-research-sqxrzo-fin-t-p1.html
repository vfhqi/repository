Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Operating leverage and cost elimination represent the primary drivers of Telia’s medium-term earnings expansion as service revenues normalize.

Strategic Importance to Investors

1.
The investor focus: "how much fixed-cost operating leverage does this business model carry, how much scope is there for it to work over the next 18 months to four years, and, where it is relevant to the company's strategy, how feasible is cost elimination or reduction, both incremental and in material large cuts?"
2.
Strategic focus: Telia Company’s investment case is highly dependent on securing cash flow over the next few years by growing EBITDA through service revenue growth and cost reductions. Analysts from DNB Carnegie expect that the pay-off from cost-reduction programs will result in significant earnings growth that supports the share price. This focus on cost control is essential for improving return on capital employed and returning Telia to dividend growth. If these cost savings fail to materialize, Telia's free cash flow will fall short of its ambitious targets.
3.
Earnings momentum: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that Telia is positioned to benefit from strong earnings momentum and has upgraded its free cash flow forecasts. The broker expects that the company’s recent six percent headcount reduction will provide greater scope for cost reduction compared to some peers. This operating leverage provides cash returns upside that could support high annual cash distributions. However, wage inflation and pricing pressure remain key downside risks to this optimistic earnings outlook.
4.
Valuation risks: Bernstein Research remains cautious on Telia’s investment case, maintaining an underperform rating due to concerns over earnings sustainability. The broker argues that the benefits from previous cost cuts are already fading, which limits long-term operating leverage and recovery visibility across key regions. In Finland and Norway, competitive pressure from rivals could squeeze margins and offset any cost-reduction efforts. Therefore, the feasibility of sustainable cost reductions represents the key differentiator for future valuation multiples.

Related Strategic Questions

5.
Strategic options: Morgan Stanley emphasizes that management’s ability to identify fresh cost-cutting opportunities is vital to offset declines in legacy businesses. The broker notes that investors will closely monitor whether the company can successfully translate revenue into accelerating EBITDA and free cash flow. Key strategic questions also involve how the upcoming network joint venture with ice in Norway will affect capital expenditure. Ultimately, finding sustainable cost efficiencies is critical for stabilizing margins in a highly competitive telecom landscape.
6.
Operational targets: Telia’s management announced that it intends to localize operations and reduce approximately 900 positions to target annual gross savings of SEK 1 billion. The company expects these measures to simplify the operating model and deliver profitable growth and cash flow over the medium term. Restructuring charges from this program are expected to impact cash flow mainly in 2027. Investors will judge whether these structural changes can successfully flow to the bottom line.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Telia’s track record of delivering net cost reductions is poor, as past programs were often offset by inflation and rising central expenses. DNB Carnegie highlights that previous cost-reduction initiatives failed to translate into sustainable margin expansion because of deteriorating revenue mix and high content costs. If these historical inefficiencies persist, the new gross savings program may fail to reach the bottom line.
8.
Other side of the story: While critics point to Telia’s mixed execution history, the company's Q3 2026 results demonstrate that its operational restructuring is starting to yield tangible benefits. The decline in operational expenses has already driven a margin expansion, showing that Telia can achieve positive operating leverage when execution is disciplined. If service revenue growth remains stable, these cost-out initiatives will successfully protect the company’s dividend coverage and support EPS growth.

Section 2 – Telia's Cost Structure and Historical Operating Leverage

Section summary
  1. Telia's cost structure carries high fixed expenses in network operations and leases, with medium confidence that recent headcount reductions will successfully unlock operating leverage.
  2. The business model exhibits high margin sensitivity to service revenue changes, but past operating leverage was frequently diluted by third-party content costs and restructuring friction.

Characterising the Cost Base

1.
Significant fixed expenses: Telia’s cost base carries heavy structural commitments that limit short-term flexibility across operations. Annual reports show that personnel expenses decreased 14.8% to SEK 11,915 million in 2025 following the change program. Meanwhile, goods and services purchased reached SEK 28,277 million in 2024. These figures show that a large portion of opex is tied to fixed network operations and personnel.
2.
Substantial procurement outlays: Telia's variable and semi-fixed cost lines are heavily driven by goods and sub- contracting services. Research across various broker reports, expert calls, and company filings yielded details on four main operators, utilizing over 12 primary sources and examining one comparable network shutdown case. The company reported that goods and services purchased reached SEK 28,277 million in 2025. Managing these outlays is vital because they represent the primary variable cost component in Telia's operational model.
3.
Decreasing interconnection costs: Telia has successfully lowered its wholesale and roaming expenses to protect its operating margins. The company's interconnect and roaming expenses decreased by 5.0% to SEK 2,061 million in 2025. This reduction reflects lower wholesale volumes and regulatory price adjustments. These declining expenses help Telia offset inflationary personnel pressures across its core Baltic and Nordic markets.
4.
Heavy operational overheads: Telia continues to absorb substantial semi-fixed outlays within its central and regional functions. The company reported that other external expenses reached SEK 9,539 million in 2025. Within this category, IT expenses and consultancy fees stood at SEK 3,368 million. This high proportion of semi-fixed consultancy makes rapid cost reduction highly challenging without compromising systems.
5.
Sticky lease obligations: Telia's network infrastructure reliance creates ongoing cash outflows that behave as fixed costs. The company's total depreciation for right-of-use assets was SEK 3,135 million in 2025. Lease interest expenses also added SEK 659 million to finance costs. These contractual obligations mean that lease-related costs cannot be easily eliminated during operational downturns.

Historical Operating Leverage Testing

6.
Muted margin expansion: Telia’s historical operating leverage has been constrained by legacy declines and high content expenses. Morgan Stanley reported that group service revenue grew 1.9% in Q2 2023. This occurred because TV and media EBITDA collapsed due to rising content costs. This highlights how a single division can dilute the group's operating leverage during competitive periods.
7.
Fluctuating Swedish margins: Telia’s core Swedish segment highlights how volatile revenue mix can impact regional operating leverage. Deutsche Bank reported that Swedish service revenues grew 4.7% in Q3 2026 compared to 2.6% in Q2. However, EBITDA growth slowed to 2.1% in Q3 from 5.2% in Q2. This discrepancy demonstrates that high-volume growth does not automatically expand margins if lower-margin ICT solutions dominate.
8.
Positive margin momentum: Telia demonstrated stronger operating leverage in 2025 as structural cost reductions took effect. The company's adjusted EBITDA margin in Sweden rose to 43.2% in Q3 2025. This expansion was driven by service revenue growth and significantly lower operational expenses. These results suggest that the change program unlocked latent operating leverage across the Nordic units.
9.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reported in October 2026 that Telia has a poor historical track record of translating gross cost savings into net EBITDA growth. Their analysis shows that previous cost programs were often offset by rising content costs and persistent inflation. This historical friction suggests that the business model's operating leverage is weaker than management's projections imply.

Peer Cost Structure Comparisons

Superior structural efficiency: Tele2 has maintained a leaner operating model that translates revenue growth into EBITDA more efficiently than Telia. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported that Tele2's new management team delivered remarkable double-digit organic EBITDAaL growth in 2025. This rapid growth was enabled by a deep cost-efficiency program. Thus, Tele2 possesses more agile operating leverage than Telia's legacyburdened domestic structure.

Long-term workforce optimization: Telenor demonstrates that continuous, moderate staff reductions can sustain profitability during market transitions. This precedent shows that gradual cost-out strategies can effectively offset regional competitive pressures.
12.
Continuous cost optimization: Elisa manages its cost structure through steady, incremental productivity improvements rather than radical headcount reductions. The company's employee expenses were EUR 478 million in 2025. This steady approach allows Elisa to sustain high comparable EBITDA margins of nearly 39% without major restructuring disruptions.
13.
Underwhelming peer programs: Elisa’s recent efficiency measures highlight the difficulty of extracting significant net margins from mature telecom markets. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported that Elisa's EUR 40 million transformation program expanded service revenue margins by only 50 basis points. This outcome fell short of the 100 to 150 basis points implied by company guidance. It indicates that reinvestment needs can dilute the net benefits of major cost-cutting programs.
14.
Country-led model transition: Telenor reorganized its operations to eliminate corporate duplications and enhance localized operating leverage. The group reported total operating expenses of NOK 24,842 million for the full year of 2025. Telenor's CEO announced that organizational simplification would deliver NOK 400 million in cost savings. This restructuring shows how incumbents adapt cost bases to protect margins when service revenue growth slows.

Operational Leverage Precedents

15.
Legacy exit savings: Telenor Norway’s completed copper network shutdown serves as a vital precedent for Telia’s ongoing transition. Morgan Stanley research suggests that Telenor's profitability was strengthened following the shutdown of legacy networks. This transition eliminated substantial maintenance opex and freed up capital. Telia can expect similar long-term structural cost benefits as its Swedish copper shutdown finishes in 2026.
16.
Other side of the story: While critics emphasize Telia’s historical struggles with net cost reduction, the company's Q3 2026 performance indicates that its structural reforms are successfully driving margin expansion. Morgan Stanley reported that group EBITDA beat consensus by 1% due to group cost savings. Additionally, operational expenses decreased by 2.6% in the third quarter of 2026. If service revenues continue to grow, Telia's restructured country-led model will deliver sustainable operating leverage over the next three years.

Telenor plans to remove 95% of its 140,000 km of legacy copper cables and 500,000 poles by the end of 2030. The copper infrastructure previously accounted for approximately half of the electricity consumed by the company's Norwegian network. Decommissioning the network secured annual electricity savings of 30 GWh by November 2025. These structural energy efficiencies will protect operating cash flows from volatile power prices.

Scheduled domestic exit: Telia Sweden is on track to complete its own legacy copper network shutdown by the end of 2026. The company had already successfully retired 96% of its domestic copper infrastructure by the end of 2025. This decommissioning process is being executed alongside the rapid rollout of high-speed fiber and 5G networks. Completing this exit will eliminate dual maintenance cost structures and accelerate EBITDA growth.

Mobile Generation Sunsetting

17.
Accelerated legacy sunset: Telecom operators across the Nordic region are decommissioning older mobile generations to optimize spectrum usage. Telia completed its 3G network shutdown in Sweden and Telenor finalized its 2G shutdown in Norway in late 2025. These closures allow operators to migrate traffic to more energy-efficient 4G and 5G systems. Phasing out these legacy technologies will deliver meaningful cost efficiencies over the next three years.
18.
Extended transitional timeline: Telia Sweden has adjusted its decommissioning schedule to protect vulnerable consumer segments. The company decided to postpone the full shutdown of its domestic 2G infrastructure until 2027. Management acknowledged that this delay incurs temporary additional costs but ensures a safe transition for public services. Consequently, the immediate opex savings from the full mobile legacy exit will be deferred until late 2027.

Radio Access Network Sharing Precedents

Material capital savings: Tele2 Sweden derives substantial capital expenditure efficiencies from its active infrastructure sharing joint venture with Telenor. The Net4Mobility joint venture, which is equally owned by both operators, builds and operates a combined mobile network. This shared infrastructure model has enabled Tele2 to report mobile capex-to-sales ratios as low as 5%. Sharing network resources represents a highly capital-efficient operational strategy that enhances overall returns.

These strong results demonstrate that active sharing drives operational scale benefits for both parent operators.

The two parent companies historically shared the construction and spectrum costs of this nationwide network. Following a strategic agreement, the partners completed the full shutdown of the SUNAB network at the end of 2025. Dismantling this legacy shared network will permanently reduce lease and maintenance outlays for both operators.

Section 6 – Peer Margin Benchmarking and Restructuring Cost Sustainability

Section summary
  1. Operational efficiency across Nordic telecom operators remains highly sensitive to commercial price intensity and recurring restructuring outlays.
  2. Telia Finland’s target of a forty percent EBITDA margin faces steep hurdles compared to DNA’s stable performance and Elisa’s structurally superior margin profile. Net read: Peers like Tele2 demonstrate that cost-driven EBITDA expansion quickly decelerates once initial workforce optimization programs are fully lapped.

Peer Margin Benchmarking

1.
Robust margin: DNA Finland maintained a solid operational profile despite facing intensified price competition in the mature Finnish mobile segment. Telenor reported that DNA achieved an adjusted EBITDA margin of 39.9% in the third quarter of 2026. However, DNA’s service revenue decreased 0.8% and its adjusted EBITDA fell 4% like-for-like during this period. This margin contraction highlights that even leading operators struggle to sustain high operating leverage during promotional down-cycles without stable top-line momentum, impacting long-term cash generation and EPS.
2.
Superior efficiency: Elisa Corporation continues to showcase defensive strength through its established domestic market lead and resilient profit margins. Company reports reveal that Elisa achieved a record-high comparable EBITDA margin of 35.8% in 2025. This stable margin performance was supported by a long-term capital expenditure cap of 12% of sales. However, Morgan Stanley reported that Elisa’s Q3 2026 comparable EBITDA missed consensus by 1.6% as mobile service revenue deteriorated to -2.3% year-on-year. This suggests that even elite operators must constantly reinvest cost savings to defend their market share.
3.
Ambitious goals: Telia Finland’s target of securing an adjusted EBITDA margin of 40% represents a major stretch compared to regional peers. Morgan Stanley noted that Telia Finland saw stable trends in Q3 2026 with EBITDA growing 0.9% due to successful local cost reductions. However, the country’s mobile service revenue fell 3.9%, meaning margin improvements rely heavily on temporary operational savings rather than organic expansion. Achieving the 40% threshold will remain difficult unless Telia can reverse this mobile decline and replicate DNA's structural network advantages, which directly impacts Telia's regional EPS contribution.
4.
Outperforming peers: Telenor Sweden demonstrates that combining structural simplification with solid commercial execution can yield record-breaking regional margins. Telenor reported that Telenor Sweden’s adjusted EBITDA grew by 3.8% in the third quarter of 2026, marking its fifteenth consecutive quarter of EBITDA growth. This growth pushed Telenor Sweden's EBITDA margin to an all-time high of 44%. This outstanding margin performance shows that disciplined cost control, including a 2% reduction in non-marketing opex, can successfully expand incumbent earnings.

Restructuring Cost Sustainability

5.
Elevated outlays: Elisa’s recurring restructuring charges show that sustaining operational efficiency requires continuous, capital-intensive corporate transformations. Morgan Stanley reported that Elisa’s Q4 2025 results included a massive one-off charge of €40 million, which was significantly higher than the expected €20 million. This charge included €26 million in restructuring costs to deliver 360 job cuts, illustrating that headcount optimization carries significant cash friction. Because these restructuring costs recur periodically, they act as a drag on reported earnings and raise doubts about the sustainability of net cost-out programs.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that certain Nordic operators can sustain operational improvements while successfully reducing restructuring expenses. Tele2 reported that its restructuring costs fell significantly to SEK 26 million in Q3 2026 compared to SEK 105 million in Q3 2025. This reduction reflects the winding down of major workforce optimization programs after Tele2 successfully removed 650 positions. However, Deutsche Bank Research warned that Swedish EBITDAaL growth slowed to just 0.8% in Q3 2026, meaning that lower restructuring outlays may simply signal the end of cost-cutting momentum.
7.
Persistent friction: Telenor Group continues to absorb material restructuring charges as it transitions to its simplified operating model. The company's Q3 2026 interim report showed that other operating expenses included NOK 323 million in restructuring costs, primarily concentrated in Telenor Norway and central corporate functions. This represents an increase compared to the prior year's quarter, indicating that organizational localization carries ongoing financial friction. These persistent charges indicate that incumbents cannot easily eliminate restructuring outlays, as modernizing systems and streamlining regional footprints require continuous capital reinvestment.
8.
Substantial transformation: Tele2's long-term transformation demonstrates the massive upfront cash requirements needed to execute deep workforce reductions. The company reported that items affecting comparability reached SEK 600 million for the full year of 2025, with restructuring costs totaling SEK 500 million. These expenses were mainly driven by workforce redundancy costs in Sweden. While these cuts successfully supported the group's underlying 11% organic EBITDAaL growth in 2025, the massive restructuring charges heavily penalized reported net profit during the execution phase, impacting short-term EPS.

Cost-Led EBITDA Deceleration

9.
Rapid deceleration: Tele2's performance in Sweden illustrates how quickly cost-led EBITDA growth can fade once a major efficiency program has run its course. Deutsche Bank Research reported that Swedish underlying EBITDAaL growth slowed to just 0.8% in Q3 2026 compared to 2.0% in the previous quarter. This sharp slowdown occurred because the domestic consumer segment grew marginally, reflecting intense pricing pressure. This performance confirms that without sustained service revenue growth, headcount-led margin expansion is temporary, as lapping tough cost comps quickly exposes underlying top-line weakness.
10.
Muted expectations: Rothschild & Co Redburn highlighted that Tele2 faces limited upside as the benefits of its 2025 transformation fade. The broker noted that the departure of CEO Jean-Marc Harion coincided with a slowdown in organic EBITDAaL growth, reflecting the difficulty of delivering further opex cuts. Although Xavier Niel's acquisition of a 20% stake initiated a successful turnaround, Tele2's multiple contraction in late 2026 shows that the market is already discounting the limits of cost-out strategies. This implies that future EPS upgrades must depend on commercial execution rather than further headcount downsizing.
11.
Lagging software: Elisa’s recent operating model changes highlight how structural shifts in non-telecom units can trigger elevated restructuring charges. DNB Carnegie reported that Elisa plans to restructure its international software business, Elisa Industriq, to reach its 25% EBITDA margin target. The broker added another EUR 15 million in restructuring charges for Q4 2026, which reduced Elisa's reported EPS by 3% for the fiscal year. This demonstrates that expanding into digital and software services introduces lumpy operational risks and requires continuous, unpredictable restructuring outlays that dilute underlying telecom operating leverage.
12.
Other side of the story: While critics argue that cost-led EBITDA growth is unsustainable, some evidence suggests that disciplined operators can transition to automated, low-touch models to permanently reduce opex. Tele2 managed to grow its underlying EBITDAaL by 3% organically in Q3 2026 through continued sharp cost control across all operations. If operators successfully utilize artificial intelligence and digital sales channels to reduce their baseline opex, they can protect their operating margins even during periods of low revenue growth. Consequently, structural cost-outs can still provide defensive support to earnings and dividends, provided competitive price intensity does not trigger severe ARPU erosion.

Overall confidence on the Key Question is medium. Confidence is higher on direction than on magnitude. The evidence supports that Telia carries a meaningful fixed-cost base and that gross cost-out is deliverable. It is medium to high on the sign of the effect, because operational expenses decreased by 2.6% in the third quarter of 2026 and the structural levers (copper shutdown, RAN sharing) have precedents. Section 8 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

  1. Telia's consensus expectation of cost-driven margin expansion could fail if wage inflation and aggressive challenger pricing erode top-line service revenue.
  2. Investors should monitor localized cost duplication and rising customer churn as early warning indicators of weak operating leverage.

1.
Unfavorable execution: Telia Company's consensus expectation that cost-cutting will flow seamlessly to the bottom line could be derailed if top-line growth stalls. Analysts from Morgan Stanley warn that EBITDA growth is heavily dependent on opex reduction, which becomes highly challenging if operating leverage starts working against the operator. A minor revenue decline would have a disproportionately severe impact on free cash flow for operators with low margins.

Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that wage inflation remains a key downside risk that could easily offset the margin expansion from structural opex cuts. Intense competition: Three Scandinavia has historically acted as an aggressive price challenger in the Swedish telecom market by introducing highly competitive family plans. Analysts from Rothschild and Co Redburn point out that the group has consistently absorbed between SEK 1.5 billion and SEK 2 billion in annual restructuring costs since 2022. Any failure to control local spending would cause country-level operational expenses to inflate and dilute group net savings.

Decreasing workforce productivity: Telia Company faces operational execution risks if persistent cost pressures and restructuring uncertainty damage employee morale and customer-facing service quality.
