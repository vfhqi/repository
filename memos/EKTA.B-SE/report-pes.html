Results-morning scoring under the Pre-Earnings SOP (step 9), in detail at Richard's request of 25 November 2026 ("The response should be more detailed, and cover most of the topics in the pre-earnings memo"). It scores the report against the Pre-earnings Memo of 24 November 2026, topic by topic. Written from the company's release (07:30 CET) before the 09:00 UK presentation; the share move and management's answers on the call follow in an update after the close. Negative numbers are in brackets.

Terms. Organic growth: sales growth at constant currency. Adjusted EBIT: the company's adjusted operating profit (earnings before interest and tax). EPS: earnings per share. Book-to-bill: orders divided by sales. TIMEA: Turkey, India, the Middle East and Africa. APJ: Asia-Pacific and Japan. Accela: the radiotherapy machine Varian (Siemens Healthineers) launched in late September 2026.

1. The verdict in brief

  • Outcome: "Clearly better", without a guidance raise. Sales grew 5% at constant currency against a bar of 1.9%, and adjusted EBIT was SEK 610m against SEK 494m. The memo gave this outcome 20% (balanced view) and 15% (sceptical view).
  • The memo's lean was wrong. It leaned modestly to the downside because it expected the last four quarters' pattern (profit at or above consensus, sales below). This is the first report under this chief executive with sales above consensus.
  • The memo's profit call was right, and too cautious. It said the profit bar was low and put profit at about SEK 530m to 570m; the result was SEK 610m.
  • The most important nuance: the sales beat came from converting old orders, not from new demand. Orders grew only 3% at constant currency and the quarter's book-to-bill was 1.00. China's sales growth is, in the company's words, "the conversion of strong order momentum from recent quarters into sales". Growth from the order book is real, but the evidence that demand itself has turned is not yet here.
  • Three weak spots: gross margin 39.0% (below about 39.3% to 40.0% expected; the beat came from operating costs); free cash flow SEK 48m against SEK 296m a year earlier; APJ sales and orders down.

3. What the rest of the year now needs

  • Sales: first-half growth is 2% at constant currency. The second half is about 54% of the year, so 2% for the year needs about 2% in the second half; 3% needs about 4%; the top of the range (4%) needs about 6%. The bottom of the range is now safe; the top needs the second half to beat the second quarter. (Derived.)
  • Margin: the first half is 12.7%. The bottom of the range (12.5%) needs about 12.3% in the second half; the top (13.5%) about 14.2%. Last year's second half was 15.6% as reported, flattered by development costs put on the balance sheet (about 1.8 points in last year's second quarter). The top of the margin range needs little or no improvement on last year's underlying second half, so unchanged guidance looks conservative. (Derived; the size of last year's second-half capitalisation benefit is not checked.)
  • What the memo said: the margin range was the safer one, and a soft second quarter threatened the sales range. The second quarter removed the sales risk and left the margin range looking conservative.

4. The scorecard verdicts, and how the lines interacted

  • The memo's rule was "orders and the Americas decide the reading; EBIT decides only whether it is an outright miss". The Americas decided it (+15%); orders were the weakest line (+3%). On the memo's own rule the reading is "growth arriving through the order book", not "growth arriving through demand".
  • Gross margin and EBIT pulled apart. Gross margin was below the bar while EBIT beat by SEK 116m, so the beat came from lower selling and administration costs, which more than offset lower capitalisation of development costs. A beat made of costs is lasting (the savings are "well above" SEK 500m a year); a beat made of the tariff refund is not. The refund's size is not in the release.
  • Cash flow did not follow profit. Free cash flow fell to SEK 48m on higher receivables and lower payables; the company expects "seasonal improved cash flow in the second half".

5. Which links in "How the issues link" held

  1. "Cost and price drive profit this year; volume does not": held, and more strongly than the memo assumed. The memo's arithmetic (repeat the first quarter's improvement) gave about SEK 550m; the result was SEK 610m.
  2. "Mix is the brake": held for gross margin (39.0%, down from 42.6% in the first quarter), but not for profit.
  3. "Orders drive the multiple": still open. Orders +3% and book-to-bill 1.00 do not yet show demand turning. The order book carried the quarter.
  4. "Currency flatters reported sales": held. Reported growth 7% against 5% at constant currency.
  5. "China and Accela are the two-sided swing": China delivered as expected (+11%); Accela is not mentioned in the release. The call is where that is tested.

8. Which view was closer, and why

  • The balanced view was closer, but neither view gave the actual outcome more than one chance in five.
  • What both views got wrong: they took the run-up and the four new Sell ratings as signs the shares would be sold, and gave too little weight to two things the memo itself recorded: the sales bar was the easiest in a year (1.9% against 3.3% in May), and China's orders had grown for three quarters and were due to convert.
  • What the sceptical view got right: the quality questions. Gross margin below the bar, weak cash flow and orders growing only 3% are the sceptic's points, and they are what the market may weigh after the first move.

9. The strongest case against the lean was the one that happened

The memo wrote that orders "of SEK 4.3bn or more with Americas and China both up double digits would turn "profit without growth" into "growth arriving" for the first time under this chief executive". Sales did that (Americas +15%, China +11%). Orders in kronor were about SEK 4.36bn, but only because of the sales they matched; at constant currency they grew 3%. So the case against the lean won on sales and only half-won on orders.

10. Threats, risks and fragilities: which showed

  • Showed: gross margin under pressure from mix (39.0%); weak cash conversion; APJ declining.
  • Did not show: a slowdown in the Americas (it grew 15%); a guidance cut or narrowing.
  • Not yet tested: Accela's effect on US orders; China pricing; the second-half hardware load.

12. The 12-to-24-month case: which of the memo's three signs moved

  • Sign 1, a first guidance raise: not yet. Guidance was unchanged; the arithmetic in section 3 makes a raise more likely in early March 2027.
  • Sign 2, two quarters of organic growth of 4% or more with gross margin above 40% excluding refunds: half met. Organic growth was 5%; gross margin was 39.0% including the refund. One quarter of two.
  • Sign 3, 2027/28 guidance at or above consensus (late May 2027): not yet testable.
  • Net: the revenue half of the case, which the memo said decides it, got its first evidence; the margin half was confirmed again.

13. Likely changes to consensus
