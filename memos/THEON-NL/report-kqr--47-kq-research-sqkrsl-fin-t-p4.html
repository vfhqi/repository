This peer model suggests that Theon's 51% Safran joint venture will require tight governance agreements to prevent operational deadlocks.

8.
Highly favorable: Theon operates its corporate holding structure out of Cyprus, which utilizes a highly tax-efficient holding company regime. Under Cyprus tax regulations, capital gains realized from the disposal of shares or other corporate securities are entirely exempt from corporate tax unless the underlying assets include immovable property located in Cyprus. This means any potential sale of the Exosens stake would generate taxfree proceeds at the corporate level.
9.
Contrary evidence: Theon's dividend distributions from its Cyprus operations are subject to a 17% special defense contribution in 2026 if paid to local tax-resident individuals. Additionally, the company must pay a 2.65% healthcare contribution on behalf of resident shareholders regardless of domicile. These local Cypriot taxes create a modest leakage on cash distributed to Cypriot natural persons, although corporate-level gains remain fully shielded.
10.
Strategic structuring: Theon's use of Cypriot holding entities provides a far more favorable tax framework than Nordic defense groups like Kongsberg Gruppen or Saab, which face higher domestic statutory rates. Saab AG reported effective tax rates of 20.7% in fiscal year 2025, showing how Cypriot tax exemptions protect capital gains far better than high-tax mainland European frameworks. This efficiency ensures maximum capital is retained for debt reduction upon any asset divestment.
