Section 2 – Tax Expense, Profit Before Tax and Effective Tax Rates

Section summary
  1. Maersk's effective tax rate exhibits extreme volatility because of the tonnage tax regime on ocean shipping and standard corporate tax rates on terminal operations.
  2. The group's consolidated tax expense remains highly sensitive to profit fluctuations between standard corporate tax jurisdictions and shipping-tax regimes.

Historical Annual Tax Trends

1.
Fluctuating historical rates: Maersk recorded a profit before tax of USD 965m and tax of USD 458m in 2019, which represented an effective tax rate of 47.5%. This high rate was followed by a tax rate of 12.3% in 2020 on profit before tax of USD 3,307m and tax of USD 407m. These figures indicate how much earnings volatility impacts the ETR under the tonnage tax system because shipping volumes can swing rapidly.
2.
Declining tax burden: Maersk achieved exceptional profitability during the pandemic peak as profit before tax reached USD 18,730m in 2021 and USD 30,231m in 2022. The corresponding tax expenses were USD 697m in 2021 and USD 910m in 2022, which drove effective tax rates down to 3.7% and 3.0% respectively. This suggests that huge shipping profits are highly sheltered from standard corporate tax rates because tonnage taxes are calculated on fleet capacity rather than actual earnings.
3.
Elevated post-peak taxation: Maersk experienced a transition in 2023 with profit before tax falling to USD 4,362m and tax expenses recorded at USD 454m, yielding an ETR of 10.4%. In 2024, profit before tax rose to USD 6,816m with a tax expense of USD 584m, representing an ETR of 8.6%. This transition demonstrates that earnings normalization alters the tax mix as non-shipping terminal activities make up a larger share of total taxable income.
4.
Contrary evidence: Maersk reported a tax expense of USD 556m on profit before tax of USD 3,471m in 2025, which represents a high effective tax rate of 16.0% compared to only 8.6% in 2024. This confirms that tax expenses can rise significantly even when pre-tax profits are cut in half because of specific deferred tax reassessments and local adjustments.
