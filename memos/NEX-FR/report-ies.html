Nexans (NEX-FR): what the Q3 2026 sales of 22 October 2026 do to the existing case

These results strengthen the existing case modestly, on the one leg it rated weakest, revenue growth, while leaving the two lines it said would decide it, the Transmission margin and the replacement order for the mass-impregnated line, exactly where they were; and the price going into them sat at the case's own progress threshold, which is the larger fact.

What changed

  • Group Q3 2026 standard sales were €1,698.2m, up 21.4 percent reported and 9.0 percent organic, against consensus of 4.6 to 5.1 percent organic. Scope added 9.6 percent and currency 2.8 percent in the quarter. Nine-month organic growth was 3.9 percent, 5.3 percent in Electrification. The case carried organic growth of +1.5 percent for H1 2026 and rated revenue growth D on it.
  • PWR-Grid grew 14.8 percent organic to €363.9m against consensus of 6.1 to 7.0 percent, an 870-basis-point beat on Jefferies' numbers. Management attributed it to European capacity ramp-up and seasonality, North American utility and data-centre frame agreements, and solar projects in South America, Turkey and Morocco, and committed about €62m to raise medium-voltage accessories capacity by 39 percent between 2026 and 2028. The case had Grid at +4.9 percent organic in H1 2026, "capped by capacity until late 2026".
  • PWR-Connect grew 10.5 percent organic to €749.8m against consensus of 6.3 to 9.0 percent. The scope contribution was 24.7 percent against the 28 percent consensus had assumed for Republic Wire and the earlier acquisitions; integration is described as "on track". No segment margin was given.
  • PWR-Transmission fell 4.0 percent organic on sales of €378.3m, against consensus of flat to -1.7 percent, after +33.3 percent in Q3 2025; the Q4 2025 comparison is +40.0 percent. The adjusted backlog was €7.8bn at 30 September 2026, up 0.7 percent on June. The replacement mass-impregnated order, which 2026 guidance assumes loads the Halden line at year-end, was not announced. The one new award, preferred-supplier status on Mallorca–Menorca 3, is a 132 kV alternating-current link, extruded rather than mass-impregnated on Bernstein's reading, with a six-month engineering phase before any contract and delivery from mid-2027.
  • Other Activities grew 24.7 percent organic against a -6.3 percent comparison created by the 2025 tariff pull-forward; the nine-month figure is still -5.2 percent.
  • Guidance was confirmed, not raised: adjusted EBITDA €770m to €840m and free cash flow €235m to €325m for 2026, with consensus already at about €820m and €258m. No Q3 or nine-month adjusted EBITDA, margin, net income, earnings per share, free cash flow, working capital or capex was disclosed. The last profit anchor is H1 2026: adjusted EBITDA €387.7m at 11.9 percent, continuing diluted earnings per share €2.68 against €3.15, cash conversion 42.7 percent.
  • The sell-side held its ground. Jefferies kept Buy and €158, calling the quarter "strong overall"; Bernstein kept Market-Perform and €150, expecting a "moderately positive" reaction, with 2026 and 2027 adjusted earnings per share of €7.91 and €9.95 unchanged. Neither note carried a revised estimate table. Deutsche Bank entered at Buy and €179.
  • The price going into the print was €122.0 on 21 October 2026 (previous close €118.6), 10.1 percent below the 200-day average, with relative strength at the 27th percentile of the universe. The dashboard's pins moved before the results: technical momentum D (from the C the memo pinned), sell-side earnings momentum B (unchanged), valuation C (from D), the weekly thematic reading D (from C) across the whole sector. Consensus 24-month-forward earnings per share are €10.79, against €10.78 at the memo; one-month revisions are -2 percent and the revision-momentum score is 5, from 7. The post-results price is not in the data.

The challenge

The results admit three readings the case does not hold, and none of them can be knocked down on the evidence.

First: Grid is not capacity-capped at mid-single digits; the demand inflection has reached the company's own volume. The case's shape rests on one finding above all others: that the forces convert into margin and cash by choice, not into revenue, which is why 'Financial Outputs' is a C, 'Demand Inflection' fits the sector better than the company, and the company's €1,150m target for 2028 is "possible, not probable" while we take €1,010m. Grid at 14.8 percent organic against a mid-single-digit guideline, broad across geographies and end markets, with about two-thirds of its demand under frameworks, with customers committing earlier to capacity because high-voltage lead times exceed 12 to 14 months, and with management adding accessories capacity on top of above-target growth, is the opposite of a company holding volume back. If this holds, the organic leg of the case's revenue decomposition (3 to 4 percent a year) is wrong by half, the 2028 target becomes the modal path rather than the upper branch, and on the case's own arithmetic that path puts earnings per share near 13 and the three-year rate near 17 percent, a B or better at any price inside the ten-year range. What would have to be true: that the quarter is not the seasonal peak management itself flagged (Europe benefits from Q2 and Q3), that Grid's Q4 and full-year prints hold high-single-digit growth against normal comparisons, and that the Grid margin holds about 15 percent as the capacity lands, which no figure in this release tests. The case has no branch for this; its upside branch is the multiple, not the volume.

Second: this is a beat that cannot be audited, and the refusal to raise guidance is the tell. Q3 was the easiest quarter in which to show profit: the seasonal high for Grid, a Transmission margin management says is improving, a Connect book growing 10.5 percent organic. The company published revenue and withheld every profit, margin and cash line. It then left an EBITDA range whose midpoint sits €15m below consensus untouched after a 4 percent revenue beat, with Bernstein noting that consensus already needs about 140 basis points of second-half margin improvement. Read in its strongest form: the mix is diluting under the top line. Connect's growth is coming from hyperscale data centres, which Bernstein says carry lower gross margins than the acquired residential and commercial book; Republic Wire's scope came in about 3 points short; European construction has fallen for 53 consecutive months; Transmission volume fell 4.0 percent with fixed costs at Halden unabsorbed; Metallurgy's 24.7 percent is a comparison effect. The case already names this as the "false friend" and dates its test to the Connect margin; what it does not accommodate is a quarter in which the test is due and the company declines to sit it. If this reading is right, FY2026 EBITDA lands at or below the €805m midpoint, FY2027 consensus of 9.18 is cut rather than confirmed, the case's Condition 2 fails, and the multiple blend falls from 13.2x toward 13.0x or 11.2x. What would have to be true: an H2 2026 Transmission margin below 13 percent, a Connect margin below 11.5 percent, or full-year cash conversion at the bottom of the range, all of which arrive only in February 2027.

Third: the Halden problem is a technology question inside the horizon, not a timing gap after 2028. The case holds the mass-impregnated line "fully loaded until mid-2028" on a replacement contract judged probable at about 75 percent, holds subsea share "stable over three to five years", and dates the loss of mega-tenders to capacity-expanding peers beyond the horizon. Against that: the order the guide assumes is still unsigned with one quarter of the case's window used; the backlog grew 0.7 percent in a quarter in which Prysmian's Transmission grew 14.3 percent; the one new award is extruded, not mass-impregnated; a Prysmian transmission director estimates Nexans has lost 3 to 4 points of European high-voltage subsea share, and a Prysmian project director expects extruded cable to displace mass-impregnated technology over five to ten years. Read together, the order book is refilling with the technology the Halden line does not make, and the replacement, when it comes, may come on the thin economics of a company keeping a strategic asset loaded, which Bernstein names as the bargaining-power risk. The case's Branch A (25 percent: the contract fails, 11.2x) is the nearest it has to this, and at €122.0 on €10.79 of 24-month-forward consensus the shares already trade at about 11.3x, the scare multiple, without the scare having returned. What would have to be true: no signed mass-impregnated order by the end of Q1 2027 (the case's own invalidate level), or an order signed with a disclosed margin below the segment's. The expert evidence is opinion, not audited share data; it is nevertheless consistent with the quarter.

The reading that fails: that guidance confirmation on a revenue beat signals a weak second half. The company's record is three beats in four prints, the initial 2026 guide excluded Republic Wire and GSI and was called "surprisingly light", and the case already rates guidance conservatism B. Confirmation is the company's habit, not news.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on, will carry what this one could not: the post-results price against the €120 to €122 threshold, and whether Jefferies, Bernstein, Deutsche Bank and the pool moved FY2026 to FY2028 estimates and targets, which decides whether the Grid reading has begun to reach the out-years. It will not carry a Q3 margin, a cash figure or the mass-impregnated order, because none was disclosed and no re-run creates them.
  • The replacement order: the company and both brokers expect it by the end of 2026; the case's invalidate level is the end of Q1 2027. Contract value, delivery schedule and margin are not disclosures the company has committed to, so an order could arrive and leave the third reading open.
  • The Q4 sales print, around February 2027: Grid against an unflattered comparison settles seasonal against structural; Transmission against +40.0 percent settles nothing on its own.
  • The full-year 2026 results in late February 2027: the Transmission margin (the case's progress line at 15 percent or better; alarm below 13 percent), the Connect margin (alarm below 11.5 percent; invalidate below 11 percent for two halves), full-year cash conversion against the 42.7 percent of H1, and the first synergy disclosure (alarm: none). These are the only disclosures that answer the second reading, and they are four months away.
  • Halden utilisation is not a published number and must still be read from management's words.
