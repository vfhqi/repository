Section 3 – Tax Rate Outlook and Intellectual Property Payments

Section summary
  1. Coloplast's effective tax rate peaked at 41% in fiscal year 2024/25 due to an extraordinary Icelandic intellectual property transfer.
  2. Management expects the effective tax rate to normalize to around 22% starting in fiscal year 2025/26 as Danish tax offsets begin.
  3. Listed peers maintain stable structural tax rates ranging from 17% to 24%, highlighting the uniqueness of Coloplast's transaction.

Icelandic IP Transfer Impact

1.
Severe spike: Coloplast's effective tax rate jumped to 40.9% in the fiscal year 2024/25 compared to its historical baseline of 22% as a result of the Kerecis integration. This dramatic increase was driven by an extraordinary tax expense of DKK 1,146 million associated with transferring Kerecis intellectual property from Iceland to Denmark. Management implemented this transfer to ensure alignment with the group's principal operating and tax model. This temporary rate spike severely depressed reported net profit for the year.
2.
Normalizing outlook: Coloplast expects its effective tax rate to decline sharply to approximately 22% for the fiscal year 2025/26. Senior leadership confirmed during the full-year earnings call that the underlying tax rate is anticipated to remain stable at this level during the Impact4 strategic period. This projected rate will benefit from the completed integration of recent intellectual property transfers for both Kerecis and Atos Medical. Lower tax rates will directly boost future earnings per share starting in late 2026.
3.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that regulatory changes and local tax audit adjustments could disrupt Coloplast's target of maintaining a stable 22% ordinary tax rate. DNB Carnegie reported on November 9, 2026, that international tariff updates and transfer pricing complexities introduce persistent tax risks for medical device companies operating across multiple jurisdictions. If global tax policies shift unfavourably, the company's long-term tax rate could rise toward 23% or higher. This indicates that the long-term tax structure remains exposed to external policy shifts.
4.
Integration execution: Coloplast initiated the transfer of Kerecis intellectual property to facilitate the operational integration of the business into its global IT infrastructure. Leadership explained on the Q1 2024/25 earnings call that establishing a simplified structure is essential for scaling the newly acquired fish-skin biologics unit. The tax hit represents a necessary upfront friction cost of simplifying corporate operating models after large transactions. This operational alignment helps explain why the massive charge was accepted.

Tax Cash Flows and Offsets

5.
Cash timing: Coloplast faces a substantial cash tax payment in Iceland of approximately DKK 2.0 billion resulting from the Kerecis asset relocation. DNB Carnegie reported on February 4, 2025, that this extraordinary cash outflow will take place in the fiscal year 2026/27 at the earliest. This delayed cash payment means that nearterm free cash flow is insulated from immediate tax outflows related to Kerecis.
6.
Offsetting mechanism: Coloplast will fully offset its Icelandic cash tax payment through reduced corporate income tax payments in Denmark over a multi-year period. Management disclosed in its interim financial reports that Danish tax deductions began in the 2024/25 fiscal year and will stretch over approximately seven years. This offsetting structure ensures that the total transaction is net-neutral on a cash basis over the long term. Consequently, the cumulative cash flow impact will balance out by 2031/32 according to corporate plans.
7.
Prior precedent: Coloplast's current tax arrangement closely mirrors the prior structural intellectual property transfer of Atos Medical which occurred after its acquisition. Note 12 of the 2024/25 annual report outlines that the Atos Medical transaction triggered a net cash tax payment of DKK 2.5 billion paid in the second quarter of fiscal year 2023/24. That payment is also being offset by lower Danish corporate tax payments in subsequent years. This historical precedent demonstrates management's consistent use of upfront tax cash payments to consolidate intellectual property.

Broker Models and Estimates

8.
Estimate reductions: Broker analysts adjusted their near-term earnings models downward to reflect the severe impact of the Icelandic tax transfer. DNB Carnegie on February 4, 2025, cut its 2024/25 earnings per share forecasts for Coloplast by 22% solely due to the extraordinary tax charge. This modeling correction highlights how non-operating items can severely distort reported annual earnings regardless of solid underlying growth.
9.
Valuation adjustment: Investment analysts look past the distorted reported figures of 2024/25 to assess Coloplast's underlying business value. Bernstein Research reported on February 4, 2025, that adjusted net profit before special items grew 17% in Q1 2024/25 when isolating the Kerecis intellectual property charge. This adjustment allows investors to focus on core operating momentum rather than non-cash corporate structuring events.
10.
Future payments: No further large-scale intellectual property transfers or related extraordinary tax payments are projected over the next 18 months. Company documents indicate that the strategic integration of both Atos Medical and Kerecis is now legally and operationally complete. This lack of upcoming relocations suggests that Coloplast's tax rate will stabilize without further extraordinary charges through 2028.

Comparative Peer Tax Profiles

11.
Peer comparison: Coloplast's normalized tax rate is highly comparable to the tax rates of other Danish healthcare peers. Hearing aid manufacturer Demant guided for an effective tax rate of around 23% for the 2026 fiscal year. Similarly, single-use endoscopy leader Ambu reported an effective tax rate of 23.6% in its 2025/26 annual report. These metrics show that a tax rate of 22% to 23% represents a highly standard rate for Danish corporations.
12.
Peer setups: Medical technology peers based in the United Kingdom display slightly higher structural tax rates. Convatec reported an adjusted effective tax rate of 23.0% for the first half of 2026, which was slightly lower than its prior-year rate of 24.0%. Meanwhile, Smith & Nephew has historically recorded group tax rates ranging from 17% to 23% depending on US profit mix. These comparisons indicate that Coloplast's normalized rate is aligned with international medtech benchmarks across Europe.
13.
Swiss contrast: Dental implant maker Straumann operates under a highly contrasting tax environment with lower base rates. Straumann recorded an effective tax rate of 21.3% in 2025, although its Swiss entities faced a much lower local rate of 11.9% prior to global minimum tax rules. The adoption of the Pillar Two qualified domestic top-up tax has pushed Straumann's effective rate upward. This demonstrates how global regulatory reforms are gradually narrowing the tax advantages of historically low-tax jurisdictions.
14.
Other side of the story: While Coloplast's underlying tax rate has stabilized, the cash flow timing gap of the Icelandic transaction poses a temporary risk to liquidity. The DKK 2.0 billion Icelandic cash outflow in 2026/27 occurs as a single lump sum, whereas the Danish tax offsets are diluted over a seven-year period. Consequently, this structural mismatch could temporarily constrain free cash flow conversion during the peak payment year. Therefore, focusing only on the P&L tax rate risks overlooking near-term cash flow pressures.

Section 5 – FX Sensitivities, Hedging and Exchange Rate Impacts

Section summary

  4. Coloplast targets gradual deleveraging to below 2.0 times net debt to EBITDA by fiscal year 2029/30 while maintaining its flat dividend.

This positive HUF sensitivity exists because the majority of production is based in Hungary while local sales are minimal.
