Industry Age Profile and Replacement Share

1.
Aging machinery base: According to TKH Group's presentation on 14 September 2026, a massive portion of the global tyre manufacturing base is currently outdated. The company reported that 40% of the non-TKH installed machine base is now older than twenty years. This aging infrastructure cannot efficiently produce modern high-performance tyres, which means that replacement spending will dictate capital budgets.
2.
Dominant replacement spending: TKH Group's investor presentation on 14 September 2026 revealed that replacement of existing tyre-building machines represents approximately 75% to 80% of total machinery sales. In contrast, new machine additions for greenfield capacity represent a minor 20% to 25% share of the market. This structural skew implies that standalone replacement capital expenditure will dominate industry demand during the 2027 to 2028 recovery cycle.
3.
Tier one outsourcing shift: Jefferies reported on 24 May 2024 that major tier-1 tyre manufacturers like Michelin and Bridgestone traditionally engineered their own tyre production machines. This in-house engineering has resulted in a relatively older, inefficient installed base compared to competitors who deployed modern commercial systems. To bridge this technology gap, these market leaders are increasingly choosing to outsource their machine manufacturing to specialists.
4.
Peer investment comparison: Pirelli's annual report on 29 July 2026 indicated that its capital expenditure for 2026 is budgeted at approximately 6.5% of revenues. This allocation is comparable to Continental's planned capital expenditure of 5.5% of sales through 2028 as detailed in Fitch's press release on 20 July 2026. These similar investment rates indicate that tier-1 players are focusing their resources on upgrading existing premium facilities rather than launching massive new greenfield expansions.

Regulatory Drivers and Euro Standards

5.

The newly adopted Euro seven tyre abrasion standards will introduce strict technical limits starting in late 2026. This regulation forces manufacturers to rapidly upgrade their processing machinery because legacy tyre-building platforms lack the precision to handle low-emission rubber compounds.
6.
Testing and compliance demands: Link Engineering Company's press release on 7 April 2026 announced its recognition by the United Nations as an equivalent facility for tyre abrasion measurements. This regulatory milestone establishes the technical testing foundation that tyre makers must meet to achieve Euro seven compliance. Consequently, manufacturers must replace legacy machinery with advanced building platforms to ensure consistent product quality and avoid regulatory penalties.

These stringent parameters mean tyre makers must retire obsolete processing equipment to adopt highly precise, modern manufacturing systems.

Section 4 – Reception and Adoption of the UNIXX Platform by Tier

Section summary
  1. Standing at a premium price point of €10 to €12 million, the UNIXX platform represents a highly automated, "hands-off, eyes-off" tire-building revolution.
  2. Tier 1 tire manufacturers display structural reluctance toward capital-intensive investments, temporarily slowing large-scale UNIXX orders.
  3. Tier 2 and Tier 3 operators are actively adopting high-end VMI systems, creating competitive pressure that will eventually force Tier 1 reinvestment.
  4. Derived modular technologies like the UNIXX Belt Maker provide lower-risk entry points, mitigating high upfront system costs.

Value proposition and technical architecture

1.
Technological revolution: The UNIXX platform represents a fundamental paradigm shift in tire manufacturing by merging component preparation and assembly into a single, highly integrated system. This integration is driven by strip winding technology, which allows base materials to be fed directly from reels and assembled on the tire line itself. This streamlined process eliminates the need for expensive, space-consuming upstream component prep machinery, significantly optimizing factory floors. Consequently, the smart factory of the future can operate with a much smaller industrial footprint. Resource and energy efficiency: VMI's UNIXX platform delivers significant environmental and cost benefits that directly address the sustainability targets of global tire manufacturers. The system is engineered to optimize the manufacturing process, yielding expected scrap savings of more than 100 tons of rubber and steel cord per year. Furthermore, the use of thinner, higher-performance materials can save up to 550 tons of rubber annually. These compounding material efficiencies lead to a direct reduction in the carbon footprint per tire. Premium pricing dynamics: According to TKH Group's August 2026 investor presentation, the complete UNIXX system is priced at a premium of €10 to €12 million per system. This pricing is approximately two to three times the cost of VMI’s standard MAXX tire-building machine. Despite the premium, the system is designed to lower total cost of ownership by eliminating separate component preparation lines. Therefore, while the initial capital requirement is high, the investment yields significant long-term operating cost advantages.

Adoption barriers and market tier dynamics

2.
Tier 1 investment reluctance: The primary commercial barrier for the UNIXX platform is the general reluctance of Tier 1 tire makers to commit to major capital-intensive projects. High interest rates, elevated energy costs, and persistent geopolitical uncertainties have caused Tier 1 operators to delay large-scale machinery orders. This cautious spending environment has directly impacted VMI's order book, as major manufacturers prioritize cash preservation over plant upgrades. Consequently, near-term adoption among top-tier brands is constrained by tight corporate capex controls.
3.
Product qualification timelines: Implementing a completely new tire-building platform requires a lengthy, meticulous product qualification process. Tire makers must thoroughly test and qualify all new tire designs, particularly for original equipment (OE) vehicle fittings, which can take anywhere from several months to over a year. To mitigate this qualification bottleneck and lower initial investment risks, VMI has introduced a modular sales strategy. This approach allows customers to purchase individual UNIXX modules, such as the FLEXX belt maker, before committing to a full system. Competitive pressure from lower tiers: While Tier 1 players have hesitated, Tier 2 and Tier 3 tire makers have actively invested in VMI’s high-end technologies over recent years. These mid-tier manufacturers are leveraging advanced automation to close the quality and efficiency gap with industry leaders. Management anticipates that Tier 1 operators will eventually be forced to resume their investment plans to avoid losing market share to these technologically advancing Tier 2 and Tier 3 competitors. This competitive dynamic is expected to drive a structural wave of Tier 1 orders as economic conditions normalize.

Commercial pipeline and derived technologies

4.
Modular commercialization path: VMI has successfully commercialized the modular elements of the UNIXX platform, creating immediate revenue streams while the market for full systems develops.
