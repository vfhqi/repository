Section 2 – Brazilian Indirect Tax Disputes and Judicial Deposits

Section summary
  1. Unilever faces significant contingent liabilities from its Brazilian indirect tax disputes that could eventually transition to judicial courts.
  2. The eventual requirement for judicial deposits or bank guarantees poses a substantial cash-drain risk that peer precedents suggest can be managed without immediate liquidity crises.

Unilever Contingent Liabilities

1.
Escalating liabilities: Unilever reported a massive €3,557m contingent liability in its FY2025 accounts regarding indirect tax disputes from a local 2001 corporate reorganization. This represents an increase of €327m compared to the €3,230m contingent liability reported in its FY2024 financial accounts. The rising dispute value reflects the continuous accrual of interest and penalties on the disputed taxes. This escalating exposure could eventually compress Unilever's future cash flows if liabilities are realized.
2.
Strained timelines: Unilever expects that its largest tax litigation cases will eventually transition from administrative tribunals to federal judicial courts. The company previously expected that three of its largest cases representing €1.8bn in liabilities would transition as early as 2020. This transition would require Unilever to make cash judicial deposits or secure expensive bank guarantees to continue disputing the assessments. Any immediate requirement for cash deposits would lock up liquidity and reduce funds available for share buybacks.
3.
Persistent confidence: Unilever management continues to hold zero provisions for these Brazilian indirect tax disputes because they believe their legal position is highly robust. The original tax dispute from the 2001 reorganization was already resolved in the courts in Unilever's favor before new assessments were raised. KPMG continued to identify these indirect tax disputes as a critical audit matter due to the complexity of local jurisprudence. The company's audit committee agreed that holding zero tax provisions was appropriate given the low likelihood of loss.

Peer Tax Precedents in Brazil

4.
Comparable exposures: Carrefour Group is exposed to significant tax risks in Brazil that reached approximately €2.8bn as of June 30, 2026. This exposure represents a €0.5bn increase from the €2.3bn in tax risks recorded at the end of December 31, 2025. The tax assessments primarily concern the deductibility of goodwill amortization and state-level value-added tax credits. Carrefour has also chosen not to record provisions for the majority of these disputes as they assess the likelihood of outflow as remote.
5.
Heavy cash-drain: Carrefour has historically made monthly judicial deposits in Brazil to discuss the full use of PIS and COFINS credits. The consolidated judicial deposits and collateral for its Brazilian subsidiary Atacadão reached R$2,935m as of March 31, 2024. Carrefour Finance also extended inter-company credit lines of 10.55 billion Brazilian reals in late 2025 to manage Atacadão's debt restructuring. These substantial cash commitments show that judicial disputes demand heavy liquidity from parent companies.
6.
Mitigated cash-drain: Ambev S.A. faces an enormous R$33.5bn dispute regarding the deductibility of interest on capital expenses as of September 30, 2026. The company has received multiple assessments disallowing goodwill amortization expenses from historical mergers, including a R$1.9bn case. Ambev management has successfully obtained injunctions to suspend tax credit enforceability without immediate cash deposits. The beer manufacturer relies on guarantee-insurance bonds and bank letters of guarantee rather than cash to secure its tax lawsuits.
7.
Escalating provisions: Heineken NV maintained provisions for taxes of €331m in its FY2025 accounts, which mainly relate to Brazilian indirect tax disputes. This represents an increase of €38m compared to the €293m in tax provisions held at the end of fiscal year 2024. Heineken was historically requested by Brazilian tax authorities to collateralise pending tax contingencies of €416m using fixed assets or lines of credit. The complexity of local tax legislation makes the timing of Heineken's cash outflows highly uncertain.

Mitigation Strategies and Judicial Deposits

8.
Contrary evidence: Petrobras avoided cash-drain from tax lawsuits in a September 30, 2026 report by utilizing production capacity guarantees instead of cash. The energy giant signed a Negotiated Legal Proceeding agreement with the National Treasury to discuss disputes without immediate disbursement. This structural agreement released cash for other processes while ensuring federal tax regularity was maintained.
9.
Structural disputes: Ambev's subsidiary Arosuco transitioned its PIS and COFINS dispute to the federal judicial level after receiving an unfavorable administrative decision in April 2024. Ambev management estimates the possible loss from these judicial proceedings at R$7.5bn as of June 30, 2026. These massive disputes often originate from complex corporate reorganizations that tax authorities subsequently challenge for lacking a valid business purpose.
10.
Persistent disputes: Heineken recognized €243m in tax credits in its 2021 interim report following a favorable Brazilian Supreme Court ruling on ICMS exclusion. Ambev similarly recorded significant PIS and COFINS credits from the exclusion of ICMS from its calculation bases over several years. However, the Brazilian tax authorities frequently contest these offsets and issue new tax assessments that drag companies back into court for years.
11.
Delayed resolutions: Legal proceedings in Brazil are notorious for lasting decades before reaching a final, unappealable judicial ruling. Ambev's disallowance of goodwill amortization from a 2005 transaction was still being discussed in court as of late 2026. This sluggish judicial timetable means that any cash outlays or deposits will likely be deferred over many years, limiting any sudden impact on annual earnings.
12.
Severe cash-drain: Unilever has a solid liquidity position with €0.6bn in cash and €5,200m in undrawn credit facilities as of December 31, 2025. However, the sheer size of the €3,557m Brazilian indirect tax dispute could severely strain its cash reserves if judicial transitions require full deposits. Unilever's annual free cash flow of €5.2bn could be largely consumed by these combined tax and separation payments. This would limit the remaining cash available to fund its planned share buybacks and pay dividends.
13.
Other side of the story: Unilever's actual cash-drain from Brazilian indirect tax disputes is highly unlikely to materialize in the near term because of long-standing legal precedents. The company has successfully defended its position in court since 2004 and continues to avoid recording any financial provisions. Peer cases like Ambev demonstrate that multinational companies can effectively utilize guarantee-insurance bonds or letters of guarantee rather than cash to secure judicial proceedings. This suggests that even if cases move to federal courts, Unilever will avoid direct cash-drain, leaving its capital allocation plan intact.

Section summary
  1. Unilever expects a cash tax and separation envelope of five billion euros on its Foods transaction. Net read: Independent estimates suggest international tax leakage could exceed official guidance if tax-free exemptions are challenged.
  2. Separation payments and restructuring cash outflows will be distributed over a three-year period post-transaction close.

1.
Substantive cash inflows: Unilever expects to receive substantial upfront cash proceeds of $15.7 bn, equivalent to approximately €14 bn, from the Foods transaction. According to a company debt issuance prospectus published on May 15, 2026, these proceeds are subject to certain closing adjustments. Management claims that these cash receipts will fully offset one-off separation and tax costs while supporting subsequent share buybacks.
2.
Managed tax liabilities: Unilever has designed the merger of its Foods business with McCormick as a Reverse Morris Trust to limit domestic tax exposure. A company press release dated March 27, 2026, states that the transaction is structured to be tax-free for United States federal income tax purposes. This tax-efficient structure is intended to mitigate some of the overall transaction-related tax costs for the parent company and its shareholders.
3.
General corporate guidance: Unilever management has guided to a broad cash envelope of approximately €5 bn to cover all transaction, restructuring, and tax separation outlays. During an investor event on November 4, 2026, the company disclosed that this €5 bn envelope includes all separation taxes and restructuring costs. Keeping separation costs within this guided envelope is vital to protect the retained business forward earnings capacity.
4.
Contrary evidence: Unilever disclosed in an investor event on November 4, 2026, that it might secure a slightly higher amount of available cash than originally guided if transaction processes are optimized. The presentation indicated that separation and tax costs are running within guided ballparks, but more surplus cash could remain. This potential surplus would provide extra capital to support the group's planned share buybacks, which would accelerate earnings per share growth.

This debt reduction strategy allows Unilever to avoid any debt refinancing in the market until 2028. Avoiding expensive debt refinancing protects the group's net finance costs and supports its modest operating margin expansion.

Stretched cash timelines: Unilever's actual cash outflows for separation taxes and restructuring will likely be spread widely across the 2027 to 2029 fiscal years. While the transaction is guided to close in mid-2027, the €1.2 bn restructuring spend and multi-year TSAs confirm that physical separation is a multi-year process. This staggered outflow means that the company's free cash flow will face moderate, persistent pressure rather than a single, devastating hit. Consequently, the remaining proceeds will steadily support the planned €6 bn share buyback program, preserving the company's mid-term EPS targets.
13.
Other side of the story: Unilever's international tax leakage could be much lower than independent estimates suggest because of structural tax exemptions under local jurisdictions. While Jefferies modeled up to €2.7 bn in international tax leakage, the company's Reverse Morris Trust structure is designed to optimize tax efficiency across multiple operating segments. If local tax authorities approve the tax-free status of the split-off, the actual cash tax leakage could fall significantly below the guided €5 bn separation budget. This positive tax resolution would free up more immediate cash to accelerate share repurchases and drive higher forward EPS.

  1. Unilever is undergoing a major structural transformation by spinning off Ice Cream and establishing six regional performance units.

Portfolio Pruning

Successful separation: Unilever completed the demerger of its Ice Cream business on December 6, 2025, establishing The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V. as an independent listed entity. The parent company distributed an 80.15% stake to its shareholders while retaining a 19.85% minority interest to maintain capital flexibility. This structural separation represents a key step in transforming the group into a pureplay home and personal care company.

Temporary operational links: The newly established Magnum Ice Cream Company continues to rely on Unilever for critical services under a Transitional Services Agreement that spans up to two years. These agreements cover IT infrastructure, marketing support, and co-packing services to ensure business continuity. The resulting fee income is recognized within Unilever's operating profit but is offset by the dual costs of service provision.
3.
Undefined transitional visibility: Jefferies reported on January 7, 2026, that investors have limited visibility on the exact transition service agreement levels being paid to Unilever during the separation period. The broker noted that stranded overheads from this exit would not fully impact Unilever's core margins until late 2025 when the ice cream operations began to stand alone. This information gap complicates the mid-term modeling of corporate overheads as the transition agreements roll off.

These regional clusters will be led by Regional Presidents with full accountability for P&L delivery across all categories. This regional model replaces the previous category-led structure to strengthen commercial excellence and local execution.

Significant strategy reversal: Jefferies described the transition to regional performance units as a U-turn on the divisional P&L structure that the company employed since 2022. The broker noted on November 3, 2026, that returning P&L responsibility to markets might create frictions between global brand managers and local country operations. Investors remain cautious about whether this continuous switching of organizational models will disrupt recent positive volume trends.

The broker highlighted that centralizing strategic choices while decentralizing local media and commerce execution will actually create a much leaner central function over time. This structural balance is expected to improve marketing ROI and support the company's volume growth targets.
7.
Critical cultural hurdles: A former operations director at Unilever stated on March 23, 2022, that shifting between matrix and category-focused structures often triggers severe internal disruption and talent loss. The expert explained that while consolidation is intended to reduce duplication, the resulting redundancies can lead to an overburdened workforce and cyclical restructuring. Overcoming these deep-seated cultural barriers is essential if the new 2027 regional units are to achieve their agility goals.
