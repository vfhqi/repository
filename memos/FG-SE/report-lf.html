EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 5 findings

CEO quality and leadership transition

Former CEO Martin Jacobsson pursued a finance-driven generalist career track without prior corporate operational experience, entering Fasadgruppen directly from sell-side equity research at Handelsbanken Capital Markets and ascending rapidly through CFO and M&A roles to CEO at age 35. His leadership exhibited remarkably transparent and self-critical communication during periods of severe underperformance, refusing to dress up weak figures while maintaining a pragmatic focus on addressable execution problems.

Despite this candour, Fasadgruppen persistently missed its mid-term financial targets across three consecutive years, with the group delivering negative organic growth of -10.40% in 2024 and failing to reach the 10% margin threshold in any year of

1.
his tenure. The board appointed acting CEO Eva Torberger in August 2026 following Jacobsson's sudden departure, selecting an experienced construction operator with extensive executive leadership at Skanska and NCC to prioritize steady operational engagement and profitability focus. The simultaneous replacement of both CEO and CFO during 2026 elevated near-term execution uncertainty across the decentralized platform, though the permanent appointment of Magnus Blomberg as CFO in October 2026 provided some continuity through his prior Group Controller role

Operational delivery and capital allocation record

2.
Under Jacobsson's tenure, Fasadgruppen experienced significant margin compression and earnings volatility despite inorganic revenue growth, with adjusted EBITA margin contracting from 8.80% in 2023 to 5.70% in 2024 before recovering modestly to 8.20% in 2025. Reported earnings per share collapsed to SEK 0.05 in 2024 from SEK 4.42 in 2023, followed by a full-year 2025 net loss of SEK 116.1 million and negative EPS of SEK -2.19, driven by integration challenges and macroeconomic headwinds. The high-margin Clear Line acquisition initially promised strong profitability contributions but suffered immediate regulatory project bottlenecks at the UK Building Safety Regulator, deferring SEK 20-30 million in EBITA during Q3 2025 and raising former regional management concerns that margins would almost collapse. Fasadgruppen's leverage spiraled to 3.3x pro forma EBITDA in late 2024, forcing management to secure alleviated bank covenant terms and execute a highly dilutive SEK 504 million rights issue in early 2026 to reduce balance sheet stress. The board cancelled the dividend for 2024 and removed the 30% payout policy entirely, while the group held 114,234 repurchased shares from a 2023/2024 buyback program rather than executing counter-cyclical purchases during market distress

Governance structure and compensation alignment

3.
Executive short-term pay is closely aligned with performance via a 75% bonus cap tied to balanced financial and sustainability metrics, with mandatory requirements that 25% of gross variable pay be used to purchase Fasadgruppen shares held for at least three years. The transition to a C1 hurdle share incentive program in 2026 mitigates dilution while former executives maintain significant equity skin-in-the-game, with Martin Jacobsson holding 509,875 shares plus 75,000 warrants representing substantial personal wealth exposure. Voting power is concentrated among a tight group of insider-aligned shareholders controlling roughly 30% of outstanding votes, while a dual-class share structure establishes C1 hurdle shares carrying only one-tenth of a vote and no dividend rights. Audit processes are managed by re-elected top-tier external auditor Deloitte AB under a structured COSO framework adapted for decentralized risks, with external auditors focusing primarily on percentage-of-completion project accounting assumptions and goodwill valuation. The heavily decentralized operating model increases the risk of subsidiary non-compliance with complex regulations, requiring active central monitoring to prevent subsidiary-level accounting irregularities
