Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics 5 findings

Section summary
  1. the global electrometallurgy sector exhibits highly cyclical, supply-sensitive dynamics where trade protections are structurally shifting the margin pool toward compliant Western producers

Global market size and driver dynamics

1.
Cyclical demand drivers: industrial demand remains structurally tied to global steel, aluminum, construction, automotive, and electronics growth cycles. Expanding silicon demand: long-term global silicon metal demand is projected to grow at a 4% CAGR through 2030. Accelerating energy transition: solar and electronic silicon applications are expected to grow at a faster 6% CAGR. Steel-driven consumption: steel production consumes more than 95% of globally manufactured ferrosilicon to enhance durability and corrosion resistance
2.
Dominant input factors: electricity represents 45% to 50% of the total production cost of energy-intensive ferrosilicon. Energy cost arbitrage: access to cheap power determines long-term cost competitiveness for metallurgical smelters

Concentration levels and competitor tiers

3.
Steep concentration curves: the Western merchant silicon metal market is highly concentrated, while the Chinese landscape remains fragmented. Consolidated leader tier: Ferroglobe holds a 66% market share in North American silicon metal capacity. Global market share: Ferroglobe commands approximately 25% of the global silicon metal market capacity, excluding China. Highly restricted competition: only two ferrosilicon producers operate in the United States, consisting of Ferroglobe and CCMA Industries. Specialized semiconductor segment: Wacker Chemie holds a dominant 50% global market share in high-barrier electronics-grade polysilicon. Western alloy competitors: primary European competitors in manganese and silicon alloys include Eramet, Elkem, Finnfjord, and Huta Laziska

Regulatory trade barriers and structural shifts

4.
Evolving safeguard frameworks: the European Commission enacted defensive trade safeguards on imports of selected ferroalloys in November 2025. Tightening steel quotas: the EU Steel Regulation took effect July 1, 2026, restricting duty-free imports to 18.3 million tonnes. Severe out-of-quota penalty: imports exceeding the EU steel quotas are subject to an aggressive 50% tariff. Strict origin traceability: a mandatory "melt and pour" declaration system begins October 1, 2026, requiring mill test certificates. Aggressive US duties: the United States finalized anti-dumping duties of 78.5% on Angolan and 173.5% on Laotian silicon imports
5.
Active anti-dumping reviews: Euroalliages initiated a European Commission interim review of Chinese silicon metal anti-dumping duties on September 1, 2026. Chinese pricing discipline: eight major Chinese polysilicon producers signed an "Anti-Involution Pledge" in August 2026 to prevent under-cost pricing
