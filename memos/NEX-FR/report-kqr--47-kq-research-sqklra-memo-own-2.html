The effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz in February 2026 disrupted approximately one-fifth of globally traded liquefied natural gas and crude oil. This disruption caused regional oil production to decline from 26 million barrels per day to 16 million barrels per day by March 2026. Consequently, middle eastern hydrocarbon exporters faced substantial economic losses and regional gross domestic product contracted by 2.1% in 2026. The analysis of this section is based on ten relevant sources, covering three key companies and one comparable past geopolitical disruption case.

These elevated energy tariffs directly increased the operating costs for European cable manufacturing facilities. If these high utility costs persist, they could derail the ongoing volume recovery in regional construction markets. Ultimately, sustained energy inflation represents a material drag on European manufacturing margins over the next twelve months.

Consequently, manufacturers must maintain higher inventory levels to mitigate severe delivery delays and freight bottlenecks.

4.
Surging polymer: Petrochemical raw material prices surged upstream from olefins to downstream general- purpose resins following geopolitical infrastructure damage in Saudi Arabia and Iran. Geopolitical conflicts severely limited shipping routes and damaged critical chemical infrastructure, including the Jubail Hub and the Assaluyah Hub. This crisis caused acute shortages of essential styrene and propylene feedstocks, pushing polymer prices to multi-year highs. Cable producers expect these elevated chemical costs to remain high as long as international oil prices remain elevated. Therefore, cable manufacturers face unprecedented polymer cost inflation that threatens low-voltage margins.

Morgan Stanley reported on July 13, 2026, that more US PVC resin cargoes were redirected to Europe to offset limited import arrivals. Downstream spot PVC prices remained highly sensitive to the geopolitical developments in the Strait of Hormuz. These logistical disruptions forced European chemical buyers to absorb higher import costs. Cable producers must absorb these surging raw material prices unless they can execute rapid contract renegotiations.
