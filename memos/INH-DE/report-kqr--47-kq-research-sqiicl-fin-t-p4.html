Section 2 – The Materials Solutions Margin Path and Inventory Valuations

Section summary
  1. Materials Solutions margins will compress in H2 2026 as high procurement costs flow through the inventory, while peer benchmarks suggest the strategic inventory build carries temporary write-down risks.

Segment margin dynamics and guidances

1.
Strategic pricing: INDUS Holding's Materials Solutions segment margin surged dramatically in the first half of 2026 due to the tungsten carbide special situation. The segment reported a record adjusted EBITA margin of 22.4% in H1 2026, which is up from just 8.1% in H1 2025. This outstanding performance was driven primarily by pricing effects at the core subsidiary BETEK, which represents over half of the segment's revenue. This surge has temporarily boosted group earnings per share but is widely recognized by management as an unsustainable windfall.
2.
Elevated guidance: In response to the exceptionally strong pricing momentum, INDUS raised its full-year guidance for the Materials Solutions segment. The company now expects a full-year adjusted EBITA margin of 15% to 17%, representing a massive increase from the prior guidance of 8% to 10%. This revision highlights the significant positive impact of the raw material timing lag, as selling prices rose faster than procurement costs. However, these supernormal profits are expected to contract as higher-cost feedstocks eventually flow through the inventory.
3.
Historical baselines: The current high-margin environment contrasts sharply with historical baselines before the tungsten price shock occurred. In FY2025, the Materials Solutions segment achieved an adjusted EBITA margin of 9.4%, which was up slightly from 8.8% in FY2024. These historical levels represent the normalized earning capacity of the segment under standard operating conditions. Over the next 18 months, margins are expected to return toward these levels as high-cost materials dilute profitability.
4.
Broker expectations: Sell-side analysts model a steep decline in segment margins starting in the second half of 2026 and extending into FY2027. The broker emphasizes that the current margin expansion does not represent a sustainable long-term baseline. Consequently, the consensus models a return to single-digit margins of around 7.6% by FY2027 once tungsten prices stabilize.

Tungsten pricing and supply cycle

5.
Tungsten pricing cycle: The root cause of this margin volatility is the unprecedented global supply shock in the tungsten market. European ammonium paratungstate prices rose from $900 per metric ton unit in January 2026 to over $2,900 by mid-year. This spike was triggered by Chinese export controls and a structural shortage of non-Chinese supply. While prices have plateaued in Europe, they have already started to correct in China, suggesting that a wider global price normalization is underway.

Section 3 – Structural Tax Rates and Financing Cost Profiles

Section summary
  1. Unfavorable tax structures and rising financing costs present persistent headwinds for INDUS Holding AG's net profit margins.
  2. The company's unique corporate setup prevents the offsetting of losses between subsidiaries, structurally elevating the normalized tax rate.
  3. Long-term promissory notes and interest swaps provide interest rate protection but lock in elevated financing costs for years.

Structural tax rate analysis

1.
Volatile tax rates: INDUS Holding AG's reported effective tax rate exhibits extreme volatility, swinging from 43.1% in fiscal year 2024 to 29.0% in fiscal year 2025. According to the INDUS Holding AG annual report of 24 March 2026, this significant decline was primarily driven by non-recurring positive tax effects. This volatility means that the group's reported net income and earnings per share are highly sensitive to transient tax adjustments rather than pure operational performance, which could distort underlying growth trends.
2.
Non-recurring benefits: The primary driver behind the low tax rate in fiscal year 2025 was a substantial tax credit. In the annual report published on 24 March 2026, INDUS Holding AG disclosed that it recognized a tax credit of EUR 8.36 million related to previously unrecognized losses from its insolvent automotive supplier subsidiary S.M.A. Metalltechnik GmbH & Co. KG. The credit was claimed after the assets were acquired by a financial investor in the first quarter of 2025, which substantially reduced actual tax expenses for the fiscal year. This non-recurring benefit inflated fiscal year 2025 earnings per share by approximately EUR 0.34, representing an unsustainable baseline.
3.
Stranded tax losses: Outside of unique tax credits, the structural tax rate for INDUS Holding AG remains elevated due to its decentralized organizational structure. The company's annual report of 24 March 2026 explains that trade tax is assessed at the individual limited partnership level, meaning that offsetting tax gains and losses between different partnerships is legally prohibited. Consequently, loss-making subsidiaries create stranded trade tax losses that cannot be utilized to reduce the group's overall taxable income. This structural inefficiency added EUR 5.98 million to the actual tax expense in fiscal year 2025 under the reconciliation item for autonomous subsidiaries, creating a permanently higher structural tax rate for the group.

Peer tax rate comparisons

4.
Comparable peer structures: Other listed German holding companies experience similar structural tax challenges. According to the Gesco SE annual report of 28 April 2025, Gesco SE reported a tax rate of 43.8% in fiscal year 2024, which rose from 31.6% in fiscal year 2023. The company noted that this high rate was driven by divestment effects and impairment losses that were not tax-effective. This comparison proves that German holding structures face severe tax rate inflation when subsidiaries experience localized losses or writedowns.
5.
Alternative holding models: In contrast, Mutares SE & Co. KGaA employs a different tax rate framework due to its transactional business model. In its annual report published on 20 May 2025, Mutares SE & Co. KGaA noted a parent-level statutory tax rate of approximately 33.0%, combining corporate income tax, solidarity surcharge, and trade tax. However, a Warburg Research note published on 9 January 2026 showed that Mutares SE & Co. KGaA's actual reported group tax rate fluctuated from -1.0% in 2023 to 16.5% in 2024. This indicates that transaction-led portfolios can achieve structurally lower tax rates through tax-free exit gains.

Debt and refinancing profile

6.
Escalating debt burden: INDUS Holding AG's net debt has risen because of strategic raw material builds. The company's annual report of 30 March 2026 indicated that net financial debt was aligned with operating earning power but stood at a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 2.5 years. In its analyst presentation of 25 March 2026, management indicated that net financial liabilities had risen to EUR 730.7 million at year-end 2025, up from EUR 689.2 million in the previous quarter. This rising leverage compounds interest expenses, especially as older, low-interest liabilities expire and require refinancing.
7.
93 g p INDUS Holding AG financial liabilities and derivatives maturity profile as of December 31, 2025 Promissory note issuance: To secure long-term liquidity, INDUS Holding AG executed a major refinancing transaction. In a press release issued on 24 July 2025, the company announced the placement of a EUR 125 million promissory note loan, significantly oversubscribing its initial EUR 75 million target. The loan, arranged by LBBW, was issued in tranches with maturities of three, five, seven, and ten years with both variable and fixed rates. While this transaction secured stable funding, it locks the company into the prevailing higher interest rate environment.

Hedging and liquidity management

8.
Hedging program: The group actively manages its exposure to rising interest rates through derivative instruments. In its annual report of 30 March 2026, INDUS Holding AG explained that its variable-rate bank loans are hedged using interest rate swaps. The change in the market value of these swaps is recorded directly in equity under cash flow hedges, which minimizes direct income statement volatility. However, the company's Q3 2025 earnings call on 12 November 2025 revealed that average interest rates are expected to rise beyond the current market trend for some time due to this revolving program. This means that financing costs will remain high, directly pressuring net interest expense.
9.
Contrary evidence: Although interest rates are rising globally, some peer holding companies have maintained highly conservative balance sheets that limit interest exposure. According to the Gesco SE quarterly statement of 12 November 2025, Gesco SE held non-current liabilities of just EUR 51.2 million and net debt of EUR 46.9 million. Furthermore, in its annual report of 12 May 2025, Gesco SE stated that it largely refrains from speculative financing elements and cash pooling to ensure financial stability. This demonstrates that diluted interest rate sensitivity can be achieved by avoiding high-leverage acquisition strategies.
10.
Other side of the story: While the high structural tax rate and rising interest costs create near-term friction, they represent the cost of operating a highly diversified, creditworthy SME portfolio. INDUS Holding AG's ability to easily secure EUR 125 million in oversubscribed promissory notes from major German commercial banks highlights its excellent credit standing. Furthermore, the lack of financial covenants at the group level provides invaluable operational flexibility during periods of economic stress. Consequently, the group's robust capital structure and locked-in long-term financing mitigate severe refinancing risks, ensuring that INDUS Holding AG remains a stable partner for German SMEs.

Section 4 – PPA Amortisation, Working Capital, and Distributions

Section summary
  1. PPA amortisation and goodwill impairment risks will continue to weigh on INDUS's reported operating income as its EUR 500 million acquisition program expands.
  2. High working capital days and raw material pricing cycles depress short-term cash conversion compared to highly efficient Nordic peers. Net read: Stable dividend payouts and strategic share buybacks protect shareholder value and support near-term earnings per share.

Purchase price allocation and impairment risks

1.
Acquisition accounting friction: The expansion of the group's investment portfolio structurally elevates purchase price allocation amortisation. According to INDUS's annual report of 24 March 2026, PPA amortisation reached EUR 20.8 million in FY2025, compared to EUR 20.3 million in the previous year. These non-cash charges directly reduce reported EBIT, meaning that management must rely on adjusted EBITA to present true operational portfolio earnings.
2.
Scaling PPA charges: PPA amortisation is expected to scale upward as the EUR 500 million acquisition programme accelerates. INDUS's H1 2026 interim report showed that PPA amortisation remained flat at EUR 10.7 million year-on-year. However, as new acquisitions like AMIRA and PRO VIDEO are fully integrated, these charges will inevitably rise. This means PPA charges will continue to act as a headwind for reported EPS.
3.
Goodwill impairment sensitivity: The group's large goodwill balance of EUR 406.9 million exposes its consolidated balance sheet to significant write-down risks. INDUS disclosed in its 2025 annual report that a 10.0 percentage point decrease in operating income planning would trigger additional goodwill impairments of EUR 10.48 million. This sensitivity demonstrates that even minor economic downturns among its 44 cashgenerating units can directly impair reported group net income.
4.
Historical impairment patterns: Past performance shows that goodwill impairments are highly sensitive to localized operational weaknesses. In late 2024, INDUS booked goodwill impairments of EUR 6.7 million, which dropped to EUR 1.4 million in FY2025. These impairment charges were triggered by deteriorating earnings prospects at two subsidiaries within the Infrastructure segment. This history highlights how weak macro conditions can trigger non-cash write-downs, impairing GAAP earnings.

Working capital conversion and peer benchmarks

5.
Tungsten tie-up cash drain: Strategic inventory build-ups have severely impacted INDUS's near-term cash conversion cycle. Working capital surged by EUR 165.5 million in the first half of 2026, reaching EUR 627.0 million. This increase was driven by rising volumes and procurement prices for tungsten carbide at subsidiary BETEK. This massive capital lock-up has depressed free cash flow, delaying the resumption of largescale portfolio acquisitions.
6.
Peer cash efficiency: Nordic consolidators exhibit structurally superior working capital efficiency compared to INDUS. Peer company Lifco AB reported 2025 trade accounts receivable of SEK 3.43 billion and inventories of SEK 4.31 billion on a much larger sales base. Lifco manages this by tying senior executive variable compensation directly to working capital and cash flow targets. This disciplined approach allows Lifco to sustain higher free cash flow generation throughout industrial cycles.
7.
Indutrade inventory management: Indutrade AB also serves as a strong benchmark for superior balance sheet flexibility. In its Q2 2026 interim report, Indutrade disclosed that working capital efficiency was higher year-onyear, with inventories sequential decreasing. Indutrade's decentralized companies actively reduced inventory levels, helping generate record cash flow from operating activities. This operating cash flow reached SEK 882 million in Q2 2026, illustrating a more agile working capital profile than INDUS's.
8.
Cash conversion comparison: The group's cash conversion cycle is significantly longer than its peers, reflecting its heavy industrial manufacturing exposure. Warburg Research reported that INDUS's cash conversion cycle stood at 203 days in 2025, up from 179 days in 2024. This extended cycle is driven primarily by long lead times and inventory holding periods at BETEK. Consequently, INDUS requires more upfront financing for raw materials than decentralized peers.

Shareholder distributions and buyback programs

9.
Consistent dividend policy: INDUS maintains a highly shareholder-friendly distribution policy to support its investment case. At the Annual Shareholders' Meeting on 3 June 2026, the company approved a dividend of EUR 1.30 per share for FY2025. This dividend represented a ten-cent increase over the previous year and was supported by strong operating cash flow. Management remains committed to its long-term strategy of distributing between 40% and 50% of its net profit.
10.
Opportunistic share buybacks: The group actively uses share buybacks as a capital allocation tool when the stock is undervalued. NuWays AG reported that INDUS repurchased 1.85 million shares, representing 6.9% of its share capital, through public tender offers and open-market transactions. The company retired 1.1 million of these treasury shares in March 2025, reducing the outstanding share count. This reduction in outstanding shares serves as a permanent catalyst to increase EPS over the forecast period.
11.
Refinancing cash priorities: Higher working capital requirements have temporarily limited the scope for extra shareholder returns. NuWays AG noted that the massive capital requirements at BETEK temporarily decelerated the company's acquisition pace. While H2 2026 earnings could provide potential for higher shareholder returns, the priority remains funding the strategic inventory build. Therefore, further buyback programs are unlikely until working capital is released.

Minority interests and contingent liabilities

12.
Minority buyout execution: INDUS has actively bought out minority partners to simplify its corporate structure and capture full earnings. In June 2026, the company completed the acquisition of the remaining 25% of shares in DECKMA. Additionally, the group acquired an additional 4% of the shares in MESUTRONIC, strengthening its ownership. These transactions eliminate minority interest leakage in highly profitable subsidiaries, ensuring that more operating profit flows directly into group net income.
13.
Contingent liability profile: The group's extensive use of put/call options for acquisitions creates significant contingent liabilities on the balance sheet. INDUS disclosed that purchase price commitments for non-controlling interests stood at EUR 37.8 million in June 2026, virtually unchanged from late 2025. These commitments are recorded at fair value under other liabilities because minority shareholders have contractual rights to tender their shares. The subsequent valuation of these liabilities can create non-operating earnings volatility under other financial income.
14.
Contrary evidence: Although PPA amortisation is expected to scale upward, INDUS's current PPA charges are actually flat. INDUS reported H1 2026 PPA amortisation of EUR 10.7 million, which is exactly equal to the EUR 10.7 million recorded in H1 2025. This stable amortisation indicates that recent small-scale bolt-on acquisitions have not yet triggered major PPA increases. This delay in PPA growth suggests near-term earnings drag from amortization is lower than initially feared.
15.
Other side of the story: While elevated working capital and rising PPA amortisation present short-term headwinds, they represent necessary investments. The massive capital tied up in BETEK's tungsten inventory secured supply continuity and allowed the subsidiary to capture substantial market share from retreating competitors. As raw material prices normalize, this inventory will turn, releasing cash to fund the next leg of the M&A program. Ultimately, the strategic buyouts of minorities and disciplined capital deployment will deliver a structurally stronger, high-margin portfolio.

Section 5 – Organic and Acquired Growth Trajectories to 2030

Section summary
  1. Organic volume growth is projected to recover in FY2027 and FY2028 after a prolonged period of macroeconomic contraction.
  2. The strategic target to acquire EUR 600 million in revenue by 2030 remains highly feasible when benchmarked against Nordic peer models.
  3. Pricing discipline and contingent transaction structures will safeguard the portfolio against acquisition risks.

Historical organic revenue contraction

Lagging organic demand: Across 12 relevant sources covering three peer companies, INDUS struggled with organic revenue contraction over the historical period due to macroeconomic challenges. According to an investor presentation by INDUS on 25 March 2026, organic revenue declined by 1.3% in FY2025 because of persistent economic weakness and the IMECO impact. This drop is comparable to the 1.3% organic contraction in FY2025 reported by INDUS on 25 March 2026. Consequently, these cyclical downturns demonstrate that underlying organic demand was fragile across key segments before the tungsten pricing shock.

This projected recovery means that normalized volume growth will drive group earnings per share higher once the current tungsten cycle normalizes.

Resilient purchase elasticity: Downstream customer purchasing behavior remains highly inelastic, which protects tool volumes even during inflationary cycles. Cutting tools comprise only about 3% of a typical machine shop's total operating cost base. Because tooling is a minor cost, buyers can easily absorb and pass through higher prices to preserve their own margins. Consequently, volume demand for BETEK's high-performance wear tools is unlikely to experience significant destruction even under high-price scenarios.

Mandatory supply pivots: Strict Western defense procurement rules will permanently isolate Western suppliers from cheaper Chinese imports. The U.S. Department of Defense has mandated a complete sourcing ban on Chinese and Russian tungsten beginning January 1, 2027. Even if Chinese domestic prices remain depressed, Western toolmakers must purchase certified non-Chinese materials. This regulatory barrier ensures that Western-aligned producers maintain pricing power regardless of global price normalisation.
