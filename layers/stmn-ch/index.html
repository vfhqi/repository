Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external environment does not help the case and in two places (the franc and the buyer) actively taxes it; it is the backdrop against which a share-gain and self-help story has to be delivered. The most important modelling insight is that the two largest external variables for reported EPS over 18 months are not demand but currency (each 5 percent of franc translation moves EPS by about 3.5 percent, and bank forecasts point to more strength while consensus seems to assume none) and the Chinese price round (a 5 to 15 percent cut on 10 to 15 percent of revenue). Demand should be modelled as flat-to-low single digits in mature markets with share gains layered on top; regulation should be modelled as a 2026 benefit reversing in 2027; and the buyer-power dynamic should be modelled as a slow price-and-mix drag on the gross margin and a working-capital drag on cash, monitored through the core gross margin excluding one-offs and days sales outstanding.

Judgement: competitive intensity is bifurcating, easing at the premium end where Straumann earns its margin and tightening at the value end, in aligners and at the dental-service-organisation buyer, which is where Straumann's growth mix is moving; on balance it is tightening modestly. Idea 1: the reported margin is an identity, and the franc is a structural term in it, not a one-year shock. Reported core EBIT margin = last year's reported margin + constant-currency expansion - currency drag. For four years the second and third terms have cancelled (constant-currency gains of 50 to 250 basis points a year, currency drag of 130 to 180 basis points), so the reported margin sat in a 25 to 26 percent band. The reason this is structural rather than bad luck: Straumann's organic growth includes local-currency price increases in markets whose inflation runs 1.5 to 2 points above Swiss inflation, and over time the franc appreciates by roughly that inflation gap (purchasing-power drift). Part of every year's "organic" growth is therefore inflation that translation takes back. My base case uses a drag of 1.5 points a year on revenue from FY2028 (roughly the inflation gap, and about a third to a half of the drag Straumann actually suffered in most recent years). Idea 2: the FY2026 margin step is mostly a second-half step lapping an abnormal second half, and it should not be extrapolated. The conclusions in one paragraph. The forecasts are internally congruent and rest mostly on things Straumann has already shown it can do. They are highly predictable print by print (FY2026 EPS within about 2.5 percent) and only moderately predictable a year out (FY2027 EPS within about minus 10 to plus 7 percent), because the widest source of variance, the Swiss franc, lies outside the company and outside its guidance. Against expectations the position is asymmetric in time. The next two events (24 February and late April 2027) have a benign set-up: the two easiest growth comparisons of the cycle, reported EPS growth of about 26 percent in the half to be reported, and a cautious first guide that two brokers already expect; free cash flow is the one likely disappointment. The model's cross-check, explained. The summary's cross-check marks no year as materially different, and none is: the model's EPS is within CHF 0.04 of the second set's own figure in every year and its free cash flow within CHF 10 million. The small gaps have two different causes.
