Technological Divergence of MI Cables

4.
Mass-impregnated cable design: Mass-impregnated technology utilizes an insulation system composed of high-density paper tapes wrapped around a conductor. These paper layers are thoroughly impregnated with a high-viscosity compound that does not flow even if the sheath is ruptured. This legacy design has been utilized since 1954, proving its long-term reliability for demanding subsea links. Thus, the decades-long track record of MI remains a key selling point for grid operators.
5.
Deepwater limitations and pressures: Subsea power transmission at extreme depths requires cables that can withstand immense hydrostatic pressures. At depths of 3,000 meters, water pressure is so immense that any physical damage can force water miles into a cable. Nexans successfully qualified a 525 kV MI cable system for installation at 3,000 meters depth in August 2026. This makes MI the only fieldproven technology currently rated for deepwater environments.
6.
Thermal performance constraints: MI cables possess a lower allowable operating temperature compared to modern extruded alternatives. This thermal limitation makes it more difficult for operators to maximize transmission capacity over the link. Furukawa Electric noted in April 2025 that lower allowable conductor temperatures represent a key technical trade-off for MI systems. Consequently, XLPE insulation is preferred for shallower projects where maximizing active power flow is the primary goal.

Comparison of XLPE and Sector Standards Extruded

7.
XLPE cable advantages: Extruded cross-linked polyethylene technology utilizes a solid synthetic polymer insulation extruded directly over the conductor. XLPE cables are capable of sustaining greater mechanical elongation compared to paper-insulated MI cables during deepwater deployment. This modern technology has become the dominant insulation choice for shallower European offshore wind farm connections. It also avoids the environmental risks associated with the fluid-impregnated compounds used in legacy designs.
8.
Commercial scale of XLPE contracts: The sheer scale of XLPE contracts dwarfs typical replacement MI projects, as evidenced by Nexans' recent order history. Nexans secured a €1 billion EPCI contract in January 2025 for TenneT's LanWin2 project in the German North Sea. This massive project involves 250 km of 525 kV HVDC XLPE subsea and land cables. While XLPE contracts deliver unprecedented high-voltage subsea backlogs, they require extensive manufacturing capacity.
9.
Industrial production at Halden: Nexans commenced the first industrial-scale production of its 525 kV HVDC XLPE subsea cable at Halden in October 2026. This milestone marks the official transition from engineering to large-scale delivery for the LanWin2 offshore grid link. Although Halden hosts both MI and XLPE lines, repurposing MI lines to XLPE is technically impossible. Therefore, Nexans must run parallel product strategies to avoid leaving expensive MI assets unutilized.
