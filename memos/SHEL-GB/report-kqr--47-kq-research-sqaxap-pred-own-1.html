Slopes and Downside Volatility Protection

4.
Defensive slopes: Long-term LNG contracts typically link gas prices to Brent using slopes ranging between 11.5% and 13.5% of the crude benchmark. These mathematical ratios function as stabilizing mechanisms for gas revenues by capping immediate downside exposure when crude markets decline. These formulaic slopes ensure that the integrated majors maintain steady cash flows despite high volatility in global energy markets.
5.
Contrary evidence: Woodside Energy experienced an unexpected 27% sequential increase in its realized LNG price to $84/boe during the third quarter of 2026, even though spot crude oil prices weakened during that same period. Buyers are increasingly unwilling to sign legacy 12% to 14% slopes, potentially forcing slopes down to 7% or 8% to secure long-term deals.
