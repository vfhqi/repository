EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Section summary
  1. strategic focus is refocusing on core hearing care through key divestments and organizational restructuring
  2. forward EPS growth is anchored by groundbreaking AI technology platforms and critical commercial channel recoveries
  3. recent financial performance shows improving Wholesale momentum and solid margin delivery despite FX headwinds
  4. management acts as an honest tracker with ambitious targets backed by a rigorous compensation framework
  5. the sell-side maintains a cautious hold-heavy consensus with tight valuation multiples relative to historical averages
  6. there is no active activist investor but structural competitive and currency risks demand a cautious lens
  7. the overall investment case strengthens structurally because of core hearing care refocusing and technological leadership

4.
Comprehensive C-suite refresh: Recent governance updates include appointing a new CEO, CFO, and Chairman to lead the next growth phase.

7.
Costco channel recovery: Sonova is regaining market share at Costco using the multi-channel, multi-brand strategy.

Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro)

Section summary
  1. underlying hearing care demand remains resilient and structurally growing despite short-term private pay volatility
  2. competitive intensity is accelerating rapidly as rivals introduce aggressive premium custom and RIC platforms
  3. technology disruption is increasingly led by deep learning AI rather than over-the-counter consumer entry
  4. regulatory intervention and Chinese VBP are creating severe pricing and volume headwinds in cochlear implants
  5. vertical integration and direct-to-retail channels are redefining supplier and distributor economics
  6. the market faces temporary cyclical upgrade delays rather than structural adoption hurdles
  7. persistent Swiss Franc appreciation remains a severe translation drag on reported revenue and profits

Demand

1.
Resilient market growth: Underlying hearing aid market growth reached approximately 3% in Q1 2026, driven by North American volume improvements.
2.
Highly valued premium: High-end premium products represent 50% to 70% of fittings in private clinics, indicating strong customer willingness to pay.
3.
Cyclical upgrade delays: Industry experts note that weakness in first-time buyer uptake is the primary driver of recent market softness.
4.
Supportive replacement cycles: Many countries offer reimbursement in five-year intervals, preparing the market for upgrades from 2021-2022 buyers.
5.
Substantial adoption runway: Adoption remains persistently low globally, with less than 15% of the clinically eligible population currently using hearing solutions.

Competition

6.
Strong Wholesale performance: Sonova's Wholesale organic growth reached 10.9% in H2 FY25/26, significantly outperforming key peers through product innovation.
7.
Hearing Aids: Bernstein Quarterly Hearing Check - Our thoughts into Q2 reporting Organic growth comparison showing Sonova outperforming peers Demant, GN, and WSA Rising custom rival: Demant's custom Oticon Zeal has captured substantial market share since its premium commercial launch.
8.
Direct share erosion: In June 2026, Sonova's total Veteran's Affairs value share slipped 40 bps to 53.7% as Demant's Zeal expanded.
9.
Pricing premium comparison: Demant's Oticon Zeal commands a premium price of USD 692 in the VA, compared to Sonova's USD 540.
10.
Custom segment shift: Demant gained 21.5% unit share in the custom category in June 2026, directly eroding Sonova's dominant volume positioning.

Disruption

11.
Innovative chip leadership: Sonova's platform uses a proprietary DEEPSONIC chip to separate speech from noise, providing a strong technology advantage.
12.
Competitor chip response: Demant is expected to counter with a third-generation deep neural network chip in its H2 2026 platform launch.
13.
Minimal OTC impact: Audiologists report that over-the-counter hearing aids have failed to disrupt prescription demand due to high consumer dissatisfaction.
14.
Abated disruption fears: Major consumer OTC players Lucid and Sony have discontinued their devices, easing competitive entry concerns.
15.
High software barriers: Audiologist familiarity with Sonova's proprietary fitting software creates a durable behavioral switching barrier against new entrants.

Regulation

16.
Severe Chinese VBP: China's Volume-Based Procurement drove Sonova's Cochlear Implants sales down -17.1% in constant currency in H2.
17.
Drastic price erosion: Chinese VBP tenders have decimated multinational pricing, forcing price reductions of up to 50% to 60%.
18.
Tightening insurance coverage: Managed care providers in the United States are reducing reimbursement coverage, putting pressure on retail pricing.
19.
Positive pediatric mandates: Improved state-level insurance mandates have expanded pediatric hearing aid access, providing localized volume support.
20.
Stable public procurement: The US VA Denver Acquisition center provides a highly stable public reimbursement channel, insulated from consumer cycles.

Supply

21.
Vertical retail integration: Amplifon's DKK 17.0 billion acquisition of GN Hearing moves the largest retailer into vertical manufacturing.
22.
Sourcing phase-out risk: Following the GN merger, Sonova faces being gradually phased out as a supplier to Amplifon by 2029.
23.
Concentrated buyer power: Growing retail consolidation is concentrating volume-purchasing power in channels like Costco and Specsavers.
24.
Direct retail expansion: Sonova operates 70 World of Hearing flagship stores to capture direct-to-consumer margins and secure its retail pipeline.
25.
Footprint cost optimization: Sonova is regionalizing its production in Mexico and Germany to optimize logistics and lower procurement costs.

Cycle

26.
Gradual value recovery: The global hearing care market value growth of 3% in Q1 2026 indicates a gradual cyclical upswing.
27.
Pent-up upgrade demand: Subdued consumer sentiment in 2025 led to temporary upgrade deferrals, creating a backlog of pent-up demand.
28.
Elevated cycle sensitivity: The US private-pay market remains highly cyclical and sensitive to inflation due to out-of-pocket expenses.
29.
European cycle insulation: High statutory reimbursement levels in France and Germany insulate European demand from macroeconomic cyclicality.
30.
Lifecycle processor drag: Cochlear implant performance is cyclically dragged down by the late-stage lifecycle of the Marvel processor.

Macro

31.
Substantial currency drag: Swiss Franc appreciation created a CHF 221 million negative translation impact on Sonova's FY25/26 reported sales.
32.
Profitability headwind: Unfavorable exchange rate developments reduced Sonova's normalized EBITA by CHF 106 million and margins by 150 bps.
33.
Projected translation impact: Early FY26/27 exchange rates are expected to reduce reported sales by 1-2% and EBIT by 2-3%.
34.
Structural currency mismatch: Sonova suffers from a currency mismatch, generating only 1% of sales in CHF but maintaining a 15% CHF cost base.
35.
Chinese consumer softness: Weak consumer sentiment in China continues to pressure premium medical and personal health equipment imports.
36.
