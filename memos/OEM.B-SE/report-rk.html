EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

OEM International faces a medium-plausibility bear case centered on the 2025 EQT ownership transition, which ended over 50 years of family governance by securing 73.68% voting control and introducing a shorter investment horizon that clashes fundamentally with the historical patient decentralized culture. The bear thesis derives credibility from verified cyclical construction soft spots in Sweden and Finland, organic sales growth decelerating to 1% in late 2025, and persistent exposure to volatile Swedish and Euro Area industrial cycles. The ownership overhang from EQT's eventual exit requirement and capital redeployment pressures compound structural threats including supplier direct-dealing risks and customer disintermediation vulnerabilities. However, claims of immediate cultural destruction remain speculative, with recent acquisitions continuing at historical multiples and the company maintaining an exceptional 30.70% return on capital employed alongside record Q1 order intake.

The FALSE FRIEND profile emerges as the winning ACH hypothesis with zero disconfirming evidence items compared to nine for the YES profile and four for the NO profile, indicating near-term M&A acceleration and margin upticks mask unnoticed long-term structural decay rather than pure execution failure. Apparent progress signals include strong Q1 2026 organic growth of 5%, five acquisitions completed in a single quarter signaling rapid departure from family prudence, and EBITA margin improvement to 16.20% seemingly proving immediate synergy realization.

Yet this apparent progress masks underlying cultural dilution and leverage concerns, with return on equity dropping to 23.40% and missing the 25% target, contingent purchase liabilities falling significantly as acquired businesses missed operational targets, and return on capital employed declining as incremental capital returns dilute. The upcoming CEO transition in 2026 represents the single most critical diagnostic indicator of operational change, threatening the decentralized leadership structure that has historically insulated subsidiary autonomy.

The highest-plausibility failure modes cluster around three interconnected vectors. The 2026 CEO transition creates cultural friction with EQT's short investment horizon, introduces severe operational execution uncertainty as the current chief executive departs after holding the position since 2000, risks stalling key corporate relationships and strategic momentum, and diverts excessive management time toward transactions at the expense of day-to-day wholesale distribution operations.

Accelerated acquisition targets under EQT ownership pressure drive dilutive asset purchases, with paying inflated multiples for lower-quality targets permanently hampering group ROCE below the historical 30% threshold based on intense bidding competition among Nordic serial acquirers and EQT's mandate for rapid capital deployment leveraging underutilized balance sheet capacity. Regional operating deleverage emerges as fixed-cost operational bases outgrow slowing sales volumes in core Swedish industrial markets, with unabsorbed opex and logistics costs dragging down EBITA margins, while prolonged weakness in Swedish and Finnish building sectors spreads to industrial installation subsidiaries and depresses localized subsidiary profitability.

OEM's revenue structure creates substantial transaction exposure as 68% of sales contracts link to purchasing currencies while the group sources 80% of technical components in foreign currencies led by 59% EUR and 21% USD against only 10% of group supply purchases denominated in SEK, creating persistent structural margin risk from importing goods in EUR and USD while selling locally.
