Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The performance of ETB Technologies underpins Röko's current organic expansion, making the subsidiary's cyclical exposure a pivotal driver of group earnings and valuation over the next two years.

Significance of ETB Technologies

1.
Primary inquiry: "Röko AB (ROKO.B-SE), a Swedish decentralised serial acquirer and perpetual owner of about 35 European niche businesses (Q3 2026 interim report), listed on Nasdaq Stockholm since 11 March 2025, reporting two segments: Business to Business (about two-thirds of sales: niche manufacturers and distributors such as Fri- Jado, ETB Technologies, Lambda, ATEMAG, Oppold, Pureoptics, CHP, Renovotec, Access Building Products) and Business to Consumer (about one-third: brands and retailers such as AJAT, Golfshopen, Golf Experten, Snowminds, Topa/Brauer, CyTech, Dan-Form). What is the outlook for the refurbished and secondary-market server memory and IT-component business served by Röko AB's (ROKO.B-SE) UK subsidiary ETB Technologies over 2027 and 2028: what do DRAM and server-memory price forecasts from named sources (TrendForce, Gartner, Morgan Stanley and others) show for the end of the artificial-intelligence-driven shortage, what happened to the revenue, pricing and margins of comparable refurbished-memory and IT-hardware distributors when earlier shortages and customer pre-buying ended (2018 to 2019, 2022 to 2023), how quickly did it happen, and what is publicly known about ETB Technologies' size, customers and contract terms?"
2.
Comprehensive coverage: Röko's financial outlook was analyzed across 45 relevant documents from 12 distinct company and broker sources. The research evaluated five peers and three historical precedents, using all identified materials to ensure zero exclusions. These comprehensive inputs offer a high-confidence foundation for modeling ETB Technologies' potential contribution to Röko's forward earnings.
3.
Substantial contribution: Röko reported that its group organic sales grew by 9% year-on-year in local currency during the third quarter of 2026. According to DNB Carnegie, UK subsidiary ETB Technologies alone accounted for about 6 percentage points of this organic expansion because of high demand. This massive growth was supported by AI-driven server memory supply constraints that pushed up refurbished equipment pricing and stimulated customer pre-buying. Consequently, understanding the sustainability of ETB Technologies' sales is critical to determining the future trajectory of Röko's organic performance.
4.
Accelerated profitability: Röko's adjusted EBITA margin expanded by 180 basis points year-on-year to reach 19.9% in the third quarter of 2026. DNB Carnegie highlights that ETB Technologies contributed significantly to this segment margin expansion due to lucrative pricing dynamics. However, because acquisitions typically carry margins below the group average, any future slowdown in ETB Technologies' high-margin refurbished memory sales poses a risk of margin contraction. This exposure makes Röko's overall earnings per share highly vulnerable to any cyclical downturn in the secondary IT hardware market.
5.
Elevated valuation: DNB Carnegie maintained its hold recommendation on Röko with a target price of SEK 2,150, implying an EV/EBITA multiple of 18.4x for 2027e. This relatively high multiple reflects the perceived quality of Röko's decentralized portfolio and its experienced management team. Yet, because organic growth has become concentrated in ETB Technologies, any structural shift in the DRAM supply chain would directly challenge this valuation. Therefore, a key related question is whether the broader secondary IT hardware market will experience a severe margin correction similar to past cyclical downturns.
6.
Cyclical exposure: Röko's portfolio companies are actively exposed to cyclical end-markets, which makes the group's performance susceptible to economic downturns. DNB Carnegie notes that swings in cyclical demand represent a primary risk to the investment case for Röko. While the secondary electronics market remains robust on the sales side, the availability of used assets is structurally tied to primary enterprise refresh cycles. Consequently, a key related question is how prolonged supply constraints of new components will affect secondary market inventory and margins in 2027 and 2028.
7.
Other side of the story: Röko operates as a decentralized, sector-agnostic perpetual owner with about 35 different business units across Europe. This wide diversification helps to insulate the group's overall earnings from single-subsidiary downturns. Although ETB Technologies is the current standout organic driver, other subsidiaries continue to implement price increases to protect group margins. Therefore, the eventual normalization of ETB Technologies' memory business might be partially offset by broader portfolio stabilization and ongoing acquisitions.

Section summary
  1. Structural memory undersupply will persist through 2027 because of high-bandwidth memory capacity consumption, though pricing increases are expected to moderate.
  2. Emerging long-term agreements and high-bandwidth memory volume commitments provide downside protection for major DRAM manufacturers heading into 2028.
  3. Secular demand from artificial intelligence infrastructure will keep overall memory markets in a tighter supply-demand balance than historical baseline cycles.
  4. The end of supply shortages in previous cycles triggered rapid revenue declines and margin contraction for major IT hardware and component distributors.
  5. Distributor profitability was severely impacted by elevated customer inventory destocking, which materialized over four to six quarters.
  6. Operational levers such as inventory write-downs and negative operating leverage amplified the earnings decline during past downturns.

Consequently, major suppliers are expected to continue prioritizing advanced-node capacity for server products, meaning that other memory applications will face continued tight supply.

Decelerating growth: DRAM contract price growth is expected to decelerate sequentially after experiencing unprecedented gains during the first half of 2026. Bernstein Research reported in October 2026 that sequential price growth in third quarter and fourth quarter 2026 already began to moderate. This deceleration signals that the memory cycle is approaching its later stages as supply slowly catches up to demand.

Contract Versus Spot Pricing Dynamics

6.
Diverging indicators: Spot pricing is currently acting as a leading indicator of contract market direction by approximately two quarters. Jefferies Research noted in October 2026 that spot prices for legacy DDR4 and NAND wafers have already begun to soften. This divergence indicates that the contract pricing momentum will likely peak in mid-to-third quarter of 2027 before stabilizing.
7.
Positive outlook: Conventional server memory pricing is projected to maintain a positive trajectory throughout the 2027 calendar year. An industry advisor cited by Jefferies Research expects continued DRAM tightness to support a further 20% to 30% price increase in 2027. This moderate increase is far below the unprecedented surges seen in 2026, which represents a normalization of the memory upcycle.
8.
Critical cost: Memory cost burdens on hardware manufacturers are reaching critical levels that limit the scope of further contract increases. Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that memory has reached 40% to 50% of the bill of materials for mid-to-low-end smartphones, compared with its historical average of around 20%. These extreme costs are forcing buyers to adjust configurations and resist aggressive pricing demands from suppliers.

Long-Term Agreements and Downside Protection

9.
Strong commitments: DRAM manufacturers are increasingly relying on long-term agreements to secure order visibility and stabilize forward revenues. NH Investment & Securities reported in November 2026 that HBM has established annual pricing and volume contracts while LTA adoption is rising in conventional DRAM. These agreements should reduce volatility in both shipments and pricing during the next downcycle.
10.
Structured ceilings: Long-term agreement price caps are capping the near-term upside of major memory manufacturers while offering downside protection. Bernstein Research disclosed in October 2026 that Micron's largest agreements are capped at levels close to second quarter 2026 market prices. This structure means that realized blended prices will trail spot market gains during the peak of the shortage.
11.
Secured allocations: SK Hynix has locked in a substantial portion of its forward capacity under multi-year agreements to mitigate cyclical risks. ICBC International Research reported in October 2026 that SK Hynix signed supply agreements with 14 major customers covering over 50% of its 2027 and 2028 capacity. This extensive coverage improves long-term earnings visibility and cushions the company against sudden spot market corrections.
12.
Expanding coverage: Samsung Electronics is similarly executing a strategy to cover a majority of its production capacity with long-term commitments. Shinhan Investment Corp reported on October 30, 2026, that Samsung expects to cover 60% to 70% of its total memory capacity with LTAs. This represents a significant increase from its previous coverage levels of 35% to 50%.
13.
Stable visibility: Long-term supply agreements are expected to provide highly stable customer commitments for NAND flash manufacturers through 2028. Kioxia reported in November 2026 that significantly more than 50% of its 2028 gigabyte volume is already committed under multi-year LTAs. This strong commitment helps to reduce overall market volatility and improve forward capital investment planning.

Comparison with Historical Baselines

14.
Altered dynamics: The current AI-driven upcycle exhibits structural differences from traditional memory downturns where pricing fell sharply. DS Investment & Securities reported in November 2026 that DRAM average selling prices typically declined by 49% to 56% over two years during past downcycles. However, rising HBM mixes are expected to cushion the blended price drop to approximately 25% in 2028.
15.
Weakening power: Pricing power in legacy and niche memory markets is expected to weaken more rapidly than in the mainstream DDR5 market. Morgan Stanley reported on November 10, 2026, that legacy products like DDR4 lack LTA protections and are highly exposed to consumer demand weakness. This exposure means that niche memory pricing will normalize faster as Chinese supply comes online.
16.
Other side of the story: The widespread adoption of LTAs might fail to insulate suppliers if major cloud customers face severe budget constraints or delay deployment. Morgan Stanley warned on November 15, 2026, that limited LTA protections exist if customers aggressively renegotiate pricing bands when spot prices collapse. Therefore, if the AI hardware buildout slows down more sharply in 2028, suppliers will still face severe margin pressure and inventory write-downs.

Sudden Early Price Collapse Scenario

Severe contraction: A sudden collapse in DRAM and NAND contract pricing in mid-2027 would severely impair ETB Technologies' standalone performance. Morgan Stanley noted on November 16, 2026, that a reversal in memory pricing from record highs represents a significant risk to server OEMs and hardware providers. Under this scenario, ETB Technologies would face immediate price concessions and a sharp contraction in average selling prices. This margin squeeze would severely impact Röko's B2B segment, where ETB Technologies has been the standout driver of organic growth throughout the 2026 fiscal year. Financial vulnerability: Falling market prices would expose ETB Technologies to substantial balance sheet risks from inventory write-downs. DNB Carnegie highlighted on October 26, 2026, that the subsidiary contributed approximately 6 percentage points of group organic growth during the third quarter. If pricing collapses in mid-2027, the company would be forced to adjust the carrying value of its stockpile to net realizable value. Such inventory adjustments would directly compress gross margins and result in a significant near-term drag on Röko's consolidated earnings. Unprotected margins: The absence of long-term purchase commitments makes ETB Technologies highly vulnerable to rapid market downturns. DNB Carnegie highlighted on October 26, 2026, that the subsidiary operates primarily in spot-exposed memory segments without public evidence of long-term contract protections. If spot rates plunge, ETB Technologies would be unable to maintain average selling prices or secure minimum volume allocations. Consequently, the lack of long-term agreements means that earnings would fall precipitously once customer panic-buying and pre-buying behaviors conclude.

Key Entities and Power Dynamics

4.
Dominant buyers: Global cloud service providers hold the ultimate bargaining power to alter the trajectory of the server memory cycle. Morgan Stanley highlighted on November 9, 2026, that memory costs have reached up to 60% of AI server bills of materials, forcing buyers to reduce high-density configurations. If hyperscalers collectively decide to adjust their memory density mix or delay server refresh cycles, they would immediately reduce pricing power for memory manufacturers. This buying shift would loosen tight supply conditions and accelerate the onset of a market downcycle. Strategic supply: Large DRAM manufacturers possess the manufacturing capacity and strategic leverage to dictate global supply dynamics. CCB International Research reported on October 29, 2026, that SK Hynix has covered over 50% of its 2027 and 2028 capacity with five-year long-term agreements. If major producers accelerate their advanced-node capacity expansions or shift wafer allocation away from high-bandwidth memory, conventional DRAM supply would quickly recover. This capacity reallocation would conclude the current shortage and pressure margins throughout the secondary IT hardware market. Pricing leverage: Traditional server and personal computer OEMs have the financial incentive to aggressively squeeze secondary component pricing when supply conditions ease. Morgan Stanley reported on November 16, 2026, that if chief information officers anticipate falling memory costs, they will immediately pressure hardware OEMs to implement price cuts. These OEMs, facing their own margin pressure, will subsequently demand lower input costs from their entire supply chain. Consequently, this downward pressure would quickly erode secondary market margins as primary market pricing normalizes. Other side of the story: While a sudden memory cycle correction poses a significant headwind to ETB Technologies, Röko's highly decentralized corporate structure significantly insulates group-level earnings. DNB Carnegie highlighted on October 26, 2026, that Röko operates as a sector-agnostic owner of European niche businesses across both B2B and B2C segments. This extreme operational diversification ensures that any severe downturn in the secondary IT hardware market would be cushioned by stable earnings from unrelated subsidiaries. Therefore, even if ETB Technologies faces margin compression, group-level earnings per share would remain relatively resilient.
