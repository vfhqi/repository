Section 8 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. A non-consensus deceleration in European offshore wind would trigger aggressive price competition as competitors redirect scarce manufacturing capacity to Europe.
  2. Structural funding bottlenecks at TenneT provide the Dutch government with the motive to delay contract call-offs to preserve credit metrics.

Macro-environmental and competitive shifts

1.
Disruptive capacity redirection: Slower US offshore wind support under the Trump administration prompts global peers to redirect Charleston and Halden factory capacities to Europe, directly undermining NKT's regional pricing premium.
2.
Intensifying pricing pressure: A drop in European offshore wind installation run rates to 13GW per year would shift the high-voltage market into a 15% industry oversupply by 2030, reminiscent of the 2018 margin collapse.
3.
Expanding Asian footprint: Backed by aggressive capacity additions, LS Cable is positioned to become a top three high-voltage supplier by 2030, capturing 9 percentage points of market share.
4.
Challenging subsea oligopoly: Hellenic Cables' successful qualification of its 525kV subsea systems in August 2026 allows it to challenge the core European triopoly on major upcoming interconnector tenders.

Client funding and structural bottlenecks

5.
Strained utility balance-sheets: TenneT's massive EUR 200bn investment plan faces grid congestion headwinds and credit rating pressure, raising the risk of delayed project awards as total debt reached EUR 35.5bn in mid-2025.
6.
Sovereign funding dependency: Failed negotiations to sell TenneT Germany force the Dutch state to provide costly shareholder loans to sustain grid capex, leaving long-term projects vulnerable to political budget revisions.
7.
Outlier: Severed transmission links: Slower interconnector deployment shifts utility investments upstream, prompting engineering contractors to capture higher-value electrical integration works within localized data centers.
8.
Outlier: Severe supply-chain retaliation: Escalating trade friction between the European Union and China could trigger disruptive raw material export restrictions, crippling NKT's access to refined copper and specialized compounds.
9.
Other side of the story: Structural demand resilience: Sceptical analysts argue that even if offshore wind installations underdeliver, the massive interconnector catch-up trade and localized data center grid reinforcements are sufficient to absorb all scheduled capacity additions through 2030.
