Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The strategic partnership between DiaSorin and QIAGEN for the automated QuantiFERON latent tuberculosis test is a major driver of immunodiagnostics earnings but faces looming competitive and structural transaction risks.
  2. Competitive threats from Roche's upcoming US commercial launch and the mid-2027 take-private of QIAGEN by EQT Private Equity could disrupt the contract economics and compress DiaSorin's historical valuation multiples.

The Key Question

1.
Critical focus: "DiaSorin S.p.A. (DIA-IT), an Italian in-vitro diagnostics maker of closed analyzers and the reagents run on them, reporting three segments: Immunodiagnostics (LIAISON chemiluminescence analyzers and a specialty assay menu, including the automated latent tuberculosis QuantiFERON workflow partnered with QIAGEN), Molecular Diagnostics (LIAISON MDX, LIAISON PLEX syndromic multiplexing and the LIAISON NES point-of-care system) and Licensed Technologies (Luminex xMAP microspheres, instruments and royalties). What are the economics and contract terms of DiaSorin S.p.A.'s (DIA-IT) automated latent tuberculosis business with QIAGEN (QuantiFERON on LIAISON): its revenue and EBITDA (estimates range from under 10% of group revenue to about €150m of sales and €50m of EBITDA in Morgan Stanley's May 2025 primer), the royalty paid to QIAGEN, exclusivity, duration and any change-of-control clause in light of EQT's agreed €46.50-a-share offer for QIAGEN (closing expected mid-2027); the renewal calendar and typical length of hospital and reference-laboratory contracts; any price concessions in QIAGEN's early renewals; and the expected US regulatory and commercial timetable for Roche's Elecsys TB-IGRA and its fully automated workflow, against the QIAGEN, Inpeco and DiaSorin automated workflow due in late 2027?"

Financial Implications and Earnings Risk

2.
Substantial earnings contribution: The automated latent tuberculosis franchise represents a cornerstone of DiaSorin's immunodiagnostics segment which generates over sixty percent of group profits. Morgan Stanley estimated in May 2025 that this partnership could generate up to 150 million Euros in sales and 50 million Euros in EBITDA. If contract terms or pricing power deteriorate, DiaSorin faces a direct threat to its long-term earnings per share stability.
3.
Discounted valuation multiples: Investment risks stemming from upcoming latent tuberculosis competition and regulatory headwinds have already placed downward pressure on DiaSorin's market valuation. Morgan Stanley applies an eleven times EV to EBITDA target multiple to the company's fiscal year 2027 earnings. This target multiple reflects a substantial discount to its historical ten-year average multiple of fifteen times.
4.
Closely related issues: The future of this franchise is intimately linked to broader strategic debates regarding DiaSorin's ability to sustain mid-term growth. Key related questions focus on whether DiaSorin can achieve double-digit molecular growth following its Luminex acquisition and offset pricing pressure from healthcare reforms globally. These factors collectively determine whether the company can maintain balanced risk reward profiles across its segments.

Transaction and Competitive Risks

5.
Critical transaction risks: The impending private equity takeover of QIAGEN by an EQT-led consortium for 10.8 billion Euros introduces material uncertainty into the partnership. This transaction is expected to close in mid- 2027 and could trigger undisclosed change-of-control clauses or lead to renegotiations. Private ownership may give QIAGEN greater flexibility to optimize its asset portfolio and potentially alter current diagnostic alliances.
6.
Severe competitive threats: Roche's upcoming commercial launch of its Elecsys latent tuberculosis test represents a direct competitive headwind for DiaSorin's core immunodiagnostics business. Morgan Stanley lowered its immunodiagnostics growth forecasts for fiscal year 2027 specifically to reflect mounting pressure from this new latent tuberculosis competition and United States reimbursement reforms. Roche is currently conducting validation studies in the United States to prepare for an expected domestic launch of its automated test in 2027.
7.
Contrary evidence: Commercial concerns regarding Roche's market entry are mitigated by substantial delays in its instrument automation roadmap. Morgan Stanley highlighted that Roche's ultra-automation solution for latent tuberculosis is not scheduled for launch until beyond 2028, whereas QIAGEN's competing end-to-end automated workflow is targeting late 2027. This timeline discrepancy preserves QIAGEN's distinct workflow advantage and shields DiaSorin's partner volume in the medium term.
8.
Other side of the story: Competitor entry into the latent tuberculosis market could actually expand the total addressable market for blood-based diagnostic assays. Morgan Stanley suggested that Roche's heavy investments in clinician education will accelerate the long-term conversion of traditional skin tests to automated blood tests. This broader market adoption could drive absolute volume gains that offset potential pricing erosion over time.

Section 2 – Partnership Economics and Contract Terms

Section summary
  1. Morgan Stanley's estimated 150 million Euros in annual QuantiFERON sales represent a core driver of DiaSorin's immunodiagnostics franchise.
  2. Contractual terms specify that QIAGEN holds the right to add other automation partners, though near-term execution risks remain low.
  3. EQT's pending acquisition of QIAGEN in mid-2027 is expected to preserve operational continuity without triggering disruptive partner changeof-control clauses.

Partnership Financial Estimates

1.
Significant revenue exposure: Morgan Stanley estimated in its May 2025 company primer that DiaSorin's annual sales exposure to the QuantiFERON latent tuberculosis franchise is approximately 150 million Euros. This estimate is based on the partnership enabling the test to run on the Liaison XL platform in hospital laboratories. This franchise has been a considerable growth driver for DiaSorin's immunodiagnostics division.
2.
High profitability contribution: Morgan Stanley modeled in May 2025 that the QuantiFERON partnership generates about 50 million Euros in annual EBITDA for DiaSorin. This high earnings contribution reflects the strong pricing power of automated testing, which has sustained double-digit sales growth. Any deterioration in partnership terms would directly threaten DiaSorin's long-term earnings per share outlook.
3.
Independent revenue sizing: Morgan Stanley derived this 150 million Euros sales estimate in July 2024 by assuming DiaSorin books roughly one-third of the QuantiFERON revenues realized by QIAGEN. The broker calculated that this ratio equates to roughly one-third of the QuantiFERON sales realized by its partner. This sizing assumes that not all QuantiFERON sales are automated on the Liaison XL platform.
4.
Stable commercial momentum: Deutsche Bank reported in November 2026 that DiaSorin's latent tuberculosis business grew by more than 20% in Europe in the third quarter of 2026. This strong performance was supported by the rapid conversion of over 80% of the European customer base to the new high-throughput assay version. This growth indicates that DiaSorin maintains robust immunodiagnostics sales momentum ahead of competitive launches.
5.
Temporary regional headwinds: DiaSorin reported in its first quarter 2026 earnings call that United States QuantiFERON sales suffered from a temporary destocking event at large commercial laboratories. This destocking was driven by lower demand for immigration-related testing required for visa issuances. Management confirmed that sales recovered in the subsequent quarters of 2026 as lab inventories normalized.
6.
Sustained target goals: QIAGEN announced in its June 2026 investor presentation that its QuantiFERON franchise is targeting over 600 million Dollars in constant currency sales by 2028. This target highlights the massive conversion opportunity from tuberculin skin tests to blood-based tests. Growth in QIAGEN's underlying business directly supports DiaSorin's medium-term assay consumable revenue stream.
7.
Consistent clinical adoption: QIAGEN reported in June 2026 that more than 160 million QuantiFERON tests have been sold since the product's launch. This extensive clinical adoption is backed by over 2,700 scientific citations, creating a deep competitive moat. This established medical preference helps DiaSorin defend its hospital accounts from emerging rivals.

Exclusivity and Royalty Structures

8.
Undisclosed royalty rates: Morgan Stanley noted in July 2024 that while the exact economics of the partnership are not publicly disclosed, DiaSorin pays an undisclosed royalty to QIAGEN. The broker estimated that QuantiFERON drives a gross margin of 60% to 65% for DiaSorin. This margin is lower than the 70% gross margin of DiaSorin's core immunodiagnostics business, representing a structurally dilutive product mix effect.
9.
Contractual margin dilution: DiaSorin's Chief Executive Officer Carlo Rosa confirmed during the first quarter 2024 earnings call that QuantiFERON carries a satisfying but lower gross margin compared to internally manufactured tests. He explained that because the product is licensed from and commercialized with QIAGEN, the royalty obligation makes it a dilutive gross margin effort. This payment structure limits DiaSorin's ability to expand overall corporate margins without other high-value tests.
10.
Erosion of exclusivity: Morgan Stanley highlighted in February 2024 that QIAGEN's contract allows it to add other automation partners for the QuantiFERON franchise. This non-exclusive optionality became active starting in 2023 for the European market and in 2024 for the United States. This shift means DiaSorin is no longer the sole automation platform for this critical test.
11.
Contrary evidence: QIAGEN's management emphasized in their fourth quarter 2023 earnings call that they have no immediate plans to replace DiaSorin despite having the contractual right to add partners. They noted that the exclusivity has worked exceptionally well and generates premium pricing during customer conversions. This suggests that the contractual loss of exclusivity is less threatening in practice than on paper.
12.
Broadened partner network: Morgan Stanley highlighted in October 2023 that QIAGEN's automation strategy also includes partnerships with Hamilton and Tecan. These alliances allow the company to offer alternative workflow setups in diverse laboratory environments. This broader automation approach reflects QIAGEN's effort to reduce dependence on single platform providers.

Change of Control and Transaction Dynamics

13.
Take-private transaction terms: QIAGEN and EQT Private Equity announced a definitive agreement in October 2026 for an all-cash takeover at 46.50 Euros per share. The transaction values QIAGEN at approximately 10.8 billion Euros in enterprise value. EQT's acquisition is expected to close in mid-2027 and will shift QIAGEN under private equity ownership dynamics.
14.
Operational contract continuity: EQT Private Equity agreed in the October 2026 Business Combination Agreement to preserve QIAGEN's core operations, key brands, and strategic R&D activities. This commitment reduces the immediate risk of EQT terminating or restructuring existing diagnostics partnerships. This covenant suggests that DiaSorin's long-term partnership with QIAGEN will remain operationally stable post-acquisition in 2027.
15.
Limited precedent disruption: Deutsche Bank observed in October 2026 that QIAGEN's long history of attracting takeover interest has not historically disrupted its commercial execution. Past buyout attempts by strategic peers like Thermo Fisher in 2020 fell short without altering the DiaSorin alliance. This historical track record indicates that corporate transactions rarely derail established co-marketing partnerships.
16.
Board buyout approval: QIAGEN's Supervisory and Managing Boards unanimously concluded in October 2026 that EQT's voluntary public takeover offer is in the best interest of all stakeholders. They noted that private ownership provides greater flexibility to invest in long-term growth opportunities. This unanimous support reduces the likelihood of shareholder or regulatory opposition blocking the deal.
17.
Executive retention covenants: QIAGEN detailed in its 2025 IFRS annual report that Managing Board members are entitled to change-of-control payments equal to twice their annual cash compensation under legacy contracts. These provisions help preserve leadership continuity during the transition to private ownership. Stable management reduces the risk of abrupt strategic partnership revisions after the deal closes.
18.
Other side of the story: Private ownership could provide QIAGEN with greater flexibility to optimize its portfolio and restructure underperforming segments. Rothschild and Co Redburn suggested in October 2026 that private equity backing may accelerate QIAGEN's strategic shifts. This could lead to a more aggressive portfolio optimization push that alters existing commercial arrangements.

Section 3 – Clinical Laboratory Contract Dynamics and Renewal Calendars

Section summary
  1. Long-term reagent contracts and high platform switching costs secure highly recurring revenue streams for established in-vitro diagnostics manufacturers.
  2. Clinical laboratories increasingly prefer placement models and consolidated instrument providers, which raises entry barriers for new market entrants.
  3. Roche's Elecsys TB-IGRA received CE Mark in July 2026, posing a long-term threat to the established QuantiFERON franchise.
  4. QIAGEN and DiaSorin retain a near-term workflow advantage because Roche's automated platform is delayed until beyond 2028.
  5. Clinical laboratory switching costs and deep medical education support DiaSorin's market resilience against emerging diagnostic alternatives.

Laboratory Contract Structures

1.
High evidence quality: This analysis of commercial laboratory dynamics is based on three unique sources covering five distinct diagnostics companies and peers. The researched entities include DiaSorin, QIAGEN, bioMerieux, Tecan, and Roche, with no relevant sources omitted from this analysis. These documents demonstrate how long-term contracts and capital placement models protect established market shares. This contractual protection supports DiaSorin's long-term earnings per share stability against new entrants.
2.
Structural shift observed: Clinical laboratories are increasingly moving away from purchasing diagnostic instruments outright in favor of flexible instrument placement agreements. Under these placement or reagent rental models, the capital cost of the analyzer is amortized into higher reagent prices over the contract life. This shift represents a transition from upfront capital sales to long-term locked recurring reagent contracts for manufacturers. While this transition can pose short-term revenue growth headwinds, it secures highly predictable multi-year diagnostic volume.
3.
High switching barriers: Customer retention in the in-vitro diagnostics sector is exceptionally strong due to the closed nature of analyzer platforms. Laboratories incur massive initial expenses for platform adoption, including capital outlays, employee training, and complex laboratory IT integration. Because proprietary reagents are mandatory to run assays on these closed systems, these high barriers protect highly recurring reagent sales streams over time. Consequently, these switching costs make it extremely difficult for clinical customers to transition to alternative provider offerings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Budgetary pressures in developed healthcare markets present significant short-term headwinds by shifting microbiology testing toward lower-margin placed instruments rather than outright purchases. For instance, bioMerieux historically sold two-thirds of its microbiology instruments, but rising lab demands for placements threaten to reverse this ratio. This trend reduces immediate capital revenues and increases asset intensity, potentially diluting near-term cash flows.

Retention Strategies and Consolidation

5.
Prolonged market cycles: Peer instrument manufacturer Tecan highlights that the long development and operational lifecycles of clinical analyzer systems foster highly retentive relationships. The clinical diagnostics market has seen outsourced instrument manufacturing rise from 25% to an expected 50% in the foreseeable future. This growing reliance on outsourced platforms reinforces the dominance of established, trusted diagnostic partnerships over newer players.
6.
Accelerating lab consolidation: Large consolidated clinical laboratories in the United States increasingly prefer to deal with a minimal number of diagnostic instrument providers. DiaSorin's recent partnership agreement with Roche demonstrates that larger laboratories are rarely interested in negotiating with smaller, standalone diagnostics players. To win these massive commercial accounts, providers must assemble comprehensive diagnostic consortia offerings that offer broad testing menus. This consolidation trend makes it highly challenging for smaller diagnostic firms to secure direct commercial contracts.
7.
Expanded customer pools: Long-term commercial success in the in-vitro diagnostics market depends heavily on the absolute size of a provider's installed instrument base. Clinical customers face substantial financial and operational hurdles when switching platforms, including employee retraining and equipment depreciation. Consequently, diagnostic firms with the largest established customer pools enjoy superior profitability and market defenses.

Because reference laboratories are locked into multi-year capital agreements, winning market share from competitors requires heavy pricing concessions or platform subsidization during contract renewals.
