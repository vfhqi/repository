The challenge

The results admit no reading yet. The price does, and it admits two that pull against each other, neither of which the case accommodates, because the case treated its re-opening price as a level and not as information.

Reading one: the price has crossed the case's re-opening level because the market is pricing the case's invalidation level, and the two triggers are about to fire together. The case re-opens below about €10.50, where the Modal return clears 20% a year at both horizons; it invalidates on H2 2026 adjusted EBITDA below €4m or Q3 2026 storage intake below €30m. Jefferies' FY26 adjusted EBITDA of €18.8m less the €16.4m delivered in H1 leaves €2.4m for H2 (our arithmetic on the review's figures), below the invalidate line. Deutsche Bank's Q3 alone is €3.0m at a 3.0% margin. If H2 storage deliveries are the company's €37m rather than Jefferies' €43m, H2 revenue sits nearer the bottom of the range the memo computed from guidance (€174m to €214m). Personnel costs rose 13.5% in H1 and the restructuring is still running. A share price that falls 18.5% in three months on no company disclosure since 18 August, to the 18th relative-strength percentile, with technical momentum pinned F, is consistent with the market leaning toward the 4% floor of guidance rather than the 5.7% consensus. On this reading the memo's clearing price is stale, because it was computed on a path with FY26 adjusted EBITDA of €21.5m and FY27 of €30m; if FY26 lands at €18.8m the FY27 step is €11.2m rather than €8.5m, the odds on the path (about one in four at the memo) fall, and a price below €10.50 is a mark, not an entry. The memo said the balance sheet, the franchise and the capitulation bounded the downside over the three-month horizon of the price patterns; the price fell a further 10% inside seven weeks. What would have to be true: Q3 adjusted EBITDA at or below €3.0m with the call guiding to the lower half of the range; storage intake below €30m; substations below 800 in the quarter.
