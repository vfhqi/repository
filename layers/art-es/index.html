Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external forces acting on Arteche are among the strongest in the universe for its fundamentals over the next 18 to 24 months: funded, dated mandates in Spain, the EU and the US, a scarcity of qualified capacity that lets a homologated supplier take share and price, and a US regulatory fence that favours exactly its product and footprint. Three things the model must carry from this group. First, the price component of growth is fading: new-order pricing has reached a high plateau and should contribute little from 2027, while executed margins can still rise into 2028 as old backlog runs off. Second, currency translation is a persistent headwind on reported growth (about half of revenue is outside the euro, unhedged). Third, the external risk to the investment case is the discount rate and sector positioning, not demand; in October 2026 the group fell 12% to 20% while orders and estimates rose. The dated turning point for the external picture is the arrival of Tier-1 and Asian capacity in 2027 to 2029.

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

D4218th of 35

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: competitive intensity in Arteche's own niches is low today and is starting to tighten at the edges; the direction is negative, the magnitude over the next six to twelve months is small, and the real step-up is dated to 2027 to 2029, when Tier-1 and Asian capacity arrives.   • Evidence that it is tightening. Siemens Energy is adding about 50% to transformer and switchgear capacity between 2026 and 2030 (Charlotte plant early 2027); Hitachi Energy announced a $528m Mississippi transformer plant (16-Sep-2026) to double US output by 2029 and a $180m Finnish plant; GE Vernova's $138m Charleroi expansion includes instrument transformers, the one confirmed Tier-1 capacity addition inside Arteche's niche; Siemens Energy's Trench bought the fibre-optic sensor firm H Nu, so Tier-1s are targeting the high-margin digital-measurement corner; Bernstein (22-Oct-2026) sees Chinese makers closing the technology gap in medium voltage; Korean makers already hold 19% of US transformer imports; JST Power Equipment is building in Poland and Mexico; India's Quality Power, through Mehru, now makes instrument transformers up to 500 kV. Morgan Stanley warns that European pricing has stabilised at high levels and that price-cost could turn into a headwind on new orders beyond 2027.
  • An offset specific to Arteche. The US exclusion of vendors tied to China and 23 other countries from equipment of 69 kV and above, with instrument transformers named, lowers intensity in Arteche's largest single market (North America, about 29% to 33% of revenue, where it holds 20% to 30% of high-voltage instrument transformers) for years.
  • For the model. New-order pricing roughly flat from 2027; executed margins can still rise through 2028 as contracts priced before 2024 run off (the Sector digest's "a price plateau at a high level is two to three more years of rising margin"); from 2028 assume price-cost neutral to slightly negative.
