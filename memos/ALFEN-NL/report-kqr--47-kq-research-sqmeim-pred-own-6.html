This transaction values Trench Group at approximately 13x its expected 2028 adjusted EBITDA or roughly 18x its expected 2026 adjusted EBITDA of 0.5 billion dollars. This policy shift requires the electricity grid's capacity to expand rapidly to support renewable generation. Meeting this target will demand approximately EUR 1.2 trillion in European grid investments through 2040.
2.
Expanding grid capex: European utility companies are raising their infrastructure budgets to accommodate the ongoing transition to clean energy. Financial analysts at ING expect the combined capital expenditure of European utilities to exceed EUR 200 billion in 2027. These elevated investment levels are supported by stable regulatory frameworks that guarantee predictable returns.
3.
Surging digital demand: Data center expansion is emerging as a massive driver of European medium-voltage equipment demand over the decade. Hyperscalers are committing billions of euros to expand Europe's AI infrastructure, with capacity expected to rise by 75% by 2030. As AI infrastructure expands, demand should extend to grid connections, substations, cooling, and electrical equipment.
4.
Intense grid congestion: Dutch grid operators Stedin, Enexis, and Liander are facing urgent capacity constraints on their distribution networks. These operators project a 13% annual increase in substation installations through 2028 to relieve grid bottlenecks. This regional acceleration provides Alfen N.V. with a strong domestic pipeline for its Smart Grid Solutions segment.

Contrary evidence: The European distribution grid continues to face severe execution delays due to structural permitting and construction timelines. Distribution-grid projects typically take eight to ten years from initial planning to final commissioning. These lengthy lead times suggest that capital investment alone cannot resolve grid congestion bottlenecks in the near term.

Open corporate charter and vulnerability

16.
Open architecture: Alfen N.V. maintains a corporate charter with no active anti-takeover defense measures or protective foundations in place. According to the Alfen Annual Report 2025, the company has not imposed any limitations on share transfers and possesses no priority shares or voting caps. This structural openness leaves the company highly exposed to unsolicited corporate raids if its market valuation continues to trade at a deep discount.
17.
Unrestricted voting: Alfen N.V. guarantees that each ordinary share carries one vote with no restrictions on voting rights. The Alfen Annual Report 2025 confirms that there are 21,750,000 issued ordinary shares, of which 21,683,091 are outstanding after excluding treasury shares. This simple share capital structure means that any hostile acquirer can purchase shares on the open market and exercise full voting power without facing voting limitations.
18.
Customary default triggers: Alfen N.V. has negotiated commercial contracts that include standard change of control provisions instead of custom defensive poison pills. The Alfen Annual Report 2025 states that the company's credit facility agreement contains events of default that are customary for this type of corporate financing. This means a hostile takeover would trigger credit defaults unless lenders consent, which represents the only immediate commercial hurdle for an acquirer.
19.
Fragmented ownership: Alfen N.V. faces structural vulnerability because its shareholder base is highly fragmented among international institutional investors. AFM disclosures published in the Alfen H2 2025 report reveal that Legal & General holds 7.27% while Schroders Plc holds 4.88% of the company's voting capital. Since there is no dominant anchor shareholder, a strategic bidder could assemble a significant voting bloc relatively easily through open market accumulations.

Structural insulation via Dutch law

20.
Mandatory regime: Alfen N.V. operates under the Dutch large company regime or structuurregime which shifts control away from shareholders to the board. The company's 2025 annual report outlines that Alfen became subject to this statutory regime on April 26, 2024, after meeting the employee and equity thresholds for three consecutive years. This transition required Alfen to amend its articles of association, fundamentally altering its corporate decision-making framework.
21.
Board appointment power: Alfen N.V. isolates its executive leadership because the Supervisory Board appoints board members directly under the structuurregime. The company's amended articles of association establish that the Supervisory Board, rather than the General Meeting, holds the sole authority to appoint and dismiss managing directors. This statutory setup shields management from quick dismissal by activist shareholders who might purchase a major stake.
