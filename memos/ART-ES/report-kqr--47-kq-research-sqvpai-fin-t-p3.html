Mechanics of the Scarcity Loop

8.
Mechanics of scarcity: Massive global utility investment programs have triggered an intense reinforcing scarcity feedback loop across the electrical equipment sector. This structural imbalance begins when grid connection backlogs force utility buyers to prioritize equipment availability over unit prices. Consequently, manufacturers are able to push contract price increases and realize expanded margins, which generates the excess cash flow needed to fund further production capacity expansions. Ultimately, these investments allow early-moving suppliers to capture additional market share while sustaining high earnings growth.
9.
Normalizing capacity effects: Industry-wide manufacturing expansions eventually activate a balancing feedback loop that limits long-term pricing power. As suppliers aggressively add assembly lines, the cumulative market capacity begins to catch up with outstanding backlogs. This increased equipment availability should drastically shorten customer lead times from historic peaks. Once lead times normalize, intense regional competition typically reappears, forcing contract prices and operating margins back to historical levels and depressing subsequent earnings per share.

Mechanics of Homologation Moats

1.
Rigid qualification: High-voltage equipment manufacturers must overcome steep technical barriers to enter utility networks because grid operators prioritize operational reliability. Bernstein Research's report of 22 October 2026 states that under the IEC 62067 standard, high-voltage cable systems must undergo a pre-qualification test involving a one-year heating-cycle voltage test followed by physical inspection. This long-term testing process creates a highly uncompressible testing time barrier, which means that established manufacturers enjoy secure long-term market positions.
2.
System-specific testing: Cable accessories and transformers cannot be qualified as standalone components because they must be validated as part of integrated utility systems. Bernstein Research's report of 22 October 2026 explains that accessory suppliers must cooperate extensively with cable makers to complete repeated testing across multiple platforms. This deep integration raises switching costs for utilities, so incumbents can secure high repeat orders over decades.
3.
Self-reinforcing installed base: Grid equipment suppliers leverage their extensive historical reference projects to capture future replacement cycles and expand their market share. Bernstein Research's report of 22 October 2026 highlights that transmission operators prioritize proven track records and local validation because component failures carry catastrophic penalties. This reputational advantage creates a powerful reinforcing installed-base feedback loop that automatically feeds into new tenders.
