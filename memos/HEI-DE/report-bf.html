Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Based on comprehensive cross-sectional analysis of Heidelberg Materials' operational and financial profile against direct peer benchmarks, several areas of relative underperformance emerge that warrant investor attention. The group's 21.8% adjusted EBITDA margin in FY 2025, while resilient, trails Buzzi's superior 29.5% margin by a substantial 770 basis points, driven primarily by Buzzi's concentrated high-margin US exposure and elevated cement mix compared to Heidelberg's broader geographic and product diversification. This margin gap represents a structural disadvantage stemming from portfolio composition rather than operational inefficiency, yet it signals persistent relative profitability pressure that may constrain through-cycle returns relative to more geographically focused peers. Buzzi's 29.50% EBITDA margin substantially exceeds Heidelberg's 21.80% margin in FY 2025, attributable to Buzzi's high-margin US exposure (38.40% margin) and elevated cement mix (71% of revenues). Furthermore, Morgan Stanley highlights that Holcim outperformed Heidelberg in Europe during Q2 2026 by expanding margins and EBIT, indicating competitive execution gaps even within shared geographic markets.

The 72 basis point EBITDA margin contraction Heidelberg experienced in Q2 2026 contrasts unfavorably with Holcim's margin expansion during the identical period, suggesting weaker operational cost absorption or pricing execution during episodes of elevated logistics and energy inflation. Working capital management reveals temporary but material cash conversion headwinds, with a EUR 1,244 million working capital outflow in H1 2026 driven by a rise in inventories of SCMs, limestone, and coal.

While management attributes this to strategic raw material stockpiling ahead of anticipated price increases, the magnitude of inventory build relative to peers signals either overly aggressive procurement or suboptimal inventory turnover that temporarily suppresses free cash flow conversion below peer standards. The 10.4% return on invested capital achieved in FY 2025, though improved from prior years, remains below the stated 12% ROIC target for 2030, indicating persistent capital efficiency gaps that require sustained operational improvement to close.

Heidelberg's high operating leverage exposes profitability to disproportionate downside during volume inflections, as evidenced by a 2% organic volume decline in 1Q 2026 causing a 30% EBIT drop. This asymmetric sensitivity to demand fluctuations contrasts with peers operating more geographically concentrated portfolios with tighter local pricing discipline, creating structural earnings volatility that may persistently compress valuation multiples relative to more stable peer businesses.

Finally, persistent residential construction weakness across European markets represents a material volume headwind that management has yet to fully offset through commercial and infrastructure channels, with sluggish winter volumes in 1Q 2026 highlighting severe seasonal macro-sensitivity, causing a temporary 4% decline in group revenue, underscoring the group's continued exposure to cyclical end-market volatility that peers with stronger infrastructure mix have partially mitigated.

Outliers worth noting

The most pronounced statistical anomaly emerges in EBITDA margin performance, where peer Buzzi commands an exceptional 29.50% EBITDA margin in FY 2024, establishing a striking 760 basis point differential above Heidelberg Materials' 21.80% adjusted EBITDA margin for the same period. This divergence is structurally rooted in Buzzi's distinct geographical and product portfolio composition, specifically its high-margin US exposure (delivering a 38.40% operating margin ) and its elevated cement concentration ( 71% of consolidated revenues derived from cement versus Heidelberg's more balanced 51% cement mix ). The margin gap persists despite Heidelberg's comparable scale, strategic positioning, and operational self-help initiatives, suggesting that geographic mix and product concentration rather than operational execution drive the variance [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 9].

A second notable divergence surfaces in relative peer outperformance during cyclical inflections, where competitor Holcim demonstrably expanded margins and EBIT in the European segment during Q2 2026, while

Heidelberg experienced a 72 basis point Group EBITDA margin contraction driven by elevated logistics, freight, and persistent energy cost inflation during the same quarter. This sequential underperformance raises questions about relative pricing power execution, fuel surcharge implementation lags, or suboptimal geographic subsegment exposure within Heidelberg's European portfolio, particularly given both operators face identical macro headwinds and carbon regulation frameworks [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 8, Section 9].

The third outlier manifests in working capital dynamics, where Heidelberg recorded an unusually severe EUR 1,244 million working capital outflow in H1 2026, primarily driven by substantial inventory build-ups in supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs), limestone, and coal stockpiles. This magnitude of seasonal outflow appears disproportionate when benchmarked against stable peer-relative efficiency metrics and highly consistent historical inventory turn patterns across CRH, Holcim, and the broader heavyside peer group.

The timing and scale of raw material pre-buying suggests either opportunistic procurement positioning ahead of anticipated supply tightness, aggressive hedging against future input inflation, or potentially suboptimal procurement coordination that temporarily trapped cash in non-productive working capital during a period of elevated borrowing costs [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 8]. The strategic rationale for this departure from peer norms remains incompletely articulated in public disclosures, warranting closer monitoring of inventory conversion velocity and procurement strategy transparency in subsequent reporting periods.
