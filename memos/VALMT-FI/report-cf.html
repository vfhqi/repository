Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro) 36 findings

Section summary
  1. external forces are diverging as a severe cyclical pulp and paper downturn is partially mitigated by structural, high-margin digitalization and energy transition demand

Demand

1.
Stable pulp growth: global pulp demand continues to grow at a long-term rate of 1% to 2% annually, translating into a structural requirement for approximately one new major pulp mill globally per year
2.
Emerging market driver: rising standard of living in emerging markets, driving packaging, board, and tissue consumption, remains the primary long-term demand catalyst across Valmet's product categories
3.
Challenging mature markets: persistent overcapacity and low operating rates in Europe and China have severely depressed immediate customer profitability and capital investment appetite. - Earnings Calls Deferred capital recovery: the recovery in pulp and paper capital equipment, initially projected for 2025, has been postponed into late 2026 or 2027 as global trade disputes and tariff turmoil weaken consumer confidence
4.
Plastic substitution tailwinds: structural demand growth is supported by an ongoing consumer and regulatory shift away from single-use plastics toward recyclable, fiber-based packaging solutions
5.
Energy transition opportunity: decarbonization commitments are driving rapid demand growth for alternative bioenergy solutions, fluidized bed biomass boilers, and emission control systems

Competition

6.
Consolidated technology duopoly: in the capital-intensive pulp equipment market, Valmet operates in an exceptionally consolidated duopoly, sharing a combined 80% to 100% market share almost exclusively with Andritz
7.
Leading paper position: while Andritz maintains a dominant upstream position in pulp mills, Valmet remains the clear global market leader in paper and board machine technology, with Voith acting as its primary fastmachine competitor
8.
Fragmented automation landscape: in Flow Control and Automation Systems, Valmet competes against large, well-capitalized process automation giants including Emerson, ABB, Honeywell, and Flowserve
9.
Final control premiums: within the valves and final control market, competitors like Emerson command premium pricing through established hardware reputations, forcing Valmet to compete on lifecycle cost and digital integration
10.
Low automation switching: the competitive dynamic in industrial automation is insulated by exceptionally high customer switching costs, as plant operators rarely change distributed control system vendors once embedded
11.
Small project profitability: competition remains highly intense for large capital projects, prompting Valmet to focus on smaller, high-margin mill improvement contracts where pricing pressure is less severe

Disruption

12.
Web-based DCS transition: the commercial launch of the fully web-based Valmet DNAe distributed control system represents a major technological leap, offering built-in cybersecurity and advanced analytics
13.
R&D ecosystem investment: Valmet has launched the EUR 55 million Industrial NEXUS R&D program, co- funded with EUR 15 million from Business Finland, to develop self-operating processes and AI-driven humanmachine collaboration
14.
Digital twin deployment: rapid advancements in cloud computing, predictive maintenance, and digital twin software are creating a new wave of services demand, allowing Valmet to expand its Valve Performance Monitoring services
15.
AI-driven tissue software: the completed acquisition of FactoryPal GmbH brings AI-driven machine learning algorithms to tissue converting, optimizing shopfloor settings and reducing manual intervention
16.
Plastic replacement innovations: technological progress in 3D/molded fiber packaging now enables cellulose fibers to be pressed directly into high-volume food packaging shapes, directly disrupting plastic thermoforming
17.
Electrically driven carbon capture: collaboration with Linde Engineering integrates electrically driven CO2 separation with Valmet’s flue gas conditioning, eliminating the need for steam in mill-level carbon capture

Regulation

18.
Strict emission baselines: environmental regulations such as the EU Industrial Emissions Directive (IED) set high baseline standards, but carbon capture installations demand flue gas pre-treatment that far exceeds these legal limits
19.
Carbon border adjustments: implementation of the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) has introduced complex quarterly reporting obligations for embedded CO2 emissions on steel and aluminum imported from outside the EU
20.
Marine sulfur restrictions: strict international marine directives limiting sulfur concentrations to below 0.1% have structurally driven the adoption of Valmet’s stainless-steel flue-gas desulfurization scrubbers
21.
Green finance integration: alignment with the EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) and the EU Taxonomy has enabled Valmet to issue EUR 250 million in green debt instruments, lowering its relative cost of capital
22.
Weaponization of trade policies: rising protectionism and the anti-competitive use of standards, environmental regulations, and administrative requirements create persistent friction in cross-border equipment deliveries

Supply

23.
Energy-intensive material exposure: Valmet's upstream supply chain remains highly exposed to energy- intensive raw materials, with steel assemblies, structures, and components constituting the primary raw material inputs
24.
Carbon pricing cost pass-through: emerging climate regulations and global carbon pricing mechanisms are expected to structurally elevate the procurement cost of steel and alloy-adjusted metals. / CSR reports Metal volatility hedging: to manage price volatility from stainless steel alloy surcharges, Valmet actively

utilizes average-price swap agreements, holding 1,483 metric tons for nickel and 1,303 metric tons for steel scrap.

25.
Complex global footprint: procurement operations span over 17,000 active direct suppliers in 50 countries, creating elevated compliance, human rights, and quality-control monitoring requirements
26.
Component lead times: while container freight and logistics costs have normalized from pandemic peaks, certain specialized electronic and electrical components continue to exhibit extended lead times and premium pricing
27.
Supply localization shift: to mitigate logistics risks and tariffs, Valmet's renewed operating model prioritizes local sourcing close to customer sites, particularly in high-tariff markets like Brazil and the US

Cycle

28.
Severe capital cyclicality: Valmet's process technology business is highly cyclical, characterized by multi-year project development times and large capital expenditure swings among global pulp and paper producers. 88 Valmet (Buy): When wishes come true Capital expenditure trends and cyclical swings for global pulp, board, and paper companies
29.
Inventory destocking correction: a prolonged period of customer inventory destocking, driven by weak consumer demand and high interest rates, has severely depressed short-term service and transactional consumables orders. - Earnings Calls Stable aftermarket buffer: the stable services and automation businesses act as a structural cyclical hedge, representing 50% of the total EUR 4.3 billion order backlog and generating more resilient cash flows
30.
Equipment backlog depletion: as major capital deliveries progress, the order backlog for deliveries beyond the current fiscal year has declined to EUR 2.1 billion from EUR 2.4 billion in the prior period
31.
Small project shift: customers are actively postponing large greenfield investments, shifting their cyclical spending toward smaller, rapid-payback mill modernization and energy-efficiency projects

Macro

32.
Global GDP deceleration: projected global economic growth deceleration to 2.9% in 2026, driven by higher effective tariff rates, directly dampens global industrial production and capital investment
33.
Elevated customer leverage: major pulp and paper customers face high debt burdens and negative free cash flow generation, preventing them from making final investment decisions on prospective mega-mills
34.
Transactional currency exposure: Valmet faces substantial transaction and translation risks, particularly across the US dollar, Swedish krona, Brazilian real, and Chinese yuan
35.
Interest rate delays: elevated global interest rates have significantly increased financing costs for major power generation and pulp projects, leading to frequent project negotiation delays
36.
Limited Middle East exposure: direct macroeconomic risks from geopolitical disruptions in the Middle East are limited, as the region accounts for only slightly more than 1% of Valmet's consolidated net sales. - Earnings Calls Other side of the story: a prolonged macroeconomic recession paired with escalating global tariff wars could force customers to implement deep production breaks, completely neutralizing the defensive buffer of the service business and leading to severe underabsorption across Valmet's European manufacturing facilities
