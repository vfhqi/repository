Business overview and structure: Metsa Board operates as a leading European producer of lightweight, virgin- fibre folding boxboard and white kraftliner, catering primarily to consumer goods, pharmaceutical, and retail packaging segments. The company is integrated into the wider Metsa Group structure, which provides secure wood sourcing through the parent cooperative Metsaliitto, owned by approximately 90,000 Finnish forest owners. Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. Prior to the Q2 2026 earnings release, the market focused on Metsa Board's slow transformation progress and severe Nordic wood cost headwinds, setting a highly cautious bar for recovery. Comparable EBITDA expectations – conservative setup: Going into the Q2 2026 release, Bloomberg consensus compiled by DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta pegged Metsa Board's comparable EBITDA expectation at EUR 28 million, compared to the EUR 6.1 million delivered in Q2 2025. Comparable operating profit – return to black: Sell-side expectations were set for Metsa Board to deliver its first positive comparable operating result since Q1 2025, with Bloomberg consensus expecting a modest EUR 2 million for Q2 2026 compared to EUR -23 million in Q2 2025. Revenue projections – sequential lift: The market anticipated Q2 2026 sales of EUR 423 million, representing a sequential increase from the EUR 394 million reported in Q1 2026 but still down 8% year-on-year against the EUR 460 million posted in Q2 2025. Transformation program scrutiny – target skepticism: Analysts maintained a highly skeptical view of the company's EUR 200 million EBITDA transformation program initiated in autumn 2025. DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta noted that while the rationalisation measures were welcome, the ultimate EUR 200 million improvement target appeared somewhat optimistic given systemic overcapacity. Transformation run-rate – delivery timeline: The buy-side was closely watching for updates on the run-rate delivery of the transformation program, where the company had previously indicated a achieved run-rate of EUR 135 million, looking for proof that these savings were starting to flow into the actual Q2 results. FBB pricing action – North American push: A major focus point was the implementation of a 4-6% price increase for folding boxboard products effective September 1, which Metsa Board announced alongside peers Smurfit Westrock and Sappi North America. Peer pricing alignment – market tracking: Jefferies' Philip Ng reported that Metsa Board's price hikes followed Smurfit Westrock's July 7 announcement of a 4-6% increase on SBS folding carton and food service grades, which the market hoped would stabilize paperboard margins.

Wood cost headwind – regional pressure: The buy-side remained deeply concerned with elevated pulpwood and log costs in Finland and Sweden, which had peaked in 2025. DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta had modeled a 10% decline in Nordic pulpwood prices for 2026, and analysts wanted to see if Q2 results validated this assumed relief. 73 US export tariffs – volume restriction: Analysts focused heavily on the impact of US import tariffs, which represented a material barrier for Metsa Board's high-margin exports to North America.

The market expected delivery volumes to remain capped as a result of these trade barriers. Operating rate trajectory – capacity management: Deutsche Bank's Hillary Cacanando highlighted that North American boxboard operating rates rose to 88.8% in Q2, and the sell-side wanted to see if Metsa Board's European mills could replicate this tightening or if they would be forced into further costly market-related curtailments.

Variable cost inflation – logistics and chemicals: In Q1 2026, Metsa Board guided for a EUR 10 million quarter-on-quarter increase in logistics and chemical costs for Q2, which DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta noted would partially offset any wood and energy cost relief. FBB oversupply concerns – European capacity: Sell-side sentiment was pressured by the increasing supply of folding boxboard in Europe, which DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta flagged as a key risk that could undermine Metsa Board's core business segment.

Consensus disconnect – forecast vulnerability: DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta repeatedly warned that broader consensus earnings estimates for Metsa Board were significantly too high, leading to his Hold rating and a 25% valuation discount applied to his fair value model. Pulp market overflow – associated company drag: The global oversupply of pulp was expected to drag on Metsa Board's 25% ownership stake in Metsa Fibre, with analysts expecting the pulp segment to remain a net negative contributor in the Q2 print.

Valuation floor – asset backing: Despite poor near-term earnings visibility, the sell-side pointed out that Metsa Board was trading at a historically low price-to-book ratio, which DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta described as a 10-year low that provided an attractive normalized valuation floor. Other side of the story: While the sell-side focused heavily on near-term cost-cutting progress and the FBB price hikes, Morgan Stanley's Julia Rizzo warned that global benchmark pulp prices were flattening or declining in Europe, which threatened to overwhelm Metsa Board's internal cost-saving efforts if paperboard demand failed to recover structurally.

  1. Metsa Board's near-term recovery is supported by rapid self-help transformation measures, but structural European FBB overcapacity and US tariff barriers pose severe headwinds.
1.
Leadership restructuring – positive transition: In response to changing market dynamics, CEO Esa Kaikkonen reshaped his Corporate Leadership Team on March 1, 2026, combining production and commercial transformation roles under CFO Anssi Tammilehto. To accelerate the March 2026 strategy, the company further reshuffled its team on September 4, 2026, appointing Maarten Florizoone as SVP of Sales to lead the global sales organisation and Erja Hyrsky to head Commercial Operations. This reshuffle aims to accelerate sales while the new commercial team focuses on faster execution of its strategy

CFO departure – unexpected transition: Prior to the structural leadership changes, CFO Henri Sederholm left the group, creating near-term visibility headwinds for the investment community. DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta noted that leadership shifts occurred at a time when the market was expecting operational stability, complicating the transparency of the financial transformation. However, the consolidation of CFO and transformation duties under Anssi Tammilehto served to streamline corporate decision-making

DNB Carnegie's Robin Santavirta originally argued that achieving the EUR 200 million target was highly optimistic without extensive European capacity closures
