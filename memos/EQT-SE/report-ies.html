EQT AB (EQT-SE) — what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the case moderately: the two drivers the case named as its fulcrum, flagship fund activation and exit normalisation, both moved the wrong way in the quarter, while the genuine strengthening on the change-forces side lands on a rating the case had already credited at B.

What changed

EQT reported Q3 2026 on 15 October 2026, the first full quarter with Coller Capital consolidated.

  • Fee-paying AUM reached EUR 194.8bn, up 40% year on year, against EUR 139bn a year earlier. It beat Morgan Stanley's EUR 186bn but missed Visible Alpha consensus of EUR 197bn. Private Capital fee-paying AUM was EUR 81.8bn against EUR 85.9bn consensus (-4.8%); gross fundraising inflows were EUR 40.4bn against EUR 45.8bn (-11.8%). Total AUM was EUR 354bn, up 33%.
  • Signed gross fund exits were EUR 2.8bn in the quarter, bringing nine months to EUR 9.8bn against a full-year ambition of about EUR 20bn. Exits and step-downs of EUR 2.3bn missed Visible Alpha's EUR 3.4bn. First-half exits were EUR 7bn, so the quarter is a sequential slowdown.
  • Activation of EQT XI was pushed from August to mid-fourth-quarter 2026; management now says "within the next few months". Infrastructure VII is guided to around year-end 2026.
  • The fundraising-cycle target was raised to more than EUR 140bn from EUR 100bn, above the EUR 125bn to EUR 130bn brokers had modelled. BPEA IX closed at USD 15.6bn against a USD 12.5bn target. Scaleup Europe has EUR 3bn committed against a EUR 5bn target.
  • EQT agreed to buy Stockbridge for USD 375m upfront plus up to USD 175m of earnout over 2027 to 2030, adding about USD 16.5bn of fee-generating assets at 9x 2027 fee-related EBITDA upfront, 14x with the earnout, against a 12.5x industry average. Jefferies puts the 2027 fee-related EBITDA contribution at USD 35m to USD 45m at a 30% to 35% margin, and notes the deal dilutes the blended management fee rate. Brokers had not modelled further large M&A.
  • About 10% of fee-paying AUM is now on NAV-based fees, up from 1% a year ago; assets eligible for recurring performance fees are about EUR 20bn, charging 12% to 15% above a 5% hurdle. Jefferies notes this too lowers the blended fee rate.
  • The AI Infrastructure strategy reached USD 11.5bn NAV from USD 9.4bn in Q2 (USD 0.9bn net inflows, USD 1.2bn appreciation). Evergreen NAV reached EUR 11bn with EUR 0.9bn of Q3 net inflows; redemptions were about 0.1% of NAV per Morgan Stanley, about 1% per Jefferies.
  • Fund marks rose: EQT X 1.2x to 1.3x gross MOIC, Infrastructure V 1.8x to 1.9x, Infrastructure VI 1.3x to 1.4x, BPEA IX 1.0x to 1.1x; 5% value creation in the quarter, 10% over twelve months. Gross investments were EUR 7bn, nearly double a year earlier.
  • Headcount reached 2,274 FTE, up 17%, of which 323 from Coller; compensation per FTE rose 9% to EUR 463k in the first half; standalone opex grew 7% in the first half against mid-single-digit full-year guidance.
  • Sell-side response: Morgan Stanley to SEK 414 from SEK 410, Overweight and Top Pick, 2027e EPS EUR 1.79 from EUR 1.74 and 2028e EUR 2.05 from EUR 2.00, but 2026e cut to EUR 1.23 from EUR 1.28 on the delayed activations, with Coller personnel costs taking about 4% off 2026 and 6% off 2027-2028 earnings, and roughly 50m shares added for September lock-up expiries. DNB Carnegie to SEK 370 from SEK 365, Buy, 2028e to EUR 2.28 from EUR 2.24. Jefferies reiterated Buy, SEK 380. All three remain cautious on carry timing, which Morgan Stanley expects to defer into late 2027.
  • Price: SEK 288.5 at 15 October, from SEK 281.6 the day before; 52-week range SEK 259.0 to 379.9. The price sits 5.6% below the 200-day average of SEK 305.7 and below the 50-day of SEK 312.1; relative strength is at the 16th percentile; twelve-month return -13.3%. P/E 13.0x at the 10th percentile of its own ten-year history. Pinned letters: technical momentum F, sell-side earnings momentum C, valuation A. Three-month EPS revisions +5%, buy share 73%, consensus target SEK 377.

The challenge

The case's central bet, in its own words, is that the change forces show up in the financials before the market re-rates the multiple on its own. The case rejected the value-trap reading at Triaging because the Q1 2026 miss sat in flow and exit timing "ahead of two large, dated catalysts, the Coller close and EQT XI activation". One of those catalysts has now happened and the other has slipped. That is where these results press. We see three readings the case does not hold.

Reading one: the exit drought is the binding constraint on the whole fee engine, not a cyclical overlay on top of it. The case treats 70% to 75% of revenue as contractual, locked-up and defensive, and confines cyclicality to carry. That is right for funds already activated. It is not right for fee growth, which needs new flagships to activate, which needs LP commitments, which needs distributions. Nine-month exits of EUR 9.8bn against a EUR 20bn ambition, an EQT XI activation that has already moved once, and three brokers and an expert call all naming LP liquidity as the gating item, together describe a chain that runs from the "cyclical" part of the business straight into the "defensive" part. In this reading the EUR 140bn target is not evidence of strength; it is a larger claim on an LP base that nine in ten surveys say is liquidity-constrained, and the delayed activations are the first sign of that constraint biting. For this to be right: Q4 exits fall well short of the roughly EUR 5bn-a-quarter pace needed, EQT XI slips into 2027, and 2027 management-fee consensus comes down as a result. Against it: EQT returned about EUR 30bn to LPs in the twelve months to June 2026, BPEA IX closed above target with 75% of capital from outside Asia, and the activation delay is so far a matter of months. We regard this reading as plausible, and it is the one that would convert "cheap" into "value trap".

Reading two: the change forces are no longer dated and delivered; they are continuous, and that changes what kind of case this is. The case valued its change forces precisely because they were "dated and largely delivered": a completed succession, a signed acquisition, two flagships about to activate. Forty-five days after Coller closed, EQT has announced a second acquisition that brokers had not modelled, an infrastructure-secondaries build-out that needs a team hired, a Middle East office, an Asian evergreen launch, a EUR 5bn EU mandate, and a fee-model pivot from committed capital to NAV. The case's own weakest Building Block is case simplicity, graded D on three live change-from-current items, and its stated condition for softening that finding was a clean Coller close with no further additions. The close was clean; the additions came anyway. In this reading the primary setup the case identified, strong corporate change, is still present, but the business is now better described as a serial acquirer and platform builder than as a company working through a bounded change programme, and the evidence is visible in the numbers: headcount up 17%, pay per head up 9%, personnel costs taking 6% off broker 2027-2028 earnings, and two separate sources of blended fee-rate dilution (Stockbridge's core strategies and the NAV-based pivot) where the case had recorded a 1.40% blended fee stable since IPO. For this to be right: fee-related EBITDA margin fails to reach the low-50s by end 2027, the blended fee rate keeps falling faster than new activations lift it, and turnover rises. Against it: the entry multiples are low, Jefferies and Carnegie both call Stockbridge value-additive, and management's cost guidance for the standalone business is still mid-single-digit. We regard this reading as plausible.

Reading three: the earnings character the case graded is changing underneath it, in both directions at once. The case grades thematic fit at C because the marginal 25% to 30% of the business, carry and realisation timing, is exit-dependent and AI-exposed. Two things in these results cut across that. First, 10% of fee-paying AUM, EUR 20bn of eligible assets, now earns performance revenue on NAV marks rather than on exits, with 12% to 15% above a 5% hurdle. That makes part of the performance revenue recurring and far less hostage to the IPO window, which the case does not credit. Second, the same shift means NAV marks now flow into revenue directly rather than only into the timing of carry, so a software de-rating of the kind Redburn warns about on the 30%-technology book would hit fee-related revenue, not just defer it; and the case's evergreen-liquidity caution grows in weight as evergreen NAV reaches EUR 11bn. Meanwhile 37% of platform assets are positioned on the AI build-out side per Morgan Stanley, a tailwind the case's AI-disruption test, graded D, does not weigh at all. For this reading to matter: the NAV-based share keeps rising toward the levels Jefferies and Carnegie describe, and marks on the technology vintages diverge from marks on infrastructure. This reading does not resolve to a direction; it says the case's thematic grade is built on a revenue mix that is moving.

Readings that fail on the evidence. The sell-side's own reading, that this was a strong quarter whose only blemish is exit timing, does not survive the detail: fee-paying AUM, Private Capital AUM, gross inflows, and exits-and-step-downs all missed Visible Alpha, and the only consensus beat was against Morgan Stanley's lower number. Equally, the clean bear reading, that fundraising itself is failing, does not survive BPEA IX at USD 15.6bn, EUR 0.9bn of evergreen inflows at 0.1% to 1% redemptions while Partners Group gates, and a cycle target raised above what brokers had modelled. The demand for EQT's funds is not the problem. The timing of cash coming back out of them is.

What would settle it

  • Exits. The decisive evidence is whether Q4 signed exits approach the roughly EUR 5bn a quarter needed for two quarters running. That is not disclosed until the full-year results in January 2027. The second review, four working days from now, cannot carry it.
  • Activations. EQT XI is guided "within the next few months" and Infrastructure VII around year-end. An EQT XI activation announcement is possible before the second review but not likely inside four working days; it is more likely a November-to-January item. A further slip past year-end would be the clearest confirmation of reading one.
  • What the second review carries. It carries the rebuilt broker models: the full 2026-2028 EPS and target set once every house has digested Stockbridge, the lock-up share count and the activation delay; the share price a week on from SEK 288.5 and whether it has recovered the 200-day average; and the dashboard's revision letter after the model changes land. That settles whether the sell-side response is a net upgrade or, once the 2026 cuts and personnel costs are through, a flat one. It does not settle exits, activations, margin, or the two disclosures below.
  • Fee-related EBITDA margin. Reading two is tested by whether the margin reaches at least 52% by end 2027 against a 55% medium-term target. That is a 2027 full-year item.
  • Coller inflows. Reading one's fundraising leg is tested by whether Coller EQT shows net inflows above about USD 1.5bn a quarter through 2027. First readable in the Q1 2027 print.
  • Two things nothing scheduled will answer. The composition and current marks of EQT IX's technology holdings by vertical and revenue model, which would settle whether the 30% software exposure is being protected or is tracking toward flat carry; and the contractually committed share and redemption notice periods of Coller's evergreen strategies, which would settle how durable the private-wealth inflow is. Neither is a disclosure the company has committed to. Until they appear, the technology question stays open on the same Redburn figure it has carried since April, and the case cannot close its second key question from public data.
