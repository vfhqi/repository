Section 16 – Conclusion

The most important change since Alfen's HY 2026 earnings release on August 18, 2026, is the company's dramatic pivot from net debt of €20.7 million at the end of FY2025 to net cash of €6.2 million, driven by exceptional working capital optimization that generated €36.5 million in operating cash flow. This balance sheet transformation represents a fundamental shift in Alfen's financial resilience and validates management's operational discipline during a period of severe margin compression.

However, this singular achievement masks a more troubling underlying reality: Jefferies analyst David Kerstens cut FY26E EBITDA by 32% to €18.8m, dragging his projected EBITDA margin to 4.0% from 6.0% previously, blaming the reduction on €8m of temporary, overlapping personnel costs during the structural reorganization. The company's revenue trajectory is heavily front-loaded in the first half of 2026, with Jefferies analyst David Kerstens expressing disappointment in Alfen's low Q2 storage order intake of €11.0m, and pointing out that the current H2 backlog of €43.0m implied 40% lower sequential revenues. Meanwhile, EV Charging delivered only 53,700 charge points in H1 2026, marking a 12.3% volume decline compared to the 61,200 units in H1 2025, with gross margins declining to 39.5% in H1 2026 from 41.3% in H1 2025 because the company initiated aggressive pricing campaigns to combat intensified home segment competition.

The core investment thesis centers on Alfen capturing structural multi-year grid reinforcement tailwinds as European electrification accelerates and Dutch regulatory fast-tracking resolves permitting bottlenecks. In Smart Grid Solutions, customer demand remained highly resilient as Alfen delivered 1,716 secondary substations in H1 2026, expanding Smart Grid revenues 15% to €111.6 million in H1 2026, with Q2 secondary substation production of 883 units beating consensus by 11%, representing 27% year-on-year volume growth on broadly stable average selling prices.

The company's 70% market share in the Netherlands for grid solutions and its newly decentralized business unit structure under CEO Michael Colijn position it to monetize the massive queue of 14,000 entities waiting for grid connections. Furthermore, the July 2026 partnership with Contemporary Amperex Technology Co., Limited to deploy 5 GWh of advanced sodium-ion battery energy storage systems across Europe enables Alfen to diversify its battery material portfolio and optimize the cost structure of its products.

The strongest counter-hypothesis challenges whether Alfen can defend margins during its transition from hardware commoditization to software-integrated solutions. Major cell manufacturers like BYD, CATL, and Samsung are systematically bypassing independent integrators, offering vertically integrated turnkey solutions that undercut the premium pricing models historically demanded by integrators. In EV Charging, Alfen's revenues contracted 17% in 1H 2026, reflecting a 12% volume drop and a 6% reduction in average selling prices because of intense pricing pressure, with this severe decline contrasting with peer Zaptec's 26% revenue growth over the same period, demonstrating that Alfen is actively losing market share ex-Netherlands due to product transitions.

More fundamentally, Alfen dramatically pulled back its ambitious long-term financial targets originally set at the May 2023 Capital Markets Day, which confidently guided toward annual revenue of at least €1 billion and adjusted EBITDA margins of 15% to 20% in the 2025 to 2027 window. The replacement of hard quantitative targets with vague qualitative ambitions of returning to consistent profitable growth in 2027 signals management's low visibility and diminished confidence in near-term execution.
