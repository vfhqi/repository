Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard faces a critical transition period over fiscal years 2027 to 2029 where pricing power directly dictates operating margins and earnings per share.
  2. Widespread promotional pressure in the United States and geopolitical tariffs in China challenge the historical growth algorithm.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Critical focus: "Pernod Ricard SA (RI-FR), the world's second-largest premium spirits group (Jameson, Absolut, Chivas Regal, Martell, Ballantine's, Malibu, Royal Stag, Blenders Pride, plus Mumm and Perrier-Jouët champagne), organised since January 2026 into two brand units (aged spirits and champagne; clear spirits and ready-to-drink) and ten market management entities, with the US, India, China, Europe and Global Travel Retail its key markets.
2.
Key question: how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: A US market in which most spirits SKUs are cutting prices (Deutsche Bank NABCA analyses, September and October 2026). Distributor destocking under high financing costs. China's minimum-price undertaking on European brandy (2025). Indian state excise changes (Maharashtra, Karnataka). The India-UK trade agreement's cut in the bottled Scotch tariff from 150% to 75% from 15 July 2026, and the customs dispute over bulk Scotch. European retailer negotiations (Poland) and excise rises in Germany and Poland in January 2027. Input-cost inflation from aged liquid, glass and freight. Emerging-market currency devaluation (rupee, lira). Consumer down-trading to smaller packs and to ready-to-drink?"
3.
Significant impact: Pernod Ricard faces critical margin expansion targets for the period from fiscal year 2027 to 2029 that depend heavily on protecting net pricing. The company expects organic operating margin expansion supported by accelerating operational efficiencies of EUR 1 bn from fiscal year 2026 to 2028. However, Morgan Stanley reports that flattish organic sales growth and underlying cost inflation will continue to pressure margins unless positive pricing or volume growth is achieved. Therefore, resolving whether pricing can hold decides whether operating margins can expand successfully.
4.
Historical missteps: Industry experts argue that aggressive price hikes following the COVID-19 pandemic severely damaged brand volumes across the spirits sector. A former Pernod Ricard director stated that recent pricing strategies post-COVID were a major mistake that led to current industry normalization challenges. Consumers reacted to these steep increases by down-trading or selectively reducing overall consumption. Consequently, Pernod Ricard must now re-evaluate its premiumization strategies to stabilize shipments.

Risks and Competitive Peer Dynamics

5.
Contrary evidence: Some key markets demonstrate that Pernod Ricard continues to successfully implement price increases and capture market share despite global headwinds. In Türkiye, which is the company's fourth largest market by operating profit, Pernod Ricard achieved strong net sales growth driven by Scotch pricing. Redburn notes that the Turkish spirits market expanded by 11% annually in volume over six years, showing that pricing power can remain highly robust under high-inflation conditions.
6.
Competitive threats: Pernod Ricard faces intense pressure in the US market as Diageo implements a major price readjustment strategy. Morgan Stanley expects spirits pricing in several key markets to remain under pressure through fiscal year 2027. Widespread price compression from peers means that Pernod Ricard must achieve volume growth to secure flat organic sales growth.
7.
Widespread discounting: Deutsche Bank reported significant pricing softness across the US spirits industry during August 2026. The broker's analysis revealed that prices reduced by more than 1% for SKUs representing 46% of the entire industry. Within this promotional environment, prices decreased for 44% of Pernod Ricard's SKUs, compared to 69% for Rémy Cointreau and 9% for Brown-Forman. These industry-wide cuts indicate that premium spirits manufacturers are giving back pricing gains to protect their market share.
8.
Regulatory hurdles: Pernod Ricard faces serious regulatory and tax challenges in key growth markets like China and India. Bernstein reports that China sales fell 25% organically in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027 due to ongoing trade disputes and distributor destocking. Meanwhile, the potential India business listing is highly anticipated, but state-level excise duty changes continue to impact local growth. These international disruptions make it harder for the group to sustain stable top-line growth organically.
9.
Other side of the story: While competitive pricing pressure and destocking present near-term headwinds, Pernod Ricard retains substantial structural advantages through its global footprint. The company's diversified premium portfolio helps mitigate local downturns, as shown by a robust 19% sales surge in India during the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. Jefferies analysts argue that the group's accelerated efficiency program will defend margins. Thus, Pernod Ricard's diversified business model may allow it to navigate regional pricing volatility successfully.

Section 2 – The US Market: Price Cuts, Destocking, and Brand Equity

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard faces severe US headwinds as distributor destocking and pricing pressure reduce shipments.
  2. Jameson and Absolut lose market share due to historical pricing mismatches and aggressive value competitors. Pernod Ricard US market profile showing 17% net sales weight and -14% organic net sales decline

Promotional Environment and Price Cuts

1.
Widespread pressure: Pernod Ricard faces an increasingly promotional US spirits market where competitors are actively reducing prices to stimulate volume. Deutsche Bank reported in October 2026 that retail pricing remains notably soft, with industry price-mix staying depressed at negative 2.5% during August. Within this challenging environment, prices decreased by more than 1% for 44% of Pernod Ricard's SKUs, indicating that the company is giving back recent pricing gains. This widespread discounting suggests that premium brand equity alone cannot protect margins.
2.
Aggressive discounting: Competitors in the US spirits sector are deploying heavy promotional strategies that far outpace Pernod Ricard's price reductions. Deutsche Bank's analysis revealed that Diageo cut prices on 71% of its SKUs, while Rémy Cointreau discounted 69% of its portfolio. By comparison, Pernod Ricard's more moderate price cuts on 44% of its SKUs represent a disciplined but volume-losing stance. This indicates that rivals are prioritizing short-term volume recovery over price.
3.
Volume contraction: The combination of price cuts and weak demand has failed to trigger a positive volume response for Pernod Ricard. Morgan Stanley reported that Pernod Ricard's US organic sales declined by 10% in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. The broker noted that off-trade scanner data through November 2026 showed Pernod Ricard's sales value declining by 5.2% trailing twelve weeks. Consequently, the company continues to experience significant underlying volume pressure in North America.

Inventory Destocking and Retail Dynamics

4.
Prolonged destocking: US distributor inventory adjustments continue to severely constrain Pernod Ricard's shipment volumes under high financing costs. An expert call with a Pernod Ricard Senior Vice President in September 2026 revealed that the US market remains heavily overstocked. The expert expects destocking to persist as a severe short-term headwind for another two years. This inventory correction means that shipments will lag behind actual consumer depletions.
5.
Retailer resistance: Premium spirits are facing structural headwinds as US retailers optimize their shelf space and working capital. A regional distributor manager reported in September 2026 that retail accounts are focusing heavily on bottles priced under $30 to match consumer budgets. Additionally, a retail channel expert from Walmart noted that spirits volume remains weak and pricing is flat because consumers are highly priceconscious. These trends suggest that premium pricing policies are meeting severe consumer resistance.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some premium segments within the United States are showing early signs of stabilizing despite the broad promotional environment. Bernstein Research reported that Pernod Ricard's underlying US volume gap to the market narrowed to negative 2% in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. Furthermore, the broker pointed out that specific brands like Malibu Rum grew 5% in late 2026, driven by new product innovations. This indicates that selective premium brands can grow during downturns.

Jameson Pricing and Share Performance

7.
Aggressive pricing: Historical price increases taken on Jameson Irish Whiskey between 2021 and 2023 have led to a significant volume backlash. Industry experts in 2026 calls noted that post-pandemic pricing was too aggressive and damaged consumer recruitment. A former Pernod Ricard director stated that these steep price hikes were a major strategic mistake. Consequently, the company has had to focus on introducing aggressive post-pandemic price hikes that alienated younger consumers.
8.
Share erosion: Jameson is experiencing market share losses in the US off-trade as consumer demand shifts toward cheaper alternatives. Morgan Stanley scanner data from October 2026 showed that Pernod Ricard lost 45 basis points of overall US value share. The broker noted that this share loss highlights ongoing competitive pressure. Consequently, the brand family is struggling to maintain its premium positioning as brand equity faces dilution.
9.
Moderate recovery: Pernod Ricard is attempting to rebuild Jameson's volume momentum through tactical price adjustments and package innovation. The company reported in October 2026 that Jameson's sell-out performance remained ahead of its competitive set during the summer. Bernstein Research also noted that Jameson's volumes accelerated by 3% in the three months trailing August 2026. This recovery suggests that careful promotional spending can restore short-term volume growth.

Absolut Vodka and Competitor Pressure

10.

Jefferies reported that Tito's Handmade Vodka holds a commanding 24% volume share in the US vodka category, while Absolut stands at just 5%. The broker noted that Tito's has emerged as a key leader by leveraging its craft image and gluten-free attributes. This structural shift shows that Tito's has captured the market through its clear value proposition.
11.
Structural decline: Absolut's brand equity continues to suffer from long-term category shifts and consumer down- trading. Morgan Stanley reported in June 2026 that middle-tier brands like Smirnoff and Absolut are being squeezed by premium polarization. A former marketing director noted that Absolut lacks the premium credibility needed to justify higher price points. Consequently, the brand is consistently losing value share to higher-end and lower-end competitors.
12.
Promotional dependency: To defend its remaining retail distribution, Absolut has relied heavily on deep promotional discounts. Bernstein Research reported that Absolut saw low-single-digit price-mix declines in Nielsen scanner data as discounting intensified. Although Absolut's ready-to-drink innovations have grown, they carry highly dilutive gross margins for the brand. Thus, volume defense has come at the expense of net pricing power.

Section 3 – China: Minimum-Price Undertaking and Cognac Competition

Section summary
  1. China's minimum pricing mechanism has increased transfer prices and squeezed import margins for major European cognac houses.
  2. Martell maintains its market leadership despite a sharp decline in consumption occasions and widespread consumer down-trading.
  3. Competitors Hennessy and Rémy Martin face similar margin compression as promotional spending rises to defend market share.

Trade Policies and Financial Impact

1.
Severe squeeze: China's anti-dumping investigation into European brandy concluded with the implementation of a minimum-price undertaking in July 2025. This research found fifteen sources, seven companies, and three precedents, and used ten sources, five companies, and one precedent, leaving out medical firm Elekta. While major cognac houses agreed to minimum price commitments, they still faced de facto price increases of 10% to 15%. This regulatory framework represents a severe margin squeeze on brand owners.
2.
Quantified damages: Pernod Ricard projected at its October 2025 annual general meeting that the 12-month financial impact from these tariffs would reach EUR 45 mm. This updated figure represents a substantial reduction from the company's previous estimate of EUR 140 mm before the minimum-price exemption was secured. However, distributor inventory levels remained elevated at fiscal year-end due to prolonged tariff uncertainty. Thus, even with the tariff exemptions, the company faces materially lower profitability in Asia- Pacific.
3.
Reduced expectations: Bernstein Research reduced its fiscal year 2026 earnings estimates for Pernod Ricard by 3.5% due to these ongoing China tariffs and broader regional weakness. The broker noted that the increased transfer price from France to China does not directly impact the selling price but increases taxes. Consequently, the higher import price incurs incrementally higher import taxes and consumption tax. Therefore, the company faces lower net pricing and operating profit.

Section 4 – India: State Excise Changes, Tariff Cuts, and Bulk Scotch Disputes

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard's premiumization strategy in India relies heavily on navigating state-specific excise policy changes and pricing controls.
  2. The India-UK trade agreement's bottled tariff cut provides immediate retail benefits, but an ongoing bulk Scotch customs classification dispute threatens local blending margins.

State Regulation and Excise Dynamics

1.
Regulated pricing: Pernod Ricard's operations in India face a highly fragmented and complex state-level regulatory system with strict pricing controls. The company reported that recent excise policy changes in Maharashtra state negatively impacted sales starting in July 2026. However, the business successfully lapped these difficult tax comparisons during the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. This state-by-state volatility remains a key risk for margins.
2.
Dynamic recovery: State-level regulatory reforms have started to drive significant volume and value acceleration for premium portfolios in key regions. Nuvama Institutional Equities highlighted that progressive tax slab rationalization in Karnataka condensed 16 slabs into 8. This regulatory adjustment helped local distillers achieve rapid premium category growth during fiscal year 2027. Consequently, supportive state-specific policies can heavily offset regional headwinds.
3.
Sturdy premiumization: Despite state-level disruptions, the broader Indian spirits industry continues to benefit from secular premiumization trends. Diageo reported that its prestige and above segment, which accounts for 90% of its sales, expanded by 7.1% during the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. This premium growth helped offset double-digit volume declines in its lower-end popular segment in Maharashtra and Karnataka. Consequently, these regulatory changes heavily penalize value portfolios.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some local spirits companies have reported severe margin compression due to rising raw material inflation despite the general premiumization trend. Nuvama Institutional Equities reported that Allied Blenders and Distillers suffered from significant supply chain disruptions and rising debt. Additionally, Radico Khaitan faced grain and packaging cost inflation that delayed its net debt-free targets. These factors indicate that Indian macroeconomic conditions do not guarantee margin expansion across all regional spirits manufacturers.

Tariff Concessions and Retail Pass-Through

5.
Substantial relief: The implementation of the India-UK Free Trade Agreement has introduced major tariff reductions that reshape the imported premium spirits landscape. The trade pact slashed customs duties on Bottled-in-Origin Scotch from 150% to 75% starting July 15, 2026. Pernod Ricard India expects this change to make premium labels like Chivas Regal ten to fifteen percent cheaper for consumers. Lower retail prices will expand the addressable consumer market.
6.
Retail adjustments: Competitors are rapidly passing these tax concessions through to retail shelf prices to stimulate consumer demand. United Spirits expects downstream shelf-price reductions of 8% to 10% on bottled Scotch as state excise cards get approved. Management reported that they have already relaunched J&B Scotch under the FTA at a highly accessible price point. Consequently, volume responses should accelerate over the next three to four months.
7.
Margin retention: While bottled imports are seeing immediate price cuts, manufacturers are taking a different approach with blended Indian whiskies. Jefferies Research reported that retail prices for Indian-Made Foreign Liquor have not yet been reduced. Instead, premium distillers are choosing to bank the FTA duty savings to drive immediate operating margin expansion. This strategy allows companies to protect their local blending margins.

Bulk Scotch Customs Disputes

8.
Customs deadlock: A severe customs classification dispute threatens to disrupt the supply chain of imported bulk ingredients in India. Indian customs authorities are holding dozens of bulk Scotch containers imported by Pernod Ricard and Diageo. The dispute centers on whether bulk shipments should be reclassified as compound alcoholic preparations under HSN code 2106. Consequently, these shipments face a potential 150% tariff instead of the preferential 75% rate.
9.
Financial burden: The proposed reclassification of bulk Scotch by the Finance Ministry would impose a substantial tax penalty on manufacturers. The reclassification and additional consumption taxes would raise the effective levy to 162.5% on bulk Scotch imports. Industry investigators suspect liquor producers used incorrect customs codes to bypass high duties. This regulatory friction could leave imports facing higher duties than before the agreement.
10.
Working capital shock: The ongoing customs deadlock has created immediate liquidity and supply chain challenges for major international distillers. More than 80 containers belonging to Pernod Ricard, Diageo, and Bacardi remain stranded at Indian ports. To secure provisional clearance, manufacturers must pay a 75% consignment value fee alongside bank guarantees. These guarantees could severely constrain corporate liquidity and raise financing costs.

Local Blending and Peer Impact

11.
Portfolio pruning: Pernod Ricard has reshaped its Indian product portfolio to focus exclusively on higher-margin, premium blended brands. The company completed the disposal of its Imperial Blue entry-level division to Tilaknagar Industries on November 30, 2025. This strategic divestment is immediately accretive to organic growth and operating margins. Consequently, Pernod Ricard India can concentrate resources on Royal Stag and Blenders Pride.
12.
Peer expansion: Tilaknagar Industries has leveraged this acquisition to significantly expand its market share and profitability. Tilaknagar expects cost optimization initiatives to drive EBITDA margin expansion of 250 to 400 basis points on the acquired Imperial Blue business. This margin expansion will be supported by lower Scotch duty costs under the India-UK FTA. However, the bulk Scotch dispute remains a key threat to these targets.
13.
Cost savings: Local distilling competitors are heavily exposed to changes in bulk Scotch import economics for their premium blending lines. Radico Khaitan imports approximately INR 2.5bn of bulk Scotch malt annually. The company expects potential input cost savings of INR 750mn as import tariffs decline to 75%. Consequently, this duty cut acts as a direct margin driver for premium domestic portfolios.

  1. European spirits sales face a soft start to fiscal year 2027 due to phasing and intense retailer negotiations.

Consequently, the sluggish European market means the group must rely on other regions to drive positive organic net sales growth.

Global Cost Pressures

10.
Margin headwinds: Geopolitical tensions in West Asia have created a significant cost drag of approximately INR 500 mm per quarter for major spirits producers. Nuvama reported on October 29, 2026, that this disruption inflated packaging materials, glass, energy, and logistics costs. These persistent supply chain headwinds mean that premium distillers must achieve positive pricing to avoid gross margin contraction.

Glass inflation: High glass packaging costs remain a severe margin challenge for premium spirits manufacturers due to long-term contract indexation lags. Emkay Research reported that glass manufacturers pass cost fluctuations downstream with a one-quarter lag under standard two-to-three-year agreements. This pricing delay means spirits manufacturers absorb immediate energy cost spikes before they can adjust retail prices.
12.
Contract frictions: Standard packaging converter contracts contain pricing pass-through clauses that are often too slow and narrow to absorb rapid cost spikes. Rabobank reported in June 2026 that converters absorb short-term cost spikes because pricing adjustments are typically quarterly or based on trailing indices. Consequently, premium spirits producers face uncovered cost inflation in freight and conversion fees.

Currency Devaluation Optics

13.
Hyperinflation accounting: Pernod Ricard uses a strict accounting policy to insulate its organic growth metrics from extreme emerging-market devaluations. The company reported in its October 2026 earnings release that local price and cost increases in hyperinflationary markets like Türkiye and Argentina are capped at a maximum of 26% annually. Capping these nominal increases ensures that reported organic sales growth reflects true underlying performance rather than currency distortion.
14.
