EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 23 findings

Merck & Co delivered $65.0 billion in reported revenue during fiscal year 2025, representing modest 1% yearover-year growth, with Keytruda family sales expanding 7% to $31.7 billion and accounting for 49% of total corporate revenues. The company operates at the intersection of profound commercial strength in oncology and imminent structural disruption from 2028 Keytruda patent expiration, requiring systematic portfolio replacement through aggressive business development and multi-modality therapeutic platform expansion.

Management maintains 81.5-82% non-GAAP gross margins while delivering 18.2% return on invested capital, substantially exceeding the peer median of 13.5% and validating high capital productivity. CEO Robert M. Davis demonstrates disciplined capital allocation, deploying over $26 billion in strategic acquisitions over the trailing twelve months while preserving investment-grade credit and sustaining $8.3 billion in annual dividend commitments.

Extreme customer concentration anomaly: Merck faces extreme downstream channel concentration risk with top three US wholesalers representing over 80% of US pharmaceutical revenue, substantially exceeding typical supplier-customer concentration ratios observed across other industrial sectors. This concentration vulnerability intensifies for specialty launches like Ohtuvayre, where five accredited specialty pharmacies account for 100% of net product sales and receivables. The operational consequence manifests in volatile quarterly revenue swings driven by wholesaler purchase timing and inventory re-balancing, including a $250 million 1Q26 US wholesaler purchasing benefit that created a corresponding top-line headwind in 3Q26, and a $30 million distributor inventory build for Winrevair in 1Q26 that unwound in subsequent quarters. This extreme customer concentration combined with inventory timing volatility creates quarterly earnings unpredictability absent in more diversified peer distribution networks, particularly when compared to consumer-facing pharmaceutical competitors with direct retail channel access. Raw materials and work-in-process inventories expanded to $11,194 million in 2Q26, with non-current Other Assets including $6,381 million in work-in-process and pre-launch inventories. This inventory carrying level substantially exceeds peer norms and reflects both defensive tariff hedging and aggressive pipeline commercialization preparation. The elevated inventory position directly correlates with management's commitment of over $14 billion in domestic capex to transition Keytruda production back to US facilities through phased intermediate drug substance and drug product manufacturing expansion and groundbreaking on a dedicated Delaware site for integrated drug substance and product supply. While this strategic buffer protects against supply disruptions and regulatory risks, it compresses cash conversion cycles and ties up substantial working capital that peers deploy more efficiently toward share repurchases or incremental M&A opportunities.
