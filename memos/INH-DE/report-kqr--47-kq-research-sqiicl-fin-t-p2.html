Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The pricing power of INDUS AG across its segments will determine whether its robust 2026 earnings per share are sustainable or represent a temporary windfall.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Critical investigation: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: (1) A tungsten feedstock price spike under Chinese export licensing, as in 2025 to 2026, when European ammonium paratungstate rose from about USD 330 per MTU in early 2025 to above USD 3,100 per MTU by August 2026; (2) a tungsten price decline or plateau while competitors regain supply, as Morgan Stanley expects from 2027 with new non- Chinese mines and recycling capacity, and as occurred after the 2011 to 2013 and 2018 to 2019 tungsten price peaks; (3) a German industrial recession with mechanical-engineering production falling (real production down 2.6% in 2026 to date and VDMA growth forecast cut to 0%, per INDUS H1 2026 interim report) and capacity utilisation near 79%; (4) a German residential construction downturn with infrastructure and digital-network spending rising (INDUS Infrastructure order intake up 23.3% in H1 2026); (5) broad input inflation in energy, steel, freight and wages, as in 2021 to 2023 and again in 2026 with Brent above USD 100 (Jefferies, October 2026); (6) US tariffs on engineering components and metal fabrications, which INDUS says affected several Materials Solutions companies in 2025?"

EPS Sensitivity and Tungsten Volatility

2.
Windfall earnings contribution: INDUS Holding AG experienced a massive earnings surge in H1 2026 driven primarily by pricing dynamics at its largest Materials Solutions subsidiary BETEK, as detailed in the INDUS H1 2026 interim report. This performance was triggered by the global supply shortage of tungsten carbide, which allowed BETEK to implement significant price increases and capture high-margin orders. However, because this tungsten special situation is considered a temporary windfall, the company's future EPS development over 18 months depends on how much of this pricing power can be maintained once the raw material supply normalizes.
3.
Post-windfall earnings contraction: Current consensus forecasts suggest that INDUS's reported earnings per share will contract significantly after the tungsten cycle peaks in 2026. Specifically, NuWays AG projected in its update of 8 October 2026 that the company's reported EPS will reach a high of EUR 5.51 in fiscal year 2026 before declining to EUR 3.63 in fiscal year 2027. This rapid contraction highlights why understanding BETEK's post-peak pricing retention is critical for investors trying to value the stock, as Warburg Research argued in its note of 13 August 2026.
4.
Contrary evidence: NuWays AG argued in its update on 8 October 2026 that a rapid price decline in tungsten carbide is highly unlikely because the material remains in high demand for defense purposes. Management also views tungsten prices as having peaked for now, suggesting that a gradual decline in raw material costs will lead to normalizing rather than collapsing margins. Thus, the earnings contraction may be far more manageable than feared, although the INDUS H1 2026 interim report warns that renewed Chinese competition could eventually dampen demand.

Sector Pressures and Portfolio Dynamics

5.
Engineering segment stagnation: The domestic German mechanical engineering sector is facing severe recessionary headwinds, which directly limits the pricing power of INDUS's Engineering segment, according to the INDUS H1 2026 interim report. German machine production fell by 2.6% in real terms in early 2026, forcing the trade association VDMA to cut its machinery production growth forecast to 0% for the year. Because capacity utilization remains depressed at roughly 79%, the Engineering segment faces margin pressure as it struggles to hold prices in a weak environment, as shown in the INDUS Q1 2026 report.
6.
Infrastructure segment resilience: In contrast to the industrial recession, the Infrastructure segment has demonstrated robust demand driven by digital network and grid spending, as noted in the INDUS Q1 2026 interim report. This structural shift has enabled the segment to achieve H1 2026 incoming order growth of 23.4%, helping to offset the severe downturn in German residential construction. Consequently, the segment's adjusted EBITA margin expanded to 11.2% in H1 2026, demonstrating that infrastructure pricing power remains resilient even when broad building materials costs rise, as reported by Warburg Research on 13 August 2026.
7.
Protected international footprint: Broad input cost inflation and protectionist US tariffs have historically pressured margins across several of INDUS's Materials Solutions businesses, as noted by Parmantier & CIE. on 25 February 2026. However, INDUS has responded by expanding its international footprint and growing its US-based manufacturing sites. This local-for-local strategy helps the company build natural resilience against US tariffs on engineering components and metal fabrications, according to Parmantier & CIE.'s update of 30 July 2026.

Structural Profitability and Holding Model

8.
Decentralized holding discount: A closely related investor concern is whether INDUS's decentralized permanent- ownership model justifies a valuation discount or enables superior pricing agility. Nordic peers serve as benchmarks, demonstrating that decentralized structures can successfully defend margins by empowering local subsidiaries to pass through costs quickly. Whether INDUS's corporate model can replicate this agility in a German downturn remains an open question for its long-term structural profitability, as noted by Parmantier & CIE. on 30 July 2026.
9.
Other side of the story: While the current industrial recession in Germany threatens the cyclical parts of the portfolio, the long-term permanent-ownership model allows INDUS to avoid the pressure of forced exits, as Warburg Research highlighted in its report of 13 August 2026. Parmantier & CIE. noted on 30 July 2026 that INDUS's "Empowering Mittelstand" strategy targets a revenue increase to EUR 3 billion by 2030, which could more than double EPS compared to 2024. This suggests that the current portfolio diversification and long-term funding model can withstand temporary macroeconomic shocks without destroying structural profitability.

Section summary
  1. Exceptional pricing dynamics at BETEK drove record performance in H1 2026 but face normalization risks as tungsten feedstock supply improves.
  2. Management successfully defended margins by executing rapid price adjustments, but historical precedents show peak pricing is unsustainable.

Extreme Tungsten Inflation and Performance

1.
Strong revenue growth: The Materials Solutions segment reported spectacular growth in the first half of 2026 due to the tungsten shortage. According to the INDUS Holding AG interim report of 12 August 2026, segment revenue surged 32.4% year-on-year to EUR 376.6 million. This indicates that BETEK capitalized on pricing effects to drive top-line momentum, which should support near-term earnings per share.
2.
Elevated profit margins: Profitability in Materials Solutions reached unprecedented levels during the first half of 2026. The INDUS Holding AG interim report of 12 August 2026 noted that adjusted EBITA nearly quadrupled to EUR 84.5 million, representing a 22.4% margin compared to just 8.1% in the prior-year period. This extraordinary margin expansion reflects record pricing power at BETEK during the peak raw material squeeze.

Contract Structures and Pricing Lags

Flexible contract structures: Passing on extreme feedstock costs forced a shift in selling price intervals. This indicates that BETEK's negotiated pricing is more agile than traditional premium tool pricing models.

Real-time indexing capacity: Other major peers utilize automated surcharge clauses to manage tungsten volatility. Jefferies' report of 7 May 2026 noted that Kennametal has historically indexed about 25% of its products in near real-time, with the remainder priced on a lagged basis. This surcharge mechanism suggests that Kennametal transfers feedstock inflation faster than BETEK's framework negotiations, protecting structural profitability.
7.
Integrated supply security: Vertical integration remains a primary differentiator for direct carbide tooling competitors. A Ceratizit regulatory release on 1 October 2026 highlighted how its integrated tungsten supply chain provides confidence in volatile markets as Western APT prices reached USD 3,210 per MTU. This suggests that competitors without captive mines face higher supply chain risks, whereas BETEK relies on long-term partnerships.

Historical Precedents of Price Peaks

8.
Negative precedent impacts: Historical tungsten price collapses show that timing benefits can quickly turn into margin headwinds once spot prices roll over. Jefferies' model update of 5 November 2019 revealed that a rapid drop in tungsten prices created a severe profit headwind for Kennametal's infrastructure segment. This past case indicates that contractual obligations to pass through price will squeeze margins if Western APT collapses.

Contrary evidence: NuWays AG argued in its report of 8 October 2026 that a rapid price decline in tungsten is highly unlikely because defense demand remains extremely strong globally. This suggests that structural demand will prevent a collapse in European APT prices, allowing BETEK to maintain its elevated pricing levels and protect its segment margins through 2027.
10.
Pricing power differences: Superior premium positioning allows market leaders to hold prices even after feedstock costs normalize. Morgan Stanley's report of 19 October 2026 argued that Sandvik's cutting tools division retains more of its price increases over the medium term than the market expects. This structural premium pricing suggests that market leaders retain elevated margins long after raw material pricing peaks.

Diversified customer base: Low customer concentration mitigates the risk of pricing pressure from any single large buyer. The INDUS Holding AG annual report of 30 March 2026 disclosed that no customer exceeds 10% of group revenue, and the ten largest account for about 17%. This broad base ensures that BETEK faces low buyer power during contract negotiations, helping to preserve margins.

Steady price expectations: Major toolmakers indicate a commitment to hold price levels heading into the next fiscal year. Section summary

These strategic additions allowed Hauff-Technik to drive international expansion and reduce dependency on the weak domestic German market. Consequently, Hauff-Technik successfully reached a turnover of EUR 92.7 million in 2023. This expansion helped INDUS sustain its infrastructure segment profitability during downturns.

While companies successfully passed on cost increases to regional distributors, they experienced volume losses and temporary margin compression. The segment's adjusted EBITA margin declined from 11.4% in 2024 to 10.4% in 2025 due to these lags. This proves that subsidiaries require several months to adjust price lists.
3.
Contrary evidence: NuWays AG argued on 16 February 2026 that pricing pressure across infrastructure end markets was fading rather than intensifying. The broker projected that the segment's adjusted EBITA margin would expand to 10.6% in 2026. This advanced engineering prevents buildings from water and gas penetration, making it a critical product for utility providers. These high-barrier proprietary components allow Hauff-Technik to dictate terms to construction clients.
5.
Measurable quality standards: Hauff-Technik's UK division collaborated with Esh-Stantec in July 2025 to introduce mechanical sealing systems in Northumbrian Water projects. These high switching costs protect Hauff-Technik's market share from cheaper competitors.
6.
Adaptive climate pricing: REMKO defends its profitability by shifting product focus toward sustainable room air conditioners and heat pumps. During H1 2026, REMKO's sales were significantly boosted by above-average temperatures that drove high demand for climate control units. This environmental tailwind allowed REMKO to execute price and volume increases that offset raw material inflation. Shifting to high-value green systems ensures REMKO retains premium pricing power.

Stable revenue contribution: The Infrastructure segment represented the largest share of INDUS's group revenue in 2025 at 34.4%. Segment sales rose 6.7% to EUR 597.2 million, driven by both acquisitions and organic growth of 4.3%. However, segment adjusted EBITA slipped slightly to EUR 62.1 million from EUR 63.6 million in 2024. This demonstrates that macro headwinds temporarily constrained cost pass-through capabilities.
