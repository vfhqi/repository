Section 1 – Leadership and Governance Change

Section summary
  1. board oversight is strengthening via independent expansion to address historic investor governance discounts.
  2. centralized executive control remains highly concentrated under the long- standing founder-led owner-operator model.
  3. decentralized lab operations foster strong local entrepreneurial accountability but create corporate integration friction.
  4. high executive turnover limits second-tier management stability due to highly centralized corporate decision-making.
  5. short-seller allegations triggered substantial valuation de-rating before independent audits verified cash holdings.
  6. related-party transactions are gradually being dismantled through multi-year strategic real estate buybacks.

Leadership Structure and Board Evolution

1.
Dominant founder control: Dr Gilles Martin maintains tight executive control, holding 69.8% of group voting rights compared to professional peer medians near zero.
2.
Unique executive archetype: Eurofins is the only top-10 global TIC player led by an owner-operator rather than professional CEO.
3.
Prolonged leadership tenure: Dr Gilles Martin's mandate as Chairman and CEO was confirmed until the 2028 AGM.
4.
Board size expansion: the Board expanded to ten members in H1 2026, adding seven independent non-executive directors to align with peer governance structures.
5.
Key financial appointment: former international CFO Gavin Hill joined to strengthen board-level financial oversight.
6.
Strategic committee refresh: Valérie Arnold joined the Board in 2026, joining the corporate governance committee.

Corporate Culture and Employee Turnover

7.
High senior turnover: executive tenure at the group level typically averages only two to three years, significantly below long-tenured professional TIC peers.
8.
Centralized decision drag: rapid senior turnover persists because key decisions remain tightly controlled by the CEO.
9.
Local operational autonomy: individual laboratories benefit from an empowered, highly modular structure, delivering strong modular entrepreneurial performance.
10.
Quality control variance: severe cost-minimization targets have historically led to localized lab contamination and failures.
11.
Staffing capacity friction: overcapacity from the post-pandemic wrap-down created persistent labor productivity bottlenecks as Covid testing volumes collapsed.
12.
Limited corporate resources: commercial excellence programs face friction due to exceptionally thin central transformation resources.

Governance Friction and Forensic Oversight

13.
Slanderous short allegations: Muddy Waters claimed in June 2024 that Eurofins' accounts were optimized for controlling shareholder siphoning.
14.
Independent cash validation: an external forensic cash audit flagged no financial discrepancies, clearing the group of siphoning.
15.
Strategic real estate overhauls: shareholders approved purchasing owned strategic sites, reducing related- party transaction exposure.
16.
Multi-tranche transaction structure: the group planned to buy 7 strategic sites for €253 million in the first tranche.
17.
Long-term lease reduction: a second tranche comprises 23 properties worth €190 million acquired over four years.
18.
Auditing partner stability: Deloitte Audit was renewed as the statutory auditor of the company to ensure compliance.
19.
Other side of the story: centralised family control and owner-operator dominance risk undermining independent board oversight, triggering a persistent governance valuation discount if related-party issues resurface.
