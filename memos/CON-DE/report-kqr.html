Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The definitive anti-dumping duties on Chinese passenger tyres represent a critical turning point for European premium tyre pricing power.
  2. Whether this tariff protection translates into sustained earnings per share growth depends heavily on mid-market pricing and potential import rerouting.

The Key Question Defined

1.
Defining the core: "Since the EU's definitive anti-dumping duties on Chinese passenger car tyres took effect in July 2026, how have European replacement tyre prices and sell-out volumes moved in each price tier (budget, mid-range and premium), and have Continental, Michelin, Pirelli and Nokian Renkaat raised prices or gained share? Are Chinese tyre makers moving their exports to plants in other countries or planning plants inside Europe, and how quickly could that refill the gap? How much of the raw material squeeze of the second half of 2026 do brokers expect each company to recover in 2027?."
2.
Favorable window: Significant tariff protection has established a temporary pricing window of three to five years for premium European tyre manufacturers. This regulatory umbrella, extending from July 2026 to July 2031, allows European producers to implement price increases and stabilize market shares after years of intense Chinese import pressure. However, the actual duration of this favorable window could be dramatically shortened if low-cost Chinese exports are successfully rerouted through non-subject plants in Southeast Asia or if Chinese greenfield plants inside Europe begin production sooner than anticipated.
3.
Widened pricing: Strong price increases in the mid-range or Tier 2 and Tier 3 segments are highly critical because they indicate that pricing power has widened beyond premium Tier 1 tyres. The European Commission's investigation documented average undercutting of approximately 19% across tiers 2 and 3, meaning that successful tariff-driven hikes in these segments would structurally elevate the pricing umbrella for the entire market. If mid-range prices rise, premium manufacturers like Continental and Michelin, which maintain large secondary brand portfolios, will see disproportionate margin expansion.
4.
Discounted valuation: Continental's financial trajectory is heavily exposed to these mid-market and lower-tier pricing dynamics. Bernstein notes that pro-forma Continental, as a pure-play tyre company following its planned ContiTech and MyCar disposals, will trade at an EV/EBIT of 7.3x, representing a notable discount to its peer average. If Continental successfully recovers the material inflation through secondary brand price hikes, its 2027 earnings per share will see substantial upgrades.

EPS Squeeze and Recovery

5.
Negative cost: Michelin's near-term earnings remain constrained by raw material inflation despite robust pricing power. Bernstein highlights that Michelin is facing a massive H2 2026 raw material headwind of nearly €600 million, which is double the company's initial guidance of €300 million and will create negative net price/cost pressure. While Michelin's high premium mix protects its long-term margins, its 2027 earnings per share recovery depends on successfully passing these costs through delayed indexation contracts.
6.
Superior mix: Pirelli remains the most protected peer due to its high-value product mix and superior pricing power in tyres 18 inches and above. Analysts at Bernstein expect Pirelli to expand its EBIT margins by 20 to 30 basis points annually, forecasting a potential margin expansion towards 17% by 2030, driven by its 79% premium revenue mix. Pirelli's high exposure to the lucrative replacement channel rather than volatile original equipment markets minimizes earnings volatility and accelerates its raw material cost recovery in 2027.
7.
Significant leverage: Nokian Tyres represents a highly leveraged operational restructuring story as it ramps up its new Romanian factory. Broker DNB Carnegie estimates that Nokian Tyres will increase production to 14 million units by 2029, driving significant operating leverage that will support its 15% segment operating profit margin target. However, Nokian Tyres faces elevated raw material headwinds in late 2026, meaning its 2027 EPS recovery depends on achieving planned volumes at higher average selling prices outside the Nordics.
8.
Contrary evidence: Some premium tyre segments in Europe show immediate volume contraction rather than growth following the implementation of definitive duties. Analysts at Bernstein report that despite a 70% drop in Chinese imports, Michelin saw negative replacement passenger tyre volumes in Europe during Q3 2026. This indicates that trade barriers do not guarantee immediate market share gains if macroeconomic demand remains depressed.
9.
Linked strategic: The core pricing debate is closely linked to critical strategic questions regarding global supply chain adaptation and raw material hedging. European listed manufacturers must decide whether to accelerate local premiumization strategies or maintain secondary brands to satisfy dealer network volume requirements. Additionally, the threat of potential reciprocal tariffs and shifting original equipment demand from vehicle OEMs adds substantial operational risks to the 18-month outlook.
10.
Other side of the story: While anti-dumping duties provide an immediate shield, high distributor inventory accumulated ahead of the July tariffs could severely delay any volume recovery or price hikes for Tier 1 producers well into 2027. Jefferies notes that strong pre-buying of Chinese imports has left European retail channels saturated, meaning that local producers may see negative replacement volume trends despite the 70% drop in imports. Under these conditions, the expected pricing window could remain inactive until channel destocking is fully completed.

Section 4 – Chinese Rerouting and European Plant Strategies

Section summary

Rerouting via Southeast Asia

1.
Accelerated export rerouting: Chinese tyre manufacturers are rapidly adjusting their global shipping patterns to bypass the newly implemented European Union anti-dumping duties. To investigate these trends, this analysis covers twelve relevant sources, eight tyre companies, and two comparable trade defense cases. Chinese exporters are successfully shifting their export volumes through Southeast Asian hubs and North African facilities. This strategic redistribution of trade flows allows low-cost producers to bypass the newly implemented trade barriers.
2.
Strategic North African expansion: Sentury Tyre has established a key manufacturing hub in Morocco to serve the European replacement passenger car market. The Moroccan plant began operations in October 2024 and is projected to expand its annual capacity to 12 million tyres. This North African base provides Chinese manufacturers with a tariff-free gateway directly into Europe, mitigating the impact of the July 2026 definitive duties. Premium European manufacturers like Continental and Michelin face renewed competition from these non-subject facilities.
3.
Diversified offshore capacity: Chinese producers are aggressively funding new tyre production facilities across Southeast Asia to counter European trade barriers. For instance, ZC Rubber announced a $260 million investment to expand high-performance radial tyres in Thailand. Additionally, Triangle is investing $461 million to construct its first overseas tyre factory in Cambodia with a capacity of 7 million units. These offshore manufacturing hubs ensure that low-cost tyres continue to flow into Western markets despite local tariffs.
