Section 3 – Terms and Structural Characteristics of the EUR 1.8bn Hybrid Bond

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg's May 2026 hybrid bond features a dual-tranche perpetual structure with cumulative interest deferral terms.
  2. Rating agencies assign fifty percent equity credit to the hybrids, but the coupons dilute adjusted earnings per share.

Structuring the dual-tranche hybrid

1.
Substantial dual-tranche issuance: Carlsberg successfully priced a massive dual-tranche hybrid bond worth EUR 1.8 billion on 6 May 2026. The transaction was executed via Carlsberg Breweries A/S with settlement on 18 May 2026. This issuance is structurally designed to support capital rebalancing, so the brewer can optimize its overall capital structure while pursuing organic expansion. Both tranches list on the Luxembourg Stock Exchange Euro MTF market to attract a broad base of international investors.
2.
Divergent tranche terms: The hybrid bond is split into a EUR 1.0 billion tranche and a EUR 800 million tranche to appeal to different duration preferences. The EUR 1.0 billion NC5.25 notes carry an initial fixed coupon of 4.37% per annum. The EUR 800 million NC8 notes carry a coupon of 4.875% per annum, which means the company faces higher interest burdens on the longer-dated paper. These pricing variations reflect prevailing market rates, unless macroeconomic shifts alter future credit margins.
3.
Perpetual maturity profiles: Both tranches of the May 2026 hybrid bond carry a formal maturity date of 18 May 3026. Jefferies explains in its note of 13 September 2026 that this 1,000-year maturity operates as a perpetual- in-practice instrument. This ultra-long duration ensures that the hybrid principal is not classified as current debt, which means refinancing pressures are significantly deferred for several decades.

Coupon schedules and payment terms

4.
Specific coupon dates: The EUR 1.0 billion NC5.25 tranche pays interest annually in arrear on 18 August, starting with a short coupon on 18 August 2026. The EUR 800 million NC8 tranche pays interest annually in arrear on 18 May, beginning 18 May 2027. Under-payment risks do not arise in fiscal 2026 because the NC8 coupon is completely deferred, which modestly benefits near-term cash outflows.
5.
Cumulative deferral mechanisms: Carlsberg retains the sole option to defer all or part of any scheduled hybrid interest payment by giving a 16-business-day notice. The prospectus of 12 May 2026 specifies that deferred interest is cumulative and compounds on subsequent interest dates. These compounding terms protect bondholders from permanent income loss, but optional deferrals would increase the ultimate debt service obligation.

Reset rates and step-up margins

6.
Initial reset calculations: The coupon rates on both hybrid tranches reset every five years after their respective first call dates. The NC5.25 tranche first resets on 18 August 2031 at the five-year euro mid-swap rate plus a 1.54% margin. The NC8 tranche first resets on 18 May 2034 at the mid-swap rate plus a 1.93% margin, which means future interest expenses will fluctuate with market benchmark shifts.
7.
Scheduled step-up margins: Significant step-up margins of 100 basis points are scheduled to take effect after the first decade of issuance. The NC5.25 margin steps up to 2.54% on 18 August 2036, while the NC8 margin steps up to 2.93% on 18 May 2039. These automatic margin hikes act as a strong incentive for early redemption, so Carlsberg is highly likely to call the notes before step-ups occur.
8.
Punitive penalty rates: Punitive interest rate increases of 5 percentage points apply if a change-of-control event occurs without early redemption. The prospectus of 12 May 2026 dictates that this step-up becomes effective immediately after the change-of-control date. This massive step-up protects bondholders, but it also creates a highly restrictive barrier against potential corporate acquisitions.

IAS 33 and adjusted EPS accounting

9.
Balance sheet classification: Carlsberg recognizes the EUR 1.8 billion hybrid bond as equity within its statement of financial position under IFRS. The company's interim report of 19 August 2026 notes that DKK 13.45 billion was added to total equity. This accounting treatment bypasses liabilities, so reported gross debt is kept artificially lower to satisfy rating agency covenants.
10.
Coupon presentation shift: Hybrid coupons no longer flow through reported net finance costs but are presented on a new line. Jefferies notes on 28 September 2026 that these coupon payments sit below non-controlling interests as "interest on hybrid bonds". This presentation shift artificially boosts reported operating net finance costs, but the underlying cash still reduces the earnings attributable to common shareholders.
11.
Dilutive adjusted EPS: Adjusted earnings per share under IAS 33 are diluted by the ongoing servicing of the hybrid bonds. Morgan Stanley reports on 26 August 2026 that the hybrid coupons will be approximately 2% dilutive to group EPS starting in fiscal 2027. This dilution occurs because gross hybrid bond costs must be deducted from net income, which means net profit available to equity holders is permanently reduced.

Rating agency equity treatment

12.
Split equity credit: Credit rating agencies apply a split methodology that differs from pure IFRS accounting standards. Moody's and Fitch assign exactly 50% equity credit to the EUR 1.8 billion hybrid notes. This 50% limit means that Carlsberg's reported net interest-bearing debt adds back DKK 6.73 billion, which means leverage targets are harder to hit than reported balance sheets suggest.
13.
Subordination and loss absorption: Rating agency equity treatment is justified by deep subordination, perpetual maturity, and coupon deferral without default. Jefferies explains on 13 September 2026 that these characteristics allow the hybrid to absorb losses ahead of senior debt. This structural safety protects senior creditors, so Carlsberg can comfortably maintain its BBB+ credit rating despite the high overall debt load.

Peer precedents and contrary evidence

14.
Comparable peer precedents: Japanese brewer Asahi Group utilizes a structurally identical approach by deducting 50% of its JPY 300 billion subordinated bonds. Both brewers benefit from these rating adjustments to manage credit metrics under pressure. However, Carlsberg's large EUR 1.8 billion size is relatively high compared to typical European mid-cap brewers.
15.
Contrary evidence: Credit analysts suggest that hybrid issuance may not provide the expected valuation boost if interest rates remain elevated. Deutsche Bank warns on 27 October 2026 that refinancing risks could wipe out the synthetic benefits of hybrid structures if base rates rise. If borrowing costs continue to climb, the long-term dilution of hybrid coupons could outweigh the short-term benefits of early senior debt redemption.

Structural overview and other side of the story

16.
Other side of the story: Investors may overstate the safety provided by hybrid bonds if they ignore potential rating agency methodology shifts. Jefferies reports on 29 October 2026 that Carlsberg's refinancing needs are modest until 2029 because of the hybrid issuance. However, if rating agencies unilaterally withdraw equity credit or change their methodologies, the company would be forced to reclassify the hybrid as 100% debt, which would immediately spike reported leverage above the 2.5x threshold.
