Currency Fluctuations

Currency Mismatches and Dollar Pass-Through

1.
Severe mismatch: Bufab faces significant transaction risk because its US dollar purchases for Asian sourcing far exceed its dollar-denominated sales. The group reported 2025 US dollar sales of SEK 1,290m against US dollar costs of SEK 2,228m in its latest annual report. This mismatch creates direct transactional vulnerability when the Swedish krona weakens against the US currency. Consequently, a stronger dollar increases cost of goods sold and directly pressures group operating margins unless offset.
2.
Negative exposure: Bufab estimated that a one percentage point strengthening of the US dollar against the Swedish krona would reduce its annual operating profit by SEK 9m. This transactional impact increased from a negative SEK 7m impact in 2024 as shown in its 2025 annual report. Similarly, a five percent strengthening of the US dollar represents a negative SEK 47m effect on operating profit. These figures show that currency fluctuations remain a persistent headwind for earnings per share.
3.
Negotiated adjustments: Bufab manages transaction currency risks primarily through negotiated price adjustments to customers rather than formal indexation clauses. Management stated in an earnings call that their agreements typically do not contain direct currency or raw material pass-through clauses. Instead, the company relies on localized negotiations to align revenues and costs over time in matching currencies. This decentralised approach means that pricing changes often occur with a lag during periods of exchange rate volatility.

Conversely, lower freight rates in early 2026 quickly supported gross margin gains of 2.0 percentage points in its UK division.

Rising pressures: Bufab highlighted rising purchase prices from Asian suppliers and elevated freight costs as ongoing risks in its 2026 interim reports. The group observed a cautious approach among customers combined with increasing purchase costs from Asian partners. Broker reports from DNB Carnegie indicate that geopolitical trade barriers could challenge margin targets if inflation accelerates.
