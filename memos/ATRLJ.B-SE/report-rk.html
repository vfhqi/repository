BEAR CASE CREDIBILITY ASSESSMENT

The bear case against Atrium Ljungberg demonstrates high operational plausibility supported by substantial factbased evidence. The company's vacancy rate surged to 13.5% in Q2 2026 following recent project completions, while negative net letting of SEK -8 million in Q1 2026 documented weak near-term demand. Carnegie modeling projects adjusted cash earnings will contract for a second consecutive year in 2026, declining further to SEK 1.94 by 2027, driven by vacancy expansion and maturing low-cost interest swaps. The negative organic rental growth of -0.7% mirrors weak rental trends observed across Swedish office peers, with peer Fabege's elevated vacancy rate of 14% highlighting systemic Stockholm oversupply. The bear evidence significantly outweighs speculative recovery projections, establishing strong operational coherence across multiple independent validation points.

The FALSE FRIEND framework correctly identifies how investment-grade ratings, ample liquidity buffers, and major lease announcements mask deteriorating fundamentals across vacancy rates, cash earnings trajectory, and comparable portfolio performance.

HIGHEST-PLAUSIBILITY FAILURE MODES

The most credible failure pathways cluster around refinancing pressures and development execution shortfalls. Maturing low-cost swap portfolios will reset to higher market rates, forcing interest coverage ratios lower than the 2.0x limit, while elevated property yield requirements could trigger significant asset valuation write-downs, pushing loan-to-value above targets. Lukewarm occupier leasing interest leaves ongoing developments with persistently low pre-letting rates, creating structural vacancy at completion, exemplified by the SEK 9.2 billion pipeline carrying a low average pre-let ratio of 56% in Q2 2026.

Failure to secure land allocations before late 2027 would invalidate the conditional 58,000 square meter Ericsson lease, removing key long-term revenue. The systemic risk cluster of maturing low-cost swaps combined with Stockholm office vacancies at historic highs since 1990 creates compounding cash flow pressure. Critical false-friend metrics create misleading operational optimism while masking structural deterioration. Stable reported interest expenses suggest high resilience but disguise a looming swap maturity wall starting in late 2026, creating deceptive near-term earnings stability.

NET CURRENCY VERDICT

Atrium Ljungberg demonstrates complete insulation from direct foreign exchange risks through its domestic operational footprint and comprehensive hedging framework. The company's domestic operations generated zero translation impact on consolidated revenues, operating profits, and net income over the last three years, as all property assets reside in Sweden. Cross-currency swaps eliminate currency exposure on foreign bonds, while the company maintained SEK 2,424 million in foreign exchange derivatives to neutralize interest and principal exposure on foreign currency debt.

Currency shocks of 10-20% generate zero impact on consolidated revenue or EBITDA because all leases are domestic and hedging derivatives perform effectively. However, if international construction costs spike, project margins could face indirect imported inflationary pressure because domestic contractors pass through pricing. The final currency verdict confirms that direct FX movements represent zero material investment risk, with operational vacancy dynamics and interest rate reset cycles presenting orders of magnitude higher downside plausibility than any currency-related scenario.
