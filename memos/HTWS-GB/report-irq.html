What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: a contracted lease-up on a fixed tower base turns 10% to 13% EBITDA growth into much faster EPS growth through fixed interest, a falling tax rate and buybacks, and the share price now needs the per-share cash story to arrive. Six judgements decide that.

  • Judgement 1, what the volume is made of. Sites rose 442 in the third quarter on 924 tenancy additions. By that subtraction, tenancies on existing towers fell from about 1,000 a quarter in the first half to about 480, while new sites rose from about 250 a quarter to 442; the tenancy ratio stood at 2.26 times. A new site with one tenant earns about 12% on capital, against about 25% with a second. If the third quarter's mix is timing, the lease-up mechanism is intact. If it is a trend, the headline test was passed with the capital-heavy kind of volume, which is why leverage has not moved.
  • Judgement 2, the FY2027 floor. More than 3,000 additions needs an average above 750 a quarter, against about 565 implied for the fourth quarter of 2026. Brokers' prior consensus was 2,200 to 2,500; we model 3,000; Morgan Stanley 3,400. If the floor rests on signed agreements, the cycle is prolonged and the out-years are low in our model. If it is pipeline, it is a floor in name only, and a re-acceleration is needed from the exit rate.
  • Judgement 3, cash in the fourth quarter. Recurring free cash flow above USD 235m needs more than about USD 101m in the fourth quarter, over twice the nine-month quarterly average (USD 133.7m in nine months); management attributes the third-quarter step-down to customer payment timing and expects "some" reversal. A full reversal keeps the cash guidance; a shortfall would be the first cut on the line the memo put its small risk of a guidance cut on.
  • Judgement 4, FY2026 EPS. The memo calls a miss at the FY2026 results near-certain: our about USD 0.07 against consensus of USD 0.090, because first-half finance costs of USD 125.8m and a 45.6% interim tax rate are already in the books. Nothing below EBITDA was guided at the update. Pre-announcing the tax rate or finance charge, or guiding on them in March, would defuse the March 2027 risk the memo names.
  • Judgement 5, the DRC renewals in 2028 to 2029. The company's own contracted-revenue schedule shows Central and Southern Africa contracted revenue falling USD 68m (15%) from 2027 to 2029 as the Airtel Africa and Orange master leases in the DRC reach their initial terms. The modal outcome is a modest concession traded for ten more years (50% to 60%); a material concession (25% to 30%) is about 13% of FY2029 EPS.
  • Judgement 6, the next step in capital returns. The buyback (USD 175m, extended to December 2027), a dividend growing over 10% a year, a 2.5 to 3.5 times leverage corridor, and IMPACT 2030 targets already beaten on current paths. A refresh with a larger distribution programme is the event that would move the narrative from growth to cash per share.

Order of play. Ask the fourth-quarter cash (A4) and the mix of additions (A3) first, then the FY2027 floor (A2), then the finance lines (A5 to A7): they are answerable and decide the March print. Then leverage (A10) and the DRC renewals (B2.1). Leave governance and disclosure to the end, or for management.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the fourth quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2027)

A1 ★ The fourth quarter of 2026. "Approximately 4,000 additions for the year implies about 565 in the fourth quarter, after 924 in the third. Is that a floor, as your guidance usually is, and how have October and November run?"
- Why: our model has about 4,100 for FY2026; additions fell for a second quarter running (1,406, 1,105, 924). Each 1,000 additions is about USD 20m of annualised EBITDA and about 9% of FY2027 EPS.
- Tell: "a floor, run-rate ahead of it" = the usual beat and the FY2027 base rises; "a natural slowdown after the first-half surge" = the memo's front-loading reading.

A2 ★ What the FY2027 floor rests on. "Is the floor of more than 3,000 additions in 2027 built from signed orders and committed rollouts, or from pipeline? Which customers and which markets carry most of it?"
- Why: more than 3,000 needs over 750 a quarter against about 565 implied now. Brokers' prior 2027 consensus was 2,200 to 2,500; Morgan Stanley now carries 3,400. The memo expected a March 2027 opening range of 2,500 to 3,000 that would read as a deceleration.

- New from the second report: on the third-quarter call Jefferies asked whether operators wanted to postpone or cancel build-to-suit rollouts, and the answer was not captured. Ask how he would answer it now.

A3 ★ The split of the third quarter's additions. "Of the 924 additions in the third quarter, how many were colocations, how many amendments and how many new build-to-suit sites?
