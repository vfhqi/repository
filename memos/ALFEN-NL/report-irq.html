One point for Richard before the questions. Alfen is not held, so the questions are weighted to whether and when to buy. The memo's case: one franchise worth owning (about 70% of Dutch secondary substations, volume rising for years on a law-dated, budget-backed grid build-out, rationed by the grid operators' technicians and permits, at a gross margin the three buyers hold at 22% to 25%) and two businesses without a moat (charging, storage) that still make about 60% of gross profit. Its verdict was a dated watch-list case, not an entry case: let the consensus reset set the price (the FY2026 results on 17-Feb-2027), then buy the grid-volume upgrade, with the asymmetry turning at about €8 to €9. The second earnings summary found the third quarter left every judgement where it was (the €4.0m EBITDA beat is a €2.5m storage contingency release; the underlying quarter is the memo's trough), weakened momentum, and suggested the reset may now come later and larger, at the H1 2027 print in August 2027. At €8.90 the price is inside the memo's €8 to €9 range, before the reset the memo was waiting for. So the call should test the three things that decide whether waiting for February still makes sense: how fast grid volume grows in 2027, whether storage margins and orders hold, and how much cost leaves in 2027.

What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: Dutch substation volume carries the whole EBITDA gain to FY2030 on a high fixed-cost base, while storage and charging set the near-term numbers, so the share price turns when grid volume visibly carries the margin. Seven judgements decide that.

  1. Grid volume in 2027 and 2028. Our model has Smart Grid Solutions revenue +10% in FY2027 and +11% in FY2028; the operators plan +13% a year; two of three installed 20% to 30% below plan in 2025. If grid grows 5% instead of 10% in FY2027, EBITDA falls about €2.7m and EPS about €0.10; held at 5% a year to FY2030, FY2030 value at 9 times EBITDA falls from about €16 to about €11.5 a share. If volume is visibly above 950 substations a quarter in 2027, then the three-year case works; if it stays at about 870, it does not.
  2. Storage margin and orders. Our model has FY2027 storage revenue of €140m at a 21.5% gross margin and intake of about €35m a quarter. Each point of storage gross margin is about €1.4m of EBITDA. At €50m a quarter of intake FY2027 EBITDA is about €29.3m; at €20m, about €19.6m. If contingency is built into every tender, then the storage margin is higher than our model; if the release was specific to two projects, nothing changes.
  3. Cost in 2027. Our model removes €5m of a €5m to €8m overlap of personnel costs, with indexation of 3.5%. All €8m out: EBITDA +€2.6m. Half of it permanent: EBITDA (€2.5m). If personnel cost in H1 2027 is at or below H2 2026, then the FY2027 margin starts nearer 6% than 5%.
  4. The FY2026 base and FY2027 guidance. Consensus FY2026 EBITDA was €22.0m at the memo against our €18.3m; nine-month EBITDA is about €20.5m (second earnings summary's arithmetic). If the €2.5m release stays in the base and FY2027 guidance brackets consensus, then the reset waits until August 2027 and comes from a worse print.
  5. Depreciation and amortisation, and EPS. Our €21m for FY2027 against €13.5m to €17m at two of the five EPS brokers is the main reason consensus FY2027 EPS (€0.35) sits far above ours (€0.06). Each €1m of EBITDA is about €0.036 of EPS. If the company confirms about €21m, then consensus EPS falls by about €0.14 to €0.20 on that line alone in February.
  6. Charging. Our model has FY2027 units +7% on price (4%); the third quarter showed units (11%) and price +4% on mix toward business chargers. Each point of charging gross margin is about €1.1m of EBITDA. If Alfen is choosing a business-charger niche, then revenue holds on mix and volume is the wrong test.
  7. The finance seat and segment disclosure. The fourth finance director since January 2024 is an interim; there is no segment EBITDA. If a permanent finance director and segment profit arrive with FY2027 guidance, then the forecasting credibility the multiple needs (9 times rising to 11 to 12 times EBITDA once grid delivery is proven) can start to build.

Order of play. Open with the fourth quarter and the form of FY2027 guidance (A1, A2), then grid volume and the operators' 2027 plans (B1.1, B1.2), then storage margin and orders (B3.1, B3.2), then the overlap of personnel costs (B5.1). If time is short, ask only the ★ questions in order.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (Q4 2026 to Q3 2027)

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Grid volume and the Dutch operators

B1.1 ★ What limits substation volume in 2027. "You delivered 870 substations in the third quarter. Is the limit on volume in 2027 the operators' technicians, permits, your own capacity, or the operators' budgets? What would it take to run above 950 a quarter?"
- Why: the operators plan +13% a year; two of three installed 20% to 30% below plan in 2025. The Almere plant can double output. Each +10% of grid revenue (about €22m) is about €5m of gross profit at almost no extra opex.
- Tell: "the operators' installation crews" = growth is paced by labour, our 8% to 11% is right and slow; "permits, and the permit-free law helps from 2027" = an upside branch from H2 2027; "our own capacity" = a surprise, and a capital expenditure question.

B1.2 ★ The turnkey transport distribution station. "How many turnkey transport distribution stations did you deliver in 2026, and what do the operators' plans imply for 2027? Does turnkey carry a higher gross margin than a standard substation?"
- Why: the memo cites Enexis moving from about 10 to about 120 such stations a year and turnkey cutting a build from over a year to four months; it attacks the operators' own bottleneck. A sustained grid gross margin above 25% would show a premium for a scarce service.
- Tell: "growing fast, better margin" = the grid moat is widening and grid can outgrow installer headcount; "same margin, small numbers" = volume only.

B1.3 The three operators' shares and frameworks. "Enexis and Liander were each above 10% of group revenue in 2025. How did call-offs from Enexis, Liander and Stedin move in 2026, and when do the main frameworks come up for renewal?"
- Why: two customers at 34.8% of revenue on frameworks with no minimum volumes. A 20% to 30% call-off cut at one operator (as in 2025) is (€20m) to (€40m) of grid revenue and (€5m) to (€10m) of EBITDA.
- Tell: "all three up, renewals are years away" = concentration risk is low in the window; any renewal in 2027 = a pricing event to watch.

B1.4 Permit-free expansion and congestion law. "What do you expect from the permit-free substation expansion law, dated for shortly after summer 2027, and has the fast-track regime for grid projects above 21 kilovolts, in force since October 2026, changed any orders yet?"
- Why: management said no FY2026 volume effect from the fast-track. If operators install at their +13% plan from 2028, grid grows 13% to 15%, worth €3m to €4m of EBITDA a year above our base.
- Tell: "a 2028 effect" = consistent with the memo's dating; "orders already reflect it" = the upgrade comes earlier.

B1.5 Private grid customers. "How large is the private grid project business now, and is demand frozen by congestion, as in Utrecht, likely to come back as connections are granted?"
- Why: the memo's counter-case treats private demand held back by congestion as a reservoir that permit-free expansion could release. Not in our model.
- Tell: a size and a pipeline = an upside branch to track; "small, operators are the business" = no change.

B2. Grid pricing and margin

B2.1 ★ Grid gross margin and indexation. "Your grid gross margin target was cut to 20% to 30%. Where is it today, and with materials indexed with a lag, does rising volume in 2027 lift the margin or do the operators take it back in price?"
- Why: the memo holds grid gross margin at 22% to 25%, capped by three buyers. Each point of grid gross margin on our FY2027 grid revenue of €233m is about €2.3m of EBITDA.
- Tell: "stable, mix drives it" = the buyer cap holds, volume only; "fixed-cost absorption lifts it" = upside to our flat margin.

B2.2 Finland. "Alfen Elkamo had a record quarter. What is driving it, and is it a second grid market or a one-off?"
- Why: our model treats grid as Dutch volume; a second growing market would broaden the one driver of the case. Not sized in our files.
- Tell: "Finnish grid investment, recurring" = broader grid base; "a large project" = no change.

B3. Storage orders, margin and the guarantee facility

B3.1 ★ How storage projects are priced. "When you tender a storage project, is a contingency built into every price, and how much of the €86 million of 2027 backlog carries contingency that could be released on completion?"
- Why: the second earnings summary's first reading: storage gross margin without the release was still about 23% in the weakest quarter, against our 21.5%. Each point on €140m is €1.4m of EBITDA; at 9 times on FY2028 a storage swing is worth about €1.8 a share.
- Tell: "every tender, similar levels" = our storage margin is low and the release recurs; "these two projects were exceptional" = the brokers' non-recurring view stands.

B3.2 ★ Order intake and the market. "With Chinese integrators compressing prices and cell makers selling turnkey, how do you win utility-scale orders, and what did fourth-quarter intake look like so far?"
- Why: storage intake has swung from €54m to €11m in a quarter; the memo's trigger for an entry was a 2027 storage book of €90m or more by December (€86m at September). At €50m a quarter FY2027 EBITDA is about €29m; at €20m, about €20m.
- Tell: "local service, grid integration, Dutch projects" = a niche that holds price; "price" = the margin drifts down as the memo expects.

B3.3 The guarantee facility. "The €80 million guarantee facility was extended to October 2029 but not enlarged. How much is used today, and does it limit how much fixed-price storage work you take on?"
- Why: the memo had €52m used and €28m of headroom, which caps the upper branch of storage intake unless enlarged; the backlog has grown since.
- Tell: "headroom is ample" = the cap is not binding; "it does shape what we bid for" = storage upside is capped by the balance sheet.

B3.4 The Chinese export rebate and cell prices. "The Chinese export VAT rebate goes to zero on 1 January 2027 and cell prices have fallen. Since pricing is back-to-back on cells, what does that do to your revenue and margin per project in 2027?"
- Why: the memo notes the rebate removal and a CATL-led price war act in the same quarter (Q1 2027). Lower cell prices cut revenue per project at the same fee.
- Tell: "the fee is fixed, margin holds" = revenue falls, profit holds; "buyers wait" = Q1 2027 intake is weak.

B3.5 Mobile storage. "Mobile storage tripled in H1 2026. How large is it now in revenue and backlog, and does it earn a better margin than utility-scale?"

- Tell: "growing, better margin" = a steadier storage floor; "small" = no change.

B6. Charging
