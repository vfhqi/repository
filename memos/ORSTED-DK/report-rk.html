Section 4 – Accounting and Governance Red Flags

Section summary
  1. Orsted's complex partnership structures and front-loaded revenue recognition create a significant disconnect between reported earnings and actual cash generation.

Revenue Recognition and Alternative Metrics

Complex revenue recognition: Orsted recognises revenue from power sales when control transfers at delivery, which typically coincides with production. The group invoices customers monthly with payments due within 10 to 30 days. For long-term fixed-price agreements, Orsted recognises revenue based on its explicit right to invoice. High power price volatility can cause invoicing patterns to diverge from actual cash generation, exposing working capital to sudden swings.

1.
Aggressive farm-down accounting: Orsted recognises upfront transaction values from selling project stakes when contract negotiations conclude, rather than aligning with future development milestones. The allocation of gains between ownership sales and construction agreements remains highly subjective and depends heavily on specific bilateral negotiations. Such contract terms allow the company to front-load earnings from partners before constructing the physical wind farms. Investors face earnings disappointment if partner payments lag behind the front-loaded accounting gains, creating a gap between reported profits and cash inflows.
2.
Asymmetric contract structures: Orsted recorded a DKK 4.8 billion upfront non-cash impact in its fourth quarter of 2025 financial statements due to the asymmetric structure of the Hornsea 3 partnership. The transaction structure allocates early operational cash flows to the partner, while Orsted receives higher distributions only in the later stages of the project life. The asymmetric commercial structure forces the group to absorb immediate accounting net present value losses despite expecting neutral lifetime cash distributions. Investors should worry that such complex partnerships depress near-term operating earnings to fund long-dated, uncertain cash flows.
3.
Inflated adjusted earnings: Orsted relies heavily on alternative performance measures, specifically EBITDA excluding new partnerships, to present its underlying operating performance. The company excludes highly volatile farm-down partnership gains because these transactions vary significantly across financial years. Reported EBITDA often differs materially from adjusted figures, which can obscure the actual economic health of the business during periods of high project cancellations. Analysts warning of persistent project write-downs suggest that adjusted EBITDA overstates sustainable profitability by ignoring recurring development losses.
