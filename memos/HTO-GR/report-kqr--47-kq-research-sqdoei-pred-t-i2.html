Section 4 – Wholesale Dynamics and Fiber Infrastructure Overbuild

Section summary
  1. The proposed fiber joint venture between Public Power Corporation and Vodafone Greece represents a long-term structural threat to OTE's national wholesale revenue.
  2. OTE's existing volume-commitment contracts provide strong near-term protection by locking in major retail operators.
  3. The planned exit from low-margin international transit revenues will contract top-line figures but leave group EBITDA intact.

Fiber Joint Venture Threat

1.
Aggressive infrastructure expansion: Morgan Stanley notes that Public Power Corporation has rapidly accelerated its alternative fiber rollout across the Greek market. The utility launched its retail offering in the second quarter of 2025 with prices at up to a 50% discount to incumbents. This deployment presents a material overbuild threat to OTE because Public Power Corporation's target is to cover 3 million households by 2030.
2.
Power utility network rollout: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that Public Power Corporation is leveraging its aerial electricity grid to roll out its fiber network. The utility has targeted rolling out fiber-to-the-home lines to 3 million premises by 2030. This aggressive buildout represents a significant infrastructure-based challenge to the country's established telecom operators.
3.
Neutral wholesale platform: Public Power Corporation and Vodafone Greece signed a non-binding term sheet in June 2026 to explore a 50:50 joint venture. This venture will merge PPC FiberGrid and Vodafone's Fiber2All into a single wholesale operator. This collaboration poses a long-term risk to OTE's wholesale dominance because it creates a scaled neutral platform covering 1.6 million homes.
4.
Joint venture announcement: Industry reports in June 2026 confirmed that Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation plan to merge their wholesale networks. This partnership will establish an open-access wholesale provider that allows internet service providers to access high-speed fiber infrastructure. The venture could transform Greek broadband services by offering competitive wholesale rates.
5.
Substantial market influence: Morgan Stanley reports that the joint fiber venture between Public Power Corporation and Vodafone Greece will cover over 1.6 million homes. This tie-up aims to combine their respective fiber-to-the-home assets to form a wholesale operator. However, the broker argues that this joint venture could ease competitive worries by rationalizing the overbuild footprint.
6.
Wholesale negotiation failures: Deutsche Bank Research reported that Public Power Corporation initially struggled to secure wholesale agreements with Greek incumbents. The utility's wholesale pricing was deemed unacceptable by Nova's parent company, United Group. Consequently, Public Power Corporation had to launch its own retail services in mid-2025 because it could not monetize its fiber network through wholesale contracts.

Barriers and Migration Speed

7.
Resilient subscriber performance: Morgan Stanley noted that OTE has maintained robust subscriber additions despite new market entrants. OTE recorded 62,000 FTTH net additions in the second quarter of 2026. This performance suggests OTE is effectively protecting its subscriber base through superior network quality and customer retention.
8.
Contractual volume barriers: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that OTE has secured its wholesale customer base through volume-commitment contracts. Vodafone Greece and Nova signed OTE's wholesale pricing framework in September 2024. This framework provides wholesale discounts up to 28% in exchange for commitments. This means OTE's contracts create strong near-term barriers that prevent sudden wholesale migration.
9.
Regulated pricing updates: Deutsche Bank Research noted that the Greek regulator EETT is adjusting wholesale prices. The regulator plans to lower FTTH wholesale rates by 10% to 13% while raising copper charges by 4% to 5%. Because OTE's pre-agreed wholesale rates are already lower than regulated tariffs, OTE is well insulated from regulatory hits to its wholesale business.
10.
Convergent market structure: Deutsche Bank Research argued that the Greek telecom market remains a rational three-player ecosystem. OTE, Vodafone, and Nova continue to deploy contiguous, non-overlapping fiber networks. This strategic division minimizes direct network overbuild and helps protect industry-wide capital expenditures.

International Transit Exit

11.
International transit exit: Deutsche Bank Research reported that OTE is executing a planned exit from its low- margin international transit business. OTE expects this exit to reduce revenues by approximately €170 million in 2026. It will also cause a further €130 million revenue contraction in 2027. However, because this business has near-zero margins, the exit will not affect operating profitability or intermediate EBITDA.
12.
Transit phase-out impact: Morgan Stanley confirmed that OTE's recent top-line weakness was driven by the planned international transit phase-out. Adjusting for the international wholesale exit, OTE's underlying revenues grew by 8% in the second quarter of 2026. Consequently, sell-side analysts already reflect this revenue reduction in their consensus models, meaning it does not threaten earnings expectations.

Portuguese incumbent NOS observed a rise in competitive pressure within six months of Digi's low-cost entry. This case indicates that disruptive entry-level pricing can threaten high-margin convergence packages.

This drop would severely impact OTE's ICT turnover which represents roughly 10% of group revenue. Consequently, a sudden public sector spending contraction will impair OTE's intermediate EPS growth post-2026.

Restrictive regulatory intervention: Unilateral retail tariff adjustments face significant pushback from the domestic regulator EETT due to Greek cost-of-living politics. The regulator's ongoing tariff benchmarking against European peers suggests that above-inflation price rises will not be permitted. Because OTE relies on these pricing steps to support retail fixed growth, any regulatory block will restrict service margin expansion through 2027. Therefore, failure to execute the December 2026 price adjustment would flatline retail revenues.

Contrary evidence: The risk of a sharp revenue decline is mitigated by substantial delayed RRF disbursements that extend beyond 2026. Competitor Alignments and Corporate Ownership

Disruptive ownership changes: Corporate ownership shifts at Nova or Vodafone Greece could trigger aggressive price competition in the Greek market. If a deep-pocketed global operator acquires United Group's Greek assets, they might sacrifice near-term margins to capture market share. This pricing pressure would directly threaten OTE's dominant 65% share of total market revenue. Under these conditions, OTE would be forced to lower retail rates, eroding its historic operating margins and EPS.

The utility's aerial grid deployment allows it to offer prices up to 50% below established incumbents. Even if OTE's volume-commitment contracts provide near-term protection, long-term retail price cuts by a major player could force OTE to renegotiate wholesale discount frameworks early. This renegotiation would dilute national wholesale revenue lines.

A sudden deceleration in OTE's retail fixed subscriber additions would signal that rival fiber rollouts are gaining commercial traction. Additionally, any legal challenges to OTE's contract indexation clauses by domestic consumer groups would indicate rising political risks. These operational indicators would warn of a material divergence from consensus EPS forecasts.

Capital Remuneration and Parent Control

8.
Parent company pressure: Deutsche Telekom's majority ownership means OTE's capital allocation remains highly sensitive to parent-level cash requirements. If the parent company demands higher upfront cash, OTE might be forced to alter its balanced distribution policy. This pressure could lead to a reduction in share buybacks to fund higher cash dividends. Consequently, a shift away from share repurchases would reduce the pershare accretion that drives OTE's long-term EPS.
9.
Financial expense risks: Unexpected upward pressure on interest and depreciation charges could undermine OTE's operating profit margins over the next three years. If OTE's capital expenditure on FTTH rollout escalates beyond planned limits, depreciation charges will rise. Higher depreciation would directly offset the benefits of historical copper-network amortization. This expense growth would dilute intermediate per-share earnings over time.
10.
Vulnerable dividend base: A prolonged contraction in free cash would directly threaten OTE's 2027 shareholder distribution. If high capital expenditure on FTTH deployment continues while wholesale revenues slide, free cash flow will contract. Because OTE pays out approximately 95% of its free cash flow annually, any capital return reduction would hurt market sentiment. Ultimately, lower free cash flow would limit the company's valuation floor and EPS growth.
