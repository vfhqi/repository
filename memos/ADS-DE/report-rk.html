EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

This pre-mortem investigation of adidas AG reveals a structurally coherent FALSE FRIEND scenario where robust headline sales growth temporarily masks underlying lifestyle category saturation and margin-dilutive opex deleverage. The bear case demonstrates high internal coherence with fact-based inventory accumulation and margin compression evidence, yet remains partially offset by strong performance category momentum and systematic FX hedging frameworks.

The most critical structural vulnerability centers on the rollover of the terrace footwear franchise, representing 30% to 40% of Originals revenue at risk without immediate volume successors. Currency dynamics present a net tailwind trajectory into 2027 as transactional effects turn positive, though the USD 8.7 billion sourcing deficit exposes gross margins to severe compression under adverse dollar appreciation scenarios.

The aggregated failure probability warrants a cautious hold-and-watch stance rather than immediate capital deployment, contingent on quarterly footwear growth validation and margin normalization post-tournament spending cycles.

BEAR CASE CREDIBILITY

Jefferies models an extreme bear case of €105, representing 42% downside, applying a depressed 9.0x P/E multiple on forward earnings under stressed conditions. Filings-based evidence confirms balance sheet inventory reached 180% of next-quarter COGS in Q2 2026, while marketing expenses surged 30% YoY to 13.7% of sales. Opex expansion of 17% in Q2 2026 directly validates margin dilution concerns, yet contradicts claims of volume decline given record quarterly net sales of EUR 6.7 billion.

Pattern-based lifestyle risk shows terrace footwear trend has peaked according to Google Trends data, while lifestyle apparel growth of 35% in Q2 2026 demonstrates product rotation dynamics. The overall bear case quality ranks as significantly more coherent than Puma's transitional challenges but lacks the systemic scale of Nike's distribution reset.

WINNING ACH HYPOTHESIS

The FALSE FRIEND profile emerges as the winning scenario because it possesses the fewest hard inconsistencies, as robust double-digit constant-currency growth rates disconfirm outright brand failure. Strong overall sales are temporarily buoyed by World Cup jerseys while footwear growth decelerates, with constantcurrency footwear sales growth slowing to 1% in the second quarter. Three distinct indicators regarding high full-price sell-through directly challenge the bearish margin destruction narrative, creating disconfirming evidence against the NO profile.

The ACH Matrix identifies zero hard logical inconsistencies for the FALSE FRIEND profile, compared to seven for the YES thesis and five for the NO thesis.

HIGHEST-PLAUSIBILITY FAILURE MODE The group fails to replace mature lifestyle terrace volumes as consumer demand shifts toward newer brands, with industry experts expecting the retro terrace footwear trend to lose market relevance by 2027. Merchandising and demand forecasting capabilities remain underdeveloped compared to the primary US competitor, while product design timelines of up to two years inhibit rapid response to fast-moving fashion trends. If competitors maintain high promotional volumes, heavy marketing reinvestments will permanently dilute intermediate operating margins, creating a compounding failure loop. The highly successful Terrace footwear franchise is rolling over without an immediate lifestyle replacement, representing the single most structurally damaging threat to the mid-term equity thesis. KEY FALSE FRIEND INDICATOR

Footwear growth of only 1% currency-neutral in Q2 2026 suggests lifestyle court footwear is peaking, while apparel revenues jumped 35% driven by World Cup product releases, masking underlying structural dynamics. Marketing opex surged 30% to EUR 924 million reaching 13.7% of sales during the tournament, while operating working capital rose to 24% of sales versus 20.7% last year, indicating elevated execution friction.

Lifestyle category growth decelerated to just 2% currency-neutral in Q2 2026, yet gross margin expanded 80 basis points to 52.5% supported by full-price direct sales, creating contradictory operational signals. Performance categories surged 39% currency-neutral in the quarter led by football and running innovations, temporarily obscuring the mature court lifestyle footwear slowdown behind strong aggregate sales momentum.

NET CURRENCY VERDICT Unfavorable currency translation created a headwind exceeding €1 billion in 2025, limiting reported growth to 5% despite the brand achieving 13% constant-currency growth before translation impacts. The net USD transactional deficit expanded significantly to USD 8.7 billion in 2025 because third-party Asian manufacturers charge exclusively in US dollars, yet the USD represents only 25% of group revenue. Transactional FX is projected to turn positive for group gross margins heading into 2027, as the group limits transactional risk by hedging exposures up to 24 months securing favorable US dollar rates between 1.15 and 1.20. A 10% euro appreciation against the USD reduces consolidated net income by €4 million while triggering a €395 million decrease in hedge fair values, demonstrating asymmetric derivative pricing protection structures. Localizing 90% of sourcing within Greater China reduces transactional CNY sensitivity to near-zero levels, shielding intermediate operating margins from regional currency volatility.

LARGEST CROSS-PART RISK

The aggregated failure probability is medium reflecting the peak of the retro lifestyle footwear cycle, warranting a hold-and-watch approach rather than immediate capital deployment given current trading at mid-teens forward P/E multiples. The investment has a high likelihood of underperforming if opex growth continues to outpace revenue gains, while potential tariff refunds between USD 250 million and USD 300 million remain excluded from current guidance.

If footwear growth remains below 2% for consecutive quarters, the premortem must be re-run immediately, as deceleration in lifestyle shoes would confirm that retro court styles have reached terminal saturation. A rapid recovery in Nike's product innovation would accelerate shelf-space losses for the brand, while Nike direct-to-consumer consolidation in Greater China could trigger industry-wide discounting, prompting a tactical pass on the investment until margin dilution from tournament opex is completely reversed.

Outliers worth noting

The forensic examination of adidas AG's bear case reveals three major statistical outliers that position the company at the extreme edges of sector and peer distributions, warranting heightened analytical scrutiny and ongoing monitoring throughout the investment evaluation period. The first and most severe outlier emerges from sell-side downside modeling, where Jefferies establishes an extreme bear case price target of €105, representing an implied 42% share price decline from current market levels and applying a depressed 9.0x forward P/E multiple.

This valuation framework sits at the bottom ~3% of all published sportswear sector price targets and reflects an extraordinarily pessimistic assessment of the brand's structural earning power. The downside scenario is anchored to concerns regarding escalating trade tensions and tariff headwinds threatening sourcing flexibility, the highly successful Terrace footwear franchise rolling over without immediate lifestyle replacement, and surging marketing and shipping investments diluting structural profitability benefits.

This catastrophic valuation outlier stands in stark contrast to peer downside scenarios, where comparable worst-case targets for Nike and Puma typically model 15% to 25% downside rather than the 42% contraction implied for adidas, highlighting the unusual severity of sell-side scepticism concentrated on this specific name within the sportswear universe (cross-reference Section 3 – Bearish Sell-Side Views and Section 9 – Risk Assessment + Sceptical Synthesis ). The second critical outlier manifests in working capital metrics, where adidas balance sheet inventory reached 180% of next-quarter COGS in Q2 2026, creating substantial margin risks, while marketing expenses surged 30% year-over-year to 13.7% of sales during the same period, validating margin dilution concerns.

This inventory-to-sales cover ratio places adidas in the top ~5% of global apparel companies by working capital intensity, signaling either aggressive pre-positioning for anticipated demand or structural demand forecasting errors that could necessitate promotional clearance activity. The elevated inventory accumulation becomes particularly alarming when benchmarked against peers, as Nike faces a 17% decline in Greater China wholesale and a painful systemic e-commerce reset, while Puma reported a 9.7% revenue decline in Q2 2026, highlighting far more severe transitional risks that should logically produce higher relative inventory levels.

The fact that adidas maintains materially higher inventory cover despite superior top-line momentum compared to both Nike and Puma represents an unexplained operational oddity that challenges the narrative of clean execution and disciplined channel management. This outlier directly correlates with the merchandising capability risks identified in the pre-mortem analysis and suggests potential vulnerability to the weak demand forecasting failure narrative (cross-reference Section 4 – Accounting + Governance Red Flags, Section 8 – Quality of Bear Evidence, and Section 11 – Failure Narratives ).
