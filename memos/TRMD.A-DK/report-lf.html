EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Chief Executive Quality and Leadership

Jacob Meldgaard brings more than 30 years of deep maritime shipping experience to TORM plc, having held senior executive positions at Dampskibsselskabet Norden A/S and J.

Lauritzen prior to his appointment as Chief Executive Officer in April 2010.

His extensive background in dry bulk and tanker operations directly aligns with TORM's strategic focus on fleet optimization and commercial shipping excellence.

Meldgaard has provided unmatched leadership stability over more than 16 years, navigating TORM through multiple shipping cycles and major corporate transitions while ensuring operational consistency and strategic focus.

Governance Structure and Independence

TORM experienced a major governance shift in October 2026 when Oaktree Capital Management sold its remaining shareholding, prompting Christopher Boehringer to resign from the board with immediate effect and eliminating historical controlling shareholder influence over board decisions.

The company completed a total restructuring of its voting framework after Oaktree's beneficial ownership fell below the critical one-third threshold on January 6, 2026, triggering the redemption and cancellation of special governance rights attached to the Class B and Class C shares and eliminating dual-class voting complexities to establish a clean public governance model.

Simon Mackenzie Smith was appointed as the independent non-executive Chair of the Board in December 2025, bringing corporate finance and investment banking expertise that enhances board oversight of capital allocation strategies.

TORM operates its Audit Committee with only two independent non-executive directors instead of the recommended three members, a composition that departs from UK Corporate Governance Code recommendations and could increase individual oversight burdens during complex transactions.

Female representation on the board stands at 20 percent with one female director and four male directors, lagging behind modern institutional expectations, though the board committed to a long-term target of 40 percent female representation by 2030.

Executive Compensation and Shareholder Alignment

TORM provided Chief Executive Officer Jacob Meldgaard with total remuneration of 9,322 k dollars in 2025 compared to 8,020 k dollars in 2024, representing a 16.23 percent increase that reflects expanding fleet scale and record-setting earnings per share during shipping market upcycles.

The company links up to 70 percent of the Chief Executive Officer's base salary to specific corporate performance metrics set at the start of each year, including Return on Invested Capital, cost structure, safety, and environmental footprint to ensure disciplined operational execution.

TORM incentivizes long-term performance by granting Restricted Share Units that vest over a three-year period, having granted 1.36 million units in 2026 at an exercise price of 167.1 Danish kroner to align the senior management team directly with the long-term share price.

In response to negative annual meeting votes, TORM's Board of Directors initiated a comprehensive review of executive pay and is evaluating the approach to the Chief Executive Officer's annual bonus to prepare a revised policy for the 2027 annual general meeting.

Expert and Industry Perspectives

A former superintendent at Scorpio Tankers emphasized that consolidating commercial and technical operations under the One TORM group significantly reduces corporate management costs and operational friction, creating a distinct competitive advantage over peer firms that rely on outsourced management and securing more resilient future profitability.

Key Execution Risks and Dependencies

TORM exhibits high key-person dependency on Chief Executive Officer Jacob Meldgaard who has served since April 2010 and accumulated over 30 years of maritime shipping experience, with his extensive industry network and deep operational knowledge making him difficult to replace without disrupting strategic progress.

Sceptics argue that the record financial performance of TORM is driven by geopolitical conflicts rather than management capabilities, with the highly spot-exposed fleet remaining vulnerable to immediate rate corrections if trade routes normalize, as sudden de-escalation of global tensions would severely depress realized rates and shrink future earnings per share.

TORM faces significant capital expenditure demands because approximately 50 percent of its LR2 fleet will turn 20 years of age, with renewing these older vessels requiring massive capital reinvestment that could limit cash available for dividends and constrain capital distribution if spot rates decline over the next 18 months.

The substantial stake held by Hafnia Limited in TORM could trigger hostile consolidation and disrupt independent leadership, with any future boardroom struggle threatening to distract executive management from fleet optimization and impact long-term earnings per share growth following the dismantling of Class B protections in January 2026.
