Elisa (ELISA-FI): what the Q3 2026 results do to the case

These results weaken the existing case, and by enough to matter: the first of the two prints the case said would decide it went against the thesis on every mobile series the case named, and the company itself moved the point at which its higher prices reach revenue from Q4 2026 to 2027.

What changed

The case was waiting for the first hard read of the back book re-pricing. Its own tests for this print were churn below 17 percent and ARPU above EUR 24.0, with an alarm if mobile service revenue was still worsening sequentially. Elisa reported on 21 October 2026:

  • Mobile service revenue fell 2.3 percent year on year, after -1.7 percent in Q2 and -0.1 percent in Q1. Finnish postpaid churn rose to 18.7 percent from 16.7 percent in Q2; the company calls that its ten-year Q3 average, DNB Carnegie calls the 200 basis point step higher than normal seasonality. Finnish postpaid subscriptions excluding M2M and IoT fell by 16,300 on the company's figure and by 22,000 on Bernstein's and DNB Carnegie's. Finnish postpaid ARPU was EUR 24.0, unchanged on Q2; Morgan Stanley reads postpaid ARPU growth at -1 percent. 5G penetration reached 61 percent, up three points in the quarter.
  • Group revenue was EUR 560.9 million, in line with consensus of EUR 561 million, but telecom service revenue of EUR 349 million was down 1.3 percent year on year and 0.9 percent below consensus. Fixed service revenue grew 1.6 percent on 20,400 fixed broadband additions (3,500 in Q2; 5,000 in Q3 2025).
  • Comparable EBITDA was EUR 209.7 million, down 1.8 percent year on year and 1.6 percent below consensus; the comparable EBITDA margin was 37.4 percent, 80 basis points below Q3 2025, with the EUR 40 million savings programme on plan and marketing costs lower than a year earlier. Comparable EBIT fell 5.1 percent to EUR 131.5 million. Comparable EPS was EUR 0.59 against EUR 0.64 a year earlier, about 7 to 8 percent below consensus. Q3 employee expenses rose to EUR 107 million from EUR 102 million on a smaller workforce. EUR 5 million of restructuring and other exceptional costs sat between reported and comparable EBITDA.
  • Comparable cash flow after investments was EUR 116 million (EUR 125 million in Q3 2025) and EUR 283 million for the nine months against EUR 320 million, down 11.7 percent. Capex excluding licences, leases and acquisitions was EUR 77 million, about 14 percent of quarterly revenue, against EUR 65 million a year earlier and Morgan Stanley's EUR 67 million; management says year-to-date capex excluding data centres is consistent with the 12 percent guidance. Net debt rose to EUR 1.52 billion, 1.9 times comparable EBITDA (1.7 times a year earlier), inside the 1.5 to 2.0 times target. The third EUR 0.60 dividend instalment was approved; EUR 2.40 for 2026 stands.
  • Guidance was kept in form: 2026 revenue at the same level as or slightly above 2025, comparable EBITDA of EUR 815 to 845 million, capex at 12 percent of revenue. Management said telecom service revenue growth would land at the lower end of the 0 to 2 percent range, that competition had "intensified slightly", that recovery was continuing at a "more moderate pace", and that new consumer mobile sales prices were above the prior year but their effect would arrive with a delay and become more visible in 2027. Q4 EBITDA is expected to exceed Q3. Nine-month revenue is down 0.5 percent; consensus full-year EBITDA was EUR 825 million.
  • International Software Services grew 10.9 percent organically to EUR 38 million, about EUR 2 million above consensus, the first double-digit quarter since Q4 2024, at a 1.9 percent EBITDA margin. Elisa Industriq is being split into two global businesses, Manufacturing and Critical Infrastructure, with a "significant" profitability improvement promised during 2027 and no savings figure, implementation cost or margin bridge; DNB Carnegie assumes a 4 percent net cost reduction and about EUR 18 million of 2027 EBITDA. Year-to-date data-centre connectivity order intake approached EUR 100 million, mostly secured in Q3, with an expanded Google relationship; revenue recognition is expected mainly from 2028, the related capex sits outside the 12 percent target, and quarterly data-centre capex was not disclosed.
  • The sell-side response: Morgan Stanley kept Equal-weight, cut its target to EUR 38 from EUR 39 and its EPS to EUR 2.22, 2.30 and 2.27 for 2026 to 2028 (from 2.35, 2.37 and 2.30), citing competition and higher depreciation. DNB Carnegie kept Hold, cut its target to EUR 38 from EUR 40, trimmed 2026 EPS by 1 percent, left 2027 and raised 2028 by 1 percent, and says a significant Q4 ARPU improvement is now needed. Deutsche Bank kept Hold at EUR 40.50 and judged the miss to be deteriorating mobile service revenue rather than phasing. Bernstein kept Underperform at EUR 26.50 with unchanged estimates, reading "more moderate pace" as a concession toward its bear case. Rothschild & Co Redburn kept Sell and cut its target to EUR 33.40 from EUR 40, arguing the dividend is not covered by free cash flow from 2027 on its estimates. Morgan Stanley notes that Telia's Finnish service revenue fell only 0.2 percent in the same quarter, with its Finnish EBITDA up 0.9 percent.

Against the case's own monitor: four of the alarm levels the case set for this print were hit (mobile service revenue still worsening sequentially; churn above 17 percent; Q3 comparable EBITDA below EUR 210 million, by EUR 0.3 million; renewed cuts after the Q3 print), ARPU sits exactly at the EUR 24.0 line, the margin alarm (below 36 percent) was not hit, and the software driver returned its confirm reading (above 8 percent with positive EBITDA) for the first of the two quarters the case requires. None of the case's invalidation levels was reached.

The price we hold, EUR 35.72, is dated 20 October, before the print, so we do not hold the market's reaction. On that price the multiple is 13.9 times consensus EPS 24 months forward of EUR 2.5662, the 5th percentile of the stock's ten-year history, above the case's 13.4 times alarm and above every price at which the case re-looks (EUR 31 to 33). The pinned letters moved between the memo and the print, on data of 16 to 18 October: technical momentum to F from C, thematic momentum to D from C, valuation to A from B, with sell-side momentum and TSR/Valuation at F unchanged. They are context, not an effect of these results.

The challenge

The case's reading of this quarter already exists: a slower turn, timing slipped once, odds on positive mobile service revenue by Q1 2027 of about six in ten. These results fit that reading at lower odds, and management's "delayed recovery" is the same reading. The readings below are the ones the case does not hold.

First: the roll-off re-prices into a lower market, not back to the old one. The case's mechanism is that 12-month discounted contracts expire in Q4 and re-price at front-book levels set by a rational triopoly that followed each other's EUR 10 rises. The quarter's evidence is that the price the back book re-prices to is set, at the low end, by operators outside the triopoly. Deutsche Bank puts Elisa's entry-level premium over the cheapest Finnish network operator at 5 percent, against 37 percent for Telia Sweden and 111 percent for Telenor Norway; finds Finnish entry-level 4G tariffs down more than 20 percent over four years; and prices the MVNO Moi about 25 percent below the comparable 1 Gbps point. DNB Carnegie puts about 30 percent of the market in 4G. Management acknowledged a competitor still using gift vouchers and a Q4 that contains Black Friday and Christmas, and Bernstein's point is that the renewal pool created in the 2025 promotional surge is exactly what operators compete for again. On this reading the roll-off still happens, speed buyers still pay more than EUR 3 a month to move to 5G, and the price-sensitive third renews at MVNO-set prices or leaves: mobile service revenue stabilises near zero rather than turning to the case's +1.5 percent, and ARPU does not deliver the EUR 0.30 Q3-to-Q4 step DNB Carnegie's history implies. The case holds low-end competition as a risk, but as "plausible, moderate and easing", and it holds the structural reading only as a competing hypothesis it rejected because the structure had repaired at the front book. What would have to be true: that the 22,000 net loss and the 200 basis points of churn above seasonal are concentrated in the 4G base; that MVNO share gains continue through Q4 (not quantified in anything we hold); and that new-sale prices "above the prior year" are above a prior year that was itself the discounted one. The first two are consistent with the quarter; the third is not disclosed either way.

Second: the market is repairing and Elisa is the one paying. The case's 'Finnish mobile market repair' driver read Telia's reported stabilisation as support. This quarter Telia's Finnish service revenue fell 0.2 percent and its Finnish EBITDA rose 0.9 percent while Elisa's mobile service revenue fell 2.3 percent and its comparable EBITDA fell 1.8 percent, and Deutsche Bank reads Elisa's weaker outcome as partly company-specific. Elisa reduced vouchers and marketing spend, states that it defends its leading share, and lost 22,000 postpaid customers at flat ARPU with churn up 200 basis points; the case's own foundations record that the network award passed to DNA and the satisfaction lead to Telia in 2025, and flagged that a back book cannot re-price without churn if the network is no longer the best. On this reading the triopoly is behaving rationally, the case's "rational triopoly" leg is intact, and it is of no use to Elisa, because the share-defence cost falls on the leader: volume at the expense of ARPU, or ARPU at the expense of volume, and the Q3 mix (flat ARPU, falling volume) is the worse half of each. This reading is worse for the case than the first, because the remedy is to spend, on vouchers or on the network (Q3 capex ran at about 14 percent of revenue), and the spending comes out of the cash the dividend needs. The case does not hold it, because it treats Elisa's mobile service revenue and the market's as one series. What would have to be true: DNA's and Telia's Finnish Q3 and Q4 net adds and ARPU running ahead of Elisa's; Elisa's churn above the market's; and an Elisa Q4 in which either ARPU or net adds improves, but not both. Telia's quarter is one of the three data points, and it points this way.

Third: the adverse drop-through is now the modal path, and the balance-sheet backstop is smaller than the case assumed. The case runs a sensitivity in which higher depreciation, dearer debt and a higher tax rate take FY2028 EPS to EUR 2.34 from a modal EUR 2.51 and the eighteen-month return to 3.7 percent a year, and leaves the rating unchanged because the return already fails. The quarter's shape is that sensitivity: a 1.6 percent EBITDA miss became a 5.1 percent EBIT fall and a 7 to 8 percent EPS miss, on higher depreciation and financial expense; Morgan Stanley cites depreciation for its multi-year cut, and its FY2028 EPS of EUR 2.27 now sits below the case's adverse case. What the case does not hold is the second half of this reading. The case funds a dividend shortfall from a 1.8 times balance sheet "for two to three years". Net debt is now EUR 1.52 billion at 1.9 times, the ceiling is 2.0 times, and on the guided EBITDA range of EUR 815 to 845 million that ceiling sits at about EUR 1.63 to 1.69 billion, roughly EUR 110 to 170 million of headroom, with EBITDA falling, quarterly capex up 18.5 percent, data-centre capex outside the envelope and a 2027 bond to refinance at prevailing rates (average interest about 2.8 percent; one point of rates is about EUR 3.9 million of annual profit). Rothschild & Co Redburn's line is that free cash flow does not cover the dividend from 2027; Bernstein has operating free cash flow 11 percent below consensus at EUR 127 million. On this reading the two to three years of backstop exists only if the shortfall is small, and if it is not, the dividend-growth pause the case treats as an alarm becomes probable rather than possible, which the case's own reading of the register says is a perception shock for income holders. What would have to be true: FY2026 free cash flow before dividends near or below the case's EUR 390 million invalidation line; 2027 depreciation and interest at or above the case's adverse assumptions (EUR 325 million and EUR 55 million); and a 2027 dividend proposed without growth. The first two are consistent with the nine-month run-rate; the third is a January 2027 event.

A fourth reading, which we considered and do not put up. The second engine turned before the first: software at 10.9 percent organic with positive EBITDA, a restructuring aimed at 2027, and EUR 100 million of data-centre orders. The case gave the software recovery one-in-three odds and sized its cleared-branch contribution at 1 to 2 percent of group EPS by FY2028, too small to move the group multiple. The quarter's own figures do not overturn that: the segment is about 7 percent of revenue at a 1.9 percent margin, Rothschild & Co Redburn has about 40 percent of it growing at about 1.5 percent, the restructuring has no bridge, and data-centre revenue begins mainly from 2028, at the edge of and mostly beyond the case's six-to-eighteen-month staircase. It is a genuine positive for the far staircase and for the odds on the cleared branch's second condition. It does not carry the group within the case's horizon.

Net of the four: the results admit the case's own slower-turn reading; two readings in which the Q4 roll-off does not deliver the turn the thesis is; and a third in which the turn arrives and the return still falls while the dividend's backstop thins. The three are not exclusive, and the second and third compound.

What it does to the case

  • 'Investment Setup' (BB 3) at C, and within it 'OSHQC CfC Clearing' (E 3, BB 3) at C: under more pressure, enough to move. The case said the fit stands or falls on the 21 October and Q4 2026 prints. The first fell. 'Clearing evidence' (RA 4, E 3, BB 3) at D was held off F "because the mechanism is dated and the indicators point the right way"; the indicators now point the wrong way on churn, net adds and ARPU, and the mechanism's realised effect has been moved by the company to 2027. 'Named concern' (RA 3, E 3, BB 3) at C loses transience on the mobile side (Deutsche Bank and Bernstein read trend, not phasing) and gains one quarter of clearing on the software side. 'Few concerns' (RA 5, E 3, BB 3) at C: the dividend-cover concern the case kept behind the two named ones has moved forward. Of the Element's three "not D" reasons, the mechanical end is intact but deferred, the front-book repair is intact in new-sale prices and broken in volume, and the multiple has paid. The nearest alternative, 'Major CfC Clearing' (E 4, BB 3) at D, is not improved, because guidance was not rebased; the pressure is toward no crisp box rather than toward that one.
  • 'Investment Thesis' (BB 1) at D: under more pressure, same letter. Its three "not F" legs: the yield is real (the EUR 0.60 instalment was paid); the balance sheet holds (1.9 times, inside the range, thinner); the Q4 turn is dated with probable odds (the odds are lower). 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1) at D: three of the four outputs the case tracks went backwards (revenue flat, EPS -7.7 percent, cash flow -6.6 percent), and the margin rose on Q2 and fell on the prior year. 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1) at F cannot fall, but what sits beneath it moves: the clearing price of EUR 31.4 moves toward the adverse case's EUR 29.3; the case's FY2026 EPS of EUR 2.39 now needs about EUR 0.62 in Q4 against EUR 0.56 a year earlier, after a Q3 of EUR 0.59 against EUR 0.64; the three-in-ten on the cleared branch is lower on its mobile condition and higher on its software condition. 'Dividends' (RA 1, E 3, BB 1) at C and 'Sustainability' (KQ 2, RA 1, E 3, BB 1) at D: more pressure, on 1.9 times and on Rothschild & Co Redburn's 2027 cover. 'Sell-side upgrades' (RA 1, E 2, BB 1) at D: more pressure, held off F only by the Q4 mechanism; three houses cut targets and one cut the three-year EPS path. 'Change Forces' (E 1, BB 1) at D: the same; guidance was softened within its range, not cut, and the internal forces remain defensive.
  • 'Foundations' (BB 2) at C: under more pressure, same letter for now. 'Value chain, industry' (E 1, BB 2) at C was held off D because the repair since March 2026 was on the record; the record now carries a relapse in churn and volume with the triopoly's prices intact, which is the second reading above. 'Industry structure' (RA 3, E 1, BB 2) at B: the "repaired within two quarters" finding is in question. 'Business' (E 2, BB 2) at B: the case's own trigger to move it to C is two more quarters of ARPU below EUR 24.0 with churn above 17 percent; this quarter gives churn above 17 and ARPU at, not below, EUR 24.0, so the trigger is half-pulled on one of the two quarters. 'Competitive advantage' (RA 2, E 2, BB 2) at B: the 5 percent entry-level premium is new evidence against the pricing-power half.
  • 'Investment Case' (BB 4) at D: the same letter, with risks the case named now actual. 'Cockroach' (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 4): cash flow and dividend cover were the probable third problem and are actual on the nine-month figures. 'No estimate cuts' (RA 5, E 1, BB 4) at D was held off F because the trend was toward finishing; the cuts have restarted. 'Limited multiple risk' (RA 10, E 1, BB 4) at D: the pre-print multiple of 13.9 times is half a turn from the case's 13.4 times alarm. The 'Guidance credibility' risk: the lower-end comment is not the "further component cut" the case set as its alarm, but it is the second softening of the same line in three months. 'Few concerns' (RA 3, E 2, BB 4) at D: still five, one quieter (software), one louder (cash). 'Staircase' (RA 4, E 2, BB 1) at C: the far steps gain a dated 2028 item and the mid steps lose most of one, as data-centre revenue slips past the case's window.
  • The software key driver: less pressure. The case's modal case was breakeven; the quarter delivered the confirm reading on growth and on EBITDA sign, with the quality caveats Rothschild & Co Redburn supplies. It is one of the two quarters the case's second way back in requires on software; the ARPU half of that route is unmet.
  • Price thresholds: none crossed on the pre-print price we hold. The case's re-look band of EUR 31 to 33, its yield floor of EUR 32.7 and its 13.4 times alarm all sit below EUR 35.72. The post-print price is not in what we hold.
  • The pinned letters (technical F, thematic D, valuation A, sell-side F, TSR/Valuation F) are the store's, measured before the print, and we do not grade them.

We re-rate nothing here. The pressure runs down on the Setup by a full grade on this evidence, down on the Thesis and the Case within their letters, down on the Foundations' industry leg, and up on the software leg.

What would settle it

  • The Q4 2026 print, with the full-year results on 29 January 2027. It settles the first and second readings against the case's own thresholds: Finnish mobile service revenue growth at +1.5 percent or better (the case's confirm; below it, the case's invalidation); Finnish postpaid net adds excluding M2M and IoT positive; ARPU up sequentially toward the case's EUR 24.50 by Q1 2027 (DNB Carnegie's historical Q3-to-Q4 step is about EUR 0.30); churn materially below 18.7 percent; Q4 comparable EBITDA above EUR 209.7 million and the full year against EUR 815 to 845 million; Q4 comparable cash flow against the case's EUR 100 million confirm and EUR 80 million warning; FY2026 free cash flow before dividends against the case's EUR 390 million invalidation; and the 2027 dividend proposal expected with those results, which settles the pause-or-grow question the third reading turns on. The second reading also needs DNA's and Telia's Finnish Q4 figures, from Telenor's and Telia's own reports, to separate the market from Elisa; Telia's Q3 is already one data point.
  • Two disclosures the company has not committed to. A quantified Industriq bridge (annual savings, implementation cost, headcount, 2027 margin): management gave none and promised none by date, and DNB Carnegie's EUR 18 million is an assumption standing in for it. Data-centre capex by quarter and the returns on the EUR 100 million of orders: management declined to give them. Neither is needed to settle the mobile question; both are needed before the fourth reading could be upgraded from "does not carry the group", and a question nothing is scheduled to answer is an open question of a different kind.
  • Q4 competitive behaviour through Black Friday and Christmas is observable in tariffs and vouchers before any print, and the case already tracks competitor tariff moves. It is the earliest evidence on the first reading, and it is not a company disclosure.
  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry the broker estimates and targets that have landed by then: the consensus FY2026 to FY2028 EPS path the case's valuation stands on (EUR 2.42, 2.49 and 2.57 at the memo), the revision skew and buy share, and the split between the phasing reading (DNB Carnegie: 2027 unchanged) and the trend reading (Morgan Stanley: up to 6 percent off 2026 to 2028). That settles how far 'Sell-side upgrades', 'No estimate cuts' and the earnings-momentum pin move, and whether consensus has put the drop-through into 2027 and 2028, which is the part of the third reading the sell-side can settle. It will not carry any Q4 operating data, any post-renewal ARPU, the Industriq bridge, data-centre capex, the dividend proposal, or DNA's Finnish numbers unless a broker reprints them. It cannot settle the first or second reading; it can only tell us which the sell-side has chosen, and the sell-side is already split.
