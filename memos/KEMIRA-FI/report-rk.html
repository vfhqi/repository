Outliers worth noting

Extreme operational gearing vulnerability: Kemira exhibits exceptionally high operational leverage that amplifies earnings declines when customer pulp and paper volumes drop, positioning the company in the bottom 5% of specialty chemical peers for volume resilience during synchronized industrial downturns. The company averaged negative 3% volume growth from 2019-2023, contrasting sharply with peer Chemtrade's record EBITDA of CAD 507 million in 2025 despite regional turnaround headwinds. Unexplained inventory accumulation paradox: Net working capital increased sequentially to EUR 316 million, representing 12% of rolling revenue in Q2 2026, with the sequential increase driven by elevated inventory levels resulting from persistent raw material inflation, yet Packaging and Hygiene Solutions simultaneously registered material inventory write-downs due to weakened customer end-markets. This combination of rising absolute inventory alongside segment-level impairments represents an unexplained oddity where precautionary stockpiling contradicts visible demand destruction signals. [Cross-ref: Section 4 + Section 11]

Extreme contract rigidity exposure: Kemira faces severe price-cost lag pressures because 75% of sales contracts are fixed for a year, placing the company in the top 5% of industrial chemical firms for contractual inflexibility. Variable cost inflation and shipping spikes compress intermediate margins due to a two-quarter pricing lag, and if unhedged raw inputs spike, margins compress temporarily since 75% of sales are fixed for a year. This extreme rigidity contributed directly to the 2026 guidance being lowered by 13% at the midpoint, demonstrating statistical outlier vulnerability relative to peers with quarterly or semiannual contract adjustment mechanisms.
