[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Hellenic Telecom is a self-help and cash-return case in a dominant, under-levered Greek incumbent, which the market has dressed as a country re-rating. At 20.1 EUR (2026-09-25) the shares pay about 7% a year in cash for about 4% a year of earnings growth, so the modal return of about 8% to 10% a year is half the framework's 20% bar; the same case would clear the bar at about 17 EUR.

  • The change is internal, executed and two years old.
    • A new chief executive arrived on 1 July 2024 and took the decisions a controlling parent had left untaken: the loss-making Romanian mobile unit was sold (completed 1 October 2025), about 300 EURm of zero-margin international transit revenue was exited (170 EURm in FY2026, 130 EURm in FY2027), indirect costs were cut from 31% to 28% of service revenue, the fibre footprint was taken to 2.2 million homes passed and 687k subscribers, and a payout of 100% of the prior year's free cash flow was codified in February 2026.
    • None of the screen's red flags is present: no caretaker, no plan without deeds, no crown jewel sold (Romania ran a 12% EBITDA margin against over 40% in Greece), no leverage (0.2x to 0.3x net debt to EBITDAaL), no debt-funded buyback.
    • The strongest external force is adverse and dated: Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation (PPC) agreed in June 2026 to merge their fibre assets into a 50:50 wholesale joint venture, targeted to complete in H1 2027, which would give the wholesale tenant that fills OTE's fibre an alternative host it co-owns.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and that is the problem.
    • It shows in the operating metrics (postpaid net additions of 62k in Q2 2026, the highest in over 17 years; FTTH net additions of 62k), in the financial statements (an adjusted EBITDAaL margin of 40.1% in Q2 2026 against 38.2% in FY2024; EBITDAaL growth of about 3%) and in the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus EPS +5.0% over the twelve months to 2026-09-25).
    • A change that has reached the estimates and the price has little left to surprise with.
      • The revision series has been flat since the 29 July 2026 results (FY2026 mean EPS +0.2% over six months, -1.2% over one), and the pattern this case fits, 'Major Corporate Change', has its entry point at "input plus early output", which passed in 2025.
  • The 'Financial Outputs' improve at the margin line and nowhere else that matters.
    • Revenue is flat by design and by deceleration: headline consensus revenue is -0.6% in FY2026 and +0.5% in FY2027 as transit leaves, and the two service lines that carry the case slowed in the same quarter, mobile service revenue from +5.5% in Q1 2026 to +2.3% in Q2, retail fixed service revenue from +1.1% to +0.3%.
    • Margin is the one output clearly improving, about 85 bp a year on our path toward 44.6% by FY2029 on the brokers' basis, but about half of that is the arithmetic of transit revenue leaving at zero margin.
    • Earnings per share step up about 14% in FY2026, to 1.62 EUR on our path from 1.42 EUR in FY2025 on the brokers' basis, and then grow about 4.3% a year.
      • The step is a lower tax charge, the absence of the Romanian loss and a 2.3% smaller share count, with EBITDA growing 3%
      • the framework's guidance is that investors do not extrapolate below-EBIT gains, and we agree.
  • What it earns: about 4.3% a year of EPS growth from FY2026, carried by margin and buybacks on a flat revenue line.
    • Revenue adds about 0.5 points a year, margin about 2.0 points, the share count about 2.35 points, and the lines below EBITDA (depreciation rising from 601 EURm to 640 EURm between FY2026 and FY2029, refinancing at about 2.95% against 0.875%, tax normalising near 22.5%) subtract about 0.6 points.
    • Our path runs 1.62, 1.69, 1.76 and 1.84 EUR for FY2026 to FY2029
    • consensus runs 1.64, 1.71 and 1.82 EUR for FY2026 to FY2028.
  • What it costs: 20.1 EUR is 11.3x consensus earnings 24 months forward, the 70th percentile of the stock's own five-year range, after a rise that was four-fifths multiple.
    • The shares are +26.5% over twelve months and +27.4% over six (2026-09-25) against FY2026 consensus EPS +5.0% over twelve months.
    • The multiple sits 4% below the ten-year median of 11.80x and 10% above the five-year median of 10.27x: the easy part of the re-rating has happened, on Greek reclassification flows and sector appetite rather than on earnings.
  • The return against the bar is half of it, and the multiple is the only swing factor.
    • On our modal multiple of 11.6x applied to the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the shares are worth about 21.6 EUR in March 2028 and 23.9 EUR in September 2029
    • with dividends of 0.92 EUR and 2.98 EUR respectively the total returns are +11.9% over eighteen months (7.8% a year) and +33.7% over three years (10.2% a year).
    • The cash return carries about 4.6 points a year of that, earnings growth about 4.4 points, and the re-rating about nothing.
    • At the ten-year 75th percentile of 13.70x the eighteen-month rate is about 19.9% a year, a B
      • at the five-year median of 10.3x it is 0%.
      • Whether the case is a D or a B is one number, and it is the number the framework tells us to be most sceptical about.
    • The earnings path has no margin below EBITDA: with depreciation rising 4% a year instead of 2%, interest up 15 EURm rather than 10 EURm and tax at 24%, EPS grows about 1.9% a year and the return falls to about 2.5% a year over eighteen months, an F.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the chief executive through the decisions to the margin, the cash and the payout, and every link can be tracked in the company's quarterly disclosure.
    • It breaks at revenue, where a fibre and postpaid migration that is real in units has not lifted service-revenue growth above 2% to 3%, and it breaks at the price, which has already paid for the part of the recovery that can be proved and a little of the part that cannot.

  • The case therefore is: a good company that has done the right things, whose earnings trajectory has been tidied rather than upgraded, trading at a price that assumes the tidying continues and the wholesale threat does not matter.

    • That is a park at 20.1 EUR and a case to re-open near 17 EUR, not a case to progress.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is real but not material enough: strong internal forces (B) act in a market whose competitive layer is hardening (external forces C, competition D), producing outputs that improve at the margin line only ('Financial Outputs' D) and a modal total return of about 8% to 10% a year ('Modal return' D), half the framework's bar.

    • The parts combine as a chain whose weakest links, the flat revenue line and the near-zero re-rating, bind the whole: a good company doing sensible things is not the same as a material change in its earnings trajectory.
    • Not C, because the one Element at C ('Change Forces') is carried by internal forces whose outputs have already arrived, and materiality is measured at the return, where the case is a D.
    • Not F, because the cash return is large and secure and the internal change is not a story.

  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against our view is not the bear case
    • it is the bull case in its strongest form, which is also the sell-side's.
  • OTE is a premium, highly predictable value grower with a 0.2x balance sheet, an A- credit rating since June 2026, a payout of 100% of free cash flow of about 570 to 580 EURm growing with a capex line that falls from about 600 EURm toward 550 EURm by FY2029, in a country being reclassified to Developed Market status with passive money arriving from September 2026, in a sector whose multiples have risen 35% to 64% for five of its peers in twelve months.
  • On that reading the ten-year 75th-percentile multiple of 13.70x is the right anchor, the eighteen-month return is about 20% a year, and Morgan Stanley's target of 23.80 EUR (16 September 2026) is conservative.

  • Its evidence is the unbroken payout record, the June 2026 rating upgrade, the sector's own re-rating, Morgan Stanley's forecast of free cash flow growth of +9% a year over 2024 to 2030, and the fact that PPC's retail fibre network had 0.6% utilisation after a year of 50% discounts.

  • First concession: we concede every fact, the quality of the business and the tide in the sector, and we concede that on a free-cash-flow-per-share footing the growth after 2028 is higher than on EPS, because depreciation runs ahead of capex in the late-build years.

  • First reason we hold our view: the re-rating to 13.70x is the whole of the difference between a D and a B, and it needs two things at once, the joint venture failing to matter and mobile service revenue re-accelerating.
    • We put the first at possible and the second at unlikely, and their product is unlikely.
    • At 13.70x OTE would trade at a premium to every cohort peer bar Telecom Italia on price to earnings, at the moment its wholesale host position is first contested.
  • Second reason: the bull case's yield argument is an enterprise-value argument, 5.0x EV to EBITDAaL against a European sector average of 6.5x, a 23% discount.
    • On the framework's own metric, a price-to-earnings multiple on the brokers' basis, the stock is at the middle of its sector (Orange 11.5x, NOS 11.4x, OTE 11.3x) and at the 70th percentile of its own five years.
    • The gap between the two multiples is near-zero leverage and a capex line at 17% of sales, not mispricing.
  • Third reason: four-fifths of the last twelve months' return was multiple, so the bull case asks us to pay for a re-rating that has partly happened.
  • The bounded-downside claim, tested like for like.
    • The bull case sometimes argues that the fibre network alone covers the enterprise value.
    • The profit line must then be EBITDAaL (1,373.5 EURm in FY2025 on the company's basis; 1,405 EURm on the brokers' basis) and the multiple an enterprise-value multiple.
    • The enterprise value is 7,258 EURm at 19.17 EUR, or about 7,620 EURm at 20.1 EUR (about 7,771 EURm of market capitalisation on 386.6 million shares, plus 450 EURm of debt and 167 EURm of pensions, less 770 EURm of cash).
    • At the lowest enterprise-value multiple in the evidence, Morgan Stanley's bear case of 4.5x, the whole group's EBITDA covers about 6,320 EURm, 83% of the enterprise value
    • at OTE's own 5.0x it covers 92%
    • only at the sector's 6.5x does it cover 120%.
    • The group does not cover itself at a trough multiple, let alone one division, so the claim is not a bound: the shares are covered only at a multiple at or above today's.
  • Where the competing hypothesis would win: if the joint venture fails antitrust clearance or is abandoned, the branch of our multiple blend that sits at 10.3x collapses and the modal multiple moves to about 12.4x
    • that alone does not reach 20% a year, but with mobile service revenue above 4% for two quarters it would.
    • Those are the two things we would watch to change our mind.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the company is improving itself (internal forces B) inside a market that is getting harder at the one layer where its excess return lives (external forces C, competition D)
    • the two do not add up to a sufficiently strong input change.
  • Propagation: the internal change has reached the financial statements and the estimates
    • the external competitive change is a signed heads of terms and a network being built, at the rivals' own choices and actions, and has not yet reached OTE's statements.
  • Direction of the two: the internal force adds a few points of margin a year
    • the external force, once complete in H1 2027, contests the wholesale line that carries the margin.
  • Not B, because the strongest external force is adverse and dated (H1 2027), and the strongest internal forces are two years old.
    • Not D, because the internal change is real, executed and low-risk on the screen's red flags.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening at the wholesale layer, where the company's excess return sits, even though retail intensity has so far been contained.
  • The new force: Vodafone Greece and PPC agreed in June 2026 to merge their fibre assets into a 50:50 wholesale joint venture covering more than 1.6 million homes, with about 130 EURm of cash to PPC, targeted to complete in H1 2027
    • it would be the second-largest wholesale FTTH platform in Greece.
  • PPC's network: FiberGrid reached 1.88 million homes passed with 1.1 million ready for service, built at about 160 EUR per home passed, targeting 2.7 million homes by end-2026 and 3 million in the long term
    • OTE's own estimate puts PPC's aerial last-mile cost up to 20% below OTE's duct-based cost.
  • Retail so far: PPC's broadband-only offers at 17.9 EUR a month for 500 Mbps and 19.9 EUR for 1 Gbps are 52% to 59% cheaper than the incumbents' comparable offers, yet had fewer than 11k subscribers at end-2025 and about 20k by early 2026.
    • Nova offers 3 Gbps at 27 EUR a month
    • Vodafone is upgrading 290,000 copper and FTTC customers to FTTH at flat bills.
  • Incumbent pricing: the incumbents raised prices with CPI for a second consecutive year in early 2026
    • OTE applied a 2.6% CPI-linked adjustment in December 2025 to about two million postpaid contracts.
  • The rivals' constraint: Vodafone Greece's operating free cash flow fell from 103 EURm in FY22 to 41 EURm in FY24
    • Nova generated -33 EURm of EBITDAaL less capex in FY2024 against OTE Greece's 784 EURm
    • Vodafone Greece lost 14,000 fixed broadband lines over the twelve months to June 2026.
  • Not C, because the joint venture changes the wholesale tenant's incentive: Vodafone and Nova together have 47% of their FTTH subscribers on OTE's infrastructure, and Vodafone would from H1 2027 co-own a rival host.
    • Management said on 29 July 2026 that it had seen no slowdown in Vodafone's migration onto OTE's network, and named future wholesale pressure a long-term risk.
  • This is the force the whole case is priced against, and it has not yet touched a single line of OTE's accounts.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the regulatory changes of 2025 and 2026 move demand and cash in OTE's favour, with one adverse tariff decision inside them.
  • Tailwinds: the 10% special pay-TV tax removed from 1 January 2026 and the 5% broadband tax on connections above 100 Mbps removed
    • anti-piracy fines of 750 EUR rising to 1,500 EUR for repeat offences against an estimated 500k to 900k illegal viewers
    • operators permitted to stop selling FTTC where FTTH exists
    • copper wholesale charges proposed up 4% to 5%
    • spectrum renewals for 900 MHz and 1800 MHz in September and November 2027 estimated at about 78 EURm against 119 EURm paid fifteen years ago
    • the Romanian disposal's Greek tax break of about 125 EURm visible in FY2026.
  • Headwinds: FTTH wholesale rates cut 10% to 13%
    • OTE remains designated with Significant Market Power under ex-ante price controls
    • the 180 EURm voucher programmes were exhausted around February 2026.
  • Wholesale scheme: the European Commission approved OTE's FTTH wholesale volume-discount scheme of up to 28%, which Deutsche Bank reads as reducing overbuild risk.
  • Not A, because the FTTH wholesale cut lands on the line the case most needs, and the SMP designation is a permanent constraint on retail pricing.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because end-market demand is not growing in units but is upgrading in value, a structural migration that is visible for years yet small in revenue terms.
  • Unit base: retail broadband subscribers flat at 2,386,158 (-0.1% year on year) and retail fixed lines down 1.1% to 2,571,438 at Q2 2026.
  • Fibre migration: FTTH subscribers reached a record 687k with 62k net additions in Q2 2026 and 120k in H1 2026
    • FTTH penetration of the broadband base rose to nearly 29% from 20% a year earlier
    • utilisation of the FTTH network rose to 42% from 39% in Q1 2026.
  • Mobile migration: postpaid net additions of 62k in Q2 2026, the highest in over 17 years
    • the postpaid base +8.0% to 3,147,844 and prepaid -6.1% to 3,952,955
    • prepaid is still 56% of lines
    • data usage +21% to 20.2 GB per user a month.
  • Value of the migration: FTTH yields roughly 10% higher revenue per broadband subscriber than copper
    • prepaid-to-postpaid yields a 3 to 4 EUR monthly ARPU uplift on Deutsche Bank's estimate or about 5 EUR on the company's.
  • Pay-TV: subscribers +7.9% to 793,212 and pay-TV revenue +9.2% in Q2 2026.
  • Not B, because the migration has not translated into service-revenue growth above 2% to 3%: mobile service revenue +2.3% and retail fixed service revenue +0.3% in Q2 2026.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the company's own cycle turned up in 2025 and has already started to flatten in 2026.
  • The turn: Greek fixed retail service revenue returned to growth in FY2025 after four years of contraction
    • FY2025 Greek revenue +3.9%.
  • The flattening: quarterly revenue growth of +8.7% in Q4 2025, +4.9% in Q1 2026 and +0.1% in Q2 2026
    • the Q2 figure carries a 60 EURm fall in international transit, and adjusted for that rundown group revenue grew about 8%.
  • Service lines: mobile service revenue growth slowed from 5.5% in Q1 2026 to 2.3% in Q2
    • retail fixed service revenue from 1.1% to 0.3%.
  • Transit: the phase-out removes about 170 EURm of revenue in FY2026 and 130 EURm in FY2027 at near-zero margin, so headline revenue is flat through FY2027 by construction.
  • Not B, because the deceleration in the two service lines that matter came in the same quarter, and the sell-side called the mobile slowdown the most significant deviation from expectations.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the Greek macro acts on this stock more than on the universe, and in the right direction.
  • Growth: Greek real GDP is forecast at 2.0% to 2.1% in 2026 and 2027 against the Euro Area's 0.3% and 1.0%
    • a large minimum wage rise came in April 2026

Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the two disruption forces in view are being contained rather than disproved: satellite is met by fixed wireless, and aerial fibre overbuild has not yet found retail customers.
  • Satellite: Greece had the second-highest rate of Starlink usage in the EU in Q1 2026, but at about 40 EUR a month plus 349 EUR of equipment against fibre at up to 3 Gbps
    • OTE's 5G fixed-wireless base rose 166.1% to 118,894 lines at Q2 2026, and OTE is using rural FTTH to win back the 150,000 to 160,000 lines the market lost to satellite and fixed wireless.
  • Aerial fibre: the structural disruptor is a utility with poles, a 20% lower build cost and a national overbuild ratio of 1.7x if it reaches 3 million homes, against 0.8x historically
    • Deutsche Bank notes aerial plant carries higher maintenance and weather exposure.
  • Not D, because the disruptor's retail utilisation is 0.6% and its wholesale pricing has been called unacceptable by United Group
    • the disruption is a threat to the wholesale model from 2027, not a present loss.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because input costs are hedged or shared, and the one concentrated supply market, radio access equipment, is buffered by the parent.
  • Energy: about 80% of FY2026 and about 66% of FY2027 consumption is hedged through virtual power purchase agreements running to 2034 and 2036
    • a 50% energy-cost spike would cost about 0.6% of FY2026 EBITDAaL

  • Equipment: procurement runs through Deutsche Telekom's framework and an intra-group equipment-sharing platform
    • vendor cost inflation at Ericsson and Nokia (memory prices up 7.5x since early 2025; optical lead times above 52 weeks) is a risk to rollout timing rather than to OTE's cost base.
  • Not A, because the force is protective rather than improving: it removes a variable cost risk, it does not add growth.

  • Hellenic Telecom is a self-help and cash-return case in a dominant, under-levered incumbent, dressed by the market as a country re-rating.
  • A new chief executive (July 2024) sold the loss-making Romanian unit, exited about 300 EURm of zero-margin transit revenue, cut indirect costs from 31% to 28% of service revenue, took the fibre footprint to 2.2 million homes passed and 687k subscribers, and codified a 100% payout of free cash flow
    • the outputs are an adjusted EBITDAaL margin of 40.1% in Q2 2026, EBITDAaL growth of about 3%, and a 532 EURm distribution for 2026.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, from the chief executive and his team through choices and actions to the operating metrics (record postpaid and FTTH additions), the financial statements (margin, free cash flow) and the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 EPS +5% over twelve months).
  • What it has not done is move the revenue line, which is flat by design and by a mobile deceleration to 2.3% in Q2 2026, or the earnings trajectory beyond about 4% a year once the FY2026 tax step is passed.
  • The one strong new external force, the Vodafone-PPC wholesale joint venture targeted for H1 2027, points against the case at the layer that carries OTE's excess return.
  • At 20.1 EUR (2026-09-25) the shares are on 11.3x consensus earnings two years forward, the 70th percentile of their five-year range, after a 26.5% twelve-month rise that was four-fifths multiple.
  • The modal return is about 8% a year over eighteen months and 10% over three years, and it depends on the multiple holding at about 11.6x and on the depreciation line behaving as consensus has it.
  • The case fits 'Major Corporate Change' loosely, at C, because the pattern's promised upgrade to the earnings trajectory has not arrived.
  • It is not compelling at this price
    • it would be at about 17 EUR.
Setup verdict: The sorting-box verdict: primary 'Target Setup', any secondary, and the mechanism tying Thesis and Foundations to the pattern (Part 2, E 3, BB 4)
  • Primary 'Target Setup': 'Major Corporate Change', fit C.
  • Secondary: 'Compounder', fit C, a live alternative reading because the framework's own guidance places a correctly-modelled high-quality business at C.
  • The mechanism: a chief-executive change in July 2024 drove a set of multi-dimensional internal decisions (the Romanian exit, the transit exit, the cost programme, the 100% payout, the fibre land-grab) whose outputs are now in the statements and the estimates
    • the 'Foundations' tie to the pattern in the right way, a dominant incumbent with the balance sheet to execute, and the 'Investment Thesis' ties to it weakly, because the change is worth about 4% a year of EPS growth and the change is at full maturity, so the market has had two years to price it.
  • None of the four cause-for-concern or demand patterns is present: the stock is near its high, estimates are rising, guidance is intact, and demand is flat in units.

  • 'Investment Thesis', D: the change is real and not material.

  • Strong internal forces act on a hardening market, the outputs improve at the margin line only, and the modal return is about 8% to 10% a year

    • materiality is measured at the return, so it is not a C.

  • 'Foundations', B: the business, the industry and the market's confirmation are robust, and every Element is at B.

  • Not A, because the one fragility, the joint venture, sits on the excess-return line, and the governance is a parent's.

  • 'Investment Setup', C: the best fit, 'Major Corporate Change', is a genuine but mature and modest instance of the pattern.

  • Not B, because the pattern's output, a major upgrade to the earnings trajectory, is absent.

  • Not C, because the framework asks for compelling.
Congruence: The congruence judgement: does the whole hang together across past, business, value chain and market environment; where ratings disagree, why (Part 4, E 3, BB 4)
  • The whole hangs together, and it hangs together as a small case.
  • With the past: the expected change is exactly what the last two years' delivery makes plausible, EBITDAaL up 2% to 3%, margin up about a point a year, free cash flow rising slowly, and nothing in the past makes a larger change plausible.
  • With the business: the thesis relies on the network, the scale, the bundles, the cost discipline and the balance sheet, all of which the business demonstrably has, and on none of the things it lacks, real pricing power and a growing market.
  • With the value chain: half with and half against
    • the migration forces (regulatory stop-sell, fibre, postpaid) sit with the thesis, and the competitive force, a second wholesale host from H1 2027 co-owned by OTE's largest tenant, sits against the leg of the thesis that carries the excess return.
  • With the market environment: the market is rewarding this kind of case and this country, and here the ratings disagree.
  • Technical momentum is A and valuation is D on the same day, because the shares have re-rated on Greek reclassification flows and sector appetite before the earnings have moved
    • the disagreement is explained, and the explanation is the finding, because it says the re-rating leg of the return has been spent.
  • The 'Foundations' at B and the 'Investment Thesis' at D disagree for the same reason at a different level: robustness is not materiality, and a business this good can carry a thesis this small without either rating being wrong.
  • The one place the congruence could break is below EBITDA: if depreciation runs 4% a year rather than 2%, the case that hangs together at a D hangs together at an F, and the recommendation does not change.

Value Chain and Industry Structure
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Vodafone-PPC wholesale joint venture
    • Vodafone Greece and the electricity utility PPC have agreed to merge their fibre assets into a wholesale venture that OTE's largest wholesale tenant would co-own.
    • It matters because OTE's excess return sits on the wholesale line the venture would contest, so it threatens both the case and the share price.
    • Completion in the first half of 2027 is probable, a material loss of wholesale volume within three years given completion is plausible, the damage if it comes is slow, and the trend is worsening as the venture moves from heads of terms to a term sheet.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Concentrated, rational three-player market
    • Three operators hold virtually all Greek subscribers, the leader holds most of the profit pool, and the incumbents have raised prices with inflation for two consecutive years.
    • It matters because it is what makes the margin path and the payout dependable.
Business
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Network, scale and balance-sheet advantages
    • The fastest network, physical fibre lock-in, bundles and near-zero leverage make the retail position stronger with each migrated line.
    • It matters because these are the only strengths the thesis relies on, and the business demonstrably has them.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Parent-shaped governance
    • A controlling parent, a board at the legal minimum of independence, a combined Chairman and chief executive and large related-party purchases put the controlling shareholder first in structure.
    • It is a share-price risk more than a case risk, because minorities have been paid in cash so far.
Leadership and management
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Chief-executive-led delivery
    • The chief executive who arrived in July 2024 acts rather than talks, and the company has never missed a numerical EBITDAaL target.
    • It matters because the whole self-help leg of the case rests on decisions being taken and numbers being met.
External
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Regulatory support for the fibre migration
    • The regulator permits stop-sell of copper products where fibre exists, has proposed higher copper wholesale charges and has approved OTE's wholesale volume-discount scheme.
    • It matters because it moves demand toward OTE's fibre and slows the rivals' alternatives.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: RRF wind-down
    • Public-sector digital projects funded by the recovery programme end in December 2026 and have driven the fast-growing System Solutions line.
    • It matters as a probable revenue cut in 2027 that is small to earnings because the line runs at a low margin; it is a share-price risk more than a case risk, and its probability is high.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: FTTH wholesale tariff cut
    • The regulator has cut the regulated FTTH wholesale rate.
    • It lands on the line the case most needs and is a modest, already-known headwind.
Internal
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Cost programme
    • Indirect costs have fallen as a share of service revenue through voluntary exits, digital channels and the coming copper decommissioning, and the transit exit lifts the margin arithmetically.
    • It decides whether the case is right, because it is the only leg growing EBITDA faster than revenue.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: 100% free-cash-flow payout policy
    • The company distributes the whole of the prior year's free cash flow through a dividend and a buyback.
    • It matters because it is the largest and most reliable leg of the return, and because it leaves no buffer if free cash flow slips.
Key operating and financial metrics
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Fibre migration and utilisation
    • Customers are moving from copper to fibre at a record pace while the broadband base stays flat, and each migrated line yields more revenue and raises the switching barrier.
    • It decides whether the case is right and whether the shares re-rate, because it drives retail fixed service revenue and the high-margin wholesale line, and it is the line the joint venture attacks.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: Prepaid-to-postpaid migration and CPI pricing
    • Postpaid additions are at a seventeen-year high, more than half of mobile lines are still prepaid, and the incumbents have passed inflation through to prices.
    • It decides whether the case is right, because it is the only source of growth in mobile service revenue.
  • ‘Case/SP Key Risk’: Mobile deceleration persisting
    • Mobile service revenue growth halved in the second quarter of 2026 as the effect of the December 2025 price adjustment faded.
    • It is the inverse of the postpaid driver, it threatens both the case and the share price, its probability is moderate, its magnitude is modest, and its trend is the thing the next two quarters will settle.
Key financial statement items
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Adjusted EBITDAaL margin
    • The margin is rising about a point a year from the cost programme and the transit arithmetic.
    • It matters because it is the one financial output that is clearly improving.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Below-EBITDA drop-through
    • Depreciation is rising as fibre assets and spectrum are amortised, refinancing costs are rising, and the tax rate normalises after the 2026 break.
    • It matters because about a third of the modal return depends on depreciation behaving as consensus has it, and it is a case risk whose probability is moderate and whose trend is adverse.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Free cash flow after leases
    • Underlying free cash flow is guided to grow modestly and is fully paid out the following year.
    • It matters because the payout is written on it.
Sell-side estimates
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Consensus earnings path
    • Consensus already carries the margin path, the buyback and the tax step, and has been flat since the July 2026 results.
    • It matters because the sell-side has little left to upgrade, so this driver is exhausted rather than adverse.
Cash return, earnings growth, re-rating
  • ‘Case/SP Key Driver’: The multiple
    • The valuation multiple has been moved by Greek reclassification flows and sector appetite rather than by earnings.
    • It decides whether the shares re-rate and not whether the case is right, and it is the whole of the difference between a D and a B return.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Cash return
    • The dividend yield and the buyback together return about a twentieth of the market value a year.
    • It carries the modal return.
  • ‘Case/SP Driver’: Earnings growth at the run-rate
    • Earnings per share grow modestly from margin and the share count on a flat revenue line.
    • It adds a modest, predictable leg to the return.
  • ‘Case/SP Risk’: Mark-to-market downside to a trough multiple
    • The multiple sits in the upper part of its own five-year range after a re-rating driven by flows.
    • It is the inverse of the multiple driver, a share-price risk rather than a case risk, and the framework's caution that multiples fall further than expected applies here.
