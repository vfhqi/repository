Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. OTE's 2027 spectrum cost can be estimated, but only as a range and not yet as a fixed number. The Key Question asks what Greece's 2027 mobile spectrum renewal will cost OTE, when it will be paid, whether a new entrant such as PPC could take part, and what this means for OTE's shareholder distributions in 2027 and 2028. The evidence suggests the cost is modest relative to OTE's cash generation. It also suggests the payment timing and the final regulatory decree matter more than the headline figure. Confidence in the direction of travel is high. Confidence in the exact euro amount stays moderate until EETT finalizes its terms. The first body of evidence concerns procedure and cost. Deutsche Bank Research highlights that EETT's draft terms permit direct renewal of existing allocations rather than a multi-round auction, with OTE's total liabilities estimated at about EUR 78 mm. Deutsche Bank's wider estimate runs from EUR 78 mm to EUR 120 mm, which it regards as manageable for the incumbent. The licences concerned are the 900 MHz band and select 1800 MHz blocks expiring in mid-2027. The draft consultation proposes reserve prices of EUR 23.3 mm for 900 MHz and EUR 15.5 mm for the 1800 MHz block, but the final decree remains pending. The terms apply equally to OTE, Vodafone Greece and Nova, so OTE is not singled out. European precedents point the same way. TIM and Iliad paid roughly EUR 0.13 and EUR 0.17 per MHz per head of population for 2020 extensions in Italy. Germany's fiveyear extension without an auction still required a small EUR 5 mm compensation payment to newcomer 1&1. Direct renewals therefore tend to be cheap, but not free of surprises.

The second body of evidence concerns timing and cash management. Deutsche Telekom's interim report confirms the re-award is scheduled for mid-2027, while exact details and upfront payment schedules are not yet finalized. Management has not confirmed whether EETT will allow deferred annual installments or demand upfront payment. OTE has also pre-positioned its balance sheet. Underlying free cash flow is EUR 570 mm to EUR 580 mm once the Romanian tax windfall is stripped out, and management is keeping those tax proceeds on the balance sheet to offset the 2027 spectrum costs. OTE's adjusted free cash flow definition excludes spectrum payments. The stated policy is to distribute 70% to 100% of net free cash flow. The 2026 payout of EUR 532 mm distributes essentially all free cash flow.

What consensus and brokers assume is broadly supportive, with one conservative modeling choice. Deutsche Bank models a dividend-to-buyback mix settling at roughly two-thirds to one-third. Morgan Stanley forecasts total shareholder returns yielding about ten percent by fiscal 2028 and rising to 98% of free cash flow by 2030. The main counterpoint is that Deutsche Bank's valuation model includes a perpetual spectrum cost of EUR 20 mm per year starting in 2026.

That implies regulatory outlays could be a more persistent drag than management's oneoff tax offset suggests. 7 On new entrants, the PPC threat is real in fixed broadband but weak in mobile spectrum. PPC's chief executive stated on May 12, 2026 that the utility is not interested in mobile retail services. PPC took part in the consultation only to explore fixed wireless access for hard-to-reach areas, as a complement to its fiber network.

It has not formally filed bidding intentions for the 2027 award. The competitive risk therefore sits in fiber. A preliminary Vodafone Greece and PPC wholesale fiber venture could bypass OTE's wholesale network. An expert call also indicates that corporate customers are moving broadband to PPC while keeping OTE for mobile.

However, PPC had signed up fewer than eleven thousand retail broadband customers by the end of December 2025. Deutsche Telekom's role is central but, on current evidence, not a source of near-term pressure. The parent uses its listed subsidiaries as essential cash contributors and monitors OTE's capital allocation to ensure a consistent flow-through of dividends. Its leverage of 2.17x sits within its target range, and its ex-US free cash flow of EUR 4.1 bn in the first nine months of 2026 already exceeds its full-year target.

The parent has pre-indicated a EUR 2 bn buyback for 2027. This reduces any urgent need to extract more from OTE. Its low leverage reduces the need for aggressive cash extraction. Hrvatski Telekom's zero buybacks in the first nine months of 2026 show that the parent tolerates temporary subsidiary cash preservation when local needs take priority.

That precedent matters if OTE needed to prioritize spectrum. Two risks cut the other way. The parent's buyback is being held below its ceiling because of US spectrum auctions scheduled for 2027. If those costs escalate, the group may prioritize group-level liquidity over generous subsidiary buybacks at OTE.

The main limitations are that final EETT reserve prices, the payment schedule, PPC's actual participation and the Deutsche Telekom board's approval of OTE's 2027 and 2028 distribution budgets are all unresolved. The board has yet to approve those budgets, and the gap is expected to close when the parent publishes its annual capital allocation guidelines. The most important catalysts are the final EETT decree, OTE's quarterly capital expenditure schedules and any formal filing on the Vodafone–PPC venture.

Taken together, the evidence suggests the spectrum bill is unlikely to be the decisive swing factor for 2027–2028 distributions. The larger sensitivity is the fixed-line competitive trajectory and the parent's liquidity needs, which later sections examine in more depth.

  1. Greek regulators are shifting toward direct renewals for expiring mobile licences in H2 2027.
  2. OTE faces a highly manageable expected spectrum renewal cost of approximately seventy-eight million euros under the proposed framework.

This comprehensive data set helps clarify the expected cost range and financial timing for Hellenic Telecommunications Organization during the upcoming license renewal cycle.
2.
Positive development: The Greek telecommunications regulator EETT published a consultation in early February 2026 regarding the cost and process to renew expiring mobile spectrum. The licensing authority is focusing on the 900 MHz band and select 1800 MHz blocks that expire in mid-2027. This proposal represents a favorable regulatory shift that provides OTE with substantial operational and financial visibility.
3.
Favorable pricing: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that the Greek regulator's draft terms permit the direct renewal of existing allocations rather than forcing a multi-round competitive auction. Under this direct renewal procedure, OTE's total spectrum liabilities are estimated to reach approximately seventy-eight million euros. This represents a significant thirty-five percent cost reduction compared to the company's spectrum outlay fifteen years ago.
4.
Delayed timeline: Deutsche Telekom's third quarter interim report published in November 2026 confirms that the formal Greek spectrum re-award procedure is scheduled for mid-2027. The regulator has not yet finalized the exact auction details or the precise upfront payment schedules. This regulatory delay helps OTE because it preserves short-term liquidity while lowering immediate cash distribution risks.

Section 3 – Potential New Entrants: The PPC Threat and Precedents

Section summary
  1. Greece's leading utility company Public Power Corporation represents an active competitor in fiber broadband but presents no direct threat in the retail mobile spectrum arena.
  2. Management statements and regulatory submissions confirm that PPC's interest in spectrum is confined to complementary fixed wireless broadband rather than a fourth national mobile network.

PPC Mobile Strategy and Consultations

1.
Explicit denial: Public Power Corporation has officially ruled out any plans to build a fourth retail mobile network in the Greek market. During the company's first quarter financial call on May 12, 2026, the chief executive officer stated that the utility is not interested in mobile retail services. This strategic decision limits competitive pressure on OTE's mobile operations because it prevents a highly disruptive retail price war.
2.
Tactical exploration: Public Power Corporation participated in the regulator's spectrum consultation solely to explore fixed wireless access solutions for hard-to-reach areas. The company's official submission published on May 13, 2026, explained that wireless connectivity would act only as a complement to its core aerial fiber network. This specific focus on fixed wireless access suggests that OTE's high-margin mobile service revenue is shielded from direct utility competition.
3.
Unfounded rumors: Greek press reports in mid-May 2026 speculated that Public Power Corporation might acquire mobile spectrum to compete directly against OTE, Vodafone Greece, and Nova. Unnamed media sources linked the utility's interest to its participation in EETT's 900 MHz and 1800 MHz spectrum consultation. However, broker analysis confirms that these market rumors overstated the actual competitive threat.
4.
Moderate threat: Morgan Stanley indicates that Public Power Corporation remains a key long-term risk in the Greek fiber-to-the-home market rather than the mobile space. The utility's rapid infrastructure deployment represents a threat of fiber overbuild that could eventually pressure OTE's broadband market share. Investors must monitor this infrastructure rollout because it represents a viable competitor in fixed broadband.

Fixed Fiber Infrastructure Competition

5.
Aggressive expansion: Public Power Corporation has dynamically scaled its advanced fiber optic network across Greece to challenge the three established telecom operators. The utility's network reached more than 1.4 m households and businesses by the end of September 2025, representing a significant two hundred percent yearon-year increase. This rapid deployment supports the company's objective to become a leading national wholesale provider.
6.
Slow commercialization: Public Power Corporation has struggled to sign up wholesale customers for its newly deployed fiber network despite extensive capital investments. The utility's wholesale pricing terms were publicly rejected by Nova's parent company United Group as unacceptable in late 2024. This commercial failure occurred because OTE, Nova, and Vodafone Greece already agreed to wholesale each other's networks.
7.
Disappointing retail: Public Power Corporation launched a retail broadband service in June 2025 with prices fifty percent below the established operators. Despite offering a fifty percent discount, the utility secured fewer than eleven thousand retail broadband customers by the end of December 2025. This low adoption rate demonstrates that consumers prefer OTE's superior customer service over extremely cheap broadband-only offerings.
8.
Dominant infrastructure: OTE maintains a significant lead over Public Power Corporation in high-quality broadband connections despite the utility's aggressive network overbuild. OTE served 2.36 m broadband customers at the end of September 2025, whereas PPC's retail customer base remained negligible. Furthermore, OTE's fiber-to-the-home footprint is scheduled to reach three million premises by 2027 to secure its broadband leadership.
9.
Severe constraints: Public Power Corporation faces significant capital structure constraints that limit its ability to fund an expensive mobile spectrum acquisition. The utility group's presentation of its strategic plan on November 14, 2024, showed that its net debt to EBITDA ratio is projected to rise from two point seventy times in September 2024 to between three and three point fifty times by 2027. This increasing leverage restricts the company's financial flexibility to compete across multiple telecom segments.

Precedents of Telecom New Entrants

10.
Severe disruption: Precedent cases in Italy demonstrate that a low-cost entrant can severely damage incumbent telecom operator margins if they acquire cheap mobile spectrum. French operator Iliad entered the Italian mobile market in 2018 and aggressively acquired multi-band spectrum extensions to support its national rollout. This aggressive expansion forced incumbents to reduce prices and diminished market-wide operating cash flows.
11.
Favorable environment: Deutsche Bank Research emphasizes that the Greek telecom market does not face aggressive price-disruptive newcomers similar to Iliad in Italy. OTE's competitors Vodafone Greece and Nova operate with highly constrained cash flows and cannot afford to start an irrational price war. This rational competitive structure allows OTE to defend its premium fixed and mobile pricing.
12.
Regulated sharing: Italian telecom regulator AGCOM proposed a spectrum renewal framework in August 2026 that allows operators with limited spectrum to access existing network holdings. Under this proposal, Iliad can access spectrum from larger peers at a regulated cost to support high-speed coverage. This precedent highlights how regulatory rules can improve spectrum access for newcomers.
13.
Growing competition: European telecom markets are experiencing rising competition from 5G fixed wireless access services that challenge traditional fiber networks. Deutsche Bank Research notes that fixed wireless access serves as an effective tool for secondary operators to erode incumbent market share in Italy and the Netherlands. This European trend explains why Public Power Corporation is exploring fixed wireless spectrum options.

Competitive Risks and Partnerships

14.
Contrary evidence: Public Power Corporation's aggressive retail broadband pricing of seventeen point ninety euros per month has successfully disrupted the enterprise and small-medium business markets. An expert call with a former Nova business director on November 20, 2026, revealed that corporate customers are splitting bundles by moving their broadband services to PPC while keeping OTE for mobile. This customer churning behavior indicates that utility pricing is already impacting established operator revenue.
15.
Strategic partnership: Vodafone Greece and Public Power Corporation began preliminary discussions in October 2026 to establish a joint wholesale fiber venture covering more than one point five million homes. This proposed infrastructure collaboration would provide Vodafone with direct access to an alternative fiber network. If finalized, this cooperative venture could bypass OTE's wholesale network and represent a viable threat to OTE's wholesale business.
16.
Other side of the story: While Public Power Corporation has officially denied retail mobile plans, its entry into fixed wireless access could still damage OTE's fixed-mobile convergence strategy. OTE successfully leverages convergence bundles to retain seventy percent of its fixed broadband residential customers on its mobile plans. If PPC successfully bundles its cheap broadband with household energy plans, it could force rivals to respond with aggressive pricing that erodes overall market profitability.

Section 4 – OTE Cash Management, Romanian Exit Proceeds, and Distribution Policy

Section summary
  1. Hellenic Telecommunications Organization maintains a highly generous shareholder remuneration policy designed to distribute seventy to one hundred percent of its net free cash flow.
  2. Management is strategically holding the Romanian exit tax proceeds on its balance sheet to fully offset the upcoming mid-2027 spectrum liabilities without diluting the ordinary distribution pool.

1.
Stable policy: OTE approved a modernized shareholder remuneration policy in late February 2026 to align its annual cash returns directly with actual free cash flow performance. The board of directors decided that the company will distribute between 70% and 100% of its net free cash flow generated in the preceding fiscal year. This structured distribution framework ensures that dividends will represent at least half of the total remuneration.
2.
Generous distributions: OTE announced a substantial total shareholder payout of EUR 532 mm for 2026, which effectively distributes 100% of its annual free cash flow. The company elevated its proposed dividend per share by 22% to EUR 355 mm while expanding the share buyback budget. This balanced payout strategy demonstrates management's commitment to returning capital while maintaining a strong balance sheet.
3.
Consistent returns: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that OTE has historically returned up to 95% of its free cash flow through a combination of ordinary dividends and buybacks. The broker notes that these high returns are secured by OTE's exceptionally clean capital structure and domestic market dominance. This low leverage of 0.3x net debt to EBITDA enables the company to sustain premium cash returns.
4.
Optimistic projections: Morgan Stanley indicates that OTE's shareholder remuneration is best-in-class and fully covered by its highly visible domestic cash generation. The broker expects that steady free cash flow expansion will drive total shareholder returns to 98% of free cash flow by 2030. This strong growth trajectory suggests that the telecom incumbent can easily absorb temporary capital requirements.
5.
Historical stability: OTE has historically managed its capital allocation defensively by keeping capital expenditures stable outside of major regulatory auctions. The company reiterated a capital expenditure guidance of approximately EUR 700 mm in past years to protect its free cash flow. This long-term discipline supports the view that OTE will avoid unplanned cuts to shareholder dividends.

Romanian Disposal and Tax Proceeds

6.
Portfolio optimization: OTE successfully completed the strategic sale of its cash-burning Romanian subsidiary Telekom Romania Mobile in October 2025. This exit allowed the group to streamline its operations and focus entirely on higher-margin domestic telecommunications services. Management immediately returned value to investors by approving an extraordinary dividend of EUR forty million.
7.
Substantial windfalls: OTE anticipated generating a total free cash flow of approximately EUR 750 mm for 2026 assuming the spectrum auction takes place in 2027. This large cash inflow is significantly bolstered by positive one-off tax benefits resulting from the Romanian transaction. This temporary liquidity spike provides OTE with a robust buffer against upcoming spectrum liabilities.
8.
Underlying strength: OTE management stated that the group's underlying free cash flow stands at EUR 570 mm to EUR 580 mm when adjusting for the Romanian tax windfall. By keeping these tax proceeds on the balance sheet, OTE can fully offset the upcoming 2027 spectrum costs. This strategic cash management ensures that the spectrum renewal will not dilute the core distribution pool.
9.
Divestment analysis: Deutsche Bank Research states that the disposal of Telekom Romania Mobile is highly positive because it removes an unprofitable international asset. The Romanian unit had historically generated negative operating free cash flow of EUR 36 mm in 2024, dragging down group profitability. By selling this unit, OTE successfully eliminated a chronic drain on free cash flow.
10.
Proceeds distribution: Deutsche Bank Research expected that the net consideration from the Romanian sale would be distributed directly to OTE's shareholders. The transaction completed in late 2025 and led to an extraordinary dividend payout of EUR 0.10 per share in December 2025. This distribution demonstrates that management prioritizes returning all surplus divestment cash.
11.
Taxation headwinds: Deutsche Bank Research reports that Telekom Romania Mobile was hit with an unexpected EUR 33.5 mm extra tax assessment in late 2024. This adverse tax audit required immediate cash payments to avoid high interest penalties, which temporarily constrained the Romanian unit's valuation. Such unpredictable tax events show that foreign assets can impose sudden cash drains.

Spectrum Cost Management and Precedents

12.
Defined metrics: OTE calculates its key performance measures by excluding voluntary exit schemes, restructuring costs, and spectrum payments from its adjusted free cash flow after lease. This regulatory reporting method ensures that large spectrum license costs do not impact the calculation of baseline free cash flow. Consequently, the standard shareholder distribution remains insulated from regulatory spectrum payments.
13.
Contrary evidence: OTE's historical filings show that the company had to revise its free cash flow guidance down to EUR 440 mm during previous cash-constrained periods due to rising capital expenditure demands. This past guidance adjustment demonstrates that despite excluding spectrum costs from adjusted metrics, high capital intensity can periodically compress overall shareholder distributions.
14.
Capital allocation: Morgan Stanley notes that OTE's capital expenditures will remain elevated at approximately EUR 600 mm per annum over the next few years due to fiber optic network expansion. The broker believes that while this network buildout keeps leverage low, it restricts the company's ability to increase cash distributions further.
15.
Prudent budgeting: OTE's past spectrum transactions show that the group has maintained a highly conservative balance sheet to absorb regulatory outlays without cutting ordinary dividends. The company utilizes its strong domestic operating cash flow of over EUR 1.1 bn to fund ongoing network investments. This consistent operational performance gives management the flexibility to handle upcoming spectrum costs.
16.
Other side of the story: Although OTE's policy of holding Romanian exit tax cash to offset spectrum payments protects short-term distributions, any regulatory delay or increase in the spectrum reserve prices could stress cash reserves. If the Greek regulator EETT demands a large upfront payment rather than deferred annual installments, OTE's net debt will rise. This balance sheet pressure could force OTE's parent company Deutsche Telekom to reduce share buyback distributions.
