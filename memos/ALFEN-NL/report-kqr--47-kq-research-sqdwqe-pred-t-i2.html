Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Alfen's financial trajectory depends on aligning internal cost and cash dynamics with broader European electrification and regulatory realities. The standard market focus on short-term volumes misses key balance sheet and cost structure inflections.

Labor Scarcity

Modeling Disconnects on Depreciation

This figure declined slightly to 22.20 million euros in fiscal year 2025 after a prior capitalized asset impairment. For the first half of fiscal year 2026, the company reported 10.20 million euros in total depreciation and amortization expenses. These historical metrics represent a stable cost baseline that directly impacts below-EBITDA earnings per share.
2.
Uncharted depreciation: Broker research from Deutsche Bank ignores below-EBITDA depreciation mapping and focuses almost entirely on short-term EBITDA margins. This analytical focus suggests that the sell-side primarily tracks operational cash flow rather than net income trends. Consequently, Alfen's actual net profit trajectory remains underrepresented in standard consensus forecasts. This lack of detailed modeling could lead to earnings surprises when actual depreciation figures are reported.
3.
Omitted details: Broker research from Jefferies does not outline specific depreciation and amortization assumptions in its published valuation models. Jefferies focuses instead on gross profit margins and the shifting product mix toward lower-margin Smart Grid Solutions. This modeling omission means that the structural impact of capitalized development cost amortization is largely unexamined. Unmodeled amortization could depress future earnings per share even as EBITDA starts to recover.
4.
Absent benchmarks: Research did not surface comparable depreciation and amortization metrics for specific grid peers like Arteche or Pfisterer. However, Alfen's capital expenditure remains capped below 4% of revenue as part of its tight cost-control strategy. This capital expenditure discipline is intended to prevent excessive asset base expansion and subsequent depreciation headwind. Achieving this expenditure target is vital to securing long-term below- EBITDA profitability and EPS growth.

Personnel Overlap and Reorganization Costs

Direct labor cost reductions accounted for 7.50 million euros of these gross savings, which immediately supported gross margin performance. This restructuring helped the company transition from a severe EBITDA loss in 2024 to profitability.
6.
Elevated expenses: Alfen experienced a temporary rise in personnel costs during fiscal year 2026 due to overlapping organizational structures. During this transitional phase, the legacy and newly implemented business unit organizations had to operate side-by-side. Consequently, third-quarter personnel expenses increased by 10% to 19.60 million euros compared to the previous year. This planned inefficiency is expected to phase out entirely by fiscal year 2027 as roles and structural transitions conclude.
7.
Slashed projections: Jefferies analyst David Kerstens cut Alfen's fiscal year 2026 EBITDA estimate by 32% to 18.80 million euros. This drastic reduction was driven by 8 million euros in higher-than-expected overlapping personnel expenses. Jefferies projects that these temporary transition costs will gradually normalize throughout fiscal year 2027. This normalization of operational expenses remains essential for restoring investor confidence and driving EPS recovery.

Labor Scarcity and Wage Inflation

10.

This escalating wage baseline will keep pressure on mid-term earnings per share unless volume growth accelerates.

This lag in pass-through execution demonstrates that unplanned wage inflation can compress margins for several quarters. Working Capital and Storage Down-Payments

1.

Alfen disclosed in its 2025 annual report that a significant slowdown in storage order intake will negatively impact operational cash flows. The company manages this exposure by matching payment schedules in sales contracts directly with procurement contracts.
2.
Reversing balances: Alfen reported a significant decline in Energy Storage contract liabilities during the first half of fiscal year 2026. Alfen reported that amounts due to customers for contract work decreased from 47.2 million euros in December 2025 to 32.6 million euros by June 2026. This cash outflow of 14.6 million euros reflects project completions and highlights the potential drag on 2027 cash flows.
3.
Detailed modeling: Jefferies analyst David Kerstens explicitly models a major cash flow drag from working capital shifts in the mid-term. Jefferies projects that Alfen will experience a negative 3.9 million euros change in working capital in fiscal year 2027. This cash drain is expected to reduce free cash flow to just 1.1 million euros in 2027, compared to 3.2 million euros in 2026.
4.
Superficial analysis: Deutsche Bank ignores specific down-payment dynamics in its valuation model and focuses only on high-level cash figures. Deutsche Bank reported that working capital benefits would help keep cash positive in fiscal year 2026. However, the broker did not model the detailed cash impact of storage downpayment reversals on fiscal year 2027 cash flows.
5.
Declining down-payments: Alfen's strategic inventory down-payments have also been steadily decreasing, which reduces cash locked up in supply chains but signals slower purchasing. Alfen reported that inventory downpayments fell from 17.4 million euros in June 2025 to 13.5 million euros in December 2025, and further to 10.3 million euros by June 2026. While this trend supports near-term cash, it reflects lower front-loaded commitments.

Wars

Chinese Policy and Sourcing Impacts

1.
Structural shifts: China announced the complete removal of value-added tax export rebates for lithium-ion battery products effective January 1, 2027. This policy follows a prior reduction from 9% to 6% implemented in April 2026. These changes are expected to alter the cost structure of Chinese manufacturers supplying European markets. Consequently, Western integrators face direct equipment procurement pricing adjustments as Chinese suppliers pass the tax burden downstream.
2.
Rapid deflation: Chinese lithium carbonate prices declined by 28% to 29% in October 2026, extending a 15% decline from September. The sharp reset was driven by methodology changes that doubled reported stockpiles and the return of previously idled mining supply. Global lithium prices have historically trailed these Chinese spot movements, which currently hover at 13 to 14 dollars per kilogram. This price reduction provides a benign backdrop for battery contract negotiations heading into fiscal year 2027.
3.
Eroding margins: Contemporary Amperex Technology Company reported a sequential gross margin contraction to 20.70% in the third quarter of 2026. This profitability squeeze was heavily driven by reduced export rebates, consumption tax implementation, and unfavorable product mix. Despite these headwinds, the battery giant maintained the highest profit per unit compared to second-tier Chinese peers. This cost pressure is expected to accelerate battery industry consolidation globally as smaller manufacturers face severe capital constraints.
4.
Contractual buffers: Alfen manages its primary battery sourcing exposure through a long-term supply partnership established with Contemporary Amperex Technology. Under these supply agreements, Alfen fixes battery cell costs directly with the supplier once individual project specifications are finalized. This pass-through mechanism successfully mitigates short-term raw material price volatility for the integrator. Consequently, fluctuations in lithium spot prices do not directly drive Alfen's margins but instead shape the upfront economics of customer project bids.
5.
Strategic diversification: Alfen expanded its partnership with Contemporary Amperex Technology in July 2026 to deploy 5 GWh of advanced sodium-ion battery systems. This collaboration introduces a lower-cost alternative to traditional lithium setups and helps Alfen buffer against volatile metal pricing. The Tianheng sodium-ion systems are scheduled for initial commercial deployment in Europe during the second half of 2027. This technology shift represents Alfen's proactive effort to optimize its energy storage product cost structure.

EV Charger Launch and Peer Rivalry

6.
Intensifying competition: Alfen's EV Charging revenues declined 17.40% to 51.10 million euros in the first half of 2026. This contraction was driven by a 12.30% drop in charge points delivered to 53,700 units and competitive pricing pressures in the residential segment. In contrast, European peer Zaptec achieved a 26% revenue increase to 506 million Norwegian kroner during the same period. Alfen is refocusing on simplifying its product portfolio to cut costs and complexity.
7.
Accelerating launches: Alfen plans to launch a range of renewed EV Charging products, including the Eve Single Plus and Eve Double Plus home chargers. The new home products feature an improved design that reduces physical installation times to just 10 minutes. This commercial refresh is designed to regain market share in a fragmented segment containing over 200 active players. Management expects these innovative features and streamlined configurations to drive a return to growth starting in fiscal year 2027.
8.
Subdued volumes: Alfen delivered 25,850 charge points in the third quarter of 2026, representing a 10.60% decrease year-on-year. However, average revenue per delivered station rose by 4% due to a favorable product mix shift toward business-oriented chargers. The division's gross margin landed at 40.90%, compared to 43.10% in the third quarter of 2025. Despite ongoing promotional campaigns, this margin remained above the midpoint of Alfen's guided 35% to 45% range.
9.
Destructive competition: European battery storage provider Polarium Energy Solutions was declared bankrupt in November 2026. Sharply falling battery prices driven by aggressive competition from Chinese suppliers and development delays severely weakened Polarium's liquidity. This failure illustrates the high execution risk for mid-tier players navigating a structural oversupply in global clean-tech markets. It underlines why Alfen's strategic pivot toward technology diversification, such as sodium-ion, remains vital for long-term survival.

Sell-Side Coverage and Cost Debates

10.
Optimistic projections: Jefferies analyst David Kerstens highlights that Alfen's renewed EV Charging portfolio will streamline products to restore competitiveness in 2027. Kerstens models a gradual EBITDA margin recovery to 5.70% in 2027, eventually reaching 9.10% by 2030. This recovery remains highly dependent on Alfen successfully expanding its geographic reach into Spain, Portugal, and Italy. However, continued competitive pressures in the residential segment could accelerate margin erosion if price cuts persist.

Papaccio notes that the introduction of the Plus chargers should help Alfen print smaller year-on-year revenue declines.
