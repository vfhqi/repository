[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. ASM International is a demand inflection in the best business of its cohort, caught after the market has recognised it. The change is real and mostly delivered, and the business is as good as the sell-side says; at EUR 883.60 the shares ask us to bet that the market keeps paying a mid-cycle multiple through the cycle's turn. We decline that bet now and would take it about a quarter lower.

  • The change is external, and it is the strongest force in the comparison set.
    • The move from FinFET to gate-all-around transistors raises the deposition content of every leading-edge wafer by a mechanical amount, independent of unit growth, and ASM is the sole or lead qualified supplier of the tools that deposit it.
    • At 2 nanometres the served market grows by about USD 400 million per 100,000 monthly wafer starts
      • The 1.4 nanometre node adds a further USD 450 to 500 million, with pilot lines taking tools from the second half of 2026
      • The 4F2 DRAM transition adds USD 400 to 450 million from 2028.
    • The company puts its single-wafer ALD share above 55 percent
      • Third parties read 60 to 65 percent
      • At the leading edge of logic the share is 70 to 75 percent.
      • Its silicon epitaxy share has doubled to about 25 to 30 percent through the gate-all-around transition.
    • The capacity leg is cyclical and visible: TSMC's capital spending is guided at USD 60 to 64 billion, Tokyo Electron forecasts wafer fab equipment of at least USD 150 billion in 2026 and USD 190 billion in 2027, and Deutsche Bank sees demand visible to the end of 2028.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • It is in the company's choices and actions, in its key operating metrics, in its financial statements and in the sell-side's estimates, which is as far as a change can travel.
    • Revenue crossed EUR 1 billion in a quarter for the first time in Q2 2026 (EUR 1,003 million, up 20.0 percent on the year)
      • The adjusted operating margin was 33.0 percent against a 28 to 32 percent target range.
    • The company lifted its 2027 revenue outlook above the top of the EUR 3.7 to 4.6 billion range it set in September 2025, guided Q3 2026 at EUR 1,100 million, 5 percent above consensus, and guided the second half more than 20 percent above the first.
    • Consensus FY2027 earnings per share has risen 57 percent in twelve months to EUR 32.62
      • The dashboard's revision-momentum score is 15, the joint highest in the 20-stock sector.
  • The internal change is real and resourced, but it is an enabler, not the cause.
    • A new chief executive since May 2024, targets raised twice in a year, capacity tripled at Dongtan and three new product markets entered explain why ASM captures the external force
    • They do not explain the force.
  • What we think it earns: earnings per share of EUR 15.05 in FY2025 rising to EUR 31.0 in FY2027 and EUR 35.7 in FY2028, about 34 percent a year, a little below consensus (EUR 32.62 and EUR 39.19).
    • We sit below consensus because China's mature-node sales normalise and the cycle's growth rate halves in FY2028 (revenue growth about 13 percent on our path, 16.4 percent on consensus, after about 30 percent in FY2027).
    • The exit rate matters more than the average: the last year of the three, FY2028, grows about 15 percent on our path, and that is the growth the multiple at the horizon is paid for.
  • What it costs is the whole question.
    • EUR 883.60 is 23.7x consensus earnings 24 months forward and about 25.5x on our path, against a ten-year median of 20.5x.
    • That is the 67th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range and the 35th percentile of the last five years.
      • The five-year window is the window of the AI re-rating itself
      • The ten-year window shows where the multiple goes in a de-rating, which is the question this case turns on.
    • The regime is a rates-led late cycle in which the equipment names are priced for the peak while their estimates still rise, and the discount rate is the environment's highest-conviction bearish call.
  • The return does not clear the bar, because the multiple takes away what the earnings add.
    • On our modal multiples of 21x at eighteen months and 20x at three years, the 'Modal return' is about minus 2 percent a year to April 2028 and about plus 1 percent a year to October 2029, against the framework's 20 percent bar.
    • The earnings leg adds about 17 percent over eighteen months and 30 percent over three years on the 24-month-forward earnings the market values
      • The cash return is about 0.4 percent a year
      • The re-rating leg subtracts about 18 and 22 percent.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (gross margin 50 percent, tax 23 percent, no translation gains, a flat share count) takes FY2028 earnings per share to EUR 33.1 and the eighteen-month return to about minus 7 percent a year
      • The rating stays F either way.
    • The branch on which the case clears the bar from today's price needs consensus-or-better earnings through FY2029 and a multiple held at 25x in April 2028
      • Both depend on the AI cycle extending through 2028, so we give the pair about one in four.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Demand Inflection', crisply, in its later stage.
    • The mandatory demand explosion is met at A, the dominant position captures it disproportionately, and the upgrades are two-thirds travelled with the price already risen on them.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the external force through the company's actions, operating metrics, financial statements and the sell-side's estimates
    • It breaks at the price, where a mid-cycle multiple on decelerating growth meets a rising discount rate.
    • The share price has already answered the strongest results in the company's history with a 19 percent fall from its July high, which is the market telling us what it pays for from here.

  • The case therefore is a business and a demand force of A quality at an entry price that is not: a case to hold ready and buy on the de-rating the cycle is likely to bring, at about EUR 650 to 700 or at a 24-month-forward multiple at or below 18.7x, not a case to progress now.

  • Rating = C, because the change is as material as any in the universe in its forces (A) and its outputs (B) and does not deliver a sufficient return from this price (F)

    • The Building Block measures the materiality of the whole chain including the return, and the chain breaks at the third leg.
    • Not B, because a B needs the return to be at least near the bar on the modal path, and the modal path is below zero at eighteen months.
    • Not D, because the first two legs are among the strongest in the comparison set and the return turns on price, which changes.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The bull's case, at its strongest: consensus is still too low and the market keeps paying for sole-source content growth.
      • ASM is the single best-placed company in the wafer fab equipment cohort for the two transitions that matter this decade (gate-all-around logic, then 4F2 and 3D DRAM)
        • Its content per wafer rises with every node independent of unit growth
        • It is sole-source where it counts
        • It has just entered three new markets (metal ALD, DRAM epitaxy, CMP)
        • It is self-funded with EUR 1.1 billion of net cash.
      • Morgan Stanley's FY2027 revenue of EUR 5.5 billion was 15 percent above consensus when set
        • ING models a 22 percent organic revenue compound rate to 2030 against the company's "at least 12 percent"
        • Deutsche Bank says consensus "failed to capture the company's true long-term earnings power".
      • The multiple has already fallen from 35.9x at the five-year peak to 23.7x, the 35th percentile of five years, a discount to US deposition peers at about 25x and a five-turn discount to the front-end peers on Morgan Stanley's count
        • The bull houses' targets of EUR 1,100 to EUR 1,350 are 25 to 53 percent above the price.
      • On consensus earnings and a held multiple the eighteen-month return is about 20 percent a year, a B.
    • What we concede: all of the business argument
      • That FY2026 and FY2027 are more likely to be beaten than missed
      • That the sector's demand read is real
      • And that if the AI cycle extends through 2028 the bull is right and the return is a B.
    • Why we still hold our view: the five-year window the bull anchors on is the window of the re-rating itself, and the ten-year window puts today's multiple above median, not below.
      • The deceleration to mid-teens growth in FY2028 is in the bull's own numbers (Morgan Stanley's wafer fab equipment path slows from plus 37 percent in 2027 to plus 14 percent in 2028), and the market prices decelerations before they arrive
        • Jefferies dates the cycle's peak to Q4 2026 with multiples pre-empting it by three to six months.
      • The discount rate is the environment's highest-conviction call and bears directly on a 24x multiple
        • The equipment names' estimates rising into the peak is what a peak looks like from inside it.
      • The bull's case needs two things at once, consensus-or-better earnings and a held multiple, that share one cause
        • We give the pair one in four.
    • Where the bull would win: hyperscaler and TSMC capital-spending guidance for 2028 still rising at this time next year.
      • That is the one condition that holds the multiple, and it is why the recommendation is a park and not an invalidation.

  • Which force is the case: the external force is the stronger of the two and is the case
    • The internal forces are enablers, not the change.
  • Standard met: the force is evidenced by customer capital spending (TSMC's capital spending raised to USD 60 to 64 billion), by industry data (wafer fab equipment forecast at least USD 150 billion for 2026 and USD 190 billion for 2027 by Tokyo Electron) and by the company's own order and revenue prints, which is the 'Demand Inflection' standard met in full.
    • Not B, for that reason.

    • It outweighs the three headwinds (China mature-node normalisation and localisation, export-control tightening, supply-chain bottlenecks), each of which is real but bounded.

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand is changing in both ways at once: structurally, as transistor-level deposition layers rise from about 50 percent of ALD layers at 2 nanometres to about 60 percent at 1.4 nanometres and the served market grows by about USD 400 million per 100,000 monthly wafer starts at 2 nanometres and a further USD 450 to 500 million at 1.4 nanometres
    • And cyclically, as leading-edge logic and high-bandwidth-memory capacity is built for AI with visibility into 2028.
    • Tailwind.
  • Quarterly prints: Q2 2026 revenue was EUR 1,003 million, up 20.0 percent on Q2 2025 and above the EUR 996 million consensus
    • Q3 2026 is guided at EUR 1,100 million, 5 percent above consensus
    • The second half is guided more than 20 percent above the first, which Morgan Stanley reads as about EUR 1,195 million in Q4.
  • 2027 outlook: revenue is now expected to exceed the upper end of the EUR 3.7 to 4.6 billion range set at the September 2025 Capital Markets Day
    • Consensus FY2027 revenue is EUR 5,513 million, up 32.3 percent on FY2026, on 14 brokers.
  • Industry read: the sector research finds that gate-all-around at 2 nanometres raises vacuum, deposition and metrology steps per wafer by about 25 to 30 percent
    • The mechanism matches the company's own USD 400 million figure, so it supports the reading without adding to it.
  • Bifurcation: the demand is split.
    • Silicon-based power and analog equipment has been in a cyclical correction since late 2023 and silicon-carbide epitaxy tools fell more than 50 percent
    • Memory was only 15 percent of first-half 2026 equipment sales
    • China mature-node logic, more than 30 percent of revenue in late 2025, is guided to decline to the low-to-high 20s percent of sales.
  • Not B, because the two legs the case rests on (leading-edge logic, then DRAM) are the ones growing, and the bifurcation is a mix question handled under the margin items.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the company's own cycle turned up decisively in Q2 2026 after a six-quarter mature-segment downturn and the near-term trajectory is up (Q3 guided at EUR 1,100 million, Q4 about EUR 1,195 million on Morgan Stanley's read), but Jefferies models the industry cycle's growth peaking in Q4 2026 and consensus has FY2028 revenue growth halving to 16.4 percent.
    • We are past the turn and approaching the peak growth rate, which is the right place for the outputs and the wrong place for the multiple.
  • Quarterly revenue: EUR 698 million in Q4 2025 (down 13.7 percent on a year earlier), EUR 862 million in Q1 2026 (up 2.8 percent), EUR 1,003 million in Q2 2026 (up 20.0 percent).
  • Industry path: Morgan Stanley's wafer fab equipment path is USD 163 billion in 2026, USD 223 billion in 2027 (up 37 percent) and USD 254 billion in 2028 (up 14 percent).
  • Not A, because an A here needs the cycle to be early
    • The sell-side revision series (FY2027 earnings per share up 57 percent in twelve months) says the market already knows the turn happened.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption forces named (batch and spatial ALD from Tokyo Electron and Kokusai; integrated multi-step platforms from Applied Materials) are being confined to memory gap-fill and non-critical layers, while the forces running the other way (selective ALD moving into high-volume manufacturing at 2 nanometres, molybdenum replacing tungsten) favour the incumbent chemistry leader.
    • Tailwind on balance.
  • In ASM's favour: selective ALD moved from research into high-volume manufacturing at 2 nanometres
    • Tokyo Electron's NT333 and Episode platforms are limited to specific memory gap-fill, and batch cannot meet the wafer-level uniformity of advanced logic.
  • Against: Kokusai holds about 70 percent of batch ALD for 3D NAND and plasma batch is expanding into DRAM and logic
    • Applied's Trillium and Centris Spectral SiN tools are being evaluated by leading logic chipmakers for gate-all-around work-function metals.
  • Not A, because the molybdenum battlefield is open and ASM's position there is new.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because intensity is tightening at the margins of the franchise (metal ALD, where Lam Research's ALTUS Halo and Applied Materials' Centris Spectral molybdenum tools compete with ASM's first metal entry, and bundling by diversified peers at 5 to 10 percent discounts) while the core, dielectric single-wafer ALD at the leading edge, is not contested in any evidence in the research.
    • A mild headwind on share of the new markets, neutral on the core.
  • Metal ALD: Lam shipped ALTUS Halo molybdenum tools into advanced memory production and claims to be the only peer with molybdenum ALD in logic and foundry production as of mid-2025
    • ASM countered with molybdenum ALD wins at 1.4 nanometres, which Deutsche Bank reads as eating into the larger CVD market.
  • Core share: the single-wafer ALD share went from 45 percent in 2021 to 52 percent in 2024 while Tokyo Electron fell from 29 to 16 percent and Lam held 10 to 11 percent
    • The core has been concentrating, not fragmenting.
  • Against: expert calls say customers actively qualify second vendors to break sole-source positions and that ASM's platform throughput and software lag the multi-chamber peers
    • Jefferies believes ASM lost DRAM ALD share over two years.
  • Not D, because no evidence in the research shows a process-tool-of-record loss at a leading-edge logic customer, which is the event that would move this to a headwind on the core.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because export controls already bar ASM's most advanced ALD and epitaxy tools from China and limit its ability to support the installed base there, and the MATCH Act introduced in April 2026 is a live proposal to harmonise allied controls on chokepoint tools
    • Against that, the US CHIPS Act and a prospective European Chips Act 2.0 subsidise the Scottsdale and Almere expansions.
    • A headwind, bounded.
  • Controls so far: the October 2022 restrictions cut China sales by an estimated 15 to 25 percent
    • The December 2024 controls were manageable and did not disrupt guided 2025 revenue
    • China stayed at about 30 percent of FY2025 revenue.
  • MATCH Act: Deutsche Bank reads it as presently more of a negotiating tactic
    • Jefferies says that if approved it is a major headwind to the 2027 outlook.
    • The sector research says to treat a high China share as a regulatory liability rather than a growth leg, and this company's own research says the same.
  • Not D, because management already guides China down to the low-to-high 20s percent of sales, and the leading-edge growth that drives the case is outside China by construction.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because supply is the binding constraint on how fast the demand force converts to revenue: lead times have lengthened again after reaching three months, upstream supplier bottlenecks are a more severe limiting factor than cleanroom space, VAT Group's valve backlog is at a record, and precursors such as trimethylaluminium and Chinese-sourced fluorine compounds carry cost and availability risk.
    • A headwind on timing and gross margin, not on demand.
  • Concentration: Asian suppliers take 68 percent of purchases and Singapore produces about 80 percent of volume
    • The company is qualifying secondary and tertiary suppliers and has tripled its Korean capacity.
  • Cost pass-through: management is implementing targeted price increases to pass base-cost inflation through, which the 51.9 percent gross margin in Q2 2026 supports.
  • Customer-side timing: the customers' own execution limits (cleanroom readiness, power for data centres, fab construction) are an external force on the timing of tool installation that peers such as ASML have already reported
    • A timing headwind the case must carry, counted here.
  • Not D, because the Q3 2026 guide of EUR 1,100 million and the second-half step-up were given with the constraints in view, and capacity (the Singapore second floor, Dongtan tripled) is ahead of demand on the company's own account.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because two macro forces act more on ASM than on the universe, one each way: the AI capital-spending cycle (above USD 1 trillion this year) is the demand engine, and the discount rate (the United States 10-year near 5.33 percent, December hikes priced at both central banks) bears on a 24x multiple more than on the universe's average.
    • Net neutral on demand, a headwind on the multiple.

  • Not D, because the demand macro is in the company's favour and the company is self-funded, with EUR 1,180 million of cash and EUR 70.5 million of debt at June 2026, so the credit leg of the regime does not touch it.
