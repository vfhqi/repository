EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

CEO quality and operational track record

Topi Manner brings experienced Nordic operator credentials spanning a 12-year executive career at Nordea Bank Abp and five years as CEO of Finnair Oyj, where he successfully navigated existential external shocks including COVID-19 pandemic disruption and Russian airspace closure through decisive cost restructuring and network reallocation.

At Elisa, revenue growth decelerated sharply to EUR 2,257m in 2025 before contracting 0.8% in H1 2026, reflecting delayed software licensing deals and intense domestic mobile price competition that forced downward guidance revisions for both telecom service revenue and international software growth targets.

Manner exhibits a plain-spoken, disciplined operator style characterized by explicit operational commitments, stating "in Elisa, we say what we do and then we do what we say" and "I repeat, we will keep our market share" during analyst calls.

The CEO demonstrates strong capital allocation discipline through rigid adherence to a 12% CapEx-to-sales ceiling and twelve consecutive dividend increases, alongside strong communication clarity, but exhibits only partial dynamism and agency in navigating domestic telecom price wars and accelerating organic software expansion.

Governance robustness and board composition

Elisa's Board exhibits 100% independence from management across all nine directors, with state anchor Solidium represented through independent board member Tuomas Hyyryläinen, providing robust institutional oversight that constrains strategic deviation while maintaining separation from executive control.

Board leadership refreshed through the 2025 appointment of Christoph Vitzthum as Chair and the addition of former Swisscom CEO Urs Schaeppi, while achieving 44% female representation that surpasses the corporate governance target of 40% and brings deep European telecom operator expertise.

Executive compensation structure demonstrates strong alignment with long-term value creation through a dominant 60% weighting on earnings per share across Performance Share Plan cycles, supplemented by 30% weighting on strategy focus area revenue growth and 10% combined weighting on employee engagement and CO2 emission reductions.

Equity alignment enforced through mandatory shareholding requirements obligating the CEO to retain 50% of net reward shares until holding 100% of gross annual base salary, complemented by explicit board clawback rights to adjust or revoke unvested and vested variable incentives upon misconduct.

The shareholder structure features equal voting rights across all 167,335,073 shares without dual-class distortions, constrained by Finnish state holding company Solidium Oy's 10.04% anchor stake comprising 16,802,512 shares that exercises structural governance leverage through Shareholders' Nomination Board appointment authority.

Related-party transactions remain negligible with zero dealings involving key management or directors during 2024 through 2026, supported by disciplined EUR 0.6m auditing fees, low EUR 0.1m non-audit service fees, and unqualified audit opinions from Ernst & Young Oy across all financial statements without material internal control weaknesses.

Insider trading signals and equity alignment

CFO Kristian Pullola executed strong open-market conviction buying of 1,006 shares in November 2025 shortly after taking office, purchasing at a volume-weighted average price of EUR 38.23 per share on Nasdaq Helsinki as a real-money vote of confidence in Elisa's operational trajectory.

Nordic peer Telenor ASA demonstrated materially stronger insider buying with primary insiders executing NOK 10.7m in open-market purchases following Q1 2026 earnings, including President and CEO Benedicte Schilbred Fasmer's acquisition of 1,750 shares at NOK 151.90 and director Ottar Ertzeid's purchase of over NOK 8.3m across tranches.

Total board and executive board shareholdings reached 98,506 shares representing only 0.06% of outstanding equity, meaning management net worth remains largely unexposed to domestic mobile ARPU erosion or potential software valuation write-downs, trailing founderled technology peer ownership benchmarks by wide margins.

Expert network sentiment versus public narrative disconnects

Competitor and supplier testimony validates Elisa's operational excellence, with a Telia Business Intelligence Head characterizing Elisa as one of the best telecom operators across European markets, a Telia Strategy Director confirming successful 'more-for-more' pricing strategy through security bundling, and a Nokia Business Development Head praising bold 5G+ investments and bundled services driving subscriber ARPU expansion.

Expert networks confirm B2B industrial automation traction through Elisa Industriq gaining enterprise market share by automating digital processes, validated by foreign telco sales of Polystar network optimization software including MasOrange's selection for Spanish national mobile network analytics.

Critical expert dissent emerges through warnings of pervasive price erosion driven by price-sensitive Finnish consumers accelerating ARPU decline, lingering software profitability skepticism from Telia executives regarding long-term organic scalability, and internal testimony revealing CEO Topi Manner became unpopular following aggressive late-2025 layoffs executed despite stable reported earnings.

Management public narrative diverges from operational reality through guidance versus execution gaps, where touted initial guidance prudence preceded actual Q2 revenue target misses and delayed mobile pricing recovery, compounded by stable profitability reports relying on recurring non-GAAP EBITDA adjustments recognized in six of seven quarters including expanded bad debt provisions and customer credit impairments.

Thesis credibility risks and execution confidence

Sell-side sentiment underwent sharp deterioration from initial continuity endorsement and Morgan Stanley's October 2024 Overweight upgrade, to DNB Carnegie's October 2025 downgrade to Hold noting lower mobile revenue removed premium valuation triggers, followed by Morgan Stanley's November 2025 downgrade to Equalweight viewing medium-term CMD targets as overly ambitious.

Rothschild Redburn initiated Sell rating in January 2026 forecasting structural top-line weakness in mobile and software, reiterating Sell in July 2026 citing management's unfortunate track record of missing revised guidance and questioning dividend coverage durability as underlying cash flow failed to cover distributions.

Growing non-GAAP accounting reliance presents thesis risk through Rothschild Redburn's observation that adjustments were recognized in six of seven quarters totaling EUR 61m in Q4 2024, with EBITDA adjustments expanding to include bad debt impairments and customer credit provisions alongside recurring restructuring costs, threatening free cash flow coverage of the EUR 2.40 per share dividend.

Sequential guidance downgrades damaged credibility as management lowered 2026 telecom service revenue guidance to 0-2% from 1-3% following Q2 mobile pricing weakness, reduced Elisa Industriq organic growth target to 5-10% from over 10% due to Middle East project delays, and pushed mobile service revenue recovery back from second half 2026 to Q4 2026.

Internal culture indicators reveal friction despite Financial Times naming Elisa among Europe's top 500 employers in 2025, as the Elisa Engagement Score dropped to 69 in 2024 from 77 in 2023 driven by mandatory three-day office attendance introduced in 2025 creating substantial internal discussion and employee dissatisfaction.

Execution confidence verdict assigns medium plausibility for delivering 25%+ IRR over a 12-24M holding period contingent on mobile price stabilization, with conditional thesis durability dependent on domestic mobile service revenue recovery in Q4 2026 enabling cash flow to comfortably cover the EUR 2.40 dividend, though prolonged domestic price campaigning combined with software growth stagnation will compress cash flows forcing a dividend freeze and permanent de-rating.
