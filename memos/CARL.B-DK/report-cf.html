EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Carlsberg is projected to deliver consensus earnings per share of DKK 64.88 for fiscal year 2026, which aligns closely with base case estimates and represents a modest increase from the DKK 60-62 range implied for fiscal year 2025. The consensus organic revenue growth expectation of 3.5% for fiscal year 2027 sits above cautious analyst models of 2.3%, reflecting divergent views on the durability of the group's multi-beverage transition.

The company has pivoted aggressively from cost containment to growth under CEO Jacob Aarup- Andersen, raising its organic revenue growth ambition to a 4% to 6% compound annual growth rate under the Accelerate SAIL strategy. The transformational DKK 29.5 billion acquisition of Britvic in January 2025 fundamentally repositioned Carlsberg as the largest combined UK beverage provider, with soft drinks and nonbeer products doubling to 30% of total volume pro forma.

Consensus Positioning and Drivers

Management raised expected annual Britvic cost synergies from GBP 100 million to GBP 110 million, achieving 30% delivery in 2025 and expecting 50% capture in 2026, which directly supports organic operating profit growth guidance of 4% to 6%. The new $344 million greenfield bottling facility in Almaty, Kazakhstan is designed to produce 1 billion liters annually and eliminate expensive third-party toll fillers, driving a robust profit step-up in 2027. The Sapporo joint venture monetized a 25% minority stake at an attractive 21.3x EBIT multiple for USD 643 million, accelerating deleveraging toward the 2.5x net debt to EBITDA target by end-2026.

Organic beer volumes fell 4.2% in third quarter 2026 while other beverages grew 3.6%, demonstrating a structural consumer pivot away from core beer that pressures future earnings per share unless non-beer volume expansion accelerates. Carlsberg is the European brewer most exposed to aluminum can costs, with a worst-case 35% increase in raw aluminum requiring a 1.3% price increase to offset, putting downward pressure on gross margins if unhedged packaging positions remain exposed.

Organizational Transformation

Jacob Aarup-Andersen succeeded Cees 't Hart as Group CEO in September 2023, pivoting aggressively from costcutting to growth and raising the medium-term organic revenue algorithm from 3-5% to 4-6%, signaling a structural organizational change. The group rewired executive compensation in 2024 to prioritize Accelerate SAIL strategic growth targets, with short-term incentives weighted toward organic revenue growth and long-term programs expanded to the top 90 global leaders.

The corporate culture is transitioning from the accountability-first Triple-A framework toward a winning culture that rewards calculated risk-taking, designed to break down regional silos and shift the organization into growth mode. Employee turnover reached 16.0% in 2025, reflecting organizational changes associated with the strategic pivot, while Vietnam achieved a Glint Inclusion Index score of 86, demonstrating strong progress in workplace inclusion.

Critics warn that aggressive transition to a growth-oriented culture could disrupt the historically disciplined cost culture, and if risktaking is prioritized over operational discipline, unexpected Britvic integration setbacks could erode operating margins.
