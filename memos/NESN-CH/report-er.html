Cocoa and green coffee inflation – severe cost of goods sold pressure: Entering the earnings release, the sellside focused intensely on Nestlé's exposure to agricultural commodity spikes, particularly cocoa and green coffee bean prices. Rothschild & Co Redburn analyst Charlie Higgs highlighted that while spot cocoa prices had retreated from historical peaks above £6,000 per ton down to approximately £3,000 per ton, forward hedging contracts locked in elevated input costs for 2026.

This dynamic limited near-term gross margin expansion across confectionery. & Co Redburn 52 & Co Redburn Confectionery hedging disadvantage – Lindt and Mondelez comparisons: The sell-side debated how Nestlé's cocoa hedging cycle compared to specialized chocolate peers. Retailer negotiations and downsizing backlash – European margin risk: Prior to results, analysts scrutinised the risk of European retailer delistings as supermarkets resisted branded price increases. Other side of the story: While the sell-side consensus focused on a gradual volume recovery driven by cost savings and brand reinvestment, a more pessimistic market view argued that structural down-trading in US petcare, persistent European retailer resistance, and long-term brand damage from infant formula recalls would lock Nestlé into sub-3% organic growth, invalidating the multi-year thesis of a return to a predictable 4%+ earnings algorithm.
