EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Harbour Energy is rapidly shifting its portfolio's center of gravity westward to mitigate domestic regulatory risks, successfully transforming into a global independent producer after tripling production through the Wintershall Dea acquisition in 2024 and entering the United States deepwater Gulf via the 3.2 billion dollar LLOG Exploration acquisition completed in February 2026. The company has updated its shareholder distribution policy to return forty-five to seventy-five percent of annual free cash flow, incorporating a three hundred million dollar base dividend alongside flexible share buybacks.

However, this transformation has required cutting around 700 cumulative jobs in the United Kingdom since 2022, which risks operational delivery and lowers employee morale.

How Much Is Changing

The company's first-half revenue increased by more than twenty percent period-on-period because of record production and elevated oil prices, surpassing the flat or declining top-line profiles of mature North Sea peers who face structural declines. Net debt rose to over seven billion dollars immediately after completing the LLOG acquisition but strong first-half operating cash flows helped reduce net debt down to five billion four hundred million dollars, leaving leverage at a comfortable zero point seven times, which is below management's longterm target of one times. Group volumes reached a record 509 kboepd in the first half of 2026 due to acquisitions, representing a huge step-change in corporate scale from the 258 kboepd produced in 2024. The effective tax rate fell to 81% in the first half of 2026 from punitive prior levels, driven by the addition of lower-tax international production in the United States. Management completed the 215 million dollar sale of Indonesian interests in May 2026, marking a strategic milestone by removing high-cost, sub-scale assets that lacked a clear path to scale.

Main Drivers of Future Earnings

Volatile commodity pricing exposure remains the most powerful cyclical driver, as fluctuations in Dated Brent and European natural gas prices directly dictate top-line revenue because forty percent of production is exposed to Dated Brent and forty percent to European gas benchmarks, with management guidance showing that a five dollar change in Brent crude impacts annual free cash flow by 170 million dollars, representing a twelve percent effect on earnings per share.

Production sustainability at 475 to 500 kboepd through 2030 represents a critical volume driver affecting both revenue and unit operating costs, with high plausibility supported by eighteen years of organic inventory and recent record production of 509 kboepd surpassing consensus. Shifting tax jurisdictions westward will structurally lower the group's effective tax rate, driven by the stark contrast between the punitive seventy-eight percent marginal rate in the United Kingdom and the competitive twenty-three percent rate in the United States, with high plausibility as international production already represents over two-thirds of total volumes and the first-half 2026 results demonstrated a dramatic decline in the effective tax rate to eightyone percent.

Waldorf tax loss utilization provides 900 million dollars of highly valuable tax-effected UK tax losses that will immediately reduce domestic cash tax liabilities, with high plausibility because the transaction is already fully completed based on audited corporate transaction terms and verified tax assets. Accelerated share repurchases will directly boost earnings per share by systematically reducing the company's share count, with management promising at least 500 million dollars in additional shareholder returns starting with a 250 million dollar buyback, carrying high plausibility due to exceptional free cash flow conversion that reached 1.8 billion dollars in the first half.
