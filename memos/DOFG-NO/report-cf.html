Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Employee turnover reached 12.4% in recent sustainability assessments, positioning DOF above the peer median for workforce attrition and suggesting potential cultural friction or compensation pressures relative to competitors such as Subsea 7 and Solstad Offshore, which maintain tighter retention metrics. This personnel instability creates execution risks for complex subsea engineering projects that depend on specialized technical expertise, as examined in Section 1 Leadership and Governance Change.

Excessive uncontracted CSV newbuild deliveries starting in late 2027 could severely depress dayrates if deepwater exploration budgets contract, with DNB Carnegie identifying 25 CSV newbuilds under construction representing roughly 20% of the active fleet globally. This supply overhang poses a structural threat to DOF's premium subsea vessel pricing power and contrasts with the disciplined capacity management exhibited by tierone peers during prior upcycles, as analyzed in Section 3 External Change: Value-Chain Forces.

Recent oil price declines to approximately USD 74 per barrel put oil companies in overspending territory, limiting further E&P spending growth, which directly threatens the sustainability of DOF's contract repricing momentum. This macro headwind disproportionately impacts DOF relative to peers with greater contract backlog coverage extending beyond 2027, creating margin compression risks detailed in Section 3 External Change: Macro and

Outstanding fleet debt of USD 890 million remains exposed to floating SOFR plus a 2.75% margin, leaving DOF vulnerable to interest rate volatility despite recent refinancing progress. Peers such as Subsea 7 maintain substantially higher proportions of fixed-rate debt, providing superior earnings stability during periods of monetary tightening, as documented in Section 4 Financial Profile Change. Consensus models underestimate 2027 maintenance capital requirements due to a 20% drydocking uptick, creating a hidden cash flow headwind that could compress dividend capacity below management's USD 0.40 per share quarterly target.

This capital intensity miscalculation represents a significant variance from peer-group maintenance spending trajectories and threatens the sustainability of DOF's shareholder return framework, as highlighted in Section 4 Financial Profile Change. The group is exposed to client concentration as Petrobras holds the largest portion of the contract backlog, creating single-client revenue dependency that exceeds peer-group medians.

This concentrated exposure to Brazilian regulatory risk, local content requirements, and potential tender delays presents a structural vulnerability absent in more geographically diversified competitors, as examined in Section 9 Key Drivers Synthesis.

Outliers worth noting

A second significant outlier involves board governance friction, where Thorstein previously resigned from the board in 2023 during complex and contentious financial restructuring negotiations, only to return as chairperson within three years–an unusual governance reversal that suggests either resolved structural tensions or potential lingering interpersonal dynamics not fully disclosed in public materials.

The third operational oddity concerns the company's peer-leading margin profile, where underlying EBITDA margin touched 49% in Q2 2026 excluding one-off insurance settlement effects, substantially outperforming the sector median and raising questions about sustainability when peer margins cluster around 38-40% ranges, particularly given the company's exposure to identical market cycles and regulatory frameworks as competitors operating in similar subsea service segments.
