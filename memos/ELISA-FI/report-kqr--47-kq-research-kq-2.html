However, disclosed terms remain constrained, as management explicitly stated that individual contract revenues and transaction values are undisclosed, leaving market analysts to estimate financial impacts from broader pipeline metrics. Section 2 – Market structure and Finnish data center growth trajectory 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Finland's data center capacity is undergoing a structural transition from 285MW to over 1.5GW by 2030, driven by AI workloads and Nordic grid advantages.

Operational baseline and regional capacity inflection

1.
Rapid capacity expansion: Operational data center capacity in Finland stands at approximately 285MW across 33 active facilities. According to market studies by Ramboll and the Finnish Data Center Association, operational capacity is projected to expand by 52% annually through 2027. Total installed capacity is forecasted to exceed 1.5GW by 2030 based on confirmed project commitments
2.
Capital pipeline scale: The broader Finnish data center pipeline contains over €13 billion in announced private sector investment projects. Confirmed investment plans represent between 3.4GW and 4.0GW of total potential capacity across development stages. Construction contractors like YIT and SRV report that data centers have become the single largest growth driver in private non-residential construction
3.
Speculative connection gap: Transmission system operator Fingrid has logged between 35GW and 50GW of formal grid connection inquiries for data center projects. Equity analysts at Bernstein emphasize that while 1.2GW of capacity has reached Final Investment Decision, over 90% of grid connection requests represent speculative phantom capacity that will never reach execution. This creates a severe divergence between raw grid queue metrics and actual deliverable fiber connectivity demand

32

4.
Secondary market shift: Primary European FLAPD hubs in Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Paris, and Dublin are constrained by 5-to-10-year grid connection wait times. Morgan Stanley Research forecasts that secondary European data center markets will overtake traditional FLAPD hubs in total capacity by 2031-2032. This geographic shift directly benefits Finland due to immediate high-voltage grid availability

Structural enablers and Nordic cost advantages

5.
Power cost economics: Wholesale electricity prices in Finland remain among the lowest in Europe, averaging under €45 per megawatt-hour. Over 80% of the national electricity grid is powered by carbon-free nuclear, hydro, and onshore wind generation. Low energy costs are a critical determinant for hyper-scalers, as power accounts for 65% to 75% of total data center operating costs
6.
Cooling and efficiency advantages: Cold climatic conditions with average annual temperatures of 0°C to 5°C deliver substantial free-cooling benefits. Local facilities achieve Power Usage Effectiveness metrics as low as 1.15, matching best-in-class global benchmarks. These environmental conditions significantly reduce total power draw required for cooling infrastructure relative to Southern European locations
7.
Heat reuse integration: Widespread integration with municipal district heating systems provides a structural sustainability moat for Finnish facility developers. Projects developed by Fortum for Microsoft in Espoo, Kirkkonummi, and Vihti utilize waste heat to supply local municipal heating networks. Similar waste-heat recovery models are deployed at atNorth's 430MW FIN04 campus in Kouvola and Pure DC's 550MW site in Seinäjoki
8.
Tax policy headwinds: The Finnish government eliminated the reduced electricity tax rate for data centers in July 2026. Electricity tax rates increased from 0.05 cents per kilowatt-hour to 2.24 cents per kilowatt-hour, adding roughly €20 per megawatt-hour to operating power costs. Although the government is designing a replacement support scheme for late 2026, tax policy shifts introduce near-term regulatory friction

Hyper-scaler mega-projects and AI pipeline dynamics

9.
Mega-campus concentration: Development activity is increasingly concentrated in campus-scale projects exceeding 100MW capacity. Pure DC is developing a 550MW campus in Seinäjoki valued at over €7.5 billion, with its initial 110MW phase fully pre-leased. AtNorth has partnered with contractor YIT to build its 430MW FIN04 campus in Kouvola under a €300 million civil engineering contract

Hyper-scaler footprint expansion: Tech giants are expanding domestic infrastructure, highlighted by Microsoft's three planned sites in Espoo, Kirkkonummi, and Vihti totaling 555MW. Google is expanding its Hamina facility with an additional €1 billion investment, bringing total site potential well beyond 1GW. DayOne is advancing a 560MW development in Nurmijärvi in partnership with Fortum

Finland ranks second in Europe only to the UK in total planned 100MW+ hyperscale projects

If 21TWh of incremental data center demand materializes without concurrent wind additions, Finnish spot power prices could rise by up to €34 per megawatt-hour as marginal pricing shifts to gas generation. However, concurrent additions of 2GW of onshore wind would mitigate severe power price escalation
