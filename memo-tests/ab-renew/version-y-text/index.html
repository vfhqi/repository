Version Y of two: a blind read

Renew Holdings plc (RNWH-GB): a high-quality compounder priced for its base case, so wait for a profit warning or two clean results before buying

Share price 959p (16 October 2026); market value about £760m; fiscal year ends 30 September; FY26 results due 24 November 2026. Definitions and sources are in the end notes.

#0Summary of key insights, implications and conclusions

What Renew is. Renew is a £1.2bn-revenue, net-cash, asset-light maintenance contractor for the United Kingdom's regulated and state-owned infrastructure owners, working under about 250 long-term frameworks: rail is about 40 percent of revenue, water about 22 percent, highways and telecoms about 20 percent, and nuclear, power grids and onshore wind the rest. Five legislated five-year spending cycles, all running to 2029 to 2031, set its demand; the contract form, not management, sets its 6 to 7 percent margin; and it spends its cash on bolt-on acquisitions at 4 to 10 times EBITDA and a progressive dividend. Earnings per share have compounded at over 15 percent a year for fifteen years.

The case in one sentence. Renew's earnings are highly predictable in shape and level but unpredictable in half-year timing, and its shares trade at their own median multiple with no cushion, in a market that is paying for upgrades and buybacks that Renew does not supply.

The five things that matter most:

Renew's growth rate has roughly halved, and the model does not expect it to recover. Organic growth averaged about 9 percent a year over five years on the company's own definition, fell to minus 2.5 percent in FY25 during a rail funding-cycle transition, and is recovering to about 5 percent, with rail flat in real terms through 2029. Earnings per share compound at 6 to 8 percent a year (the model: 71p, 76p, 83p and 89p for FY26 to FY29) with a flat margin and no pricing power. That is good, not exciting.
The main risk is one variable, the clients' call-off timing, and it lands inside single half-years. When Network Rail deferred work in January 2025, profit expectations fell 9 percent and the shares fell 22 percent in a day. I put a 20 to 25 percent probability on a similar warning in the next 18 months, most likely in April or May 2027: the first half of FY27 is compared with a record winter, and both the Budget of 28 October 2026 and the creation of Great British Railways press on rail spending.
The share price reflects the base case and nothing more. At 11.6 times two-year-forward earnings Renew trades at its ten-year median multiple. Its three brokers all rate it Buy, sit within 2 percent of each other and have not moved for a year, and Renew's "in line" reporting convention allows only negative surprises. Sell-side and technical momentum are both rated D.
At 959p the base-case return is modest, and the probability-weighted 18-month return is close to nil. On my fair multiple of 11.0 to 11.6 times the 18-month total return is plus 5 to plus 11 percent (plus 3.5 percent at the guideline multiple of 10.79 times). Weighting that against a 20 to 25 percent chance of a 20 to 35 percent fall gives an expected 18-month return of roughly nil to plus 3 percent.
A positively skewed bet on Renew exists, but not at today's price. Renew's franchise has survived every timing shock intact, so the asymmetric entry is the morning after a timing-driven profit warning: in 2025 the shares fell from 1,093p to 659p (9.1 times earnings) and recovered 40 percent within eight months. A smaller opportunity follows two clean organic growth results (24 November 2026 and May 2027) with the multiple still below 12 times; that opens FY28 to FY29 upside from acquisitions outside consensus, a new chair with a record of margin gains and capital returns, and £45m to £60m of idle cash.
Implication for the decision. Do not buy Renew at 959p for a 12-to-24-month holding: the base-case return is below what the Stoxx Europe 600 benchmark demands for a stock that can fall 20 percent in a day. Put the stock on a dated watch list with two buy triggers, set out in #13. A warning-driven fall to 600p to 700p (9 to 10 times reduced earnings) is the high-conviction entry; two clean organic growth results with the price still under 12 times FY28 earnings is the medium-conviction entry.

#1Tailwinds and headwinds in the operating environment

Renew's demand is legislated, non-discretionary and long-dated, which is rare. Renew sells maintenance and renewal work into five funded spending cycles, each set by a regulator or the Treasury and each running beyond the two-year horizon (chart below). Water's Asset Management Period 8 (£104bn, 2025 to 2030) is 70 to 100 percent larger than the previous period and still growing: a further £3.4bn was provisionally approved in August 2026, and Pennon raised its capital plan by £1bn on 7 October 2026. Highways renewals rise about 75 percent under Road Investment Strategy 3.

Government policy and industry consolidation both favour an accredited maintenance specialist. Infrastructure strategy and procurement rules favour care of existing assets, awards weighted to quality, and collaborative contracts; and 3,805 construction insolvencies in the year to June 2026 have left the surviving tier-one contractors bidding for margin rather than volume.

The spending reaches Renew's revenue late and unevenly, and rail, Renew's largest market, is flat in real terms.

  • Water is the growth engine. Renew now has frameworks with 10 of the 12 water companies, up from 3; 8 of its 10 water regions were at or ahead of plan at the half-year; client spending is back-end loaded into 2027 to 2029.
  • Power is small but the fastest-growing market, and highways spending steps up from April 2027.
  • Rail is shrinking in real terms. The rail regulator found in July 2026 that inflation has removed £2bn of Network Rail's buying power and cut renewals to 83 percent of plan. Record weather-driven emergency repairs supported Renew's rail volumes in FY25 and the first half of FY26, and may not repeat.
  • Nuclear is depressed by industrial action at Sellafield, with no end date.
The headwinds are all on the client side, and all affect the timing of work rather than its volume. They are: the Budget of 28 October 2026 (a £428m cut to Road Investment Strategy 3 is already proposed); the creation of Great British Railways, which reorganises rail procurement from 2027 and runs the re-let of Renew's largest position (Control Period 8, bids in 2028 to 2029); the abolition of the water regulator, Ofwat, amid political anger over bills; and strained water-company balance sheets (Thames Water seeking £2.4bn to operate to autumn 2027, Pennon's £550m rights issue, South East Water's downgrade). Every water client underspent its year-one allowance, so Renew's water ramp-up is more likely to arrive in FY28 to FY29 than in FY27.

The mitigant is structural: deferred maintenance on safety-critical assets must eventually be done, so client weakness delays work rather than cancelling it. In FY25 organic revenue fell 2.5 percent, yet operating profit rose 1.7 percent.

Net, the environment supports mid-single-digit organic growth and a stable margin with above-average visibility, but gives Renew neither real price rises nor a reason to re-rate. Over the last six months demand has improved slightly, while client finances and politics have worsened slightly.

Score: 62. Legislated demand to 2031 is unusually visible for a European industrial, but the clients' finances and two regulator reorganisations put all the timing risk on the supplier.

#2Internally driven corporate change forces

Renew's strategy is complete rather than changing, and that completeness protects its predictability. Renew has spent a decade becoming a pure-play, asset-light engineering services group on regulated frameworks funded from clients' operating budgets, and its last three moves finished the job. It sold its building business, Walter Lilly, in October 2024, adding about 40 basis points to the group margin. It built a high-voltage power distribution business from nothing in two years through four acquisitions. And it won 29 water framework positions in the latest water re-let. Joint bids by several Renew brands have also become a real route to market, winning £626m of frameworks in three years that no single brand could have bid for.

The one internal change that could raise Renew's growth rate is the new chair. Pete Raby became chair designate on 5 October 2026 and takes over at the annual general meeting on 27 January 2027. At Morgan Advanced Materials he raised the margin, consolidated sites and published a financial framework (record below). Renew's own medium-term framework has not been raised in years, although Renew has beaten it on growth and cash in most of them. I put the probability of a capital markets day with a reset framework in 2027 at 35 to 40 percent, with its earnings effect in 2028.

Everything else is stable, and the frictions are ordinary for a decentralised acquirer integrating four businesses at once. The chief executive and chief financial officer are unchanged and hold shares worth 471 and 333 percent of salary; no activist is pressing for change; management prefers reinvestment to buybacks. The frictions:

  • Full Circle's French wind subsidiary is in restructuring within a year of purchase: the one misjudged acquisition, small and outside the United Kingdom's regulatory regime.
  • The executive board is two people, and an external internal-audit provider is only now being appointed, late for a ten-brand group with £135m of unbilled contract assets.
  • Executive salaries rose by up to 42 percent in two years, around the first profit warning in twenty years, which is a presentational risk at the January 2027 annual general meeting.
Score: 55. A well-executed programme that protects the model's predictability rather than lifting its growth rate; the chair succession is the only internal force that could change the growth rate, and the new chair's agenda is unannounced.

#4Guidance, sell-side expectations and the delivery record

Renew's guidance convention means good news arrives as small nudges and bad news as large cuts. Renew gives no numeric annual guidance; four times a year it says only whether trading is "in line with the board's expectations", and those expectations are, in practice, the published forecasts of its house broker, Deutsche Bank. Consensus is therefore tight, credible and without a cushion, and a miss surfaces suddenly at a trading update, which is why the share price reacts so violently.

Renew's medium-term framework is conservative, but the annual forecast is only fair. The framework's floors (5 percent organic growth, 60 percent cash conversion) sit well below delivery (about 9 and 69 percent). But flat real rail budgets, a late water ramp-up and the labour constraint support only 5 to 6 percent organic growth over the next three years, so the growth floor gives little cushion. Management's informal commentary runs a little ahead of the numbers.

Renew's delivery record is reliable on profit, includes one externally caused miss, and is merely in line on cash. FY25 revenue and operating profit came within 0.1 percent of forecast, the first half of FY26 was in line, and FY26 was confirmed "in line" at record revenue of about £1,193m and adjusted operating profit of about £77.7m. The one miss, in January 2025, was a 9 percent cut caused by Network Rail's deferrals and was beaten afterwards; because the 2019 and 2020 funding-cycle starts passed without a warning, the miss reflected a real-terms budget squeeze that is still in place.

The common view that Renew "consistently beats on cash" is wrong; Renew meets on cash. FY26 net cash before leases of £13.2m beat the £6.3m consensus only because of a £6.9m insurance receipt; underlying net cash matched consensus exactly. The FY25 cash beat came from lower cash tax.
Score: 55. Honest, early-warning, framework-level conservatism with a good delivery record; marked down because the "in line against the house broker" convention leaves no annual cushion and the cash record is flattered by one-offs.

#5Pricing power, operating leverage, financial leverage, cost control and feedback loops

Renew passes cost inflation through to clients, but it cannot raise real prices. Its contracts are target-cost, cost-reimbursable or inflation-indexed, so wage and materials inflation reaches its rates within one or two reporting periods; the gross margin held between 13.2 and 14.4 percent for seven years through more than 20 percent cumulative input inflation. Price therefore adds about 3 points a year to revenue growth and nothing to the margin. Real prices can move only at re-procurements: possibly upward at the highways framework (April 2027) and the power distribution price control (April 2028), and possibly downward at rail's Control Period 8 re-let (April 2029).

Renew's operating leverage is a half-year timing exposure that cuts deeper on the way down. Renew keeps its directly employed crews (27 percent of revenue) through dips in work. So profit falls by 20 to 25 percent of any revenue clients withdraw at short notice, but rises by only 13 to 14 percent of revenue that arrives as planned. The seasonal gap between first-half margins of 5.6 to 5.7 percent and second-half margins of 7.3 to 7.4 percent, on an unchanged cost base, confirms the sensitivity.

Renew's balance sheet is an unused lever, while every peer is using its own. Net debt including leases is about 0.2 times EBITDA against a self-imposed ceiling of 1 times. A £50m buyback at about 960p would retire 6.6 percent of the shares and add about 6 percent to earnings per share, leaving leverage at 0.5 times. I put the probability of a buyback below 20 percent within 18 months and at perhaps 35 to 40 percent by FY29, when surplus cash reaches £45m to £60m under a chair with a record of returning cash. Balfour Beatty, Keller, Kier, Costain and Galliford Try are all buying back shares.

Renew has no costs worth cutting. The brands carry the overhead because their identities win the frameworks, and the crews are the moat; identifiable savings are £1m to £3m in FY27 and nothing after. A margin above 7 percent would therefore come from business mix, and one below 6 percent from deferred work.

Two reinforcing loops add growth and three balancing loops hold the margin in its corridor. The two leading indicators of trouble are acquired growth outpacing organic growth (the base case has about 2 points against 4.5) and contract assets rising above £150m.

Score: 38. Revenue growth converts to profit growth at roughly one for one and no better; there is no pricing lever, no cost lever, the operating leverage cuts harder downward than upward, and the balance sheet lever is real but unused.

The base case asks Renew to do less than it has done, so faster growth than the past sits only in the upside branch. Double-digit earnings growth needs organic growth of 7 percent or more plus continued acquisitions, or a buyback. A margin above 7 percent needs higher-margin businesses (power at 8 to 10 percent, Full Circle at its 14 percent target) to outgrow rail for three to four years. Peers show a 200 basis point gain over three to five years is achievable (Balfour Beatty's support services on power mix; Keller from 3.7 to 7.1 percent), and the incoming chair has done it before, but the current team has not tried in a decade. I therefore carry a 7 percent margin by FY29 only in the upside branch.

The 24 November 2026 results must answer three questions, in order of consequence:

  1. Is Renew's cash flow weaker than the model assumes? The house broker models working capital absorbing about £30m more cash by FY27 than the model did, FY26 underlying net cash came in about £6m below the model's inputs, and receivables rose £33m to £240.9m at March 2026. I have moved the model toward the broker's path; the 30 September 2026 balance sheet will show how far to go.
  2. How large is Renew's water business? The model carries water at about 22 to 23 percent of revenue, consistent with £269m of FY24 water revenue, but management called its "Environmental" segment 13 percent of revenue in May 2026. At 13 percent, water would add 1.3 points of group organic growth rather than 2 to 2.5.
  3. Which organic growth definition is Renew using? Renew's presentations count an acquired business's growth as organic from the day of purchase (the "9 percent average"); the audited accounts exclude acquisitions from the current and previous year (FY25's minus 2.5 percent). This memo uses the audited basis, and the two must not be mixed when reading the results.
Score: 45. A 6 to 8 percent earnings compounder with a flat margin and no leverage: durable, cash-backed and below the double-digit bar; the scope for a higher slope than the past is confined to the upside branch.

#7Scope for upgrades to guidance, consensus and the narrative over 6 to 12 months

Inside 12 months, Renew's upgrades are small, mechanical and reliable, while its downgrade risk is large, dated and driven by clients.
  • Acquisitions deliver a small, reliable upgrade. Consensus includes no further acquisitions, yet Renew buys £30m to £40m of businesses a year, and each deal lifts the house broker's forecast on announcement (the May 2026 purchases added 1 percent to FY27 and FY28). That is worth 1 to 2 percent a year, regardless of clients, but is too small to change the sell-side momentum rating.
  • A trading-driven upgrade has a 10 to 15 percent probability. It needs second-half FY26 organic growth of 7 to 8 percent and first-half FY27 growth above 4 percent. The house broker already assumes the water ramp-up, so a water beat must be large to show.
  • A structural upgrade depends on the new chair. A 2027 framework reset (35 to 40 percent) would lift FY28 and FY29 estimates by 3 to 6 percent and could turn the story from "a compounder at its median multiple" into "a margin and capital-allocation story", but it falls mostly beyond 12 months.
  • A downgrade for deferred work has a 20 to 25 percent probability. It would cut FY27 profit expectations by 5 to 12 percent at the April 2027 trading update or May 2027 interim results. Triggers include Budget cuts to rail renewals, Great British Railways delays, a mild winter, a water client re-phasing (Yorkshire Water is already reviewing schemes for removal) or a Thames Water administration. A cash downgrade with profit intact, if contract assets outgrow revenue, is a second route.
Score: 42. Upgrades over the next year are small and mostly from acquisitions; the only large revision the guidance convention allows inside 18 months is a negative one, and it carries two to three times the probability of a trading-driven upgrade.

#8Current momentum in guidance, estimates, narrative, share price and peers

Renew's estimates are flat, and with only three brokers they will stay flat until one event moves all three. FY27 consensus earnings per share moved minus 0.6 percent over three months and plus 0.2 percent over twelve; FY28 rose 2.8 percent over twelve months on the May 2026 acquisitions. The median target price is 1,300p (range 1,220p to 1,450p), all three brokers rate the shares Buy, and the sell-side momentum rating is D.

Renew's story is told by a single broker, and with so few voices, "well known" means "priced". The house broker has set out every driver of FY27, and there is no independent investor commentary on file. The published story omits two negatives (the true quality of the cash record; the water clients' slow first year), one positive (acquisitions boosting FY28 and FY29), one risk (the Control Period 8 re-let) and one option (capital allocation under the new chair).

Renew's share price is drifting, moves on company-specific news and trades thinly. At 959p the shares are 5.4 percent above a rising 200-day average, up 4.5 percent over twelve months, at the 48th percentile for relative strength, with a technical momentum rating of D; about £1.6m to £2.1m trades a day. The industrial-services basket explains only 10 percent of Renew's daily returns. When Renew does move with the market, the driver is gilt yields, which also drive its clients' budgets, so the macro pressures on Renew's multiple and on its earnings arrive together.

Peers are being re-rated for buybacks and rising estimates, and Renew offers neither. The market in 2026 pays for self-funded buybacks and upgrades and treats organic decline hidden by acquisitions as a trap. Renew is not suspected of the trap, which is why it holds its median multiple while peers re-rate around it.

Score: 35. Every momentum reading is flat or D while the closest peers are in upgrade and buyback cycles; the thing that would create momentum, an organic print, is something the narrative has already assumed.

#9Riskiness and simplicity of the forecasts and expectations

Renew's investment case is simple to follow, but its operations are hard to see. The case fits in three sentences: regulated demand runs to 2031, the contract form holds the margin, and the cash funds bolt-ons and dividends. But Renew reports one segment across ten brands and five end markets and gives no numeric guidance, so none of its drivers (water call-offs, rail work, power contract wins, Sellafield, the French unit) shows in its numbers until after the event.

About 70 percent of the base case rests on demonstrated strengths; the other 30 percent rests on client timing. The base case requires four things:

  • water growing about 10 percent a year, which clients' plans support but no printed organic figure yet shows;
  • rail emergency volumes staying near their weather-driven record;
  • acquisitions continuing at 7 to 8 times EBITDA, although private-equity-backed buyers now pay 8 to 9 times for the same specialists;
  • contract assets (£135m, the auditor's key audit matter) growing no faster than revenue.
The share-price risk is larger than the business risk. Renew has no fixed-price lump-sum work, no megaprojects, no net debt and no contract above a few million pounds, so its sudden business risks are each small. But it is a thinly traded AIM-listed stock with unanimous Buy ratings and a precedent of a 22 percent one-day fall on a 9 percent profit cut. A 10 percent earnings miss would move the price by 25 to 35 percent; a 5 percent beat, by 10 to 15 percent.

The deferral risk can be tracked, but only through the clients' documents (Network Rail's delivery plan updates, the water companies' performance reports, Ofwat's determinations, the Budget) and Renew's early-April trading update, the update it used to warn in January 2025.

Of the four ways United Kingdom contractors have historically disappointed, Renew is exposed mainly to the dip at a funding-cycle transition; its next two transitions are rail in April 2029 and water in April 2030. Renew has every past source of outperformance in the sector except one: a margin step-up the market initially disbelieves.

Over 18 months the range of FY27 earnings outcomes is skewed down, about minus 15 to plus 7 percent around the centre; beyond 18 months the skew turns neutral to positive. In the deferral branch the margin falls to 5.5 to 6.0 percent, which is what Renew's operating leverage implies if organic growth runs 2.5 to 4.5 points below plan. Later, the water construction peak (2027 to 2029), power distribution spending 30 to 50 percent higher from April 2028, the idle balance sheet and acquisitions outside consensus outweigh the rail and water transitions.

Score: 58. A simple, thin-tailed, well-disclosed case whose one risk variable is undisclosable until it has happened and whose share price mechanics amplify it; safer than any tier-one contractor on fundamentals, riskier than its flat margin suggests on timing.

#10Where the stock is in its lifecycle

Renew is in late Bottoming, and two dated results will decide whether it moves to Turning Up. The fundamental trough was FY25 and the price trough March 2025 (659p, 9.1 times earnings). Since then the multiple has recovered to its median, the order book has set records and FY26 closed "in line" at record profit. Turning Up still needs a printed re-acceleration in organic growth and a first estimate upgrade, and the obstacle is the first half of FY27, which is compared with a record winter.

Score: 50. Past the fundamental and price lows but not yet past the test that converts a recovery into an upgrade cycle; the next two prints decide the phase.

#11The fair multiple

Viewforth's rule gives Renew a fair multiple of 10.79 times two-year-forward earnings, 7 percent below today's 11.57 times. The rule values a Bottoming-phase stock at the 30th percentile of its own ten-year range.

My fair multiple is 11.0 to 11.6 times, central 11.3 times; 10.79 times belongs to the deferral branch, not the base case. The rule is right that a stock with no printed organic recovery and flat estimates should not trade above its median. But in the last two years Renew has traded at 10.79 times only during the January 2025 warning and the March 2026 gilt sell-off, so the rule treats a 20 to 25 percent branch as the base case. Three facts support my band:

  • Renew's earnings are more predictable than the sector's: its margin stayed within a 60 basis point band through a funding-cycle transition and two inflation shocks.
  • Renew has net cash and no megaproject exposure, which is why it has never traded at the contracting sector's 6 to 8 times.
  • Renew's relative de-rating has already reversed: its discount to European infrastructure-services peers is 5 percent against a five-year average of 28 percent.

A multiple of 12 times or more would need an upgrade cycle or capital returns, and neither is in the base case; the 2024 peak of 15 to 16 times reflected 16.6 percent organic growth that will not recur.

Over the next 18 months two organic growth results and one Budget, not the sector, will set Renew's multiple: two passes justify 11.6 to 12.6 times; a deferral means 9 to 10 times reduced earnings, as in January 2025.

Score: 55. Fairly valued at today's median multiple for the base case, which is the problem: there is no multiple cushion below and no re-rating mechanism above until the organic prints and the chair's agenda arrive.

#13Summary conclusion

Renew is a high-quality, low-drama compounder, but at 959p it is not an unusually skewed bet. The market already knows and prices everything that makes the business good. Everything that could move the price inside 18 months is a client-timing event, with the large move about twice as likely to be down as up. The things that could give the case a new leg (acquisitions outside consensus, the new chair, £45m to £60m of idle cash by FY29, the power business at the April 2028 re-let) are 2027 to 2029 events that the market will not pay for until organic re-acceleration is in print.

The constructive plan: watch Renew against two dated triggers, and buy on either.

High conviction
Trigger one: buy at 600p to 700p after a timing-driven profit warning. If the April or May 2027 update cuts FY27 expectations by 5 to 12 percent on rail deferrals, a mild winter or water re-phasing, and the shares fall to 600p to 700p (9 to 10 times reduced earnings of 64p to 68p), buy, provided three franchise checks hold: net cash intact; framework retention intact and the order book above £900m; no write-down of contract assets.
