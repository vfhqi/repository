Section 2 – The US Dollar Hedge Book and EBIT Sensitivities

Section summary
  1. MTU Aero Engines possesses a highly naturally hedged operating model that provides superior transactional protection compared to its commercial aerospace peers.
  2. The step-up in MTU's dollar hedge rates is well understood, but unhedged outer-year sensitivities leave outer-year earnings highly exposed to spot movements.

MTU Dollar Hedge Parameters

1.
Steadily rising: MTU Aero Engines manages a rolling foreign exchange book that dictates its near-term transactional currency exposure as of September 2026. The company reports in its October 2026 presentation that average hedge rates step up from 1.14 in 2026 to 1.16 in 2027, 1.18 in 2028, and 1.19 in 2029. According to the same October 2026 document, hedged volumes peak at 2,120 million dollars in 2026 and 2,360 million dollars in 2027 before dropping to 1,160 million dollars in 2028 and 460 million dollars in 2029. This structured path ensures that near-term earnings are highly protected but forces the group to absorb higher average euro rates over the mid-term.
2.
Vulnerably unhedged: Deutsche Bank outlined in October 2026 that MTU Aero Engines faces substantial unhedged sensitivity once its formal derivative cover rolls off. The broker indicates in the same October 2026 note that unhedged foreign exchange sensitivity stands at 1 billion euros on revenue and 300 million euros on operating profit for every 10-cent move. This vulnerability is particularly acute for the year 2030 because the company currently carries zero hedges for that period. Consequently, any unexpected strengthening of the euro would severely drag on long-term earnings per share if spot rates remain elevated.
3.
Critically evaluated: MTU Aero Engines' sensitivity has historically been modeled by Rothschild and Co Redburn in August 2025 at 1.9% of operating profit per cent of hedge rate. This external claim highlights how tightly nearterm profitability is bound to currency movements. Because a Deutsche Bank report from February 2022 notes the company naturally hedges approximately 75% of its revenues through dollar procurement, the net operational exposure is relatively small. This high level of natural hedging reduces volatility but makes the remaining unhedged currency cash flows highly sensitive to spot movements.

Comparative FX Hedging Structures

4.
Highly protected: Safran SA confirmed in October 2026 that its currency hedging policy insulates its propulsion aftermarket earnings from near-term foreign exchange volatility. The group's October 2026 report outlines a target hedge rate fixed at 1.12 dollars per euro from 2026 through 2028 with net annual exposure capped at 17 billion dollars. The same October 2026 report shows this coverage extends into 2029 where the targeted hedge rate is set between 1.12 and 1.14 dollars. This firm multi-year framework provides far greater nearterm transactional earnings predictability than the stepping hedge rates of MTU Aero Engines.
5.
Leveraged sensitivity: Safran SA faces high sensitivity in its core propulsion segment where currency changes directly impact its high-margin spare parts business according to Deutsche Bank in October 2026. The same broker reported in February 2022 that a one-cent change in the hedge rate impacts Safran's operating profit by 60 million euros. This high sensitivity reflects the fact that only 45% of Safran's group revenues are naturally hedged by dollar costs. Consequently, Safran's underlying earnings are more sensitive to hedge rate movements than MTU's highly naturally hedged operating model which covers 75% of exposure.

This target blended rate is significantly less favorable than MTU's average hedge rates which remain under 1.20 through 2029. With 60% of revenues naturally hedged, Airbus models an operating profit sensitivity of 250 million euros per single cent of exchange rate variance.

The company achieves an average sterlingdollar hedge rate of 1.33 and an average euro-dollar hedge rate of 1.15. Unlike its peers, Rolls-Royce is highly sensitive at a cash flow level where each single cent change in the achieved rate impacts pre-tax cash by 35 million to 45 million pounds. This high sensitivity means that currency volatility has a direct and immediate impact on the group's highly watched free cash flow.

Contrary evidence: Jefferies adjusted its long-term spot assumption down to 1.12 dollars per euro in October 2026 to reflect a highly favorable dollar forward curve. This revision suggests that MTU Aero Engines will experience substantial currency tailwinds rather than headwinds from 2028 onward. This positive outlook contradicts the view that stepping hedge rates will inevitably depress group margins over the mid-term. If the euro remains weak, MTU's unhedged dollar revenues will convert at highly profitable spot rates and pave the way for substantial earnings outperformance.

Marginally improving: MTU Aero Engines raised its cash conversion rate guidance for 2026 to reflect stronger operational performance.
