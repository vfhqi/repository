EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Polenergia S.A. is executing a high-stakes transformation anchored on consolidating 720 MW of offshore wind capacity through joint-venture projects Bałtyk 2 and 3, targeting first power delivery in 2027 and aiming to reach PLN 1.6 billion in EBITDA by 2030. The company has streamlined its portfolio by exiting underperforming hydrogen ventures, electromobility operations, and the prosumer solar segment, while simultaneously securing 80% of 2027 generation at PLN 402/MWh through long-term power purchase agreements and a 25-year contract for difference at PLN 492.32/MWh for Bałtyk 1. However, the investment case remains burdened by severe nearterm headwinds including 22% year-on-year revenue contraction in Q1 2026, 1,378.7 GWh of grid curtailments in 2025, and exposure to EUR 280 million in contingent parent equity guarantees for offshore construction cost overruns.

Leadership stability has improved significantly under CEO Adam Purwin, who assumed the role on January 1, 2025, after three CEO transitions in under two years, while employee turnover plummeted from 32.55% in 2022 to 11.53% in 2023 through professionalized HR business partnering programs and strengthened compliance frameworks. The shareholding structure shows no active activist presence, with ownership tightly controlled by Mansa Investments at 43.65%, BIF IV Europe Holdings at 32.04%, and institutional pension funds

Allianz Polska OFE and Nationale-Nederlanden OFE holding 7.10% and 5.92% respectively. Strategic portfolio optimization proceeded through the PLN 139.7 million sale of gas-fired Elektrociepłownia Nowa Sarzyna to Axpo and the divestment of Polenergia Fotowoltaika, with management explicitly stating funds will realign to highreturn assets.

(Poland) The materiality of change signal is highly material as Polenergia transitions from a diversified renewable energy operator into a pure-play large-scale offshore and onshore wind developer. Management executed a gradual withdrawal from electromobility and hydrogen activities, abandoning the H2Silesia project with a PLN 5.4 million impairment loss and halting the eFuels project after extended delivery times and limited component availability. The revised strategy concentrates on Bałtyk 1, 2, and 3 projects totaling up to 3 GW, supported by PLN 4.6 billion in planned capital expenditures through 2030, while terminating all retail B2C operations by merging Polenergia Sprzedaż into Polenergia Obrót to simplify structure and increase efficiency in signing long-term PPAs.

Fourth, profile and balancing cost inflation presents a MEDIUM plausibility risk as the market cost of solar profiles reached 36.6% in 2025 versus 26.4% in 2024, while grid operators ordered 1,378.7 GWh of renewable curtailments in 2025 that inflated balancing costs and limited actual output. Fifth, Romanian onshore expansion shows MEDIUM plausibility through the development of up to 685.6 MW of wind projects in Tulcea with average wind speeds of 8.0 m/s, though ready-to-build status was pushed back to mid-2027 with final investment decisions targeted in 2027.

Plausible problems to monitor

Polenergia's most recent quarterly performance revealed material earnings pressure as Q1 2026 consolidated sales contracted by 22% year on year to PLN 924.9 million, with onshore wind EBITDA slumping by PLN 31.1 million due to poor wind speeds and lower electricity prices, compressing adjusted net profit to PLN 14.8 million

EBITDA fell to PLN 509.9 million from PLN 642.1 million in FY2024, marking a 20.6% decline that significantly underperforms regional European utility peers [Cross-ref: Section 5]. While the company defended onshore wind EBITDA at PLN 106.0 million in Q1 2026, this performance was achieved despite experiencing lower wind speeds than the prior year, exposing the company's vulnerability to cyclical resource volatility that is materially more severe than baseload-heavy state utilities like PGE and TAURON [Cross-ref: Section 6 + Section 7].

Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive analysis of Polenergia S.A. reveals several statistically abnormal or highly unique qualitative characteristics that distinguish the company from its regional and European peer group. These outliers represent both structural advantages and idiosyncratic risks that materially affect the forward investment thesis. The first major outlier is the dramatic improvement in employee turnover, which plummeted from 32.55% in 2022 to 11.53% in 2023, representing a 68% reduction in annual churn within a single year.

This rate now sits substantially below the Polish utilities sector median of approximately 18-22% and contrasts sharply with the prolonged organizational instability that typically follows rapid CEO rotation cycles. The company experienced three CEO transitions in under two years between early 2024 and January 2025, yet workforce stability paradoxically strengthened rather than deteriorated during this period. The introduction of structured HR business partnering programs appears to have decoupled operational workforce retention from executive-level volatility, creating an unusual organizational resilience pattern rarely observed in mid-sized European energy developers undergoing strategic transformation [Cross-ref: Section 1].

The fourth outlier is the extreme working capital inefficiency, with trade receivable rotation stretching to 334 days in 2025 from 306 days in 2024, as development project companies are invoiced only once per year. This receivables cycle is approximately 4-5 times longer than the European renewables sector median of 60-80 days and represents a fundamental structural difference from vertically integrated utilities that invoice retail or commercial customers monthly.

The annual invoicing lag reflects the project development business model but creates significant cash conversion friction that peers with more diversified customer bases and traditional utility operations do not experience to the same extent. This working capital burden compounds during the capitalintensive offshore construction phase when cash preservation becomes critical [Cross-ref: Section 5 + Section 7].

The fifth outlier is management's complete absence of short-term annual performance forecasts despite setting an ambitious long-term 2030 target of PLN 1.6 billion EBITDA and 1.5 GW capacity, with the company formally stating it does not publish any short-term annual performance forecasts. This guidance posture is highly unusual among European renewable developers of comparable scale, where quarterly or annual EBITDA guidance bands are standard investor relations practice.

Millennium DM characterizes management as an honest-tracker archetype that establishes highly conservative long-term projections and frequently omits potential developmentstage project gains, creating systematic positive surprise runways but preventing investors from tracking nearterm execution milestones with precision. This opacity contrasts with peers like EDPR and Orsted that provide detailed annual guidance frameworks with quarterly tracking updates, reducing short-term earnings predictability despite strong strategic visibility [Cross-ref: Section 10 + Section 11].
