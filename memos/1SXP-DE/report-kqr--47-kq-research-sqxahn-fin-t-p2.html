Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the long-term pricing power of SCHOTT Pharma across volatile macroeconomic conditions remains critical for determining its future earnings trajectory.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Critical question: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: a supply shortage (2020 to 2022 pandemic period); post-pandemic destocking and overcapacity in bulk glass (2023 to 2025); industry-wide capacity additions in glass syringes and ready-to-use formats with utilisation falling (2026 to 2030); multi-year contract renewals and take-or-pay renegotiations; sudden volume cuts by a single large customer; input cost inflation in energy and labour, including a strong Swiss franc; per-dose price deflation in GLP-1 drugs as generics and lower US list prices arrive; regulatory-driven format change (EU GMP Annex 1 and the US FDA's August 2026 draft container-closure guidance)?"

Impact on Future Earnings

2.
Substantial risk: SCHOTT Pharma faces potential earnings volatility over the next three years if industry-wide glass syringe capacity outpaces demand. For instance, an industry expert at Aptar Pharma projected a 20% syringe overcapacity by 2030, which could shift pricing leverage to pharmaceutical buyers and trigger a marginsqueezing price war. This capacity surge represents a major risk to earnings per share because it threatens to erode the premium margins currently enjoyed on High-Value Solutions.
3.
Critical exposure: Peer group comparisons highlight how sudden volume adjustments by single anchor customers can reset corporate growth outlooks. In November 2026, Stevanato lowered its 2027 organic growth guidance to low-single digits after experiencing a volume reduction for Nexa syringes from a major GLP-1 customer. This precedent demonstrates that customer concentration represents a significant risk to near-term earnings per share unless contract protections are enforced because switching costs prevent rapid volume backfilling.
4.
Accelerated adoption: Regulatory mandates like the revised European Good Manufacturing Practices Annex 1 are structurally transforming primary packaging requirements. According to a Deutsche Bank research report in November 2026, these regulations support customer conversion from bulk vials to sterile, ready-to-use cartridges and prefilled syringes. This format shift acts as a positive driver for earnings because ready-to-use formats carry substantial margin premiums and simplify the customer's downstream manufacturing operations.
5.
Pricing durability: Historical precedents from top-tier suppliers like West Pharmaceutical Services show that premium, specialized components maintain strong pricing resilience. A Deutsche Bank report in October 2026 noted that West realized price contributions of over 400 basis points in the third quarter of 2026, even though its long-term pricing model assumes annual increases of 2% to 3%. This suggests that specialized primary packaging companies can defend their margins against input cost inflation.
6.
Related question: Another closely related query is how the transition from single-dose autoinjectors to multi-dose pens affects overall packaging volume and unit economics. A Morgan Stanley analysis from August 2026 suggested that a U.S. shift toward multi-dose formats could create a 30 to 60 basis point drag on revenue growth because multi-dose pens require fewer syringe barrels. Consequently, any rapid format shift directly impacts the trajectory of earnings per share because pen packaging utilizes far fewer high-value prefilled syringes.
7.
Other side of the story: Contrary evidence indicates that the primary packaging market remains highly sticky and protected by formidable regulatory barriers, which significantly mitigates the threat of price erosion. A Deutsche Bank research report in October 2026 emphasized that once a drug manufacturer selects a primary container, the regulatory hurdles and stability testing costs required to switch vendors are prohibitively high. Therefore, despite the looming threat of syringe overcapacity, established players like SCHOTT Pharma are wellpositioned to defend their baseline earnings.
