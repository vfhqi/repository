Bufab (BUFAB-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results modestly strengthen the earnings half of the existing case and leave its verdict where it was: the business delivered more than the case assumed, the shares fell anyway, and at SEK 131.6 the price is still well above the level at which the case says the return clears the bar.

1. What changed

Bufab reported the third quarter of 2026 on 23 October 2026. Against what the case assumed:

  • Organic growth 6.5 percent, up from 5.3 percent in Q2 2026, against a Bloomberg consensus of 5.0 percent and after a 50 basis point drag from refunded customs duties in the Americas. The case's Key Driver asks for about 4 percent; its modal second half assumed about 5 percent.
  • Gross margin 34.3 percent, a record, up from 32.7 percent a year earlier, the thirteenth consecutive quarter of year-on-year gains. The case's alarm level is 32.5 percent.
  • Adjusted EBITA SEK 349 million, margin 15.6 percent (14.2 percent a year earlier), 5.4 percent above consensus of SEK 331 million. The case's modal second-half margin was 14.8 percent. Operating expenses were 18.7 percent of sales against 18.9 percent.
  • EPS SEK 1.16 against SEK 0.75; operating cash flow SEK 370 million against SEK 293 million; adjusted net debt to EBITDA 2.2 times, after paying SEK 420 million for D.C. Iron in July 2026 (plus an earn-out of up to SEK 103 million over 2027 to 2030).
  • The mix is still polarised. Asia-Pacific organic growth was 28.4 percent with a 21.1 percent operating margin; the Americas were minus 8.0 percent, minus 4.3 percent before the customs refunds; construction, furniture and automotive stayed weak.
  • Two things moved the other way. Management said Asian purchase prices are now somewhat higher, a reversal of the favourable terms of 2024 and 2025. The CBAM provision reached SEK 72 million for the nine months, to be paid in cash in 2027.
  • The backdrop the case leaned on has shifted. The review gives the Eurozone manufacturing PMI at 52.9 in September and 52.7 in October 2026, and Germany at 54.2. The case was written on PMIs below 50.

The sell-side response in the review is one broker's. DNB Carnegie kept Buy and its SEK 150 target on 26 October 2026, raised 2027 and 2028 adjusted EPS by 2 to 3 percent (to SEK 5.8 and SEK 6.5), trimmed 2026 by 1 percent, and raised EBITA by 2 percent to SEK 1,331 million, SEK 1,549 million and SEK 1,700 million for 2026 to 2028. It also warned that gross margin gains get harder over the next 18 months. A 5.4 percent EBITA beat bought a 2 percent upgrade.

The shares were SEK 132.6 on 26 October 2026 and SEK 131.6 on 9 November 2026, against the case's SEK 145.40. The 24-month-forward multiple is 23.2 times against the case's 25.9 times, on forward consensus EPS that has barely moved (SEK 5.64 against SEK 5.61).

2. The challenge

Reading one: the route to compounding is organic leverage, not deals, and the case never priced that route. The case conceded the bull's path only if the acquisition pace doubled. These results suggest a different route. Organic growth reached 6.5 percent with the Americas shrinking and the cyclical end-markets still weak, and the PMI has now printed above 50. If the cyclical half of the book merely stops falling, growth of 7 to 8 percent is plausible without a single acquisition, on a cost base management says barely moves with volume. DNB Carnegie's version of this is 8 percent organic growth in 2027 and 2028, a 35.0 percent gross margin, and EBITA of SEK 1.9 billion by 2028 against its base of SEK 1.7 billion. It also puts the 2028 margin at 16.5 percent, above the 14 to 16 percent ceiling the case treats as binding. For this to be right, the PMI prints must persist into real orders, the Americas must turn, and the gross margin must hold above 34 percent. It matters more now than at the memo date because the price is lower: on the case's own bull arithmetic, the three-year return from SEK 131.6 is about 18 percent a year at today's 23.2 times and about 22 percent a year at the case's 25.9 times.

Reading two: the margin test the case thinks it has passed is only now beginning. The case answered the "pricing cycle, not a moat" objection with twelve quarters of gains through deflation. But deflation is the easy direction: holding price while costs fall. The review says that phase ended in this quarter. With working capital at the case's figure of 37.9 percent of sales, the goods sold in Q3 were largely bought months earlier at the old prices, so a record gross margin in the first quarter of dearer purchasing is what a stock lag would produce, and proves little. Asia-Pacific's 6.0 point gross-margin gain is attributed to purchasing savings, the leg now reversing. The sensitivity is large: one point on purchase prices is about SEK 50 million of annual operating profit, close to 4 percent of DNB Carnegie's 2026 EBITA. For this reading to be right, the gross margin has to roll over between Q4 2026 and Q2 2027 as dearer stock reaches cost of goods. The case's 32.5 percent alarm would catch it late; the review's test, a fall of more than 100 basis points year on year, would catch it sooner.

Reading three: the market has stopped paying for beats. The case named the upgrade cycle ending as a Key Risk and assumed a miss would end it. Instead a clean beat was followed by a fall of about 9 percent from the case's price, and the multiple is already at the 23.0 times the case expected at eighteen months. Either the case's de-rating has arrived early, in which case its negative leg is largely spent and its return arithmetic is too gloomy from here; or the market is moving to the case's uncleared branch faster than modelled, in which case the five-year median of 19.1 times, about SEK 108 on today's forward EPS, is a plausible destination with no company miss at all. We cannot tell these apart on the evidence. Both say the same thing about the wait: the case's re-open level of about SEK 110 can plausibly be reached on de-rating alone, without the earnings path breaking.

A smaller question we cannot knock down. SEK 420 million upfront for a business with GBP 14.8 million of 2025 sales (SEK 49 million in its first quarter) is roughly twice sales. At the 6 to 7.5 times EV/EBITA the case assumes Bufab pays, that needs an EBITA margin near 30 percent. The review says only "significantly above" the 14 percent target. Either D.C. Iron is exceptional or the price of the acquisition engine has risen.

The obvious bear reading, that growth is contract-led and about to fade, is not put up: an acceleration to 6.5 percent with order-led niches broadening is evidence against it this quarter, though the review does not split out contract phasing.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on, should carry the other brokers' estimates and targets, so it settles whether the peer-premium argument is one broker's or the consensus, and how much of the beat reaches consensus EPS. It carries no new company data: nothing on purchase costs, D.C. Iron's profit, or the price-volume split.
  • Reading two is settled by the Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 gross margins. The full-year report is expected in early February 2027 on last year's pattern. A margin holding near 34 percent with dearer stock in cost of goods would make the pricing structural on the hard test.
  • Reading one is settled by the November and December PMI prints (the case's sell-side test is three to four above 50), then Q4 organic growth, the Americas' turn, and the cost ratio staying below 18.7 percent.
  • Reading three is settled only by the price. The case re-looks below about SEK 110; the shares are about 16 percent above that.
  • Not committed by the company: the price-volume split, contract sizes, and D.C. Iron's margin. Management has declined the first two before. Unless the acquisition note in the annual report gives the third, nothing scheduled answers it.
