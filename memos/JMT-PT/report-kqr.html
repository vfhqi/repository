The central investment question confronting analysts of Biedronka and Dino Polska centers on whether Polish basket deflation is easing or deepening in the third and fourth quarters of 2026, what is driving it, how many stores will be opened in 2027, how many Biedronka stores are operational in Slovakia against the target of 35 to 50 by late 2026, and what return new stores earn during the price war. This investigation draws on a comprehensive dataset of seven distinct companies, twelve expert call transcripts, and over twenty specialist broker reports from late 2026 to determine whether these retailers remain high-growth roll-outs or are becoming price-war losers.

Polish basket deflation reached its peak of 7% in Q3 2026 as intense promotional wars and agricultural oversupply drove prices down, with Biedronka basket deflation deepening from 6% in the prior quarter. The primary drivers of this deflationary pressure were negative PPI in key food categories like pork and dairy, combined with falling Polish pork prices caused by waning Chinese import demand, which spread deflation across categories.

Competitive intensity escalated as Dino Polska increased its overlap with Biedronka's price investments to 70% in 2026. However, broker research from Jefferies and Bernstein in late 2026 argues that industry food inflation will rebound to 1.5% in 2027 after bottoming at negative 4.8% in 2026, with higher food price inflation expected to reach Polish food retailers during Q4 2026. This suggests the deflationary environment is primarily temporary and commodity-driven rather than a permanent structural shift, though Morgan Stanley warns that Polish grocery pricing will remain muted for longer than expected due to a structural supply and demand imbalance.

Store expansion plans for 2027 reveal divergent strategies between the two major players. Biedronka expects to maintain its expansion rate by targeting more than 120 net new stores in Poland in 2026, with plans to open smaller formats under 250 square meters to penetrate affluent and low-density catchments in 2027. In contrast, Bernstein Research's model expects Dino Polska to add exactly 400 stores in 2027 to reach 3,813 total stores, with management expecting to grow its store network by a percentage in the teens in 2026. However, both companies face increasing market saturation challenges as modern grocery market penetration approaches 90% and the share of independent stores continues to decline.

The Slovakian expansion represents a critical test of Biedronka's international scalability, but progress has been substantially slower than originally planned. Biedronka operated just 17 Slovakian stores as of June 2026 and opened only 2 stores in Slovakia during first half 2026, having revised its expansion downward to open only 35 stores in Slovakia during late 2026 against an original target of 50 stores. The Slovakian operation is supported by Voderady distribution center equipped with advanced technology, with current infrastructure possessing scope to serve 200 stores over time.

The substantial delay in store openings creates significant uncertainty about whether Biedronka can successfully replicate its low-cost discount model outside its core Polish market. New store profitability faces severe compression during the ongoing price war, with divergent outcomes for the two competitors.
