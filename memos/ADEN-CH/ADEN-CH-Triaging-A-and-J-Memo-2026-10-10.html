[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Adecco is a cyclical recovery case with self-help inside a leveraged, low-quality business, and the shares have already been bought through the first part of that recovery. At CHF 23.84 the modal return is about 8 to 11 percent a year, half the framework's bar, because the yield and the earnings growth are real but the re-rating waits on a balance sheet that takes two years to repair while a scrip dividend dilutes the share count.

  • The change is external and cyclical, and it has arrived at the company.
    • European temporary-staffing demand fell for about two years and has turned.
    • Adecco has grown organically for five consecutive quarters, +5.3 percent in Q1 2026 and +5.6 percent in Q2 2026, against Randstad's +1.9 percent in the same quarter.
    • The turn is broad: Americas +12 percent, North American SME +23 percent, APAC +10 percent, Iberia +22 percent in Q2 2026
      • France, the largest market at 24 percent of revenue, was -1 percent.
    • The visibility is two quarters, not two years.
      • Belgian temporary-staffing hours fell 7.6 percent year on year in August 2026, worse than July, and comparables harden by about 300 basis points from Q3 2026.
  • The internal change is mostly delivered, which is why it drops through.
    • The cost base was cut by over EUR 200 million against 2022 (EUR 174 million delivered against a EUR 150 million target), SG&A sits at 3.2 percent of revenue, and the agentic AI programme covers 50 percent of revenue with a 70 percent target for December 2026.
    • So Q2 2026 EBITA rose 21 percent organically on 5.6 percent revenue growth, a 64 percent organic drop-through, and adjusted EPS rose 31 percent to EUR 0.61.
    • What is not there: no new chief executive (in post since July 2022), no raised financial guidance (a 3 to 6 percent EBITA corridor with a 3 percent floor), and a balance sheet being repaired rather than used.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned up on three of four lines and the fourth is the business model.
    • Revenue growth, operating margin and leverage are all improving against the last three years
    • Cash conversion fell to 83 percent trailing twelve months because 6 percent organic growth absorbs working capital before it is paid for.
    • The gross margin, the line the cost programme cannot reach, fell 30 basis points year on year to 18.6 percent in Q2 2026 on enterprise mix.
      • Everything in the margin case depends on that line stabilising.
  • The change has travelled the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain into one quarter of financial statements and not into the sell-side's estimates.
    • The chief executive's choices (the cost programme, the AI deployment, the pruning) became actions, the actions moved the key operating metrics (SG&A 3.2 percent of revenue, Akkodis Germany utilisation 91 percent, DSO 53 days), and Q2 2026 printed the result.
    • Consensus FY2026 EPS has moved +0.5 percent in the three months since a 22 percent EPS beat, after a 12.5 percent cut over twelve months.
      • Price targets rose 18 percent in the same three months, chasing the share price rather than the estimates.
  • The price has travelled further than the chain.
    • The shares rose from CHF 14.54 in April 2026 to CHF 23.84, +34 percent over three months, and the 24-month-forward multiple moved from the two-year median of 7.85x to 8.73x, the 81st percentile of two years and the 59th of five.
    • That multiple is struck on consensus earnings that assume a margin recovery to about 4 percent EBITDA and a flat share count.
      • On our earnings, which carry the scrip's dilution, the stock is at about 9.1x.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Adjusted EPS of EUR 2.46 in FY2026 (consensus) rising to about EUR 2.66 in FY2027 and EUR 2.87 in FY2028 on our modal path, about 8 percent a year: 3 points from revenue, 5 from margin, 1 from the lines below EBITDA, minus 1.5 from the scrip.
    • Consensus has about 11 percent a year to EUR 3.05 because it appears to carry no dilution.
    • On that path, with a 4.2 percent cash yield and a multiple blended between the five-year and ten-year medians, the 'Modal return' is 8.4 percent a year over eighteen months and 10.9 percent a year over three years.
      • The framework's anchor for 10 to 12 percent a year is D.
    • The plausibly worse earnings path, a gross margin that keeps sliding so that the EBITDA margin stays at 3.7 percent, gives FY2028 EPS of about EUR 2.54 and a return of about -5.6 percent a year over eighteen months: an F.
      • The bull path, consensus delivered without dilution and a re-rating to Randstad's multiple, gives 23 to 32 percent a year.
      • The skew is wide and not favourable enough to pay for the width.
  • What it costs.
    • CHF 23.84, or 8.73x 24-month-forward consensus earnings, 9.4x FY2027 consensus EPS of EUR 2.71 and 10.3x FY2026 EUR 2.46, a 23 percent discount to Randstad's 11.2x that the leverage (2.7x against 1.3x) and the lower return on capital (9.0 against 11.3 percent) largely explain.
    • The case clears the 20 percent bar on our modal path only at or below about EUR 20 to 22 on the block's quote, 14 to 21 percent below today, which is where the shares traded in June and July 2026.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Trough on Trough', caught six months late.
    • Demand troughed, the company and its competitors right-sized, pricing never broke, estimates were cut and guidance rebased, and volume is now returning onto the smaller base.
    • The two mandatory conditions that describe the price (a bottom-quartile multiple; capitulation) held in April 2026 and do not hold now.

  • The case therefore is coherent and not compelling.

    • Coherent because every link from volume to gross profit to EBITA is published and visible quarter by quarter
    • Not compelling because the modal return is about half the bar and the risk that the volume is the research's "false friend" (enterprise-led growth that dilutes the gross margin while the scrip dilutes the share count, in a softening European labour market) is live.

  • Rating = D, because the change is ordinary for its sector and partly delivered ('Change Forces' C), the outputs are improving from a low base with sell-side upgrades possible rather than probable ('Financial Outputs' C), and the 'Modal return' on the modal path is about 8 to 11 percent a year against a 20 percent bar (E 3 D), so the expected change is not material enough at this price

    • The three Elements combine as a chain whose weakest link, the return, sets the level.
    • Not C, which was live: C is the rating on consensus earnings with the scrip ignored and the multiple taken straight to the ten-year median of 9.5x within eighteen months, a path we show at (E 3, BB 1) and do not adopt because it gives the deleveraging a certainty the evidence does not.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against this rating is the bull's, and it is strong.
    • Adecco is the first European generalist staffer back in organic growth, five quarters running and 370 basis points ahead of Randstad.
    • It trades at 8.6x earnings that themselves sit at a ten-year margin low, the 29th percentile of its own ten-year range and a 23 percent discount to Randstad.
    • It yields 4.2 percent.
    • It has removed about EUR 200 million of cost that does not return when volume does, and the 64 percent drop-through of Q2 2026 shows what that cost base does.
    • It is deleveraging at about 0.5x a year.
    • The sell-side is still 46 percent Hold-or-worse with estimates flat after a 22 percent beat, so the upgrade cycle has not started
      • And the Belgian series is one small country in one month.
    • On that reading consensus earnings are delivered without dilution (the scrip ends in 2027 as leverage passes 2x) and the multiple goes to Randstad's 11x within eighteen months: EUR 3.38 of 24-month-forward earnings at 11x plus EUR 1.07 of dividend is EUR 38.3, a 51 percent return, 32 percent a year, an A.

    • Bernstein's "Into the up-cycle" and Deutsche Bank's "Recovery broadens, value remains" are the research's versions of it.

    • What we concede: the cost base is real and the drop-through is real

      • The valuation is low against the long history and against the one true peer
      • The sell-side has not yet moved its estimates
      • And if leverage reaches 2.2x by mid-2027 the fair multiple is the ten-year median of 9.5x, so our 8.9x blend at eighteen months is too low by about 7 percent.
      • At 9.5x the eighteen-month return is 13.0 percent a year, a C, and that is the conceded floor of this case.
    • Why we hold the view: the three things the bull needs to go right at once (the gross margin inflecting; the leverage condition; the AI productivity staying with the recruiter rather than passing to the client as price) are each at even odds or worse, and they are correlated through one driver, European hiring, which the latest monthly data say is softening.
      • The dilution is a fact (5.27 million new shares in 2026, 3.1 percent) that consensus appears not to carry, so the "cheap" earnings are about 3 percent too high per share before anything else.
      • The discount to Randstad is mostly leverage and lower returns, which on the company's own plan take until end-2027 to close.
      • Our modal path accepts consensus revenue and consensus margin and adds only the dilution, and it gives 8 to 11 percent a year
      • The bull path needs the price to repeat the re-rating it has already had.
    • Where the bull would win: FY2026 leverage below 2.5x with free cash flow above EUR 350 million, and two quarters of gross margin above 19.0 percent with EBITA above 3.2 percent.
      • Both are testable from the next two results, 2026-11-12 and February 2027.

External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the forces acting on Adecco from outside are, on balance, a modest tailwind in the near term (early-cycle volume recovery, firm pricing, talent scarcity) and a headwind in the medium term (AI disintermediation of clerical and permanent placement, a fragmenting market, a softening European macro), and none meets the 'Demand Inflection' test of sector-level change with two-plus years of visibility.
  • Direction by force: own cycle, tailwind (B)
    • End-market demand, tailwind now and fragile (C)
    • Macroeconomic, headwind and more for Adecco than for the universe (D)
    • Disruption, headwind with a company-specific offset (C)
    • Competition, neutral (C)
    • Supply side, tailwind to price and constraint to volume (C)
    • Political and regulatory, mixed and net neutral (C)
    • Currency, a persistent modest headwind (C).
  • For the case: the forces that are with the thesis are the near-term ones
    • The forces against it are the structural ones.
    • The eighteen-month horizon favours the thesis, the three-year horizon does not.

  • Sector peers: Randstad +1.9 percent and ManpowerGroup +6.0 percent constant currency in Q2 2026, both beating consensus.
  • Permanent placement: gross profit trend improved from -7 percent in Q1 2026 to -1 percent in Q2

Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because a macro force, European industrial and labour-market softness in a late-cycle rates squeeze, acts on Adecco more than on the universe: about 40 percent of revenue is European manufacturing, logistics and automotive, France is 24 percent of revenue and its largest market, and the direction of the force this quarter is against the company.
  • Exposure: Deutsche Bank describes the macro profile as "highly skewed to European blue-collar industries", with manufacturing, logistics and automotive about 40 percent of revenue, a higher cyclical exposure than the UK white-collar peers.
    • France runs a sub-2 percent underlying operating margin with growth weighted to low-margin enterprise accounts.
  • Direction: Eurozone GDP 0.9 percent in 2026 and labour costs +3.8 percent in 2027 (Rabobank)
    • ManpowerGroup's survey puts hiring slowdowns most pronounced among organisations over 5,000 employees, driven by the economy rather than AI (9 December 2025).
  • Environment context: the Assistant Portfolio Manager's weekly reading of 2026-10-09 has the French sovereign spread at 152 basis points over Germany, the widest since 2011, and holds sector K.9 Underweight as "the most recession-sensitive service there is, in a regime where labour is softening".
    • We cite it as context
    • The rating rests on the 40 percent and 24 percent exposures.
  • Not C, because the exposure is measurably above the universe's and the force is currently running against the company.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the disruption force, AI in recruitment, is strengthening in both directions at once: it is Adecco's own cost lever, deployed faster than any product in its history, and it is the mechanism by which clients and platforms bypass intermediaries in clerical and permanent placement
    • On the evidence to date neither side has been disproved.
  • Deployment: agentic AI across 50 percent of revenue by Q2 2026, target raised to 70 percent by December 2026
    • 2.2 million agent conversations
    • Fill rates +10 percent
    • Candidate submission time -40 percent
    • "25 to 35 percent" recruiter time savings
    • A fixed-cost, unlimited-volume licence with Salesforce across 40 countries and 27,000 employees (15 September 2026).
  • Financial evidence: gross profit per recruiter rose only 2 percent year on year (UBS), so the productivity has not reached the financial line yet.
  • Disintermediation: Jefferies holds that automated tools "easily replicate core repeatable tasks" and that clients will move recruitment in-house and demand to share the gains (28 January and 11 March 2026)
    • Morgan Stanley points to the OpenAI and Indeed partnership putting job discovery in ChatGPT and to displaced entry-level white-collar roles.
    • Randstad routes about EUR 4 billion of annualised revenue through digital marketplaces and its Zorgwerk platform runs at two to three times branch productivity.
  • Not D, because the deployment is real, fast and fixed-cost, and the one test of disintermediation that would matter, temporary-volume loss to AI, shows no evidence yet (Jefferies: AI job destruction "remains speculative").
    • Not B, because the per-recruiter gross profit figure says the benefit is not yet in the numbers.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity among the majors is unchanged and rational while boutiques and platforms keep taking share at the edges
    • It is neither easing nor tightening in a way that moves the case.
  • Pricing conduct: pricing "firm across all segments", the bill-to-wage spread positive and improved sequentially in Q2 2026 (the CFO)
    • Randstad "has never competed on price"
    • Hays cut consultant headcount 14 percent in FY2026 and PageGroup 1.6 percent quarter on quarter rather than cutting price.
  • Share leakage: the top three global staffers' combined share fell from 19 to 11 percent over fifteen years (Morgan Stanley, 4 February 2026)
    • Adecco lost 3.5 points of global share since the financial crisis against Randstad's 2.
    • Expert calls report clients cutting reliance on large agencies "by 50 percent for specialised tech roles" and Randstad gaining in mid-senior roles.
  • Own share gain: 160 basis points against major competitors in Q2 2026, outgrowing the market in 14 of the past 16 quarters.
    • We share the leadership research's reading that this is "mechanically driven by large-client enterprise dilution rather than structural competitive advantage": the share comes with a lower gross margin.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the supply side (candidates and recruiters) is scarce in the skilled segments, which holds price but caps volume, and abundant in the generalist segments, where it gives labour little leverage: a tailwind to gross margin per placement and a constraint on growth at once.
  • Skilled scarcity: pricing "sticking close to all-time highs" on talent scarcity in engineering and technical roles (Morgan Stanley)
    • Aerospace skilled-talent bottlenecks lift margins and overtime costs 8 to 15 percent (expert call)
    • 72 percent of employers report difficulty finding specialised technical skills (Water Tower Research).
  • Recruiters: attrition about 30 percent a year across the sector, 20 percent at Adecco
    • 10 percent of recruiters on flexible contracts
    • Specialised AI and technical role compensation up 10 to 15 percent.
  • Long run: Rothschild projects a labour-supply deficit of 12 percent over the next decade with AI offsetting 6.6 points, a net 5.4 percent deficit (7 July 2026): structural scarcity is the decade-long support for the business, not an eighteen-month force.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the regulatory changes are many, small and in both directions, and the net effect on demand, pricing and cost over eighteen months is close to nil, with compliance load favouring scale.
  • Favourable: Germany's proposed July 2026 reform extends fixed-term contracts without cause from 24 to 48 months from January 2027 (Bernstein)
    • The German Federal Collective Bargaining Compliance Act (1 May 2026) restricts public contracts to compliant firms.
  • Unfavourable: French corporate tax surcharges lift the group's effective tax rate guidance to about 34 percent (Morgan Stanley), a cash headwind of up to EUR 10 million
    • The German minimum wage +8.4 percent to EUR 13.90 (January 2026)
    • Spain's Royal Decree 723/2026 (5 October 2026) and Italy's pay-transparency decree (7 June 2026) add disclosure load
    • US immigration policy removed about 1.8 million workers from the labour supply (Jefferies, 24 September 2026).
  • Net reading: "increasing compliance complexity", with the counter that compliance is a barrier to entry that favours scaled agencies (Hays' annual report).
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because one material external dynamic is not caught above, currency, and it is a persistent, modest headwind rather than a case-changing one.
  • Translation: currency cut reported revenue by 3 percent in Q4 2025 and 4 percent in Q1 2026, and Morgan Stanley expects a 2 percent drag for FY2026.
  • Mismatch: headquarters costs and the dividend are in CHF while results are in EUR, so CHF strength (which the French sovereign stress the environment describes would feed) inflates overhead
    • A 20 percent CHF appreciation is a 4 percent EPS hit on the company's own sensitivity.
Internal forces: are there sufficiently strong internal change forces under way? (RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the internal forces are two strong (standards improvement; perimeter reduction) and one moderate (strategy change, carried by the speed of the AI rollout), against the screen's test of at least three strong or a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase: the chief executive is in his fifth year, financial guidance was narrowed rather than raised at the November 2025 Capital Markets Day, and the balance-sheet and shareholder-return lines run the wrong way for this attribute.
  • Red flags checked: not a caretaker chief executive (appointed July 2022 after a one-year process; aged 61)
    • Not a presentation-only transformation (EUR 174 million delivered against EUR 150 million)
    • The raised target is an operating KPI (agent-enabled revenue 50 to 70 percent), not a one-off guidance rise
    • No crown jewels sold (Entegee was margin-dilutive)
    • No empire-building now (the EUR 2 billion AKKA deal was 2022)
    • Leverage is dangerous by the company's own target (2.7x against 1.5x)
    • No debt-funded buyback (buybacks halted since early 2022).
    • Two of seven flags are present: the leverage, and the 2022 acquisition as its origin.
  • Not B, because the forces that would make it B (a raised financial target; a balance sheet being put to work) are absent or reversed.
    • Not D, because the cost delivery and the AI deployment speed are above the comparison set's.
Standards improvement: execution, operational measures, governance, cost discipline? (choices and actions) (KQ 5, RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because standards have measurably improved on the lines management controls: G&A savings of EUR 174 million against EUR 150 million targeted (nearly EUR 200 million cumulative against the 2022 base by end-2025), SG&A at 3.2 percent of revenue against a 3.5 percent target, DSO 53 days against Randstad's 57.6, Akkodis Germany utilisation 91 percent, selling FTEs -2 percent with contribution per selling FTE +6 percent, and a 64 percent organic drop-through in Q2 2026.
  • Overhead programme: G&A intensity was "over 4 percent" of revenue in 2022

    • Delayering cut regional management costs 20 percent.
  • One-offs: recurring restructuring charges (EUR 59 million in 2025; EUR 30 to 43 million in H1 2026) dilute the delivered savings, and the leadership research warns that cost-cutting may leave frontline teams under-resourced if demand spikes.
  • Not A, because the gross margin, the line the cost programme cannot reach, fell 30 basis points year on year in the same quarter
    • The improvement is in the overhead, not in the business.
Perimeter or composition reduction: divestments, simplification, exit of low-return units? (actions) (KQ 6, RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the perimeter is being pruned in several small, consistent steps: Entegee (US technology staffing) sold in June 2026
    • Akkodis Germany non-core units sold in late 2025, removing EUR 3 million of negative EBITA
    • General Assembly exited B2C training
    • MSP Pontoon moved from LHH into the Adecco business unit to simplify the client-facing structure
    • 450 Akkodis Germany consultants cut.
  • Redeployment: the disposal proceeds went into aerospace acquisitions (Axiscades ASD; Sogéclair Airframe and Cabin Engineering), so this is composition change more than shrinkage.
  • Not A, because the steps are small against a EUR 23 billion revenue base (Akkodis is 14 percent of group; Entegee's size is not given) and the 60-country footprint is untouched.
Strategy or strategic-priorities change, articulated and resourced? (choices) (KQ 3, RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the strategy was re-set at the November 2025 Capital Markets Day (RUN and CHANGE; AI-first delivery; a pivot of Akkodis toward aerospace and defence to 20 to 25 percent of its revenue by 2030; offshore delivery), it is articulated with KPIs and resourced (a fixed-cost enterprise AI licence; disposal proceeds redeployed), and the AI part has been executed faster than guided.
  • Delivery ahead of plan: agent-enabled revenue reached 50 percent in Q2 2026, a target set for end-2026
    • Raised to 70 percent by December 2026.
    • Aerospace and defence grew 20 percent in Q2 2026 and is 17 percent of Akkodis revenue
    • Akkodis offshore workforce from 5 percent toward 15 percent (the business research) or 50 percent (the earnings review) by end-2026, two figures the research does not reconcile.
  • Market verdict: the shares fell 11 percent on the day of the Capital Markets Day because management stopped quantifying organic growth targets by business unit (Morgan Stanley).
  • Not A, because the strategy's financial end-point, a 6 percent group EBITA margin, is an ambition the research treats as structurally difficult (Deutsche Bank), and the growth targets were withdrawn.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = D

    • Volumes are turning, pricing is holding and the three generalists behave rationally
    • But the industry has de-concentrated for fifteen years, the margin pool is migrating to platforms and specialists, and the AI thematic treats a labour intermediary as the exposed party.
    • The top three global staffers' share fell from 19 to 11 percent over fifteen years
      • Adecco lost 3.5 points, Randstad 2.
    • Bill-to-wage spreads stayed positive through a two-year downturn and the majors cut capacity rather than price.
      • That conduct is the one supportive part of the structure, and it has not yet been tested in an upturn.

    • The margin sits below its own FY2019 level of 4.3 percent and below Randstad's historical 4.1 percent
