EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This comprehensive assessment of Carlsberg's leadership, credibility, and governance yields an overall high rating for management quality, tempered by significant structural challenges that warrant close monitoring over the next eighteen to thirty-six months. Chief Executive Jacob Aarup-Andersen brings exceptionally strong credentials to Carlsberg, combining a rigorous financial background with a proven operational turnaround record at ISS A/S, where he delivered 22% shareholder returns and executed a successful restructuring strategy. His leadership style demonstrates direct and highly polished communication paired with genuine ownership commitment, evidenced by his DKK 10.8 million personal investment in Carlsberg shares upon appointment. Under his tenure, Carlsberg has executed a transformational strategic pivot from cost-cutting to growth, raising mid-term organic revenue targets to 4-6% and completing the £3.3 billion Britvic acquisition that fundamentally reshapes the company's European footprint and establishes a multi-beverage platform representing 30% of total volume. Delivery against financial commitments has been exceptionally strong, with Britvic cost synergies upgraded to £110 million and reaching 30% delivery in the first year, with management expecting approximately 80% cumulative realization by end-2026.

Carlsberg maintains its historical culture of underpromising and overdelivering, consistently beating initial EBIT guidance by 300 to 870 basis points almost every year since

2016. Against direct peers, performance is mixed but improving–Carlsberg outperformed Heineken but trailed AB InBev in shareholder returns during 2024 and 2025, while executing an 18.8% revenue increase in 2025 driven by the Britvic consolidation.

Governance structures demonstrate substantial strength in board composition, with the Supervisory Board comprising thirteen non-executive members and shareholder-elected representatives showing seventy-five percent independence –a ratio that exceeds Anheuser-Busch InBev's twenty-seven percent. Recent board refreshment introduced modern digital and marketing capabilities, with committees staffed by highly skilled FMCG, finance, and supply chain experts, including Audit Chair Lilian Fossum Biner bringing expert financial and consumer strategy credentials. However, the Carlsberg Foundation's 77.5% voting control with only 30% economic ownership creates a 47.5 percentage point gap that dilutes formal non-executive independence to forty-six percent across the entire board, potentially restricting independent shareholder advocacy if operational performance deteriorates.

Executive share dealing signals strong conviction, with CEO Aarup-Andersen purchasing 2,000 B-shares in openmarket transactions during September 2026 for DKK 1.69 million, increasing his total holding to 38,070 B-shares. Executive compensation structures demonstrate partial alignment with shareholders through performance shares tied to five equally weighted metrics including total shareholder return, adjusted EPS growth, organic revenue growth, ROIC, and ESG targets, with DKK 66.6 million total remuneration for executive directors in 2025.

Capital allocation discipline remains visible through suspended share buybacks to prioritize rapid deleveraging following the Britvic transaction, with management committed to reaching a 2.5x leverage target by end-2027.

Critical discrepancies emerge between management's public optimism and external evidence, particularly across Asian markets. Former regional executives reveal that growth in India underperformed due to persistent partner disputes, strategic progress in Vietnam was delayed until recently due to underinvestment, and premiumization in China is slowing, directly impacting flagship brands like Kronenbourg 1664 which faces frequent price competition.

European execution faces pressure from highly price-competitive mainstream segments in Poland where experts predict Carlsberg will become more price aggressive to maintain local volumes. The single largest risk to credibility over the next eighteen to thirty-six months is the fragility of the organic growth strategy underpinning management's ambitious pivot. Carlsberg's underlying organic beer volume growth stands at only 0.3%, forcing the company to rely heavily on low-margin soft drinks bottling operations that introduce heightened operational execution risk while the Britvic acquisition strained leverage to 3.28x net debt to EBITDA in fiscal 2025. Large-scale soft drinks bottling expansions in Kazakhstan initially diluted gross margins by 40 basis points during the first half of 2026, and regional margin contraction was driven by Kazakhstan soft drinks volumes before in-house production commenced. Additionally, weak consumer spending and adverse weather led to a 6% volume drop in China during Q2 2026, though Deutsche Bank views these market headwinds as broadly temporary, setting up solid recovery for 2027.

The EPS forecast over the next eighteen to thirty-six months faces material downside risk if European consumer demand deteriorates further or if the multi-beverage integration fails to offset structural beer volume declines at the anticipated pace. Management's conservative guidance culture led Jefferies to model 2.4% organic sales growth for 2027 against 3.5% consensus, reflecting normalized volume trends. The strongest case for delivery rests on Aarup-Andersen's proven operational turnaround playbook and faster synergy capture, while the strongest case against hinges on sluggish embedded beer volume growth of 0.3% to 2030 that undermines the revenue expansion narrative.

If execution falters, the Foundation's absolute voting control insulating management from shareholder pressure could delay necessary strategic corrections, amplifying credibility damage and prolonging earnings underperformance relative to the aggressive growth targets management has established.

Outliers worth noting

283 The second major outlier concerns the unprecedented speed and scale of Carlsberg's multi-beverage transformation under CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen. The acquisition of Britvic plc in January 2025 fundamentally shifted Carlsberg's scale in Western Europe, raising soft drinks to 30% of total volume and reducing beer concentration. This strategic pivot places Carlsberg in the top 5% of global brewers for non-beer revenue diversification, a position highly anomalous given the company's 175-year brewing heritage.

CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen raised the mid-term organic revenue growth target from 3-5% to 4-6% in February 2024, marking a pivot from cost-cutting to active growth reinvestment. As examined in Section Section 2, this represents a complete reversal of the margin-led philosophy established by predecessor Cees 't Hart, with the culture shift seeking to reward calculated risk-taking to support the Accelerate SAIL strategy, representing a sharp contrast to the marginbiased predecessor.

The transformation speed is particularly striking given Britvic cost synergies were upgraded to £110 million and reached 30% delivery in the first year, with management expecting to deliver approximately 80% of synergies by the end of 2026, placing Carlsberg's integration execution in the top decile of European M&A transactions.
