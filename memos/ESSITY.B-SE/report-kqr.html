Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The strategic review of Consumer Tissue decides whether Essity becomes the purest and earliest play on hygiene margin expansion or remains tied to pulp price volatility.
  2. The resolution of this review will directly dictate Essity's EPS development and valuation multiples over the next 18 months to 3 years. To address the core investment thesis for Sector A.4 (Household Products), this report investigates the following Key Question word-for-word: "Which outcome do brokers and industry experts now expect (a sale to a tissue company, a sale to private equity, a spin-off or a partial sale), at what price, and by when? What multiple do brokers apply to the health and hygiene business that would remain, compared with the multiple on the whole group today, and how much of the proceeds do they expect to be returned to shareholders?" DNB Carnegie argues that a successful separation would improve the remaining group's profit margins, return on capital employed, and balance sheet strength. Resolving the review would also reduce the company's structural vulnerability to volatile raw material cycles, which has historically driven significant margin instability. Jefferies Research remains cautious on whether a separation would unlock substantial value, warning that cost pressures could offset strategic improvements. They argue that rising pulp and energy costs are highly likely

to hit profits in the near term, potentially drowning out the long-term benefits of the strategic review.
