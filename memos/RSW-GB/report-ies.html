Implications — Renishaw (RSW-GB): FY2026 results, 23-Sep-26

These results strengthen the existing case on the side it already rated strong and press harder on the side it already rated weak, so the net is a modest strengthening rather than a shift, with one exception the case did not anticipate at all: capital expenditure is being roughly doubled into the very order book the case's false-friend flag says may not be real.

What changed

The case assumed a semiconductor-led recovery into a guidance range of £775m to £805m revenue and £145m to £165m adjusted PBT, capex settling at c. £40m a year for three years, no capital return, Position Measurement needing to hold above 20% growth, and a fourth quarter that on Jefferies' model needed 22% growth to reach the top of the range. What was reported, for the year to 30 June 2026:

  • Revenue £815.8m, up 14% reported and 17% constant-currency, above the top of the April range; flat against the consensus reset after the July pre-announcement. Adjusted PBT £168.0m, 1% ahead of that consensus and above the top of the case's range. Adjusted operating profit £152.9m, up 36%; margin 18.7% against 15.7%, with the second half at 21.2%, above the group's own 20% long-term target. ROIC 17.5% against 13.2%, above the 15% through-cycle target. Sterling cost 2.2 points of margin; at constant currency operating profit would have been 62.6% higher.
  • Position Measurement revenue £260.9m, up 26% (29% constant-currency), margin 27.4%, up 490 basis points. Sales fell short of Jefferies' £268.4m; the margin beat its 26.5%. On the review's own sequence (12% at the half, 22% at nine months, 26% for the year) the fourth quarter ran faster than any earlier point in the year. The CFO said the growth was entirely volume, with no price in it. Demand visibility in the division has extended to over one quarter from the usual one month.
  • Industrial Metrology, 55% of sales: revenue £447.4m, margin 17.1%, down 40 basis points, standard sensor sales flat on weak European machine-tool demand; below Jefferies' £451.6m and 18% margin. Specialised Technologies: revenue £107.5m, up 43%, margin 4.4% against a 13.2% loss, every product line profitable; Jefferies had £96.7m and break-even.
  • Cash £291.0m against £273.6m, above consensus; cash conversion 79% against 91%, on working capital for fourth-quarter orders. Capex £38.3m against £46.3m. FY2027 capex guided to £65m to £75m to double encoder production assets; Hall 4 at Miskin stays mothballed.
  • A 70.0p special interim dividend, c. £51m, and a 65.2p final, up 5%. The CFO urged investors not to project the special as recurring policy. Remuneration re-weighted to cash conversion and ROIC; a new long-term incentive plan goes to the 2026 AGM. Fixed cost savings of £21m delivered on a 7% headcount cut, with UK wage and national insurance inflation described as an ongoing headwind rather than netted.
  • The global Dynamics 365 ERP rollout is paused to the end of the calendar year after a UK deployment judged unfit for global scale; the CFO has taken over its governance. The CEO said higher-end laser encoders are export-controlled and direct sales to Chinese electronics manufacturers have been restricted for 18 months; China is 25% of revenue. Sir David Grant is confirmed permanent Chair from April 2026 for up to two years, with a successor search running to 2028. A strong start to FY2027 was flagged.
  • Sell-side: Jefferies reiterated Buy, target 6,090p, having moved from Hold at 3,450p in July to Buy on 10 September with EBITA estimates raised 24%, 33% and 35% for FY2026 to FY2028, and now calling mid-20% EBITA margins feasible. Deutsche Bank held at Hold, target 5,800p after five raises from 3,940p in six months, modelling a 20.6% margin by FY2028 and warning the demand may not last. Only those two brokers published. Two expert calls in the review put true semiconductor-equipment demand at 30% to 40% of the current order surge on customer double- and triple-ordering, and describe tool vendors holding elevated component inventory with a near-term plateau highly probable. Against that, ASML raised 2026 guidance to EUR 43bn to 46bn and is adding 30% EUV capacity for 2027, Bernstein has TSMC capex up 57% to US$64bn, and Applied Materials is doubling output capacity by 2028.
  • Price 5,600p on 24-Sep-26, against the case's 4,948p entry point and above every target the case knew (3,450p to 4,960p); 52-week high 5,810p; 24.6% above the 200-day average; relative-strength percentile 92. The dashboard pins technical momentum B, sell-side earnings momentum A and valuation D; P/E 22.5x at the 52nd percentile of its own ten-year history. The dashboard's revision fields are dated 16-Jul-26 and predate both the September upgrades and the print.

The challenge

The results admit more than one reading, and the two that matter most are ones the case does not hold.

First reading: the order book is the false friend, and the company has just committed to build into it. The case's false-friend flag (E8.RA8 [F]) rests on Renishaw's own history, the 2009-11 margin swing and the FY2023 optical-component overstocking, and it holds that encoder call-offs can be cancelled at will. It does not hold that the orders now on the book are two or three times true demand. Two expert calls now say exactly that: customers double- and triple-ordering on supply anxiety, true demand 30% to 40% of the surge, tool vendors already sitting on elevated component stock. Visibility stretching to over one quarter, which the sell-side reads as strength, is also what capacity-securing over-ordering looks like from the supplier's side. Management's response is to guide capex to £65m to £75m, 1.7 to 2.0 times FY2026's £38.3m, specifically to double encoder assets, in a division whose growth was entirely volume and carried no price. The case assumed c. £40m a year and counted the finished capital cycle as its clearest structural positive inside an otherwise weak base (E1.RA2 CQ2 [B]). That assumption is now wrong. Cash conversion has already fallen to 79% on working capital, and Renishaw carries stock at c. 25% of sales by policy. In this reading FY2026 is the peak print, FY2027 brings a plateau with new fixed cost arriving as it does, and the 37% historical operational gearing the review cites runs the other way. For it to be right: the expert calls have to be broadly right; ASML's third-quarter release on 15 October 2026 or TSMC's October call has to show deceleration; and Position Measurement order intake has to fall sequentially at the February 2027 interims. Against it: ASML, TSMC and Applied Materials are all adding capacity through 2027-28 on customer commitments, the CEO framed the capacity investment as cautious and cyclically aware, and Hall 4 is still mothballed. It is a plausible reading, not a probable one, but its cost if right is severe because the gearing is symmetrical.

Second reading: the margin floor has reset, and the case's central risk is a stale precedent. The case's key risk is bi-directional elasticity: 3.5% margin in 2009, 10.0% in FY2020, 27% in 2011, 24.1% in FY2022. The strongest form of the alternative runs: four things have changed permanently since those episodes. £21m of fixed cost is out on 7% fewer heads. Mix has shifted toward a 32%-of-sales division earning 27.4%. Specialised Technologies has gone from a 13.2% loss to profit in every product line. The neurological drag is closed. The second half printed 21.2%, a margin Deutsche Bank does not model the full year reaching until FY2028, and ROIC 17.5% clears the group's own threshold. And the multiple has not expanded: P/E is 22.5x at the 52nd percentile of its own history, from the case's 24.3x at the 63rd, while the price rose 13%. The rally has been earned by estimates, not by re-rating, which is the opposite of the "Cyclical Peak Trap" the case matched to Thematic 1 and of Thematic 4's "multiple expanding faster than earnings". Under this reading the already-priced read (E1.RA2 CQ4 [D]), the not-low-multiple read (CQ5 [F]) and the bear-market suitability grade (E5.RA1 [D]) all overstate the downside. For it to be right: Position Measurement margin has to hold above 25% through any demand plateau; Industrial Metrology margins have to stop drifting down; and the cost base has to hold against UK wage and national insurance inflation the review lists as ongoing and does not net. Against it: the CFO said the 26% growth had no pricing in it, so the 27.4% margin is a volume outcome under high gearing, not evidence of pricing power; Industrial Metrology, 55% of sales, compressed; and Deutsche Bank still values the stock at a 41% premium to capital goods. This reading and the first cannot both be right, and the results do not choose between them. That is the honest state of the evidence.

Third reading, which the case half-holds: the capital-return shift is policy, not an event. The case named a special dividend as the single swing factor that would move E8.RA3's shareholder-return and balance-sheet Core Questions (both [F]) and could promote the corporate-change setup to primary. It has happened. The strongest form adds that remuneration is now weighted to cash conversion and ROIC, that a long-term incentive plan is proposed where the case recorded none as a FTSE 250 outlier, that £291m of cash is barely dented by a £51m payment, and that a special dividend rather than a buyback is exactly what the case's own Deltam voting-split analysis predicts, since a buyback needs a special resolution and a dividend does not. What the case does not accommodate is the pairing: the same board returned £51m and guided capex to nearly double in the same announcement, and the CFO told investors not to project the special. A board doing both at once is either confident the cycle is structural (second reading) or returning cash at the peak while investing at the peak (first reading). For this reading to hold on its own terms: a further special or buyback has to come at the September 2027 results with cash still above c. £280m, which is the review's own test, and the LTIP has to pass the AGM.

We do not put up an alternative reading on Industrial Metrology or the ERP pause, because neither challenges the case; both confirm it. The bifurcated-demand read (E11.RA1 [C]) is exactly what printed, and the ERP was already counted as a live change item (E7.RA4 [F]). The one new point is that the second-half margin cleared 20% without the ERP or One Renishaw delivering anything, so the productivity programme is not load-bearing for the margin the case worries about; it is load-bearing for the sales-efficiency leg the review's outlier call attaches to it.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on, carries the post-print consensus: whether any broker beyond Jefferies and Deutsche Bank published, where FY2027 and FY2028 EPS and targets settle, and whether the dashboard's revision fields, still dated July, catch up. That settles MR2 and CQ4. It does not carry anything that decides between the first and second readings, because nothing the company discloses in that window bears on order quality.
  • ASML's third-quarter release, 15 October 2026, and TSMC's October call are the first external tests of the overbooking reading: any cut to ASML's EUR 43bn to 46bn range or the 30% EUV capacity plan, or sequential deceleration in TSMC capex, is the signal.
  • Renishaw's first-quarter update, which last year came on 23 October, gives the first sequential read on Position Measurement after the year-end surge, though as a trading statement it is unlikely to carry divisional margin.
  • The February 2027 interims carry the two figures that resolve the first reading: Position Measurement order intake sequentially, and whether divisional margin holds above 25%. They also show whether cash conversion recovers or the working-capital build continues.
  • Not committed to by the company: a semiconductor inventory-to-sales ratio, which is the case's named early-warning signal and which no disclosure in the review provides; any statement on a Deltam relationship agreement; and any forward commitment on capital returns beyond the CFO's caution. The recurrence test for the third reading is the September 2027 results, a year away. The ERP resumption is due at the end of the calendar year and is the one execution item with a date.
