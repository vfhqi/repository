Outliers worth noting

The comprehensive analysis of CMB.TECH reveals five material statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies that warrant heightened scrutiny. The first and most striking anomaly concerns the extraordinary magnitude of capital gains from vessel disposals, which reached USD 127.5 million in the second quarter of 2026 alone, representing an atypically high proportion of reported net profit that masks underlying operational earnings volatility.

When combined with the first quarter's USD 267.4 million in net gains from six older VLCCs and two Capesize bulk carriers, the total first-half 2026 vessel sale gains of approximately USD 395 million constitute a material anomaly that significantly distorts year-over-year earnings comparability and raises questions about the sustainability of reported profitability absent continued aggressive asset recycling (cross-reference: Section 5 Historical Earnings Trajectory, Section 6 Most Recent Earnings Delivery).

The second outlier concerns the initial net asset value dilution that occurred when CMB.TECH acquired its Golden Ocean stake in early 2025 at a premium valuation, creating an unexplained disconnect between the strategic rationale for fleet diversification and the immediate shareholder value destruction.
