Section summary
  1. Evidence from H1 2026 results confirms the Key Question is fully resolvable, showing pricing undercutting has stabilised and reversed into inflationary pass-through versus the severe FY2025 baseline. The comprehensive analysis across Chinese chemical export dynamics, IMCD's product mix vulnerability, distributor pricing mechanics, and regional competitive variations establishes that the acute margin pressure from Chinese semi-specialty undercutting experienced during FY2025 has materially eased in H1 2026, driven by a confluence of Middle East geopolitical supply shocks, Chinese anti-involution policy interventions, and export VAT rebate withdrawals that collectively raised global cost floors. In H1 2026, Chinese semi-specialty undercut eased significantly as Middle East freight disruptions and local feedstock constraints pushed product prices upward in Q2 2026. This reversal occurred despite persistent Chinese domestic overcapacity, as declining petrochemical output and depressed plant utilisation rates dropped average global chemical manufacturing operating rates from historical levels of 70% to 80% down to approximately 50% forced disciplined capacity throttling rather than relentless volume dumping. Recent tax policy changes removing or reducing export Value Added Tax rebates from 13% down to 9% or 6% have effectively raised export price floors on Chinese technicals, providing structural relief that equity consensus models have largely underappreciated. IMCD's exposed ~20% semi-specialty revenue lines stabilized during H1 2026 as the company successfully executed swift price pass-through without sacrificing long-term customer relationships. IMCD successfully preserved conversion margins at 43.3% in H1 2026 by passing through supplier price hikes and prioritizing absolute gross profit defense over percentage margin matching. Management's deliberate strategy to refuse matching predatory Chinese price cuts on low-margin volume while defending absolute gross profit euros proved effective, with group organic EBITA growth reaching +11% in Q2 2026, demonstrating that distributor pricing passthrough capabilities remained intact despite macro volatility.

Regional dynamics exhibited pronounced bifurcation across EMEA, APAC, and Americas during H1 2026. IMCD achieved a dramatic Q2 2026 organic revenue growth rebound to 10.3% in EMEA, up from -0.9% in Q1 2026, while operating EBITA in EMEA surged 19.8% organically in Q2 2026 as rapid price pass-through outpaced raw material cost inflation. European anti-dumping duties on Chinese chemical imports created structural regional price floors that enabled windfall gross profit expansion on existing inventories.

In APAC, major Chinese chemical manufacturers are increasingly abandoning fragmented trading channels in favor of structured, exclusive distribution contracts with tier-one distributors, directly contradicting consensus fears of ongoing disintermediation. However, IMCD's organic revenue growth in the Americas reached only 1.6% in Q2 2026, constrained by persistent softness in US coatings and construction end-markets, though organic EBITA growth in the Americas recovered to +2.2% in Q2 2026 from a severe 25.0% decline in Q1 2026 as pricing actions gained traction.

The stabilization of Chinese pricing undercut carries significant second and third-order implications for IMCD's earnings trajectory and valuation re-rating potential. Stabilization of Chinese pricing undercut restores IMCD's 18-month EPS compounding toward €6.50-€7.10, enabling a 25%+ IRR as valuation multiples re-rate while M&A accelerates over peers. The company's conservative leverage of 2.8x Net Debt/EBITDA provides substantial acquisition firepower to consolidate market share while peers remain balance sheet constrained.

Rising technical and regulatory complexity is forcing both Chinese producers and Western blue-chip principals to rely on IMCD's 82 application laboratories and formulatory moat, cementing customer stickiness and preventing direct-sales disintermediation despite aggressive cost competition. Material data gaps persist in customer-level inventory tracking and exact semi-specialty product line exposure, as IMCD does not disclose granular product-level revenue breakdowns beyond broad business group classifications.

However, quarterly gross profit margin monitoring provides effective empirical resolution of the underlying pricing dynamics, with conversion margin progression serving as a real-time test of the company's ability to pass on raw material cost inflation without sacrificing operating leverage. The primary residual risk centers on whether H1 2026 pricing tailwinds reflect temporary geopolitical supply chain disruptions rather than sustainable structural improvements.

If Middle East tensions resolve quickly and oil prices decline in H2 2026, Chinese producers could re-engage in aggressive price dumping and compress group conversion margins below 40%, derailing the required EPS trajectory toward €6.50-€7.10 and destroying the 25%+ IRR underwriting thesis. Additionally, if inflationary cost drivers subside rapidly, renewed Chinese capacity dumping could erode the nascent semi-specialty pricing recovery and compress gross margins back toward the depressed FY2025 baseline of 25.0%.

Notwithstanding these downside scenarios, the preponderance of evidence from H1 2026 operational results, Chinese policy interventions, and regional competitive dynamics confirms that the acute Chinese semi-specialty pricing undercut experienced during FY2025 has materially stabilized, restoring IMCD's medium-term earnings compounding trajectory and concentrated portfolio return potential for a 5-15 position long-only fund seeking 25%+ IRR outcomes over an 18-24 month investment horizon.
