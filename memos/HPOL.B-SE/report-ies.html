Hexpol (HPOL.B-SE): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results leave the case where it was, parked on price, but they change what it is parked on: the doubts about volume and about deployment ease, and the margin, which the case treated as the leg that held, becomes the live question.

1. What changed

  • Growth beat by a wide margin. Q3 2026 (July to September, reported 23 October 2026) net sales were 5,300 MSEK, up 13 percent on 4,692 MSEK in Q3 2025, with organic growth of 12 percent against a consensus of 7 percent. Volumes rose in all three business areas; Thermoplastic Compounding grew 19 percent organically to 1,566 MSEK. Automotive volumes were unchanged. North American sales rose 15 percent in SEK, Europe 10 percent, Asia 16 percent; European Rubber Compounding volumes fell.
  • The profit did not follow. Adjusted EBIT was 723 MSEK, up 5 percent, a margin of 13.6 percent against 14.7 percent a year earlier, 14.6 percent in Q2 2026 and a consensus of 14.4 percent. Adjusted EPS was 1.46 SEK against a consensus of 1.54 SEK. Rubber Compounding's adjusted EBIT margin was 14 percent against the broker's 16 percent, and the review names it as the main source of the miss. Reported EBIT was 651 MSEK after 72 MSEK of non-recurring cost from the management changes.
  • A second deal was signed. On 21 October 2026 Hexpol agreed to buy 80 percent of Symplast (Turkey; annual sales EUR 34 million) for EUR 54 million, closing expected by Q1 2027. Vipa completed on 1 October 2026. The chief executive's appointment was confirmed as permanent on 23 October 2026.
  • Balance sheet and cash. Net debt to EBITDA was 1.02x at 30 September 2026, net debt 3,263 MSEK; operating cash flow was 669 MSEK, 103 percent of EBIT. That leverage figure predates both the Vipa payment and Symplast.
  • Sell-side. Only DNB Carnegie had published: Hold and a SEK 95 target, both unchanged; 2026 adjusted EPS cut 2 percent to 6.0 SEK; 2027 and 2028 raised 2 percent to 7.0 SEK and 7.4 SEK, and the review attributes that rise to including Symplast.

Against the case's own test for this print: the drop condition (organic growth below 2 percent with a Rubber Compounding margin below 14 percent) is not met, because growth is far above it. The confirming condition needed a margin above 14 percent; Rubber Compounding sits on that line as the review rounds it, and the group is below it.

Two points of our own arithmetic. Sales were flat on Q2 2026 (5,280 MSEK to 5,300 MSEK), so the step from 8 to 12 percent organic growth is the easier comparable, which the case expected, and not a faster run-rate. And 608 MSEK of extra sales year on year produced 35 MSEK of extra adjusted EBIT, an incremental margin of about 6 percent.

2. The challenge

The case's counter-case was built around one failure: a price-led, pre-bought Q2 followed by a destock. Q3 is close to the opposite of that, so it is the case's own main doubt that weakened. The readings below are the ones it does not hold.

Reading one: the growth is real, and the new regime is buying it. The case modelled the margin as contributing nothing, flat, and treated volume recovery as the uncertain half. Q3 shows the reverse: volume arrived and brought almost no profit with it. Management says the higher operating cost is deliberate (sales coverage, R&D, IT), that Q2 and Q3 cost was at a similar level, and that this is a reasonable run-rate. In its strongest form, the reading is that captive conversions and share gains are being won with cost and with price, on excess capacity, and that the fastest-growing segment earns about 10 percent (157 MSEK on 1,566 MSEK, our arithmetic, on a perimeter changed on 1 July 2026) against 14 percent in rubber. Thermoplastics is already about 30 percent of group sales before Vipa; the case expected about 28 percent by FY2028 with it. For this to be right, cost has to keep stepping up with volume and the converted volume has to be priced below the existing book. One piece of evidence fits it: a 5 percent sales beat produced a cut to the broker's 2026 EPS and no organic upgrade beyond it. If it is right, revenue keeps beating and the case's EPS path of about 8 percent a year is the ceiling, not the base.

Reading two: the sequential margin fall is not overhead. Overhead explains the fall against last year; the review puts it at 110 basis points. It does not explain the fall against Q2. Sales were flat, cost was by management's account similar, and adjusted EBIT still fell 49 MSEK and the margin 100 basis points. What is left is gross margin: mix, price given away, or pass-through lagging an input spike the review puts at 38 to 40 percent on spot synthetic rubber and carbon black against the 2025 average. This matters more than its size suggests, because the case's Invalidate level is a sequential adjusted EBIT margin below 14 percent with pass-through cited as the cause. The first half of that condition is now met. The second is not: management cites overhead and says prices rose sequentially. For this reading to be right, Q3 gross margin has to be below Q2's. For it to be wrong, the gap has to be seasonal or mix, and the review gives no figure that settles it. We cannot knock this reading down and we cannot confirm it.

Reading three: the speed of the second deal is a warning about price, not proof of an engine. The case partly holds this, through its Alarm on a deal above 12x EBITA, but it reads a second deal as good news. Symplast is under half Vipa's size, so the case's progress trigger (a deal of Vipa's scale at or below about 10x EBITA) is not met on scale. The EBITA multiple is not in the review. The sales multiple is: about 2.0x for Symplast and 1.9x for Vipa on our arithmetic, against 0.6x to 0.8x that the review gives as the sector's history, alongside the observation that sellers push prices up because Hexpol is a known buyer. At 2.0x sales, 10x EBITA needs a margin of about 20 percent and 12x needs about 16.5 percent. Management says only that both targets earn above the group average. The gap between "above a group at 13.6 percent" and 16.5 percent is where the case's Alarm sits.

3. What it does to the case

  • Drop trigger on Q3: not met. Less pressure.
  • The pre-buy counter-case at the Investment Thesis: less pressure. A second quarter with volumes up in all three areas is not what it predicted, though easy comparables carry until Q2 2027.
  • 'No acquisition within twelve months of Vipa' (Alarm and drop): defused. The "PowerPoint transformation" flag eases, since this is the first deal signed under the new chief executive. The cleared branch's acquisition leg, held at about 60 percent, is under less pressure on activity and more on price.
  • Key Driver 'pass-through holding the EBIT margin at or above 14 percent': more pressure, and the most of anything here. The group is at 13.6 percent, below the case's line and below the company's own 14 to 16 percent target band.
  • 'Financial Outputs' and the margin component within earnings growth : more pressure. The case's forming risk, Rubber Compounding's operating-cost inflation, has formed.
  • 'Output evidence' in the Investment Setup : the same. Two quarters of growth now, but the EPS trajectory has not been upgraded organically.
  • 'Modal return' : the same. The broker's 6.0 SEK for 2026 is in line with the case's 6.05 SEK path; more of 2027 is now acquired.
  • Operator quality : the same. Permanence helps; the 72 MSEK charge and two presidents leaving by end-2027 do not.
  • Price: SEK 93.6 on 9 November 2026 against the case's SEK 97.05. The case progresses at or below about SEK 85 and clears at eighteen months at about SEK 90. Neither is crossed.

For context, the ratings store's pinned letters now differ from the memo's pins: Sell-side Momentum A against B, Thematic Fit/Momentum C against B, TSR/Valuation C against B. The last two are where the memo already argued they belonged. We do not grade them.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry the other brokers' estimates and targets, and so whether 2027 and 2028 EPS rose on anything other than Symplast, and what margin the sell-side now assumes. It is unlikely to carry the Q3 gross margin, the Symplast multiple or the captive-conversion contribution, because those are company disclosures and not estimates.
  • Q4 2026 results, probably late January 2027 on last year's pattern; the date is not confirmed. Vipa consolidates from 1 October 2026 at a margin management puts above the group's, and cost is said to be flat. If the group margin does not rise on that, reading one or two is right. The comparable is 508 MSEK of adjusted EBIT at 11.9 percent.
  • The review's own later test: an adjusted EBIT margin back above 14.5 percent by Q2 2027, the quarter that also meets the first hard growth comparable.
  • Not committed by the company: the price paid for Symplast as a multiple of profit; volume from captive conversions against market growth; thermoplastics' margin excluding acquisitions. Reading three in particular may stay open until Symplast's contribution appears in the accounts after closing.
