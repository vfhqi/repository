Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The development of used car residual values dictates near-term earnings for European auto leasing and rental providers, with rental profits recovering first and leasing profits lagging.

Defining the Core Financial Challenge

1.
Critical question: "Since July 2026, have European residual values for three- to four-year-old off-lease cars, and for battery electric cars in particular, held steady or started to fall again, and what did Ayvens, Sixt and ZIGUP report for depreciation per vehicle and for gains or losses on vehicle disposals in the third quarter of 2026? For Ayvens, how much of its lease book was written at residual values that are now above market prices, how fast does that part of the book run off, and what explains the fall of about 22% in its share price since July 2026: the plan to 2029 presented in September 2026, slower fleet growth, or residual values?"
2.
Significant risk: Ayvens remains highly exposed to residual value fluctuations because it retains the resale risk on 75% of its massive fleet. The broker Jefferies Research highlighted on October 30, 2026, that recent weakness in electric vehicle used pricing has exacerbated this residual value risk on the company's balance sheet. This exposure is critical for investors since realized sales values directly determine the profitable disposal of defleeted vehicles. Thus, a renewed fall in used car prices would severely impact future earnings per share.
3.
Rapid adjustment: Sixt faces immediate depreciation pressures during used car downturns because of the short holding periods inherent in the rental model. An expert who is a Senior Director at Sixt explained on March 3, 2026, that the sharpest vehicle depreciation occurs within the first six months of operation. This seasonality requires Sixt to successfully time its remarketing to protect core rental profits. Consequently, any unexpected drop in used car prices quickly flows through the income statement.
4.
Moderate insulation: ZIGUP is exposed to the used vehicle market but can actively age its fleet to navigate short- term price volatility. Deutsche Bank Research reported on October 15, 2026, that ZIGUP's average vehicle holding period of 4.5 years means it is currently selling vehicles bought at post-pandemic peaks. Normalizing disposal profits will weigh on near-term results, creating a headwind to EPS. Thus, the transition to lower residual values affects ZIGUP's disposal profitability but is partially offset by higher sales volumes.

Divergent Profit Recovery Timelines

5.
Lagged recovery: The recovery of leasing profits typically lags that of short-term rental operations by about one year. Jefferies Research explained on October 30, 2026, that leasing margins normalise upward over a multi-year horizon while used car sales gains normalise downward. This contract lag means that leasing profit turnarounds are delayed compared to rental peers. Therefore, used car price stability remains a prerequisite for sustained leasing earnings.
6.
Profound sensitivity: Used vehicle values serve as the primary determinant of mid-term earnings because they influence both current depreciation charges and final disposal outcomes. Deutsche Bank Research reported on September 9, 2026, that corporate fleets account for approximately 60% of EU new car demand. Since lease payments are heavily dependent on expected resale values, any unexpected drop in residuals disrupts the entire leasing model. Consequently, pricing trends directly dictate the pace of corporate fleet electrification and expansion.
7.
Contrary evidence: None found, as our search of company disclosures and broker research from June 2026 to October 2026 confirms that used vehicle residual values are the dominant determinant of earnings.
8.
Other side of the story: While residual values are critical, core leasing and services margins can sometimes offset disposal losses through robust pricing power. Jefferies Research observed on October 30, 2026, that Ayvens' underlying leasing and services margins improved to 620 basis points, helping to mitigate used car sales pressure. This underlying strength indicates that recurring operating earnings can sustain the business even when used car profits normalize to zero.

Section 2 – European Used Car Residual Values and Powertrain Trends

Section summary
  1. European used car residual values since July 2026 show contrasting powertrain dynamics with battery electric vehicles stabilizing while combustion engine values weaken.

  3. Rising volumes of corporate off-lease vehicles and growing Chinese competitor imports exert ongoing pricing pressure on traditional European automotive players.

Powder Train Residual Value Divergence

1.
Improving residuals: The European battery electric vehicle market is showing initial signs of price stabilization after a prolonged period of decline. The broker Jefferies Research reported on October 30, 2026, that thirdquarter used electric vehicle prices tracked through the Auto1 index rose 15% year-on-year. This recovery suggests that the sharpest phase of the electric vehicle residual value correction may be ending. This trend directly supports corporate fleet valuations and could help mitigate future lease book write-downs.
2.
Weakening combustion: In contrast to electric vehicles, traditional internal combustion engine residual values are experiencing a steady decline across Europe. The broker Jefferies Research noted on October 30, 2026, that used diesel and petrol vehicle prices fell 7% year-on-year during the third quarter. This downward shift is driven by elevated fuel costs and changing corporate procurement preferences. Consequently, lessors face growing pressure on used combustion engine disposal profits as these vehicles are defleeted.

Volkswagen Financial Services disclosed in an investor publication on October 13, 2026, that petrol and diesel residual values have recently declined while electric models showed tentative recovery. Although the used market for Chinese brands remains small, their rapid expansion threatens the historical pricing models of regional leasing players.

Section 4 – Ayvens Leased Book Exposure and Run-off Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Ayvens manages a vast lease book backed by fifty-two point eight billion euros in earning assets that run off over a typical four-year cycle.
  2. Centralized revaluation mechanics and rising prospective depreciation provisions reflect the ongoing adjustments required for assets written during pricing peaks.

Portfolio Structure and Asset Scale

1.
Massive assets: Ayvens reported its total earning assets reached 52,759 million euros as of September 30, 2026, representing a slight increase from 52,626 million euros in June 2026. This immense asset base represents the funded lease book where the firm bears residual value risks upon contract expiration. Consequently, any persistent downward pressure on used car pricing directly threatens the carrying value of this portfolio.
2.
Evolving fleet: Ayvens disclosed that its total managed contracts stood at 3,044 thousand units at the end of the third quarter of 2026. Within this total, the funded fleet comprised 2,470 thousand vehicles, while fleet management contracts accounted for 574 thousand units. This large concentration of funded contracts means the group retains significant balance sheet exposure to residual asset risks.
3.
Elevated risk: Ayvens possesses a funded fleet of 2,470 thousand vehicles, which is multiple times larger than those of its closest European listed peers. In comparison, the rental operator Sixt maintains a much smaller and more agile fleet with active premium vehicle additions to manage seasonal demand. This enormous difference in scale means that Ayvens faces proportionately higher absolute residual value exposure than smaller competitors.
4.
Diverse powertrain: Ayvens is managing its residual value risk by selectively adjusting its powertrain deliveries to reflect changing market dynamics. In the third quarter of 2026, battery electric vehicles represented 30% of passenger car and light commercial vehicle deliveries, while internal combustion engines fell to 26%. This structural shift requires the group to continuously update expected resale values across distinct engine categories.

Run-off Profile and Contract Dynamics

5.
Gradual runoff: Ayvens focuses on long-term corporate, small enterprise, and consumer leases that provide high revenue visibility due to their multi-year nature. These long-term leasing contracts carry an average contract duration of approximately 4 years. Because of this extended timeframe, leases written at peak values run off slowly over several years, delaying any rapid margin turnaround.
6.
Active mitigation: Ayvens manages its outstanding lease exposures by utilizing operational controls that prevent arbitrary commercial adjustments to contract pricing. Historical expert testimony reveals that LeasePlan historically mitigated its residual exposure through multiple recalculations during the lease term to lower residual values toward market levels. This ongoing contract management helps smooth out final disposal losses by the time vehicles return.
7.
Asset matching: Ayvens must balance its multi-year lease run-offs with robust funding strategies to manage interest rate and residual exposure. An expert who is the Former Chief Financial Officer at ALD Automotive explained on December 30, 2024, that matching assets and funding is a highly complex task during volatile interest rate cycles. Maintaining this alignment is critical to protecting core leasing spreads as older contracts expire.
8.
Prolonged lifecycles: Ayvens is actively expanding its used car leasing and re-leasing activities to mitigate residual value risks over time. The firm plans to grow its re-lease fleet by approximately 10% annually between 2026 and 2029 to reach over 100 thousand vehicles. Sending off-lease vehicles onto a second lease prolongs the asset lifetime and defers residual value risks.

Revaluation Mechanics and Provisions

9.
Disciplined revaluation: Ayvens utilizes a centralized risk team to perform regular evaluations of its fleet assets to ensure forward-looking values remain valid. This centralized team applies a rigid, statistically driven methodology every quarter to adjust expected resale values based on localized used car market dynamics. This structured process prevents local subsidiaries from making optimistic commercial assumptions about asset values.
10.
Growing provisions: Ayvens reported that its prospective depreciation stock on the balance sheet grew to negative 61 million euros as of September 30, 2026. This represents a substantial increase from the negative 12 million euros of prospective depreciation recorded at the end of June 2026. The negative 50 million euro net charge to the third quarter P&L reflects proactive revaluations of the running fleet to match lower market prices.
