EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Ayvens stands as the global scale leader in multi-brand full-service leasing with 3.2 million managed vehicles, representing the largest operational footprint in the industry and providing unmatched procurement leverage through the purchase of 700,000 vehicles annually. The group is executing a complex post-merger integration that has already delivered €357 million in pre-tax synergies in FY2025 toward a €440 million annual target by 2026, driving meaningful cost efficiencies and operational consolidation.

Strategic ownership by Societe Generale at 54.81% provides preferential funding access and deep integration with parent banking infrastructure, creating a structural funding cost advantage over independent competitors. The revenue model has shifted toward service margin dominance, which reached €1,680.3 million in FY2025 and now exceeds leasing contract margins, providing more defensive and predictable earnings. However, used car sales margins collapsed to just €410.9 million in FY2025 and further deteriorated to €470 gross per unit in Q1 2026, driven by severe battery electric vehicle residual value depreciation that threatens future balance sheet stability.

Customer concentration risk is exceptionally low with the top 10 clients representing only 5.6% of fleet, while average contract duration of 47 months delivers high revenue visibility and multi-year cash flow predictability. Geographic diversification is excellent, with France at 15% of revenues, Italy at 12%, and the UK at 11%, substantially reducing single-country exposure and mitigating domestic political risks. Capital strength remains robust with a CET1 ratio of 13.9% in March 2026 and €1,150 million in total capital distributions for FY2025, demonstrating strong internal generation capacity and regulatory headroom.

Return on tangible equity progressed to 12.9% for FY2025 and reached 13.9% in Q1 2026, tracking toward management's medium-term target range of 13% to 15% through structural margin improvement and synergy delivery. The competitive landscape is consolidating, with closest peer Arval managing 1.9 million financed vehicles and potentially adding 400,000 vehicles through the Athlon acquisition, narrowing Ayvens' historical scale advantage and intensifying B2B corporate pricing pressure.

Management deliberately contracted the fleet by 3.2% in 2025 through strategic defleeting of unprofitable brokered business, prioritizing margin protection over volume growth in a disciplined capital allocation framework. The December 2025 CEO transition to Philippe de Rovira shifted corporate priorities toward customer satisfaction and operational improvements, bringing 27 years of automotive industry experience from senior Stellantis roles. Procurement advantages are reinforced through deep volume discounts and framework agreements, including an unprecedented Stellantis agreement for up to 500,000 vehicles over three years. Downstream remarketing capabilities are anchored by Ayvens Carmarket, operating as a B2B digital auction platform across 39 countries with AI predictive pricing models active in 20 countries to optimize resale margins. Barriers to entry remain formidable, requiring minimum fleet scale of 30,000 to 35,000 vehicles and extremely high capital intensity to finance balance sheet vehicle assets, protecting the industry from new entrants.

The single largest strategic risk is severe battery electric vehicle residual value volatility, with lessors facing significant write-downs on returned EVs as realized resale values fall below initial contractual estimates due to rapid technology cycles and OEM price cuts. Vertical integration threats are intensifying as captive finance arms like Volkswagen Financial Services manage over 20 million contracts globally and Renault's Mobilize targets a 1 million vehicle fleet, potentially bypassing independent multi-brand lessors and compressing third-party market share. Integration execution risks persist with final platform migrations yet to occur in the UK, Germany, and the Netherlands, creating temporary customer service bottlenecks that could elevate contract non-renewal risks and damage historically strong retention rates. Procurement power is eroding as traditional dealer discounts and volume bonuses are declining compared to the pre-merger era, with OEMs managing production capacity more tightly and reducing manufacturer rebate generosity. Return on average earning assets has declined from 7.4% pre-Covid to around 5.5% post-merger, with standalone competitor Arval achieving materially better cost-to-income ratios and higher ROTE, highlighting persistent operational efficiency gaps.

The forward trajectory depends critically on normalized used car margin recovery and successful multicycle EV leasing industrialization to absorb residual value shocks, while defending share against bank-backed peer consolidation and aggressive captive expansion. Capital allocation flexibility through special distributions and potential minority buyout by Societe Generale provides optionality but does not resolve core EV asset depreciation exposure on the balance sheet.

Long-term structural positioning benefits from corporate fleet outsourcing trends and regulatory EV adoption mandates, though Chinese OEM penetration and direct-to-consumer distribution models represent material paradigm risks to traditional leasing intermediation.

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Ayvens faces severe residual value compression in its used electric vehicle portfolio, with gross used car sales margins crashing to €470 per unit in the first quarter of 2026, representing a catastrophic decline from historical through-cycle norms and significantly underperforming peer Arval's remarketing execution. The normalized remarketing results of €410.9 million in FY2025 mask the underlying structural deterioration, as used battery electric vehicle prices exhibit extreme volatility due to rapid technology cycles and OEM price cuts, forcing lessors to absorb significant write-downs on returned vehicles.

This negative peer-outlier performance in used car sales suggests that Ayvens' massive procurement scale advantages may be entirely offset by downstream asset residual volatility, a vulnerability detailed in Section 2 Financial Profile and Section 4 Value Chain Position. The combination of rapidly evolving EV regulations, declining business-to-business demand, and used EV oversupply relative to market appetite creates strong downward pressure on residual values, threatening the entire remarketing profit pool that historically contributed 10-15% of gross operating income.

The group deliberately contracted its funded fleet by 3.2% in 2025 through strategic defleeting actions targeting unprofitable retail contracts in the UK, Germany, and Turkey, signaling structural margin weakness in multiple core European markets. While management frames this as disciplined value-over-volume execution, return on average earning assets has declined from 7.4% pre-Covid to around 5.5% post-merger, suggesting that profitability improvements are driven primarily by portfolio pruning rather than organic pricing power gains.

Standalone competitor Arval achieves a materially better cost-to-income ratio and higher return on tangible equity than Ayvens, revealing a persistent operational efficiency gap despite similar business models and bank affiliation structures. This competitive disadvantage in cost discipline, explored in Section 7 Competitive Landscape and Section 9 Operator Quality, raises fundamental questions about whether the LeasePlan integration can deliver sustainable margin normalization or whether structural diseconomies remain embedded in the merged operating platform.

Standard operational leases exhibit a structural lag in recovering input cost inflation due to fixed-price terms, with commercial pricing discipline prioritized over volume through planned defleeting of unprofitable distribution channels, revealing insufficient inflation pass-through mechanisms that compress service margins during periods of persistent input cost pressure. Supplier cost inflation persists with Michelin price consensus at +1.8% for 2025, Belron guiding to mid-single digit organic growth driven by pricing, and parts provider PHE expecting mid-single digit growth on volume, creating a sustained margin squeeze as service revenues lag underlying procurement cost escalation.

While Ayvens has systematically integrated service indexation and inflation clauses into new commercial agreements with automatic adjustments when general service inflation exceeds predefined thresholds, this protection applies only to newly originated contracts and provides no relief for the existing €53 billion earning asset base with weighted average contract duration of 47 months. The mechanical margin headwind detailed in Section 6 Supplier Analysis suggests that Ayvens faces a multi-year period where input cost inflation outpaces contractual revenue indexation, permanently destroying the historical 25-35% gross margin profile in operational leasing unless the group executes widespread contract renegotiations that risk accelerating customer attrition to more competitively priced alternatives.

Outliers worth noting

Ayvens exhibits exceptionally low customer concentration risk with the top 10 international corporate clients accounting for less than 6% of the fleet and operations spanning 90% of the managed fleet across Western Europe and the Nordics, representing a structural anomaly relative to most capital-intensive industrial businesses. This diversification profile is statistically extreme when benchmarked against typical B2B service providers, where top-10 customer concentration frequently exceeds 25% to 40% of revenues.

The group's geographic and customer dispersion is further reinforced by balanced European asset distribution with France at 15% of FY2024 revenues, Italy at 12%, the UK at 11%, and the Netherlands at 10%, eliminating single-country political or regulatory concentration risks that plague competitors. This extraordinary diversification is analyzed comprehensively in Section 1 Business Model + Organisation and Section 5 Customer Side Analysis, where contract duration visibility and retention dynamics are peer-anchored.

The combination of 45-month average lease duration and multi-year corporate agreements ranging from 36 to 48 months provides unmatched revenue predictability that most cyclical industrial operators cannot achieve.

Ayvens maintains a uniquely diversified funding architecture with 31% from retail deposits totaling €14.1 billion via Ayvens Bank N.V. and 23% from bond issues representing €10.4 billion in EMTN debt, creating a structural funding cost advantage that independent multi-brand lessors lack entirely. This deposit franchise represents a massive competitive moat as the group's 54.81% ownership by Societe Generale provides preferential funding access with outstanding net financial debt of €11.47 billion and strategic capital support including €750 million in Additional Tier 1 capital fully subscribed by the parent bank.

The funding outlier becomes stark when peer-anchored, as independent lessors must access wholesale capital markets at materially higher spreads without deposit-taking licenses or parent bank liquidity lines. This upstream integration advantage is dissected across Section 4 Value Chain Map + Ayvens' Position and Section 9 Operator + Foundation Quality, where the preferential cost of capital is identified as a primary barrier to competitive entry.

The deposit base also provides balance sheet resilience during credit market dislocations, allowing Ayvens to maintain fleet purchasing capacity when undercapitalized competitors are forced to contract volumes, further compounding its scale leadership over multiple economic cycles.
