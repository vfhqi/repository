[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Signify is a self-help case at a trough price that has not yet become a recovery case. A new external chief executive has cut the dividend by about 40 percent, suspended the buyback, launched a EUR 180 million cost programme and split the portfolio into Build and Harvest, but at EUR 14.16 the shares pay about 12 to 13 percent a year on our modal path, because the margin the case needs, one that holds the savings instead of handing them to the trade channel, is not yet in the numbers. The next scheduled event, the Q3 2026 results on 2026-10-23, is more likely a guidance cut than a confirmation, so the case is one to pay for after the trough is declared rather than before.

  • The change inside the company is real, recent and internal, and it is being met by external forces running the other way.
    • An external chief executive took office on 2025-09-01, completed a portfolio review in nine months, launched a EUR 180 million indirect-cost programme (900 roles) in January 2026 and reset the dividend and the buyback in June 2026.
    • Three of the framework's twelve internal forces are strong, which meets its decision rule.
    • But every external force except regulation is a headwind: Chinese capacity at 50 to 60 percent utilisation redirected into Europe by US tariffs, European construction weak since the first quarter of 2023, copper up 32 percent and silver up 23 percent year on year, and a 4.8 percent currency hit to first-half 2026 sales.
    • Two internal forces run in reverse: balance-sheet use and shareholder returns were both cut, and guidance went down rather than up.
    • The one external tailwind, building-efficiency regulation, is dated 2027 to 2030 and is not yet in any order book the research shows.
  • The change has travelled three of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and stopped.
    • People, choices and actions are in place.
    • At the key operating metrics the first positive price/mix in fifteen quarters (plus 30 basis points in Q2 2026) is outweighed by a net price-cost headwind of minus 90 basis points and a Consumer division margin of 3.0 percent.
    • At the financial statements and the sell-side's estimates everything is still falling.
    • In Q2 2026 the EUR 180 million of gross savings showed as 40 basis points of margin against 90 basis points of volume deleverage and 50 basis points of currency.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are falling, not improving, on every line but cash conversion.
    • Consensus earnings per share on the brokers' basis falls 30.4 percent in FY2026 from the FY2025 actual of EUR 2.40
    • The sell-side has cut FY2027 by 37.3 percent and FY2028 by 46.1 percent over twelve months
    • The ratings store pins sell-side earnings momentum at F.
    • The eighteen-month and three-year lines turn up off the FY2026 base, but as a rebound from a trough the company has not yet confirmed.
    • The cuts are slowing (one-month earnings-per-share revision minus 1 percent, target price unchanged, at 2026-10-08), which is what the end of a downgrade cycle looks like
      • The Q3 print has not happened.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Our modal path, labelled as our own estimate because no Viewforth model exists, is an adjusted EBITA margin of 7.2 percent in FY2026, below the guided 7.5 to 8.5 percent, and earnings per share of EUR 1.60 in FY2026 rising to EUR 1.88 in FY2027, EUR 2.08 in FY2028 and EUR 2.29 in FY2029.
    • From the 24-month-forward earnings the price now capitalises (EUR 2.04) to the 24-month-forward earnings three years out (EUR 2.51) that is 7.1 percent a year, below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
    • Most of that growth is margin and below-EBITDA drop-through, not revenue
      • Revenue contributes about 0.9 percent a year.
  • What it costs.
    • EUR 14.16 at 2026-10-08, or 6.8x consensus 24-month-forward earnings of EUR 2.08 and 6.9x our own EUR 2.04: the 17th percentile of the stock's own ten-year range and the lowest multiple of the 25 in the dashboard sector.
    • But on the stock's five-year 24-month-forward series the multiple was 7.54x on 2026-10-02, the 61st percentile
    • On the earnings the company now has, the market is paying about what it has paid for five years.
    • The price already assumes a modest further cut to consensus and no re-rating
      • It does not price the bear path (about EUR 8.6 on Morgan Stanley's FY2027 earnings per share of EUR 1.34 at 6.4x) nor the bull path (about EUR 21).
  • The return does not clear the bar.
    • Valuing the shares at 7.0x the earnings two years beyond each horizon and adding dividends, the 'Modal return' is 13.2 percent a year over eighteen months and 12.4 percent a year over three years against the 20 percent bar.
    • Earnings carries the return, the cash return of about 4.6 percent a year is the floor, and the re-rating is the swing factor: at the ten-year median multiple of 8.4x the same path gives 18.6 percent a year, but that needs growth the company's own 2029 target of 0 to 1 percent does not promise.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (a dearer refinancing of the May 2027 bond, a 25 percent tax rate, the margin 50 basis points lower throughout) takes both horizons to F: minus 1.2 percent a year at eighteen months and 5.3 percent a year at three years.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Major Corporate Change', on the input side only.
    • The inputs pass the framework's rule
    • The outputs have not begun to translate.
    • The setup the case could become is 'Trough on Trough': the trough multiple and the demand trough are present, but guidance has not been kitchen-sinked and the company has not capitulated, so two mandatory conditions fail.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds at the inputs and at cash: the restructuring charges are taken (EUR 94 million in the first half of 2026), working capital is at 6.3 percent of sales, free cash flow was above 100 percent of reported net income in FY2025, and the reset dividend is covered about twice.
    • It breaks at the key operating metrics, where three years of cost programmes have been absorbed by the trade channel: the rolling margin is down 170 basis points since 2023 against a 20 percent headcount cut.

  • The case therefore is a coherent, cheap, cash-generative self-help thesis that is waiting for an event it is more likely to fail than to pass.

    • It becomes compelling at a price below about EUR 12, where the modal path alone clears 20 percent a year, or after a guidance rebase that resets the base the recovery is measured from.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is not yet material on the framework's test: the input forces are real but net negative ('Change Forces' at D), the financial outputs are still falling ('Financial Outputs' at F), and the 'Modal return' is 12 to 13 percent a year (at D), short of 20 percent.

    • The parts combine as a chain in which the weakest link, the outputs, sets the level, because a thesis about a margin recovery that has not begun cannot be material yet.
    • Not F, because the internal forces are not cosmetic (a new external chief executive, EUR 94 million of restructuring charges already taken, a dividend yield above 5 percent that is now covered), the price has fallen to a level at which modest delivery gives a low-teens return, and the downgrade cycle is slowing.
    • Not C, because every output line except cash conversion points down over the next six months and the sell-side is still cutting.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: Signify is a 'Trough on Trough' already, and the second half of 2026 shows it.
      • The price is at a 52-week low after a 34 percent fall
      • The multiple is 6.8x and the lowest in its sector
      • 38 percent of the sell-side says sell and only 8 percent buy
      • Estimates are down 37 to 46 percent in a year
      • And the FY2026 guidance set in January 2026 was the kitchen-sink, since Morgan Stanley's note of 2026-01-30 said it implied 12 to 25 percent EBITA downgrades.
      • The business has already right-sized: headcount down about 20 percent since end-2023, Conventional at three plants and back at an 18 percent margin in Q2 2026.
      • The cost programme is ahead of schedule with its benefits concentrated in the second half, after union consultations that closed only in the second quarter.
      • Retailer stock of Hue is back to 8 to 10 weeks, on or slightly below normal by management's measure, so Consumer sell-in inflects in Q3
        • Underlying sell-out is healthy and the installed base of 175 million light points is growing.
      • Price/mix turned positive for the first time since Q3 2022
        • The Q2 quarter carried one-off plant-consolidation costs in Conventional.
      • Free cash flow was EUR 268 million in FY2025 on FactSet's definition, 15 percent of the EUR 1,778 million market capitalisation, with consensus at EUR 289 million for FY2026.
      • If the second half delivers the 7.5 percent floor, FY2027 earnings per share of about EUR 2.0 to 2.5 (ING has EUR 2.54) at the ten-year median of 8.4x is EUR 17 to 21 plus a 6 percent yield: 25 to 30 percent a year.
        • The dividend has been reset so it cannot disappoint again
        • The downside is a stock already at 6.8x.
    • What we concede.
      • The price is low and the yield is real and now covered.
      • The cash conversion is real.
      • The cost programme is real and ahead of its timetable.
      • The downgrade cycle is decelerating.
      • The Conventional surprise shows the Harvest playbook can work.
      • A trough multiple on a depressed base is where the framework's best setups are found.
      • We also concede that the free-cash-flow figures above are before about EUR 72 million a year of lease payments and before restructuring cash-outs, so the yield to equity after those is nearer 10 to 12 percent than 15 to 16.
      • We make no claim that connected lighting or any division covers the enterprise value, because no input gives a profit line for connected lighting on which a like-for-like multiple could be put.
    • Why we still hold our view.
      • First, 'Trough on Trough' needs the company to have given up, and it has not: a guidance range reiterated with the first half at 37 percent of the year is a target at risk, not a rebased floor, and the framework's capitulation test needs guidance, the sell-side and the price all to have given way.
      • Second, the margin case rests on savings reaching the margin, and the evidence of the last three years and of the latest quarter (40 basis points of savings against 90 of deleverage, 50 of currency and a net price-cost of minus 90) is that they do not, because the counterparty taking them, Chinese trade-channel pricing, is a policy flow with 33 percent of Signify's sales in its path and under 5 percent of Acuity's.
      • Third, the re-rating to 8.4x that makes the bull arithmetic work needs growth, and the company's own target is 0 to 1 percent by 2029.
      • Fourth, management quality on the record is D, which parks the case under the framework's gate line, and the new team's first act under pressure was to reiterate rather than rebase.
      • On the narrower claim that the second half shows the margin step: this is possible but less likely than not, because the research gives no series that confirms the sell-in inflection, the price-cost headwind is not a one-off, and the company's own history of back-half delivery (about 60 percent of EBITA in the second half, achieved once in five years) is against it.
    • Where the case against would win, and what we would do.
      • A Q3 print on 2026-10-23 showing a Consumer margin above 5 percent, a reiterated range with a stated second-half bridge, and price-cost at or above zero would move this Building Block to C.
      • We would rather pay for the trough after it is declared: the price at which the modal path alone clears 20 percent a year is about EUR 12, and a guidance cut on 2026-10-23 is the probable route there.

Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C

  • Not B, because the Directive permits country-level implementation flexibility that lets timelines slip, so the tailwind's timing is 2027 to 2030, beyond the eighteen-month horizon.
