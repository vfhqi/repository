EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

CVS Group plc operates a highly predictable, subscription-backed veterinary services model across the United Kingdom and Australia, spanning companion animal, referrals, equine, farm, diagnostics laboratories, and online retail through Animed Direct. The group generated £712.8 million of revenue in fiscal year 2026, with veterinary practices contributing £648.2 million, diagnostics £35.0 million, and online retail £51.0 million.

Australian operations expanded rapidly to £79.1 million revenue (11% of group total) from £52.1 million in the prior year, demonstrating successful international scaling through targeted acquisitions. The Healthy Pet Club subscription scheme generated £95.5 million with 508,000 active members, locking in repeat client visits and enhancing cash flow predictability despite slight membership contraction. Chief Executive Officer Richard Fairman announced his retirement after almost seven years, creating near-term leadership transition uncertainty that requires securing strong next-generation management to sustain medium-term strategy.

Financial Profile Demonstrates Stable Margins with Geographic Diversification Offsetting Domestic Headwinds

CVS Group accelerated revenue growth to £712.8 million (up 5.9%) through acquisitions while organic like-for-like sales increased only 2.1%, strategically offsetting weak domestic volumes through geographic diversification to support long-term earnings per share growth. Six Australian practice acquisitions totaling £45.6 million were completed at an average multiple of 6.0 times EBITDA, significantly below typical UK multiples, making these transactions highly accretive to earnings per share over the medium term. Adjusted EBITDA margins remained stable at 19.9% (£141.5 million) despite substantial wage inflation, demonstrating the group's ability to maintain stable operating margins through disciplined cost management.

Underlying margin compression occurred when excluding research and development tax credits, with adjusted EBITDA margins falling to 17.6% from 17.8%, indicating cost headwinds are capping domestic operating leverage and potentially restraining near-term earnings per share growth unless pricing power improves. Adjusted operating cash conversion declined to 70.6% from 76.9% due to increased working capital outflows of £12.8 million, though lower capital expenditure guidance of approximately £30 million annually will enhance free cash flow generation.

Balance Sheet Strength and Capital Allocation Support Accretive Growth Strategy

CVS Group reported leverage of 1.63 times net debt to EBITDA, comfortably below the self-imposed target ceiling of 2 times, preserving substantial financial flexibility to deploy capital for accretive acquisitions without compromising balance sheet strength. The group completed a successful refinancing in May 2026, extending bank debt facilities to May 2030 with a £125 million term loan and £225 million revolving credit facility, providing substantial capacity to accelerate its acquisition pipeline across targeted markets.

The board proposed an ordinary dividend of 9.0 pence per share for fiscal year 2026 (up from 8.5 pence) alongside a £50 million share buyback programme launched in May 2026, with £11.7 million completed by year-end, enhancing adjusted earnings per share through consistent shareholder returns. The group benefited from substantial research and development tax credits of £15.7 million (up from £15.1 million), providing meaningful support to earnings per share, though analysts closely monitor underlying margins excluding this recurring tax benefit.

Return on capital employed declined to 16.1% from 17.3% due to rapid lower-return international acquisitions, highlighting that while Australian roll-up acquisitions provide immediate revenue growth, they carry higher local tax rates and pressure near-term return profiles until mature purchasing synergies are achieved.

The UK veterinary services market is valued at £6.7 billion with strong defensive characteristics, supported by stable pet populations, increasing pet medicalisation, and advanced treatment options that maintain durable demand for healthcare services during economic downturns. Corporate consolidation has reached approximately 60% of UK practices compared to only 20% in Australia, allowing scaled platforms to leverage purchasing power and operational efficiencies systematically.

CVS Group holds the second-largest UK corporate veterinary operator position with 9% market share, and the top five corporate groups collectively control approximately 45% of UK veterinary surgeries, enabling the company to reinvest cash flows into expansion efficiently. Scaled corporate veterinary clinics can achieve mature adjusted EBITDA margins of 20% or higher, with CVS Group targeting a long-term range of 19% to 23% supported by procurement synergies and centralized administrative functions.

CVS Group continues pursuing geographic expansion through targeted companion animal clinic consolidation, successfully scaling the Australian practice network to 57 clinical sites (representing 11% of consolidated revenues) under Richard Fairman's leadership, who remains committed to executing this corporate growth strategy until a suitable successor is identified.
