Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. The multiple arbitrage engine faces unprecedented structural pressure as private equity platforms bid up entry multiples, threatening the 25% IRR trajectory of classic European decentralized compounders.

The core question and strategic intent

1.
Strategic investment thesis: The verbatim key question is how the multiple arbitrage engine, which relies on buying private targets at 5 to 8 times EBITA and trading at 17 to 26 times EBITA, can sustain its structural returns as private equity switches it on at under-levered laggards while entry multiples rise for everyone.
2.
Targeted performance criteria: Resolving this question is critical for a concentrated, long-only investor because achieving a 25% IRR over an 18-month to 3-year horizon requires absolute predictability of the under-levered laggards' EPS compounding trajectory.
3.
Underlying purchase mechanics: Historical transaction data demonstrates that players like Lagercrantz have sustained high returns by routinely buying targets at 7 times EBITA and immediately revaluing them within a listed platform.
4.
Valuation spread compression: The broader universe trades at premium valuations, with high-quality compounders like Addtech historically trading at 22 times EBITA or higher.

Adjacent questions and private equity threats

5.
Escalating deal competition: Specialist industry advisors note that increased competition, especially in Scandinavia, has driven acquisition multiples to 8-10x or higher and forced serial acquirers to look outside their home markets.
6.
Aggressive capital replication: Premium private equity sponsors like Blackstone and Apollo are bidding up assets to high-teens multiples, directly squeezing the traditional market boundaries of listed B2B distributors.
7.
Platform capital discipline: Established platforms like Bunzl are balancing high-return bolt-on pipelines with excess cash distributions, executing share buybacks of 500 million pounds when target multiples exceed discipline thresholds.
8.
Acquired growth contribution: Analytical forecasting models indicate that unannounced M&A usually represents two-thirds of total earnings growth for top-quartile compounders, making entry multiple inflation the single largest threat to future EPS estimates.
9.

  3. Rising private equity competition and elevated entry valuations present long-term headwind risks, but the arbitrage window for small bolt-on acquisitions remains highly viable.
