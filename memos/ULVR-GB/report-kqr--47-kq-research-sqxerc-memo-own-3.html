Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Unilever's structural transformation into a pure-play home and personal care entity significantly alters its valuation framework and risk profile.
  2. The impending transaction with McCormick forces a fundamental re-evaluation of its standalone valuation multiples relative to historical global peers.

Restructuring and Strategic Context

1.
Investigating valuation: "Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB), a global home and personal care company reporting Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care and Home Care, plus a Foods business being combined with McCormick through a Reverse Morris Trust (completion guided for mid-2027 at the latest); Ice Cream was demerged in December 2025 as The Magnum Ice Cream Company (19.9% retained); the company moves to six regional P&L units from 1-Jan- 2027. For Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB), how do brokers value the home and personal care business that remains after the Foods combination with McCormick: which multiple (price-to-earnings, EV/EBITDA) does each apply to it and how does each value the McCormick shares to be distributed and the retained 9.9% and 19.9% (Magnum) stakes (for example Deutsche Bank's published 18.8x FY2027 earnings for the standalone business against 17.4x for the group on 11-Nov-2026, and Jefferies' 15.5x); where do Unilever's EV/EBITDA and its price-to-earnings ratio relative to European consumer staples and relative to Procter & Gamble and Colgate-Palmolive stand today against their ranges over the last ten years, on a basis unaffected by the change of perimeter and of leverage; how did the multiples of comparable companies move before, during and after a large separation or simplification (Procter & Gamble 2014 to 2021, Reckitt around the Essential Home disposal, Haleon after its 2022 demerger, Kellanova after 2023, The Magnum Ice Cream Company since December 2025), and how long after the event did any re-rating come; and what is known about how shares distributed to European holders in such transactions traded in the first six months (selling by holders unable to keep a United States listing, index treatment), including what brokers and investors expect for the McCormick shares that Unilever shareholders will receive?"

Mandate Restrictions and Forced Selling

1.
Severe selling pressure: European institutional investors face strict investment mandates that restrict them from holding assets listed outside their benchmark regions. Bernstein reported on 1-Jun-2026 that many European funds are benchmarked against regional indices and cannot easily hold United States listings. This structural mismatch will likely force immediate selling of distributed shares upon completion of the Foods transaction, especially because active managers prefer to reserve foreign limits for technology. Such forced liquidations represent a persistent near-term risk to the McCormick pro forma share price.
2.
Substantial overhang risk: European portfolio managers frequently choose to allocate their limited United States exposure to high-growth sectors rather than defensive consumer staples. Bernstein analyst Andrew Wood noted on 1-Jun-2026 that regional managers prefer using foreign risk limits on technology leaders rather than packaged food shares. Consequently, the distributed McCormick equity will likely suffer from persistent technical overhangs in the first six months. This technical pressure could severely depress pro forma valuation multiples and dilute initial shareholder returns.
