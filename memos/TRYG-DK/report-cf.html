EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Tryg has undergone orderly leadership transitions during 2023-2026, with CEO Johan Kirstein Brammer and CFO Allan Kragh Thaysen assuming their roles through internal promotions backed by direct equity ownership and regular share grants under standard remuneration policies. The supervisory board refreshed its chairmanship in March 2026 under Steffen Kragh, maintaining highly structured and independent governance oversight, while strategic IT leadership additions including Group CIO Joakim Eldin bring targeted integration skills from competitive Topdanmark experience. & Liv (Swedish) Strategic Transformation and Capital Allocation Capital returns are projected to reach DKK 17-18 billion over 2025-2027, supported by strong cash flows and a solid 196% solvency ratio, with no active activist presence identified.

The United Towards 2027 strategy targets a DKK 1 billion increase in normalized insurance service result through three pillars, aiming for a combined ratio of around 81% across the three-market portfolio. Structural simplification includes a major EUR 550 million IT contract with TCS to streamline legacy systems, driving an approximate 33% IT head-count reduction, while the complete orderly exit from real estate assets is on track with property exposures planned to fall to DKK 1.9 billion by Q3 2026.

Top Six Load-Bearing Drivers Forward earnings rely on six major operating and structural drivers led by Norway repricing, which delivered its lowest combined ratio in ten years at 77.3% in Q2 2026. Sweden represents the primary engine of organic retail growth through Private motor expansion, with new motor partnerships generating SEK 130 million in annual premium volume. The Scale and Simplicity pillar targets DKK 500 million of normalized insurance result improvement through IT consolidation, while the TCS agreement drove an approximate 33% reduction in IT staff by 2026.

Complete property divestment lowers investment risk and frees up regulatory capital, with real estate holdings expected to reach DKK 1.9 billion by end-Q3 2026. Strategic capital repatriation remains highly supportive of forward EPS accretion, with Tryg generating 73 percentage points of capital annually to fund continuous extraordinary buybacks. Abating Scandinavian claims severity supports underlying underwriting margins, with the private segment underlying claims ratio gaining 60 basis points in Q2 2026.

Most Recent Earnings Delivery Headline results for Q2 2026 exceeded expectations driven by strong investment income and robust solvency metrics, with reported net profit reaching DKK 874 million beating consensus of DKK 722 million and the Solvency II ratio increasing to 196% exceeding consensus of 192%. The Danish Supreme Court unfavourably lowered the threshold for loss of earning capacity to 5%, resulting in Tryg booking a DKK 1.2 billion one-off pre-tax workers' compensation charge, though the net impact on solvency was limited to 4% due to offsetting balance sheet actions.

Underlying claims ratio improvements accelerated, anchored by a record-breaking Norwegian combined ratio of 77.3%, with the underlying claims ratio improving by 50 basis points year-on-year beating consensus expectations by 20 basis points. Weather-related claims tracked near guided baselines at DKK 91 million, though large claims reached DKK 360 million substantially exceeding the quarterly target of DKK 200 million. 10 - Earnings Calls Forward Guidance Credibility Tryg's 2027 strategy targets a combined ratio of around 81% by 2027 and expects to reach DKK 8.0-8.4 billion insurance service result by the end of 2027.

Historical performance demonstrates consistent delivery on technical targets, with Tryg successfully meeting all its 2024 financial targets and delivering a 2025 actual combined ratio of 80.3% that remained better than the targeted level of around 81%, producing a reported 2025 insurance service result of DKK 7.9 billion tracking near the 2027 target range. Earnings remain highly sensitive to interest rate shifts and Scandinavian currency fluctuations, with a one percentage point change in exchange rates impacting the annual insurance service result by DKK 50-75 million, while a 15% decline in SEK exchange rates reduces annual insurance service result by DKK 488 million.

Management is classified as an honest tracker with sandbagging tendencies based on conservative target setting and systematic overdelivery potential.

Sell-Side Positioning and Peer Momentum

The active sell-side coverage pool comprises three primary institutional broker houses fielding seven named individual analysts, with rating mix showing 9 Buys, 7 Holds, and 3 Sells representing a moderate buy consensus, though price targets display wide dispersion from a low of DKK 145 at Morgan Stanley to a high of DKK 190 at DNB Carnegie. The bull case focuses on operational turnaround in Norway with pricing actions driving its lowest combined ratio in 10 years at 77.3%, while the bear case emphasizes that Tryg's organic growth of 3-4% underperforms the high-single-digit expansion seen at key peer Gjensidige.

Tryg's premium growth lags behind its larger Nordic peers, delivering 3.3% organic revenue growth in Q2 2026 trailing its major Nordic competitors, while Morgan Stanley expects Tryg's 5% EPS growth to lag peers who average over 10% operating CAGR. Estimate revisions have degraded significantly following court provisions, with Tryg's 2026 EPS expectations falling by 10% after the workers' compensation provision compared to moderate peer adjustments with Gjensidige's EPS estimates cut by 8% in the same period.

Activist Presence and Key Risks No active activist presence has been identified in available documentation, reflecting the stable majority ownership structure under TryghedsGruppen. Aggressive volume growth targets in Sweden and Norway may trigger underwriting losses if price hikes fail to cover inflation, while extreme weather and severe inflation may overwhelm reinsurance limits if price hikes trigger severe customer churn in Norway and Sweden.

The elevated 40% ROOF is driven by balance sheet compression, and if premium growth fails to reaccelerate capital repatriation will decline significantly. Unexpected claims inflation and falling interest rates could compress underwriting margins, leaving the DKK 8.0 billion target unachievable as premium growth decelerates. The Nordic P&C market faces persistent cost inflation headwinds from expensive electric vehicle repairs, global reinsurance pricing pressure, and escalating weather-related claims frequency.

The structural nature of this earnings momentum outlier signals fundamental constraints on Tryg's ability to deliver peer-level shareholder value creation despite comparable or superior underwriting margins.
