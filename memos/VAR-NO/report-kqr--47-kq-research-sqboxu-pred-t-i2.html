Section 2 – Norwegian Tax-Instalment Lag and Currency Volatility

Section summary
  1. The Norwegian petroleum tax-instalment lag shifts cash outflows to periods of lower prices, creating significant cash flow headwinds during commodity downturns.
  2. Non-cash exchange rate fluctuations under Vår Energi's dollar functional currency introduce severe effective tax rate volatility, dragging down earnings per share unpredictably.

Tax Instalment Mechanics and

1.
Lag Delayed payments: In Norway, petroleum taxes are paid in six bi- monthly instalments, with three instalments paid during the income year and three in the first half of the following year. This six-month delay means cash flows are heavily impacted by historical earnings rather than current commodity prices. The tax timing lag can cause significant cash flow distortions during price transitions.
2.
Looming payments: For Vår Energi, the tax-instalment lag will culminate in a massive cash tax payment in the fourth quarter of 2026. The fourth-quarter cash tax outflow is estimated at USD 1.7 bn, which represents a major liquidity drag. This massive payout occurs even as bottom-line results are pressured by operational and currency factors.
3.
Uneven payments: DNB Carnegie notes that Norway's bi-monthly petroleum tax instalments result in one payment in the first quarter, two in the second quarter, one in the third quarter, and two in the fourth quarter. This uneven payment schedule creates intra-year cash flow volatility for operators like Vår Energi. Working capital builds can occur in quarters with concentrated tax payments, complicating liquidity management.
4.
Stark comparisons: Peer producer Equinor faces a similar cash tax step-up in the fourth quarter of 2026, with three Norwegian Continental Shelf payments totalling NOK 93bn. Equinor's cash flow after taxes rose fiftythree percent year-on-year to USD 8.1bn despite paying USD 6.2bn of Norwegian taxes in the third quarter. This heavy front-loading of taxes compares to Vår's smaller operations but shows the scale of Norwegian tax obligations.
5.
Negative projections: DNB Carnegie forecasts that Vår's cash taxes will rise and production will decline, leading to average negative free cash flow of USD 100 mm in 2027-2028. This negative cash flow is driven by the tax lag from highly profitable prior periods being settled in lower-price environments. Consequently, the company must reserve its elevated 2026 cash flow to fund future dividend payments.
