Overview

1.
Organic Growth and Margins: Growth across major pharma and biotech was highly divergent. The sub-sector benefited from volume acceleration in key blockbusters, such as Sanofi's Dupixent (+35% CER) Organic Growth and Margins (cont.): and UCB's Bimzelx. However, hyper-growth in obesity decelerated; Novo Nordisk’s organic growth slowed sequentially to +4% CER (+2% YTD) due to a negative price, channel, and product mix in the US obesity franchise, alongside deep Wegovy injectable pricing erosion of 30% to 35% YTD from discounts and commercial access negotiations.
2.
Margins: Margins were supported by positive product mixes and efficiencies (GSK beat margins by +120 bps, Novartis beat core EBIT margins by 294 bps Margins (cont.): ), but were pressured by launch costs (Bayer Margins (cont.): ), R&D write-downs (Novartis' $1.90 bn Phase III impairment ), and rising interest costs from acquisitions (GSK's Nuvalent financing ). Pharma Manufacturing Services and Tools (CDMO & Life Science Tools)
3.
Organic Growth and Margins: This sub-sector showed clear signs of bottoming and gradual recovery, primarily driven by robust secular demand in Biologics and specialized modalities. Lonza confirmed stable CDMO growth of 11% to 12%, while Merck KGaA's Process Solutions surged +11% organically. Sartorius Stedim Biotech reported satisfactory recovery with constant-currency sales up 6.5%.
4.
Drivers: Volumes in consumables led the recovery, while bioprocess equipment order books are rebuilding more gradually. Margins are accelerating due to capacity utilization improvements and fixed-cost absorption (Lonza upgrading mid-term margins to the "high thirties" ). Tariff headwinds remained a minor friction point, with Sartorius Stedim noting up to a €35 million impact from US tariffs Drivers (cont.):, and Merck KGaA experiencing a -200 bps impact on Process Solutions from customer tariff refunds. Medical Technology (Consumables and Capital Goods)
5.
Organic Growth and Margins: Bifurcation was most pronounced here. Consumables saw solid growth, driven by positive volume trends and procedural volume acceleration (e.g., Getinge ACT consumables up +5%, Convatec Infusion Care reaching double-digit growth, and Philips Personal Health up +7.7% Organic Growth and Margins (cont.): ). Conversely, high-end Capital and Imaging goods faced persistent delays and procurement bottlenecks in China due to centralized procurement reforms (affecting Siemens Healthineers Organic Growth and Margins (cont.):, Philips Diagnosis & Treatment, and Elekta ).
6.
Drivers: Positive price adjustments and productivity programs helped expand margins (Getinge expanded underlying margins by >1 pp, Philips gained €170 million in productivity savings, and Elekta overdelivered on its SEK 500 million cost-savings program Drivers (cont.): ). However, margin acceleration was severely capped by US raw materials and freight inflation, US reimbursement resets in outpatient skin substitutes (disrupting Smith & Nephew Drivers (cont.): and Coloplast Drivers (cont.): ), and competitive pricing pressures in China.
7.
Audiology/Hearing Care: Stood out with massive product cycle accelerations. Demant’s organic growth surged +11% on the Oticon Reveal launch Audiology/Hearing Care (cont.):, and Sonova delivered +7.5% CC growth behind Infinio. Hospitals and Care Organic Growth and Margins: Fresenius SE saw strong organic sales (+5%) and margin expansion (+50 bps) driven by its Helios hospital network and Kabi growth vectors (Biopharma up +29%). Conversely, Fresenius Medical Care (FMC) stagnated (+0.1% organic) with US treatment volumes deteriorating to -1.1% same-market treatment growth (SMTG) due to prior patient losses. FMC margins were severely compressed by labor cost inflation and transport costs due to Middle East tensions, driving inflation beyond its €300 million cap.
