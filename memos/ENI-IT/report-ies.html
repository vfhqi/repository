Eni (ENI-IT): what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the existing case

These results strengthen the company side of the case a little and leave the investment conclusion where it was: the park stands, and at EUR 24.985 (9 November 2026) the shares sit further above the level at which the case says the return clears the bar, not nearer to it.

1. What changed

Eni reported its third quarter of 2026 on 23 October 2026. Set against what the case assumed:

  • Profit beat, of lower quality than the headline. Adjusted net profit was EUR 2,801m against a company-compiled consensus of EUR 2,480m, 12.9 percent ahead. The adjusted group tax rate fell to 32 percent from 37 percent in the second quarter, and on Morgan Stanley's arithmetic a steady rate cuts the beat to 4 percent. Adjusted operating profit of EUR 3,699m missed consensus of EUR 3,829m by 3.4 percent. Exploration and production EBIT of EUR 2,704m was 7.3 percent below consensus although production of 1,829 kboe/d beat 1,810 kboe/d. Cash flow from operations before working capital, EUR 4,287m, was in line with EUR 4,300m. The case's drop-through sensitivity is built on this tax line: it treated the second quarter's 35 percent as a favourable mix that would reverse, and the third quarter went further the same way.
  • Distributions came in above the case's modal and inside its rule. The 2026 buyback rose to EUR 3.8bn from EUR 3.4bn, short of the EUR 4.0bn ceiling the case names. An extraordinary dividend of EUR 0.46 a share, about EUR 1.30bn, is payable in December 2026, against EUR 1.1bn expected by Morgan Stanley. The trigger was a Standard Eni Refining Margin of USD 28.10 a barrel in the quarter against a threshold of USD 9; the margin was USD 18 a barrel in October 2026.
  • Guidance was raised again. Underlying production growth for 2026 is now 6 to 7 percent, from 5 percent. Cash flow from operations is guided at EUR 16.5bn, from EUR 15bn, against a Visible Alpha consensus of EUR 15.2bn. Net capital expenditure is guided below EUR 4.5bn, from below EUR 5bn. The case assumed about 5 percent and about EUR 15bn.
  • The balance sheet moved sharply. Adjusted net debt was EUR 6,702m at the end of the quarter, from EUR 11,271m at the second quarter, against EUR 9,354m expected. Gearing was 10 percent before leases and 18 percent after; pro forma gearing was 9 percent; gearing including leases and hybrids fell to 25 percent from 30 percent. The Plenitude reorganisation completed on 1 October 2026 and is credited with 4 points of gearing.
  • Venezuela moved from optionality to plan. Junin-5 Phase 1 targets a final investment decision by the end of 2026 and about 200 kboe/d gross within 18 to 24 months, with Phase 2 guided to 400 thousand barrels a day gross by 2029, and management says no incremental group capital expenditure. About 600 kboe/d of new production for 2030 was sanctioned during 2026.
  • Chemicals got worse. Versalis lost EUR 255m at the operating level against an expected loss of EUR 209m.
  • The sell-side split. Rothschild & Co Redburn raised its target to EUR 32.80 from EUR 28.80 (26 October 2026), its 2026 earnings per share to EUR 3.17 from EUR 2.87 and its 2028 figure to EUR 2.88 from EUR 2.44, on lower upstream tax and Venezuelan volumes, and models 2.5 million boe/d by 2030, a 7.5 percent compound rate from 2025. Jefferies called the print an incremental positive. Morgan Stanley held Equal-weight and EUR 23.80, read the beat as in-line and left its thesis unchanged.

2. The challenge

The obvious alternative is not one. That the quarter is a cyclical peak, lifted by a refining margin already down by a third and a tax rate unlikely to hold, is the case's own reading. It confirms the case; it does not challenge it. Two readings do.

Reading one: the upgrade phase is not behind the shares, because the volume trajectory has stepped up. The case says the change is fully mature, that nothing is left to propagate, and that earnings per share grow about 5 percent a year from here, of which about 3.5 points is buyback. It rests on the case's 4 percent a year of volume growth to 2030 and on a 2028 consensus of EUR 2.45. Against that: 6 to 7 percent growth this year, 600 kboe/d sanctioned in one year, a capital-free Venezuelan phase the case filed under sovereign risk and never under earnings, and one broker now at EUR 2.88 for 2028, 18 percent above its own prior figure. In its strongest form the outer-year estimates are where the next leg of upgrades lands, and the earnings leg becomes volume-led rather than buyback-led.

What would have to be true:
- Eni's net entitlement at Junin-5 is material against the gross figures. The production-sharing and cost-recovery terms are not disclosed.
- The United States licence for Venezuelan operations holds through first oil.
- The lower upstream tax rate is a durable change in geographic mix rather than one quarter's.

Even granted in full, this reading does not reach the bar at today's price. On the case's own method, a 24-month-forward figure of EUR 2.88 at eighteen months and the case's 9.5 times gives about EUR 27.4, plus dividends including the extraordinary one, about EUR 29.5, or roughly 12 percent a year from EUR 24.985. That is our rough re-basing, not a re-rating. The reading changes the earnings leg; it still needs the multiple.

Reading two: the debt reduction is perimeter, not cash, so the balance sheet bounds the downside less than the case assumes. The case leans on record-low gearing twice: it says the balance sheet can carry a year of shortfall, and that the cash yield and the balance sheet bound the ordinary downside. Net debt fell by about EUR 4.6bn in a quarter in which cash flow from operations before working capital less organic capital expenditure of EUR 1,345m left about EUR 2.9bn before any dividend or buyback. The rest is structure: the Plenitude deconsolidation, and a EUR 1.80bn contribution from private equity investors in exchange for a future dividend stream from an upstream infrastructure portfolio, which is closer to borrowing against future cash than to earning it. Morgan Stanley's reading (5 November 2026) is that Eni has relied on disposals and on deconsolidating satellite debt to soften the effect of rising shareholder returns. In the first half of 2026 operating profit from upstream joint ventures rose 56 percent while dividends received fell 6 percent to EUR 558m. Total 2026 distributions are now EUR 8.20bn.

In its strongest form: each deconsolidation lowers reported gearing and lowers the parent's claim on cash at the same time, while the distribution rule ratchets payouts up. The case holds satellite complexity as a concern that is "neither an advantage nor a flaw yet". This reading says it is already the mechanism producing the headline the case treats as protection.

What would have to be true:
- Satellite and joint-venture distributions to the parent keep lagging their earnings.
- The EUR 1.80bn contribution carries a fixed or prior claim on dividends.
- The partners' rights bind in a downturn.

Two points need checking before this reading is weighed. The Plenitude completion is dated one day after the quarter end, so which balance sheet carries its 4 points is unclear. And second-quarter pro forma gearing is given as 15 percent where the case recorded 10 percent at June 2026; the bases differ.

No Building Block rating would move. By rating, in the case's own terms:

  • 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1), F: more pressure, same letter. The price is up about 7 percent on the case's EUR 23.38 and the 24-month-forward consensus is up about 3 percent, to EUR 2.5095 from EUR 2.4368. Re-based roughly on the case's method the modal return is nearer 7 percent a year than the case's 9. The case re-looks at about EUR 20; the shares are about a quarter above that, and above their 200-day average of EUR 22.43.
  • 'Three years' (KQ 3, RA 3, E 2, BB 1), D, and 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1), C: less pressure, if reading one holds. This is where a full-grade move at Key Question level is plausible.
  • 'Maturity' (RA 3, E 2, BB 3), A: the letter is safe; the inference drawn from it, that nothing is left to propagate, is under more pressure.
  • 'Guidance raised' and 'Shareholder returns' (KQ 4 and KQ 12, RA 2, E 1, BB 1), both A: confirmed, same. 'New geographies' (KQ 7), B: less pressure.
  • 'Sell-side upgrades' (RA 1, E 2, BB 1), B: more pressure. The triggers the case named for October have fired, so they are behind us. The pinned sell-side letter is now C, the revision-momentum score 7 against 15 at the case's date, and the buy share 35 percent, down 5 points over three months.
  • 'Net debt' (KQ 4, RA 2, E 2, BB 1), B, 'Sustainability' (KQ 2, RA 1, E 3, BB 1), C, and 'Riskiness' (E 1, BB 4), D: more pressure under reading two. Production is above the case's 1,730 kboe/d alarm, pro forma gearing below its 15 percent alarm, guided cash flow above its EUR 13.8bn alarm, the Plenitude close inside its 31 October date, and the multiple of 10.0 times (56th percentile of ten years) below its 11.2 times alarm.
  • The pinned letters have moved since the case: technical momentum A from C, valuation C from D, sell-side earnings momentum C from A at the case's date and B at the 1 October re-pin. TSR/Valuation stays F.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry where consensus has settled now that broker estimates and targets have landed: above all whether the 2027 and 2028 consensus moves toward EUR 2.88 or leaves it an outlier, which is the first test of reading one. It is unlikely to carry anything on reading two, and it cannot carry the Venezuelan terms.
  • The Junin-5 final investment decision, targeted by the end of 2026. It settles reading one only if it comes with net entitlement and fiscal terms. The company has not committed to disclosing them, so this may stay a question nothing answers.
  • The parent's cash claim on the satellites. Payout ratios and timing for Plenitude and Enilive are not disclosed and no date is committed. The year-end accounts should at least show dividends received against joint-venture profit, and the terms attached to the EUR 1.80bn contribution. The date of those results is not in our evidence.
  • The fourth-quarter refining margin and tax rate. A margin near October's USD 18 a barrel and a tax rate back in the high thirties would confirm the case's reading of the beat; a rate held near 32 percent would be evidence for reading one.
  • The March 2027 Capital Markets Update, the case's own dated step. It sets the 2027 buyback against the case's alarm level of EUR 2.8bn and its modal of EUR 1.5bn to EUR 2.5bn, and it is where a volume plan above 4 percent a year would become guidance rather than one broker's model.
  • Versalis in the fourth quarter. A second loss above EUR 200m would make the case's 2027 invalidation level a live question rather than a dated one.
