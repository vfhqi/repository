Section 16 – Conclusion

CAF's H1 2026 earnings transformed the investment case from a recovery-focused narrative into a structural profitability re-rating story, driven by Solaris's unprecedented operational turnaround. The company delivered a historic record backlog of €17,743 million alongside a remarkable 114% year-on-year EBIT surge at Solaris to €60 million, fundamentally shifting the earnings composition away from capital-intensive rolling stock toward higher-margin zero-emission bus operations.

Consolidated revenue growth of 16% to €2,527 million and EBIT expansion of 32% to €150 million beat published consensus expectations of €138 million, demonstrating that management's commercial selectivity and operational efficiency initiatives have begun to translate into tangible bottom-line outperformance. Net profit attributable to the parent company expanded by 36% to €99 million, reflecting robust underlying momentum that extends well beyond cyclical volume tailwinds.

The single most important thing that changed in this earnings event was the confirmation that Solaris's profitability recovery is structural rather than transitory. The bus subsidiary achieved a record 6.30% EBIT margin in 2025, expanding from just 1% in 2023, with H1 2026 delivering 1,631 buses representing a solid 7% volume increase. This margin expansion trajectory validates management's post-acquisition integration strategy and demonstrates that zero-emission bus manufacturing can sustain premium pricing despite intensifying competitive pressures from Chinese OEMs.

Solaris generated €60 million in EBIT with a record 7.3% margin in H1 2026, while the railway segment delivered €90 million in EBIT with a stable 5.3% margin, proving that the group's dual-segment model provides balanced earnings resilience across economic cycles. consensus-deviation details that most significantly altered the investment thesis center on three core dimensions. First, the order backlog expanded 9% compared to late 2025 while order intake reached €4,035 million, representing a substantial 31% increase and pushing the book-to-bill ratio to 1.6x, providing growth. Second, consolidated free cash flow reached positive €12 million, improving from a negative cash outflow of €28 million in the first half of 2024, demonstrating that working capital management has materially improved despite the production ramp at Solaris. Third, analysts viewed Solaris's extraordinary contribution as a structural profit margin turnaround rather than a cyclical fluke, with the €17,743 million backlog supporting unrivalled long-term revenue visibility that justifies a valuation premium.

However, the strongest counter-hypothesis to this bullish conclusion revolves around three interconnected risks that could reverse the positive momentum. CEO Gorka Martínez Ojinaga raised warnings about unfair competition from Chinese OEMs, noting that if European municipalities opt for cheaper imports, CAF's backlog pricing power could erode. This competitive threat is particularly acute given Yutong's European market share rising from 7.6% to 15.5%, suggesting that aggressive pricing from Chinese competitors could cap Solaris's future margin expansion.

Additionally, working capital increased by €130 million during the first half driven by the manufacturing ramp-up at Solaris, and if Solaris's inventory fails to clear rapidly in the second half of the year, it could severely constrain the full-year cash conversion rate below historical targets. Finally, while CAF is tracking well toward its 2026 strategic financial target of achieving sales close to €4.8 billion with an EBIT margin exceeding 6%, key peer Alstom issued a margin warning of around 6% for FY25/26 due to severe execution and homologation delays, demonstrating that industry-wide execution risks remain elevated.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND about this structural re-rating thesis would be a combination of two specific trigger events occurring within the next six to nine months. While railway sales grew 3% to €1.703 billion, the segment's margin was still affected by backlog inflation, and operating profit in the rail business reached €84 million in H1 2024, showing flat performance compared to H1 2023, though order intake in rail services has remained highly resilient.

This margin stagnation represents a notable outlier relative to Solaris's explosive profitability recovery and suggests that historical fixed-price contracts signed during the 2021-2022 inflationary period continue to depress realized margins. Achieving the targeted €35 million to €50 million cost-saving program remains a critical execution milestone, where any delay would prevent operating recovery back to the pre-pandemic level of 6.3%.
