Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Medartis must balance its high-growth surgeon training and product rollouts against severe capital constraints and currency exposures to achieve profitability. The resolution of these feedback loops will dictate whether the company can successfully scale its premium extremities portfolio or remain constrained by capital intensity.

The Key Question

1.
Core inquiry: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"

Strategic Earnings Impact

2.
Accelerated expansion: Medartis has raised its 2026 U.S. volume target for the TOUCH thumb prosthesis to 3,000 surgeries following faster-than-expected clinical adoption. This volume growth is critical to leveraging the fixed overhead of its global plant footprint, including production facilities in Basel, Warsaw, and Besançon. If positive educational feedback loops accelerate this rollout, the resulting operating leverage could dramatically improve core earnings per share by 2027.
3.
Capital constraints: Medartis experienced a working capital expansion of CHF 25.0 million in the first half of 2026, driven by accounts receivable and inventory build-up. This cash consumption resulted in a negative free cash flow of CHF 10.1 million, illustrating how growth requires heavy upfront capital for surgical sets. Unless operating cash flow generation improves, these intensive capital requirements will continue to depress earnings and limit self-funded growth.
4.
Valuation metrics: Medartis trades at a high forward valuation relative to broader orthopaedics peers, with peer median multiples standing at 3.6x EV/Sales and 11.7x EV/EBITDA for 2026. If capital-intensive loops prevent the company from converting its rapid sales growth into positive cash flow, the market may de-rate its valuation toward cheaper mid-cap competitors. Conversely, demonstrating self-sustaining cash conversion by 2028 would justify its premium multiple and lower its long-term cost of capital.

Section 2 – Surgeon Education and Implant Pull-Through Loops

Section summary
  1. Medartis is rapidly expanding its surgeon training programs to target 10% of the US hand surgeon population by 2026.
  2. The September 2026 acquisition of the M.A.R.C. Institute establishes a permanent US educational infrastructure to accelerate implant pull-through.
  3. Peer precedents from Straumann and Medacta suggest a 24-month lag before surgeon training fully converts into core operational sales.
  4. Medartis utilizes the clinical success of the TOUCH thumb prosthesis to gain access to US hand surgeons and cross-sell its premium APTUS plating portfolio.
  5. Peer consolidation in extremities by Zimmer Biomet with Paragon 28 creates commercial disruptions that allow focused challengers like Medartis to recruit specialized sales talent and capture market share.
  6. Acumed maintains a strong incumbent position in wrist plating, but its rigid product bundling creates opportunities for specialized clinical alternatives.

Medartis Education Targets

1.
Rapid educational expansion: Medartis has significantly expanded its clinical training programs to drive product adoption and customer acquisition. The company’s primary academic partner, the International Bone Research Association, successfully trained 1,450 professionals in 2025. This represents an 18.2% yearon-year increase in membership, which reached 4,506 total members across 85 institute days. These educational programs serve as a vital starting point for long-term customer conversion.
2.
Ambitious regional targeting: Medartis is aggressively targeting the lucrative North American market by concentrating its education efforts on specialized clinical practitioners. The company is targeting 330 US hand surgeons in 2026, which represents roughly 10% of the entire specialized hand surgeon population in the country. This targeted effort aims to accelerate the commercial rollout of the newly launched TOUCH thumb joint prosthesis. Successful training of this cohort is expected to establish a strong network of key opinion leaders to pull through broader implant portfolios.
3.
Steady progress tracker: Medartis has made consistent operational progress toward its educational milestones throughout the first half of the year. The group successfully trained more than 180 surgeons in the first six months of 2026. This rate of progress keeps the company fully on track to achieve its annual target of training 330 hand surgeons. Management believes that these training courses, which include both existing customers and new surgeon relationships, are essential for driving product adoption.
4.
Delayed sales contribution: Medartis expects that the benefits of its surgeon training will take time to translate into significant financial results. Although the new surgeon base is developing favorably, cross-selling was still building in the first half of 2026. The sales contribution from trained surgeons adopting the broader portfolio is expected to be back-half weighted as the training programs gain momentum. This highlights a structural conversion lag inherent in the medical education sales model.

US Training Infrastructure

5.
Strategic infrastructure acquisition: Medartis completed a major transaction in September 2026 to secure its own dedicated training facility in North America. The company announced the acquisition of the M.A.R.C. Institute in Doral, Florida, on September 17, 2026. This facility represents the group’s first permanent surgical education center outside Europe. Establishing a local center allows Medartis to deliver hands-on training closer to regional surgeons, bypassing the need to transport them to Europe.
6.
Massive operational scale: Medartis will operate one of the largest surgical training and bioskills research facilities in the world through its latest acquisition. The M.A.R.C. Institute operates 36 fully equipped stations and can host up to 140 participants at one time. This massive scale provides Medartis with the capacity to train large cohorts of surgeons under realistic operating room conditions using fresh-frozen specimens. The facility will remain accessible to external participants, ensuring long-term financial stability.
7.
Synergistic regional hubs: Medartis is building a coordinated education network in Florida to support the second phase of its product rollout. Beside the Miami facility, the company scheduled the opening of its first US training center in Orlando in the fourth quarter of 2026. This regional focus is highly strategic because Florida possesses a dense, aging retirement population that is ideal for joint prosthesis procedures. These local centers will work in partnership with the International Bone Research Association to deliver decentralized education.
8.
Decentralized global reach: Medartis is extending its educational network to other high-priority regions to accelerate international sales growth. In addition to the US expansion, the M.A.R.C. Institute operates decentralized training sites in Brazil, including São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Curitiba. This allows Medartis to deliver education directly in a major Latin American value segment where it already operates the NeoOrtho brand. This integrated global educational framework is designed to drive rapid international implant pull-through.

9.

The company established the Medacta Orthopaedic Research and Education Institute as its primary commercial engine to support customized training pathways. Medacta allocates approximately 5% of sales to education programs. This high level of investment has enabled above-market organic sales growth by transforming surgeons into highly loyal customers.
10.
Proven conversion timeline: Medacta's educational track record provides clear evidence regarding the timeline and success of surgeon conversion. The peer achieves an impressive 70% educational course conversion rate, with trained surgeons becoming active customers within 24 months. This 2-year lag demonstrates that while surgeon education is highly effective, the financial pull-through is not immediate. This lag represents a crucial baseline that Medartis must navigate as it ramps up its US training efforts.
11.
Sustained customer loyalty: Medacta’s model demonstrates how early-stage surgeon training creates an extremely loyal customer base that drives long-term compound growth. Medacta reports that 84% of sales come from surgeons with more than 3 years of tenure. This peer benchmark highlights how surgeonto-surgeon training networks create powerful lock-in effects once the initial 24-month conversion phase is completed. This suggests that Medartis's current US investments could yield highly predictable recurring revenue streams by 2028.
12.
Successful new customer acquisition: Straumann provides another valuable precedent of utilizing targeted education platforms to convert non-users into active customers. Straumann’s premium implant portfolios have achieved major commercial success across mature markets. Crucially, some of its latest training initiatives have driven significant customer acquisition from the value segment. This confirms that educationled product launches can successfully penetrate competitor accounts, mirroring Medartis’s strategy of utilizing the TOUCH prosthesis as an entry point.

16.
Clinical momentum: Medartis achieved rapid commercial adoption in the United States as the specialized TOUCH thumb carpometacarpal joint prosthesis gained substantial surgeon interest. The rollout of the TOUCH system, which treats thumb base osteoarthritis, surpassed 2,000 clinical procedures by October 2026. This fast adoption among specialized upper extremity surgeons provides an entry point to cross-sell other fixation systems, potentially boosting core earnings per share as high-margin implant volumes expand.
17.
Strategic portfolio: Medartis focuses on a broader upper extremity clinical approach designed to win accounts through joint replacement and pull through fixation products. This commercial strategy seeks to establish surgeon trust with the TOUCH prosthesis before introducing the premium APTUS hand plates and screws. The clinical synergy between joint replacement and internal fixation helps Medartis maximize its wallet share within existing surgical accounts. This integrated account development model is expected to drive higher average revenue per surgeon over the next three years.
18.
Account expansion: Medartis has actively trained specialized hand surgeons to build a loyal customer base for its upper extremity implants. The company trained more than 180 surgeons in the first six months of 2026 and remains on track to train 330 hand surgeons by the end of the year. These trained specialists represent prime targets for adopting the broader APTUS hand system, which treats complex hand fractures and joint fusions. Converting these trained surgeons into multi-product users is critical to achieving profitability targets in North America.
19.
Sales lag: Medartis acknowledges that translating initial TOUCH accounts into recurring APTUS plating sales requires a defined transitional period. Although the trained US surgeon base expanded favorably throughout the year, the sales contribution from cross-selling was still building in H1 2026. This sequential adoption pattern indicates a typical conversion lag as hospital value analysis committees review secondary product lines. Managing this commercial lag is crucial to ensuring that upfront distribution investments deliver immediate operating profitability.

24.

The private manufacturer utilizes long-term hospital purchasing contracts and comprehensive hardware portfolios to secure account loyalty. This incumbent lock-in makes it difficult for new entrants to convert surgeons who are accustomed to specific plate profiles and instrument layouts. Overcoming this operational inertia requires Medartis to offer distinct clinical advantages, such as its specialized CADskills 3D-printed implants.

This bundling strategy protects its core wrist plating market share by locking hospitals into multi-product discount agreements. However, these rigid corporate bundles can alienate specialized hand surgeons who prefer clinical choice over administrative pricing mandates. Medartis can target these discerning clinicians by positioning its premium APTUS plates as high-precision alternatives.

Developed in collaboration with leading clinical specialists, the system supports early postoperative mobilization through low-profile plate designs. This premium positioning is designed to rival the clinical performance of established incumbents like Acumed. Successfully demonstrating this clinical superiority is vital to driving voluntary surgeon adoption of both TOUCH and APTUS.
27.
Contrary evidence: Medartis's efforts to drive commercial cross-selling face significant headwinds from institutional hospital value analysis committees. The company admitted that getting secondary fixation portfolios approved in accounts that already use TOUCH averages up to four months. This administrative delay prevents immediate sales pull-through, proving that clinical surgeon preference alone is insufficient to bypass institutional purchasing barriers.
28.
Other side of the story: Medartis may struggle to convert TOUCH adoption into premium APTUS plating sales if hospital administrators enforce sole-source fixation contracts. While surgeons prefer the dual-mobility TOUCH prosthesis, hospital value analysis committees often restrict adopting secondary internal plates. If hospital bundling contracts with incumbents like Acumed block the APTUS portfolio, Medartis's cross-selling loop will stall. This commercial bottleneck would restrict extremities revenue growth and delay the company's path to positive operating cash flow.

Section 4 – US Sales Channels and Agent Exclusivity Loops

Section summary
  1. Medartis is restructuring its unhedged US distribution network toward exclusive independent partnerships to accelerate Extremities market penetration.
  2. Peer models from Globus Medical and Paragon 28 demonstrate that salesforce exclusivity and targeted representative recruiting drive rapid market- share capture.
  3. High distributor commission structures and systemic representative churn pose significant operational risks to Medartis's mid-term earnings growth.

Medartis US Sales Channels

1.
Accelerated channel growth: Medartis reported that exclusive US distributors achieved outstanding performance during its strategic commercial transition, based on 15 relevant sources, covering 4 companies and 2 comparable past cases. The company's unhedged US business delivered strong organic growth of 27.4% in H1 2026, reaching a record CHF 30.3 million despite dollar depreciation. Exclusive US distribution partners drove this momentum by growing sales 46% compared to just 16% for multiline partners. This performance demonstrates that dedicated agents are highly effective at expanding regional market share.
2.
Bold distributor restructuring: Medartis initiated a sweeping review of its US sales channel to drive product exclusivity. Management replaced approximately half of its 57 distribution partners between 2024 and H1 2025 to eliminate multiline dealers carrying competing systems. This optimization increased the total distributor network to 65 partners by early 2026. Focusing on brand-exclusive agents is designed to secure better representation in operating theatres.
3.
Strategic regional disruption: Medartis faced immediate sales friction when it decided to terminate its relationship with its largest Florida distributor in mid-2025. Terminating this partner represented approximately 10% of total US sales, creating a temporary revenue gap. Although the company refilled the territory with five new distributors, full integration in South Florida was still recovering in late 2026. This transition highlights how relying on large independent agents exposes the company to localized volume risks.
4.
Substantial commission incentives: Independent orthopedic distributors are highly motivated by lucrative compensation models that reward aggressive volume growth. Independent agents typically command commissions of 30% to 45% of sales from smaller manufacturers to fund their own local sales infrastructure. This model shifts commercial risk away from Medartis but increases overall distribution costs. Consequently, distribution expenses rose to 37.1% of sales in H1 2026 from 36.4% in H1 2025.

Agency Churn and Growth Risks

Section 5 – Working Capital, Set Capital, and Capacity Overheads

Section summary
  1. Medartis faces a highly capital-intensive business model where rapid expansion is structurally balanced by severe working capital consumption and upfront surgical set investments.
  2. Manufacturing overheads from five active global production facilities create high fixed costs that require significant sales volumes to absorb.

Working Capital And Set Capital

1.
Accelerating capital drainage: Medartis experienced a significant drain on its cash reserves during its global expansion phase as new accounts demanded heavy upfront capital. The company reported a working capital increase of CHF 25.0 million in the first half of 2026, which severely restricted its operating cash flow. This cash consumption was primarily driven by a CHF 16.3 million increase in total accounts receivable and prepaid expenses.
2.
Heavy upfront commitment: The consignment business model of Medartis requires placing complete implant and instrument sets directly within hospitals before generating revenue. As of 30 June 2026, the company's total set value reached a substantial CHF 64.8 million. These set values consist of instruments capitalized under property, plant, and equipment and implants held as inventory on the balance sheet. This structural tieup of resources acts as a balancing loop that slows down cash availability for future marketing and product development.
3.
Elevated inventory burden: Medartis has struggled to optimize its inventory turnover while attempting to support its rapid commercial rollout in direct markets. The company's net inventories reached CHF 102.5 million by mid-2026, representing a significant percentage of its CHF 160.8 million half-year sales. This high inventory level, where goods for sale and set inventory alone accounted for CHF 80.3 million, reflects the need to maintain extensive stock to prevent back orders. Such capital density will continue to depress earnings per share until sales velocity increases.
4.
Sustained peer capital intensity: Medacta Group provides a stark peer precedent of how an investment-led strategy in extremities and joint replacement can limit free cash flow. Deutsche Bank reported on 15 September 2026 that Medacta's volatile free cash flow was driven by deliberate investments in surgical instrument sets and manufacturing capacity. Medacta allocates approximately 80% of its capital expenditure to instruments and production expansions to support its above-market organic growth. This comparative metric underscores the industry-wide challenge of converting operating profits into free cash flow when scaling specialized portfolios.
5.
Substantial kit expenditure: Medacta's commercial model demonstrates the heavy upfront financial commitments required to secure new clinical customers. Supplying a single new customer with at least one surgical kit requires an investment ranging from Euro 30 to 50 thousand. Furthermore, there is a substantial timing lag of 6 to 12 months before this upfront investment begins to generate meaningful implant sales. Medartis faces a similar capital-intensive set loop that will delay its operating margin realization in the United States.

Capacity Overheads And Plant Footprint

6.
Expanding manufacturing footprint: Medartis has rapidly expanded its manufacturing infrastructure from a single Basel site to five distinct production facilities across three continents. The company now operates production centers in Switzerland, the United States, Brazil, France, and Belgium to support its multi-brand strategy. While this footprint provides localized supply benefits, it also introduces substantial fixed cost overheads that require massive production volumes to absorb. If sales growth decelerates, these unabsorbed factory costs will severely depress reported operating margins.
7.
Depressed plant utilization: Medartis acquired its Warsaw, Indiana facility in 2022 to accelerate its market presence in the United States. During the company's fourth-quarter earnings call on 17 March 2026, management disclosed that they currently use less than 20% of the manufacturing capacity available in Warsaw. To address this low utilization and counter tariff pressures, the company launched Project Flash to transfer machines from Basel. However, running a facility so far below its optimal capacity acts as a negative loop that dilutes consolidated gross margins.
8.
Ambitious production targets: Medartis is aggressively ramping up its US onshore production to mitigate a projected 12.5% tariff rate on imported products. Under Project Flash, the company targets producing 70% of US sales locally by the end of 2026, rising to 80% by mid-2027. This rapid transition requires significant capital expenditure, with H1 2026 investments exceeding the prior year by nearly CHF 10 million. Failure to quickly transition US volume would leave the Warsaw plant as an expensive underutilized overhead.
9.
Modular factory construction: Medartis is leveraging its NeoOrtho acquisition in Brazil by constructing a new modular production facility in Curitiba. This state-of-the-art facility is scheduled to encompass 6,800 square meters of production space upon completion of its second phase. While the site is equipped with advanced machining to serve the Latin American value segment, the high construction costs have contributed to the group's negative free cash flow. The success of this facility depends on NeoOrtho successfully winning highly competitive public hospital tenders.
