EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report examines how German plant-engineering and automation companies comparable to INDUS Holding AG's Engineering subsidiaries have converted record order backlogs booked in a trough into revenue and margin in the following two years, what share of backlog margin was typically lost to cost inflation, project delays and under-absorption, and what capacity utilisation or revenue level the INDUS Engineering segment needs to reach its guided band of 8.5% to 10.5%.

The analysis draws on comprehensive evidence from four listed peers (Dürr, Krones, Jenoptik, and Gesco) across 15 distinct documents, supplemented by broker research and expert interviews to establish conversion patterns, margin leakage mechanisms, and operational leverage requirements. The Key Question can be partly answered based on available evidence. The overall confidence in what the evidence shows regarding INDUS Holding's backlog conversion and margin path is medium.

The Engineering segment posted a weak EBIT margin of 5.60% in H1 2026 on revenues of EUR 279.60m, significantly below its target margin corridor of 8.50% to 10.50%, despite accumulating an all-time high order backlog of EUR 430.40m as of June 30, 2026. While peer conversion dynamics and historical margin erosion patterns provide robust comparative benchmarks, critical operational details–including precise subsidiary-level capacity utilization rates, contractual price-escalation clauses, and customer call-off schedules–remain undisclosed in public documents, limiting the precision of near-term margin forecasts.

Section 2 – Backlog Conversion Dynamics of Listed Peers

Section summary
  1. German plant-engineering peers demonstrate highly variable backlog conversion timelines of 6 to 24 months, driven primarily by project size and customer execution speed.
  2. Book-to-bill normalisation near 1x across peers indicates a transition from rapid trough bookings to more sustainable, execution-focused revenue generation phases in late 2026.
  3. High-end technology niches like semiconductor optics exhibit much faster conversion than complex customized paint shops and turnkey logistics systems which suffer from severe customer-induced delays.

Peer conversion timelines and patterns

1.
Extensive peer database: This analysis evaluated 15 distinct documents covering four comparable peer companies to understand their backlog execution dynamics. According to Dürr’s annual report of 5 March 2026, the company represents a key baseline for custom paint and assembly engineering. Dürr’s annual report from 5 March 2026 reveals that systems business revenue recognition typically follows order intake with a lag of 6 to 12 months. Dürr's annual report of 5 March 2026 states that the majority of system orders are recognized over time using the percentage of completion method.
