Section 1 – Business Model and Organisation

Section summary
  1. Tecan operates two distinct pillars to generate revenue from global laboratories, achieving sequential sales recovery in late 2025 and 2026.
  2. Direct workstation sales and OEM contract manufacturing drive underlying revenue, backed by value- based pricing and commercial channel expansion.
  3. Historical earnings targets faced post-pandemic volume declines, prompting proactive management changes and executive leadership resets to secure business continuity.
  4. Structural transformation through the Rewired program and state-of-the-art NVIDIA partnerships position Tecan to lead AI-driven laboratory workflows.

Section 3 – Industry Structure, Concentration and Where Profits Are Made

Section summary
  1. The global laboratory diagnostics market is expanding structurally due to automated workflows, despite near-term headwinds from regional reimbursement devaluations.
  2. Direct competition in liquid handling is led by high-end customizable workstation suppliers, while specialized OEM players focus on routine diagnostic instrumentation.
  3. Organized diagnostic chains utilize scale and automated workflows to expand margins, while smaller regional laboratories face severe fee pressure.
  4. Standardized laboratory platforms and clinical validation determine industry winners, while sub-scale players face severe margin erosion from rising pricing pressure.

Global Size and Structural Drivers

1.
Steady expansion: The global in vitro diagnostics market grew from USD 66.8 bn in 2018 to USD 118.1 bn in 2025 according to estimates by China Insights Industry Consultancy. Analytical forecasts project that the global market will reach USD 171.4 bn by 2030, driven by the increasing automation of laboratory workflows. Expanded clinical applications and rising global diagnostic testing volumes support this long-term growth trajectory compared with historical baselines.

Automated acceleration: The emergence of artificial intelligence agents is transforming traditional research workflows by connecting predictive computational models directly with physical laboratory automation. The traditional design-make-test-analyze cycle is evolving into a continuously operating, machine-assisted learning loop. Proprietary offline data and validated physical screening shift competitive moats toward proprietary datasets and automated wet-lab infrastructure rather than standalone algorithms.
3.
Rapid shift: The Asia-Pacific region is experiencing the fastest demand expansion for advanced blood processing technologies and automated diagnostic instruments. Governments in China and India are accelerating healthcare expenditure and domestic initiatives to modernize laboratory infrastructure. Easing regulatory barriers and an aging population drive accelerated adoption of novel therapies across Asian decentralized regions compared with European markets.
4.
Severe pressure: European clinical laboratory providers face consolidation demands due to tightening government reimbursement frameworks and rising operational costs. Proposed medical tariff reforms in Germany could devalue private diagnostic billing by an estimated 34% according to accredited laboratory association drafts. Shifting testing volumes toward larger laboratory groups allows operators to mitigate these tariff cuts through network optimization and hardware automation compared with sub-scale clinics.
