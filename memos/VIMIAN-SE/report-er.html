Section 16 – Conclusion

The Q2 2026 earnings print delivered a definitive operational inflection point for Vimian Group, with consolidated revenue of EUR 121.6 million representing 17% total growth and adjusted EBITA of EUR 29.7 million marking a 17% increase, both beating consensus by approximately 4%. This performance was anchored by an acceleration in organic revenue growth to 12%, significantly outpacing the 5% delivered in Q2 2025 and exceeding the broader animal health market.

The quarter fundamentally reshapes the investment case by confirming that Vimian's decentralized platform can simultaneously execute across all four business segments, validating management's thesis that its specialty healthcare niches possess structural resilience despite persistent macroeconomic headwinds affecting broader companion animal volumes.

The most significant operational achievement was the MedTech segment's return to 11% organic growth with margin expansion to 25.8%, driven by the successful global integration of the veterinary dental platform and stabilization of North American orthopedics. Specialty Pharma sustained its leadership position with 14% organic growth and a stable 30.1% margin, though adjusting for a US nutrition sales campaign reveals an underlying 9% growth rate.

Veterinary Services expanded clinic memberships to 11,900 while delivering 10% organic growth, and Diagnostics contributed 7% organic expansion following the integration of Italian provider I-Vet. However, the quarter's primary operational blemish was a severe contraction in operating cash flow to EUR 3.8 million from EUR 22.2 million in the prior year, driving calculated operating cash conversion down to 13% from 87%.

Management attributed this cash squeeze to net working capital increasing by EUR 21.3 million due to elevated trade receivables of EUR 15.7 million and tax payments of EUR 13.5 million following a concentrated June sales finish.

The single most important change that alters the investment case is the restoration of MedTech commercial credibility through the veterinary dental platform rollout. The transformative acquisition of iM3 in October 2024 established a full-suite global platform for dental equipment, imaging, consumables, and software, with management actively leveraging iM3's established infrastructure to accelerate expansion outside the United States.

Strategic bolt-on acquisitions including Dental Focus for advanced imaging and AllAccem for its highmargin SANOS preventative sealant have successfully offset persistent volume declines in the US orthopedics segment. This dental strength allowed the MedTech segment to achieve an 11% organic revenue growth rebound in Q2 2026, consolidating Vimian's position as a leading companion animal player. The dental expansion demonstrates that Vimian can successfully pivot its portfolio mix toward higher-growth, highermargin specialty categories without relying solely on surgical volume recovery, fundamentally de-risking the medium-term earnings trajectory and providing a credible pathway to the ambitious EUR 300 million adjusted EBITA target by 2030.

The strongest counter-hypothesis challenges this constructive view by arguing that the Q2 operating cash flow collapse signals structural deterioration in business quality rather than temporary working capital timing. The 83% drop in cash flow from operating activities to EUR 3.8 million and the calculated operating cash conversion decline to 13% represent alarming deviations from Vimian's historical asset-light profile.

While management attributed this to elevated trade receivables following a strong June sales finish, the magnitude of the EUR 21.3 million net working capital increase raises concerns about sustainable cash generation. Under this counter-hypothesis, the group's inability to restore its long-term average cash conversion above 70% would fundamentally compromise its self-funded M&A pipeline and force increased leverage toward the 3.0x ceiling.

If working capital fails to normalize rapidly as projected by management, the group faces persistent cash constraints that will restrict self-funded bolt-on acquisitions, potentially forcing increased leverage and endangering its long-term target of achieving EUR 300 million in adjusted EBITA by 2030. This scenario would trigger a valuation de-rating back toward the bear-case target of SEK 25, as investors reassess whether Vimian's decentralized platform can deliver predictable cash flows or if the rollup model demands perpetual external capital infusions.
