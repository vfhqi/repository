Section 4 – Hydropower, Pumped-Storage, and Grid Firming Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Structural grid-firming demand and wind-solar integration catalyse unprecedented pumped-storage backlogs for major equipment manufacturers.
  2. Elongated turbine manufacturing cycles of three to five years lock in long-term capacity while elevating pricing power.

1.
Consolidated triopoly: The global electromechanical market outside China is dominated by a triopoly of Andritz, Voith Hydro, and GE Vernova.
2.
Leading shares: Market assessments by Bernstein Research show Andritz leading the global market with a twenty-two percent share, closely followed by Voith Hydro at twenty-one percent.
3.
Distant third: The same Bernstein Research analysis ranks GE Vernova third with a twelve percent market share while local suppliers capture highly price-sensitive smaller projects.
4.
Fragmented remainder: The remaining forty-four percent of the global market is highly fragmented among numerous local manufacturers and specialised Chinese players like Dongfang Electric.
5.
Regional dominance: In regional markets like Canada, Andritz holds a commanding position with an estimated forty to forty-five percent share of the active provincial fleet.

Demand tailwinds and aging infrastructure

6.
Systemic instability: Rapid solar and wind expansion decreases grid inertia, driving structural demand for flexible assets that can provide immediate frequency and voltage control.
7.
Aging fleet: The market opportunity is heavily underpinned by a massive replacement cycle, as approximately forty percent of the global fleet is already older than forty years.
8.
Refurbishment focus: Analysts at Morgan Stanley point out that mature European and North American markets offer lucrative upgrade and service opportunities where fleet age exceeds thirty years.
9.
Scalable batteries: Pumped storage has emerged as the most mature long-duration option, operating as scalable water batteries for grid balancing over multi-hour cycles.
10.
Strategic prioritisation: Governments increasingly treat pumped-storage assets as strategic infrastructure to strengthen domestic energy security while evacuating regional renewable power generation.

13.

14.
Indian momentum: Voith Hydro's Indian unit showcases strong regional demand, securing a twenty-two billion rupee order book with multi-year execution visibility.
15.
Polish selection: Expanding its European footprint, Voith Hydro secured a municipal contract from PGE Energia Odnawialna for construction and equipment works in Poland.

Swiss award: In Switzerland, Kraftwerke Oberhasli awarded Voith Hydro a contract to supply two seventy- megawatt variable-speed pump turbines for the Grimsel four project.

Lead times, pricing power, and contract escalation

Elongated schedules: Extreme engineering complexity and factory constraints extend equipment lead times to between three and five years on large-scale hydro developments.
