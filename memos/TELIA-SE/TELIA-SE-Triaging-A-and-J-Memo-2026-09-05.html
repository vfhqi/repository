Executive Summary -- We are looking for a clear, conviction-carrying statement of the investment thesis, key ratings, and actionable conclusion; avoiding vague or overly hedged language.
Judgement summary
  • Overall verdict, park rather than progress. C Telia has delivered a genuine operational change, but the price already carries it, so the case fails on entry point rather than on execution
  • Valuation upside is the binding objection (Element 3, sufficient total-shareholder-return). D Dashboard consensus target of SEK 43.48 sits below the SEK 43.82 spot price, so the sell-side sees no upside at all from here
  • Trading multiple is the worst single reading. D Forward price/earnings of 19.7x sits at the 99th percentile of its own ten-year history, and the 2026 unlevered cash yield of 4.5% lags a 6.3% sector average
  • Case simplicity fails the guardrail (Element 7, required case simplicity). D Five named key drivers against a four-driver ceiling, six operating markets, and at least seven concurrent change-from-current items running at once
  • Secular growth is structurally absent (Element 13, high secular growth potential). D Swedish postpaid mobile average revenue per user has been flat since the first quarter of 2019, and European mobile service revenue growth has stagnated to near flat
  • The risks report recommends parking, and this memo adopts that recommendation. Its stated verdict is to park until sustainable organic service revenue growth is demonstrated, with a re-evaluation trigger at organic growth below 1.50%
  • The risks report's reasoning is rejected even though its verdict is adopted. C Its competing-hypotheses matrix picks "false friend" as winner, but that finding rests on earnings quality, not on the transmission opacity that defines a false friend
  • Earnings base quality is flattered by cash phasing. Both 2026 quarters beat on cash for phasing reasons: Q1 free cash flow was SEK 1,917m against SEK 1,018m, Q2 SEK 2,200m against SEK 1,264m
  • Returns on capital remain bottom-quintile against the peer set. Return on capital employed reached 9% in 2025 from 4% in 2023, against Elisa at 18.50% and Tele2 at 22.00%
  • Two of three historical cost programmes missed. The 2021 programme targeted SEK 2bn of savings by 2023 and delivered a net operating expense increase of SEK 1.1bn, which is the base rate the current programme must beat
  • Balance sheet is now a genuine strength. Net debt to adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation fell to 1.93x at end-2025 and 2.06x in Q2 2026, inside the 2.0x to 2.5x target corridor
  • The good news is narrow, and it is the corporate-change setup. A All nine corporate-change deltas fired at once, from a new chief executive to a halved central function, the strongest reading in this memo
  • Operating delivery has genuinely inflected. Q2 2026 like-for-like service revenue grew 2.8% to SEK 17,838m, the highest growth rate in four years, beating the compiled consensus of SEK 17,662m by 1.0%
  • Setup fit is corporate change, not mispricing (Element 8, fit with acceptable case setups). C The clearing-event setup that fitted at Triaging no longer does: the stock rose 30.9% over twelve months and estimates are being raised, not cut
  • Transmission mechanism is unusually clear (Element 18). B Headcount to operating expense to earnings to free cash flow to dividend is traceable link by link, and every link has a published quarterly series
  • Sell-side earnings momentum is the pinned bright spot (Master Rating 2). B Earnings-per-share revisions run positive across every window, at plus 2 over one month and plus 8 over twelve months
  • Evidence base is deep and caps conviction only mildly. Eight research families are on disk and all eight were read, including a Q2 2026 earnings review and a key-question report on the Norwegian venture
  • Recommendation, park with medium conviction. Re-engage on a de-rating toward the 6.50% twenty-year free cash flow yield, or on two consecutive quarters above 2% organic growth
Business Description (B.1) 18 findings
Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
Core activity. Telia Company sells fixed and mobile network connectivity, broadband, television and enterprise information-technology services to households and businesses across the Nordic and Baltic region
2.
Subscription base. The group serves approximately 25 million mobile, broadband and television subscriptions, billed monthly, which makes service revenue highly recurring by construction
3.
Revenue mix by type. Service revenue is 85.8% of total group revenue, split between mobile services at 43% and fixed services at 38%; equipment sales are 14.2% of revenue
4.
Equipment role. Handset and hardware sales are a low-margin customer-acquisition tool rather than a profit engine, and they introduce quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility
5.
Geographic mix. Sweden is approximately 45% of group revenue, Finland 18%, Norway 17%, Lithuania 7% and Estonia 5%, so Sweden is the dominant profit engine
6.
Reporting structure. Segments are country-based, with Telia Towers, Telia Finance and the remaining Latvian operations reported under other operations, and Nordic towers moved to Telia Asset Management in 2025
7.
Customer split. Residential consumers are 54% of segment revenue and enterprise customers 46%, with low concentration on any single account
8.
Enterprise anchors. Named public and sovereign accounts include the Swedish Armed Forces, the Swedish Contingencies Agency, a Norwegian Armed Forces contract worth over NOK 300 million, and the Finnish government procurement frame agreement Hansel
9.
Revenue scale and trend. Net sales were SEK 80,981m in 2025 against SEK 80,965m in 2024, so the reported top line was flat year on year
10.
Margin profile. Adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation reached SEK 31.92bn in 2025, up 5.20% like for like, on a group margin near 39.4% (calculated)
11.
Operating income. Reported operating income fell to SEK 10.43bn in 2025 from SEK 10.83bn in 2024, held back by a SEK 3.71bn non-cash asset-retirement provision
12.
Cash generation. Free cash flow was SEK 9.3bn in 2025 against SEK 3.4bn in 2024, and management guides to around SEK 9bn for 2026
13.
Balance sheet. Net debt to adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation was 1.93x at end-2025 and 2.06x at end-Q2 2026, with Moody's affirming a Baa1 rating
14.
Capital intensity. Booked capital expenditure excluding spectrum was SEK 12.83bn in 2025, and management guides below SEK 13bn for 2026
15.
Ownership. The Swedish state holds 41.1% of the shares, a single-class structure applies, and executive management receives zero variable compensation
16.
Recent portfolio moves. Television and Media was sold to Schibsted for an enterprise value of SEK 6.55bn, Bredband2 was acquired for SEK 3.1bn, and a Latvian exit is targeted for the second half of 2026
17.
Leadership. Patrik Hofbauer has been chief executive since February 2024 and Eric Hageman chief financial officer since September 2023, operating a country-led model launched on 1 December 2024
18.
Investment style implication. The financial character is a regulated-utility-like income and cash compounder, not a growth compounder and not a cyclical, with earnings driven by cost and capital discipline rather than volume

Answer, change forces are present, deep and predominantly internal. B Telia is running the most complete corporate reset in its peer set, while the external environment supplies modest tailwinds and material headwinds in roughly equal measure
2.
The internal leg is the strong one. A new chief executive, a new chief financial officer, a rebuilt country leadership team and a halved central function arrived inside a twenty-four-month window
3.
The external leg is thinner than the narrative suggests. Defence and sovereign demand is genuine but small: the change-forces report sizes mission-critical business at plus 1% to plus 1.5% of group service revenue annually to 2027
4.
Peer anchor. Against Elisa, whose change-forces reading is a credibility inversion, and Tele2, whose programme is comparable in intensity, Telia sits at the top of the peer set on breadth of internal change

Core Question 1, are external change forces or tailwinds strong? C They are real but modest, and partly offset by regulatory and input-cost headwinds running the other way
5.1
Defence and mission-critical demand. Management explicitly targets a doubling of mission-critical service revenues between 2023 and 2027, with Swedish business-solutions revenue up 2.5% in Q1 2026
5.2
Named sovereign contracts anchor the claim. Telia Finland secured NATO framework certification, and the group holds a Norwegian Armed Forces contract valued at over NOK 300 million across four years
5.3
A regulatory tailwind specific to Telia. Finland plans to extend its exclusion of Chinese network vendors to base stations, and because Telia Finland uses Nokia exclusively it gains against Elisa and Telenor
5.4
Spectrum and framework reform helps predictability. The proposed European Digital Networks Act of January 2026 introduces longer, renewable-by-default spectrum licences to improve investment predictability
5.5
The offsetting regulatory drag is priced in tariffs. Wholesale data roaming charges step down to one euro per gigabyte from 2027, and the network and information security directive raises compliance spend
5.6
Input costs turned against the group in 2026. Hofbauer stated that hardware prices have risen dramatically since the start of the year, and optical component lead times now exceed 52 weeks
6.
Core Question 2, are internal change forces strong? A They are exceptional, and every one of the nine corporate-change levers has fired inside two years
6.1
Leadership was rebuilt end to end. Patrik Hofbauer arrived February 2024 and Eric Hageman September 2023, joined by a new chief technology and information officer in March 2025 and new heads of Finland, Norway and the Baltics
6.2
The operating model was replaced, not adjusted. The country-led model went live exactly on schedule on 1 December 2024, moving profit-and-loss accountability to country heads and halving central products and systems
6.3
The workforce reduction is the largest in the peer set. The change programme cut 3,000 positions, no less than 15% of the end-2023 baseline, with employees and consultants down about 25% since early 2024
6.4
The savings are quantified and audited by delivery. At least SEK 2.6 billion of annualised savings was targeted, of which SEK 2.3 billion sits in operating expense, and group operating expense fell 2.2% in Q1 2026
6.5
The perimeter was reshaped by five transactions. Television and Media went to Schibsted for SEK 6.55 billion, Denmark to Norlys for DKK 6.25 billion, and Bredband2 came in at SEK 3.1 billion
