What the company does

Shell is an integrated oil and gas supermajor valued on 16 October at 240,384 million euros. It reports through Integrated Gas, Upstream, Downstream, Marketing and Renewables and Energy Solutions. Upstream revenue tracks Brent crude; liquefied natural gas sells mostly through multi-year oil-linked contracts; Integrated Gas provides 33 percent of group operating cash flow.

The centre of gravity is gas. Shell holds the largest publicly listed liquefied natural gas portfolio, handling nearly 20 percent of global demand against TotalEnergies' estimated 10 percent, alongside 44,109 retail stations and the world's number one lubricants business for 19 consecutive years. Adjusted earnings were 18.53 billion dollars in 2025.

A plausible investment thesis

A cash machine that shrinks its own share count. Since Wael Sawan became chief executive on 1 January 2023, Shell has pivoted to high-margin hydrocarbons, capped cash capital expenditure at 20 to 22 billion dollars a year to 2028, and promised 40 to 50 percent of operating cash flow to shareholders with free cash flow per share growth above 10 percent a year to 2030. The mechanism to earnings per share is arithmetic: the share count fell 19 percent in three years on 43 billion dollars of buybacks, a nineteen-quarter run of at least 3 billion dollars a quarter, and structural cost savings reached 5.83 billion dollars by mid-2026 against a 5 to 7 billion target for 2028.

The second-quarter 2026 print showed the machine at full power: adjusted earnings of 9.84 billion dollars beat consensus by 10 percent, free cash flow surged to 17.52 billion dollars and gearing dropped to 18.7 percent from 23.2 percent. The 16.4 billion dollar ARC Resources acquisition, completed 2 September 2026, lifts production growth to 2030 to 4 percent a year from an organic 1 percent. Drivers to track: Brent (400 million dollars of liquids cash flow per dollar a barrel), gas prices, refining margins, the buyback pace, the Pearl gas-to-liquids restart and the reserve replacement ratio.

Business quality

High on moats, middling on returns. The moats are physical: destination-flexible liquefied natural gas contracts, a trading desk backed by real assets, wellhead-to-water integration and unit production costs cut 47 percent to 8.54 dollars a barrel by 2024. Gearing of 18.7 percent is the strongest among European majors, and the 2026 distribution yield of 8.2 percent leads TotalEnergies' 7.1 percent and BP's 5.1 percent. Against that, return on average capital employed fell from 13.4 percent in 2023 to 9.8 percent in 2025, downstream is the weakest link, and the business foundations digest of 29 July names resource longevity the single largest foundation risk.
