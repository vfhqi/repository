EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

MATERIALITY OF CHANGE

The leadership and governance change at Hexpol represents extreme executive volatility with two CEO transitions in less than three years, including the sudden passing of Georg Brunstam in September 2023 and the dismissal of Klas Dahlberg in May 2026 due to sluggish M&A execution, creating significant strategic continuity risk during a critical transformation period. Henrik Elmin assumed the CEO role on August 1, 2026, bringing extensive operational experience from Atlas Copco, yet the pending permanent leadership stabilization remains a critical execution gate for the 2030 strategic plan targeting above 10% EPS CAGR.

The group executed a structural business reorganization by breaking out Thermoplastic Compounding as a separate reporting segment in early 2026 and transferring Izmir and Vilafranca units to build critical scale, representing a major strategic pivot toward replicating the successful rubber compounding consolidation journey in the large thermoplastics market. Material acquisitions include the EUR 143.5 million Vipa Group purchase completed October 1, 2026, and the EUR 54 million Kabkom Kimya acquisition in Turkey, both operating at above-group profitability levels and delivering immediate high-margin wire and cable electrification exposure. Organic sales growth inflected positive at 8% in Q2 2026, ending twelve consecutive quarters of contraction, while adjusted EBIT margin reached 14.6%, settling within the revised 14-16% range versus the previous 17% cycle target, marking a fundamental inflection point in operational momentum.

Driver #1: Vipa Group M&A Integration – The completed acquisition boosts the wire and cable segment with above-market compounding profitability, strengthens exposure to data centers and high-value energy infrastructure, and prompted DNB Carnegie to raise adjusted EPS forecasts by 1% to 5%, delivering immediate earnings accretion with high plausibility and confidence. Driver #2: Full Balance Sheet M&A Deployment – Tremendous firepower exists with net debt-to-EBITDA at low levels of 1.17, and deploying fully leveraged balance sheets adds 34% to 53% to earnings, representing the highest magnitude impact driver but constrained by medium plausibility due to elevated target valuation expectations.

Driver #3: Automotive Volume Recovery – Light vehicle compounding represents approximately 40% of group volume exposure, with DNB Carnegie expecting production to grow steadily across European and US regions, providing steady baseline operating leverage with medium plausibility tied to consumer interest rate sensitivity. Driver #4: Electrification Grid Demand – Electrification tailwinds support double-digit organic growth in the thermoplastic compounding segment, delivering high plausibility structural demand with minimal cyclical dependency.

Driver #5: Procurement Scale Advantage – Hexpol commands a 3% to 3.50% gross margin advantage over smaller compounding competitors, representing a high confidence defensive moat that protects profitability across raw material cycles. Driver #6: Dynamic Pass-Through Pricing – Rapid monthly price adjustments mitigate volatile petrochemical cost spikes to preserve absolute operating profits, providing high plausibility margin protection despite distorting percentage margins.

Driver #7: Footprint Consolidation Programs – Closing the Kennedale plant optimizes North American asset utilization and reduces fixed manufacturing overheads, consolidating California operations from two sites to one improves regional cost structures, and the innovative Mixing 5.0 project aims to yield substantial cost improvements via advanced factory production automation, delivering self-help margin expansion with medium confidence given extended startup timelines.

Compound Risk Analysis – A simultaneous drop in automotive and European building activity presents the largest stacked downside scenario, with negative volume swings rapidly diluting operating margin percentages due to fixed manufacturing overheads, while achieving the joint bull case is estimated near 15% probability, highlighting severe execution sensitivity to multiple concurrent drivers.

RECENT EARNINGS DELIVERY (Q2 2026) Organic sales growth inflected positive 8% year-on-year, ending twelve quarters of contraction and beating the industrial sector median of negative 2%, while reported sales reached SEK 5,280 million, beating consensus of SEK 5,050 million by 4.6% on strong volume recovery, marking a decisive positive inflection versus historical underperformance. Adjusted EBIT reached SEK 772 million, exceeding analyst estimates of SEK 745 million by 3.6% on volume leverage, though adjusted EBIT margin was 14.6%, down 50 bps, matching the updated mid-term target of 14-16% on negative mix, demonstrating strong absolute profit delivery despite modest margin compression.

Rubber compounding organic growth hit 7% driven by cable and construction volumes, Thermoplastic compounding organic sales rose 12% benefiting from medical and electrification projects across major European markets, while Engineered products organic sales contracted 3% impacted by weak Swedish demand, revealing wide segmental performance dispersion with thermoplastics leading growth. Sales in Europe jumped 10%, outperforming regional compounding peers despite a minor 1% negative foreign exchange headwind, while North American sales rose 3% despite severe economic uncertainty and a 4% foreign exchange translation drag, highlighting strong European recovery offsetting persistent US softness.

Operating cash flow reached SEK 678 million, converting 93% of operating profit into cash, though net working capital developed negatively by SEK 84 million driven by logistics safety stock in the Red Sea, demonstrating robust underlying cash generation despite temporary working capital headwinds. DNB Carnegie raised its target price to SEK 88 acknowledging the first organic growth inflection in twelve quarters, subsequently boosting the target to SEK 95 to reflect the completed Vipa Group acquisition, while reiterating a HOLD rating awaiting a permanent CEO to execute the thermoplastic expansion strategy, reflecting improving but cautious sell-side sentiment.

FORWARD GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY

Historical performance shows chronic long-term target misses with Hexpol continuously missing its 10% sales growth targets due to prolonged organic contraction prior to the mid-2026 volume rebound, prompting management to adjust historical 17% EBIT targets to a realistic 14-16% range reflecting structural polymer mix shifts, classifying management as an honest target tracker archetype that transparently adjusts long-term strategic ambitions.

Net debt-to-EBITDA remains low at 1.17, securing substantial capital deployment capability under the expanded leverage framework, though meeting the updated 10% EPS CAGR targets rests heavily on accelerating acquisitions, creating high dependency on M&A execution to achieve forward earnings ambitions. Extended customer automotive plant closures in Q4 2024 drove significant volume declines across the North American business units, while capital expenditures remained consistently below depreciation supporting robust free cash flow throughout the cyclical demand troughs, and strong localized control allowed rapid manning adjustments mitigating margin compression during sequential industrial demand declines, demonstrating strong operational cost flexibility as a defensive safety buffer.

The absence of short-term quarterly guidance forces reliance on sell-side consensus as an earnings proxy, while automotive volume sensitivity at 40% of group volumes creates high cyclical exposure that threatens the 14% margin floor during prolonged light vehicle build downturns. DNB Carnegie reiterates that a permanent CEO remains essential to fully unlock valuation re-ratings, positioning leadership stabilization as the critical credibility gate for the 2030 strategic plan.

DNB Carnegie raised its target price to SEK 95 acknowledging positive organic volume growth trends, yet Hexpol trades at a 20% discount to peers reflecting its historical organic growth underperformance, indicating improving but still skeptical consensus positioning. The average recommendation mix skews heavily toward buy ratings accounting for 61% of total coverage, while neutral hold ratings represent 35% reflecting near-term organic growth caution, and negative sell recommendations remain an extreme minority at just 4%, revealing broadly constructive but cautious analyst sentiment.

FUNDAMENTAL PEER POSITIONING

Hexpol recorded positive organic growth of 8% year on year ending twelve consecutive quarters of contraction, while Avient posted organic sales growth of 4.3% in Q2 2026 securing its first organic growth expansion, and AirBoss Rubber Solutions continued to face market softness across most sectors due to ongoing North American headwinds, positioning Hexpol as a relative organic growth leader among North American compounding peers.

Hexpol maintains superior operating margins of 15.4% well ahead of regional compounding competitors, while Avient achieved a record adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.3% supported by positive net pricing execution, demonstrating top-tier but not best-in-class profitability within the specialty polymer peer universe. Hexpol kept net working capital under 8% of sales, retaining its best-in-class operational efficiency, representing a clear competitive advantage in capital velocity versus traditional compounders with much higher working capital days.

Slower M&A activity has dampened short-term investor enthusiasm despite excellent underlying balance sheet capacity, creating a valuation discount opportunity relative to peers executing more aggressive consolidation strategies. The compound probability analysis indicates Hexpol faces lower downside volatility than peers due to its diversified geographic footprint and superior procurement scale, yet the 40% automotive volume concentration creates cyclical sensitivity parity with traditional auto-exposed compounders.

ACTIVISM PRESENCE

No active activist presence is documented for the advanced polymer group, with Melker Schörling AB remaining the largest shareholder with 45.8% votes and 24.8% capital, while SHB Funds and Lannebo Funds represent the next largest Swedish institutional holders with 6.2% and 4.9%, indicating a highly concentrated and stable ownership structure without activist pressure. Carnegie analysts note the board should prioritize share buybacks instead of paying generous dividends, while management explicitly rejects buybacks as first priority is compounding M&A to drive growth, revealing a persistent capital allocation debate but no formal activist campaign to force redistribution policy changes. The absence of activist intervention leaves capital allocation decisions fully in the hands of the board and dominant family shareholder, reducing near-term catalyst uncertainty but also eliminating potential shareholder-friendly governance pressure to accelerate M&A deployment or initiate buybacks during the current valuation discount period. TOP RISKS Risk #1: Ongoing Executive Volatility – Two CEO transitions in less than three years undermine strategic continuity, while upcoming European departures of Carsten Rüter and Ralph Wolkener by the end of 2027 deprive the compounder of three decades of technical expertise, creating severe execution disruption risk during the critical 2030 strategic plan roll-out. Risk #2: High Employee Turnover – The employee turnover rate reached 23.0% in 2025 reflecting elevated labor instability, while operational decisions are made locally increasing reliance on key managers amid high turnover, threatening localized customer relationship disruption across the decentralized manufacturing platform. Risk #3: Thermoplastic Acquisition Dilution – Aggressive acquisition-led expansion into thermoplastics could dilute Hexpol's historically high margins if integration synergies fail to materialize in fragmented markets, representing the highest magnitude strategic execution risk to the forward margin profile. Risk #4: Compounding Raw Material Shocks – Severe compounding raw material shocks coupled with prolonged automotive cycle stagnation could completely erode pricing power benefits and trigger substantial margin compression, positioning petrochemical feedstock volatility as the primary external threat to profitability stability. Risk #5: Cyclical Volume Leverage – A simultaneous drop in automotive and European building activity presents the largest stacked downside scenario, with negative volume swings rapidly diluting operating margin percentages due to fixed manufacturing overheads, highlighting severe operational deleveraging risk if multiple end-markets contract concurrently. Risk #6: Cyberattack Production Disruption – Modern industrial compounding relies on IT networks, exposing operations to severe cyberattack production disruptions, representing an emerging operational continuity threat across the highly automated decentralized footprint.

The compound downside probability of joint cyclical decline and failed thermoplastic integration could trigger margin compression below the 14% floor, while the 20% valuation discount to peers offers limited downside protection if organic growth inflection proves temporary and customer pre-buys dissolve in H2 2026.

Outliers worth noting

Hexpol's financial profile and operational structure reveal several pronounced statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies when benchmarked against global polymer compounding peers and specialty chemical sector base rates. These outliers span governance stability, operational efficiency, market positioning, and strategic capital allocation, with each deviation carrying distinct implications for the firm's forward execution credibility and valuation trajectory.

The most striking outlier concerns employee turnover, which reached 23.0% in 2025, a figure that substantially exceeds manufacturing industry norms and raises fundamental questions about organizational culture and talent retention within a decentralized platform that explicitly depends on localized technical expertise. This turnover rate appears particularly anomalous given Hexpol's market-leading compensation capacity and stable end-market presence, suggesting potential friction between the lean corporate headquarters model and operational site autonomy.

The high churn coincides with multiple senior executive departures across critical regional divisions, including the Americas and European operations, compounding execution risk during a period when management is attempting to replicate its rubber compounding consolidation playbook within fragmented thermoplastic markets. Cross-referenced analysis in Section 1 documents this pattern alongside three decades of technical knowledge loss from departing European executives, creating an unusual concentration of human capital flight that peer compounders like Avient and Rogers have not experienced at comparable scale.

A second pronounced outlier emerges in Hexpol's working capital efficiency, which consistently operates below 8% of sales, a performance metric that stands in stark contrast to the specialty chemical peer median of 12-15% and represents best-in-class capital discipline across global manufacturing platforms. This exceptional efficiency appears partially explained by Hexpol's dominant market share positions enabling superior supplier payment terms, yet the persistence of this advantage through volatile petrochemical cycles and Middle East logistics disruptions remains statistically unusual.

Section 4 and Section 7 document how this metric has held steady even as peers experienced working capital deterioration, with Hexpol maintaining approximately 400-700 basis points of structural advantage over the closest polymer compounding competitors despite operating across similar automotive and industrial end-markets with comparable customer concentration profiles. The third material outlier concerns Hexpol's estimated 80% market share in Mexican compounding operations, a dominance level that appears inconsistent with typical industrial market fragmentation patterns and raises questions about competitive barriers, customer lock-in mechanisms, and potential regulatory scrutiny risk.

Expert testimony in Section 3 confirms this exceptional market position, yet the figure appears anomalous given the relatively modest capital intensity of compounding operations and the absence of proprietary technology moats that would typically sustain such concentrated market power. This Mexican dominance contrasts sharply with Hexpol's more fragmented European positions and suggests either unique historical acquisition timing advantages or unexplained competitive withdrawal by global peers from a strategically important nearshoring manufacturing corridor.

A fourth statistical outlier manifests in Hexpol's chronic twelve-quarter organic sales contraction from Q3 2023 through Q1 2026, a prolonged revenue decline that significantly exceeded peer performance during the same period and cannot be fully explained by automotive cycle timing or raw material deflation alone.
