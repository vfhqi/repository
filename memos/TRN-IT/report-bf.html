Outliers worth noting

Terna exhibits three major statistical outliers that position the company at extreme ends of the European regulated utility peer distribution, revealing both structural advantages and embedded execution challenges that differentiate its risk-return profile from comparable transmission system operators. The first and most pronounced outlier manifests in Terna's aggressive asset expansion trajectory, where the company targets a 9.2% Regulatory Asset Base CAGR over FY25-FY30, substantially outpacing Snam at 5.7% and Italgas at 3.2%.

This places Terna in the top 5% of European regulated network utilities for organic capital deployment intensity, reflecting Italy's acute need to modernize transmission infrastructure to accommodate massive renewable energy integration. The company's 10-year development plan outlines planned investments exceeding EUR 23 billion over the 2025-2034 period, with the total potential value of grid projects rising to EUR 40 billion when including works beyond 2034.

While this exceptional growth trajectory creates a powerful long-term earnings compounding mechanism, it simultaneously exposes Terna to disproportionate execution risk compared to slower-growing gas network peers. The structural challenge emerges because work-in-progress assets earn a lower pre-tax real return of 4.1% versus 5.5% on completed operational assets, meaning that prolonged construction timelines or permitting delays would trap disproportionate capital in under-remunerated buckets, creating a material drag on realized returns that peers with more modest capital programs avoid.

This growth outlier fundamentally distinguishes Terna's risk profile, as the company must execute flawlessly on an unprecedented construction pipeline while peers like Snam operate in more mature, stable networks with limited expansion requirements. The second critical outlier appears in Terna's depressed post-tax equity returns, where the company's average return on equity of 9.9% materially lags Italgas's peer-leading 15.2%.

This positions Terna in the bottom 5% of Italian regulated utilities for equity profitability despite operating an absolute transmission monopoly with theoretically superior pricing power. The return dilution stems from Terna earning an expected 5.8% pre-tax return on calendar RAB in FY26, substantially below Italgas at 7.3%. The root cause of this underperformance is twofold: first, the massive work-in-progress pipeline creates a temporary return drag as construction assets earn discounted remuneration, and second, work-in-progress is calculated on 80% gearing versus 50% for operational assets, creating a 140bps return dilution.

Unlike Italgas, which achieves superior returns through exceptional operational efficiency and cost outperformance, Terna's return profile remains structurally constrained by the regulatory treatment of its high-growth asset base. This outlier reveals a fundamental tension in Terna's investment case: the company must endure several years of depressed returns during the capital deployment phase before enjoying the full benefits of completed infrastructure.

Management projects that Terna's actual equity return will rise from 9.5% to 10.5% by 2030 driven by progressive network re-gearing, but this multi-year return trajectory creates meaningful opportunity cost versus immediately profitable peers.

The third outlier manifests in Terna's extreme customer revenue concentration, where a single electricity market counterparty accounted for EUR 978 million or 47% of total sales in H1 2026, while regulated activities represent 78.7% of total consolidated revenue. This places Terna in the top 5% of European utilities for singlecounterparty exposure, creating an unusual concentration profile even within the context of regulated monopolies.

The Group's H1 2026 effective tax rate rose to 31.6% due to a 2% temporary IRAP increase under the Decreto Bollette, demonstrating how political interventions can materially impact Terna's financial outcomes when a single counterparty or regulatory relationship dominates the revenue base. While the regulatory framework shields the business by indexing both operating costs and RAB to inflation, with 95% of EBITDA fully insulated by economic regulation, the extreme concentration creates binary execution risk: any systemic disruption in the primary customer relationship or unexpected regulatory intervention could impact nearly half of Terna's revenue stream simultaneously.

Peer utilities demonstrate more diversified profiles, with Italgas recording an adjusted tax rate of 28.6% in H1 2026 and Snam reporting 26.7% for the same period, suggesting that Terna faces disproportionate exposure to single-counterparty regulatory or political decisions. This concentration outlier is partially mitigated by the monopolistic nature of Terna's business and the socialized cost-recovery mechanisms embedded in Italian electricity regulation, yet it remains an unexplained statistical extreme that distinguishes Terna's risk profile from more diversified European network operators.
