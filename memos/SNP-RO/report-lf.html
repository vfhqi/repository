EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Christina Verchere demonstrates strong operational leadership and exceptional strategic execution at OMV Petrom, having delivered 160% total shareholder return from 2022 to 2025 while advancing the Neptun Deep project to over 85% completion.

Her 25-year career in upstream oil and gas, including regional presidencies at BP in Canada and Asia Pacific, provided deep sector expertise directly relevant to OMV Petrom's operational needs.

She exhibits five strong qualities out of six assessed in the management framework: relentless drive, bold decision-making, strategic adaptability, disciplined capital allocation, and plain-spoken direct communication.

Her only partial quality is the achievement of exceptionally high standards compared to Western European peers, where she successfully contained hydrocarbon decline to the second-lowest rate in eight years but has not yet matched the massive buyback programs executed by competitors like Repsol.

Her delivery record at OMV Petrom is robust, characterized by progressive dividend increases of 5% to 10% annually, the allocation of 40% to 70% of operating cash flow to shareholders, and the strategic reallocation of EUR 1 billion from unproven low-carbon technologies back to high-return exploration and production investments.

She successfully de-risked the Neptun Deep gas project, which is projected to produce USD 3 per barrel plateau barrels and reduce overall exploration and production costs to USD 9 by 2030, while expanding the renewable portfolio to over 2.5 GW by 2030, surpassing the initial 1 GW target.

Chief Executive Quality and Delivery

Verchere displays strong relentless drive in executing the complex Neptun Deep project, which is now over 85% complete, shows strong capabilities in bold decision-making by successfully transitioning to sole operator of Neptun Deep after ExxonMobil's exit, demonstrates strong adaptability to market conditions by pragmatically reducing low-carbon capital allocations to 25% of investments, maintains strong disciplined capital allocation policies by distributing significant base and special dividends while avoiding share buybacks, and exhibits strong plain-spoken non-evasive communication on earnings calls by setting firm boundaries with persistent analysts.

She demonstrates partial execution of high standards compared to peers, containing hydrocarbon decline but trailing Repsol's massive buybacks.

Under Verchere, separate sales revenues reached RON 30,735 million in 2025, growing 4.44% year-on-year from 2024, while she contained domestic hydrocarbon decline, achieving the second-lowest decline in eight years during 2025.

Verchere accelerated Neptun Deep development, which is now over 85% complete and on track for first gas in 1H 2027, with the project projected to produce USD 3 per barrel plateau barrels, reducing overall exploration and production costs to USD 9 by 2030.

Verchere delivered exceptional shareholder returns, averaging approximately 30% TSR per year over the 2022-2024 period, with cumulative returns of 160% total shareholder return from 2022 to 2025, while distributing EUR 0.8 billion in total dividends in 2025, including EUR 0.3 billion in special dividends.

Governance Robustness and Structural Limitations

OMV Petrom's overall governance is clean and highly transparent despite the inherent concentration of control with its parent company, with shareholders benefiting from equal voting weight as the company maintains no dual-class or preferential share structures.

OMV AG maintains a controlling fifty-one percent economic interest in OMV Petrom, leaving minority investors with limited strategic influence, although the company strictly applies the one share, one vote principle across all of its outstanding stock with no gap between economic ownership and voting power.

The Supervisory Board comprises nine active non-executive members who oversee the Executive Board, with OMV AG holding a majority fifty-one percent stake and appointing most non-independent board representatives, while the Romanian State holds twenty-one percent of shares and nominates representatives to protect national interests, resulting in only four out of nine members qualifying as independent under local governance standards.

The Audit Committee is chaired by Jochen Weise, an independent nonexecutive director with extensive energy experience, with four out of six members on the Audit Committee being fully independent non-executive directors, while the Nomination and Remuneration Committee contains only one independent member out of four, reflecting parent company control.

Shareholders approved the reappointment of KPMG Audit SRL as external auditor for the 2026 financial year, with the company rotating its statutory auditor from Ernst and Young in 2022 to KPMG for subsequent terms, and OMV Petrom has no material prior period restatements or internal control failures reported by its auditors.

Executive Alignment and Share Dealing Signals

Executive pay at OMV Petrom is partly aligned with shareholder value due to strong operational metrics but historically weak shareholdings, with the updated remuneration framework introducing purely local performance criteria and payouts denominated entirely in company shares, where from 2026 onwards all variable pay performance criteria refer exclusively to OMV Petrom performance and the long-term incentive plan is paid exclusively in company shares.

Executive alignment is limited by negligible direct share ownership among board members despite progressive long-term incentive structures, with CEO Christina Verchere holding zero shares of free float directly as of early 2026 and CFO Alina Popa holding exactly 100 shares assigned during a legacy employee distribution in 2010.

Executives at OMV Petrom executed zero open-market share transactions over the last 12 months, with CFO Alina Popa holding only 100 shares assigned during a historical employee distribution in 2010 and Executive Cristian Hubati maintaining a minor static holding of 100 shares from the same 2010 distribution, while Board member Radu-Sorin Căprău holds exactly 30,000 shares directly without any active trading during the year, resulting in direct open-market ownership by active executives remaining at a negligible level relative to the overall free float.

Western European peers demonstrate far more dynamic executive equity transactions and structured share-based incentive payments, with parent CEO Alfred Stern settling his 35,922 vested LTIP shares entirely in cash rather than equity on March 31, 2026, OMV AG CFO Reinhard Florey similarly settling 13,044 vested LTIP shares in cash on March 31, 2026, and Galp board member Diogo Mendonça Tavares divesting his entire equity holding of 30,450 shares on March 10, 2026.

Zero active insider selling could signal positive executive confidence in the upcoming 1H 2027 Neptun Deep gas startup.

Single Largest Credibility Risk

The most fragile leadership quality is production stability because domestic oil and gas volumes remain in mature decline, and delays in the start-up of Neptun Deep before 1H 2027 would leave OMV Petrom without immediate volume growth.

The strongest evidence for plan delivery is Neptun Deep's advanced completion stage, which will slash future upstream costs, while the strongest evidence against plan delivery is the joint mandate expirations in April 2027, posing critical senior succession risks.

The company lacks an obvious designated internal CEO successor within its current public corporate disclosures, although all five executive board member mandates run jointly until April 2027 to ensure operational execution continuity.

Simultaneous mandate expirations could trigger massive transition risks in April 2027 if multiple directors depart.

Sudden regulatory shifts or punitive fiscal regimes in Romania could undermine the project's economics regardless of management's high execution quality.

Outliers worth noting

OMV Petrom exhibits several genuine outliers when measured against its European integrated energy peers, ranging from exceptional shareholder returns to unusual governance structures and unexpectedly limited executive equity ownership. The most striking outlier is exceptional shareholder return performance, with OMV Petrom delivering approximately 30% total shareholder return (TSR) per year over the 2022-2024 period and a cumulative 160% total shareholder return from 2022 to 2025.

This performance places the company in roughly the top 5 percent of European energy sector returns during a period when many peers struggled with energy transition costs and volatile commodity prices. The scale of outperformance is particularly notable given OMV Petrom's status as a mid-cap regional player rather than a global major, suggesting that management's combination of progressive dividend policies distributing significant cash to shareholders and strategic execution on high-return conventional assets like Neptun Deep has resonated exceptionally well with investors during this cycle.

A second notable outlier relates to executive equity alignment, where CEO Christina Verchere holds zero shares of free float directly and CFO Alina Popa holds only 100 shares assigned during a legacy employee distribution in 2010. This level of direct ownership is far below the peer norm for European energy executives, where even mid-sized companies typically require meaningful personal equity stakes to align management interests with shareholders.

The complete absence of active open-market share transactions by any OMV Petrom executive over the last 12 months stands in sharp contrast to the dynamic buying and selling patterns observed at Western European peers like Galp and Neste. While this may partially reflect limited local market liquidity compared to Western European peers, the zero direct ownership by the CEO remains an unusual governance outlier that contradicts the company's public emphasis on shareholder value creation.
