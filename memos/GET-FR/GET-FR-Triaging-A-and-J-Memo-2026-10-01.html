[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Getlink is a yield-and-optionality hold on a monopoly asset, not a growth case, and at €18.83 it is not compelling. A 4.5 percent dividend yield plus high-single-digit earnings growth on a concession that runs to 2086, fairly priced at its own five-year median multiple, pays about 12 percent a year over eighteen months and about 10.5 percent a year over three years against our 20 percent bar. The parts that would make it interesting, rail liberalisation from 2030 and a lower rate environment, sit beyond the horizon and are not the company's to deliver.

  • The external change is a mild, cyclical tailwind acting on the edge of the profit pool, not on its core.
    • The cross-Channel market is flat, down 0.2 percent over the last twelve months, and the shuttle volumes fell in the first half of 2026 (cars -2 percent to 962,452, trucks -1 percent to 587,710), so nothing outside the company is lifting its core demand.
    • What is moving is the competitors' cost base: the three ferry operators' combined fuel and carbon surcharge reached about €50 a crossing in H1 2026 against Eurotunnel's €32 electricity surcharge, and LeShuttle's truck share rose 90 basis points in May and 160 basis points in June 2026 on that gap.
    • Against that sit three live headwinds: the EU Entry/Exit System, whose partial passenger rollout from April 2026 cost about 2 percent of passenger shuttle traffic
      • UK business rates rising from €6 million in 2026 to €24 to 27 million by 2028
      • And a UK industrial cycle in which vehicle output fell 7.5 percent in H1 2026 while about 70 percent of Fixed Link demand is UK-driven.
  • The internal change is a tidy set of second-order actions, none of which re-sets the company's trajectory.
    • The dividend was re-set to €0.80 for FY2025 (+38 percent) with a policy of +€0.05 a year to €1.00 by 2030
    • The 2030 EBITDA target of €1 billion is +16 percent over five years
    • Unbundled fares lifted the premium tier to 19 percent of passenger volumes and yield 4 percent
    • The customs and solar projects are small.
    • There is no chief-executive change (Yann Leriche has held the post since July 2020), no perimeter change and no balance-sheet action, and the July 2026 guidance rise was 1.5 percent at the midpoint.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain at ElecLink and only as far as the operating metrics at the Fixed Link.
    • ElecLink's revenue rose 71 percent to €157 million in H1 2026, 98 percent of 2026 capacity is sold and 71 percent of 2027 has fetched €256 million, and the sell-side's EBITDA estimates carry it
    • That is the chain complete.
    • At the Fixed Link the change shows in yield (+4 percent) and share (cars 60.2 percent), not in volume, and Q2 2026 pricing slowed to +3 percent from +5 percent underlying in Q1.
    • Sell-side EPS estimates have barely moved, +0.7 to +2.0 percent over twelve months, because the EBITDA beats are absorbed below the operating line by depreciation, inflation indexation, the ElecLink provision unwinding and tax
      • The H1 2026 net profit missed consensus by 5.2 percent on a revenue and EBITDA beat.
  • 'Financial Outputs' improve, but slowly and in the wrong places.
    • Consensus revenue rises 8.0 percent in FY2026 to €1,723 million and then 3.8 percent and 0.8 percent
    • The rise is ElecLink, and the core Fixed Link grows 3 to 4 percent a year.
    • The EBITDA margin drifts from 53.9 percent in FY2025 to 50.9 percent in FY2026 on consensus
      • Company free cash flow fell from €714 million in 2022 to €374 million in 2025 as capex rises toward €170 to 220 million a year to 2032
      • Net debt is 3.9x EBITDA and falling slowly.
    • Our modal EPS path on the brokers' basis is €0.551 for FY2026 (below FY2025's €0.580), €0.617 for FY2027, €0.646 for FY2028 and €0.692 for FY2029: about 7.9 percent a year compound from FY2026, half the floor of the framework's 12 to 20 percent band, and about half of that growth comes from below the operating line.
  • The price already pays for what can be proved and nothing more.
    • At €18.83 the shares stand on 29.5x the 24-month-forward consensus EPS of €0.6385, which is the stock's own five-year median (29.56x), the 69th percentile of two years and the 30th percentile of ten years
    • Our own path gives €0.639 at the same point, so the price is 29.5x our earnings too.
    • Against the airport peers (14.3x to 21.1x) the premium is 40 to 105 percent, and it is structural: a 61-year concession against 25-to-50-year terms, near-zero cash tax on €5.4 billion of credits, and leverage that makes equity earnings a thin slice of EBITDA.
      • The peer gap is no basis for a re-rating either way.
    • What is priced: the FY2026 guidance beat (consensus €877 million against a range of €835 to 870 million), the dividend path, the two anchor holders' floor and ceiling, and ElecLink's 2026 book.
      • What is not: an ElecLink 2027 print above €350 million, a lower rate environment, and rail entrants from 2030.
  • The 'Modal return' is about 12 percent a year over eighteen months and 10.5 percent a year over three years, and it is carried by the dividend.
    • The three components: cash return 4.5 percent of the entry price over eighteen months (€0.85 on €18.83)
    • Earnings growth 1.137 in the 24-month-forward figure (€0.6385 to €0.726)
    • Re-rating 1.000 at a flat 29.5x.
    • The product is €22.27 over eighteen months (+18.3 percent, 11.9 percent a year) and €25.39 over three years (+34.8 percent, 10.5 percent a year).
    • The modal path clears the 20 percent bar only below €16.94 over eighteen months and €14.69 over three years
      • Eiffage's last block was at €17.40.
    • The re-rating branch to the ten-year median of 31.7x would lift the eighteen-month return to 17.1 percent a year, a C
      • We give it about 28 percent odds, the product of real yields falling (plausible, about 40 percent) and the yield narrative holding through Q3 and Q4 2026 (probable, about 70 percent).
      • The blend at those odds is 30.1x and 13.3 percent a year: the bottom of the C band, against the top of D on the modal branch.
  • The chain holds at the operating line and breaks in two places: below it, and at the multiple.
    • A plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to EPS (depreciation €260 million, tax 15 percent, finance €10 million higher) cuts the EPS path about 10 percent and the return to 4.5 percent a year over eighteen months, an F on the Element
    • And the multiple moves with bond yields rather than with anything the company does, a 50 basis point move being worth 7 to 10 percent of value.
  • The pattern it fits is 'Compounder', loosely.
    • The proven-upgrader half is genuine (FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 EBITDA all at or above the guided ceiling, and a stated policy of under-promising)
    • The mis-modelled half is thin, one small line (ElecLink 2027) set against consensus that is too generous below the operating line.

  • The case is therefore a safe 4.5 percent yield plus high-single-digit growth on a monopoly asset, fairly priced, with the interesting parts beyond eighteen months.

    • It is not material enough to progress at this price, and the price at which it would be is about €17.00.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is not material enough to clear the bar: the forces are second-order (Change Forces C), the outputs improve at 2 to 3 percent a year at EBITDA and about 8 percent a year at EPS (Financial Outputs D), and the 'Modal return' of 10 to 12 percent a year sits in the D band ('Modal return' D).

    • The parts combine as a chain in which the weakest link, the outputs, sets the level, because the return cannot exceed what the outputs deliver when the multiple is held flat.
    • Not C because none of the three Elements reaches C on the return that matters
    • Not F because the return is positive, the yield secure and the downside bounded by the anchor holders.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case for a higher rating is that Getlink is a mis-modelled compounder of cash returns.
    • On that view the sell-side models ElecLink 2027 revenue at €313 million when 71 percent of capacity has already fetched €256 million, a full-year run-rate above €350 million
      • It models Fixed Link EBITDA flat when truck share rose 160 basis points in a single month on ferry cost inflation
      • And it ignores that consensus 2030 EBITDA of €954 million sits below a target management has never missed.
    • EPS would then compound at 12 percent a year, and a multiple supported by a 9.0 percent cost of equity and two anchor buyers at €17.40 to €17.70 would re-rate toward 32x for a 20 percent a year return.
    • What we concede: the guidance record is excellent, three consecutive years at or above the ceiling
      • The ElecLink 2027 line in consensus is probably conservative
      • And the Eiffage floor is real.
    • Why we hold our view: the ElecLink upside is halved by the 50 percent profit share above a 13 percent IRR and the provision already stands at €580 million
      • Fixed Link EBITDA was flat in H1 2026 at €294 million with opex up 6 percent and Q2 pricing decelerating to 3 percent
      • Below EBITDA the H1 net profit missed by 5.2 percent on depreciation and tax, which points the drop-through the wrong way
      • And EPS revisions of +0.7 to +2.0 percent over twelve months show the sell-side has already absorbed the EBITDA beats.
      • Twelve percent EPS growth would need Fixed Link volume growth, and the volumes are falling.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external forces net to a mild tailwind (ferry cost inflation, a moderately wider power spread, rail liberalisation far out) against three live headwinds (border friction, UK business rates, the UK freight cycle), and the internal forces are a tidy set of second-order actions, none of them the kind that re-sets a company's trajectory.
  • Ferry cost umbrella: the one force that is both real and dated, a €50-a-crossing surcharge against Eurotunnel's €32, is acting on the competitors' costs rather than on the market's size.
  • Core volumes: no force in either set is strong enough to move the group's core volumes, which are flat to falling.
  • Not D: the ferry cost umbrella and the contracted ElecLink revenue are real and dated.
    • Not B: no force moves the core.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the strongest external forces act on the periphery of the profit pool (ferry costs, power spreads, border checks) while the core cross-Channel market is flat, down 0.2 percent over the last twelve months to September 2026, and the direction nets to a slight tailwind that is cyclical, not structural.
  • Direction by force: demand a headwind (cars -2 percent, trucks -1 percent in H1 2026)
    • Competition a tailwind (ferry surcharges of about €50 a crossing against €32)
    • Disruption a headwind now and neutral later (the Entry/Exit System)
    • Political and regulatory mixed (business rates a headwind; carbon pricing and seafarer wage laws a tailwind; the ElecLink profit-sharing methodology unresolved at Ofgem)
    • Supply side neutral (energy hedged, one refurbishment supplier in dispute)
    • The company's own cycle flat
    • Macro a headwind (UK industrial output).
  • Not B: the 'Demand Inflection' test is not met, because no sector-level demand change with two-plus years' visibility exists on the Short Straits.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because end-market demand is flat to falling: the cross-Channel market was down 0.2 percent over the twelve months to September 2026, Passenger Shuttle traffic fell 2 percent to 962,452 vehicles and trucks 1 percent to 587,710 in H1 2026, and August 2026 passenger shuttle traffic fell 4.9 percent while trucks rose 4.6 percent on a working-day effect.
  • Rail demand: the only structural upside is high-speed rail: Eurostar carried 5.84 million passengers in H1 2026 (+4 percent, a first-half record), and the company's target of 22 million high-speed passengers by 2035 from 14 million in 2025 is a 2030-plus story.
  • Freight base: truck volumes in FY2025 remained 31.3 percent below the 2018 peak.
  • Not F: the mix is improving (the premium tier is 19 percent of passenger volumes against 15 percent) and rail passenger demand is growing at 4 percent.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because competitive intensity is easing on cost, not on capacity: the three ferry operators' combined fuel and carbon surcharge reached about €50 a crossing in H1 2026 against Eurotunnel's €32 electricity surcharge, their carbon surcharge alone rose to about €10 from €3 in 2024, and LeShuttle's truck share rose 90 basis points in May and 160 basis points in June 2026 while its car share rose 35 basis points to 60.2 percent.
  • Capacity: it has not eased.
    • Bernstein counts up to 13 vessels on Dover-Calais
    • The earlier count of 11 falling to 8 predates that
    • P&O lost £95 million in 2023.
  • Not A: the tailwind is fuel-price-driven and reversible.
    • Bernstein warns the gap closes if oil normalises, and "under-the-radar" ferry price cuts of 20 percent have been recorded.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the UK industrial cycle bears on Getlink more than on the universe: UK vehicle output fell 7.5 percent in H1 2026 to 385,979 units and exports 5.6 percent, and about 70 percent of Fixed Link demand is UK-driven.
  • Sterling: unhedged, with one third of revenue in sterling against about 60 percent of costs in euros
    • A 10 percent sterling fall cuts EBITDA by €46 million.
  • Inflation: cooling inflation is a small offset on the indexed debt (the indexation charge was €26 million in H1 2026 against €31 million a year earlier).
Catch-all: any other material external dynamic, positive or negative? (KQ 8, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) optional Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because rising sovereign bond yields act on the multiple of a concession running to 2086, and the direction now is a headwind: Bernstein models a 50 basis point rise in the discount rate as a 10 percent cut in its target, and the change-forces research gives 7 percent per 50 basis points of real yield.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because regulation cuts both ways and the cost side is dated and certain: UK business rates add €6 million in 2026, €14 million in 2027 and €24 to 27 million by 2028 after the failed Valuation Office Agency appeal, while EU ETS on ferries (70 percent of emissions in 2025, 100 percent in 2026), the UK ETS from July 2026 and seafarer wage laws lift ferry costs by 6 to 12 percent.
  • ElecLink profit-sharing: CRE has approved the methodology and Ofgem has not
    • The provision stood at €580 million at 30 June 2026, up from €516 million
    • No cash has been paid, and Morgan Stanley projected a first cash payment in 2027.
  • UK rail freight: Getlink froze all UK rail-freight investment in protest at a 69 pence tax on each new pound of UK revenue.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the live disruption force, the EU Entry/Exit System, is a headwind that is being managed rather than disproved: the passenger rollout began in April 2026 with partial implementation, negative UK press coverage cost about 2 percent of passenger shuttle traffic, and average waits tripled to 20 minutes, while the truck and coach rollout since October 2025 has worked.
  • Readiness: Getlink spent €80 million on pre-registration zones
    • Management doubts the September 2026 EU deadline.
  • Not D: the friction is symmetric across the Channel, and Getlink's terminal readiness is better than the ports'.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the company's own cycle is flat and not visibly turning: Eurotunnel revenue rose 3 percent in H1 2026 on yield (+4 percent) against volume (cars -2 percent, trucks -1 percent)
    • Q2 car pricing decelerated to +3 percent from +5 percent underlying in Q1
    • August 2026 cars were -4.9 percent and trucks +4.6 percent.
  • Group revenue: the 13 percent jump in H1 2026 is ElecLink (+71 percent to €157 million), not the core.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because energy input costs are contained (ARENH replaced by power purchase agreements and solar for two-thirds of former volumes, a 24-month rolling hedge for the rest, consumption cut 14 percent) while the specialised rolling-stock supply base is concentrated (180 suppliers are 80 percent of spend) and one key supplier walked out of the shuttle refurbishment in April 2025, now in arbitration with no resolution expected this year.
Internal forces: are there sufficiently strong internal change forces under way? (RA 2, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the internal forces are real but second-order: a dividend re-set (+38 percent to €0.80, then +€0.05 a year to €1.00 by 2030), a modest guidance rise (+1.5 percent at the midpoint), a 2030 EBITDA target of €1 billion (+16 percent over five years), unbundled pricing, bolt-on customs deals and a €50 million solar programme.
  • Absent forces: no chief-executive change, no perimeter change, no balance-sheet action.
  • The screen's rule: at least three forces strong, or chief-executive change plus a guidance rise
    • The case does not pass it.
  • Not D: the dividend policy and the guidance rise are actual, not mooted.
    • Not B: none of the twelve forces is strong.
