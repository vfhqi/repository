Arteche (ART-ES): questions for the investor relations call, 5-Nov-2026

From the Investment Analyst, for Richard. Written on the assumption that the Strategic Plan had not yet been presented when the call was held. Built only from what the Investment Analyst has written on the stock so far: the Stock-specific Investment Case A&J Memo of 1-Nov-2026 (price €31.3; the main source), the earlier memo of 10-Sep-2026, the Case Update Mini-Memo of 1-Oct-2026, the Sellside Analysts' Memo of 3-Nov-2026, the master digest of 31-Jul-2026, and the Industry, Sector and Geography Digests. Arteche is a live holding. ★ marks the questions to ask first if time is short.

What the call should settle, and why

The Investment Analyst's case in one line: the business is better than its share price is likely to be over the next twelve months. The call cannot test the share price, but it can test the four operating judgements that decide whether the next twelve months bring upgrades or cuts.

  • Judgement 1, the H2 2026 margin. The top of guidance (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, EBITDA, €100.4m) implies an H2 margin of only 15.8% against 18.4% in H1; the memo assumes 17.6%; consensus (€105m) needs about 17% to 18% depending on H2 revenue. If the H2 margin holds near 18.4%, FY2026 EBITDA is €106m or more and the FY2027 base rises; if the company really expects 16%, consensus is already too high.
  • Judgement 2, how the first FY2027 guidance and the Strategic Plan will be set. Management sets the first range low (FY2026's first range was revenue +9% to +15% at a flat margin) and raises at the half. Applied to FY2026 actuals that reads about €112m to €120m of FY2027 EBITDA against consensus €126m (Santander €135m). The memo puts a 70% chance on the first FY2027 range landing below consensus: the most probable negative event of the next six months.
  • Judgement 3, the three lines nobody guides. Tax (14.2% in FY2025, modelled at 19% for FY2027 and 22% by FY2030), net financial items (about €9m, where the FactSet median has about €1m) and minorities (about €2.7m) explain about €0.20 of the gap between the memo's FY2027 earnings per share of €1.22 and the consensus median of €1.40. If management confirms a rising tax rate and a €9m financial line, consensus earnings per share is full even if the operating numbers are right.
  • Judgement 4, whether new-order pricing is still rising. The direct margin rose from 29.7% (FY2022) to 40.9% (H1 2026); about half of that is scarcity price, which Tier-1 and Asian capacity from 2027 to 2029 can take back. Rising new-order prices support the sell-side's 19% to 21% margin path; flat or falling prices confirm the memo's plateau at 18% to 18.6%.
  • Judgement 5, the next placement. The family sold about €67m in June at €33 and intends to lift the float toward 20% to 25%. Share-side, not business-side, but it is the largest single risk to the multiple and investor relations can speak to the float target, lock-ups and the preferred route.

Order of play. Ask the pricing, H2 margin and guidance-philosophy questions first: they are where the memo's numbers and consensus diverge most. Leave disclosure and governance for the end.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades over the next twelve months

A1 ★ The H2 2026 margin. "The top of your EBITDA range implies an H2 margin near 15.8%, against 18.4% in H1 and no H2 below H1 in the last three years. What in H2 takes the margin down: the copper lag, SEG Electronics' consolidation, plant ramp costs, Latin American timing, mix? Or is the range simply conservative?"
- Why: each 1 point of margin on H2 revenue of about €300m is €3m of EBITDA; consensus already sits 4.6% above the top of the range.
- Tell: named H2 cost items at 100 to 200 basis points = the range is honest and consensus is high; "we always guide this way" = €106m-plus is in reach.

A2 ★ Guidance philosophy for FY2027 and the plan. "Your first FY2026 range was revenue +9% to +15% and a flat margin, then raised in July. Should we expect the same shape for the first FY2027 range, and will it come with the Strategic Plan or with the FY2026 results on 24-Mar-2027?"
- Why: consensus FY2027 EBITDA is €126m; the memo expects a first range of about €112m to €120m. Whether that range arrives in November or March sets the date of the likely consensus reset.
- Tell: "the plan will carry 2027 numbers" brings the event forward; "we guide once a year in March" keeps it dated; any hint that the first range will bracket consensus lowers the memo's 70% risk.

A3 ★ The Strategic Plan: horizon, date and what it will contain. "Your investor agenda says Strategic Plan 2027-2029 on 30-Nov; earlier wording was a 2030 plan in October or November. Is 30-Nov firm given the 29-Nov election? Will the plan give revenue, EBITDA margin, capital spending, working capital, payout, return on capital and acquisition targets, and will it also update FY2026?"
- Why: the plan is the one dated event that can reset the earnings path; the memo gives 70% to targets near its own path (2030 revenue €845m to €950m, margin 18% to 20%), 25% to the bull broker's €950m-plus and 21%, 25% to an explicit FY2026 raise, 40% to a payout cut.
- Tell: a 2030 margin ambition at 20% or above moves the case toward the bull branch; "we set plans to be beaten, as in 2024-26" (2026 revenue €520m to €540m and 12.5% to 13% margin, beaten by 8% and 500 basis points a year early) confirms the memo's reading.

A4 ★ What they make of consensus. "Consensus sits at the top of your revenue range and about 5% above the top of your EBITDA range. Is that a reasonable reading of the year, and where do you think analysts are most likely to be wrong, up or down?"
- Why: the visible beat against consensus is €0m to €2m, not €5m; an unbroken four-year record of beating guidance has not kept the sell-side conservative.
- Tell: discomfort with the EBITDA number = cut risk; comfort with it = the H2 margin is holding.

A5 ★ Tax rate. "FY2025's effective rate was 14.2%. With Bizkaia's new 19% minimum effective rate, R&D deductions of about €3.7m a year and a €15.1m deduction asset, and more profit in Mexico (30%), Brazil (34%) and Germany (about 30%), what should we model for FY2026 and FY2027, and when does the deduction asset run out?"
- Why: each point of tax is about €0.9m; Santander models 16% flat, the memo 19% rising to 22%; the FY2025 rate flatters earnings per share by €0.07 to €0.08.
- Tell: "high teens to 20%" confirms the memo and removes €0.05 or more from consensus FY2027; "mid-teens for years" supports consensus.

A6 ★ Net financial items and minorities. "Net financial items ran about €9m on only €5m of interest on gross debt. How much is lease interest, factoring and guarantee costs, exchange differences and Turkish and Argentine hyperinflation accounting, and how much of it falls as net debt falls? And what should we expect for minorities from AGIT (the gas-insulated instrument transformer venture, 49% Hitachi Energy) and Arin (45.85% Elewit)?"
- Why: the FactSet median nearly omits the financial line (worth about €0.11 a share) and minorities (about €0.05); the two domestic brokers model them inconsistently.
- Tell: a structural €7m to €9m confirms the gap to consensus is real and non-operating.

A7 ★ Will they guide below EBITDA? "Guidance stops at EBITDA. With a main-market listing and new international coverage starting, will the plan or the FY2026 results give EBIT, tax, net income or earnings per share guidance, or at least the SEG amortisation?"
- Why: new initiations anchor on management's plan and a normalised tax rate; the memo expects the FY2027 mean to drift to €1.25 to €1.30 with the buy share still near 100%, a cut with no downgrade.
- Tell: a yes brings consensus toward the memo faster; a no leaves the correction to March 2027.

A8 ★ Orders, backlog and pricing since July. "Book-to-bill was 1.22x in H1 and backlog €417m. How have order intake, backlog and constant-currency order growth run since July, by region (Europe, the Middle East and Africa +23.4% and North America +22.6% in H1; Latin America down), and are you willing to give a nine-month figure?"
- Why: the memo's thresholds: book-to-bill below 1.05 and backlog below €320m are warnings; a backlog at or above €420m and orders at or above 10% in constant currency keep FY2027 inside the cone. There is no scheduled number between now and March.
- Tell: backlog above €420m and double-digit orders = FY2027 revenue of €660m-plus is safe; a slowing European line is the first real tell of the cycle turning.

A9 Capacity conversion and the FY2027 revenue ramp. "FY2027 revenue is a ramp forecast, not a demand forecast: Measurement and Monitoring +20%, Tepeji del Río +25%, Indonesia doubling, China under construction. How far through the ramps are you, what is utilisation, and is the backlog cover of 9.4 months lengthening because you cannot deliver faster?"
- Why: each 5% of FY2027 revenue (€33m) is about €0.18 of earnings per share; peer base rate is a 12-to-18-month ramp with a 100-to-150 basis-point margin drag.
- Tell: plants at full run-rate by mid-2027 supports consensus H2 2026 revenue (+21%) over the memo's +16%.

A10 The upside triggers, named. "Which of these could add to FY2027: the Department of Energy rules due 24-Dec naming instrument transformers and favouring US-Mexico-Canada-compliant supply; Hitachi Energy routing instrument-transformer or bushing volume to AGIT now that GE Vernova is buying Trench; SEG cross-selling into your utility base; a bolt-on from the €150m to €250m of debt capacity?"
- Why: these are the memo's listed upgrade triggers; the Department of Energy rules could lift US prices 3% to 5% (about 0.5 point on group margin); a €100m bolt-on at about 10x adds 5% to 7% to earnings per share.
- Tell: a concrete AGIT or Department of Energy answer is the one upgrade source the sell-side has not modelled.

A11 Wider coverage and what new brokers are being told. "The 1-Oct note listed Berenberg (US, 18-Nov) and Goldman Sachs (2-Dec) as scheduled marketing; do you expect international initiations before the FY2026 results, and what are you telling them about mid-term margins, tax and the financial line?"
- Why: coverage is two named domestic brokers at 100% buy; the sell-side's central argument is how long pricing power lasts after 2027 and its upgrades rest on SEG's margins.
- Tell: if management is steering new analysts to normalised tax and an 18% to 19% margin, the consensus reset comes through initiations rather than cuts.

A12 Sell-side momentum. "FY2027 earnings per share estimates rose 34% in twelve months and 0% in the last month. From your side, what new information arrives between now and March that could move estimates either way?"
- Why: revisions have stopped; the six- and twelve-month figures roll off mechanically by April 2027 even with no cut.
- Tell: "nothing until March" confirms the next six months run on peers and regulators, not on Arteche's own numbers.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Pricing power and the margin plateau

B1.1 ★ New-order prices. "Industry prices rose 5% to 8% last year and 8% to 12% at high voltage. On the orders you are booking today, are prices higher, flat or lower than the orders in the backlog, by product: high-voltage instrument transformers, relays, medium voltage, Latin American tenders?"
- Why: the memo splits the 11-point direct-margin gain since FY2022 about half scarcity price and half mix and efficiency; a 2% fall in new-order prices from 2028 costs about 1.6 points of margin and 12% of FY2028 earnings per share. The backlog turns in nine months, so there is no tail of old low-priced contracts to roll off.
- Tell: "flat" at high voltage is the plateau; "still rising" is the bull branch; any softness in medium voltage or Latin America is the first crack.

B1.2 The SF6-free premium. "SF6-free product went from 22.8% to 39.3% of mix in a year and you launched the first 400 kV SF6-free instrument transformer. What premium does SF6-free carry now (the file says 10% to 15%, earlier 20% to 30%), and does the re-qualification cycle reset the field in your favour?"
- Why: mix is the half of the margin gain that capacity cannot take back; regulatory re-qualification is one of the three feedback loops the memo says work in Arteche's favour.

B1.3 Indexation and the copper lag. "Your contracts index to copper, aluminium and steel with a lag, and the 2021-22 episode cost 4.9 points of direct margin. What share of the backlog is indexed, what is the lag, and why is commodity hedging only €3.4m against roughly €17m a year of copper?"
- Why: a cost shock not passed through for a year takes €20m off EBITDA and €0.28 off earnings per share; indexation does not cover components, freight, energy or labour.
- Tell: a short lag and 80%-plus indexation makes the margin robust to a 2026-27 metals spike; a long lag keeps the 2021-22 precedent live.

B1.4 Capitalised development inside EBITDA. "About €7m a year of development is capitalised and uncertified development doubled to €5.3m. Is the policy unchanged after the listing and after SEG, and does the plan's margin ambition include it?"
- Why: about 1 point of margin that Landis+Gyr and ABB expense; a write-down is one of the master digest's re-pre-mortem triggers.

B2. Conversion: overheads, working capital, cash

B2.1 ★ Structural costs. "Structural costs rose to 23.9% of income in H1 2026 from 22.3% in FY2025, with plants, SEG, listing costs and a €4.5m long-term incentive accrual landing ahead of volume. What ratio do you target by 2027-28, and which of those items are one-off?"
- Why: overhead re-absorption is the only margin lever left after FY2026 (about 1.1 points by FY2028 if structural costs grow 11% then 8% against revenue +14% then +10.5%); the memo's "what would change my view" is structural costs at or below 22.5% in H1 2027.
- Tell: a stated target near 22% supports 19%-plus margins; "we invest ahead of growth" means the plateau.

B2.2 ★ Working capital and supplier finance. "Net working capital is 20.9% of revenue (13.0% in H2 2023); inventories went from €84m to €108m in six months; reverse factoring is €33.8m of €79m authorised and non-recourse factoring €12.4m. What is the target, what drives the build (safety stock, work in progress, Spain's prompt-payment law), what are the tenor and conditions of the bank lines, and do you count supplier finance in your own leverage?"
- Why: free cash flow in FY2026 (€14m to €18m) is below the €22.6m dividend paid in May; on the strictest view leverage is 1.0x to 1.3x, not 0.5x; a bank withdrawing reverse-factoring lines absorbs about €34m at once (Cembre, 2023); inventories are already above the 31-Jul-2026 master digest's €90m warning line.
- Tell: a target at or below 19% by end-2026 with factoring flat is the memo's positive sign; "working capital grows with the backlog" means cash conversion stays at 0.3x to 0.4x.

B2.3 Capital spending and depreciation. "Capital spending roughly doubles to about €40m in FY2026. Is €40m to €45m the run-rate for 2027-28, when does it fall back, and what does depreciation reach (the model has €18.3m rising to €29m)?"
- Why: each €10m of depreciation is about €0.14 of earnings per share after tax; returns dilute in FY2026-27 as plants and goodwill enter the base before volume.

B2.4 Free cash flow and the dividend. "With a 50% payout, €40m of capital spending and working capital building, free cash flow does not cover the dividend in FY2026. Is the payout a commitment, and would you cut it toward 30% to 45% to fund acquisitions?"
- Why: the memo gives 40% to a payout cut at the plan; domestic income holders would read it badly, allocators well.

B3. SEG Electronics and acquisitions

B3.1 ★ SEG's real margin. "SEG cost €50.6m with €47.4m of goodwill on €3.2m of net assets. The pro-forma disclosure (net profit only €0.66m higher with SEG from 1-Jan; revenue €9.9m for five months) implies a run-rate of €23m to €27m and a margin well below the 'above 20%' Aurelius advertised. What was SEG's standalone revenue and EBITDA margin in H1, what is it running at now, and when is the purchase-price allocation final?"
- Why: the sell-side's upgrades rest on SEG's margins; the memo models 17% to 20%; a 5-point miss at SEG is only 0.25 point for the group, but the credibility of "accretive from day one" matters to the multiple.
- Tell: "high teens" = the memo; "above 20% and growing 15%" = the market's view holds.

B3.2 SEG synergies and the data-centre channel. "No synergy targets were given. What are the revenue synergies (SEG's 350 industrial clients and data-centre channel into your utility base; your relays into DACH), and will SEG be reported inside Automation or separately?"
- Why: Automation is the richer margin pool (operating profit margins of 15% to 23.5%) and the pivot toward it is where Siemens, ABB, Schneider and GE Vernova bundle software.

B3.3 Acquisition capacity and the next deal. "The balance sheet carries €150m to €250m of debt capacity by FY2028 and de-gears to net cash by 2030 without deals. What size and type of acquisition is next, at what multiples (SEG was about 8x to 13x EBITDA), and will the plan set a return-on-capital hurdle?"
- Why: one more SEG-sized deal within 18 months is likely and not in the base case; each €100m at about 10x adds 5% to 7% to earnings per share.

B4. The United States and trade

B4.1 ★ Tepeji del Río under the new trade rules. "North America is about 30% of revenue and the Mexican plant is the route. Section 232 duties of 15% on grid equipment expire on 31-Dec-2027; Section 301 puts 10% on Spanish exports; the US-Mexico-Canada agreement moves to annual reviews from 2027 with guidance tying reduced duties to 85% US-melted metal content. How exposed is Tepeji to the metal-content test, and could you meet it?"
- Why: the memo's largest sudden fundamental risk: 10% to 15% plausibility over 24 months, a temporary EBITDA loss of €10m to €30m.
- Tell: a clear sourcing answer removes the tail; "we are studying it" keeps it live into 2027.

B4.2 The Department of Energy rules of 24-Dec-2026. "The executive order fences out vendors tied to 24 countries from bulk-power equipment at 69 kV and above and names instrument transformers. Do you expect Mexican assembly to count as compliant, have US enquiries or prices moved since August, and does Korean or Chinese exclusion change your US share (20% to 30%) over two years?"
- Why: the one positive regulatory novelty on the calendar; a 3% to 5% US price lift is about 0.5 point on group margin.

B4.3 CFE and customer concentration. (CFE is Mexico's state utility, Comisión Federal de Electricidad.) "The largest client is about 14% of revenue and the CFE framework in Mexico supports plants representing a large share of group output. What is the status and tenor of that framework and of CFE's budget?"
- Why: loss of a frame agreement is one of the memo's named sudden risks; it correlates with Latin American margin and peso translation.

B5. Spain and demand timing

B5.1 Spain's real order value and timing. "Royal Decree 640/2026 authorised €17.9bn to 2030, back-loaded (about €1.7bn in 2027 rising to €6.4bn in 2030); only 12% of grid-access requests were approved in 2025 and 86.5% of substations are saturated; the election on 29-Nov may push Redeia's plan to late 2027. The Geography Digest notes the January 2026 withdrawal of the 2024-26 forecasts as an unresolved signal that management may see Spanish deceleration first.

B5.2 Latin America. "Latin American orders fell 17.9% in H1 on contract timing and revenue fell 8.6% on recloser timing, with Brazil's BRL 236bn distribution plan to 2029 behind it. Has the timing reversed, and what do you expect from frame contracts in H2 and 2027?"
- Why: Network Reliability's FY2027 rebound in the model depends on Latin American frame contracts and the Spanish package.

B5.3 The demand second derivative. "Several brokers put the grid-equipment pricing peak in 2026-27 and the decline from 2028-29, with Tier-1 lead times still 2.5 to 4.5 years. Are your customers' lead-time expectations, or their willingness to pay for delivery certainty, changing at all?"

B6. Competition and industry structure

B6.1 ★ GE Vernova buying Trench. "GE Vernova agreed on 28-Oct to buy Trench, the instrument-transformer leader, for about $9bn at 13x 2028 EBITDA on a 37% margin. Hitachi Energy and Siemens Energy now buy bushings and instrument transformers from a direct competitor. B6.2 Capacity additions and Asian entrants. "Siemens Energy is adding 50% to transformer and switchgear capacity by 2030; Hitachi Energy's Mississippi plant comes in 2029; GE Vernova's Charleroi expansion includes instrument transformers in 2028; Ritz, Pfiffner and India's makers are adding high-voltage lines; Chinese makers deliver 95% to 98% of Western quality at 60% to 80% of the cost and are redirecting to Europe and Latin America as the US fences them out. B6.3 Homologation as the moat, and where it does not reach. "You hold approvals with about 90% of the world's utilities and recurrent clients are over 55% of orders. B6.4 Supply-side bottlenecks. "Grain-oriented steel comes from very few mills, cast-resin parts and relay modules are single-sourced, and days payable fell from 87 to 73 under Spain's prompt-payment law. Which component is the binding constraint on the ramps today?"
