Macroeconomic and Customer Disruptions

1.
Alternative trajectories: The consensus expects QT Group to easily beat its 2026 guidance floors and enjoy a substantial margin recovery in 2027. If macroeconomic headwinds in Western markets worsen, developer license demand could drop significantly as customers defer new product designs. This downturn would prevent the anticipated earnings leap and force downward consensus revisions across all broker models. Consequently, instead of a valuation expansion, the stock would face persistent pressure as the growth story slows.
2.
Customer impacts: Key automotive customers like Daimler or LG could choose to conserve cash by reducing their overall software engineering staff rather than investing in competitive UI/UX design. If major manufacturers choose lower-cost development approaches, they will purchase fewer developer licenses. Such cost-cutting behaviors would directly disrupt core scalable revenue and delay the adoption of next-generation digital interfaces. Eventually, this trend would force a re-evaluation of the company's long-term organic growth rate.
3.
Supplier dynamics: Silicon suppliers such as Infineon or Renesas play a crucial role in enabling the performance of microcontrollers that run the company's software. If these hardware manufacturers experience supply disruptions or slower-than-expected chip ramps, device makers will delay product shipments. Slower device shipments would severely compress volume-based royalty earnings for developer tool companies. This bottleneck would directly hit the high-margin revenue stream that investors expect to drive mid-term profitability.

Competitive threats: Modern open-source and browser-based toolkits like Flutter or Slint present a growing challenge in embedded and automotive markets. Although the company holds a strong ecosystem, aggressive pricing by competitors losing market share could trigger pricing pressure. If clients begin migrating to these cost-effective alternatives, the company's historical pricing power will weaken. This competitive pressure would cap long-term margin scalability and limit the success of new product offerings.
