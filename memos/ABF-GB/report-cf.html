EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Earnings Outlook and Consensus

Earnings are set to fall again in FY2027 before recovering from FY2028. Management expects group adjusted operating profit and adjusted earnings per share to decline year-on-year because of severe cyclical weakness in the sugar segment. Upside from recent sugar price improvements is deferred until 2028. Consensus FY2027 EPS sits around 147p, and the direction of revisions is down. Bernstein projects 138p, which is 6% below the 147p consensus. Morgan Stanley projects a 3% decline to 147.4p. Jefferies cut its FY2027 EPS expectation by 10%. FY2026 (this year) adjusted EPS came in at 156.0p. That beat the 153.2p consensus. It was still down 11% year-on-year.

Main Drivers of Future Earnings

Sugar price recovery after 2027 and the Primark demerger are the two largest drivers, and they depend on each other. Lower European acreage is expected to return the market to a deficit in 2028. The joint probability of success is lower than for any single driver, so the group needs flawless execution. The near-term sugar drag is large.

Management guided to a sugar adjusted operating loss of £70 million to £170 million in fiscal year 2027. Most FY2027 contracts were agreed at lower historical prices. Primark growth rests on space, not like-for-like sales. New stores in Europe and franchise markets are expected to add 4% to sales growth in 2027.

Management has pivoted from rapid US rollouts to improving the existing US estate. Online home delivery in Great Britain is planned by the end of calendar 2027. Cost levers are in place but carry friction. Management expects demerger transaction costs of about £75 million and annual dis-synergies under £45 million. Energy costs are hedged up to eighteen months ahead.

Latest Results (FY2026)

FY2026 was a modest beat on a lower base, and the exit rates matter more than the beat. Adjusted operating profit fell 13% but finished slightly ahead of the 1,504 million pound consensus. Underperforming exit rates in retail and sugar are driving downward estimate revisions. Weather and sugar pricing look cyclical, but continental European weakness and the sugar cost base need more evidence before they can be called temporary.

Primark and British Sugar moved in opposite directions. Primark's like-for-like sales declined 2.6%. Its adjusted operating margin of 10.2% beat consensus. British Sugar posted an adjusted operating loss of £47 million.

Revenue was flat and margins compressed. Revenue of 19,582 million pounds was flat at constant currency. Adjusted operating margin fell from 8.9% to 7.7%. Cash held up and leverage stayed modest.

Free cash inflow was flat at 652 million pounds. A 228 million pound working capital inflow supported it. Leverage rose slightly to 1.1x from 1.0x.

Reliability of Management Guidance

Guidance looks conservative, but it does not shield earnings from external shocks. Modest profit beats against consensus over the last four reporting periods reflect conservative initial guidance. Larger misses have clustered around crop failures and unseasonal weather. Critics argue the beats owe more to low-ball guidance than to structural progress.

Next year's EPS is sensitive to margin, currency and the sugar outcome. A 0.5 percentage point shift in group operating margin would move adjusted EPS by about 10.13p, or 6.49%. A Malawian kwacha devaluation is a £25 million swing factor. Jefferies models a bull case of 195.0p and a bear case of 140.0p.

FY2027 guidance parameters. Primark is guided to about 4% sales growth and an adjusted operating margin of around 10%. Management guided to flat grocery and ingredients profits, improved agriculture returns and a sugar loss of £70 million to £170 million.
