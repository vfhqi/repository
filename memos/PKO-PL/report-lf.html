Section 8 – Executive Compensation + Alignment

Section summary
  1. pay structures are tightly regulated under state-enterprise salary caps, creating low overall executive compensation relative to regional and private European peers
  2. incentive structures are strongly aligned with long-term capital stability, utilizing extensive deferrals and malus reductions to restrict unwarranted payouts

Executive pay structure and L3Y metrics

1.
Regulatory salary caps: PKO BP executive compensation is strictly governed by the Polish Act of 9 June 2016 on the terms of setting the remuneration of managers of certain state-controlled companies. Under these guidelines, the President's fixed pay is capped at fifteen times the average monthly enterprise sector salary, while other Management Board members are capped at 14.5 times the average. Internal pay disparity: The maximum ratio of the average total annual gross remuneration of the Bank's Management Board members to the average total annual gross remuneration of other employees of the Bank is set at 22:1. This is wider than the domestic state-controlled peer Bank Pekao S.A., where the actual reported internal pay disparity ratio was 10.12 in 2025. Equity incentive shift: The variable compensation system was modified in 2024 to increase alignment with equity owners by shifting the cash-to-instrument ratio of variable awards from 50:50 to 45:55. The non-cash portion is granted in phantom shares, which are converted into cash after a mandated retention period. Long-term deferral timeline: Variable remuneration components are split into a non-deferred portion and a deferred portion, which are subject to a five-year deferral period. The total vesting and evaluation cycle for these stock-linked phantom shares extends over seven years to align management with long-term shareholder value.
