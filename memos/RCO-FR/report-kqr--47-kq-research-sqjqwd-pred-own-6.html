Section 4 – EU-China Brandy Tariffs and Price Undertaking Status

Section summary
  1. The EU-China brandy minimum import price undertaking has successfully shielded major European cognac exporters from severe definitive anti-dumping duties.
  2. Higher transfer prices under the price floors inflate regulatory duties and compress profit margins for imported spirits in China.
  3. Our confidence in the status and impact of these brandy tariffs is high, supported by detailed regulatory filings from Rémy Cointreau and consistent broker analysis.

Price Undertaking Status

1.
Easing tension: The trade dispute over European brandy imports into China transitioned from provisional anti- dumping duties to a minimum price undertaking on July 5, 2025. Under this agreement, major cognac producers avoid the definitive 34.3% duty by committing to a minimum transfer price. This regulatory compromise prevents severe trade disruptions but forces a de facto price floor on imported spirits. The implementation of this minimum price undertaking ensures that major exporters can maintain their route-tomarket access without immediate customs stops.
2.
Punitive pressure: Smaller spirits manufacturers that did not participate in the agreement face much harsher import conditions. These non-compliant brands are subject to the full definitive anti-dumping duties of up to 34.9% in China. This regulatory dual-speed structure significantly damages the market competitiveness of minor European producers. Smaller importers must choose between absorbing the steep 30% tariff or raising shelf prices, which severely dampens consumer demand.
3.
Inflated costs: The minimum price undertaking increases the transfer price from Europe to Chinese distribution subsidiaries. Because Chinese import taxes, excise duties, and value-added taxes compound on top of the transfer price, the mechanism drives substantial tax inflation. Consequently, distributors must absorb these higher costs because they cannot easily pass price increases to consumers. This cost pressure weighs heavily on localized operating margins across the entire imported spirits sector.
