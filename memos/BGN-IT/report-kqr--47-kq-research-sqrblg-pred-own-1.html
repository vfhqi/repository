Section 2 – Basis of Reporting and Segment Reconciliation

Section summary

Accounting Framework and Reclassification Reconciliations

1.
Management approach definitions: Banca Generali reports its segment financial data under the management approach outlined by IFRS 8, aligning internal reporting used by corporate decision-makers with its statutory financials. In its FY2025 Annual Integrated Report, the group defined its reportable operating segments as Private Banking (PB), Senior Partner (SP), and Corporate Center (CC). While the consolidated statutory net banking income was reported at EUR 796.7m in FY2024 and EUR 885.2m in FY2025, the sum of these three segments' net banking income before consolidated reclassifications stood at EUR 981.1m in FY2024 and EUR 1,000.3m in FY2025. This apparent discrepancy of EUR 184.4m in FY2024 and EUR 115.1m in FY2025 is reconcilable through specific accounting reclassifications.
2.
Fee provisions reclassifications: The primary driver of the difference between the segment total and the statutory consolidated net banking income is the reclassification of fee provisions for commercial networks. Banca Generali's segment reporting is stated in accordance with the management approach, which reclassifies fee provisions for sales and recruitment incentives directly into the "Fee expense" line item under consolidated statutory presentation. For FY2025, these reclassified net provisions for incentives related to sales and recruitment plans amounted to EUR 19.3m, compared with EUR 15.7m for FY2024. This accounting treatment reduces consolidated statutory net banking income while keeping the operating segments' standalone gross fee metrics higher, directly affecting how analysts must model the recurring fee run-rate.
3.
Double-counting and eliminations: The consolidation process requires the elimination of intra-segment double- counting, which primarily impacts the Corporate Center segment. The Corporate Center segment includes Banca Generali's proprietary trading activities, residual activities, and the activities of Intermonte Partners SIM. Intermonte Partners SIM, whose acquisition process began in 2025 and was completed via a merger in February 2026, generated EUR 4.4m in net banking income in FY2025 on a standalone basis. The integration of these entities introduces intra-group transaction flows, such as institutional brokerage and placement fees, which must be eliminated at the consolidated level.

Transfer Pricing and Interest Allocation Mechanisms

Liquidity Transfer Pricing (LTP) mechanisms: Net interest income (NII) within each segment is heavily adjusted by internal notional interest allocations based on the bank's Liquidity Transfer Pricing framework. Interest expenses for the PB and SP commercial segments are determined using actual interest paid on direct net inflows, while their interest income includes actual interest accrued on client loans. Crucially, both components are recognized net of a "notional interest" share calculated via the LTP and attributed directly to the Corporate Center segment.

This transfer pricing mechanism effectively shifts treasury and duration management profits from the commercial networks to the Corporate Center, which reported an LTP-boosted net interest income of EUR 121.4m in FY2025 compared to EUR 14.5m in FY2024.

4.
Fee income segment allocation: Fee income, including performance fees, is allocated using a direct placement methodology. Performance fees generated by BG Fund Management Luxembourg (BGFML) are allocated directly to the business segment (PB or SP) whose advisors placed the underlying products. For FY2025, total consolidated fee income was EUR 1,245.7m, with the PB segment generating EUR 990.4m, the SP segment generating EUR 162.5m, and the CC segment generating EUR 92.8m. These segment fee revenues are reported gross of fee expenses, which totaled EUR 485.5m for PB, EUR 100.8m for SP, and EUR 14.5m for CC in FY2025.

Contrary Evidence and Segment Definition Risks

5.
Contrary evidence: The bank's segment reporting structure experienced significant commercial reorganization on 1 January 2024, which limits long-term historical comparisons. In this reorganization, the group split the network into the Senior Partner Network (advisors with AUM over EUR 150m) and the Private Banking Network (comprising other advisors and Relationship Managers). Because of this reorganization, FY2023 historical baseline data had to be restated, making independent historical trend analysis before 2024 highly uncertain.
6.
Other side of the story: Although management emphasizes the operational logic of the management approach and its reclassification of sales incentive provisions, this presentation can obscure the underlying profitability of individual financial advisor networks. By reclassifying commercial incentives into "Fee expense" only at the consolidated level, the segment-level operating profits appear artificially inflated before consolidation. For instance, the PB operating segment reported an estimated net result of over EUR 264m for FY2025, but this figure does not fully reflect its standalone share of systemic network maintenance and recruitment costs.
