Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary

  2. Quantifying rate decay trajectories and reporting lags is critical for assessing valuation multiples and downside earnings risks.

Section 2 – Historical Precedents of Rate Normalization

Section summary
  1. Historical precedents demonstrate that maritime transit recovery after major geopolitical crises is a prolonged process.
  2. Rates do not normalize instantly upon ceasefires due to residual security concerns and mine clearance requirements.
  3. Fleet supply adjustments through vessel demolition typically lag rate declines by several quarters.

Post-Crisis Shipping Recovery Timelines

1.
Slow recovery: Allianz SE on June 16, 2026 stated that commercial shipping took several weeks to begin recovering after the 1988 ceasefire. The same source documented that restoring normal operating conditions took more than three months despite naval escorts. This historic precedent suggests that freight rate normalization will experience a multi-week lag rather than a sudden drop. Such delays will temporarily support tanker earnings.
2.
Escorted stabilization: NH Investment and Securities on March 4, 2026 noted that the United States launched Operation Earnest Will in July 1987 to protect commercial vessels. This military intervention stabilized passage but did not immediately drive rates to pre-war lows. Historical data shows that even with convoy operations, risk premiums persist until formal peace treaties are executed by all combatants. Consequently, spot rates remain elevated longer than general spot models assume.
3.
Persistent premiums: Jefferies Research on October 21, 2026 reported that tanker traffic through Suez remained below pre-attack levels nearly three years after the initial 2023 disruptions. This behavior illustrates how charterer and owner caution outlasts the active conflict headlines. Such caution maintains elevated insurance premiums and regional freight costs. Thus, TORM's realized TCE should stay protected during early normalization phases.
4.
Frictional resumption: Allianz SE on June 16, 2026 reported that mine-clearing operations alone require 30 to 50 days to complete before regular transit can resume. Because de-escalation frameworks are slow and frictional, immediate rate declines are typically prevented by physical bottlenecks. The 1988 precedent confirms that restoring normal commercial traffic is a multi-month process rather than a sudden event. This timeline delays the erosion of spot tanker rates.
5.
Historical comparison: Morgan Stanley on April 9, 2026 compared the 2026 Hormuz disruption to the 1973 and 1979 oil crises. During past shocks, shipping costs as a percentage of total cargo value reached peak ranges of 20% to 35% historically. This indicates that modern freight rates have historically had even more room to expand before demand destruction occurs.
