EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Alfen's material share price underperformance relative to Fastned over the last three months is highly resolvable, driven by a sharp divergence between equipment manufacturer margin compression and pure-play operator volume leverage. The performance gap reflects fundamental structural differences in business model resilience, financial trajectory, and exposure to European electric vehicle market dynamics. Alfen operates as a diversified hardware manufacturer split across Smart Grid Solutions at 43%, EV Charging at 28%, and Energy Storage at 29% of FY25 revenues, leaving it exposed to intense supply chain bottlenecks, inventory write-downs, and volatile raw material costs.

In sharp contrast, Fastned functions as a pure-play fast-charging operator, focusing capital on securing high-traffic prime locations along European motorway networks. The most critical driver of Alfen's underperformance is severe margin compression during a disruptive corporate transformation phase. Alfen's full-year adjusted EBITDA margin is projected to fall to a low of 4.0% because of persistent transformation challenges.

This margin collapse is compounded by €8 million in temporary transformation personnel costs during its ongoing reorganization. Meanwhile, Fastned's H1 2026 operational EBITDA more than doubled to €37.4 million on the back of rising utilisation. The backlog deterioration at Alfen further undermines investor confidence, as the Energy Storage division experienced a steep sequential drop in its order backlog from €130 million in the first quarter of 2026 to €93 million at the end of the second quarter.

More alarmingly, Alfen's Energy Storage order intake was only €11 million in the second quarter of 2026 compared to €54 million in the first quarter.

Fastned's operational leverage expansion provides a stark counterpoint to Alfen's struggles. Fastned delivered 112 GWh of electricity during the first half of 2026, representing 38% year-on-year volume growth. This volumetric expansion translated directly into profitability gains, with Fastned raising its full-year 2026 operational EBITDA margin guidance to approximately 45% from its previous 35% to 40% range. The company's utility-like cash flow profile further insulates it from the equipment supply chain volatility plaguing Alfen, as Fastned operates longterm assets with lease terms typically exceeding 15 years. The divergent market exposures between hardware suppliers and charge point operators amplify the performance gap. Alfen reports that its first half 2026 EV charging revenue decreased by 17% year-on-year to 51.1 million euros. This hardware sales weakness persists despite robust European battery electric vehicle adoption, as German battery electric vehicle sales grew by 61.7% year-on-year in July 2026.

The disconnect between strong vehicle registrations and weak equipment sales reflects intense home-segment price competition and product transition bottlenecks that disproportionately impact equipment manufacturers. Alfen's charger gross margin fell to 39.5% due to a 6% lower average selling price driven by intense home-segment competition.

6 8

The leadership instability at Alfen introduces additional execution risk during this critical transformation period. CFO Onno Krap stepped down on short notice effective July 1, 2026, and was immediately replaced by Bart Meussen as interim CFO, tasked with executing the strategic transformation toward a customer-centric business unit structure. This governance disruption occurs precisely when the company requires stable leadership to navigate margin recovery and backlog rebuilding.

In contrast, Fastned maintains strategic execution continuity evidenced by successfully building 20 new fast-charging stations during the second quarter of 2026, expanding its operational footprint to 434 stations. Looking forward, the resolution of this performance divergence hinges on whether Alfen's margin compression represents a transitional or structural impairment. For a concentrated, long-only equity investor with a 12-24 month holding period, resolving this performance divergence is critical to establishing whether Alfen's margins have structural or merely transitional impairments.

The company's recovery trajectory depends on successful execution of its corporate restructuring, stabilization of Energy Storage backlog conversion rates, and its ability to regain pricing power in the intensely competitive EV charging hardware segment. Conversely, Fastned's continued outperformance relies on sustaining utilization growth amid potential grid connection constraints and intensifying competition from well-capitalized oil majors for premium motorway locations.

The divergence between these two investment cases reflects a fundamental shift in investor preference from cyclical equipment exposure toward predictable infrastructure-like cash flows within the European electric vehicle value chain.
