[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Teleperformance is a self-help and sum-of-the-parts case in a business the market prices as terminal, not a growth case. At EUR 71.80 (2026-10-09) the shares pay a covered 6.3 percent dividend yield to wait for a disposal that has not been made: on the modal path the return is 11.5 percent a year over eighteen months, a D against the framework's 20 percent bar, and the case clears the bar only on the one-in-three branch where a sale of Specialized Services assets re-rates the whole. The right people have arrived at the right price in the wrong value chain, and the return above the bar is in a sale they have not yet signed.

  • The change is internal, broad and seven months old.
    • The founder who ran the company for 48 years left in March 2026
    • The people and the choices have changed more in seven months than in the prior decade.
    • Jorge Amar, an AI-native consultant who led McKinsey's digital customer-care practice from 2023 to 2026, became chief executive on 2026-03-16 and acted within 100 days: a review of every segment, the restructuring programme expanded to EUR 150 to 170 million of run-rate savings, and 70 days of client visits.
    • Moulay Hafid Elalamy became Chairman, separating the roles, and put up to EUR 355 million of his own capital behind the stock through a swap on 2026-03-02, taking Saham's stake to 14.9 percent
      • A permanent finance director, a chief AI officer, a new Specialized Services chief executive and four new directors followed.
    • The plan is what every sell-side house asked for: EUR 150 to 170 million of cost out, outcome-based pricing, exit of low-margin countries and contracts, deleveraging to 1.2x by FY2028, and a portfolio review that names LanguageLine, TLScontact and Health Advocate as under review for sale.
    • The screen's red flags do not fit: EUR 109 million of restructuring cash was booked in H1 2026, so this is not a presentation
      • Acquisitions have stopped
      • The appointment is permanent.
  • The external forces run against the core on five of seven fronts, and they are the stronger set.
    • Generative AI is automating the routine voice and moderation seats that are most of the revenue, and clients take the savings at every renewal.
    • Trust & Safety automation took 340 basis points off group organic growth in H1 2026
      • Renewal discounts run at 15 to 25 percent and automation discounts at 5 to 10 percent on hourly labour
      • Client-led offshoring deflates revenue by about 150 basis points a year.
    • Delivery-hub regulation and wages are rising faster than they can be passed on: Colombia's 2026 minimum wage rose 23 percent nominal and its workweek falls to 42 hours from July 2026
      • Philippine wage boards and India's Labour Codes add cost beneath that.
    • Only the company's own cycle (turning off a shallow trough: like-for-like -2.2 percent in Q1 2026, -1.2 percent in Q2) and macro (a mild currency tailwind ahead on the sell-side's forecasts) are not headwinds.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have not moved beyond one quarter's signal.
    • The internal change has reached layer 3 of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, actions, with one early reading at layer 4 and nothing at layers 5 and 6.
    • At layer 4: the H1 2026 recurring EBITA margin held at 13.6 percent on revenue down 4.5 percent reported, beating consensus by 20 basis points
      • The second quarter improved sequentially to -1.2 percent like-for-like, and +1.7 percent excluding Trust & Safety.
    • At layer 5: reported diluted earnings per share fell to EUR 3.70 in H1 2026 from EUR 4.19, and free cash flow is lower, not higher, because restructuring costs EUR 120 to 140 million in cash this year.
    • At layer 6: consensus earnings per share are flat over three months (+1 percent) after falling about 9 to 11 percent over twelve
      • The buy share fell 16 points in three months to 27 percent.
  • What we think it earns: adjusted earnings per share of EUR 13.20 in FY2025 rising to about EUR 14.9 in FY2028, 4.1 percent a year, with consensus at EUR 14.79.
    • The growth comes mostly from the legs the framework weights least: the tax rate falling to 29.5 percent adds about 1.7 points a year and interest leverage 0.7
      • Revenue (0.8) and margin (0.9) add less than 2 points together.
    • On the reported basis earnings are EUR 8.48 per share for FY2025, 56 percent below the adjusted figure, because amortisation, restructuring and impairments are excluded from the adjusted line and recur.
  • What it costs: EUR 71.80, or 4.93 times 24-month-forward adjusted earnings of EUR 14.55, and 8.5 times reported earnings.
    • That is the 1st percentile of the stock's own ten-year history on the dashboard's current multiple, the 22nd percentile of five years, but the 54th percentile of the last two years (median 4.55x): the move from 3.3x to 4.9x has already happened, and the shares are 68 percent above their 52-week low of EUR 42.661.
    • It is the second-lowest multiple in the 20-stock comparison set (median 10.5x), a 54 percent discount that is mostly the market's judgement of the cohort's AI exposure, which the facilities and catering peers do not share.
    • The price discounts a terminal revenue decline of over 20 percent on Bernstein's reverse discounted cash flow
      • The mean target of EUR 76.98 is 7 percent above the price, so the market gives the disposal some weight but not Bernstein's.
  • The return on the modal path is a D, and the disposal branch is the whole of the swing.
    • The cash return carries about 6.3 percent a year, earnings growth about 4 percent, and the re-rating about nothing at a multiple of 5.0 times that is where the shares already sit.
    • Eighteen months: value EUR 80.0 plus a EUR 4.50 dividend, EUR 84.5, 11.5 percent a year
      • Three years: EUR 83.0 plus EUR 13.50 of dividends, EUR 96.5, 10.4 percent a year.
    • On the disposal branch (a binding sale within eighteen months, 45 percent; a re-rating to 6.5 times given a sale, 70 percent; together 32 percent, possible) the eighteen-month return is 31.7 percent a year
      • The arithmetic blend of the two branches is 18.1 percent a year over eighteen months and 13.3 percent over three, shown beside the modal case and not applied.
    • The modal path clears the eighteen-month bar only below about EUR 64.3
      • The shares were EUR 68.68 on 2026-10-05 and EUR 71.80 on 2026-10-09.
  • The pattern it fits: 'Major Corporate Change', at C: crisp on the inputs (a chief executive change plus three further strong forces, the screen's rule met) and not on the outputs (no output at layers 5 and 6).
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the people to the choices to the actions, and the cash is real on the basis the company reports it (EUR 299 million of normalised net free cash flow in H1 2026, a dividend covered 2.7 times by consensus FY2026 free cash flow).
    • It breaks at the top line and at the margin: price is falling in nominal and real terms, and whether a third or two-thirds of the EUR 150 to 170 million of savings reaches the margin rather than the client is unobservable until the quarters print.
    • It breaks again on the earnings basis: if the EUR 120 to 140 million of annual restructuring is an operating cost, FY2028 earnings per share are about EUR 13.4 rather than EUR 14.9, and the modal return falls to 4.3 percent a year over eighteen months, an F.

  • The case is therefore a watch, not a deployment: a new, aligned management may sell part of a 33-percent-margin franchise at a multiple the market will not give the whole, while the core stops shrinking

    • If neither happens we are paid 6.3 percent to wait on a 4 percent grower at five times earnings.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is material on the input side ('Change Forces' at C, with internal forces at B) but not yet on the output side ('Financial Outputs' at D) and not sufficient on the return ('Modal return' at D on the modal path): the chain from the new leadership's choices to the sell-side's estimates has travelled through layer 3, actions, with one early signal at layer 4 and nothing at layers 5 and 6, and a 4 percent earnings grower at an unchanged multiple with a 6.3 percent yield is a 10 to 12 percent return.

    • Not C, because C would require the disposal branch to be the modal case, and we rate it possible, about one in three, not probable.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The hypothesis, at its strongest: the quarterly stability we would use to confirm the case is produced by offshoring gains and consolidation accounting that mask a core whose contract values are deflating permanently
      • This is the profile the risk research names its winning one, a false friend.
      • Clients move from seat-based to outcome-based pricing and keep the automation savings: a former Concentrix director's example is a USD 5 million human contract falling to USD 400,000 or less.
      • The EUR 120 to 140 million of annual restructuring is the operating cost of that deflation, so the adjusted earnings on which the 4.9 times multiple is struck overstate true earnings by about a third: EUR 284 million of adjustments in 2025, 25 percent of reported profit against a 15 to 18 percent industry median.
      • Specialized Services, the asset the bull wants sold, has itself just lost a visa contract and shrank 3.9 percent like-for-like in H1 2026, and its 330-basis-point margin jump to 33.0 percent was temporary price negotiation on an expert's reading.
      • The consultant chief executive has no record in low-margin human operations, and the disposal, if it comes, sells the one franchise automation does not reach and leaves shareholders holding the deflating core: a one-time event that removes the reason to own the rest.
      • Deutsche Bank's 12.7 percent FY2028 margin and Morgan Stanley's EUR 10 bear case are this hypothesis in numbers.
    • What we concede.
      • The adjusted basis is flattered, and treating restructuring as recurring takes the modal return to an F
      • The Specialized margin jump may be partly temporary
      • The leading indicators are quarterly and the hypothesis is designed to pass them
      • The price has already moved 68 percent from the low on inputs alone
      • And no disposal has been signed in seven months.
    • The bounded-downside claim does not survive the arithmetic.
      • The bull's claim is that the Specialized assets cover the enterprise value or the market capitalisation, and they do not at any multiple in the range.
      • Specialized Services' recurring EBITA is about EUR 460 million a year (FY2025 segment revenue EUR 1,485 million at the 30 to 33 percent margins in the research)
        • At Bernstein's 5 to 10 times EBITA that is EUR 2.3 to 4.6 billion against an enterprise value of about EUR 7,843 million at EUR 71.80 (market capitalisation EUR 4,423 million on 61.6 million diluted shares, plus debt EUR 4,364 million, less cash EUR 1,027 million, plus pensions EUR 83 million): 30 to 59 percent of it.
      • On post-tax earnings of about EUR 324 million, the set's lowest multiple of 3.8 times gives EUR 1.23 billion (28 percent of the market capitalisation), the stock's own 4.9 times EUR 1.59 billion (36 percent), the set median of 10.5 times EUR 3.40 billion (77 percent).
      • The sum-of-the-parts works only at the top of Bernstein's range: Specialized at 10 times EBITA (EUR 4.6 billion) plus Core at 4 times its roughly EUR 1,020 million of EBITA (EUR 4.1 billion) less net debt and pensions of EUR 3,420 million is EUR 5.3 billion of equity, about EUR 86 a share, 20 percent above the price
        • At 5 times Specialized it is EUR 3.0 billion, about EUR 48 a share, 33 percent below.
        • The downside is bounded, if at all, by the cash yield.
    • Why we still hold our view, as far as we hold it.
      • Three facts are inconsistent with the hypothesis and are the reasons the modal path is positive rather than negative.
      • The underlying volume is not collapsing: growth excluding Trust & Safety was +1.7 percent in H1 2026, Core Services improved to -1.0 percent in Q2, top-100 client tenure is 14 years and switching costs are six months of compliance audits and two months of retraining
        • The deflation is in price and mix, which cost and offshoring can offset for a time, as the 13.6 percent margin on revenue down 4.5 percent shows.
      • The cash is real on the basis the company reports it and better than the closest peer's: EUR 299 million of normalised net free cash flow in H1 2026, up EUR 20 million
        • 9.5 percent of sales over four years against Concentrix's 7 percent
        • A dividend covered 2.7 times
        • Maturities extended to 4.5 years in May 2026.
      • The people and the alignment have changed in a way the hypothesis does not allow for: it needs management to keep allocating capital into the deflating core, and this management has stopped acquiring, is deleveraging, and is reviewing the portfolio for sale with a chairman who bought EUR 355 million of the stock at the bottom.
      • Our view is held at the odds stated: the case clears the bar only on the disposal branch, about one in three, and on the modal branch it pays a D.
        • That is a reason to watch, not to deploy.

Setup verdict.   • The 'Foundations' reading sharpens one thing in 'Investment Thesis': the internal forces (RA 2, E 1, BB 1, at B) are the case, so the setup that fits is the one built on them.

Congruence: The congruence judgement: does the whole hang together across past, business, value chain and market environment; where ratings disagree, why (Part 4, E 3, BB 4)

  • With the market environment: mixed, and the mix is explained.
    • The week's environment (2026-10-09) pays for visible existing cash and refuses duration, projects and leverage
