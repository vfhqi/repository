Section 2 – The Orderbook and Scrapping Supply-Side Loops

Section summary
  1. High freight rates have triggered a surge in product tanker orders that will deliver with significant lags, while temporarily halting the retirement of older vessels.
  2. A future decline in rates is expected to accelerate the scrapping of the large block of vintage vessels, acting as a natural supply stabilizer.

Newbuilding Lags and Ordering Cycles

1.
Rapidly expanding: The global product tanker market is experiencing rapid orderbook growth as high seaborne freight rates incentivize owners to place newbuilding commitments. Deutsche Bank reported on November 9, 2026, that the product tanker orderbook-to-fleet ratio climbed from a multi-year low of 5.51% in March 2022 to 20.4% in November 2026. This massive influx was heavily driven by extensive ordering of long-range product tankers during 2023 and 2024.
2.
Heightened pricing: The global shipbuilding market faces elevated newbuilding prices and tight shipyard slots that act as a minor balancing loop to deter speculative ordering. Deutsche Bank Research reported on August 31, 2026, that while high rates encourage ordering, shipyard output is in an expansionary phase primarily in China. This ongoing build-out of capacity keeps newbuilding asset values elevated, which historically prevents a repeat of the unconstrained speculative ordering spikes seen in prior cycles.
3.
Extended delivery: Global shipyard capacity constraints mean recently ordered vessels will face prolonged construction timelines before entering active service. Deutsche Bank noted on October 21, 2026, that typical shipyard lead times currently average about 3.5 years. Consequently, approximately 63% of the active product tanker orderbook is scheduled for delivery in 2028 and beyond.
4.
Historical comparison: The global product tanker orderbook remains far below the hyper-inflationary peak that preceded the prior structural shipping collapse. Jefferies Research highlighted on October 21, 2026, that the product tanker orderbook of 23% is manageable. This current orderbook stands in stark contrast to the unprecedented fifty percent peak seen in 2008.

Scrapping Cushions and Old Fleet Retainers

5.
Heavily delayed: The global tanker fleet's immediate spot profitability continues to delay the decommissioning of old, fuel-inefficient vessels. Jefferies Research observed on October 21, 2026, that exceptionally high rates have incentivized owners to keep vintage ships operational. This rate environment has temporarily frozen the retirement of the twenty-two percent of product tankers aged twenty years or older.
6.
Cyclical stabilizer: The product tanker freight market's eventual retreat will force heavy financial burdens on owners that make vessel operations uneconomical. Jefferies Research expects that lower rates will discourage owners from paying for expensive special five-year drydock surveys on vintage ships. Consequently, any market softening will trigger an acceleration in vessel scrapping to balance supply.
7.
Depressed recycling: Global product tanker demolition activity has reached historical lows as the market remains structurally short of active compliant tonnage. Hafnia Limited reported in its 2025 annual report that product tanker demolition peaked at 81 units in 2021 during weak earnings, but fell to a record low of six ships annually during 2023 and 2024. This drop demonstrates how strong freight markets halt the supply-side balancing loop of vessel scrapping.
8.
Contrary evidence: The product tanker fleet retirement loop may be hindered because vintage vessels can easily slip into shadow operations. Deutsche Bank reported on August 31, 2026, that high spot rates continue to incentivize older, sanctioned vessels to remain active in dark trades rather than retire. This alternative trade route keeps compliant capacity tight but prevents older ships from being permanently dismantled.

Section 3 – The Clean-Dirty LR2 Crossover and Crude Market Interaction Loops

Section summary
  1. Strong crude tanker rates have drawn a substantial portion of the global long-range product tanker fleet into dirty trades, tightening compliant clean transport capacity.
  2. Strategic positioning varies among spotheavy operators, with Scorpio Tankers actively participating in the crossover while TORM maintains its product focus.
  3. The rapid return of long-range vessels to clean trades when crude rates soften represents a key supply-side balancing loop that dampens product tanker upcycles.

Crossover Mechanics and Fleet Migration

1.
Highly profitable: TORM plc observed that high crude freight rates have pulled long-range tankers out of refined product transportation and into dirty trades. In its third-quarter 2026 interim report published on November 4, 2026, TORM reported that approximately two-thirds of the global LR2 fleet was trading dirty. This migration contracted clean petroleum product transport capacity by 4% since the start of 2026, which will support TORM's earnings per share by keeping product freight rates elevated.
2.
Strongly supportive: The product tanker owner d'Amico International Shipping S.A. reported that the migration of larger product tankers into crude trades has created a supportive supply-side deficit. In its interim report published on November 5, 2026, d'Amico stated that the clean-dirty crossover of LR2 vessels reduced their availability for refined products, which directly benefited smaller LR1 and MR vessels. This capacity squeeze supports broader product tanker freight rates, thereby boosting industry-wide earnings per share.
3.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reported on October 28, 2026, that although crude rates have driven the crossover, any reopening of the Strait of Hormuz could quickly reverse this migration and flood clean trades with capacity. The broker expects that a normalization of Middle East trade would ease shipping bottlenecks and return LR2s to clean product transport. This reversal would significantly deflate product tanker rates and depress TORM's near-term earnings per share.
4.
Highly opportunistic: Scorpio Tankers Inc. has aggressively positioned its long-range fleet to capitalize on the clean-dirty crossover trade during the tanker market upcycle. In its third-quarter earnings presentation on October 28, 2026, Scorpio disclosed that the share of the global LR2 fleet trading in crude or dirty products reached 69% in September 2026 compared to just 45% in January 2023. This opportunistic allocation maximizes Scorpio's near-term cash generation and supports higher earnings per share.

Section 4 – The Macro Demand, Fuel Prices, and Restocking Loops

Section summary
  1. High freight costs and elevated fuel prices have acted as powerful balancing loops by depressing short- term refined product demand and delaying critical stock replenishment.
  2. Depleted global oil inventories, which fell at an unprecedented rate in mid-2026, represent a massive deferred demand tailwind that will eventually support product tanker rates.
  3. Industry experts and operators expect a prolonged restocking cycle of up to two years to rebuild strategic and commercial fuel reserves to secure energy supply chains.

Balancing Demand Destruction Loops

1.
Substantially depressed: High shipping costs and soaring oil product prices have triggered a temporary contraction in global oil consumption. The International Energy Agency reported in October 2026 that global oil demand is forecast to decline by 2.6 million barrels per day in 2026, marking its first annual contraction since 2020. This demand squeeze serves as a powerful balancing loop that cools over-heated spot markets and limits near-term tanker rates.
2.
Severely strained: Exceptional ocean transportation costs have raised the delivered price of energy commodities, eroding consumer purchasing power. Morgan Stanley Research noted on November 4, 2026, that the greatest pain point in the global oil system is shipping costs. These high freight rates act as a balancing loop by squeezing refiners' margins and dampening near-term refined product demand across major importing regions.
3.
Contrary evidence: Some markets show that consumer fuel demand remains resilient despite exceptionally high retail energy prices and logistics costs. Morgan Stanley reported on October 27, 2026, that daily gasoline and diesel sales at service stations in Turkey showed little sign of demand destruction. This resilient consumption suggests that high freight and product costs do not always trigger immediate balancing demand contractions in all regions.
4.
Strongly discouraged: Elevated fuel prices have incentivized commercial buyers to postpone the replenishment of their industrial stocks. Jefferies Research reported on October 20, 2026, that exceptionally high flat prices are currently discouraging buyers from refilling tanks. This delay represents a balancing loop that temporarily restricts seaborne import volumes but creates a massive restocking reservoir of deferred demand for the product tanker sector.
5.
Marginally mitigated: Strategic stock releases in major consuming markets help to temporarily support consumption and avoid rapid demand destruction. TORM plc reported on November 4, 2026, that European countries released 50 million barrels of diesel from strategic inventories. While releasing inventories does not immediately generate seaborne trade, it protects the underlying consumption base from collapsing under high pricing pressures.

Section 6 – Regulatory, Sanctions, and Consolidation Supply-Side Loops

Section summary
  1. Mounting environmental rules and slow steaming act as structural balancing loops that shrink compliant vessel supply and support spot rates.
  2. Tight sanctions on the shadow fleet constrain active clean trading capacity, but a future easing of restrictions represents a key market sentiment risk.
  3. Increasing corporate consolidation among listed owners has historically supported commercial discipline, helping to mitigate the traditional industry tendency toward speculative over-ordering.
