Plausible problems to monitor

Outliers worth noting

The financial and operational profile of Altri SGPS SA reveals several statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies when benchmarked against European pulp sector peers and historical base rates. The most pronounced outlier emerges in leverage metrics, where Altri's net debt to EBITDA LTM surged to 5.5x in Q2 2026, a figure that exceeded Navigator's comfortable 1H26 leverage of 2.29x by a substantial margin. This represents an extreme divergence from peer norms and positions Altri in the top 5% of leveraged European forestry companies during a period of cyclical earnings recovery.

The velocity of leverage expansion–from 3.5x in FY25 to 5.5x in Q2 2026 –occurred despite sequential EBITDA improvements and signals either aggressive capital deployment or structural cash flow challenges that contradict typical deleveraging patterns observed during pulp price stabilization phases. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 6]

A second outlier manifests in Altri's margin compression trajectory, where the FY25 EBITDA margin of 13.4% represented a collapse from 25.5% in FY24, yet this margin trailed Navigator's superior 2025 EBITDA margin of 21% and only marginally exceeded ENCE's weak 11.1% in FY25. While sector-wide cyclical headwinds explain directional pressure, Altri's margin deterioration velocity and absolute positioning in the bottom quartile among Iberian peers suggests structural cost disadvantages beyond typical commodity cycle dynamics.

The gap persists even after accounting for temporary storm disruptions, indicating that Altri operates with fundamentally higher cash costs per ton than integrated competitors despite occupying similar geographic and regulatory environments. [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7]

A fourth outlier emerges in raw material procurement dependency, where Altri sources 80% to 85% of its wood from external Iberian suppliers, creating extreme supply chain vulnerability wherein a 5% increase in wood prices reduces operating results by EUR 12.2 million. This external dependence rate significantly exceeds the sector median of 50-60% for vertically integrated pulp producers and positions Altri in the bottom 10% for selfsufficiency among European forestry companies.

The sensitivity magnitude–representing approximately 13% of FY25 EBITDA for a single-digit percentage wood price movement–creates asymmetric downside exposure that distinguishes Altri from peers with captive forest estates and suggests that recent margin recoveries remain structurally fragile to regional supply shocks. [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 9]

The valuation architecture reveals a fifth outlier in forward multiple compression expectations, where Altri trades at a 20.4x P/E multiple for 2026 compared to its 2027 multiple of 11.5x, with a 2026 EV/EBITDA of 10.5x exceeding competitor Ence's 9.2x, yet consensus projects free cash flow yield rising from 5.3% in 2026 to 9.1% in 2027. This dramatic multiple compression path–implying 43% P/E contraction in one year –places Altri in the top 5% of European industrials for anticipated near-term earnings growth acceleration.

However, the valuation oddity lies in the market's apparent willingness to assign premium multiples during a year of depressed profitability while simultaneously forecasting exponential cash generation improvements that depend on multiple unproven strategic initiatives materializing simultaneously. The disconnect between current valuation richness and execution risk suggests either market mispricing or informational asymmetries not captured in public disclosures. [Cross-ref: Section 8, Section 9, Section 10]
