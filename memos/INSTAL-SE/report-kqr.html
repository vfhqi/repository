Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

1.
Key Question: Can Instalco deliver on Per Sjöstrand's plan to restore group margins to 8 percent under the Instalco 2.0 program while managing complex below-EBITA headwinds, and will this recovery translate into the consensus FY2027 EPS of SEK 3.01? The resolution of this question will determine whether Instalco AB (INSTAL-SE) can fulfil investor expectations over the next 18 months to 3 years, a period during which the company faces fifteen interconnected operational and financial dynamics that will collectively shape its earnings trajectory and risk profile. These dynamics span operating margin recovery mechanisms, post-EBITA line item pressures, working capital management, leverage normalization, acquisition restart timing, and leadership succession planning. The interplay between these factors will dictate whether the company can achieve sustainable earnings per share growth or whether structural headwinds will force downward consensus revisions. The institutional context for this question centers on the return of founder Per Sjöstrand as CEO, who implemented the Instalco 2.0 program to reverse margin deterioration that had plagued the group during the downturn. Sjöstrand has publicly committed to remaining in the chief executive role only until margins sustainably reach 8 percent on an EBITA basis, creating a defined timeline and performance threshold that anchors investor expectations. This turnaround program relies on selective bidding discipline, operational restructuring, and the normalization of legacy contract roll-offs to restore profitability across the Swedish segment, which remains the largest contributor to group earnings. However, the path to achieving these margin targets is complicated by closely related themes that extend well beyond operating performance. The lack of disclosure regarding the fixed-versus-floating interest mix on the SEK 3.4 billion credit facility creates substantial earnings modeling uncertainty, with financial items expected to consume 15 to 23 percent of EBIT during the high-rate cycle. This interest burden represents a material drag on pre-tax earnings that could persist longer than consensus models assume, particularly if the Riksbank's easing cycle proceeds more slowly than expected. Simultaneously, the 51 percent consolidation of German platform Fabri AG on 1 August 2026 has expanded minority interests, with DNB Carnegie forecasting total minority interests expanding to SEK 86 million in 2027. This structural leakage means that consolidated EBITA growth does not translate directly to parent shareholder net income, creating a disconnect between operating momentum and bottom-line EPS delivery. Furthermore, acquisition-related amortisation will remain high at SEK 110 million in 2027 and SEK 100 million in 2028, which will suppress reported earnings per share compared to cash-based operational measures. The working capital and leverage dimensions add further complexity. Operating cash flow has been weaker because of more capital tied up in accounts receivable, a normal consequence of rapid organic expansion and late-quarter billing that will continue to impact near-term earnings per share. The group reported net debt to EBITDA of 2.6x at the end of the second quarter 2026, slightly above the company's long-term target of 2.5x, which will continue to restrict aggressive M&A unless the seasonal fourth-quarter cash release materializes as expected.

These fifteen dynamics are also interwoven with external competitive pressures and macroeconomic risks. The Nordic data-centre build-out is absorbing significant competitor capacity, creating beneficial second-order pricing effects for Instalco because primary competitors are occupied with hyperscale contracts. However, this competitive reprieve is potentially temporary and reversible, meaning that any slowdown in data-centre investment could flood the medium-sized project market with excess capacity and reignite pricing wars. The ultimate impact on earnings per share over the next 18 to 36 months hinges on whether the operating margin recovery proves durable and whether the post-EBITA headwinds remain contained within current broker forecasts. If the Swedish segment achieves the DNB Carnegie path of 7.2 percent margins in 2027 and 7.6 percent in 2028–still below management's 8 percent target–and if interest expenses, minority leakages, and amortisation charges evolve as modeled, then the consensus FY2027 EPS of SEK 3.01 appears achievable. Conversely, if wage inflation accelerates in the spring 2027 Swedish wage round, if fixed-price contracts without indexation dominate the backlog, or if the Fabri integration introduces unexpected costs, then EPS could fall materially short of consensus expectations, triggering a de-rating of the equity story and potentially delaying the founder's planned succession timeline.

Section 2 – Operating Margin Dynamics and Pricing Power

Section summary
  1. Operating margin recovery at Instalco is initially driven by self-help labor capacity restructuring rather than immediate pricing power.
  2. Normalized bad debt provisions and the roll-off of low-margin legacy contracts provide a material margin tailwind through 2027.
  3. High peer exposure to complex data-centre installations tightens market capacity and indirectly supports pricing leverage for Instalco.

Sources of Sweden Margin Recovery

1.
Pruned capacity leverage: Labor restructuring in late 2024 provided the initial operational cushion because Instalco eliminated underperforming segments. According to a DNB Carnegie report dated 20 December 2024, the group laid off 110 employees and terminated 100 subcontractors, which was expected to improve gross margin utilization by 35 basis points. This self-help action helps restore margins but its impact could fade unless backlog execution remains fully staffed.
2.
Pricing power initiatives: True pricing power remains a secondary driver because market conditions remain mixed across regions. Instalco's CEO Per Sjöstrand stated in the fourth quarter earnings call on 12 February 2026 that the group is implementing value-added pricing under the Instalco 2.0 program to offset historical volume pressure. This strategic shift should support long-term earnings per share, but its effectiveness depends on selective bidding over volume.
3.
Sweden segment recovery: Sweden is leading the operating recovery because regional turnaround plans are gaining traction. DNB Carnegie reported on 17 July 2026 that Swedish organic sales grew 9 percent year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, which exceeded group average expansion. This performance suggests that the Swedish segment can achieve sustained margin improvements unless local labor bottlenecks reappear.

Contract Roll-off and Bad Debt Normalisation

4.
Bad debt normalization: Legacies of high customer insolvencies are fading because write-offs are normalizing. DNB Carnegie highlighted in its report dated 23 January 2026 that normalising bad debt provisions should boost group EBITA margins by approximately 40 basis points compared to 2024 levels. This normalisation will directly drop to pre-tax earnings unless further contractor bankruptcies occur.
5.
Contract roll-off progression: Low-margin legacy contracts signed during the downturn are rolling off and being replaced by higher-quality projects. Instalco's executive comments in the first quarter earnings call on 29 April 2025 noted that legacy price pressure would continue to weigh on intermediate margins as the group delivers on older backlog. This transition represents a structural trend rather than a one-time step because selective bidding keeps margins healthy.
6.
Write-offs impact: Historical contract write-downs severely depressed past margins but now present an easy comparison base. DNB Carnegie noted on 23 April 2025 that Q1 EBITA of SEK 123 million was dragged down by provisions for doubtful debt like Northvolt and Serneke. This cleanup supports a strong earnings recovery because subsequent quarters face significantly lower impairment drag.

Wage Inflation and Indexation Risks

7.
Swedish wage round: Rising labor cost pressure in the upcoming spring 2027 wage round poses a threat to margins. DNB Carnegie's report on 23 January 2026 noted that labor shortages in the sector remain persistent, which can trigger significant wage inflation. This pressure threatens earnings unless contract indexation clauses are sufficiently robust to pass through costs.
8.
Backlog indexation risk: Fixed-price contracts without inflation indexation represent a material risk because of long execution cycles. Instalco's interim report on 16 July 2026 stated that its order backlog grew 17 percent to an order backlog of SEK 10,937 million but much of this contains medium-sized projects. This backlog exposure could dilute profits if wage rounds exceed historical norms.
9.
Peer indexation comparison: Peers like SPIE SA show superior pricing resilience because of automated pass- through mechanisms. Morgan Stanley reported on 17 March 2026 that SPIE possesses robust indexation clause formulas that protect margins against inflation. This structural difference means SPIE's earnings are more insulated than Instalco's relatively fixed-price Swedish model.

Competitor Capacity and Segment Paths

10.
Competitor capacity absorption: The Nordic data-centre build-out is absorbing significant competitor capacity and easing regional price competition. DNB Carnegie argued in a report on 29 September 2026 that beneficial second-order pricing effects from a tighter subcontracting market are highly beneficial for Instalco. This capacity drain reduces bidding wars because primary competitors are occupied with hyperscale contracts.
11.
Peer data centre orders: Regional peers are booking historic order volumes that tie up their white-collar and engineering resources. Bravida's interim report on 13 July 2026 highlighted a SEK 20,372 million backlog, driven by massive data centre orders like the SEK 2.2 billion Kouvola project. This massive allocation of peer resources leaves the medium-sized project market open for Instalco.
12.
DNB Carnegie segment path: DNB Carnegie's Swedish segment projections remain below the company's long- term targets. In its small-cap report on 10 March 2026, DNB Carnegie published a Swedish segment EBITA margin path of 7.2 percent in 2027 and 7.6 percent in 2028. This forecast is more conservative than management's 8 percent target because of intermediate construction recovery doubts.
13.
Consensus margin relationship: The gap between group consensus margins and Carnegie's Swedish segment path reflects conservative assumptions. DNB Carnegie's report on 17 July 2026 notes that restoring group margins to 8% requires substantial operational leverage. This discrepancy suggests that consensus EPS estimates of SEK 3.01 could face downward pressure if Sweden underperforms.
14.
Contrary evidence: Swedish margin weakness during the turnaround phase indicates that recovery is not uniform. DNB Carnegie reported on 29 April 2026 that Swedish segment margins fell below expectations in the first quarter of 2026 despite optimistic management commentary. This operational slip shows that the labor utilization recovery can experience temporary seasonal setbacks before translating into stable profits.
15.
Other side of the story: Slower economic recovery and persistent pricing pressure in northern Sweden could delay Instalco's target timeline. While the reduction in subcontractors and bad debt normalization provide an easy baseline, the Swedish construction market continues to face cautious client decision-making processes. This prolonged sluggishness means Instalco might struggle to restore group margins to 8% by 2027 if competitor capacity returns to the medium-sized market.

Section 3 – Below-EBITA Line Items and Consensus Disconnects

Section summary
  1. High interest expenses and undisclosed debt structures create significant earnings modeling uncertainty.
  2. Fabri AG's majority consolidation expands operating profit but introduces substantial minority leakage and amortisation charges.
  3. The market consensus FY2027 EPS of SEK 3.01 fails to fully reflect these post-EBITA headwinds.

Interest Expenses and Rate Sensitivities

1.
Undisclosed facility terms: The lack of disclosure regarding the fixed-versus-floating interest mix on the SEK 3.4 billion credit facility creates substantial earnings modeling uncertainty. DNB Carnegie's report of 16 January 2025 noted that financial items were expected to consume 15 to 23 percent of EBIT during the high-rate cycle. While refinancing risks are absent because the group secured a bank consortium agreement, the ongoing interest drag remains substantial. This undisclosed mix means that rapid monetary easing will accelerate earnings per share recovery only if the floating portion is high.
2.
Rising finance costs: DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 projects net financial items of SEK negative 238 million in 2026, falling to SEK negative 159 million in 2027 under expected Riksbank rate cuts. This forecast incorporates the undisclosed fixed-floating mix of the group's SEK 3.4 billion credit facility. Unless interest rates fall faster than expected, this heavy debt burden will continue to restrict intermediate earnings per share growth because financing expenses consume a significant share of operating profit.
3.
Divergent consensus paths: The FactSet consensus path on 21 October 2026 shows pre-tax profit less EBIT falling from about SEK 234 million in 2026 to SEK 133 million in 2028. This projection reflects an optimistic scenario of rapid deleveraging and aggressive interest rate cuts across the Nordics. However, DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 models a more conservative EBIT-to-PTP bridge of SEK 238 million in 2026 and SEK 135 million in 2028. This discrepancy suggests that the market might be underestimating persistent high interest expenses over the next 18 months.

Minorities, Amortisation, and Tax Dynamics

4.
Expanding minority leakage: Existing subsidiary minorities cost Instalco SEK 32 million in FY2025 and grew to SEK 36 million in H1 2026. Following the 51 percent consolidation of German platform Fabri AG on 1 August 2026, minority leakages are set to step up. DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 forecasts total minority interests expanding to SEK 86 million in 2027 to reflect this German addition. This means a larger portion of operating success will fail to reach parent shareholders, depressing net income.
5.
Fabri deal structure: The multi-phase option structure for acquiring Fabri AG involves pre-negotiated tranches that scale up to 100 percent ownership by 2033. DNB Carnegie's report of 23 January 2026 highlights that the stage 2 option requires a payment of approximately SEK 350 million to purchase an additional 27 percent. While this transaction expands consolidated EBITDA, it also creates a massive minority interest estimated at SEK 600 million to SEK 650 million. These complex option steps mean that minority leakages will dilute net profit margin gains unless Fabri's local profitability exceeds historical averages. Non-cash amortization charges: Amortisation of acquired intangibles remains a persistent headwind because of Instalco's active historical roll-up strategy. DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 projects acquisitionrelated amortization at SEK 130 million for 2026, compared to SEK 132 million reported in 2025. This high level of charges is far more significant than that of peers with organic growth focus. Consequently, this structural charge will continue to depress reported net income margins even as the group's underlying operating cash flow improves.
6.
Elevated amortisation headwind: The consolidation of Fabri AG requires a new purchase price allocation that increases the amortisation of acquired intangibles. DNB Carnegie's model update on 17 July 2026 notes that updated amortisation assumptions make the positive operational effect on EPS less pronounced. According to DNB Carnegie's forecast of 29 September 2026, acquisition-related amortisation will remain high at SEK 110 million in 2027 and SEK 100 million in 2028. This heavy non-cash charge will suppress reported earnings per share compared to cash-based operational measures.
7.
Volatile tax assumptions: Instalco reported an effective tax rate of 28.1 percent in FY2025, which fell to approximately 20 percent during H1 2026. In response to these fluctuations, DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 models an effective tax rate of 22.0 percent for 2027 and 22.3 percent for 2028. Other covering brokers like Nordea have historically paid less attention to these specific adjustments in their public previews. These shifting tax assumptions can materially distort near-term EPS consensus if seasonal writeoffs spike again. Contrary evidence: Despite the complex below-EBITA headwinds, some historical periods show that short-term tax and interest rate fluctuations do not permanently impair cash generation. In its initiation of coverage on 21 November 2022, DNB Carnegie argued that brief tax rate spikes have a marginal impact on Instalco's ability to generate cash flow for further EBITA growth. This historical resilience suggests that the current focus on immediate tax normalization might exaggerate the underlying earnings risk if operational cash conversion remains near management's 100 percent target.

Consensus EPS and Peer Transparency Disconnects

8.
Disconnected EPS consensus: The FactSet consensus on 21 October 2026 places the FY2027 EPS target at SEK 3.01. This optimistic figure is built on aggressive margin recovery assumptions that are not yet reflected in conservative broker estimates. For instance, DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 projects an adjusted EPS of only SEK 2.99 for 2027. This gap shows that investors are overlooking the below-EBITA drag from interest, minorities, and amortisation, focusing instead on top-line Swedish recovery. Narrow analyst focus: Sell-side and investor attention remains heavily concentrated on top-line metrics and founder Per Sjöstrand's direct operational oversight. DNB Carnegie's report of 17 July 2026 highlights that the Swedish margin and order backlog are the primary items monitored by the market. Virtually no commentary on retail platforms like Avanza or in the financial press details the below-EBITA bridge. This narrow narrative focus means that any negative surprise in non-operating items could trigger unexpected earnings downgrades.
9.
Shrouded peer transparency: Peer disclosure on post-EBITA dynamics remains highly uneven across the European technical installation sector. For instance, Fasadgruppen Group AB suffered a 50 percent share drop after its unexpected rights issue, which serves as a cautionary tale of poor balance sheet transparency. Meanwhile, Burkhalter Holding AG experienced an unexplained 28 percent six-month share slide that left investors questioning its underlying financing costs. This sector-wide opacity means that Instalco's detailed disclosure on the Fabri integration is critical for restoring investor trust in its leverage profile. Other side of the story: The market's hyper-focus on Swedish operating margins and Per Sjöstrand's turnaround strategy may create a blind spot regarding the cumulative drag of below-EBITA items. If interest rates remain restrictive and minority leakages from Fabri AG expand as modeled by DNB Carnegie, the consensus FY2027 EPS of SEK 3.01 will be mathematically out of reach. Consequently, investors who ignore these technical adjustments run the risk of holding a stock that has re-rated ahead of actual earnings power.

Section 4 – Working Capital, Leverage, and Fabri Integration

Working Capital and Q4 Release Dynamics

1.
Tied-up growth capital: High activity levels at the end of quarters lead to temporary cash outflows. Instalco's interim report on 17 July 2026 reported that operating cash flow was weaker because of more capital tied up in accounts receivable. This capital consumption is a normal consequence of rapid organic expansion and late-quarter billing. This seasonal pattern will continue to impact near-term earnings per share unless invoicing schedules are smoothed.
2.
Contract assets expansion: The expansion of work-in-progress and contract assets reflects a busy project pipeline. Instalco reported rolling 12-month contract assets of SEK 808 million from SEK 551 million previously as project activity ramped up. This increase in contract assets represents unbilled revenue that restricts operating cash. These balances must be billed and collected to prevent cash constraints from impacting EPS growth.
3.
Rolling cash conversion: Cash conversion has dipped slightly below historical levels due to these working capital fluctuations. Instalco's interim report on 17 July 2026 showed a cash conversion of 90 percent rolling at the second quarter of 2026 compared to 108 percent in late 2025. While still healthy, this dip reflects the cash required to fund current project starts. Meeting the long-term 100 percent target remains dependent on seasonal year-end releases.
4.
Projected Q4 release: The fourth quarter typically brings substantial working capital relief and cash generation. DNB Carnegie's report on 23 January 2026 notes that Instalco usually achieves impressive net working capital releases in the final quarter of the year. This historical trend supports the group's ability to deleverage rapidly after seasonal autumn peaks. This Q4 cash release is critical for funding the subsequent year's acquisition agenda and supporting EPS.

Leverage Definition and Covenants

5.
Reported leverage calculation: The reported leverage ratio of the group differs from the bank covenant definitions. Instalco's interim report of 16 July 2026 reported a net debt to EBITDA of 2.6x at the end of the second quarter. This measure remains slightly above the company's long-term target of 2.5x. This leverage level will continue to restrict aggressive M&A unless cash conversion returns to historical highs.
6.
Pro forma definitions: Integrating acquisitions on a pro forma basis paints a more favorable picture of the group's leverage. DNB Carnegie's report of 23 January 2026 indicates that adjusting EBITDA for extraordinary restructuring and bad debt write-offs keeps leverage below 3.0x even during seasonal peaks. The banks allow these adjustments, which are not visible in the reported leverage. This means the risk of covenant breaches is low despite high nominal debt.
7.
Fabri debt consolidation: The consolidation of Fabri AG will alter the group's leverage metrics as debt is added. Instalco's report on 16 July 2026 notes that the step two consideration of EUR 35.7 million is financed through existing credit facilities. This financing will increase the absolute net debt of the group. This rising debt burden will restrict interest coverage ratios unless Fabri's EBITDA contribution offsets the cost.
8.
EBITDA annualization benefits: Annualizing Fabri's full earnings helps protect the group's leverage ratios from deteriorating. DNB Carnegie's report of 23 January 2026 argues that net debt to pro forma EBITDA should remain below 2.7x because 100 percent of Fabri's EBITDA is consolidated. This consolidation matches the newly added debt with a larger profit pool. This balance means the transaction does not dilute EPS despite the cash outlay.

Fabri AG Option Structures

9.
Multi-phase option design: The German entry is structured to minimize upfront risk through a gradual buyout. Instalco's interim report on 16 July 2026 outlines a multi-phase plan structured around options to eventually own the entire platform. The first step was completed in 2025 and the second step was signed in 2026. This structured process reduces the initial cash conversion burden and protects EPS.
10.
Step three hurdles: The acquisition of step three is contingent on meeting specific financial performance metrics. Instalco's annual report of 17 March 2026 states that step three is contingent on agreed earnings thresholds for the German platform. This 17 percent tranche will be executed in 2029 at the earliest. This hurdle protects the parent from paying for underperforming assets and safeguards earnings.
11.
Pricing formula details: The pricing formulas for the various equity tranches are tied to sales metrics. DNB Carnegie's report on 23 January 2026 notes that Instalco agreed to pay 0.9x sales for Fabri equity tranches. This predefined deal structure provides long-term valuation visibility for the remaining options. This formula-based pricing helps avoid overpayment risks that typically dilute compounder models.
12.
Steps four options: The final steps of the acquisition structure ensure long-term alignment of local German management. Instalco's presentation on 12 February 2025 highlighted that the final options run from 2030 to 2033 to complete the buyout. This extended timeline means local founders remain highly motivated and heavily invested in Fabri's growth. This alignment ensures that operational integration does not disrupt local profitability or group EPS.

Peer Benchmarking and Cash Conversion

13.
SPIE cash model: International peers like SPIE SA show superior cash conversion metrics due to negative working capital. Deutsche Bank's research note on 1 September 2026 highlights that SPIE has a track record of FCF conversion >100% supported by structurally negative working capital. SPIE achieved a working capital of negative 34 days of revenue in late 2025. This negative working capital structure generates substantial cash inflows during expansion phases that boost EPS.
14.
SPIE seasonality focus: SPIE also suffers from high cash seasonality but actively manages it to stabilize cash flows. Morgan Stanley's report of 30 July 2026 notes that SPIE's management is focusing on reducing the seasonality of cash generation through earlier collections. This effort aims to pull a greater share of cash flow into the first half. This proactive management reduces balance sheet volatility and ensures consistent capital allocation.
15.
Salix Group comparison: Regional peers like Salix Group exhibit much higher working capital requirements that restrict cash conversion. DNB Carnegie's report on 24 September 2026 notes that Salix's net working capital to sales ratio averaged about 19.9 percent. This capital intensity means Salix's cash conversion will likely decline to 43 percent as growth returns. This comparison highlights that Instalco's 90 percent cash conversion is structurally superior to distribution peers.
16.
Contrary evidence: Weak Q2 free cash flow because of seasonal working capital build-up can occasionally surprise the market. DNB Carnegie reported on 22 August 2024 that Instalco's Q2 free cash flow conversion reached only about 25 percent against the 50 percent expected. This negative variance shows that seasonal swings in work-in-progress can temporarily impair cash conversion. These periodic cash drains delay debt repayment and can temporarily suppress quarterly EPS performance.
17.
Other side of the story: While management and optimistic analysts highlight pro forma adjustments that keep leverage under control, absolute net debt remains elevated and continues to expose the group to high interest costs. If the seasonal working capital release in Q4 2026 falls short of expectations and Fabri's integration costs exceed the budgeted EUR 35.7 million, reported leverage could exceed the 3.0x mark. This leverage pressure would prevent the group from restarting its core Nordic M&A strategy, forcing a continued reliance on organic execution to restore group margins to 8%.

Accelerating transaction pace: Instalco is preparing to restart its core Nordic acquisition strategy after a period of balance sheet consolidation. DNB Carnegie's report of 29 September 2026 notes that as Instalco approaches its 8 percent margin target and reduces leverage, a return to more frequent acquisition activity will act as a key rerating catalyst. This potential resumption of dealmaking is critical because acquisitions are historically the primary driver of the group's sales growth.
