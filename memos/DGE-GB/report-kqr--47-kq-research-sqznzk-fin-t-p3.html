Section 2 – Marketing Intensity and Promotion Loops in North America

Section summary
  1. Diageo's decision to cut its advertising and promotion budget has triggered a negative feedback loop of market share loss, forcing the company into defensive price resets.
  2. The collapse of agave prices has enabled aggressive competitor promotions, squeezing Diageo's premium tequila portfolio and necessitating surgical price cuts on Casamigos to protect volume.
  3. While increased marketing spend allows competitors like Campari to gain share, Diageo's surgical resets aim to stabilize its volume without triggering a category-wide price war.
  4. Wholesaler destocking represents a highly disruptive balancing loop where elevated interest rates and high distributor carrying costs compress supplier shipments well below retail depletions.
  5. Diageo's shipment volumes have broadly reconnected with retail demand, but the company remains vulnerable to promotional cycles as trade partners prioritize hyper-lean working capital.
  6. Pernod Ricard experienced peak inventory adjustments in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, with destocking expected to remain an ongoing but moderating drag throughout the year.
  7. Improving on-time-in-full delivery from 84% to 98% represents a powerful commercial service loop designed to secure retail shelf space and structurally reduce out-of-stocks.

Marketing spend and performance bifurcation

1.
Decreasing investment: Diageo's decision to cut its organic advertising and promotion spend by 13.6% in fiscal year 2026 has significantly impacted its brand competitiveness. This reduction in marketing investment coincided with a major overall US spirits net sales decline of 11.5% for the company. Consequently, Diageo reported that only 35% of its business segments held or gained market share in fiscal 2026. Reducing marketing support has clearly accelerated share losses across key brands.
2.
Clear outperformance: Deutsche Bank research indicates that consumer staples companies that increase their advertising and promotion spend consistently outperform their peers. Specifically, companies that step up brand investment outperform those that decrease spend by 122% in cumulative share price performance since 1998. This marketing outperformance loop supports organic sales growth by an average of 80 basis points. Underinvesting in marketing risks locking companies into a negative loop of volume decline and forced promotional recovery.
3.
Severe pressure: Pernod Ricard has also faced intense market challenges, resulting in a 13% decline in net sales in the United States during the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The company's operating margins for US distillers peaked well before the current sales cycle turned negative. This margin contraction has forced competitors to re-evaluate their high-end promotional strategies. Unless Pernod Ricard stabilizes US wholesaler inventory levels, its shipments will continue to trail retail depletions.
4.
Successful expansion: Davide Campari-Milano has successfully countered industry trends by increasing its advertising and promotional expenses to 17.4% of net sales. This strategic step-up in marketing spend allowed the company to deliver 2.9% organic topline growth in the first nine months of fiscal 2026. Campari's volume performance, particularly for its Espolon tequila brand, has remained highly resilient compared to major competitors. This outperformance confirms that investing heavily in brand building during market downturns preserves market share.

Tequila price repositioning in North America

5.
Drastic repositioning: Diageo has implemented a significant 10% to 15% recommended retail price reduction on Casamigos in the United States to address affordability. This pricing reset has started to drive a positive volume reaction, with Casamigos Blanco volume returning to growth at 4% recently. However, the sharp pricing cuts have created a highly negative price-mix dynamic for the brand. It remains highly uncertain whether this volume recovery will offset the negative margin impact over the next 18 months.
6.
Margin dilution: Jefferies Research indicates that the price repositioning of Casamigos is an explicit admission that the brand had overshot consumer affordability. During the pandemic tequila boom, Diageo aggressively premiumized the brand, pushing prices above $45. Rebuilding volume through lower pricing will likely drag Diageo's operating margins back towards a 15% to 16% range over the medium term. This case study suggests that restoring brand relevance comes at the expense of profit margin percentage.
7.
Contrary evidence: Diageo's price cuts have not triggered a runaway price war across the US tequila category so far. Brown-Forman reported in September 2026 that only one major brand did a price repositioning, while core 750ml bottles saw only 1% to 2% price deflation. Competitor pricing across US spirits has declined by only 0.5%, indicating that major manufacturers are maintaining pricing discipline. This suggests that category-wide deflation remains a localized, brand-specific threat rather than a market-wide race to the bottom.
8.
Escalating competition: The cognac segment has seen a significant pricing reset, with Hennessy lowering its average prices to close the gap to competitors. Remy Cointreau was forced to adjust its promotional support to remain competitive, leading to a 5% average price decline. Additionally, the US cognac market has increasingly become a five-player battleground since Bacardi launched D'Usse. This aggressive price and promotional pressure highlights how premium brands are being squeezed across multiple categories.

Agave glut and competitive tequila dynamics

9.

This oversupply has flooded the market with cheap, relatively high-quality tequila that challenges high-end brands. Diageo's premium portfolio is highly exposed to this downtrend because its average case price remains significantly higher than the market. Pressured consumers are trading down, which will continue to compress margins across the industry through fiscal 2027.
10.
High competitive pressure: Becle, the producer of Jose Cuervo, has experienced substantial net sales declines of 15.3% due to lower volumes and a promotional environment. The company's gross margin decreased to 52.8%, driven by heavy price discounting in the US and unfavorable regional mix. To remain competitive, Becle is deploying targeted tactical promotional activity and short-term pricing adjustments. This aggressive promotional loop threatens long-term category profitability unless overall demand recovers.
11.
Sustained underperformance: Morgan Stanley indicates that Becle continues to lose market share in tequila and overall US spirits. The broker has maintained an underweight rating on the stock due to persistent top-line challenges and intensive pricing competition. Although lower agave prices support Becle's gross margin, discounts in the US are taking a heavy toll on profitability. This intense competitive backdrop limits the potential for any near-term valuation re-rating.
12.
Strategic repositioning: Brown-Forman is actively addressing value consciousness in the tequila category by repositioning its el Jimador brand. The company is moving the brand into the faster-growing $20 to $30 range with new premium packaging and communications. This price repositioning aims to capture market share from consumers trading down from ultra-premium categories. If this strategy succeeds, it will stabilize the company's tequila volume growth and offset broader category declines.

Synthesizing the marketing feedback loops

13.
Critical feedback loop: A lower marketing intensity has historically triggered a reinforcing loop of market share loss followed by forced promotional spending. Diageo's organic A&P cut of 13% in FY2026 has left its core brands highly vulnerable to share erosion. Underinvesting in brand building weakens consumer top-of-mind relevance and drives volume share losses to better-invested competitors. Eventually, underinvesting firms are forced to deploy aggressive promotions to reclaim lost shelf space, leading to long-term margin erosion.
14.
Other side of the story: Diageo's surgical price resets and targeted marketing investments could successfully break the negative share-loss loop. By repositioning Casamigos to a more accessible price point, the company is addressing the affordability limits of cash-strapped consumers. This pragmatic, category-led strategy accepts modest margin dilution in exchange for restoring volume growth. If these targeted actions rebuild brand volume and retailer support, they will stabilize earnings without triggering a wider price war.

The mechanics of the distributor destocking loop

15.
Interest rate pressure: High distributor carrying costs have triggered a powerful balancing loop where trade partners actively reduce their working capital exposure. Distributors and retailers are unwilling to tie up cash in expensive physical inventory when financing costs are elevated. Consequently, trade partners maintain the leanest possible inventory levels, forcing spirits suppliers to bear the financial burden of holding mature stock. This shift has broken the historical trade support model where distributors willingly absorbed excess supplier volumes during slow periods.
16.
Shipments lagging depletions: The immediate operational result of this destocking loop is a severe divergence between supplier shipments and retail depletions. Wholesalers satisfy consumer demand by drawing down existing warehouse stocks rather than placing new orders. This destocking dynamic compresses supplier net sales and distorts underlying consumer demand trends. To break this cycle and stimulate order volumes, suppliers are frequently forced to increase promotional discounts and trade support. These defensive promotions erode gross margins and create additional near-term earnings pressure.
17.
Macroeconomic triggers: Adverse macroeconomic conditions directly amplify these stocking disruptions. Cash flow constraints and credit defaults among distributors trigger defensive inventory adjustments. Wholesalers aggressively manage their cash positions, causing sudden shifts away from ordinary ordering patterns. This volatile environment complicates supplier production planning and leads to elevated supplierlevel inventory gluts. Ultimately, the financial pain of managing aged inventory cycles is pushed entirely back onto the distillers.

Logistics service loops and the OTIF delivery target

20.
Securing shelf space: Diageo has established a clear operational target to raise its on-time-in-full (OTIF) delivery rate from 84% to 98%. Improving logistics performance represents a powerful reinforcing feedback loop. High OTIF rates minimize out-of-stock occurrences at the retail level, ensuring that Diageo's core brands maintain continuous shelf presence. Reliable service levels strengthen distributor relationships, allowing Diageo to secure preferred shelf placement and defend its category market share.
21.
Precedents in logistics: Historical precedents in the consumer goods sector demonstrate that superior logistics execution yields structural market share gains. Retailers aggressively penalize suppliers that fail to meet strict delivery windows, often reallocation shelf space to more reliable competitors. Conversely, achieving a 98% OTIF rate allows a manufacturer to become a preferred category captain. This status grants Diageo greater influence over local retail inventory planning and promotional calendars, reinforcing its volume momentum.
22.
Contrary evidence: However, improving physical logistics service cannot fully overcome weak consumer demand or structural industry challenges. Morgan Stanley argues that despite Diageo's operational turnaround efforts, there is no evidence of improving US market share trends in recent scanner data. The broker notes that developed market spirits demand faces persistent headwind from health and wellness trends. Consequently, while a 98% OTIF rate reduces operational friction, it may simply prevent further share erosion rather than driving positive organic growth. Other side of the story: While distributors continue to enforce lean trade inventories, the physical reconnection of shipments to depletions limits further downside risk. By aligning sell-in with actual retail demand, Diageo and Pernod Ricard have eliminated the massive inventory overhangs that disrupted the market post-pandemic. If underlying consumer demand stabilizes, the combination of clean wholesaler inventories and high logistics service levels will allow shipments to rebound rapidly. Under these conditions, the operating leverage of the distillers will recover, driving positive revisions to earnings per share.

Section 4 – Guinness Portfolio Expansion and Draught Innovation Loops

Compounding loop of Guinness expansion

This low-teens growth rate significantly outperforms the global premium beer sector which averaged only 5% growth over the same three-year period. This impressive expansion is underpinned by a 30% return on invested capital, which is roughly twice the average return level of the wider Diageo group.

This major supply infrastructure investment includes 340 mm dedicated to Phase 2 of the Littleconnell brewery in County Kildare. This program is designed to increase total Guinness production capacity by more than 50% to satisfy surging global consumer demand.

This extensive regional development establishes Ireland as a highly efficient global hub for next-generation, lowcarbon brewing.

Recruitment and non-alcoholic incrementality

4.
Highly incremental: Diageo reports that 77% of Guinness 0.0 shoppers are entirely incremental to the parent Guinness brand. This exceptional rate of recruitment demonstrates that the non-alcoholic variant successfully targets completely new consumer cohorts rather than cannibalizing existing brand drinkers. In the United States, only 2% of overlap exists between consumers of Guinness 0.0 and core Guinness Draught in a Can.

The company added 3,500 Microdraught points for Guinness 0.0 in Great Britain alongside 1,000 new outlets in Ireland during fiscal 2026. These modular innovations bypass traditional line hygiene barriers to unlock significant incremental sales in previously untapped premises.

Contrasting loops of Heineken and Murphy's

6.
Pioneering scale: Heineken remains the process and scale leader in low-alcohol beer, with its non-alcoholic exposure standing at 3.8% of its total volume. This exposure sits ahead of named brewing peers Carlsberg at 3.0% and Anheuser-Busch InBev at 2.0% of volumes. The company reached a major milestone by installing Heineken 0.0 draught in over 10,000 European outlets by early 2026.
7.
Strong challenge: Heineken-owned Murphy's Irish Stout has emerged as a direct balancing loop to Guinness's market dominance in Great Britain. A nationwide shortage of Guinness Draught in late 2024 allowed Heineken to rapidly place Murphy's into 1,550 new pubs. Murphy's capitalized on this supply friction to skyrocket its sales by 632% in December 2024, proving that retail supply constraints can quickly break historical brand loyalty.
8.
Contrary evidence: Murphy's rapid expansion has not stopped the underlying absolute growth of Guinness in its core British market. Nielsen YTD data to October 2026 shows Guinness grew retail value by 8.7% and increased its total beer share to 4.8% from 4.1% two years ago. Although Murphy's captured a 4.4% share of the British stout category in fiscal 2026, it primarily acted as an additive force expanding the overall stout segment.
9.
Other side of the story: While Guinness continues to demonstrate strong global momentum, its core domestic performance faces immediate competitive and cyclical headwinds. Diageo reported that Great Britain and Ireland beer sales declined by a low-single-digit rate in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. If Murphy's persistent on-trade expansion and lower pricing continue to win over cost-conscious pub operators, Guinness's core volume recovery could stall, dragging down total European beer margins.

The state transitioned to an alcohol-in-beverage based taxation structure that taxes actual alcohol strength rather than total volume. This structural rationalization reduced consumer prices by 10% to 15% and unlocked powerful double-digit volume growth in premium spirits. Trade agreements and tariff disputes

9.
Transformative agreement: The implementation of the UK-India Free Trade Agreement is expected to be highly transformative for premium spirits in India. Under the agreement, import tariffs on Scotch whisky and gin immediately decreased from 150% to 75% in July 2026. These concessional rates are scheduled to progressively decline to 40% over the next decade. This tariff relief directly improves affordability and recruits younger consumers to international brands.
10.
Customs deadlock: United Spirits' near-term margin benefits from the UK-India trade deal have been temporarily delayed by a customs dispute. Indian customs authorities are holding containers of bulk Scotch imported by major distillers due to an investigation into HSN classifications. While bottled imports receive the lower 75% tariff, the status of bulk Scotch imports remains under active government examination. This ongoing regulatory review creates near-term margin volatility for domestic blending operations.
11.
Contrary evidence: The ongoing customs dispute over bulk spirit imports has significantly delayed the immediate financial benefits of the India-UK agreement. Industry reports indicate that this bulk Scotch import debate carries an estimated three to four percent negative earnings impact relative to original trade expectations. While Bottled-in-Origin Scotch is receiving the lower 75% tariff rate, the delayed bulk resolution restricts expected margin expansion. Consequently, United Spirits' near-term profitability improvement from the trade deal is less immediate than originally anticipated.
12.
Stellar outperformance: Radico Khaitan Limited has significantly outperformed United Spirits by delivering a stellar 39% growth in its Prestige and Above segment revenue during the second quarter of fiscal year 2027. This strong outperformance is supported by premium volume growth of 38% and better state mix. While United Spirits has struggled with regulatory disruptions, Radico's strong premiumization and backward integration have driven record-high profitability. This peer outperformance demonstrates that local agility and category focus are key differentiators in India.
13.
Multinational competitiveness: Pernod Ricard SA reported strong high-single-digit underlying net sales growth of 19% in India during the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. The company's premium imported portfolio delivered robust double-digit growth, supported by the initial tailwinds of the UK-India trade agreement. To further sharpen its premium focus, Pernod Ricard divested its lower-margin Imperial Blue brand to Tilaknagar Industries. This strategic divestment highlights how multinational competitors are aggressively restructuring their portfolios to capture high-margin premium growth.
14.
Other side of the story: Despite intense execution challenges and the temporary bulk tariff delay, United Spirits remains the absolute market leader and prime beneficiary of India's multi-decade premiumisation trend. While headcount cuts have generated operational friction, the company's dominant position in premium Scotch and localized innovations like Smirnoff flavors provide a highly resilient growth runway. Once the bulk import customs dispute is resolved, the compounding benefits of lower duties and progressive state reforms will drive substantial operating leverage. Consequently, United Spirits is well-positioned to deliver double-digit earnings per share growth over the medium term.
