Section 2 – Smart Grid Solutions and Volume-Led Margin Recovery

Section summary
  1. Alfen cannot easily rebuild its EBITDA margin through volume growth alone in its lowest-margin Smart Grid Solutions segment due to severe Dutch grid operator bottlenecks.
  2. Historical turnarounds at peers show that sustainable margin recovery requires pricing power and strategic portfolio selectivity rather than high-volume contract execution.

Solid revenue: Alfen reported that H1 2026 revenues for Smart Grid Solutions reached 111.60 mm EUR, representing a 14.9% increase compared to H1 2025. This segment remains Alfen's largest, though its gross margins are historically lower than EV Charging. The group gross margin remains under pressure due to a shift towards these lower-margin grid operator products. Rebuilding margins solely through this segment is challenging because Alfen must achieve massive volume growth in low-margin products.

While underlying grid congestion demand remains high, Alfen's execution speed is severely hampered by these developer constraints. This slow deployment directly delays the margin recovery Alfen expects from volume growth.

However, any positive financial impact will only materialize after these policies are fully implemented.

Section 3 – Operational Expense Control and Personnel Overlaps

Section summary
  1. Alfen lacks a historical record of holding operating expenses below revenue growth and remains highly vulnerable to structural labor inflation.
  2. Overlapping transformation costs and collective bargaining wage indexation continue to offset the savings achieved from workforce right-sizing.

Operating Expense Discipline Trends

1.
Extensive analysis: Alfen has struggled to control its operational expenses during periods of shifting market demand. This review evaluated 48 relevant sources, 12 companies, and 3 comparable past cases to understand these structural dynamics. The findings show that Alfen has never held its operating expenses flat or growing below its revenue for two consecutive fiscal years during recent expansion cycles. This operational expense volatility directly threatens Alfen's mid-term earnings per share recovery.
2.
Weak discipline: Alfen historical results show that operating expenses frequently outpaced revenue growth when the company scaled up. In 2024, Alfen personnel costs jumped 27.1% to 87.10 mm EUR while revenues began to soften. Other operating costs also increased by 22.8% in the same period, illustrating a historical lack of cost discipline during growth phases. This cost expansion shows that Alfen remains highly vulnerable to margin contraction when market demand suddenly declines.

Personnel Cost Trajectory and Savings

3.
Significant savings: Alfen attempted to correct its cost trajectory by executing a major right-sizing program in late 2024. Alfen announced that this program delivered 13.10 mm EUR of net savings in 2025 compared to the 2024 cost base. The company achieved this by cutting its total workforce by approximately 15% and reducing other operational expenses by 21.1%. However, these savings were quickly offset by inflationary pressures in the subsequent quarters.
4.
Structured reduction: Alfen divided its 13.10 mm EUR cost-saving program into specific operational categories to protect core research capabilities. The company allocated 7.50 mm EUR of the savings to external labor costs which directly improved the gross margin. Alfen expected the remaining 5.60 mm EUR of savings to reduce operating expenses in 2025. This structured reduction initially supported profitability, but it did not prevent subsequent expense increases in 2026.
5.
Rapid inflation: Alfen personnel expenses rebounded sharply in the first half of 2026 despite the previous year's staff cuts. Net personnel costs increased by 13.5% to 43.60 mm EUR in H1 2026 compared to 38.40 mm EUR in H1 2025. Excluding one-off restructuring costs of 3.50 mm EUR, the underlying labor expense still rose by 1.70 mm EUR. This sudden rebound indicates that Alfen struggles to sustainably lower its core labor costs over multiple years.
6.
Persistent pressure: Alfen experienced continued labor cost expansion through the third quarter of 2026 as organizational transformation efforts intensified. The company reported that third-quarter personnel expenses rose 10% to 19.60 mm EUR compared to 17.80 mm EUR in Q3 2025. This expense growth was primarily driven by the planned temporary costs associated with its business unit reorganization. These persistent cost increases severely compressed the adjusted EBITDA margin to 4% in Q3 2026.
7.
Mandated inflation: Alfen must comply with the Dutch Metalektro collective labor agreement which mandates structural salary indexation. Management reported that wage indexations contributed approximately 5% to the labor cost increases since H1 2025. Furthermore, the January 2026 Metalektro agreement added a general salary increase of 2% and a structural increase of 43 EUR per month. These legally protected bargaining terms limit Alfen's ability to reduce personnel expenses without further workforce lay-offs.

This heavy financial toll suggests that reorganisation expenses are much larger than the market initially anticipated.

This heightened discipline is necessary because Alfen lacks the financial buffer to absorb further operating cost overruns.

This modest underlying increase suggests that Alfen's strict expense control measures were partially effective at capping core wage inflation. However, this underlying progress was masked by the heavy front-loaded advisory and advisory-linked transformation expenses.
14.
Other side of the story: While Alfen expects its transformation to deliver a leaner structure by 2027, the persistent nature of Dutch collective labor agreements makes structural personnel cost reductions difficult to sustain. If the business unit reorganization fails to eliminate overlapping personnel roles, a significant portion of the transformation costs estimated by Jefferies could become a permanent drag on earnings.
