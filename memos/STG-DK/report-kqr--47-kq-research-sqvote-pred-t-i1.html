Section 2 – Historic Guidance, Management Credibility and the Rebasing Record

Section summary
  1. Scandinavian Tobacco Group has rebased its financial targets after failing to meet its initial annual guidance in three of the past four years.
  2. Management initiated highly conservative targets for fiscal year 2026 to rebuild capital market credibility and establish a lower hurdle rate.

Historical Guidance and Performance Gaps

1.
Disappointing initial targets: Scandinavian Tobacco Group established its initial fiscal year 2026 adjusted EPS expectation at DKK 9.0 to 11.0, representing a clear decline against the DKK 10.80 achieved in the prior year. This cautious initial target was designed to absorb structural volume declines of 3% to 5% annually across the group's core cigar portfolios. Consequently, the lowered hurdle rate allowed the company to easily surpass expectations during the first half of the year. This strategic rebasing represents a major pivot to restore long-term investor confidence in the company's financial planning.
2.
Persistent performance misses: Scandinavian Tobacco Group has historically struggled to meet its initial annual targets, missing its start-of-year forecasts in three of the past four fiscal years. The group posted significant adjusted EBITDA misses of 6% in 2022, 6% in 2023, and a severe 12% miss in 2025. These frequent guidance cuts occurred because management consistently underestimated the pace of consumer migration toward cheaper alternatives. These repetitive operational shortfalls severely damaged management credibility and forced a complete reset of shareholder expectations.
3.
Contrary evidence: Scandinavian Tobacco Group delivered exceptionally strong financial outcomes during the pandemic years, beating its initial annual EBITDA targets by 11% in both fiscal year 2020 and fiscal year 2021. Cautious guiding approach: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that Scandinavian Tobacco Group management adopted a highly cautious guidance approach for fiscal year 2026 to address consecutive years of declining profitability. This methodology helped to rebuild capital market trust after prior guidance failures.

  1. Extreme deviations from consensus estimates would reshape Scandinavian Tobacco Group's long-term multiple and capital allocation potential.
  2. Monitoring alternative retail performance and US inventory movements provides early insight into underlying operational shifts.

The Upside Bull Case Scenario

1.
Positive margin rebounds: Scandinavian Tobacco Group could experience a strong financial recovery if European machine-rolled cigar operations stabilize faster than the market expects. DNB Carnegie highlights that a bull-case scenario where EBITDA margins rebound to 23% and the NTM adjusted P/E multiple expands to 9x would support a valuation of DKK 120 per share. This valuation expansion represents a major recovery compared to historical averages, driven by stronger volume stabilization across core European regions. Under these conditions, the company would accelerate its capital returns and easily exceed conservative consensus forecasts.
2.
Accelerated strategic delivery: Deutsche Bank Research outlines a highly constructive bull case where the Focus2030 strategy delivers organic growth across key European markets. This scenario assumes 2.0% annual organic sales growth from 2027 through 2030 alongside a European Branded EBITDA margin of 24.8% by the end of the period. Better operating leverage and successful portfolio simplification would drive this margin expansion above the base-case forecasts. Consequently, stronger volume leverage across US channels would drive group adjusted EPS to DKK 15.7 by 2030, representing a 7.7% compound annual growth rate.
3.
Explosive pouch expansion: Scandinavian Tobacco Group's next-generation brand XQS could capture a much larger share of the emerging European nicotine pouch market. The Swedish pouch segment already exhibits rapid expansion, with XQS exceeding a 13% market share and expanding into the United Kingdom. If this momentum replicates across other European markets, modern oral sales would expand significantly. These next-generation gains would offset traditional volume declines entirely and re-rate the stock as a modern growth player.
4.
Enhanced capital efficiency: Scandinavian Tobacco Group could deliver substantial EPS upgrades if the completed divestment of its German fine-cut tobacco business results in more aggressive share repurchases. DNB Carnegie suggests that the transaction reduces group leverage to approximately 2.0x Net Debt/EBITDA. This strategic improvement enables the company to launch an unanticipated DKK 0.50 bn buyback program. Reducing the outstanding share count under a flat base of DKK 78.9 mm outstanding shares would drive consensus earnings per share revisions significantly higher over the next eighteen months.

The Downside Bear Case Scenario

5.
Severe margin contraction: Scandinavian Tobacco Group faces meaningful downside risks if traditional cigar volume declines accelerate beyond expected rates. DNB Carnegie cautions that a bear-case scenario where margins contract to previous lows of 17% would press the NTM adjusted P/E down to 7x. This underperformance would imply a depressed share price of DKK 60 and severely limit future share buyback capacity. These negative dynamics would materialize if price hikes fail to offset structural volume declines of 3% to 5% annually.
6.
Persistent structural pressures: Deutsche Bank Research models a downside scenario where intense European category pressures and competitive threats cannot be mitigated. In this bear case, the group suffers a negative 2.0% annual organic sales decline from 2027 through 2030. Reduced volumes would restrict cost-saving benefits, leaving the European EBITDA margin at 21.8% in fiscal year 2030. Under these conditions, the company's long-term return on invested capital would remain depressed and prevent a valuation rerating.
7.
Compounding retail challenges: Scandinavian Tobacco Group's extensive North American online and retail operations remain highly sensitive to consumer spending volatility. Rising promotional pressure and shifts toward value-for-money products in the United States could severely disrupt premium handmade cigar sales. If US wholesale inventory patterns contract, the online division's gross margins would experience immediate downward revisions. This contraction would be exacerbated by a depreciating US Dollar, which creates severe translation headwinds.
8.
Dilutive manufacturing agreements: Scandinavian Tobacco Group could experience lower gross profit margins if its contract manufacturing agreement with Japan Tobacco is renegotiated on unfavorable terms. The divested BREAK and Moro brands historically represented a highly profitable segment of the group's fine-cut portfolio. Replacing this business with a contract manufacturing model lowers capacity utilization. If Japan Tobacco modifies its procurement strategy, the segment's gross profit would face permanent downward pressure.

Key Early Warning Indicators

9.
Alternative channel indicators: Scandinavian Tobacco Group's financial trajectory can be predicted early by tracking specific operational data points ahead of consensus shifts. For example, monitoring US B2B order timing and the performance of retail superstores provides clear signals on premium handmade demand. A rapid deceleration in retail same-store sales would act as an early sign that North American consumer sentiment is deteriorating. This alternative retail data would trigger downward revisions long before formal quarterly updates.
10.
Scandinavia pouch trends: Scandinavian Tobacco Group's success in modern oral nicotine remains a critical indicator of its long-term strategic viability. Analysts must closely monitor monthly retail audit data in Sweden and Denmark to track XQS market share fluctuations. Any slowdown in pouch growth or aggressive pricing discounts from global competitors would suggest that NGP margins are coming under pressure. This competitive threat is particularly high as first movers utilize established brand equity to restrict new entrants.
11.
Contrary evidence: Scandinavian Tobacco Group's machine-rolled cigar positions in key European markets have shown significant volatility during major administrative transitions. For instance, European volume market shares declined from 29.9% to 28.1% in early 2024 due to supply disruptions and ERP implementation issues. This historical volatility suggests that operational execution risks can override positive underlying pricing trends and disrupt expected earnings recoveries. These disruptions demonstrate that internal execution is often a larger swing factor than external market conditions.
12.
Other side of the story: Scandinavian Tobacco Group may avoid downside cases because its strong free cash flow generation provides a highly resilient buffer. Even if structural volume declines persist, the group's low capital expenditure requirements and administrative cost efficiencies protect cash yields. Consequently, the balance sheet flexibility recovered from recent brand divestments ensures that shareholder payouts remain highly stable under most operating conditions. This cash generation focus mitigates near-term earnings volatility and supports a stable floor for the valuation multiple.
