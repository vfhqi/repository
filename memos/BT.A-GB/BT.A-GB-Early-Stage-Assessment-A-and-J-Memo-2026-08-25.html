B Executive Summary C 501 findings
Executive Summary -- We are looking for a clear, conviction-carrying statement of the investment thesis, key ratings, and actionable conclusion; avoiding vague or overly hedged language.
Judgement summary
  • Overall verdict, park not progress. C BT is a genuine cash-flow inflection inside a shrinking market, and the inflection is real, but the case fails three Viewforth tests that Early-Stage Assessment exists to apply
  • Worst finding, the growth test fails outright. D Adjusted earnings per share ran 18.1p, 18.8p, 18.3p across FY24 to FY26, with FY27 guided to low-single-digit growth, nowhere near the 12% to 20% bar
  • Second, the risks report recommends parking. Its own stage verdict is to "Park the BT Group investment thesis" until cash tax, pension funding and competitive tensions clear; this memo adopts that recommendation
  • Third, simplicity fails badly. D Eight live change-from-current items and a headwind list no European incumbent peer matches breach the two-fulcrum-driver and zero-headwind guardrails at once
  • Sell-side earnings momentum is weak. D Master-dashboard revision counts show earnings per share revisions of 0, minus 1, minus 3 over one, three and six months, positive only at twelve months
  • Openreach line losses are un-halted. 825,000 broadband lines lost in FY26 with roughly 800,000 guided for FY27, against typical European incumbent churn of 2% to 3% of base
  • Secular growth is absent. D European telecom market growth runs near 1.5% a year, below expected inflation, and BT's own three-year revenue compound rate is minus 1.5%
  • Returns on capital lag the peer set. D BT's return on invested capital of 7.8% sits against KPN's 15.0%, Tele2's 11.5% and Orange's 8.9% on the business-foundations peer table
  • Balance sheet is the binding constraint. Net debt of £20.0bn including leases plus a £4.2bn accounting pension deficit equals roughly 123% of FY26 revenue, a 90th-percentile European burden
  • Valuation cannot be settled from permitted data. The master-dashboard forward multiple of 13.7x is marked stale and is internally inconsistent with its own forward earnings field, so dependent grades are marked ungradeable
  • The good news is narrow but real. B Capital expenditure peaked at £5,127m in FY26 and steps down beyond £1.0bn to £4.3bn, the single most mechanically certain driver in the file
  • Cost delivery is running to plan. B The gross savings target rose from £3.0bn to £3.7bn by FY30, with £1.5bn cumulative already banked at a £0.8bn cost to achieve
  • The operator is a genuine strength. B Allison Kirkby delivered 52% total shareholder return at Tele2 against 6% for peers, and BT's 4.8m premises build pace leads Europe
  • Setup fit is corporate change, not demand. B Raised guidance, a full leadership refresh, five divestments and the Verizon joint venture make this a corporate-change earnings-power case, not a demand story
  • Entry point is unattractive on the permitted read. Relative strength sits at the 29th percentile and the price trades below its 150-day average of 205.43p despite clearing the 200-day
  • Recommendation, park with four named gates. Park at moderate, not high, conviction: a live valuation percentile, the December Competition and Markets Authority ruling, the autumn triennial pension outcome and H1 FY27 line losses
Business Description (B.1) 20 findings
Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
Judgement summary
A domestically concentrated, heavily capitalised telecoms incumbent whose reported cash generation is about to step up sharply as a decade-long fibre build completes.
1.
National telecoms incumbent. BT Group is the United Kingdom's largest fixed and mobile telecommunications operator, serving nearly 31 million customer connections across four customer-facing units
2.
Four operating units. Openreach, Consumer, Business and International are the reporting units, with International reclassified as a discontinued operation after the June 2026 Verizon agreement
3.
How Openreach earns. Openreach rents regulated wholesale access to over 700 communications providers over the country's largest owned fixed access network of ducts, poles and fibre
4.
How Consumer earns. Consumer connects 13.1 million United Kingdom households through the BT, EE and Plusnet brands, with 8.2 million broadband lines and 15.7 million mobile connections
5.
How Business earns. BT Business supplies nearly one million private and public sector organisations with connectivity, managed networks, voice and security services
6.
Revenue scale. FY26 adjusted revenue was £19,646m, down 3.55% year on year, and reported revenue was £19,654m, down 3% from £20,360m in FY25
7.
Profit scale. FY26 adjusted earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation was £8,230m, against £8,209m in FY25 and guidance of £8,200m to £8,300m
8.
Operating profit. FY26 adjusted operating profit rose 2.4% to £3,355m, and reported profit before tax grew 8% to £1.44bn on an adjusted effective tax rate of 22.1%
9.
Earnings per share. Adjusted earnings per share was 18.3p in FY26, down 3%, against 18.8p in FY25 and 18.1p in FY24; reported basic earnings per share was 11.0p
10.
Cash generation. Normalised free cash flow was £1,508m in FY26, down 6% from £1,598m, after cash capital expenditure of £5,127m at a 26.3% capital intensity
11.
Balance sheet. Net debt including leases was £20.0bn at 31 March 2026, net financial debt excluding leases £15.78bn, and net leverage roughly 2.4x earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation
12.
Pension. The accounting pension deficit rose to £4.2bn from £4.1bn, with £790m of top-up contributions paid in FY26 and £780m a year committed through FY30
13.
Segment mix, Openreach. Openreach revenue grew 1% to £6,190m in FY26, and its Q1 FY27 division margin on earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation was 69.4%
14.
Segment mix, Consumer. Consumer revenue fell 2% to £9,494m in FY26, with broadband average revenue per user down 1% to £41.70 and postpaid mobile down 1% to £19.30
15.
Segment mix, Business. Business revenue fell 2% to £5,257m with divisional earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation of £1,266m, down 5% year on year
16.
Dividend. The FY26 dividend rose 2% to 8.32p a share, with policy set at low-to-mid single-digit growth until leverage reaches metrics consistent with a BBB-plus credit rating
17.
Workforce. Total labour resource fell 7% to 108,000 in FY26, down from 130,000 in FY23, with a 75,000 to 80,000 target for FY30
18.
Ownership. Bharti Global holds 24.95% and Deutsche Telekom 12.06%, together with three non-independent board seats, and the government opposes Bharti moving above 24.95%
19.
Geographic concentration. Roughly 89.2% of FY25 adjusted revenue was sterling-based, leaving only 10.8% exposed to non-sterling currencies before the International carve-out
20.
Investment style implication. The profile is an event-driven, post-peak-capital-expenditure cash-inflection incumbent, not a compounder and not a cyclical, with the event being a capital intensity step-down
