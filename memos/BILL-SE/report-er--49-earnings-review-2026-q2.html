Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. European margin pressure remained the central worry for investors as pricing declines and overcapacity plagued the region.
  2. North American operations offered a strong counterweight with solid demand and strategic plans to transition toward packaging.
  3. Sell-side models heavily relied on self-help measures including a massive headcount reduction to offset persistent input cost inflation.
  4. Nordic pulpwood cost deflation was expected to provide relief, but rising chemical and geopolitical logistics costs threatened to offset these gains.
1.
Established industrial producer: Billerud operates as a specialized European packaging and US graphic paper manufacturer created through the landmark acquisition of Korsnäs in 2012. The corporate footprint spans nine production units located across Sweden, Finland, and the United States. Wood supply and raw material procurement are managed locally to support these global operations. Europe represents the largest customer market for the group.
2.
Strategic geographic expansion: Billerud expanded its international footprint significantly by acquiring US- based graphic paper assets in 2022. The acquisition of Verso added the Escanaba and Quinnesec mills in the Midwest region. Management plans to slowly transition some of this North American capacity to packaging board production over the next decade. Domestically produced paper in the US offers better supply chain security to regional customers.
3.
High product exposure: Billerud maintains a unique product mix compared to Nordic forest peers who own massive timberlands. Competitors like Svenska Cellulosa AB and Stora Enso benefit from high wood selfsufficiency. Billerud lacks backward integration in forestry and power. The lack of forest assets makes the group sensitive to rising wood costs in Scandinavia.
4.
Severe margin contraction: Billerud experienced substantial financial volatility over the last three years due to extreme cost inflation. Net sales declined from SEK 41,224 million in 2023 to SEK 40,488 million in 2025. Group EBITDA margin fell from 12% in 2024 to 10% in 2025 as wood costs surged. Elevated wood raw material costs in Sweden slowed down the recovery trajectory.
5.
Challenging peer environment: Packaging peers experienced similar margin pressure throughout the commodity down-cycle. Stora Enso struggled with high Scandinavian wood costs that depressed industrial margins. Overcapacity across the European paperboard market prevented sustained price increases for regional producers. Industry profitability remained low as competitors added new capacity.
6.
Depressed equity valuation: Billerud shares suffered a persistent downward trajectory leading up to the Q2 2026 earnings release. The stock price declined by 7.4% during 2025. Equity values fell as absolute performance lagged the broader Stockholm exchange. Industrial material markets faced a downturn of 13% in the sector index.

Lagging relative performance: Billerud shares underperformed the broader Swedish market over the twelve months prior to the results.
