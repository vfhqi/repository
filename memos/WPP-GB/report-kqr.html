EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The pricing dynamics of generative artificial intelligence and offshoring savings will determine whether lagging advertising agencies and research groups can close their substantial margin gaps, with the critical question being how much cost reduction reaches operating margins versus being clawed back by clients through lower fees during contract renewals and re-pitches. The industry margin landscape reveals a highly polarized structure, with Publicis reporting a sector-leading 18.2% operating margin in 2025 while WPP delivered only 13.0%, Havas reported 12.9%, and Ipsos recorded 12.3% for the same period. Traditional time-and-materials billing remains highly dominant, with hourly fees still accounting for approximately 75% of WPP's group net sales as of February 2026, exposing the agency to automation-led revenue contraction. However, WPP is actively transitioning away from legacy models, reporting that performance-linked contracts already represent 20% to 30% of its net sales in its first quarter 2026 trading update.

Client procurement teams are successfully demanding direct fee reductions, with WPP Chief Executive Officer Mark Read acknowledging in August 2026 that clients expect the agency to pass efficiency gains directly back to them. Sophisticated clients are aggressively auditing agency workflows to identify automated processes and refusing to pay legacy creative rates for work performed by artificial intelligence. The traditional offshore labor arbitrage model is losing its defensive moat as the industry transitions from geo-arbitrage to intelligence arbitrage, with the lowest-cost labor now effectively being a graphics processing unit rather than low-wage offshore employees.

WPP's Elevate28 program targeting £500 million in gross savings faces severe execution challenges, with Deutsche Bank reporting in August 2026 that the initial £100 million of in-year 2026 savings are being entirely reinvested into technology and staff incentives rather than flowing through to operating margins. Management confirmed that cost savings are heavily back-loaded to the second half of the year but are offset by rebuilding staff incentives and ongoing WPP Open platform investments, with WPP expecting its second-half operating margin to decline by 200 basis points compared to the prior period.

Havas represents a strong margin catch-up opportunity if it successfully implements its offshoring plan, with Bernstein reporting in March 2026 that Havas maintains a significantly lower proportion of staff in low-cost regions compared to peers and intends to unlock significant margin gains through its new Centers of Excellence strategy. Morgan Stanley expects Havas to reach the top end of its mid-term guidance at 15% margin by 2028, representing substantial expansion from its 2025 baseline of 12.9%.

Deutsche Bank reported that Havas successfully grew its organic business in 2025 while underlying headcount decreased by 1.2%, utilizing artificial intelligence to continuously improve operational productivity without increasing employee expenses. Publicis continues to outperform lagging peers through its highly integrated offshore and data delivery networks including Epsilon and Sapient, maintaining an industry-leading 18.2% operating margin and winning massive consolidated client contracts based on business outcomes rather than aggressive price discounting.

Ipsos operates on a largely fixed-cost infrastructure that translates into substantial operating leverage, aiming to expand its operating margin from 12.3% in 2025 to 13.5% by 2028 through Globally Managed Services expansion and operational productivity gains from artificial intelligence. & Co Redburn & Co Redburn Bernstein Research models that if agencies successfully retain automation savings, a net cumulative 5% staff efficiency gain would yield 3 percentage points of margin accretion for WPP, lifting its operating margin to 18% from its 2024 baseline, representing an underappreciated catalyst for substantial margin expansion.

However, major advertisers including Diageo and Unilever are already demonstrating procurement leverage by utilizing AIdriven content production efficiencies to demand lower fees, with Diageo successfully reducing its non-working media spending from 21% to 14% and Unilever reducing its reliance on external agencies through automated content localization. Industry bodies are sounding urgent warnings, with the ad agency body VoxComm advising agencies to abandon legacy hourly billing models because artificial intelligence reduces revenue by cutting billable hours, meaning agencies are penalized for operational efficiency under traditional time-andmaterials structures.

The evidence demonstrates that structural margin trajectories for WPP, Havas, and Ipsos depend critically on their ability to defend pricing power during the transition from time-based to outcome-based contracts, successfully execute offshore labor arbitrage strategies before intelligence arbitrage commoditizes these savings, and prevent sophisticated procurement teams from capturing the full value of generative artificial intelligence productivity gains through contractual clawbacks and aggressive re-pitch negotiations.

Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The pricing dynamics of automation and offshoring will decide if lagging agencies can close their wide margin gaps.

The Structural Margin Gap Challenge

1.
Critical investigation: "When advertising agencies and research groups cut staff costs through generative artificial intelligence and offshore delivery, how much of the saving reaches their operating margin and how much do clients take back in lower fees? For WPP, Havas, Publicis and Ipsos since 2025, what share of revenue is still billed on time-based fees, how have fee rates per hour or per deliverable moved at renewals and re-pitches, do clients now demand a share of productivity gains at renewal, and how much of WPP's Elevate28 savings of 500 million pounds and of Havas's offshoring plan do brokers expect to reach the margin rather than be reinvested or passed to clients?"
2.
Stark profitability disparities: The industry margin landscape is highly polarized because Publicis reported a sector-leading operating margin of 18.2% in 2025. WPP delivered a lower 13.0% margin in 2025. Havas reported a 12.9% margin while Ipsos recorded 12.3% for the same period. Resolving the Key Question determines whether these lagging peers can close their substantial margin gap to Publicis.
3.
Critical catch-up trajectories: Bernstein argues that Havas represents a pure margin catch-up story if it successfully expands profitability through offshoring. Furthermore, the broker notes that other agencies like WPP are heavily restructuring to stabilize their operations. These strategic dynamics mean that retaining cost-efficiency benefits remains the main driver of earnings recovery across the sector.

Automation Savings Versus Client Clawbacks

4.
Threatened billing structures: Morgan Stanley highlights that traditional headcount-based models penalize agencies for operational efficiency because automated processes reduce billable hours. This means client demands for lower fees could strip away potential automation gains unless agencies rapidly reform pricing. Consequently, the inability to retain these savings directly threatens future earnings per share.
5.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reports that Havas has historically managed to provide more deliverables for the same price due to productivity gains and does not see client pricing pressure being exacerbated by artificial intelligence. This indicates that clients are not currently capturing all automation savings but are instead accepting increased volume. This stability suggests that agencies might successfully retain early productivity benefits over the short term.
6.
Evolving billing dynamics: Rothschild & Co Redburn observes that agencies are actively pursuing a shift towards outcomes-based pricing to better align incentives with clients. This shift has been long discussed in the industry but artificial intelligence has added a greater sense of urgency. Adopting these models allows agencies to retain more of their automation benefits rather than passing them on to clients.
7.
Differentiation in restructuring: WPP is currently attempting to deliver 500 million pounds in gross savings under its Elevate28 program. Deutsche Bank notes that WPP intends to reinvest its early 100 million pounds of inyear 2026 savings entirely into growth engines and artificial intelligence. Unless these investments translate into organic expansion, the broker warns that reinvested savings will fail to reach the bottom-line operating margin.

Offshoring and Strategic Stakes

8.
Strategic labor optimization: Bernstein emphasizes that offshoring remains a powerful lever because salary differentials across regions are highly material. Both WPP and Publicis maintain about 50% of their total headcount in lower-cost regions to protect profitability. Retaining these labor arbitrage savings is vital for closing peer margin gaps over the next three years.
9.
Other side of the story: Bernstein cautions that WPP has experienced severe negative operating leverage from contract losses which offsets its structural cost advantages. Furthermore, a former WPP Growth Lead warns that artificial intelligence will inevitably reduce agency headcount and trigger pricing pressure from clients. This means that client cost clawbacks could ultimately overwhelm any gross efficiency gains achieved from restructuring programs.

Section 2 – Billing Models and the Resilience of Time-Based Fees

Section summary
  1. Time-based billing models remain highly resilient but are gradually giving way to performance structures.
  2. Major agencies are actively introducing alternative outcome and tech-licensing fees to defend their margins.
  3. Long-term pricing erosion continues to threaten legacy creative fee rates during competitive re-pitches.

Prevalence of Legacy FTE Billing

1.
Resilient traditional billing: WPP's traditional billing models remain highly dominant because time-and-materials contracts still represent the majority of its business. Morgan Stanley reports in February 2026 that high concentration of hourly fees still accounts for 75% of group net sales. Although management is guiding this ratio downward, the high concentration of hourly fees exposes the agency to automation-led revenue contraction. This structural rigidity means WPP must quickly transition to alternative pricing to protect its long-term operating margins and EPS growth.
2.
Transitioning billing structures: Deutsche Bank highlights that WPP is presenting a robust defense of its ability to adapt to non-time-based pricing structures. The broker noted in February 2026 that WPP is successfully moving toward outcome-based and tech-licensing fee models. This transition is critical because traditional time-and-materials billing currently covers three-quarters of group revenue. Consequently, transitioning away from hours-based work is crucial to protect WPP's margins from artificial intelligence productivity headwinds.
3.
Accelerating performance pricing: WPP is actively building operational capabilities to support alternative contract designs as client pricing preferences evolve. In its first quarter 2026 trading update, WPP reported that performance-linked contracts already represent 20% to 30% of its net sales. The company is also introducing tech licensing fees via WPP Open Pro to diversify away from headcount billing. This structural evolution will determine whether WPP can sustain stable margins as automation tools reduce required labor hours.
4.
Defending revenue streams: Havas protects its profitability by anchoring its media and creative services in resilient contractual arrangements. In its 2025 annual report, Havas stated that its margin expansion was supported by its stable "cost plus" model for advertising. Additionally, its media services are successfully shifting from traditional commissions to retainer and outcome-based fees. If this transition continues, Havas will be highly insulated from headcount-based revenue deflation, supporting positive EPS growth.
5.
Moderate pricing resilience: Havas continues to navigate long-standing client fee pressures without facing immediate escalation from generative artificial intelligence tools. Morgan Stanley reports in February 2026 that Havas has historically managed pricing pressure by delivering more deliverables for the same fee. Furthermore, the broker notes that major pricing reductions are concentrated in the production business rather than core creative. Unless automation accelerates client fee demands, Havas's existing billing structures should sustain its medium-term operating margin targets.

Transition to Alternative Pricing Models

6.
Emerging outcome contracts: Publicis maintains a highly resilient revenue profile because its exposure to pure software and outcome-based pricing remains extremely limited. Deutsche Bank reports in July 2026 that pure SaaS and outcome-driven contracts represent less than 1% of Publicis's total revenue. However, the broker notes that about 10% of its conventional headcountbased work is now structured around KPIs and outcomes. This disciplined pricing architecture helps Publicis protect its sector-leading margins from the immediate threat of AI-driven hours deflation.
7.
Controlled pricing evolution: Publicis is carefully managing its transition toward performance-linked fees to ensure that new models do not dilute its operating margin. Morgan Stanley reported in April 2026 that approximately 10% of Publicis's total fees are linked to outcomes. The agency also avoids participating in aggressive pitches that do not support long-term business value. This defensive pricing strategy should allow Publicis to protect its profitability and sustain its forecasted EPS trajectory.
8.
Offsetting price deflation: Ipsos is actively combating structural pricing deflation in the market research sector by scaling its high-margin automated platforms. Bernstein reported in May 2026 that Ipsos Digital generated 140 million euros in 2025 revenue, representing strong 27% growth. Management expects that falling average prices for market research will be entirely offset by expanding volumes. This volume- driven strategy is critical for Ipsos to meet its mid-term margin target of 13.5% between 2026 and 2028.
9.
Accelerated digital execution: Ipsos's leadership transition is designed to accelerate the monetization of its AI- enabled and globally managed research offerings. Bernstein reported in September 2026 that new CEO Kelly Beaver plans to speed up the Horizons plan. This strategy focuses on scaling Globally Managed Services and driving broader adoption of the Ipsos Digital platform. Accelerating this execution is vital to offset competitive pressures from agile AI-driven market research startups.
10.
Severe client fee pressure: Clients are increasingly aware of artificial intelligence capabilities and are refusing to pay legacy rates for automated deliverables. A former WPP Growth Lead expert explained in June 2026 that clients will inevitably demand lower pricing as they adopt AI tools themselves. The expert notes that there is no scenario where agencies cannot maintain their historical creative rates for AI-assisted projects. This shift will degrade agency topline growth unless firms aggressively restructure their standard billing contracts.

Pricing Deflation and Competitive Dynamics

11.
Secular fee erosion: The continuous expansion of project deliverables is failing to prevent a long-term decline in overall agency compensation. A Senior VP at Havas Group noted in March 2026 that agencies are delivering substantially more assets while overall client fees contract. The expert highlighted a general secular erosion of fee structures as individual employees become ten times more productive. This structural mismatch will depress operating margins unless agencies quickly move away from time-based pricing.
12.
Prevailing fixed fees: Despite growing interest in outcome-based compensation, traditional pricing models will remain the primary method for structuring agency contracts. A Senior Director at Sanofi stated in March 2026 that while outcome-based billing is growing in media, fixed fees will continue to hold the lion's share. The expert expects AI to place severe pricing pressure on basic content production, forcing a reallocation of budgets. This persistent reliance on fixed fees means that agencies face significant margin risks if they fail to control production costs.
13.
Stubborn hours billing: Agency holding groups remain structurally restricted by their legacy reliance on billing hours rather than delivering measurable business value. A Head of Experience and Integration Strategy at WPP stated in May 2026 that holdcos are still primarily selling deliverables and hours-based work. The expert emphasized that none of the major networks have achieved a true competitive advantage in outcome-based billing. This lack of structural differentiation leaves agencies highly vulnerable to client procurement heads demanding direct share of productivity gains.
14.
Contrary evidence: Havas maintains that client pricing pressure has not been exacerbated by generative artificial intelligence. Morgan Stanley reported in February 2026 that Havas's management has historically managed fee pressures through productivity gains without seeing any pricing pressure has not been exacerbated by AI tools. This suggests that agencies may successfully retain early productivity gains under existing contract structures. If clients continue to accept more deliverables for the same fee, agency margins could expand significantly as labor costs fall.
15.
Other side of the story: Publicis is leveraging its superior data and integration capabilities to aggressively discount creative services and capture massive media accounts from peers. Former Publicis executives revealed in March 2026 that the agency frequently offers creative work for free during pitches to secure high-margin media business. This aggressive pricing tactic helped Publicis win the massive Mars media account from WPP in June 2025. While this strategy depresses creative margins, it successfully drives market share gains and supports Publicis's premium valuation relative to peer holdings.

Section 3 – Client Clawbacks and Productivity Sharing Demands

Section summary
  1. Client procurement teams are successfully demanding direct fee reductions and structural transparency as agencies deploy generative artificial intelligence.
  2. Intelligence arbitrage is rapidly replacing

traditional geographical labor arbitrage, compressing traditional agency hours-based contract values.

Client Demands for Productivity Sharing

1.
Demanding fee concessions: Global clients are increasingly inserting explicit clauses into contract renewals to capture agency automation gains. WPP's Chief Executive Officer Mark Read acknowledged in August 2026 that clients expect the agency to pass efficiency gains directly back to them. Unless WPP can persuade clients to reinvest these savings into broader brand transformation, this procurement behavior will permanently impair fee structures and drag on mid-term earnings per share.
2.
Heightened contract oversight: Procurement departments are aggressively auditing agency workflows to identify automated processes and strip out human hours. A former WPP Growth Lead explained in June 2026 that sophisticated clients refuse to pay legacy creative rates for work done by artificial intelligence. This insistence on transparency means agencies cannot easily retain productivity gains, forcing a rapid shift away from standard hourly fee arrangements.
3.
Restricted content contracts: Clients are writing strict guidelines into service agreements regarding the use and ownership of machine-generated outputs. Naavik's specialist research in October 2026 reports that corporate clients are increasingly demanding full disclosure and even imposing outright bans on AI-generated content. This contractual tightening limits the agency's ability to deploy low-cost automated workforces without facing severe fee clawbacks.
4.
Contrary evidence: Havas argues that client pricing pressure remains stable and has not been worsened by the introduction of generative tools. Morgan Stanley reported in February 2026 that Havas has historically managed fee pressures by delivering more assets for clients without lowering overall retainer fees. This stability indicates that some agencies are successfully avoiding direct clawbacks by increasing delivery volume rather than cutting prices.
5.
Declining asset valuations: Agencies are producing higher volumes of creative assets while facing continuous fee erosion from client procurement teams. A Senior VP at Havas Group noted in March 2026 that employees are becoming ten times more productive, but this efficiency is driving general secular erosion of fee structures rather than higher margins. Unless agencies restructure their billing models, delivering more deliverables for the same fee will fail to offset structural margin contraction.
6.
Outcome billing resistance: While agencies attempt to shift from hours to outcome-based contracts, clients are leveraging AI to demand lower base retainers. A Senior Director at Sanofi stated in March 2026 that AI will place severe pricing pressure on basic content production, though fixed fees will continue to hold the majority of contract values. This persistent client preference for fixed fees limits the agency's ability to capture the upside of automated productivity gains.

Geo-Arbitrage Versus Intelligence Arbitrage

7.
Shifting cost structures: The traditional model of low-cost offshore delivery centers is losing its defensive moat as virtual agents emerge. Jefferies highlights in September 2026 that the lowest-cost labor is now effectively a graphics processing unit, initiating a transition from geo-arbitrage to intelligence arbitrage. This shift breaks the historic link between agency headcount and revenue, exposing labor-heavy agencies to significant structural margin compression.
8.
Eroding location advantage: Specialized offshore translation and localization services are facing direct cannibalization from highly capable foundational models. Jefferies notes that automated localization tools can deliver up to 90% time reductions and 25% cost savings for complex project analyses. This technological deflation means that traditional offshore delivery models can no longer protect margins, forcing a complete renegotiation of multi-year outsourcing contracts.
9.
Shrinking contract values: Client demands for automated workflows are directly cannibalizing existing consulting and business process outsourcing fees. A Director Sales at Wipro explained in October 2026 that clients routinely demand to reduce a 100 million dollar contract to 90 million dollars by embedding generative automation. This structural deflation forces providers to rapidly scale new high-value services to offset the direct loss of billable hours.
10.
Contrasting headcount reduction: WPP and Havas are managing headcount adjustments differently as they navigate automation and client cost pressures. WPP's organic headcount fell by 8.7% in 2025 to 98,655 employees as part of its structural consolidation program. In contrast, Havas reported a smaller organic headcount decrease of 1% in 2025 while growing its underlying business. This peer divergence suggests that WPP is restructuring more aggressively to protect its lower operating margins from client fee clawbacks.
11.
Defensive margin strategies: To defend against client clawbacks, lagging agencies are forcing cost-cutting measures to protect their operating profitability. WPP is targeting 500 million pounds in savings under Elevate28 to support its mid-term margin recovery. This cost program is critical because WPP's 13.0% operating margin in 2025 lagged far behind Publicis's sector-leading 18.2% margin. If client procurement teams successfully claw back WPP's automation savings, the agency will fail to close this substantial profitability gap.

Historical Parallels of Cost Clawbacks

12.
Historic transparency disputes: Past procurement battles over agency rebate models provide a cautionary precedent for how clients will claw back automation gains. Public court records from the WPP GroupM whistleblower case in February 2026 revealed that the agency generated substantial non-product related income from hidden rebates and proprietary inventory deals. This history shows that clients will eventually detect hidden margin cushions and demand strict financial audits.
13.
Leveraging transparency data: Clients will use automated auditing tools to monitor agency performance just as they did during previous programmatic media disputes. The GroupM whistleblowing disclosures showed that Google, the top client with 2.3 billion dollars in US billings, had 99.5% of its proprietary inventory go entirely unused. This massive underutilization underscores how procurement teams will use data to claw back unearned agency fees and enforce strict performance-linked billing.
14.
118 side of the story: Publicis demonstrates that agencies can defend their margins against procurement pressure by offering highly integrated data and media solutions. Former Publicis executives noted in March 2026 that the agency frequently offers creative services for free during pitches to lock in high-margin media and CRM business. This integrated approach, supported by Epsilon and Sapient, allows Publicis to maintain a sector-leading 18.2% operating margin, showing that scale and technological depth can completely offset client clawbacks.

Section 4 – Offshoring and Restructuring: Elevate28 and Havas's Plans

Section summary
  1. Reinvestment and operational inflation will consume the vast majority of WPP's restructuring savings.
  2. Havas is positioned to capture a material margin catch-up opportunity through its offshore centers.
  3. Publicis and Ipsos demonstrate that superior delivery platforms protect margins from client price clawbacks.

WPP's Elevate28 and Reinvestment Drag

1.
Reinvested cost efficiencies: Deutsche Bank reported in August 2026 that WPP remains on track to deliver £100 million of in-year savings from its Elevate28 program. However, the broker notes that these short-term gains are being entirely reinvested into technology and the pool for staff incentives. This structural reinvestment means that the initial efficiencies will fail to expand operating margins or boost earnings per share in the current fiscal year.
2.
Restructuring margin pressure: WPP confirmed in its half-year 2026 report that the group's full-year headline operating profit margin is expected to land between 12% and 13%. Management explained that cost savings from the Elevate28 initiative are heavily skewed to the second half of the year. But this back-loaded benefit is offset by rebuilding staff incentives and ongoing investments in the WPP Open platform. Consequently, WPP expects its second-half operating margin to decline by 200 basis points compared to the prior period.
3.
Gradual margin rebuilding: Morgan Stanley projected in February 2026 that WPP will struggle to expand its profitability due to persistent revenue contraction. The broker expects WPP's adjusted operating margin to reach approximately 14% by 2028 as internal efficiencies offset declining operating leverage. This gradual expansion depends on WPP's ability to limit reinvestment in artificial intelligence while successfully rationalizing its global headcount and back-office structures. Failing to execute these structural cost reductions would put the company's long-term EPS trajectory at significant risk.

Havas's Offshoring and Centers of Excellence

4.
Untapped offshoring potential: Bernstein reported in March 2026 that Havas represents a strong margin catch-up story if it successfully implements its offshoring plan. The broker noted that Havas maintains a significantly lower proportion of staff in low-cost regions compared to market-leading peers like Publicis. By deploying its new Centers of Excellence strategy, Havas intends to unlock significant margin gains through offshore labor arbitrage. This labor cost optimization remains the primary mechanism for Havas to expand its EPS and narrow its structural profitability gap.
5.
Expanding operating margins: Morgan Stanley reported in February 2026 that Havas has guided for its 2026 adjusted EBIT margin to rise to 13.2% to 13.5%. The broker expects Havas to reach the top end of its midterm guidance at 15% margin by 2028. This margin expansion is supported by business integration, cross-selling, and streamlining lower-margin country operations. If Havas achieves this rapid operational recovery, it will drive high-single-digit compound annual growth in adjusted earnings.
6.
Productivity-led headcount reductions: Deutsche Bank reported in February 2026 that Havas successfully grew its organic business in 2025 while its underlying headcount decreased by 1.2%. The broker notes that Havas is utilizing artificial intelligence to continuously improve operational productivity without increasing employee expenses. If Havas maintains this operational discipline, it will preserve its margin expansion trajectory from client cost pressures.
7.
Contrary evidence: Rothschild & Co Redburn cautioned in September 2026 that WPP faces persistent negative operating leverage as revenues contract. The broker estimates that WPP's operating margin will decline to 12.8% in 2026 despite the execution of its Elevate28 cost savings program. This underperformance shows that structural revenue declines and increased investment in talent and incentives are completely absorbing restructuring benefits. Unless organic growth recovers, the cash costs of restructuring will continue to dilute WPP's long-term earnings per share.

Comparative Peer Margin Trajectories

8.
Premium platform execution: Publicis continues to outperform lagging peers due to its highly integrated offshore and data delivery networks. Rothschild & Co Redburn reported in October 2026 that Publicis maintains an industry-leading operating margin of 18.2% through assets like Epsilon and Sapient. This integrated platform allows Publicis to win massive consolidated client contracts based on business outcomes rather than aggressive price discounting. Consequently, Publicis's superior structural model insulates its premium valuation and protects its EPS from technology-driven deflation.

Structured operating leverage: Ipsos reported in its 2026 interim results that its high-margin DIY platform Ipsos Digital continues to generate double-digit growth. Bernstein explained in July 2026 that Ipsos operates on a largely fixed-cost infrastructure, which translates into substantial operating leverage. The company aims to expand its operating margin from 12.3% in 2025 to 13.5% by 2028 through GMS expansion and operational productivity gains from artificial intelligence. This digital transition protects Ipsos's profitability from market research pricing pressures, supporting its long-term EPS trajectory.
10.
Other side of the story: Former media executives note that structural reorganizations can degrade agency culture and lead to severe client churn. Bernstein warns that WPP's frequent structural transformations and headcount reductions have eroded its competitive position, leading to massive net account losses. These persistent losses reduce WPP's operating leverage and prevent cost savings from flowing through to the bottom line. Consequently, unless lagging agencies stabilize their revenue bases, aggressive cost-cutting and offshoring plans will fail to expand operating margins or prevent earnings dilution.

Section 6 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Shifting pricing power to clients through direct AI clawbacks could decimate WPP's and Havas's margin recovery.

Financial Impact of Alternative Scenarios

1.
Severe margin compression: If clients successfully claw back offshore savings, agency margins will contract sharply. Deutsche Bank notes that WPP's Elevate28 cost savings of £100 million are being entirely reinvested, meaning any additional client clawbacks of offshore savings would directly depress operating margins. If client procurement teams claw back the expected efficiencies from offshore delivery, WPP's Headline operating margin will fail to expand toward target. This downside scenario would severely dilute earnings per share over the next three years.
2.
Significant margin accretion: Conversely, if agencies retain 100% of artificial intelligence productivity gains, the bottom-line expansion will be massive. The broker notes that while agencies are staff-intense businesses, a decoupling of staff costs and revenues is an underappreciated catalyst for margin expansion. Early Indicators of Pricing Power Shifts

7.
Demands for alternative billing: Industry bodies are already sounding the alarm on the financial viability of legacy pricing structures under AI. Morgan Stanley reports that the ad agency body VoxComm has urgently advised agencies to abandon legacy hourly billing models because AI reduces revenue by cutting billable hours. Under the traditional time and materials model, agencies are penalised for operational efficiency. This structural lag suggests that pricing power is shifting toward clients during competitive renewals.
8.
Adtech pricing pressure: Early signs from adtech negotiations demonstrate that clients are successfully extracting severe pricing concessions from major agencies. Rothschild & Co Redburn reports that Publicis audited its relationship with The Trade Desk and temporarily ended its decade-long association in March 2026, eventually signing a new agreement that was underpinned by heavy discounts. This disruption indicates that even market leaders face intense pressure to lower their transactional fees. If such discounting behavior spreads, it will compress media margins across the entire holding group peer network.
9.
Deflationary volume offsets: In the market research sector, technology-driven price deflation could be successfully countered by expanding operational volume. Bernstein Research reports that Ipsos is actively leveraging its high-margin automated platform, Ipsos Digital, which generated €140 million in 2025. While automated research tools lead to falling average market research prices, Ipsos expects this deflation to be entirely offset by expanding project volumes. If this volume growth materializes, it will support Ipsos's mid-term target of expanding its operating margin to 13.5% by 2028.
10.
Accelerating offshore margins: If Havas successfully executes its offshoring plan, the margin expansion could exceed current market expectations. Bernstein Research reports that Havas is currently underexposed to offshoring compared to its peers, with only 34% of staff in low-cost regions. Seizing this offshoring opportunity via its Centers of Excellence could unlock significant margin gains through labor arbitrage. This suggests that Havas can narrow its structural profitability gap to Publicis and drive high-single-digit compound annual growth in adjusted earnings.

Defensive Scale Strategies

11.
Other side of the story: Despite intense procurement pressure, scale and proprietary data assets may allow agencies to maintain their pricing power. Morgan Stanley argues that a self-serve platform model is completely unsuitable for complex global corporate campaigns, ensuring that large advertisers remain dependent on holding groups. This structural moat means that large agencies can leverage their deep data assets to protect fees. Consequently, market leaders like Publicis are well positioned to defend their premium valuations and protect their EPS from technology-driven deflation.
