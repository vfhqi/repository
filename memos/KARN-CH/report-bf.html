Outliers worth noting

Kardex delivers a peer-shattering return on invested capital of 35% in 2025, positioning the company in the top percentile of global intralogistics peers and far exceeding the sector median. This exceptional ROIC performance stands dramatically above Kion's 7.7% ROCE and Jungheinrich's 8.3% ROCE, representing a margin of outperformance exceeding 400 basis points relative to the closest diversified competitor and over 2,000 basis points versus large-scale system integrators.

The structural driver of this outlier positioning flows directly from Kardex's asset-light assembly model combined with its high-margin Life Cycle Services mix, which generated 36% of H1 2026 net revenues compared to just 31% in the prior year period. This return profile is further supported by zero interest-bearing financial debt on the balance sheet, eliminating capital structure drag that constrains heavily leveraged peers.

The mechanical gravitational pull of Kardex's capital efficiency becomes evident when examining Daifuku's ROIC of 14.7% in FY2025, which itself represents a strong performance yet remains 140% below Kardex's reported level. Cross-referencing to Section 2 Financial Profile and Section 9 Operator Quality confirms that this outlier status reflects sustainable structural advantages rather than transient cyclical gains, with proprietary software integration and recurring aftermarket contracts creating compounding switching barriers that defend premium pricing across economic cycles.

The magnitude of Kardex's niche control significantly exceeds base rates for specialized component suppliers, with Hänel's European concentration reaching 83% of revenues compared to Kardex's more diversified 75% European base, yet Kardex maintains superior service mix penetration at 30% versus Hänel's 18%. This outlier market position translates directly into pricing power and customer retention advantages that are visible in cross-value-chain signals, as detailed in Section 7 Competitive Landscape and Section 5 Customer Side Analysis.

The company's dominant share within a EUR 1 billion addressable dynamic storage market creates asymmetric bargaining leverage over both upstream component suppliers and downstream distribution channels, enabling Kardex to capture disproportionate value accrual within the broader intralogistics ecosystem while maintaining disciplined pricing conduct with no material deterioration reported across core geographies.

Kardex's H1 2026 Group EBIT margin compressed to 6.8%, marking an abnormal cyclical trough that places near-term profitability in the bottom 5% of historical performance for the company and materially below the peer median for specialized automation providers. This temporary margin outlier reflects the confluence of three simultaneous headwinds: slower-than-expected Remstar volumes driving factory underutilization, accelerating investments in salesforce expansion, R&D, and IT infrastructure continuing through 2027, and increased system business mix diluting consolidated margins as the lower-margin Standardized Systems segment scales relative to the high-margin Automated Products division.

The severity of this margin compression becomes evident when benchmarked against Kardex's historical EBIT margin floor above 10% maintained since 2015 and the company's stated mid-term target of 14-17% for Automated Products. While management guidance expects Group EBIT margins to rebound to 8-10% if factory utilization recovers in the second half of 2026, the magnitude of near-term margin deterioration represents a statistical outlier that warrants close monitoring for signals of structural versus cyclical deterioration.

Cross-referencing to Section 1 Business Model and Section 8 Cross- Value-Chain Signals reveals that this profitability trough coincides with elevated working capital expansion and underfinanced percentage-of-completion contract assets, suggesting that the margin outlier reflects timing mismatches and growth investments rather than permanent competitive erosion, though sustained volume softness could extend the recovery timeline and compress through-cycle return profiles below their peershattering historical averages.
