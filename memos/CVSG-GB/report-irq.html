What the call should settle, and why

The Investment Analyst's case in one line: the earnings are predictable because the growth is bought and divided over fewer shares; the share price is not, because the re-rating needs a named chief executive and a 3%-plus UK organic print. CVS is a UK (about 89% of revenue) and Australian (about 11%) veterinary consolidator: over 400 UK practices, 61 Australian sites, three reference laboratories and the online pharmacy Animed Direct. About 60% of FY2027 EPS growth is the share count already bought back; most of the rest is acquired EBITDA already owned. The UK organic core grows only through price.

The call can test six judgements. The first three decide changes to guidance and consensus over the next four quarters. The last three decide what the stock is worth holding for beyond that.

  • Judgement 1, the R&D credit in FY2027. FY2026's credit of £15.7m included a one-off release of £6.6m; only £2.9m of that provision remains. We carry £12.5m for FY2027. The memo says no covering broker in our files has written about the release. This is the main reason we are 1% below consensus EBITDA and about 1.7p below consensus EPS. A full repeat would add about 7p; loss of the regime would cost about 13p. £1m of EBITDA is about 1.06p of EPS.
  • Judgement 2, the half-year phasing. We expect an H1 FY2027 margin flat at about 19.1% and an H2 margin down about half a point (20.2% against 20.7%), because the H2 FY2026 comparative held most of the release. Group LFL should print about 1.5% in November and January against 2.7% a year ago, even while UK practice LFL improves from 1.0% to about 1.4%. If the market reads those prints as deceleration, the shares fall on a business that is improving; that is the memo's "soft optics" risk.
  • Judgement 3, UK practice volume against price. In FY2026 price was about 4% to 5% and volume about (3)% to (4)%. We carry price 4.0% and volume (2.5)% for FY2027. 1 point of UK price is about £5.8m of EBITDA and 6.2p of EPS; 1 point of volume about £2.9m and 3.1p. A FY2025-style volume year, (4)%, takes FY2027 EPS to about 88.8p, (4.8)%.
  • Judgement 4, cash conversion: a timing dip or a new run-rate. Operating cash conversion fell to 70.6% from 76.9%; the company target is over 70%. If 70.6% is the new level, about £50m of acquisitions, about £30m of capital expenditure and the £38.3m left on the buyback cannot all be funded from £69.2m of free cash flow without leverage passing 2.0 times, which management has said it is willing to do temporarily.
  • Judgement 5, the chief executive and the reset. The memo puts the chance that the incoming chief executive resets the 4% to 8% LFL framework within a year of appointment at about 60%, and buyback renewal at about 55%. The appointment is the one undated event that could pull the entry window forward; a respected multi-site operator would move the case in a day, a caretaker would confirm it.
  • Judgement 6, deals and the out-years. Consensus FY2029 (112p) only holds if acquisitions continue; brokers either omit them or model them quietly. Our FY2029 EBITDA is 3.6% above consensus because we model about £50m a year. The share count is under-modelled: consensus implies about 68.5m shares against 66.3m once the buyback completes. Removing every unannounced deal and buyback puts FY2029 EPS at about 103p, (9)% against our 112.9p.

Order of play. Start with the AGM statement and the calendar (A1), then the mechanical questions where our numbers and the brokers' diverge: consensus, the R&D credit, the half-year phasing and the share count (A2, A3, A4, A7). Then LFL and the price and volume split (A5, B1.1, B1.2), then the Healthy Pet Club (B2.1, B2.2). Then cash and the funding of the plans (B3.3). Then Australia (B4.1), the chief executive process (B5.1) and capital returns and the Laboratories (B6.1, B6.2). Leave incentives and disclosure to the end.

Expect two limits on the answers. On the chief executive search, expect "the process is ongoing"; ask instead what stage it is at, whether the board's brief has changed, and what the new chief executive's first statement is likely to cover. On the R&D credit, expect "we don't guide on it"; ask it as an accounting question (how much was recognised in which half, and what the remaining provision is), which the Chief Financial Officer can answer.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the AGM statement to the FY2027 pre-close update in July 2027)

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. UK practice volume, price and the CMA remedies

B1.2 ★ Prescriptions and online medicines.
