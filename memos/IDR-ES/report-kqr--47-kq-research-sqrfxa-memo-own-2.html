Accounting under IAS 20 and IFRS 9

5.
Government loan recognition: Indra Sistemas received EUR 2.19 billion in January 2026 under several royal decrees regulating loans for the Special Modernisation Programmes. These advances are granted by the Spanish Ministry of Industry as repayable loans at a zero, fixed, and non-revisable interest rate. Under IFRS 9 and IAS 20, such interest-free government loans must be initially recognized at fair value based on prevailing market rates.
6.
Amortized cost accounting: French technology peer Kerlink SA reported in its 2021 financial statements that its zero-rate innovation loans were recognized initially at fair value and subsequently at amortized cost. Kerlink SA applied a discount rate of 3% to reflect the market-equivalent rate under IFRS 9. This practice indicates that defense and technology firms must impute an implicit interest benefit over the life of the loan.
7.
Systemic defense pre-financing: Spanish industry news reported on July 16, 2026, that the government has granted a total of EUR 14.22 billion in interest-free loans to defense companies. Within this funding package, programs involving Indra received EUR 7.94 billion, while Airbus and Navantia received EUR 4.03 billion and EUR 2.29 billion respectively. This widespread state support demonstrates that zero-interest loans are a standard financing tool for Spanish defense contractors.
8.
Precedent rate adjustments: European aerospace peer Airbus SE reported in its 2021 financial statements that it adjusted the carrying value of its Spanish and French A350 refundable advances using market-equivalent interest rates. Airbus SE utilized risk assessment benchmarks approved by the World Trade Organization to determine the appropriate discount rate. These accounting adjustments demonstrate how implicit interest benefits fluctuate with market rates and credit spreads.
