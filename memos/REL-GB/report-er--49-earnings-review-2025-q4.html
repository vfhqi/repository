Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

  1. The strategic transition from information databases to AI-driven workflow tools serves as the primary engine for RELX's accelerating organic growth profile.
  2. Reassuring execution across key verticals is complemented by unprecedented capital returns and targeted tactical acquisitions to consolidate
  3. Structural shifts in publishing models and emerging legal-tech competitors present ongoing medium-term friction points that require active product reinvestment.
1.
Retiring divisional executive: Long-time CEO of the Risk division Mark Kelsey announced his retirement after 14 years in the role and 43 years at the company. He will be succeeded by the current CEO of Legal Mike Walsh, who will take over leadership of both the Risk and Legal divisions, though the segments will remain operationally distinct.
2.
Targeted geographic expansion: RELX announced the strategic acquisition of France-based legal tech start-up Doctrine in April 2026 for a consideration estimated by Bernstein's Cherblanc at approximately £400m. This transaction enhances the company's civil-law primary content and workflow solutions.

Expanding operating margins: Group adjusted operating profit margins expanded by 0.9 percentage points to reach 34.8% for the full year 2025. Deutsche Bank's Steve Liechti projects group adjusted operating margins to expand further to 35.2% for fiscal year 2026.
5.
Accelerated capital return: Following the completion of its £1.5bn buyback program in 2025, RELX proposed an increased full-year dividend of 67.5p. For 2026, management upgraded its capital return ambitions, announcing a £2.25bn share buyback program.
6.
Accelerating legal execution: Underlying organic revenue growth in the Legal segment stepped up from 9% in FY25 to 10% in the first half of fiscal year 2026. This acceleration was driven by robust enterprise adoption of the generative-AI-enabled Lexis+ platform.
7.
Improving scientific momentum: Underlying organic revenue growth in Scientific, Technical & Medical accelerated from 5% in FY25 to 6% in the first half of 2026. This positive trend was supported by accelerated adoption of the LeapSpace AI virtual research assistant.
8.
Resilient risk core: The Risk segment maintained highly stable demand, delivering 8% year-to-date underlying revenue growth at the nine-month stage of fiscal 2026. This growth continues to be underpinned by machine-to-machine transaction volumes and digital fraud prevention tools.
9.

Expanding total addressable market: Management is actively shifting its focus from traditional legal research databases to higher-value transactional legal workflow and decision-support software. This strategic pivot expands the company's addressable US legal market opportunity from $4-5bn up to $20bn.

Leveraging internal AI: The company is aggressively deploying generative AI across its internal software development lifecycle. Deutsche Bank's Steve Liechti noted that RELX is using agentic AI to automate and customize websites across its global portfolio to capture significant productivity gains.
12.
Rising startup competition: AI-native legal platforms such as Harvey and Legora represent a growing competitive workflow threat in the legal services space. Legora intensified this pressure by launching a primary legal research product in August 2026.
13.
Clinical decision support rivals: Wolters Kluwer's UpToDate platform faces intensified competition from clinical AI startup OpenEvidence. This rising competitive platform has achieved rapid clinician adoption, potentially threatening the pricing power and renewal rates of established medical information synthesizers.
14.
Transitioning scientific publication: The STM sector continues to undergo a long-term transition from academic library subscriptions to open access models. While this shift increases client negotiating power, it has also driven elevated research article submission volumes that support Elsevier's scale.
15.
