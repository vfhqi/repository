Consolidated Cost Structure Analysis

1.
Flexible cost proportions: SPIE SA's cost structure is heavily weighted toward variable expenses which protects operating margins. According to the company's 2025 universal registration document, variable costs represented approximately 58.9% of total costs while fixed costs accounted for 41.1% of the operating base. This configuration mirrors the 2024 split of 58.7% variable and 41.3% fixed costs. Consequently, this variable proportion protects earnings per share during volume contractions because purchases scale down immediately.
2.
Purchases and external services: Material consumption and third-party contracts represent the primary components of SPIE SA's variable cost base. The company's 2025 universal registration document shows purchases consumed of EUR 1,416.0m and external services of EUR 4,296.1m. These external services include approximately EUR 1.8bn of subcontracting, which provides immediate operational scaling. Consequently, these massive variable lines allow management to reduce external expenses very quickly when project volumes decline, thereby preserving operating margins and stabilizing earnings.
3.
Employment cost baseline: Internal headcount represents the largest single cost line and constitutes the core of the fixed operating base. SPIE SA reported FY2025 employment costs of EUR 3,677.9m, which represented 37.0% of the company's overall cost structure. This reflects a moderate increase from the EUR 3,507.1m reported in FY2024, driven primarily by corporate acquisitions and organic expansion. Because internal labor is relatively fixed in the short term, sudden volume drops compress margins unless the group can reallocate core technicians efficiently across active segments.
4.
Historical cost comparisons: Broker assessments highlight that SPIE SA has structurally enhanced its cost flexibility compared to previous economic cycles. A Jefferies research note from 17 March 2020 indicated that personnel on SPIE's own payroll accounted for 38% of the cost base, which was down by 7% to 8% compared to historical cycles. Jefferies argued on 10 August 2020 that this lean structure would facilitate a swift margin recovery after lockdowns because the majority of project costs remained variable. This structural shift ensures that the company carries a lower breakeven point, which directly supports earnings per share resilience.
