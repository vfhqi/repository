What the company does

Renishaw is a British engineering company that makes precision measuring equipment: probes that check parts on machine tools, gauges and measuring machines for factory floors, and position encoders, the sensors that tell a machine exactly where its moving parts are. Its Position Measurement division sells encoders into chip-making tools, where it is number one in fibre-laser encoders for wafer inspection and number two in open optical encoders. The founding families control 50.25% of the votes.

A plausible investment thesis

The thesis is that artificial intelligence-driven spending on chip-making equipment has reached Renishaw's most profitable division, and a heavy fixed-cost base turns that volume into outsized profit growth. In the year to June 2026 Position Measurement grew 26% with its margin up to 27.4%; because 70 to 75 pence of each extra pound of sales drops to profit, group adjusted operating margin rose to 18.7% and adjusted earnings per share rose 30% to £1.79. The customers' plans run two years out: ASML adding 30% extreme ultraviolet capacity for 2027, Applied Materials doubling output by 2028.

The catch is timing. The memo puts a plateau in calendar 2028 at about 60% and a run-through at 40%, because Renishaw's two previous up-legs each lasted about two years. Its path gives earnings per share of £2.13, £2.30 and £2.24 for the years to June 2027, 2028 and 2029.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Crisp, not robust. The memo rated Case Riskiness C. The chain from tool-makers' plans to earnings per share is short, but the order book is under three months and customers can cancel. An expert call of 22 September put true demand at 30 to 40% of the order surge, on double- and triple-ordering. China is 25% of revenue, and the earnings summary of 23 September flagged capital spending roughly doubling to £65m to £75m into that same order book.

Business quality

A good business with cyclical outputs. The memo rated Foundations' Robustness B on five evidenced moats, including design-in lock-in, a service network in 36 countries Hexagon failed for decades to replicate, and about 1,800 patents. Return on invested capital is 17.5% and net cash £277m. Against that, margins swing between 10% and 25% through the cycle, cash conversion fell to 79%, and the memo rated the operator C, citing a paused enterprise resource planning rollout, years of cash hoarding and a board 44% independent.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros. Renishaw sits at the highest-margin stage of its chain, and the Sell-side Momentum reading of 29 September is A: estimates of earnings per share up 12% in a month and 29% in a year.

Cons. The Portfolio Fitness for Fighting of 25 September reads a rates-led late cycle and holds factory equipment Underweight, because hiking into a purchasing managers' index of 57 is how the capital spending cycle ends. The thematics register calls artificial intelligence capital spending stable, not strengthening, and the Thematic Fit/Momentum reading of 30 September is C.

The strongest counter-case

This is structural, not cyclical. Each new chip generation needs about 30% more measurement per wafer, Position Measurement accelerated to 36 to 41% growth in the third quarter, and a profit mix tilted to a 27%-margin division deserves a higher multiple. For it to win, ASML's results of 15 October and Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company's next two calls must hold 2027 plans, and Position Measurement orders must rise with margin above 25% in February 2027. Even then the memo's arithmetic reaches only a C from here: "The bull is not wrong; the bull is paid."
