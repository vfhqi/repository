EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The evidence shows that OTE's expected EBITDAaL acceleration can be partly answered with medium confidence. OTE's earnings per share are forecast to grow from €1.23 in FY25 to €1.51 in FY26e and €1.68 in FY27e, underpinned by management's target to accelerate EBITDA growth to approximately 3% in 2026 compared to 2.0% delivered in FY25. However, this trajectory depends critically on whether core telecom momentum can fully replace temporary public-funded ICT revenues as the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility terminates in December 2026, creating substantial uncertainty about the sustainability of growth beyond the near term.

OTE's retail fixed services returned to growth at 1.1% in 2025 driven by FTTH rollout, while mobile service revenues grew 3.1% in fiscal year 2025 through successful customer migrations from prepaid to postpaid and targeted pricing actions. These structural growth drivers in core telecom represent genuine competitive strengths rather than capabilities the company needs to acquire. OTE achieved postpaid net additions at a 17- year high of 62,000 in Q2 2026, bringing total postpaid subscribers to over 3.1 million, while prepaid accounts still represent 56% of the base, indicating significant future monetization potential compared to more mature European peers.

System Solutions revenues surged by 33.4% during 2025, with fourth-quarter growth reaching 57.8% due to public sector digitalization, though these temporary public digital transformation projects have temporarily boosted overall group revenues. This exceptional growth carries a critical vulnerability because OTE's ICT business earns an average EBITDAaL margin of 20.0%, compared to 42.0% for standalone Greek operations. The impending revenue cliff from winding down low-margin ICT projects could paradoxically improve blended margins even as it threatens top-line momentum, creating conflicting implications for investor expectations.

Current CEO Kostas Nebis successfully improved Hrvatski Telekom's year-on-year revenue trajectory from negative 1% in 2019 to positive 6% in 2024, while adjusted EBITDAaL growth improved from negative 4% in 2019 to positive 5% in 2023. This proven operational track record demonstrates that the leadership possesses relevant turnaround experience. However, OTE already operates in a consolidated three-player market structure with dominant market shares in both postpaid mobile and fiber broadband, meaning further domestic market consolidation remains unlikely, suggesting that many of the most powerful levers utilized in Croatia have already been exhausted in Greece.

The wholesale competition threat from Public Power Corporation's FiberGrid represents the single most material long-term risk to consensus expectations. A non-binding term sheet exists to form a fifty-fifty joint venture between PPC FiberGrid and Vodafone's Fiber2All, combining assets to cover over 1.6 million homes and creating the second-largest wholesale FTTH platform in Greece. Once operational in H1 2027, this combined entity could trigger accelerated wholesale revenue erosion as Vodafone migrates its retail subscribers away from OTE's infrastructure.

The Greek regulator is proposing a 10% to 13% decrease in OTE's FTTH wholesale charges, which will compress OTE's domestic wholesale margins unless FTTH volume uptake accelerates dramatically.

Conflicting evidence exists regarding the immediacy and severity of competitive threats. Deutsche Bank's research reports that FTTH network overbuild remains immaterial in Greece, suggesting that competitive threats from late entrants are significantly overstated and that OTE's infrastructure lead is highly insulated from rival wholesale networks. However, OTE's long-term growth is heavily exposed to rising competitive pressures from utility overbuilders, and a viable rival fiber competitor could severely compress OTE's historical broadband dominance and premium pricing power in retail fixed segments. The resolution of this conflict depends entirely on whether PPC's joint venture can successfully commercialize its network and attract meaningful wholesale commitments, which remains uncertain given that Nova's owner United Group rejected PPC's wholesale rates as unacceptable, and PPC has failed to secure any meaningful wholesale commitments from the domestic triopoly. Greek HICP inflation stood at 3.8% from January to September 2026 and accelerated to 5.1% in September 2026, though demand remains highly resilient due to network superiority. This persistent inflation creates a dual challenge: it enables CPI-linked pricing adjustments that support near-term revenue growth but simultaneously threatens consumer affordability and regulatory scrutiny. The Polish consumer protection regulator UOKiK initiated formal proceedings against operators regarding abusive inflation clauses in retail contracts, suggesting that OTE's ability to execute automatic CPI adjustments will face increasing European regulatory resistance.

Morgan Stanley's consensus assumptions embedded in its September 2026 research already anticipate that OTE's mobile service revenue remains highly insulated from macroeconomic shocks due to its premium network quality, enabling easy monetization of data volume growth and prepaid-to-postpaid transitions even as consumers experience falling real incomes. However, OTE's mobile service revenue expansion slowed to 2.3% in Q2 2026 from 5.5% in Q1 2026, and once initial prepaid top-up effects and CPI-linked price steps fully normalize, OTE's medium-term earnings per share growth could flatten significantly. This sequential deceleration represents an early warning signal that the current growth rate may not be sustainable. The critical information gaps preventing a definitive answer include the precise margins of OTE's System Solutions division, the binding commercial terms of the PPC-Vodafone joint venture, and the regulatory timing of FTTH wholesale price cuts. While OTE's Q2 2026 earnings release reported that System Solutions revenues expanded by 52.1%, the actual profitability of this segment is not explicitly disclosed.

Euronext Athens confirmed a non-binding agreement to combine fiber networks covering 1.6 million homes compared to OTE's 2.2 million, but binding contract disclosures and regulatory approvals in H1 2027 are necessary to evaluate the joint venture's actual subscriber migration timeline.

Cost efficiency levers provide some defensive protection. OTE's voluntary leave schemes attracted over 1,300 employees, delivering annualized personnel savings of €60 million net of implementation expenses, while OTE's 10-year Power Purchasing Agreements hedge 80% of its projected 2026 power consumption, offsetting a €30 million power cost headwind. These operational improvements demonstrate active cost management that supports margin resilience even if top-line growth moderates.

The most probable outcome is that OTE will successfully deliver its 3% EBITDAaL growth target for 2026 through a combination of core mobile momentum, temporary ICT tailwinds, and cost efficiencies. However, the post- 2026 trajectory faces material downside risks unless OTE can accelerate fiber adoption, maintain wholesale pricing power against the PPC joint venture, and successfully navigate regulatory pricing interventions.

The evidence supports a view that OTE's structural strengths are genuine but that consensus earnings per share forecasts for 2027 and beyond embed overly optimistic assumptions about the company's ability to replace lowmargin public ICT revenues with equally strong core telecom growth while simultaneously defending against formidable wholesale competition.
