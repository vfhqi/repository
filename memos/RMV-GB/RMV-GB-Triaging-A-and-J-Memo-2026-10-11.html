[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Rightmove is a cause-for-concern-clearing case on an oversold, high-quality business, not a growth case. At 490.5p (9 October 2026) the shares pay 13.2 times two-year-forward earnings for a portal with 90% of UK consumer property-portal time and 96% agency retention, because the market priced three fears at once, and the fear that set the multiple, disintermediation by conversational AI, is measurably absent so far. The return clears the 20% bar only if that fear is withdrawn from the multiple; without it the buyback and the price escalator deliver 15 to 16% a year on a downside bounded by a multiple already at the bottom of its own and its peers' ranges.

  • The change we count on is mostly internal, already in motion, and defensive in purpose.
    • The board borrowed for the first time since listing to return cash at a ten-year-low multiple, and the product engine has been rebuilt
    • Neither lifts the top line.
    • A £200m revolving credit facility (21 July 2026, to July 2029) funds a capital return of more than £400m by July 2027, about £330m of it buybacks, which retires about 9% of the shares at today's price
      • £74.0m was executed in H1 2026
    • Product releases rose 40% year-on-year to over 4,000 in H1 2026 with 84% of code changes written agentically
      • The cloud migration completed on 31 July 2026 after 18 months
    • Tier migration lifts average revenue per advertiser 7 to 8% a year without volume: core ARPA +7% to £1,726 a month and agency ARPA +8% to £1,636 in H1 2026, with Optimiser Edge at 36% of independent agents and Ascend at 36% of developers
    • Guidance went the other way twice in nine months: the mid-term margin from 70% to 67% (November 2025) and FY2026 revenue growth from 8 to 10% to 6 to 8% (31 July 2026)
  • The one external force that matters is a fear being disproved, and the price has not finished absorbing the disproof.
    • Less than 0.5% of traffic is referred from large language models, flat since December 2025, while over 85% arrives direct or organic and about half of the listing metadata cannot be scraped.
    • The other external forces net to a mild headwind: UK transactions flat at 1.05 to 1.15 million, new developments at their lowest since January 2017, five-year fixed mortgage rates at 4.7 to 5.1%, and a £1.56bn opt-out collective claim in the Competition Appeal Tribunal with its certification hearing in November 2026
  • 'Financial Outputs' are not improving at the top line or the margin; earnings per share reach the 12 to 20% band only from FY2027 and largely through the share count.
    • Revenue growth has slowed from 9.0% in FY2025 to 6.7% in H1 2026, the underlying margin has been rebased from 70% to a 67% floor through FY2028, and cash conversion is high but flat.
    • Our path: earnings per share 30.5p in FY2026, 34.7p in FY2027 (+14%), 39.1p in FY2028 (+13%), 43.9p in FY2029 (+12%)
      • About 13% a year compound, of which about five points a year come from the share count and the operating earnings underneath grow 7 to 8% a year
    • Consensus: 30.6p, 34.2p and 37.8p for FY2026 to FY2028, growth +5.5%, +11.7% and +10.6%
      • Our path sits 1.5% above consensus for FY2027 and 3.4% above for FY2028
    • The H1 2026 margin of 69% beat the 68% consensus against a 67% floor, and H1 2026 earnings per share rose 6%
  • The change has travelled four of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain and has not reached the sell-side's estimates.
    • It shows in the company's choices (the November 2025 strategy, the July 2026 facility), its actions (the buyback, the completed cloud migration, the release cadence) and its key operating metrics (retention, time share, ARPA)
    • It shows in the financial statements only in H1 2026 earnings per share up 6% and the share count down 3%.
    • Twelve-month sell-side revisions are -5.6% for FY2026, -6.6% for FY2027 and -9.7% for FY2028
      • The three- and six-month revisions for FY2027 and FY2028 have turned flat to slightly positive (+0.8% and +0.9% at FY2027), which is the cuts stopping, not upgrades starting
  • What the price assumes is zero terminal growth, and nothing for the multiple returning toward its own history.
    • At 490.5p the shares trade at 13.18 times 24-month-forward consensus earnings of 37.2p, the 11th percentile of their five-year range (11.45x to 30.24x, median 19.82x), and the dashboard's current multiple of 12.0x is at the zero percentile of ten years.
    • The stock is level with a classifieds peer group that has de-rated as a group (Autotrader 10.1x, Baltic Classifieds 12.3x, Scout24 13.6x, Hemnet 10.3x, Moltiply 10.5x), so there is no peer discount to close
      • The whole group sits at its own floor
    • What is priced: the margin rebase, the New Homes cut, part of the buyback (the shares are +12% since 31 July 2026) and perhaps a third weighting on a bad legal outcome
      • What is not priced: any re-rating, and any upgrade from the share count or the margin running above its floor
  • What we think it earns: 26.4% a year over eighteen months and 20.7% a year over three years, of which the re-rating is the swing factor.
    • We value the shares at a blended multiple of 15.0x at eighteen months and 15.3x at three years on the earnings two years beyond each horizon
    • The blend weights a cleared branch at 18x at 39% odds and an uncleared branch at 13x otherwise.
    • With no re-rating (13.18x held) the return is 16.2% a year over eighteen months and 15.2% over three years, a C
      • At the cleared branch alone (18x) it is 42.5% and 27.2% a year
      • At the uncleared branch alone (13x) it is 15.1% and 14.7% a year
    • The cash return is small and the only predictable leg (2.4% over eighteen months, 7.9% over three years)
      • Earnings growth carries most of the rest
  • The pattern it fits is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', at a B for crispness.
    • A high-quality business, oversold on every measure, with a named central concern (the AI disintermediation fear) that is clearing on hard evidence and an investment-phase concern dated to end in FY2028
    • The fit is dented because there are four simultaneous concerns against the setup's two and one of them, the Newman claim, is a dated binary rather than a fading fear.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the choices through the actions into the operating metrics, and it holds at the balance sheet, where the earnings arithmetic of the buyback needs no operating news.
    • It breaks at the sell-side's estimates, which have stopped falling and not started rising, and it can break at the price escalator, which is the contested pillar of the case: the whole of the growth is price-led on a flat membership, the customers who pay it are organising against it, and a tribunal hearing in November 2026 is the first test of that lock-in in a forum where goodwill does not decide.

  • The case therefore is a defensive, high-quality value case with a mechanical earnings leg and a sector-driven re-rating leg, compelling on the downside and only moderately compelling on the upside

    • It turns on the Competition Appeal Tribunal certification hearing in November 2026 and on whether the price escalator survives customer and regulatory tolerance.

  • Rating = C, because the forces are real but defensive and mostly internal, the outputs are flat at the top line and the margin so that earnings growth inside the 12 to 20% band from FY2027 is carried by the share count, and the modal return clears the 20% bar only with a re-rating that we put at possible-to-plausible odds

    • B was live because the modal return clears the bar and the quality of the earnings is high, but the chain has not reached the sell-side's estimates and the swing factor is not ours to earn.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: the pricing-power pillar fails.
      • The portal's subscription take is already 6 to 12% of a structurally low UK commission pool (over 6% per Jefferies, about 12% per Morgan Stanley)
      • Agents describe themselves as captive and call the platform a necessary evil
      • Developers face 15 to 20% annual rises without added value at the bottom of a build cycle
      • CoStar has shown it will spend £46.5m a year to give agents a cheaper alternative and OnTheMarket now holds the second-largest inventory
      • And a £1.56bn opt-out claim in the Competition Appeal Tribunal, funded by Innsworth Capital (Elliott-backed), puts the pricing model in front of a regulator-like forum from November 2026.
      • On this reading ARPA growth decays toward 1 to 2% (Jefferies), the 67% margin becomes a ceiling rather than a floor as defensive marketing is added (Morgan Stanley's 63% case), earnings-per-share growth falls to about 2% (Jefferies), and the multiple stays at 13x or falls toward Autotrader's 10x, giving a fair value of 300p to 380p (Jefferies' severe case; Morgan Stanley's bear case)
    • What we concede.
      • The take-rate is the highest among global peers and that is why the claim exists
      • The growth is price-led on a flat membership so there is no volume cushion
      • And the tribunal hearing is a binary we cannot analyse away.
    • Why we hold our view: the output data runs the other way at every point where the hypothesis should show first.
      • Retention stood at a ten-year high of 96% in H1 2026 after two and a half years of CoStar spending
      • Agency ARPA rose 8% with over 50% of agents voluntarily spending above their package threshold
      • The Ascend tier rose from 28% to 36% of developers in six months at the bottom of the build cycle
      • The 2020 "Say No To Rightmove" boycott and the 2024 OpenRent exit both reversed within weeks
      • The claim's own mobilisation (1.5% of agencies; over 250 of about 16,600 branches) says either that the harm thesis is weak or that agents fear the commercial cost of leaving, and either supports the lock-in
    • Where the hypothesis is right, and why the case survives it.
      • The escalator has a ceiling.
      • Our path does not need it to rise faster than 7% a year, and the return clears the bar at 13x with a 15% a year C if the ceiling holds the multiple down.
      • What would make us drop the view: retention below 90%, core ARPA growth below 5%, or a certified claim with a fee-cap remedy on the table.

  • The claim: it alleges excessive pricing against agents
    • A certification could lead, in the bear reading, to a cap on branch fees (Jefferies).
    • Estate agency support for the claim was 1.5% of the addressable base, over 250 agencies
    • An online petition for a CMA probe gathered over 2,000 signatures.
