Competitor outperformance: In September 2026, Campari was the clearest share gainer in spirits with 8.4% value growth, compared to Diageo’s decline of 3.5%. 2.
Customer execution: Diageo is working to improve its execution in the on-trade, which has not fully recovered universally post-COVID. The company uses commercial platforms like EDGE 365 and Diageo One to improve sales insights and forecasts for its distributors and customers. Strengthening relationships with wholesalers and expanding direct-to-consumer channels are noted as responses to intensifying competition from other beverage providers.
3.
Spirits market stability: Competitors like Remy Cointreau reported that industry oversupply has heightened competitive intensity, requiring higher levels of investment to compete. AB Inbev and Heineken both reported profit increases overseas driven by higher selling prices and cost reductions, despite weak consumer conditions. This suggests that while the consumer backdrop is difficult, peer management teams are successfully using cost and price levers that Sir Dave Lewis is only now beginning to implement.
4.
Market share Trajectory: Diageo's current plan is to move from a share-donor position in FY27 to holding share in FY28 and gaining share by FY29. Currently, 65% of the North American portfolio is facing market share challenges. The business needs to demonstrate that its pivot to "Adult Beverages" can actually stop these losses without destroying the premium image of Johnnie Walker or Casamigos.
5.
Other side of the story: The absence of specific mentions of Diageo on major supplier calls could be interpreted as a loss of "category captaincy" status. Jefferies (Mundy, 2026) believes the next 12 months will show whether Diageo can move from share donor to holder in US spirits. However, if major retail customers like Walmart or Best Buy continue to prioritise more affordable alternatives, the CEO's "Adult Beverages" strategy may be too late to regain lost shelf space.
