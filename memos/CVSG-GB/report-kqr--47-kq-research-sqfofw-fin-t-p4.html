EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The evidence indicates that below-EBITDA inputs–particularly R&D tax credits, Australian acquisition multiples, depreciation trajectories, rising tax rates, and share buyback execution –will be the primary determinants of CVS Group's adjusted earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years. The Key Question is highly answerable based on disclosed financial data, broker research, and expert interviews, though critical gaps remain regarding organic Australian growth rates, HMRC audit risks, and new CEO capital allocation priorities. CVS Group's inclusion of £15.7m of R&D tax credits inside adjusted EBITDA for FY2026 artificially inflates reported margins to 19.9%, versus a true margin of only 17.6%, creating significant sustainability risk if HMRC restricts future claims. The company targets Australian practice acquisitions at 6x EBITDA multiples compared to historical UK multiples of 10x, though former directors cite actual entry prices of 9x to 10x for premium clinics. Management expects acquired Australian practices to deliver post-acquisition EBITDA margins of 25% or more, which would be highly accretive to consolidated earnings if achieved consistently across the expanding international footprint. Capital expenditure is guided to stabilize at approximately £30.0m annually, down from £36.4m in FY2026, which should normalize adjusted depreciation to around 6% of revenue and support earnings per share.

The group maintains a £100.0m interest rate swap to fix borrowing costs until February 2028, partially insulating finance charges from SONIA volatility, though the remaining £118.0m of floating-rate debt remains exposed to rate movements. The consolidated adjusted tax rate rose to 27.4% in FY2026 from 26.9% in FY2025, driven by the rising proportion of Australian profits taxed at 30.0% versus the UK's 25.0% rate. CVS Group launched a £50.0m share buyback programme in May 2026 and repurchased 2.3m shares for £28.8m by early September, reducing the share count to 66.7m and providing direct accretion to adjusted earnings per share. Working capital pressures from delayed R&D tax credit receipts created a £12.8m cash outflow in FY2026, compressing adjusted operating cash conversion to 70.6% and reducing free cash flow to £69.2m.

Broker consensus assumes that CVS Group trades at an attractive entry point of approximately 7.4x EV/EBITDA for FY2027, reflecting that the market has already priced in significant recovery upside despite below-EBITDA headwinds including National Insurance hikes. Where conflicts exist between management's 6x EBITDA Australian acquisition target and the 9x to 10x multiples cited by former directors, this report favors the more conservative higher-multiple assumptions in Section 2 given competitive intensity from private equity consolidators.

The Laboratories division delivered exceptionally strong performance with revenue growth of 11.5% to £35.0m and adjusted EBITDA up 25.6% to £11.3m, representing a premium 31% margin that partially offsets weak UK companion-animal volumes. The likelihood of a further buyback programme remains highly uncertain following the retirement of CEO Richard Fairman in late 2026, as investors await visibility on new leadership priorities before projecting future capital allocation between acquisitions and shareholder returns.

Section 2 – Australian Strategy: Acquisition Pace, Multiples, and Margins

Section summary
  1. CVS Group is rapidly expanding its Australian footprint through accretive practice acquisitions.
  2. The group targets lower valuation multiples and higher margins to drive earnings per share.
  3. Concerns remain regarding actual transaction multiples and vendor integration challenges.

Rapidly Growing Acquisition Pace

1.
Accelerating international expansion: CVS Group has shifted its capital allocation focus toward the highly fragmented Australian veterinary services market. The group entered Australia in July 2023, and by June 2026, it had rapidly expanded to 57 active practice sites representing about 11% of consolidated group revenues. This rapid build-out should support future earnings per share because international diversification mitigates localized regulatory pressures.
2.
Low consolidation runway: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in July 2026 that the Australian veterinary market is only 15% to 20% consolidated compared to 60% in the UK. This low level of corporate ownership provides CVS Group with a massive runway for future inorganic growth. Consequently, this structural backdrop implies that acquisition momentum can be sustained over the next four years to drive long-term earnings per share.
3.
Consistent acquisition deployment: CVS Group intends to spend approximately £50m annually on practice acquisitions to drive its international consolidation strategy. During the financial year ended June 2026, the company deployed £45.4m for six new acquisitions comprising 14 practice sites. This consistent inorganic deployment will likely boost adjusted earnings per share unless integration bottlenecks or staffing shortages slow down the operational ramp-up.

Sustained post-close momentum: CVS Group continued its rapid Australian roll-up immediately after the close of the 2026 financial year. This continuous deal flow indicates strong momentum in the Australian market which directly enhances near-term revenue and earnings projections.

However, management expressed a willingness to temporarily exceed this 2.0x limit to capture larger, high-quality strategic opportunities. This flexibility ensures that the company can maintain its aggressive roll-up pace even if prime regional groups become available.

Debating Australian Entry Multiples

6.
Attractive entry valuations: Deutsche Bank Research reported in July 2026 that CVS Group is completing Australian acquisitions at an average multiple of approximately 6x EBITDA. This average transaction multiple is lower than historical UK practice multiples of around 10x EBITDA as reported by Deutsche Bank Research in October 2025. This valuation discipline supports elevated returns on capital, which means accretive earnings per share growth is highly achievable.
7.
Diverging valuation viewpoints: A former director of CVS Group in October 2025 claimed that entry multiples actually range between 9x and 10x EBITDA for premier companion-animal practices. This claim contrasts with management's stated 6x EBITDA entry target, which suggests that the company might face intense bidding pressure for top-tier clinics. If actual multiples trend toward these higher estimates, the acquisition-driven growth will deliver lower incremental earnings per share than current broker models assume.
8.
Supportive independent evidence: A former Chief Financial Officer at Vets Central in January 2025 stated that typical independent clinic acquisition multiples in Australia range from 5x to 8x EBITDA. This expert view aligns closely with the company's reported 6x average entry multiple, which indicates that the Australian market remains less consolidated than the UK. Consequently, CVS Group should find ample room for disciplined consolidation at attractive valuations to support adjusted earnings per share.
9.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies Research cautioned in July 2026 that increasing competition from private equity-backed consolidators could force Australian practice multiples higher. This competitive friction might prevent CVS Group from deploying its targeted £50m of capital at its guided 6x EBITDA multiple, which would limit the anticipated inorganic EPS expansion. If entry multiples inflate toward historical UK levels, the long-term return on capital will suffer.

High Post Acquisition Margins

10.
Elevated profitability targets: CVS Group expects its acquired Australian clinics to deliver post-acquisition EBITDA margins of 25% or more. This targeted profitability is significantly higher than typical group averages, which means the successful execution of these transactions will be highly accretive. Achieving these high margins is vital to offset the higher corporate tax rates in Australia and protect consolidated earnings. Peer margin comparison: Vimian Group expects its consolidated group margins to trough at approximately 24.5% in the 2026 financial year before recovering. This peer benchmark is highly comparable to CVS Group's target of 25% plus post-acquisition margins for its newly acquired Australian practices. If CVS Group can consistently achieve these targets, it will match top-tier peer profitability and drive substantial adjusted earnings per share expansion.
11.
Synergy-driven margin accretion: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in October 2025 that Australian practices generate margins that sit comfortably above the group average. This margin premium is expected to expand further as CVS Group establishes procurement, shared services, and corporate synergies. If these integration synergies materialize as planned, consolidated operating margins will rise and directly drive adjusted earnings per share.

Deferred Consideration and Peer Structure

12.
Deferred consideration mechanism: CVS Group structured its recent Australian acquisitions by paying 80% of the practice valuation upfront, while the remaining 20% is deferred and earned over time. This standard transaction structure mitigates early operational risks and aligns vendor interests with post-acquisition performance. This deferred structure helps preserve cash flow and supports adjusted earnings per share by delaying cash outflows until clinics meet profitability targets.
13.
Peer deferred structures: Vimian Group also utilizes structured contingent consideration, with Q3 2026 acquisitions comprising €26.4m in upfront cash and €9.5m in deferred and contingent payments. This deferred portion represents approximately 26.39% (calculated) of the total purchase price, which is slightly higher than the 20% ratio utilized by CVS Group. Adopting substantial deferred consideration protects earnings per share if acquired practice volumes underperform post-close.
14.
Comparative valuation multiples: Vimian Group has historically acquired animal health assets at an average multiple of approximately 9x EBITDA, which is higher than CVS Group's Australian entry multiples. This highmultiple peer benchmark shows that CVS Group's 6x target represents a highly cost-effective consolidation route to expand cash earnings per share. If competitive intensity increases, CVS Group might be forced to pay multiples closer to these peer averages and compress its investment returns.
15.
Comparable past case: Greencross Limited utilized deferred settlement mechanisms during its early consolidation of the Australian veterinary market in 2012 and 2013. Annual reports from Greencross Limited in August 2013 showed that acquisitions such as Maitland Veterinary Hospital relied on deferred contingent considerations equal to 1x EBIT. This historical precedent suggests that deferred structures are highly effective in managing veterinary retention and protecting acquirer cash flows. This means CVS Group's similar 20% deferred structure is a proven framework to secure long-term earnings per share.
16.
Other side of the story: While the rapid build-out in Australia offers high margins, escalating integration costs and labor inflation could undermine the targeted 25% post-acquisition margins. Furthermore, if actual entry multiples drift toward the 9x to 10x EBITDA cited by a former director, the return on capital will compress. Unless CVS Group enforces strict valuation discipline, the Australian segment could become a drag on consolidated earnings per share.

Restarting domestic consolidation: CVS Group has resumed its UK practice acquisitions following the conclusion of the long-standing regulatory probe. The formal resolution of the investigation allows management to deploy capital back into its mature, high-density domestic market where it holds an eight to nine percent market share. Post-restart transaction metrics: The group's first post-restart transaction demonstrates its commitment to disciplined domestic expansion at substantial scale. Management confirmed in its investor presentation on 24 September 2026 that it has exchanged contracts on a two-site practice with nine full-time equivalent veterinarians for an initial consideration of £15.0m. Diagnostic Laboratories Profit Trajectory

5.
Robust laboratory performance: The Laboratories division has delivered exceptionally strong revenue and profit growth, outperforming the core veterinary practice segment. The group's annual report on 24 September 2026 showed that Laboratories revenue grew by 11.5% to £35.0m in FY2026. This strong top-line performance drove adjusted EBITDA up by 25.6% to £11.3m, representing a highly profitable segment. Continued diagnostic volume growth will remain a major positive contributor to consolidated earnings per share.

High segment margins: The Laboratories segment maintains a premium margin profile that enhances the overall profitability of the group. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 26 February 2026 that Laboratories generated high EBITDA margins of approximately 31% during the first half of FY2026. This compares favorably to the core veterinary practice segment margins of 21.3% reported in the same period. Sustaining these attractive laboratory margins above thirty percent will help offset rising clinical wage inflation in the UK.

Vertical integration synergies: The integration of diagnostic laboratories within the broader veterinary practice network creates significant margin benefits. Management stated during its investor meeting on 4 March 2026 that having internal laboratory services allows CVS to capture the full diagnostic profit stream. If CVS had to utilize third-party laboratories, it would lose both the revenue and the high margins associated with these diagnostic tests. External volume growth: The Laboratories division is successfully expanding its customer base by capturing third- party clinical diagnostic volumes. According to the company's annual report on 24 September 2026, the volume of cases performed grew by 4.3% to approximately 442,000 tests. Diversifying the lab revenue base away from internal clinics provides highly resilient third-party profit streams.

Consequently, this structural advantage ensures that Animed Direct remains highly competitive against independent retailers.

R&D Tax Credit Sustainability

1.
Inflated operating margins: CVS Group plc's practice of including Research and Development Expenditure Credits in adjusted EBITDA significantly inflates its reported profitability. The company's annual report on 24 September 2026 disclosed a net R&D tax credit of £15.7m inside adjusted EBITDA for FY2026, which follows £15.1m recognized in FY2025. Deutsche Bank Research calculated on 24 September 2026 that excluding these credits, the group's EBITDA margin was only 17.6% compared to the reported 19.9%. This adjustment means that consolidated margins are highly sensitive to regulatory changes in R&D tax eligibility, which could directly reduce future adjusted earnings per share.
2.
Diminishing reserve release: CVS Group's ongoing reliance on historical claim reviews to boost current year earnings may not be sustainable over the long term. The group's annual report on 24 September 2026 stated that the FY2026 credit of £15.7m included a provision release of £6.6m, which was up from £3.0m in FY2025. Management justified this release based on another year of successful claim history with HM Revenue and Customs. This means that if future tax audits become more stringent, the reserve releases will eventually exhaust, reducing adjusted earnings per share.
3.
Negative working capital: CVS Group's cash flow dynamics have deteriorated due to the lag in receiving R&D tax credit payments. The group's annual report on 24 September 2026 reported a working capital outflow of £12.8m for FY2026. This timing mismatch implies that while adjusted EBITDA remains supported, cash earnings per share will suffer until the outstanding R&D tax receipts are fully collected.

Capital Reinvestment and Depreciation Path

Stabilizing depreciation trajectory: The normalization of depreciation charges reflects the peaking of major infrastructure and IT modernization programs.
