Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Nestlé's financial profile and operational performance reveal several significant statistical anomalies that deviate meaningfully from both peer group benchmarks and historical sector base rates. These outliers warrant heightened scrutiny as they may signal either temporary measurement distortions or structural vulnerabilities that could materially affect forward earnings quality and valuation sustainability. The most pronounced structural outlier concerns Nestlé's capital intensity mismatch, where the historical capexto-depreciation gap represents a potential 270 basis points headwind to EBIT as new completed assets begin depreciating.

This divergence is extraordinary compared to European consumer staples peers, who typically maintain depreciation charges closely aligned with ongoing capital expenditure. Morgan Stanley identifies this low depreciation charge relative to capex as inflating reported operating profits artificially, suggesting that Nestlé's reported EBIT margins of 16.4% in H1 2026 may overstate true economic profitability by approximately 200-300 basis points once normalized depreciation schedules commence.

This creates a deferred earnings quality risk as the asset base matures and depreciation expenses normalize upward in the N2Y-N3Y period. Cross-reference: Section 4 – Financial Profile Change for detailed capital intensity analysis and peer benchmarking.

A second material outlier emerges in Nestlé's balance sheet leverage trajectory, which has stretched significantly beyond both historical norms and peer averages. Nestlé's net debt/adjusted EBITDA reached 3.2x as of H1 2026, presenting a stretched balance sheet versus historical averages, and this elevated leverage compares unfavorably with Danone's lower net debt/EBITDA ratio of 1.5x projected for FY26. This leverage differential of approximately 170 basis points above peer median represents a statistical outlier given Nestlé's historical positioning as a conservatively capitalized defensive equity.

The deviation is particularly unusual given that Nestlé simultaneously maintains elevated dividend commitments, with the ordinary dividend payout of approximately CHF 8.1 billion exceeding Nestlé-defined organic free cash flow generation. This combination of stretched leverage and uncovered dividends creates an unusual risk profile relative to the European staples peer set. Cross-reference: Section 4 – Financial Profile Change and Section 8 – Sell-Side Commentary Survey for Morgan Stanley's bearish leverage analysis.

The third notable outlier appears in Nestlé's H1 2026 earnings quality, where temporary accounting benefits materially distorted reported profitability. Nestlé's H1 operating profit margin of 16.4% benefited from a 30 basis points pension credit that will not repeat, while Morgan Stanley highlighted that operating margins benefited from a pension credit, representing lower earnings quality versus cash-driven EBIT.

This nonrecurring 30 basis point margin tailwind is statistically anomalous compared to peer reporting periods, where pension accounting typically generates negligible period-to-period margin volatility. The temporary nature of this benefit suggests that H2 2026 margins face a structural step-down unless offset by operational improvements, creating a potential forward guidance credibility gap if management maintains full-year margin targets without explicitly adjusting for the pension credit reversal. Cross-reference: Section 6 – Most Recent Earnings Delivery for detailed earnings quality decomposition.

& Co Redburn A fourth outlier emerges in the polarized divisional performance exhibited during the most recent reporting period, where intra-company growth dispersion reached unusual extremes. Waters & Premium Beverages organic growth reached 6.6% in Q2, significantly beating the consensus estimate of 2.9%, while simultaneously Petcare organic growth slowed to 2.8% in the second quarter, missing consensus estimates of 3.6% and category historical average of 5.0%.

This 380 basis point growth differential between Waters and Petcare represents an extreme divergence relative to typical intra-portfolio variance among diversified consumer staples companies, where business unit performance typically clusters within a 150-200 basis point range during stable operating periods. The magnitude of this dispersion suggests either temporary demand shocks (such as retailer destocking in Petcare) or structural execution gaps that may persist if category-specific issues prove systemic rather than cyclical.
