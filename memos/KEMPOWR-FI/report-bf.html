EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Section summary
  1. Kempower is transitioning from a Nordic-focused hardware manufacturer to a balanced global player leveraging modular DC fast-charging systems
  2. the revenue model remains point-in-time dominated, but is driving high-margin recurring services and ChargEye SaaS toward a 2030 target
  3. scale economies and fixed-cost operating leverage are starting to improve profitability after a period of premature factory over-expansion
  4. recent leadership restructuring under CEO Bhasker Kaushal aims to strengthen technological and global operational execution across key regions

Kempower operates as a pure-play DC fast-charging hardware manufacturer transitioning from Nordic-centric sales toward balanced global exposure across Europe and North America. The company's modular distributed architecture separates power cabinets from satellite dispensers, achieving 50% smaller physical footprints compared to conventional all-in-one chargers and enabling dynamic load-balancing across 12 satellites without grid upgrades. The satellite dispensers require up to 50% less real estate at charging sites, and a single 1,200 kW power unit can dynamically distribute electrical power across up to 12 charging satellites. This technological foundation creates structural switching costs through physical site integration and ChargEye SaaS backend lockin, validated by 99%+ operational uptime across 13 million charging sessions. However, the business model remains 95% hardware point-in-time revenue, with recurring software and lifecycle services representing only 5% of 2025 sales despite management's strategic pivot toward materially higher recurring streams by 2030.

Outliers worth noting

Kempower demonstrates an exceptionally high gross margin of 54.4% in 2020, fluctuating between 46.5% and 52.1% across subsequent years, placing the company in the top 5% of global hardware manufacturers in the capital equipment sector. This profitability positioning substantially exceeds typical DC fast-charging peers and reflects the premium pricing power enabled by Kempower's modular satellite architecture and dynamic power-sharing capabilities.

The peer comparison table reveals that CTEK achieved an 18.3% adjusted EBITDA margin in 2025, but this outlier is driven by its legacy consumer battery charging business rather than EV infrastructure hardware, making Kempower's sustained gross margin performance even more remarkable within

Kempower achieved an extraordinary revenue scaling trajectory, growing from EUR 3 million in 2020 to EUR 284 million in 2023, representing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 300% over three years. This hypergrowth velocity places Kempower in the top 1% of European industrial technology companies during the 2020-2023 period and dramatically outpaces all direct competitors except Zaptec, which operates primarily in the lower-complexity AC charging segment.

While Kempower's EUR 251 million revenue scale in 2025 sits below Alfen's EUR 436 million, the company's 2021-2025 sales CAGR of 74% substantially outperforms all European peers, demonstrating superior commercial execution during the critical EV infrastructure build-out phase. The company's ability to capture early-mover positioning across Nordic, Continental European, and North American markets enabled this exceptional scaling, though revenue declined by 21% in 2024 to EUR 224 million due to customer destocking, exposing the cyclical volatility inherent in hardware-led business models.
