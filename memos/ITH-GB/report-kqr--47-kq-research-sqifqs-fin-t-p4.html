Section 2 – Tax Dynamics, Loss Utilisation, and Deferred Tax Asset Risks

Section summary
  1. Ithaca Energy's $9.3 bn UK tax loss shelter is expected to protect its baseline operations from the standard 40% regime through 2030 but cannot offset Energy Profits Levy payments.
  2. Structural cash tax lags and Energy Profits Levy payments will drive cash taxes higher through 2028, with high confidence based on UK and Norwegian peer precedents.

Ithaca Tax Losses and Guidance

1.
Robust protection: Ithaca Energy carries massive UK tax shields that are expected to protect its baseline upstream operations from the standard 40% tax regime through 2030. According to the DNB Carnegie report dated September 16, 2026, the company held approximately $5.1 bn of ring-fence corporation tax losses, $4.2 bn of supplementary charge losses, and $2.3 bn in investment allowances at the end of 2025. These enormous balances are expected to prevent material baseline cash tax payments for the foreseeable future.
2.
High alignment: Ithaca Energy's near-term cash tax guidance for 2026 matches independent broker expectations, confirming a highly predictable short-term tax schedule. In its trading update on August 28, 2026, the company reiterated its full-year 2026 cash tax guidance of $290 mm to $340 mm. This guidance is highly aligned with historical tax payments of $263 mm reported in the prior year. This alignment demonstrates that short-term fiscal liabilities remain well understood by the market.
3.
Contrary evidence: Ithaca Energy's massive $9.3 bn in UK tax losses cannot be used to offset liabilities from the Energy Profits Levy, which limits the near-term cash tax protection they offer. According to Harbour Energy's annual report on March 5, 2026, UK ring-fence tax losses cannot be offset against profits subject to the levy, nor are deductions allowed for decommissioning. Consequently, these fiscal rules mean that EPL liabilities are paid in cash regardless of loss carryforwards.

According to DNB Carnegie's initiation report on September 16, 2026, cash taxes are projected to spike from $316 mm in 2026 to $677 mm in 2027. This delayed cash outflow is driven directly by high commodity prices feeding into lagged tax payments. Ultimately, this lag means that lower cash flows are visible in broker models during 2027 and 2028.

Section 3 – Commodity Hedging and Realised Price Effects

Section summary
  1. Ithaca Energy's near-term oil downside is protected by a substantial 2027 collar book, while its open gas position exposes cash flows to spot volatility.
  2. Weak disclosure on 2028 hedging and rigid hedge structures compare unfavorably to more transparent peer programs at Harbour Energy and EnQuest.

Ithaca Hedging Position and Protection

1.
Robust protection: Ithaca Energy manages its near-term price risks through a systematic hedging program that covers a high percentage of oil production. According to the DNB Carnegie report dated September 16, 2026, the company has hedged approximately 75% of its 2027 oil volumes. These hedges predominantly utilize collar structures that allow the company to maintain exposure to commodity price upside while securing floors. Consequently, these derivatives protect baseline cash flows against any sudden collapse in the oil market.
2.
Specific terms: Ithaca Energy has established specific volume and price floors for its 2027 commodity exposure to secure its development program. Filings from Companies House on September 17, 2026, show that the company has hedged 3.1 mmboe of oil at a weighted average floor of $66/bbl for 2027. For gas, the company secured 2.0 mmboe of hedged volume with a weighted average floor of 82p/therm over the same period. These positions ensure that the company can maintain capital investment even if spot prices drop.
3.
Contrary evidence: Ithaca Energy does not provide any detailed hedging volumes or strike prices for the year 2028, leaving its medium-term pricing protection highly uncertain. Filings from Companies House on September 17, 2026, show that while 2027 volumes are clearly defined, the 2028 hedge schedule remains undisclosed. This lack of transparency prevents analysts from modeling post-2027 realized prices with high confidence. Thus, the company's protection is much weaker than it appears.

Section summary
  1. Ithaca Energy's $860 million acquisition of Suncor's Canadian assets marks its first major international expansion designed to diversify away from UK regulatory volatility.
  2. The transaction adds 30 kboe/d of net production but requires the assumption of C$1.4 billion in decommissioning liabilities and a C$500 million regulatory well compliance program.

Transaction Terms and Financing

1.
Strategic expansion: Ithaca Energy agreed to acquire Suncor's offshore East Coast Canadian portfolio for an upfront cash payment of $860 million. This major international step-out is intended to help the producer diversify its geographic footprint away from the UK North Sea. Jefferies points out that the transaction represents an attractive entry point into a stable basin with significant operational overlaps.
2.
Contingent payments: Suncor Energy negotiated up to $250 million in additional payments that are directly linked to future Brent crude benchmarks. This contingent structure shares cash flows on a 50/50 basis if Brent oil prices exceed specific price targets through September 2028. Consequently, the ultimate acquisition cost remains highly dependent on future oil prices over the next two years.
3.
Funding structure: Ithaca Energy intends to fund the transaction using cash on hand, its existing borrowing base facility, and secured in-country financing. Jefferies notes that the funding plan is designed to keep leverage comfortably within the group's net debt to EBITDA target of under 1x. Ultimately, this structure minimizes immediate balance sheet strain during a period of heavy organic investment.
4.
Complex integration: Suncor Energy expects the transaction to close in early 2027 subject to regulatory approvals and partner consents. Suncor's filings reveal that the deal carries an effective date of July 1, 2026, with cash flows accumulating prior to formal completion. Jefferies notes that this timeline gives management sufficient time to prepare for operational integration.

Production Profiles and Operations

5.
Robust volumes: Suncor Energy reported that the acquired assets produced a combined average of 17,000 bbls/d during the first nine months of 2026. This substantial volume addition helps offset mature decline across the company's UK North Sea asset base.

The acquired assets include a 48% operated interest in Terra Nova and non-operated working interests of 40% in the White Rose field. Jefferies projects that the portfolio's production will grow to peak levels of 35 to 40 kboe/d by 2029 as development wells are completed. This growth trajectory depends on successful project execution at the non-operated White Rose growth lands.

Carbon Tracker notes that Cenovus's capital expenditure strategy prioritizes completing West White Rose to drive its medium-term production ramp-up. This partnership reduces technical execution risk for Ithaca given Cenovus's extensive offshore operating expertise.

9.

Suncor's financial reports show that the total estimated abandonment and lease liabilities carry a substantial present value of $1.4 billion. Jefferies notes that this transfer presents a major long-term cost that could impact future corporate cash flows.

Favorable deferral: Ithaca Energy believes that there is substantial potential to defer meaningful decommissioning obligations into the 2040s. Management's presentation slides indicate that the fields carry visible production lives extending through at least 2037 and 2039 respectively. This extensive asset life helps defer immediate abandonment cash outflows well beyond the current planning horizon.

Attractive margins: Suncor Energy's historical E&P segment results demonstrate that its East Coast offshore assets operate with relatively low cash costs. Suncor's management noted that these fields have delivered strong operating contributions during periods of high commodity price environments. Consequently, this high-margin production supports Ithaca's near-term dividend capacity despite the heavy regional capital expenditure requirements.

Suncor's reports state that this writedown was triggered by classifying the assets as held for sale following the agreement with Ithaca. This substantial charge indicates Suncor accepted low pricing to expedite its strategic exit from non-core conventional offshore operations.

Proven capability: Ithaca Energy intends to assume direct operatorship of the Terra Nova field upon the successful completion of the transaction. The company notes that its existing experience with UK shallowwater and floating production infrastructure provides a solid foundation for managing Canadian operations. This transition represents a critical operational challenge as the group establishes its first international operating hub.
15.
Favorable incentives: Ithaca Energy highlights that Canada's recently announced fiscal deductions will significantly support the financial economics of new capital investments. Jefferies notes that the Productivity Mega Deduction is expected to further reduce the marginal effective tax rate on new development work. This supportive regional fiscal framework lowers after-tax capital intensity compared to the increasingly punitive tax regime operating in the UK.
16.
Other side of the story: While the Canadian acquisition provides valuable geographic diversification and near-term production growth, the assumption of massive future liabilities represents a substantial financial risk. Jefferies warns that assuming C$1.4 billion in decommissioning liabilities and a C$500 million well compliance program requires significant capital commitments. Section summary
  1. Ithaca Energy faces rising absolute DD&A charges and complex debt refinancing schedules, although sterling hedging shields near-term operating costs.
  2. Geographic expansion into Canada introduces material Canadian dollar capital exposures that could increase foreign exchange volatility in future financial reports.

Depreciation and depletion trends

1.
Elevated charges: Ithaca Energy recorded substantial depletion, depreciation, and amortisation charges of $483.6 million during the first half of 2026, compared with $438.9 million in the first half of 2025. This absolute charge represents a significant increase that reflects the integration of newly acquired production assets. Consequently, these higher non-cash charges will continue to weigh on reported accounting earnings per share over the next several quarters unless production volumes grow dramatically.
2.
Stable rates: Ithaca Energy experienced a unit depletion, depreciation, and amortisation rate of approximately $20.83 per barrel in the first half of 2026 based on production of 23.22 million barrels of oil equivalent. This unit charge is slightly higher than the historical 2025 depletion rate of $19.4 per barrel, but remains below the 2024 peak rate of $23.6 per barrel. No direct unit depletion rates for named peer companies were available for comparison in the retrieved documents. Ultimately, this stable unit rate shows that recent asset integrations have moderated depreciation spikes.
3.
Shifting mix: Ithaca Energy benefited from the integration of lower-depreciation assets that helped offset steeper legacy depletion rates across its mature fields. Reports from Companies House on July 12, 2026, indicated that newer assets acquired by the group carry significantly lower unit charges than legacy assets. This mix shift suggests that future capital additions, such as the Canadian assets, must achieve low depletion rates to prevent depressing future net profit margins further as mature fields decline.
4.
Contrary evidence: Ithaca Energy saw its total annual depletion, depreciation, and amortisation expenses surge to $840.6 million during 2025 from $600.2 million in 2024. This massive increase was driven primarily by higher production volumes from newly integrated portfolios and asset impairment adjustments. This trend indicates that growth through acquisitions elevates absolute opex and depletion and could pressure near-term earnings despite delivering larger operational scale.

Debt facilities and refinancing needs

5.
Successful refinancing: Ithaca Energy completed the refinancing of its near-term debt maturities to push out its repayment obligations and enhance liquidity. The group issued $750 million in senior unsecured notes carrying an 8.125% coupon due in October 2029 to fully repay its older $625 million 9.0% notes due in 2026. This refinancing lowered the group's nominal interest rate while extending the debt runway to support longterm projects.
6.
Growing notes: Ithaca Energy expanded its outstanding debt through subsequent debt placements to bolster its liquid reserves ahead of peak development. The company placed an additional €155 million in 5.5% senior notes due in 2031, expanding its existing €450 million note issue. Swapping these notes into US dollars achieved an effective all-in interest rate of approximately 6.7% for the expanded notes.
7.
Robust liquidity: Ithaca Energy maintained significant corporate liquidity of $1.9 billion as of June 30, 2026, to support its massive capital program. This liquidity position was supported by a private placement and was supplemented by an available accordion facility of over $430 million. Jefferies noted on August 19, 2026, that this financial foundation provides excellent firepower for patient pursuit of M&A opportunities in both domestic and international basins.
8.
Sustained interest: Ithaca Energy faces high annual debt service requirements that could limit the cash available for equity holders. Financing costs for 2025 included senior notes interest of $68.6 million alongside loan interest and charges of $54.6 million. If interest rates remain high, refinancing the 2029 notes could increase interest expenses and drag on future earnings per share unless production cash flows grow.

Currency risk and GBP exposures

9.
Persistent liability: Ithaca Energy remains exposed to significant balance sheet translation risks due to holding substantial sterling-denominated net liabilities. The company reported sterling-denominated net liabilities of £107 million at the end of 2025, up from £55 million in the prior year. These balances mean that fluctuations in the sterling exchange rate can cause non-cash volatility in pre-tax profits upon translation.
10.
Active hedging: Ithaca Energy manages its sterling exposure through a dedicated foreign exchange hedging program to protect operating budgets. The group hedged an average of £70.1 million per quarter for 2026 and 2027 using collar structures with a floor of $1.221 per pound and an average ceiling of $1.267 per pound. This strategy is designed to protect the dollar-denominated operating costs of its UK North Sea business against exchange rate spikes.
11.
High sensitivity: Ithaca Energy experiences direct earnings volatility from sterling movements because its functional and reporting currency is the US dollar. Sensitivity analysis in the 2025 annual report showed that a 10% weakening of sterling would have reduced pre-tax profits by $14.1 million. This relationship indicates that sterling strength increases reporting headwinds on dollar-denominated financial statements by raising local cost bases.
12.
Altered revenues: Ithaca Energy saw its net sterling exposure increase following the integration of gas-heavy assets. Management stated in the 2024 annual report that incorporating Eni UK assets increased gas production and sterling revenues. Consequently, this revenue change altered the net sterling exposure of the operating group by providing a natural local currency hedge.

Canadian dollar operational exposures

New exposure: Ithaca Energy expects to face meaningful Canadian dollar capital and operating exposures following its acquisition of Suncor assets. The transaction transfers massive Canadian dollar decommissioning liabilities of C$1.4 billion and a compliance program of C$500 million to the group.
