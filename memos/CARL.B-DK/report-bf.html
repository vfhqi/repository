EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Carlsberg maintains a resilient multi-beverage business model characterized by high exposure to regional Challenger positions and growing soft drink integration, with non-beer products now accounting for a significant share of group volume, reducing dependence on structural beer volume declines.

The company operates across three geographical reporting regions–Western Europe, Asia, and Central Europe–with Western Europe representing 58% of group revenue in 2025.

Soft drinks and non-beer products account for approximately 33% of Carlsberg's total group volumes, creating a more diversified portfolio than pure-play beer competitors.

The company benefits from economies of scale in procurement and production driven by large market shares in core geographies, and has consistently beaten initial operating profit expectations by 300 to 870 basis points annually since 2016. 1 4 7 2 5 8 3 6 9 Leadership under CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen, who succeeded Cees 't Hart in September 2023, has pivoted strategy from cost to growth.

The refreshed Accelerate SAIL strategy raised medium-term organic revenue growth targets to 4% to 6%, and Carlsberg completed the transformational acquisition of soft-drink player Britvic in January 2025, significantly expanding portfolio diversification with soft drinks now constituting more than 55% of Western European volumes.

Executive compensation was restructured to reward organic net revenue growth over local cost management, aligning incentives with the new strategic direction. Financial Profile and Peer Comparison: Carlsberg achieved an organic net revenue compound annual growth rate of 3.1% from 2015 to 2025, slightly trailing Heineken's 3.5% organic compound annual growth rate over the same period.

The company increased its operating margin from 12.9% to 15.9% between 2015 and 2019 through disciplined cost execution.

Carlsberg targets rebuilding its gross margin to pre-pandemic levels of 48% to 50% globally, with recent reported gross profit margin of 45.5% in first half 2026.

The balance sheet shows elevated leverage following the Britvic acquisition, with net debt to EBITDA rising to 3.28x post-Britvic in 2025, though the company maintains strong cash conversion capabilities. 22 25 28 23 26 29 24 27 30 Critically, Carlsberg delivered the best return on invested capital of the European brewers at 12% in fiscal year 2024, significantly outperforming Heineken at 8% and Anheuser-Busch InBev at 7%.

The company targets a dividend payout ratio of approximately 50% of adjusted net profit, demonstrating commitment to shareholder returns despite near-term acquisition integration priorities. Industry Structure and Competitive Position: The global brewing industry faces modest structural headwinds, with global beer volumes forecasted to expand at just 0.2% compound annual growth rate through 2030, though category value is projected to grow at 3.4% annually through 2030 driven by premiumization.

Anheuser-Busch InBev leads the industry by controlling approximately 25% of volume globally, while Heineken represents roughly 12% and Carlsberg holds about 5% of global volumes.

China represents a massive 30% of total beer volume for Carlsberg in 2025, creating significant geographic concentration risk. Industry margins remain favorable for scale players, with Anheuser-Busch InBev setting the margin ceiling with a 25.6% operating profit margin pre-pandemic, while Carlsberg and Heineken historically define operating margin floors of 13% to 15% globally.

Large scale brewers prioritize a value ahead of volume strategy to offset structural declines, maintaining rational pricing discipline across most markets. Value Chain and Customer Dynamics: Carlsberg operates as an integrated player occupying core brewing, packaging, and distribution stages of the beverage value chain, capturing margins of 13% to 15% which represents the highest profit share along the chain.

The company faces pressure from consolidated European off-trade supermarkets that form large buying groups exerting strong pricing pressure on mainstream beer product segments.

However, in key growth markets, 95% of Carlsberg and industry volume is sold through traditional trade outlets, where the company maintains stronger negotiating leverage. Integrated beer and soft drink distribution creates significant shipping cost synergies through single-truck deliveries, with integrated beer and Pepsi distribution in Sweden delivering operating margins above average group levels.

The unified Carlsberg Britvic portfolio strengthens customer negotiation position by offering full multi-beverage choices, partially offsetting retail consolidation pressures. Supply Chain and Distribution Network: Carlsberg manages global sourcing through its centralized Carlsberg Supply Company AG subsidiary based in Switzerland and protects near-term gross profit margins by hedging aluminum and energy on rolling 12-to-15 month horizons.

Cans represent the largest packaging format at 38% of total volume mix, creating exposure to aluminum price volatility.

The company maintains diverse shipping networks and delivers directly to major retail chains but relies on distributors for traditional off-trade accounts in Western European operations.

Competitive Landscape and Innovation: Carlsberg competes against significantly larger peers, with Heineken maintaining the most premium portfolio at 35% of its volume in premium, compared to 29% for Carlsberg.

Carlsberg has a larger presence in lower-margin economy segments than Heineken, though this provides greater share gain optionality in emerging markets.

Anheuser-Busch InBev's no-alcohol beer portfolio outperformed the broader industry with a 27% quarterly revenue increase, setting a competitive benchmark for innovation.

Carlsberg agreed to purchase PepsiCo bottlers in Georgia and Armenia to build an integrated South Caucasus cluster, accelerating its soft drink scaling strategy.

Market Signals and Working Capital: Value chain participants confirm mixed demand signals, with glass supplier Verallia reporting that beer was the bright spot with rising volumes in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, while experts report that Southern European supermarkets increased promo volumes to 70% to maintain retail volume.

Carlsberg achieved a highly favorable negative cash conversion cycle of -116 days in fiscal year 2025, driven by Days Payable Outstanding remaining elevated at 201 days in 2025, showcasing dominant supplier bargaining power.

However, Carlsberg's Days Sales Outstanding rose to 31 days in 2025 from 24 days in 2024, signaling some customer leverage shift.

3.
Management Quality and Business Strength: Carlsberg displays high management quality and a strong competitive advantage supported by soft drinks, with CEO Jacob Aarup-Andersen raising organic revenue targets to 4% to 6% under the refreshed Accelerate SAIL strategy.

CEO Aarup-Andersen brings deep financial turnaround expertise from previous leadership roles at Goldman Sachs and ISS, and demonstrated commitment by buying DKK 10.8m of Carlsberg shares immediately upon joining.

The company has maintained exceptional execution discipline, with Carlsberg beating initial organic EBIT guidance by 300 to 870 basis points annually since 2016. The Carlsberg Foundation shields the company from hostile takeovers and supports long-term investment, providing strategic stability.

Carlsberg holds challenger positions across 66% of volumes, offering high market share gain optionality, compared to AB InBev which holds leader positions across 75% of volume.

Premium beer brands like Tuborg and Carlsberg consistently outperform mainstream beer segments, with premium Carlsberg brand volumes growing organically by 16% in the first half of 2026.

Capital return metrics demonstrate exceptional efficiency, with Carlsberg delivering an estimated tangible return on invested capital of 98% in fiscal year 2025, far exceeding peer Heineken's tangible return on invested capital of 28% over the same period.

New Pepsi franchises expand Carlsberg to 15 bottling markets across Europe and Asia by 2029, automatically widening distribution density advantages.

However, the company faces concentration risk, as high China volume concentration represents the single largest risk, with China accounting for only 3% of Heineken's total volumes by comparison. Long-Term Disruption and Portfolio Fit: Brewing facilities and logistics infrastructure create high barriers to entry for prospective sector entrants, while complex state-by-state excise policies and distribution permits favor established local market incumbents like Carlsberg.

The company faces secular headwinds from younger generations consuming less alcohol, driving a structural moderation trend across developed markets, with almost half of European Gen Z drinkers seeking to reduce their weekly alcohol intake.

Additionally, fast-growing spiritsbased ready-to-drink options cannibalize traditional beer consumption occasions in North America. Carlsberg has positioned its portfolio to address these trends through large non-beer exposure that insulates the group from beer volume declines in Western Europe, with integrating Britvic soft drinks expanding non-beer to one-third of pro forma group volume.

High-quality dealcoholisation technology allows Carlsberg to drive double-digit volume growth in alcohol-free brews, successfully defending social consumption occasions.

Expanded PepsiCo bottling franchises generate substantial distribution synergies across Central Asia, with the new $344 million Almaty facility supporting Central Asian growth.

4.
Single Most Important Strength: Carlsberg's industry-leading capital efficiency and integrated multi-beverage distribution model represents its most valuable competitive advantage.

The company delivered the best return on invested capital of the European brewers at 12% in fiscal year 2024, achieved a tangible return on invested capital of 98%, and delivered a return on invested capital excluding goodwill of 30.9%.

Integrated beer and Pepsi distribution delivers operating margins above average group levels in Norway and Sweden, with regional PepsiCo bottling partnerships creating substantial route-to-market cost synergies.

This combination of superior capital returns and structural distribution advantages creates a durable moat that compounds over time as new Pepsi franchises expand Carlsberg to 15 bottling markets across Europe and Asia by 2029, automatically widening the company's scale and density benefits.

5.
Single Most Important Weakness: Carlsberg's excessive geographic concentration in China represents the most significant structural vulnerability to the investment case.

High China volume concentration represents the single largest risk, with China accounting for 30% of total beer volume for Carlsberg in 2025, while China accounts for only 3% of Heineken's total volumes, reducing its geographic sensitivity.

This concentration exposes Carlsberg disproportionately to normalizing premium beer volume declines that would signal deteriorating competitive advantage, with weather disruptions and destocking expected to keep Chinese volumes subdued into late 2026.

The company faces the risk that severe consumer spending downturns could disrupt premium brand volume recovery and restrict future cash returns, with structural weaknesses in China potentially limiting medium-term organic growth below targeted ranges.

This single-market dependency undermines portfolio resilience and creates asymmetric downside risk that cannot be fully offset by the company's otherwise strong competitive positioning and execution capabilities.

Plausible problems to monitor

Carlsberg faces a structurally lower embedded beer volume growth rate of 0.3% compared to Heineken's 1.0% tailwind, reflecting its heavier concentration in mature European markets rather than faster-growing emerging economies. This geographic footprint disadvantage places Carlsberg at a persistent structural disadvantage relative to peers with better-balanced developed and emerging market exposure, limiting its organic volume expansion potential over the medium term.

The company exhibits dangerous geographic concentration risk with China representing 30% of total beer volume, far exceeding Heineken's 3% China exposure. This single-market dependency creates substantial earnings volatility from regional consumer sentiment shifts, weather disruptions, and distributor destocking cycles that disproportionately impact Carlsberg's consolidated performance relative to more geographically diversified brewing peers.

Carlsberg maintains a larger presence in lower-margin economy beer segments than premium-focused competitor Heineken, making its volume growth more reliant on challenger market share expansion rather than category mix enrichment. While Heineken positions 35% of its volume in premium segments, Carlsberg trails at 29%, indicating a less favorable brand portfolio composition that constrains per-hectoliter revenue realization and limits operating margin expansion potential.

Carlsberg recorded a net debt to EBITDA leverage ratio of 3.28x post-Britvic acquisition in 2025, compared to Anheuser-Busch InBev's 2.87x. This elevated balance sheet leverage restricts near-term capital allocation flexibility, delays the resumption of share buyback programs, and exposes the company to greater refinancing risks during periods of elevated interest rates compared to less-leveraged brewing peers. The loss of the UK San Miguel brewing contract creates a material 2% to 3% EBIT drag in fiscal 2025, highlighting Carlsberg's vulnerability to concentrated customer dependencies and contract renewal risks.

This revenue and profit headwind underscores the company's weaker customer retention capabilities relative to sector norms, particularly in mature Western European markets where competitive intensity and retailer consolidation pressures continue to intensify.
