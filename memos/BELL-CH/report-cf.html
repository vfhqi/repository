EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Bell Food Group delivered its best half-year result in company history during H1 2026, posting a record net profit of CHF 55.4 million that exceeded the prior-year period by 21.23 percent, while consolidated EBIT reached CHF 76.4 million, representing a 15.41 percent increase compared to H1 2025.

The company reported net revenue of CHF 2,413.3 million in the first half of 2026, supported by organic growth of 1.9 percent that successfully overcame a CHF 19.6 million negative foreign exchange effect and a CHF 16.3 million inorganic contraction.

Management provided an optimistic full-year 2026 EBIT guidance range of CHF 180 million to CHF 195 million under stable operating conditions, reflecting high confidence that strategic and operational focus is proving highly effective.

The group achieved a significant cash flow inflection during the first half of 2026, with free cash flow reaching CHF 40.5 million compared to a deficit of CHF -24.2 million in H1 2025, driven by positive working capital movements of CHF 39.1 million.

STRUCTURAL DRIVERS AND CYCLICAL FORCES Bell Food Group is executing a multi-year Swiss modernization program centered on the Oensingen logistics and slicing center, which began phased commissioning in June 2026 and is expected to bolster domestic operating profit margins starting in 2027 by consolidating external storage capacities and deploying state-of-the-art automation.

Hilcona is executing the third phase of its multi-year facility development plan in Schaan, integrating a modernized picking platform and automatic high-bay warehouse that will increase capacity for super-fresh convenience products and support segment EBIT margins over the next three years.

Eisberg divested its underperforming Eastern European subsidiaries in Poland, Hungary, and Romania to Green Factory in 2025, generating a one-off EBIT gain of CHF 11.3 million and eliminating low-margin regional drag on group earnings.

Bell International successfully integrated German air-dried ham specialist Hermann Wein in early 2026, which is expected to generate immediately accretive earnings contributions and strengthen the group's dominant position in the European cured ham market.

LATEST RESULTS AND OPERATIONAL PROGRESS Bell Switzerland posted net revenue growth of 2.22 percent to CHF 1,202.9 million in H1 2026, supported by a 2.05 percent increase in sales volume and representing the highly profitable domestic backbone of the entire group.

Hubers/Sütag delivered exceptionally strong organic revenue growth of 8.3 percent to CHF 381.4 million during the first half of 2026, driven by solid demand for regional turkey and confirming that consumer preference for localized poultry continues to strengthen absolute business volumes.

Hilcona grew its net revenue by 4.06 percent to CHF 289.6 million in H1 2026, propelled by positive retail channel momentum and innovative plantbased product launches under The Green Mountain brand that aim to capture high-margin vegetarian market share.

Eisberg recorded an organic revenue decline of 4.3 percent to CHF 138.0 million following deliberate portfolio adjustments, while total segment revenue dropped by 29.81 percent due to the completed sale of Eastern European sites, a strategic move designed to improve the convenience division's profitability.

Hügli's net revenue contracted organically by 6.2 percent to CHF 180.5 million because of planned site and range consolidations, including the closure of its Redditch facility in the United Kingdom to relocate production to Germany and the Czech Republic, a restructuring expected to improve long-term production efficiency once completed by late 2027.

GUIDANCE CREDIBILITY AND CAPITAL ALLOCATION Bell Food Group has historically demonstrated a conservative habit of meeting or beating its guidance, as evidenced by management lowering its full-year 2026 investment projection to CHF 300 million from the original CHF 340 million level due to rigorous cost management and tighter project discipline.

The company's 2026 EBIT targets rely on successful Oensingen facility commissioning, stable personnel costs, and effective raw material cost pass-through, with a comfortable safety margin of approximately CHF 15 million in the guidance range before forward earnings per share fall below consensus.

Management plans to reduce annual capital expenditure to a baseline level of CHF 170 million after 2026, focused strictly on replacement projects and regular maintenance, a substantial investment drop that will release significant future cash resources and support the group's net debt ratio target below 2.5 times EBITDA.

Bell Food Group recorded a marked turnaround in operating free cash flow to CHF 4.1 million in the first half of 2026, representing a solid improvement from the negative operating free cash flow of CHF -51.3 million reported in H1 2024, which will underpin upcoming dividend payouts and accelerate balance sheet deleveraging.

PEER POSITIONING AND COMPETITIVE CONTEXT Bell Food Group's H1 2026 organic revenue momentum of 1.9 percent outpaced Aryzta AG, which posted negative organic growth of 2.7 percent, and Orior AG, which recorded a negative organic contraction of 5.7 percent, though it lagged behind dairy peer Emmi Group, which delivered stronger organic growth of 3.6 percent in the same period.

Bell Food Group operates with elevated inventory requirements totaling CHF 534.3 million at the end of fiscal year 2025, which constrains cash conversion efficiency compared to asset-light food processors, though disciplined working capital management helped deliver a CHF 39.1 million working capital cash inflow in H1 2026.

The company trades at an EV/EBITDA multiple of 5.4 in mid- 2026, representing a discount to Northern European poultry peer Scandi Standard AB, which commands a higher multiple of 10.2, a valuation gap that operational efficiency improvements from completed Swiss investments should narrow over the next 18 months.

Bell Food Group delivered superior organic growth and financial stability compared with closest Swiss peer Orior AG, which was forced to revise its full-year revenue guidance downward because of Dutch customer losses, confirming that Bell Food Group remains highly competitive in core channels and provides a more stable baseline for earnings per share.

EXTERNAL FORCES AND MARKET DYNAMICS Depressed consumer confidence across European markets causes a pronounced shift in product choice toward lower-priced entry-level tiers, which will ultimately dilute consolidated gross profit margins as such structural migration to cheaper ranges carries thinner margins over the next eighteen months.

European retail markets are highly consolidated and dominated by a small number of powerful supermarket chains, with retailer buying alliances exerting intense pressure on food processors to lower wholesale product prices, a dynamic that is highly likely to squeeze future operating profit margins unless Bell can offer highly differentiated product lines.

The persistent strength of the Swiss Franc against the Euro heavily influences Switzerland's domestic economic performance by stimulating shopping tourism as Swiss residents travel across borders to purchase cheaper groceries, directly pressuring domestic Swiss retail volumes and threatening to drag on Bell's domestic meat revenues over the next three years.

Elevated food inflation across Europe continues to exceed general consumer price inflation, driven by inflated costs for raw materials, animal proteins, and energy, which significantly burdens household purchasing power and will restrict premium convenience segment growth in key Western European markets.

Bell Food Group faces volatile supply conditions for cattle, pigs, and poultry in its core European markets, with domestic cattle and poultry supplies tight while an oversupply of pigs pressured regional pork prices, volatility that directly impacts meat segment gross margins depending on the species sales mix.

The Board of Directors proposed a consistent dividend of CHF 7 per share for the 2025 financial year, matching the CHF 7 per share distributed for the 2024 financial year, demonstrating management's commitment to shareholder returns despite volatile reported GAAP net profits.

TOP RISKS AND EXECUTION CHALLENGES Elevated operational start-up costs and facility commissioning delays in Oensingen pose an immediate downside risk to near-term earnings per share, as the complex ramp-up is expected to generate up to CHF 20 million in start-up costs, and technical bottlenecks would prevent the realization of planned cost synergies and drag down near-term operating margins.

Extensive production relocations and geographic restructuring outside of Switzerland introduce massive operational execution risks, with Hügli's long-life convenience operations relocating from the United Kingdom to Germany and the Czech Republic triggering a CHF 60 million extraordinary write-down and potentially causing unforeseen supply chain bottlenecks.

Severe animal disease outbreaks such as African Swine Fever or avian influenza present severe supply chain and export risks, with infected wild boars triggering immediate non-EU export bans on European pork products that cause local oversupply and would pressure Bell's international pig farming margins.

Compounding macroeconomic and regulatory pressures could simultaneously disrupt Bell Food Group's core markets and severely depress earnings if intense retail price wars in Germany coincide with escalating labor costs, severe livestock epidemics, or unfavorable weather conditions that restrict raw material availability and lead to immediately inflated procurement costs.

Rising non-operating costs and persistent capital commitments could delay the projected free cash flow inflection, as Bell Food Group expects operating depreciation in connection with its capital investments to increase by around CHF 30 million by 2029, and if additional growth investments exceed the guided CHF 170 million baseline, the group's net debt ratio will overshoot the targeted 2.5x threshold.
