Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Sanofi's transition to a pure-play biopharmaceutical company heightens its financial reliance on its core Dupixent, ALTUVIIIO, and vaccine franchises.
  2. The company's earnings per share and risk profile between 2027 and 2030 will be heavily dictated by the execution of these franchises and strategic pipeline expansion.

Defining the Strategic Business Model

1.
Critical inquiry: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)? Start from these possible dynamics: Start from these possible dynamics: (1) Dupixent sales growth raises the Regeneron profit-share expense and the US dollar share of sales, limiting operating leverage and raising currency sensitivity (balancing); (2) chronic patients accumulate on therapy, so volume grows even as new-patient share falls, while competitors' consumer advertising enlarges the treated market (reinforcing); (3) late-stage pipeline failures increase reliance on external late-stage and commercial acquisitions, which raise acquisition prices, lower returns on capital and the valuation multiple, and narrow future options (reinforcing negative); (4) repeated reorganisations raise attrition and damage launch execution, which leads to further restructuring (reinforcing negative; evidence in expert calls from 2025 and 2026 on the US specialty organisation and the Tzield relaunch); (5) holding R&D flat raises nearterm margins but thins the replacement pipeline (balancing over time); (6) a low share-price multiple makes buybacks highly accretive but also makes share-funded acquisitions unattractive, pushing toward debt-funded deals; (7) the expanded Regeneron alliance (October 2026) re-using the Dupixent sales force and payer relationships for next-generation long-acting antibodies (reinforcing if the successors arrive before biosimilars)."
2.
Strategic transition: In April 2025, Sanofi completed the sale of a 50% controlling stake in its consumer healthcare business, Opella, to Clayton, Dubilier & Rice for around €10 billion in cash. This milestone marked Sanofi's emergence as a pure-play biopharmaceutical company focusing on innovative medicines. This structural shift enables management to allocate capital more dynamically to high-margin pipelines.
3.
Extensive reliance: Sanofi's flagship immunology drug Dupixent generated sales of €5,687 million in Q3 2026, representing a 34.8% growth rate. Management expects Dupixent sales to reach approximately €25 billion by 2030, cementing its position as the central engine of the company. The massive scale of this single asset means its future trajectory will heavily dictate operating margins.
4.
Accelerating momentum: Sanofi's rare disease franchise is led by ALTUVIIIO, which generated €408 million in Q3 2026 sales primarily driven by the US market. Meanwhile, the vaccine franchise represents another pillar of growth targeting €9 billion in sales by 2030. Together, these assets must diversify revenues to offset the impending loss of Dupixent exclusivity.

Midterm Outlook and Strategic Risk

5.
Positive compounding: Morgan Stanley forecasts a base case of approximately 5% revenue and 7% business EPS compound annual growth rate for Sanofi between 2027 and 2030. This steady growth is expected to be driven by Dupixent expansion and cost-control measures. However, this outlook relies heavily on the successful execution of clinical programs and subsequent product launches.

Section 2 – Dupixent Profit Share, USD Sensitivity, and Chronic Patient Accumulation

Section summary
  1. The full repayment of the Sanofi development balance has shifted a larger share of Dupixent economics to Regeneron, reducing Sanofi's operating leverage.
  2. Severe US dollar concentration exposes Sanofi to intense currency sensitivity, despite the drug's remarkable commercial expansion across multiple immunology indications.
  3. Chronic patient accumulation creates a highly resilient revenue tailwind for Dupixent that is further reinforced by competitive marketing efforts.

Loops

Profit Share Mechanics and Leverage

1.
Accelerated payments: Sanofi completed the full repayment of its development balance to Regeneron in the second quarter of 2026. This repayment means that Regeneron now collects its full share of collaboration profits from Dupixent sales. Consequently, Regeneron's share of profits as a percentage of net sales rose to 38% in the third quarter of 2026, compared to 29% in the prior year period. This transition increases the profit-sharing outflow from Sanofi, dampening its operating margin expansion.
2.
Divided returns: The longstanding collaboration agreement dictates that Sanofi and Regeneron equally split profits on Dupixent sales within the United States. For sales outside the United States, the parties share profits on a sliding scale starting at 35% and ending at 45% for Regeneron. Because the US market represents the vast majority of Dupixent revenues, Sanofi faces a persistent 50% profit-share leak on its most profitable geography. This structural profit division structurally limits Sanofi's ability to achieve full operating leverage as the franchise scales.

4.
Constrained margins: Sanofi reported a strong 9% sales beat in the third quarter of 2026, which was heavily driven by a 10% outperformance from Dupixent. However, the company's other operating expenses rose by 28% due to higher profit-sharing payments to Regeneron. While business operating income still beat consensus, the automatic scaling of profit-share expenses highlights how collaboration terms restrict operating leverage. This balancing feedback loop ensures that top-line gains are continuously shared, preventing Sanofi from capturing the full margin benefit of its commercial success.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that Sanofi's gross margin benefits from operational improvements and gross-to-net adjustments, which partially offsets the profit-sharing drag. Favourable US gross-to-net adjustments contributed to a significant gross profit beat in the third quarter of 2026. Additionally, ongoing cost discipline in research and development and selling expenses has allowed Sanofi to raise its operating income and guidance despite higher collaboration payouts.

Geographic Concentration and Currency Sensitivity

6.
High concentration: Dupixent sales exhibit an extreme geographic concentration in the United States, which generated $5,064.1 mm of the drug's $6,540.5 mm total global sales in the third quarter of 2026. This heavy concentration means the United States accounts for over 77% of Dupixent revenues. Because the US dollar dominates the revenue mix of Sanofi's most valuable asset, the company's financial performance is exceptionally sensitive to fluctuations in the EUR/USD exchange rate.
7.
Negative exposure: Sanofi expects currency movements to act as a headwind if the foreign exchange rates from October 2026 persist. Specifically, management projects a 1% currency drag on both total sales and core earnings per share. This negative exposure is amplified by the fact that US dollar-denominated Dupixent sales must be translated back to Euros for reporting. Consequently, any depreciation of the US dollar directly thins Sanofi's reported earnings growth.
8.
Adverse comparisons: Compared to peers like AstraZeneca, which maintains a more globally diversified revenue stream, Sanofi's high reliance on US Dupixent sales elevates its vulnerability to unilateral currency swings. For instance, while AstraZeneca rebuilt its pipeline through a reinvestment-led loop that diversified its global launch footprint, Sanofi remains tethered to a single US-centric immunology asset. This geographic imbalance exposes Sanofi's business earnings per share to greater volatility than its international pharmaceutical peers.
9.
Contrary evidence: Historical prescription data suggests that strong volume growth in international territories is beginning to expand, with OUS Dupixent sales rising 19% to $1,476.4 mm in the third quarter of 2026. This international expansion is supported by recent regulatory approvals in China and Europe across multiple indications. However, because international sales are shared on a sliding scale where Regeneron's share can rise to 45%, OUS growth does not fully liberate Sanofi from collaboration margin constraints.

Chronic Patient Accumulation and Market Expansion

10.
Resilient compounding: Dupixent benefit flows from its clinical role as a chronic therapy for type 2 inflammatory diseases, where patients remain on treatment indefinitely. Over 1.5 mm active patients globally are currently receiving Dupixent across nine approved indications. This massive patient base means that volume grows through accumulation, even as the share of brand-new patient starts begins to moderate. This reinforcing loop provides a highly predictable, long-term revenue stream that supports Sanofi's reported growth.
11.
Expanding markets: Competitors entering the atopic dermatitis and asthma space spend heavily on direct-to- consumer advertising, which serves to enlarge the total treated market rather than steal Dupixent's share. Clinicians report that Dupixent's established efficacy and long safety history make it the preferred first-line biologic choice once patients seek treatment. Consequently, competitive marketing by other pharma companies acts as a reinforcing loop that ultimately increases Dupixent's prescription volume.
12.
Historical precedents: AbbVie's management of its blockbuster Humira demonstrates how a dominant immunology asset can maintain a massive revenue tail through market expansion and rebate walls. Although Humira's core US composition patent expired in 2016, AbbVie secured settlements that delayed biosimilar entry until 2023. By aggressively reinvesting Humira's cash flows into next-generation immunology assets Skyrizi and Rinvoq, AbbVie successfully navigated its patent cliff. This precedent highlights how a pharmaceutical company can leverage a dominant market position to fund its post-LOE transitions.
13.
Contrary evidence: Some medical experts note that emerging competitors, such as Pfizer's tilrekimig, are designing phase 3 trials specifically powered to demonstrate clinical superiority over Dupixent. In phase 2 trials, tilrekimig demonstrated placebo-adjusted itch reduction benefits of up to 44%, compared to Dupixent's historical 26% to 29% range. If these competitor assets successfully prove superior efficacy in head-to-head trials, they could disrupt the chronic patient accumulation loop and trigger market share erosion.
14.
Durable cash: The combination of chronic patient accumulation and competitor-driven market expansion ensures that Dupixent will remain a highly durable cash-generative engine for Sanofi through 2030. Despite the profit-share leaks to Regeneron and foreign exchange volatility, this immunology franchise provides a highly visible foundation for earnings. Therefore, the primary driver of Sanofi's future EPS growth will be its ability to use this stable base to execute strategic moves.
15.
Other side of the story: Skeptical investors point out that raising Dupixent peak sales expectations to €25 billion by 2030 actually deepens Sanofi's portfolio concentration risk. This extreme dependence on a single drug makes the stock trade at a persistent 20% to 26% valuation discount compared to its European pharmaceutical peers. If Sanofi fails to establish a viable successor or execute large-scale late-stage acquisitions, the eventual biosimilar erosion will create a significant earnings risk.

By utilizing debt rather than undervalued shares, Sanofi can secure accretive commercial assets that immediately support earnings. Section 4 – Repeated Reorganizations, Sales Force Attrition, and Launch Execution

Section summary
  1. Repeated restructurings at Sanofi have triggered extensive talent attrition and field-force disruption, directly compromising early launch execution for new assets.
  2. AbbVie's highly coordinated commercial playbooks for Skyrizi and Rinvoq demonstrate the immense value of commercial continuity and centralized execution structures.
  3. Roche's post-2018 transition successfully utilized a rapid launch engine to offset multi-billion franc biosimilar erosion, highlighting an alternative model of commercial agility.

Loops

Sanofi Attrition and Tzield Relaunch

1.
Disruptive shifts: Sanofi's frequent waves of organizational changes after the pandemic created deep job insecurity among field representatives. This disruption led directly to a loss of experienced talent with long-standing customer relationships across the United States. Ultimately, the resulting morale drop eroded commercial execution and slowed down product pull-through.
2.
Severe cuts: Sanofi executed a highly disruptive move by laying off its entire pediatric specialty sales force. This organizational change left strategic account managers without any field reps to secure pull-through in key health systems. Consequently, these repeated structural reorganizations fractured internal commercial capabilities and diminished the company's vaccine market power.
3.
Execution stumbles: Sanofi's acquisition and relaunch of Tzield suffered from severe integration friction and a lack of pre-market preparation. Field directors reported that internal corporate secrecy and functional delays severely hampered field readiness and delayed the setup of essential screening programs. Consequently, Tzield recorded a very slow commercial ramp with 2025 sales reaching only $73 mm (calculated).
4.
Declining capability: Sanofi experienced severe talent attrition among its medical affairs and scientific leadership following global cost-cutting restructurings. The company routinely replaced senior scientific leaders with less experienced personnel to protect its short-term operating margins. This loss of deep industry expertise compromised Sanofi's relationship with key opinion leaders and disrupted localized product pre-launches.
5.
Contrary evidence: Some industry reports suggest that Sanofi's commercial execution remains highly resilient in Europe and other international markets. The company upgraded its full-year 2026 sales guidance to a low-teens percentage, driven by strong short-term commercial execution. This performance indicates that despite local specialty care restructurings, global top-line momentum has not been fully derailed.

The company created highly coordinated, cross-functional brand teams that leveraged a centralized data platform to streamline field force execution. This structured focus prevented field-force attrition and maximized physician engagement during major product rollouts.

Advanced systems: AbbVie integrated critical field data from Veeva, IQVIA, and SAP into a single centralized database. This technological change allowed leadership to implement standardized execution metrics across international sales divisions. This data-driven execution model supported the rapid and highly synchronized launches of Skyrizi and Rinvoq.

Targeted scaling: AbbVie actively expanded its dedicated dermatology and gastroenterology sales forces to support new product indications. This commercial expansion ensured that representatives maintained a dominant share of voice in highly competitive markets. Consequently, this aggressive commercial structure drove massive momentum, pushing combined Skyrizi and Rinvoq sales past $25.90 bn in 2025.

Roche Rapid Launch Transition

10.
Erosion pressures: Roche faced a massive commercial challenge from 2018 to 2020 as cheap biosimilars eroded its legacy oncology franchise. Branded sales for MabThera, Herceptin, and Avastin fell by a combined CHF 5.10 bn in 2020 alone. This massive cliff forced the company to execute an extremely rapid commercial transition to protect its operating margins.

In 2020, Ocrevus generated CHF 4.30 bn in sales, while Hemlibra reached CHF 2.20 bn and Tecentriq hit CHF 2.70 bn. These new launches contributed CHF 3.90 bn in incremental sales, shielding the firm from severe top-line contraction.
12.
Agile execution: Jefferies Research indicates that Roche's rapid commercialization of new blockbusters successfully sustained its operating margins. By avoiding disruptive internal restructurings, Roche preserved its specialized commercial capabilities and accelerated regional uptake. This precedent demonstrates that a stable, highly focused commercial team can successfully navigate steep blockbuster loss of exclusivity.
13.
Other side of the story: Skeptical investors point out that while Sanofi's restructuring has created short-term commercial friction, it represents a necessary pivot to match competitor margins. The company is aggressively shedding low-margin general medicines to concentrate resources on high-value specialty launches. If this lean specialty structure stabilizes, it could ultimately deliver superior operating margins and support long-term earnings per share growth through 2030.

Section 5 – Capital Allocation, Buybacks vs Debt M&A, and the Expanded Regeneron

Section summary
  1. Sanofi's discounted valuation multiple of seven point five times EV to EBITDA makes equity-funded acquisitions highly dilutive while increasing the earnings accretion of debt-financed buybacks.
  2. The expanded October 2026 Regeneron alliance leverages the Dupixent commercial engine to transition patients to long-acting antibodies before biosimilar erosion begins.
  3. Competitive long-acting assets like AbbVie's zumilokibart are entering late-stage trials years ahead of the alliance's pipeline, threatening its pre-emptive launch strategy.

Alliance Loops

Valuation Multiples and Capital Allocation

1.
Depressed multiples: Sanofi trades at a discounted valuation multiple of approximately 7.5x EV/EBITDA, which sits well below the broader European large-cap pharmaceutical sector average. low valuation multiple makes the issuance of new equity to fund large-scale acquisitions highly unattractive and financially dilutive for current shareholders. Consequently, management is heavily deterred from executing stock-funded transactions, which structurally narrows their strategic options. This balancing loop forces the company to rely on its substantial cash flows and debt capacity to secure external assets.
2.
Accretive repurchases: The company's low valuation multiple of approximately 8.7x estimated P/E makes share buybacks highly accretive to earnings per share compared to historical peers. Sanofi initiated a €5 billion share buyback program to fully offset the dilution associated with the spin-off of its Opella consumer health division. This financial engineering tactic provides immediate support to business earnings per share while the pipeline undergoes a turnaround. Management continues to balance these buybacks with strategic bolt-on opportunities to sustain long-term growth.
3.
Inorganic capacity: Sanofi's strong balance sheet and low leverage of 0.9x net debt to EBITDA provide the firm with substantial debt-funded acquisition capacity of €20 billion to €30 billion. Management intends to maintain its solid credit rating while aggressively deploying this non-dilutive capital into late-stage and commercial-stage assets. This conservative debt-led strategy allows the company to pursue attractive therapeutic additions without resorting to expensive equity issues. By utilizing debt instead of undervalued shares, Sanofi can immediately secure accretive revenues to support its mid-term earnings.

The Expanded Regeneron Immunology Alliance

4.
Expanded alliance: Sanofi and Regeneron expanded their long-standing collaboration in October 2026 by adding next-generation, long-acting antibodies targeting the type 2 inflammation pathway. Under the terms of the revised agreement, Regeneron received an upfront payment of $1 billion, with the potential for up to $7 billion in future milestone payments. The partners will equally share all future development and commercialization costs, while splitting any resulting global profits 50/50. This commercial arrangement is highly favorable for Regeneron compared to the original Dupixent profit-share model.
5.
Commercial synergy: The newly expanded alliance is designed to directly reuse the established Dupixent sales force and extensive payer relationships. By leveraging these existing commercial channels, Sanofi can seamlessly transition its massive active patient base of over 1.5 million people to next-generation therapies. This proactive commercial reuse ensures that the substantial brand equity and commercial infrastructure built over two decades do not dissipate. This reinforcing feedback loop is designed to secure long-term earnings per share stability by protecting the core immunology franchise.
6.
Pipeline progression: The lead clinical asset in the expanded collaboration is REGN20423, a long-acting anti- IL-13 antibody currently in Phase 1 development for atopic dermatitis. The alliance also includes three preclinical assets–a long-acting IL-4/IL-13 bispecific, a long-acting IL-4 antibody, and a long-acting IL-4Rα antibody–which are expected to enter Phase 1 trials in 2027. Dosing healthy volunteers with REGN20423 has demonstrated a prolonged half-life that could support convenient three-monthly or six-monthly maintenance dosing. This extended dosing profile directly targets the market's demand for more convenient therapeutic options.

Biosimilar Defenses and Competitive Threats

7.
Exclusivity runway: Sanofi and Regeneron aim to launch their long-acting anti-IL-13 antibody prior to the entry of Dupixent biosimilars, which are expected to erode the US market starting in March 2031. Management intends to utilize registrational clinical data to delay biosimilar competitors by one to two years, stretching the exclusivity window into 2033. This defensive lifecycle strategy is critical because approximately €20 billion of Dupixent's peak sales will face loss of exclusivity. If the alliance can successfully transition patients to these next-generation antibodies, Sanofi can protect its long-term operating margin and mitigate severe post- 2031 revenue cliffs.
8.
Competitive speed: AbbVie's mid-2026 acquisition of Apogee for approximately $11 billion has intensified the competitive threat by bringing zumilokibart, a long-acting anti-IL-13 antibody, into Phase 3 development. Zumilokibart has an engineered 75-day half-life supporting quarterly or semi-annual dosing, and is positioned to launch as early as 2029. This early launch timeline gives AbbVie a massive head start of at least one to two years over Sanofi's REGN20423 asset. Consequently, AbbVie's mid-2026 acquisition of Apogee represents a significant threat to Sanofi's ability to capture the long-acting market.
