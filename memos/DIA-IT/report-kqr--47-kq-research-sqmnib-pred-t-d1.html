Section 2 – Plausibility of the 2030 Long-Term Plan Against the Delivery Record

Section summary
  1. DiaSorin's transition to its 2030 long-term plan faces substantial skepticism due to its history of guidance revisions and delayed platform rollouts.
  2. Recent acceleration in molecular diagnostics provides positive near-term momentum but remains a small portion of the overall group business.

Tracking the Execution Record

1.
Historical baseline metrics: DiaSorin recorded €1.15 bn of revenue and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 33% in 2025. The company has historically relied on its core immunodiagnostics segment to drive overall profitability. However, the acquisition of Luminex in 2021 introduced significant margin dilution due to Luminex's lower operating margin profile. This baseline highlights the difficulty of maintaining premium operating margins while expanding molecular diagnostics.
2.
Strategic targets shift: DiaSorin originally established ambitious medium-term targets during its December 2023 Investor Day. These targets promised a high single-digit revenue CAGR and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 36% to 37% by 2027. However, these plans were abandoned in May 2026 when management presented its revised 2030 business plan. This shift underscores the unpredictability of the molecular testing transition.
3.
Past guidance cuts: DiaSorin surprised investors in November 2025 by significantly lowering its full-year guidance. The company cut its 2025 ex-COVID revenue growth outlook from 8% down to approximately 5%. This revision was driven by sustained headwinds in China and an unexpected slowdown in European testing volumes. These repeated downgrades have forced analysts to place a valuation discount on management forecasts.
4.
Contrary evidence: DiaSorin successfully met its immediate financial expectations by delivering solid results for the full year 2025. The company reported FY25 revenues of €1.19 bn, which represented 5% constantexchange-rate growth excluding the COVID business. Adjusted EBITDA reached €394 mm, maintaining a stable margin of 33%. This delivery in line with the revised guidance suggests that near-term operational stabilization is achievable.

Consequently, the delayed timeline has deferred meaningful revenue contributions to the outer years.

Section summary

Specialty Lock-In and Menu Breadth

1.
High-value lock-in: DiaSorin maintains strong customer retention through its closed-analyzer razor-razorblade business model. This model ensures that over 80% of sales are generated from proprietary reagents run on its closed LIAISON systems. This specialty immunoassay lock-in provides stable cash flows that insulate core earnings from pricing pressure.
2.
Selective niche positioning: DiaSorin focuses commercial efforts on clinically complex, low-volume specialty assays to avoid direct commodity competition. The company holds a 50% to 70% market share in calprotectin testing and sees room for further market share gains. This differentiated strategy allows the firm to sustain premium group profitability despite headwinds in other segments.
3.
Strategic partner leverage: DiaSorin leverages established partnerships to expand its specialty menu and automate workflows. Its automated latent tuberculosis QuantiFERON assay is partnered with QIAGEN to drive high-throughput testing. This collaboration has secured steady double-digit growth in U.S. and European hospitals, supporting solid immunodiagnostics performance.
4.
Broad peer differentiation: In contrast to large automated diagnostics peers like Siemens Healthineers and Abbott, DiaSorin operates in highly specialized segments. Large peers remain heavily exposed to high-volume routine chemistry tests that face severe pricing pressure. By maintaining an exclusive focus on rare pathologies, DiaSorin preserves superior commercial pricing power.

  2. Established incumbents Cepheid and bioMérieux dominate with massive installed bases of over 60,000 and 28,500 instruments respectively.

Molecular Testing

The platform expanded its global installed base from 20,000 systems in 2019 to over 60,000 systems by late 2024. This massive footprint represents the largest worldwide installed base for a single sample-to-answer molecular testing system. This immense scale enables Cepheid to maintain formidable commercial entry barriers for late entrants.
2.
Rapid expansion: bioMérieux has scaled its point-of-care SPOTFIRE platform exceptionally quickly since its official commercial launch in mid-2023. The system's installed base grew to nearly 8,200 instruments by late September 2026. This rapid deployment represents a massive 60% expansion compared to the prior year's platform footprint. Such rapid scaling shows that prioritizing highly efficient distribution channels drives immediate commercial traction.

Operational and Commercial Factors of Success

5.
Broad menu: Broad assay menus represent the single most critical factor for driving long-term clinical platform adoption. For instance, Cepheid's GeneXpert offers a market-leading portfolio of over 30 clinical tests spanning respiratory and sexual health. In contrast, late entrants often experience slow sales because their initial launch menus are highly restricted. Laboratories refuse to purchase expensive instruments unless they support broadly multifunctional testing capabilities across diverse panels.
6.
Rapid turnaround: Outstanding processing speed serves as a vital differentiator for point-of-care testing systems. For example, bioMérieux's SPOTFIRE platform delivers comprehensive 15-plex panel results in approximately 15 minutes. This rapid testing capability allows physicians to make immediate, actionable treatment decisions during a single patient visit. Systems with slower processing times fail to secure critical outpatient clinical prioritization over traditional lab testing.
7.
Menu expansion: Continuous clinical menu expansion is highly necessary to maintain robust system utilization and reagent sales. Danaher has driven sustained double-digit growth in non-respiratory molecular sales by launching novel multiplex panels. Similarly, bioMérieux plans to expand its SPOTFIRE settings by launching a vaginal panel in early 2027. Expanding the testing menu is vital for stabilizing recurring consumables revenue during weak winter respiratory seasons.

Pitfalls of Delayed Entry and Integration Challenges

8.
Narrow menu: GenMark's historical ePlex platform illustrates the commercial dangers of launching with a highly restricted assay portfolio. The platform struggled to gain rapid market share because it lacked critical gastrointestinal and sepsis panels at launch. This menu limitation allowed established competitors to entrench themselves before GenMark could expand its clinical offerings. Delayed platform rollouts severely restrict a company's ability to capture highly lucrative hospital contracts early.
9.
Integration friction: Strategic diagnostics acquisitions frequently suffer from severe execution and regulatory delay risks. Roche's acquisition of GenMark in 2021 was intended to rapidly accelerate ePlex platform adoption. However, Roche subsequently faced a one-year regulatory extension for launching its ePlex gastrointestinal and meningitis panels. This delay demonstrates that assimilating complex molecular technologies requires more timeline buffer than management models.
10.
High costs: High operating costs and expensive consumable cartridges represent major barriers to widespread clinical platform adoption. Laboratories hesitate to adopt advanced molecular platforms because payer reimbursement rates remain highly restricted. Successful diagnostics companies must lower manufacturing costs to maintain high margins despite severe reimbursement cuts. Late entrants with expensive platforms face diminished customer conversion rates in price-sensitive settings.

This precedent shows that combining advanced technology with global distribution is essential for achieving commercial scale.

Large laboratory networks heavily favor standardizing their molecular diagnostics on a single major vendor's system. Consequently, late entrants are typically placed as the third or fourth instrument on the lab bench. This low prioritization severely limits their commercial pricing and bargaining power during contract renewals.

Cepheid improved its profitability from break-even in 2016 to over 30% operating margins under Danaher's ownership. This dramatic improvement was driven by standardizing testing across large hospital networks and expanding menu utilization. Transitioning to a highmargin model requires sustained long-term capital commitment from parent organizations.
