Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The Telia-ICE Norwegian RAN-sharing joint venture terms are partially resolvable through public disclosures and peer consensus, but the SEK 270m annual EBITDA restoration target by 2028 remains an uncorroborated Telia-side estimate unconfirmed by Lyse Group financial reporting, leaving the investment thesis dependent on operational execution risk rather than contractually guaranteed outcomes. The joint venture structure establishes a 50/50 Joint Operation holding 100% of both operators' mobile radio access network infrastructure while preserving full commercial independence through separate core networks, with the joint entity functioning as a wholesale vendor selling capacity back to both partners based strictly on actual network usage rates. While Telia disclosures confirm the equal ownership framework and capacity-based pricing mechanics, the one-off balancing compensation payment from ICE to account for Telia's substantially larger initial network footprint remains unquantified in public filings, creating valuation uncertainty around initial site rationalization costs.

The financial target trajectory centers on Telia management's projection to restore SEK 270m annual EBITDA by 2028 expanding to SEK 400m+ beyond 2028, yet Lyse Group annual filings confirm joint venture principles but omit specific cash allocation figures, indicating the SEK 270m target represents a unilateral Telia estimate rather than a bilaterally agreed cash-flow allocation.

This restoration aim seeks to recover the historical SEK 380m to SEK 400m annual EBITDA contraction Telia experienced after ICE transferred its national roaming contract to Telenor in mid-2025, but if site consolidation delays occur, network opex savings will stall and leave Telia unable to recover wholesale margin losses on schedule.

The Norwegian turnaround contribution proves material to group-level capital efficiency objectives, as reaching Telia's 12% Return on Capital Employed threshold by 2028 requires SEK 2bn in cumulative net cost reductions across operations, with the SEK 270m EBITDA restoration from Norway providing over 13% of the additional cost reduction needed to hit group ROCE targets. DNB Carnegie equity research incorporates the SEK 270m Norwegian EBITDA uplift directly into its 2028 forecast and lifts its target price to SEK 50, yet this analyst confidence relies on management guidance absent third-party corroboration from ICE parent Lyse Energi. Competitive landscape dynamics introduce structural execution risk as Lyse Group controls a formidable communications footprint through Altibox with a 31% landline broadband market share alongside ICE holding a 15% mobile subscriber share, while Lyse poses a severe structural threat by cross-selling ICE mobile subscriptions into its 33% fixed broadband customer base where upselling mobile into fiber relationships proves historically easier than reverse migration.

Intense promotional campaigns across Norwegian B2C mobile increased subscriber rotation and compressed retail ARPU expansion during 2026, with ICE escalating promotional pressure by extending discounted pricing onto unlimited data tariffs, raising the risk that if Telenor responds to subscriber losses by initiating a broad retail price war, margin erosion across all three network operators will outweigh any joint venture capex synergies.

Operational execution track record introduces legitimate skepticism as Telia's historic cost reduction programs frequently missed net targets, with the 2021 restructuring target of SEK 2bn savings by 2023 resulting in a net opex increase of SEK 1.1bn due to soaring Nordic energy costs and broader inflationary headwinds, demonstrating that external cost pressures can overwhelm gross efficiency gains. However, the 2024-2026 change program demonstrates improved execution discipline through completing an unprecedented workforce restructuring by eliminating 3,000 positions representing 15% of total headcount to generate SEK 2.6bn in annual run-rate savings, providing tangible evidence of management's capacity to deliver structural cost reductions. joint venture parameters around balancing payments, capacity tariffs, and decommissioning schedules remain undisclosed trade secrets, with the exact asset valuation gap between Telia's larger mobile footprint and ICE's network remaining unquantified and the precise capacity usage tariff formula charged by the joint operation back to both partners undisclosed, making it impossible to model traffic divergence risks or validate management's EBITDA restoration pathway through public information alone.

If ICE postpones joint venture capital contributions into 2027, Telia will bear asymmetric site rationalization expenses pushing the SEK 270m EBITDA restoration timeline beyond 2028 and threatening management's SEK 10bn 2027 free cash flow ambition, while failure to achieve the Norwegian EBITDA uplift directly undermines Telia's group Return on Capital Employed target of 12% by 2028 and leaves group return metrics anchored near European peer averages.

The investment decision framework for a concentrated long-only investor seeking 25%+ annualized returns over 18-24 months confronts a fundamental mismatch, as the Norwegian joint venture EBITDA restoration contributes materially to group earnings stability but insufficient magnitude to bridge the gap between Telia's baseline 4.5% to 5.1% free cash flow yield and the required return threshold absent aggressive multiple re-rating.

The resolvability verdict therefore concludes that while joint venture structural terms are adequately documented in principle, the SEK 270m 2028 financial target remains uncorroborated by ICE parent disclosures and subject to execution risks spanning site rationalization delays, competitive promotional intensity, and capacity pricing mechanics that cannot be validated through current public evidence.

Section 4 – Norwegian market dynamics and competitive landscape 16 findings

Section summary
  1. Norwegian mobile market dynamics face structural realignments as Lyse leverages fiber dominance to accelerate fixed-mobile convergence, while retail promotional intensity compresses operator ARPU growth.
  2. Radio access network consolidation between Telia and ICE restructures passive tower tenancy demand, shifting growth reliance toward multi-operator colocation across Nordic tower portfolios.

Market structure and fixed-mobile convergence dynamics

1.
Dominant incumbent baseline: Telenor maintains a market-leading position in Norwegian mobile, holding approximately 43% subscriber share and 51% revenue share across consumer and business segments
2.
Challenger market footprint: Lyse Group controls a formidable communications footprint through Altibox with a 31% landline broadband market share alongside ICE holding a 15% mobile subscriber share
3.
Fixed-mobile convergence threat: Bernstein Research emphasizes that Lyse poses a severe structural threat by cross-selling ICE mobile subscriptions into its 33% fixed broadband customer base, where upselling mobile into fiber relationships proves historically easier than reverse migration
4.
Asymmetric market exposure: Bernstein Research notes Telenor and Telia face elevated churn exposure because Telenor holds a 46% mobile share against only a 29% broadband share, leaving mobile-only accounts vulnerable to Altibox bundles
5.
Infrastructure acquisition offset: Telenor expanded its domestic broadband presence by acquiring GlobalConnect's consumer fiber business and Enivest, lifting its Norwegian fiber market share from 22% toward 30%

Roaming migration timelines and retail ARPU pressure

6.
Interim roaming windfall: Telenor captured temporary high-margin wholesale revenues after ICE transferred its national roaming contract from Telia to Telenor in early 2025
7.
Wholesale revenue phase-out: Deutsche Bank Research forecasts that Telenor's NOK 0.4bn annual wholesale payments from ICE will diminish through 2027 and largely disappear by late 2028 as traffic shifts to the joint RAN
8.
Commercial operational window: Lyse Group target disclosures indicate the joint radio access network with Telia will become operational during H2 2026, establishing full infrastructure competition by 2027
9.
Aggressive promotional headwinds: Intense Q2 2026 summer promotional campaigns across Norwegian B2C mobile increased subscriber rotation and compressed retail ARPU expansion for major operators
10.
Broadened discount scope: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that ICE escalated promotional pressure by extending discounted pricing onto unlimited data tariffs rather than restricting campaigns to low-end entry tiers

Passive tenancy drag: DNB Carnegie reports that Telia Towers faces revenue growth decelerations to 4% in 2026 down from 13% in 2025 due to joint venture site rationalization and network decommissioning
12.
Colocation growth offset: Telenor Towers expanded its mobile tenancy ratio to 1.7x by securing external colocation agreements and third-party tenancy additions across Norwegian tower sites

Tower operator renegotiation: A former director at Cellnex notes that mobile network consolidation forces tower operators to negotiate short-term fee discounts or tenancy adjustments in exchange for long-term lease renewals

Edge regulatory shift: Nkom's removal of price controls on national roaming grants established network operators tactical pricing flexibility while maintaining three-year wholesale access mandates
