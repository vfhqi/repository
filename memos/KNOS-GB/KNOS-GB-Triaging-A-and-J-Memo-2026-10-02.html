[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. Kainos is a self-help case riding a demand recovery: a founder has returned to the company he built, re-set it as a service business wrapped around a software business, and done so as UK public-sector digital procurement came out of a two-year, election-induced trough. At 1211p the shares have already paid for the revenue recovery; what they have not paid for, because it has not yet appeared, is the margin.

  • The change is internal and external at once, and the two arrive in the same accounts.
    • Brendan Mooney returned as chief executive on 11 December 2024 after a fifteen-month tenure under his successor ended in guidance cuts
    • Within three months he had restructured 190 roles and within ten he had re-set the strategy toward a £200m annual recurring revenue (ARR) target by 2030, prime-contractor roles on the largest public programmes, Canada and a dedicated AI business.
    • UK public-sector digital procurement normalised over the same period after the trough that cut public-sector revenue 15% to £62m in H1 FY2025.
    • The internal change decides what the company earns on the recovery
    • The recovery decides how much there is to earn on.
  • The change has travelled all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain on growth.
    • Resources (the chief executive and a rebuilt software leadership bench), choices (the strategy and the 18 August 2026 upgrade), actions (restructuring, contractor mobilisation, the Davis Pier acquisition, new products, £90m of buybacks), key operating metrics (bookings +32% to £505.3m, contracted backlog +18% to £433.9m at 31 March 2026, ARR +23% to £89.0m), financial statements (revenue +17.4% in FY2026 and +27.6% in its second half) and the sell-side's estimates (consensus FY2027 EPS +11.3% in three months and +15.3% in twelve, to 2026-09-25).
  • The one output that has not moved is the margin, and that is the shape of the case.
    • Adjusted pre-tax margin fell to 15.6% in FY2026 from 17.9% because the company staffed its wins with contractors (259 from 69; contractor cost £18.5m from £4.5m).
    • FY2027 guidance read at consensus (£84.2m of adjusted pre-tax profit on £542m of revenue) implies about 15.5%, no recovery at all.
    • The recovery is guided to the second half of FY2027 and is a forecast whose first test is the H1 FY2027 results on 2026-11-09.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving at a top-decile rate on the top line and sell-side estimates have risen without a single cut.
    • Revenue growth has stepped from a three-year average near 5% to +27.6% in H2 FY2026 and a consensus +25.8% for FY2027.
    • Modal EPS growth on our path is about 30% in FY2027, 15% in FY2028 and 19.4% a year compound from FY2026 to FY2029 (consensus 16.7%), inside or just above the framework's 12 to 20 percent band, and front-loaded in the way the framework marks down for over-extrapolation risk.
  • What it costs. 1211p is 19.3x the dashboard's consensus 24-month-forward EPS of 62.63p (2026-10-01): the stock's own five-year median (19.59x), the 92nd percentile of its last two years (median 15.64x), and about 55% above the peer median of about 12.2x.
    • The shares are +61.0% over six months and 34.3% above their 200-day average.
    • The upgrade and the £100m ARR milestone are in every broker's model
    • The margin recovery is in none of them.
  • What we think it earns, and the return against the bar.
    • Adjusted diluted EPS of 41.1p in FY2026 rising to 53.5p in FY2027, 61.5p in FY2028 and 70.0p in FY2029 on our path.
    • Valuing the shares at 19.0x the earnings two years beyond the horizon, the modal return is 16.9% a year over eighteen months (value including cash returns 1530p at 2028-04-01) and 12.8% a year over three years (1739p at 2029-10-01), against the framework's 20% bar.
    • Earnings growth carries the whole of that return
    • The re-rating leg is slightly negative
    • The cash return adds about 2.6% a year.
    • The case clears the bar on the modal path only below about 1160p.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from inputs through revenue and estimates, because the top line is contracted (backlog near one year of revenue, recognising only 50% to 70% of contract value) and policy-backed to 2030.
    • It breaks at the margin if contractor dependence proves structural rather than a timing effect, or if the 5% to 10% discounts integrators are bidding into public frameworks follow Kainos into the larger programmes.
    • On that path EPS still compounds at about 10% a year from revenue, so the case becomes mediocre at this price rather than wrong about the company.
  • The pattern it fits.
    • 'Major Corporate Change' at B: a chief-executive change plus a guidance increase, with strategy, products, geography and leadership also changed, translating into revenue, bookings and estimates and not yet into margin.
    • 'Compounder' is a secondary fit at C, carrying the two named lines in consensus that look mechanically wrong (FY2028 revenue growth of 8% after a 26% year; a margin that assumes contractor cost persists through FY2027).

  • The case therefore is a materially changing company whose change is proven on growth and unproven on profitability, at a price that has discounted the first and depends on the second.

    • The thesis is right
    • The price has got there first.

  • Rating = B, because the change is material on the measure this Building Block takes: strong and broad input forces ('Change Forces' at B) have already driven improving outputs through all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain ('Financial Outputs' at B), with revenue growth in the top decile of the comparison set and modal EPS growth inside the framework's band

    • The parts combine so that the inputs and the outputs carry the rating, and the 'Modal return' at C is the binding constraint on deploying capital at 1211p rather than on the materiality of the thesis.
    • Not C, which was live: a thesis whose modal return at today's price is 16.9% a year over eighteen months and 12.8% over three is not a sufficient return, and rated on the return alone this Building Block would be a C
    • We hold B because the framework names materiality of the change as the measure and carry the price-dependent leg into the recommendation.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against is the one the risks research of August 2026 called the false friend: bookings and revenue surge while profit never follows.
    • The contractor dependence is structural, because public contracts must be staffed in weeks (a contractor deploys in four weeks, a permanent hire takes five months) and retention has slipped to 90% from 93%
      • Global systems integrators displaced from weak commercial markets have made 5% to 10% discounts the price of public work and took Kainos's share of the Digital Outcomes and Specialists framework from 10% to 4.6% over 2023 and 2024
      • The Workday partner channel has nearly doubled in the UK (60 to 114 active partners) and the vendor's own deployment agent already cuts implementation hours by an estimated 30% with 50% in sight
      • And generative AI is passing 5% to 7% of delivery savings back to clients.
    • The evidence for it is FY2026 itself: revenue +17%, adjusted pre-tax profit +2%, gross margin down 1.8 points, Workday Services gross margin down 6 points, and FY2027 guidance that at consensus implies a 15.5% margin.

    • On that path FY2029 EPS is about 63p rather than 70p, the multiple falls to the two-year median of 15.6x, and the shares are worth about 980p to 1050p, a 15% to 20% loss.

    • We concede the whole of the FY2026 evidence.

    • We concede that the margin recovery is a forecast whose first test is six weeks away.
    • We concede that at 19.3x the false-friend path returns roughly nothing over eighteen months, which is why the 'Modal return' is a C and not a B.
    • We still hold the view for five reasons.
    • The top line is contracted rather than hoped for: £433.9m of backlog that recognises only 50% to 70% of contract value, and a £150m multi-year DEFRA award that alone exceeded Digital Services' whole FY2025 bookings of £202m.
    • The mix shift toward Workday Products at a 77.8% gross margin, growing roughly twice the group rate, lifts group gross margin by about 0.3 points a year by arithmetic, whatever the services do.
    • The contractor cost has a named, dated cause, and management showed in the March 2025 restructuring that it will cut cost to protect margin.
    • The fixed-price mix (37% of Workday Services, 20% of Digital Services by value) keeps the AI productivity gain inside Kainos for now.
    • And even on the false-friend path EPS compounds at about 10% a year from revenue alone, so the hypothesis makes the case mediocre at this price, not wrong about the company.
    • Where it bites is the price: that is why the entry level, not the thesis, decides the recommendation.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because a founder-chief-executive's return and a decisive re-articulation of strategy (the company as a service business around a software business) have coincided with a sector-level recovery in UK public-sector digital procurement, and together they have already propagated to the sixth layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain (consensus FY2027 EPS +11.3% in the three months to 2026-09-25)
    • The forces are strong and broad, and are held below A by two external forces that run the other way, competitive pricing on public frameworks and AI-driven deflation of services pricing.
  • Propagation: all six layers have moved on growth.
    • Resources (the chief executive and the software leadership team)
    • Choices (the strategy and the guidance)
    • Actions (restructuring, contractor mobilisation, an acquisition, product launches, buybacks)
    • Key operating metrics (bookings +32%, backlog +18%, ARR +23%, multi-product customers 41% of the product base)
    • Financial statements (revenue +17.4% in FY2026, +27.6% in H2)
    • Sell-side estimates (FY2027 EPS consensus +15.3% over the twelve months to 2026-09-25).
  • The gap: the margin has not moved.
    • Adjusted pre-tax margin 15.6% in FY2026 from 17.9%
    • Consensus FY2027 about 15.5%.
    • The chain is complete on growth and incomplete on profitability.
  • Not A, because the internal forces are a reversion to a founder's playbook rather than a new one, and the external force the company cannot control, competitive pricing, is tightening.

Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because the company's own cycle has turned sharply: revenue growth ran +2.0% (FY2024), -4.0% (FY2025) and +17.4% (FY2026), with the half-years at -5.2%, -2.6%, +7.1% and +27.6% from H1 FY2025 to H2 FY2026, consensus FY2027 revenue growth is +25.8% from the FY2026 base of £431m (six brokers, 2026-10-01), and bookings growth of 32% leads revenue.
  • The trough, dated: the September 2024 warning of tougher services trading, the sell-side's 20% pre-tax profit cut of October 2024 and the March 2025 restructuring (190 roles, an £8.4m charge) mark the bottom.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because competitive intensity is tightening on both sides of the business: global systems integrators displaced from weak commercial markets are bidding 5% to 10% discounts into public frameworks and took Kainos's Digital Outcomes and Specialists framework share from a historical 10% to 4.6% in 2023 and 2024, and the Workday partner ecosystem has roughly doubled to more than 1,300 partners with 21,000 certified consultants (September 2025), UK active partners rising from 60 to 114 in a year, with standard implementation pricing down 20% to 25% year on year.
  • Kainos's response: decline the price-first bids and compete on quality and project-based work.
    • It won more than double the contract volume of its nearest competitor on the DOS framework and closed twice as many deals above $1m in FY2026 as in FY2025 (18 May 2026).
    • Margins have been defended at the cost of share.
  • Not C, because the direction is unambiguously tighter, and the sell-side's own counter-case in three places is a price war spilling from commercial into public bidding.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because generative AI is strengthening as a force in both directions at once: it compresses implementation effort (channel experts report 20% to 25% less effort on complex deployments with 5% to 7% of the saving passed back to clients; Workday's Deployment Agent already cuts implementation hours by an estimated 30% with a 50% aim, May 2026), and it creates demand for a trusted deployer of AI inside critical systems (27 new AI opportunities worth £4.7m of pipeline at mid-2026; data and AI revenue £45.8m, +11%, 19% of Digital Services; Actonomy launched 21 September 2026).
  • Disproving evidence: enterprise AI remains largely in proof-of-concept stages on the experts' account (February 2026), and automated testing cannot yet replicate multi-entity validation.
  • Strengthening evidence: Workday's agent economics, and the sell-side's advice to assume deflationary pressure on implementation pricing (March 2026).
  • Not D, because the force has not yet shown in the numbers (Workday Services revenue is growing double digits, and fixed-price contracts, now 37% of that division, let Kainos keep the productivity gain)
    • Not B, because the pricing mechanism is credible and sector-wide.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because regulation is moving demand toward Kainos on three fronts: the EU Pay Transparency Directive (effective June 2026) created a compliance product that secured 35 customers within five months of its November 2025 launch
    • The Procurement Act 2023 frameworks and the HMRC-led DALAS vehicle (£4.2bn, Kainos on two of seven lots) widen the channel
    • And the UK government's plan to halve consultancy spend is judged by the sell-side to target general consultants rather than digital engineering.
  • The other way: the planned disbandment of NHS England threatens 25% to 30% of anticipated healthcare business for the coming fiscal year (on the August 2026 risks research, before the update, and unaddressed in the later files), and a fiscal squeeze at the autumn Budget could pause mobilisations.
  • Cyber Security and Resilience Bill: a watchpoint for delivery guidelines, not a demand mover.
  • Not A, because the healthcare reorganisation risk is real and recent, and healthcare was the fastest-growing segment (+55% to £74.9m in FY2026).

    • The vendor is both channel and competitor.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the input the business buys is people, and the price of people spiked in FY2026 through the contractor route (contractor costs £18.5m from £4.5m; third-party supplier costs £30.1m from £14.7m; contractors 259 from 69 at March 2026) while core wage inflation has moderated to low single digits (August 2026) and employer National Insurance added £3.0m
    • The headwind is real now and easing, with hiring speed the constraint (four weeks to deploy a contractor, five months to hire permanent staff).
  • Workforce: retention softened to 90% in FY2026 from 93%
    • Headcount grew 21% to 3,475 across 17 or 18 countries with the Americas at 18% of headcount
    • The Gdansk hub is an unhedged Polish-zloty cost base.
  • Not D, because wage inflation has moderated and the contractor cost is tied to a specific, dated mobilisation rather than a structural scarcity.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the macro forces acting on Kainos are mostly those acting on the universe, and the one that acts more (UK fiscal policy toward departmental budgets) cuts both ways: UK GDP is running at a 2.4% annualised rate in mid-2026 and public investment programmes are accelerating (23 September 2026), while CPI rose to 3.1% in August 2026 and is projected above 4% in early 2027, lifting the cost of high-wage technical staff
    • FDM Group's H1 2026 revenue fall to £78.6m shows the state of the commercial consultancy market Kainos has largely left.
  • Currency: 43% of revenue is international and nothing is hedged
    • A 10% sterling appreciation dilutes EPS by about 1.3p, about 3% of FY2026 adjusted EPS
    • Consensus has GBP/USD at 1.34 by mid-2027.
    • A modest, unmanaged headwind.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • Kainos is a self-help case riding a demand recovery: a founder returned to the company he built in December 2024, re-set it as a service business wrapped around a software business, and did so as UK public-sector digital procurement came out of a two-year, election-induced trough.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain on growth: new leadership and strategy, a restructuring, a channel agreement and new products, then bookings up 32% to £505.3m and contracted backlog up 18% to £433.9m at 31 March 2026, then revenue up 17.4% in FY2026 and 27.6% in its second half, then an unscheduled upgrade on 18 August 2026 that took consensus FY2027 EPS up 11% in a month to 52.8p.
  • What it has not done is reach the margin: adjusted pre-tax margin fell to 15.6% from 17.9% because the company staffed its wins with 259 contractors, and FY2027 guidance read at consensus implies no recovery.
  • The recovery is guided to the second half of FY2027, rests on a timing cause (contractors deploy in four weeks and are replaced in five months) and on a mix shift to 77.8%-gross-margin software that adds about 0.3 points of group gross margin a year by arithmetic, and is a forecast whose first test is the H1 FY2027 results on 2026-11-09.
  • The case fits 'Major Corporate Change' at B, crisply on inputs and on the top line and loosely on the one output the pattern most wants.
  • The business is a good operator with real but bounded advantages, growing with a single, policy-committed buyer through a channel partner that is also a competitor, in an industry structure that is cutting price around a company that refuses to.
  • The case is compelling as a business and fully priced as a share: at 1211p the 24-month-forward multiple is 19.3x, the stock's own five-year median and about 55% above the peer median, the shares are up 61% in six months, and the modal return from here is 16.9% a year over eighteen months and 12.8% over three, against a 20% bar.
  • The thesis is right
    • The price has got there first.
