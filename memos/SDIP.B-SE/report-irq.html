Terms used throughout. EBITA: adjusted earnings before interest, tax and amortisation of acquired intangibles, the company's main profit measure. EBITDA: EBITA before depreciation. EPS: the memo's model earnings per share (adjusted EBITA less net financial items, taxed at 24%, over ordinary shares, before amortisation of acquired intangibles), unless another basis is named. PPA amortisation: amortisation of intangibles from purchase-price allocation on acquisitions. Organic: growth in units owned in both periods, at constant currency. Leverage: net debt including earn-outs to adjusted EBITDA, on the company's definition, against its 3.0x ceiling. Earn-outs: deferred payments to sellers that depend on the acquired unit's later profit; write-backs are gains booked when the expected payment is cut. Cash conversion: operating cash flow over EBITDA, on the company's measure (target 70% to 90%). ROCE: return on capital employed. STIBOR: the Stockholm interbank rate on which the bonds and bank debt are priced. The Riksbank: Sweden's central bank. FY2026: the financial year to December 2026; H1: first half. bp: basis points.

A first point for Richard before the questions. Sdiptech is held as a Core Investment at 5.63% of the fund, so the questions are weighted to whether to add, hold or cut, and to what would break the case. The memo (31-Oct-2026) calls it "good niche infrastructure businesses whose group-level record was spoiled by what it paid and how it financed itself, and which a new management has cleaned up quickly and well": EPS flat at SEK 11 to 13 for FY2022 to FY2025 while revenue grew 48%, and now compounding at 15% to 17% (SEK 15.27 to 27.96 over FY2026 to FY2030) because dilution, a seven-fold rise in interest and 9x entry prices have gone. About a third of FY2027's EPS increase is already contracted. Its verdict on sizing: "a case to size for the multiple's volatility and to add to on the fourth-quarter proof point rather than before it", that proof point being fourth-quarter cash conversion and year-end leverage on 10-Feb-2027. Two things since: the third-quarter earnings summary (29-Oct) found the two drivers the case needed (organic growth, deals) well ahead and the margin, cash and Water & Bioeconomy alarm going the wrong way; and the Industry's relative strength fired Sell Criterion 1 for Sdiptech with four other holdings on 10-Nov-2026 (an Industry-wide reading, not a stock-specific one). What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: Sdiptech's EPS now compounds mechanically because the drags have gone, and two numbers decide whether it keeps doing so, the conversion of growth into profit and cash, and the price and pace of acquisitions inside a 3.0x ceiling; the share price, meanwhile, answers to the Riksbank and the cohort. Six judgements decide whether to add, hold or cut.

  1. Judgement 1, the fourth-quarter cash release and year-end leverage. Third-quarter conversion was 62% and leverage 2.90x to 2.95x against the 3.0x ceiling; we have 2.6x to 2.7x at year end after the seasonal release (134% in the fourth quarter of 2025). Above 100% and 2.6x to 2.7x on 10-Feb-2027 unlocks the engine at SEK 100m a year; below 90% and 2.8x pauses deals for two quarters, and the memo's proof point for adding fails.
  2. Judgement 2, the drop-through in H1 2027. Organic sales +7%, +11%, +13% in 2026 against organic EBITA +4%, +5%, +6%. We read most of the gap as about SEK 50m a quarter of near-zero-margin chassis pass-through at Hilltip and the Mecno overrun, both ending in the fourth quarter of 2026. If H1 2027 shows organic EBITA growing faster than organic sales, RedEye's "structural cost pressure" reading is disproved; if Supply Chain & Transportation stays at 18% to 19%, organic sales of 4% and a 20.2% margin take FY2027 EPS to about SEK 17.1, 7% below the model.
  3. Judgement 3, the acquisition engine's price and pace. Base SEK 90m of EBITA in 2027 and SEK 100m a year after, at about 6.5x upfront and 8x total; each SEK 10m closed adds about 0.7% to EPS and consensus models none. A year of SEK 50m costs 2.3% of FY2027 EPS and 3.3% of FY2028, and stops the upgrade stream; deals at 8x upfront instead of 6.5x add about 25% less EPS; a deal at 9x total is the memo's alarm.
  4. Judgement 4, the earn-out write-backs. About SEK 90m of gains in nine months of 2026 against SEK 48m in 2025, the 2026 figure unattributed. If they trace to units bought in 2024 and 2025 missing their plans, the quality of reported profit and the deal record both weaken; if timing, as in 2025, no change.
  5. Judgement 5, sterling and the finance line together. 49% of sales in the United Kingdom, hedges within twelve months; 40% to 45% of credit swapped. A 10% weaker pound removes SEK 83m of EBITA and SEK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS; 100bp on STIBOR adds SEK 11m to 33m of interest (SEK 0.2 to 0.7); together, 5% and 100bp take FY2027 EPS to about SEK 17.3 (6% lower).
  6. Judgement 6, the margin's floors: Water & Bioeconomy and Safety & Security. Water & Bioeconomy at 20.3% has triggered our alarm (below 21.8%); Safety & Security at 36.2% is called unsustainable by the Chief Executive. Water & Bioeconomy still below 22% after the fourth-quarter and first-quarter reports meets the case's invalidation level; Safety & Security falling below the model's 31% with a data-centre pause costs SEK 30m to 50m of EBITA (SEK 0.6 to 1.0 of EPS).

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the FY2026 report on 10-Feb-2027, the first-quarter report in late April 2027, the second-quarter report in mid-July 2027 and the third-quarter report in late October 2027)

A1 ★ The fourth-quarter cash release and leverage at year end. "Third-quarter conversion was 62% as inventory and receivables were built for growth. How much of that unwinds in the fourth quarter, and where should leverage including earn-outs land at the year end after the earn-outs due?"
- Why: Q4 2025 conversion was 134%; the target corridor is 70% to 90% and the twelve-month figure 81%. Leverage was 2.90x in the report and 2.95x on the call, against the 3.0x ceiling. DNB Carnegie models 3.0x at end-2026 (the case's alarm level); we have 2.6x to 2.7x. About SEK 205m of earn-outs were due in the rest of 2026 on the sell-side's July count. One number on one date gates three of the forecast's components (deal pace, upgrades, the multiple).
- Tell: "a normal seasonal release, leverage well inside 3.0x" = Judgement 1 on track and the proof point likely met; "growth keeps absorbing working capital" = the headroom is consumed by success, deals slow in H1 2027, and adding should wait.

A2 ★ The drop-through in 2027. "Is our reading right that the gap between 13% organic sales and 6% organic EBITA is mostly Hilltip's truck-chassis pass-through and Mecno's fixed-price rail projects, both ending with the fourth quarter, so that the first half of 2027 should show organic EBITA growing faster than organic sales?"
- Why: about SEK 50m a quarter of near-zero-margin chassis revenue explains the gross-margin fall from 60.4% to 58.1%; Mecno is about SEK 8m to 10m a quarter; Supply Chain & Transportation's margin fell to 18.5% from 21.0%. The underlying incremental margin is about 25%. Each 50bp of EBITA margin is about SEK 0.58 of EPS; RedEye reads three quarters of gross margin below 60% as possibly structural.
- Tell: "yes, both end in the fourth quarter, indexation in place" = the debate ends in two reports and Judgement 2 passes; "Hilltip's larger road segment continues" or "cost pressure broader than Mecno" = the margin leg turns negative and FY2027 EPS is nearer SEK 17.

A3 Supply Chain & Transportation's margin path. "What margin should we expect from Supply Chain & Transportation through 2027, and how much of Mecno's and the area's other fixed-price work now carries indexation?"
- Why: the memo's test is the margin back above 20% by the second quarter of 2027; the model books Mecno's recovery in H1 2027 (SEK 15m to 25m of EBITA, SEK 0.3 to 0.5 of EPS). Indexation coverage is not a disclosure the company has committed to. This is the largest segment (38% of 2025 EBITA) and the one the Chief Executive ran from 2018.
- Tell: "back to 20% to 21% by mid-2027" = our base; "18% to 19% is the new level with this mix" = the first reading in the earnings summary, and a lower margin path.

A4 ★ Organic growth in 2027. "How should we think about organic growth next year, given comparisons of 7%, 11% and 13%, the transport refrigeration orders that customers moved from 2025 into 2026, and the chassis sales leaving the base?"
- Why: we model about 7% for FY2027 (9%, 7%, 6%, 6% by quarter), the sell-side about 6%; the third and fourth quarters of 2027 carry a further four-point headwind from the chassis leaving, while reported growth jumps to about 13% from the second quarter of 2027 as divested units leave the comparison and deals annualise. Each point of organic growth is about SEK 0.23 of EPS. Below 3% for two quarters would change the case.
- Tell: "mid-to-high single digits, the postponed orders were a one-off" = our base, and the slowdown is normalisation, not a warning; "double digits continue" = upside the case's conservative 5% to 7% does not carry.

A5 ★ The acquisition pace. "You had about SEK 75 million of acquired run-rate EBITA by October against the SEK 100 million target for 2026. Will you reach it by year end, and what pace should we assume for 2027 inside the 3.0x ceiling?"
- Why: we assume about SEK 95m bought in 2026 and SEK 90m in 2027 with half a year's contribution; consensus models only announced deals, so each SEK 10m closed lifts EPS about 0.7%. The 2025 target was halved. The memo expects the SEK 100m to be missed by SEK 5m to 25m, which the sell-side will excuse as timing.
- Tell: "close to 100 this year, same pace next year" = the upgrade stream runs at 1.5% to 2% a quarter; "we will wait for the cash release" = Judgement 3's downside for H1 2027.

A6 ★ What the deals cost. "What multiples, upfront and including earn-outs, did you pay for A.U.T. Wheels and Castors and Vale Engineering, and would you consider disclosing total consideration per deal as a matter of course?"
- Why: the past cycle paid about 9x, against 6x to 8x at the Nordic median, which is why ROCE is 13.6% against 18% to 22% at Lifco, Addtech and Indutrade; the research puts the break point at 8x to 9x. At 6.5x upfront and 8x total the deals yield about 12.5% before tax, below the group's ROCE, so the engine barely lifts returns. The 2024 deals averaged 5.3x upfront and 7.9x total. Our "deal above 9x" alarm cannot be read because the multiples are not stated.
- Tell: "about 6x upfront, 7x to 8x total" = the discipline the case relies on; "higher for the UK platforms" = returns close more slowly and the discount to the compounders stays.

A7 ★ The earn-out write-backs. "Earn-out write-back gains were about SEK 90 million in the first nine months against SEK 48 million in all of 2025. Which acquisitions do they come from, and are they timing, as GAH's postponed fleet orders were last year, or units missing their plans?"
- Why: the memo calls this the most useful disclosure gap to probe and reads it as a sign some acquired units may be missing plans; write-backs flatter reported profit while they last and cushion leverage in a downturn (earn-outs fall about 40% if acquired profits stay flat). The 2025 figure was traced to GAH; the 2026 figure is unattributed.
- Tell: "timing at one or two units" = no change; "several 2024 to 2025 deals below plan" = Judgement 4 bites, and the case's deal record weakens.

A8 The earn-out schedule. "How should we model earn-out payments over 2027 to 2029?"
- Why: we have SEK 159m to 162m in 2027 and SEK 392m to 436m in 2028 to 2029, absorbing 20% to 35% of free cash flow at the peak, just as the swaps roll off.
- Tell: a lower schedule = more room for deals in 2028; a higher one = the 2028 to 2029 squeeze the memo names.

A9 ★ The finance line and the hedges. "After the refinancing at STIBOR plus 2.00%, what net financial items should we model for 2027, how much of the debt is swapped, and when do the swaps mature?"
- Why: we have SEK (262)m against the brokers' about SEK (275)m (SEK 0.3 of EPS); a flat finance line turns 13% EBITA growth into 16% to 17% EPS growth. 40% to 45% of utilised credit is swapped for one to 3.5 years at undisclosed strikes; the Riksbank is expected at 2.0% now and 2.25% in February (SEB 2.50%). Each 100bp on STIBOR is SEK 0.2 to 0.7 of EPS. The third quarter carried a SEK 13m one-off on the bond redemption.
- Tell: "about SEK 260m, swaps rolled forward" = our base; "higher as swaps expire at low strikes" = the rates leg of Judgement 5 arrives in 2027.

A10 Sterling. "With about half of sales in the United Kingdom and currency hedged within twelve months, what is the right sensitivity to the pound, and does sterling debt offset it?"
- Why: a 10% weaker pound removes SEK 83m of EBITA (about 8% of core EBITA), SEK 0.9 to 1.7 of EPS; part of the debt is in sterling, so leverage is roughly unchanged. EUR/SEK is forecast at 10.70 to 10.96 by end-2027 from about 11.00, a stronger krona.
- Tell: "translation only, debt matched" = an EPS but not a balance-sheet risk.

A11 ★ The building blocks in February. "Will the February report revisit the building blocks: the 5% to 10% organic EBITA band, SEK 100 million of acquired EBITA, and the path to SEK 2 billion of EBITA by 2030? Is a capital markets day planned for 2027?"
- Why: the company has never raised a target; the memo puts about 65% on blocks met and reaffirmed, 15% on something raised (most plausibly the deal pace or a dividend policy), 20% on a block missed. Our FY2030 EBITA of SEK 1,695m is 15% short of the 2030 ambition, which would need SEK 150m a year of deals from 2028. DNB: "2027 could be a strong year with 15% EBITA growth including recent acquisitions"; we have +14%.
- Tell: "reaffirmed" = upgrades show as delivery above the bands, which the market rewards less; "a higher deal target" = a formal raise, and the memo would mark guidance up ten points.

A12 ★ Water & Bioeconomy under its new head. "Water & Bioeconomy's margin was 20.3% in the third quarter. What does Anders Petré plan, how much of the higher cost base is the new normal, and how do the April 2027 UK wage rise and the fixed-price insurer contracts affect next year?"
- Why: the margin fell from 23.8% (FY2024) to 20.3%; the area has had three heads in about a year; management said sustained profit growth there is "not yet fully secured". Our alarm (below 21.8% at the third quarter) is triggered, and still below 22% after two more reports meets the case's invalidation level. The National Living Wage rises 3.9% in April 2027 against one-to-three-year fixed-price contracts with a six-to-nine-month lag. We model 21.3% by FY2028.
- Tell: "repricing at renewal, 21% to 22% in 2027" = the alarm clears in time; "cost base stays, margin around 20%" = the invalidation level is met and Foundations' Robustness (55) comes down.

A13 Safety & Security's margin. "Safety & Security earned 36.2% in the third quarter, which the Chief Executive called unsustainable. What margin is sustainable, and how much of the area's profit is linked to data centres?"
- Why: the model takes the margin to 31% (SEK (20)m to (30)m of EBITA as it normalises); the shift to software and secure communications lifted it from 30%. The data-centre-linked part of the group is under 10% of sales, perhaps 5% to 10% of EBITA, and is where the peak margin sits.
- Tell: "low thirties" = our base; "high twenties" = a further SEK 0.2 to 0.4 of EPS at risk.

A14 The working-capital programme. "What is the target for working capital as a share of sales, and how much cash should it release over 2027 and 2028?"
- Why: about 20% of sales against 24.7% on FactSet's basis, about SEK 200m of one-off cash if met (consignment stock, smaller batches); it is one of the two routes by which ROCE reaches 14%.
- Tell: "20% by end-2027" = ROCE near 14% by the FY2027 report.

A15 A first dividend. "At what leverage would the Board consider a dividend?"
- Why: we model none; the memo's staircase has leverage low enough for a first distribution in months 18 to 36, as one of the steps toward a mid-tier multiple.
- Tell: "below about 2x" = 2028 at our leverage path (2.1x at end-2028).

A16 ★ The road to a 15% return on capital. "ROCE was 13.6% in the third quarter against your target above 15%. What is the bridge: organic profit on a stable capital base, working capital released, or the deals' returns, and by when?"
- Why: ROCE rose from 12.0% a year ago and 13.2% at the second quarter; we model about 14% by the FY2027 report and 15% only by 2030, because deals at 6.5x upfront and 8x total yield about 12.5% before tax, below the group's return. The discount to the compounders (11.1x against about 15x 2027 EV/EBITA) closes only as returns close; ROCE is now in the 2026 bonus plans.
- Tell: "organic profit and working capital first, 15% in 2028" = ahead of our path and a reason for a higher fair multiple; "as the deals mature" = our path, a 2028 to 2029 re-rating, not 2027.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. The operating units

B1.1 Hilltip's complete vehicles. "Is the larger road product segment at Hilltip a recurring line, with complete-vehicle orders in 2027, or was 2026 a one-off?"
- Why: the counter-case to Judgement 2 is that complete-vehicle orders recur, keeping the pass-through dilution; a complete-vehicle order in the third quarter of 2027 would weaken our reading. About 0.8 points of group margin.
- Tell: "one-off fleet deliveries" = our base; "a new recurring segment" = the margin dilution stays.

B1.2 Energy & Electrification and Rolec. "Energy & Electrification grew 17% at 24.2%. How much is grid and electrification demand, and how is Rolec doing against Chinese charging hardware?"
- Why: the structural tailwinds (grid modernisation, data-centre power) reach about 37% of EBITA with Safety & Security; Rolec is about 6% of EBITA in the one commoditised niche (more than 60 UK suppliers; Chinese hardware at USD 25 to 40 a unit), now growing again on UK fleet demand.
- Tell: "orders ahead of sales" = the structural leg holds into 2027; "Rolec price pressure" = a small margin drag.

B1.3 Order visibility. "Most units hold about two months of orders. How has order intake moved through the autumn, and which units carry longer books?"
- Why: only about SEK 210m of orders run beyond twelve months; visibility is short, so the first-quarter 2027 report is where a slowdown would first show.
- Tell: "intake ahead of sales into the winter" = the 2027 organic base holds; "slower since September" = toward the lower end of the 5% to 7% path.

B2. Data centres and the thematic segments

B2.1 ★ Data-centre exposure. "How much of sales and profit now comes from data-centre customers, and with the hyperscalers' and neoclouds' credit spreads widening in October, have you seen any project timing change, at Eagle or Phase 3 for example?"
- Why: nobody connects the theme's financing fragility to the segment carrying the peak margin (Safety & Security at 36%); a pause costs SEK 30m to 50m of EBITA with Energy & Electrification (SEK 0.6 to 1.0 of EPS), more than the model's normalisation assumes. Most units hold about two months of orders.
- Tell: "under 10% of sales, projects on schedule" = the risk is small and dated; "some timing slips" = Judgement 6's downside.

B3. The United Kingdom

B3.1 United Kingdom demand. "With half of sales in the United Kingdom, construction weak and public capital budgets under pressure, where do you see United Kingdom demand next year, and how much does AMP8 water spending support it?"
- Why: the old company missed consensus in five of six quarters in the last soft-UK period (2024 to mid-2025); AMP8 is GBP 104bn for 2025 to 2030; UK CPI may top 4% in 2027. The UK is also where the deal pipeline is deepest.
- Tell: "regulated niches unaffected" = the structural leg holds; "softer order intake" = organic growth toward 3% to 5%.

B3.2 ★ Price rises in 2027. "In the service and project units, prices follow costs with a six-to-nine-month lag. How much price are you putting through for 2027, and how much of the group now has indexation in its contracts?"
- Why: pricing is real in the product niches (GAH's EBITA +76% after acquisition on value-based pricing; Energy & Electrification held about 25% through component inflation) and lagged in service and project units; price adds about 2 to 3 points of revenue a year and about nothing to the group margin. Component inflation is flagged for 2027; Brent is above USD 100 and the EU steel safeguard applies.
- Tell: "3% to 4% with indexation spreading" = the lag shortens and the margin holds; "we absorb part of it" = a margin drag in H1 2027 on top of Water & Bioeconomy's wage rise.

B4. Acquisitions and oversight

B4.1 The pipeline and the competition for targets. "How has competition for small targets changed, with Addtech buying in the United Kingdom, and when might you open a German platform?"
- Why: about 25,000 profitable European small companies pass typical filters, a pipeline of about 800; private equity pays 8x to 12x above EUR 50m but is largely absent below it; Nordic compounders' deals outside the Nordics rose from 28% to 61% of the total over a decade; a first German platform deal is on the memo's 6-to-18-month staircase.
- Tell: "prices stable at 5x to 7x" = the engine's economics hold; "rising" = Judgement 3's 8x branch.

B4.2 ★ Oversight across 35 units. "With about 35 units on different accounting systems and six to eight deals a year, how do you check subsidiaries' numbers between audits, and is an internal audit function planned?"
- Why: there is no internal audit function; the Chief Executive admitted subsidiary oversight had been too loose; the Storskogen precedent is a SEK 30m diligence charge at one unit and later SEK 600m of impairments. An accounting or control failure in one unit is about 5% plausible over 18 months for a (10%) to (25%) share-price fall. Subsidiary performance is visible only through Companies House filings up to 18 months late.
- Tell: "internal audit from 2027, monthly reporting tightened" = a named gap closing; "the decentralised model relies on unit management" = the risk stays.

B4.3 The clusters. "How far have the clusters in cold chain, rail, lifting and road maintenance gone in joint buying and shared sales, and what are they worth?"
- Why: clusters are the group's intended substitute for scale; the memo puts them at SEK 5m to 15m of EBITA, already inside the margin path; they are early.
- Tell: named savings = a small group-level benefit beginning to show.

B5. Capital structure

B5.1 The ceiling and the covenants. "Is the 3.0x ceiling measured including all earn-outs and leases, and how much headroom is there to the covenants?"
- Why: SEK 1.75bn of undrawn facilities; covenants at 3.25x and 4.50x against the 3.0x policy; the next bond maturity is September 2030. FactSet's basis shows 2.5x, the company 2.90x to 2.95x.
- Tell: "comfortable headroom" = the riskiness is in perception more than fundamentals, as the memo's counter-case argues.

B5.2 Equity. "Is equity off the table as a funding source for acquisitions, and how much dilution do the warrant programmes bring?"
- Why: the last cycle issued shares (the share count rose 25% over FY2019 to FY2025); we model no issuance and about 1.7% from warrants in 2028 to 2029.
- Tell: "no equity, deals from cash flow" = the EPS compounding holds.

B5.3 ★ Further divestments. "Is the divestment programme finished, or are more units under review, and would any further sale be used for deals or to cut debt?"
- Why: eleven units plus Metus (15% of sales, 5% of EBITA, a 7.4% margin) were sold at 6.7x EBITA within twelve months; ten divestments in nine months took leverage from 3.31x to 2.81x. A further sale would add headroom just as the ceiling binds (Judgement 1).
- Tell: "finished, a product-only group" = the perimeter is stable for 2027 models; "a few more" = more headroom, and a slightly smaller base.

B6. People and incentives
