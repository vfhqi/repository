Outliers worth noting

The collapse in adjusted operating cash conversion to 40.7% in H1 2026 from 65.7% in H1 2025 represents a statistical outlier that sits well below the corporate target of above 90% and appears unexplained by reported revenue or margin trends. This severe cash generation failure was driven by a $44.4 million working capital outflow following the termination of the debtor factoring program, pushing average trade working capital to sales to 25.7% compared to 23.7% in December 2025.

The magnitude of this cash drag is unusual for a business reporting strong underlying operating margins and suggests potential inventory accumulation or customer payment deterioration that has not been adequately explained in management disclosures. This anomaly directly supports the False Friend pre-mortem thesis where superficial margin strength masks underlying cash generation weakness.

The requirement for manual year-end revenue adjustments to correct for dispatched goods where control has not yet passed to customers represents an accounting anomaly that creates heightened cutoff misstatement risks. The group's default software records revenue on dispatch rather than delivery, necessitating manual interventions that increase audit complexity and error probability. Deloitte specifically identified revenue cutoff and adjusting items classification as key audit areas, highlighting that this system limitation requires elevated scrutiny relative to peers with automated revenue recognition controls aligned to delivery-based IFRS 15 principles.

The persistence of this manual adjustment requirement despite recent digital transformation investments suggests either underinvestment in core ERP capabilities or complex distribution arrangements that resist standardization, both of which represent operational execution risks that differentiate Elementis from better-controlled specialty chemical peers.
