Section 3 – Contingent Consideration and the Fabri AG Structure

Section summary

  3. The returning founder's margin-over-volume strategy could conflict with the growth-oriented earnout structures of local German subsidiaries.

Contingent Consideration Liabilities

1.
Elevated liability potential: Instalco maintains significant potential liabilities related to future subsidiary performance that could impact its cash reserves. According to the Instalco Interim Report for January to June 2026, the carrying value of the group's estimated contingent consideration was recorded at SEK 50 million. However, the maximum undiscounted cash payout that could be required is SEK 240 million if all targets are met. This wide gap means that future EPS remains highly dependent on whether acquired companies achieve their maximum performance targets.
2.
Acquisition liability changes: The balance sheet carrying value of contingent considerations has shown notable volatility due to recent acquisition activity and revaluations. According to Instalco's Q2 2026 interim report, the carrying amount increased from SEK 33 million at December 2025 to SEK 50 million at June 2026. This expansion was driven by SEK 28 million added through new acquisitions during the first half of 2026. Paid contingent considerations of SEK 10 million and a minor SEK 1 million revaluation gain partially offset this increase.
3.
Peer liability comparison: Instalco maintains a much lower carrying value for its immediate contingent liabilities when compared to some of its major Nordic peers. According to Instalco's Q2 2026 interim report, its carrying value for contingent considerations was only SEK 50 million. By comparison, the peer Fasadgruppen reported a massive SEK 713 million of outstanding subsidiary-share options at June 2026. This lower reported balance suggests that Instalco has structured its short-term earnouts more conservatively than peers or has already settled its largest outstanding liabilities.
4.
Historical cash settlements: The cash required to settle outstanding contingent considerations has historically been a significant drain on group capital. According to Instalco's Annual Report of 17 March 2026, the group paid out SEK 131 million in settled contingent considerations during the full year of 2025. This represents a decrease from the SEK 151 million paid during the full year of 2024. Managing these substantial outflows is crucial for keeping net debt below the long-term target of 2.5x EBITDA.

Fabri AG Multi-Step Roadmap

5.
Phased international expansion: Entering the German market represents the most significant geographic expansion in Instalco's history. According to Instalco's Annual Report of 17 March 2026, the group acquired its initial 24 percent minority interest in Fabri AG in March 2025. This first step required an investment of approximately EUR 15 million, which was settled with EUR 13 million of newly issued shares and EUR 2 million in cash. Taking a minority position initially allowed management to assess the German market without taking on full operational consolidation risk.
6.
Step two majority: Achieving majority ownership in Germany marks a critical milestone for the group's international growth. According to Instalco's press release on 16 July 2026, the group signed an agreement to acquire an additional 27 percent stake in Fabri AG. This transaction increased Instalco's total ownership to 51 percent and initiated full financial consolidation. The cash consideration for this step amounted to EUR 35.7 million and was funded entirely through the group's existing credit facilities.
7.
Revenue valuation multiple: The financial terms of the Fabri transaction are designed to limit overpayment risks for the group. According to DNB Carnegie's report of 23 January 2026, the initial transaction was structured with pre-negotiated valuations based on a multiple of 0.9x sales for the various equity tranches. This 0.9x sales multiple is highly attractive for Instalco given that Fabri's historical EBITA margin reached a strong 12.7 percent in 2025. This pricing structure ensures that the transaction remains earnings-accretive upon full consolidation.

Step Three Thresholds and Options

8.
Conditional earnings thresholds: The third step of the German acquisition is heavily protected by specific performance milestones. According to Instalco's Annual Report of 17 March 2026, the group plans to acquire an additional 17 percent stake in Fabri AG. This third step is expected to be completed in 2029 at the earliest. However, the transaction is strictly conditional upon the Fabri Group achieving agreed earnings and profit thresholds. If German macroeconomic demand weakens, these thresholds will not be met, and Instalco can postpone the cash outflow.
9.
Step four options: The final phase of ownership will allow Instalco to take complete control of the German business. According to Instalco's Annual Report of 17 March 2026, the fourth step consists of options to acquire the remaining 32 percent of Fabri AG. These options are scheduled to be exercised during the period 2030 to 2033 inclusive. This multi-year structure keeps the local founders highly motivated because their ultimate payout is tied to the long-term profitability of their regional subsidiaries.
10.
Expert integration perspective: Operational integration of decentralized acquisitions requires strong local management rather than central oversight. According to an expert interview with a former Head of Division at Bravida on 10 June 2025, decentralised companies succeed because they employ high-performing local entrepreneurs who do not require intensive corporate training or administrative support systems. Keeping local German management in place at Fabri is essential for maintaining high margins. This supports the view that the phased buyout model is necessary to retain local leadership.
