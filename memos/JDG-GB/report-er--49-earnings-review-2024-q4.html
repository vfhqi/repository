Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The FY2024 results were broadly in line with the lowered earnings bar and management presented the weakness as temporary, but the March 2025 release and subsequent H1 2026 interim report did not validate a full recovery; instead, demand weakened materially through H1 2026, with the Q3 order recovery to near parity suggesting timing and procurement delays rather than a complete collapse, while the next Geotek coring expedition moved from an expectation of early 2027 to no earlier than 2028. The selected earnings changed expectations by confirming that Judges' earnings recovery depends on external demand normalisation rather than internal self-help, exposing greater operating leverage, funding sensitivity and execution risk than the historical compounder narrative implied, and establishing that the three-year EPS path requires sustained United States research funding, Chinese procurement normalisation, margin recovery from higher volumes and resumed acquisitions rather than simply a return to the 7% organic revenue and 9% organic EBIT compound growth rates previously achieved.

FY2024 revenue was £133.6m, down 1.8% from £136.1m, while adjusted operating profit fell 20% to £27.9m and adjusted basic EPS declined 24% to 283.4p from 374.6p, broadly meeting Zeus's 283.9p expectation after management's November guidance of 270p to 300p, so the reported result was effectively in line with the reduced market bar rather than a genuine earnings beat. Organic revenue fell 8%, or 2% excluding the Geotek coring contract, while acquisition-related revenue partly offset the decline; Shore had expected FY2024 revenue of approximately £133m and adjusted EPS of 274.6p against consensus of 276.8p, so the reported figures were modestly ahead of Shore's cautious forecast but did not approach the earlier 349p EPS expectation, confirming that the November reset was a material miss against the original FY2024 case and establishing a weaker earnings base for the recovery.

The adjusted operating margin fell to 20.9% from 25.6%, as lower volume and the absence of Geotek coring exposed Judges' operating leverage; Shore's revised FY2025 case assumed a rebound to a 25.2% margin, a 460- basis-point improvement, making the margin collapse the most important EPS variable because it demonstrated that Judges' employee-heavy, decentralised cost base creates substantial asymmetric risk when demand weakens.

H1 2025 revenue increased 15% to £70.2m, organic revenue rose 7% to £64.7m and adjusted EPS rose 14% to 141.4p from 123.7p; Jefferies' H1 forecast was £66.9m of revenue and adjusted EPS of approximately 142p, so the revenue delivery exceeded its model while EPS was broadly on target, but management acknowledged that excluding Geotek, the other organic businesses were affected by roughly onethird, proving that Geotek masked underlying deterioration rather than supplementing a broad portfolio recovery.

H1 2025 organic order intake was initially 4% ahead of the prior year, but North American intake was down 18%, and the later update said year-to-date organic intake excluding coring had fallen back to flat; Jefferies therefore treated the unchanged FY2025 guidance as a relief rather than an upside signal because weak United States order intake threatened the second-half earnings bridge, establishing that orders contradicted revenue momentum and exposing the recovery case to execution risk.

H1 2026 order intake fell 12% year on year, after Q1 declined 18% and Q2 moved closer to prior-year levels; by the September results date, year-to-date order intake had improved from 12% below the prior year to only 1% below, which was better than the market's preresults concern but still represented near-flat demand, so the Q3 recovery was incomplete and insufficient to validate the earnings acceleration embedded in FY2026 consensus.

Judges said some customers had already ordered equipment but asked the group to delay shipment until uncertainty around funding and procurement processes was resolved; management expects a greater-than-usual final-quarter revenue weighting because improvements in order intake occurred late and because China's taxexemption process is only beginning to resolve, creating unusually high Q4 execution risk because the 200.5p FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS expectation depends on customer decisions, production slots and shipment execution in a short period.

United States order intake declined 7% in H1 2026 as federal research-funding uncertainty reduced academic demand, less severe than the 23% fall in 2025 orders but short of the recovery Jefferies had hoped could follow Congressional restoration of funding; Shore Capital's Patel said United States customers were still placing orders for high-value research instruments, but funding flows remained uneven, confirming that United States funding weakness persists and remains a material constraint on the academic portion of Judges' demand.

China and Hong Kong order intake fell 47%, primarily because customers delayed government-funded equipment purchases while new research-and-development tax-exemption processes were finalised; the company's statement that tax-exemption processes were beginning to resolve supports a release of deferred orders, but it does not establish that underlying Chinese budgets or customer sentiment have improved, meaning China's recovery is timing not demand proof and a one-off administrative catch-up would lift FY2026 revenue without establishing a durable three-year growth rate.

Founder David Cicurel moved from chief executive to non-executive chair in February 2026, Tim Prestidge became chief executive, and Chief Financial Officer Brad Ormsby will step down as director and finance chief on 23 September 2027 after remaining for a 12-month handover, introducing leadership transition risk at a time when adjusted first-half 2026 EPS fell 72% to 39p and leverage rose to 2.1 times, removing the executive most closely associated with financial discipline and investor relationships.

The 2024 purchases of Luciol, Rockwash and Teer provided diversification and earnings contribution, while the 2025 purchase of Geotek do Brasil's remaining 18% minority interest was immediately earnings enhancing; no acquisition was completed in the first half of 2026, extending the unusually long gap in deal activity, establishing that acquisition cadence has weakened and removing the normal deal contribution from base-case EPS forecasts until a transaction is signed, funded and shown to be earnings enhancing.

FY2024 cash generated from operations rose to £34.0m from £31.3m, and cash conversion reached 122% versus 90% in FY2023, better than the market's concern after H1 conversion of only 64%, but management attributed part of the improvement to timing and advance Geotek payments, meaning cash beat earnings quality but should not be capitalised as a permanent improvement because the benefit was partly linked to a non-recurring contract milestone.

Adjusted net debt increased to £51.7m from £45.2m and leverage rose to 1.7 times, while working capital reached £26.0m, or 19% of revenue, versus management's longer-term aspiration of approximately 10%; the £140m bank facility, £50m accordion and 3-times gearing covenant preserved acquisition capacity, but future EPS accretion still depended on restoring earnings and releasing working capital, so leverage remained manageable but not unconstrained and the balance sheet provides optionality rather than an immediate earnings catalyst.

Zeus said market concentration is increasing as large, diversified instrumentation companies use scale, broader portfolios and mergers and acquisitions to gain share; Chief Executive Tim Prestidge confirmed that Judges is seeing increased competition from Chinese companies seeking to acquire United Kingdom scientific businesses for intellectual property, a development that can raise acquisition prices or remove attractive targets from Judges' reach, establishing that competitive risk has increased at both ends: larger consolidators are gaining scale while Chinese buyers are contesting the deal pool, potentially raising acquisition multiples and reducing Judges' historical 3-to-7-times EBIT discipline.

Management attributed H1 2026 weakness to United States research funding, offshore-wind delays, China's taxexemption process and product-specific issues, while separately acknowledging that new scientific developments could make products obsolete and that inadequate research and development could reduce market share; the September earnings changed the emphasis from 'technology is a long-term opportunity' to 'technology must be monitored so that today's niche instruments remain relevant', although no portfolio-wide disruption audit or quantified revenue-at-risk disclosure was provided, indicating that disruption risk is understated and Judges' decentralised portfolio has no comparable group-wide software or automation platform disclosed while larger peers build integrated workflows. Jefferies accepted management's expectation that FY2025 adjusted EPS would be in line with the 288.7p consensus, but the significance was relative to the already reduced July forecast rather than the original recovery case; the broker noted that H1 EPS was below its own estimate and that organic orders had flattened, so retaining the full-year view required a heavily second-half-weighted delivery, confirming that sell-side relief was conditional and no broker raised estimates after the H1 2025 results because the recovery depended on Geotek, deferred orders and second-half execution rather than evidence of renewed growth.

Jefferies retained Buy and a 7,380p target, valuing the shares at approximately 16 times FY2026 enterprise value to EBITA and 21 times price to earnings, below the company's long-term averages; the discount supported a positive rating, but the broker's rationale still required US research spending to recover, cost control to improve and the timing of Geotek's next contract to provide upside, meaning valuation is attractive but conditional and the low multiple reflects execution uncertainty rather than a proven earnings floor.

The most important single change was the confirmation that Judges' margin recovery depends on volume returning rather than on a clearly quantified structural cost programme, making the three-year EPS trajectory more sensitive to external demand normalisation–particularly United States research funding, Chinese procurement and industrial capital spending– than to internal operational improvement, acquisition discipline or portfolio restructuring.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Evidence of durable improvement would require United States order intake growing rather than declining 7%, China and Hong Kong orders stabilising after the 47% fall and returning to positive year-on-year growth, alongside adjusted operating margin recovering toward the 25.6% FY2023 level from the 20.9% FY2024 and 8.6% H1 2026 outcomes, with at least one earnings-enhancing acquisition completed and the order book increasing to materially above the 17.3 weeks reported at H1 2026.

The observable test is whether FY2026 adjusted EPS reaches or exceeds the 200.5p consensus published before the September interim results, delivered by March 2027, with quarterly order intake returning to positive year-onyear growth by H1 2027 and evidence that United States academic customers are converting funding approvals into instrument purchases rather than merely expressing interest; absent those outcomes, the recovery case should be treated as delayed rather than intact.

The two most important questions next disclosures must answer are: first, did delayed Chinese and United States orders convert into Q4 2026 revenue and operating profit at the margin required to deliver 200.5p adjusted EPS, or did customer delays extend into 2027 and require another earnings reset; and second, has management identified quantified cost actions, acquisition targets or portfolio changes that can restore operating margin toward 25% and return on total invested capital toward the 30% medium-term ambition, or does the recovery remain dependent solely on external demand returning to Judges rather than on demonstrable operational improvement.

Other side of the story: The optimistic reading is that near-parity Q3 orders, a 17.3-week backlog, early Chinese procurement normalisation and resilient semiconductor and battery demand show deferred purchases are returning rather than disappearing, while specialisation may still outweigh scale because Judges owns small, specialised businesses that larger groups may overlook, and if niche customer relationships and proprietary applications remain decisive, Chinese entrants may win commoditised volume without displacing Judges' highest-return businesses, allowing margins and acquisition returns to recover faster than current evidence suggests; however, the contrary case is that the recovery is still only flat year on year, the United States remains funding-constrained, and the unusually Q4-weighted forecast leaves too little evidence to support a durable return to historic organic growth.

Section 1 – Brief company description and history

1.
Business model, niche compounder: Judges Scientific is an AIM-listed scientific-instrument group that acquires and develops small businesses serving specialised global niches, with shareholder returns generated through acquisitions, organic growth, debt reduction and dividends. Its subsidiaries sell instruments and services to universities, research facilities, manufacturers, original-equipment manufacturers and regulatory authorities, rather than competing as a single vertically integrated product company.
2.
History, successful pivot: Judges was originally listed as Judges Capital in 2003 as an investment vehicle, but changed direction in 2005 when it acquired Fire Testing Technology for £3.7m from a retiring owner, discovering a repeatable opportunity among roughly 2,000 UK scientific-instrument businesses. It has since acquired 25 businesses, while increasing total revenue at a 19% compound annual growth rate and organic revenue at 7% through 2025. Portfolio breadth, differentiated exposure: The portfolio spans fire testing, geotechnical analysis, soil and rock testing, electrophysiology, microscopy, vacuum technology, thin-film deposition, fibre-optic testing, surface coatings and scientific services. Recent additions included Luciol Instruments, Rockwash Geodata and Teer Coatings in 2024, while the acquisition of Geotek’s remaining Brazilian minority interest in December 2025 was described as immediately earnings enhancing.
3.
Autonomous operating structure: Acquired companies retain substantial operational autonomy, while Judges supplies capital, financial controls, leadership support, acquisition expertise and group-wide experience. This makes the model less centralised than Oxford Instruments, Renishaw or Spectris, but it also makes group-level forecasting less transparent because performance is dispersed across many small subsidiaries. Acquisition discipline, central EPS engine: Judges generally targets strong exporters with defensible niche positions, sustainable profits, cash generation and robust margins, paying 3 to 7 times earnings before interest and tax and borrowing up to 3 times earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Management’s expectation entering the selected period was that disciplined, earnings-enhancing acquisitions would remain the principal accelerator of EPS once organic demand normalised.
4.
Export and end-market mix: More than 85% of revenue is exported, with the FY2024 mix including 25% from North America, 27% from the rest of Europe and 10% from China and Hong Kong. End markets were approximately 20% to 25% geotechnical, about 20% industry optimisation and roughly 40% to 50% life sciences and semiconductors, giving Judges broader exposure than a company dependent on one research segment.
5.
Three-year financial trajectory, sharp reversal: Revenue rose from £113.2m in 2022 to £136.1m in 2023 before falling to £133.6m in 2024, while adjusted operating profit increased from £30.1m to £34.8m and then fell to £27.9m. Adjusted earnings per share moved from 363.8p in 2022 to 374.6p in 2023 and 283.4p in 2024, so the earnings base entering the selected results was materially below the prior peak rather than continuing its historic compounding path.
6.
Margin and return deterioration: Adjusted operating margin declined from 25.6% in 2023 to 20.9% in 2024 because lower volumes exposed the group’s fixed-cost base and the absence of Geotek coring revenue removed a high-margin contribution. Return on total invested capital fell from 22.7% to 16.5%, against management’s medium-term 30% aspiration, making recovery in volume and operating leverage more important to EPS than modest revenue growth alone.
7.
Cash and leverage resilience: Cash generated from operations improved from £31.3m in 2023 to £34.0m in 2024, with cash conversion rising from 90% to 122%, although the company acknowledged that working capital remained well above its pre-Covid norm. Adjusted net debt nevertheless increased from £45.1m to £51.7m after three acquisitions, while leverage stood at 1.7 times, leaving capacity for further deals but less balance-sheet flexibility than the headline cash conversion implied.

8.
Peer positioning: Jefferies’ benchmarking found Judges ahead of Oxford Instruments, Renishaw and Spectris on the combination of margin, organic growth and return metrics, while also identifying Judges as a sector leader in EBITA margin and long-term organic performance. The comparison supported a premium valuation before the earnings deterioration, but the subsequent profit warnings weakened the case for treating Judges as a consistently superior compounder.

Expectation gap, high-risk setup: The recovery case was materially more demanding than the FY2024 earnings base because it required both the coring contract and a return toward normal organic growth, whereas Jefferies did not expect margins to regain FY2023 levels until FY2027. Other side of the story: The bullish interpretation was that Judges’ diversified portfolio, 85%-plus export exposure, cash generation and disciplined acquisition model made the 2024 weakness temporary, but the absence of exact peer financial and market-return data and the widening gap between historic returns and current guidance leave a credible risk that the business was being valued on a recovery that had not yet become observable.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. The bar entering the selected earnings was already divided: the recovery case depended on Geotek, deferred orders, margin leverage and acquisitions, while the more cautious view centred on US research funding, China, order timing and Judges’ reduced forecasting transparency.
1.
Recovery thesis, qualified: Jefferies’ January 2025 view expected a better year because Geotek coring, deferred orders, recent acquisitions and services contracts could offset weak end markets, but the broker had already downgraded Judges to Hold and cut its target price to 8,500p from 11,700p because visibility on recovery was poor.
2.
Published consensus, modest: The clearest pre-earnings benchmark was Zeus’s January estimate of 283.9p adjusted basic earnings per share for FY2024, against company guidance of 270p to 300p and market consensus of 276.8p, leaving the immediate bar close to the lower half of management’s range rather than implying a return to the prior earnings peak.
3.
FY2025 recovery, ambitious: Shore Capital’s Speakman and Patel modelled FY2025 revenue of £147m, adjusted operating profit of £37.1m, a 25.2% margin and adjusted EPS of 375.1p, implying 37% EPS growth and a 460-basis-point margin recovery from FY2024, while the note supplied no separate consensus benchmark for those figures.
4.
Organic growth, high bar: Jefferies expected approximately 7% organic growth in FY2025, although that forecast benefited materially from Geotek, and assumed a 300-basis-point margin improvement as higher volumes flowed through Judges’ operatingly geared cost base; this was more optimistic than the company’s recent underlying order growth, which had been only 2% excluding the coring order in FY2024.
5.
Geotek, central swing factor: Analysts treated the Japanese coring expedition as the principal determinant of the near-term earnings shape because it made Judges unusual among scientific-instrument peers in having a first-half-weighted year, whereas the prior year had contained no coring revenue; the market therefore focused on execution and timing rather than treating Geotek as recurring organic growth.
6.
Deferred projects, expected release: Shore expected projects postponed from FY2024 to be delivered during FY2025, supporting a stronger first half and allowing revenue to recover even without a broad improvement in demand; however, the broker did not quantify how much of its £37.1m operating-profit forecast depended on those projects rather than on sustainable new orders. Order book, supportive but temporary: The January trading evidence showed an organic order book of 18.7 weeks versus 16.7 weeks at FY2023 year-end, which encouraged Shore’s recovery case, but the broker provided no market forecast for order-book duration, leaving investors unable to distinguish genuine demand improvement from the accumulation of delayed deliveries. US research funding, principal downside: Jefferies had reduced FY2025 and FY2026 estimates because sentiment around US scientific research had deteriorated, with the United States representing about 25% of Judges’ revenue and roughly half of that exposure estimated to be academic; the broker expected order delays to remain prevalent even while retaining a long-term positive view.
7.
China, structural concern: Jefferies expected China to remain difficult and described some Chinese headwinds as potentially structural rather than merely cyclical, contrasting with the recovery thesis that assumed normalisation in scientific-instrument end markets; this made regional mix and order conversion an important test of the FY2025 recovery.
8.
Peer read-through, mixed: Jefferies’ June assessment of the broader instrumentation sector was that Europe and the United Kingdom showed some momentum, China remained subdued and the United States was slowing, suggesting Judges entered the earnings with a more uneven geographic outlook than a simple global recovery narrative implied. Scientific end markets, secular support: The positive market view rested on structural demand from higher education, scientific research, process optimisation, batteries and semiconductors, with Zeus citing a global scientific-instruments market forecast of 4.5% compound annual growth through 2034; the near-term question was whether Judges could capture that growth while public research budgets remained constrained.
9.
Operating leverage, asymmetric risk: Brokers focused on volume because Judges’ employee-heavy, decentralised model creates substantial operating leverage: Shore expected larger orders to generate disproportionate profit growth, but the same cost structure had reduced profitability sharply when organic revenue weakened, making any recovery forecast more sensitive than the headline revenue assumptions suggested. Margin recovery, not yet proven: Shore’s 25.2% FY2025 adjusted operating margin forecast implied a recovery toward the historical range, but the broker did not identify a quantified cost-reduction programme or a separate margin bridge; investors therefore had to assume that deferred projects and Geotek would restore utilisation rather than merely replace lost volume.
10.
Acquisition pipeline, expected option: Jefferies regarded the enlarged balance sheet as capable of supporting further accretive acquisitions and highlighted a pool of approximately 2,000 UK scientificinstrument companies, while also acknowledging that Judges had entered its longest acquisition-free period since 2021 and that seller uncertainty could restrict deal flow.
11.
Acquisition pricing, discipline versus pace: The market valued Judges’ history of buying niche businesses at sensible multiples, but the absence of a new deal before the selected earnings created a tension between quality control and EPS momentum; Jefferies explicitly expected no change in the model but recognised that acquisition multiples would probably rise as the group became larger.
12.
Integration, limited disclosure: Judges’ autonomous subsidiary model was viewed as a strength because local managers retained operational responsibility, yet Jefferies’ post-acquisition review contained no clear group-wide integration milestones or quantified synergy assumptions, so the market concentrated more on deal selection and historical returns than on measurable integration execution.
13.
Cash conversion, recovery assumption: Shore expected FY2024 cash conversion to return above 90% after a 64% first-half outcome and inferred second-half conversion above 100%, while modelling a £0.6m workingcapital inflow in FY2025 after a £3.7m outflow in FY2024; this made cash release an implicit support for both deleveraging and acquisitions.
14.
Working capital, unresolved drag: The company’s FY2024 disclosures showed working capital still well above its pre-Covid level, and management identified its reduction as a continuing priority; brokers therefore treated cash conversion as a forecast variable rather than a fully normalised structural feature, especially because Geotek advance payments had supported the reported cash outcome.
15.
Debt capacity, acquisition enabler: Investors focused on the July 2024 refinancing, which increased facilities to £140m including a £50m accordion and extended maturity to July 2028, because the facility supported future acquisitions; however, the £51.7m year-end adjusted net debt and 1.7-times gearing meant acquisition capacity still depended on cash conversion and earnings recovery.
16.
Dividends, secondary priority: The proposed 104.5p FY2024 dividend, up 10% and covered 2.7 times by adjusted EPS, reassured investors that capital returns remained intact, but management made future increases conditional on earnings cover and retaining resources for acquisitions, so brokers treated dividend growth as supportive rather than the principal EPS catalyst.
17.
Valuation, recovery priced in: Shore’s March review described Judges as trading at more than a 40% discount to its five-year average price-to-earnings and enterprise-value-to-EBITDA multiples, while still forecasting a strong earnings recovery; the low valuation reduced downside if recovery occurred, but also signalled that the market required evidence before restoring the former compounder premium. Transparency and execution, rising concern: Jefferies said the multiple FY2024 guidance downgrades were unprecedented for Judges and had exposed additional risk from its relatively opaque reporting structure, meaning the pre-earnings focus was not only whether EPS would recover but whether management could forecast subsidiary-level performance with its former reliability.
18.
Management execution, self-help test: Jefferies expected the executive team to address business-specific issues and believed there was scope for firmer cost control after several years of investment in growth, creating a differentiated pre-earnings test: recovery had to come from operational improvement as well as Geotek and acquisition contribution.
19.
Peer valuation, premium challenged: Jefferies had previously justified a 25% premium to a quality-industrials peer group because Judges led on margins, organic growth and return metrics, but by early 2025 it had reduced that premium to 5% and downgraded the shares to Hold, showing that the market’s central concern was whether historical superiority remained durable through the downturn. Other side of the story: The bullish reading was that a depressed valuation, strong balance sheet, deferred orders, Geotek and secular scientific-instrument demand created substantial EPS recovery potential, but it could be wrong if US funding weakness persisted, China remained structurally impaired, acquisitions stayed absent and operating leverage turned the expected recovery into another forecast shortfall.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Demand mix, shifting: Growth is increasingly expected to come from commercial and industrial applications such as semiconductors, battery development and process optimisation, which have shown greater resilience than academic research, although the group remains exposed to delayed United States funding and publicsector budgets.

Financial profile, temporarily weaker: Adjusted net debt increased to £45.3m at 30 June 2026 from £42.6m at 2025 year-end, and gearing rose to 2.1 times from 1.5 times, reflecting weaker earnings and working-capital needs rather than a change to the long-term leverage policy.
11.
Funding runway, extended: The multibank facility was extended by two years to 1 July 2030 with its covenants unchanged, preserving acquisition capacity beyond the previous 2028 maturity and reducing refinancing risk during the expected recovery period.
12.
Dividends, maintained: Judges continues to target at least 10% annual dividend growth subject to cover and acquisition capacity, but the 36.0p interim dividend was covered only 1.1 times by adjusted earnings versus 4.3 times previously, making further increases increasingly dependent on profit recovery.
13.
Buybacks, unchanged: The Board has considered share repurchases but continues to regard them as less effective than acquisitions, investment in subsidiaries and progressive dividends, so buybacks are not a plausible near-term EPS support under the current capital-allocation framework.
14.
Strategic targets, unchanged: Management has retained its disciplined buy-and-build model, progressive dividends and medium-term 30% return on total invested capital ambition, despite current performance remaining below that aspiration and the acquisition pipeline producing no first-half transaction.
15.
External disruption, persistent: United States research-funding uncertainty, delayed offshore-wind investment, Chinese procurement-tax delays and geopolitical tension have replaced the temporary 2024 order weakness as the central external risks to revenue timing and EPS through the next 18 months.
16.
Industry outlook, mixed: The global scientific-instrument market retains long-term growth from research complexity, industrial measurement and quality control, but larger diversified competitors are gaining scale and regulatory scrutiny is lengthening approval timelines, creating a plausible slower-growth scenario even if Judges’ niche positions remain defensible.
17.
EPS priority, clear: Operating leverage from a recovery in order intake is the dominant moving part because it can restore margins on the existing portfolio, while Geotek timing and acquisitions are secondary accelerators and leadership or financing changes are primarily execution risks. Other side of the story: The recovery could arrive sooner than current caution implies if United States funding begins flowing, Chinese procurement normalises, offshore wind investment resumes and a high-quality acquisition closes, but the company’s recent forecast history means investors should require delivered orders and margins rather than rely on pipeline or strategic assurances.

Section 16 – Conclusion

Section summary
  1. The September 2026 interim results changed Judges Scientific's outlook from a broad recovery case supported by Geotek, deferred orders and acquisition momentum into a conditional, heavily back-end-weighted forecast that depends on delayed China procurement converting in the fourth quarter, United States research funding normalising without further policy disruption, and operating leverage restoring margins before the next

Geotek expedition arrives no earlier than 2028. The FY2024 results broadly met the lowered consensus bar of approximately 283p adjusted earnings per share after November guidance had already reset expectations from 349p, while H1 2026 delivered 39.0p adjusted EPS, down 72% from 141.4p a year earlier, demonstrating that the underlying portfolio deterioration was more severe than the FY2025 Geotek-supported headline suggested.

The single most important change is that the earnings base entering the selected period assumed a return to historic organic growth and margin levels, whereas the evidence now shows twelve businesses regressing against seven progressing, a 2.1-times leverage ratio versus the earlier 1.5 times, an 8.6% first-half operating margin against a 20.4% prior-year comparison, and no acquisition completed despite an extended £140m banking facility and stated healthy pipeline, making the 18-month to three-year EPS path dependent on demand recovery, cost actions and disciplined deal execution rather than on the simple resumption of the historic compounding model that investors previously capitalised.

The first material deviation from consensus and broker expectations is that H1 2026 order intake fell 12% year on year before recovering to approximately 1% below the prior year by September, whereas Shore Capital's unchanged FY2026 forecast of 200.2p adjusted EPS requires approximately 161p in the second half after only 39p in the first half, implying roughly 80% of annual earnings must be delivered in a concentrated final quarter that depends on China's tax-exemption resolution, delayed United States customer shipments converting and deferred offshore-wind projects materialising rather than on observable broad-based demand momentum.

The second deviation is that North American order intake declined 18% in H1 despite Congress rejecting proposed researchbudget cuts, because Jefferies' survey of 50 university employees representing more than $20 billion of institutional spending found 94% had experienced National Institutes of Health or National Science Foundation grant cancellations and average biomedical research spending was down approximately 16% year on year, contradicting the market's earlier assumption that restored headline funding would quickly release instrument purchasing.

The third deviation is that China and Hong Kong order intake fell 47% as customers postponed government-funded equipment purchases while new research-and-development tax-exemption procedures were finalised, which was materially worse than the earlier expectation of Chinese normalisation after weak 2024 trading and pushed potential revenue into an even more compressed fourth quarter rather than providing a durable recovery signal.

The fourth deviation is that the adjusted operating margin fell to 8.6% in H1 2026 from 20.4% a year earlier and 20.9% in full-year 2024, demonstrating that Judges' employee-heavy, decentralised cost structure creates substantial negative operating leverage when volumes weaken and that management's targeted cost actions removing approximately £1.8m from underperforming businesses were insufficient to offset the revenue shortfall.

The fifth deviation is that adjusted net debt rose to £45.3m at June 2026 from £42.6m at December 2025 and gearing increased to 2.1 times from 1.5 times, while the interim dividend was covered only 1.1 times by adjusted earnings versus 4.3 times previously, meaning the progressive-dividend policy now consumes cash during the weakest earnings period and reduces acquisition and deleveraging capacity before operating profit has recovered.

The sixth deviation is that no acquisition was completed in H1 2026 despite the multibank facilities being extended to 1 July 2030 with unchanged covenants, removing the normal buy-and-build EPS accelerator and making the medium-term forecast more dependent on organic recovery than the market's historic valuation of Judges as a disciplined acquisition compounder implied. The seventh finding is that the next Geotek coring expedition has moved from an expectation of early 2027 to no earlier than 2028, eliminating a previously plausible near-term earnings catalyst and making the recovery case more reliant on sustained order conversion, industrial and commercial demand resilience and margin leverage rather than on the high-margin, project-based revenue that supported FY2025 and management's initial FY2026 guidance. The eighth finding is that eight businesses grew during H1 2026, helped by semiconductor manufacturing, battery development and other industrial research, which was more resilient than fundingsensitive academic demand and supports management's strategy of increasing commercial exposure, but these gains were not large enough to offset weakness in the remaining portfolio and Shore estimates that the commercial market is four to five times larger than the academic segment, implying the structural shift will take years rather than quarters to change Judges' earnings mix.

The ninth finding is that Chief Financial Officer Brad Ormsby will leave on 23 September 2027 after a 12-month handover, removing the executive most closely associated with financial discipline, investor relationships and the credibility built through Judges' expansion, precisely when adjusted first-half earnings per share fell 72% to 39p, leverage rose to 2.1 times and the market requires precise order conversion, cost control and integration execution to restore confidence in the forecasting model.

The tenth finding is that competitive pressure has increased at both ends of the market, with larger consolidators such as Halma, Oxford Instruments and Spectris using scale, broader portfolios and integrated software to gain share, while Chinese competitors are moving upmarket through lower-cost products, local infrastructure and intellectual-property acquisitions that contest Judges' deal pool and threaten niche margins, making the historical assumption of robust pricing power and disciplined acquisition multiples less secure over the next three years.

The eleventh finding is that disruption risk has shifted from a simple long-term opportunity toward a nearer-term threat because Danaher is combining instruments, robotics, workflow software and NVIDIA accelerated computing to create autonomous laboratory loops, Waters is pursuing cloud-connected artificial-intelligence workflows linking hypotheses to automated testing, and Judges' decentralised portfolio has no comparable group-wide software or automation platform disclosed, leaving subsidiaries exposed if customers increasingly buy integrated systems rather than standalone equipment.

The twelfth finding is that political and regulatory exposure has become a material earnings variable through United States research-funding policy uncertainty, China's procurement-tax delays, the United Kingdom's increased Corporation Tax and Employers' National Insurance contributions adding approximately £3m a year to tax contributions, and geopolitical trade risks that remain unquantified but are explicitly cited as threats to sales in China, Taiwan and global industrial activity.

The thirteenth finding is that return on total invested capital fell to 12.9% in H1 2026 from 17.8% in full-year 2025 and 22.7% in 2023, against management's medium-term ambition to restore it toward 30%, so the target now represents a substantial recovery requirement that depends on higher operating profit and better capital utilisation rather than a level close to current performance, while the absence of quantified structural cost savings or a disclosed margin bridge leaves the trajectory more vulnerable to execution risk than the headline strategy unchanged message suggests.

The fourteenth finding is that supply conditions are better than during the 2021- 22 disruption but have not fully normalised, with Armfield still redesigning its manufacturing base after several suppliers exited, working capital remaining 16% of revenue versus a longer-term objective of approximately 10%, and cash conversion of 122% in FY2024 and 136% adjusted for Geotek timing flattered by advance payments rather than proving that inventory and procurement risks have disappeared.

The fifteenth finding is that the order book increased to 17.3 weeks from 15.7 weeks at FY2025 year-end and was broadly unchanged from 17.4 weeks a year earlier, which was better than the market's concern after weak opening orders, but management acknowledged that some customers had already ordered equipment but asked the group to delay shipment until funding and procurement processes were resolved, meaning the backlog contains demand that is not yet revenue and creating execution risk if conversion is slower than the reported order recovery implies. The sixteenth finding is that the strongest case against this conclusion is that near-parity Q3 orders, a 17.3- week backlog, early Chinese procurement normalisation, resilient semiconductor and battery demand, targeted cost actions already delivering approximately £1.8m of savings, and an unmodelled acquisition pipeline supported by an extended £140m facility show that deferred purchases are returning rather than disappearing, while Judges' niche positions, local supplier relationships, 85%-plus export exposure and decentralised responsiveness may allow margins and returns to recover faster than the current weak evidence suggests, particularly if United States funding begins flowing, offshore-wind investment resumes and a high-quality acquisition closes at a disciplined multiple.

The seventeenth finding, a non-obvious consensus reading genuinely supported by the evidence, is that Judges' decentralised portfolio structure and autonomous subsidiary model, which the market previously valued as entrepreneurial and operationally flexible, may now be a disadvantage because Jefferies describes the reporting as relatively opaque or a black box, the multiple FY2024 guidance downgrades were unprecedented for Judges, and the absence of group-wide software, service scale or quantified integration milestones leaves investors unable to distinguish genuine demand improvement from delayed deliveries, temporary Geotek effects and administrative catch-up in China rather than a durable return to the historic 7% organic revenue and 9% organic EBIT compound annual growth rates.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Observable evidence that FY2026 adjusted earnings per share exceeds 200p while H2 order intake grows mid-single digits or better year on year, United States academic orders stabilise or improve sequentially, China delivers high-single-digit or better growth excluding prior-year comparisons, and working capital falls below 15% of revenue by year-end, all confirmed no later than the March 2027 full-year results, would demonstrate that the recovery is broad-based rather than concentrated in fourth-quarter timing, that customers are committing budgets rather than merely releasing delayed procurement, and that cash generation can support both deleveraging and acquisitions without requiring another guidance reset or prolonged margin suppression.

If those conditions are met and management announces an earnings-enhancing acquisition at a multiple within the historical 4-to-6-times EBIT range during the following six months, the combination would justify restoring a three-year EPS compounding assumption above the current cautious trajectory and would support re-rating the shares toward the historical quality-compounder premium that Jefferies removed when it downgraded Judges to Hold and cut its target price to 8,500p from 11,700p in January 2025.

The two most important questions the next disclosures must answer are, first, whether FY2026 delivers the heavily back-end-weighted 200p-plus adjusted EPS consensus without another guidance reduction, with transparent disclosure of how much fourth-quarter revenue came from China administrative catch-up, delayed United States shipments and deferred projects versus new commercial orders that support sustained FY2027 growth, and second, whether management can name a successor Chief Financial Officer, complete an immediately earnings-enhancing acquisition at a disciplined multiple, reduce working capital materially below 16% of revenue and restore return on total invested capital toward 20%-plus within the next 12 to 18 months, because those outcomes would prove that the strategy, financial discipline, balance-sheet capacity and portfolio quality that investors historically capitalised remain intact despite the unprecedented earnings volatility and leadership transition.
