Theon International (THEON-NL): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results modestly strengthen the operating half of our case and give its balance-sheet hinge its first better-than-expected print, but they put the case's largest disagreement with consensus, the tube cap, under real pressure from the company's own raised target, and they miss the one test the case itself named for this quarter.

1. What changed

  • The operating lines beat, as the case expected. Q3 2026 revenue was €134.1 million, up 40.4% year on year and about 3% above Deutsche Bank's €130 million; the case expected about 132 EURm. Adjusted EBIT was €35.1 million, a 26.2% margin against Deutsche Bank's 26.3%. The case expected a company-basis margin near 23.5% and called that "not a miss"; the print was almost three points higher. Nine-month revenue was €382.8 million and adjusted EBIT €100.2 million (26.2%).
  • The balance sheet beat, which the case did not expect. Net debt at end Q3 2026 was €251.1 million, 10% below Deutsche Bank's €279.0 million, at 1.6x LTM EBITDA. Working capital absorbed 42.7% of nine-month revenue against Deutsche Bank's 45%; the case also expected "near its seasonal 45% peak". The case's record was two 2026 net-debt prints worse than expected, and a third would have triggered downgrades in two of its topics. FY2026 capex guidance fell to about €25 million from €30 million, with the balance deferred to FY2027.
  • The case's own test was missed. The case said the nine-month book-to-bill "at or above 1.0x is the number that matters". It was 0.74x, on order intake of €285.2 million (up 22.5%). Soft backlog fell to €1,377.0 million at end September 2026 from €1,455.9 million in June. Deutsche Bank had expected a seasonally soft quarter, so the sell-side does not read this as a miss; on our arithmetic the case's bar was probably unreachable from the first half. Either way the test moves to Q4, where Deutsche Bank puts the order intake needed for a full-year 1.0x at €440 million.
  • The medium-term target went up, not out. Organic revenue growth guidance was raised to more than 20% a year from more than 15%. The case expected the medium-term ambitions to be the ones "most likely to be pushed out". FY2026 guidance (about €600 million, margin above 26%) was reconfirmed.
  • HGH timing. The company says the €300 million HGH acquisition is on track to close in late Q4 2026; the case has it closing late in Q1 2027.
  • Sell-side response. Only Deutsche Bank has published: Buy, €39 target unchanged, no major change to revenue estimates. It notes consensus revenue is about 5% below management's targets and adjusted EBIT 7% to 9% below, and repeats that interest dilutes adjusted EPS by about 5% and that the Exosens stake lacks a strategy.

2. The challenge

Reading one: the tube cap has lifted, and the case is now the outlier on the wrong side. The case holds two judgements that these results set against each other: that this management under-promises, and that tubes cap night-vision growth at 4% to 7% a year after FY2027. A management with that record has now raised a multi-year target into the cap. The arithmetic is stark: more than 20% a year from about €600 million is more than €1.0 billion of organic revenue by FY2029, before HGH, Merio and the Safran venture. The case has 945.5 EURm in FY2029 including them. The case was 2% to 4% below consensus; consensus is now 5% below the company. For this reading to be right, tube supply must be rising faster than the case's 80,000 to 90,000 a year, or non-night-vision must be scaling well ahead of the case's build. There is evidence for both: Exosens reports capacity ramping ahead of schedule, Harder tubes are already going into goggle orders, non-night-vision reached 17.6% of nine-month revenue from 7.1%, and Platform revenue of €26.0 million was 18% ahead of Deutsche Bank. The case holds an allocation increase as a counter-case; it does not hold it as the company's stated base. We think this reading is plausible, and it is the one we find hardest to dismiss.

Reading two: the guidance has changed character, and the raise is cover for a weak order quarter. The same release that raised the target showed a falling backlog and a 0.74x book-to-bill, weeks before the company takes on the HGH debt. A controlling founder who needs the market to tolerate peak leverage has a reason to move the conversation to growth. If so, the pressure falls on the case's trust in guidance, not on the tube cap, and the case's "contracted, policy-floored" demand reading is less complete than it looked: the case's 902 EURm of options "expected to be exercised shortly" do not appear in the reported intake. For this to be right, Q4 orders must fall well short, and the first FY2027 guidance must sit below 20% organic. Against it: Deutsche Bank reports Bundestag approval of nearly €200 million for an initial 25,000 night-vision attachments, about half its Q4 estimate, and €45 million of post-period contracts. We rate this possible, not probable, but the case has no place for it.

Reading three: the leverage test arrives earlier and reads worse than the case's path. If HGH closes in December, the print on 19 March 2027, which the case treats as the benign early read at about 250 EURm, instead carries the full debt: Deutsche Bank projects about €573 million and 3.4x at end FY2026; management says about 3.0x pro forma. That is the case's mid-2027 debt landing on a smaller EBITDA base, against a covenant the case puts at 3.5x or possibly 3.0x. The Q3 beat would then be the last clean print rather than the first of a series, and part of it is timing (deferred capex, and less inventory built because fewer orders came in). For this to be right, closing must fall before year-end and the test must run on reported, not pro-forma, EBITDA.

Readings one and two cannot both be right; reading three can accompany either.

3. What it does to the case

  • Scope for upgrades to guidance, consensus and the narrative (D, 45): materially less pressure. The case called a 1% to 3% raise small and expected the ambitions pushed out. A five-point lift to the medium-term rate, with consensus below it, is the opposite. Offsetting: the dashboard shows consensus EPS down 3% over one month and 4% over three, as the case predicted.
  • The tube-cap judgement on FY2028 revenue (model 853 EURm): more pressure, and with it the case's claim to hold "the largest disagreement with consensus that nobody has yet written down". The case expected resolution against consensus; the company has just argued the other way.
  • Conservative guidance and the delivery record (B, 76): pulled both ways. The net-debt record improved; whether guidance is still conservative is exactly what readings one and two dispute.
  • Internally-driven change forces (C, 66): less pressure. The downgrade trigger of a third worse net-debt print was avoided, and mix delivery continued.
  • Riskiness (D, 42) and Case Riskiness : the same, with the timing less comfortable. One good print against a possibly earlier and higher peak.
  • Thesis's Change Forces' Strength , Foundations' Robustness , Setups' Fit/Crispness : the same. The setup still turns on the leverage question.
  • Price. The shares are 28.62 EUR (10 November 2026), below the case's 30.42 EUR and its 29.20 EUR return base; against the case's 32.93 EUR value the 18-month return is about 15%, inside the same band. Relative strength has fallen to the 15th percentile from the case's 32nd and the three-month return is -24.6%. Neither threshold the case names has been crossed: the price is above the 23.34 EUR low and 9.0% below the 200-day average the case wanted regained. Pinned letters are unchanged: technical momentum F, sell-side earnings momentum C, valuation B.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It should carry the other brokers' estimates and targets, and so whether consensus moves toward the raised target or holds 5% below it, and whether the EPS cuts continue. It is unlikely to carry anything on Q4 orders, tube allocation, the covenant or the HGH closing date.
  • Q4 orders. Order announcements through December, and the full-year book-to-bill with the FY2026 results on 19 March 2027. The Bundestag stage needs confirming: the case had the committee on 12 November and the vote on 27 November.
  • HGH closing. An announcement before or after 31 December 2026 decides whether reading three is live.
  • 19 March 2027. End-2026 net debt, and whether the first FY2027 guidance carries more than 20% organic growth. That guidance is the cleanest test between readings one and two.
  • Exosens' Q4 release for tube volumes, the case's own marker for the cap.
  • Not committed by the company: the covenant level and its EBITDA definition, Theon's tube allocation, and the years and base to which "more than 20%" applies. Nothing scheduled answers these.
