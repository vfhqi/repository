Section 2 – Debt Policy, Leverage Capacity, and Lease Obligations

Section summary
  1. Convatec targets a net debt to adjusted EBITDA ratio of approximately 2.0 times, providing a balanced leverage policy that supports shareholder returns while maintaining financial flexibility.
  2. The exclusion of lease liabilities from Convatec's leverage definition understates its total debt burden but expands its calculated borrowing capacity.
  3. Convatec maintains significant debt headroom to fund its share buybacks, supported by its recently expanded 2031 credit facility and flexible leverage target.

Historical Leverage and Deleveraging Path

1.
Prudent capital structure: Convatec targets a long-term leverage ratio of approximately 2.0 times net debt to adjusted EBITDA to balance operational risk and capital returns. According to the H1 2026 interim report, management is comfortable operating above or below this target depending on mergers and acquisitions or other investment opportunities. This flexibility ensures that the company does not restrict its growth capital to maintain a rigid leverage ratio.
2.
Elevated short-term leverage: Convatec saw its leverage ratio rise temporarily to 2.3 times in the first half of 2026, up from 1.9 times in the first half of 2025. In the interim results released on August 3, 2026, the company attributed this increase to normal working capital seasonality and stated its expectation to return to approximately 2.0 times leverage by the end of the year. This seasonal fluctuation highlights how shortterm capital commitments can alter the immediate capacity for share buybacks.
3.
Disciplined historical deleveraging: Convatec has historically executed a disciplined deleveraging strategy to restore its financial health and operational flexibility since its public listing. As reported in the FY2016 earnings call on March 8, 2017, the group had an elevated net debt ratio of 6.9 times EBITDA at the end of 2015, which it aggressively deleveraged to 3.0 times EBITDA by the end of 2016. This successful reduction demonstrates management's commitment to maintaining a robust balance sheet before deploying surplus cash.
4.
Successful balance sheet normalization: Convatec sustained its leverage reduction over the subsequent years, bringing the metric down from 2.7 times in 2018 to 2.0 times in 2020. According to the FY2020 earnings call on March 5, 2021, this reduction was supported by strong operational cash flow of over $500 million, which closed in on their long-term leverage target. This historical pattern suggests that management prioritizes structural balance sheet health over speculative distributions.

Lease Treatment and Leverage Calculations

5.
Modified leverage definition: Convatec defines its leverage ratio by dividing net debt excluding lease liabilities by adjusted EBITDA, which lowers the reported metric. In the 2025 annual report, the company reported a leverage ratio of 2.0 times with net debt at $1,330 million, whereas including lease liabilities of $120 million would have raised its total borrowings to $1,518 million. This calculation methodology means that the company's true economic leverage is slightly higher than the reported figure.

Conservative comparative leverage: Convatec's leverage target of 2.0 times is lower than some of its highly leveraged European medical technology peers. Other side of the story: Convatec's conservative leverage calculation and flexible policy could allow for a much larger debt-funded buyback program if M&A remains subdued. If the group fails to find attractive acquisitions, this latent capacity could be redirected to fund a larger buyback without breaching covenant levels.
