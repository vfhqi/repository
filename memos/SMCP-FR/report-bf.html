EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business Configuration and Financial Health

1.
Four-brand portfolio structure: SMCP operates Sandro (50% of revenue), Maje (38%), and Other brands including Claudie Pierlot and Fursac (12%), with Sandro and Maje generating solid adjusted EBIT of EUR 37 million and EUR 28 million respectively in H1 2026, while Other brands posted a EUR 12 million adjusted EBIT loss.
2.
Network rationalization headwind: aggressive store closures reduced the total points of sale to 1,589 in H1 2026, with 41 net closures including targeted China restructuring reducing the retail footprint by one-third to around 130 stores and the closure of Saks corners during US bankruptcy proceedings.
3.
Full-price commercial discipline: the average discount rate improved by 2 points in H1 2026 through strict full-price strategy execution, though aggressive store closures and rigid pricing risk permanently shrinking the customer base.
4.
Below-peer profitability trajectory: SMCP EBIT margins contracted from 9.2% in 2022 to 4.4% in 2024 before recovering to 7.8% in 2025, registering a NOPAT ROCE of only 5.6% in 2025 versus Ralph Lauren's 16.0% ROIC, despite achieving 40.4% free cash flow conversion and reducing net debt excluding leases from EUR 237.2 million to EUR 147.5 million.
5.
Lease-adjusted leverage burden: SMCP net debt including IFRS 16 liabilities reached EUR 531.3 million at the end of 2025, with lease liabilities accounting for EUR 383.8 million, while maintaining low capex intensity of 2.3% of sales versus historical levels of 6.5%

6.
Structural growth deceleration backdrop: the accessible luxury industry exhibits 5.4% projected CAGR through 2030, with mainland Chinese personal luxury spending contracting by 3% to 5% in 2025 and the middleclass consumer cohort facing severe cost inflation pressures.
7.
Downstream retail integration advantage: SMCP captures highly profitable retail profit pools by generating 88% of H1 2026 revenue through directly operated retail, delivering high management gross margin of 76.0% in H1 2026, though high retail operating costs create volatile EBIT margin exposure.
8.
Asset-light manufacturing flexibility: SMCP outsources 100% of garment assembly across a balanced 50% EuroMed and 50% Asia geographic split, protecting against upstream capital intensity while maintaining five-tonine-week in-season replenishment agility.

Customer Dynamics and Channel Evolution

Expansive loyalty database with aspirational pricing risk: SMCP maintains over 5 million contactable European customer profiles with a consolidated Net Promoter Score of 77, yet aggressive price hikes over 18 to 24 months triggered customer volume resistance among middle-class buyers, while strict full-price discipline negatively impacted e-commerce volumes.

Sourcing Footprint and Manufacturing Models

1.
Asset-light manufacturing model: SMCP owns zero raw material production, fabric mills, or sewing factories, outsourcing 100% of its garment assembly to external specialists. Dichotomous production models: the sourcing mix consists of 33% cut-and-sew contract work (where SMCP directly buys fabrics and subcontracts manufacturing) and 67% finished product procurement.

EuroMed geographic cluster: near-shore partners are located in France, Italy, Spain, Portugal, Tunisia, Turkey, Morocco, and Eastern Europe. Asia geographic cluster: far-shore manufacturing and material partners are concentrated in Mainland China, Vietnam, Cambodia, and India.

This return deficit becomes particularly concerning when contextualized against the peer set, as even mid-tier competitors like Hugo Boss achieve 10.0% ROIC, suggesting SMCP operates with structurally higher capital intensity or margin compression that prevents competitive cash-on-cash returns.
