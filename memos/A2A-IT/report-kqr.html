Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Regulatory returns and concession terms will heavily influence the capital deployment and valuation multiples of Italian utilities over the next three years.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Regulatory baseline defined: "What has Italy's energy regulator decided, or what do brokers now expect it to decide, in the trigger review of allowed returns for 2027 for electricity distribution, gas distribution and water, and what is the effect for A2A, Acea, Hera, Iren and Enel company by company; what do brokers expect from the full recalibration of allowed returns for 2028 to 2030, and when will it be published; and how many of the electricity distribution concession extensions under Budget Law 2025 and Resolution 392/2025 have been signed, at what fee, and with what addition to each company's regulated asset base?"
2.
Material earnings sensitivity: Changes in regulatory allowed returns directly alter the remuneration on existing asset bases and work-in-progress investments. A mark-to-market analysis by Bernstein Research on 1 October 2026 suggests that a potential WACC reduction of 30 to 40 basis points for 2027 would decrease nominal returns across electricity and gas networks. This compression directly dampens earnings power unless offset by higher inflation-linked regulatory asset base growth. Consequently, these regulatory updates represent a key risk for earnings per share over the next eighteen months to three years.

Valuation Gaps and Capital Deployment

3.
Valuation multiple discount: Italian integrated utilities trade at a discount compared to pure-play regulated network peers. A comparative analysis by Bernstein Research on 15 September 2026 indicates that European energy network operators trade at a average forward price-to-earnings multiple of approximately 14.8 times, with Terna trading at 18 times. In contrast, diversified cohorts such as Enel trade at lower multiples of 12.4 times for 2026. If the upcoming 2028 regulatory reset proves supportive, these integrated companies could narrow their discount relative to the thirteen point eight times multiple benchmark of network-led groups.
4.
Concession extension opportunity: The prolongation of electricity distribution concessions under Budget Law 2025 offers a substantial path for capital deployment and regulated asset base expansion. Under the proposed framework detailed in a Jefferies Research note on 22 July 2025, operators must submit extraordinary investment plans in exchange for an upfront concession fee. This fee will be incorporated into each company's regulated asset base, driving long-term earnings growth. If these extensions are successfully executed, they will significantly enhance the regulated capital expenditure pipeline and support higher EPS.
5.
Divergent company exposure: The operational and earnings impact of the regulatory updates varies considerably across individual corporate profiles. Bernstein Research argued on 1 October 2026 that pure-play networks like Terna are highly sensitive to allowed return changes, whereas diversified utilities are relatively insulated. While Enel remains protected by its global scale, operators like Acea derive approximately 95% of EBITDA from regulated activities, highlighting elevated regional regulatory sensitivity. These dynamics mean that localized regulatory decisions will determine the relative cost of capital and capital allocation of each utility.

Divergent Strategic Horizons

6.
Other side of the story: While near-term regulatory adjustments pose downside risks, the sharp rise in sovereign bond yields since 2025 provides a strong foundation for medium-term recovery. Bernstein Research highlighted on 1 October 2026 that the 2028-2030 full WACC recalibration is highly likely to prove more supportive than the 2027 interim review. Higher sovereign yields, Country Risk Premium adjustments, and potential tax allowances are expected to bolster allowed returns. These positive adjustments could reverse the near-term cuts and narrow the valuation gap to pure networks over the next three years.

Section 2 – The 2027 Trigger Review of Allowed Returns

Section summary
  1. Italian regulated returns face a near-term downward recalibration of thirty to forty basis points in the upcoming 2027 review.
  2. Spread compression in Italian sovereign bonds remains the primary driver of allowed return cuts despite rising risk-free rates.
  3. Sovereign basket methodology adjustments concerning France could mitigate the severity of the return reductions.

Trigger Mechanics and

1.
Parameters Tightening regulatory adjustment: Under Resolution 513/2024, ARERA reduced the WACC trigger threshold from fifty basis points to thirty basis points. This narrower threshold means that if recalculated market inputs deviate by more than thirty basis points, ARERA will adjust the allowed WACC for 2027. Consequently, the annual review introduces heightened WACC volatility for Italian power networks. This volatility directly affects corporate planning cycles unless offset by mitigating regulatory indexation mechanisms.
2.
Evolving debt assumptions: The regulator utilizes European Central Bank inflation expectations to translate nominal funding costs into real allowed costs of debt. For the 2027 trigger review, Bernstein Research assumes an inflation estimate of 2.10%, representing an increase from the 1.90% embedded in the previous framework. Higher inflation parameters lower the real allowed cost of debt within the WACC formula. This deflationary pressure on debt costs creates additional downward return pressure for capital-intensive utilities.
3.
Shifting regulatory frameworks: The introduction of the ROSS Base framework by ARERA represents a major shift toward expenditure and service objective regulation. This framework immediately recognizes a portion of capital expenditure as fast money in allowed revenues rather than slowly depreciating it through the regulatory asset base. Bernstein Research notes that while this mechanism boosts immediate operating income, it does not alter the underlying return on regulated equity. Consequently, utilities must manage their expenditure profiles to protect returns.
4.
Decreasing water returns: Within the water sector, tariff updates are progressing under the fourth regulatory period known as MTI-4. According to Acea, the local water WACC is set to decrease from 6.13% to 6.06% for the 2026-2027 two-year period. This marginal reduction reflects the regulator's attempt to balance consumer affordability with grid investments. Thus, water operators must rely on operational quality bonuses to sustain regulated capital returns.

Technical Calibration of Sovereign Metrics

5.
Sovereign basket recalculations: The risk-free rate within the WACC calculation is derived from the real yield of ten-year government bonds of Germany, France, Belgium, and the Netherlands. Following France's sovereign rating downgrade below the double-A threshold, ARERA is considering whether to exclude French bonds from the benchmark basket. Bernstein Research estimates that retaining France implies a real risk-free rate of 2.77%, whereas excluding France would lower it to 2.64%. Modifying the basket composition remains a highly sensitive regulatory decision for 2027.
6.
Compressing country risk: The country risk premium represents the spread between Italian ten-year sovereign yields and the benchmark basket average. Although nominal yields have risen, a sharp compression in Italian sovereign spreads has significantly reduced the calculated country risk premium. Bernstein Research projects that the real country risk premium could collapse from 1.32% to 0.49% if France is retained, or 0.62% if France is excluded. This compression represents the primary driver of lower allowed returns in the upcoming intermediate return review.
7.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank argues that the WACC cut will be limited to a forty basis point reduction across 2027 and 2028, with thirty basis points coming from the trigger mechanism and ten basis points from the broader WACC review. Their analysis suggests that the regulator will prioritize regulatory continuity over aggressive cost reductions. This means that while some investors fear drastic cuts driven by affordability concerns, ARERA is likely to maintain a stable and predictable regulatory approach.

Peer Comparisons and

8.
Benchmarks Disparate European regimes: European network operators exhibit highly divergent regulatory frameworks, particularly regarding inflation protection. Unlike the Italian real regulatory system, Spanish distribution grids operate under a nominal pre-tax WACC set at 5.58% for Endesa, which offers no regulatory asset base indexation to inflation. This means that Spanish utilities are fully exposed to cost inflation during their regulatory periods. Consequently, Italy's framework screens as a substantially more generous regime compared to its Iberian neighbors.
9.
Indexed Iberian returns: In Portugal, Redes Energeticas Nacionais operates under an annual WACC indexation mechanism tied to ten-year Portuguese government bond yields. For the first half of 2026, Portuguese base allowed returns on assets stood at 5.20% for electricity transmission and 5.30% for gas transmission. This mechanism ensures rapid cost pass-throughs, which means that Portuguese networks enjoy strong protection against interest rate volatility. This dynamic provides a predictable cash flow horizon relative to fixed nominal regimes.
10.
Baltic regulatory structures: In Lithuania, Ignitis Group operates under a traditional regulatory framework where the National Energy Regulatory Council approved a networks WACC of 5.56% for 2026. The group's returns are supported by an additional tariff component that helps maintain leverage at 5.50 times EBITDA. This structural support allows Ignitis to target a stable adjusted return on capital employed of 6.50% to 7.50% over the medium term. These benchmarks demonstrate that regulated returns remain highly protective across Northern Europe.

Section 3 – Company-by-Company Effects on A2A, Acea, Hera, Iren and Enel

Section summary
  1. ARERA's 2027 allowed return adjustments will differentially impact Italian utilities based on their asset concentration.
  2. Diversified generation and robust retail portfolios partially insulate larger players from tariff compression.
  3. Local multi-utilities rely heavily on water and gas assets to sustain regulated capital remuneration.

Regulated Exposure of Enel

1.
Substantial grid remuneration: Enel maintains an extensive domestic networks portfolio that provides highly predictable infrastructure returns under domestic tariff structures. Analysts at Jefferies Research indicate that Enel Grids' Italian regulated asset base is projected to reach approximately 29 billion euros by 2027. This massive capital foundation ensures that grid operations remain the primary engine of domestic regulated earnings. Consequently, any regulatory return adjustments will directly alter the long-term remuneration of domestic electricity distribution networks.
2.
Insulated earnings profile: The financial impact of the upcoming 2027 allowed return compression remains remarkably limited for the consolidated group. According to Bernstein Research, a 30 basis points reduction in allowed return would reduce Enel's adjusted net income by only 0.70%. This low sensitivity is due to the group's massive exposure to competitive global generation and supply activities. Therefore, Enel's diversified business mix will effectively protect consolidated earnings per share from localized interim regulatory return shocks.

The group reported a 7.5% rise in adjusted EBITDA for the first half of 2026, driven by solid infrastructure performance. This organic growth was achieved despite recent regulatory adjustments that compressed allowed returns on gas distribution. Thus, Hera's efficient capital execution continues to insulate consolidated return metrics from near-term WACC headwinds.

This strategic shift means A2A is increasingly leveraged to electricity grid expansion.

Strong outperformance margins: A2A mitigates regulatory return cuts by achieving superior operational and financial efficiencies. Analysts at Morgan Stanley highlight that A2A has historically achieved an outperformance WACC of 4.5% in gas and 4.6% in electricity networks. These achieved returns sit comfortably above the regulatory baselines set by ARERA. Section 4 – The 2028-2030 Recalibration of Returns

Section summary
  1. The full 2028-2030 regulatory recalibration offers a substantially more supportive return outlook than near-term reviews.
  2. ARERA will fully recalibrate allowed returns in 2028 by fully capturing elevated Eurozone sovereign bond yields.
  3. The transition to full TOTEX frameworks under the ROSS-integrale regime introduces new capital capitalization uncertainties.

Recalibration Timelines and

1.
Guidelines Shifting regulatory timelines: ARERA plans to fully implement the new ROSS-base regulation starting in 2028, signaling a full shift toward expenditure-based incentives. In its March 2026 presentation, Ascopiave stated that ARERA has postponed the comprehensive ROSS implementation to 1 January 2028, with initial guidelines concluding by 30 November 2027. This delay means that multiutilities must operate under transitional rules until the complete recalibration takes effect. This timeline allows companies to adapt their investment strategies to the new expenditure and service objective regulation.
2.
Expanded gas framework: ARERA has initiated consultations to extend ROSS criteria to gas distribution networks, bringing uniformity across the energy sector. Italgas noted in its July 2026 disclosure that Consultation Document 267/2026/R/gas outlines orientations for the sixth regulatory period beginning in 2028. The regulator aims to apply ROSS criteria to gas operators serving over 300,000 delivery points. This reform will determine the long-term remuneration of gas distribution networks.
3.
Evolving capitalization rules: The gradual transition toward a TOTEX model aims to eliminate historical capital expenditure biases. An industry review by Oxera Consulting in November 2024 showed that ARERA is utilizing a phased approach to transition from hybrid rate-of-return models to the ROSS-base framework. The new regime establishes standard capitalization rates to fund corporate investments. This shift means that utilities must closely monitor fast money and slow money allocations.

This reset is highly likely to reverse the near-term cuts by upwardly recalibrating market-driven inputs. Consequently, these supportive adjustments are expected to raise the electricity distribution WACC.

Asset beta parameters: The differentiation of WACC across Italian networks remains anchored on activity-specific asset beta assumptions. Bernstein Research noted on 1 October 2026 that France's downgrade below the double-A threshold could lead to its exclusion from the risk-free rate basket in the 2028 review. Retaining France would keep the Country Risk Premium lower, whereas excluding it would upwardly adjust sovereign spreads. This methodological decision will alter the baseline risk-free rate.

Section 5 – Concession Extensions and RAB Additions

Section summary
  1. Delayed ministerial decrees continue to postpone the signing of electricity concession extensions across Italy.
  2. Concession fees will eventually expand corporate regulated asset bases once the regulatory framework is finalized.
  3. ARERA has formally extended tariff integration timelines to accommodate persistent government policy delays.

Regulatory Deadlines and

1.
Delays Pending ministerial decree: The execution of electricity distribution concession prolongations remains frozen due to political bottlenecks. According to A2A S.p.A.'s interim financial report of 30 June 2026, the Ministry of Environment and Energy Security has not yet adopted the mandatory ministerial decree required by the 2025 Budget Law. This administrative delay has stalled the formal application process for all municipal operators. Consequently, Italian utilities cannot finalize their long-term grid modernization plans until this legal baseline is published.
2.
Tariff timeline extension: Regulators have adjusted their schedules to accommodate the persistent legislative backlog. In its guidebook dated 9 October 2026, Acea explained that ARERA Resolution 77/2026 formally extended the deadline for defining network tariff integration methods. The previous deadline of 31 March 2026 under Resolution 237/25 has been shifted to eight months after the ministerial decree is published. This postponement means that tariff clarity will not emerge until well into the upcoming regional regulatory sub-period.
3.
Competitive policy objections: The proposed twenty-year prolongation framework faces scrutiny from national antitrust authorities. Banca Profilo highlighted on 29 April 2026 that the Italian Competition Authority issued an opinion arguing that such long extensions could clash with competitive principles. The antitrust body recommended that extraordinary investments must remain strictly complementary to public funds. This opposition threatens to complicate the legislative approval of the pending ministerial concession decree.

Concession Fee Calculation and

4.
Tariffs Proposed calculation parameters: The financial burden for extending grid concessions is linked directly to historical infrastructure revenues. In its investor presentation on 14 September 2026, Acea noted that ARERA Resolution 392/2025 proposes to calculate concession fees as a percentage of 2023 capital cost revenues. This calculation method aims to avoid placing excessive burdens on end-users. Consequently, the final charges will reflect past asset remuneration rather than speculative future grid valuations.
5.
Regulated base integration: The mechanism for recovering the upfront extension costs relies on tariff-backed capital indexation. Under the terms of the 2025 Budget Law, the fees paid by operators will be incorporated directly into their regulated asset bases. This integration allows distributors to generate an allowed return and depreciation allowance on the paid fee. This structural inclusion ensures that concession fees support future EPS growth through expanded regulated capital remuneration.
6.
Structured investment windows: The regulator has established clear timeframes for operators to present their network modernization schemes. According to Iren S.p.A.'s base prospectus dated 22 July 2026, Resolution 392/2025 outlines two distinct submission windows for five-year extraordinary plans. Plans submitted shortly after the decree approval will cover 2027 to 2031, while later submissions will cover 2029 to 2033. This phased approach allows utilities to align investments with their broader strategic planning horizons.

7.
Compressed capital assumptions: Large energy operators are revising down the capital allocated for concession prolongations in their plans. Bernstein Research reported on 23 February 2026 that Enel's updated business plan incorporates c. €1 billion of concession-extension fees. This represents a steep reduction from the €2.8 billion earmarked in its previous strategic iteration. This downscaling suggests that Enel is mitigating upfront cash outflows to preserve its overall balance sheet flexibility.
8.
Capital return sensitivities: The integration of concession fees into regulated assets provides a predictable driver for equity earnings. Jefferies Research estimated on 22 July 2025 that every €1 billion increase in Enel's domestic regulated asset base increases net income by approximately 0.50%. This sensitivity highlights the lucrative nature of grid asset expansion compared to volatile power generation. Therefore, executing the extension remains highly accretive for shareholders over the lucrative medium-term investment horizon.
9.
Strategic asset shift: Mid-sized utilities are actively expanding their power distribution perimeters to capture these regulatory opportunities. A2A S.p.A.'s press release from 14 May 2026 reported that its electricity regulated asset base grew by 9.0% to reach €1.838 billion in early 2026. This expansion reflects the integration of Enel's former network assets in Milan and Brescia. Consequently, A2A is rapidly increasing its exposure to stable regulated power tariffs.
10.
Continued transitional operations: Local operators are managing their current distribution systems under fixed transitional arrangements until tenders are resolved. In its Q2 2026 interim report, A2A S.p.A. confirmed that its electricity concessions remain valid until 31 December 2030. The lack of immediate decree progress forces the company to rely on these baseline timelines. This transitional state means that near-term operational cash flows are secured but long-term growth remains capped.

Stalled business updates: Multi- utilities are delaying their broader corporate updates due to the persistent lack of concession details. During an earnings call on 14 May 2025, Acea's management stated that their business plan update was contingent on the formal extension of electricity concessions. The company noted that it had not included any specific figures for concession fees in its targets. This omission means that strategic visibility remains limited until the underlying ministerial decree is resolved.

Regulated portfolio dependence: Regional multiutilities rely heavily on concession-based operations to shield corporate margins from market volatility. Iren S.p.A. noted in its base prospectus on 22 July 2026 that regulated concessions accounted for 57.1% of consolidated EBITDA in 2025. The company's key electricity distribution concessions in North Italy are also set to expire in 2030. Thus, obtaining supportive extensions is critical to maintaining the group's highly predictable dividend trajectory.

Growing baseline assets: Local electricity operators continue to register strong asset growth despite the overarching legislative delays. Hera S.p.A. reported in its 2025 annual report that its electricity regulated asset base grew by 9.8% to reach €0.490 billion. This increase was driven by organic network developments and capitalized investments. This expansion shows that Hera can grow its asset base even without the immediate benefit of concession extensions.
