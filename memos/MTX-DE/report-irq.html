Terms used throughout. EBIT: the company's adjusted earnings before interest and tax. EPS: adjusted earnings per share on the brokers' basis (adjusted net income after minority interests over basic shares; FY2025 €17.79). FCF: the company's adjusted free cash flow. Cash conversion: FCF over adjusted net income. OEM: the Commercial and Military Engine Business segment (original equipment); commercial OEM is its series engines, spare engines and spare parts. MRO: the Commercial Maintenance Business segment (maintenance, repair and overhaul). Spare-engine share: spare engines as a share of engine deliveries. GTF: Pratt & Whitney's PW1100G geared turbofan engine and its family (PW1500G, PW1900G); the powder-metal issue is the 2023 recall of engines with suspect powder-metal parts, which grounded aircraft and led to compensation paid to airlines. Pre-financed receivables: GTF shop visits MTU has carried out but airlines pay for only at later normal overhauls. V2500: the International Aero Engines V2500 engine, in which MTU holds 16%. LEAP: CFM International's competing narrowbody engine. GEnx: a GE Aerospace widebody engine. GTF Advantage and Hot Section Plus: Pratt & Whitney's durability upgrades. Chapter 11: United States bankruptcy protection. At-equity income: MTU's share of its joint ventures' profit (EME Aero in Poland, with Lufthansa Technik; MTU Maintenance Zhuhai, with China Southern Airlines). FCAS: the Franco-German Future Combat Air System fighter, abandoned in June 2026; GCAP: the Global Combat Air Programme of the United Kingdom, Italy and Japan. The hedge rate: the average dollar-per-euro rate of MTU's currency hedges for a year. FY2026: the year to 31 December 2026; H1 and H2: first and second halves; Q1 2027 and the like: a quarter of that year; 9M: the first nine months.

What the event should settle, and why

The case in one line: MTU is a high-quality aftermarket business whose cash is clearing as GTF compensation ends in 2026, but whose earnings grow about 7% a year against a flat consensus, so the next twelve months turn on whether cash and capital returns are upgraded before earnings are trimmed.

  1. Where FY2026 lands. Nine months gave EBIT of €1,042m; the €1.35bn to €1.45bn range needs a fourth quarter of €308m to €408m (Q4 2025: €356m). Consensus €1,439m needs about €397m; ours €1,435m. If the top: in line with consensus, protection rather than upgrade fuel; if the midpoint: FY2026 EBIT consensus falls about 3% and the February guide opens lower.
  2. How FY2027 opens on 17 February 2027. The FY2026 range started at the FY2025 result (€1,351m). The same habit gives €1.45bn to €1.55bn, a midpoint 4% below consensus of €1,561m; we put 60% on a midpoint below consensus. If set the same way: FY2027 consensus EPS trims about 1% in March, and the better entry is after February; if set around consensus: EPS holds and there is no reason to wait.
  3. The spare-engine share and spare parts in 2027. Deutsche Bank has the spare-engine share falling from 22% to 15% in 2027; on Redburn's figures each point is about €9m of EBIT (our arithmetic), so that path costs €35m to €60m against about €20m in our base. If "high teens": our base holds; if "nearer 15%" without a catalogue price offset: FY2027 EBIT consensus is 2% to 4% too high.
  4. The dollar basis of the new targets. The 2030 targets (revenue €13bn to €14bn, EBIT margin 14.5% to 15.5%, EBIT €1.9bn to €2.2bn) were set at 1.10; nothing is hedged for 2030 and Deutsche Bank puts the sensitivity at €300m of EBIT per 10 cents. If restated at today's rate and held in euros: a real upgrade of about 10% (our arithmetic); if restated lower in euros: FY2029 consensus (EBIT €1,969m, five brokers) is cut and the day is negative.
  5. Cash conversion and capital returns. Compensation's end is worth about 14 points of conversion in 2027; consensus FCF of €733m implies about 66% (our arithmetic on consensus net income of €1,117m), ours about 71% and €780m. If the 40% payout applies from FY2026, the dividend is about €7.50 against consensus €4.50; if a buyback is named, it is new to the market (none in consensus); if "deleverage and invest first", nothing changes.
  6. Current momentum against the trackers. V2500 flight hours fell 14.2% year on year in October and storage is about 13%, while management saw "almost no retirements". If inductions hold through 2027: the low camp of brokers (Oddo BHF, UBS, Rothschild & Co Redburn) is wrong for the next twelve months; if not: spare parts slip below mid-teens and FY2027 consensus is cut.

Current momentum, and where FY2026 lands

A1 ★ Trading since the third quarter. "Since the third-quarter report, have spare parts and MRO revenue held the mid-teens growth you guided for the year, and is there anything in the fourth quarter you would point us to?"
- Why: spare parts are an estimated €1.4bn to €1.5bn of revenue at about 55% margin, about 55% of group EBIT (our estimate; not disclosed). Jefferies (29-Oct-2026) cut spare parts growth to 6% for 2027; Rothschild & Co Redburn raised 2026 to 14%; Deutsche Bank saw a stronger pricing tailwind in the fourth quarter of 2026.
- Tell: "mid-teens held, pricing helping" = FY2026 in the upper half and Jefferies' 6% for 2027 looks low; "softer, with fuel and older fleets" = the low camp, and FY2027 consensus at risk.

A2 ★ FY2026 EBIT against the top of the range. "Rothschild & Co Redburn has €1.48 billion of EBIT for this year, above the top of your range, and consensus is about €1.44 billion. How comfortable are you with the upper half, and was the Chapter 11 charge the only thing that stopped a raise in October?"
- Why: the top needs about €408m in the fourth quarter (+15% on Q4 2025's €356m) after +4.7% for 9M; consensus implies about €397m, ours €393m. Each €10m of EBIT is about €0.14 of EPS (our arithmetic).
- Tell: "upper half, the top in reach" = FY2026 in line, a small beat possible; "around the midpoint" = FY2026 EBIT consensus falls about 3% and February opens from a lower base.

A3 ★ Fourth-quarter cash and the last compensation. "You paid about $170 million of compensation in nine months against about $250 million for the year. Is the rest all in the fourth quarter, does any of it slip into 2027, and do the third quarter's military prepayments reverse?"
- Why: Morgan Stanley reads the 55% to 60% guide as fourth-quarter FCF of €100m to €150m; ours €124m and €590m for the year, against consensus €572m (Rothschild & Co Redburn €547m). The end of compensation is about 14 points of 2027 conversion (our estimate).
- Tell: "all in Q4, nothing in 2027, prepayments stay" = FY2026 conversion near the top and the 2027 step intact; "some in 2027" or "prepayments unwind" = FY2027 FCF consensus (€733m) is at risk.

A4 ★ The V2500 against the trackers. "Fleet data showed V2500 flight hours down about 14% year on year in October and about 13% of the fleet stored, while in the spring you saw almost no retirements and full shops. What are you seeing in V2500 inductions and workscopes for 2027?"
- Why: the low camp (Rothschild & Co Redburn, Sell, €325; Oddo BHF; UBS, Sell) rests on this. Jefferies puts the V2500 retirement rate at 1.6% for the year (the memo's invalidation is above 2%); lease rates fell from $90,000 to $80,000. The gap between Jefferies' 9% spare parts growth for 2028 and our 5% to 6% is about 2% of FY2028 EPS.
- Tell: "inductions steady at about 800 a year, booked through 2027" = the low camp is wrong for the next twelve months; "fewer second shop visits, more aircraft parted out" = spare parts below mid-teens and FY2027 consensus is cut.

A5 The GTF fleet and the turnaround. "Your heavy GTF shop visits take under 100 days, yet the trackers still show just over 500 aircraft parked and Wizz Air quotes about 300 days per engine. Where is the bottleneck now, and does the cold-weather blade issue Jefferies reported change the fleet management plan or its cost to you?"
- Why: compensation follows grounded aircraft; a new directive or a new defect is the named route to a 2027 cut. Wizz Air had 22 aircraft grounded at 30 September against 35 a year earlier and expects 15 to 20 by March 2027; Jefferies (19-Nov-2026) says the blade issue may take two years to resolve.
- Tell: "parts, easing through 2027; the cold-weather item small and inside the plan" = no new cost; "under review with Pratt & Whitney" = 2027 cash and the end-date of compensation at risk.

The new targets and FY2027

A6 ★ The dollar basis of the new targets. "Are the new 2030 targets stated at 1.10 dollars per euro, as in June 2025, or at today's rate? At today's rate, where does the 2030 EBIT target of €1.9 to €2.2 billion land?"
- Why: nothing is hedged for 2030; Deutsche Bank puts it at €300m of EBIT per 10 cents. Our base is about €2.0bn at 1.10 and €1,756m at 1.165. Bernstein reads an unchanged revenue target as "a four percent underlying upgrade"; Jefferies says the EBIT target could be raised.
- Tell: "restated at today's rate, held in euros" = a real upgrade of about 10% (our arithmetic) and FY2029 consensus rises; "kept at 1.10" = no news; "restated lower in euros" = FY2029 EBIT consensus (€1,969m) is cut.

A7 ★ How FY2027 guidance will open. "This year's EBIT range started at last year's result. Should we expect FY2027 guidance in February to be set the same way? Deutsche Bank has 2027 EPS of €21.59 and Oddo BHF €18.74: which is nearer your thinking?"
- Why: the same habit gives €1.45bn to €1.55bn, a midpoint 4% below consensus EBIT of €1,561m; we put 60% on a midpoint below consensus. Ours: EBIT €1,530m, EPS about €20.3 against consensus €20.51. FY2027 is hedged almost in full at 1.16.
- Tell: "a range built around where the business is, not from last year up" = FY2027 EPS holds and there is no reason to wait; "the same discipline as 2026" = the usual trim of about 1% in March and a better entry after February.

A8 ★ The spare-engine share in 2027. "You said absolute spare-engine deliveries stay stable while the ratio falls. Deutsche Bank has the share going from 22% this year to 15% in 2027. Is that the right order of magnitude?"
- Why: Rothschild & Co Redburn puts spare engines at 14% of FY2025 operating income (about €190m), so each point of ratio is about €9m of EBIT (our arithmetic): Deutsche Bank's path costs €35m to €60m in 2027 against about €20m in our base (19%). The largest identified risk to FY2027 EBIT not in our numbers; Q1 2027 is the first test.
- Tell: "high teens" = our base; "nearer 15%" = FY2027 EBIT consensus 2% to 4% too high and the OEM margin below the 28% floor (two quarters below is the memo's alarm).

A9 ★ Spare parts price and volume in 2027. "What catalogue price increase is set for 2027, and after this year's mid-teens growth how should we split next year's spare parts growth between price and volume?"
- Why: we have the catalogue up 6.5% in 2027, about €80m of EBIT; Jefferies has total spare parts growth of 6% in 2027, 9% in 2028. Price is what offsets the falling spare-engine share and the hedge step (A8, A14).
- Tell: "price high single digits, volume still positive" = Jefferies is low and FY2027 can be upgraded; "price only, volume flat" = our base; "used parts taking share" = a cut (B1).

A10 Commercial OEM deliveries and the catch-up. "The commercial OEM growth guide was cut to mid-to-high single digits on Embraer GTF, Pratt & Whitney Canada and GEnx bottlenecks. How much of the shortfall comes back in 2027, and does the catch-up dilute the OEM margin?"
- Why: 9M commercial OEM revenue grew only 1.65%; Airbus needs a record 323 deliveries in the fourth quarter for about 870. New series engines are sold at a loss, so a catch-up raises revenue more than EBIT. We have commercial OEM +6% in 2027.
- Tell: "most comes back in 2027" = revenue consensus (Jefferies €11.1bn) supported but the margin lower; "bottlenecks into 2027" = nearer RBC's €10.45bn.

A11 ★ The MRO margin. "The MRO margin was 8.0% for nine months, with GTF work about 45% of the segment and Fort Worth ramping. When should we model the step into the 8.5% to 9.5% corridor: 2027, or later?"
- Why: we have 8.2% in 2027 and 8.35% in 2028 (Rothschild & Co Redburn 8.0% in 2028); each 0.1 point on about €7.6bn of 2027 MRO revenue is about €8m of EBIT (our arithmetic). Joint-venture profit lifts the reported margin with no revenue (B3).
- Tell: "into the corridor from 2027" = an upgrade route for FY2027; "as GTF work matures, 2028 on" = our base; "near 8% for longer" = the Redburn camp, FY2028 EPS about 2.5% lower.

A12 MRO growth against tough comparisons. "MRO grew 29% organically in the first half of 2026. With engine maintenance capacity for 2027 reported as fully booked, is next year's MRO growth limited by your capacity or by demand, and how much of it is material passed through at little margin?"
- Why: we have MRO +11% in dollars in 2027, about half of it price, with material escalation at near-zero margin; H1 2027 laps a Q2 2026 MRO rise of 37%. Revenue beats from pass-through do not reach EBIT.
- Tell: "capacity-bound, pricing firm" = revenue safe, margin up; "pass-through heavy" = revenue beats without EBIT beats.

A13 Military in 2027. "Military grew 13% in nine months against a mid-teens guide, and prepayments helped cash in the third quarter. How should we model military revenue in 2027, and how much of this year's prepayment unwinds next year? Or save for a later investor relations call."
- Why: we have military +10% in 2027 (€767m); Rothschild & Co Redburn raised FY2026 FCF 5% on prepayments. Germany's draft 2027 defence budget is €139bn.
- Tell: "low teens, prepayments mostly stay" = our base; "a large part unwinds" = FY2027 cash below our €780m.

The cost lines that swing, below EBIT and cash

A17 ★ The FY2027 cash conversion guide. "With compensation ending, consensus free cash flow of €733 million implies about 66% conversion for 2027. What else moves cash next year, the receivables build, capital spending, prepayments, and should the first guide start above 60%?"

A18 The receivables path. "Will you publish the pre-financed GTF receivables and their expected path? Brokers range from about €1.1 billion (Bernstein) to €1.2 to €1.3 billion (Deutsche Bank), building about €200 million a year to a peak in 2028."
