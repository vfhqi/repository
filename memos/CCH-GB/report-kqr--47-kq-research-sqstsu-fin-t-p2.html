Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. CCH-GB's pricing power is the primary driver of its mid-term earnings growth, but this capability faces rising commodity costs and emerging market devaluations.
  2. While the incidence-pricing model aligns CCH-GB with its brand owner, declining marketing support and geographic revenue dilution present persistent headwinds to earnings per share expansion.

Defining the Core Question

1.
Critical question: "Coca-Cola HBC AG (CCH-GB), the Coca-Cola system's anchor bottler and distributor of non- alcoholic ready-to-drink beverages (sparkling, energy, water, juice, ready-to-drink tea, coffee, sports drinks) and a small premium spirits line across 29 countries, reported in Established (e.g. Italy, Greece, Switzerland, Ireland, Austria), Developing (e.g. Poland, Czechia, Hungary) and Emerging (e.g. Nigeria, Egypt, Romania, Serbia, Ukraine, Russia) segments, and acquiring 75% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa (14 African markets led by South Africa), with completion expected by the end of 2026. Key question: how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: the naira devaluations of 2023-24 and the Egyptian pound devaluations of 2023-24; the consumer boycott of Western brands in Egypt and other Muslim-majority markets in 2023-24; the input-cost inflation of 2022-23 and the commodity cost rise the company expects for 2027 with lower hedge cover; the low-inflation normalisation of 2025-26, when revenue per case growth fell and mix did most of the work; sugar taxes (South Africa's Health Promotion Levy from 2018, Poland's sugar tax from 2021, Italy's sugar excise of EUR 0.10 a litre from January 2027, Ethiopia's excise rise from 14% to 25% of the selling price); deposit return scheme launches (Romania, Poland, Austria, Greece); annual negotiations with consolidated Western European retail buying alliances; a newly acquired market facing a priceled challenger (Egypt after 2022; South Africa against Varun's BevCo and Twizza); fuel-price inflation squeezing emerging-market consumers in 2026?"
2.
Significant impact: The capacity to sustain pricing above input costs is the primary determinant of the group's long-term profitability and earnings path. Deutsche Bank Research reported on November 16, 2026, that managing input costs effectively while achieving balanced volume and price growth remains crucial to sustaining CCH-GB's sector-leading growth algorithm. Successful execution of this strategy directly influences whether the company can maintain its compelling earnings compounding trajectory over the 2027-2028 period.
3.
Resilient alignment: Under the system's value-based model established in 2017, concentrate pricing is directly tied to the bottler's realized price and mix per pack rather than volume. Jefferies Research highlighted on February 4, 2026, that this structure aligns the financial incentives of both entities in real time and eliminates the historical one-year lag. Although this shared revenue focus drives system-wide growth, it means a portion of every price increase automatically flows back to the brand owner.
4.
Strategic cushioning: The company utilizes diverse pack and product innovations to defend its volume share during intense macroeconomic shocks and major currency devaluations. Jefferies Research noted on October 1, 2026, that introducing smaller entry packs at lower price points effectively protects consumer demand when spending power is constrained. This revenue growth management approach aims to mitigate volume loss while maintaining positive price mix optimization across diverse geographies.

Structural Headwinds and Analytical Scope

5.
Moderate dilution: Expanding rapidly in lower-priced African jurisdictions structurally dilutes the group's overall average revenue per case despite superior volume growth. Bernstein Research reported on April 24, 2026, that faster-growing Emerging and Developing regions are partially offset by lower-growth Established markets. This geographic mix headwind becomes more pronounced as CCH-GB integrates the newly acquired African bottler into its emerging market division.
6.
Increasing headwinds: Escalating soft commodity costs present a tougher environment for CCH-GB heading into the 2027 financial year as hedging levels normalize. Jefferies Research pointed out on November 4, 2026, that the company's hedge cover stands at 40% for 2027 compared to 100% cover in 2026. This lower cover exposes CCH-GB to upward price pressures in PET and sugar, which must be offset through advanced commercial and pricing toolkits in the marketplace.
7.
Declining support: Direct marketing support from the brand owner has decreased recently, placing a heavier burden on the bottler's local promotional efforts. Coca-Cola HBC reported in its 2025 integrated annual report that net contributions received for marketing and promotional incentives fell to EUR 113.20 mm in 2025 from EUR 155.80 mm in 2024. This reduction requires the group to co-invest more heavily to sustain robust commercial brand building across its 29 territories.
