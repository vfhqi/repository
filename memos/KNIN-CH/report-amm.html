The one-line view. Bullish as a working hypothesis: the world's largest freight forwarder, capital-light and family-controlled, saw operating profit fall to a conversion of 14.1 per cent of gross profit in 2025 and is rebuilding it through 300 to 350 million Swiss francs of cost and artificial intelligence savings, so every point of conversion recovered falls to earnings per share without needing volume; the catch is that freight yields, which management does not control, can take it all back, and at 22.8 times earnings the shares already price much of the recovery.

What the company does

Kuehne + Nagel buys transport capacity from shipping lines, airlines and road hauliers and sells it on to 400,000 customers, owning neither ships nor aircraft. It is the largest forwarder in the world by sea and air volume, moving 4.31 million twenty-foot container equivalent units and 2.2 million tonnes of airfreight in 2025. Net turnover that year was 24,476 million Swiss francs and operating profit 1,242 million: Sea Logistics earned 538 million, Air 454 million, Contract Logistics 217 million and Road 58 million. Klaus-Michael Kuehne holds 55.4 per cent of the votes, which rules out activists and takeovers alike.

A plausible investment thesis

The mechanism is conversion, not volume. Procurement scale, a proprietary cloud transport management system and customer integration give gross margins of 32 to 38 per cent against a sector average of 20, so gross profit is defended; what collapsed was the share of gross profit reaching operating profit, from 34 per cent in 2022 to 14.1 per cent in 2025, against a 2015 peak of 29.3 per cent. Roadmap 2026 takes out 200 million Swiss francs of annualised cost, with more than 120 million of gross savings due in 2026, and the artificial intelligence roadmap targets a further 100 to 150 million of operating profit by the end of 2027. Against 2025 operating profit of 1,242 million that is roughly a quarter more profit if retained. The second quarter of 2026 showed it working: conversion rose 130 basis points to 16.9 per cent, operating profit of 381 million beat consensus by 8 per cent, and 2026 guidance rose to 1.35 to 1.55 billion.

Drivers to track: quarterly conversion against 16.9 per cent; gross profit per container against operating cost per container, since savings competed away show as the first falling faster than the second; idle container capacity rising from 2.2 per cent.

Business quality

High, with a cyclical weak spot. Return on capital employed was 50 per cent in 2025 against a through-cycle target above 25, free cash flow was 917 million Swiss francs, up 48 per cent, on capital expenditure of 238 million, and no customer exceeds 10 per cent of revenue. The weak spots are volume and mix: sea volumes fell 1.4 per cent in the second quarter while DSV grew, combined air and sea margins lag DSV by about 300 basis points, and the 2030 conversion target of 35 per cent concedes ground to DSV's 55.

The strongest counter-case

Conversion fell to 14.1 per cent for reasons nobody in our files has explained, and the second quarter beat was cost-led while gross profit was merely in line. If yields normalise quickly, which a 42.5 per cent orderbook makes likely, the savings pot is negated, and the cost cutting behind the beat has a record of impairing the sales force and losing volume to DSV. Paying 22.8 times earnings for a consensus plausible return of 9.9 per cent is paying a growth multiple for a cyclical whose next move in yields is down; Morgan Stanley's 12 times bear multiple and Jefferies' 143 downside sit roughly a third below the price.
