One point for Richard before the questions. Theon is held, as a Core Investment, at 1.85% of the fund. The memo's case is a predictable operating business bolted to a balance sheet whose test is dated: net debt peaks after the HGH purchase, the company promises leverage below 2.5 times in 2027, the bank covenant is 3.5 times or possibly 3.0 times (the company has not said), and the Exosens stake (about EUR 283m at the memo's date) could settle the question on any day management chooses. The third-quarter update beat on every operating line and on net debt, and raised the medium-term organic growth target to more than 20% a year. Yet the second earnings summary (17-Nov-2026) judges the case modestly weaker: the shares fell, one broker left Buy, and Deutsche Bank still projects end-2026 net debt of about EUR 573m (3.4 times) after the beat. So for a holder the call is about the balance sheet more than the operations: the covenant, the leverage definitions, HGH's closing date, the cash cost of faster growth, and the stake. The order questions matter too, because the case's own test (nine-month book-to-bill at or above 1.0 times) was missed at 0.74 times.

A second point: what this call can and cannot give. Investor relations will not forecast. They can confirm definitions (adjusted EBIT, the working-capital ratio, the leverage and covenant basis), the calendar (HGH's closing, the Bundestag stages, the results date), what drove the reported numbers, and what management has already said. Most questions therefore ask "how should we model this" or "is our reading of what you published right". Kostas Derekis of investor relations wrote on 3-Nov-2026 that Theon will be at the Berenberg and Morgan Stanley conferences in London on 30-Nov and 1-Dec-2026; questions marked "or save for London" are better put to management there if time runs short.

What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: the operating lines are contracted and predictable, and the share price now depends on the balance sheet closing its question, by a leverage print or by a sale of the Exosens stake. Six judgements decide that, and the call can test all six in part.

  • Judgement 1, fourth-quarter orders. Nine-month order intake was EUR 285.2m, book-to-bill 0.74 times, and the soft backlog fell to EUR 1,377m from EUR 1,456m in June. About EUR 440m of fourth-quarter intake is needed for a full-year 1.2 times; the DAMON MR call-off of nearly EUR 200m is about half of that. The memo counted EUR 902m of options "expected to be exercised shortly". If the options and the German call-off land in the fourth quarter, the order question ends and FY2027 revenue of about EUR 752m is safe. If they slip, a one-quarter OCCAR slip alone is (5.6)% on FY2027 EPS and takes end-2027 leverage to about 2.67 times, above the company's target.
  • Judgement 2, what "more than 20%" means. More than 20% a year from about EUR 600m is more than EUR 1.0bn of organic revenue by FY2029, against our EUR 945.5m including acquisitions. The company's own FY2029 mix (about half night vision in about EUR 1bn) implies about EUR 500m of non-night-vision, against our roughly EUR 357m. If the target is organic and from FY2026, our non-night-vision build is too low and the tube cap is not the binding constraint the memo says. If it includes acquisitions or starts from a later base, the raise is smaller than it looks.
  • Judgement 3, the leverage path and the covenant. The memo has end-2026 net debt of about EUR 253m (HGH closing in 2027) and a mid-2027 peak of about 2.9 times; Deutsche Bank has about EUR 573m and 3.4 times at end-2026; management says about 3.0 times pro forma. A covenant at 3.5 times on pro-forma EBITDA leaves about a 16% cushion; at 3.0 times on reported EBITDA the cushion is near nil and the stake becomes the fix, or equity.
  • Judgement 4, the cash cost of faster growth. Working capital is about 42.7% of revenue; each extra EUR 100m of revenue absorbs about EUR 43m. The memo's free cash flow (EUR 94m and EUR 120m in FY2028 and FY2029) depends on growth slowing. If growth stays above 20% with no customer prepayments, leverage barely moves but free cash flow is deferred; if new frameworks carry prepayments, both improve.
  • Judgement 5, the lines below EBIT. Interest rises from EUR 6.8m in FY2025 to about EUR 27m in FY2027; the memo uses a 25% tax rate against an underlying rate near 30% in the first half of 2026; HGH's acquired-intangible amortisation may sit inside adjusted EBIT. These are factual, answerable questions, and each feeds straight into EPS: one point of tax is EUR 0.02, one point of margin EUR 0.07.
  • Judgement 6, the Exosens stake. 4.98m shares, liquid, tax-free to sell under Cyprus law, no dividend, and the board-seat reason for holding it dropped in September 2026. A sale takes end-2027 leverage to about 1.2 times and adds about 5% to FY2028 EPS. The memo puts it at 30% inside 18 months; any change in how management describes the stake moves that.

Order of play. Ask the fourth-quarter orders and options (A1, A2) first, then the leverage definitions and covenant (A7, A8) and HGH's closing (A6): they are answerable and decide the next print. Then the growth target (A3) and the lines below EBIT (A11). Leave governance, people and disclosure to the end, or for London.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the fourth quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2027)

A1 ★ Fourth-quarter order intake. "Nine-month intake was EUR 285 million against revenue of about EUR 383 million. Since then the German parliament has approved DAMON MR, with an initial call-off of nearly EUR 200 million, and on 16 November you announced about EUR 370 million of new orders, EUR 160 million of them firm. How much of both will be booked as fourth-quarter order intake, are options counted only when exercised, and is anything else close to signing before year-end?" (Wording updated: New from the reports.)
- Why: about EUR 440m of fourth-quarter intake gives a full-year book-to-bill of 1.2 times; after the German call-off about EUR 240m more is needed. The case's own test for this year (1.0 times) was missed at nine months.
- Tell: "booked in December, with other signatures in hand" = the order question closes before the FY2026 results; "phased into 2027" or "depends on the call-off schedule" = the full-year book-to-bill is low, and the narrative on 19-Mar-2027 starts with orders, not net debt.

A2 ★ The options. "Earlier this year you described about EUR 900 million of contract options as expected to be exercised shortly. How much has been exercised since June, are exercised options counted in order intake when exercised, and what is the timing for the rest?"
- Why: the memo has EUR 902m of options outside the soft backlog and treats their exercise as the mechanism for the FY2027 mid-year guidance raise. The first earnings summary found no sign of them in reported intake.
- Tell: "a large part exercised in the fourth quarter" = the FY2027 raise mechanism is intact; "no fixed timing" = take the options out of the 2027 raise case.

A3 ★ The growth target. "You raised medium-term organic growth to more than 20% a year. From which base year, over which years, and does 'organic' exclude HGH, Merio and the Safran joint venture? How does it fit with the ambition of about EUR 1 billion of revenue in 2029 with about half from night vision?"
- Why: more than 20% from about EUR 600m is more than EUR 1.0bn organic by FY2029, against our EUR 945.5m including acquisitions; half night vision implies about EUR 500m of non-night-vision against our roughly EUR 357m. Consensus is about 5% below the company's targets on revenue and 7% to 9% below on adjusted EBIT (Deutsche Bank).
- Tell: "organic, from FY2026" = our FY2028 and FY2029 revenue is too low and consensus has room to rise; "including bolt-ons" or "an ambition, not guidance" = the raise is smaller than the headline, and our view holds.

A4 ★ Night vision against tube supply. "Night-vision sales were slightly below consensus over nine months, while thermal and platforms were well ahead. Was night vision held back by tube availability, by delivery timing, or by customers' schedules? What tube volume do you have for 2027?"
- Why: the memo's largest disagreement with consensus is that tubes cap night-vision growth at about 11% in FY2027 and 4% to 7% after (Exosens has contracted "well over 400,000" tubes for 2026 to 2030, about 80,000 to 90,000 a year). 10,000 more tubes a year is about EUR 55m of FY2028 revenue and 7% of FY2028 EPS.
- Tell: "tubes" = the cap is real and the growth target leans on non-night-vision; "timing" = night vision catches up in the fourth quarter; any figure for 2027 tube volume above about 100,000 = the cap is lifting.

A5 ★ The fourth quarter and the FY2026 margin. "Guidance of about EUR 600 million implies fourth-quarter revenue of about EUR 217 million. Is the margin above 26% for the year on the same adjusted EBIT definition as last year, and does adjusted EBIT include amortisation of acquired intangibles?"
- Why: the fourth quarter carries 30% to 35% of sales and most of the fixed-cost absorption; a 10% slip in it moves the full-year margin 0.5 to 0.7 points. If amortisation stays inside adjusted EBIT after HGH closes, the FY2027 margin prints about 25.1% against "above 26%" with nothing changed in the business.
- Tell: "amortisation is excluded" or "we will redefine it when HGH closes" = no optical margin cut in 2027; "included" = expect a headline margin below the target in 2027 that is not a real miss.

A6 ★ HGH's closing. "You told investors yesterday that the HGH approvals are complete and closing is expected in December. Is a date set, will it be consolidated from closing, and what revenue, EBITDA and amortisation should we model for 2027?" (Wording updated: New from the reports.)
- Why: the memo had closing late in the first quarter of 2027; closing in December puts the full debt into the end-2026 print on 19-Mar-2027 (Deutsche Bank about EUR 573m, 3.4 times), instead of the memo's about EUR 253m. HGH is about EUR 40m of revenue at an EBITDA margin above 40%, bought for about EUR 300m.
- Tell: "December" = the leverage test arrives three months early and on a smaller EBITDA base; "early 2027" = the end-2026 print is the benign early read the memo expected.

A7 ★ Which leverage, on which EBITDA. "You say leverage is about 3.0 times pro forma after HGH; Deutsche Bank has 3.4 times at end-2026. Is your target of below 2.5 times in 2027 measured on reported or pro-forma EBITDA, at year-end or at the peak, and does net debt include the related-party loans or deduct the Exosens stake?"
- Why: the gap between 3.0 and 3.4 times is mostly definition. The memo has 2.48 times at end-2027 against "below 2.5 times": one point of working capital (EUR 7.5m) or EUR 15m of EBITDA tips it.
- Tell: "pro forma, year-end, stake not deducted" = the memo's path; any deduction of the stake or use of pro-forma EBITDA at the peak = the target is easier than the market fears.

A8 ★ The covenant. "A term sheet from December 2025 cites a net leverage covenant of 4.25 times, tested quarterly, with an equity cure. Is that the covenant on the term loan and the EUR 300 million revolving facility today, or is it 3.5 or 3.0 times? On what EBITDA, reported or pro forma, is it tested?" (Wording updated: New from the reports.)
- Why: at 3.5 times on pro-forma EBITDA the cushion at the peak is about 16%; at 3.0 times it is about 2%. The company has not said, and the memo names this as the one fact that would lower the riskiness rating a notch.
- Tell: "4.25 times" or "3.5 times, pro forma" = the covenant question ends and Case Riskiness eases a notch; "3.0 times" or a refusal = the stake sale or equity becomes the base case for the fix, not the tail.

A9 Working capital. "Working capital was 42.7% at end-September. Is that against last-twelve-months or nine-month revenue? What do you expect at year-end, and do DAMON MR or the OCCAR deliveries carry any customer advance payments?"
- Why: our two earnings summaries describe the ratio on different bases. Each point is EUR 7.5m of cash and 0.036 times of leverage at FY2027 revenue; three points better or worse is the pass or fail of the 2.5 times target. Advances were only EUR 1.5m at June 2026, against about 15% of order value at Hensoldt.
- Tell: "advances on the new contracts" = cash and leverage improve together; "no prepayments, ratio stays near 42%" = faster growth defers free cash flow, the second earnings summary's reading two.

A10 Capital expenditure. "You cut 2026 capital expenditure to about EUR 25 million and moved the balance into 2027. What should we model for 2027 including the Athens plant and the US investment?"
- Why: the memo has capital expenditure of EUR 31m to 45m a year; the US programme (USD 30m, Virginia and Oregon) is not in its numbers.
- Tell: a figure near EUR 45m including the US = within the memo's range; materially higher = a further drag on 2027 cash.

A11 ★ Interest and tax in 2027. "What interest cost should we model for 2027 after HGH, including swaps, and what tax rate? The first-half 2026 underlying rate looked close to 30%."
- Why: the memo has net financial items of about EUR (27)m and a 25% tax rate; consensus implies only about EUR 20m of FY2027 interest and brokers use 23% to 24% tax. Euribor up 100 basis points is about EUR 6m, or (3.5)% of EPS; each point of tax is EUR 0.02.
- Tell: "low-to-mid twenties tax, swaps cover most of the debt" = consensus EPS holds; "close to 30%" or "mostly floating" = consensus FY2027 EPS of about EUR 1.66 falls 3% to 5%, as the memo expects.

A12 Exosens marks in reported earnings. "The Exosens stake is marked through the income statement. How do you want investors to treat that in EPS, and will you publish an adjusted EPS that excludes it?"
- Why: each 10% move in Exosens' price is about EUR 0.36 of reported EPS; some brokers include the marks and some do not, so the FY2026 consensus EPS is on mixed bases (EUR 1.28 to EUR 1.95).
- Tell: an adjusted EPS excluding the marks = cleaner consensus and fewer "cuts" that are accounting only; "no" = expect noisy EPS revisions the sell-side momentum rating will read as deterioration.

A13 Gross margin and operating costs. "Nine-month gross profit was about 5% ahead of consensus but adjusted EBIT under 1% ahead. Where did the difference go: headcount, research and development, new plants? What operating-cost growth should we model in 2027?"
- Why: the memo's fixed block is 9% to 11% of revenue and stepping up (headcount up 55% in a year, the Athens plant from the second quarter of 2027, acquired engineering teams). The mix gain did not drop through.
- Tell: "one-off integration and start-up costs" = margin upside in 2027; "investment for growth that continues" = the flat 26.4% margin in the memo is the right base.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Tubes and supply

B1.1 ★ Exosens' allocation. "Exosens' capacity is rising about 40% by end-2027. Does the supply agreement give Theon a share of that increase, or a fixed volume? Have you discussed volumes for 2027 to 2030 beyond the contracted 400,000-plus?"
- Why: the tube cap is the memo's largest out-year disagreement with consensus (FY2028 revenue: our model EUR 853m, consensus EUR 870m, UBS EUR 916m, Deutsche Bank EUR 923m). The 2030 renewal is the structural event beyond the forecast.
- Tell: "a share of new capacity" = consensus is reachable and the growth target is credible; "fixed volumes" = night vision grows 4% to 7% after FY2027.

B1.2 Harder Digital. "Which tubes go into DAMON MR: Harder's 18mm tubes, or the 25,000 Exosens 4G+ tubes announced on 14 November? (New from the reports.) Our reading was that DAMON MR uses Harder's 18mm tubes. What is Harder's annual capacity today and at end-2027, what Figure of Merit do the tubes reach, and do NATO customers other than Germany accept them?"
- Why: our files give Harder's capacity as about 13,000 for internal use (the memo), about 15,000 a year (the second earnings summary) and doubling to 20,000 (the memo). Nearly EUR 200m of orders now depends on it.
- Tell: "20,000 next year, accepted by other NATO buyers" = supplier dependence eases materially; "Germany only" = a concentration on a new, small tube source.

B1.3 Exosens as a competitor. "Exosens bought NVLS and won a Spanish monocular order. How do you see Exosens' move into devices, and does the supply agreement protect Theon's priority?"
- Why: Theon is about 40% of Exosens' revenue, and Exosens is diversifying. The memo names this as a slow balancing loop on Theon's volume after OCCAR.
- Tell: "complementary, our priority is contractual to 2030" = no change; any hint of renegotiation = the moat narrows at its most important layer.

B1.4 Raw materials. "Has gallium or any other input caused delays in 2026, and do you hold buffer stocks?"
- Why: gallium is about 95% refined in China; tube lead times are 18 months. The memo puts a tube or gallium disruption at 5% to 10% probability, with a 20% to 40% revenue delay in the year.
- Tell: "no delays, buffers held" = the risk stays in the tail.

B2. Customers and contracts

B2.1 ★ OCCAR phasing. "What is the delivery schedule for the OCCAR framework across 2027 to 2029, and has any tranche moved? The receivables from the Hensoldt joint venture rose to about EUR 23 million at June from about EUR 7 million at end-2025: is that timing?"
- Why: OCCAR is about 35% to 40% of FY2027 to FY2029 revenue at fixed prices. A one-quarter slip (about EUR 30m) is (5.6)% of FY2027 EPS and +0.2 times of leverage; about one in three comparable German programmes has slipped a quarter or more since 2023.
- Tell: "on schedule, receivables collected in the fourth quarter" = the memo's base; "a tranche moved to 2028" = the bear branch's first step.

B2.2 DAMON MR beyond the first call-off. "The contract is for up to 60,000 units worth more than EUR 450 million. What is the expected call-off schedule after the first 25,000, and are prices fixed for all units?"
- Why: the remaining 35,000 units (about EUR 250m) would be a second Germany-sized leg after OCCAR.
- Tell: "call-offs through 2028 at fixed prices" = visible revenue, pricing set; "subject to annual budgets" = a Bundestag risk each year.

B2.3 Concentration and the pipeline outside Germany. "OCCAR and Germany are a large share of revenue. Which other large frameworks are you bidding for in 2027, for example SAFE-funded orders or Nordic programmes?"
- Why: one customer was 38% of FY2025 revenue and stays near that level while OCCAR is delivered.
- Tell: named bids with dates = concentration falls after 2027; vague = the case still rests on two programmes.

B2.4 A ceasefire. "If a ceasefire in Ukraine came in 2027, which of your contracts could be slowed or reduced, and are options cancellable?"
- Why: the memo correlates a ceasefire with slower option exercise, inventory left on the balance sheet, and a lower Exosens stake value, all in the half-year leverage peaks.
- Tell: "frameworks are funded multi-year budgets, options are the only flexible part" = the risk is to the options, not the backlog.

B3. Non-night-vision products

B3.1 ★ What is driving thermal and platforms. "Thermal was about EUR 31 million over nine months against consensus of EUR 23 million, and platforms EUR 26 million against EUR 22 million. Which products and customers, and are margins in these lines similar to night vision?"
- Why: non-night-vision reached 17.6% of nine-month sales from 7.1%, and the company's FY2029 mix needs it near half. The memo builds EUR 160m to 280m of organic non-night-vision in FY2027 to FY2029 at 26% to 27.5% margins; the second earnings summary rates the company's level possible.
- Tell: "repeat orders from several customers, margins similar" = our non-night-vision build is too low; "one-off orders" = the beat does not compound.

B3.2 New products and the joint venture. "When do the Safran gimbal joint venture, ARMED Next and the fused ORION goggle start to contribute revenue, and at what scale in 2027?"
- Why: the memo has the Safran joint venture at EUR 5m to 45m over FY2026 to FY2029 at 5% to 15% margins; ARMED Next is ahead of the Capital Markets Day timetable.
- Tell: faster than that = upside to both revenue and the non-night-vision share; later = no change.

B3.3 The US investment. "What is the USD 30 million US programme in Virginia and Oregon for, and when does it produce revenue?"
- Why: it is not in the memo's numbers, and the memo does not say what it is for.
- Tell: a dated US contract = new optionality; "long-term positioning" = cost now, revenue later.

B4. Pricing and margin

B4.1 OCCAR pricing. "How are OCCAR prices adjusted each year, and how do you protect the margin above 26% as OCCAR becomes the largest single contract?"
- Why: OCCAR is fixed-price with annual adjustments capped near 10%; the exposed cost is mainly German and Greek wages. Each point of margin is EUR 0.07 of FY2027 EPS.
- Tell: "indexed, tubes fixed for ordered volumes" = the flat margin holds; "absorbed by mix" = the margin depends on non-night-vision growth.

B4.2 Exosens' price rises. "Are Exosens' annual price rises passed through on bilateral and export contracts?"
- Why: the memo assumes pass-through at a constant gross-margin percentage, with price adding about 1.5 points a year to night-vision revenue and nothing to margin.
- Tell: "fully passed through" = the memo's assumption holds.

B5. Acquisitions and integration

B5.1 ★ HGH integration. "Who runs HGH after closing, what are the revenue synergies in 2027, and is the EUR 30 million revenue synergy target by 2029 still the only one?"
- Why: HGH is four times Kappa's size, French and software-heavy, and integration has not started; the memo puts integration failure at 10% (EBIT (5)% to (8)%). Management said on 8-Sep-2026 that integration takes priority over new deals.
- Tell: a named leader and a dated plan = lower execution risk; "we will set targets after closing" = the risk the memo flags stays open.

B5.2 More deals. "Do you rule out further debt-funded acquisitions until leverage is below 2.5 times?"
- Why: the memo says a further debt-funded deal before leverage is below 2.5 times would downgrade the change forces from "strong but costly" to "reckless".
- Tell: "yes, integration first" = supports the deleveraging path; "opportunistic" = the leverage risk widens.

B6. Capital allocation and the Exosens stake

B6.1 ★ The stake. "After the board-seat plan was dropped, how does the board now see the Exosens stake: strategic, financial, or available to fund the balance sheet? Is there any lock-up or condition on selling it?"
- Why: the stake was about EUR 283m at the memo's date, liquid, tax-free to sell in Cyprus, pays no dividend, and the tube agreement is legally independent of it. A sale takes end-2027 leverage to about 1.2 times and adds about 5% to FY2028 EPS. Deutsche Bank says the stake lacks a strategy.
- Tell: "financial, we will review it" = raises the memo's 30% probability of a sale; "strategic, long-term" = the covenant question must close by the leverage print alone.

B6.2 Dividends. "Is the 20% to 30% payout policy unchanged while leverage is above 2.5 times?"
- Why: the memo has dividends of EUR 0.32 to 0.53 a share over FY2026 to FY2029, about a 1.1% to 1.7% yield; the payout was already cut from 30% to 40%.
- Tell: "unchanged" = no change; "suspended until deleveraged" = small cash saved, signal of strain.

B6.3 Equity. "Do you rule out issuing equity to fund the balance sheet?"
- Why: the December 2025 rights issue was at EUR 17.40, a 30% discount. The memo's bear branch has half the covenant fix as a discounted raise of 5m to 8m shares, a further (6)% to (9)% on EPS.
- Tell: a clear "no, the stake is the lever" = removes the dilution tail.

B7. Governance and people

B7.1 The board in January 2027. "All seven board mandates expire in January 2027. Do you plan to add independent directors?"
