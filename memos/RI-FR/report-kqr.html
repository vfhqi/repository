Section 3 – Below-the-Line Mechanics and Balance Sheet Leverage

Section summary
  1. Pernod Ricard relies on hybrid refinancing and scrip dividends to artificially lower reported leverage.
  2. Sell-side analysts remain divided on the sustainability of the current dividend payout without strategic asset sales.

Hybrid Debt Accounting Mechanics

1.
Elevated financing costs: Pernod Ricard issued €1.50bn of deeply subordinated hybrid notes split into two tranches to optimize its capital structure. The hybrid notes carry high initial resettable coupon rates of 5.625% and 6.125% per annum. IFRS rules, these hybrid coupons are recognized in equity rather than through the income statement financing costs. This accounting treatment distorts the net income calculation by omitting the true economic interest expense burden from the recurring finance cost line.
2.
Substantial earnings dilution: Morgan Stanley indicates that the hybrid refinancing could dilute earnings per share by three to four percent because coupon payments are deducted for EPS calculations. The broker notes that while this refinancing strategy lowers reported interest expenses under standard IFRS guidelines, the cash outflow is still deducted. This below-the-line adjustment directly reduces the earnings available to ordinary shareholders unless the coupon payments are deferred indefinitely by management.
3.
Structured rating relief: Pernod Ricard utilizes hybrid debt because major rating agencies treat these instruments as fifty percent equity. This accounting treatment enables a technical leverage reduction of approximately 0.2x net debt to EBITDA. However, the real interest rate on these hybrids remains high compared to traditional bonds, which raises the group's overall cash cost of capital. This structure creates a significant divergence between reported debt levels and actual cash interest commitments.
