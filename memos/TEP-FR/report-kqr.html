Section 3 – The Savings Treadmill and Earnings Divergence

Section summary
  1. Client-mandated price concessions and restructuring programs create a self-reinforcing savings treadmill that keeps Teleperformance's reported earnings persistently below adjusted metrics.
  2. Benchmarking against peers like Concentrix and Firstsource shows that structural margin defense requires continuous reinvestment, compromising near-term cash quality across the BPO sector.

Mechanics of the treadmill loop

1.
Persistent pricing pressure: Teleperformance operates in an environment where clients continuously demand price reductions and cost concessions during contract renewals. To protect its margins, the company is forced to engage in continuous restructuring and workforce adaptation to extract operational efficiencies. However, these restructuring actions require substantial non-recurring cash outflows that keep reported earnings far below adjusted figures. This dynamic represents a self-reinforcing treadmill where cost savings are recycled into client discounts, requiring further operational restructuring.
2.
Decreasing contract visibility: Enterprise buyers are increasingly utilizing contract renewals to demand revised pricing and higher automation integration from their service providers. Teleperformance is forced to offer upfront discounts or transition-period concessions, which dilutes near-term revenues and margins. Consequently, the company must execute continuous cost-saving programs to offset this pricing pressure, creating a constant need for organizational redesign. This structural loop suggests that the cost savings achieved are rarely retained as permanent margin expansion but are instead given back to maintain market share.
3.
Elevated cash consumption: The cash cost of Teleperformance's continuous restructuring programs has significantly degraded its long-term free cash flow generation. In fiscal year 2026, the company was forced to uprate its restructuring program to address persistent macro headwinds. Non-recurring cash costs are expected to reach at least EUR 100 million in fiscal year 2026, driving down reported net cash flows. This high level of ongoing restructuring cash-out indicates that these charges are structurally recurring rather than truly non-recurring events.

Reconciliation and earnings divergence

For fiscal year 2025, the company's adjusted EPS stood far above basic reported EPS of EUR 8.48. This structural divergence is driven by substantial non-operating items and recurring adjustments that shield adjusted figures. Unless these restructuring expenses subside, reported basic EPS will continue to underperform adjusted metrics, impairing shareholder returns.

Heavy reconciliation burdens: The large gap in Teleperformance's fiscal year 2025 results is primarily explained by several significant non-recurring expenses. The company recorded EUR 218 million in amortization of acquisition-related intangible assets alongside a EUR 97 million goodwill impairment charge. Additionally, Majorel synergy generation and French reorganization costs added another EUR 16 million of cash expenses.
