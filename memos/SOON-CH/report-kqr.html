Section 4 – Retail channel structure and the German market after KIND

Section summary
  1. Demant's KIND acquisition establishes a market-leading German retail footprint of over 900 clinics, driving unprecedented in-house wholesale volume conversion and retail margin expansion.
  2. Nonspecialty optical chains like Fielmann are emerging as formidable competitors by rapidly scaling highly profitable audiology shop-in-shops across Germany.

German audiology retail market structure

1.
Consolidating retail footprint: Demant completed its acquisition of Germany's leading independent retailer KIND in December 2025, expanding its nationwide footprint to over 900 clinics across the country.
2.
Shifting market leadership: This transaction combined Demant's existing German footprint of 200 clinics with KIND's 600 locations, establishing Demant as the largest hearing care retailer in Germany with approximately 12% market share.
3.
Realigning competitive scale: Prior to the deal, Sonova's GEERS network led the German market with 740 clinics, while Amplifon operated approximately 580 to 600 stores and KIND held the second-largest position.

In-house supply conversion and margin synergy playbooks

4.
Shifting share wallet: Demant's integration playbook focuses on migrating KIND's product assortment from third-party manufacturers to its own wholesale brands, aiming to achieve over 95% share of wallet within two years.
5.
Accelerating internal volumes: Early integration progress is validated by Demant's internal wholesale revenues, which recorded robust 19% organic growth in Q1 2026 as KIND clinics accelerated their product transition.
6.
Expanding retail profitability: Synergies from this in-house supply conversion are expected to expand KIND's operating EBIT margins from approximately 15% in 2026 to 20% by 2028.
7.
Eliminating margin dilution: By routing manufacturing of KIND's product volume to Demant's highly efficient production facilities in Poland, the group successfully replaces bought-in competitor wholesale products with higher-margin internal technology.

Optical shop-in-shops and independent buying networks

8.
Scaling sensory networks: Large European optical chains are actively expanding their presence in audiology, with German family-owned Fielmann Group operating 412 hearing aid shop-in-shop locations across Europe by the end of 2024.
9.
Capturing attractive economics: Fielmann Group has established a dedicated audiology business unit to drive profitability, exploiting structurally higher gross margins in hearing care that deliver a highly accretive 20% to 25% EBITDA margin.
10.
Defending local independent channels: Independent audiology clinics still represent over 45% of global market value, leveraging joint purchasing organizations and buying groups to protect their local customer relationships against manufacturer-owned retail networks, though they remain highly vulnerable to consolidation over the long term.

Section 5 – Vertical integration: Amplifon and the GN Hearing acquisition

Section summary
  1. Amplifon's transformational acquisition of GN Hearing marks a decisive pivot to vertical integration, targeting substantial manufacturing synergies.
  2. The strategic shift introduces heightened execution risk and cultural friction while threatening GN's independent wholesale business.

Transaction value and capital structure

1.
Surprise transformational deal: DNB Carnegie reports that Amplifon agreed to acquire GN's Hearing business for approximately 2.3 billion euros on a debt-free, cash-free basis.
2.
Secured financing mix: Morgan Stanley highlights that the transaction's cash component of 1.69 billion euros is backed by a 449 million euro equity raise and a 1.35 billion euro senior loan secured in June 2026.
3.
Material share dilution: Bernstein Research notes that Amplifon will issue 56 million new shares directly to GN Store Nord on completion, causing material dilution of outstanding equity.

Synergy capture and production insourcing

4.
Highly executable synergies: Morgan Stanley expects the combined entity to capture 60 to 80 million euros in net run-rate EBITDA synergies by the end of 2029.
5.
Aggressive insourcing targets: DNB Carnegie notes that Amplifon plans to increase its internal sourcing ratio from 40% to approximately 90% by 2029 through wholesale consolidation.
6.
Backend loaded benefits: Morgan Stanley argues that synergy realization will be back-end loaded because long-dated supply agreements delay full procurement internalization until late 2028 or 2029.

Cultural friction and talent retention

7.
Organizational culture clash: Bernstein Research identifies substantial integration complexity in combining an Italian retail customer-service organization with a Danish technology and engineering firm.
8.
Talent defection risks: Bernstein Research warns that the geographical proximity of rivals like Demant and WS Audiology increases the risk of key staff defections from GN's Denmark headquarters during integration.
9.
Integration complexity: A former global CRM team member at Amplifon expects cultural differences to create resistance in certain regional subsidiaries during the post-merger integration phase.

Industry backlash and supplier dynamics

10.
B2B customer churn: A former president of WS Audiology expresses skepticism regarding backward integration, predicting that independent retailers will seek alternative sourcing partners to avoid funding a direct competitor.
11.
Demant revenue resilience: Jefferies reports that Demant management downplayed any material negative impact from future Amplifon insourcing because related sales account for less than 1% of Demant's group revenue.
12.
Peer market opportunities: Bernstein Research expects Sonova and Demant to exploit commercial friction by consolidating relationships with independent audiologists who reject the combined Amplifon-GN entity.
13.
Other side of the story: Bernstein Research notes that abandoning a pure retail strategy to become a vertically integrated manufacturer means Amplifon will forfeit its historical margin stability and face severe product cycle volatility.
