Outliers worth noting

The most striking statistical anomaly in MVV Energie's recent history was the massive FY23 adjusted EBIT of EUR 880 million, representing an extraordinary 151% outperformance against initial guidance and propelling the company's adjusted ROCE to an unprecedented 33.5%. This exceptional profitability was driven by extreme wholesale power price spikes during the European energy crisis, with adjusted sales expanding 79% year-on-year to EUR 7,531 million.

This level of trading-driven profitability represented a fundamental departure from traditional utility operating norms and was entirely attributable to external commodity market volatility rather than operational execution. The subsequent collapse in adjusted ROCE from 33.5% in FY23 to 12.5% in FY24 and further to 9.8% in FY25 underscores the non-recurring nature of this windfall and highlights the structural challenges facing the business as it transitions away from volatile commodity-driven earnings. 250 A second critical outlier is the company's exceptionally narrow sell-side coverage, with MVV covered by only one active financial institution, Landesbank Baden-Württemberg, which maintains a buy rating and a sole price target of EUR 32.10 as of December 2025.

This represents an extreme deviation from typical public company coverage patterns and reflects the structural consequences of the company's highly restricted equity float. The remaining 4.82% free float prevents meaningful public equity market influence, creating an information vacuum that fundamentally limits institutional investor engagement and market transparency. Historical analyst commentary explicitly highlighted that very low free float restricts institutional investment and that a lack of operational reporting granularity structurally caps valuation multiples, suggesting that the coverage desert is both a cause and consequence of the company's concentrated ownership structure.

MVV's market positioning in direct marketing of solar capacity also represents a notable outlier, with the company directly marketing 1,000 megawatts of solar capacity in Germany, establishing it as a dominant player in this specialized segment despite its relatively modest overall scale compared to larger European utilities. This regional market share concentration provides structural advantages in power purchase agreement structuring but also creates geographic revenue concentration risk.

A concerning operational outlier emerged in workforce dynamics, where employee turnover reached 11.4% in FY25 as 764 staff departed, representing a significant increase that raises execution risk during a period of intensive strategic transformation and capital deployment. This occurred even as personnel totals expanded to 6,811 employees in FY25, suggesting underlying organizational friction rather than simple downsizing.

The combination of rising headcount and elevated attrition creates dual cost pressures that threaten operating margin stability during the capital-intensive transition period.
