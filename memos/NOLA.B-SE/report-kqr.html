Customer Concentration and

4.
Stevanato Cut Negative shock: Stevanato Group shocked the market on November 3, 2026, by cutting its 2027 organic growth guidance to the low-single digits compared to previous expectations of 10%. This guidance reset initially raised intense investor fears regarding demand stability across the entire pharmaceutical packaging supply chain.

Idiosyncratic headwind: Deutsche Bank Research clarifies that Stevanato's growth setback is idiosyncratic and does not reflect weak underlying market fundamentals. The setback is heavily tied to a rapid market shift toward multi-dose pens, which utilize a single cartridge instead of four prefilled syringes. Since Nolato manufactures pen injector components, this transition provides a structural buffer rather than a direct risk.

Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reported on October 31, 2025, that the Hungarian project's validation timeline had concluded in the second quarter of 2026, which was slightly later than initially expected. Device Price Deflation and

7.
Modality Substitution Price erosion: Jefferies warns that semaglutide is heading toward its loss of exclusivity, which will trigger intense pricing pressure across the GLP-1 class. Drug makers are responding with lower-cost formats, which will likely force pen and autoinjector contract manufacturers to reduce device assembly pricing over time. Contract manufacturers must continuously improve production automation to protect their margins from these inevitable price concessions.
8.
Limited cannibalization: Deutsche Bank Research indicates that oral GLP-1 formulations are highly unlikely to cannibalize injectable formats in a meaningful way. Expert interviews show that orals suffer from a poor bioavailability of only 5%, making them far more expensive to produce. Consequently, orals are viewed as additive tools for market expansion rather than a direct threat to Nolato's device volumes.
9.
Dosing shift: Jefferies highlights that pharmaceutical companies are actively developing monthly GLP-1 injectables to improve patient compliance over weekly regimens. Consumer research reveals a strong preference for monthly injections, which would significantly reduce the number of devices needed per patient annually. This shift toward less frequent dosing could cap the long-term volume growth of standard weekly autoinjector pens.

Section 4 – Cost Inflation, Below-EBITA Leakage, and Currency Headwinds

Section summary

  2. Post acquisition interest expenses and intangible amortization from recent acquisitions further weigh on the company's net profitability.

Elevated Corporate Costs

1.
Spiking expenses: Nolato reported that its Parent Company adjustments rose to a negative SEK 56 million in the first nine months of 2026 compared with a negative SEK 15 million in the prior period. This dramatic escalation reflects a significant expansion of corporate-level expenses that directly limits group net income. Consequently, this internal cost inflation acts as a persistent drag on overall earnings per share progress.

Nolato Parent Company condensed income statement showing rising corporate costs and operating losses Severance burdens: Nolato experienced a negative effect of SEK 6 million in the second quarter of 2026 due to one-off severance pay. While these charges are temporary, they create near-term volatility for consolidated quarterly operating results.
3.
Higher underlying costs: DNB Carnegie reported on October 29, 2026, that Nolato's underlying intra-group costs reached approximately SEK 12 million per quarter. Analyst Mikael Laséen noted that this run-rate is slightly above previous market expectations. Unless these corporate overheads are trimmed, they will continue to restrict the operating profit margin expansion of the group.

The broker noted that sequential margin improvements are highly dependent on these central expenses returning to historical averages. However, persistent cost inflation remains a primary concern for conservative sell side models.

Below EBITA Cash Leakage

5.
Rising tax rates: Nolato reported that its effective tax rate normalized at 22.8% for the first nine months of 2026 compared with 19.0% in the same period of 2025. In the third quarter of 2026 alone, the tax rate jumped to 23.2% from 16.3% in the prior year's quarter. This tax normalization represents a significant cash leakage that directly dilutes net earnings.
6.
Historical tax comparisons: Nolato reported an effective tax rate of 21.0% for the full year of 2025 and 22.4% for the calendar year of 2024. Management noted that tax variations between foreign subsidiaries and Swedish operations frequently shift the group's overall tax obligations. The recent normalization toward a higher rate indicates a permanent earnings headwind.
7.
Increased interest expenses: Nolato reported that its net financial debt rose to SEK 1,537 million at the end of the third quarter of 2026 compared with SEK 928 million in the prior year. This leverage increase was driven by financing completed acquisitions and higher interest rates. Consequently, these rising finance costs impose severe below EBITA cash leakage.
8.
Amortization headwinds: Nolato confirmed that non-current intangible assets from the Unitron and M&M Qualtech acquisitions will be amortized over a ten-year period. The group expects yearly customer relationship amortization of SEK 4 million for Unitron and SEK 9.6 million for Qualtech. These structural amortization charges will permanently lower reported net profit after tax.
9.
Earn out obligations: Nolato recognized a contingent purchase consideration of approximately SEK 145 million based on projected EBITDA for the M&M Qualtech acquisition. The present value of this liability is registered as an expected future contingent consideration on the balance sheet. This substantial earn-out obligation limits the group's free cash flow flexibility over the next three years.

A stronger home currency directly lowers translated foreign subsidiary sales revenues.

Peer financial costs: GVS SpA reported that its adjusted net financial expenses increased to EUR 7.9 million for the first nine months of 2026 from EUR 7.5 million in the prior period. This financing cost increase was driven by EUR 109 million in new credit agreements and short term facilities. This rising debt burden creates a similar below-EBITA cash leakage to the financing challenges at Nolato.

15.
Contrary evidence: GVS SpA reported that its net profit including foreign exchange impact surged to EUR 41.8 million in the first nine months of 2026 from EUR 20.7 million in the corresponding period of 2025. Consequently, financial headwinds can easily reverse during favorable foreign exchange cycles.
16.
Other side of the story: Investors must realize that Nolato's below-EBITA leakage factors may be easily mitigated if the newly acquired Unitron and M&M Qualtech entities exceed their initial profitability targets. If these electronics-intensive businesses generate superior operating cash flows, the increased interest expense and amortization charges will be easily absorbed without diluting consolidated earnings. Furthermore, any future weakness in the Swedish krona would act as a powerful tailwind, translating foreign sales at highly favorable rates and driving substantial earnings per share growth.

This customer-specific disruption highlights that medical packaging providers are highly vulnerable to abrupt volume reallocations in the GLP- 1 market. Reduced therapy volumes: Jefferies warns that the rapid development of monthly GLP-1 injectables could significantly reduce the number of devices required per patient.
