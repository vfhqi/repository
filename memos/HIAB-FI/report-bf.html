Import tariffs on European components continue to weigh on North American profitability, and changes in U.S. customs regulations in August 2025 broadened steel tariff scopes to finished products, compounding the effective duty burden on Hiab's European-sourced equipment destined for North American markets. While Hiab captured between EUR 20 million and EUR 30 million in tariff surcharges during fiscal year 2025, this recovery represents only partial mitigation of the underlying cost inflation, and if US tariff pressures escalate unexpectedly, passing through full duty increases could hit volumes.

Peer manufacturing platforms with higher degrees of vertical integration and localized North American component sourcing face proportionally lower tariff exposure per revenue dollar, creating a structural margin headwind of an estimated 50 to 100 basis points for Hiab's cross-border equipment flows relative to fully localized competitors.
