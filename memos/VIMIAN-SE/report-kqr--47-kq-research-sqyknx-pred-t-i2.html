Section 2 – Operational Profitability and Below-EBITA Accounting Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Vimian’s organic growth is offset by rising central costs and capitalized development, which inflates EBITDA relative to cash-backed EBITA.
  2. Below-EBITA accounting variations, including tax rate distortions and contingent-consideration revaluations, create a wide spread in broker EPS definitions.

Margin conversion and capitalization dynamics

Vimian's margin conversion has faced intense scrutiny from investors as double-digit organic growth fails to translate into proportional cash earnings. In the third quarter of 2026, Vimian reported an organic revenue growth rate of 11%, bringing total revenue to EUR 120.3m. However, adjusted EBITA margin declined slightly to 24.3% in the third quarter of 2026, down from 24.5% in the prior year period. Management attributed this flatto-declining margin performance to continuous growth investments and higher central administrative overhead.

Capitalized development costs have surged, significantly widening the spread between reported EBITDA and adjusted EBITA measures. During the first nine months of 2026, investments in intangible assets, which consist of internal R&D, software development, and R&D partnerships, doubled to EUR 7.7m compared to EUR 3.6m in the first nine months of 2025. This aggressive capitalization of R&D expenses artificially inflates EBITDA by shifting operating costs off the profit and loss statement and onto the balance sheet.

DNB Carnegie notes that a key requirement for a premium valuation multiple is the better conversion of EBITA growth into actual EPS. Because capitalization policies vary, analysts are increasingly focused on clean cash generation metrics rather than adjusted EBITDA to evaluate Vimian's underlying profitability. This capitalization trend represents a high risk to earnings quality if capitalized software and R&D assets must be written down in future periods.

Tax rate distortions and EPS definitions

Vimian’s effective tax rate is highly volatile due to non-deductible financial adjustments and discrete tax events. In the first quarter of 2026, the company's income tax expense was EUR 5.3m, representing an unusually high effective tax rate of 35%. This Q1 rate was severely distorted by a non-deductible write-down of shares in associates amounting to EUR 2.5m, alongside other non-deductible expenses.

Prior year tax assessments and non-deductible adjustments routinely inflate Vimian's annual tax expenses. For the full year 2025, Vimian reported a tax expense of EUR 13.5m, representing an effective tax rate of 29%. Management noted that the underlying effective tax rate would have been 24% if adjusted for these nondeductible items and reassessments.

DNB Carnegie applies a standardized model for adjusted EPS, projecting EUR 0.14 for 2026e, EUR 0.17 for 2027e, and EUR 0.20 for 2028e. The wider sell-side consensus exhibits a broad spread in definitions, with EPS estimates ranging from EUR 0.089 to EUR 0.140 for fiscal year 2026. This spread exists because some brokers fail to normalize the tax rate or exclude non-cash contingent-consideration adjustments and capitalized R&D.
