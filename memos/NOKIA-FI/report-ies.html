Nokia Oyj (NOKIA-FI): what the Q3 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case, modestly on the fundamentals and by a full grade on the price reading: they confirm the demand leg the case had already accepted, and they press on the earnings-quality gate and remove part of the balance-sheet cushion the case leaned on.

1. What changed

Nokia reported Q3 2026 on 22 October 2026.

  • Headline beat. Comparable net sales were EUR 5.15 bn, up 10% year on year and 2.1% above consensus of EUR 5.047 bn. Comparable operating profit was EUR 605 mm, up 35% and 33.55% above consensus of EUR 453 mm. Comparable operating margin was 11.7% against 9.5% a year earlier.
  • Demand leg. Sales to AI and cloud customers rose 123% at constant currency, to 12% of group revenue. IP Networks grew 43% and Optical Networks 18% at constant currency. The company guided combined Optical and IP Networks growth of 25% to 30% for 2027, against consensus of about 20%. AI and cloud orders were EUR 1.5 bn, down from EUR 2.8 bn in Q2 2026. The case's own monitoring floor is a EUR 1.0 bn quarterly run-rate, so that floor held.
  • Where the beat came from. Mobile Infrastructure grew 5% at a 14.5% operating margin, helped by revenue accelerated out of Q4 and by pre-buying in Japan, India and EMEA. Sales to telecom operators were flat. Fixed Networks fell 22% at constant currency.
  • Margin and cash. Comparable gross margin slipped to 44.6% from 44.8%, attributed to mix and the ramp of new AI and cloud products. Free cash flow was negative EUR 99 mm, after negative EUR 749 mm in Q2 2026. Full-year 2026 free cash flow conversion guidance was cut to 35% to 55% from 55% to 75%, on a further EUR 400 mm of working capital to secure components. Net cash was EUR 2.584 bn at the end of Q3 2026, against EUR 3.001 bn a year earlier.
  • Restructuring. 2026 restructuring charges were raised to EUR 1.1 bn from EUR 800 mm, with cash outflows of EUR 800 mm to EUR 900 mm.
  • Guidance. Full-year comparable operating profit guidance of EUR 2.1 bn to EUR 2.6 bn was held, tracking above the midpoint. The 2028 target of EUR 2.7 bn to EUR 3.2 bn was not raised.

The sell-side split rather than converged. Jefferies kept Buy and EUR 13.80. Morgan Stanley kept Overweight and EUR 14.00, raised revenue and operating profit forecasts by low-to-mid single digits and cut 2026 reported earnings per share by about 10% to 12% for restructuring. Deutsche Bank kept Buy and cut its target to EUR 10.00 from EUR 11.50, citing peer de-rating. DNB Carnegie kept Sell and cut to EUR 6.50 from EUR 7.80, citing peer de-rating and earnings quality.

The price. The shares were EUR 6.982 on 6 November 2026. That is 12% below the case's pinned entry price of EUR 7.932 and 21.5% below the 200-day average, where the case had them 1.77% above it. The case argued that the fall from the high had partially unwound the "zero margin of safety" objection but could not confirm a multiple. The multiple now exists: 15.1x, at the 82nd percentile of the stock's own ten-year history (8 November 2026). A lower price has not produced a low multiple.

2. The challenge

The case holds its two central findings apart with one distinction: the setup fit rests on revenue and demand evidence, the false-friend caution rests on margin and earnings-quality evidence in patents, R&D cuts and working capital, and these are different parts of the income statement. These results admit three readings that distinction does not hold.

Reading one: the margin problem has moved into the growth leg. The case treats AI-optical growth as clean and volume-driven, and cites fixed-cost leverage as realised. In this quarter the growth leg grew fastest and group gross margin fell, with the AI and cloud ramp named as a cause. Deutsche Bank reports investor anxiety about gross margin dilution from hyperscaler contracts, and says Nokia plans to use its in-house indium phosphide capacity to compete on price against Ciena. If hyperscaler business carries a structurally lower gross margin than the group, then the 25% to 30% growth guide is a revenue upgrade more than an earnings upgrade, and the case's primary setup, a demand-driven earnings upgrade, is weaker than its grade implies. For this to be right, the Q3 dilution has to be contract pricing and not ramp cost. Management says the opposite: it expects gross margin to recover in Q4 2026 through scale and pricing discipline. One quarter of 20 basis points does not prove the reading. It does mean the case's clean separation is now an assumption.

Reading two: the beat is the false-friend pattern in a new form. The operating profit beat was EUR 152 mm against consensus, yet full-year guidance did not move. The guided 20% to 24% sequential rise in Q4 sales implies, on our arithmetic from the reported quarterly figures, year-on-year growth of roughly 3% to 6%, against 10% in Q3. That is what a timing shift looks like. At the same time comparable profit rose while restructuring charges, reported earnings estimates and cash all went the other way. The case's named mechanisms were patent catch-up and R&D cuts. This is a third: profit pulled forward in the legacy segment and adjusted for an expanding restructuring line. For it to be right, Mobile Infrastructure's 14.5% margin has to fall back in Q4 2026 and 2027 as pre-buying unwinds and the cost inflation DNB Carnegie flags arrives. Against it: the review does not show patent catch-up payments in the quarter, and cost programmes are meant to lift margin.

Reading three: vertical integration turns supply fragility into capital fragility. The case treats the in-house fabs as the mitigant to its weakest Element, and treats net cash and 72% conversion as the mitigant to its riskiness. These results show the first mitigant consuming the second: negative free cash flow of EUR 848 mm across two quarters, a further EUR 400 mm of working capital, and an agreement to acquire a third fabrication site. Management describes the supply constraints as more broad-based than in 2021. Ciena is arguing to customers that an asset-light model is the safer supplier. For this reading to be right, the working capital has to prove structural and not a one-off pre-build, or yields at the 6-inch ramp have to disappoint. Jefferies forecasts the opposite, with free cash flow of EUR 1.271 bn in 2027.

The reading in the other direction. We do not think the evidence is one-sided. The case graded the mis-modelling setup as partial because consensus had caught up and sentiment was euphoric. The company has now guided above consensus, consensus 2028 operating profit of EUR 3.53 bn sits above the company's own target range, and the shares have fallen while estimates rose. That is closer to an entry-point trade than anything the case saw. It requires the de-rating to be about the sector and not about Nokia, which is what Deutsche Bank and Morgan Stanley say. The 82nd-percentile multiple is the evidence against, unless a ten-year history as a telecom vendor is the wrong yardstick for this mix.

3. What it does to the case

  • E1 RA1 (Change forces?) [B]: less pressure. The demand evidence is stronger than when the case was written.
  • E1 RA2 (Robust base?) [D]: the same grade, with the composition changed. Delivery congruency is better on the headline and worse on quality. The low-multiple test is no longer unconfirmed; it fails on data.
  • E8 RA1 (Mis-modelled EPS upgrader?) [C]: upward pressure. Guidance above consensus reopens the gap the case said had narrowed. The formal target upgrade the case named as the trigger has not happened.
  • E8 RA2 (Big demand change-driven EPS upgrade?) [B]: more pressure on the fixed-cost-leverage test, less on the demand tests.
  • E8 RA8 (False friend?) [D]: more pressure. This is the case's dominant gate, and the quarter adds to it.
  • E11 RA5 (Supportive supplier side?) [D]: the same. The second San Jose fab moving to 24/7 is progress on a plan. Yields are not disclosed.
  • E5 RA1 (Bear market suitability) [D] and Group 4 (Case riskiness) [D]: more pressure. The net-cash and cash-conversion offsets both rely on are weaker.
  • MR1 Technical momentum [C]: the pinned letter is now D. The case's reading rested on a price above the 200-day average and a 93rd-percentile relative strength. Relative strength is now the 26th percentile.
  • MR2 Sell-side earnings momentum [NR]: now gradeable, pinned at B.
  • The two conditions. The false-friend condition is under more pressure. The T-Mobile condition is untouched: the review reports no change in the contract, only a joint AI-RAN call on its network.

4. What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry any further broker estimate and target changes. Most of the first response is already in hand, so we expect it to refine the split, not resolve it. It will not carry any new company disclosure, because the company has reported nothing since 22 October 2026.
  • Q4 2026 results. These are the first real test. The review gives no date; last year's fourth-quarter call was on 29 January 2026. They settle reading one through gross margin, which management has said recovers, and reading two through Mobile Infrastructure margin and whether Q4 sales land in the guided range. Brokers also expect any 2028 target upgrade to wait for this point. Deutsche Bank thinks it may wait longer, until yields stabilise.
  • Cash through 2027. The review's own marker is conversion back above 65% in the second half of 2027. Reading three stays open until then.
  • What nothing is committed to answer. The company has not committed to disclose fab yield or throughput, the gross margin or terms of its hyperscaler contracts, or comparable margin excluding patent catch-up. The review also does not confirm that the San Jose move to 24/7, due by the end of October 2026, happened on time. The case's thresholds on yield, component lead times and book-to-bill cannot be tested from what the company publishes today. Those remain questions we would have to infer and cannot observe.
