1. Attendees

Patrik Johannesson, Investor Relations. His acceptance of the invitation still showed as pending this morning; confirm the call. The Executive Assistant holds no profile of him.

2. Objective and higher intent

  • Objective: find out how early the company would see this cycle turning, and whether the third quarter's surge was real end demand.
  • Higher intent: Richard's own note of 16-Jul-2026 says data centres carry "the boom-and-bust risks of FTTH", that it will "collapse at some point", and that "companies are biased". The memo expects two more easy quarters and a harder second half of 2027. The holding is large. So the call is to test the company's awareness of the turn, not to hear the growth story.
  • What would move the position. Towards holding through 2027 or adding on a pullback: evidence of end use, glass secured under contract, the open seats filled, candour about 2023. Towards trimming before the second half of 2027: customers ordering further ahead, no supply contracts, "normalisation" that looks like lost share, "this time is different".

3. Strategy

He will give no numbers and will cite "competitive reasons". So ask for direction, ranking and comparison with 2023, and put competitors' and analysts' statements to him to react to. Corning, Belden, Clearfield and AFL have all said things in public that cut against Hexatronic's account; use them.

4. Category

Sorting box teleological probing.

5. Banana skins and navigation plan

  • The growth story. A first call with investor relations tends to become the presentation. Lead with T1 so the frame is the last bust, not this boom.
  • "We do not disclose that." Order intake, utilisation, the submarine order book and sales lost to supply are all refused. Ask "fuller or emptier than a year ago" instead of the figure.
  • Industry forecasts. Richard's note: "Industry forecasts are pointless/inane". If he quotes one, ask what the company's own customers are doing.

6. What management has said, and what others have said since

  • North America: the chief executive said in the third-quarter report that North American Fiber Solutions grew 13% organically and is "shifting back towards growth". Regional sales rose 80%.
  • Data Center: SEK 692m in the third quarter, +44% organic; management steers to "normalisation" in the fourth quarter.
  • Supply: glass fibre and some connectors "were beginning to restrict growth".
  • BEAD: a "meaningful driver" from the second half of 2026 (said April 2026).
  • Harsh Environment: its 15% margin target "has not yet been proven".
  • Others since: Clearfield (10-Nov-2026) sees no BEAD "hockey stick" until mid-2027. Corning and Belden have signed long-term supply agreements. AFL sees "no real relief" on glass until 2029 to 2030. Jefferies and Morgan Stanley see power shortages pushing data-centre completions from 2028 into 2029.

7. Topics (check-pick list)

T1 ★ The last bust. "In 2023 the industry said fibre to the home was good for a decade, and it fell within a year. Some people say data centres are the same film. What do you watch internally that would tell you first that this one is turning?"
- Pick from: "Which of those indicators has moved the wrong way in the last three months?" / "What did you miss in 2023 that you would catch now?" / "Your order book covers about four months. Was it the same in early 2023?"
- Positive: named indicators, candour about 2023. Negative: "this time is different"; an industry forecast.

T2 ★ North America: demand or buying ahead? "North American sales rose 80% in the quarter, while your chief executive put Fiber Solutions there at 13%. Clearfield says AT&T has soaked up much of the cable made in the United States. Are your customers ordering ahead because fibre is short?"
- Pick from: "How much of the 80% was the Superior acquisition?" / "Are orders being placed further ahead than a year ago?" / "Your chief executive expected BEAD to matter from the second half of 2026; Clearfield says mid-2027. Who is right?"
- Positive: order patterns unchanged; a split of the 80%. Negative: longer or larger orders; the BEAD date slipping.

T3 ★ What "normalisation" means in Data Center. "Management has steered to a normal fourth quarter in Data Center after SEK 692m in the third. Corning's sales to data centres grew 63% over nine months against your 44%. Some people read 'normalisation' as losing share to suppliers that own their glass. What is the right reading?"
- Pick from: "How much of the third quarter was schools and other seasonal work?" / "Analysts now see power, not fibre, moving data-centre completions from 2028 to 2029. Are your customers moving dates?" / "Is a normal quarter nearer the second quarter's level or the third's?"
- Positive: seasonal, sized, repeatable. Negative: no size; customers' dates moving.

T4 ★ Glass: sales deferred, or sales lost? "You said glass and connectors were beginning to restrict growth. Corning and Belden have locked in supply under long contracts. Forecasts for relief run from early 2027 at Redeye to 2029 or 2030 at AFL. Which do you plan on, and do you have contracts like theirs?"
- Pick from: "Did the shortage cost you sales in the quarter, and did those sales wait or go elsewhere?" / "Are you paying more for glass, and does all of it pass through?" / "Some people say a company of your size cannot secure glass against Corning. What is the answer?"
- Positive: contracts in place; sales deferred. Negative: "we are monitoring"; customers splitting orders with integrated rivals.

T5 ★ The margin floor in Fiber Solutions. "Fiber Solutions made 9.4%, up from 5.4% a year earlier. Last cycle it gave everything back. If volumes fell to 2024 levels again, where would the margin settle after the SEK 120m of savings?"
- Pick from: "Is there any sign of life in European fibre to the home, or is the plan to shrink to fit?" / "Are prices for duct in the United States still falling?"
- Positive: a floor with reasons. Negative: "we do not expect volumes to fall".

T6 The open seats. "The finance chief moved to run Fiber Solutions on 1 November, Data Center has an acting head, and the deputy chief executive who led acquisitions left in June. Some people would call that a lot of empty chairs at the top of a cycle. When are they filled?"
- Pick from: "Why did they open in the same six months?" / "Who runs acquisitions now?" / "Will both be named before the February report?"
- Positive: names or dates. Negative: "the process is ongoing".

T7 Harsh Environment. "Harsh Environment shrank 4% in the second quarter, and management says its 15% target has not yet been proven. What has to happen to prove it, and by when?"
- Pick from: "Is the submarine order book for 2027 fuller or emptier than 2026's was a year ago?"

T8 The target and the incentive. "The 2028 target is a 12% margin, yet the 2026 share plan pays out in full at an average of about 11%. Which should investors build their models on?"
- Pick from: "Does SEK 11bn of sales need acquisitions?" / "Redeye worries the cash for them dries up if Fiber Solutions turns. Would you issue shares again, as in May at SEK 38?"
- Positive: 12% defended with a path. Negative: "targets are ambitions".

T9 The sceptics. "Only Redeye publishes detailed numbers. What reason do the investors who will not buy give you?"
- Pick from: "The shares fell 16% on a first-quarter report that was in line. What did the market hear that the numbers did not show?" / "Is more coverage coming?"

The final, difficult question. "If your chief executive had to bet on one of the three businesses being smaller in two years than it is today, which would it be?"
