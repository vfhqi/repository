These organizational changes will directly affect postseparation corporate expenses and necessitate rigorous benchmarking to avoid margin erosion at the remaining parent company.

The analysis of standalone corporate-function benchmarks across seven industrial automation and machinevision peers reveals substantial variation in overhead structures that will critically inform expectations for TKH's remaining Automation parent.

Critical evidence gaps and uncertainties

1.
Unspecified pricing: TKH Group has not yet disclosed the exact pricing terms and service level agreements for the upcoming transitional arrangements. While the July 2026 circular lists consolidation software and payroll systems, it lacks specific cost figures. Shareholders cannot fully evaluate immediate margin impacts until final commercial agreements are released next year. This means post-separation earnings projections will remain highly sensitive to cost assumptions until definitive contract pricing is published by the executive board.
2.
Unallocated overhead: Standalone central corporate overhead for the remaining Automation parent is not detailed in current disclosures. TKH Group excludes unallocated head-office costs from its new 17% to 19% EBITA margin targets. Investors require dedicated segment cost reconciliations to assess potential stranded overhead. Consequently, standalone corporate-function expenses are difficult to model with high precision until management publishes standalone financial statements, which limits near-term consensus reliability.
3.
Uncertain transaction terms: The active dual-track separation process introduces significant variance in expected transaction terms. TKH Group has not finalized whether Electrification will undergo a public listing or a private sale. Each pathway carries distinct tax, advisory, and restructuring costs. Clarifying this final structural choice is critical to determining net proceeds for future capital deployment, which will shape the scope of the parent's planned buy and build strategy.
4.
Contingent liability status: The precise legal mechanism for transferring the parent's corporate bank guarantees is currently undisclosed. TKH Group held €29.7 million in outstanding corporate guarantees at the end of fiscal 2025. If lenders do not fully release the parent, these contingent liabilities could persist after the demerger. Resolving this exposure is essential to assessing parent balance-sheet risk post-separation, as any unexpected defaults would immediately dilute the parent's high-margin focus.
5.
Unquantified rebranding costs: TKH Group's filings acknowledge upcoming IT and rebranding expenses but fail to provide a formal capital budget. Precedent separations like Thyssenkrupp indicate that rebranding timelines often extend over many months. Lacking a defined budget, the Automation parent faces unmodeled cash outflows. Expert calls or detailed prospectus disclosures are required to clarify these cash impacts on short-term earnings before the executive transition occurs on 1 January 2027.
6.
Frictionless logistics transition: Standalone logistics and procurement frameworks represent another vital operational gap for the remaining business. The 22 July 2026 circular notes that temporary logistical arrangements are required for Electrification. Disentangling shared supplier contracts typically leads to a loss of purchasing leverage. Therefore, investors must wait for post-separation supply contracts to verify long-term margin preservation for the Automation parent under the new operating model.
7.
Unspecified insurance costs: Dedicated insurance premium allocations for the separated Automation parent remain completely unquantified in public filings. Historic precedents like Sandvik's Alleima spin-off show standalone insurance costs rising materially post-separation. TKH Group's upcoming standalone prospectuses must explicitly list historical corporate allocations. Shareholders need these figures to assess corporate risk-management overhead inflation over the next 18 months, which represents a key variable for standalone profitability.
8.
Unconfirmed divestment pricing: The exact valuation and timing for the divestment of the Digitalization segment remain unconfirmed. TKH Group intends to divest about €250 million of non-core turnover to streamline its operations. However, the lack of historical segment profitability disclosures makes evaluating divestment proceeds difficult. Securing high multiples for these assets is vital to funding future acquisitions and hitting long-term leverage targets.
9.
Other side of the story: The rapid financial recovery of the Electrification business may render these analytical gaps irrelevant. High standalone profitability could enable Electrification to build standalone corporate services rapidly. This rapid transition would allow the Automation parent to wind down transitional agreements ahead of schedule. Consequently, any stranded corporate overhead would be rapidly eliminated post-merger, protecting parent earnings.

Divergent valuation and peer multiple scenarios

1.
Severe downside valuation: TKH Group's share price could collapse if operating performance falls back to historic averages during the transition. Jefferies' research report of 13 August 2026 outlines a severe downside scenario of €30 per share. This projection assumes a stable EBITA margin of 11.7% over the coming years, which is in line with the historical average profitability recorded over the last decade. If structural dis-synergies persist, the post-separation parent will experience significant margin compression, which means that future earnings per share could decline relative to consensus expectations.
2.
Valuation multiple sensitivities: The remaining Automation parent faces a lower valuation if the market applies standard conglomerate discounts post-separation. Jefferies' sum-of-the-parts analysis of 13 August 2026 applied a 30% conglomerate discount to arrive at its base case target price. That report valued the Vision Technologies division at 16.6x EV/EBITDA and Automated Machinery at 14.5x. If the demerger fails to eliminate the holding structure complexity, the market will continue to discount the core Automation business because it remains structurally combined with non-core digitalization assets.
3.
Pure-play premium valuations: Peer valuations suggest that standalone automation listings command significantly higher multiples than diversified conglomerates. Morgan Stanley's report on 16 October 2026 shows Cognex trading at an EV/EBITDA multiple of 27.2x. This premium valuation reflects the market's high willingness to pay for dedicated industrial machine-vision exposure. The remaining Automation business can unlock this premium valuation over time, provided it successfully eliminates historical conglomerate discounts.

Operational execution and competitor risks

4.
Aggressive Chinese competition: Competitor strategies represent a significant external threat to the remaining Automation parent's long-term market share. An expert call with the former President at a global industrial sensor leader on 22 October 2026 highlighted that Chinese machine vision manufacturers are rapidly advancing in technology. The expert warned that low-cost suppliers could overtake established players within 10 years. These dynamics suggest that price competition in commoditized hardware will intensify, which would severely erode profit margins unless TKH Group differentiates on proprietary software features.
5.
Transition overhead expenses: The remaining Automation business faces elevated risk of central cost inflation if administrative services are not duplicated efficiently. TKH Group's shareholder circular of 22 July 2026 notes that separate corporate governance structures will create legal, IT, and rebranding costs. Datalogic SpA's annual report of 26 May 2025 noted that operating costs rose as a percentage of sales from 38% to 39.9% due to lower revenue absorption. If the Automation parent fails to right-size its corporate center, stranded central overhead will weigh heavily on post-separation margins and depress earnings per share.
6.
Corporate guarantee exposure: Financial risks for the parent remain high unless lenders release TKH from historical liabilities. TKH Group's annual report published on 7 July 2026 showed that corporate guarantees provided to banks reached €29.7 million at the end of 2025. This liability represents a massive increase compared to the €12 million reported at the end of 2024. If the separated Electrification business defaults on its debt, the parent's contingent balance sheet exposure would trigger immediate cash outflows, which means that the planned buy and build strategy is restricted.
7.
Operational gearing benchmark: Technology peers illustrate how high operational gearing can accelerate margin expansion if central expenses are tightly controlled. Deutsche Bank's report on 21 October 2026 highlighted that Jenoptik's preliminary Q3 EBITDA margin reached a strong 27.3%, prompting a raised full-year margin guidance of 22%. This margin improvement was primarily driven by semiconductor equipment demand and underlying mix effects. TKH Group can achieve similar operating efficiencies, but only if the remaining parent implements strict cost containment measures immediately after the Electrification demerger.

Early indicators of transaction deviation

8.
Weakening tire building: Early indicators of strategic underperformance are emerging in TKH's Automated Machinery segment. Jefferies' research note on 2 October 2026 highlighted that the core activities in Automation will likely face pressure due to delayed order intake in tire building. This weakness contrasts with the strong 160% EBITA recovery reported in the Electrification division. A sustained drop in capital expenditure by Tier 1 tire manufacturers would severely impact automated machinery earnings and offset any margin expansion from Vision Technology.
