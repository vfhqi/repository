Section 3 – External Change: Value-Chain Forces (Demand, Competition, Disruption, Regulation, Supply, Cycle, Macro) 37 findings

Section summary
  1. deepwater demand remains highly resilient in Brazil and Guyana with breakevens well below spot prices
  2. competitive field is consolidated among top-tier operators but face growing tension in major tenders
  3. transition technologies are strategically relevant but commercialization timelines are shifting to the right
  4. local content frameworks impose strict execution parameters in primary South Atlantic basins
  5. Asian shipyard capacity is exceptionally tight with orderbooks at multi-year highs threatening execution
  6. the offshore capital cycle is plateauing at an elevated level extending the duration of cash generation
  7. macro volatility and high financing costs weigh on project economics and FIDs despite oil price strength

Accelerated Guyana production: Guyana targets oil production of 1.3 million bpd by 2027 and 1.7 million bpd by 2030, up from 900,000 bpd

Suriname development pathway: TotalEnergies awarded the GranMorgu drilling contracts in Block 58, targeting first oil in 2028 via a 220,000 bpd FPSO
4.
Attractive deepwater breakevens: Deepwater projects average a competitive breakeven of USD 43 per barrel, making them highly resilient to price downturns
5.
Receptive exploration frontiers: Petrobras expanded its African footprint with a 42.5% stake in Namibia's Orange Basin to replicate pre-salt success

Solid market leadership: SBM Offshore remains the global leader, owning and operating 20 units, including five under construction as of 1H26

Persistent peer downtime: MISC Berhad suffered a 20.8% YoY decline in offshore profits due to the ongoing shutdown of FPSO Kikeh
