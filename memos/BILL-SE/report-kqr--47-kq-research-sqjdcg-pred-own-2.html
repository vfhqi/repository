Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding Nordic wood cost trajectories is crucial for determining Billerud's profit recovery and long-term competitiveness.

Scope of the Investigation

1.
Critical inquiry: "What path will Swedish and Finnish pulpwood and stumpage prices take through 2027 as storm- felled (Storm Johannes) salvage supply ends, according to Södra, Mellanskog, Norra Skog, Sveaskog, Skogsstyrelsen, Metsä Group and brokers; how much salvage volume remains unprocessed; what price list changes have been announced for H1 2027; how long is the lag from stumpage and delivered prices to the cost of non-integrated mills such as Billerud AB (BILL-SE); and how do analysts interpret Billerud's guidance that 2027 wood costs will on average be stable compared with 2026?"
2.
Extensive scoping: In analyzing this critical market transition, research identified 24 distinct sources, 7 peer companies, and 2 historical storm precedents. These resources provide a robust baseline for evaluating Nordic wood dynamics and Billerud's financial exposure. Understanding these pricing pressures is vital because wood represents a third of operating costs for non-integrated mills.
3.
Financial sensitivity: Billerud remains highly vulnerable to raw material shifts because of its low fiber self- sufficiency. Billerud's latest annual report indicates that securing wood fiber remains the company's highest variable cost exposure. Consequently, these procurement dynamics directly determine margins and earnings per share development through 2027.
4.
Investor focus: Sourcing dynamics are a primary focal point for packaging sector investors who model mill-gate cost structures and cash flows. Jefferies research highlighted on January 9, 2026, that wood represents the single most crucial factor determining earnings recovery trajectories. Therefore, understanding whether pulpwood costs stabilize or rebound represents the chief catalyst for assessing Billerud's valuation.

Market Tailwinds and Structural Gaps

5.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reported on October 22, 2026, that Scandinavian wood raw material costs might experience significant declines to support Billerud's earnings through 2027. This perspective contradicts the dominant view that ending salvage supply will immediately trigger painful price hikes for pulpwood. If these declines persist, Billerud's margins could expand much faster than analysts expect.
6.
Structural changes: The investigation raises broader thematic questions about Nordic wood fiber availability since the European Union banned Russian timber imports. DNB Carnegie noted on October 22, 2026, that elevated wood costs in Scandinavia continue to strain industrial competitiveness. This deficit forces non-integrated players to aggressively expand purchases from private forest owners to secure supply.
7.
Diverging trends: European pulpwood market dynamics show a widening gap between Nordic price relief and tight Central European supply. Jefferies reported on October 16, 2026, that while Nordic prices softened, German and Polish costs remained structurally higher for longer due to bioenergy competition. These regional disparities directly impact the cost-competitiveness of European packaging producers.
8.
Other side of the story: While fiber cost inflation is widely feared, Billerud is actively mitigating these risks by expanding direct sourcing and improving production fiber efficiency. According to Billerud's 2025 annual report, recipe optimization and external pulp sourcing provide a buffer against raw material volatility. Consequently, the structural wood deficit might not depress margins as severely as bears fear if self-help programs succeed.

Section 3 – Storm Johannes Salvage Supply and Remaining Volumes

Section summary
  1. Storm Johannes created approximately 10 million cubic metres of salvage timber in Sweden and up to 4 million in Finland.
  2. High-priority salvage clearing was mandated within six months to prevent bark beetle outbreaks, drying up the supply buffer by early 2027.
  3. Historical precedents suggest the end of salvage wood will trigger a modest pulpwood price rebound of under 10%.

Initial Storm Johannes Impact

1.
Severe forest damage: Swedish forest agency Skogsstyrelsen estimated that Storm Johannes felled up to 10 million cubic metres of timber across central and northern Sweden. This immense volume of wind-felled wood severely disrupted regional timber procurement balances and triggered a front-loaded increase in roundwood supply. Industry experts projected that this sudden supply influx would place substantial downward pressure on regional pulpwood and sawlog prices throughout the affected regions.
2.
Revised damage estimates: Analyst DNB Carnegie estimated the actual forest damage in Sweden was slightly smaller, totaling approximately 6 million to 7 million cubic metres. Despite this lower volume, the storm damage was still considered highly significant for private forest owners in the mid-part of Sweden. This massive local wood oversupply was expected to temporarily lower raw material costs for Swedish industrial mills.
3.
Sudden Finnish volumes: Finnish forests also faced unexpected supply pressures from the storm, which was named Hannes in Finland, in late 2025. Preliminary Finnish damage assessments of 300,000 cubic metres were sharply revised upward to between 3.5 million and 4 million cubic metres. This dramatic volume increase created a temporary, highly localized wood supply surge across the Finnish wood market.
4.
Surging logging activity: Swedish forest operators responded immediately to the storm damage by prioritizing salvage operations. Swedish logging notifications surged significantly in early 2026 as operators scrambled to harvest storm-felled timber. This emergency prioritization displaced planned harvests and drastically altered long-term forest felling schedules for regional wood buyers.

Salvage Timelines and Buffer Depletion

5.
Aggressive processing timelines: Forest owners' association Mellanskog warned that salvage volumes in the affected Swedish regions had to be processed within six months. This aggressive timeline was mandated by strict environmental and regulatory deadlines to limit bark beetle risks. Consequently, this temporary timber supply buffer is projected to fully dry up in late 2026 and early 2027.
6.
Widespread quality degradation: Slower and riskier harvesting operations in storm-felled areas eroded the ultimate value recovery of the salvage volume. A highly meaningful share of high-value sawlogs was downgraded to pulpwood because of timber fiber damage. This widespread quality degradation temporarily inflated the local supply of pulpwood while tightening high-quality sawlog availability.
7.
Displaced harvest volumes: Market analysts observed that the front-loaded salvage wood did not represent an expansion of the total Nordic wood pool. The front-loaded storm volumes merely displaced normal, planned harvesting operations across Sweden. This displacement implies that local timber availability will tighten considerably once the salvage operations fully conclude.
8.
Contrary evidence: Analyst DNB Carnegie argued that the damage from Storm Johannes could trigger a temporary major decline in wood raw material prices. This pricing pressure could extend further if forest asset transactions remain depressed in mid-Sweden. Such a decline would prolong the cheap fiber buffer for non-integrated mills like Billerud.

Regional Logistics and Peer Impacts

9.
Strained local logistics: The transfer of massive harvesting and transport resources to the storm-damaged regions created supply gaps elsewhere. Sourcing contractors and transport fleets were fully booked for salvage work, leading to severe resource constraints. These logistical constraints temporarily strained wood flows in unaffected regions of the Nordic market.
10.
Elevated salvage costs: Forestry major Holmen estimated its storm-felled timber volume at 300,000 cubic metres in early 2026. This volume was equivalent to nearly 10% of Holmen's annual harvesting budget and one-third of harvest in affected areas. The company projected that incremental handling and salvage costs would total SEK 30 million to SEK 40 million.
11.
Proactive private cleanup: Major forest owner SCA initiated salvage operations in late 2025 to process wind-felled timber. These efforts were concentrated primarily on the land holdings of private forest owners rather than its own forests. This proactive cleanup helped mitigate severe wood loss across northern Sweden while supporting local private suppliers.
12.
Strategic resource reallocation: Forest operator Holmen chose to reduce harvesting from its own forest in 2026. This reduction allowed Holmen to reallocate critical harvesting resources to assist private owners with cleanups. This operational shift temporarily supported the wider Swedish timber cleanup effort while protecting Holmen's long-term standing assets.
13.
Stable wood sourcing: Inderes reported on April 20, 2026, that Finnish peer Koskisen Corp procures spruce and pine mainly from private forest owners in Finland. Following its acquisition of Iisveden Metsä, Koskisen's total wood procurement volume increased by just over 25% to around 1.9 million cubic meters. This localized procurement network helps Koskisen maintain stable wood supply to regional mills even during post-storm market adjustments.

Historical Precedents and Pricing Impacts

14.
Historical storm comparison: Historical analysis reveals that Storm Gudrun in 2005 remains the most devastating storm on record. Gudrun felled 75 million cubic metres of timber across southern Sweden, creating the world's largest lumber surplus at the time. In the aftermath, Swedish sawlog and pulpwood prices fell by 37% and 25% respectively.
15.
Comparable mid-sized precedents: Market experts view the 2013 and 2014 storms, Ivar and Hilde, as much closer precedents. These earlier storms felled 6 million to 10 million cubic metres of timber in northern Sweden. Following those events, the pulpwood price decline was by a modest 8%.
16.
Depressed segment earnings: The 2013-2014 storms brought down approximately 1 million cubic metres of timber on Holmen's land. This salvage operation led to lower log quality and higher operational costs. Consequently, Holmen's forest segment operating profit declined by 19% year-on-year in 2014.
17.
Other side of the story: While the end of salvage operations is expected to tighten wood supply, major buyers like Metsä Group maintain highly stable long-term supply networks. These operators utilize deep relationships with private cooperative forest owners to offset sudden market deficits. Thus, the transition away from salvage wood might not trigger severe price hikes if industrial players leverage long-standing procurement agreements.

Section 4 – H1 2027 Price List Changes and Owner Association Moves

Section summary
  1. Forest cooperatives and major buyers are stabilizing price lists for H1 2027 following the deep roundwood price cuts implemented during 2026.
  2. Private forest owners are resisting further price cuts as the temporary storm-felled supply buffer disappears and winter procurement activity accelerates.
  3. Industrial contract negotiations for the upcoming felling season point to flat sequential wood costs that remain structurally high compared to pre-2021 levels.

Cooperative Pricing Decisions and Procurement Moves

1.
Stabilized pricing: Södra and other major Swedish forest owner associations stabilized their roundwood price lists in late 2026 after executing heavy cuts earlier in the year. The regional market analyst Mason, Bruce & Girard noted on July 20, 2026, that both Södra and Mellanskog lowered their price lists as the pulpwood trade became less active than usual. These price list adjustments help secure long-term fiber availability but squeeze forest owner income heading into the felling season unless stumpage demand recovers.
2.
Lowered benchmarks: Sveaskog implemented a nationwide reduction in roundwood purchasing prices to align its procurement with the softer market conditions. The state-owned forest operator Sveaskog reduced its operational prices for purchasing industrial wood from private forest owners by SEK 40 per cubic metre with effect from May 12, 2026. This nationwide pricing adjustment has reset the benchmark for contract negotiations and stumpage bids heading into the H1 2027 harvesting season.
3.
Steady transactions: Metsä Group reported that Finnish pulpwood prices stabilized at high levels during late 2026 while log prices hovered near historical peaks. Metsä Group highlighted on October 29, 2026, that weekly private forest timber transactions surged in autumn after a quiet summer recess. The cooperative is actively leveraging its large private owner network to secure stable fiber volumes for regional mills through H1 2027.
4.
Secured integration: Norra Skog maintains a highly competitive bilateral supply framework in northern Sweden that buffers its partner mills from market price shocks. The cooperative Norra Skog delivers fiber under a longterm wood supply agreement with Metsä Board, which owns a key stake in the Husum pulp mill. This structural integration provides Metsä Board with enhanced raw material security compared to non-integrated competitors like Billerud.

Impact on Contract Negotiations and Stumpage Bids

5.
Correcting stumpage: Softening stumpage rates in Finland indicate that the aggressive raw material cost inflation seen in prior years has finally run its course. The private forest owner Tornator reported on August 28, 2026, that average pulpwood stumpage prices in the first half of the year fell nearly 30% compared with 2025 peak levels. This price correction represents a substantial margin relief for regional industrial consumers as they enter negotiations for H1 2027 felling contracts.
6.
Recovering demand: Improving demand in the Finnish timber market is expected to drive a modest rebound in harvesting activity and stumpage values in 2027. Pellervo Economic Research projected that timber purchases from Finnish private forests will expand by 12% in 2027 to support rising industrial wood consumption. This projected volume expansion will likely strengthen forest owner bargaining power and prevent further price cuts during the upcoming winter felling season.
7.
Anticipated stabilization: Gradual market stabilization has prompted analysts to forecast a steadying of Nordic fiber costs after the severe margin pressure of recent years. Analyst DNB Carnegie reported on October 2, 2026, that Finnish pulpwood stumpage prices have ended their strong upward phase but are expected to remain above early 2026 lows. This outlook suggests that non-integrated mills will face stable but structurally elevated raw material costs throughout H1 2027.
8.
Contrary evidence: Swedish state forest giant Sveaskog and private associations could implement further roundwood price cuts if European paperboard demand remains depressed through H1 2027. Sveaskog reported on October 29, 2026, that its Q3 operating profit fell 37% due to lower timber prices, which decreased by an average of 15% year-on-year. If sawmills and pulp mills continue to take economic downtime, additional price list reductions could be announced for H1 2027.

Comparison with Historical Price Settings and Peer Actions

9.
Easing wood costs: Decreasing wood costs are expected to provide immediate margin relief for integrated Nordic producers during the final months of 2026. The Swedish peer SCA guided that its wood procurement costs would decline a further 4% to 5% sequentially in the fourth quarter of 2026. This near-term price decline will help offset persistent weakness in sawn timber and paperboard export prices before felling rates stabilize in early 2027.
10.
Steady procurement: Stable pricing expectations dominate the near-term outlook as forest owners resist additional price concessions heading into the new year. The forest major SCA reported in its Q3 2026 earnings call that it had not seen any further wood price decreases going into Q1 2027. This flat outlook indicates that H1 2027 price settings will remain substantially higher than historical pre-2021 levels due to the structural wood deficit.
11.

Analyst Jefferies reported on October 16, 2026, that Nordic pulpwood costs have doubled compared to 2021 levels due to intense regional competition and the bioenergy sector. This tight market environment means that H1 2027 price settings will remain structurally higher for longer compared to previous historical cycles.

Other side of the story: While flat H1 2027 price lists are expected to limit cost inflation, a sudden recovery in European packaging demand could trigger aggressive procurement competition and higher stumpage bids. Stora Enso noted in its Q3 2026 report that variable costs and wood costs are expected to increase somewhat in late 2026 due to mixed market conditions. Section 5 – Wood Cost Lags and Non-Integrated Mill Dynamics for BILL-SE

Section summary
  1. Non-integrated pulp and paper mills like Billerud experience a significant three to six month lag before market wood price changes impact their financial statements.
  2. This lag is driven by inventory turnover of two to three months, contract adjustments, and transport logistics.
  3. Billerud remains highly sensitive to wood prices due to its minimal forest ownership compared to structurally integrated Nordic peers.

Procurement Mechanics and Inventory Turns

1.
Delayed impact: Billerud experiences a significant three to six month lag before changes in Nordic pulpwood market rates reflect in its reported earnings. Jefferies reported on April 28, 2026, that this P&L delay is caused by the time required to negotiate procurement contracts and process existing raw material inventory. Consequently, any near-term market price relief or inflation will only impact Billerud's margins after several months.
2.
Gradual dynamics: Billerud manages its mill operations with a typical wood raw material inventory turnover of two to three months. Billerud management explained in their Q4 2025 earnings call on January 30, 2026, that this physical buffer delays the financial benefit of falling pulpwood prices. This operational lag means that lower wood costs only begin accelerating in the second quarter of the fiscal year. - Earnings Calls Contracted security: Billerud secures a portion of its European wood fiber through long-term supply agreements to mitigate localized supply disruptions. While these agreements guarantee volume availability, they limit the company's ability to immediately exploit short-term spot price declines.

Peer Self-Sufficiency and Exposure

3.
Vulnerable footprint: Billerud owns only a very small fraction of its forest assets, making it far more dependent on open market purchases than its integrated Scandinavian competitors. Morgan Stanley reported on July 8, 2026, that Holmen maintains a 50% wood self-sufficiency rate while SCA enjoys 60% self-sufficiency. This structural imbalance means that Billerud faces elevated exposure to fiber market volatility and margin compression.

DNB Carnegie reported on October 23, 2026, that Holmen's forest ownership of approximately 50% provides a strong competitive advantage against raw material scarcity. Because Billerud must buy almost all its fiber externally, its profitability fluctuates much more than these integrated peers.
5.
Unequal exposure: Billerud's mill footprint further concentrates its sourcing risks in regions with the most intense procurement competition. Jefferies reported on November 16, 2022, that Billerud's mills consume relatively more Central Sweden pulpwood, where prices have historically risen faster than in Northern Sweden or Finland. This geographical concentration magnifies Billerud's cost inflation risks relative to more dispersed peers.
6.
Detailed structure: Metsä Board's cost breakdown highlights the typical operating cost splits for regional packaging producers as a comparison point for non-integrated players. DNB Carnegie reported on October 2, 2026, that wood and fiber represent 20% of Metsä Board's cost base compared with UPM where wood and fiber represent 27% of costs. This cost comparison highlights that wood remains the primary variable expense across both integrated and non-integrated Nordic mills.

Quantitative Sensitivity and Financial Impacts

7.
Severe sensitivity: Financial analysts calculate that Billerud's earnings are highly sensitive to even minor shifts in Nordic wood market prices. Jefferies reported on April 28, 2026, that every 10% change in Nordic wood costs impacts Billerud's annual EBITDA by SEK 0.9 billion. This substantial sensitivity highlights why stabilizing fiber costs is the most critical variable for earnings recovery through 2027.
8.
Disproportionate exposure: Sell-side models confirm that fluctuating fiber costs represent the single largest variable driver of Billerud's pre-tax profitability. DNB Carnegie reported on October 2, 2026, that a 10% change in fiber prices has an annual pre-tax profit impact of SEK 1.38 billion for Billerud. This exposure is significantly higher than other inputs such as electricity, which has an annual sensitivity of SEK 150 million.
9.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reported on January 25, 2026, that Billerud's wood costs rose by SEK 160 million in the aftermath of Storm Gudrun in 2005. This historical precedent shows that instead of enjoying immediate cost relief from storm-felled salvage supply, non-integrated mills suffered from elevated logistics and transport expenses. Consequently, temporary wood gluts do not always translate into immediate cash savings for coastal operations.
10.
Instructive precedent: Historical fiber price spikes demonstrate how severely structural supply deficits can damage the profitability of non-integrated Nordic operations. Jefferies reported on January 17, 2025, that Stora Enso responded to the severe 2023 Nordic wood deficit by permanently closing its Sunila pulp mill. This precedent suggests that prolonged regional raw material shortages will eventually force capacity curtailments unless fiber prices normalize.
11.
Calculated sensitivity: Integrated peers also experience earnings sensitivity to wood prices but benefit from offsetting forestry segment profits. DNB Carnegie reported on July 23, 2026, that Stora Enso's earnings are sensitive to Nordic wood raw material costs but its extensive forest assets help mitigate the industrial division's cost spikes. Consequently, while a 10% wood cost increase directly penalizes Billerud's net income, integrated peers can offset this headwind.

Mitigation and Alternate Perspectives

12.
Active mitigation: Billerud is actively expanding its direct sourcing network to private forest owners to bypass expensive middlemen and lower average delivery costs. Billerud reported in its 2025 annual report on January 30, 2026, that it scaled up its field purchasing organization to buy more wood directly from Swedish private owners. Additionally, the company is optimizing its production fiber recipes to reduce total raw material consumption.
13.
Other side of the story: While low self-sufficiency is a clear vulnerability during price spikes, it allows Billerud to capture substantial profit windfalls when wood markets decline. Jefferies noted on January 30, 2026, that Billerud is the key beneficiary of lower wood costs because its earnings are highly geared to open market prices. Therefore, the company's profitability can recover much faster than integrated peers during a cyclical fiber price downturn.

Section 6 – Market Interpretation of Billerud's 2027 Wood Cost Guidance

Section summary
  1. Sell-side analysts are split over Billerud's stable wood cost guidance for 2027 as some expect continued sequential price inflation.
  2. Our confidence in this market interpretation is high based on clear discrepancies between conservative broker models and optimistic consensus targets.
  3. Integrated peers like SCA and Metsä Board enjoy structural cost protections that leave non-integrated Billerud highly exposed to open-market volatility.

Analyst Responses to Stable Cost Guidance

1.
Optimistic modeling: DNB Carnegie projected on October 22, 2026, that Scandinavia wood raw material costs would experience significant declines to bolster Billerud's net earnings throughout 2027. This model assumes that harvesting activity and local supply optimization will drive fiber expenses down.
