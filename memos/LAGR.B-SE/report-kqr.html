Executive Summary

1.
Bottom line: the Key Question can be answered only partly. The report asks what EBITA margins Lagercrantz's acquired companies had at acquisition and afterwards, and how much of the reported EBITA margin comes from remeasuring earn-outs. The earn-out half can be answered fairly well from filings. The post-acquisition margin half is only patchy, because the group does not publish standalone subsidiary margins. Overall confidence in what the evidence shows is medium. Acquired cohorts have been priced at margins below the group's. The 2025/26 acquisitions carried pro forma net revenue of MSEK 1,072 and operating profit of MSEK 164 before acquisition costs, implying about 15.3% against a group margin of 18.1%. The 2024/25 cohort was about 17.0%, below the group's 17.5%. The H1 2026/27 cohort looks dilutive on the same basis. The eight acquisitions add roughly MSEK 660 of annual revenue and, on Redeye's SEK 100m to 110m estimate, imply a margin of 15.15% to 16.67% against the group's H1 margin of 17.40%. Cited broker figures assume acquired margins of 15% to 18%, and none was found that models a postacquisition margin path. Redeye's 28 October 2026 modelling puts the eight H1 acquisitions at SEK 660m of annual revenue and assumes historic EBITA margins of 15% to 18%. Its transaction maths yields SEK 100m to 110m of EBITA. Redeye also notes that Lagercrantz does not explicitly disclose the standalone profitability of acquired businesses in its interim reports. No broker or consensus figure was found that explicitly forecasts how acquired margins evolve after consolidation or how much earn-out gains contribute to forecast EBITA.

Management's 20% target relies on acquisitions, and the cohort data sits awkwardly with it. On the Q3 2025/26 call, management said about half of the margin expansion would come from newly acquired companies. Management also expects roughly two-thirds of revenue growth over three years to come from acquisitions. Disclosed cohorts of 15% to 17% sit below both the group margin and the target. By my reading, that half can therefore only be delivered through post-acquisition improvement or a mix of highmargin outliers.

High-margin proofpoints exist but look like outliers. Mastsystem was acquired in late November 2024 with revenue of about MEUR 15 and EBITA of MEUR 6, roughly 40%. Stalon showed 2025 net sales of SEK 36.2m and EBITA of SEK 11.8m, about 32%. Principal Doorsets was about 18% at acquisition. I found no quantified support in the filings for the Friggeråkers 20% to 25% figure.

Post-acquisition outcomes diverge by end-market. Principal Doorsets' profit before tax fell from GBP 0.94m to GBP 0.69m in the year to 31 March 2026. Turnover fell to GBP 9.25m from GBP 12.20m. The implied profit margin of 7.46% compares with Redeye's 18% EBITA estimate at acquisition. This is a pre-tax margin set against an EBITA estimate, so it is not like-for-like, but the direction is clear. I Holland, by contrast, grew turnover to GBP 26.94m from GBP 24.36m and operating profit to GBP 3.29m from GBP 2.61m. The pattern is that high-margin niches such as Mastsystem and I Holland expand or hold up, while constructionexposed units contract.

The group's reported margin trajectory does not depend on them. Peers show both accretive and dilutive acquisition patterns. Lifco's five-year average acquired EBITA margin was 27% against a group margin of 23%. Halma's average acquired margin since 2021 reached 34%.

Diploma's recent acquisitions averaged 21% against a group margin of 26.5%. Lagercrantz's 15% to 18% acquired margins therefore sit at the lower end of the group. On earn-outs, Indutrade recognised SEK 223m from contingent-consideration remeasurement in its 2025 report and Addtech recognised SEK 66m, while an expert call indicated that Lifco uses no earn-outs.

Recent group margin direction is flat to slightly lower. Lagercrantz reported an 18.10% EBITA margin for 2025/26. H1 2026/27 came in at 17.40%, against 17.70% in H1 2025/26. The key evidence gap is disclosure, not accounting. Lagercrantz does not disclose standalone subsidiary margins after acquisition and reports only consolidated divisional figures. Individual earn-out targets and perdeal purchase prices are also not disclosed. Statutory filings (such as UK Companies House), expert calls and the full-year 2026/27 annual report are the most direct ways to close these gaps. Interpretation and outlook. The weight of evidence suggests that Lagercrantz buys at margins below its own group average and relies on post-acquisition development, plus a few very high-margin niches, to lift the group toward 20%. Earn-out gains are real but modest, so they do not explain the reported margin level. The unresolved issue is whether acquired margins rise, hold or decay after consolidation, and the one fully traced UK example points to decay in a construction-linked niche. A single statutory account cannot represent a portfolio of roughly 85 to 90 companies, so the answer remains provisional. This summary offers no investment recommendation and no valuation conclusion.
