The Pre-Mortem Memo

The question: it is February 2028 and the shares are at EUR 16.5, down 40% from EUR 27.5 (close of 13-Nov-2026). What are all the plausible reasons why? Five writers answered alone, each from one seat: the short-term trader, the short seller, the quarterly earnings speculator, the sector specialist and the long-term fundamental investor. This memo merges them.

Summary

  1. Verdict: a 40% fall is plausible but not the base case, at about 25% likely by February 2028. A fall of about 25%, to the 14 times earnings that the small grid-equipment companies trade on, is close to an even chance.
  2. The top three causes: the price-to-earnings multiple converges on the small-company grid group; scarcity pricing ends and the direct margin gives back 2 to 3 points; order growth slows below 10%.
  3. What they share: they are one exposure seen three ways. Both Arteche's earnings and its multiple rest on a shortage of grid equipment that can be delivered on time, and Arteche's moat protects its volume, not its price.
  4. So the causes arrive together and do not offset each other. A 40% fall needs lower earnings and a lower multiple at once, and only the end of scarcity delivers both.
  5. The market will see them first in peers, competitors' lead times and Arteche's own share price, months before Arteche's twice-yearly accounts. The first leg of that pattern, the share price lagging, is already in place.

How to read this memo

  • "Likelihood" is the chance the cause is visibly at work by February 2028. Causes overlap, so likelihoods do not add to 100%.
  • "Size" is how many of the 40 percentage points the cause would explain on its own. The rank is likelihood times size.
  • "Direct margin" is revenue less the direct costs of making the product (materials, direct labour, direct operating costs); it is where price and input costs show. Each point is about EUR 6.6m of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA), or EUR 0.09 of earnings per share (EPS) (00-core.md, Judgement 1).
  • "Book-to-bill" is new orders divided by revenue. Above 1.0 means the backlog is growing.

What this memo changes about how the case is watched

The earlier memo carries "What would change my view" lines in eleven sections. Three things change.

  • From inside to outside. Most of the earlier triggers are Arteche's own half-yearly numbers. This memo puts competitors, peers and the share price first, because they move first.
  • Triggers that cannot fire are dropped. Sections #4, #7, #8, #9 and #10 wait on a third-quarter trading update, backlog or order figure. Investor relations told Richard none is planned (Richard's notes).
