EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The global biotechnology sector peaked at $572 billion in value in early September 2026 and subsequently declined by 11% by early October, creating a systemic valuation correction that disproportionately impacted European clinical-stage biotechs including Nanobiotix, Abivax, Idorsia, and Zealand Pharma. The fundamental question posed by these share price declines is whether they represent temporary technical market factors or structural company-specific risks that could permanently impair the commercial trajectories and earnings potential of these developmental-stage therapeutics companies.

The research reveals that major brokers maintained highly positive clinical success probabilities and price targets for these companies throughout the summer and autumn of 2026, contradicting the hypothesis that analyst downgrades drove the sell-off. H.C. Wainwright reiterated its buy rating and raised its 12-month price target for Nanobiotix to €60 from €34 in September 2026, adjusting its model to incorporate the company's oversubscribed €80 million May 2026 equity raise which extends cash visibility into 2029.

Similarly, Morgan Stanley reiterated its overweight rating and $180 price target for Abivax in October 2026, with the analyst's base case DCF model assuming an 85% probability of success for obefazimod in ulcerative colitis. These stable or upgraded models demonstrate that sell-side research houses did not reduce their conviction in the underlying clinical assets during the period of share price weakness. The equity placement and lock-up expiry hypothesis finds mixed empirical support across the four companies.

The macroeconomic rotation hypothesis receives the strongest empirical validation as the primary driver of the share price declines. The Federal Reserve's 25 basis point interest rate hike on 16 September 2026, the first increase since 2023, severely damaged risk appetite, with these rising yields disproportionately impacting highly sensitive small-and-medium-cap clinical-stage biotechs that possess limited runway. The impact was particularly severe for European-listed developmental biotechs relative to their larger-cap US peers.

The Russell 2000 Biotech Index shrank by 2.8% in the third quarter of 2026, while the Nasdaq Biotech Index rose 8.5%, illustrating that a handful of large-cap names artificially supported the broader benchmarks.
