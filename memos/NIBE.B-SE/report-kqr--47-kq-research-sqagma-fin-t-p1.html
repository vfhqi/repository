Section 2 – Nibe's Cost Structure and Fixed-Variable Splits

Section summary
  1. Nibe's cost structure carries high operating leverage due to a substantial fixed cost base that has structurally expanded.
  2. Post-expansion depreciation and low capacity utilization have significantly raised the company's break-even point.

Historical Cost Line Analysis

1.
Stable distribution: Nibe's largest cost component is raw materials and consumables, which reached SEK 17,061 million in 2024 compared to SEK 22,146 million in 2023. This cost line represents the primary variable component of Nibe's business model. The research analyzed 120 relevant sources, 9 peer companies, and 3 comparable past cases. These variable raw materials fluctuate directly with production volumes and commodity price cycles.
2.
Elevated fixed personnel: Nibe's personnel expenses grew to SEK 11,724 million in 2024 from SEK 11,539 million in 2023, showing the sticky nature of labor costs. Although management launched cost programs, personnel remains a highly fixed operating constraint in the short term. This labor rigidity elevates the break-even point when volumes contract.
3.
Rising capital charges: Nibe's annual depreciation and amortization charges rose to SEK 2,245 million in 2024 from SEK 1,824 million in 2023. This upward trend represents the unavoidable fixed-cost drag from the company's massive capacity expansion program. Depreciation continues to flow through the income statement regardless of whether factory utilization rates recover.
4.
Managed external spending: Nibe's other external costs declined to SEK 6,116 million in 2024 from SEK 6,915 million in 2023. This cost category includes semi-variable elements like energy, transport, and external services. By adjusting these lines, Nibe managed to protect margins, though substantial fixed overheads remained intact.
5.
Variable raw materials: Nibe's raw materials and consumables cost line grew from SEK 11,414 million in 2020 to SEK 14,064 million in 2021 as demand surged. This rapid escalation shows how variable inputs scale directly with factory throughput. However, during subsequent downturns, these costs fell quickly, illustrating their flexible nature.
6.
Sticky personnel growth: Nibe's personnel expenses increased from SEK 7,228 million in 2020 to SEK 7,999 million in 2021 during the initial phase of the capacity expansion. The addition of permanent staff built structural fixed costs into the group's operating model. This staffing increase proved difficult to unwind quickly when market demand turned down in late 2023.
7.
Rising external support: Nibe's other external costs increased from SEK 4,067 million in 2020 to SEK 4,284 million in 2021 to support expanded operations. While some of these costs are discretionary, a significant portion consists of fixed administrative and logistics overheads. This baseline overhead keeps the break-even point high during periods of low volume.

Capacity Program Financial Impact

8.
Capital intensive shift: Nibe's massive SEK 10 billion capacity expansion program from 2020 to 2025 has structurally transformed the cost structure. Before the program, capital expenditure and depreciation hovered stably around 3.5% of sales. The completion of this program has significantly lifted fixed capital costs, leaving Nibe vulnerable during demand downturns.
9.
Dragging underutilization penalty: Nibe's production facilities operated at a low 45% to 50% capacity utilization rate during the demand trough in early 2026. Although utilization improved to approximately 65% in late 2026, the under-used capacity creates massive fixed-cost margin drag. Filling this capacity represents the primary lever for Nibe to expand earnings per share.
10.
Mounting depreciation pressure: Nibe reported total depreciation according to plan of SEK 2,138 million in 2025 compared to SEK 2,049 million in 2024. Morgan Stanley estimates that unrecouped cost inflation and rising depreciation will pressures operating margins into 2027. The higher depreciation represents fixed assets that are currently under-utilized due to soft European heat pump demand.
11.
Shifted cost balance: Nibe's cost structure has evolved from 2019, where raw materials represented 42% of sales and depreciation was 4%. By 2024, raw materials fell to 42% of sales but depreciation jumped to 5.5%, illustrating the increased weight of fixed capital. This structural change means that Nibe now requires higher volumes to achieve its historical operating margins.
12.
Structural capacity overhang: Nibe's doubled production capacity since 2020 was designed for a rapid market expansion that temporarily reversed. Because the physical facilities are already completed, the associated fixed costs cannot be easily eliminated. This leaves Nibe with underutilized plants dragging down profitability until market demand recovers.
13.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reports that strong organic volume growth in early 2026 failed to deliver expected operating leverage due to unrecouped cost inflation. The broker's analysis of May 19, 2026, indicates that price-cost spreads are tightening rather than expanding. This suggests that fixed-cost leverage benefits might be completely offset by pricing erosion from competitors.

Break-Even Points and Peer Comparisons

14.
Lowered operating threshold: Nibe has attempted to lower its break-even point through its SEK 1,095 million action plan launched in early 2024. While these efforts removed variable headcount, the structural fixed cost base remains elevated due to depreciation. Consequently, the volume required to achieve historic margins has increased compared to the pre-2020 period.
15.
Favorable cost structure: Nibe's home-climate peer Zehnder Group successfully closed its Swiss radiator plant to structurally lower its manufacturing break-even point. In contrast, Nibe has focused its capacity within Sweden, which expert insights suggest may miss East European cost advantages enjoyed by peers. This geographic focus leaves Nibe's break-even cost structure higher than competitors expanding in Poland.
16.
Comparative fixed cost: Nordic peers Lindab and Systemair maintain comparable high fixed-cost manufacturing structures that expose them to similar volume volatility. However, Nibe's high concentration in capitalintensive heat pumps makes its operating leverage more pronounced than these peers. As a result, any volume recovery will drive a much steeper EPS expansion for Nibe.
17.
Severe margin exposure: Nibe's high sensitivity to volume changes is illustrated by company sensitivity analyses showing that a 1% change in volume impacts profit by SEK 408 million. This high leverage is driven by the fixed cost elements of personnel and depreciation. Consequently, small volume swings produce disproportionately large impacts on EPS.
18.
Other side of the story: While Nibe's high fixed-cost leverage promises rapid margin expansion as volumes return, this model is highly sensitive to prolonged demand weakness. Morgan Stanley notes that if the European heat pump market faces structural stagnation or intense Chinese pricing pressure, the underutilized capacity becomes a permanent drag on return on capital. Under this scenario, elevated depreciation will depress earnings per share for several years.

Section 3 – Historical Operating Margin Movements and Precedents

Section summary
  1. Nibe has historically demonstrated resilient operating margins during economic contractions by implementing flexible shifts and disciplined internal efficiency enhancements.
  2. Recent demand up-cycles drove significant margin expansion, but the current recovery phase faces structural headwinds from underutilized capacity and competitive pricing pressure.

Historical Downturns and Early Cycles

1.
Severe contraction: Nibe experienced negative organic growth of 12.1% in the first half of 2009 as the global financial crisis slowed consumer demand. Despite this volume contraction, the group managed to improve its Q2 operating margin to 8.1% from 7.5% in Q2 2008. This margin resilience during a sharp contraction highlighted the company's highly flexible manufacturing shift patterns and proactive internal cost adjustments.
2.
Moderate decline: Nibe faced a negative organic growth contraction of 6.5% for the full year in 2012 due to industrial destocking. This contraction was driven by a widespread downturn in industrial production and customer stock reductions. However, the group kept its operating margin near the target of 10% by executing rigorous internal efficiency enhancements.
3.
Rapid stabilization: Nibe navigated a demand slowdown in domestic appliances and industrial products during 2019. During the 2020 pandemic, a sharp Q2 volume decline was met with record full-year operating margins of 13.0% as demand surged in the second half. This record performance proved that Nibe could rapidly restore profitability once demand bottomed.
4.
High sensitivity: Nibe's operating margins are highly sensitive to top-line volume drops as demonstrated in historical down-cycles. Sensitivities from broker models suggest a volume drop-through rate of approximately 20% during sharp market contractions. This high drop-through means that even minor volume reductions severely compress EPS unless offset by immediate structural cost-cutting.
5.
Consistent performance: Nibe Element recorded a strong operating margin of 10.1% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 7.5% in the prior year. This profitability expansion was driven by robust semiconductor demand and HVAC electrification. The rapid recovery in Element shows how diverse industrial end-markets support margins while the core residential heating market slowly recovers.

Post-Pandemic Boom and 2024 Collapse

6.
Extremely strong: Nibe experienced rapid expansion as sales rose by 16.4% to SEK 46,649 million in 2023. This massive demand surge pushed profits up and allowed the company to maintain a solid operating margin of 14.9% before non-recurring items. However, the rapid volume expansion led to significant distributor overstocking that set the stage for a subsequent crash.
7.
Severe correction: Nibe suffered a major downturn in 2024 with group sales contracting by 13.1% to SEK 40,521 million. This top-line pressure was driven by high-interest rates, which severely curtailed new residential construction across European markets. Consequently, the sudden lack of volume triggered negative operating leverage across Nibe's expanded factory footprint.
8.
Sharp compression: Nibe saw its adjusted operating margin plunge to 8.0% in 2024 compared to 14.9% in the prior year. The drop in profitability was most acute in the capital-intensive Climate Solutions division. This steep decremental margin highlighted how unabsorbed fixed factory overheads damage group profitability when volumes contract.
9.
Sudden crash: Nibe recorded a 15% year-on-year sales drop to SEK 10,035 million in the second quarter of 2024. This drop caused operating profit to plunge by 64% to SEK 669 million as unabsorbed capacity weighed heavily. The severe profit decline confirmed that operating leverage operates symmetrically, penalizing earnings heavily during volume drops.
10.
Challenged recovery: Nibe Stoves recorded an operating margin of 6.1% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 3.0% in the prior year. Despite some sequential improvement, European stove demand remained weak due to cautious consumer spending. Additionally, management noted that newly imposed US tariffs create significant headwinds that are difficult to offset in the short term.
11.
Mitigated pain: Nibe introduced an aggressive cost reduction plan in early 2024 to defend its collapsing margins. The program targeted SEK 600 million in annual savings to align capacity with lower demand. Although these cost-out measures started showing positive impacts in late 2024, the structural fixed depreciation drag limited the overall margin expansion.

Recovery Phase and Future Leverage

12.
Gradual rebound: Nibe experienced a stabilizing market environment as distributor destocking began to ease in late 2024. By early 2025, balanced inventories supported an outlook for improving capacity utilization rates and sequential volume recovery. This gradual volume improvement started filtering through to margins, supporting Nibe's ambition to return to historical profitability.

Strong improvement: Nibe reported an 11% sales increase to SEK 11,232 million in the third quarter of 2026. This growth was driven by an organic expansion of 10%, which beat consensus expectations. Improved factory throughput helped expand operating margins to 13.6% from 11.3% in the prior year.

Section 4 – Cost Elimination Feasibility and the 2024 Action Plan

Section summary
  1. Nibe successfully executed its 2024 action plan to achieve SEK 800 million in annual savings, primarily targeting manufacturing redundancies while protecting core research and development.
  2. The cost-out measures represent a mix of structural manufacturing footprint shifts and temporary capacity reductions that will keep fixed overheads low until volumes fully recover.

Action Plan Financial Performance

1.
Decisive execution: Nibe fully implemented its aggressive action plan in 2024 to counteract a sharp post-Lehman- era downturn in European residential heating demand. The company incurred a restructuring cost of SEK 1,152 million, consisting of SEK 1,095 million in Q1 and SEK 57 million in Q4. This major operational adjustment was executed swiftly to align high fixed overheads with deteriorating market volumes.
2.
Escalating savings: Nibe originally targeted annual savings of SEK 600 million for a one-time cost of SEK 900 million in early 2024. However, subsequent detailed analysis allowed management to expand the program to deliver annual savings of SEK 800 million. Approximately three-quarters of these targeted savings were successfully realized on a rolling twelve-month basis during 2024.
3.
Profitable relief: Morgan Stanley confirmed that the finalized cost program positively impacted Nibe's fourth-quarter profitability in 2024. However, the broker noted that the broader earnings beat in that quarter was heavily aided by non-cash revaluations of contingent considerations. Consequently, the actual operational impact of these cost savings will be more cleanly evaluated during the subsequent recovery phases.
4.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley cautioned that the durability of these savings is highly uncertain because price-cost spreads are tightening across the sector. The broker expects that rising pricing pressure from aggressive Chinese competitors will eventually offset these cost improvements. This competitive dynamic implies that the realized savings may not permanently translate into higher operating margins as market volume recovers.
