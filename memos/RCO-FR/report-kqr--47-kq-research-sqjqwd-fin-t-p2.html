Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The pricing power of Rémy Cointreau is under severe strain from competitive, regulatory, and structural headwinds.
  2. Industry oversupply and geographical concentration amplify these threats, directly risking the group's mid-term earnings trajectory.

Defining the

1.
Key Question Critical question: "how much pricing power does this company have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: a US cognac price war led by Hennessy (Hennessy VS prices reported down 12.7% since early 2023, Jefferies); industry ageing stocks reported at about ten years of depletions (Rothschild & Co Redburn); tequila price deflation as agave prices collapsed (tequila pricemix reported at -5.3%, Jefferies, November 2026); China's minimum import price undertaking for European brandy effective 5 July 2025 and the May 2025 ban on alcohol at official banquets; US tariffs on EU spirits; consumer down-trading and pack-size shifts (375ml formats growing while 750ml falls, Consumer Edge, November 2026); currency swings in the US dollar and renminbi; a polarised consumer, with ultra-premium resilient and mainstream squeezed; the poor 2026 French grape harvest?"
2.
Severe pressure: French distiller Rémy Cointreau faces unprecedented margin threats as its core Cognac division struggles with elevated inventories and discounting. With over 60% of total sales concentrated in the US and China, any prolonged deterioration in retail prices directly risks stripping out the group's operating leverage. Analysts warn that underperforming volumes combined with high promotional spends could dilute returns on capital to historical lows.
3.
Heightened risk: The ongoing pricing battle between major global houses directly impacts Rémy Cointreau's ability to maintain its high-end brand equity. An industry oversupply has pushed ageing stocks of eaux-de-vie to nearly ten years of depletions. If competitors continue to slash prices, Rémy Martin will either lose critical market share or be forced to follow.
4.
Uncertain outlook: Geopolitical trade tensions and regulatory developments in China present substantial earnings risks for European distillers over the next three years. Tariffs and government bans on alcohol consumption at official events have already depressed local consumer demand. Unless luxury demand recovers, margin progression will remain constrained.
