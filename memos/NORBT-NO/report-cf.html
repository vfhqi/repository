Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Norbit ASA exhibits several identifiable plausible problem to monitor when benchmarked against relevant peer medians and sector base rates, though the company's underlying fundamental position remains structurally sound. These signals primarily reflect operational friction points, structural vulnerabilities, and transient cost pressures rather than fundamental business model failures. The most material plausible problem to monitor concerns second-level management turnover, which has been notably elevated over the assessment period.

The company experienced a complete replacement of the Chief Technology Officer, Chief Commercial Officer for Connectivity, and Chief Operating Officer within a compressed twelve-month window during 2025, with the CCO role lasting only twelve months before replacement. While the CEO and CFO have maintained exceptional continuity since 2001 and 2020 respectively, this repeated churn at the operational execution layer introduces execution risk during a critical scaling phase, particularly as the company pursues aggressive NOK 6.0 billion organic revenue targets by 2030.

This pattern of mid-tier instability is materially worse than the stable executive benches observed at Nordic peers such as Kongsberg Gruppen and Kitron, and could signal cultural misalignment or strategic friction as the organization scales. [Cross-ref: Section 1] A second pronounced plausible problem to monitor emerges from customer concentration risk within the PIR contract manufacturing segment, where the five largest customers accounted for 59% of 2024 segment revenues.

This concentration exposes Norbit to material revenue volatility if any single defense or industrial client experiences budget cuts, program delays, or strategic pivots. The risk is amplified by the segment's rapid growth trajectory, with PIR expanding from NOK 85.5 million in Q3 2023 to NOK 367.1 million by Q2 2026, making it the largest revenue contributor while simultaneously diluting group gross margins from 65% down to 52%.

Management has explicitly acknowledged this concentration as a key 2030 strategic priority requiring active diversification, but current execution remains highly dependent on a narrow customer base compared to the more diversified revenue streams typical of pure-play EMS peers like AQ Group and Note. [Cross-ref: Section 8, Section 5] The third material plausible problem to monitor involves persistent gross margin compression driven by unfavorable segment mix shifts.

Norbit's consolidated gross margin deteriorated from 65% in Q1 2023 to 52% in Q2 2026, primarily reflecting the rapid scaling of lower-margin PIR defense manufacturing and memory chip cost inflation in Connectivity. While the company's blended EBIT margin of 25% remains significantly above the EMS peer median near 8%, the structural margin trajectory is negative and represents a clear departure from the high-margin profile that historically justified Norbit's valuation premium.

Connectivity gross margins specifically compressed from 64% to 55% year-on-year due to spiking memory chip prices driven by global data center demand, with no nearterm relief visible in semiconductor spot markets. This margin erosion stands in contrast to subsea sensing

A fourth plausible problem to monitor manifests in elevated inventory levels and working capital intensity, with management targeting working capital below 20% of revenues to improve return on capital employed. Strategic component stocking to mitigate semiconductor supply risks has resulted in elevated buffer inventories, creating higher working capital requirements compared to leaner EMS peers who operate with just-in-time procurement models.

While operating cash flow conversion reached an impressive 108% of EBITDA in Q2 2026, the absolute working capital base remains structurally higher than sector norms, constraining capital efficiency metrics and creating vulnerability to potential inventory obsolescence if technology cycles accelerate or customer demand shifts unexpectedly. This differs markedly from peers like Incap, which has actively released working capital through inventory rationalization programs. [Cross-ref: Section 7, Section 4] The fifth identifiable plausible problem to monitor concerns lumpy quarterly revenue recognition patterns driven by project-based order timing, particularly within the Oceans and PIR segments.

Large defense and security contracts introduce unpredictable quarterly volatility, with management acknowledging that short order book visibility limits forward projection precision. This lumpiness creates material quarterly earnings swings –evidenced by Q4 2025 revenues missing consensus by 7% due to postponed OBU deliveries–and complicates investor confidence in near-term delivery cadence. The pattern stands in contrast to peers with more recurring revenue models or longer-duration frame agreements that provide smoother quarterly profiles.

While the underlying demand fundamentals remain robust, the execution visibility gap represents a structural disadvantage relative to peers with more predictable order flows. [Cross-ref: Section 5, Section 3] A sixth plausible problem to monitor emerges from unhedged foreign currency exposure, with a substantial share of revenues denominated in EUR and USD while operating costs are largely NOK-based. A strengthening Norwegian Krone reduces reported growth and margins on currency translation, introducing material quarterly volatility independent of operational performance.

While management maintains monthly short-term currency rebalancing across trade receivables and payables, the company remains structurally exposed to medium-term FX shifts. Norne Securities explicitly highlights this as a near-term margin compression risk, particularly as recent NOK appreciation has created translation headwinds. This contrasts with multinational peers who maintain more sophisticated hedging programs or naturally balanced currency exposures through globally distributed cost bases. [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 10]

These seven plausible problem to monitor collectively represent material but manageable vulnerabilities rather than existential threats to the investment case. The company's strong CEO continuity, robust cash generation, and differentiated IP position in subsea sensing provide meaningful offsets. However, investors should monitor these signals closely, particularly second-level management stability, customer diversification progress in PIR, and gross margin trajectory as key leading indicators of execution quality and strategic delivery against the ambitious 2030 targets.

Outliers worth noting

Norbit's EBIT margin of 25% in Q2 2026 represents a genuine statistical outlier, positioned at the extreme top ~5% of comparable peers, significantly exceeding the Electronic Manufacturing Services peer median of 8% and the subsea sensing peer median of 22.4%. This premium profitability position stems from the company's unique hybrid model combining proprietary intellectual property ownership in the Oceans segment with robotized domestic manufacturing capabilities across all divisions [Cross-ref: Section 4, Section 7].

The company's pre-tax return on capital employed of 48% in Q2 2026 further reinforces this outlier status against the long-term sector target of over 30%, suggesting either temporary cyclical strength or a structurally advantaged business model that peers cannot easily replicate [Cross-ref: Section 4]. cash flow conversion reached 108% of EBITDA during Q2 2026, representing an unexplained operational oddity when benchmarked against typical industrial manufacturing norms where conversion ratios cluster between 80-95% of reported EBITDA.

This above-100% conversion phenomenon appears driven by favorable customer prepayment dynamics and aggressive working capital management [Cross-ref: Section 6], yet raises questions about the sustainability of such elevated conversion rates as the company scales toward its NOK 6.0 billion revenue ambition by 2030 [Cross-ref: Section 2]. Net interest-bearing debt to EBITDA stood at 0.7x in June 2026, reaching a comfortable pro forma 1.1x including the Water Linked acquisition, positioning Norbit at the bottom ~5% of leverage compared to the subsea sensing peer median of 1.4x [Cross-ref: Section 4].

This exceptionally conservative balance sheet appears inconsistent with the company's aggressive M&A strategy and NOK 250 million annual acquisition budget [Cross-ref: Section 2], suggesting either deliberate strategic restraint ahead of larger consolidation moves or an unexploited debt capacity that could accelerate inorganic growth beyond current execution patterns [Cross-ref: Section 4]. Structural gross margin degradation from 65% down to 52% over fourteen reporting periods represents a statistical outlier decline driven by high-volume manufacturing contract growth in PIR, which expanded from NOK 85.5 million in Q3 2023 to NOK 367.1 million in Q2 2026.

This rapid margin compression trajectory deviates sharply from peer Electronic Manufacturing Services companies, which typically maintain stable 15-20% gross margins across volume expansion cycles [Cross-ref: Section 5, Section 7]. The thirteen percentage point margin decline over such a compressed timeframe signals either a deliberate strategic pivot toward lower-margin defense manufacturing or an operational integration challenge that management has yet to fully address through pricing power or manufacturing efficiency gains [Cross-ref: Section 5].
