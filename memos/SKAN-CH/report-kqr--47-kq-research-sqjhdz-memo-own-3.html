SKAN Group AG – Post-Listing Operational Trajectory & Volatility IPO and Early Execution (October 2021) SKAN Group AG (SKAN-CH), a specialized Swiss provider of isolators and aseptic process systems for sterile drug manufacturing, went public on the SIX Swiss Exchange on October 28, 2021. The offering was priced at CHF 54 per share, at the upper end of its CHF 44 to 55 IPO range. Early market reception was bolstered by a highly robust order backlog of CHF 220.80 million (as of June 30, 2021) and a 33% year-over-year expansion in H1 2021 order intake to CHF 156.80 million, ensuring immediate visible pipeline execution.

FY 2024 Business Trends For the 2024 financial year, SKAN reported net sales of CHF 361.30 million, growing 12.90% year-over-year. While this top-line performance fell slightly short of original expectations due to postponed on-site project installations, profitability outperformed. The adjusted EBITDA margin reached 15.80% (EBITDA of CHF 57 million), exceeding management's guided target.

- Earnings Calls At this stage, the order backlog stood at CHF 318.30 million, supporting forward planning. In March 2025, management issued initial 2025 guidance targeting mid-teens sales growth and an EBITDA margin of 14% to 16%.

H1 2025 Underperformance and Record Orders During H1 2025, SKAN reported a 17.80% decline in net sales to CHF 135 million. This decline was driven by a high comparative base in H1 2024, when the company recorded extraordinary closed vial sales, and by significant project postponements. Consequently, net profit dipped into negative territory.

Despite the temporary earnings contraction, forward order metrics were highly robust: order intake grew 20.20% to CHF 213 million, pushing the order backlog to record levels of CHF 346 million (later rising to CHF 390 million). Backed by a book-to-bill ratio of 1.60x, management confidently reiterated its full-year mid-teens sales guidance at its August 2025 reporting.

46 The November 6, 2025 Profit Warning On November 6, 2025, SKAN issued a severe ad-hoc profit warning. The company stated that global demand for vaccine lines had softened, and a major GLP-1 project had been temporarily suspended during its design review. These issues shifted significant December revenues into 2026.

Accordingly, SKAN cut its FY 2025 guidance to an upper-single-digit sales decline and a low-double-digit EBITDA margin, causing the share price and forward P/E multiples to hit historic lows.

FY 2025 Results and H2 Catch-Up (March 2026) In March 2026, SKAN reported audited FY 2025 results that aligned with its revised guidance:

1.
Net Sales: Declined 7.70% to CHF 333.34 million (6.40% in constant currency).
2.
EBITDA: Fell 32.30% to CHF 38.60 million.
3.
EBITDA Margin: Settled at 11.60%, missing the initial 14% to 16% guidance range.
4.
H2 Recovery: A strong H2 2025 operational surge saw net sales expand by approximately 48% over H1, driven by a catch-up in standard sterility testing system shipments.
5.
Order Backlog: Closed at CHF 346.13 million (+8.70%), while total order intake rose 3.10% to CHF 370.60 million. Following this catch-up and under the leadership of incoming CEO Jonas Greutert (effective January 1, 2026), the share price began a sharp recovery rally. By November 23, 2026, SKAN’s share price had risen approximately 48% over the preceding 12 months, despite a parallel ~30% fall in the FactSet consensus 2027 EPS estimate. The mean broker target price rose 15% in the six months leading to November 2026, with the stock trading inside its 5- year 24-month-forward P/E range of 17.9x to 64.0x (FactSet).

Based on the precedents, several operational indicators help distinguish whether a multiple-led recovery rally will be sustained or reversed:

26.
Backlog Visibility: Sustained rallies typically featured high backlog-to-sales visibility (often >1.2x) and a solid book-to-bill ratio, which protected near-term revenues from project postponements (e.g., SKAN's record backlog of CHF 390 million and Lonza's strong pipeline ).
27.
Customer and Contract Concentration: Rallies that reversed were highly vulnerable to customer-specific concentration risks. For example, Stevanato collapsed because of a volume reduction from a single GLP-1 Nexa syringe customer, and Siegfried fell due to pending contract confirmations for a single product representing 2% to 3% of sales.
28.
Execution of Product Transitions: Companies that ran into product transitions without a sufficient sales bridge suffered sharp de-ratings. Chemometec's turnaround rally reversed because NC-200 instrument sales declined faster than the XM range could ramp up, creating a temporary product vacuum.
29.
Consensus Earnings Upgrades: Ultimately, the key differentiator was whether consensus estimates caught up with the multiple. In cases like Lonza and Repligen, multiple-led rallies were sustained because subsequent reporting dates delivered consecutive guidance upgrades, bringing forward-looking P/E multiples back to sustainable historical ranges.
