KERRY TRANSLATION EXPOSURE MECHANISMS

1.
Severe translation: Kerry Group faces significant currency translation risk because a major portion of its consolidated earnings is generated outside the eurozone. Annual report details from fiscal year 2025 confirm that developed markets represent the largest share of geographic exposure, with North America representing approximately 55% of global revenue. This high geographic concentration means that exchange rate fluctuations will heavily impact reported financial performance despite stable operations.
2.
High natural hedging: Kerry Group minimizes transaction foreign exchange risk through an extensive local operational footprint that aligns costs directly with regional sales. Financial analyses from Deutsche Bank highlight that roughly 96% to 97% of group revenues are naturally hedged, leaving translation to comprise over ninety-five percent of net sensitivity. This natural alignment isolates transaction headwinds to minor cross-border channels, verifying management claims regarding translation concentration.
3.
Substantial dilution: Kerry Group experienced severe headwinds in fiscal year 2025 as the US dollar and emerging- market currencies depreciated against the Euro. The average exchange rate of the US dollar moved to one point thirteen and the Brazilian real to six point thirty-one, cutting reported adjusted earnings per share growth by 4.5%. This significant translation drag restricts reported bottom-line expansion even when constant currency growth remains resilient at seven percent or more.
4.
Easing headwinds: Kerry Group anticipates a sequential reduction in currency headwinds during fiscal year 2026 based on updated currency spot rates. Management initially projected a translation drag of 4% in late 2025 but gradually reduced this outlook to a modest 1% headwind by October 2026. This sequential improvement helps stabilize reported adjusted earnings per share, aligning close to conservative consensus expectations of 511.7 cents.
