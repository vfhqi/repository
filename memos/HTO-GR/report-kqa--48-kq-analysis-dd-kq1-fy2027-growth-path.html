Defining the Key Question

1.
Defining the core inquiry: Hellenic Telecommunications Organization (OTE) (HTO-GR): Greece's incumbent fixed and mobile operator, majority-owned by Deutsche Telekom. After the third-quarter 2026 results and conference call of 30 October 2026, which path for OTE's 2027 adjusted EBITDA after leases (EBITDAaL) growth does the evidence best support, and what drives it? Competing hypotheses: (1) growth similar to the 2026 guidance of about 3%; (2) growth clearly below the 2026 rate, as the end of the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility (RRF) funding, domestic wholesale decline and cost inflation offset price rises; (3) flat or negative growth; (4) growth above the 2026 rate.
2.
Stable projection: Answer A posits that OTE will achieve an adjusted EBITDAaL growth of about 3% in fiscal year 2027 . This outcome assumes that the steady expansion of retail mobile and fiber services will maintain current momentum. Under this scenario, the company successfully balances its operational costs with tariff adjustments.
3.
Slowing performance: Answer B projects that OTE's adjusted EBITDAaL growth in 2027 will fall clearly below the 2026 rate . This scenario occurs if the termination of public sector funding and domestic wholesale contraction outpace retail gains. These pressures would prevent the company from sustaining its target profitability levels.
4.
Negative trajectory: Answer C describes a path where OTE experiences flat or negative growth in 2027. This hypothesis requires severe macroeconomic headwinds and aggressive price wars from infrastructure overbuilders. Such extreme competitive forces would directly compromise the underlying retail fixed and mobile service margins.
5.
Accelerating growth: Answer D suggests that OTE will deliver adjusted EBITDAaL growth in 2027 above the 2026 rate . This trajectory would depend on a faster-than-expected recovery in enterprise IT spending and strong postpaid migration. It also requires that energy costs decline faster than currently modeled by the market.

7.
Increasing competitive risk: Competitive dynamics in the Greek telecom market introduce key risks for OTE's long-term fixed and mobile business margins . 8.
Consistent historical execution: Analyzing OTE's historical performance reveals that management has consistently met or exceeded guidance targets.
