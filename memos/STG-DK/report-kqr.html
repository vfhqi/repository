Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The unresolved EU excise tax and product restrictions on modern oral nicotine pouches represent a major regulatory pivot that directly dictates the medium-term EPS trajectory of European listed tobacco manufacturers.
  2. Harmonized standards under TPD3 are expected to elevate compliance costs but ultimately consolidate market share toward highly compliant, scaled market leaders.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Unresolved directive: The Key Question is: "Since the Council withdrew the tobacco tax directive from the June 2026 Ecofin agenda for lack of unanimity, with Sweden opposing stricter pouch taxes, what do brokers, policy experts and the companies expect from the Irish Presidency on the pouch minimum tax (the May 2026 compromise text phased it from 10 percent of the retail price in 2028 to 50 percent from 2032), what will the 2027 drafts of the Tobacco Products Directive propose on pouch flavours, nicotine strength and packaging, and which large markets not yet settled, such as Italy, Spain and Poland, lean toward keeping pouches legal?"
2.
Regulatory stakes: Tighter modern oral pouch regulation through TPD3 could introduce severe product standards that materially threaten rapid volume growth if restrictive flavor bans are implemented EUwide.

Section 2 – The Irish Presidency and the Pouch Minimum Tax

Section summary
  1. The collapse of the June 2026 Ecofin consensus has delayed the harmonized pouch tax framework indefinitely under the Irish Presidency.
  2. Member states remain deeply polarized between risk-proportionate taxation advocated by Sweden and aggressive excise hikes pushed by France.
  3. Proponents of modern oral products expect the Irish Presidency to prioritize public health alignment over fiscal standardization.

Aftermath of the Swedish Veto

1.
Abrupt withdrawal: The European Council withdrew the proposed Tobacco Excise Directive revision from the June 2026 Ecofin agenda because Sweden exercised its national veto power to oppose stricter excise burdens on modern oral nicotine pouches.
2.
Direct rejection: The European Parliament subsequently voted down the Tobacco Excise Directive revision report on June 17, 2026, with an overwhelming 439 votes against and 181 in favor of the draft tax levels.
3.
Fragmented authority: Following these legislative blockages, Cyprus transferred the Presidency of the Council of the European Union to Ireland, which now assumes the difficult task of mediating subsequent tax negotiations.
4.
Postponed transition: Though the Commission initially targeted an implementation date of January 1, 2028, experts and brokers expect extended transitional periods of four years once an agreement is finally reached.
5.
Strategic priorities: British American Tobacco noted in its September 29, 2026 presentation that while Cyprus made progress on the Tobacco Excise Directive compromise, the Irish Presidency now holds the regulatory helm.
6.
Policy delays: Deutsche Bank Research noted on June 23, 2026, that Swedish opposition and parliamentary pushback mean the implementation of a revised tax directive remains highly uncertain and is likely delayed toward the decade end.

Debating the Excise Compromise

7.
Drastic proposals: The European Commission's initial July 2025 proposal aimed to tax modern oral pouches at a punitive minimum rate of €143 per kilogram by 2032, representing a massive tax increase that would severely penalize Swedish consumer prices.
8.
Mitigated suggestions: In response, the European Parliament's Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs advocated for a much lower pouch tax of €50 per kilogram to prevent a sudden consumer price shock.
9.
Phased implementation: Under the contested May 2026 compromise text, the European Council proposed phasing in the pouch excise starting from 10% of the retail price in 2028 and increasing to 50% by 2032.
10.
Economic rationale: Morgan Stanley observed on July 31, 2026, that a harmonized excise framework is critical because it dictates the long-term price gap between combustible cigarettes and less harmful smokeless categories.
11.
Industry expectations: Morgan Stanley asks whether tax harmonization will ultimately expand the legal category or conversely fuel illicit trade networks across high-tax jurisdictions.

Polarized Member State Positions

12.
Coalition resistance: Sweden leads a coalition of member states including Poland, Italy, Greece, Romania, and Bulgaria that actively defend risk-proportionate tax structures to encourage smoking cessation.
13.
Restrictive alliances: Conversely, a strict regulatory bloc consisting of France, Spain, Belgium, the Netherlands, and Ireland continues lobbying for high minimum tax levels and advertising bans.
14.
Unanimity barrier: Because any final EU-wide tax reform requires unanimous approval from all 27 finance ministers, any aggressive proposal is highly vulnerable to repeated vetoes by harm-reduction states. Other side of the story: While risk-proportionate advocates seek to delay tax harmonisation, a prolonged absence of EU-wide standards perpetuates market fragmentation and leaves operators vulnerable to unpredictable national tax shocks.
