Section summary
  1. Siemens Healthineers AG faces critical non-operational headwinds and portfolio separations that will heavily influence its near-term earnings base.

How brokers define adjusted EPS (FactSet brokers' basis FY2026 EUR 2.42) and what clean base they use?"

Financial baseline and growth mechanics

2.
Adjusted baseline: Siemens Healthineers reported an adjusted EPS of EUR 2.42 for FY2026, but this figure was significantly inflated by non-recurring benefits. Management explicitly stated that stripping out EUR 0.16 of tariff refunds and other material fourth-quarter one-offs yields a clean base of approximately EUR 2.30. Identifying this clean operational starting point is critical for forecasting true organic growth because it prevents non-operational noise from distorting future earnings trajectory.
3.
Rising headwinds: Siemens Healthineers faces severe below-the-line pressures including higher interest expenses and tax rate normalization. Management expects net financial expenses to increase to between EUR 400m and EUR 440m in FY2027 as intercompany debt is refinanced. These factors combined with a tax rate normalization to 24% to 26% create significant non-operating earnings headwinds that mask strong underlying operational momentum.
4.
Delayed offsets: Siemens Healthineers experiences persistent input cost inflation, particularly from memory chip prices, alongside continuing gross tariff exposures. While management targets cost-savings and pricing initiatives to offset these headwinds, a cost and mitigation timing mismatch will pressure FY2027 profitability. This near-term mismatch suppresses adjusted margins before the productivity gains fully materialize in FY2028.
5.
Structural separation: Siemens Healthineers has initiated the legal carve-out of its underperforming Diagnostics segment, rebranded as Olyrian Diagnostics, with standalone execution targeted by mid-calendar year 2028. This strategic separation aims to create a pure-play diagnostics business while removing a long-standing growth drag from the synergetic core. However, the carve-out process introduces substantial stranded costs and brand transition fees that initially weigh on the group's financial metrics.

Management's deliberate caution has resulted in a flat to declining revenue growth assumption for China in the synergetic core. This prudent framework helps insulate the near-term guidance from unexpected policy shifts but increases reliance on pricing power in other regions.

Section 3 – Tariff Exposures, Refunds, and Supply-Chain Inflation

Section summary
  1. Siemens Healthineers faces transitional tariff pressures and supply-chain inflation before mitigation efforts fully offset these headwinds.
  2. Competitors encounter similar supply-chain and tariff dynamics, though structural advantages vary across the medical technology sector.

Tariff Impacts and Refund Dynamics

1.
Significant relief: Siemens Healthineers experienced a notable earnings boost in fiscal year 2026 from regulatory refunds. The company received refunds for paid U.S. IEEPA tariffs that added approximately EUR 0.15 per share to its adjusted earnings. This one-off benefit inflated the reported adjusted EPS of EUR 2.42, establishing a clean operational baseline of EUR 2.30. Management does not expect any further material tariff refunds to recur in the upcoming fiscal years.
2.
Projected headwinds: Siemens Healthineers faces ongoing gross tariff exposures that require active strategic countermeasures. The company guides for a gross tariff headwind of EUR 0.10 per share in fiscal year 2027. To counteract this drag, management plans to achieve full tariff mitigation by fiscal year 2028 through pricing and cost-saving programs. This mitigation trajectory is back-end loaded, meaning near-term margins will remain under pressure.
3.
Balanced offsets: Siemens Healthineers is implementing a dual-track strategy to neutralize the financial impact of trade barriers. The expected tariff mitigation of EUR 0.10 per share is split roughly equally between internal cost productivity and market-adaptive pricing. While price increases help protect gross margins, there is a pronounced lag in backlog conversion before these adjustments flow to the bottom line. This timing mismatch means operational pressure will persist throughout the transition period.
4.
Unresolved risks: Siemens Healthineers remains exposed to potential regulatory changes under pending US trade investigations. The US Department of Commerce initiated a Section 232 investigation into imports of medical equipment, creating industry uncertainty. Management has stated that they cannot reasonably estimate potential Section 232 tariff impacts or timing. However, the company believes its global manufacturing footprint provides sufficient flexibility to shift production if needed.
