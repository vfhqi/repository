Plausible problems to monitor

Ferretti's three-year revenue growth CAGR of 7.5% materially lags Sanlorenzo's 13.0% CAGR, indicating that the group is capturing a smaller share of structural luxury yachting market expansion despite commanding a larger absolute market share position. Operating margin faces downward pressure from lower fixed-cost absorption in H1 2026, suggesting that the recently expanded Ravenna capacity is not yet achieving its intended manufacturing efficiencies during a period of lengthened customer decision cycles. This growth underperformance becomes particularly concerning when contextualized against Sanlorenzo's superior EBITDA margin of 18.8% in FY2025 compared to Ferretti's 16.5%, revealing a dual handicap in both topline momentum and profitability optimization relative to the closest listed Italian peer. The combination of slower revenue expansion and margin compression points to structural operational leverage challenges that require management attention before the next cyclical downturn materializes. [Reference: Section 2]

Ferretti's Middle East and Africa new yacht revenue contracted 25.5% year-over-year to EUR 163.8 million in H1 2026, representing a severe regional performance deterioration that far exceeds normal quarterly volatility patterns observed across other luxury goods categories. Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East cause delayed order conversions and postponed delivery schedules for larger vessels, introducing material backlog conversion risk across the high-margin Made-to-Measure segment that historically contributed disproportionate profitability.

This regional concentration weakness is amplified by the Middle East and Africa region contributing 28% of H1 2026 new yacht revenue, meaning nearly one-third of the business faces acute near-term headwinds that cannot be rapidly offset by other geographies given the multi-month production lead times inherent to luxury yacht manufacturing. The magnitude of this contraction substantially exceeds typical luxury sector regional volatility and signals deeper structural order pipeline fragility. [Reference: Section 3] Rolling book-to-bill fell below 1x in Q1 2026 reaching approximately 0.9x including composite yachts, marking a critical inflection point where new order intake no longer replaces delivered revenue at parity levels and foreshadowing potential backlog erosion over the next twelve to eighteen months.

Made-to-Measure and Super Yachts segments experienced book-to-bill compression to approximately 0.8x in early 2026, revealing that the weakness is disproportionately concentrated in the highest-margin product categories where customer switching costs and customization lock-in should theoretically provide the strongest demand insulation. This deterioration is particularly alarming because customer decision cycles have structurally lengthened due to macroeconomic caution and Middle East geopolitical tensions, suggesting the sub-parity book-to-bill reflects not merely timing delays but genuine demand hesitation among the ultra-high-net-worth target demographic.

The compression to 0.8x in premium segments falls materially below the 1.0x replacement rate required to sustain current revenue trajectories and implies potential revenue growth deceleration extending into fiscal year 2027.
