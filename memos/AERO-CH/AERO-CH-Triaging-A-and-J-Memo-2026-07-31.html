B.1 Business Description 10 findings
Business Description -- A plain-English description of what the company does, how it makes money, and its primary market.
1.
What it does. Montana Aerospace AG designs and manufactures highly engineered structural components and complex assemblies for commercial and defense aviation, in aluminum, titanium and composite materials, listed on the SIX Swiss Exchange since 12-May-2021
2.
Core segment. Aerostructures is now the only material growth segment, covering fuselage, wing, landing-gear and engine structural content, plus a small residual Alpine Metal Tech industrial unit re-acquired Aug-2024 that is currently loss-making at the EBITDA line
3.
Pure-play transformation complete. The group divested its E-Mobility unit (Alu Menziken) in Nov-2024 and its Energy unit (ASTA Group) in Sep-2025, eliminating the conglomerate discount that weighed on the equity story for years, per the company's own investor materials
4.
Scale. FY25 revenue of EUR 979.3m, up 15.1% year on year, against a market capitalisation of roughly CHF 1.6bn as of 31-Mar-2026, small enough that institutional liquidity constraints are a live consideration
5.
Margin character. Group adjusted EBITDA margin 16.5% and Aerostructures segment margin 18.2% in Q1 2026, both well above the 10 to 12% traditional peer range, driven by deep vertical integration across recycling, casting, extrusion, machining and assembly
6.
Balance sheet. Net debt fell from over 2.0x EBITDA at its prior peak to 0.4x at Q1 2026, with a net cash target by year-end 2026, a genuine and fast deleveraging trajectory by industrial standards
7.
Manufacturing footprint. 16 locations across four continents, where best-cost hubs in Romania and Vietnam handle machining and assembly while Belgium, the United States and Germany hold precision, certification-heavy work close to key customers
8.
Customer mix. Roughly 40% Airbus, 40% Boeing, 20% defence, space and other programmes, with the two largest customers accounting for a combined 56% of Aerostructures net sales in Q1 2026, a genuine concentration risk
9.
Investment style implication. This is a corporate-change / turnaround-into-quality case, not an established quality compounder: the change-from-current story (divestments, deleveraging, vertical-integration margin ramp) is doing most of the analytical work, ahead of a business-quality read that is itself still developing
10.
Defence exposure caveat. Despite the universe's "F. Defence" industry classification, defence programmes (F-35, A400M) are under 10% of the Aerostructures backlog, the dominant driver is the commercial aerospace build-rate recovery, not defence spending, a classification nuance worth flagging explicitly
