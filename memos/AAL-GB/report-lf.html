EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Duncan Wanblad brings a deeply technical and internally loyal background with normal standards of management execution, having ascended through over thirty years of international mining experience spent entirely within Anglo American and its subsidiaries before his appointment as chief executive in April 2022.

The chief executive has accelerated the transition toward copper while delivering portfolio simplification that refocused the business on future-enabling commodities, completing the demerger of Valterra Platinum in May 2025 and agreeing the sale of Steelmaking Coal to Dhilmar for up to $3.875 billion in cash in May 2026.

However, the decision to slow Woodsmith development pace to support balance sheet deleveraging resulted in a second impairment of $1.6 billion on Woodsmith in July 2024 following 2022's writedown, with Woodsmith capital expenditure restricted to $250 million annually for 2026 and 2027 to preserve cash.

Previous tenures show normal standards of operational delivery with a strong asset restructuring focus, though he left prior roles before long-term project results like Quellaveco copper were fully realized, with major copper developments completed under subsequent management tenures rather than during his direct leadership.

Strategic Execution and Delivery Record

Wanblad has prioritized cash preservation through disciplined capital cuts, progressing the landmark merger with Teck Resources with expected completion between September 2026 and March 2027 to deliver annual synergies of $800 million by the fourth year post-merger.

Operational stability was maintained at Quellaveco and Collahuasi mines during the transitional phase of portfolio restructuring, while the Group achieved the lowest recorded injury frequency rate in the company's operational history during 2025, though expressing deep sadness over two fatal work-related incidents in Brazil and Zimbabwe.

Capital allocation remains disciplined, with the Group reducing total planned 2026 capital expenditure by $0.4 billion to support balance sheet flexibility while paying dividends of 40% of underlying earnings, delivering a $0.23 per share interim dividend in H1 2026.

The Board deferred the final investment decision on Woodsmith until 2028 subject to feasibility studies and partner syndication, evaluating potential equity syndication of 25% interest in Woodsmith with long-term partner Mitsubishi Corporation.

However, Wanblad's delivery was heavily inherited from previous management's long-term asset configurations and timing rather than purely his own work, with portfolio simplification accelerated under hostile activist pressure following opportunistic buyout bids from rival BHP Billiton.

Leadership Style and Market Perception

Wanblad demonstrates normal standards of leadership, combining realistic operational guidance with a clear, direct communication style and utilizes plain-spoken and transparent language during earnings calls, addressing complex divestment steps and market challenges openly.

The chief executive demonstrates an owner-like mindset by prioritizing cash returns to shareholders through a planned $4.5 billion special dividend while avoiding empire-building by deferring long-dated development projects like Woodsmith to preserve balance sheet liquidity.

Market perception of the chief executive's capability is normal, with positive shifts following strategic portfolio restructuring and copperled growth alignment, as research identifies four brokers and ten named analysts covering Anglo American's strategic transformation with Jefferies reiterating a buy rating with a price target of 5,000p.

Analysts view the proposed Teck merger as a highly credible step that creates a copper leader with strong growth potential, though skeptical experts suggest that strategic portfolio restructuring remains reactive and primarily driven by hostile takeover defense, with demergers accelerated under activist pressure rather than being a proactive management initiative.

Governance and Financial Oversight

Anglo American maintains high governance standards and clean board independence, with the current board consisting of ten active and highly experienced directors representing a balanced mix of executive and nonexecutive leadership, with no non-executive director exceeding nine years of service and female directors representing 40% of the active board.

The audit committee is composed of four fully independent directors led by Hilary Maxson, the remuneration committee comprises three independent non-executive directors under Senior Independent Director Ian Tyler, and the nomination committee is dominated by five independent nonexecutive directors chaired by non-independent Chairman Stuart Chambers.

CFO John Heasley has maintained normal standards of financial governance with strong capital discipline that successfully deleveraged the balance sheet, with net debt reduced to $8.2 billion in the first half of 2026 achieving a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 1.0x by mid-2026.

Executive pay is partly aligned with shareholder value as the 2026 LTIP awards link 40% of vesting to relative Total Shareholder Return and 40% to Return on Capital Employed, with annual bonus awards applying a safety-deductor mechanism that reduces payouts if workplace fatalities occur, though the 2023 LTIP vested at just 21% of maximum reflecting weak historical returns.

However, the ROCE performance metric excludes capital from Woodsmith, which could shield management from major project overruns, and formally rigorous targets are undermined by the committee's discretion to adjust LTIP payouts if formulaic outcomes do not reflect underlying performance.

Cultural Signals and Expert Discrepancies

Anglo American's culture is mixed as rising employee engagement initiatives are offset by massive headcount reductions, with voluntary turnover remaining stable at 4.2% in 2025 but the company reducing its headcount to 43,000 in 2025 down from 54,000 in 2024, driven by the Valterra Platinum demerger which completed in May 2025.

Leadership is restructuring regional hubs to drive local operational accountability where on-the-ground employees make key decisions, though the upcoming Teck merger will move the corporate headquarters to Canada causing organizational uncertainty with the London corporate office cut back significantly from its prior 700 employees, and former executives state that Anglo American moves relatively slowly due to deep-seated bureaucracy.

Share dealing at Anglo American represents a balance of routine transactions with no strong directional conviction over the past year, as executive directors regularly acquire negligible share volumes through structured all-employee share incentive plans and dividend reinvestments, with the complete absence of material open-market purchases suggesting that directors lack true individual confidence in near-term commodity cycles.

External commentary indicates mixed perceptions of management credibility, with peers praising strategic copper and iron ore moves but criticizing operational reliability, as BHP abandoned its takeover attempt citing strong strategic merits but chose to focus on its own organic growth options, while former Rio Tinto advisors note Anglo lacks the sheer volume velocity of Western Australian iron ore operations run by BHP and Rio Tinto.

Single Largest Risk to Management Credibility

The single largest risk to management credibility is operational disruptions and execution failures that have fractured key transactional relationships and delayed strategic divestments, as Peabody Energy terminated its acquisition of Queensland coal assets citing longwall production disruptions at the offline Moranbah North mine, while the European Commission issued objections to MMG's nickel acquisition due to concerns over ferronickel supply diversion from European steelmakers, and environmental regulators temporarily suspended Collahuasi's desalination permit in mid-2026.

Independent experts express strong skepticism regarding the commercial feasibility and premium pricing assumptions of the slowed Woodsmith polyhalite project, with a BHP strategist warning that Anglo's polyhalite is a very unique, highly niche product compared to widely known standard potash which limits broad agricultural customer adoption compared to standard crop nutrients, while former procurement managers argue that loose capital procedures create massive capital cost overruns threatening single-digit project returns that disadvantage high-return copper developments in favor of legacy iron ore.

This operational execution weakness, combined with the defensive nature of the portfolio restructuring that was triggered by hostile buyout pressure rather than being an entirely proactive, long-planned strategy, creates significant uncertainty around management's ability to independently deliver transformational strategic outcomes without external forcing mechanisms.
