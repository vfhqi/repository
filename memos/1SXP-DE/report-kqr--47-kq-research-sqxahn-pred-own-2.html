Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

How are Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk allocating primary packaging formats and suppliers for their GLP-1 and nextgeneration obesity drugs in 2027 and 2028: glass prefillable syringes, single-dose vials, multi-dose pen cartridges, autoinjectors and oral forms? Which customer and product lay behind Stevanato Group's Nexa syringe volume cut announced on 3-Nov-2026, does SCHOTT Pharma supply the same programmes, and what share of SCHOTT Pharma's GLP-1 revenue is in syringes against vials and cartridges?

This question sits at the heart of understanding SCHOTT Pharma's near-term earnings trajectory and its ability to sustain premium valuation multiples in the face of structural shifts within the obesity therapeutics market. The allocation decisions made by Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk directly determine which glass packaging suppliers– SCHOTT Pharma, Stevanato Group, or Gerresheimer –will capture the most lucrative share of the high-growth GLP-1 revenue pool over the next twenty-four months.

Because these two pharmaceutical giants control virtually the entire commercial market for injectable incretin therapies, their procurement strategies, format preferences, and supplier diversification programs represent the single most important external variable driving revenue visibility for primary packaging manufacturers. The precision of the answer matters because SCHOTT Pharma's valuation rests on the assumption that its obesity-related revenues will continue to expand at double-digit rates through fiscal year 2028, supported by long-term supply agreements with both major drugmakers.

If the evidence reveals that Eli Lilly or Novo Nordisk are accelerating their transitions toward multi-dose pen cartridges, oral formulations, or captive manufacturing capacity, then the growth runway for third-party glass syringe suppliers contracts sharply. Conversely, if the data confirms that both pharma companies remain committed to outsourcing prefillable glass syringes and singledose vials through existing partnerships, then SCHOTT Pharma's earnings per share growth remains well supported by contracted volumes and premium margin profiles.

The Stevanato Group Nexa syringe volume cut announced on November 3, 2026, provides a critical datapoint for understanding whether demand volatility is isolated to a single supplier or represents a broader industry recalibration. If this volume reduction stems from Novo Nordisk reducing its single-dose syringe forecasts in favor of multi-dose pens or oral tablets, then the implications extend beyond Stevanato to any packaging supplier heavily exposed to the same programs.

Determining whether SCHOTT Pharma supplies the same customer programs affected by this cut is essential for assessing whether the German manufacturer faces similar near-term revenue headwinds or whether its dual-customer diversification strategy insulates it from the downside that devastated Stevanato's stock price by twenty-eight percent in a single trading session. Understanding the format mix within SCHOTT Pharma's GLP-1 portfolio–specifically the split between syringes, vials, and cartridges –directly determines the company's margin expansion potential and operating leverage.

Prefillable glass syringes account for approximately 70% of SCHOTT Pharma's total GLP-1 revenue opportunity, while the remaining 30% is generated from high-value ready-to-use sterile cartridges for multi-dose pens. These two formats carry vastly different margin profiles, with syringes commanding EBITDA margins exceeding thirty-five percent and cartridges facing greater pricing pressure due to volume discounting for large pharmaceutical customers.

If the obesity market shifts more aggressively toward multi-dose delivery systems, SCHOTT Pharma's product mix would tilt toward cartridges, which could compress overall profitability despite rising absolute volumes.

The evolution of SCHOTT Pharma's earnings per share through 2027 and 2028 hinges on three mechanical drivers that flow directly from the answers to this Key Question. First, the absolute volume of glass units shipped to obesity programs determines top-line revenue growth, which analysts currently model at mid-to-high single digits organically. Second, the format mix within that volume determines the gross and EBITDA margin profiles, with high-value syringes delivering superior profitability compared to standard cartridges or bulk vials.

Third, the capital utilization rate of recently commissioned syringe and cartridge capacity determines the speed at which SCHOTT Pharma converts its heavy capital expenditure program into incremental free cash flow and bottom-line earnings. If the evidence confirms that Eli Lilly remains committed to outsourcing glass syringes through its diversified triple-supplier strategy and that Novo Nordisk's volume adjustments are isolated to Stevanato, then SCHOTT Pharma's earnings trajectory remains intact with projected adjusted EPS expanding from €0.98 in fiscal year 2026 to €1.18 by fiscal year 2028.

However, if the data reveals that both pharmaceutical sponsors are accelerating their pivot toward captive fill-finish capacity, oral formulations, or alternative delivery platforms, then consensus earnings estimates will require downward revision, and SCHOTT Pharma's valuation multiple will compress as investors reprice the durability of its obesity-driven growth narrative. The valuation implications are particularly acute because SCHOTT Pharma trades at a premium multiple relative to peers like Gerresheimer and Stevanato Group, justified by its superior margin profile and assumed insulation from single-customer concentration risks.

Any evidence that undermines these two pillars–whether through margin compression from unfavorable format mix shifts or through revenue volatility from shared customer program cuts –would trigger a re-rating of the stock toward peer multiples. Conversely, confirmation that SCHOTT Pharma maintains balanced dual-customer exposure and continues to capture the highest-margin syringe volumes would support its premium valuation and provide a clear path to multiple expansion as earnings visibility extends through the decade.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

The current market consensus assumes a gradual evolution of GLP-1 packaging formats through 2028, with injectable delivery systems maintaining dominance and suppliers like SCHOTT Pharma, Gerresheimer, and Stevanato Group capturing predictable revenue streams from multi-year contracts. However, if the actual packaging allocation and supplier strategy diverges materially from these baseline assumptions, the earnings consequences for primary packaging manufacturers would be severe and asymmetric.

The most consequential divergence scenarios involve an accelerated transition to oral formulations, abrupt dual-sourcing reconfigurations by pharmaceutical originators, or unexpected capacity internalization by Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk that displaces external suppliers faster than anticipated. The most structurally disruptive scenario involves oral obesity prescriptions accelerating significantly from 2027 onward, with oral options like Wegovy pills and orforglipron potentially capturing up to a 50% share of prescriptions by 2028, up from zero in 2025, which would compress the demand for sterile glass syringe systems.

If this transition materializes twelve to eighteen months ahead of current market models, SCHOTT Pharma would face immediate underutilization of its recently commissioned glass prefillable syringe and sterile cartridge capacity. If oral peptide treatments capture a significant share of the weight-loss market by 2028, the demand for prefilled glass syringes and sterile cartridges will experience a sharp contraction, leaving newly installed glass capacity underutilized and depressing the group's profit margins.

This outcome would be particularly damaging because SCHOTT Pharma's €700 million lifetime syringe contract and aggressive capital expenditure program of €140 million to €160 million are predicated on sustained injectable volume growth through the end of the decade. The earnings per share trajectory currently projected to expand from €0.98 in fiscal 2026 to €1.18 by fiscal 2028 would collapse if oral adoption undermines utilization rates before the company recovers its invested capital. The second major divergence risk centers on sudden dual-sourcing reconfigurations by Eli Lilly or Novo Nordisk that reallocate contracted volumes away from incumbent suppliers. During the pandemic, companies signed large reservation contracts with multiple suppliers to secure packaging capacity, but actual volumes did not always match initial contract frames once demand normalized, indicating that contracted volumes carry execution risks for suppliers and long-term commitments can overstate actual mid-term packaging demand.

If either pharmaceutical originator determines that its captive fill-finish capacity from the Catalent acquisition or new Pennsylvania and Ohio sites can absorb a larger share of commercial demand, the company could reduce outsourced glass volumes by 30% to 40% within a single annual planning cycle. Aggressive captive expansion by pharmaceutical sponsors represents a major risk for contract manufacturers, and if Novo Nordisk continues to build internal peptide and oral capacities beyond current plans, external supplier utilization could fall and depress glass supplier earnings by 2028.

This scenario would hit Stevanato Group hardest given its concentrated exposure to Novo Nordisk, but SCHOTT Pharma's two-thirds dependence on Eli Lilly means any unexpected insourcing decision by the Indianapolis-based drugmaker would trigger a double-digit revenue miss and force painful capacity write-downs. The entities possessing unilateral power to disrupt the status quo are Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk, which control product format decisions, supplier allocations, and the pace of oral formulation adoption.

West Pharmaceutical Services occupies a secondary but still consequential position as the dominant elastomer supplier, since any decision by West to integrate downstream into glass manufacturing or establish exclusive partnerships with either pharmaceutical originator would reshape competitive dynamics across the supply chain. Conversely, packaging suppliers like SCHOTT Pharma, Gerresheimer, and Stevanato Group have limited ability to influence format choices or contractual volumes once initial agreements are signed, leaving them structurally exposed to customer-driven demand volatility.

Early signals of potential divergence are already visible but remain underappreciated by consensus forecasts. Stevanato Group reported a decline in year-over-year GLP-1 packaging sales during 2026 and expects these therapies to become a high-single-digit growth headwind in 2027, driven by lower demand for Nexa syringes from a single customer, proving that individual supplier volume allocations can decline unexpectedly and revealing that the GLP-1 packaging market is susceptible to sudden downside revisions.

This abrupt volume cut, which eliminated 300 million units of expected 2027 demand, demonstrates how quickly pharmaceutical originators can adjust procurement forecasts during routine budgeting cycles. Novo Nordisk revised its syringe procurement down because of a supply and demand imbalance, inventory adjustments, and market share losses to Eli Lilly, and is seeing faster growth in its multi-dose pen formulations compared to single-dose prefilled syringes which require more glass units, directly compressing the demand for high-value glass syringes.

This shift toward multi-dose pen cartridges reduces the total number of glass units required per patient treatment, creating a structural headwind for syringe-focused suppliers even if overall patient volumes continue expanding. Another overlooked signal involves the pricing pressure emerging in high-value cartridge markets. Ready-to-use cartridges and syringes face unique competitive pricing dynamics, and high-speed cartridges face greater pricing pressure because of volume discounts for large customers, meaning margins are a step-down from top-of-theline ready-to-use vials. If Eli Lilly or Novo Nordisk leverage their dominant market positions to demand 10% to 15% annual price concessions on contracted packaging volumes, the superior EBITDA margins of 35% to 45% currently enjoyed by SCHOTT Pharma and peers would compress toward the mid-twenties range, eliminating the financial advantage of high-value solution specialization.

This margin erosion would be masked initially by volume growth but would become undeniable once oral adoption decelerates injectable demand after 2028. The most subtle but potentially decisive divergence signal lies in glass overcapacity projections that exceed current market awareness. Overcapacity in the global glass syringe market is projected to reach twenty percent by 2030, and significant capacity expansions by major glass players coincide with conservative demand from pharma companies adopting oral formulations.

If this overcapacity threshold is breached two years earlier than anticipated, the resulting pricing environment would shift bargaining power decisively toward pharmaceutical buyers and trigger margin compression across all glass suppliers regardless of their current contract protections. SCHOTT Pharma's vertical integration advantage through its parent company's raw glass tube supply would provide only modest insulation in this scenario, since the bottleneck would shift from raw material access to finished product demand.

The knock-on consequences of these divergence scenarios extend beyond immediate revenue misses to include permanent valuation multiple compression for publicly traded packaging suppliers. If SCHOTT Pharma's GLP-1 revenue growth stalls or reverses in fiscal 2028 rather than continuing its projected trajectory, the equity market would reassess the company's long-term earnings power and re-rate its shares from a growth story to a cyclical industrial with substantially lower price-to-earnings multiples.

The company's current valuation assumes that high-value solution capacity expansions will generate attractive returns on invested capital, but any scenario that leaves this capacity underutilized would destroy shareholder value through stranded asset write-downs and reduced return on equity. The severity of this outcome would be amplified by SCHOTT Pharma's concentrated customer base, since a single adverse procurement decision by Eli Lilly could eliminate more than €100 million in annual revenues within eighteen months.

Ultimately, the current market view rests on the assumption that injectable GLP-1 delivery formats will evolve gradually and that long-term supplier contracts provide meaningful revenue visibility through 2028. If this assumption proves incorrect and oral adoption, capacity internalization, or dual-sourcing reconfigurations accelerate beyond consensus expectations, the financial impact on SCHOTT Pharma and its peers would be immediate, material, and difficult to reverse through operational adjustments alone.

Investors currently underestimate the binary nature of these risks because recent volume growth has masked the structural vulnerabilities embedded in the supplier business model, but the Stevanato Nexa syringe cut provides a clear precedent for how quickly the earnings outlook can deteriorate when pharmaceutical originators reassess their packaging requirements.
