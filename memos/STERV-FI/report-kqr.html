Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Stora Enso's H1 2027 demerger of its Swedish forest holdings represents a critical test of whether public markets will fully value forestry assets.
  2. The transaction could reshape European forestry valuations by establishing a new pure-play benchmark and forcing peers to defend their integrated models.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Strategic inquiry: "Stora Enso plans to list its Swedish forests as Bergslagets Skogar in the first half of 2027, with forest value given as about EUR 5.8 billion and EUR 0.8 billion to EUR 1.3 billion of debt to move with it. What are the expected terms: will shareholders receive the shares, will Stora Enso keep a stake, how much debt moves, and what dividend policy is planned? At what discount or premium to forest value do brokers expect a listed Swedish forest company to trade, compared with private forest land transactions? Do brokers expect that listed price to narrow the discount at which SCA (0.7 times book value) and Holmen trade, and have SCA's or Holmen's managements or owners commented on separating their own forests?"
2.
Significant re-rating of Stora Enso hinges on whether the H1 2027 demerger can successfully highlight the underlying value of forest assets that are currently obscured by highly cyclical packaging operations. Jefferies notes that the strategic forest review has raised the absolute valuation floor for the stock, which historically depressed Stora Enso's earnings multiples during sector downturns. If public markets reward this standalone carve-out, the transaction could support future earnings per share growth through significantly reduced interest expenses and a right-sized balance sheet.
3.
- Earnings Calls Nordic forest owners like Holmen and SCA face intensified investor scrutiny as the listing of a dedicated Swedish forest peer creates a direct comparable asset class for capital allocators. Jefferies indicates that a positive market reception for Bergslagets Skogar could narrow the steep liquidity and conglomerate discounts currently applied to these peer balance sheets. However, failure to narrow these public discounts could lock up capital and limit long-term EPS upside across the forestry sector unless private transaction activity accelerates.

Sector Implications and Contrary Views

4.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies cautions that spinning off the Swedish forest assets may actually shrink Stora Enso's market capitalization and make the remaining entity less liquid and relevant to major indices. Some institutional investors question whether a standalone forest entity can trade near its underlying book value, given that its low biological cash yield might prompt equity markets to demand persistently higher discounts. Consequently, the demerger might fail to re-rate peer forest owners and could instead highlight deep-seated public market valuation limits.
5.
Divergent corporate strategies are highly visible at UPM-Kymmene, which is currently executing its own plywood demerger rather than spinning off its extensive timberland holdings. DNB Carnegie highlights that UPM- Kymmene's sum-of-the-parts valuation relies on maintaining a stable integrated forest asset base valued at EUR 3.6 billion to anchor its downstream chemical operations. This fully integrated approach contrasts sharply with Stora Enso's structural break-up strategy, demonstrating that the optimal path to maximizing shareholder value remains highly contested among industry leaders.
6.
Other side of the story: Nordic paper and packaging peers may choose to maintain their integrated corporate structures because owning wood supply directly protects their downstream industrial mills from severe raw material cost inflation. Holmen's management recently decided to split its forest division internally to optimize operational cash flow rather than pursuing a dilutive public spin-off. Unless public equity markets apply an undeniable premium to standalone forest listings, major integrated competitors will likely resist demerging their land holdings.

Section 2 – Expected Terms and Structure of the Bergslagets Skogar Spin-off

Section summary
  1. Stora Enso has finalized its cross-border partial demerger plan to list Bergslagets Skogar by March 31,
  2. The transaction shifts EUR 800 million in net debt to the new entity and establishes an aggressive dividend payout policy.
  3. Overall confidence in these terms is high, supported by Stora Enso's board approval and matching financial releases on October 30, 2026.

Planned Separation and Distribution Mechanics

1.
Approved demerger: Stora Enso has officially approved the partial demerger plan to separate its Swedish forest assets into Bergslagets Skogar by March 31, 2027. The board of directors finalized this structural milestone on October 30, 2026, ahead of the scheduled Capital Markets Day. This initiative represents a highly anticipated corporate split that is expected to unlock hidden equity value.
2.
Shareholder distribution: Stora Enso shareholders are set to receive one share of the corresponding class in the new forest company for every share they currently hold. The transaction will preserve the existing dual-class voting structure consisting of class A and class R shares. This direct distribution ensures that current investors retain proportional ownership of assets without facing immediate dilution.
3.
Investor support: Nordic institutional investors representing major voting blocks have already signaled their intent to approve the demerger at the upcoming extraordinary general meeting. The company's five largest shareholders control 69.9% of the total votes and 28.6% of the outstanding shares. This strong backer concentration makes the shareholder vote highly likely to pass on December 15, 2026.
4.
Dual listings: Bergslagets Skogar will establish public listings on both the Nasdaq Stockholm and Nasdaq Helsinki exchanges immediately after registration. Management expects to publish the official listing prospectus prior to the commencement of public trading in the first half of 2027. The dual-market listing aims to maximize liquidity for Nordic retail and institutional shareholders alike.

Financial Strategy and Debt Allocation

5.
Debt reduction: Stora Enso expects its consolidated net debt to decrease by approximately EUR 800 million upon completion of the transaction. Moving this leverage to the new balance sheet will help stabilize the remaining industrial business. Analysts at Morgan Stanley note that the forest demerger occurs as high Nordic roundwood prices begin to roll over.
6.
Leverage transfer: Financial analysts at Jefferies highlight that the transfer of EUR 800 million in net debt matches their previous expectations. This debt allocation is designed to ensure that both entities maintain investment grade credit profiles. Right-sizing the capital structures is critical for supporting future earnings per share expansion.
7.
Dividend policy: Bergslagets Skogar intends to implement an aggressive dividend policy returning 80% to 100% of adjusted net income to its shareholders. This high payout ratio contrasts with Stora Enso's historical policy of distributing 50% of net profits over the business cycle. Investors are expected to value the new entity primarily as a stable cash-generating utility company.
8.
Earnings impact: Stora Enso's consolidated operating profit will decrease by an amount equal to the historical earnings generated by the Swedish forest operations. Illustrative financial calculations indicate that Stora Enso's adjusted EBIT for the twelve-month period ending September 30, 2026, would drop from EUR 580 million to EUR 400 million without the Swedish forest division. This reduction highlights that the demerger materially shrinks the industrial parent entity's operating profit base.

Wood Supply and Operating Mandate

9.
Growth targets: Bergslagets Skogar has established a strategic operational target to achieve 1% annual growth in its standing forest stock. The company manages over 1.2 million hectares of sustainable forest land located in Sweden. Standing stock growth represents the primary driver of long-term biological asset appreciation for shareholders.
10.
Yield targets: Financial calculations by Jefferies indicate that the new company's 2% annual free cash flow return target implies substantial operational expansion. Achieving a 2% yield on the EUR 5.8 billion forest valuation requires growing annual free cash flow by approximately 27% to 33% over recent levels. Standalone management must execute on significant operational efficiency improvements to meet this target.
11.
Supply agreements: Stora Enso has established a long-term wood supply agreement with the demerged entity to secure its raw material requirements. This contract features gradually decreasing volume commitments spanning more than 18 years to give the forest company operational flexibility. This commercial setup ensures predictable demand and stable cash flows based on market pricing.
12.
Industrial focus: Demerging the low-yielding forest holdings allows Stora Enso to focus its capital allocation exclusively on high-growth packaging and renewable materials. The remaining industrial businesses will target an adjusted EBIT margin of more than 10% over the cycle. Separating these distinct corporate profiles helps clarify the investment proposition for different types of global investors.

Legal Approvals and Tax Hurdles

13.
Tax neutrality: Stora Enso has secured a formal statement from the Finnish tax authorities confirming that the demerger will be fully tax neutral for Finnish shareholders. This regulatory confirmation eliminates immediate tax liabilities that would otherwise arise from receiving the spin-off shares. Securing tax-efficient paths is vital for institutional support of the transaction.
14.
Contrary evidence: Stora Enso's planned timeline faces potential regulatory delays because the Swedish Tax Agency has appealed a key tax ruling. While the Swedish Council for Advance Tax Rulings initially approved the demerger's tax-exempt status, the agency's appeal is pending a final decision before the Swedish Supreme Administrative Court. Any adverse ruling could trigger immediate taxation for Swedish shareholders and derail the H1 2027 listing.
15.
Other side of the story: Standalone forest listings are highly exposed to public market valuation discounts that do not affect private land transactions. Jefferies notes that public equity markets could demand a liquidity discount of more than 30% on the demerged forest assets. If the public markets fail to value the assets near their EUR 5.8 billion book value, the transaction could destroy significant shareholder value compared to a direct private sale.

Section 3 – Valuation Discrepancies Between Listed Forest Companies and Private

Section summary
  1. Public equity markets persistently apply a 10% to 30% discount to listed Nordic forest assets compared to their underlying private transaction values.
  2. High-value private transactions like Stora Enso's 2025 land sale highlight the substantial valuation gap between public equities and direct property markets.
  3. Confidence in these valuation discrepancies is high based on detailed broker SOTP models and Stora Enso's 2025 transaction benchmarks.

Persistent undervaluation: Nordic forest assets held within integrated packaging companies historically trade at substantial public market discounts. DNB Carnegie notes in its October 2026 report that Svenska Cellulosa AB trades below its reported book value. This persistent public market discount restricts the company's capability to expand its earnings per share through equity-funded expansions.
2.
Depressed equity multiples: Public market investors often apply heavy valuation discounts to integrated Nordic forestry peers compared to pure-play assets. Jefferies indicates in its October 2026 report that Svenska Cellulosa AB forest assets are valued at SEK 104 billion before applying a 25% liquidity discount. This conservative public valuation restricts the company's overall sum-of-the-parts multiple, directly limiting the longterm outlook for earnings per share.

Valuation discount comparison: Peer forest holdings like Holmen face identical public equity valuation discounts that drag down overall corporate value. Jefferies reports in October 2026 that Holmen's forest assets are valued at SEK 57 billion or SEK 370 per share before discounts. Demerger valuation debate: Stora Enso's upcoming Bergslagets Skogar spin-off is expected to trigger a significant debate regarding public versus private market valuations. This anticipated public market discount could limit initial earnings per share appreciation unless standalone management achieves its aggressive free cash flow targets.
