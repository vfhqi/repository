EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

TORM operates as a pure-play product tanker specialist managing 95 vessels across three primary segments– Large Range 2, Large Range 1, and Medium Range–with a highly integrated operational platform that captures elevated spot-market revenues during periods of geopolitical disruption and refinery dislocation. The company relies on its in-house One TORM platform to unite commercial, technical, and operational capabilities, managing 95 vessels on the water by September 2026, consisting of 22 Large Range 2 vessels, 9 Large Range 1 vessels, and 64 Medium Range vessels.

TORM earns the vast majority of its revenue from spot-market charters rather than long-term contracts, enabling the company to capture immediate upside when freight rates surge, measuring performance using Time Charter Equivalent earnings to reflect actual net shipping revenues.

The company delivered exceptional financial performance in 2026, achieving high average Time Charter Equivalent rates of 40,102 dollars per day across its fleet in the third quarter of 2026, rising substantially from 31,012 dollars per day in the third quarter of 2025, driving a forty-five percent revenue increase to 499 million dollars. TORM repeatedly upgraded its full-year 2026 financial guidance, raising Time Charter Equivalent revenue expectations to between 1.75 billion dollars and 1.9 billion dollars in November 2026, and upgraded fullyear 2026 EBITDA guidance to a range of 1.35 billion dollars to 1.5 billion dollars. TORM delivered an impressive adjusted Return on Invested Capital of twenty-five percent in late 2026, representing a major expansion from the thirteen percent achieved in late 2025.

Structurally, TORM benefits from powerful industry tailwinds including refinery dislocation and persistent geopolitical conflicts. Global product tanker demand is structurally boosted by the growing geographical separation between refining hubs and end consumers, as seaborne trade patterns shift and refinery imbalances force refined products to travel over longer distances, increasing global ton-mile demand and supporting fleet utilization.

Geopolitical conflicts in key maritime corridors serve as immediate revenue multipliers, with attacks on commercial shipping forcing vessel rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope, constraining effective vessel capacity by doubling sailing distances and elevating global freight rates. These disruptions enabled TORM to capture an increasingly large share of value chain profits, with tanker owners capturing over fifteen dollars per barrel in upside in late 2026, compared to refiners' additional margins of ten to twelve dollars per barrel, driving extraordinary transport economics for vessels.

Competitively, TORM operates within a moderately consolidated peer group that includes Scorpio Tankers, Hafnia, Ardmore Shipping, and d'Amico International Shipping. Scorpio Tankers operates the world's largest product tanker fleet, managing seventy-nine vessels across the LR2, MR, and Handymax classes, ranking as the largest LR2 owner globally with twenty-nine vessels ahead of TORM's twenty-one LR2 vessels, commanding strong commercial advantages when negotiating charters with major oil companies.

Hafnia operates as one of the largest global operators with a massive fleet of one hundred fifteen owned vessels including ten LR2, thirtyfive LR1, fifty-one MR, and twenty-four Handysize vessels, allowing it to optimize global trade routes and capture premium index-linked charter rates. Global shipyard capacity remains tight at eighty-five percent of previous peak, scheduled to rise to ninety-seven percent by 2030, with long backlogs preventing shipowners from securing immediate newbuilding slots for fleet expansion.

TORM maintains favorable customer relationships with low concentration risk. The single largest customer accounted for exactly 8% of TORM's freight revenues in 2025, 2024, and 2023, suggesting that TORM's relationship with its top counterparty remains exceptionally consistent and secure over time. Premium clients like Shell, ExxonMobil, Vitol, Trafigura, Chevron, and TotalEnergies charter TORM's tankers, with no single customer exceeding ten percent of TORM's revenues, protecting earnings from the risk of losing any single key account.

TORM leverages its strong brand reputation to secure cargo bookings with premier global energy companies, with brand reputation and operational reliability increasingly important for securing business with major oil companies, maintaining commercial credibility and securing repeat business with highly demanding counterparties.

The company employs a formulaic capital allocation policy that maximizes shareholder returns. TORM pays out one hundred percent of its free cash flow net of debt repayments quarter by quarter, distributing 388 million dollars in dividends during the first nine months of 2026, based on maintaining a calculated cash reserve per vessel, returning substantial cash to shareholders during the market upcycle. TORM reduced its net loan-tovalue ratio to twenty-five percent by late 2026, representing a large reduction from the peak of fifty-six percent in 2017, with regular debt amortization and rising vessel valuations strengthening its financial foundation for future cycles.

TORM's competitive advantages stem from three primary sources: its integrated One TORM platform, extensive scrubber installations, and operational scale. TORM derives its primary competitive advantage from the integrated One TORM operating platform, an in-house commercial, technical, and support framework that allows TORM to manage the entire maritime value chain internally, enabling optimization of vessel-level performance and accelerating commercial decision-making.

TORM systematically retrofitted its product tanker fleet with exhaust gas cleaning systems, with scrubbers installed on a substantial portion of its 95 vessels allowing TORM to burn cheaper high-sulfur fuel oil, securing significant fuel cost savings during periods of wide marine fuel differentials.
