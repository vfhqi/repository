Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence indicates that Orsted's operating profit and its reported earnings per share will diverge between 2026 and 2029. The widest gaps come from non-operating, non-cash and below-the-line items, not from wind-farm execution. Jefferies projects that growth in capacity will lift EBITDA, but a simultaneous spike in depreciation and interest will squeeze net earnings, so operating trends decouple from EPS. The points below summarise the most critical evidence.
1.
Core divergence: Operating trends decouple from EPS through 2029 because the construction cycle moves cost from the balance sheet to the income statement as farms commission. Substantial divergence between operating profit and reported EPS is expected during this commissioning supercycle.
2.
Depreciation wave: Straight-line charges start in late 2026 and keep rising. Orsted fully commissioned the 913 MW Borkum Riffgrund 3 in August 2026 and the 704 MW Revolution Wind in October 2026, which triggers immediate straight-line depreciation. Sunrise Wind and Baltica 2 are on track for 2027 and Hornsea 3 for late 2027 or early 2028, which guarantees a sustained rise in depreciation.
3.
Capitalised interest: The shield against high interest rates ends at commissioning. Bernstein indicates Orsted capitalised approximately 49% of its total interest payments in recent years. That benefit ceases at commercial operation, forcing interest expenses back onto the profit and loss statement.
4.
Refinancing and hybrids: 62% of gross debt and hybrids mature or become callable by 2030. Bernstein puts this at DKK 48 bn. The nearest flashpoint is a EUR 600m 1.75% hybrid with a first call in December 2027. Further hybrids at 5.25% and 5.125% are callable in December 2028 and December 2029.
5.
Consensus versus brokers: Net financial expenses are where the market and brokers differ most. DNB Carnegie models DKK 2.51 bn in 2026, DKK 2.57 bn in 2027 and DKK 2.62 bn in 2028. Bernstein models DKK 2.67 bn, DKK 3.08 bn and DKK 2.41 bn. Consensus sits between them at DKK 2.49 bn, DKK 2.70 bn and DKK 2.73 bn. The consensus path is a steady climb, which may understate a near-term spike. A modest sensitivity also applies: a 75 basis point rise in refinancing costs would cut earnings by DKK 500m by 2030, about 3% of EPS.
6.
Hedging reserve: The largest single reported-versus-adjusted wedge is the hedging-reserve transfer. The third- quarter report showed a pre-tax hedging reserve loss of DKK 12.8 bn, with DKK 11.3 bn scheduled to flow into EBITDA. The schedule is DKK 1.6 bn in late 2026, DKK 3.1 bn in 2027 and DKK 5.8 bn after 2027. Management intends to offset these losses with higher realised prices on future unhedged production.
7.
US impairments: Non-cash write-downs track long-dated US yields. A rise in long-dated US rates triggered a DKK 3.4 bn impairment in Q3 2026 and took 9M 2026 impairments to DKK 5.98 bn. Because WACC is assessed in 25 bps bands, impairments are lumpy, and Jefferies estimates a DKK 3.5 bn risk from a 75 bps rise. Treasury approval of a 10% bonus credit for Revolution Wind has already prompted the removal of previous impairments.
8.
Partner leakage: Asymmetric partner distributions erode the parent's share of profit. Orsted and Apollo share Hornsea 3 cash flows equally for three years, then roughly 70% to Apollo and 30% to Orsted for 18 years. This forced an upfront non-cash EBITDA loss of DKK 4.2 bn in fiscal 2025. Ongoing minority payouts to Brookfield on its 12.45% stake will also reduce consolidated net income.
9.
Farm-down gains: One-off gains inflate reported EPS in transaction years. The Greater Changhua 2 closing is expected to generate an accounting gain in late 2026, which is non-recurring and creates a temporary EPS peak. The Sunrise Wind farm-down has been pushed to 2027, so Orsted must fund construction capex on a 100% basis during 2026.
10.
Tax: The effective tax rate is volatile but a major cash risk has eased. The effective rate reached 43% in the first nine months of 2026 against 14% a year earlier, driven by US tax-equity deferred tax liabilities and unrecognised deferred tax assets on write-downs. The Danish transfer pricing dispute over Walney Extension and Hornsea 1 was resolved in September 2026 and is covered by existing provisions. Race Bank and Hornsea 2 remain unfinalised.
11.
Share count and dividend: Dilution caps per-share growth while guidance and brokers differ on the payout. The rights issue created 900,816,600 new shares and lifted the share count to 1.32 billion. DNB Carnegie calls the dilution the largest driver of near-term EPS downgrades. Management guides to the lower end of DKK 2 to 5 per share, rising annually through 2028. Deutsche Bank assumes DKK 3 per share instead of its previous DKK 5. Morgan Stanley estimates DKK 3.34 against a consensus of DKK 3.59.
12.
Limitations and open questions: Overall confidence is medium. Visible near-term commissioning and hedging profiles are offset by opaque partner distribution structures. Apollo's internal rate of return hurdles have not been disclosed. The timing of Federal Reserve rate cuts is unpredictable. There is also a counter-view: Deutsche Bank reports that Orsted's three-year turnaround remains on track, with two of six major projects already in commercial operation. Operating growth could therefore offset some below-the-line pressure. The analysis is qualitative. It relies on broker and company disclosures that are not fully reconciled, and later quarterly filings, partner agreements and US rate moves will determine which scenario prevails.
