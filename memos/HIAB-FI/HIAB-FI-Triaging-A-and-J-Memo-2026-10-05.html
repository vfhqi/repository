[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Hiab is a 'Major Corporate Change' case with a cyclical tailwind: a standalone duopolist that has raised its 2026 margin floor three times, reset its cost base and spent a borrowed USD 1,035 million on Labrie, and whose consensus 2027 earnings per share have risen 30 percent in six months. At EUR 57.3 the shares pay 13.6 times the earnings two years out, which already capitalises the 2028 result, so the modal return is about 10 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The business story is compelling; the investment at this price is not.

  • The change is internal and multi-dimensional, and it is already acting.
    • The demerger removed the conglomerate
    • The balance sheet it freed was used on 1 July 2026 to buy Labrie, a North American refuse-vehicle maker, for USD 1,035 million at 9.2 times LTM EBITDA
    • A EUR 20 million cost programme and a three-business-area operating model reset the cost base
    • The 2026 margin floor was raised three times to above 14.5 percent.
    • Labrie adds about a quarter to sales and about a third to operating profit, at a 17 percent comparable operating margin on USD 491 million of LTM sales.
    • Of the twelve internal forces the framework screens, one is A and nine are B, so the screen's rule of three strong forces is met with room to spare.
    • The chief executive is not new: Scott Phillips has run the Hiab business since October 2018 and became standalone CEO on 1 April 2025.
      • The change is of mandate and scope, not of leader.
  • The external cycle has turned at the same time, from a two-year decline, but it is a recovery and not an inflection.
    • Orders rose 16 percent in Q2 2026 to EUR 437 million, a four-year high, with book-to-bill above one in every region for two quarters.
    • EMEA orders rose 17 percent to EUR 220 million
      • Americas orders rose 20 percent to EUR 191 million, of which a single EUR 37 million Moffett order from a US home-improvement retailer.
    • Organic sales were still down 3 percent in Q2 2026, after falls of 6 percent in 2024 and 4 percent in 2025.
      • Visibility is about twelve months, not the two-plus years the 'Demand Inflection' test needs.
    • US trade policy, interest rates and the dollar are headwinds that net the external forces to neutral.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have followed as far as the estimates.
    • Consensus 2027 earnings per share rose 30 percent in six months to EUR 3.83, which implies growth of 30 percent in 2026 and 26 percent in 2027 on the brokers' basis, then about 10 percent a year as the 2028 targets (a 16 percent margin, 7 percent growth) are met.
    • Services printed a record 26.1 percent comparable margin in Q2 2026 against consensus of 23.7 percent
      • The H1 2026 group margin was 14.3 percent on lower sales.
    • The one output going the wrong way is cash: operative cash flow was EUR 100 million in H1 2026 against EUR 183 million a year earlier, and net working capital rose to 27.2 percent of sales from 23.7 percent.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain for Services and the guidance floor, and sits at layers 3 to 4 for Labrie and the savings.
    • The sell-side has modelled the acquisition and the cost programme
    • Neither has a reported quarter behind it, and the first is due on 2026-10-23.
  • What it costs on the valuation is where the chain breaks.
    • At EUR 57.3 the shares trade at 13.6 times consensus earnings 24 months forward (EUR 4.22), which is in effect FY2028, the year of the 16 percent target with Labrie fully in and earnings per share about 80 percent above 2025.
    • Against the stock's own history that multiple is above the five-year median (12.4x) and the ten-year median (11.7x), and below only the post-demerger two-year median (15.4x): the 60th percentile of five years, the 70th of ten, the 6th of the last two.
    • Against the peers it is cheap against the sector median of 17.1x and at the top of its lifting cohort (Konecranes 13.8x, Kalmar 11.4x, Alimak 11.4x)
      • The sector fits poorly and the cohort is the better guide.
    • So the price already capitalises the acquisition, the savings, the European recovery, a modest US turn and the 2028 targets.
  • On our modal path the return is about 10 percent a year on both horizons, a D.
    • We value the shares at 13.5 times the earnings two years beyond each horizon, a blend of even odds between a cleared branch at 15.0x (Labrie's margin and the cash conversion both shown) and an uncleared branch at 12.0x
    • That blend is within a tenth of today's 13.6x, so the re-rating leg is zero.
    • The cash return carries 2 to 3 percent a year
      • Earnings growth in the earnings the multiple is applied to carries about 7 to 8 percent a year, because FY2028's step is already inside today's 24-month window.
    • On a one-year-forward footing the same path gives 13.1 percent a year, a C
      • The convention gives less because it stops us paying twice for the same acquisition.
    • Under a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to earnings per share (tax at 27 percent and EUR 10 million more interest, about 6 percent off every year's earnings per share) the return falls to 5.4 percent a year over eighteen months and 7.4 percent over three years, both F.
      • The case has no cushion at the modal multiple.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change' at B, with 'Demand Inflection' a loose secondary that fails its mandatory condition.
    • The fit is crisp on inputs and early outputs
    • It is not crisp where the chief executive is the same man with a bigger job, the largest force has no reported quarter, and the earnings-trajectory upgrade is three-quarters acquisition arithmetic.

  • The case is therefore a business transformation the price has already paid for.

    • It clears the 20 percent bar below about EUR 50 on the modal path, or at today's price only on the branch where Labrie's margin and the cash conversion both show up over the next three prints and the market pays 15 times again
    • That branch gives about 17 percent a year.

  • Rating = C, because materiality has two halves and they do not agree: the expected change is material to the business ('Change Forces' B, 'Financial Outputs' B: a transformed company with earnings per share rising about 80 percent from 2025 to 2028) and not material to the return from this price ('Modal return' D: about 10 percent a year), because the shares already capitalise the 2028 result at a multiple above the stock's own long-run medians.

    • The parts bind rather than carry: the strong change cannot lift a return the price has absorbed.
    • Not B, because a B thesis needs the return within reach on the modal path without a re-rating we can only give even odds
    • Not D, because the change is real, funded and partly delivered, and the cleared branch at EUR 57.3 gives 17 percent a year.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against the thesis is that the price is right and the risks are under-weighted.
    • In that reading Q2's strength is the risks report's 'false friend' profile: a recovery that is not one, because the Americas order growth was one EUR 37 million account, defence was 6 percent of orders and some buying was pulled forward ahead of tariffs, over a commercial market still shrinking (Equipment organic sales down 3 percent).
    • Labrie was bought at a private-equity margin peak (its EBITDA margin rose to 21.8 percent from 15.1 percent in the year before Wynnchurch sold) with debt that took a net-cash company to about 70 percent gearing against a target below 50 percent.
    • Cash conversion has collapsed (H1 2026 operative cash flow EUR 100 million against EUR 183 million) for reasons management has not fully explained.
    • The tariff and dollar cluster can take the 16 percent target away.
    • The risks report's competing-hypotheses matrix found zero hard inconsistencies for this profile, against four for the bull and five for the bear.
    • Our response: the Services margin of 26.1 percent on EUR 123 million of sales is pricing power in the installed base, not a false friend
      • Book-to-bill was above one in every region for two quarters and Equipment orders rose 21 percent, which one account does not do
      • The CFO attributed the July guidance raise to continuing operations, not Labrie
      • The surcharge model has held the profit-and-loss account for eighteen months
      • And the balance-sheet and cash risks are real but dated and testable in the next two prints, with named thresholds.
    • What we concede is every fact in the case against, and that none of our answers makes the shares cheap: on the modal path the return is a D, and the bear case does not need to be right for the recommendation to be neutral.
