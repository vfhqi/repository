Revenue Provisions and

1.
Capitalized Costs Aggressive adjustments: Grifols relies on complex estimates for Medicaid claims and distributor chargeback discounts in the United States to calculate net revenues. The provision for U.S. chargebacks is recognized at the time of sale, meaning small changes in these valuation assumptions can significantly affect reported revenues. Grifols adjusts these provisions periodically based on historical settlement experience and channel inventory levels, but these judgments create earnings volatility. Sizing this exposure remains difficult for investors because of opaque US distribution channel data relative to standard biotech disclosures.
2.
Unusual recognition: Grifols uses a cost-tocost input method to recognize toll manufacturing revenue over time rather than upon product delivery. Toll manufacturing accounting allows Grifols to record revenue and proportionate margins on work in progress before the finished products are actually delivered to customers. Although Grifols has an enforceable right to payment for capitalized amounts under early termination clauses, this practice pulls forward reported earnings ahead of physical product shipments. Slower customer acceptance or production delays could therefore result in sudden revenue reversals.
3.
Aggressive capitalization: Grifols historically capitalized a substantial portion of its operating costs, including information technology development and clinical trials, to bolster its short-term profitability. Capitalizing these expenditures allows management to defer expenses over average periods of up to thirty years, creating a significant gap between cash outflows and reported operating profits. Deutsche Bank Research pointed out that capitalization of research and development remains a primary concern for investors because Grifols' free cash flow continues to lag operating results. High spending on intangible assets represents a significant drag on cash conversion relative to international biotechnology competitors.
4.
Frequent impairments: Grifols faces recurring impairment charges on its capitalized assets, raising doubts about the historical valuation of its acquired research projects. The company recognized 49 million euros in impairment charges during 2025, which were primarily driven by a failed Alkahest research project. Additional impairments of 24 million euros were recorded in the first half of 2026 because management closed twenty-nine underperforming collection centers in the United States. Repeated write-downs demonstrate that Grifols' historical capitalization of R&D and center expansion frequently fails to generate cash as originally modeled.
