Business Model + Organisation – Orkla operates as a decentralised holding company managing 10

independent portfolio companies, anchored by Orkla Foods and Orkla Snacks across Nordic and European markets, having divested non-core Hydro Power assets (NOK 6.1 billion) and Pierre Robert Group to simplify capital allocation and reduce portfolio complexity from 12 to 10 companies. Revenue predictability remains highly transactional with minimal recurring streams, exposing the group to cyclical retail negotiations and macroeconomic sensitivity, particularly from cocoa cost inflation pressuring Orkla Snacks margins. The decentralised structure creates severe operational drag limiting procurement and distribution synergies across 10 separate corporate entities. [Section 1]

Financial Profile + Peer Benchmarking – Group operating revenues reached NOK 71,547 million in

fiscal 2025 with organic growth of 3.5%, though volume recovery remains fragile. Adjusted EBIT improved to NOK 7,647 million with consolidated portfolio companies achieving 11.8% annualized EBIT growth and margin expansion from 9.0% to 10.5% by mid-2026. Return on capital employed strengthened to 12.3% on a rolling 12- month basis, though this remains at an expensive premium relative to historical ROIC and structurally lower than large-cap peers like Unilever and Nestlé.

Operating cash flow declined from NOK 8.9 billion to NOK 6.9 billion with cash conversion dropping to 32% in Q2 2025. Net debt including leases rose to NOK 20,683 million by mid- 2026, driven by aggressive shareholder distributions. Historical organic growth of 1.5% from 2015-2019 sat below European consumer peer averages, with Bernstein forecasting moderate 3.7% mid-term organic growth. [Section 2 – heavily weighted] 4

with three players controlling 97% of the market (NorgesGruppen 44%, Coop Norge 30%, REMA 1000 24%), creating a defensive retail oligopoly with superior purchasing power. Vertically integrated Nordic grocers capture EBITDA margins of 8.4-8.8%, outperforming Western European retail averages. Lower-than-average private label penetration of 18% in Norway protects branded manufacturer margins against discount formats, though accelerating private label expansion and coordinated retail buying alliances threaten to compress downstream margin pools. stages (Foods 12.6% EBIT margin, Jotun protective coatings capturing superior technical specialization margins) while maintaining minimal upstream integration, leaving asset-heavy agriculture and raw input processing to thirdparty suppliers. Section 1 – Business Model + Organisation 8 findings

Section summary
  1. Orkla has successfully transitioned into a decentralised industrial investment company, reducing its core portfolio from 12 to 10 companies while divesting non-core energy assets to simplify capital allocation.

Operational structure and segment breakdown

1.
Decentralised portfolio model: Orkla operationally functions as an active holding company managing 10 independent portfolio companies, having deliberately dismantled its previous centralized matrix structure in March 2023. Core anchor segments: The portfolio is anchored by Orkla Foods (supplying local branded foods across 10 European markets and 32 factories) and Orkla Snacks (holding dominant positions in salty snacks, confectionery, and biscuits in the Nordics and Baltics)
2.
Growth and specialized businesses: The remaining portfolio includes Orkla Food Ingredients (OFI), Orkla Health, Orkla India, Orkla Home & Personal Care, The European Pizza Company, Orkla House Care, Health and Sports Nutrition Group (HSNG), and a 42.7% equity-consolidated stake in global coatings leader Jotun. Geographic revenue concentration: Historically concentrated in the Nordic region, Orkla has established highly localized international hubs, with Orkla India generating 70% of its revenues specifically from high-spending South Indian states. Other side of the story: Operating 10 separate corporate entities creates severe operational drag and limits procurement and distribution synergy capture, leaving the cost base highly exposed to localized inflation

Revenue model and predictability

3.
Grocery channel dominance: The primary revenue model across the food, snacks, and personal care segments is transactional and highly reliant on cyclical volume negotiations with highly concentrated Nordic retail buying groups. Contractual and B2B exposure: The Food Ingredients division operates on a more contractual, volume-committed model supplying industrial bakeries, though it faces transactional spot pricing on raw agricultural inputs. Direct-to-consumer and Out-of-Home: Out-of-Home franchise networks like New York Pizza and Da Grasso rely on master franchise fee structures, while the HSNG and Orkla House Care units are expanding via direct-to-consumer digital channels
4.
Macroeconomic and input sensitivity: Revenues are highly sensitive to raw material spot price shocks, with Orkla Snacks experiencing major margins pressure from extreme cocoa cost inflation. Other side of the story: Orkla possesses minimal direct-to-consumer subscription or recurring revenue streams, meaning sales predictability relies entirely on maintaining shelf-space listings during annual retail negotiations

Leadership and strategic execution

5.
Restructured holding executive: President and CEO Nils K. Selte, appointed in April 2022, simplified the Orkla ASA headquarters in November 2024 by merging the Group Executive Board and Investment Committee into a single decision-making body
6.
Active ownership mandate: Leadership has transitioned Orkla's corporate mandate toward behaving as an investment manager, emphasizing return on capital employed (ROCE) and standalone portfolio company accountability. Targeted growth platforms: Orkla Foods, under CEO Aku Vikström, has focused expansion capital on three specific growth pillars: frozen meals, sauces/dilutables, and its "local diamonds" brand portfolio. Underperforming turnaround focus: Strategic execution in the Orkla Health segment, led by CEO Mats Palmquist since August 2025, focuses on complexity reduction by shutting down three factories by 2027. Other side of the story: Frequent senior leadership turnover, including the stepping down of long-standing Food Ingredients CEO Johan Clarin in August 2026, risks disrupting mid-term strategic integration

Portfolio simplification and M&A pipeline

7.
Non-core asset divestment: Capital flexibility has been significantly enhanced through the completed NOK 6.1 billion divestment of the group's Hydro Power assets
8.
Complexity reduction exits: Orkla reduced its portfolio company count from 12 to 10 by selling the Pierre Robert Group to Jotunfjell Partners and divesting Lilleborg to Solenis. Confectionery capacity expansion: Orkla Snacks completed the acquisition of The European Candy Group (CCI) for an enterprise value of EUR 207 million in 2026 to scale production of its high-growth vegan candy brand, BUBS. Asian sauces partnership: Orkla Foods acquired a 40% stake in Dutch producer Go-Tan in June 2026, securing a call option to purchase an additional 45% stake in 2030. Other side of the story: Divesting high-margin, cash-generative assets like Hydro Power permanently dilutes the group's defensive cash-flow profile and increases overall earnings volatility

Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

Orkla's historical long-term organic growth of 1.5% from 2015 to 2019 sat below the average of European consumer peers, establishing a persistent structural underperformance pattern that continues to pressure valuation multiples. The company's historical return on invested capital was structurally lower than large-cap peers like Unilever and Nestlé, indicating fundamental asset inefficiency relative to the global consumer staples cohort.

This capital productivity gap becomes particularly concerning when examined alongside DNB Carnegie's observation that Orkla's EBIT margins remain structurally lower than those of global fast-moving consumer goods peers, suggesting the company operates with a permanent margin disadvantage that limits its ability to compound shareholder value at competitive rates. Cash conversion fell to 32% in the second quarter of 2025, down from 64% in the prior year, representing a dramatic deterioration in working capital efficiency that occurred precisely when the business should have been converting improved operational performance into cash.

This collapse in cash conversion was further compounded by net debt including leases rising to NOK 20,683 million by mid-2026, driven by aggressive shareholder distributions, demonstrating a capital allocation framework that prioritizes short-term returns over balance sheet resilience during a period of heightened input cost volatility. Morgan Stanley notes that Orkla historically has a slow track record of recovering input costs through pricing, a critical competitive weakness in an environment where the company faces persistent supply constraints and soaring costs for marine resources like cod liver oil and fish oil, while remaining highly exposed to volatile cocoa prices which directly affect 25% of the Orkla Snacks segment's total revenues.

The combination of weak pricing power and extreme commodity exposure creates a structural margin trap where cost inflation cannot be passed through without triggering volume losses to private label alternatives.

Orkla's long-term organic growth of 1.5% underperformed its global consumer staples peers due to private label gains, while simultaneously Orkla's top five brands represent a smaller share of its total business compared to global staples peers, revealing a fragmented portfolio that lacks the concentrated brand power necessary to defend shelf space against retailer vertical integration threats. This portfolio dilution becomes particularly dangerous given that consolidated Nordic retailers are increasingly expanding private labels, though private label penetration in Norway remains structurally low at roughly 18%, suggesting the defensive moat may be narrower than management acknowledges.

Outliers worth noting

Net debt including leases rose to NOK 20,683 million by mid-2026, driven by aggressive shareholder distributions, yet group cash flow from operations fell to NOK 6.9 billion in 2025 from NOK 8.9 billion in 2024, with cash conversion declining sharply to 32% in Q2 2025 from 64% in the prior year. This represents a highly unusual juxtaposition where management simultaneously executed NOK 11.2 billion in dividend payments during 2025 plus a NOK 4 billion share buyback program completed in July 2026 while operating cash generation deteriorated by 22% year-on-year.

The magnitude of capital return relative to weakening operational cash generation suggests either exceptional confidence in near-term margin recovery or a structural shift in capital allocation philosophy that prioritizes shareholder distribution over balance sheet preservation. Management commentary does not adequately reconcile why such aggressive capital return timing coincided with the sharpest annual decline in operating cash flow observed across the reported period.

Trade payable liabilities rose to NOK 9,442 million in 2025, supported by supply chain financing programs that doubled from NOK 357 million in 2024 to NOK 723 million in 2025, with payment terms under financing agreements extending 30 to 90 days longer than standard terms. This 102% year-on-year increase in supply chain financing represents an operational oddity whereby Orkla has aggressively extracted supplier credit at precisely the moment when critical raw material supply constraints intensified for cod liver oil, cocoa prices affecting 25% of Snacks revenues, and ocean freight costs escalated due to Middle East conflicts.

The doubling of supplier financing programs while simultaneously facing supply chain stress creates a structural tension: extending payables by up to 90 additional days may damage supplier relationships and reduce procurement flexibility during periods of acute input volatility. Management disclosures provide mechanical descriptions of the financing arrangements but do not address the strategic rationale for intensifying supplier credit extraction during a period of heightened supply-side risk.

Trade and other receivables reached 16.9% of group sales in H1 2026, up from 15.2% in late 2025, while group inventories rose to 15.0% of sales in H1 2026 compared to 13.9% of sales in 2025. This simultaneous expansion of both receivables and inventory as a percentage of sales creates a statistical anomaly when considered against multiple disclosed customer destocking events in Norway, personal care one-time customer destocking in late 2025, and a 3.8% organic volume contraction in Orkla Foods during Q1 2025 attributed to retailer inventory reductions.

If consolidated Nordic retailers were actively destocking throughout 2025 and early 2026, working capital metrics should exhibit downward pressure on both receivables and inventory, not simultaneous expansion totaling 280 basis points of additional sales tied up in working capital.
