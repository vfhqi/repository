1. The verdict

The Healthcare tailwind is supportive and strong today. The base expectation is that the Healthcare tailwind is supportive and moderate over the next three months and fades to neutral by twelve months, unless the guidance for 2027 that companies give in February and March 2027 lifts estimates. All three of the sources Richard asked about are present, so the Relative Strength is real and broad: the median Healthcare stock is 11 points ahead of the index over the six months to 18 November 2026, first or second of 16 Industries. The three sources are unequal in durability, and the most durable source, fundamental improvement, is the weakest of the three.

Throughout, the index is the Stoxx Europe 600, the universe is the roughly 1,300 European stocks in our files, the multiple is the two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple, and a percentile places a multiple in the stock's own ten-year range, where 0 is the cheapest.

Richard's three questions, answered for European Healthcare:

  • Fundamental improvement (question 1): present, moderate, rising, and the most durable. Consensus 2027 earnings per share for the median Healthcare stock fell 6% over twelve months, were unchanged over six months and rose 1% over three months. Forward earnings account for about 1 point of the 12-point gap between the median Healthcare stock's six-month share price rise and the universe's, and the multiple accounts for about 8. One-off items, mainly tariff refunds and rebate adjustments, flatter 2026 earnings in all three large Sectors
  • Money returning to an out-of-favour Industry (question 2): present, strong, and more than half used for the Industry as a whole. The median Healthcare stock rose 3% in the three years to May 2026 against 37% for the universe. The median Healthcare multiple sat 24 percentile points below the universe's in May 2026 and sits 10 below today. Global investors already hold healthcare above its historical average (section 3.2). The out-of-favour source differs by Sector:
  • Strong for both medical technology Sectors
  • Present for Pharma manufacturing services and supplies, for Hospitals, clinics and care homes and for Healthcare distribution and retail
  • Weak for the typical Pharma/biotech stock, and present on valuation only for some of the largest drugmakers: Sanofi, Novo Nordisk and GSK remain cheap against their own history, and Novartis does not
  • Defensive buying (question 3): present, strong in October 2026, and the least durable. Weighted by company size, 8 of Healthcare's 10 points of six-month gain came in October 2026. A smaller gain of the same kind in June 2026, of 2.9 points, was given back in full in July 2026. The buying was mainly a move out of technology shares, and brokers also name tension in the Middle East, selling of banks and French politics as causes

The three answers read together: only fundamental improvement supports stock-specific work for the 12 to 24 months Richard holds a stock, and fundamental improvement is the smallest of the three sources. Most of the Relative Strength came from the two sources that end. Money returning to a cheap Industry stops when the valuation gap has closed. Defensive buying reverses when technology shares recover, or when bond yields settle and money returns to cyclical shares. Richard's test was that three strongly positive answers are the most supportive case. European Healthcare gives one moderately positive answer and two answers that are strong today and temporary.

What the verdict means for a stock bought now:

The first is the stock's own earnings upgrades, which the market is paying for now: the most upgraded third of the Sector's stocks rose 43% over six months and the most downgraded third fell 1%. The second is the clearing of the worry that holds the stock's multiple down, which is what would close the valuation gap. On the results days of November 2026 the shares moved on whether the day cleared the stock's main worry, not on the direction of guidance. Coloplast rose 3.0% on 3 November 2026 after a cut that removed an unrealistic target, and Convatec rose 9.4% on 18 November 2026 after a raise that showed delivery. The Investment Analyst's stock-specific work on whether a company's earnings will be upgraded, or whether the company's worry will clear, is therefore the question the market itself is asking. The median multiple sits at the 14th percentile of its own ten-year range, yet the valuation room and the upgrades sit mostly in different companies:

  • Several of the upgraded stocks are no longer cheap against their own history: Sonova sits at the 60th percentile and Demant at the 33rd
  • Cheapness alone has not been rewarded in Medtech - consumables: the rank correlation between a stock's six-month price change and its starting percentile is only (0.16)
  • Medtech - capital goods: the Industry has already done most of what cheapness can do. The median multiple moved from the 15th percentile to the 44th in six months, so a Medtech - capital goods stock bought now needs its own earnings to be delivered. The early part of the Medtech - capital goods move, from May to September 2026, was not defensive buying.   • Pharma/biotech: the help since October 2026 is of the kind that can reverse within weeks. The counter-case in brief: the move is a one-month flight from technology shares that looks like a recovery. Weighted by size, almost all of the gain came in October 2026; global fund managers reported a net 32% overweight in Healthcare in July 2026; Deutsche Bank's data put global healthcare positioning at the 64th percentile of its history on 13 November 2026 (via AlphaSense); estimates for 2027 are still 6% lower than in November 2025; and several brokers' outlooks for 2027 lean towards cyclical shares. Section 6 argues the counter-case in full and sets out the evidence against the counter-case.

Confidence: medium on today's diagnosis, at the low end of medium on the three-month verdict, and low to medium beyond three months. The monthly timing, the estimate revisions, the valuation percentiles and the behaviour on falling days all point the same way, and a second price source reproduces the headline figures. The three-month confidence is at the low end of medium for two reasons: Morgan Stanley and Deutsche Bank called the defensive trade mature between 28 October and 18 November 2026, and the drugmakers' third-quarter results raised 2026 estimates more than 2027 estimates (both via AlphaSense). Confidence beyond three months is lower for four reasons: we hold no direct fund-flow data for European Healthcare, the evidence on who owns the Industry describes the largest companies and not the median European stock, the test of days when the index fell rests on few large falls, and one of the eight AlphaSense reports on broker research commissioned on 17 November 2026 has not returned: seven are folded in, and the eighth, on what explains the outperformance since mid-2026, was sent again on 26 November 2026 (section 7).

3. The three sources of the Relative Strength, tested

The three sources played three different parts. Fundamental improvement set the conditions, money leaving technology shares pulled the trigger, and valuation decided where the money went. The evidence for each part follows, and section 3.4 shows that the mix differs so much by Sector that one Industry tailwind means three different things.

3.1 Fundamental improvement

Verdict: present, moderate in strength, rising, and the most durable of the three sources. Estimates for the median Healthcare stock stopped falling around May 2026 and have begun to rise. The rise in estimates is small beside the rise in share prices, so fundamental improvement explains why Healthcare was chosen more than how far Healthcare rose.

The tests we ran, and what each can and cannot show:

  • Revisions to consensus 2027 earnings per share show the direction of forecasts and when the direction changed. Revisions cannot show the quality of the earnings, and analysts sometimes follow the share price
  • The median Healthcare stock: (6)% over twelve months, 0% over six months, 1% over three months
  • The median stock in the universe: 0% over twelve months, 1% over six months, 0% over three months
  • Healthcare has therefore moved from much worse than the market to slightly better, and ranks fourth of 16 Industries on the three-month revision
  • The count of stocks upgraded and downgraded over three months shows breadth: 59 against 44 of 110 in Healthcare, and 586 against 523 of 1,249 in the universe
  • The count of analysts raising and cutting estimates in the 45 days to 17 November 2026 covers the third-quarter results season. The count shows breadth and not size. Raises exceed cuts by 16% of estimates in Healthcare (460 raises, 308 cuts), third of 16 Industries, against 5% for the universe
  • The split of each share price change into forward earnings and the multiple shows how much of the move earnings can account for. Forward earnings here are earnings two years ahead, which rise with the passing of time as well as with upgrades, so the fair comparison is with the universe
  • The median Healthcare stock over six months: price up 14%, forward earnings up 8%, multiple up 5%
  • The median stock in the universe over six months: price up 3%, forward earnings up 7%, multiple down 3%
  • Forward earnings therefore account for about 1 point of the 12-point gap in price and the multiple for about 8. Each figure is the median of its own measure, so the two figures do not sum to 12
  • The split undercounts fundamental improvement where the market raises the multiple of an upgraded stock, which the next test shows the market did
  • The rank correlation between each stock's six-month price change and its six-month earnings revision shows whether the market rewarded upgrades. The correlation is 0.48 inside Healthcare against 0.33 in the universe. The most upgraded third of Healthcare stocks rose 28% and the most downgraded third fell 2%. An upgrade and a price jump often arrive on the same results day, so the correlation shows association and not cause

What the research adds:

  • Results were strongest at the drugmakers. Six of nine large drugmakers raised their 2026 guidance in the third-quarter season (Novartis, Sanofi, Novo Nordisk, Bayer, Merck and UCB), and profit grew faster than sales at most of them (our public-source results report of 17 November 2026, from company releases). Roche and AstraZeneca held guidance. GSK held its sales and operating profit guidance and lowered its guidance for earnings per share growth to the lower half of 7% to 9%, from the upper half (Bernstein, 28 October 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • Results in medical technology were stock by stock. Demant, Sonova, Convatec and Fresenius raised guidance between 9 October and 18 November 2026. Fresenius, which our universe places in Medtech - consumables, raised its 2026 guidance on 4 November 2026 to core earnings per share growth in the upper half of 10% to 15% at constant currency (company release, 4 November 2026, via AlphaSense). Medartis raised guidance on 18 August 2026, before the third-quarter season. Ambu gave new guidance for its 2026/27 financial year on 5 November 2026, of 9.5% to 11.5% organic growth, which is new guidance and not a raise. Guidance or ambitions were cut or narrowed at Coloplast, Smith & Nephew and Alcon (our public-source reports of 17 and 18 November 2026)
  • Outside medical technology, Fresenius Medical Care, which our universe places in Hospitals, clinics and care homes, lowered its 2026 profit outlook on 3 November 2026 (our public-source report of 18 November 2026)
  • The largest policy threat to the drugmakers has a known cost until 2029. All 17 large drugmakers named in the United States tariff proclamation had signed a pricing agreement and a manufacturing plan by 31 August 2026, which gives each of the 17 a 0% tariff until 20 January 2029 (Managed Healthcare Executive, 29 September 2026)
  • Orders for hospital equipment have improved. Siemens Healthineers took equipment orders worth 1.14 times its equipment sales in its 2026 financial year (company release, 5 November 2026), and Philips reported comparable orders up 8% in the third quarter of 2026 (company release, 3 November 2026)
  • The improvement in orders is one point above the long-run pace: Morgan Stanley puts the combined order growth of Siemens Healthineers, Philips and GE HealthCare at 6% in the third quarter of 2026, against a long-run average of 5% (Morgan Stanley, 10 November 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • Orders are turning into sales slowly: Philips's order book is growing faster than Philips's sales (Jefferies, 12 November 2026, via AlphaSense), and Siemens Healthineers' management is cautious on how fast orders become sales because of centralised buying in China (Jefferies, 9 November 2026, via AlphaSense)

What limits the conclusion:

  • Policy clearing did not move the shares by itself. Most of the large pricing agreements were signed by December 2025 (Fierce Pharma, December 2025), yet April 2026 was global healthcare's weakest month against the market in 17 years (payoff.ch, 10 June 2026), and our size-weighted Healthcare series lost 4.5 points in April 2026. Fundamental improvement was a condition for the move and not the trigger
  • One-off items flatter 2026 earnings in more than one Sector
  • In Medtech - capital goods, Siemens Healthineers' earnings per share for its 2026 financial year were €2.42 with tariff refunds and other one-off items and €2.30 without, and Philips counts about one point of 2026 margin from its refund (our public-source capital goods report of 17 November 2026)
  • In Medtech - consumables, tariff refunds lifted 2026 profit by $58 million at Alcon and 25 million Danish kroner at Demant (Bernstein, 10 November 2026; Jefferies, 10 November 2026; both via AlphaSense). Getinge had a refund of about 337 million Swedish kronor announced on 3 June 2026 (DNB Carnegie, 8 July 2026, via AlphaSense) and 45 million Swedish kronor in the third quarter (Getinge report, 21 October 2026, via AlphaSense). Ambu expects a further 70 million Danish kroner of refunds in FY2026/27 (DNB Carnegie, 28 August 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • At the drugmakers, about €300 million of Sanofi's third-quarter result, about 2 percentage points of the sales beat, came from adjustments to United States rebates that will not recur (Morgan Stanley, 30 October 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • The drugmakers' third-quarter beats moved 2027 estimates little. Rothschild & Co Redburn's notes of 23 October to 5 November 2026 show 2027 earnings per share estimates higher by 2% at Novartis, 0.8% at Roche, 0.2% at AstraZeneca and 4% at GSK, and lower by 4.1% at Sanofi and 2.6% at Novo Nordisk (Rothschild & Co Redburn, 23 October to 5 November 2026, via AlphaSense). The fall at Sanofi reflects the sale of a business. The six figures agree with our measured revision of 1% over three months for the median Pharma/biotech stock
  • Forecast coverage is partial. Consensus forecast blocks cover 56 of the 115 Healthcare stocks, and section 5 shows how much growth those forecasts already require

One connection inside the revisions matters for Medtech - capital goods. The Sector has the best three-month revisions in Healthcare (11 stocks upgraded to 2 downgraded, a median upgrade of 4%, analysts net 32% positive) and the worst twelve-month revision of the three large Sectors, at (12)%. Analysts in Medtech - capital goods are restoring part of a deep cut. Over the same twelve months the median Medtech - capital goods stock beat the index by 17 points. Investors in Medtech - capital goods are therefore paying a higher multiple for 2027 earnings that are lower than the earnings expected in November 2025.

Siemens Healthineers shows the same pattern in one company, and shows that the Sector's best revisions belong to the median stock and not to the Sector's largest company. Bernstein calculates that Siemens Healthineers' shares rose 30% in the six months to 13 November 2026 because the forward price-to-earnings multiple rose from 14 times to 19 times, while consensus earnings per share for FY2027 fell from €2.65 to €2.31 (Bernstein, 13 November 2026, via AlphaSense). Bernstein's 19 times compares with 16 times for Philips and 15 times for GE HealthCare. Bernstein's multiple is not on the same basis as our two-year-forward multiple of 16.5 times, which sits at the 12th percentile of Siemens Healthineers' own ten-year range.

Durability: fundamental improvement depends on the companies and not on the market's mood, which makes fundamental improvement the most durable source. The next test has a date: the guidance for 2027 that most companies give with full-year results in February and March 2027. On the evidence of the six months to 18 November 2026, faster forward earnings alone were worth about 1 point of Relative Strength in half a year. The larger reward went to the individual stocks whose estimates were upgraded, and that reward arrived through the multiple.

3.2 Money returning to an out-of-favour Industry

Verdict: present, strong, and more than half used for the Industry as a whole. The out-of-favour source does not apply equally to every Sector, so the verdict is given by Sector:

  • Both medical technology Sectors: strong. The out-of-favour source is largely unspent in Medtech - consumables and largely spent in Medtech - capital goods
  • Pharma manufacturing services and supplies, Hospitals, clinics and care homes, and Healthcare distribution and retail: present. The median stock in each of the three Sectors rose between 2% and 7% in the three years to May 2026 against 37% for the universe
  • The typical Pharma/biotech stock: weak. The median Pharma/biotech stock had not lagged the universe and its multiple has not risen
  • The largest drugmakers: present on valuation only, and for some of the largest drugmakers and not for all
  • The median multiple of the six global drugmakers sat at the 7th percentile on 22 October 2026 (our Sector note of 22 October 2026)
  • On 18 November 2026 the median of the five global drugmakers we can measure sits at the 18th percentile: Sanofi at the 3rd percentile (8.6 times), Novo Nordisk at the 7th (11.3 times), GSK at the 18th (9.5 times), AstraZeneca at the 25th (14.5 times) and Novartis at the 85th (14.6 times). Our valuation file of 18 November 2026 holds no value for Roche
  • The group is therefore split: Sanofi, Novo Nordisk and GSK are still cheap against their own history, and Novartis, the largest weight in the size-weighted Industry, is not
  • Deutsche Bank puts the European healthcare sector, weighted by size, at the 44th percentile of its ten-year history, on a different measure from ours (Deutsche Bank, 18 November 2026, via AlphaSense)

  • The median Healthcare stock rose 3% in the three years to May 2026 against 37% for the universe, and fell 15% over the five years to May 2026 against a rise of 35%

  • Brokers measured the same low against the market.   • None of these tests can show who bought. We hold no fund-flow data for European Healthcare

What the research adds on ownership: global investors are overweight Healthcare on two measures, and only European managers report an underweight.

  • Global investors said they were already overweight. Public summaries of the Bank of America global fund manager survey, a monthly poll of about 200 to 300 professional investors, show Healthcare at a net 14% overweight in June 2026, a net 32% overweight in July 2026 (the largest of any sector) and a net 23% overweight in October 2026 (Reuters, 5 August 2026; Atrani Capital, 15 October 2026)
  • European managers moved the other way. The European version of the same survey had Healthcare as the top overweight in February 2026 (Dow Jones, 17 February 2026) and at a net 3% underweight in October 2026 (Powergame, 13 October 2026)
  • Money did flow in, at a modest pace. United States healthcare funds saw outflows from March to May 2026 and inflows of about $1.5 billion in June 2026 and $2.4 billion in July 2026 (Lipper data via Reuters, 5 August 2026)
  • Deutsche Bank's positioning data agree with the global survey for healthcare
  • Deutsche Bank puts global healthcare positioning at the 60th percentile of its history on 6 November 2026 and at the 64th percentile on 13 November 2026, the largest overweight in nine months (Deutsche Bank, 6 and 13 November 2026, via AlphaSense)
  • Discretionary investors' positioning in equities as a whole stood at the 13th percentile, and positioning in utilities at the 1st percentile. The under-ownership that Deutsche Bank describes in defensive sectors therefore lies outside healthcare (Deutsche Bank, 13 November 2026, via AlphaSense)

What the ownership evidence means: global investors reported a large overweight in July 2026, and the size-weighted Industry still made no progress until October 2026. The global survey therefore removes one argument for durability, a large underweight still to be closed. Deutsche Bank's positioning data close the same argument for global investors: healthcare is held above its historical average. The out-of-favour source is therefore less durable for the Industry weighted by size, and the verdict of more than half used stands. Only the European survey, at a net 3% underweight in October 2026, still points to room for European managers to buy. Both global measures give the largest companies the most weight and do not describe the median European Healthcare stock.

How much of the source is used: on our own measure, 14 of the 24 percentile points of valuation gap to the universe have closed. Our public-source researcher reaches a similar answer by a different route. MSCI Europe Health Care beat MSCI Europe by about 9.5 points in the three months to 30 October 2026, which is about the size of the full-year gains against the market in the recoveries of 2018 and 2022. The researcher reads the three-month gain as two thirds or more of a normal recovery, with low to medium confidence.

The used share differs by Sector. The median percentile in Medtech - capital goods rose from the 15th to the 44th in six months, above the universe's 39th, so the out-of-favour source there is largely spent. The median percentile in Medtech - consumables rose only from the 9th to the 14th, so the valuation gap there is largely open.

One conflict between sources on valuation: MSCI's factsheets of 30 October 2026 put European healthcare on 16.2 times forward earnings against 14.1 times for the European market, a premium, while BNP Paribas Wealth Management (November 2026) puts European healthcare on 15.2 times, near the low end of a twelve-year range of 14 to 18 times. Both figures can be true, because healthcare usually trades at a premium to the market: UBS's Chief Investment Office puts the ten-year average premium of global healthcare at 5% (30 June 2026). 3.3 Defensive buying

Verdict: present, strong in October 2026, and the least durable of the three sources. The defensive buying was mainly a move out of technology shares into the cheap defensive Industries and not a response to fear of recession. Brokers also name tension in the Middle East, selling of banks and French politics as causes, so more than one event could end the buying.

The tests we ran, and what each can and cannot show:

  • The timing, weighted by size: 8 of the 10 points of six-month gain came in October 2026
  • The split of the gain between days the index fell and days the index rose (an equal-weighted basket of Healthcare stocks, six months to 18 November 2026): 19 points gained on the 58 days the index fell and 9 points lost on the 68 days the index rose or was flat
  • Any group that moves less than the index gains against the index on falling days, so the split alone proves little. The evidence is the change in behaviour
  • From November 2021 to November 2025 the Healthcare basket moved 0.95% for each 1% move in the index and gained nothing against the index on falling days. In the six months to 18 November 2026 the basket moved 0.60% for each 1%
  • The test rests on few large falls: the index fell 1% or more on only 9 days in the six months, and those 9 days account for 5 of the 19 points
  • How closely Healthcare's daily moves against the index follow those of Consumer staples: a correlation of 0.47 over the six months to 18 November 2026, against 0.27 over the six months before and 0.16 from November 2021 to November 2025
  • None of these tests can show motive. A group can gain on falling days because investors seek safety or because the sellers of other shares need somewhere to go

What the research adds: October 2026 was a month in which global technology shares fell more than 13% while global healthcare shares rose about 10% and global consumer staples had their best month since 1998 (European Business Magazine, 10 November 2026). The United States ten-year bond yield fell 0.35 points in the month and the German yield 0.24 points (United States Treasury and Bundesbank data collected by our public-source researcher on 17 November 2026, not re-checked by us).

The first connection: the defensive buying was selective, and valuation did the selecting. Two of the four defensive Industries rose and two fell. Healthcare and Consumer staples are 11 and 9 points ahead of the index over six months; Utilities and Telecoms are 4 and 8 points behind. The same split holds in October 2026 alone: the equal-weighted Healthcare and Consumer staples baskets gained 4 points each against the index, and the Utilities and Telecoms baskets lost 0.4 and 0.7 points. In May 2026 the median multiple sat at the 21st percentile in Healthcare and the 18th in Consumer staples, against the 69th in Utilities and the 59th in Telecoms. The money that left technology shares went to the defensive Industries that were cheap and passed over the defensive Industries that were dear. Richard's second and third questions therefore describe one flow and not two: the reason to move was defensive, and the destination was chosen by valuation.

The second connection: the fear was about technology first, and was not about a recession. Eurozone output grew 0.4% in the third quarter of 2026 against 0.2% expected (Eurostat via Associated Press, 30 October 2026), the European Central Bank raised interest rates in June and September 2026 (Euronews, 10 September 2026), and the Stoxx Europe 600 closed at a record on 11 November 2026 (Saxo, 12 November 2026). Morgan Stanley also names uncertainty over the French election (Morgan Stanley, 16 November 2026, via AlphaSense), and Deutsche Bank describes money leaving banks as well as technology shares (Deutsche Bank, 18 November 2026, via AlphaSense). The defensive source therefore ends most directly when technology shares recover. Section 5 gives three further triggers that brokers name and that do not need technology shares to recover.

The evidence against a purely defensive reading:

  • Healthcare kept its gain after the index's record close of 11 November 2026. Weighted by size, Healthcare added a further 2 points against the index between 1 and 18 November 2026

Appendix H. Method, sources and limits

What was measured, and from which files

  • Share prices and the index: our price files hold daily prices for 1,358 European stocks and the Stoxx Europe 600 to the close of 18 November 2026. Relative Strength is a stock's return minus the index's return over the same period
  • Multiples: our valuation files hold each stock's two-year-forward price-to-earnings multiple and its own ten-year range, where 0 is the cheapest. The monthly percentile series, used to compare with six and twelve months ago, and the official percentile of 18 November 2026 come from different files and can differ by a few points
  • Estimate revisions: FactSet consensus for calendar year 2027, as at 17 November 2026: the change in earnings per share and sales estimates over one to twelve months, the stocks upgraded and downgraded over three months, and analysts raising minus cutting in the last 45 days
  • Consensus forecasts: the financials block held for each stock, with FactSet consensus sales, operating margin and earnings per share by financial year

How groups were formed, and why three figures can differ

  • The median stock is the middle stock of a group, so it shows what a typical stock did and one extreme mover cannot shift it
  • An equal-weighted basket averages every stock each day, with each daily move capped at 25%. The daily tests need a basket, and a few large movers can pull it
  • Weighting by company size uses each stock's market value at 31 August 2026 and shows what an index investor experienced
  • The three can differ: over twelve months Medtech - capital goods is +17 points on the median stock, +16 on the equal-weighted basket and +5 by size
  • Healthcare's rank over six months is first or second of 16 Industries: the median Healthcare stock is level with Transportation at 11 points, and which of the two ranks first depends on the price source; Healthcare is first at one month and at three months

The tests for the three sources, and what each can show

  • Fundamental improvement is tested by revisions, by whether the upgraded stocks rose most, and by splitting each share price change into forward earnings and the multiple. Revisions follow results with a delay, and forward earnings rise partly because the two-year window moves on
  • Money returning to an out-of-favour group is tested by how far the group lagged in the three years to May 2026, by where its multiple sits in its own range, and by whether the cheapest stocks rose most. A low multiple can also reflect lower growth, and none of these tests observes money moving
  • The out-of-favour source is rated Sector by Sector: strong for both medical technology Sectors; present for Pharma manufacturing services and supplies, Hospitals, clinics and care homes and Healthcare distribution and retail; weak for the typical Pharma/biotech stock and present, on valuation only, for some of the largest drugmakers (Sanofi, Novo Nordisk and GSK, and not Novartis, on our valuation file of 18 November 2026)
  • Defensive buying is tested by the month in which the gain came, by the split of the gain between days the index fell and days it rose, and by how closely the group's daily moves follow those of Consumer staples. The tests show that a group behaved defensively, not why investors bought it
