Repsol (REP-ES): what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the case

These results leave the case where it was, and by a clear margin on the ratings: a 6 percent EBITDA beat, faster deleveraging and a buyback at the top of the expected range all landed inside what the record had already conceded, no price or margin line the case drew has been crossed, and nothing would move a grade; but two readings of the quarter press on the case's reasoning rather than its ratings, and the first of them weakens one of the four reasons the record gave for holding its view.

What changed

Repsol reported on 29 October 2026. Adjusted EBITDA was EUR 4,518 million against a Visible Alpha consensus of EUR 4,249 million, a 6.33 percent beat, and against EUR 1,652 million a year earlier. Adjusted net income was EUR 2,708 million against EUR 2,625 million, a 3.16 percent beat. The beat did not come from refining: the Industrial division made EUR 1,985 million against consensus of EUR 2,062 million, a miss at a Spanish indicator margin of USD 36.20 a barrel (USD 14.00 in the second quarter) and a realised premium of about USD 10.00 a barrel. Upstream (EUR 415 million against EUR 388 million) and Customer (EUR 244 million) carried the surprise, helped by an effective tax rate of −47 percent in Corporate and Others and a positive inventory effect of EUR 120 million. Operating cash flow of EUR 3,526 million missed consensus of EUR 3,637 million by 3.05 percent on high cash taxes and a working-capital build of nearly EUR 1,000 million with storage at its limit; organic free cash flow before working capital was EUR 3,786 million against EUR 2,816 million.

Net debt fell to EUR 2,038 million from EUR 3,667 million at June 2026, against a consensus of about EUR 2,400 million; gearing fell from 11.3 percent to 6.2 percent. A third buyback tranche of EUR 950 million took 2026 buybacks to EUR 1.8 billion, near the top of the EUR 800 million to EUR 1,000 million the market expected for the tranche; the January 2027 gross dividend was raised by EUR 0.03 to EUR 0.56 a share. Production averaged 574 kboe/d in the quarter, 590 kboe/d in October and 600 kboe/d on the day of the call; 580 to 600 kboe/d was given as a cautious 2027 planning basis, not guidance. Utilisation was 97.8 percent.

The outlook is the part that matters. October refining margins averaged USD 18 to 19 a barrel; Morgan Stanley put late-November spot at USD 24 to 25; management expects indicator plus premium of about USD 30 a barrel in the fourth quarter and guided a 2027 indicator of about USD 19 a barrel with a premium above USD 4. Strategic planning still assumes Brent at USD 65 and an indicator of USD 6.50. Brent moved back above USD 100 a barrel in October. Organic capex of EUR 8.5 to 10.0 billion for 2026 to 2028 was reiterated, pro-cyclical upstream acquisitions were ruled out, Repsol and EIG have ruled out an upstream liquidity event in 2026, and the company is in the final stages of securing a one-third interest in Petrocarabobo in Venezuela (plateau 210 kboe/d gross). The review's headline structural change, the 2030 carbon-intensity target moderated to 25 percent with net renewable capex capped at EUR 0.4 or 0.5 to 1.0 billion, dates from the March 2026 capital markets day, not from this quarter, and the case already holds a bounded low-carbon programme.

The sell-side response was modest upgrades on a profile that still falls steeply. Redburn (Neutral, EUR 31.00) moved EPS to EUR 6.34 for 2026, EUR 4.63 for 2027 and EUR 3.39 for 2028 (up 0.1, 2.2 and 2.8 percent); Jefferies (Buy, EUR 35.00 from EUR 34.00) to EUR 7.19, EUR 4.99 and EUR 4.16 (up 3 percent, 4 percent and under 1 percent) on a fourth-quarter Brent assumption of USD 95; Morgan Stanley (Equal-weight, EUR 27.00) lifted 2026 EPS by about 5 percent and expects the indicator at USD 6.50 by 2028. Redburn's mid-cycle case puts the organic free cash flow yield at 4.7 percent. The shares were EUR 28.54 at the results. The revisions come from three brokers only.

The challenge

The quarter admits two readings the case does not hold, and two obvious ones it does. The obvious ones first, because dismissing them is part of the reasoning.

The balance sheet running ahead is not an alternative reading. Net debt of EUR 2,038 million at 6.2 percent gearing puts net cash in 2027 within reach, and the record's own counter-case already has net cash in 2027 and 6 to 7 percent a year of buybacks; the record conceded the balance sheet, the 2026 cash and the dividend, and its one-in-three odds on the bull branch are odds on the margin, not on the balance sheet. The leg that was never in doubt arriving early moves nothing. Nor is the composition of the beat an alternative: an Industrial miss at a USD 36.20 indicator, a −47 percent tax rate, EUR 120 million of inventory gain, EUR 80 million of forecourt discounts from March to September and EUR 1,000 million of working capital swallowed at full storage are the case's leakage argument (43 percent leakage, the "Pret sandwich") printing on schedule. Both support the case; neither challenges it.

The first reading that does: the company has walked toward the bull, and the case's third reason has weakened. The record gave four reasons for holding its view against the structural-scarcity counter-case; the third was that the company itself does not believe the bull case, because it guides USD 20 and plans on USD 6.50. A management the record calls very prudent has now put about USD 19 a barrel on the 2027 indicator, a year further out than the guide the record quoted, and about USD 30 a barrel including premium on the fourth quarter. The case's own line for a structural re-basing is the indicator above USD 15 a barrel through the fourth quarter of 2027; management's 2027 number sits above it. In its strongest form the reading is that the guide is evidence, not hope: delayed competitor maintenance, Russian outages and Middle East disruption have held for a year, Northwest Europe cracking margins were USD 46 a barrel in the third quarter, middle-distillate inventories sit below their ten-year range, and a company that plans on USD 6.50 does not say USD 19 lightly. For it to be right, three things must hold: the fourth-quarter indicator must print near the guide rather than fade from the USD 18 to 19 October average; the indicator must stay above USD 15 through all of 2027, not just open there; and the realised premium must hold above the case's USD 8 test. The last is where the reading is weakest, and the honest statement is that management guides the 2027 premium only above USD 4, against about USD 10 realised in the third quarter. The indicator half of the case's re-basing test is now in the company's guidance; the premium half is being guided away from it. The planning basis stayed at USD 6.50, and the review calls the gap between the two numbers the central uncertainty. The reason has weakened, not fallen: the company now gives the bull a 2027 indicator and the bear a 2027 premium.

The second reading: the case is a one-driver case and the quarter ran on a second driver. The record names three key drivers (the margin and premium, the capital framework, the multiple), assumes Brent at USD 65 to 70 on its modal path, and gives volume low single digits with 'Revenue' D. This quarter's beat came from Upstream and Customer while Industrial missed; production ran at 600 kboe/d on the day of the call against 548 kboe/d for 2025, and the 2027 planning basis of 580 to 600 kboe/d is 6 to 9 percent above 2025 (our arithmetic on the review's figures); Pikka has first oil; Petrocarabobo is in its final stages and the review's expert sources say conservative broker models omit it entirely; Brent is above USD 100 a barrel and European gas reached USD 28 per million British thermal units with storage at 75 percent. On the review's own sensitivity, USD 10 a barrel of Brent is EUR 360 million of annual EBIT, so Brent USD 30 above the case's modal assumption is of the order of EUR 1 billion a year of EBIT the case does not model (our arithmetic, before tax and before the euro). Redburn titled its post-results note on the upstream outlook, not the refining. In its strongest form the reading is that the 2027 earnings path has a support the case never priced, so the fall from 2026 to 2028 that every broker publishes is shallower than the record's consensus path of EUR 5.40 to EUR 3.59, and the re-look prices built on that path are too low. For it to be right, Brent must hold far above USD 65 to 70 through the horizon, which the review itself treats as fragile (realised gains could decline if geopolitical risk premiums compress), production must deliver the top of its range, and the Venezuelan interest must close and produce. The case's answer would be that an oil spike reverses like a crack spike and that the upstream at EIG's 2022 valuation of USD 19 billion is already half the enterprise value. That answer holds for the valuation and does not hold for the earnings path: a case built on one margin series has no reading for a quarter in which the other segments beat and the margin segment missed.

What it does to the case

The share-price question first. The shares are EUR 27.52 at 26 November 2026, down from the record's EUR 29.18 reference and from EUR 28.54 at the results, below the 50-day average of EUR 29.49, 11.3 percent above a 200-day average of EUR 24.73 that has risen in 12 of 12 months; three-month return +1.0 percent, twelve-month +79.5 percent; relative strength at the 91st percentile, from the 97th. The case re-looks below about EUR 22 at the conceded 7.80x anchor and about EUR 19 on the modal path; neither is near. The dashboard's current P/E has moved from 8.0x at the 77th percentile of ten years to 6.9x at the 59th and the valuation pinned letter from D to C; most of that is the denominator, a record quarter rolling into trailing earnings, and the series the case names as the first sign of de-rating, the 24-month-forward multiple below 6.80x, is not in the dashboard data, so whether the de-rating has begun cannot be read from here. A price down 5.7 percent through a beat, a top-of-range buyback and a dividend raise, with the consensus target at EUR 31.79 above it, is consistent with the case's sequence and too little, too soon, to be called its confirmation.

By rating, in the digest's terms:

  • 'Thesis's Change Forces' Strength' C ('Change Forces' C, 'Financial Outputs' C): the same. The outputs improved again and the near-term upgrades the record called probable landed; the profile still falls steeply (Redburn EUR 6.34 to 3.39, Jefferies EUR 7.19 to 4.16). The forces remain the cycle's. The second reading argues 'Financial Outputs' deserves a second engine in its "because"; it does not argue a grade.
  • 'Foundations' Robustness' C: the same, with the premium under watch. The USD 10 premium held in the quarter (the case's 'Supply side' B); management's 2027 guide of above USD 4 is the first company number below the case's USD 5 mid-cycle premium assumption, so pressure on 'Value chain, industry' C is slightly up. 'Business' C is unchanged by an Industrial miss at the peak.
  • 'Setups' Fit/Crispness' C: the same. The quarter is the mechanism the record described: the internal change showed in net debt and the share count, the cycle in the earnings. The deferred EIG liquidity event removes one mid step from 'Staircase' C; the October tranche and the raised January dividend confirm the near steps.
  • 'Case Riskiness' D ('Riskiness' D, 'Simplicity' C): the same, pressure marginally down on risk and marginally up on simplicity. Gearing at 6.2 percent makes "a lower buyback, not distress" more certain; the working-capital build at full storage is the value chain under stress the record named; the second reading adds Brent as a fourth driver, which argues against 'Few Key Drivers' B.
  • 'Sell-side Momentum' B: the same. EPS revisions +32 percent over three months, targets +16 percent, revision-momentum score 11 from 9, the buy share flat at 31 percent after falling 13 points; the late-cycle pattern the record read has paused rather than reversed.
  • 'Thematic Fit/Momentum' A and 'Technical Momentum' A: the same letters. The technical A has softened inside the grade (91st percentile, below the 50-day, +1.0 percent over three months); the record said an A here is a risk measure as much as a confirmation, and that now describes it better than it did.
  • 'TSR/Valuation': the one place a re-rating would have to decide something, and not because of this quarter. The memo pinned D; the ratings store now holds F on the re-based 'Modal return', while the valuation pinned letter has moved to C, so pin and re-based return disagree by two grades or more. The record anticipated this. These results themselves push the other way, a lower price on higher near-term earnings, without reaching the 13 to 17 percent band on the case's own arithmetic at any multiple in the stock's range.
  • 'Investment Thesis' D, 'Foundations' C, 'Investment Setup' C, 'Investment Case' D: the same. One qualification: the re-look prices under 'Investment Thesis' were built on a consensus path of EUR 5.40, 4.05, 3.59 and a 7.52x start; both inputs have moved, the 2027 figures up 2 to 4 percent at the brokers in the review and the starting multiple down, and the thresholds are probably higher than EUR 22 and EUR 19 once re-based. The direction of the park does not change; the price at which it is revisited plausibly does.

What would settle it

  • The fourth-quarter indicator and realised premium, at the full-year 2026 results. The record's first sign of the engine slowing is the fourth-quarter indicator against the USD 38 third-quarter print; the first reading needs it near the USD 30 combined guide, and Jefferies has already trimmed its fourth-quarter realised margin to USD 26. A premium printing near USD 4 rather than USD 8 to 10 settles the premium half against the bull and for the case's mid-cycle assumption.
  • The 2027 buyback programme, at the same results. The record's alarm is cumulative 2026 to 2027 buybacks above EUR 3.5 billion at gearing below 15 percent; 2026 is EUR 1.8 billion at 6.2 percent, so a 2027 programme at or above EUR 1.7 billion fires it. The bull's own refutation is 2027 buybacks guided below EUR 1.0 billion.
  • The 2027 indicator, quarter by quarter. The case's re-basing test (above USD 15 through the fourth quarter of 2027, premium above USD 8) cannot be met before the end of 2027; the guide is the claim, four quarterly prints are the evidence.
  • Brent and the geopolitical premium. No disclosure settles this; it is a daily series, and the second reading turns on whether USD 100 is a 2026 event or a 2027 level.
  • Petrocarabobo. "Final stages" is not a committed date; until the interest is signed and producing, the upstream engine is 580 to 600 kboe/d and no more.
  • The second Earnings Review, four working days on. It is probable it carries a fuller set of post-results estimates and targets than the three brokers here, which is what the memo needs to re-base the modal path and the re-look prices; that is the one item on this list it can deliver. It cannot carry the fourth-quarter indicator, the 2027 buyback or the realised premium, which wait for the full-year results, and it cannot carry the 24-month-forward multiple series the case's first alarm is written on, which is the dashboard's to add.
