REGULATORY FRAMEWORK AND PEER ANALYSIS

1.

According to the official European network report published on February 3, 2025, the regulator locked the weighted average cost of capital at a constant 7.478% for the 2023-2028 period. This framework applies specifically to distribution system operators that committed to the Charter for the Effective Transformation. Consequently, PGE Dystrybucja has a predictable return baseline that protects it from sudden regulatory shifts.
2.
Decreasing regulatory bonuses: The additional returns granted to Polish distribution companies are experiencing downward pressure as premium structures are revised. In its half-year report published on September 10, 2026, Enea revealed that its reinvestment premium declined from 4.284% in 2025 to 1.867% in 2026. This adjustment directly reduced the total allowed return from 11.762% in 2025 to 9.345% in 2026. PGE Group similarly noted that reinvestment premiums have been reduced even as market interest rates escalated.
3.
Normalizing peer returns: The downward trajectory of regulatory returns is consistent across major domestic peers in the Polish utility sector. According to a credit report from Fitch on September 28, 2026, peer utility Tauron benefited from a high allowed return of 10.5% in 2024 and 10.8% in 2025. However, the regulator approved a lower allowed return of 9.5% for 2026 as returns begin to normalize from their recent historic peaks. This normalization suggests that PGE Dystrybucja will face a similar step-down in its allowed return for the 2027 tariff cycle.
4.
Moderate forward expectations: Credit analysts anticipate that allowed returns will settle at a lower baseline over the medium term. In its rating assessment on September 28, 2026, Fitch assumed that allowed returns for Tauron would average 8% during the 2027-2030 period. Across the entire Polish utility sector, Fitch modeled an average distribution segment WACC of approximately 8.3% from 2026 through 2030. This normalization of returns is expected to compress distribution EBITDA but keep earnings relatively solid.
