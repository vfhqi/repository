Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

DEME Group NV exhibits several material underperformance indicators when benchmarked against peer medians and sector averages, primarily concentrated in growth trajectory, structural leverage to cyclical forces, and governance constraints. The company operates under concentrated blockholder control with reference shareholders Ackermans and van Haaren and Vinci collectively controlling 74% of outstanding shares, which fundamentally limits minority shareholder influence during strategic capital allocation decisions and creates potential governance friction during periods of strategic pivot.

Revenue expansion registered only 1% year-onyear growth in FY25, materially trailing peer Van Oord's 36% offshore revenue surge in the same reporting period, signaling that DEME's top-line momentum has plateaued relative to direct competitors despite operating in a structurally favorable offshore wind environment.

The company's order backlog at the two-year forward horizon declined by 25% from its June 2023 peak, representing a stark deterioration in medium-term revenue visibility compared to peers maintaining stable or growing forward pipelines. This backlog contraction coincides with prolonged near-term procurement soft patches as EU governments slowly establish new Contracts for Difference tender structures, creating structural exposure to a projected operational gap in 2028 that peers with more diversified geographic exposure may navigate more effectively.

DEME's stated EBITDA margin of 22.4% in FY25 remains materially below unlisted peer Boskalis's superior 29.7% margin in its FY25 results, suggesting either more conservative revenue recognition policies or structurally lower operational efficiency in comparable contracting activities.

The company faces asset pricing risk from the Havfram acquisition, with the purchase price per vessel representing approximately 20% premium over vessel orders placed in 2024, indicating potential capital misallocation if secondary market vessel pricing normalizes or if the acquired assets face extended idle periods during the anticipated offshore wind execution lull. Heavy capital outlays on the Havfram transaction triggered negative free cash flow of EUR 394 million in FY25, temporarily impairing the company's ability to self-fund organic fleet expansion or sustain elevated dividend growth without incremental leverage.

Analyst EPS projections exhibit extreme dispersion, with ING estimating a severe EPS contraction to EUR 10.42 in 2027F and EUR 8.11 in 2028F, sitting 47% below consensus, reflecting deep uncertainty regarding the company's ability to sustain record margins through a full offshore wind contracting cycle and highlighting material downside scenario risk relative to current market expectations.

Outliers worth noting 1 finding

The comprehensive analysis of DEME Group NV reveals several genuine statistical outliers and unexplained anomalies that position the company at the extreme ends of industry distributions. DEME achieved a record EBITDA margin of 22.4% in FY25, representing a 380 basis point expansion compared to the 18.6% margin in FY24, placing the company in approximately the top 5% of global marine contractors when benchmarked against historical sector performance.

This margin level substantially exceeds management's own conservative mid-term target range of 16% to 20%, creating an unexplained divergence between stated guidance philosophy and actual operational delivery that warrants close monitoring for sustainability. The Offshore Energy segment delivered an extraordinary EBITDA margin of 30.7% on revenues of EUR 2,134 million in FY25, representing a statistical outlier when compared to unlisted peer Boskalis's overall EBITDA margin of 29.7% and significantly surpassing Van Oord's 15.6% margin in the same period.

This segment-level profitability places DEME's Offshore Energy division in the top decile of specialized marine installation contractors globally, though the drivers of this exceptional performance beyond fleet utilization remain partially unexplained in public disclosures.

The Havfram acquisition represented an aggregated transaction value of approximately EUR 900 million, constituting roughly 97% of FY24 EBITDA and placing the deal size in the top 5% of marine contractor M&A transactions over the past decade by relative scale. The magnitude of this single strategic bet, combined with negative free cash flow of EUR 394 million in FY25, represents an outlier level of balance sheet commitment that sharply contrasts with the company's historically conservative financial posture and creates unusual execution dependency on immediate post-acquisition vessel utilization.

ING's EPS estimate of EUR 8.11 for FY28 sits 47% below Bloomberg consensus, representing one of the widest analyst forecast dispersions observed across European industrial coverage universes. This extraordinary analytical divergence places the stock in the bottom 5% by consensus cohesion, signaling fundamental disagreement about cyclical mean reversion assumptions and highlighting severe uncertainty regarding the sustainability of current margin levels through the anticipated 2027-2028 offshore wind installation gap.
