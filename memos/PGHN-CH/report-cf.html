EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Partners Group Holding AG faces a critical inflection point as imminent leadership transitions coincide with severe evergreen redemption pressures that threaten near-term earnings momentum. The firm's Co-CEO succession model beginning January 2027 introduces accountability dilution risks precisely when retail product gating demands decisive operational responses. Orderly leadership transition sees David Layton stepping down as CEO on January 1, 2027, to focus solely on investment activities, while Roberto Cagnati and Juri Jenkner assume joint leadership as Co-CEOs, yet reverting to Co-CEOs may confuse decision-making lines during heightened retail redemption pressures.

Meanwhile, record gross fundraising reached USD 16.0 billion in H1 2026, representing a thirty-one percent year-on-year increase, but net assets raised collapsed to USD 1.1 billion as heavy tail-downs and redemptions neutralized gross capital inflows. The platform's industry-leading 63% EBITDA margin remains defensible through flexible compensation structures, though FY twenty-five performance fees reached CHF eight hundred nineteen million, up sixty percent year-on-year while H1 twenty-six performance fees declined thirty-nine percent year-on-year to CHF two hundred sixteen million, highlighting extreme cyclical volatility in earnings quality.

Leadership & Governance Transition Risks Strategic co-CEO structure sees Roberto Cagnati and Juri Jenkner assuming joint leadership as Co-CEOs starting in January 2027, representing a dilution of single-point accountability during turbulent market conditions. Roberto Cagnati has twenty-two years of tenure, most recently serving as Chief Risk Officer, while Juri Jenkner previously led business development and infrastructure before his appointment as President and Co-CEO, ensuring deep internal continuity but raising questions about external market credibility.

Joris Gröflin joined as CFO in 2024, bringing industrial experience from Axpo and Rieter, providing fresh financial leadership during the transition. Departing operational leaders Sarah Brewer and Andreas Knecht stepped down from the Executive Team at the end of 2025, while Ana Campos and Anette Waygood joined the Executive Team in 2026 to manage human resources and legal, signaling substantial operational turnover across critical functions.

Dr. Urban Angehrn was elected to the Board in May 2025 to drive insurance-focused growth initiatives, reinforcing institutional distribution capacity. Evergreen Redemption Crisis & Net Flow Collapse Gross assets raised reached USD 16.0 billion in H1 2026, representing a thirty-one percent year-on-year increase over H1 2025, demonstrating sustained client interest in Partners Group's customized solutions. However, net assets raised collapsed to USD 1.1 billion as heavy tail-downs and redemptions neutralized gross capital inflows, exposing structural liquidity mismatches in flagship vehicles.

Closed-ended program tail-downs accelerated to USD 6.6 billion, up one hundred and six percent compared to H1 2025, reflecting natural program maturation cycles. Client redemptions reached USD 3.8 billion in H1 2026, increasing thirty-six percent yearon-year, indicating deteriorating retail sentiment toward illiquid private asset exposure. Mature evergreen vehicles face ongoing gating pressures expected to constrain total asset growth for several quarters, with management acknowledging prolonged recovery timelines extending into 2028. 15 16 17 Performance Fee Volatility & Realization Delays FY twenty-five performance fees reached CHF eight hundred nineteen million, up sixty percent year-on-year, representing a cyclical peak driven by favorable exit environments.

Execution delays in the direct portfolio postponed key monetization events into the next fiscal year, forcing the FY26 performance fee target to be cut to 20-25% of revenue, falling below the historical range of 25-40%.

Carry from direct infrastructure programs is highly unlikely before the next fiscal year, further constraining near-term earnings recovery. Direct buyout performance was impacted by fewer top-performing assets, reducing expected realization multiples to 2x, suggesting vintage quality deterioration in recent programs.
