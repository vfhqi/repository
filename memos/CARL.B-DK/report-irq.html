Two points before the questions

  1. The case, and what the holding means for the questions. The memo's verdict: "a self-help and capital-return compounder with no demand growth", where "the entry is better after the spring than before it": February 2027 brings a conservative opening for 2027, the hybrid shown below net income and revenue cut towards 3%; August 2027 brings the beat, the upward narrowing and the buyback. The shares have risen 6% since the memo. Carlsberg is a Core Investment, so the questions weigh three things: whether February will disappoint as the memo expects (a reason to add after the spring rather than now); whether anything brings the good news forward (India, an early buyback); and what would break the case (China becoming a share loss, price failing to cover 2027 costs).
  2. What Peter Kondrup can and cannot answer. He will not forecast 2027 or pre-announce the February range. So the questions put sell-side figures to him ("Deutsche Bank has 6.2%; what are your thoughts on that?"), ask how to model a line, or test a reading of what the company has published. His treasury background makes him the best person on the company's side for the lines below EBIT, leverage and the buyback (Drivers 4 and 5).

What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: EPS compounding about 8% a year from self-help and, from August 2027, buybacks, on a multiple the sector half decides.

  1. Where 2026 lands. If 6% or above, a small beat against FactSet's consensus (which sits at the guidance midpoint) and nil against Morgan Stanley's own 5.8% and the 5.8% consensus Deutsche Bank reports (Bloomberg's, per the memo); the habit holds, but it no longer produces upgrades.
  2. How February opens 2027 against consensus (5.7% organic EBIT growth in Jefferies' count; the memo gives 5.9%). If the midpoint is 4% to 5% (the memo puts that at about 70%), a sentiment dip and revenue cut towards 3%; if the range is 4% to 7% or narrower, the positive case and a reason to add before the spring.
  3. Price against cost in 2027. Each 0.5 point of price and mix is DKK 300m to 450m of EBIT (2% to 3%): the widest source of variance in 2027 EBIT.
  4. China in 2027: a cycle or a share loss. A second weak summer costs about DKK 320m of EBIT and DKK 1.1 of EPS; share loss would turn the spring's cuts from composition into fundamentals.
  5. What reaches EPS below EBIT in 2027. A February finance-cost guide at or below DKK 1.6bn adds DKK 1 to 1.5 to the sell-side's EPS; the hybrid coupon shown below net income takes DKK 1 to 3.6 a share from brokers who have not deducted it.
  6. The buyback's date. A one-year delay costs about 3.7% of 2028 EPS; India's proceeds are the only thing that brings it forward.

0. Momentum now, and where 2026 lands

0.1 ★ "Deutsche Bank has 6.2% organic EBIT growth for 2026, a little above the top of your 4% to 6% range, and Morgan Stanley and consensus about 5.8%. With October and most of November behind you, what are your thoughts on 6.2%?"
- Why: each point of 2026 organic growth is about DKK 137m of EBIT. FactSet's EBIT (DKK 14,472m) sits at the guidance midpoint; Viewforth expects 5.5% to 6.0% (DKK 14,554m). The beat over the initial midpoint has shrunk from about 300bp (2024) to 200bp (2025).
- Tell: no pushback on 6.2% raises the odds of a small beat in February and of the sell-side holding its 2027 numbers through a conservative opening; "the range is the range" or talk of a tough fourth quarter lowers them and says the beat is shrinking faster than the memo's 150bp to 200bp.

0.2 ★ "Chongqing's third-quarter sales fell 9.6%, and Sichuan's beer output fell 26.8% in October. How has China's fourth quarter traded so far against the third quarter's fall of about 10%, and is sell-out now running ahead of what you ship to distributors?"
- Why: China is about a fifth of group EBIT (about DKK 2.7bn to 2.9bn). Jefferies writes that distributor inventories normalised in September; the memo would treat a Chongqing fourth quarter worse than (5)% as turning the spring's cuts from composition into fundamentals. Viewforth has China volume +2% in 2027.
- Tell: "better than the third quarter, sell-out ahead of shipments" supports the easy comparisons from the second quarter of 2027 and the sell-side's numbers; "similar or worse" lowers the odds of China's 2027 recovery and raises the odds of revenue cuts in the spring.

0.3 ★ New from the second report. "The Kazakhstan plant opened on 1 October, and on 3 November Jacob Aarup-Andersen said the business moves to standard profitability over three years. Morgan Stanley describes 'a step-up to 10% organic EBIT growth in 2027'. How is the ramp-up going against plan, and how much of the three-year profit step falls in 2027, in kroner?"
- Why: Viewforth puts the 2027 step at about DKK 200m (1.4 points of group EBIT growth), against a group EBIT increase of DKK 730m to 790m; a three-year path could put less of it in 2027. Jefferies says the plant build was a 40bp drag on gross margin in the first half of 2026. A Kazakh sugar-drinks tax, dropped from the new tax code, could still pass in 2026 (Beverages Sector Digest, 20-Nov).
- Tell: on plan and DKK 200m or more in 2027 supports a February opening near consensus; "spread evenly over three years", or a 2027 step nearer DKK 100m, lowers the odds of 2027 landing at consensus (5.7% to 5.9%).

0.4 ★ "Soft-drink volumes in Central and Eastern Europe and India rose 41.2% in the third quarter on the PepsiCo ramp-up, and Jefferies flags tough comparisons in 2027 there and in Western Europe. Driver 1. Price against cost in 2027

1.1 ★ New from the second report. "Heineken said last month that 80% to 90% of its 2027 commodity and currency exposure is already covered. Where is Carlsberg's cover for 2027, and where does cost per hectolitre sit after hedges? Bernstein reads you as expecting only modest pressure from aluminium, glass and logistics; Jefferies expects cost inflation above this year's."
- Why: Viewforth has input inflation of about 5.4%, about 160bp of gross margin, with more than 80% covered in money. Jefferies puts 24% of group sales in Verallia's main glass markets. If revenue per hectolitre rises only 1.5% against inflation above 5%, 2027 EPS is about DKK 68.5 (3.4% below Viewforth's base); if price holds at 3%, about DKK 72.9. In April the Chief Financial Officer said you would raise price rather than eat into margins.
- Tell: cover near Heineken's and inflation below 4% raises the odds of 2027 landing at or above consensus; low cover or "higher than last year" points to the downside branch and a February opening below consensus.

1.2 ★ "Redburn thinks soft-drink mix caps your net price and mix at about 1% a year. What price and mix should we expect in 2027, and is 1% too low?"
- Why: Redburn estimates about 30bp of negative category mix a year. Viewforth has pure price about 2.6% and revenue per hectolitre about +2.1% after mix. Each 0.5 point of price and mix is DKK 300m to 450m of EBIT.
- Tell: "nearer 2% than 1%" supports consensus EBIT for 2027; agreement with Redburn's 1% means the sell-side above 5.5% organic EBIT growth is too high.

1.3 New from the second report. "On 3 November Jacob Aarup-Andersen said you are deliberately avoiding deep discounting in Polish mainstream beer despite aggressive competitor pricing; the gap to economy beer is narrowing, and in the United Kingdom supermarkets are fighting on standard lager. For 2027, does that stance hold in both markets if volume keeps falling?"
- Why: an industry expert (an AlphaSense Expert Interview of 26-Oct-2026) expects narrowing price gaps to force Carlsberg to become more price-aggressive in Poland; Redburn sees a visible volume drag on standard lager in British supermarkets. Carlsberg leads in only 24% of its volume. Viewforth's base assumes it concedes volume rather than price.
- Tell: "the stance holds, accept volume" keeps price over cost intact but cuts revenue; any sign of following price down lowers the odds on gross margin and on 2027 EBIT.

1.4 "Heineken has cited long negotiations with European retail groups, and Coca-Cola Europacific Partners lost volume in Germany in September while it agreed 2027 terms. Are any of your 2027 negotiations open where a delisting is a real risk?"
- Why: Morgan Stanley reports retailers more willing to accept gaps on shelf than concede price. Price against cost is the widest swing in 2027 EBIT; a delisting in a large Western European market would hit the first quarter.
- Tell: "terms largely agreed" raises the odds that 2027 price lands; "still negotiating in several markets" is a first-quarter risk to volume and to the opening range.

1.5 New from the second report. "Marketing was 8.3% of revenue in 2025, diluted by Britvic, against your 8.5% framework. If price runs ahead of cost in 2027, does the surplus go to margin, or into closing that gap?"
- Why: closing the gap takes about 20bp of margin (our arithmetic). Viewforth has gross margin flat to (30)bp and EBIT +5%. A surplus spent on marketing caps the upside to consensus.
- Tell: "to margin" is the route to a raise above the initial range in 2027; "reinvested" caps it.

- Tell: "the whole market, premium holding share" supports a cyclical recovery in 2027; "Heineken is winning in premium" says part of the fall is share and lowers the odds of China's 2027 recovery and of the sell-side's revenue for 2027.

2.2 ★ "Jefferies has your 2027 organic revenue at 2.3%, partly on lower premium beer growth in China. Does your 2027 plan assume China volume grows on the easy comparisons from the second quarter, and by how much?"
- Why: China's second and third quarters earn about two-thirds of its profit. Viewforth: China volume +2% and China EBIT +3% to 5% (about +DKK 100m). A second weak summer costs about DKK 320m of EBIT (2.1%) but only DKK 1.1 of EPS, because 40% of Chongqing belongs to others.
- Tell: "modest growth on easy comparisons" fits the sell-side's base; "we plan China flat" makes Jefferies' 2.3% look closer to right and the consensus 3.7% too high.

- Tell: "a level we hold" supports the buyback path and upgrades to 2028 EPS from August 2027; "lower" or "investment first" means smaller buybacks and a weaker case.

4.4 New from the second report. "Sapporo said on 11 November it is on track to close by the end of December. Is anything on your side still outstanding, and where does leverage land at the year end with and without it?"
