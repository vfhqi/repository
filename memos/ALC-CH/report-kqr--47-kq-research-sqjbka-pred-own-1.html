Section 3 – Clareon Vivity Pro vs. TECNIS PureSee Clinical and Visual Outcomes

Section summary
  1. Alcon's upcoming United States launch of the Clareon Vivity Pro in early 2027 is designed to reclaim premium market share lost to competitive launches.
  2. Clinical feedback indicates that Johnson & Johnson's TECNIS PureSee has captured market share from Alcon's standard Vivity by offering a monofocal-like visual profile.
  3. Surgeons expect the transition to Clareon Vivity Pro to be rapid due to Alcon's deep equipment integration in ambulatory surgical centers.

Optical Design and Visual Profiles

1.
Superior engineering: Alcon's Clareon Vivity lens utilizes its proprietary X-WAVE wavefront-shaping technology to stretch light rather than splitting it. This non-diffractive design uses transition elements to create advanced and delayed wavefronts for an extended range of vision without diffractive rings. Ophthalmic clinicians rely on this design because it avoids the visual disturbances typical of traditional multifocal lenses while still delivering functional intermediate vision.
2.
Innovative design: Johnson & Johnson has developed the TECNIS PureSee as a non-diffractive extended-depth- of-focus lens with a purely refractive profile. This design delivers a monofocal-like side effect profile that eliminates the typical night glare or halos associated with diffractive optics. Consequently, surgeons frequently select this lens for active patients who drive at night but still desire dashboard spectacle independence.
3.
Enhanced satisfaction: Johnson & Johnson has successfully positioned the PureSee lens to capture volume from patients who are highly sensitive to dysphotopsia. Ophthalmic surgeons report that the refractive design of PureSee provides excellent visual quality with low side effects. This visual performance has elevated surgeon confidence and driven a substantial increase in patient recommendations.
4.
Broader candidacy: Johnson & Johnson's PureSee lens features less stringent patient selection criteria compared to older diffractive premium options. Ophthalmic practitioners utilize the PureSee lens for patients with mild epiretinal membranes or questionable maculas who would not qualify for a diffractive multifocal. This technological flexibility has allowed Johnson & Johnson to expand the premium patient pool by approximately 10% in some academic settings.

Clinical Feedback and Surgeon Confidence

5.
Accelerated adoption: Johnson & Johnson's PureSee lens has rapidly captured a larger portion of the clinical EDOF segment. Ophthalmic practices report that PureSee has actively supplanted other premium options due to its exceptional clinical feedback. This shift has directly eroded Alcon's market position, forcing some high-volume practices to reduce their standard Vivity utilization by up to 15%.
6.
Rigorous comparison: Alcon has sponsored the Contralateral Claims Study of Clareon Vivity Pro to directly assess visual outcomes against its competitors. This active clinical trial compares Vivity Pro against the standard Vivity and the TECNIS PureSee across 165 participants. Ophthalmic researchers expect the resulting data to provide the clinical evidence needed to validate Vivity Pro's superior visual performance.
7.
Growing resistance: Ophthalmic practices face ongoing economic headwinds as inflation and rising costs increase patient out-of-pocket price sensitivity. Private clinics report a 10% decrease in premium lens selections as patients become more conservative with their healthcare spending. This macro-driven pressure means that any competitive technology must offer clear visual advantages to justify its premium cost over enhanced monofocals.
8.
Weakened relationships: Alcon faces some near-term clinical friction due to surgeon dissatisfaction with its aggressive consignment practices. Ophthalmic surgeons have noted that Alcon's sales tactics and inventory requirements compare unfavorably to Johnson & Johnson's support. This service-level discrepancy has lowered Alcon's recommendation rates among certain academic ophthalmologists, allowing peers to capture incremental clinical share.

Revenue Trajectory and Market Outlook

9.
Competitive compression: Alcon reported a 2% decline in its implantable segment revenues during the third quarter of 2026 due to intense competition in the United States. This decline stood in contrast to Johnson & Johnson's domestic surgical business, which expanded by 14% over the same period. This discrepancy demonstrates that Johnson & Johnson's new product portfolio has temporarily disrupted Alcon's high-margin revenue stream.

This contraction represents a significant decline compared to Alcon's historical peak of 80% share achieved during its dominant 2021 product cycle. Financial analysts expect the upcoming Vivity Pro launch to help stabilize Alcon's market share and restore growth in fiscal year 2027.

Delayed acceleration: Historical commercial data from Redburn Atlantic demonstrates that premium IOL launches typically experience a six to nine month lag before driving revenue. Following the introduction of Synergy and Eyhance in the United States, Johnson & Johnson required several quarters to stabilize its declining market share. This precedent suggests that Alcon's upcoming Vivity Pro launch in January 2027 will take until late 2027 to fully translate into consolidated EPS expansion.
12.
Strong integration: Alcon maintains a massive structural advantage because its phacoemulsification systems and custom consumables packs dominate surgical suites. Ophthalmic surgeons note that these long-term capital equipment agreements make transitioning away from Alcon highly costly for ambulatory surgical centers. Consequently, the launch of Clareon Vivity Pro in January 2027 is expected to see immediate, frictionless clinical adoption.

Contrary evidence: Some high-volume private eye clinics report that Alcon's clinical support and robust volume rebates have kept its premium share stable. Despite the successful national rollout of Johnson & Johnson's PureSee, Alcon continues to dominate these high-volume accounts. Other side of the story: While Alcon's Clareon Vivity Pro is expected to leverage deep equipment integration to drive immediate volume, Johnson & Johnson's PureSee may retain its clinical advantage due to superior refractive optics. Surgeons have established strong clinical confidence in PureSee's monofocal-like visual profile, which successfully minimizes postoperative patient complaints. Section 4 – Commercial Positioning, Pricing, Rebates, and Sampling Tactics

Section summary
  1. Alcon and Johnson & Johnson are engaged in an intense commercial battle involving volume-based rebates, bundled equipment contracts, and aggressive competitive lens sampling.
  2. Johnson & Johnson's PureSee sampling program has temporarily depressed Alcon's U.S. implantable margins, but Alcon plans to counteract this with the Clareon Vivity Pro launch in January 2027.

Commercial Rebates and Bundling

1.
Substantial rebates: Alcon maintains market dominance in premium intraocular lenses by offering substantial volume-based rebates linked to clinic-wide utilization thresholds. Ophthalmic surgeons report that maintaining a 90% volume threshold with Alcon typically yields an attractive 30% rebate on their intraocular lens pricing. This commercial locking mechanism heavily incentivizes practices to maximize Alcon lens utilization over competing products to preserve high operating margins.
2.
Bundled contracting: Alcon leverages its extensive capital equipment footprint to bundle surgical consumables and custom packs with its implantable lens portfolio. Practices utilizing Alcon phacoemulsification platforms, viscoelastic solutions, and customized tubing packs receive compounded financial incentives and volume discounts. This deep technological integration creates a significant competitive moat because switching to alternative lens manufacturers threatens to escalate overall procedure costs.
3.
Discounted entry: Johnson & Johnson has actively disrupted Alcon's market position by pricing its newer intraocular lenses highly competitively to incentivize clinical switches. In price-sensitive patient demographics, Johnson & Johnson offers discounted contract pricing and aggressive introductory rates on newer platforms. Ophthalmic practices with high commercial access to Johnson & Johnson are shying away from Alcon because J&J has improved its visual outcome profiles.

High-volume clinics that previously met Alcon's 90% premium volume threshold fell into lower rebate tiers because free PureSee samples captured forward momentum. This drop in rebate tiers raised the net cost of Alcon's remaining monofocal and premium lenses, impairing overall practice margins.

Alcon's core implantables revenue fell 1% in constant currency during the third quarter of 2026 due to these intensifying competitive dynamics in the United States. Because J&J continues to achieve strong commercial execution with its PureSee and Odyssey platforms, Alcon must rely on upcoming product upgrades to reclaim lost volume.

To support this launch, management is increasing its focus on direct-to-patient marketing campaigns and compelling head-to-head clinical comparisons. Other side of the story: While J&J's PureSee sampling has pressured Alcon's near-term implantable margins, Alcon's massive phacoemulsification installed base provides a highly secure commercial anchor. This diversification is driven by the steady recurring revenues of daily contact lenses, surgical consumables, and high-margin over-the-counter Systane drops.
