Hearing instruments currently face zero tariffs in the United States, so any action would be a new operating risk rather than a continuation of existing costs.
2.

The Nairobi Protocol is codified in US law under HTS subheadings 9817.92, 9817.94 and 9817.96, and federal customs rules explicitly include permanent hearing loss within the scope of protected physical disabilities.

A Minimed Group filing of August 2026 affirmed that recent government actions have not restricted use of the Nairobi Protocol for importing assistive devices duty-free.

The likely routes to a harsh outcome are country caps and company-specific deals, not an automatic exemption.

3.
Sonova's exposure: Sonova's baseline exposure is structurally high. All of its hearing care products sold in the United States are imported, and over ninety percent of primary hearing instruments ship from assembly plants in Asia and the Pacific, primarily Vietnam and China. The April 2026 metals revision applies the twenty-five to fifty percent rate to the full customs value rather than just metal content. Hearing aid manufacturers' baseline gross margins of seventy to seventy-five percent provide a buffer.

Supply-chain response: Mitigation centers on Mexicali, which opened in February 2024 to support both hearing instruments and cochlear implants. Nairobi Protocol Protections

12.
Robust legal protections: Assistive devices designed for disabled individuals have historically enjoyed strong protections from import taxes under international humanitarian agreements. A report by US Customs and Border Protection on November 26, 2025, explained that the Nairobi Protocol was codified into US law under subheadings 9817.92, 9817.94, and 9817.96 of the Harmonized Tariff Schedule. This treaty-based legislation provides durable duty-free import status for products that are specially adapted for chronic physical impairments.
13.
Broad impairment definitions: Federal customs rules explicitly include permanent hearing loss within the scope of protected physical disabilities. According to a US Customs and Border Protection bulletin on November 26, 2025, the legal definition of handicapped individuals covers any person suffering from a chronic physical impairment that substantially limits major life activities including hearing. This statutory classification ensures that hearing instruments qualify for duty-free entry under Chapter 98.
14.
Favorable baseline assumptions: Most financial analysts expect the historical humanitarian carve-outs to survive any upcoming executive action on medical equipment. A Bernstein broker note published on December 16, 2025, stated that the baseline forecast assumes currently exempted devices like hearing aids will remain protected. However, the ongoing Department of Commerce investigation creates a minor risk that executive orders could override Nairobi exemptions to force manufacturing onshoring.
15.
Contrary evidence: A public hearing transcript published by the US Trade Representative on July 10, 2026, revealed that medical firms are actively lobbying to defend their duty-free status. During the hearing, Cochlear requested that the government maintain the duty-free status of implantable devices specifically because tariffs would cause negative economic impacts for disabled Americans. This active defense indicates that the survival of the exemption is actively contested.

Global Manufacturing Footprint

1.
Decentralized production hubs: Sonova centralizes its advanced manufacturing and research activities in Stäfa, Switzerland, while utilizing assembly facilities in China and Vietnam to lower baseline production costs. The group expanded its operational flexibility by opening a major manufacturing center in Mexicali, Mexico, in February 2024 to support both hearing instruments and cochlear implants. This geographic diversification allows the group to rebalance production across sites when facing localized trade barriers or regional logistical disruptions.
2.
Vulnerable import routes: Sonova faces elevated trade exposure because all of its hearing care products sold in the United States are imported from facilities outside the country. The group ships over ninety percent of its primary hearing instruments from assembly plants located in the Asia and Pacific region, primarily Vietnam and China. This concentrated logistics pathway makes the company highly dependent on trade exemptions to maintain its current margins in the United States market.
3.
Flexible footprint structures: Sonova maintains a balanced manufacturing network across Europe, the Americas, and Asia, similar to the global footprints of major medical equipment peers. While Demant manufactures the vast majority of its global devices in Poland, GN Hearing relies on assembly in the United States, China, and Malaysia. This deep Asian supply integration means that local assembly alone cannot fully insulate the company from tariffs levied on essential internal components sourced from China.

5.

Under the updated framework, the tariff rate of twenty-five to fifty percent applies to the full customs value of the product rather than just its specific metal content. This adjustment significantly increases the total tax burden on medical instruments that contain steel, aluminum, or copper parts.

Quantifiable margin headwinds: A theoretical ten percent tariff on Sonova's United States imports would generate an annualized cost increase of sixteen million Swiss francs. Consequently, the group must accelerate its supply chain regionalization to protect its long-term operating margins from evolving trade regulations.

However, the group's strategic decision in March 2026 to classify the consumer hearing business as a discontinued operation slated for divestment has structurally reduced this operational risk. Selling this division successfully removes a key source of import duty volatility, allowing management to focus on insulated healthcare segments going forward.
8.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies reported in May 2025 that Sonova's core hearing aids and cochlear implants remained completely exempt from the initial waves of import duties. The brokerage firm noted that the direct impact of existing trade disputes was immaterial to the group's near-term guidance because of these active humanitarian carve-outs. Section summary
  1. Wholesale pricing varies significantly across channels with independent audiologists paying the highest prices and large consolidated retail networks extracting deep discounts.
  2. Fixed contract structures in public channels such as the Veterans Affairs delay tariff pass-through for up to five years, whereas private channels allow rapid price adjustments.
  3. A potential twenty-five percent import tariff would introduce an unmitigated one hundred sixty basis point gross margin headwind, though nearshoring and pricing power limit actual EPS damage.

Wholesale Channel Pricing Dynamics

1.
Highly variable channel pricing: Bernstein Research reported on October 23, 2026, that wholesale prices vary significantly depending on channel bargaining power. Independent US audiologists pay the highest wholesale prices of $600 to $1,100 per device, while the Veterans Affairs pays an average of $540. Furthermore, US managed care pays $300 to $500, while Costco pays $300 to $400, and Amplifon pays only $150 to $200. This distribution indicates that wholesale margins depend heavily on customer mix.
2.
Strong pricing power in independent channels: Bernstein Research reported on June 19, 2026, that independent audiologists represent the shrinking but highest-priced channel in the retail market. Manufacturers capture their highest gross margins when selling directly to these independent clinics because they pay higher wholesale average selling prices compared to retail chains. Consequently, Sonova and Demant are structured to serve these high-priced independent channels more actively than peers like GN Hearing and WS Audiology. This focus supports higher baseline wholesale gross margins for Sonova during normal operating periods unless pricing pressure intensifies.
3.
Moderate margin dilution in large retail channels: Morgan Stanley reported on September 9, 2025, that big box retail contracts are historically dilutive to manufacturer profit margins in the United States. Sonova's return to the high-volume Costco channel in 2025 is expected to compress local US margins, though it remains accretive to the overall group gross profit. This indicates that volume growth in wholesale can offset pricing headwinds, provided that the company maintains disciplined operational cost control. Therefore, larger retail accounts provide significant volume support but weaken average wholesale pricing power over the long term.
4.
Contrary evidence: An expert interview with a dispensing audiologist at a university clinic on October 22, 2026, revealed that wholesale prices for comparable devices rose by fifteen percent year-over-year. This substantial price increase applied broadly across major brands despite high competitive pressures and macroeconomic challenges in the United States. This suggests that manufacturers retain significant baseline pricing power even when channels are under pressure. Consequently, hearing aid manufacturers may pass tariff costs to clinics far more easily than financial analysts anticipate.

Owned Retail Pricing Structures

5.
High margin capture in owned retail clinics: Demant reported in its Capital Markets Day presentation on November 23, 2026, that vertically integrated retail operations capture the full retail value of hearing instruments. These manufacturer-owned networks are highly loyal to their parent company's product line and primarily distribute their own brands. 9.
Prolonged pricing lags in government channels: Bernstein Research reported on October 23, 2026, that the US Department of Veterans Affairs purchases hearing devices under fixed-price, indefinite-quantity contracts. Because wholesale prices are only renegotiated upon contract renewal, manufacturers cannot quickly pass through tariff-related cost increases to the VA. This contractual rigidity means that any sudden trade penalties would directly compress manufacturer margins in this high-volume public segment.

Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

The report's central conclusion is that the market treats the Section 232 investigation as a manageable overhang, not an earnings event. A DNB Carnegie note dated November 13, 2026 states that hearing instruments currently face zero tariffs in the United States. Bernstein's baseline forecast assumes that currently exempted devices like hearing aids will remain protected. Jefferies reports that trade consensus expects any final medical tariffs to settle around ten to twelve percent. This section examines two outcomes that sit outside that consensus: a total elimination of exemptions, and a complete tariff waiver for healthcare products. It then identifies the signals that could show the market is mispricing either case.

1.
Scenario one: exemptions removed entirely. The first-order effect would be mechanical. All of Sonova's hearing care products sold in the United States are imported. More than ninety percent of its primary hearing instruments ship from assembly plants in the Asia-Pacific region, primarily Vietnam and China. The tariff rate and the valuation base would then matter as much as the removal itself. Under the April 2026 revision of the Section 232 metals framework, a twenty-five to fifty percent rate applies to the full customs value of a product rather than just its metal content. If a similar structure were applied to hearing devices, the burden would be far larger than a ten to twelve percent consensus implies. Rothschild & Co Redburn estimated that a blended twenty-five percent tariff would create a 160bp unmitigated headwind to group gross margins. Scaling that estimate, a fifty percent tariff would widen the unmitigated headwind to 320bp. The pharmaceutical precedent also shows the administration can set punitive headline rates. The April 2, 2026 proclamation imposed a default one hundred percent tariff on patented drugs. Section 301 duties on Chinese surgical gloves reached one hundred percent on January 1, 2026.
