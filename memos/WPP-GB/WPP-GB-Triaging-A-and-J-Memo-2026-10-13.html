[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = B

Thesis. WPP is a self-help turnaround bought at a trough multiple. A new chief executive from outside the industry has collapsed a holding company of twenty-five agencies into four operating units on one platform, cut the dividend, sold the periphery and committed £500m of gross cost savings, after sixteen quarters in which WPP lost share to Publicis and Omnicom and the market priced it for permanent decline. At 378.6p the shares trade at 6.3x the earnings two years out, so the case needs only that net sales stop falling in 2027 and that about three points of margin come back by FY2029 for the return to clear the 20 percent bar at eighteen months; it does not clear the bar at three years, and the foundations run against it.

  • The change is internal, broad and resourced, and it is the case.
    • Five of the twelve internal forces the framework's screen names are strong and dated within fourteen months, against a rule that asks for three.
    • Cindy Rose succeeded Mark Read on 1 September 2025 after nine years at Microsoft
      • Philip Jansen became chair on 1 January 2025
      • A chief operating officer, four divisional chiefs, a chief people officer, a chief transformation officer and a group chief strategy officer are new since September 2024.
    • Elevate28 (26 February 2026) runs in three dated phases: Stabilise 2026, Build 2027 with a return to organic growth, Accelerate 2028 with margin expansion
      • £500m of gross annualised savings by end-2028, £100m of them in 2026, funded by £400m of integration cash over two years.
    • The holding company became four units (WPP Media from May 2025, WPP Production from January 2026, WPP Enterprise Solutions from 1 July 2026, WPP Creative with four regional profit centres) and one reporting segment
      • 25 legacy agencies became four
      • 1,400 legal entities were removed.
    • The false-positive checks clear: the chief executive is not a caretaker (external hire, long-dated incentives, two open-market purchases), the plan is funded with cash, the disposals are non-core rather than crown jewels, and the dividend was halved rather than borrowed for.
  • The external forces run the other way, and they are already in the base of the numbers.
    • Client budgets grow at the market level and the agency layer leaks it
    • Competition and disruption are headwinds that intensify
    • Only the company's own cycle and regulation help.
    • Global advertising is forecast to grow 8.9% in 2026, yet the top five holding companies grew 1% in 2025 against a 6% market
      • Publicis grew 4.8% organically in the second quarter of 2026 against WPP's -2.8%
      • Generative AI is deflating time-based fees, which are 75% of the business.
    • WPP's own cycle has turned from a deep trough: like-for-like net sales went from -6.9% in the fourth quarter of 2025 and -6.7% in the first quarter of 2026 to -2.8% in the second (-3.8% underlying), and the 500 to 600 basis point drag from announced account losses is dated to FY2026 and rolls off in 2027.
  • The change has travelled four of the six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The people, the choices and the actions are in place, and the operating metrics turned in the second quarter of 2026
    • The financial statements have not turned and the sell-side has moved one notch.
    • Second-quarter like-for-like net sales of -2.8% against a consensus of -6.0%
      • Headline operating profit of £398m against £351m
      • The margin held at 8.4% (+20 basis points) on a falling top line
      • First by net reported billings for net new business in the half.
    • FY2026 headline earnings per share still fall about 16 percent to about 53p from 63.2p
      • The first consensus upgrades since 2022 arrived in August 2026 and lifted FY2026 earnings per share by 4.8% over three months.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned from falling to less bad, not to growth, which is why this Element is a C and the thesis is a B rather than an A.
    • No output line has yet grown, the FY2026 margin is guided at 12% to 13% below FY2025's 13.0%, and the second half is guided down by up to 200 basis points as the incentive pool is rebuilt.
  • What we think it earns.
    • Headline diluted earnings per share of about 53p in FY2026, 58p in FY2027, 66p in FY2028, 73p in FY2029 and 78p in FY2030, about 10 percent a year from FY2027.
    • The growth is a margin story on a flat top line: net sales grow about 2.3 percent a year from FY2027, the headline margin rises from 13.0% to 15.3% by FY2030, and the ratio below operating profit improves a little as the Kantar proceeds cut interest
      • The share count is flat at about 1,078m.
    • That rate sits below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band at every horizon measured from today, so the earnings leg is not what makes the case
      • The price is.
  • What it costs. 378.6p is 6.3x consensus earnings two years forward of 59.9p and 5.9x our own 64.4p, the 12th percentile of the stock's ten-year range (median 8.30x), the 25th percentile of five years (median 7.36x) and the 62nd percentile of two years (median 5.68x).
    • Against the agency pair the discount is 43 percent to Publicis at 11.0x and 13 percent to Havas at 7.2x
      • Only Ipsos at 6.0x is cheaper in the comparison set, and the discount is company-driven, so it is WPP's to close.
    • What is priced is the stabilisation of the second quarter and a flat 2027: consensus net sales are £9,742m for FY2026 and £9,739m for FY2027, and the consensus margin rises only from 12.3% to 12.8% by FY2028.
      • No organic growth, no margin rebuild and no Kantar proceeds are in the price.
  • The return clears the bar at eighteen months and sits at the top of the C band at three years.
    • Valued at 7.0x the earnings two years beyond each horizon, the shares are worth 544p including dividends at eighteen months (27.3 percent a year) and 609p at three years (17.2 percent a year).
    • The three legs are a 4.0 percent dividend yield (15p, the predictable leg), earnings growth of about 10 percent a year on the fiscal-year series, and a re-rating from 5.9x to 7.0x.
    • The 7.0x is a blend at even odds of 8.3x if FY2027 organic growth is reported positive (the ten-year median, the multiple the stock earned as a 15% to 17% margin business) and 5.7x if it is not (the two-year median, the market's price for a WPP that shrinks)
      • The gap between the two branches is 46 percent of value, so the multiple is the swing factor and it is set by one reported condition.
    • On a one-year-forward footing the same path gives 20.5 percent a year at eighteen months and 15.6 percent at three years
      • The two-year convention adds about seven points a year at eighteen months because it reaches the FY2029 and FY2030 margin before the market would normally pay for it, and the eighteen-month A rests on that.
    • The three-year return reaches 20 percent a year only at a price of about 352p, 8 percent below today
      • The eighteen-month return clears the bar at any price up to about 414p.
  • Where the chain breaks is the drop-through of savings into earnings.
    • The value chain is taking fees down as fast as most agencies can take costs out, the company declines to quantify the AI deflation inside its net sales, and the first-half margin was flattered by £51m of lower severance and an incentive pool at a low 1.2 percent of net sales now being rebuilt.
    • If the ratio of net income to operating profit stays at 48 percent instead of rising to 52 percent, the return is 21.4 percent a year at eighteen months and 14.3 percent at three years, and the Element is unchanged.
    • If, in addition, the margin rebuild is halved (FY2029 at 13.5% rather than 14.8%), the return is 14.3 percent a year at eighteen months and 11.4 percent at three years, a C and a D, and the 'Modal return' Element falls to C.
      • That adverse case is plausible on the second-half guidance and the record of the two previous restructurings.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', crisply on the inputs and acceptably on the outputs, with 'Major CfC Clearing' as a secondary that fails only the capitulation test because the price has already moved 45.9% in six months.

  • The case therefore is a plausible, not probable, self-help recovery whose arithmetic clears the bar at eighteen months because the multiple prices decline, and whose foundations argue that the decline may be the right price.

    • We take the bullish side at low conviction, with the shares already 8 percent above the level at which the three-year return clears the bar.

  • Rating = B, because the internal change is material by the framework's own screen several times over, the first output evidence has arrived with the 6 August 2026 interim results, and the 'Modal return' on our path is 27 percent a year at eighteen months and 17 percent a year at three years from a multiple at the 12th percentile of ten years

    • Not A, because earnings-per-share growth on the modal path is about 10 percent a year from FY2027, below the 12 to 20 percent band, the output evidence is one half-year old and the return leans on the re-rating leg
    • Not C, because the breadth of the internal change and the depth of the trough multiple make the change material to the share price if it holds.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The case against, at its strongest: WPP's stabilisation is a temporary cyclical bounce dressed by a cost holiday, and the structure keeps deteriorating beneath it.
      • This is the 'false friend' reading, and the research that tested three competing hypotheses selected it as the best supported.
      • The first-half margin beat came from £51m less severance and an incentive pool at 1.2 percent of net sales, both reversing in the second half by up to 200 basis points.
      • The second quarter's -2.8% was -3.8% underlying, flattered by a 2.9-point easier comparison and by 2026's World Cup and United States midterms.
      • The growth gap to Publicis widened in the same quarter (-2.8% against +4.8%) and has run sixteen quarters
        • The media win-to-loss ratio of 1.3 against Publicis's 3.3 says gross losses continue (IBM and Novo Nordisk went to Omnicom since), and about $3.5bn of billings are under review.
      • Creative is about 35 percent of sales and AI deflates its time-based fees faster than any savings programme
        • The data model is leased while the market pays for owned data, which is why Publicis at 11x looks 'justified' to the sell-side.
      • The chief financial officer resigned seven weeks after the results the research called a turning point
        • The balance sheet at 2.2x cannot buy the capability Publicis bought
        • The previous two restructurings failed on the same promise
        • And the whistleblower suit, if any of it is proven, breaks the trust on which the media arm's margin rests.
      • On that path FY2027 organic growth stays negative, the margin stalls at 12% to 13%, the multiple returns to 5.7x and then toward the 4x to 4.15x that the bear case and the five-year record both hold, and the three-year return is about 5 percent a year with dividends on our uncleared branch, or negative at the trough multiple.
    • What we concede.
      • The first-half margin quality is weak and the second-half step-down is real
      • The second-quarter rate overstates the run-rate by about a point
      • The data deficit is structural and the thesis does not fix it
      • The leadership gap is a live risk
      • The base rate of WPP turnarounds is poor
      • And the price has already taken the easy half of the re-rating.
    • Why we still hold the bullish side, at low conviction.
      • The account-loss drag is arithmetic, not narrative: it is 500 to 600 basis points, it is dated to FY2026, and it rolls off.
      • Both halves of the growth engine have turned: existing-client spend excluding losses is already positive, and first-half net new business ranked first by net billings.
      • The competing hypothesis names its own disconfirming tests, a positive organic quarter and a margin above 13 percent without severance holidays, and both fall inside eighteen months and are observable at the quarterly updates.
      • The entry multiple already prices the competing hypothesis: the sell-side's own reading is that 6x discounts a 10 percent terminal decline in revenue and 10 percent margins.
        • So the modal path need not be probable for the odds to favour the long
        • It need only be plausible, and we put the one condition that sets the multiple at about 50 percent.
      • The downside on the uncleared branch is bounded by a 4.0 percent yield and a multiple already at the two-year median.
    • Where the case against would win.
      • A Coca-Cola loss plus a third-quarter like-for-like worse than -3.5% would take the odds on the one condition to about a third and the blended multiple to about 6.3x, today's price with no return but the yield.
      • That is the pair of dated tests, both inside three months, on which this record turns.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • WPP is a self-help turnaround bought at a trough multiple, a 'Major Corporate Change' case with a 'Major CfC Clearing' shape behind it.
  • A new chief executive from outside the industry has collapsed a holding company of twenty-five agencies into four operating units on one platform, cut the dividend, sold the periphery and committed £500m of gross cost savings, after sixteen quarters in which WPP lost share to Publicis and Omnicom and the market priced it for permanent decline at 6.3x the earnings two years out, the 12th percentile of ten years.
  • The change has travelled through the first four layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain: the people and the structure are in place, the actions are dated, and the operating metrics turned in the second quarter of 2026 (like-for-like net sales -2.8% against -6.0% expected; first for net new business; the margin held at 8.4% on a falling top line).
  • It has not reached the financial statements, since FY2026 earnings fall about 16 percent to about 53p, and it has moved the sell-side one notch, the first upgrades since 2022.
  • The outputs the case needs are modest: net sales that stop falling in 2027 and grow about 2 percent a year, and a headline margin that climbs from 12.3% to about 15% by FY2029 as savings outrun reinvestment and fee deflation.
  • On that path headline earnings per share compound at about 10 percent a year from FY2027, the dividend yields 4.0 percent, and the multiple needs to move only from 5.9x our forward earnings to 7.0x, a blend of a cleared 8.3x and an uncleared 5.7x at even odds, for the return to be 27.3 percent a year over eighteen months and 17.2 percent over three years.
  • It is compelling on the arithmetic and on the breadth of the change, and it is not compelling on the foundations: the value chain is taking fees down as fast as agencies can take costs out, the company's data model is the one the market is not paying for, and two of the three top executives who launched the plan are leaving within its first eighteen months.
  • The one condition that sets the multiple, FY2027 organic growth reported positive, we put at about 50 percent, and two dated tests inside three months (the third-quarter update in late October 2026; the Coca-Cola decision by December 2026) move those odds either way.
  • We take the bullish side at low conviction, with the price already 8 percent above the level at which the three-year return clears the bar.
