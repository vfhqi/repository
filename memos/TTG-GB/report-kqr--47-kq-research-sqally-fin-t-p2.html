Section summary
  1. The Key Question on TT Electronics' pricing power can be partially answered with medium confidence.

Pricing power is the critical determinant of TT Electronics' profit recovery over the next 18 to 36 months, with structural margin expansion relying on successfully passing through rising input costs and executing divisional restructuring. Peer comparisons demonstrate that highly engineered components allow better price retention than transactional products. However, significant gaps remain around the sustainability of Components division margins, the speed of Malaysian footprint ramp-up, and the extent to which contractual co-terms protect volumes versus merely protecting absolute margins.

TT Electronics demonstrated robust pricing power during the 2021-2022 inflationary surge by fully recovering approximately £40 million of cost inflation through re-pricing offerings and collaborative customer negotiations. However, detailed analysis reveals that of this £40 million recovery, approximately £28 million was zero-margin cost pass-through of raw materials, with only a 3% pricing contribution in the 14% organic growth.

This indicates that while the company successfully protected absolute profits, true discretionary pricing power beyond structural pass-through mechanisms remains more limited than management's headline claims suggest.

The European operations achieved a 16.9% increase in adjusted operating profit to £22.1 million in fiscal year 2025, driven by decisive actions to address underperforming European contracts through customer repricing agreements and repricing negotiations. This turnaround demonstrates that targeted commercial interventions can unlock margin progression even when volume recovery remains subdued, providing evidence of genuine pricing leverage in specific legacy contract portfolios.

TT Electronics relies heavily on zero-margin pass-through mechanisms to insulate absolute profitability from extreme raw material price spikes, with 2023 revenue including £19.9 million of zero-margin pass-through revenues that transparently shift severe material cost inflation to customers with no margin mark-up. The scale of these pass-throughs has declined steadily, dropping to £5.3 million in 2024 with projections reaching nil during 2025 as raw material cost spikes subsided. This structural shift helps restore reported adjusted operating margins but underscores the group's dependence on contractual protections rather than pure pricing power.

Under these agreements, the customer effectively picks up additional tariffs when products are imported into the United States.
