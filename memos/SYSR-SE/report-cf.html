EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

This comprehensive analysis of Systemair AB synthesizes the company's strategic positioning, financial trajectory, and forward earnings potential across eleven dedicated analytical sections. The investigation reveals a manufacturing-led ventilation equipment group undergoing simultaneous leadership renewal, portfolio rationalization, and margin expansion while navigating cyclical European construction headwinds and regulatory-driven retrofit tailwinds.

The research examines Robert Larsson's ambitious seven-year salesdoubling mandate, the credibility of the 10% operating margin target, and the sustainability of recent organic growth outperformance relative to Nordic peers Swegon and Lindab. Analysis draws from primary company disclosures, three active sell-side analysts (Jefferies, DNB Carnegie, and Redeye), and peer-relative benchmarking to assess whether Systemair's current 14x EV/EBIT valuation adequately reflects the structural profitability inflection underway.

Leadership transition to CEO Robert Larsson on January 2, 2026 brings fresh industrial scaling expertise from AFRY's SEK 10 billion Infrastructure Division, succeeding Roland Kasper after ten years during which Systemair doubled its sales turnover. CFO Anders Ulff maintains deep institutional continuity since 1999, while board leadership shifted to Patrik Nolåker as Chair in August 2025, though founder Gerald Engström retains

43.5% of capital and votes as Vice Chair. Employee turnover of 17.1% in 2024/25 exceeds industry averages, driven by Menerga's production relocation, while LTIP 2024 and 2025 share incentive programs align executive interests through strict TSR, growth, margin, and ESG targets.

The board mandates CEO Larsson to double group sales within seven years while targeting minimum 10% operating margins over a business cycle, with Q1 2026/27 adjusted operating margins reaching 9.9% putting the target within immediate reach. No active activist presence has been identified in the AlphaSense library, and segment reporting consolidated into two geographical divisions (Europe; Americas/MEA/APAC) enhances transparency.

Portfolio pruning includes the March 2023 divestment of commercial AC business to Panasonic for EUR 100 million generating a SEK 445 million capital gain, exit of Russian operations following the March 2022 invasion with SEK 168 million impairment charges, and restructuring of German and Swedish sales organizations to boost efficiency. Strategic acquisitions include Canada-based Temspec Incorporated (July 2026, SEK 246 million purchase price, SEK 200 million annual revenues with margins exceeding 10% ) and Chennai-based Nadi Airtechnics (August 2025, EUR 13.5 million revenue contribution) to strengthen North American and Indian market positions.

External demand forces reveal that retrofit revenues surged 33% during the fiscal year while commercial ventilation represents approximately 30% of total group revenues and 85% of sales link to commercial buildings. Competition remains fragmented among local niche suppliers and a limited number of large multinational players, with Systemair possessing the broadest ventilation product offering though facing aggressive pricecutting strategies from larger competitors.

Digital disruption progresses through Systemair Access Control enabling remote startup and trend logging, intelligent airflow regulation using sensors for temperature and carbon dioxide, and newly launched energy-efficient axial fans reducing operational energy consumption by 21%. Regulatory drivers include CSRD compliance, EU Taxonomy alignment reaching 28%, updated EPBD guidelines setting tougher requirements for building conversion, and F-Gas regulatory updates driving natural refrigerant adoption.

Supply chain concentrations center on Ziehl-Abegg and ebm-papst for critical fan motor supplies, with delivery interruptions posing group-wide factory production disruption risks, while fluctuating steel plate pricing poses direct gross margin risks. Cyclical exposure shows sales evenly split between new construction and renovation, with European newbuild activity at very low levels though building retrofits and extensions soften the cyclical downturn.

Macro headwinds include rising interest rates adversely affecting net interest expenses, strengthened SEK against TRY, CAD, and INR creating a 1% sales headwind, and North American import tariffs forcing successive price increases on Canadian shipments. Adjusted operating margin reached 9.4% in FY25/26, up from 9.1% in the prior year and exceeding the historic ten-year average operating margin of 7.8%, with DNB Carnegie highlighting that reaching double-digit operating profitability aligns Systemair with historical levels of peers Swegon and Lindab.

Organic growth of 6% in FY25/26 expanded ahead of markets, contrasting with negative organic growth of 1% reported by peers Swegon and Lindab, while group gross margin remained stable at 36.5% in Q1 2026/27 despite rising component and freight costs. Balance sheet strength shows equity/assets ratio of 61.8% in FY25/26 comfortably exceeding the

Board's minimum target of 30% and the ten-year average of 50.1%, with net leverage ending FY25/26 at 0.54x sitting well below the maximum limit of 3.50x. Operating cash flow fell by 16% in Q1 2026/27, hampered by elevated trade receivables and rising safety inventories, while the board proposed a dividend of SEK 1.45 per share representing 39.4% of consolidated profit. Historical earnings trajectory shows SEK 12,503 million in FY2025/26 sales representing strong multi-year compounding, with gross margins structurally stable near 36% despite volatile component expenses, though capital gain distortions from Panasonic AC divestment, pension accounting adjustments unlocking a SEK 25.8 million benefit, and Turkish hyperinflation retrospective adjustments under IAS 29 creating a SEK -26.1 million headwind have distorted underlying operating margin profiles.

Relocating Menerga production to Slovenia triggered SEK 125 million charges in FY24, CEO transition costs of SEK 14.6 million weighed on Q1 2025/26, and management maintains a consistent "Outlook is favourable" heading across quarterly reporting cycles, behaving as an honest-tracker archetype. Q1 2026/27 net sales of SEK 3,281 million matched consensus estimates, expanding 6% year on year, while adjusted EBIT reached SEK 324 million, rising 13% year on year to beat consensus expectations by 4%.

Adjusted operating margin reached 9.9%, up 60 basis points against the 9.3% margin reported in Q1 2025/26, with gross margin remaining stable at 36.5% despite facing higher costs for components and international freight. Operating profit included SEK -13 million net non-recurring items primarily related to U.S. tariff provisions, and 6% organic growth outpaced peers like Swegon and Lindab Ventilation which reported negative organic growth of 1% during the same period.

Geographic performance shows North American organic sales surged 15%, Eastern Europe and CIS delivered 11% organic sales growth, Nordic organic growth rebounded strongly to 8%, while Western European organic growth slowed to 3% as construction markets remain sluggish. European external sales reached SEK 2,354 million generating a strong regional operating margin of 12.9%, while Americas, MEA, and APAC segment reported external sales of SEK 927 million with a lower operating margin of 6.3%.

Jefferies raised its DCF target price to SEK 105 maintaining its Buy rating, DNB Carnegie raised its target price to SEK 100 from SEK 97 reiterating Buy, while Redeye maintained its weighted fair value estimate of SEK 91 per share labeling the stock fairly priced. Operating cash flow fell 16% to SEK 183 million compared to SEK 218 million in Q1 2025/26, with higher safety stock inventories to secure component supplies and increased trade receivables from late-quarter sales growth temporarily constraining working capital efficiency.

Peer-relative analysis reveals Systemair delivered 6% organic top-line growth in Q1 2026/27, outperforming main European ventilation peers, with Nordic competitor Lindab reporting only 1% organic growth in its ventilation segment and Latour-owned Swegon returning to 5% organic growth in H1 2026 supported by strong data centre demand. Systemair successfully implemented price increases to offset higher component expenses, demonstrating superior pricing power over competitors, while Carnegie notes Lindab is experiencing severe pricing pressure in Western Europe capping near-term operating margin recovery.

Net debt to adjusted EBITDA leverage ratio of only 0.65x retains ample dry powder for M&A, with Carnegie highlighting that low leverage provides substantial headroom for future acquisitions, though operating cash flow contraction due to inventory and receivable accumulation temporarily restricts free cash flow generation. Jefferies raised Systemair's DCF target price to SEK 105 reiterating a firm buy rating, Carnegie raised its adjusted EPS estimates by 1-2% for the outer years driven by solid gross margin durability, while Carnegie cut Lindab's adjusted EPS by

6-9% due to persistent cost pressure and Jefferies trimmed its Zehnder price target by 5% on slow residential radiator recovery. Sell-side coverage consists of three active brokers including Carnegie, Jefferies, and Redeye, with prominent regional brokers ABG Sundal Collier, SEB, and Kepler Cheuvreux currently lacking active research reports in the AlphaSense database. Named analysts include Anna Widström at DNB Carnegie, Adela Dashian and Martin Comtesse at Jefferies, and Henrik Alveskog at Redeye, with the current active pool dominated by Buy ratings (Jefferies and Carnegie) while Redeye notes Fairly Priced.

Active price targets span from SEK 91 at Redeye to SEK 100 at Carnegie and SEK 105 at Jefferies, with Jefferies highlighting that the market values Systemair like a cyclical building products business at 14x EV/EBIT 2026/27e. Jefferies employs a DCF model assuming 5% intermediate growth and 11% sustained EBIT margin, Carnegie utilizes a medium-term exit multiple of 14.5x EV/EBITA NTM on its FY2027/28 estimates discounted at 8%, while Redeye uses a blended approach assigning 45% weight to relative multiples and 55% weight to fundamental DCF value.

Jefferies argues the long-term 10% EBIT margin target should be viewed as a floor rather than a peak-cycle ceiling, Carnegie attributes solid growth in the Nordics and North America to internal initiatives and sales reorganisation, while Redeye highlights that elevated safety stock continues to weigh on cash conversion causing Q1 operating cash to drop 16%. Key forward EPS drivers include European retrofit legislation (estimated +5.0% EPS impact, high plausibility and confidence, dependent on public funding) as strict European climate directives mandate building energy retrofits to double regional renovation rates, North American school ventilation (estimated +1.5% EPS impact, high plausibility and confidence, dependent on local tax budgets) as Canada-based Temspec strengthens school and multi-family ventilation market shares, digital platform integration (estimated +0.6% EPS impact, medium plausibility and confidence, dependent on installer adoption), group pricing power (estimated +0.5% EPS impact, high plausibility and confidence, dependent on customer acceptance) as June price increases were successfully implemented to fully offset component cost inflation, Slovenian plant relocation (estimated +0.56% EPS impact, high plausibility and confidence, dependent on local labor ramp-up) as Menerga production relocation to Slovenia is expected to yield SEK 70 million in annual cost savings, and sourcing and motor supply (estimated -0.24% EPS impact, low plausibility and confidence, dependent on supplier deliveries) as fluctuating steel plate pricing poses direct gross margin risks and Systemair relies heavily on ebm-papst and Ziehl-Abegg for critical fan motors.

Management exhibits an honest-tracker archetype as the company does not provide formal short-term quantitative forecasts, with the long-term strategic model targeting at least 10% annual sales growth over an economic cycle and adjusted operating margins reaching 9.9% in Q1 2026/27 bringing the 10% target within immediate reach. Average annual sales growth of 11% since FY11 validates management's 10% growth target, with DNB Carnegie expecting Systemair to reach its 10% operating margin target during the current fiscal year and Jefferies increasingly viewing the 10% operating margin as a floor rather than a peak-cycle ceiling.

Sensitivity analysis reveals a 5% change in average selling prices impacts operating profit by SEK 625 million (±55% EBIT impact), a 5% change in material input costs impacts operating profit by SEK 465 million (±41% EBIT impact), a 5% shift in selling and administration expenses impacts operating profit by SEK 174 million (±15% EBIT impact), and a 5% fluctuation in the SEK/EUR rate impacts net assets by SEK 73 million (±6% EBIT impact).

Jefferies base case models 5% intermediate growth and 11% sustained EBIT margins yielding SEK 105 fair value, bull case assumes 6% growth and a 12% EBIT margin yielding SEK 125 fair value, while bear case assumes 3% growth and a 7% EBIT margin yielding SEK 60 fair value. Net debt to adjusted EBITDA ended the year at 0.54x comfortably below the 3.50x covenant ceiling, with rolling interest coverage standing at a strong 20.33x far exceeding the bank requirement of 3.50.

Historical delivery track record shows Systemair delivered 9.6% organic growth in Q4 2025/26 beating net sales consensus by 8%, though quarterly adjusted operating margins fell to 7.0% in Q3 2025/26 missing consensus EBIT estimates by 18% due to FX, before rebounding in Q1 2026/27 with adjusted EBIT beating consensus by 4% reaching a solid 9.9% margin. Average annual sales growth of 11% since FY11 validates the cycle growth target of no less than 10%, with DNB Carnegie expecting Systemair to reach its 10% EBIT target by the current fiscal year-end, while EBIT margins averaged 7.8% over ten years but adjusted operating margins reached 9.4% in fiscal year 2025/26.

Past earnings misses cluster around relocating Menerga production to Slovenia triggering SEK 125 million charges in FY24, exiting Russia causing impairment charges of SEK 168 million, relying heavily on Ziehl-Abegg and ebm-papst for critical fan motor supplies presenting localized production stoppage risks, Roland Kasper's departure triggering CEO transition costs of SEK 14.6 million, and fluctuating steel prices posing gross margin risks mitigated by June price increases.

The analysis concludes that Systemair's strategic repositioning under new CEO Robert Larsson, combined with portfolio rationalization, regulatory tailwinds from European retrofit mandates, and operational efficiency gains from factory relocations, positions the company to achieve its 10% operating margin target while sustaining mid-single-digit organic growth outperformance versus European peers. However, forward credibility remains highly sensitive to steel and component inflation requiring disciplined price realization, with elevated working capital requirements and high supplier concentration in fan motor manufacturing presenting near-term operational headwinds.

The sell-side consensus reflects increasingly constructive views on margin durability with widespread price target upgrades post-earnings, though the 14x EV/EBIT valuation suggests the market has not fully priced the structural profitability inflection from an aspirational cycle target to an operational baseline.

Plausible problems to monitor

Systemair's operational and financial performance reveals seven critical Plausible problems to monitor where the company demonstrably trails peer medians or its own historical baselines, presenting material risks to forward earnings credibility and investor confidence. Operating cash flow conversion has emerged as a persistent vulnerability, contracting 16% to SEK 183 million in Q1 2026/27 compared to SEK 218 million in the prior-year period, driven by elevated safety stock inventories and rising trade receivables that temporarily constrain working capital efficiency.

This cash generation weakness sits in stark contrast to peers like Volution Group, which continues to deliver superior cash conversion dynamics supported by less cyclical residential repair and maintenance exposure, as detailed in Section 7. profitability imbalances represent a second structural concern, with the Americas, MEA, and APAC segment reporting external sales of SEK 927 million at a materially lower operating margin of 6.3%, dramatically underperforming the European segment's robust 12.9% margin and falling short of the group's consolidated 9.9% adjusted operating margin achieved in Q1 2026/27.

This geographic margin dispersion, explored in Section 6, signals unresolved operational inefficiencies in developing markets that dilute overall group profitability and threaten the Board's 10% cycle margin target.

This dual sourcing fragility and commodity price sensitivity contrasts unfavorably with more vertically integrated peers and exposes Systemair to sudden margin compression absent successful price pass-through mechanisms. Quarterly earnings volatility represents a fifth persistent signal, with adjusted operating margins falling sharply to 7.0% in Q3 2025/26, missing consensus EBIT estimates by 18% due to foreign exchange headwinds, as documented in Section 11.

This intra-year margin swing–from trough levels below 8% to peak levels approaching 10%– reflects the company's project-heavy business model and creates forecast uncertainty for investors attempting to model normalized through-cycle profitability, particularly when management provides no formal short-term quantitative guidance. Geographic growth dispersion emerges as a sixth concern, with Western European organic growth slowing to just 3% in Q1 2026/27, lagging both North America's robust 15% expansion and the Nordic region's 8% rebound, as detailed in Section 6.

This uneven regional momentum exposes Systemair's persistent vulnerability to sluggish commercial construction activity in its largest geographic market, where peers like Lindab face similar pricing pressures but with less offsetting growth from other territories, threatening consolidated top-line delivery if Western European weakness proves protracted. Residential construction exposure represents the seventh aggregate weakness, as Nordic market headwinds from residential construction downturns in Norway negatively impacted regional volume growth, dragging consolidated organic growth down in specific historical periods, as chronicled in Section 5.

This cyclical sensitivity to new-build residential activity–despite management's assertions of balanced exposure between new construction and renovation–creates episodic demand volatility that compounds forecasting challenges and differentiates Systemair negatively from peers with higher repair-maintenance-improvement mix like Volution. These seven Plausible problems to monitor collectively underscore that while Systemair has demonstrated strong topline momentum and margin expansion trajectory toward the 10% target, the company simultaneously faces material operational execution risks in cash conversion, geographic margin homogeneity, labor retention, supplychain resilience, earnings predictability, Western European growth normalization, and residential cycle exposure that distinguish its risk profile unfavorably relative to both peer medians and its own historical performance baselines established over the prior decade.

Outliers worth noting

The historical financial trajectory and operational profile of Systemair reveal several extreme statistical outliers and unexplained operational anomalies that deviate materially from both peer benchmarks and the company's own historical baselines. A pension accounting adjustment in Norway unlocked a SEK 25.8 million benefit during the fourth quarter of FY2022/23, representing an outsized accounting windfall that lacks clear operational precedent within the group's standardized reporting framework and appears disconnected from core ventilation manufacturing performance.

Turkish hyperinflation adjustments under IAS 29 created significant reporting volatility in operating profit, with a SEK -26.1 million headwind in specific periods, introducing extreme quarter-toquarter swings that obscure underlying operational momentum and complicate historical trend analysis relative to peers like Swegon and Lindab that lack material hyperinflationary exposure.

Relocating Menerga production to Slovenia triggered massive one-off charges of SEK 125 million during Q3 2023/24, an exceptionally concentrated restructuring expense that dwarfs typical quarterly operational adjustments and represents approximately 11% of full-year FY2023/24 adjusted EBIT, raising questions about the original strategic rationale for the Menerga acquisition and the timing of this late-stage manufacturing realignment.

Divesting the low-margin commercial air conditioning business to Panasonic in FY2022/23 yielded a massive one-off profit of SEK 445 million, creating a temporary surge in reported earnings that far exceeded normalized annual net income and distorted year-over-year profitability comparisons, yet management provided limited disclosure on the strategic triggers for exiting this adjacent HVAC category at that specific valuation inflection point.
