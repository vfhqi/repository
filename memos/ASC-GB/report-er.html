Section 1 – Brief company description and history 12 findings

Section summary
  1. ASOS has transitioned from a high-growth retail consolidator to a highly disciplined, margin-focused operator, striving to rebuild its unit economics after severe post-pandemic overstocking. Corporate origin – early inception: ASOS was originally launched in June 2000 by founders Nick Robertson and Quentin Griffiths under the online name "asSeenonScreen". The initial business model focused on selling apparel replicating outfits worn by celebrities on television. This early setup evolved rapidly,

prompting the company to simplify its public identity and rename itself ASOS plc by 2003. The group officially transitioned to a listed entity through a reverse takeover of Brindle Ltd on the AIM market

37 38 39 Product expansion – category growth: The business achieved a major milestone in 2004 by introducing its very first own-label womenswear products. Following this success, the brand expanded further by launching ASOS Beauty in 2005 and its initial own-label collection for men in 2007. Over the subsequent decade, the company grew its active customer base six-fold to establish itself as a dominant online pureplay. This expansion was supported by the launch of premier labels like ASOS DESIGN and COLLUSION to consolidate its market position

Target demographic – generation focus: ASOS operates as a global online fashion retailer solely dedicated to serving fashion-loving 20-somethings. Management estimates that approximately 80% of its customer base consists of Gen-Z or Millennial shoppers. The platform delivers highly relevant, trend-led clothing at accessible price points to a global young adult audience. This targeted demographic demands continuous product freshness, requiring high agility in supply chain execution

Strategic shift – profitability focus: For several years, the group's historical business model prioritized rapid top-line volume growth fueled by aggressive promotional discounting. This legacy expansion strategy relied heavily on excessive stock availability, heavy markdowns, and expensive performance marketing. However, following severe overstocking post-pandemic, CEO José Antonio Ramos Calamonte initiated a comprehensive transformation toward capital discipline and margin recovery. The platform now optimizes for order profitability rather than absolute revenue volume
5.
Operating model – strategic restructuring: The company's turnaround is structured around its "Driving Change" agenda, focusing on speed and margin protection. This strategy leverages three key operational pillars consisting of Relevant Fashion Product, an Inspirational Shopping Experience, and an Efficient Operating Model. To support this transition, the group has scaled its flexible Test & React sourcing model to capture emerging fashion trends in real-time. This approach reduces absolute inventory risk by sourcing products closer to customer demand
6.
Fundamental decline – transition pressures: In fiscal year 2023, the group faced massive headwind pressures as revenue declined to £3.55bn. This decline reflected a deliberate reduction in less profitable sales alongside high inflation. The company recorded an EBITA loss of -£249m due to heavy clearance activity of aged stock. Despite the top-line contraction, the group began reducing inventory by approximately 30% under its initial driving change initiatives
7.
Operating progress – structural clearance: During fiscal year 2024, the top-line contraction intensified with revenue falling to £2.91bn. This drop was severely impacted by the execution of a massive commercial model stock write-off to clear legacy collections. Despite the sales deleverage, the group delivered a positive Adjusted EBITDA of £80.1m. This result confirmed that the underlying unit economics were starting to stabilize under the new model
8.
Margin expansion – profitable turnaround: In fiscal year 2025, the group's transition to a profitable model bore significant fruit despite revenue falling further to £2.48bn. Adjusted gross margin expanded by a substantial 370 basis points year-over-year to 47.1% owing to a lower markdown mix. Adjusted EBITDA surged more than 60% year-over-year to reach £132m. This delivery was driven by improved fullprice sell-through and extensive supply chain opex efficiencies
