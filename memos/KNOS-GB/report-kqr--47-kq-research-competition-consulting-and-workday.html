Section 1 – Key question and brief 7 findings

Section summary
  1. The key question is highly resolvable through current broker research, corporate reporting, and partner ecosystem data, exposing a bifurcated trajectory across Kainos's core divisions. The verbatim key question under examination is: how is competitive intensity changing, and what risks does it create, for Kainos Group plc (KNOS-GB) in (a) its UK consulting and digital delivery business (Digital Services, mostly UK government, won through frameworks such as Digital Outcomes and Specialists) and (b) its Workday services and products business, over the last six months (April to October 2026) and the next 12 months? The higher intent driving this research is to equip a concentrated, long-only equity investor with the structural insight required to assess whether Kainos can deliver a predictable 18-month to 3-year earnings per share trajectory capable of supporting a 25% or greater annualized internal rate of return. Adjacent and sequential key questions include whether the rapid, high-margin scaling of Workday Products annual recurring revenue can successfully offset temporary subcontractor cost headwinds, and how the return of

co-founder Brendan Mooney as chief executive officer will reshape operational execution and medium-term capital allocation. its current stage, Kainos is navigating a complex operational transition, marked by robust public sector bookings and rapid software-as-a-service product scaling, alongside near-term margin pressure from an expanded contractor workforce.

Core Investment Context and Higher Intent

1.
Bifurcated growth: Kainos delivered strong top-line momentum with revenue growing 17% to £431.1 million in the fiscal year ended 31 March 2026
2.
Robust bookings: The company entered the current period with excellent operational visibility as overall bookings increased 32% to £505.3 million and backlog rose 18% to £433.9 million
3.
Margin pressure: Near-term profitability is constrained by elevated contractor reliance, which dragged adjusted PBT margins down to 15.6% from 17.9% in the prior year
4.
SaaS scaling: The high-margin Workday Products division remains a primary driver of long-term value, with annual recurring revenue exceeding £89 million at year-end and pacing toward a £100 million calendar 2026 target
5.
Leadership transition: Investor confidence is supported by the return of co-founder Brendan Mooney as chief executive officer, who previously led the group's transformational growth from 2001 to 2023
6.
Discretionary delays: The broader consulting landscape exhibits mixed signals, as peers like Capgemini and Accenture have faced delays to large-scale implementation projects as corporate clients defer discretionary spending
7.
& Co Redburn Other side of the story: If the competitive intensity question remains unresolvable, the investor faces the risk that margin compression is structural rather than transient, meaning the historical high-multiple rating cannot be sustained if global integrators permanently depress public framework pricing

Section 2 – Competitive Dynamics in UK Digital Services 12 findings

Section summary
  1. Aggressive bidding by displaced commercial integrators has diluted Kainos's historical public framework share, but a strategic pivot to larger prime contracts is driving a robust recovery in booking momentum.

Public Framework Shifts and Market Share Realities

1.
Framework share dilution: Deutsche Bank Research reports that Kainos's share of DOS contract value fell to 4.6% in 2023 and 2024 out of the £2.4 billion awarded
2.
Aggressive competitor penetration: Large global systems integrators, specifically Capgemini and PA Consulting, have successfully gained market share from Kainos within the UK government frameworks
3.
Diversified procurement routes: The emergence of alternative procurement frameworks like the £4.2 billion DALAS and £1.2 billion DIPS frameworks has reduced government reliance on the historic DOS framework
4.
Niche volume dominance: Rothschild & Co Redburn emphasizes that Kainos continues to dominate its niches, winning twice as much DOS business as its nearest competitor over three years
5.
Healthcare sector outperformance: Kainos achieved exceptional healthcare revenue growth of 55% to £74.9 million in the fiscal year ended 31 March 2026, driven by massive NHS England contract wins
6.
Disciplined bid selection: Kainos is actively avoiding projects where price is overly dominant to protect its services margins from aggressive public sector tender pricing

Commercial Displacement and Defensive Pivots

7.
Aggressive pricing pressure: Deutsche Bank Research notes that global systems integrators, facing soft commercial demand, diverted resources to the public sector and initiated aggressive 5% to 10% underbidding
8.
Prime contractor evolution: In response to larger public tenders, Kainos is increasingly acting as the prime contractor overseeing third-party consortia, improving commercial control and predictable delivery
9.
Fixed-price contract growth: Fixed-price engagements rose to 20% of Digital Services revenue in the fiscal year 2026, representing a significant 44% year-on-year increase
10.
Commercial capacity redeployment: Kainos strategically deprioritized acquiring new commercial clients, allowing staff to be redeployed into higher-growth public sector and NHS healthcare opportunities
11.
Canadian public expansion: International growth provides an offset, with Canadian digital revenues surging 127% to £20.2 million following the successful integration of the Davis Pier acquisition
12.
Other side of the story: If global integrators persist with underbidding, Kainos's public sector margins face structural compression that high contractor costs in the initial phase of recovery will exacerbate

Section 3 – Competitive Dynamics in the Workday Partner Ecosystem 13 findings

Section summary
  1. Exponential growth in the Workday partner channel has triggered standard implementation pricing declines, even as Workday aggressively transitions its core services to partner-led delivery.

Ecosystem Expansion and Margin Compression

1.
Aggressive partner expansion: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that Workday's partner ecosystem has expanded dramatically to more than 1,300 unique partners globally
2.
Certified talent scaling: Jefferies Research reports steady expansion in delivery capacity, noting that the number of certified Workday professionals grew by a weighted average of 2.9% in the second quarter of fiscal 2027
3.
UK market crowding: Expert interviews indicate intense local competition as the number of active UK partner organizations has climbed from 60 to 114 in a compressed timeframe, creating a highly saturated regional delivery landscape
4.
Severe pricing deflation: Industry channel checks reveal that standard implementation pricing has experienced declines of 20% to 25% year-on-year as standard deployments commoditize
5.
Contract size compression: A systems integration lead confirms that average deal sizes for new Workday projects have contracted 10% to 15% year-on-year due to client-side budget constraints
6.
Partner margin squeeze: Morgan Stanley notes that while Workday actively discounts software licensing to win business, delivery partners are under pressure to lower implementation rates to remain competitive in mid-market engagements

Shifting Channel Strategies and Mid-Market Push

7.
Strategic services offloading: Bernstein Research reports that Workday is deliberately keeping its professional services revenue flat-to-down, projecting FY27 services revenue of $710 million as it leverages partner delivery
8.
Pervasive partner reliance: A technology consulting director confirms that under Carl Eschenbach's leadership, Workday is pushing standard implementation projects 100% into the partner ecosystem on purpose
9.
Accelerated mid-market deployment: Bernstein Research highlights that the newly launched Workday GO suite slashes implementation times to 30-60 days via standardized, preconfigured templates
10.
Predictable budget appeal: A global consulting partner reports that Workday GO compresses sales cycles by 20% to 35% by offering mid-market buyers highly predictable, fixed-price deployment costs
11.
Deployment hours reduction: Morgan Stanley highlights that Workday's generative AI-powered Deployment Agent is already delivering an estimated 30% reduction in implementation hours on active projects
12.
Partner sentiment divergence: Jefferies Research surveys show emerging friction, with the percentage of partners believing the partner program needs improvement rising to 26% as Workday occasionally competes for services
13.
Other side of the story: If Workday's partner-led strategy succeeds in fully commoditizing mid-market deployments, boutique integrators like Kainos face structural erosion of services margins as preconfigured packages and AI-driven deployment tools permanently lower the billable hours required per project

Section 6 – Data gaps 7 findings

Section summary
  1. Material reporting gaps in segment pricing and contractor cost details limit immediate forecast precision, but systematic channel checks and public portal monitoring can effectively resolve these key intelligence blind spots

Segment Disclosure Gaps and Resolutions

1.
Unreported pricing details: Kainos does not publish standard hourly rates or average discount levels in its Workday Services business. This pricing opacity makes measuring severe ecosystem price pressure difficult without direct expert consultation
2.
Undisclosed co-selling financials: Company reports do not specify the individual fee structures or profit- sharing percentages under the expanded Workday co-selling partnership. Analysts must rely on monitoring future company filings to model long-term software licensing economics
3.
Contractor margin distortion: Standard financial statements combine permanent staff salaries and external contractor fees within cost of sales. A programmatic wait-and-monitor approach to track interim contractor additions is necessary to gauge margin recovery
4.
International profitability opacity: Financial reports do not segment operating margins for nascent geographies like Canada from broader digital consulting. Resolving this requires conducting systematic executive interviews or waiting for regional subsidiary filings

Commercial Contract Visibility Gaps

5.
Unspecified win metrics: Kainos does not publish its precise bid-to-win ratios for individual UK frameworks like Digital Outcomes and Specialists. This metric gap can be bridged by tracking public tender portals to compile all single-supplier awards
6.
Mid-market template traction: Public reporting fails to isolate bookings or contract wins generated through the newly launched Workday GO suite. Resolving this requires initiating targeted expert calls with Workday implementation leads to verify pipeline adoption
7.
Other side of the story: Attributing these data gaps to incomplete analyst scope overlooks deliberate corporate nondisclosure designed to prevent competitors from undercutting premium services pricing on public frameworks
