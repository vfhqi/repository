  2. Stiff competition from Ibiden, Shinko, and Unimicron threatens AT&S's volume share unless the company improves advanced packaging yields.

Dissecting Conflicting Supplier Allocation Reports

1.
Exclusive supplier selection: Deutsche Bank's report of 2 July 2026 argues that Intel has selected only Ibiden and AT&S for high-volume EMIB-T substrates to support complex packaging. This select group represents a tiny circle because EMIB-T demands the world's most complex substrate to embed silicon bridges. Securing this exclusivity would propel AT&S's microelectronics revenue because it shuts out larger Asian competitors.
2.
Broader supplier base: Jefferies' report of 30 July 2026 claims that Intel's EMIB-T substrate allocation will be distributed equally among Ibiden, Shinko, and Unimicron. This assessment does not name AT&S as an immediate high-volume beneficiary, which directly contradicts Deutsche Bank's exclusive selection thesis. If this broader allocation model is correct, AT&S faces the risk of lower capacity utilization at its newly expanded Kulim plants.
3.
Reconciling supplier lists: Morgan Stanley's report of 25 May 2026 bridges these views by identifying Ibiden, Unimicron, Shinko, and AT&S as the core group of substrate suppliers. The analyst notes that Samsung Electro-Mechanics could also be added as a fifth supplier to mitigate supply chain bottlenecks. This implies that Intel is actively qualifying multiple sources to ensure sufficient advanced packaging capacity by 2028.
4.
Assessing technological readiness: Bernstein Research's note of 14 September 2026 states that while Ibiden remains the most experienced substrate partner, Unimicron is still in the learning phase. The report indicates that Shinko is also progressing well in substrate yield improvement, but Unimicron needs another year of development before mass production. This technological gap means AT&S can capture early share if its Kulim K2 facility ramps smoothly.
