Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Unlocking TKH Group's valuation discount depends on successfully separating its asset-light Automation division from capital-intensive Electrification cabling.
  2. Delivering on medium-term strategic targets will determine whether the group can rebuild its market credibility and drive double-digit growth.

Framing the feedback loops

1.
Formulated core problem: "Which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"
2.
Ambitious structural transition: TKH Group is transitioning from a complex conglomerate into a dedicated, asset-light automation leader by carve-out of its cabling business. This separation aims to eliminate the historical conglomerate discount of approximately 30% that has long depressed its share price. Reinvesting the proceeds into the Automation business is expected to accelerate its organic earnings per share growth through high-margin acquisitions.
3.
Operational margin recovery: The success of the cable division's separation depends on scaling subsea cable production at the Eemshaven facility. Earlier teething problems and startup losses in 2025 severely depressed Electrification margins and delayed output. Management expects that achieving optimal utilization levels at Eemshaven will expand segment margins and unlock a higher valuation multiple from potential trade buyers or listing investors.

Capital allocation and growth drivers

4.
Strict capital hierarchy: Proceeds from the separation and a €250 million non-core divestment program are earmarked for high-return reinvestments. However, completing the authorized 10% share buyback remains gated on keeping the leverage ratio below 2.0x. Resolving this capital-allocation loop will directly impact near-term earnings per share expansion unless acquisition multiples exceed expectations.
5.
Cyclical manufacturing demand: VMI tire-building machinery experiences highly cyclical demand with over 40% of competitor machines being older than 20 years. Deferrals of major capital expenditures by Tier 1 tire manufacturers in 2025 and 2026 have restricted order intake. If these delayed projects trigger a substantial replacement wave in 2027 and 2028, Automation's profitability will surge.

This massive decline suggests that the anticipated recovery in tire-building machinery may face persistent delays, which would severely restrict the group's near-term automation revenue growth and EPS recovery.
7.
Divergent research intensity: TKH's software consolidation under the Allied Vision and LMI brands seeks to raise cross-selling and software attach rates. However, the group's research and development spending of approximately 5% of sales is significantly lower than major vision peers. Sell-side analysts warn that this constrained research intensity could weaken long-term competitive edge against peers.

Restoring market credibility: Beating the qualitative 2026 guidance framework is critical for TKH to rebuild its depressed valuation multiple. Historical precedents from 2021 and 2022 show that consistent operational delivery triggered substantial valuation re-ratings and share price appreciation. Failing to deliver on these targets would likely prolong investor skepticism and prevent the stock from narrowing its 46% peer discount in the medium term.

Section 2 – The Eemshaven Utilisation Loop

Section summary
  1. Scaling production at Eemshaven represents a critical reinforcing feedback loop driving margin expansion.
  2. Delays in offshore wind farm construction can trigger the reversal of this loop through deferred cable call-offs.
  3. Strong operational recovery in Electrification supports the planned dual-track separation.

Operational scaling step by step

1.
Substantial capacity expansion: TKH Group invested over €150 million to complete its state-of-the-art subsea cable manufacturing facility in Eemshaven. The plant was built to expand the company's annual inter-array production capacity from 600 kilometres to a maximum of 1,200 kilometres. This scale is expected to help the group capture a 20% share of the European subsea high-voltage market. Achieving high utilisation of this capacity is critical to driving structural gross margin expansion over the near term.
2.
Teething production issues: The Eemshaven facility experienced output issues and technical startup complexities during 2025. These delays limited total production output and adversely impacted group profitability, leading to a temporary sub-committee being formed for oversight. TKH Group managed to resolve most critical processes by the end of 2025, enabling the successful implementation of key line upgrades. Improving these operational yields is essential to transitioning the plant to serial manufacturing successfully.
3.
Operational margin recovery: Operational improvements at Eemshaven helped Electrification turnover grow by 33.3% organically to €328.2 million in the first half of 2026. This revenue surge expanded the segment's adjusted EBITDA margin to 12.6% from 8.9% in the prior year period. Management is targeting a further profitability recovery toward an EBITDA margin of over 19% in the medium term. Higher capacity utilisation will determine if the company can sustain this strong margin trajectory through 2028.

If similar financing constraints spread to offshore wind developers, Eemshaven's planned ramp-up could face material delays in the near term.

Bankability and project execution: Late design changes, environmental permitting delays, and logistical bottlenecks are increasingly weakening lender confidence in offshore wind. These structural constraints force developers to carry high costs without returns, directly influencing project bankability and final investment decisions. Because cable suppliers are critical to a project's execution certainty, execution issues can trigger substantial contract penalties. A project's failure to secure timely financing would stall the subsea order book of cable manufacturers.

Carve out and valuation

10.
Breakup of the conglomerate: TKH Group is currently executing a dual-track process to separate its Electrification business, which is scheduled for completion in late 2026 or early 2027. Shareholders approved this separation at the September 2026 Extraordinary General Meeting to help accelerate the group's transition into an asset-light Automation leader. This strategic move is expected to unlock significant value by eliminating the conglomerate discount. The success of this transaction depends heavily on proving the operational viability of the Eemshaven plant.

Divestment valuation targets: Jefferies estimates the standalone value of TKH's Electrification division at approximately €0.8 billion, representing over 50% of the group's total market capitalization. This valuation is based on a multiple of 7.5x estimated fiscal year 2026 EV/EBITDA. Broker research assumes that Electrification profitability will successfully recover toward its medium-term EBITA margin target of 12% to 15% by 2028. Proving Eemshaven's stable capacity output is critical to achieving this premium valuation from potential buyers.
12.
Other side of the story: While the Electrification carve-out aims to maximize shareholder value, divesting the business means losing the group's fastest-growing segment. Electrification delivered a stellar 81.6% turnover growth in Offshore Energy during the first half of 2026, serving as the primary growth engine. If the remaining parent company cannot generate comparable standalone organic growth in its core Automation segments, the planned re-rating of TKH shares may fail to materialize.
