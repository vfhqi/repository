Openreach broadband line losses of 175k were 12% better than the 198k consensus. The pension deficit fell from £3.7bn to £2.7bn, and distribution headroom was set at up to £1.5bn in FY27, rising to up to £2.0bn by FY30. These items lower the risk around the cost base and the balance sheet. The report holds no explicit EPS consensus revision data, so the direction of the EPS change is an inference from cost, pension and volume drivers.

The evidence supports a modestly firmer, better-protected EPS trajectory whose payoff still depends on second-half cash delivery and on revenue quality.

Structural contradictions. The first contradiction is between top-line headline and top-line quality. Revenue growth was the first in two and a half years, yet the beat was attributed to higher retail handset revenues.
