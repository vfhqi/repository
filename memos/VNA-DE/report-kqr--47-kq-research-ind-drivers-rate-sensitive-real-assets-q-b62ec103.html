Part 1: Macroeconomic Context & Sovereign Yield Dynamics

The European interest-rate-sensitive real asset sectors–encompassing residential and commercial real estate, regulated utilities, renewable energy generators, telecommunications infrastructure, transport concessions, and homebuilders–currently trade near the bottom of their historical 10-year valuation ranges. This underperformance has been driven by persistent central bank tightening and upward pressure on sovereign yield curves. While operating fundamentals across many of these industries remain resilient, share price performance continues to exhibit a high negative correlation to long-term government yields. Key macroeconomic data points and yield shifts across Europe include:

1.
Sovereign Yield Pressures: Spanish 10-year government bond yields have hovered around 4% –the highest level in its recent listing history.
2.
Regional Yield Spikes: Yields have moved sharply higher, with 10-year sovereign rates rising by over 100 bps across major European jurisdictions, including increases of 120 bps in France and 105 bps in Italy.
3.
Yield proxy correlations: High-rate-sensitivity stocks, such as German residential giant Vonovia, have shown a -90% correlation ($80 % \ R^2$) with the 10-year Bund yield, trading essentially as liquid bond proxies. Similarly, National Grid exhibits a short-term correlation of -0.50 with UK gilt yields.
