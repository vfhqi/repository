EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 findings

Redes Energéticas Nacionais (RENE) presents a defensive regulated utility investment case anchored by a transformative regulatory re-rating and structural de-risking of its electricity transmission business, which accounts for 70% of consolidated EBITDA. The company operates within a highly supportive Portuguese regulatory framework established by ERSE for the 2026-29 period, delivering a pre-tax nominal WACC of 6.94% for electricity transmission and 6.70% for distribution networks.

Management has successfully pivoted toward capital-light operational efficiency following activist investor Impactive Capital's intervention in December 2024, which forced a €40 million share buyback program and strategic focus on positive free cash flow generation. The forward investment case is underpinned by a 25% increase in annual capital expenditure to €450-550 million, targeting a 3.8% domestic RAB CAGR through 2030, while Portuguese fiscal reforms including CESE special energy tax relief unlock an estimated €40 million net profit uplift in 2026.

The activist shareholder dynamic materially influences capital allocation priorities, with US-based Impactive Capital holding a 10.1% stake and issuing a formal public letter urging immediate share repurchases due to a valuation compression from 11.7x to 6.1x forward EBITDA between 2019 and 2024. The company responded swiftly by launching the buyback program, though founder-CEO René Moos retains an 11.7% stake, creating potential alignment tensions between founder-led management and activist demands for enhanced shareholder returns. RENE's leverage profile remains elevated at 4.84x net debt to EBITDA, significantly above peer Enagás at 3.66x, which constrains near-term capital allocation flexibility despite offering a resilient 4.1% dividend yield supported by stable regulated earnings. The forward earnings trajectory is driven by five high-conviction catalysts: first, the ERSE electricity transmission WACC framework provides a +10.00% estimated earnings impact with HIGH plausibility, anchored by the 6.94% pre-tax nominal allowed return; second, domestic grid capex acceleration targeting €450-550 million annually delivers an estimated +3.80% RAB CAGR with HIGH plausibility despite medium confidence due to potential supply chain constraints; third, CESE special energy tax relief unlocks a +€40 million net profit impact with HIGH plausibility and HIGH confidence following enactment of the 2026 Portuguese State Budget Law; fourth, hydrogen capex de-prioritization reduces gas investments by 50% with HIGH plausibility, reflecting commercial-scale hydrogen deployment delays to 2032; and fifth, sovereign yield indexation creates a +/-30 bps rate-of-return adjustment per 100 bps bond movement with HIGH plausibility but medium confidence given evolving Eurozone monetary policy.

These drivers exhibit strong cross-driver dependencies, particularly between capex expansion and sovereign yield dynamics, which jointly determine the profitability of the regulatory asset base growth program.

RENE's recent delivery trajectory demonstrates operational resilience, with FY25 EBITDA reaching €516 million, representing a stable 2% year-on-year increase that matched consensus estimates through asset base expansion and opex remuneration. Peer comparison reveals modest growth relative to Redeia's 4% EBITDA growth to €1,258 million in FY25, though management targets average annual EBITDA of €540-560 million over the 2026-27 period, aligning with analyst expectations of €551 million for the same timeframe.

High-voltage electricity transmission revenues drove an 11% EBITDA increase in the first quarter of 2026, validating the regulatory transition's positive momentum. The historical execution track record positions management as an honest-tracker archetype, with RENE meeting its 2021 financial targets despite pandemic conditions, reducing average cost of debt to 2.5% in 2025, and delivering FY25 EBITDA of €516.10 million representing a modest 0.21% beat versus guidance.

However, grid infrastructure projects suffered execution delays in 2022, classified as self-inflicted operational shortfalls, though the company successfully accelerated capex in 2023 to recover prior delays while maintaining consistent dividend execution and reducing net debt by 4.8% to €2.42 billion in

2023.

Forward guidance credibility is rated HIGH, supported by RENE's operation under a highly visible regulated asset base framework that de-risks forward estimates through multi-year EBITDA targets of €540-560 million and recurrent net income targets of €150-160 million for the 2026-27 period. Management maintains a 15.4 cents minimum dividend guidance aligned with the Strategic Plan, operating as a disciplined utility that consistently meets or modestly beats conservative long-term projections.

The company demonstrates strong baseline accuracy, with historical variances showing FY25 recurrent net profit of €159.8 million growing 8.2% year-onyear and a recommended gross dividend of 0.160 euros per share, following historical policy limits. EPS sensitivity analysis reveals material exposure to regulatory WACC movements (50 bps increase delivers +6.64% EPS impact), interest rate fluctuations (100 bps increase creates -8.82% EPS drag), and Portuguese inflation indexation (100 bps increase provides +2.65% EPS support), with a sufficient margin of safety provided by the

WACC floor of 5.29% that shields earnings from severe compression scenarios. Sell-side positioning reflects a highly concentrated coverage pool led primarily by Bernstein Research's Jorge Alonso Suils, who upgraded RENE to Outperform from Market-Perform in October 2025 with a current price target of €4.20, representing significant upward revisions from prior €3.70 and €3.15 targets. The upgrade trajectory was catalyzed by the electricity transmission draft de-risking 70% of EBITDA, favorable Portuguese State Budget Law CESE relief, and the upgraded capital expenditure forecast to €450-550 million.

Analyst price targets have moved structurally upward, driven by the updated electricity transmission framework and tax breaks, with valuation methodology applying a sum-of-the-parts discounted cash flow approach using 3.7%-3.9% WACC assumptions anchored to sovereign yields. The dominant bull narrative emphasizes accelerated grid integration of renewable capacity to resolve severe Iberian solar price cannibalization, while sceptical bear views highlight persistently lower regulated asset base growth compared to high-growth European network peers and lingering concerns over the Portuguese special energy tax compressing net profitability despite recent relief measures.

Fundamental peer ranking positions RENE in a defensive middle tier, with EBITDA margins at sector-leading 77.30% in FY23, outperforming diversified gas network operators by over 1,000 basis points and exceeding the regulated networks average by over 1,500 basis points. However, top-line acceleration lags high-growth peers, with FY23 revenue growth of 10.80% trailing Terna's 15.00% and Elia Group's projected 29.93% for FY26.

Peer comparison reveals RENE's modest organic trajectory relative to Redeia's 4.72% first-quarter 2026 revenue expansion and Enagás's 8.20% gas demand recovery, though RENE's exceptional margin performance and stable margin control compared to Enagás's compressed 64.91% EBITDA margin and Elia's diluted 42.83% margin demonstrates superior operational efficiency. The stock trades at an attractive 17% premium to 2026e RAB, representing a discount to Terna's 29% premium and National Grid's 62% RAB premium, highlighting RENE's cheaper relative valuation despite elevated leverage constraints.

Plausible problems to monitor

RENE's leverage profile remains elevated at 4.84x net debt to EBITDA, materially exceeding peer Enagás's 3.66x ratio and constraining near-term capital allocation flexibility during a period when the company is simultaneously attempting to execute an ambitious €450-550 million annual capital expenditure program. This combination of high leverage and stepped-up investment requirements creates a structural vulnerability that distinguishes RENE negatively from better-capitalized European grid operators.

The balance sheet constraint is particularly concerning given that forward net debt is projected to rise further to €2.6-2.7 billion by 2027 to fund capital expenditures, with high investment cycles implying that free cash flows will remain negative for several years, mirroring the challenged cash generation profile of peer Elia. The company's growth trajectory suffers from a fundamental momentum deficit relative to high-performing European infrastructure peers.

REN delivered FY25 EBITDA of €516 million representing a stable 2% year on year increase, materially lagging peer Redeia's EBITDA growth of 4% to €1,258 million. This growth underperformance becomes more pronounced when examining regulated asset base expansion dynamics, where RENE's 3.8% domestic RAB CAGR projection through 2030 significantly trails the more aggressive capital deployment schedules of peers like Elia Group, which is targeting infrastructure investment that drives sector-leading revenue growth trajectories approaching 30% in forward periods. A critical regulatory vulnerability centers on the annual indexation mechanism linking RENE's allowed rate of return to Portuguese sovereign bond yields. The annual indexing of allowed returns to sovereign yields leaves RENE highly vulnerable to rapid monetary easing; if Portuguese bond yields fall below 2.0%, the pre-tax WACC would compress significantly, neutralizing the benefit of RENE's high capex cycle.

This sensitivity creates an inverse relationship between favorable monetary policy conditions and RENE's earnings power, a structurally unfavorable dynamic that introduces significant forward earnings volatility. The regulatory framework's 30 basis point adjustment in allowed return for every 100 basis point movement in sovereign yields means that a normalization of Portuguese government borrowing costs would immediately pressure the company's ability to maintain its 4.1% dividend yield without eroding dividend coverage ratios.

The operational execution track record reveals latent project delivery risks that could derail the forward capital deployment schedule. Grid infrastructure projects suffered execution delays in 2022, though RENE accelerated CAPEX in 2023 to recover from prior project delays; however, past project delays suggest hidden operational bottlenecks, and if supply chains tighten, capex execution will fail, breaking the RAB growth trajectory.

These historical stumbles become particularly material when considered alongside broader European equipment procurement and grid component delays that are hindering planned capital expenditure deployment across the utilities sector. The company's ability to execute its elevated €450-550 million annual investment program remains unproven at this scale, and any material slippage would simultaneously disappoint regulatory asset base growth expectations while failing to address the critical grid congestion bottlenecks that currently afflict 90% of the Iberian electricity distribution grid.

RENE faces a structural competitive disadvantage in the form of its relatively concentrated Portuguese market exposure compared to geographically diversified European grid operators. The company operates in a market characterized by consolidated grid ownership where five dominant players control 95% of Iberian electricity distribution RAB, limiting RENE's ability to capture market share gains through geographic expansion or opportunistic acquisitions.

This concentration risk is compounded by intense concession bidding pressure from smaller agile firms actively targeting renewable connections, threatening legacy player market shares in nextgeneration grid infrastructure development. The forward investment case carries significant execution dependencies across multiple drivers that must simultaneously materialize for the thesis to deliver. The company's earnings trajectory relies on the convergence of ERSE electricity transmission remuneration stability, domestic grid capex acceleration, CESE special energy tax relief persistence, hydrogen capex de-prioritization, and favorable sovereign yield indexation dynamics.

Any single driver failure, particularly around tax policy reversals or accelerated monetary easing, would disproportionately impact the consolidated earnings outlook given the high degree of cross-driver dependency embedded in management's forward guidance framework. The bull case implicitly assumes that Portuguese fiscal and monetary authorities will maintain current policy trajectories over a multi-year period, an assumption that introduces meaningful political and macroeconomic execution risk into what is ostensibly a regulated utility investment.

Outliers worth noting

The financial report on Redes Energéticas Nacionais (RENE) reveals several statistical anomalies and unexplained operational metrics that warrant particular attention from investors and analysts. These outliers span regulatory dynamics, financial positioning, operational execution, and peer comparisons, collectively highlighting areas of structural divergence from typical utility sector patterns. The most striking anomaly concerns RENE's exceptional EBITDA margin of 77.30% in FY23, which represents a massive 1,500 basis point premium over the regulated networks average and substantially exceeds peer operators such as Redeia, Enagás, and Elia Group.

This margin performance is statistically unusual for a transmission and distribution operator, particularly when contextualized against peer Redeia's FY25 EBITDA growth of 4% to €1,258 million and Enagás's leverage of 3.66x. The magnitude of this margin advantage raises questions about the sustainability of cost structures and whether RENE benefits from unique regulatory accounting treatments not available to comparable European grid operators.

The lack of detailed operational cost breakdowns in public filings leaves this margin outlier partially unexplained. A second critical anomaly emerges from RENE's elevated leverage profile, with net debt of €2.5 billion representing approximately 4.84x adjusted net debt to EBITDA. This ratio is notably higher than peer benchmarks and sits uncomfortably alongside the company's ambitious forward capex targets of €450-550 million annually, which imply that free cash flows will remain negative for several years.

The combination of above-peer leverage and sustained negative free cash flow during a high interest rate environment creates a financial profile outlier within the European regulated utilities space. This positioning becomes more puzzling when juxtaposed with RENE's 4.1% dividend yield, which sits materially below Spanish peer Redeia's 5.3% and Enagás's 6.7%, suggesting that either the market questions dividend sustainability or that RENE's equity valuation contains unrecognized risk premiums.
