EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Attendo operates as the leading Nordic private care provider, managing approximately 770 local units serving 28,000 customers across Sweden, Finland, and Denmark through a highly decentralized operational model. The company has successfully restored group profitability by strategically focusing on own operations, which now represent 92% of group revenue, while aggressively exiting low-margin outsourcing contracts that historically depressed returns. The business operates under an asset-light execution model, leasing substantially all care facilities under 10-15 year indexed contracts from institutional real estate partners, thereby minimizing capital expenditure intensity to approximately 1% of net sales and driving exceptional free cash flow conversion of over 128%. This structural positioning creates both operational leverage benefits and rigid long-term lease obligations that inflate reported leverage metrics under IFRS 16 accounting standards.

Business Model and Strategic Positioning

1.
Strategic refocusing success: CEO Martin Tivéus has fundamentally reshaped Attendo through Norway exit, Danish pruning, and outsourcing reduction to 8%, concentrating resources on higher-return own-operations care homes that deliver 6-10% lease-adjusted EBITA margins versus outsourcing's thin 2-4% returns.

[Cross-ref: Section 1]

2.
Dominant geographic mix: Attendo Finland generates 58% of total group net sales and operates across 22 welfare regions, while Attendo Scandinavia contributes 42% of sales with 98% concentrated in Sweden across 290 decentralized municipalities and minimal Danish exposure.

[Cross-ref: Section 1]

3.
Asset-light real estate model: Attendo implements a strict low capital expenditure model, leasing facilities from developers like SBB and Northern Horizon while subleasing rooms directly to individual residents under public payor frameworks, creating negative working capital of SEK -600m and sustaining high operational leverage.

[Cross-ref: Section 1 + Section 4]

4.
Revenue security and indexation: Approximately 90% of Attendo's revenue is tax-funded, paid directly by Swedish municipalities and Finnish welfare regions, with the absolute majority of contracts linked to cost indices that provide inflation pass-through protection on wage and rent escalations despite documented timing lag risks.
