Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Historical shipping cycles show that LR2 and Aframax tankers rapidly migrate between dirty and clean trades depending on relative freight differentials.
  2. Fleet switching is heavily driven by opportunistic public corporates like Scorpio Tankers and Frontline, whereas sector averages show slower overall transitions.
  3. Rate spreads of at least USD 1,200 per day are required to incentivize owners to transition vessels. Net read: Direct cleaning costs and vessel off-hire days represent significant upfront financial friction for operators.
1.
Investigating dynamics: "For TORM plc (TRMD.A-DK) and the product tanker market it trades in, how fast and in what numbers have LR2 and Aframax tankers returned from crude (dirty) to clean product trades after past falls in crude tanker rates, what crude-to-clean rate differential and hull-cleaning cost have triggered the switch, and how did LR2 and MR product tanker rates respond in the following one to three quarters, given that about two-thirds of the LR2 fleet (about 355 against 152 clean) was trading dirty in late October 2026 according to TORM's Q3 2026 report?"

Market Supply and Earnings Risks

2.
Severe oversupply: The high volume of LR2 tankers operating in crude trades represents a massive latent supply that could disrupt the refined product market. According to Scorpio Tankers, about half of the current LR2 fleet is trading dirty, leaving clean product markets tight but highly vulnerable to a sudden reversal. If crude rates decline, these vessels will migrate back to clean carriage, introducing hundreds of vessels that would suppress time charter equivalent rates and compress margins across the sector.
3.
Extreme sensitivity: TORM remains heavily exposed to spot market fluctuations due to its active commercial platform. TORM management reports that a mere USD 1,000 per day change in fleet-wide charter rates impacts annual EBITDA by USD 4 mm. This spot exposure means any rapid influx of clean-trading LR2 tonnage will directly undermine TORM's earnings per share and reduce excess cash distributions to shareholders.
4.
Direct peer impact: Scorpio Tankers exhibits similar high spot exposure, making its operating income exceptionally sensitive to rate corrections. Morgan Stanley reports that a USD 1,000 per day shift in spot rates affects Scorpio's annual operating income by approximately USD 29 mm. Consequently, a mass return of dirty LR2s would trigger a synchronized earnings collapse for both TORM and its primary public peer group.
5.
Closely related questions: Investors must also evaluate how quickly shipowners can clean dirty hulls and what specific rate spread justifies this transition. Morgan Stanley indicates that clean-to-dirty spreads have turned negative, prompting immediate questions about the timing of clean fleet re-entry. Another key uncertainty is whether refinery outages or geopolitical blocks will persist long enough to absorb the returning capacity.

Section 3 – Economic Triggers: Rate Differentials and Hull-Cleaning Costs

Section summary
  1. Re-entering LR2 vessels depress clean spot rates across key trading basins when crude tanker markets soften.
  2. Strong structural product restocking and persistent transit inefficiencies mitigate the downward pressure on product rates.

Clean-Dirty Freight Rate Differentials

1.
Wider spreads: DNB Carnegie on July 31, 2022, reported that the rate spread between clean LR2 and dirty Aframax tankers must widen significantly to motivate switching. While a USD 700 per day spread in 2020 was insufficient, a premium of USD 1,700 per day in 2022 created a USD 600k annual netback that justified switching. This shows that owners require substantial forward margins before undertaking the financial risks of converting clean vessels.
2.
Critical inflections: DNB Carnegie on June 24, 2021, highlighted that a spread of USD 1,200 per day YTD represented an important economic inflection point for shipowners. This rate differential generated a USD 500k netback over 12 months, which began to outweigh the friction of converting vessels. When spreads sit below this threshold, owners typically maintain their current trade patterns to avoid operational disruption.
3.
Volatile dynamics: Morgan Stanley on October 27, 2026, analyzed how volatile clean-dirty spreads impact refining margins and fleet positioning. The benchmark Singapore-to-Australia clean rate versus the Indonesia-to- Australia dirty rate flipped to a negative USD 1.60 per barrel spread in October 2026. This sharp decline from the five-year average premium of USD 2.30 per barrel disincentivizes clean trading and encourages dirty carriage.
4.
Reversal pressures: Masterlink Securities on August 27, 2026, reported that the average 1-year daily hire rate for Aframax crude tankers was USD 56k, while LR2 product tankers averaged USD 54k. Sincere Navigation strategically acquired one LR2 and two Aframaxes in late 2026 to exploit these strong rates. This narrow USD 2k negative spread for clean vessels illustrates why a massive portion of the global LR2 fleet remains locked in dirty trades.
5.
Contrary evidence: Okeanis Eco Tankers on November 13, 2025, indicated that fleet switching can occur even without extreme rate spreads due to strategic positioning. The corporate operator cleaned an uncoated vessel in 15 days to capture regional trading opportunities. This suggests that local freight volatility, rather than global annualized rate differentials, can trigger switching behaviors for agile owners.

Cleaning Costs and Off-Hire Realities

6.
High expenses: Scorpio Tankers on October 29, 2024, emphasized that dirty vessels must clean up at great expense before returning to clean trades. Shipowners must invest heavily to remove heavy crude residues to prevent contamination of refined products. These upfront physical costs require a massive freight rate premium to ensure that the switching process is economically viable.
7.
Significant downtime: Jefferies Research on July 23, 2025, documented a comparable past case where Okeanis Eco Tankers cleaned VLCCs to switch from dirty to clean product trading. This extensive process took 20 days and removed 150 tons of sludge. The transition succeeded because the clean product market significantly outperformed crude trades in the second half of 2024, offsetting the off-hire loss.
8.
Elevated costs: HD Hyundai Marine Solution on March 13, 2026, noted that off-hire drydocking costs for large tankers range from USD 40k to USD 50k per day. This high daily rate makes any delay in cargo preparation or tank cleaning extremely expensive for vessel operators. Shipowners must plan meticulously because a ten-day overrun puts up to USD 500k of operating capital at risk.
9.
Continuous maintenance: Nautica on June 1, 2026, reported that traditional hull-cleaning methods using divers and cleaning rigs remain the primary fleet standard. These standard operations cost between USD 45k and USD 55k annually per vessel. This base maintenance cost is a necessary expense that owners must pay to maintain speed and fuel efficiency across cycles.
10.
Complex transitions: Scorpio Tankers on July 30, 2024, explained that returning to clean trades requires finding intermediate cargoes that are not prone to contamination. Operators must secure cargoes like condensate to clean tank walls. Once cleaned, charterers typically demand that vessels be redelivered in dirty condition, making clean transitions a highly temporary operational move.

Structural Eco and Scrubber Premiums

11.
Structural advantages: Deutsche Bank on January 6, 2025, estimated the daily scrubber premium for Aframax and LR2 tankers at USD 1,935 per day. This compares to a premium of USD 3,920 per day for VLCCs and USD 1,131 per day for MR product tankers. Scrubber-fitted vessels achieve higher net time charter equivalent earnings by burning cheaper high-sulfur fuel oil, which helps buffer the initial expenses of tank cleaning.
12.
Elite efficiencies: Jefferies Research on April 23, 2025, reported that Scorpio Tankers captures superior margins because its fleet is 100% eco-design with scrubbers. This fuel-efficient profile allows Scorpio to outperform older, non-eco fleets by burning heavily discounted fuel. Consequently, Eco-class operators can tolerate lower clean-dirty rate spreads than peers with older vessels when deciding to transition.
13.
Heavy penalties: Jefferies Research on October 30, 2025, noted that Teekay Tankers operates a fleet that skews older with limited eco-design and no scrubbers. Unlike Scorpio's modern fleet, Teekay's older vessels face higher fuel burn rates and contamination risks. These technical limitations prevent older vessels from switching clean-to-dirty fluidly, leaving them heavily exposed to spot crude market corrections.
14.
Other side of the story: While high cleaning costs and off-hire downtime theoretically restrict the return of LR2s to clean product trades, extreme crude market declines can trigger a rapid, synchronized migration. Frontline on November 27, 2024, noted that owners will immediately clean vessels to capture product premiums if crude rates collapse completely. Given that two-thirds of the LR2 fleet is trading dirty, even a partial migration would overwhelm clean product markets.
