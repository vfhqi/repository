Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. European chemical margins rebounded in 2026, but the recovery is highly fragmented and heavily reliant on transient supply-chain disruptions.
  2. Permanent capacity closures represent a structural driver, whereas shipping disruptions provide a temporary pricing cushion that is already starting to normalize. Net read: Our confidence in this attribution is medium because while permanent closures are structurally documented, spot spread contractions clearly show that shipping disruptions drove the near-term recovery.

1.
Key Question: "How much of the 2026 recovery do brokers and industry experts attribute to permanent European closures (Dow Bohlen, Lanxess, Solvay, BASF Ludwigshafen and others) and how much to Asian imports blocked by the disruption? Where do they expect European capacity utilisation in 2027 and 2028 as announced closures land, if shipping returns to normal? Which producers gain most volume from rivals' closures, and what does each point of utilisation add to their EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation)?"

Macro Drivers and Spread Divergence

1.
Surging contract margins: Deutsche Bank reports on 21 October 2026 that European ethylene-naphtha contract margins rose 50% year over year in October. This rapid expansion was driven by higher ethylene contract prices and lower domestic naphtha processing costs. However, Deutsche Bank notes that the global chemical cycle remains weak due to Chinese overcapacity, which could cap the recovery. Consequently, this margin spike will fail to sustain long-term chemical earnings unless underlying domestic demand stages a recovery.
2.
Expanding sales volumes: Deutsche Bank's report on 29 July 2026 highlights that BASF achieved a 5.8% volume increase and an 11% price increase in its core European business during the second quarter. This performance exceeded analyst consensus and was supported by customers prioritizing procurement security over cheaper imports. However, Deutsche Bank's update on 17 June 2026 warns that margins are expected to normalize as temporary supply disruptions in the Middle East begin to ease. Therefore, these volume gains will likely reverse unless competitor plant shutdowns permanently remove capacity from the regional market.
3.
Fading spot spreads: Jefferies reports on 30 June 2026 that European spot chemical spreads have fallen 27% from their mid-April peak. This sharp decline indicates that the pricing tailwind from Middle East shipping disruptions is already normalising. According to Jefferies, the move was led by a more pronounced 15% drop in European cracker product spreads as pricing benefits diminished. This mismatch suggests that upstream chemical margins will face downward pressure unless producers aggressively shutter redundant assets.
4.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies research on 20 May 2026 argues that the pricing uplift was driven entirely by supply- side disruptions and pre-buying behavior rather than structural demand recovery. Thus, any near-term gains from competitor closures could be diluted by rapid normalisation of imports once shipping returns to normal.

Section 3 – Outlook for European Capacity Utilisation in 2027 and 2028

Section summary
  1. European chemical utilisation is projected to remain severely depressed through 2028 due to massive global overcapacity.
  2. Structural plant closures will only partially offset the persistent energy squeeze and rising import pressures in Europe.
  3. We have high confidence that regional operating rates will fail to recover to historical mid-cycle levels before 2030.
  4. Surviving integrated chemical producers stand to gain substantial volumes as structural closures consolidate the uncompetitive European footprint.
  5. Upstream operating leverage remains high, meaning that minor utilisation improvements will drive disproportionate EBITDA expansions.
  6. Consolidated peer margins show wide divergence because specialty players are exposed to transient energy price spikes.

Structural Utilisation Trough

1.
Depressed regional utilisation: European chemical capacity utilisation has fallen to historically low levels. According to a Bernstein Research report on 22 September 2026, average European chemical utilisation rates have steadily declined to approximately 73% in 2025 compared to a historical norm of 83% in 2017. This persistent weakness reflects weak regional demand and global overcapacity, which means producers face severe fixed-cost overhangs. Consequently, this structural underutilisation will continue to depress downstream chemical corporate earnings unless massive capacity rationalisation occurs.
2.
Sustained overcapacity headwind: Global oversupply will continue to cap the recovery of European operating rates. A Deutsche Bank report on 24 November 2025 forecasts that global chemical utilisation will remain weak and bottom out in 2027 to 2028 due to massive additions from China. Deutsche Bank expects China to represent 30% of global chemical capacity by 2028 compared to 27% in 2025, which will intensify regional import pressures. This suggests that European asset utilisation will struggle to recover to the minimum 80% threshold required for pricing power.
3.
Insufficient closure impact: Announced European plant shutdowns are too small to restore balance to the regional market. A Bain and Company press release on 19 October 2026 asserts that chemical plants will continue running below historical averages because regional closures are insufficient to offset Chinese additions. Bain and Company estimates that the global industry requires the net closure of approximately 7.5% of global olefin capacity by 2030 to return to normal operating rates. Therefore, this mismatch will delay margin recovery and prevent any meaningful near-term improvement in European earnings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some industry analysts argue that the regional supply balance is tightening much faster than expected. A Deutsche Bank report on 29 May 2025 highlights that European chemical producers have already executed 11 million tonnes of capacity closures out of 13 million tonnes announced. The broker argues that this means the required capacity cuts have happened and the European market is currently bottoming out. If this view is correct, remaining integrated assets like BASF will experience rapid volume rebounds and stronger pricing power.

Product Chain Dynamics and Divergences

5.
Ethylene chain weakness: The European ethylene chain faces persistent operating rate pressure. A Deutsche Bank report on 20 April 2026 reveals that Chemical Market Analytics forecasts global ethylene operating rates to bottom at a historical low of 79% in 2028 compared to 87% in 2021. This global surplus directly limits the ability of European crackers to raise rates, which weakens upstream operating leverage. This means that European producers will suffer from compressed margins unless they execute much deeper local cracker rationalisation.
6.
Polymer market imbalances: Underutilisation remains a critical challenge for European polymer and recycling networks. An Agilyx ASA transcript from 1 October 2026 indicates that European plastic recycling utilisation sits at only approximately 58% of installed capacity. The company attributes this underutilisation to high domestic energy costs and cheap seaborne imports of both virgin and recycled resins, which forces uncompetitive plants to close. This indicates that specialty polymers will remain under margin pressure unless trade barriers restrict imports.
7.
Silicones market contraction: High cost structures have also triggered permanent shutdowns in specialty silicone chains. A Bernstein Research report on 11 September 2026 confirms that Dow Chemical plans to close its siloxanes facility in Barry, Wales by mid-2026. This shutdown removes approximately 30.5% of European siloxane capacity, representing a major consolidation of the regional footprint. This structural reduction will support the operating rates of remaining domestic silicone producers like Wacker Chemie.
8.
Acetic acid pressures: European downstream acetic acid operating rates are projected to remain subdued. A Huatai Research report on 13 October 2026 projects that domestic Chinese acetic acid operating rates will average approximately 80%, 76%, and 73% in 2026, 2027, and 2028, respectively. This high level of Asian supply will flood European intermediate chemical channels as shipping flows normalize. Consequently, European synthetic acid producers will face intense price competition, which will drag down regional cash generation.

Energy Squeeze and Cost Pressures

9.
Depleted gas storage: Unseasonably low European gas inventories threaten to exacerbate the regional energy squeeze. A Jefferies report on 6 October 2026 details that European energy storage is 17% below the fiveyear historical average. Jefferies notes that European TTF gas prices have soared to approximately 24 dollars per million British thermal units, which squeezes energy-intensive chemical margins. This ongoing energy squeeze will force unintegrated producers like Lanxess and Solvay to curtail production, which will prevent utilisation from recovering.
10.
Persistent power premium: European electricity costs remain structurally uncompetitive compared to other chemical manufacturing hubs. A Morgan Stanley report on 4 February 2026 highlights that baseload power prices across major European countries remain 10% to 70% higher than pre-crisis levels. This persistent cost penalty drives energy-heavy manufacturing relocation out of Europe. Consequently, this structural disadvantage will continue to depress the operating rates of remaining domestic assets unless governments implement massive subsidies.
11.
Elevated carbon costs: Rising environmental compliance liabilities represent an additional drag on domestic operating rates. A Jefferies report on 20 May 2026 argues that carbon costs under the Emissions Trading System are becoming a material headwind for European chemical producers. This regulatory penalty compounds the regional energy disadvantage against US Gulf Coast competitors. Consequently, these cumulative cost pressures will keep European upstream utilisation depressed as uncompetitive plants are permanently idled.

Historical Analogue and Future Outlook

12.
Restructuring historical precedent: Past cycles of capacity rationalisation show that supply-side adjustments are slow and expensive. A Jefferies report on 16 November 2023 highlights that chemical producers typically operate at reduced rates or force majeures for six to eight quarters before making irreversible closure decisions. This lag occurs because permanent closures trigger immediate site remediation liabilities, which companies seek to defer. This past case suggests that the current excess capacity in Europe will persist through 2028 unless financial distress forces immediate bankruptcies.
13.
Projected capacity contraction: Further structural retrenchment is expected as shipping lanes normalize and import competition returns. A Bernstein Research report on 11 September 2026 estimates that approximately 3.4% of European upstream chemical capacity will close over the next few years based on current announcements. However, Bernstein calculates a scenario where up to 9% of European sites close by 2030 if industrial support remains weak. This contraction will help remaining integrated players like BASF achieve higher utilization and stable EPS.
14.
Other side of the story: Some market observers believe that European capacity utilisation will experience a sharp structural recovery by 2028. A Bernstein Research report on 11 September 2026 argues that the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz will serve as the ultimate catalyst to force uncompetitive assets offline. This structural rationalisation will rapidly concentrate remaining volumes onto highly integrated, cost-competitive Verbund sites. Consequently, surviving European producers will enjoy dramatically higher operating rates and substantial margin expansion once the inefficient capacity is permanently removed.
