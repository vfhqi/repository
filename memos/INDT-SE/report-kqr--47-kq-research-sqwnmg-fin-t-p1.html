Section 2 – Cost Structure and Fixed-Variable Splits below Gross Profit

Indutrade Subsidiary Cost Structure

1.
High subsidiary overhead: Indutrade operates with a cost base below gross profit that is primarily composed of decentralized selling and administrative expenses spread across more than 220 subsidiaries. In the 2025 annual report, selling costs reached SEK 5,113 million while administrative expenses were SEK 2,127 million. This structure implies that operating expenses are highly fixed at the subsidiary level. This decentralized model prevents easy cost consolidation, which hurts earnings per share when demand falls.
2.
Indutrade's 2025 condensed income statement showing selling and administrative expenses Significant employee costs: Personnel costs represent the largest component of Indutrade's operating expenses below the gross profit line. Employee benefit expenses rose to SEK 7,745 million in 2025 from SEK 7,496 million in 2024, representing the primary driver of subsidiary opex. Because these personnel costs are distributed across hundreds of independent companies, they remain difficult to cut quickly without damaging local commercial relationships. Consequently, stagnant organic growth will pressure margins unless management maintains strict hiring freezes across all business units.
3.
Rising administrative expenses: Indutrade's total administrative expenses grew to SEK 2,127 million in 2025 from SEK 2,110 million in 2024, showing persistent cost inflation below gross profit. In addition, other operating expenses doubled from SEK 214 million to SEK 409 million in 2025 due to asset impairments and transaction costs. These escalating expenses dampened the overall EBITA margin, which dropped to 13.8% in 2025 from 15.0% in 2023. This cost behavior illustrates that operating leverage operates in reverse when administrative inflation is not countered by robust organic sales.

Downturn Stagnation and Recovery Leverage

4.
Negative volume leverage: During the 2024 to 2025 downturn, Indutrade's cost base exhibited rigid behavior as organic sales fell by 2% in 2025. Because the group went into 2025 with expenses scaled for a more optimistic demand environment, the lack of top-line growth resulted in unabsorbed subsidiary capacity. This mismatch between costs and sales caused the reported EBITA margin to fall from 15.0% in 2023 to 13.8% in 2025. This cost rigidity highlights that margins are highly sensitive to small organic volume declines due to the fixed nature of subsidiary selling structures.
5.
Rapid leverage rebound: The second quarter of 2026 demonstrated the strong upside of Indutrade's operating leverage as organic sales grew 5% and total sales increased 11%. In its report on 16 July 2026, Indutrade highlighted that SG&A growth was held to 8%, allowing revenue to outgrow overhead costs. DNB Carnegie highlights that this positive cost jaw expanded the underlying EBITA margin by 120 basis points to 14.9%. This operating leverage means that earnings per share will grow rapidly as under-absorbed capacity is fully utilized over the next few quarters.
6.
Under-absorbed opex capacity: The capacity built during the 2023 to 2025 SG&A ramp-up remains partially unabsorbed within some subsidiary niches. DNB Carnegie argues that the return of organic growth will allow subsidiaries to absorb this idle capacity over the next several quarters. Management noted that more than half of its companies continue to grow, which justifies selective head count additions in stronger segments like energy and life science. Consequently, the group can support higher business volumes without a linear increase in overhead, boosting future profitability.
