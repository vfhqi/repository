EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The evidence shows that Solid State's strategic progression from 11.0p EPS in FY26 toward its 20p target hinges on executing its high-value defense contracts and expanding operating margins, and the Key Question regarding specific earnings drivers can be partly answered with medium overall confidence. Near-term revenue phasing and contract execution are well supported by the £102.4m order book as of 31 May 2026, but longer-term margin expansion to reach the 2030 target remains contingent on uncertain UK defense procurement timelines, component pricing pass-through efficiency, and successful facility utilization at Ashchurch.

The research findings reveal that revenue phasing benefits from strong order book coverage with an £18.0m underlying improvement when excluding a non-repeating NSPA order, although the group faces a demanding first-half comparator after recognizing revenue of £85.7m in H1 2026 boosted by a £23.3m major NSPA communications program. The defense awards pipeline shows promise through Project CAIN with an initial £8.0m shipment completed in Q1 FY27, yet delays to the publication of the UK Defence Investment Plan have created mid-term spending uncertainty that could affect the pace of future order book growth.

Gross margin expansion demonstrates operational progress as Solid State reported a significant rise in its group gross margin to 33.5% in FY26 from 31.5% in the prior year, primarily driven by improved product-level margins and a more favorable revenue mix, with particularly strong performance in the Power division where gross margin expanded to 35.8% from 31.7% in the prior year attributed to stronger operational execution and the successful replacement of lower-margin contracts.

However, margin sustainability faces headwinds from lengthening component lead times as global artificial intelligence infrastructure spending drove semiconductor and memory shortages toward the end of the year, requiring proactive back-to-back pricing adjustments with customers.

Operating cost pressures constrain near-term margin progression as adjusted selling, general, and administrative expenses rose sharply to £42.0m in FY26 from £33.3m in FY25, driven by investments in employee-related costs and circa £3.3m in performance-related overheads. Additionally, legislative changes introduced in the UK Autumn Budget are expected to increase annual costs by over £0.5m in the year ahead, primarily driven by revised employer National Insurance contributions and national minimum wage increases.

The Ashchurch facility investment creates near-term dilution as the newly commissioned integrated systems facility added approximately £3.0m in annual overheads ahead of revenue realization, and these fixed costs dilute operating margins in the short term.

Capital expenditure commitments elevate cash requirements with capital expenditure anticipated in excess of £7.5m over the next two financial years, representing a significant step up from the £2.70m capex recorded in FY26, targeted toward expanding advanced manufacturing capacity in the Systems and Power divisions, specifically investing in the new Tewkesbury integrated systems facility and a state-of-the-art RF testing chamber to manufacture larger, more complex defense systems.

Working capital intensity delays cash generation as group inventory rose to £30.5m in FY26 from £28.24m in the prior year, consisting of finished goods of £19.55m and work in progress of £10.96m, with the work in progress increase primarily driven by the build-up of Systems communications inventory ahead of Project CAIN deliveries, while ongoing supply chain constraints affecting memory and certain semiconductor products have required further strategic inventory investments, placing short-term pressure on cash flows before customer shipments are completed.

Below-the-line items show improving stability as Solid State secured a new £15.0m multi-currency revolving credit facility carrying a ratcheting margin of 2.3% to 2.6% over SONIA, EURIBOR, or SOFR depending on leverage, allowing the company to mitigate variable interest rate risks by drawing debt in foreign currencies. The tax position benefits from US loss recognition with an adjusted effective tax rate of 24.1% in FY26, though the underlying effective tax rate was reported as 26% by management due to overseas rate variations, as US entities recognized previously unrecognized tax losses to lower the current tax expense.

Share dilution remains controlled as total issued share capital remained flat at 57.1m ordinary shares in FY26, preventing any immediate dilution of basic earnings per share, although the group awarded up to 730,000 share options on 13 May 2026 subject to a performance condition requiring FY28 adjusted EPS to reach a threshold of 11.5p for vesting, with the maximum award vesting if adjusted EPS reaches a ceiling of 16.0p.

Currency sensitivity presents material translation risk as Solid State conducts extensive trade in US dollars and euros, and a 10% weakening of these currencies against sterling would cause an estimated £0.82m profit decrease for the year, with no forward contracts in place at 31 March 2026.

The path to 20p EPS depends on execution excellence because severe slippage in the publication of the UK Defence Investment Plan would postpone high-margin systems deliveries indefinitely, and historically when a key $25m communications contract was delayed in H1 2025, Zeus Capital was forced to reduce Solid State's EPS estimates by up to 60%, demonstrating that political procurement delays represent the most significant threat to the group's mid-term earnings bridge.

Section 3 – Gross Margin and Operating Cost Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Product-level pricing improvements and higher value-added contract transitions successfully expanded Solid State's group gross margin to 33.5% in FY26.
  2. Lengthening semiconductor and memory lead times require proactive back-to-back pricing adjustments and strategic buffer stock management to protect margins.
  3. Mandatory tax and wage headwinds combined with initial facility overheads at Ashchurch will constrain near-term operating margin progression.

Deconstruction of Gross Margin Dynamics

1.
Improved pricing mix: Solid State reported a significant rise in its group gross margin to 33.5% in FY26 from 31.5% in the prior year. This margin expansion was primarily driven by improved product-level margins and a more favorable revenue mix. The company successfully executed higher-value programs to offset inflationary pressures. This positive development suggests that structural profitability is improving steadily across key business lines.
2.
Refocused product portfolio: The Power division delivered a standout performance as its gross margin expanded to 35.8% from 31.7% in the prior year. Management attributed this margin improvement to stronger operational execution and the successful replacement of lower-margin contracts. The division benefited particularly from high-value military programs in the US autonomous vehicle market. This refocused strategy will enhance long-term earnings per share unless US defense procurement slows.
3.
Divergent divisional profitability: Solid State's Components division achieved a gross margin of 26.8% in FY26 compared to 25.0% in FY25. Meanwhile, the Systems division reported a gross margin of 38.7% which was slightly down from 39.7% in the prior year. Higher-margin antenna and imaging sales largely offset dilutive high-volume communications deliveries within Systems. Consequently, the group's total margin profile remains highly sensitive to divisional product mix.

Sensitivity to Semiconductor and Memory Pricing

4.
Lengthening component lead times: Favorable early supply conditions deteriorated as global artificial intelligence infrastructure spending drove semiconductor and memory shortages toward the end of the year. Management reported that lead times for certain critical components are beginning to extend again. These supply-side constraints present both pricing risks and procurement opportunities for the group's distribution business. If shortages persist, they could delay near-term project deliveries and suppress operational performance.
5.
Managing price sensitivities: Rapidly rising component costs, particularly for memory products, threaten to make certain contract programs unviable in the short term. Solid State mitigates this risk by collaborating with customers and executing proactive back-to-back pricing adjustments. The company also maintains strategic buffer stocks of memory components to protect its manufacturing margins. These pricing pass-through mechanisms are vital to defend the group's adjusted earnings pathway.
6.
Contrary evidence: A Zeus Capital research note dated 29 June 2026 warns that volatile memory pricing and shortages remain major potential headwinds for Solid State. The broker cautions that these supply chain disruptions could delay customer projects and impact margin progression. Such external cost pressures could squeeze operating profitability despite robust commercial demand across defense markets. This indicates that gross margin expansion is highly dependent on international raw material pricing.

Analysis of Operating Cost Run-Rate

7.
Heightened overhead investments: Solid State's adjusted selling, general, and administrative expenses rose sharply to £42.0m in FY26 from £33.3m in FY25. This significant increase was driven by investments in employee-related costs to enhance engineering capability. The run-rate also reflects circa £3.3m in performance-related overheads driven by the group's strong recovery. These investments will pressure near-term operating margins unless sales volumes expand rapidly.
8.
Regulatory cost headwinds: Legislative changes introduced in the UK Autumn Budget are expected to increase annual costs by over £0.5m in the year ahead. These additions are primarily driven by revised employer National Insurance contributions and national minimum wage increases. Management notes that these mandatory cost increases will partially offset the group's previously anticipated overhead savings. Consequently, this regulatory headwind represents a direct threat to the H1 FY27 operating margin recovery.
9.
Facility utilization dilution: The newly commissioned integrated systems facility at Ashchurch added approximately £3.0m in annual overheads ahead of revenue realization. Because the Tewkesbury site is currently running at modest utilization levels, these fixed costs dilute operating margins in the short term. However, management expects the facility to cover its costs in the current financial year as production ramps up. Securing large-scale systems contracts at Ashchurch remains essential to unlock operational gearing.
10.
High-margin capacity expansion: Solid State has also invested heavily in its high-margin antenna business unit, which historically delivers product margins of 60% to over 70%. The group completed a state-of-the-art near field RF testing chamber to increase advanced manufacturing capacity in the UK. These technical overhead investments are designed to support growing demand from Tier-1 defense primes. This capacity buildup will accelerate long-term earnings once multi-year military contracts are secured.

Benchmark Against discoverIE Group

11.
High value design-in: Peer discoverIE Group serves as a primary gross margin benchmark, reporting a robust gross margin of 40.5% compared to its 47.4% ten-year average. This superior profitability is supported by an asset-light model where application-specific components are custom-designed directly into customer systems at an early stage. This engineer-led sales model creates high barriers to substitution and provides strong pricing power through the cycle. Solid State's move toward value-added systems is designed to replicate this resilient margin profile.
12.
Pricing lag dynamics: In managing pricing, discoverIE's management guides to a four to six month lag between order intake and actual sales conversion. The peer maintains robust gross margins by passing on freight inflation and adjusting prices on a case-by-case basis with clients. This disciplined pricing approach helps cushion profitability against raw material price shocks during inflationary cycles. This indicates that Solid State must carefully manage similar pass-through lags to protect its FY27 earnings.
13.
Operational scaling investment: To support future top-line recovery, discoverIE recently invested £4.4m in operating expenses, including engineering resource and Asian capacity expansion. This growth investment has temporarily constrained near-term operating margins to 13.8% in FY26. Nevertheless, discoverIE's management remains confident of achieving its ambitious 17.0% adjusted operating margin target by FY30. This peer experience demonstrates that proactive capacity investment is necessary to secure highmargin organic growth.
14.
Other side of the story: Solid State's ambitious margin expansion and cost reduction plans could be compromised by persistent industrial weakness and inflation. While management expects the Ashchurch and antenna investments to cover their costs in FY27, low capacity utilization represents a severe downside risk to operating profits if defense awards slip further. Furthermore, unlike peer discoverIE, Solid State's substantial components distribution segment remains exposed to commodity price swings, which limits the group's overall operating margin potential. Therefore, the path to a sustainable 9% operating margin depends on favorable external demand.

Section 4 – Capital Expenditure, Depreciation, Amortisation and Working Capital

Section summary
  1. Strategic capital expenditure of over £7.5m in FY27-FY28 will expand advanced manufacturing capacity but temporarily elevate fixed overheads.
  2. Sustained working capital intensity driven by strategic inventory buffer stock will delay net cash generation despite strong operating cash flows.

Capital Expenditure Plans and Purposes

1.
Elevated investment plans: Solid State anticipates capital expenditure could be in excess of £7.5m over the next two financial years. Group Finance Director Peter Owen James clarified on 30 June 2026 that this figure is cumulative across FY27 and FY28 rather than an annual run-rate. This investment represents a significant step up from the £2.70m capex recorded in FY26.
2.
Targeted asset allocation: The planned capital expenditure will be primarily directed toward expanding advanced manufacturing capacity in the Systems and Power divisions. Specifically, the company is investing in its new Tewkesbury integrated systems facility and a new state-of-the-art RF testing chamber. These infrastructure upgrades are crucial to manufacture larger, more complex defense systems for major clients.
3.
Ample funding capabilities: Zeus Capital notes that Solid State ended FY26 with net debt of £4.20m, which was slightly better than broker expectations. The broker upgraded its earnings estimates, anticipating that the company's strong balance sheet provides the opportunity to invest in the business for future growth. However, Zeus Capital also cautions that the timing of these capital commitments must be carefully managed to maintain adequate liquidity headroom.
4.
Peer capital comparison: In benchmarking capital intensity, peer discoverIE Group operates a highly capital-light model with capex at just 1.5% of sales. The peer invested £6.60m in FY26, focusing on facilities in Thailand and India, and expects next year's capex to rise to £9m. This comparison shows Solid State is investing more intensively relative to scale to secure medium-term defense growth.

Depreciation and Amortisation Methodologies

5.
Granular accounting splits: Solid State's depreciation and amortisation charge for FY26 reached £6.10m, which was down from £8m in FY25. This total included PPE depreciation of £1.71m, right-of-use lease asset depreciation of £1.47m, and total intangible amortisation of £2.90m. Acquired-intangible amortisation was stable at £1.76m, which the board consistently classifies into head office overheads to represent underlying divisional trading.
6.
Broker adjustment policies: Zeus Capital assumes that Solid State's adjusted operating margins will expand from 6.2% in FY26 to 6.3% in FY27. To achieve this, the broker adds back acquisition-related intangible amortisation and share-based payments to adjusted operating profit. This adjusted methodology helps analysts evaluate ongoing cash-based performance on a consistent basis.
7.
Peer capitalization practices: Benchmark peer XP Power capitalises its direct product development costs and amortises them over an expected useful life of five to seven years. In FY25, XP Power spent £8.70m on product development, which represented 8% of its total assets. Solid State similarly capitalises development costs, investing £1.20m in software and R&D intangibles in FY26, but amortises these over one to five years.
8.
Peer amortisation methodologies: In comparison, peer discoverIE Group reported an amortisation of acquired intangible assets of £16.30m in FY26. The peer amortises these acquired intangibles over five to ten years depending on commercial relationships. This shows how acquisitive groups experience heavy non-cash charges that dilute statutory operating profits.

Working Capital Dynamics and Inventory

9.
Elevated inventory components: Solid State's group inventory rose to £30.50m in FY26 from £28.24m in the prior year. This balance consisted of finished goods and goods for resale of £19.55m, beside work in progress of £10.96m. This significant work in progress increase was primarily driven by the build-up of Systems communications inventory ahead of Project CAIN deliveries.
10.
Milestone billing terms: Solid State managed trade receivables down to £20.16m in FY26, reflecting improved collections and tighter control of working capital. Meanwhile, contract liabilities, which represent customer advances received on future deliveries, rose slightly to £6.28m. Managing these advance customer payments helps balance the cash challenges of working capital during program scale-up.
11.
Contrary evidence: An analysis of Solid State's final results on 29 June 2026 reveals that capital expenditure and strategic memory stock builds will offset the expected working capital unwind from Project CAIN. This means that cash conversion may fall below historical levels if defense delivery timelines slip. Consequently, net debt could rise in the near term, which would restrict the group's capacity to execute further earnings-accretive acquisitions.
12.
Strategic stock costs: Ongoing supply chain constraints affecting memory and certain semiconductor products have required Solid State to make further strategic inventory investments. The group utilizes advance purchasing to secure supply of critical components. While this proactive stock building protects manufacturing margins, it places short-term pressure on cash flows before customer shipments are completed.
13.
Peer growth comparisons: Peer Volex provides an instructive example of the working-capital cost of program growth, reporting a massive working capital outflow of $55.20m in FY26. Volex had to significantly build inventory, particularly in its Data Center market, which operates through a hub model with longer cash cycles. This precedent proves that rapid scaling of high-value manufacturing requires substantial upfront working capital commitments to prevent supply chain bottlenecks.
14.
Working capital efficiency: Benchmark peer discoverIE Group managed to reduce its working capital as a percentage of sales from 17.2% to 16.6% in FY26. Despite investing £5.50m in working capital to support sales growth, discoverIE maintained a strong cash turn. This disciplined management helps discoverIE achieve exceptionally high operating cash conversion averaging c.100% over the last decade.
15.
Peer inventory optimization: Peer XP Power prioritised the strengthening of its balance sheet by reducing inventory by £14.10m in FY25. This aggressive destocking allowed XP Power to convert 225% of adjusted operating profit into cash. This demonstrates that tight control of stock is highly effective to restore financial flexibility during industrial downturns.
16.
Other side of the story: While Solid State's capital-light peers like discoverIE maintain strong operating cash conversion above 100%, Solid State's strategic decision to hold buffer stock will delay net cash generation. This inventory holding strategy provides competitive advantages during component shortages but leaves the company highly vulnerable to cash flow volatility. Therefore, earnings per share growth will be limited unless working capital efficiency improves.

Section 5 – Finance, Tax, Share Dilution and Currency Sensitivity
