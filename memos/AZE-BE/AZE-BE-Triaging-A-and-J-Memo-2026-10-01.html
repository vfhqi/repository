[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Azelis is a cause-for-concern clearing in a solid but leveraged specialty distributor, and it is coherent rather than compelling. At EUR 11.9 the shares already pay for the clearing that has happened (customers have finished destocking, supplier price rises are passing through, EUR 20m of cost is out) and for little of the clearing that has not (leverage back inside 3.0x), so the modal return reaches the framework's 20 percent bar over eighteen months only if that second clearing arrives, and falls short of it over three years whatever happens to the multiple.

  • The concern was three things at once, and all three sit in the share-price history.
    • Organic revenue fell for three years (-5.9 percent in 2023, -1.1 percent in 2024, -1.6 percent in 2025, -3.9 percent in Q1 2026)
    • Leverage overshot to 3.4x net debt to EBITDA against a 2.5x to 3.0x target and stopped the acquisition engine that had done 64 deals in 8.5 years
    • And 2025 was the third consecutive year of sell-side downgrades.
    • Together they took the shares to a 52-week low of EUR 6.7231 and the multiple to 7.0x EV to EBITDA in January 2026, an all-time low.
  • The change is mostly external and partly temporary.
    • Customers have finished destocking: group organic growth was +3.9 percent in Q2 2026 against consensus of 1.8 percent, after four quarters of decline, and IMCD grew 7.5 percent organically in the same quarter, so the end of destocking is a sector fact rather than a company claim.
    • Suppliers announced price increases of up to 30 to 40 percent on selected portfolios in H1 2026 and customers largely accepted them, which lifted the gross margin by 100 basis points to 24.7 percent in Q2 2026.
    • The pricing part of this rests on Middle East disruption and on two to three months of low-cost inventory, and management itself expects both to ease in H2 2026.
  • The internal change is discipline, not transformation.
    • The chief executive appointed on 1 January 2024 has run a defensive tenure: a EUR 20m run-rate cost programme delivered ahead of plan (EUR 21m in 2025, EUR 11m in H1 2026), a refinancing that saves EUR 6.5m a year of interest from March 2026, portfolio pruning in Australia and New Zealand, a new chief financial officer from 1 January 2026, and a guidance step for FY2026 from flat adjusted EBITA to positive adjusted EBITA growth.
    • The three strategic pillars are unchanged, there is no perimeter reduction of size, no buyback beyond EUR 1.5m of incentive cover and no balance-sheet deployment.
    • The screen's rule of three strong internal forces is met only on a literal reading.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned, from a low base.
    • Q2 2026 adjusted EBITA rose 11.5 percent to EUR 128m at an 11.4 percent margin
    • The conversion margin moved from 42.3 percent in Q1 2026 to 46.0 percent in Q2
    • Guidance was lifted on 30 July 2026
    • And the sell-side has raised FY2026 EPS by 5.4 percent over three months and 9.8 percent over six, with targets up 16 percent over six months.
    • Against that, H1 2026 free cash flow fell 19 percent to EUR 122m as working capital rose to 16.0 percent of revenue, and leverage rose from 3.3x at December 2025 to 3.4x at June 2026.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, and that is exactly why it is not a bigger case.
    • It shows in the company's choices (the cost programme, the refinancing, the pruning), its actions (price increases), its key operating metrics (organic growth, gross margin, conversion margin), its financial statements (H1 2026 adjusted EBITA EUR 233m) and the sell-side's estimates.
    • What the change has not done is restore the growth algorithm: without acquisitions the business grows 3 to 4 percent a year organically, and the acquisition engine waits on leverage that is not yet falling.
  • What we think it earns is a 9 percent a year earnings path, below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band.
    • On the brokers' adjusted basis that reconciles to the FY2025 actual of EUR 0.830, our modal path is EUR 0.90 in FY2026, EUR 1.01 in FY2027 and EUR 1.08 in FY2028, a compound rate of 9.2 percent a year.
    • Our operating view is consensus: revenue at the median and adjusted EBITA equal to the consensus EBITDA path less depreciation.
    • The gap to the FactSet median EPS (EUR 0.858, EUR 0.889, EUR 0.950) is basis and the interest line, not optimism.
    • The composition of the growth is the wrong way round for the framework's preference: the largest contributor is the below-EBITA line (net financial expense falling from EUR 124m toward EUR 103m), which investors do not extrapolate, while revenue growth (3.7 percent a year) and margin expansion (1.3 percent a year) together give EBITA growth of only 5 percent a year.
  • What it costs is a multiple in the bottom third of the stock's history, but no longer at the bottom.
    • At EUR 11.9 the shares are 12.9x consensus 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 0.9243, the 29th percentile of the ten-year history and the 25th percentile of the five-year series (12.65x on the financials block's own price), against a ten-year median of 15.10x and IMCD at 14.9x
    • But against the last two years the stock is at the 61st percentile, and the price is 77 percent above the 52-week low and 5 percent below the 52-week high.
    • On our own earnings basis the same price is 11.2x.
    • The multiple is about half company-driven, so it can fall on company news alone.
  • The return is at the bar on eighteen months and short of it on three years, and the eighteen-month figure rests on the re-rating.
    • Valuing the shares at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond each horizon, at a blended multiple of 13.1x (an uncleared branch at 11.5x and a cleared branch at 15.0x, at odds of 45 percent on clearing by March 2028), the eighteen-month total return is 31.8 percent, 20.2 percent a year
    • At 13.4x (55 percent odds by September 2029) the three-year return is 50.3 percent, 14.5 percent a year.
    • The re-rating contributes 17.0 percent of the eighteen-month return, the earnings growth 11.3 percent and the cash return 1.9 percent.
    • At the consensus median earnings path the eighteen-month return is 7.9 percent a year
    • On a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITA to earnings per share it is 7.9 percent a year at the blended multiple and below zero at the uncleared one.
  • The earnings line the market prices is itself read two ways, which is a risk to what is priced.
    • The broker table for FY2026 splits into a cluster at EUR 0.858 to EUR 1.00 and a cluster at EUR 0.43 to EUR 0.70, on two earnings definitions
    • The FactSet median of EUR 0.858 sits at the top of the gap between them.
    • On the upper cluster's earnings the stock is at about 11x
    • On the lower cluster's about 16x.
    • If the price is built on the lower cluster, the re-rating leg is already spent.
  • The pattern is 'Major CfC Clearing', seen from the middle rather than the start.
    • The mechanism is exactly the pattern's: sticky principal and customer relationships mean the franchise survives a destocking cycle intact, the organic line turns when customer inventories normalise, pricing pass-through lifts gross profit ahead of cost, the asset-light cash model converts the recovery into debt reduction, and once leverage is back inside target the market restores the multiple it pays a consolidator that can consolidate.
    • The estimates were cut deeply, guidance was rebased and the disruption is abating
    • But the trough multiple passes only at the boundary and the capitulation, real in the price and the guidance in 2025, was never complete in the sell-side's ratings and is nine months behind us.
  • Where the chain holds and where it breaks.
    • It holds from the external change through the company's actions to the operating metrics, the statements and the estimates: every layer has moved in the same direction in 2026.
    • It breaks, or may break, at two links: whether the Q2 2026 organic growth was volume or price, which the company's disclosure does not separate and which the Q3 2026 update in late October 2026 will test
    • And whether the cash arithmetic takes leverage inside 3.0x during 2027, which the working-capital build of H1 2026 has so far denied and which the chief financial officer has declined to put a landing date on.

  • The case therefore is a coherent recovery at a fair price: a clearing whose earnings leg is probable and modest, and whose re-rating leg is a near-even bet on the balance sheet.

    • It is a park at EUR 11.9.
    • It becomes a progression below about EUR 10.4, where both horizons clear the bar on the modal path, or on a Q3 2026 update that shows organic growth of 3 percent or more with volume named as the driver and the working-capital build unwinding.

  • Rating = C, because the change is real and has propagated to the sixth layer of the chain, but the input forces are mostly external and partly temporary, the modal earnings growth of about 9 percent a year sits below the framework's 12 to 20 percent band, and the modal return clears the 20 percent bar over eighteen months by a fraction and misses it over three years

    • Materiality is average, not high.
    • Not B, because a B needs a modal return comfortably inside the 18 to 24 percent band on both horizons and an earnings path inside the target band
    • Here the eighteen-month figure needs a re-rating we give near-even odds and the three-year figure is a C, and a B should survive the consensus earnings path, which this does not.
    • Not D, because the change has reached the estimates layer and the sell-side is upgrading, which a D case does not show.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The counter-case at its strongest: the Q2 2026 inflection is the final pricing gasp of a multi-year destocking cycle, not a volume recovery.
    • The 30 to 40 percent supplier price increases and two to three months of low-cost inventory produced the 100 basis point gross margin gain and the 46.0 percent conversion margin, a jump of the kind that reverts
      • Industrial Chemicals' Q2 growth of 4.2 percent was predominantly pricing rather than volume
      • EMEA Life Sciences fell 6.6 percent organically in H1 2026 and the European long tail of SME manufacturers that justifies the high-touch model is shrinking
      • A de-escalation in the Middle East reverses the pricing
      • Working capital absorbed EUR 29m of free cash flow in the half and leverage rose to 3.4x
      • Brenntag was downgraded in June 2026 on the same pricing peak
      • And the sell-side's ratings never capitulated, so the fourth year of downgrades has room to run once H2 2026 disappoints.

    • On this reading the shares, 77 percent off the low, have re-rated to a level that leaves no room for the disappointment, and the Risks report's own analysis of competing hypotheses gave this false-friend profile the win with two disconfirming items against nine.

    • What we concede.

      • The Q2 beat was assisted by price and inventory, and management said so.
      • The volume evidence is regional (APAC and the Americas at high-single-digit volume growth) and one quarter old, with EMEA flat to down.
      • The working-capital and leverage lines moved the wrong way in the half.
      • And our own drop-through sensitivity shows that a world in which the margin and the interest line do not improve gives an eighteen-month return below zero at the uncleared multiple.
    • Why we still hold our view, in part.
      • The destocking end is corroborated by IMCD's 7.5 percent organic growth in the same quarter, which the false-friend reading does not explain.
      • APAC's +12.7 percent was described by the company as volume-led and came after ten quarters of decline and a deliberate pruning of low-margin volume, which is not what a pricing gasp looks like.
      • The cost saving (EUR 11m in H1 2026 against a EUR 20m run-rate) and the coupon saving (EUR 6.5m a year) are contractual or arithmetic, not cyclical.
      • And consensus at the FactSet median already discounts a 3 to 5 percent EPS growth path, so the shares at 12.9x are not priced for the bull case
      • Our path exceeds the median mainly on basis and the interest line.
    • Where the counter-case bites is the return, and there we agree with it.
      • If pricing fades and interest does not fall, the eighteen-month return is about 7.9 percent a year at the blended multiple and below zero at the uncleared one.
      • The competing hypothesis does not overturn the direction of the case
      • It removes the margin of safety at EUR 11.9, which is why the recommendation is to park rather than to progress.

Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the supply-side change is the clearest tailwind in the research: suppliers announced price increases of up to 30 to 40 percent on selected portfolios in H1 2026 and customers largely accepted them, Jefferies models product inflation of 5 percent in Q2 2026 and 10 percent in H2 2026, and a distributor on a spread model passes this through with a lag that first compresses and then expands the gross margin.
  • The mechanism: gross profit margin 24.7 percent in Q2 2026, up 100 basis points, helped by two to three months of lower-cost inventory
    • Conversion margin 46.0 percent in Q2 2026 against 42.3 percent in Q1 2026.
  • The other side: Chinese supply is structurally cheaper and non-exclusive
    • Principals reclaim large accounts in downturns
    • The top ten principals are 33.3 percent of revenue.
  • Not A, because the force is judged temporary by its own sponsors: management expects the pricing and inventory benefits to ease in H2 2026, and Deutsche Bank warns that a rapid de-escalation in the Middle East would reverse it.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because end-market demand is recovering cyclically from a destocking trough, not changing structurally: Q2 2026 group organic growth was +3.9 percent after four quarters of decline, and the two-year visibility the framework asks for is absent.
  • The trough: organic revenue -5.9 percent in 2023, -1.1 percent in 2024, -1.6 percent in 2025, -3.9 percent in Q1 2026.
  • The turn: Q2 2026 +3.9 percent against consensus of 1.8 percent
    • APAC +12.7 percent, the Americas +5.9 percent, EMEA -1.3 percent
    • H1 2026 organic growth +0.3 percent.
  • Quality of the turn: Morgan Stanley reads it as primarily led by high-single-digit volume gains in APAC and the Americas, but Industrial Chemicals' Q2 growth of +4.2 percent as predominantly pricing rather than volume
    • Pre-buying was exceptionally limited.
  • Structural backdrop: specialty distribution outgrows the producers by 2.7 percent a year, and the market grows about 3.5 percent a year to 2030.
  • Not D, because the inflection is corroborated by IMCD's +7.5 percent organic growth in Q2 2026 and by management's reading that customer inventories are at lean levels.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because two macro forces act on Azelis more than on the universe and both are headwinds: European industrial demand (EMEA is 45.5 percent of revenue; EMEA Life Sciences fell 6.6 percent organically in H1 2026; European de-industrialisation is a high-plausibility failure route in the Risks report) and the euro (currency cut reported revenue by 3.8 percent in FY2025 and 4.5 percent in Q1 2026; APAC's Q2 2026 growth carried a 3.4 percent currency drag).
  • Currency sensitivity: a 10 percent move in the US dollar moves earnings per share by about 12 percent
    • 32 percent of revenue is in US dollars.
  • The offset: the refinancing removed a rate headwind, and the Turkish hyperinflation drag ended on 1 April 2025.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is easing at the margin in APAC, where Chinese export pressure eased slightly in H1 2026 on domestic supply constraints in Asia, while it remains a structural headwind in semi-specialties and while the better-capitalised peer IMCD out-executes in EMEA (+10.3 percent organic growth against Azelis's +0.4 percent in Q2 2026).
  • Chinese oversupply: Deutsche Bank says it continues to fuel aggressive export volumes into non-regulated emerging markets
    • Latin American gross profit fell 15 percent in FY2025 and APAC organic gross profit fell 11.6 percent.
  • Easing: Jefferies models a gradual APAC margin recovery as pressure in Southeast Asia and Latin America eases
    • APAC organic growth was +4.0 percent in Q1 2026 and +12.7 percent in Q2 2026.
  • Consolidation race: IMCD completed three acquisitions (EUR 70m of revenue) in H1 2026 at 2.8x leverage while Azelis paced its deals at 3.4x
    • Barentz and Caldic at 5x to 6x leverage are in effect restricted.
  • Not D, because the direction over the last six months is easing, not tightening.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because two changes pull in opposite directions: the Middle East conflict moves cost and pricing (a temporary pricing tailwind; a logistics and demand headwind on 6 to 7 percent of revenue), while tariff uncertainty has faded and REACH-type compliance keeps rising as a barrier that favours scale.
  • Middle East: direct exposure 6 to 7 percent of revenue, the largest in the peer set (IMCD under 1 percent)
    • EMEA Q2 2026 organic growth of -1.3 percent was attributed to logistics disruption.
  • Tariffs: Jefferies reports that the earlier concerns have continued to fade and that customers and distributors have fully digested the initial shock.
  • Regulation: an MSCI ESG AAA rating (June 2026) and SBTi validation (May 2026) are read by management as mandate-winning credentials.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the digital-disintermediation force is neither strengthening nor disproved: Morgan Stanley keeps it as a structural risk to gross margins on small accounts, while three separate experts (from Univar, a former Azelis M&A manager, a former Brenntag chief executive on index contracts) argue that producers cannot serve fragmented small-volume demand direct.
  • Force contained: zero churn among the top 20 principals over ten years
    • Only 5 percent of distribution volume is on long-term index contracts.
  • Force live: 10 to 20 percent of Azelis's own commercial data is inaccurate, so its digital defence is weaker than presented.

    • The asset-light cash model then converts the recovery into debt reduction
    • And once leverage is back inside target the market restores the multiple it pays a consolidator that can consolidate.
    • The earnings leg of the thesis is the first half of that chain and the re-rating leg is the second half.

    • The disruption is abating on the evidence of Q2 2026: destocking ended, APAC past its integration issues at +12.7 percent organic growth, Vigon resolved, the Turkish hyperinflation drag ended, Chinese export pressure eased slightly.
