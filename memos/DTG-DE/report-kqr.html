Section 1 – Key question and brief

Section summary
  1. The structural integrity of the European heavy truck aftermarket moat remains a key debate as low-cost Chinese electric entrants partner with independent workshop networks to bypass proprietary dealer barriers.

Central Question and Strategic Mandate

1.
Critical structural debate: The central investment question is whether the profitable dealer and service network moat (representing 20-25% of revenue and most of the profit for European truck OEMs like Daimler Truck, Volvo, and Traton) can withstand the entry of Chinese electric trucks (e.g., Sany, BYD) arriving at 220,000 euros compared to European pricing of 300,000-400,000 euros. Analysts from DNB Carnegie note that while European incumbents have historically been protected by high barriers around proprietary service coverage, the transition to electrification threatens to weaken parts of that moat while exposing a structural cost gap. This structural threat is highly relevant because customers purchase uptime and lifetime support as much as they purchase the physical heavy vehicle itself.

1 2 Lucrative aftersales pool: Deutsche Bank Research estimates that aftermarket parts and services represent approximately 25% of total revenue for leading truck manufacturers and yield highly resilient recurring earnings streams. Significant margin contribution: Bernstein Research notes that aftermarket parts and services represent the best profit pool in the commercial vehicle industry due to considerable pricing power over fragmented customer bases. This high-margin services business accounted for roughly 20% of Volvo's truck segment sales, proving vital to offset vehicle cost inflation. The broker also observes that early data from Brazil shows Chinese manufacturers capturing a 67% market share in electric trucks compared to only 1% in non-electric trucks, demonstrating that electrification can disrupt traditional moats.
4.
Stable service contribution: Traton SE financial reports demonstrate that its Vehicle Services business consistently contributes 20 percent of its total sales revenue, providing robust stability during periods of softer vehicle shipments. Operating return on sales across Traton Operations reached 8.1% in the second quarter of 2026, supported in part by positive pricing effects and resilient customer demand for parts. The group is actively targeting higher lifecycle service penetration to drive more profitable long-term revenue streams.

Adjacent Uncertainties and Transition Stage

5.
Low initial volumes: DNB Carnegie highlights that battery-electric heavy trucks represented a mere 2.1 percent of European heavy truck registrations in 2025. This low market penetration reflects substantial vehicle cost premiums, lack of megawatt charging, and fleet anxiety over truck residual values. However, the adoption curve is expected to become highly transformative in the 2030s when stricter European carbon emissions rules mandate a 45% reduction from a 2019 baseline.
6.
Accelerating competitive pressure: Deutsche Bank Research reports that registrations of all-electric heavy trucks in Europe surged by 81 percent year-over-year in the second quarter of 2026. Although absolute volumes are still modest, Chinese entrants are seeking to enter the European market with an aggressive 30% pricing discount. Because fleet operators prioritize total cost of ownership as their primary buying criterion, legacy manufacturers face valuation multiple compression risks if this pricing pressure damages terminal margin expectations.
7.
Critical infrastructure limitations: An industry expert and former Vice President at Volvo Trucks stresses that fast-charging and grid integration remain the most significant bottlenecks for heavy-duty electric vehicle adoption in Europe. Fleet operators are hesitant to transition to battery-electric platforms until they can comfortably achieve 600 miles of range without range anxiety. Furthermore, early trial phases require extensive public subsidies, which can elongate the commercial purchase cycle significantly.
8.
Durable dealer defensibility: Other side of the story: Analysts from DNB Carnegie note that the incumbent service network remains much deeper than any independent alternative, as Mercedes-Benz Trucks alone operates over 1,400 service touchpoints across Europe. It is highly probable that fleet operators will remain intensely loyal to established brands because cross-border long-haul routes require absolute uptime credibility that independent workshop networks cannot replicate overnight. Therefore, the threat of rapid market disruption from Chinese entrants may be vastly overextended if customer adoption remains slow and geographically isolated.

Section 2 – Battery-Electric Pricing and TCO Parity Dynamics

Section summary
  1. The deep purchasing price discount of Chinese electric trucks challenges European incumbents, though high battery costs and staggered subsidy rollouts delay widespread total cost of ownership parity across major logistics corridors.

Price Arbitrage and Battery Cost Structure

1.
Stark pricing discount: Chinese electric heavy trucks are priced around 200,000 to 250,000 euros, representing an aggressive 30 percent discount compared to legacy models according to Deutsche Bank Research.
2.
Incumbent premium barrier: Both Volvo and Scania confirm that Western battery-electric trucks remain two to three times more expensive than diesel equivalents, translating to about 300,000 to 400,000 euros per vehicle depending on specific configurations according to DNB Carnegie.

2.

Shifting bearing specifications: The transition to electric drivetrains requires insulated, high-speed bearings to underpin higher average selling prices and offset traditional internal combustion engine component losses.

Section 8 – Data gaps

Section summary
  1. Major empirical data gaps regarding long-term asset depreciation, real-world maintenance costs, and unsubsidized fleet transition rates currently prevent definitive multi-year forecasts for European batteryelectric commercial vehicle markets.

Unresolved Commercial and Performance Metrics

1.
Uncertain asset depreciation: The absolute shortage of secondary market transactions prevents analysts from conducting rigorous depreciation curve and residual value analyses for early battery-electric heavy trucks.
2.
Unproven lifecycle maintenance: Real-world repair and maintenance cost deltas over 500,000 kilometers remain highly speculative because long-term parts wear remains too early to model with high accuracy.
3.
Variable payload penalties: Heavy battery weights impose severe payload limitations on long-haul routes, but fleet operators struggle to quantify how this restricts net payload of heavier shipments across specific cargo types.
4.
Unsubsidized demand uncertainty: Widespread commercial adoption remains highly unpredictable because genuine market demand is very conditioned by the presence of volatile government purchase incentives.

Proposed Empirical Resolution Channels

5.
Targeted fleet surveys: To resolve these information gaps, analysts must conduct direct fleet manager surveys to track real-world electricity and localized maintenance costs across different regional transport corridors.
6.
Contractual credit checks: Reviewing OEM financial reports represents a critical channel to monitor receivables from residual value guarantees which disclose the scale of risk sitting on manufacturer balance sheets.
7.
Strategic channel checks: Conducting direct channel checks at European trade shows allows investors to verify whether early independent service networks prove credible to support commercial long-haul operations.
8.
Other side of the story: Rapid technological obsolescence of initial battery packs may be mitigated by strong long-term durability and second-life potential of lithium iron phosphate technology in stationary grid storage applications.

Section 9 – Second and third-order implications

Section summary
  1. Rapid Chinese electric truck adoption triggers cascading infrastructure stresses and supplier margin erosion, creating severe structural risks across the European automotive value chain.

Cascading Value Chain Pressures

1.
Severe grid congestion: Aging infrastructure and rapid distribution network upgrades create a structural bottleneck, as forty percent of European distribution grids exceed forty years of age, causing severe connection delays for megawatt charging depots.
2.
Accelerated workshop disruption: Emerging Chinese entrants are aggressively partnering with independent networks like Alltrucks to bypass OEM networks, allowing fleet operators to utilise over six hundred independent partner workshops across Europe.
3.
Severe salvage complexity: The rapid influx of electric trucks creates operational bottlenecks at salvage yards because testing, handling, and diagnosing retired battery-electric packs requires highly specialized technician training.
4.
Closed battery circularity: Closed-loop recycling is scaling rapidly as CATL's subsidiary Brunp processed 210,000 tonnes of batteries in 2025, recovering ninety-nine point six percent of nickel, cobalt, and manganese directly for new production.
5.
Eroding supplier margins: Traditional automotive tier-1 suppliers face severe margin compression because electric commercial powertrains substantially reduce mechanical components such as exhaust systems and traditional fuel-injection systems.
