EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The evidence best supports Answer B with high confidence –AB InBev's earnings per share will grow faster than Carlsberg's over the next 18 months to 3 years, driven by superior emerging market exposure, premium brand compounding, and digital platform monetization that collectively outpace Carlsberg's complex soft drinks integration model. The quantitative earnings trajectory shows a stark divergence, with AB InBev projected to deliver a c. 12.6% EPS CAGR through fiscal year 2029 compared to Carlsberg's c. 6.9% CAGR, while AB InBev has also achieved 22% positive consensus EPS revisions year-to-date versus Carlsberg's 10% positive revisions. This fundamental gap in earnings momentum reflects distinct strategic positioning, where AB InBev's premium megabrands generate superior gross profit per hectoliter that shields consolidated operating margins from regional headwinds, while Carlsberg's embedded beer volume growth remains highly restricted at a 0.3% CAGR, making organic expansion harder to achieve. Carlsberg's strategic pivot centers on leveraging its multi-beverage model following the Britvic acquisition, with the company expecting to deliver 50% of the total GBP 110 million in cost synergies during fiscal year 2026. The acquisition transformed its UK business into the largest combined beer and soft drinks provider in Great Britain, with soft drinks expanding to 30% of group volume post-acquisition. However, while Carlsberg's accelerating Britvic synergies will maximize near-term earnings growth in European soft drinks, AB InBev's defensible emerging market footprint will drive superior long-term volume compounding in years 2 and 3, creating a temporal mismatch where near-term operational wins cannot offset medium-term structural advantages.

The regional volume dynamics further reinforce the relative growth differential. China remains a significant volume drag representing 30% of Carlsberg's beer volume compared to only 13% for AB InBev, while Carlsberg's Western European beer volumes declined by 1.0% organically due to unfavorable consumer dynamics in Poland. Although Carlsberg possesses the financial means to rapidly deleverage below 2.5x net debt and its accelerated Britvic cost synergies will provide near-term margin protection, the company's beer volume compounds at a narrow 0.3% CAGR, creating a structural ceiling on organic expansion that soft drinks diversification can only partially offset.

AB InBev's competitive advantages extend beyond traditional brewing operations, with its BEES platform capturing USD 15.0 billion in GMV during Q2 2026, which monetizes route-to-market as an asset and generates high-margin digital revenue streams. This digital ecosystem creates a sustainable moat that complements AB InBev's above-core beer portfolio, which generated 35% of total revenue, with Corona revenue increasing by 8.3%, demonstrating the company's ability to capture premiumization trends across multiple geographies simultaneously.

The integration risks facing Carlsberg introduce execution uncertainty, as any failure to capture Britvic synergies would severely impair the company's ability to achieve mid-term margin targets, while the soft drinks portfolio carries a negative price-mix impact of c. 30bps per annum relative to high-margin premium beer. Carlsberg's relative margin expansion may stall due to elevated aluminum and freight cost inflation, creating additional operational headwinds beyond the core volume challenges.

These integration complexities contrast sharply with AB InBev's more straightforward premium brand playbook, where established global platforms can scale incrementally without transformative M&A integration risk. & Co Redburn The unresolved question centers on continued consumer weakness in China, which remains an unresolved volume headwind that impacts the premium beer category across Asia, although upcoming bond redemptions present a negligible Year 3 EPS impact of 0.6% for AB InBev and 1.1% for Carlsberg.

Critical monitoring points include monthly volume data from China and Western Europe, quarterly Britvic integration updates, and the pace of BEES platform expansion, as these metrics will determine whether Carlsberg's multi-beverage soft drink pivot can offset Western European beer declines quickly enough to narrow the earnings growth gap. The verdict could change if Carlsberg delivers superior organic EBIT growth through accelerated Britvic synergies that exceed targets while AB InBev's US volumes remain structurally impaired, creating a scenario where soft drinks integration outperforms base case assumptions.

The plausibility assessment shows that Answer A possesses medium plausibility tied to unprecedented PepsiCo European bottling expansion, while Answer B has high plausibility because AB InBev's highly profitable emerging market footprint insulates it from developed market headwinds. The most likely way the verdict proves incorrect would be a faster-thanexpected Chinese consumer recovery combined with Britvic synergy overdelivery, though current evidence suggests this represents a lower-probability outcome given the 0.3% embedded beer volume CAGR constraint and ongoing Asian market softness.
