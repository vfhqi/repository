Section 2 – Organic Growth Trajectory and Price vs Volume Dynamics

Section summary
  1. SPIE seeks to accelerate organic growth to 3% to 4% from historical averages of 1.3%.
  2. Historical organic expansion relied heavily on pricing pass-through, which now faces headwinds from normalizing inflation.
  3. Persistent weakness in French tertiary demand and fiber rollouts acts as a primary drag on volume growth.

Historical Growth and Price-Volume Splits

1.
Historical top-line performance: SPIE has historically delivered modest organic revenue expansion over the long term. Deutsche Bank's report on September 1, 2026 indicates that the company's historical organic growth average is only 1.3%. This historical growth rate places the firm below the peer median in eighteen of thirtythree quarters analyzed between 2018 and 2026. Transitioning to higher growth is essential because sub-par organic expansion would severely limit future earnings per share acceleration.
2.
Shifting price-volume dynamics: Post-pandemic organic expansion was heavily driven by pricing pass- through rather than real volume gains. Morgan Stanley's report on May 17, 2024 states that price contributed two-thirds of organic growth post-pandemic, while volume contributed only one-third. This tracker implies that historical volume growth was flat pre-pandemic and accelerated to only 2.0% to 2.5% after 2022. Future earnings growth will depend on whether volume can expand as price inflation normalizes.
3.
Imbalanced market pricing: Structural supply shortages of qualified technicians have historically supported SPIE's pricing power. Morgan Stanley's report on February 7, 2025 notes that an imbalance between high demand and scarce supply of skilled workers enables rational pricing. This supportive environment has allowed the company to comfortably pass on labor cost inflation to its customers. Strong pricing power underpins future EPS durability unless peer competitive behaviors turn irrational.
4.
Contrary evidence: Weak tertiary real estate demand in France has recently forced SPIE to sacrifice volume to protect margins. Morgan Stanley's note on September 10, 2026 highlights that residential builders are entering tertiary electrical and mechanical markets, accepting lower margins to win volume. To combat this, SPIE has chosen to remain highly selective, which has negatively impacted short-term volume growth in France. This volume sacrifice means that pricing power is weakening in commoditized segments, threatening near-term organic targets.

This target remains unchanged despite the company reporting lower organic growth in recent quarters. Meeting this target is essential to supporting the company's valuation multiple of thirteen times forward earnings.
