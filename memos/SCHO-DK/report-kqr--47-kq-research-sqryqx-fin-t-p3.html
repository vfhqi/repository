Section summary
  1. Analyzing feedback loops in Schouw & Co.'s business model is crucial for assessing its 2030 EBITDA targets and its persistent conglomerate valuation discount.

Earnings and capital allocation impact

3.

Section summary
  1. BioMar relies heavily on supplier finance and factoring to buffer raw material price spikes.
  2. The divergence between operating cash flow and rising EBITDA mirrors historical inflationary episodes.

Supplier finance and factoring dynamics

1.
Elevated financing utilization: BioMar expanded its supply chain financing to DKK 1,333 million at Q2 2026. This reverse factoring program optimized payment terms to 136 days compared to 120 days for nonparticipants. Rising input costs and procurement activity drove this expansion, which temporarily shields earnings per share from cash constraints.
2.
Intensive factoring use: BioMar increased its non-recourse factoring utilization to DKK 894 million at Q2 2026. Management uses this program to manage commercial credit risks with specific customers, particularly in the salmon segment. While factoring improves capital efficiency, a sudden reduction in facility limits would compress group liquidity.
3.
Extended supplier credit: BioMar actively leverages its supply chain financing program to negotiate favorable payment terms with strategic suppliers. The program supports long-term relationships while allowing BioMar to maintain average payment terms of 136 days. This arrangement allows the group to reduce its cash conversion cycle compared to peers without these automated systems.
4.
Credit risk mitigation: BioMar utilizes non-recourse factoring specifically to limit its exposure to commercial trade receivables risks with high-volume salmon customers. Factoring utilization rose to DKK 894 million by June 2026, driven by higher sales activity and rising commodity prices. While this program stabilizes receivables and secures liquidity, it binds the company to the financial health of its banking partners.

Section 3 – Salmon-Farmer Consolidation and Customer Integration

Section summary
  1. Customer consolidation in Norway directly threatens independent feed suppliers by concentrating purchasing power.
  2. Stricter environmental regulations and taxation in Norway limit volume growth for conventional open-net salmon farming.
  3. Integrated feed ventures face high biological complexity, as shown by Mowi partnering with Skretting in late 2025.

Customer consolidation and volume pressure

1.
Contract volume setbacks: BioMar suffered a significant volume contraction in its core Norwegian salmon division during early 2026. Management reported in its Q2 2026 interim report that Norwegian feed sales fell 11% year-on-year due to a loss of contract from customer consolidation. This loss contributed to a wider 4% volume decline in BioMar's global salmon segment during H1 2026, highlighting how customer-level mergers can instantly delete historical volume. Consequently, ongoing consolidations among major growers threaten to permanently cap independent feed volumes unless BioMar secures new accounts.
2.
Consolidated market shares: The global salmon feed market is highly concentrated, leaving independent suppliers vulnerable to shifts in client alignment. DNB Carnegie reported in July 2026 that the top three independent feed producers control 70% of the market, with Skretting and Cargill at 25% each and BioMar at 19%. Meanwhile, captive feed producers control 23% of global volume, meaning the addressable market for independent suppliers is structurally restricted. This tight competitive dynamic indicates that losing even one large customer can materially compress BioMar's market share.
3.
Bargaining power imbalances: Large salmon farmers utilize centralized procurement and in-house nutritional experts to exert strong pricing pressure on feed suppliers. Morgan Stanley noted in July 2026 that BioMar's top ten salmon customers represent 61% of its segment sales, creating a highly dependent revenue structure. This concentration allows consolidated farmers to demand lower margins or shift volumes to competitors, directly impacting BioMar's long-term profitability. To preserve margins, independent suppliers must offer specialized, high-performance formulations that justify premium pricing.

Regulatory constraints and biomass limits

4.
Restricted capacity development: Stricter environmental regulations in Norway limit the expansion of conventional open-net pens, capping total feed demand growth. DNB Carnegie notes that Norway's traffic light system limits growth to a maximum of 6% every second year based on wild salmon sea lice pressure. This strict ceiling has kept actual capacity growth well below 6% since its 2017 introduction, suppressing the potential volume expansion of feed suppliers. Consequently, independent feed companies must focus on increasing value per metric ton rather than relying on volume expansion.
5.
Taxation and capital shifts: The introduction of the Norwegian resource rent tax has altered farming capital allocation and slowed traditional sea-based investments. Norne Securities observed in August 2026 that the effective tax rate of 25% applies heavily to the sea phase of salmon farming, encouraging operators to shift capital elsewhere. This tax policy initially triggered the cancellation or postponement of several major development projects, which directly dampened immediate feed demand. regulations change, this tax burden will continue to incentivize farmers to prioritize land-based or closedcontainment farming technologies.
6.
Closed containment opportunities: Emerging closed-containment and land-based farming technologies require highly specialized feed formulations, benefiting innovative suppliers. DNB Carnegie highlights that upcoming regulatory updates will facilitate widespread investment in closed containment units across Norway. These advanced systems require tailored feeds with precise physical and nutritional qualities, which carries higher manufacturing complexity. While traditional volumes remain capped, this shift toward premium functional feeds will support BioMar's product mix and gross margins.

Customer integration and feed division partnerships

7.
Integrated strategic reversals: Integrated salmon farming companies have historically attempted to produce their own feed, but rising biological complexity is reversing this trend. Mowi entered feed production in 2014 to build a fully integrated value chain and stabilize its internal operating costs. However, Mowi announced in December 2025 that it would exit independent feed formulation by partnering with Skretting. This partnership proves that even the world's largest salmon farmer struggled to maintain competitive formulation R&D internally, highlighting the severe operational barriers to full vertical integration.
8.
Economic integration penalties: Mowi's ten-year venture into independent feed formulation resulted in significant financial and operational underperformance before its strategic shift. DNB Carnegie reported in July 2026 that Mowi's internal feed division suffered an estimated EUR 134 million in lost EBIT and 70,000 metric tons of lost volume due to underperforming formulations. This historical failure illustrates that feed formulation requires immense scientific scale, deterring other mid-sized farmers from integrating backward. As a result, independent feed formulators like BioMar retain a strong competitive advantage over self-formulating farmers.
9.
Sourcing and R&D alliances: Under its new partnership, Mowi retains its physical feed factories but relies entirely on Skretting's proprietary formulas and purchasing scale. Mowi's Q4 2025 earnings call confirmed that partnering with Skretting will deliver at least EUR 55 million in annualized net cost savings. This structure allows Mowi to secure the lowest possible feed costs while retaining the basic processing margins of its feed plants. For BioMar, this model suggests that major integrated customers may seek similar formulation service agreements rather than building competing R&D facilities.

Precedents and defensive contract structures

10.
Historical integration precedents: Vertical integration in the aquaculture sector has historically run in multi-year cycles of combination and eventual separation. DNB Carnegie recalled in July 2026 that historical integrations, such as the combination and separation of EWOS and Cermaq, proved highly complex. Similar difficulties occurred when Nutreco acquired Marine Harvest in 1999 and subsequently divested its farming assets in 2006 to focus purely on feed. These historical precedents suggest that the high capital intensity and divergent operational focuses of feed and farming eventually break integrated structures apart.
11.
Scale constraints at peers: Integrated farmers with smaller operational scales face severe efficiency and R&D bottlenecks in their captive feed divisions. Bakkafrost operates its own feed division, Havsbrun, which provides full control over marine ingredient sourcing and internal feed supply. However, DNB Carnegie points out that Havsbrun remains constrained by its small volume of 163,000 metric tons, leading to higher unit costs and lower R&D efficiency compared to global feed leaders. - Earnings Calls This scale bottleneck indicates that independent producers will continue to dominate high-performance feed markets.
12.
Defensive contract insulation: Long-term cost-plus contracts and specialized product portfolios insulate independent feed manufacturers from sudden volume and margin shocks. BioMar utilizes medium-term to longterm contracts that typically exceed three to five years in duration, providing high volume visibility. These agreements incorporate automatic raw material pass-through clauses, protecting BioMar's absolute margins from commodity price volatility. Furthermore, BioMar's focus on functional feeds and deep technical support secures customer loyalty, preventing rapid, price-driven supplier switching.
13.
Contrary evidence: BioMar actually expanded its overall volume guidance for 2026 to a range of 1,630 to 1,700 thousand tonnes despite the Norwegian contract loss. Management stated in its Q2 2026 report that this upward revision was driven by gaining new customers and contracts in other salmon regions. This recovery proves that BioMar's global multi-species platform can successfully absorb localized customer consolidation hits. Consequently, the self-reinforcing consolidation loop is partially balanced by organic growth in emerging markets like Ecuador and Vietnam.

Long-term market outlook

14.
Other side of the story: While customer consolidation and vertical integration represent structural risks for independent aquafeed suppliers, the extreme complexity of feed formulation acts as a natural barrier that preserves the independent market. DNB Carnegie argues that Mowi's historic retreat from self-formulation will deter other major farmers from trying to bypass specialized feed manufacturers. Furthermore, BioMar's superior EBIT margins compared to peers Skretting and EWOS since 2022 demonstrate that its commercial excellence program and focus on high-value functional products successfully offset customer bargaining power. Consequently, customer integration is highly unlikely to cap BioMar's long-term salmon volumes, as farmers ultimately prioritize biological feed performance over captive production.
