Section 3 – Geographic Tax Disclosures and Profit Before Tax

Geographic Profitability Gaps

1.
Geographic profit limits: Arteche does not disclose granular profit before tax or specific tax charges for individual foreign jurisdictions like Germany, China, or the United States in its public accounts, meaning investors must rely on regional indicators. According to the consolidated annual accounts of 26 February 2026, the company operates across ten main countries but only discloses consolidated before-tax results. Consequently, analyzing individual regional profitability remains difficult because the necessary country-by-country breakdown is omitted from standard reports, which limits precise local EPS modeling.
2.
Rising profit performance: Consolidated profit before tax demonstrated substantial growth due to strong operational execution across key global electrification projects. Arteche's consolidated report of 26 February 2026 states that consolidated result before tax reached 54.31 million euros in FY2025, representing a significant increase from 32.38 million euros in FY2024. This operating improvement implies that underlying demand remains robust across major transmission and distribution markets, which helps to sustain a positive EPS trajectory unless overseas tax burdens increase.
3.
Overseas tax drag: The tax effect of differing foreign rates represents a persistent headwind for the group because international operations carry higher statutory tax rates. Arteche's tax reconciliation from 26 February 2026 reveals that the application of different tax rates added 535 thousand euros to tax expense in FY2025, compared to 750 thousand euros in FY2024. Thus, divergent regional tax burdens drag down consolidated earnings because overseas business expansion increases exposure to high-tax jurisdictions, thereby reducing net profit margins for shareholders.
