EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 1 finding

MOL Hungarian Oil & Gas delivered exceptional Clean CCS EBITDA of USD 3,369 million in full-year 2025, representing a 9.6% year-on-year increase, and achieved an extraordinary USD 1,297 million in Q2 2026, substantially beating consensus expectations by 38%. The company operates as a conservative honest-tracker, setting baseline guidance using USD 65/bbl Brent assumptions while actual commodity prices and refining margins delivered far stronger results.

However, the investment case remains highly bifurcated between stellar cyclical downstream performance driven by temporary geopolitical supply disruptions and persistent structural headwinds from government price caps, carbon taxation, monopolistic pipeline transit costs, and a massive internal workforce exodus that threatens medium-term execution capacity.

Materiality of strategic and operational change

Leadership transitions remain orderly but consolidate control under long-serving company insiders, with József Simola departing as Group CFO on 15 August 2025 and Dr. Ákos Székely appointed as his successor. However, structural workforce transformation under Project HIVE triggered a massive spike in employee turnover to 13% in H1 2026 versus 6% in H1 2025, with total leavers surging to 3,201 employees compared to 1,561 in the prior year period, representing a doubling of the historical baseline. Strategic capex allocation of USD 12 billion over 2025-2030 balances USD 5.1 billion for strategic low-carbon projects against USD 6.5 billion for sustaining fossil fuel assets, yet execution remains constrained by uncertain EU funding for green hydrogen projects and unresolved pipeline bottlenecks. MOL signed an agreement to acquire a 35% non-operated stake in Cyprus's Aphrodite gas field for consideration of up to USD 720 million, expected to close in early 2027, absorbing substantial near-term free cash flow and introducing material geological and execution risks.

The total workforce contracted 3% to 24,674 employees, while ethical reports rose 67% to 74 cases in H1 2026, signaling elevated internal friction and cultural strain.
