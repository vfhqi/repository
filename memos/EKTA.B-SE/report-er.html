Section summary
  1. Stagnant top-line execution and intensifying duopolistic competition offset Elekta's ambitious

Elekta's first quarter of fiscal year 2026/27, reported on August 27, 2026, delivered a sharply bifurcated performance that fundamentally altered the investment narrative by validating management's profitability-first turnaround while simultaneously exposing persistent top-line execution weakness. The quarter recorded a 2% organic sales contraction that missed consensus expectations of flat growth, yet achieved a dramatic adjusted gross margin expansion to 42.6% from 37.0% in the prior year and doubled the adjusted EBIT margin to 11.2% versus 6.5%.

This margin-led earnings beat, partially supported by a one-time US tariff refund of SEK 53m contributing 150 basis points, demonstrated that the new leadership's aggressive cost restructuring and pricing discipline are delivering tangible financial results even as revenue growth remains elusive. The investment case now hinges on whether Elekta can successfully convert impressive margin execution into sustainable top-line momentum.

Management's medium-term targets call for mid-single digit constant-currency revenue growth and a 14% to 16% adjusted EBIT margin by FY 2028/29, representing a substantial acceleration from recent stagnant performance. Sell-side analysts remain divided, with Deutsche Bank's

Friedrichs characterizing the 14% to 16% margin target as far from conservative and noting that achieving these goals implies growth acceleration to around 6% in outer years. The company's structural transformation under CEO Jakob Just-Bomholt centers on a newly implemented five-region decentralized operating model replacing the previous three-region structure, which grants regional presidents direct CEO reporting with end-to-end P&L accountability to accelerate commercial responsiveness.

Regional performance patterns revealed stark divergence between mature and emerging markets that will define near-term earnings trajectory. The Americas region posted 19.1% EBIT margin as sales grew 2%, driven by double-digit solution order growth following Elekta Evo's regulatory approval, with over 25% of Evo orders representing direct competitive flips from rival installed bases. Europe delivered solid execution with 5% constant currency sales growth.

However, these gains were severely offset by China's 16% revenue contraction stemming from lingering anti-corruption campaign headwinds, while APJ sales declined 12% due to constrained healthcare budgets. The critical question is whether three consecutive quarters of order growth in China signals genuine market stabilization or merely reflects easy prior-year comparatives.

The quarter's most significant strategic shift involved management's explicit pivot toward cash-backed earnings quality over accounting-driven metrics. Leadership is actively aligning R&D capitalization with amortization to ensure reported EBIT reflects true cash generation, while reducing the capitalization rate from approximately 60% to 40% removes a historical margin tailwind and forces genuine operational efficiency.

Concurrently, the establishment of a dedicated dynamic pricing desk enforces strict floor pricing guidelines to raise average selling prices, and management delivered double-digit contract price increases on its extensive service base. These commercial initiatives, combined with annualized run-rate cost savings exceeding SEK 500m from workforce reductions, represent a fundamental shift in operational intensity that prioritizes margin expansion over volume growth.

Sell-side reactions crystallized around this tension between impressive margin delivery and disappointing revenue execution. DNB Carnegie's Liljeberg upgraded adjusted EPS forecasts by 4% for fiscal year 2026/27 while maintaining a Buy rating and SEK 70 target price, arguing that bottoming gross margins and substantial opex savings will drive near-term earnings. Conversely, Jefferies' Dormois maintained a Hold rating and SEK 60 target price, expressing concern that average order intake has declined by 2.5% over the last 18 months, creating ongoing short-term risk to revenue recovery.

Deutsche Bank's Friedrichs lowered his target price from SEK 60 to SEK 52 with a Hold rating, highlighting that medium-term targets set an unrealistically high bar given competitive pressures. The competitive landscape presents the most significant external threat to Elekta's turnaround trajectory. The global radiotherapy market remains a duopoly where Siemens Healthineers commands approximately 62% market share with USD 4,445m in sales, while Elekta maintains a much smaller 25% share representing USD 1,768m.

This massive scale disadvantage is compounded by Siemens' installed base of approximately 10,000 systems compared to only 5,000 for Elekta, limiting service operating leverage. Varian's anticipated nextgeneration radiotherapy platform launch at the late September ASTRO meeting could trigger customer purchasing delays just as Elekta's Evo gains commercial momentum. Additionally, domestic Chinese competitor United

Imaging's market share escalated from less than 10% five years ago to over 30% by 2025, directly threatening Elekta's historically strong regional position. forward, three critical datapoints will determine whether this margin-led turnaround translates into genuine shareholder value creation. First, Q2 FY2026/27 organic sales growth must inflect positive to validate management's full-year guidance of 2-4% constant currency net sales growth and 12.5-13.5% adjusted EBIT margin, demonstrating that Evo's US momentum and China's stabilization can offset emerging market weakness.

Second, the book-to-bill ratio of 1.11x must sustain above parity for multiple consecutive quarters without significant order cancellations to prove backlog quality has genuinely improved following management's cleanup that cancelled SEK 2.2bn in stale orders. Third, adjusted gross margin must hold above 40% excluding one-time benefits to confirm that pricing discipline, software mix improvements, and COGS engineering can structurally overcome persistent foreign exchange headwinds and component cost inflation.

The single most important change to the investment case is that Elekta has credibly demonstrated its capacity to rapidly expand profitability through operational discipline and pricing power, but this margin recovery remains decoupled from top-line momentum in a market where the competitive intensity is escalating. The strongest counter-hypothesis is that aggressive pricing discipline and floor margin enforcement will cause Elekta to systematically lose emerging market tenders to low-cost competitors while Siemens Healthineers' upcoming product launches trigger widespread purchasing delays in mature markets, resulting in sustained revenue underperformance that prevents the company from achieving its ambitious medium-term targets.

The evidence that would definitively invalidate this bearish view would be Q2 FY2026/27 delivering organic sales growth exceeding 3% while maintaining adjusted gross margins above 40% without one-time benefits, combined with sustained double-digit order growth in the Americas and positive order momentum across all five geographic segments.
