[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Straumann is an oversold high-quality compounder whose two causes for concern, an unexplained chief executive handover and a second Chinese price round, are dated and clearable, while the margin, the cash and the sell-side's estimates are already improving underneath them. At CHF 92.16 the shares pay us about 17 percent a year for that on our modal path, short of the 20 percent bar; below about CHF 89 they pay 20.

  • The change is internal, and it is already in the numbers.
    • A new finance director's procurement programme across about 16,000 vendors, the closure of the German aligner plant with production outsourced to Smartee (CHF 20 to 30 million of savings), a Shanghai campus producing at 20 percent lower cost of goods, and a premium product cycle (iEXCEL at nearly 40 percent of premium implant volumes in H1 2026) have taken the constant-currency core EBIT margin from 25.2 percent in FY2025 to 26.9 percent in H1 2026.
    • The FY2026 margin guidance was raised on 17 June 2026 from +30 to +60 basis points to +140 to +170 basis points at constant currency, and confirmed on 19 August 2026 with the first half delivered.
    • Free cash flow rose 49 percent to CHF 168.6 million in H1 2026 as capex fell to CHF 74.1 million from CHF 113.0 million, off a FY2025 peak of CHF 223.5 million.
      • That part of the case is arithmetic, not narrative.
  • The external forces do not help, and two of them hurt.
    • Demand is structurally under-served (16 million of 220 million eligible patients treated a year) but cyclically flat in the United States (practice volume -2 percent in Q2 2026) and down mid-single digits in mainland China
    • the Swiss franc took 550 basis points off H1 2026 reported growth
    • consolidating dental service organisations extract discounts of 15 to 25 percent
    • China's second implant price round is due in early 2027.
    • So this is not a demand case.
      • The growth engine has slowed from double-digit organic growth in 29 of 31 quarters to 7.8 percent in H1 2026, and our thesis needs it only to hold at about 9 percent on share gains, not to re-accelerate.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain except the layer the market reads first.
    • It shows in the company's choices and actions, in its key operating metrics (constant-currency margin, organic growth by region, cash) and in the sell-side's estimates, which have risen 6 to 7 percent over six months and carry a pinned earnings momentum of A.
    • It does not yet show in reported Swiss-franc earnings: H1 2026 core EPS was CHF 1.64 against CHF 1.66 a year earlier, because the franc took the growth.
      • That is why the shares can be cheap while estimates rise.
  • The price pays for the concerns, not for the improvement.
    • At CHF 92.16 the stock trades at 21.6x 24-month-forward consensus EPS of CHF 4.26, the 2nd percentile of its own ten-year range, below the ten-year 'crash' band of 23.8x and against a two-year median of 24.7x
    • the shares fell 14.4 percent in the three months to 29 September 2026 through a period in which guidance was raised and estimates rose.
    • Roughly half of that de-rating is the sector's: eleven of fifteen peers with data sit at the bottom 5 percent of their own ten-year multiple ranges, and the peer median is 14.8x.
      • The other half is the company's own news, the chief executive's departure on 19 August 2026 and the coming price round.
  • The earnings path clears the framework's band; the return does not clear the bar.
    • On the brokers' basis we expect EPS of CHF 2.99 (FY2025) to grow to about CHF 4.40 (FY2028), 13.8 percent a year, at consensus
    • about 16 percent in FY2027 and about 9 percent in FY2026.
    • Over eighteen months the return is earnings growth of about 16 percent on the 24-month-forward figure the market prices (CHF 4.26 to CHF 4.96, about 10.7 percent a year), a partial re-rating of about 8 percent (21.6x to a blended 23.3x) and a cash return of about 1 percent: about 26.6 percent in total, 17.0 percent a year.
      • Over three years it is 13.9 percent a year.
    • The re-rating is the swing factor and it rests on two conditions, a credible successor by his first results in February 2027 and a VBP 2.0 price cut inside the 5 to 15 percent the sell-side expects, with joint odds a little short of even.
      • The bar is cleared if and only if the stock regains at least its two-year median multiple within eighteen months.
  • The pattern is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', reasonably crisply.
    • Quality passes, the stock is oversold against its own history, the concerns are named and dated
    • the fit is less than crisp because the clearing evidence is a calendar rather than a fact and the drawdown of 16 percent from the 52-week high is not a capitulation.

  • The case therefore is a margin and cash self-help story on a de-rated number one, compelling on quality and on the stock's own valuation history, and not yet compelling on return at this price.

    • The entry level that makes it compelling is about CHF 89 on the eighteen-month horizon and about CHF 79 on both.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material and internal, the outputs are improving at every layer but reported earnings, and the 'Modal return' at CHF 92.16 is about 17 percent a year over eighteen months and about 14 percent over three years, at the top of the framework's C band and short of the 20 percent bar.

    • It is not a B because the return is the part that binds and it does not clear the bar at this price
    • every other part of a B is in place and the rating would be a B below about CHF 89.
    • It is not a D because the internal forces and the output improvement sit well above the universe's median.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the margin expansion is a False Friend.
    • It is real in the accounts and false as a signal, because it is made of deferrals and cost-outs (a Chinese price round postponed to 2027, Brazilian tariffs cut, a plant closed, production outsourced, a vendor base rationalised) while the franchise's premium price erodes underneath.
    • Evidence for it: two former insiders describe premium implants matched down to USD 100 to 200 for dental service organisations and a "persistent margin crunch"
      • a former ClearChoice clinical officer says the premium-to-value distinction is eroding into commodity perception
      • the value tier is outgrowing premium in the company's own mix and value average selling prices are compressing about 3 percent
      • the organisations go from about 20 percent of implants today to 30 percent by 2029
      • days sales outstanding have lengthened from 48 to 68 days since 2021
      • the orthodontics franchise has consumed an estimated CHF 600 to 700 million and may be sold
      • capitalised software of CHF 43.5 million and CHF 120 million of non-core adjustments flatter core earnings
      • Bernstein finds the stock the only medtech peer with a large negative residual (-328 basis points) in its free cash flow yield model, with long-term growth expectations down 362 basis points since FY2023
      • the franc takes 400 to 500 basis points a year off reported growth
      • and the chief executive who built the record has left without a stated reason.
    • Where it ends: the multiple does not recover.
      • The stock is a 15x European medtech consumable in waiting, and Jefferies' downside of CHF 75 to 78 is the destination, a 17 to 19 percent capital loss from CHF 92.16.
  • What we concede.
    • The mix shift to value is real and will dilute the gross margin percentage over time.
    • The discounting is documented by people who did it.
    • The FY2026 margin step is partly deferral and partly one-off relief.
    • Reported EPS in Swiss francs has grown about 3 percent a year since FY2022 and the franc structure that caused it is permanent.
    • The departure is unexplained and the successor is untested in dentistry.
    • Our own arithmetic shows the case falling to a D if the drop-through from EBIT to earnings does not improve.
  • Why we hold our view: the commoditisation thesis has had its hardest possible test and the numbers passed it.
    • Through VBP 1.0, a 55 percent state-mandated price cut in the company's fastest-growing market, the core gross margin stayed above 70 percent (71.4 percent in FY2024, 70.1 percent in FY2025, 70.5 percent in H1 2026 with one-offs) and world share rose from 29 to 35 percent.
    • Share gains are premium-led: North America grew 8.4 percent in Q2 2026 on a market at -2 percent, won with iEXCEL (about 20 percent of its users converted from competitors), not with discounts
      • 58 percent of surveyed US practitioners still name the brand best in class
      • Envista's rival S-Series needs multiple surgical kits where iEXCEL needs one.
    • Not all the cost-outs are deferrals: Smartee's CHF 20 to 30 million and the removal of a high-double-digit-million orthodontics loss are permanent, and the free cash flow inflection is arithmetic on a capex line that has already halved.
    • The series that would show the bear right are disclosed: core gross margin excluding one-offs, the premium-to-value mix and days sales outstanding are all in our indicator list
      • if they turn, we see it before the estimates do.
    • The price already carries much of the bear's conclusion: at 21.6x the stock is below its ten-year 'crash' band, so the False Friend must not merely be true but become visible to cost us the further 17 to 19 percent to Jefferies' downside, while the ordinary outcome returns about 17 percent a year and the cleared one about 26.
  • Where the case against would win: if the core gross margin excluding one-offs falls below 70 percent for two halves with the mix explained by price rather than by Neodent's growth, or if the new chief executive reopens the 2030 targets at his first results.
    • Either would invalidate the case, not merely reduce it.
