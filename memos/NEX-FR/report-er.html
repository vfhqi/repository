Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Nexans delivered a robust third-quarter 2026 performance on 22 October 2026, significantly exceeding consensus expectations with 9% organic growth and confirming its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance of 770 million to 840 million euros, while the subsequent November 2026 Greek interconnect subcontract award materially de-risked transmission capacity utilization through mid-2028 and improved the long-term earnings per share trajectory toward the ambitious 2028 target of 1,150 million euros EBITDA.
1.
Strategic transformation complete: Nexans successfully completed its multi-year portfolio rotation to become a pure-play sustainable electrification leader by divesting its final non-core Autoelectric automotive wiring business for 207 million euros in July 2026. This strategic simplification positions the group to capture structural energy transition opportunities across transmission, grid modernization, and last-mile connectivity segments. The divestment marks the absolute end of non-electrification activities and allows management to focus entirely on high-margin power infrastructure projects.
2.
Exceptional organic growth beat: The company recorded 9% organic sales growth in the third quarter of 2026, substantially beating the compiled broker consensus expectation of 4.6% by 440 basis points.

This acceleration was driven by outstanding performance in the PWR-Grid division, which delivered 14.8% organic growth against a consensus estimate of 6.1%, and the PWR-Connect segment, which achieved 10.5% organic growth versus expectations of 6.3%.

The beats demonstrate the effectiveness of Nexans' capacity expansion strategy and its ability to capitalize on accelerating utility distribution investments and hyperscale data center demand.

3.
Revenue significantly exceeded expectations: Nexans generated group standard revenues of 1,698.2 million euros during the third quarter of 2026, exceeding the compiled company consensus of 1,633 million euros by 4%.

The reported quarterly revenue grew by 21.4% year-on-year compared to the 1,398.7 million euros recorded in the third quarter of 2025.

Strong demand in grid and connect segments drove the substantial top-line acceleration, validating management's strategic focus on electrification markets.

4.
Grid division outstanding acceleration: The PWR-Grid segment was the primary operational highlight, posting 14.8% organic growth and generating standard sales of 363.9 million euros in the third quarter. The segment outperformed consensus expectations by 870 basis points, driven by progressive capacity ramp-up, seasonally stronger utility distribution activity in Europe, and accelerating network investments by major power utilities. The group is actively expanding its European manufacturing footprint to capture long-term grid modernization trends through 2028.
5.
Connect division robust performance: The PWR-Connect division delivered 10.5% organic sales growth and achieved standard sales of 749.8 million euros in the third quarter, beating consensus expectations by 420 basis points. Hyperscale data center demand in North America and solar projects in South America drove this significant low-voltage outperformance. Management intends to prioritize high-margin projects over bare volumes to improve connect division profitability going forward, despite persistent weakness in high-margin Nordic construction markets.
6.
Transmission normalization as expected: The PWR-Transmission segment experienced a 4% organic sales contraction during the third quarter, recording standard sales of 378.3 million euros. Management clarified that the negative growth was simply a mechanical normalization following the exceptional 33.3% growth in the third quarter of 2025. High-voltage subsea profitability continues to improve despite the lower volumes, and the division maintains good visibility until the end of 2028 with a stable adjusted backlog of 7.8 billion euros.
7.
Critical capacity de-risking achieved: The subcontract, representing an estimated size of approximately 250 million euros, will utilize the group's critical Mass-Impregnated production lines from late 2026 throughout 2027 and until mid- Interconnector restart.

The French-flagged research vessel Abeille Marengo began maritime survey operations under this diplomatic framework with monitoring from Greek and Turkish naval frigates.

Successful survey resumption significantly improves the long-term prospects of restarting GSI cable manufacturing by 2028, providing substantial backlog upside for Nexans beyond the secured Greek replacement contract.

8.
Guidance confirmed with upside potential: Nexans confirmed its full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance range of 770 million to 840 million euros and maintained its free cash flow target of 235 million to 325 million euros.

Chief Financial Officer Vincent Piquet stated that the group is working to exceed the 805 million euro midpoint of the guidance.

Backlog execution, capacity additions, and synergy capture are expected to push earnings toward the top end of the range, demonstrating management's confidence in the underlying business momentum.

Management is implementing a planned 30% manufacturing capacity extension at the acquired facility, and sourcing, product design, and procurement synergies are expected to materialize rapidly.

The transaction establishes a critical low-voltage platform to capture expanding North American data center opportunities and is on track to deliver 23 million euros in run-rate synergies within three years.

9.
Ambitious 2028 targets maintained: Nexans continues to target an adjusted EBITDA of 1,150 million euros by fiscal year 2028, representing a substantial increase from the 728 million euros recorded in 2025 and the 804 million euros generated in 2024.

The group aims to sustain a return on capital employed above 20% and maintain a free cash flow conversion rate above 45% by 2028.

The company plans 1.2 billion euros in capital expenditures over the 2025-2028 period to support sustainable electrification growth, with cumulative free cash flow before mergers and acquisitions targeted at approximately 1.4 billion euros.

The analyst emphasized that the current valuation of 8.7x 2027 enterprise value to EBITA is highly attractive.

Bernstein analyst Leslie maintained a Market-Perform rating with a target price of 150 euros, remaining cautious on near-term execution risks.

10.
Data center demand accelerating rapidly: Bernstein observed that the acceleration of European data center projects is manifesting much sooner than management originally anticipated.

The group is actively preparing bids for massive 1 gigawatt data center deployments, representing a substantial scale-up from fifty megawatt projects in early 2026.

Approximately two-thirds of recent United States industrial production growth is concentrated in data center and data center-adjacent sectors, driving massive low-voltage cable volumes and creating a highly lucrative opportunity for Nexans' advanced cable systems.

11.
Grid capacity constraints progressively easing: The Grid division's organic growth moderated to 4.2% in the second quarter of 2026 due to severe capacity limitations, but accelerated to 14.8% in the third quarter as new manufacturing lines commenced operations.

Nexans committed to expanding its European Grid manufacturing capacity by approximately 40% between 2025 and 2028.

The company also plans to increase its medium-voltage accessories production capacity by 39% between 2026 and 2028, requiring a capital investment of approximately 62 million euros across the United States and Europe.

Global electricity consumption is entering a multi-decade expansion cycle, with projections indicating that global power demand will grow at a 4.2% compound annual rate to reach 36.3 trillion kilowatt-hours by 2030. Approximately 40% of Europe's distribution networks are currently more than forty years old, creating a multi-decade source of demand for Nexans' medium-voltage and high-voltage transmission cables.

Average power cable prices increased by 5% to 8% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, supported by escalating raw material costs and tight manufacturing capacities. The European power cable market has transitioned from a price-driven procurement model to a capacity-and-execution model, where utility clients now prioritize delivery certainty and production slot reservations over obtaining the lowest price.
14.
Supply chain vertically integrated: Nexans is heavily insulated against commodity market volatility because of its highly vertically integrated copper supply chain, managing its own metallurgy business to supply approximately half of its raw metal requirements internally. The group maintains robust metallurgical operations with four dedicated rod mills boasting a total production capacity of 600,000 metric tons of copper wire rod per year. Management secured a comprehensive seven-year agreement with a top-tier copper miner to protect the business from logistics blockages and supply disruptions.

The vessel measures 150 meters long and 31 meters wide, matching the size of the existing Nexans Aurora fleet.

Leadership transitions completed smoothly: Nexans appointed Julien Hueber as Chief Executive Officer in October 2025, bringing over two decades of operational experience within the group.

The reshuffled executive committee focuses on scaling up the operating model through growth and competitiveness, with structured management layers helping Nexans maintain its trajectory in volatile markets.

European construction markets continue to experience a delayed inflection as central bank policies and government debt levels complicate the outlook for interest rates.

Persistent weakness in the high-margin Nordic construction market continues to drag on Connect segment margins despite robust data center demand in Southern Europe and Latin America.

High-voltage margin protective barriers are sustained by severe qualification requirements that limit the threat of cheap price competition from lower-cost Asian manufacturers.

However, the medium-voltage cable market is highly vulnerable to increasing competition from Chinese manufacturers over the long term, as technical qualification barriers are much lower compared to high-voltage applications.

Technology disruption creating opportunities: Rapid architectural shifts toward 800VDC power delivery architectures are disrupting traditional alternating current power distribution systems inside next-generation artificial intelligence data centers.

The solid-state transformer market could exceed 1 billion dollars by 2030 as it begins to displace traditional magnetics, with silicon carbide semiconductors allowing advanced systems to reduce space and weight by at least 30% compared to conventional transformers.

Innovative high-temperature superconducting cables are transitioning from academic research to commercial deployment in highly congested urban power networks, offering extremely high current-carrying capacities.

Regulatory tailwinds accelerating approvals: The European Union's Permitting Acceleration Directive seeks to establish binding two-year limits for grid approvals, while the United States Senate is reconciling permitting packages to set a strict two-year limit for federal Environmental Impact Statements.

European transmission system operators must expand grid capacities by 50% by 2040 under European Commission targets, creating a solid multi-decade demand pipeline for Nexans' advanced subsea products.

21.
Consensus EPS expectations elevated: Bloomberg consensus estimates compiled in October 2026 projected Nexans' basic EPS to reach 6.64 euros for 2026, growing further to 8.42 euros for fiscal year 2027 and 10.39 euros for 2028.

Consensus estimates project the dividend per share to increase to 3.19 euros for fiscal year 2026 and expand further to 3.35 euros per share for 2027.

Strong execution across the group's electrification portfolio is driving positive mid-term estimate revisions among covering brokers, supported by a robust highvoltage backlog and disciplined capital allocation.

22.
Long-term EPS outlook materially improved: The combination of the third-quarter organic growth beat, the secured Greek interconnect contract de-risking transmission capacity through mid-2028, the improving geopolitical visibility for the Great Sea Interconnector, and the successful Republic Wire integration has materially strengthened Nexans' long-term earnings trajectory.

The group's ambitious 2028 EBITDA target of 1,150 million euros, combined with disciplined capital allocation, strong cash conversion above 45%, and a conservative leverage target below 1.0x, positions Nexans to deliver sustained EPS expansion over the next 18 months to 3 years, with consensus expectations projecting EPS growth from 6.64 euros in 2026 to 10.39 euros by 2028, representing a compound annual growth rate of approximately 25%.

23.
Other side of the story: The positive momentum could be severely undermined if prolonged geopolitical disputes completely derail the Great Sea Interconnector or if operational execution on the complex 525 kilovolt subsea backlog suffers from bottlenecks and costly contract penalties.

Persistent stagnation in European residential construction markets could permanently drag on Connect segment profitability, while a sudden moderation in hyperscaler capital expenditure programs could lead to severe margin dilution through fixed-cost underabsorption at the newly expanded United States and Moroccan manufacturing facilities.

Additionally, if massive global capacity additions by competitors eventually outpace long-term transmission demand, intense competitive pressure would erode the industry's pricing power and dilute Nexans' high-voltage transmission profitability.

The consensus appears to harbor several non-obvious blindspots regarding Nexans' business model and competitive positioning. Major cable manufacturers have significantly reduced backlog risks by adopting highly selective contracting strategies since the pandemic, with contractors aligning bidding structures with actual risk profiles through cost-plus models and indexation clauses to pass through raw material inflation, ensuring that backlog quality remains highly protected against unexpected project cost overruns.

Sell-side models may be underestimating the sustainability of this pricing discipline, as most analysts continue to apply historical cyclical margin patterns rather than recognizing the structural shift toward a capacity-constrained supply model where utility customers prioritize delivery certainty over price. Additionally, consensus estimates appear to overlook the compounding effect of accessories capacity expansion, which represents a materially higher-margin business than conventional cable sales.

Deutsche Bank expects Nexans to accelerate its transition toward higher-value applications by expanding its medium-voltage accessories production capacity by 39% by 2028, noting that the accessories sub-segment is highly profitable and critical for utilities as approximately 90% of grid failures occur at cable connection points, and investing in local accessories manufacturing will accelerate the group's earnings expansion and improve the mid-term mix.

Furthermore, the market continues to treat Nexans' metallurgy operations as a low-value commodity business rather than recognizing it as a strategic competitive moat that provides superior raw material security and margin protection compared to peers like NKT who lack vertical integration. Finally, consensus models appear to underweight the long-term structural demand implications of the artificial intelligence-driven data center boom, treating it as a temporary capex cycle rather than a multi-decade architectural shift in power infrastructure requirements.

Two core questions should guide future disclosure reviews and investor scrutiny. First, what is the actual utilization rate and margin contribution of the Republic Wire manufacturing assets on a standalone basis, and what percentage of its current production is being successfully redirected toward premium data center specifications versus remaining in commoditized residential and commercial channels? This transparency would reveal whether the acquisition truly represents a strategic platform for high-margin growth or merely adds scale in lower-return verticals.

Second, what is management's detailed contingency plan for filling the Mass-Impregnated production capacity beyond mid-2028 if the Great Sea Interconnector fails to resume by late 2027, and what is the pipeline visibility for alternative European or Mediterranean subsea projects that could provide succession contracts? Understanding this succession risk is critical because the transmission division's ability to sustain high-teens margins depends entirely on maintaining continuous high-value backlog loading, and any extended idle capacity would quickly erode the group's consolidated profitability toward the lower end of its 2028 guidance range.
