Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

The report is organised around one question, which is stated here word-for-word: "What do Elekta's guidance and targets in force, its informal management commentary and the published forecasts of every covering broker imply for each of the company's next four reporting events, and what has changed in guidance, broker forecasts, target prices, ratings and news flow since the Q1 FY2026/27 report, and in particular since 1 November 2026 and since the Q2 report of 25 November 2026?" The question matters because the guidance framework is simple but the path to it is uneven.

Elekta reiterated its FY2026/27 constant-currency net sales growth guidance of 2 to 4 percent, and expects an adjusted EBIT margin between 12.5 and 13.5 percent. Management confirmed these targets as unchanged at the second-quarter presentation. Beyond that horizon, the FY2028/29 framework targets mid-single-digit constant-currency sales growth and an adjusted EBIT margin of 14 to 16 percent. The four reporting events are the checkpoints at which the market will test whether the one-year range and the three-year framework are consistent. The Q4 report is the most consequential, because it brings the first FY2027/28 guidance.

Seasonality sets how much each event can show. Q1 usually accounts for only about 10 percent of full-year EBIT, while Q2 has accounted for approximately 25 percent over the past five years and Q3 for another 25 percent. The

Q3 report therefore sets the run-rate for the year, but the Q4 conversion of the order book decides the outcome. Deutsche Bank argues that reaching the midpoints of the confirmed ranges still requires significant second-half acceleration. That depends on converting the SEK 34 billion order book into recognised revenue. The FY2027/28 events face demanding comparables.

A SEK 53 million US tariff refund lifted Q1 FY2026/27 gross margin by 150 basis points, and a SEK 33 million refund added 80 basis points in Q2. Q2 gross R&D capitalisation fell to SEK 183 million while amortisation rose to SEK 185 million, which creates a further drag on reported margins. Several things have changed since 1 November 2026. Elekta beat consensus by 23 percent on adjusted EBIT and 31 percent on adjusted EPS, against a pre-report full-year consensus of 2.70 percent organic growth and a 13.20 percent adjusted EBIT margin.

Guidance itself did not move. Estimates did: Jefferies projected that consensus EPS would rise by 3 to 5 percent, and DNB Carnegie lifted its full-year EPS estimate by 6 percent. DNB Carnegie's 13.90 percent FY2026/27 margin forecast sits above the guided range. Ratings were largely unchanged: Jefferies kept its hold rating and Deutsche Bank also maintained hold, while DNB Carnegie reiterated its buy rating.

A search of company releases, European procurement databases and Swedish financial news for 1 to 26 November 2026 found only positive developments, so the post-Q2 news flow offers little contrary evidence. The implications for EPS, valuation and risk follow from this. For EPS, upward revisions rest on the delivered cost programme, since Elekta exceeded its annualised SEK 500 million savings target in Q2. The offsets are fading tariff refunds and lower capitalisation.

For valuation, the debate is about the sustainability of growth. DNB Carnegie sees scope for a re-rating if organic growth of 3 to 5 percent becomes consistent. Jefferies links the size of the discount to European medtech peers to whether FY2026/27 outcomes land at the weaker or stronger end. For the risk profile, book-to-bill weakened sequentially to 1x, order intake has declined by an average of 1 percent over 18 months, and Q2 free cash flow was only SEK 48 million.

Competitive and regional pressures add to this: Siemens Healthineers launched Accela, Chinese provincial guidelines require at least 70 percent domestic purchasing of high-end equipment, and Elekta remains among the most heavily shorted shares on Nasdaq Stockholm. The limits of this analysis should be stated. One coverage check found research documents only from DNB Carnegie and Jefferies, with other brokers unavailable in that review. The evidence therefore skews toward those houses, and the broker-by-broker comparison in Section 3 is incomplete as a result. The evidence also ends in late November 2026, so the Q3 FY2026/27 report and the three later events remain forecasts, not outcomes. Later sections test each of these points in turn.

Section 2 – Financial Targets, Cost Programmes and Management Guidance

Section summary
  1. Elekta targets immediate margin stabilization and cost savings through organizational decentralization.
  2. Ambitious medium-term targets depend heavily on structural product cost reductions and commercial recovery.

Restructuring and Cost Reductions

1.
Significant cost-cutting: Elekta implemented a new operating model in late fiscal year 2025/26 designed to simplify operations, improve execution speed, and decentralize divisional accountability. The company expects this structural reorganization to deliver annualized run-rate cost savings of more than SEK 500 million. This initiative aims to correct years of irregular commercial execution and lower operating costs.
2.
Substantive personnel reductions: The organizational restructuring involved a reduction of about 10 percent of Elekta's global workforce, equivalent to more than 500 employees. This transformation successfully achieved annualized savings exceeding SEK 500 million starting in fiscal year 2026/27. Associated restructuring charges reached SEK 417 million in the third quarter of fiscal year 2025/26, reflecting the front-loaded nature of these actions.
3.
Decisive balance sheet cleanup: Elekta cleared its backlog by canceling SEK 2.2 billion of legacy orders to align its balance sheet with current business assumptions. Management initiated this aggressive measure to improve the overall quality of its future earnings. This adjustment ensures the order book reflects highly realistic commercial projects rather than obsolete contracts.
4.
Contrary evidence: A former regional sales director warns that Elekta's focus on short-term cost-cutting has resulted in high staff turnover and depleted commercial capabilities. This expert argues that recent margin gains are temporary and do not stem from sustainable product innovation. Such resource reductions could threaten long-term competitiveness against aggressive Chinese competitors and newer entrants.

Management Financial Guidance and Outlooks

5.
Stable near-term outlook: Elekta reiterated its fiscal year 2026/27 constant-currency net sales growth guidance of 2 to 4 percent. The company also expects its adjusted EBIT margin to land between 12.5 and 13.5 percent. Management confirmed these targets remain unchanged during the second-quarter earnings presentation, citing positive momentum in key regions.
6.
Gradual margin improvement: Elekta aims to drive its adjusted gross margin back toward its historical pre- pandemic level of above 40 percent. DNB Carnegie expects this turnaround to be supported by a new updated product portfolio and improved operating cost management. These operational efficiency improvements should accelerate earnings growth and support a valuation re-rating.
7.
Divergent regional performance: Elekta expects Americas and China to serve as the key drivers of shipment recovery in fiscal year 2026/27. Sales in the Americas rose 15 percent in the second quarter, while China sales grew 11 percent in constant currency. However, capital constraints continue to pressure the Asia- Pacific and Japan region, leading to a 17 percent sales decline.
8.
Favorable tariff refunds: Elekta's profit margins received a significant boost from US tariff refunds under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. A refund of SEK 53 million provided a 150 basis points margin uplift in the first quarter. Management reported that the final refund in the second quarter would be around three million dollars.

R&D Capitalization and Balance Sheet Quality

9.
Improving earnings quality: Elekta is shifting its financial policy to align capitalization with amortization to improve its quality of earnings. This strategic reset aims to reduce reliance on capitalized R&D on the balance sheet. Jefferies notes that this structural pivot will remove a historical accounting tailwind and better reflect actual cash flows.
10.
Rising amortization headwinds: Elekta's shift toward lower capitalization and higher amortization of R&D reduced its operating margins by 170 basis points year-over-year in the second quarter. Gross R&D expenditure is expected to stabilize at approximately 10 percent of sales. Deutsche Bank notes this margin target must be achieved through genuine operational improvements rather than accounting adjustments.
11.
Aggressive asset write-downs: Elekta recognized a non-cash impairment charge of SEK 1.4 billion in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025/26. This charge included SEK 851 million of capitalized R&D for projects that were removed from the strategic roadmap. These actions reflect the new CFO's efforts to align the balance sheet with current business assumptions.

Capital Allocation, Dividend and Leverage Policies

12.
Generous shareholder distributions: Elekta maintains a capital allocation policy that prioritizes a minimum 50 percent net income distribution to its shareholders. The Board of Directors proposed a dividend of SEK 2.40 per share for fiscal year 2025/26, matching the previous year's payout. Dividends remain the primary channel for returning capital, supported by a solid financial position.
13.
Well-managed leverage ratios: Elekta managed its capital structure with a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 1.24x at the end of fiscal year 2025/26. The leverage ratio remained stable at 1.30x at the end of the second quarter of fiscal year 2026/27. This conservative leverage supports the company's financial flexibility as it secures new credit facilities for R&D funding.
14.
Soft near-term cash generation: Elekta reported free cash flow before dividends of SEK 48 million in the second quarter, down from SEK 296 million in the prior year. This soft performance was driven by temporary working capital movements, particularly an increase in customer accounts receivable. Management expects cash conversion to normalize and improve significantly during the second half of the year.

Medium-Term Financial Targets

15.
Ambitious medium-term targets: Elekta set a mid-term framework for fiscal year 2028/29 targeting mid-single- digit constant-currency sales compound annual growth. The framework also aims for an adjusted EBIT margin of 14 to 16 percent by the target horizon. Additionally, the company is targeting robust free cash flow before dividends of approximately 10 percent of sales.

Section 5 – Historical Performance Against Guidance and Market Reactions

Section summary
  1. Elekta's historical performance demonstrates a persistent disconnect between strong order intake and actual revenue conversion.
  2. Severe guidance cuts and sudden leadership transitions have repeatedly triggered sharp negative share price reactions.

Early Guidance Trajectory and Supply Disruptions

1.
Inconsistent financial guidance: Elekta initiated a medium-term plan in May 2021 targeting a net sales compound annual growth rate of above 7 percent through fiscal year 2024/25. However, the company refrained from issuing annual guidance for fiscal year 2021/22 due to extreme pandemic-related uncertainties. This decision left investors without clear near-term visibility during a period of volatile global installation schedules.
2.
Severe supply headwinds: Elekta reported a preliminary operating profit of SEK 375 million for the third quarter of fiscal year 2021/22, which fell 20 percent year-over-year. Severe logistics bottlenecks and soaring component prices reduced the gross margin to 36.7 percent. This performance missed consensus expectations and triggered a sharp 10 percent share price decline on the announcement day.
3.
Persistent margin pressure: Jefferies Research observed that Elekta struggled to convert its growing order backlog into recognized revenue during fiscal year 2022/23. Rising supply chain lead times and inflation pressured hardware gross margins by 200 basis points. Consequently, the broker remained underperform on the stock as earnings fell 8 to 10 percent below consensus.
4.
Contrary evidence: Elekta reported a surprisingly strong gross margin beat during the first quarter of fiscal year 2023/24, exceeding consensus EBITA estimates by 79 percent. However, DNB Carnegie noted that this substantial expansion was heavily supported by a one-off inventory revaluation of SEK 130 million. This temporary accounting tailwind did not reflect a permanent turnaround in structural operating profitability.

Sequential Recovery and Missed Targets

5.
Temporary margin expansion: Elekta delivered a solid financial performance in the second quarter of fiscal year 2023/24 by leveraging its historical order backlog. The company recorded stable order growth of 4 percent, which supported an adjusted EBIT margin of 12.4 percent. This short-term stabilization encouraged a temporary stock rally as earnings predictability improved.
6.
Disappointing order decline: Elekta reported a mixed set of third-quarter results for fiscal year 2023/24 with a major 14 percent order intake miss. While net sales of SEK 4,537 million aligned with consensus, the adjusted EBIT of SEK 525 million missed by 3 percent. This order shortfall highlighted growing competitive pressures and delayed installations.
