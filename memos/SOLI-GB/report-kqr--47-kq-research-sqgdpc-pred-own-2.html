EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The upcoming Capital Markets Day in November 2026 represents a critical catalyst to define Solid State's long-term EPS trajectory.

This report examines Solid State plc's upcoming Capital Markets Day scheduled for 25 November 2026, assessing how the company will present its divisional strategies, operational progress, and 2030 financial ambitions under new chief executive John Macmichael. The analysis evaluates expected broker responses and draws lessons from comparable UK small-cap industrial peers that have held first Capital Markets Days under new leadership.

The core question centers on whether management can credibly demonstrate a pathway to doubling adjusted EPS to 20p and achieving £250m in revenue by 2030 while navigating near-term operational challenges at the Ashchurch facility and replacing a substantial one-off NATO contract that elevated FY26 revenues. The Systems division achieved a strong 14.3% adjusted operating margin in FY26, while the Components division lagged at just 4.0%, creating significant operational gearing potential if management can articulate clear margin bridges across all three divisions at the upcoming event.

The company maintains a disciplined balance sheet with gearing of 6.3% and net debt of just £4.2m, providing flexibility for strategic investments and bolt-on acquisitions. However, the newly opened Ashchurch integrated systems facility currently represents a near-term margin drag as capacity is being added ahead of the full revenue ramp, making facility utilization metrics critical to investor confidence.

The £154.1m FY26 revenue baseline includes a challenging comparator driven by a one-off £23.3m NATO communications contract that delivered fully in H1 FY26, raising questions about organic growth sustainability. One customer accounted for 17% of FY26 revenue, introducing meaningful concentration risk that management must address through program diversification and long-term partnership development. The 12% group operating margin target previously discussed by analysts was not repeated in the FY26 annual report, with management instead expecting operating margins to remain broadly stable in the near term due to ongoing facility investments.

Zeus Capital, the sole active broker providing detailed forecasts, assumes that the opportunity to expand operating margins is material, which directly supports the broker's upgraded FY27 EPS forecast of 11.6p. The broker views Solid State's valuation of 17.2x FY27 EPS as highly undemanding, falling to 15.7x on FY28 estimates and representing a significant discount to the wider sector. However, broker reactions from Cavendish and Berenberg remain unavailable as the Capital Markets Day has not yet occurred, creating uncertainty around consensus formation.

Comparable peer precedents provide mixed signals for Solid State's upcoming event. Volex plc presented highly ambitious medium-term targets at its inaugural Capital Markets Event on 22 April 2026 under CEO Nat Rothschild, committing to $2 billion of revenue and an expanded 12% underlying operating margin, which triggered immediate positive earnings revisions. Volex experienced immediate consensus forecast upgrades following its April 2026 strategic presentation, with Jefferies raising its EBITA forecasts for FY27 and FY28 by 4% and 6% respectively, demonstrating how well-executed Capital Markets Days can catalyze sustained re-ratings.

Conversely, TT Electronics chief executive Peter France stepped down with immediate effect on 11 April 2025 after North American operational issues offset his previous Project Dynamo turnaround goals, highlighting that bold mid-term margin targets can fail if near-term execution is severely disrupted. TT Electronics suffered material consensus downgrades as operational issues overshadowed its strategic targets, with Jefferies reducing FY24 to FY26 EPS forecasts by up to 39% following persistent manufacturing inefficiencies, underscoring the execution risks inherent in leadership transitions. discoverIE Group outlined its updated mid-term targets at its Capital Markets Day on 11 September 2024, establishing an operating margin target of 15% by FY28 alongside a goal to double EPS every five years, providing a structural blueprint for margin expansion through operational platforms.

Volex shares delivered strong positive performance over the six months following its Capital Markets Event, trading at a multiple of 14.3x forward earnings and successfully transferring its listing to the Main Market on 24 July 2026, demonstrating that credible strategic plans can facilitate market re-ratings and listing upgrades. - Analyst/Investor The report's confidence in assessing post-event broker reactions and share price movements remains medium to low because the November 2026 Capital Markets Day has not yet occurred, limiting the availability of post-event research notes and consensus adjustments.

Critical evidence gaps include precise Ashchurch facility utilization trajectories, the timing and scale of Project CAIN contract expansions, detailed divisional margin bridges, and management's updated capital allocation framework under new leadership. A divergent outcome in defense procurement timing would materially depress Solid State's earnings relative to current broker expectations, particularly given that the Systems division previously experienced order deferrals when the UK Strategic Defence Review was announced in late 2024, which slashed near-term profit expectations.

The structural defense tailwinds driven by elevated geopolitical tensions and NATO modernization programs provide a favorable backdrop for Solid State's long-term ambitions, yet near-term execution at Ashchurch, successful program diversification away from concentrated customers, and the ability to articulate credible margin expansion pathways will ultimately determine whether the upcoming Capital Markets Day catalyzes a sustained re-rating or exposes operational vulnerabilities that defer the 2030 financial targets.
