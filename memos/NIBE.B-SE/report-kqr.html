Section 2 – Channel Inventory and Market Destocking Cycles

Section summary
  1. European heat pump channel inventories have transitioned from the severe overstocking of the post- 2023 crisis to structurally normalized levels in 2025 and early 2026.
  2. Balance sheets show finished goods inventories plateauing as OEMs control production and focus on seasonal working capital builds rather than speculative distributor restocking.

Balance Sheet Evidence and OEM Inventory Paths

1.
Optimized working capital: Ariston Group successfully reduced its consolidated inventories from a peak of €694.9 million in June 2023 to €470.4 million by December 2024 through tight supply chain controls. This represented a significant drop to 12.7% of net sales, although subsequent seasonal restocking in H1 2026 lifted inventories back to €582.8 million or 20.9% of last-twelve-months revenue, confirming that Ariston has reduced its consolidated inventories to baseline levels.
2.
Frugal inventory management: Nibe Industrier reduced its inventory from SEK 13,227 million in 2023 to SEK 9,167 million by the end of 2025, which helped improve cash flow. Despite a seasonal build-up to SEK 9,825 million in Q2 2026, working capital relative to sales declined to 23.6% compared to 24.3% in the prior year period, confirming that excess manufacturing stock is largely cleared.
3.
Stubborn inventory build: Daikin Industries has struggled to reduce its balance sheet inventory, which rose to ¥1,137,903 million as of March 31, 2026, compared to ¥1,052,868 million in 2025. According to Jefferies, this persistent oversupply has prevented a rapid recovery in profitability, leaving the company as it struggled to reduce its balance sheet inventory while its newly established Polish factory running at low utilization.
4.
Stabilizing European demand: Watts Water Technologies reported that its European net sales declines of 11.8% organically in 2024 and 4.6% in 2025 have finally bottomed out. Deutsche Bank highlights that European HVAC components have stabilized in 2025 with improved outlooks heading into late 2026 as OEM destocking concludes.
5.
32 10K Mixed margin conversion: Carrier reported strong 20% growth in European heat pump sales in Q2 2026, yet its broader climate segment experienced a 70 basis point margin decline. Morgan Stanley notes that this margin compression, driven by pricing promotions and adverse product mix, indicates that volume recovery does not guarantee immediate profitability.
6.
Temporary pre-buying relief: While some HVAC players have noted sequential order improvements, Morgan Stanley argues that some of this momentum represents pre-buying ahead of scheduled price hikes. This channel build creates downside risks of shipment payback in late 2026, meaning underlying residential demand remains too soft to support sustained restocks.

7.
Converging order indices: Rothschild & Co Redburn tracking shows that distributor "sell-out" orders outperformed manufacturer "sell-in" orders from March 2023 to July 2025, but have since converged on an index basis. More recently, sell-in orders have slightly outperformed sell-out orders, suggesting that distributors are no longer actively destocking and are instead purchasing to match ongoing retail sales.

Chief Executive Officer Maurizio Brusadelli noted that seasonal restocking is underway, bringing sell-in and sell-through into equilibrium for the first time since the 2023 market disruption.

Nibe's management remains comfortable with stable pricing, confirming that installations are correlating better with sell-ins as distributor inventories reach normalized levels.
10.
Depressed factory absorption: DNB Carnegie notes that European heat pump manufacturing capacity utilization remains depressed at an estimated 30% to 40%. This low operating rate will limit near-term profitability, meaning that operating leverage benefits will be delayed until volumes recover.
11.
Weak residential construction: Jefferies argues that the end of destocking will only lead to flat underlying demand because the European residential market remains fundamentally weak. Nearly half of surveyed contractors cite a preference for repair over replacement, meaning that recovery in new-build demand remains muted despite regulatory push factors.

Section 3 – Subsidy Architectures and Regulatory Environments

Section summary
  1. European heat pump adoption is increasingly polarized as governments shift from broad-based fiscal subsidies to highly targeted, income-restricted support schemes.
  2. Policy rollbacks of mandatory heating transition laws have removed immediate regulatory demand catalysts, forcing manufacturers to rely on gradual long-term building standards.

Germany's Technology-Neutral Shift and BEG Reforms

1.
Eased building laws: Germany passed the Gebäudemodernisierungsgesetz on July 28, 2026, which abolished the strict sixty-five percent renewable heating mandate for new installations in favor of a technology-neutral framework.
2.
Targeted subsidy reductions: Under the KfW/BEG subsidy reform effective July 21, 2026, the German government reduced the eligible cost cap from €30,000 to €28,000 with subsequent €750 step-downs scheduled every six months starting February 1, 2027.
3.
Cancelled efficiency bonuses: The BEG reform eliminated the five percent efficiency bonus for natural refrigerants since natural refrigerants are now legally mandatory, while gradually reducing the climate speed bonus by four percentage points every six months until its complete removal by August 2028.
4.
Regressive wealth impact: German subsidies now favor lower-income households with a maximum 80% grant, but support for childless higher-income households will fall from eighteen percent of total costs to just €12,900, preserving the status quo for the wealthy.
5.
Eroded investment payback: At the current midpoint subsidy level in Germany, a residential heat pump upgrade carries a twenty-six year payback period relative to gas boilers, and planned subsidy cuts will extend this metric to 37 years by 2028.
6.
Pre-emptive demand spikes: German monthly subsidy approvals grew more than one hundred percent year-on-year in August 2026 as middle-class consumers scrambled to pre-empt the scheduled subsidy cuts implemented after the parliamentary summer recess.

United Kingdom and Nordic Policy Landscapes

7.
Increased off-grid grants: The UK Boiler Upgrade Scheme expanded on July 21, 2026, allowing eligible off- gas-grid homes to receive up to nine thousand pounds when replacing old oil or liquid petroleum gas heating systems.
8.
New air-to-air funding: The amended Boiler Upgrade Scheme regulations in 2026 added exhaust air systems and approved a new grant of £2,500 for domestic air-to-air heat pump installations.
9.
VAT fiscal relief: To improve heat pump running cost economics against gas boilers, the UK government reduced the domestic electricity VAT from five percent to zero percent effective October 1, 2026.
10.
Eased new-build mandates: While the UK has stepped back from its proposed 2035 ban on residential gas boiler sales, the Future Homes Standard will mandate heat pump installations in all newly built homes starting in 2028.
11.
Swedish tax credits: Sweden offers stable support via a thirty percent tax deduction on the direct labor costs of heat pump installations up to €5,000 per year, though this relief excludes new-build homes.
12.
Finnish structural support: Finland supports heat pump adoption by offering grants up to €4,000 alongside up to sixty percent coverage in tax deductions for households replacing obsolete oil-fired burners.

Italy and Netherlands Subsidy Adjustments

13.
Drastic Superbonus contraction: Italy phased down its generous Superbonus scheme from 110% to ninety percent in 2023 before cutting the deduction rate further to 70% in 2024 and 65% in 2025.
14.
Abolished credit options: The Italian government acted decisively in 2024 to ban the transfer of tax credits and invoice discount options, which forces homeowners to pay upfront costs and claim the subsidy over ten years.
15.
Dutch budget reductions: The Netherlands reduced its Sustainable Energy Investment Subsidy in 2025, cutting air-to-water subsidy coverage from €2,100 down to €1,250 for residential installations up to 70kW.
16.
Mandated hybrid transition: The Dutch Climate Agreement continues to mandate the enforced installation of hybrid units during any standard heating system replacement starting in 2026, supporting a target of 2 million heat pumps by 2030.
17.
EPBD new-build mandates: Under the newly transposed Energy Performance of Buildings Directive, the Netherlands will mandate that public structures become zero-emission from 2028, extending this to all new-builds from 2030 onwards.
