EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Chief Executive Quality and Leadership Profile Bernard Gustin represents an unconventional CEO appointment, transitioning from a 7.5-year Board Chairmanship directly into the operational helm in January 2025 following former CEO Chris Peeters' late-2023 departure. His career reflects structured corporate finance training at Procter & Gamble, strategic advisory leadership at Arthur D. Little, and a ten-year aviation turnaround mandate at Brussels Airlines, establishing credible restructuring expertise but limited power-grid engineering depth. Since assuming executive control, Gustin has demonstrated high-velocity decision-making and aggressive capital deployment, accelerating group capital expenditure to €1,926 million in 1H26 (+29.4% YoY) while executing a pragmatic pause on the Princess Elisabeth Island HVDC converter unit that eliminated €3.0 billion in capital inflation exposure. His leadership style combines plain-spoken stakeholder communication with conservative guidance discipline, reiterating FY26 net profit targets of €690-740 million despite absorbing €57 million in annual hybrid bond coupon costs.

However, his generalist background in labor-intensive transportation sectors rather than rate-regulated utility capital deployment creates potential technical execution risks during hyper-accelerated offshore grid expansion. Financial Delivery Track Record and Capital Execution Elia Group's operational performance under Gustin's executive tenure reflects strong financial momentum and disciplined capital allocation, with consolidated net profit attributable to shareholders reaching €348.7 million in 1H26 (+29.4% YoY) and underlying net profit expanding to €556.6 million in FY25 (+32.1% growth over FY24).

The CEO successfully orchestrated a landmark €2.2 billion equity package in April 2025, comprising an €850 million private placement and €1.35 billion rights issue that secured cornerstone institutional capital from ATLAS Infrastructure, Future Fund, BlackRock, and CPP Investments. Management reaffirmed the ambitious €31.6 billion 2024-2028 investment program, targeting over 20% annual Regulated Asset Base expansion with group RAB reaching €22.6 billion in 2025 (+22.5% YoY).

Capital deployment execution remains robust, with record-high FY25 capex of €5.2 billion supporting critical Belgian projects Ventilus and Boucle du Hainaut alongside German North Sea offshore grid connections. The executive team complemented equity financing with a €900 million perpetual hybrid bond issuance in April 2026, preserving BBB/BBB+ credit ratings while maintaining net financial debt at €14.60 billion with a disciplined 3.04% weighted average cost of debt.

Sell-side consensus projects sustained double-digit EPS growth, with Morgan Stanley estimating a 14% EPS CAGR from 2025 to 2030 and Jefferies framing Elia as Europe's fastest-growing TSO trading at a compelling 1.0x RAB premium-to-growth ratio versus the 2.0x peer median. Governance Structure, Board Independence, and Compensation Alignment Weaknesses Elia Group operates under a monistic non-executive board structure comprising 12 directors, yet true independent oversight remains structurally constrained by 45.21% reference shareholder control through NextGrid Holding and municipal holding Publi-T, with only four directors satisfying strict independence criteria under Belgian corporate governance standards.

The Board explicitly derogates from 2020 Belgian Corporate Governance Code provisions by maintaining six-year director mandates rather than four-year terms, granting zero equity remuneration to non-executive directors, and waiving minimum shareholding guidelines for both directors and executive officers. Executive remuneration exhibits a low-risk, cash-settled structure with total management board compensation reaching €6.21 million in 2025 (+26.5% YoY), yet the complete absence of share-based incentives creates a weak skin-in-the-game profile that disconnects leadership rewards from stock price performance.

CEO Bernard Gustin earned €1.38 million total compensation in 2025, comprising 39.8% fixed salary and 37.3% variable cash bonuses including €185,000 in extraordinary capital raise bonuses, while maintaining zero personal equity ownership. Management operates a multi-year cash-settled Long-Term Incentive plan rather than equity grants, with shareholders approving the 2024 Remuneration Report at only 74.18% majority versus the prior year's 96.94% approval level.

The governance framework imposes no personal shareholding thresholds for executive officers, despite total executive board ownership standing at a negligible 7,348 shares (<0.01% of outstanding equity), materially lagging peer TSOs Terna and Redeia that maintain performance share plans tied to long-term shareholder returns. Insider Transactions, Expert Network Divergence, and Execution Risk Signals The insider landscape reveals a complete absence of open-market share purchases by directors or executive officers during FY25 and 1H26, with zero stock options or restricted stock unit grants awarded to management, driven by formal corporate governance derogations that prioritize reference shareholder stability over executive equity alignment. Expert network insights expose operational divergence between official management messaging and ground-level realities, particularly highlighting that 50Hertz lags Belgian sister unit ETB in operational deployment of Dynamic Line Rating sensors and faces acute transformer lead-time pressure after German government mandates compressed 400 MW grid connection timelines from six years to two years.

Industry specialists emphasize that European high-voltage equipment manufacturers hold multi-billion backlogs spanning 3-4 years, creating a sellers' market where suppliers dictate contract terms and selectively choose tenders, with 50Hertz forced to secure €4.6 billion in long-term cable contracts with Prysmian and NKT to reserve critical subsea capacity. Third-party commentary from suppliers and regulators validates Elia's scale and execution authority, yet Belgian regulator CREG's approval of the 2028-2031 tariff methodology offering an average ~8.0% post-tax ROE contrasts sharply with extended German regulatory uncertainty, where final BNetzA determination of post-2028 cost-of-equity parameters remains delayed until late 2027, creating multi-year regulatory return overhangs.

Management's impairment of €99.1 million against the US energyRe Giga portfolio and strategic restructuring of Princess Elisabeth Island demonstrate pragmatic de-risking, yet severe STEM talent scarcity across Europe and rapid workforce growth of over 700 annual hires risk diluting core operational capabilities during hyper-accelerated grid expansion. Largest Thesis Risks and Plausibility Assessment The primary investment thesis faces four critical execution and structural risks that could materially impair shareholder value creation.

First, post-2028 German regulatory return uncertainty represents the single largest thesis risk, where failure to secure cost-of-equity determinations above market capital costs would force management to either dial back the €31.6 billion growth program or execute further dilutive capital raises beyond the €2.2 billion 2025 package. Second, severe European equipment supply chain bottlenecks and transformer lead-time pressures create structural execution friction, with expert testimony confirming that emergency government mandates have stretched procurement channels and granted dominant negotiating leverage to Siemens Energy, Prysmian, and other high-voltage suppliers.

Third, the complete absence of executive equity ownership and cash-settled LTI structures disconnect management compensation from stock price performance, enabling leadership to collect substantial cash bonuses for capital deployment regardless of whether shareholder TSR underperforms regulated utility peers. Fourth, pervasive municipal shareholder representation through Publi-T's 45.21% voting block and board interlocks with distribution system operators risk prioritizing local political dividend stability over aggressive long-term capital discipline, with governance structures unable to protect equity holders if BNetzA regulatory decisions prove unfavorable.

CEO Gustin's generalist restructuring background in non-utility transportation represents a fragile leadership marker when supervising complex offshore HVDC deployment, while recent senior executive turnover including former interim CEO Catherine Vandenborre's June 2025 departure and Chief Digital Officer Michael von Roeder's December 2025 exit concentrate institutional memory within a narrow top leadership layer. The final thesis execution plausibility is assessed at medium confidence, contingent on regulatory return frameworks in Belgium and Germany remaining supportive; if BNetzA sets post-2028 German WACC returns below actual capital costs, hyper-accelerated capex will destroy equity value while a generalist management team operating with zero personal skin-in-the-game collects guaranteed cash bonuses.
