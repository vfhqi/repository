APM Mini-Memo: Symrise

The one-line view. Symrise is a steady flavour and fragrance house priced near the bottom of its own ten-year valuation range, and the story depends on returning volume reaching profit before dearer raw materials absorb it.

What the company does

Symrise is one of the large flavour and fragrance houses, worth 12.3 billion euros at 97.28 euros a share on 13 November 2026. The company designs the tastes and scents that makers of food, drink, household and personal care products build into their goods, and it sells the ingredients that make pet food appetising. That description is my own reading, because our files hold no research on the business.

Customers' volumes set most of Symrise's growth. Our Sector Digest of 25 October found that the houses pass on only about half of a rise in raw material costs, so their gains come from volume rather than price.

A plausible investment thesis

Real demand is returning, and Symrise is the purest way to own that return. Our sector note of 25 October judged that food and drink volumes grew in 2026 on real demand, not only on customers restocking, and named Symrise first among the beneficiaries. Jefferies adds a second source of volume: pet food ingredients are turning, with Nestlé's pet care growth of 4.3% as the supporting evidence (23 October).

Volume reaches earnings per share with leverage. Jefferies says each extra unit of volume earns about twice a company's average margin, because the cost base is largely fixed. Consensus expects earnings to grow 8.4% a year, and the weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 12 November is C, so brokers' forecasts are not yet moving up with the story. Sustained volume growth would therefore arrive as upgrades.

Three drivers carry the thesis: organic volume growth in each division, the gap between raw material costs and contract prices, and pet food ingredient volumes. Symrise's third-quarter sales of 28 October were the first test, and the result is not in our files. The full-year results in early 2027 are the next test.

The Target Setup

The Target Setup is 'CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal', and that read is my own, with no Investment Analyst memo behind it. The cause for concern was weak volume that restocking flattered, and the concern is clearing as real demand returns. Symrise's multiple sits in the lowest quarter of its own history, so a return to normal growth could bring a return to a normal multiple. The high-quality label is my assumption until a memo tests it.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

The case is simple to state, and nobody has rated it yet. No Investment Analyst memo exists, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings. One thing needs to go right: volume growth must turn into profit growth through 2027.

The main risk is that costs and a fragrance hangover absorb the volume gain. Brent crude rose above 106 dollars a barrel in October 2026, and contract prices catch up with raw material costs only after six to twelve months. The same lag in 2022 cut Givaudan's gross margin by 3.9 percentage points and its share price by 39%. Household and personal care customers also bought fragrance early, and that early buying unwinds in the first half of 2027.

Chinese price cuts are the third pressure. Chinese producers are cutting the prices of commodity aroma molecules, and prices in Symrise's Scent & Care division were 3.1% lower than a year earlier in the second quarter. Fragrance ingredients are 27% of that division's sales, against 9% at Givaudan. The three things to track are Symrise's gross margin, Scent & Care pricing, and fragrance volumes in the first half of 2027.

Business quality

Probably good, and unproven in our files. Testing a new recipe takes 12 to 18 months, so the supplier that wins a recipe tends to keep it, and the market values these houses as steady growers. My own reading is that a flavour or scent is a small part of a customer's cost and defines the product, which makes customers slow to switch. Commodity aroma molecules are the weaker part of the franchise.

A memo should establish the numbers. Symrise's margins, returns on capital and balance sheet are not in our files.

Plausible returns and valuation

The valuation is the strongest part of the case. Symrise trades on 19.1 times earnings, the 4th percentile of its own ten-year range, while Givaudan stood on 29.1 times, at percentile 73, on 23 October. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 13 November is A, and that rating reads valuation only.

The plausible return clears Richard's bar only if the multiple recovers. The weekly routine's figures of 12 November are 45.5% a year over eighteen months and 26.1% a year over three years, both above the 25% that marks A. Those figures assume earnings growth of 8.4% a year, dividends, and a multiple moving from 19.1 to 29.2 times. Earnings growth of 8.4% is below the 12% that marks D, so the rerating carries the return.

The share price supports the timing. The weekly Technical Momentum rating of 13 November is A, with the share 5% below its 52-week high after a pullback inside an uptrend. The flavour and fragrance houses rose 37% in the six months to 23 October, yet their median multiple was still at percentile 5. Our sector judgement is that a story pays most where the multiple has not yet moved.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

The pros: Symrise sits behind the strongest theme in its sector. Our Summary of Key Themes and Ratings of 1 November put the sector's net force at B, medium, with real demand returning as the strongest theme. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 13 November is B, and that rating reflects the sector's environment only. Fragrances and ingredients rose a median 26.8% over twelve months, one of the few rising groups in Materials.

The cons sit on both sides of the chain. Oil-linked raw materials press from behind, and food companies press their suppliers on price from in front. American recipe changes favour suppliers that sell a whole package of ingredients, where Kerry is the purest play, and Symrise's position in that work is not in our files. A cartel investigation into the fragrance industry is open.

In short

  • Interesting because cheap against itself: 19.1 times earnings is the 4th percentile of Symrise's ten-year range, and the multiple has not moved with the story
  • The story is volume: real demand in food and drink, plus pet food ingredients turning, on a cost base where extra volume earns about twice the average margin
  • The return needs a rerating: 8.4% earnings growth alone falls short of the bar, and a move towards 29.2 times lifts the plausible return above 25% a year
  • Track three numbers: gross margin against oil, Scent & Care pricing, and fragrance volumes in the first half of 2027
  • A memo is the next step: nothing on the business is on file, starting with the third-quarter sales of 28 October
