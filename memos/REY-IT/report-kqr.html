Swift valuation expansion: The European IT services sector experienced a rapid valuation recovery following the severe 2008 to 2009 financial downturn. Morgan Stanley reported in a research document dated 18 January 2010 that P/E multiples began to expand before revenues returned to positive growth. This multiple re-rating was led by discretionary consulting and systems integration, which historically experience the highest volatility during cyclical downturns but rebound first. Stronger sentiment subsequently supported double-digit EPS expansion as enterprise IT budgets normalized.
2.
Delayed sector recovery: The European IT services market faced a protracted multiple compression period during the 2011 to 2012 sovereign debt crisis. Anand Rathi reported in a sector update on 7 January 2013 that the Indian IT index underperformed the broader market and remained seventeen percent below its prior peak. This prolonged de-rating left the sector trading at a supportive forward P/E of 17x compared to its three-year high of 29x. Consequently, depressed valuations eventually underpinned a steady EPS recovery as macro risks receded.

Ambit Capital highlighted in a research note on 21 March 2013 that weak new license sales at Oracle validated persistent demand weakness. This soft spending environment meant that system integrators like Infosys struggled to expand multiples until client decision cycles accelerated. Delayed deal conversions temporarily restricted near-term EPS upgrades across discretionary IT consulting firms.

Moderate valuation discounts: European IT services multiples traded below their historical peak levels during the sector de-rating of 2015 to 2016. Deutsche Bank observed in a sector report dated 12 January 2017 that services multiples carried a lower premium relative to the Stoxx 600 index than historical averages. This discount reflected investor concerns over macroeconomic disruption and rising wage inflation, which made services earnings appear more volatile than software. However, stabilizing margins eventually triggered a significant multiple re-rating as organic growth stabilized.
