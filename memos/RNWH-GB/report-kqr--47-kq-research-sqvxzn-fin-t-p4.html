EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The specific financial inputs of Renew Holdings plc directly determine its earnings per share trajectory over the medium term, and understanding these micro-drivers is vital to assessing the group's compounding potential and capital allocation shifts under new leadership. The Key Question regarding what the evidence says about the specific inputs that will drive Renew's earnings per share over the next 18 months to four years can be substantially answered with high confidence across most dimensions, supported by comprehensive regulatory disclosures, detailed annual reports, and thorough broker research covering tax rates, finance costs, acquisition mechanics, working capital dynamics, and share count evolution. Renew's adjusted effective tax rate in FY25 was notably low at 21.64%, which Deutsche Bank identified as driven by "lower cash tax" that contributed to the group's adjusted EPS outperformance of 67.1p. However, the tax rate normalized significantly in H1 FY26 to 25.60%, and the evidence strongly indicates that Renew's effective tax rate will align closer to the UK statutory corporation tax rate of 25% for FY27 through FY29. This normalization path contrasts with peers such as Keller Group, which maintains a lower underlying effective tax rate of 23% due to jurisdictional profit mix differences, and Kier Group, which benefits from historic tax loss utilization.
  2. The specific financial inputs of Renew Holdings plc directly determine its earnings per share trajectory over the medium term.
  3. Understanding these micro-drivers is vital to assessing the group's compounding potential and capital allocation shifts under new leadership.

Renew's net finance cost structure is transparently split between its refinanced £140m revolving credit facility extended to October 2029 with an improved margin of 150bps (reduced from 180bps previously) and IFRS 16 lease interest that increased to £1.262m in FY25 from £0.800m in FY24. The group's net finance costs are projected to remain low from FY27 to FY29 due to net cash accumulation, with Deutsche Bank forecasting net finance costs falling to approximately £1m by FY27 and FY28, which contrasts sharply with peer Kier Group's high interest outlays from its 9% coupon £250m bond and Morgan Sindall's substantial net finance income generated from average daily net cash of £423m.

Renew's serial bolt-on acquisition strategy is structured with performance-linked deferred remuneration, and the group defines organic growth as year-on-year revenue growth excluding current or comparative period acquisitions, delivering 40% organic revenue growth compared to 80% total revenue growth over the five years ending 30 September 2025. Recent acquisitions include Emerald Power (£11.3m gross consideration, £1.9m EBITDA at an attractive 4.1x EV/EBITDA multiple), Edwards Diving Services (up to £13m with £10m initial cash), EDES (up to £9.0m based on £650k sustainable EBITDA), and PWR-X (up to £1.1m), with deferred consideration typically structured as future remuneration linked to ongoing employment.

Amortisation of acquired intangibles was £9.2m in FY25 and is forecast by Deutsche Bank to persistently drag statutory EBIT at £9.8m in FY27 and £10.3m in FY28, though these costs are adjusted out of reported EPS.

Renew's joint-venture accounting causes historical database discrepancies because standard screens exclude non-statutory revenue shares, with the group's share of joint-venture revenue totaling £35.15m in FY25 and £48.02m in FY24, while joint-venture profit after tax increased to £62,000 in FY25 from £25,000 in FY24. Data providers like FactSet historically exclude Renew's JV revenue share, creating discrepancies, though Deutsche Bank and other brokers reconcile these differences by modeling the group's alternative performance measures, with Deutsche Bank modeling FY25 revenue at £1,116m to match management's reporting and projecting growth to £1,187m in FY26.

Renew maintains strong cash conversion supported by low capital intensity across regulated infrastructure markets, with confidence in the working capital pathway rated as high and supported by historically consistent cash conversion averaging 63.40% over five years. The group's contract assets grew to £135.18m in FY25 from £129.78m in FY24, representing unbilled work in progress, while contract liabilities increased to £24.27m in FY25 from £20.15m in FY24, offsetting working capital outflows.

This profile differs significantly from peers, with Balfour Beatty generating massive inflows from advances and mobilization payments that drove a £259m working capital inflow in H1 2026, representing negative working capital of 17.30% of revenue that funds continuous share buybacks.

Renew's share count remains highly stable with minimal dilution from outstanding LTIP awards, with the ordinary share count standing at 79,156,691 shares as of December 2025 and only 232,130 options granted under the LTIP on 17 December 2025, with the dilutive effect increasing the share count by only 13,000 shares in FY25. The incoming chair Pete Raby, appointed as Chair designate on 5 October 2026 and succeeding David Brown at the January 2027 AGM, is expected to maintain the strategic focus on M&A over share buybacks, with the board stating that returning additional excess cash to shareholders would only be considered after organic and M&A funding requirements are achieved.

This contrasts with peer groups like Keller and Kier, which have implemented substantial share buybacks, with Keller completing a £50m buyback and launching a subsequent £100m program in March 2026, and Kier completing an initial £20m buyback followed by a £25m program in March 2026.

Critical gaps in the evidence remain regarding separate margins of individual subsidiaries such as Carnell or QTS, specific contractual terms and cash distribution policies of joint ventures like Cappagh Browne, the exact historical organic contribution of earlier acquisitions once they enter the organic base, the identities and exact sizes of future M&A targets, and Pete Raby's long-term stance on share buybacks versus acquisitions, which has not been formally tested yet. However, the overall visibility of Renew's earnings remains extremely high because regulated infrastructure frameworks govern over 80% of the group's mid-term order book, rendering these micro-level gaps minor.

A rapid normalization of Renew's tax rate combined with prolonged working capital drags would materially depress earnings per share growth below current consensus expectations. Conversely, if the incoming chair Pete Raby shifts capital allocation policy toward structured buybacks similar to peer precedents, the per-share earnings trajectory could accelerate beyond current broker models, particularly if net cash accumulation continues to exceed acquisition deployment rates.

The forward pathway for Renew's EPS through FY29 therefore depends critically on the resolution of tax rate stability, the sustained efficiency of working capital conversion, the continued accretive deployment of capital into bolt-on acquisitions, and the strategic choices of new leadership regarding capital returns versus reinvestment in consolidating fragmented infrastructure services markets.
