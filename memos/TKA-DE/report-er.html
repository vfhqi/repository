EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Thyssenkrupp's transformation into a financial holding company has set a clear strategic path, but the thirdquarter fiscal 2025/26 results delivered a mixed operational picture that materially shifted the 18-month to 3- year earnings trajectory relative to both buy-side and sell-side expectations. Group sales increased by 8 percent to €8.8 billion, ahead of expectations, while adjusted EBIT improved to €183 million from €155 million in the prioryear quarter, driven by Steel Europe and Materials Services operational progress.

However, free cash flow before M&A was negatively impacted by a temporary working capital buildup, missing Morgan Stanley's expectations and consensus, creating a significant deviation from the anticipated cash conversion profile that underpins medium-term valuation models.

The most critical strategic delta in these results centered on the accelerated execution of the Steel Europe standalone restructuring and the imminent tk accelis spin-off, both of which have materially altered the sum-of- the-parts framework. The exit from HKM and the termination of the slab supply contract resulted in an impairment reversal of €407 million in Steel Europe, reflecting the improved standalone profitability of the remaining steel business. This €407 million accounting write-up was substantially higher than sell-side forecasts and represents a fundamental re-rating of Steel Europe's medium-term equity value.

At the Steel Capital Markets Day in September 2026, management unveiled its Transform 30+ program, targeting over €800 million in self-help cost improvements and boosting adjusted EBITDA to greater than €1.2 billion, a target that exceeded Morgan Stanley's fiscal year 2030 forecast of €1.13 billion and sits 27 percent above the Visible Alpha consensus of €0.94 billion.

The net change to the EPS trajectory over the next 18 months to 3 years has been materially positive but remains highly execution-dependent. Deutsche Bank's Bastian Synagowitz raised his target price to EUR 18 from EUR 9 in August 2025, demonstrating substantial long-term target price appreciation, while Morgan Stanley's Alain Gabriel raised his target price significantly from EUR 10.70 to EUR 14.50 in August 2026, well above his historical target of EUR 8.30 from March 2026.

These target upgrades of 50 to 100 percent reflect a structural reassessment of the group's ability to crystallize standalone segment value through the ACES 2030 framework. However, Deutsche Bank's adjusted EBIT forecasts remained 2 to 23 percent ahead of consensus for fiscal years 2026 to 2028, indicating that the broader street has not yet fully capitalized the self-help potential. The actual financial delivery deviated from expectations in three critical dimensions.

First, Automotive Technology experienced lower segment sales and earnings due to higher special freight charges and weaker volumes, missing the sell-side's prior segment forecasts, forcing management to cut divisional guidance and highlighting persistent automotive demand weakness. Second, Automotive Technology segment experienced lower order intake and sales in Q3, driven by a steep decline in serial production volumes within key markets like China, trailing historical levels and undershooting sell-side expectations, representing a structural headwind that could delay the targeted margin recovery.

Third, despite the operational EBIT beat, persistent structural cash drains and the upcoming €400 million in HKM-related cash payments over the coming years could severely pressure the net cash position and delay the targeted ratings recovery if working capital does not unwind.

The strategic results exceeded expectations in critical areas. Shareholders granted a 99.99 percent approval rate for the tk accelis spin-off at the Extraordinary General Meeting on August 7, 2026, distributing a 49 percent minority stake of Materials Services to shareholders while the parent retains a controlling 51 percent interest. Deutsche Bank highlighted that the upcoming listing of tk accelis in late 2026 could crystallize EUR 1 to 3 per share in value above street expectations, representing a tangible near-term catalyst that was not fully reflected in pre-earnings consensus models.

Furthermore, management reported that 4,000 workforce reductions have already been completed out of the 11,000 planned FTE cuts scheduled through 2030, demonstrating 36 percent completion of the labor rightsizing and materially de-risking the execution timeline relative to historical German industrial restructuring precedents. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish sum-of-the-parts thesis centers on Steel Europe's massive legacy liabilities and persistent cash burn. Despite management's optimistic standalone narratives, the carve-out of Steel Europe faces immense financial hurdles, as the steel division holds a massive €2.4 billion pension deficit alongside heavy decarbonization capex demands, which could drain cash balances and leave standalone equity value negative if the business cannot self-fund through cyclical downturns. Morgan Stanley warned that despite the positive medium-term cash flow targets for Steel Europe, the exact timing and capital structure of the separation remain completely undefined, as the business still carries significant transition cash outflows of EUR 800 million over the coming years, limiting the immediate sum-of-the-parts re-rating.

Additionally, tighter import quota regimes and CBAM run a severe risk of triggering downstream demand destruction, as downstream manufacturers buying higher-cost European steel face compressed operating margins when competing against finished assembled goods that enter Europe without equivalent carbon-related duties, potentially undermining the domestic steel demand base and reversing the recent margin inflection. The exact quantitative and qualitative triggers that would fundamentally alter this investment view include: First, failure to achieve €600 million in annual self-help savings by fiscal 2028, which would indicate structural labor resistance or execution slippage.

Second, a sustained collapse in European hot-rolled coil prices below €650 per tonne for more than two consecutive quarters, signaling that trade safeguards have failed to durably reset pricing power. Third, delays in the tk accelis listing beyond Q1 2027 or a listing valuation below €2.5 billion enterprise value, which would indicate that the conglomerate discount persists. Fourth, a net cash position falling below €1.5 billion by fiscal year-end 2026/27 without clear Steel Europe separation funding, indicating that restructuring cash outflows are exceeding internal generation.

Fifth, green hydrogen infrastructure delays beyond 2031, forcing the Duisburg direct reduction plant to operate permanently on natural gas and triggering prohibitive carbon penalty costs as free EU ETS allowances phase out. The next crucial indicators for the portfolio manager include: the actual tk accelis listing valuation and trading performance in late 2026, which will provide the first independent market test of segment value crystallization; Q4 2025/26 and Q1 2026/27 Steel Europe adjusted EBITDA per tonne, which must exceed €50 per tonne to validate the medium-term €1.2 billion EBITDA target; European hot-rolled coil spot prices in January 2027, as contract resets will determine whether the trade-protection-driven margin uplift is durable; workforce reduction progress, requiring 7,000 cumulative FTE cuts by fiscal 2027 to maintain restructuring credibility; and cash payments related to HKM obligations, which must not exceed €200 million in fiscal 2026/27 to preserve balance sheet flexibility for Steel Europe separation funding.

Section 16 – Conclusion 1 finding

Thyssenkrupp's strategic transformation into a financial holding company under the ACES 2030 framework represents the single most important structural delta emerging from recent earnings, fundamentally altering the investment thesis from a struggling integrated conglomerate to a portfolio of independently valued assets.

The Q3 2025/26 results demonstrated operational resilience with group sales expanding 8 percent to €8.8 billion and adjusted EBIT improving to €183 million, but the real significance lies in the €407 million impairment reversal following the completed HKM joint venture exit, which removes structural loss-making obligations and enables Steel Europe's standalone turnaround trajectory.

The investment case fundamentally hinges on successful execution of three interconnected value-crystallization milestones that are systematically dismantling the conglomerate discount. The imminent tk accelis spin-off, approved by 99.99 percent of shareholders in August 2026, will distribute 49 percent of the Materials Services business directly to existing shareholders, while the October 2025 Marine Systems listing successfully established a precedent for standalone market valuations with thyssenkrupp retaining 51 percent controlling stakes.

Most critically, Steel Europe's Transform 30+ program unveiled at the September 2026 Capital Markets Day targets medium-term adjusted EBITDA of at least €1.2 billion, representing a dramatic escalation from the prior-year €0.3 billion. However, the strongest counter-hypothesis centers on Steel Europe's ability to achieve self-funding status amid massive legacy burdens and cyclical demand headwinds. Morgan Stanley's Alain Gabriel cautions that the steel division carries €2.7 billion in pension liabilities and heavy restructuring requirements that could leave standalone equity value negative without significant funding clarity. The near-term cash conversion remains severely challenged, with Q3 free cash flow missing expectations due to temporary working capital buildup. Furthermore, automotive demand compression persists as the weakened sector accounts for up to 50 percent of Steel Europe shipment volumes, creating sustained pressure on volume recovery expectations. The decarbonization strategy introduces additional capital intensity concerns that could materially alter the investment outlook.

The ambitious €3 billion Duisburg direct reduction plant is currently absorbing half of group capital expenditures, weighing heavily on near-term cash generation, while management acknowledges that green hydrogen infrastructure will arrive later and at higher costs than initially assumed, forcing extended natural gas operations that fail to produce genuine low-carbon steel. This technological transition risk is compounded by new EU conditionality linking free carbon allowances to verified decarbonization investments starting in 2031- 2035, increasing pressure on capital allocation.

Despite these structural challenges, recent regulatory developments have materially strengthened the European steel competitive positioning. The European Commission's immediate implementation of country-specific import quotas on July 1, 2026, combined with the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism, successfully stabilized regional pricing and could leave Europe structurally short by 10 to 15 million tonnes of steel. Northern European hot-rolled coil offers have tested €800 per tonne delivered, up from €770 per tonne, driving significant potential for consensus EBITDA upgrades.

This trade protection framework provides a critical tailwind that the broader market has not yet fully capitalized. The exact quantitative triggers that would validate the bullish structural transformation thesis center on three measurable execution milestones. First, successful completion of the tk accelis listing in late October 2026 with a stable trading price that crystallizes €1 to €3 per share in value above street expectations as estimated by Deutsche Bank.

Second, Steel Europe must demonstrate quarterly adjusted EBITDA progression toward the €300 million quarterly run-rate implied by the medium-term €1.2 billion target, with particular focus on achieving EBITDA per tonne sustainably above €50 versus the current low-€40s baseline. Third, group free cash flow must turn structurally positive on a trailing twelve-month basis, demonstrating that working capital normalization and restructuring cash outflows are not permanently impairing the balance sheet.

Conversely, the bearish case would be confirmed if Steel Europe's standalone separation timeline extends beyond mid-2027 without clear legal structure or external funding commitments, signaling that the €800 million in transition cash outflows and undefined capital structure remain unresolved. A second critical negative trigger would be failure to maintain European hot-rolled coil pricing above €750 per tonne for two consecutive quarters, indicating that trade protections are insufficient to offset downstream demand destruction.

Finally, if Steel Europe headcount reductions stall below 7,000 full-time equivalents by September 2027 versus the agreed 11,000 reduction target through 2030, it would demonstrate that German co-determination laws and labor opposition are materially delaying the restructuring roadmap. The qualitative triggers revolve around management's ability to navigate the complex interplay between political support and operational autonomy.

Successful execution requires that German federal and state governments maintain their €2 billion green steel subsidy commitments despite potential political transitions, while simultaneously demonstrating progress on national hydrogen grid infrastructure that enables genuine low-carbon steel production by 2030. Additionally, management must prove capable of achieving the aggressive workforce reductions and cost savings embedded in Transform 30+ without triggering major labor disputes that could derail the separation timeline or force dilutive equity raises to fund pension obligations.
