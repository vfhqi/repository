Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Billerud faces a critical decision in 2027 regarding the Gävle recovery boiler replacement to secure its European asset competitiveness.
  2. The multi-billion capital project directly impacts long-term return on capital employed and earnings per share while it overlaps with the North American Evolution program.

Strategic Project Context

1.
Key Question: "What size, timing, phasing and financing is expected for Billerud AB's (BILL-SE) Gävle recovery boiler replacement (stated as above SEK 3bn, decision in 2027, production targeted around 2030, Capital Markets Day December 2024); which technical options and partnerships are being considered; what did the Frövi recovery boiler cost and how was it delivered; and what do brokers and management say about its effect on capex, depreciation, net debt, the dividend and any share buyback from 2027 to 2030?"
2.
Strategic urgency: Billerud management disclosed at its December 2024 Capital Markets Day that one Gävle recovery boiler is approaching its end of life and requires replacement by 2030. This massive asset reinvestment is expected to cost over SEK 3bn and represents the company's only major planned capital project in Europe. The final investment decision will directly shape Billerud's ability to reach its long-term return on capital employed target of above 11% over the business cycle.
3.
Prudent leverage: Billerud maintains a long-term capital allocation framework that prioritizes a leverage ratio of net debt to EBITDA of below 2.5x. This conservative debt target is crucial because Billerud plans to fund the massive Gävle boiler project largely through its own operating cash flows and existing credit facilities. Maintaining strong cash conversion remains a cornerstone of capital discipline during periods of elevated investment intensity.
4.
Strategic pivot: Billerud is simultaneously executing its Evolution program in Region North America to transition its Escanaba and Quinnesec mills toward packaging board. Management has committed SEK 1.4bn of strategic capex from 2024 through 2027 to unlock higher-margin paperboard sales. This investment runs parallel to Gävle preparations, meaning any capital overrun in Sweden could restrict the financial flexibility of North American operations.

Efficiency and Precedents

5.
Operational leverage: Billerud targets a step-change in European mill efficiency, where improving overall equipment effectiveness by just 1% yields SEK 100m in EBITDA. Achieving these operational gains is critical because European operations are burdened by historically high Nordic fiber costs. Upgrading the Gävle recovery boiler is designed to structurally strengthen long-term competitive positioning and lower energy costs.
6.
Contrary evidence: Billerud decided to withdraw from its Follum BCTMP joint venture in Norway in early 2026 due to changed market conditions and permitting delays, demonstrating that large Nordic capital projects carry severe execution risks. This cancellation suggests that the Gävle project could face similar regulatory hurdles or strategic re-evaluations before its 2027 decision. Furthermore, wood costs in Sweden remain at historically elevated levels, which threatens the eventual return on capital employed for Gävle.
7.
Other side of the story: Billerud has proven it can execute large-scale recovery boiler projects successfully, as demonstrated by the Frövi recovery boiler which was delivered on time and under its SEK 2.6bn budget in late 2023. While brokers highlight that Gävle capex will elevate net debt and depress near-term free cash flow, the successful commissioning of Frövi shows that modern boilers materially enhance resource efficiency and pulp output. Consequently, the Gävle replacement may prove highly value-accretive, offsetting near-term capital constraints through long-term cash flow generation.

Section 3 – Technical Options, Suppliers, and Engineering Partnerships

Section summary
  1. Billerud is evaluating technical options and engineering partnerships for the Gävle recovery boiler replacement to optimize long-term chemical recovery and efficiency.
  2. The technology market is dominated by a tight global duopoly of Andritz and Valmet, both of which possess established historical relationships with Billerud.
  3. Upgrading to a brand-new boiler offers superior energy self-sufficiency and lower emissions compared to partial rebuilds, aligned with recent European industry precedents.

Technology Suppliers and Duopoly

1.
Consolidated market: The global chemical recovery boiler market is characterized by high entry barriers and intense consolidated competition. The sector is dominated by a tight global duopoly consisting of Andritz and Valmet. Deutsche Bank Research reported in October 2026 that Andritz is the global leader with a market share of over 70% in recovery boilers. This market concentration means Billerud has limited vendor options for Gävle.
2.
Strong relationships: Billerud maintains deep historical ties with both major engineering suppliers across its European mill network. For example, Billerud selected Andritz to supply its successful Frövi recovery boiler in 2021. Additionally, Billerud commissioned Procemex, an Andritz Group subsidiary, to install a Gävle monitoring system in 2025. These historical collaborations increase the likelihood of Andritz securing the upcoming Gävle recovery boiler contract.
3.
Alternative options: Valmet represents the primary competitor to Andritz for the Gävle recovery boiler contract. DNB Carnegie highlighted in October 2026 that Valmet's biomaterials equipment segment, which includes pulp mills and boilers, faces exceptionally consolidated industry dynamics globally. This competitive positioning makes Valmet a highly viable technology alternative. Therefore, Valmet will likely submit a highly competitive bid during the Gävle procurement phase.
4.
Custom consultant: The procurement process for modern recovery boilers requires deep technical collaboration rather than standard transactional purchasing. Andritz sales managers stated in February 2026 that their team acts as consultants to co-design tailor-made boilers for customers. This bespoke engineering approach is critical for Billerud to align the boiler with Gävle's specific capacity. Therefore, any final supplier choice will involve extensive engineering studies prior to the 2027 decision.

Rebuild versus New Installation

5.
Technical trade-offs: Billerud is examining the trade-offs between a complete recovery boiler rebuild and a brand- new installation. AFRY specialists noted in January 2026 that modernizing recovery boilers improves a mill's power balance and minimizes fossil-based power purchases. Although a complete rebuild requires lower upfront capital, a brand-new installation maximizes long-term energy self-sufficiency. Consequently, a new boiler is the preferred option to achieve Billerud's emission goals.
6.
Contrary evidence: The findings indicate that alternative chemical recovery technologies remain unproven at a scale suitable for Gävle's large pulp operations. For instance, post-combustion carbon capture systems are still in the preliminary engineering phase. Svante reported in February 2025 that its rotary solid sorbent technology is only undergoing a pre-front end engineering design study in Ashdown. This early stage means Billerud cannot rely on these advanced alternative recovery systems by 2030.
7.
Advanced integration: Modern recovery boilers feature advanced digital tools and low-emission solutions that older systems lack. For example, Andritz offers advanced process controls and Lo-NOx solutions to minimize dust and nitrogen emissions from the boiler. Additionally, integrating an ash leaching system helps remove chloride and potassium from the recovery cycle. These digital and environmental upgrades are crucial for Billerud to meet its strict sustainability targets.
8.
Lower risk: Safety innovations are a major factor in choosing a brand-new recovery boiler over a partial rebuild. Andritz engineers emphasized in October 2025 that modern boilers utilize larger tube diameters in furnace walls to improve water circulation and avoid leakages. Additionally, automated smelt spout vacuum cooling systems reduce the need for manual operator interventions. These safety enhancements protect workers and reduce the risk of catastrophic mill explosions.
9.
Rapid restart: Modern boiler designs incorporate structural elements that reduce planned maintenance shutdown durations. Andritz technology directors explained in October 2025 that a refractory-free lower furnace floor allows mills to shorten shutdown times by 24 hours. This operational efficiency is vital because pulp mills lose all fiberline production during recovery boiler downtime. Minimizing maintenance delays directly supports Billerud's long-term profitability targets.

Mondi completed a major €128 million recovery boiler replacement at its Ružomberok mill in Slovakia. The new boiler made the Slovakian mill totally electricity self-sufficient while increasing pulp capacity. This precedent shows how a brand-new boiler yields substantial operating cost savings.
11.
Polish benchmark: Mondi executed a similar recovery boiler upgrade program at its Świecie mill in Poland. This capital project required an investment of €166 million to replace both the recovery boiler and coal-fired boilers. By accelerating this replacement, Mondi significantly improved its environmental footprint and lowered maintenance costs. This successful peer installation highlights the competitive necessity of modernizing recovery infrastructure.
12.
Swedish comparison: Other Nordic peers are actively upgrading their aging Swedish pulp infrastructure to remain competitive. Mondi Dynäs commenced a multi-year modernization program in late 2025 at its Kramfors mill in Sweden. The upgrade includes a new fiber line, continuous digester, and bark boiler to improve longterm energy efficiency. These regional peer investments pressure Billerud to execute the Gävle boiler replacement without delay.
13.
Finnish precedent: Recent Nordic investments confirm that recovery boiler upgrades are highly effective at strengthening pulp assets. Metsä Board upgraded its Husum pulp mill by installing a new recovery boiler. This Husum modernization has substantially improved electricity self-sufficiency and operational cash flow. This successful peer upgrade indicates that a new boiler will strengthen Gävle's competitiveness.

Billerud's management stated in December 2024 that they are exploring different technical options to find the most cost-efficient option. Simultaneously, Billerud signed a modernization agreement with ABB in February 2026 to upgrade Paper Machine 5 at Gävle. This concurrent automation work ensures that Gävle's overall mill infrastructure is prepared for a new boiler.

Section 4 – Precedent Study: The Frövi Recovery Boiler Project

  1. Billerud successfully executed its Frövi recovery boiler replacement project ahead of schedule and marginally under its SEK 2.6 billion budget.

  3. Completing the Frövi installation safely and on budget rebuilt Billerud's credibility with investors following historical execution struggles at its Gruvön mill.

Billerud recorded capital expenditures of SEK 0.4 billion in 2021, SEK 1.1 billion in 2022, and SEK 1.0 billion in 2023. The company allocated an additional SEK 0.1 billion during 2024 to finalize the boiler project.

However, the company secured a SEK 1.3 billion loan agreement with the European Investment Bank in November 2023. This bilateral financing supported the environmental upgrade and secured high environmental performance for the integrated pulp mill.

Schedule Delivery and Sourcing Disruptions

7.
Rapid project commissioning: Billerud successfully put the Frövi recovery boiler into production in September 2023. This launch occurred four months ahead of schedule compared to the original timeline targeting the end of 2023. The operational handover from the primary equipment supplier to the mill line organization was completed shortly after in November 2023.
8.
Severe external disruptions: Billerud achieved this early commissioning despite facing numerous external supply chain challenges during construction. Project construction was affected by the Swedish cement supply crisis and the second wave of Covid-19 pandemic lockdowns. Despite these overlapping macro issues, the project teams successfully avoided any schedule slippage at the Frövi site.

Critical steel shortages: The Frövi project encountered severe engineering material constraints due to geopolitical conflicts in eastern Europe. Mill Director Richard Morén noted that the project faced challenges in obtaining vital structural steel from Ukraine. To resolve these constraints, the mill management established a completely transparent relationship with its technology supplier.
10.
Flexible vendor negotiations: Billerud selected Andritz as its key engineering supplier because of the vendor's price, quality, and flexibility. The company was highly impressed by the technical design of the equipment and Andritz's commercial flexibility during the sales phase. This collaborative procurement process ensured that the bespoke engineering requirements of the Frövi mill were met.
11.
Effective labor mobilization: The construction phase of the Frövi recovery boiler demanded a massive allocation of regional engineering labor. The project successfully integrated approximately 1.2 million work hours completed by 2,500 individual workers. This high-density construction program was executed safely with zero major accidents reported during assembly.

The new recovery boiler started up during the mill's planned annual shutdown while the original 1960s boiler remained in place. Local operators likened this delicate switchover process to changing an airliner engine mid-flight without stopping production.

Strategic Lessons and Operational Benefits

13.
Rebuilt market credibility: The successful Frövi project restored investor confidence in Billerud's project management capabilities. Financial analysts had expressed doubts about the company's engineering execution following past timeline struggles with the KM7 board machine build. Delivering Frövi on budget and ahead of schedule proves Billerud's ability to execute highly complex capital investments.
14.

The old boiler suffered from frequent maintenance issues and could not meet the strict 2023 regional environmental regulations. Investing in a modern unit was necessary to renew the mill's environmental permits and safeguard cartonboard production.
15.
Expanded pulp capacity: The modern recovery boiler delivered substantial operational benefits and increased resource efficiency. Operating entirely on fossil-free biofuel, the new unit significantly reduced air emissions and increased energy production. The boiler's design features also allow for higher pulp production in the future once Billerud decides to expand.
16.
Environmental emission compliance: The Frövi recovery boiler replacement was designed to achieve the lowest possible air and water pollution levels. The newly commissioned biofuel boiler successfully reduced nitrogen oxides and particulate emissions below statutory limits. Achieving these environmental targets is vital for Billerud to protect its long-term production permits across Sweden.
17.
Operational optimization measures: Billerud continues to optimize the Frövi boiler's process controls to meet strict emissions targets. The company implemented nitrogen oxide reduction measures during 2025 to keep emissions well below regulatory limits. These ongoing system tweaks are crucial to maintain strict compliance with environmental permits over the long term.
18.
Other side of the story: Although the Frövi recovery boiler replacement was successfully completed on budget, this precedent may not fully guarantee a seamless execution for Gävle. The Gävle project is projected to exceed SEK 3 billion, which represents a larger financial and engineering scale than Frövi's SEK 2.6 billion budget. Furthermore, structurally higher Nordic wood costs could pressure Billerud's European margins, meaning that even a flawless boiler installation might struggle to meet ROCE targets if fiber inflation persists.
