Britvic Integration Progress

1.
Strategic transformation: Carlsberg completed the acquisition of Britvic in mid-January 2025 to establish an integrated multi-beverage business. In the 2025 annual report, the company reported that Britvic delivered 24 million hectoliters in volume and DKK 15.6 billion in revenue. This acquisition successfully doubled the group's soft drinks exposure to 30% of total volumes to build long-term resilience. This transition provides stable cash flows that should help protect the adjusted earnings per share against structural declines in Western European beer.
2.
Rapid synergies: Carlsberg raised its annual cost synergy target for the combined UK business from GBP 100 million to GBP 110 million in late 2025. In its H1 2026 interim report, the group announced that it delivered 30% of these synergies in 2025 and expects to reach 80% cumulative synergy delivery by end-2026 due to faster integration. This rapid cost-saving realization supports the group's narrow organic operating profit growth guidance of 4% to 6% for 2026.

Hybrid Bond Engineering

1.
Technical treatment: Carlsberg issued a EUR 1.8bn hybrid bond in May 2026 that is treated entirely as equity under IFRS guidelines. However, major credit rating agencies like Moody's and Fitch count only 50% of the bond's value as equity, treating the remaining half as interest-bearing debt. This technical 50/50 debt treatment is reflected directly in Carlsberg's internal leverage calculations.
2.
Leverage reduction: Carlsberg used the hybrid proceeds to repurchase outstanding EMTN bonds, which dramatically strengthened its balance sheet health. The group's reported net interest-bearing debt to EBITDA ratio fell from 3.3x at the end of 2025 to 3.0x in the first half of 2026. - Earnings Calls 192 & Co Redburn This rapid balance sheet optimization represents a significant slice off financial leverage ahead of original corporate timelines.
3.
Dilution impact: Morgan Stanley calculated that the EUR 1.8bn hybrid bond issuance will dilute Carlsberg's earnings per share by approximately 2% in fiscal year 2027. Because the hybrid coupons do not flow through net finance costs under IFRS, they are instead recorded directly in equity as interest on hybrid bonds. This structural allocation will cost approximately DKK 475m in FY27 and acts as a minor bottom-line headwind.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some market participants note that the high interest rate on hybrid instruments can create more permanent financing drag than traditional debt instruments. Bernstein Research reported that Carlsberg's hybrid bonds carry a significantly higher coupon than the group's current debt average, which directly creates a 1% dilutive impact on FY26 earnings per share. This elevated funding cost means that while leverage metrics improve, the absolute cash cost of capital remains comparatively high.
5.
Finance savings: Carlsberg successfully reduced its expected full-year net finance costs, excluding foreign exchange, from DKK 2.2bn down to DKK 1.8bn. This improvement was driven by the early redemption of outstanding EMTN bonds following the hybrid issuance in the second quarter of 2026. - Earnings Calls Consequently, the reduced traditional interest expenses offset the hybrid coupon dilution during the initial integration phases.
