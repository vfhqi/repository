Acquisition Option Structures and Profit Allocation

1.
Significant acquisitions: The SKAN Group expanded its consolidation scope in July 2025 by acquiring a 76% stake in Slovenian software provider Metronik. This transaction leaves 24% of the shares in Metronik held as minority interests, meaning that nearly one-quarter of Metronik's profits will leak out to minority holders. Consequently, this minority stake represents a persistent dilution of SKAN's reported net earnings over the near term.
2.
Flexible valuations: Metronik's put and call option pricing relies on a contractually defined methodology that calculates the fair market value of the group at exercise. Because this valuation fluctuates with Metronik's future operational performance, SKAN faces the risk of a much higher acquisition cost if Metronik's profits surge. This variable liability makes it difficult to forecast exact cash needs for full buyout in 2029.
3.
Regulated options: SKAN established put and call options for the remaining 24% of Metronik shares that can be exercised starting in 2029 at the earliest. The purchase price for these shares is contractually tied to Metronik's fair market value at the time of exercise. These options are not automatically exercisable, which gives SKAN substantial flexibility in managing its future cash outflows for full ownership.
4.
Distinct consolidation: SKAN acquired an 84% stake in rapid transfer specialist ABC Transfer in July 2025 but chose a completely different accounting treatment. Due to a mutual and irrevocable promise to purchase the remaining 16%, the acquisition is treated under the principle of substance over form. This means ABC Transfer is fully consolidated with no reported minority interests in the financial statements.
5.
Recognized liabilities: SKAN reported the outstanding purchase price for the remaining shares of ABC Transfer as a discounted financial liability. This future payment is recognized within non-current financial liabilities on the consolidated balance sheet, which avoids any operational profit share leaking to minority holders. This structure prevents EPS dilution but increases SKAN's long-term debt and balance sheet leverage over the next four years.
6.
Gradual repurchase: SKAN successively increased its ownership in Aseptic Technologies to 90% by purchasing a 5% tranche on May 27, 2024. This transaction followed a contractual schedule from 2024 to 2026, which required the virtual repurchase of shares from the Société Régionale d'Investissement de Wallonie. This phased buyback successfully reduces the minority profit leakage to ten percent to support bottom-line growth.

The theoretical amortization charge rose to CHF 8.346 million in 2025 compared to just CHF 1.389 million in 2024. If capitalized, this shadow amortization would reduce SKAN's reported net profit to CHF 9.219 million in fiscal year 2025.

Section 8 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. A slower commercial ramp of Pre-Approved Services and prolonged validation delays would severely compress SKAN Group's mid-term earnings per share.
  2. Unresolved regulatory trials and escalating minority buyouts represent underestimated cash drains that could restrict future capital allocation.
  3. Potential tax rate inflation under global minimum tax standards presents a hidden headwind to consolidated profit margins.
  4. Rising capital intensity and declining returns on capital employed reflect the high execution risks of SKAN's capacity expansion cycle.

Validation Delays and Revenue Risks

1.
Unfavourable delays: Slower-than-expected commercial ramp-up of SKAN's Pre-Approved Services facility represents a primary threat to consensus earnings. The company remained free cash flow negative during the peak of this investment cycle, which highlights the risk of prolonged delays. Any further qualification delays would extend this unprofitable period, defer Swissmedic approval beyond 2026, and depress mid-term earnings per share significantly.
2.
241 - Earnings Calls Scrutinized orders: Customers in SKAN's biopharmaceutical market are increasingly subjecting capital projects to stricter economic scrutiny, which slows order execution. The suspension of a major GLP-1 project during its design review phase illustrates the risk of sudden order deferrals. These delays prevent SKAN from recognizing high-margin project sales, increase work in progress, and weigh heavily on equipment revenues.

Contrary evidence: SKAN's order backlog rose significantly to CHF 442.3 million in the first half of 2026 to provide substantial planning visibility. While management projects EBITDA margins of up to 50% at full capacity, initial under-absorption will drag on profitability. Tax Escalation and Funding Burdens

6.
Escalating taxes: Escalating taxes from shifting cantonal tax rules and international Pillar Two legislation present an underestimated risk of tax rate inflation for SKAN. The expected tax rate of 21.7% in 2025 reflects the average rate across operating regions. If tax loss carryforwards deplete faster than modeled, the effective tax rate will rise toward this expected baseline and squeeze bottom-line margins.
7.
Variable interest: Variable interest expenses from SKAN's syndicated senior credit facility could elevate funding costs during periods of monetary tightening. The CHF 120 million bank loan is linked to SARON and matures within five years. If Swiss interest rates rise unexpectedly, the increased debt servicing costs will directly erode bottom-line earnings per share.
8.
Depressed yields: Depressed yields on SKAN's cash holdings prevent the company from offsetting its rising financial expenses. Cash and cash equivalents generated only CHF 2 thousand of interest income from third parties in 2025. This negligible yield creates a negative net financing result that hampers overall interest coverage and limits financial flexibility.
9.
Ongoing trials: Ongoing trials and legal proceedings involving senior executives create persistent corporate governance concerns that could affect SKAN's investor sentiment. While the head of services was acquitted of insider trading, proceedings involving other personnel continue. Any negative legal outcome would disrupt leadership, damage corporate reputation, and threaten operational execution success during critical expansion phases.
10.
Declining returns: Declining returns on SKAN's capital employed reflect the high capital intensity of recent growth investments and acquisitions. The group's return on capital employed fell to 10.3% in fiscal year 2025 due to a larger capital base. If these investments fail to generate expected profits quickly, they will impair long-term capital efficiency ratios.

Minority Leakage and Structural Shocks

11.
Dilutive leakages: Dilutive leakages to minority partners will continue to dilute bottom-line earnings attributable to SKAN's major shareholders. Minority holders retain a 24% stake in Metronik and participate fully in its opportunities and risks. Strong operational performance at acquired subsidiaries will increase cash leakage to minorities rather than supporting parent EPS.
12.
Contractual liabilities: Contractual liabilities from put options on the remaining 24% of Metronik shares introduce substantial balance sheet liabilities for SKAN. These options are exercisable starting in 2029 based on Metronik's fair market value at the time. A sharp rise in Metronik's profitability would inflate the final buyout price and exhaust SKAN's liquid resources.
13.
Offline amortization: Offline amortization of goodwill under Swiss GAAP FER shields SKAN's reported income statement but weakens overall equity ratios. SKAN recorded goodwill additions of CHF 86.81 million in 2025 which were written off against reserves. Tracking shadow amortization of CHF 8.3 million reveals the theoretical earnings drag from premium-priced acquisitions.
14.
Integration challenges: Integration challenges from recent acquisitions could slow the realization of SKAN's expected synergies and drag on operating margins. The inclusion of Metronik and ABC Transfer increased Services segment revenue share to 39% in 2026. If integration costs exceed expectations, segment profit margins will suffer despite higher absolute sales.
15.
Other side of the story: Strong demand for SKAN's customized high-speed lines in North America and Europe could accelerate the commercial absorption of new facilities. If the Pre-Approved Services business achieves rapid Swissmedic approval in 2026, operating leverage will improve quickly. This operational success would easily overshadow near-term tax rate increases and surpass conservative sell-side forecasts.
