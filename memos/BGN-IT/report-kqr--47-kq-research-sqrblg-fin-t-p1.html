Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. The evidence demonstrates that Banca Generali carries substantial variable flexibility through its adviser payout structure, delivering meaningful operating leverage during growth cycles while maintaining cost resilience in drawdowns. However, accelerated IT, AI, and regional platform roll-outs have driven short-term cost spikes, prompting a temporary increase in core operating opex guidance to 8-9% for fiscal year 2026, with normalization to the 6-8% target band projected for 2027. The upcoming expiry of legacy loyalty programs on 31 December 2026 will eliminate significant long-term liabilities and free up cash flow, though operational-risk capital requirements under the CRR3 Basel 3 framework have meaningfully increased capital absorption, scaling up alongside growth in gross fees. Peer case studies reveal that well-executed efficiency programs can successfully eliminate fixed overheads, yet bolt-on acquisitions temporarily inflate costs before synergies materialize.

The fixed-cost split shows that Banca Generali's presentation of 30 July 2025 shows operating costs included €601 million of variable adviser costs and €361 million of fixed bank costs, with consolidated staff expenses grew by 7.4% to €86.4 million in fixed costs during 1H26 and outsourced IT services through CSE climbed to €26.7 million in administrative expenses. This cost architecture provides meaningful flexibility, as supplementary network incentives are capitalized and amortized over five years under IFRS 15, smoothing the impact of competitive recruitment cycles. The alignment of ordinary sales incentives with five-year recruitment amortization under IAS 8 further stabilizes the expense trajectory, though prospective changes to accounting standards could alter the timing and recognition of these costs in the 2027-2029 period.

Historical drawdown performance confirms that Banca Generali exhibits strong cost resilience and structural operating leverage during market downturns, with total operating costs rising moderately by 5.9% to €256.5 million in H2 2022 despite high inflation, reflecting tight containment of core operating expenses. During that period, variable fees declined to €19.3 million, reaching their lowest level in ten years, demonstrating the capacity to flex growth-related payouts. Management emphasized during past cycles that great flexibility in growthrelated fee expenses ensured the sustainability of corporate profits, and the ordinary payout to the advisor network is stable around 36% over the cycle. Importantly, total cost on assets decreased from 51 basis points to 33 basis points, showing strong operating leverage working very well over the multi-year period through 2018.

The 2026 loyalty program expiry represents a critical inflection point for the cost base. The legacy Framework Loyalty Programme will expire on 31 December 2026, with €36.6 million in total incentives paid out in June 2026 to eligible financial advisers. Notably, the legacy program was actually suspended in 2021 with no additional cycles activated, and a €34.2 million cash advance granted in 2022 vests definitively in H1 2027. This removes a material long-term cash obligation, though the extended amortization of past incentives under IFRS 15 will continue to impact reported operating expenses through the outer years of the forecast period.

Peer comparisons provide valuable context on achievable efficiency gains and structural cost positioning. Fideuram leverages its massive scale within Intesa Sanpaolo to maintain a cost-to-income ratio of around 31.40%, representing the industry benchmark for operational efficiency in the Italian wealth management sector. FinecoBank maintains the most stable operating jaws due to a diversified fee structure without performance fee dependence, running at a cost-to-income ratio of 30% in 2022 compared to Banca Mediolanum's higher ratio of 43%. In terms of cost reduction programs, Julius Baer overachieved its 2025 efficiency targets by delivering CHF 130 million in gross savings on a run-rate basis, compared to the initial target of CHF 110 million, and St. James's Place's cost program remains on track to remove £100 million from addressable costs by fiscal year 2027 through commercial vendor renegotiations and real estate rationalization. However, the multi-year implementation of the charging structure incurred total post-tax costs of £119.40 million, coming in near the top of initial expectations, illustrating that restructuring programs carry material upfront costs before benefits accrue.

- Earnings Calls Mid-term strategic initiatives are temporarily elevating the cost base but are expected to normalize. 2025 core operating expenses grew by 7.7% to €288.6 million on a like-for-like basis due to accelerated IT outlays, and the 2026 core cost growth guidance was raised to 8% to 9% to accommodate the speed of strategic initiative implementation. These costs include approximately €5 million in accelerated IT, AI, and data infrastructure project spend, as well as startup charges of €11.1 million in fiscal year 2025 for the Lugano-based BG Suisse.

The acquisition of Intermonte Group added €38.3 million in operating costs during fiscal year 2025, but Intermonte generated €7.8 million in revenue synergies in the first half of fiscal year 2026, with projections that Intermonte's cost-to-income ratio will decline from 80% to below 60% by 2030 through margin internalization. Capital dynamics represent an underappreciated headwind to reported profitability and flexibility.

The new Basel 3 CRD IV framework increased capital absorption by €86.6 million due to operational risk calculations, and the combined CRR3 and Intermonte changes had a negative capital ratio impact of 3.8 percentage points from operational risks. This operational-risk capital scales mechanically with gross fee revenue growth, creating a structural drag on return on equity as the business expands, particularly during periods of elevated recruitment investment and M&A activity.

The risk of divergence from consensus expectations centers on three key scenarios: prolonged tech project overruns, persistent tax headwinds, and competitive recruitment pressure. Jefferies Research's note of 3 August 2026 shows Visible Alpha consensus EPS of €3.84 for 2027, which could suffer from operating leverage failures during sudden market volume drawdowns, and a €20 million Luxembourg tax charge acts as a material earnings headwind for the 2026 and 2027 fiscal years.

Additionally, G&A opex rose by €9.5 million due to higher costs for IT infrastructure and charges related to strategic projects, and the Replatforming FEP/FER project requires gradual migration of legacy systems to prevent operational platform overruns. On the competitive front, maintaining the industry's highest payout to advisers limits the bank's operational flexibility during prolonged down-cycles, particularly as peer Banca Mediolanum expanded its network to 7,000 active family bankers.

Nevertheless, high recurring fee margins provide a highly resilient cushion against near-term cost overruns, supported by Banca Generali's positioning in the high-end wealth segment where management fee margins remained robust at 1.34% of assets, highlighting resilience of core asset management fees.

Section 6 – What Is Known and What Is Not

The overall confidence level in the findings of this report is medium to high. The analysis benefits from comprehensive disclosure in Banca Generali's regulatory filings, particularly on cost breakdowns, historical performance during drawdowns, and strategic initiatives such as the Intermonte integration and Swiss expansion. Peer case studies from St. James's Place, Julius Baer, and EFG International provide robust benchmarks for costreduction feasibility. However, critical gaps remain in three domains: the precise contractual terms and exit costs of outsourced IT arrangements with CSE, the detailed implementation timeline and P&L impact of prospective IAS 8 changes on ordinary network incentives, and the granular cost and revenue attribution for Intermonte across the 2026 to 2030 integration horizon.
