Section summary
  1. Shell's planned sale of its US chemicals business aims to release unproductive capital and support capital allocation.
  2. The transaction's resolution will directly impact segment profitability, return on capital, and future share buyback capacity.

Defining the Strategic Inquiry

1.
Key inquiry: "Shell plc (SHEL-GB), the London-listed integrated energy major, reporting five segments: Integrated Gas, Upstream (oil and gas exploration and production, mainly deepwater in Brazil and the Gulf of America), Marketing (retail mobility, lubricants and business fuels), Chemicals & Products (refining, chemicals and oil products trading) and Renewables & Energy Solutions (power, hydrogen and carbon capture). What are the likely terms, timing and accounting effects of Shell plc's (SHEL- GB) planned sale of its US chemicals business (including the Monaca polyethylene complex and the Deer Park and Norco chemicals assets): which bidders remain (ExxonMobil and LyondellBasell were reported in August 2026), what valuations have comparable petrochemical asset sales achieved in 2024-2026, what is the carrying value against likely proceeds, and what do the European chemicals closures and the Singapore exit of 2025 imply for Chemicals & Products earnings?"

Although multiples can breach this spectrum during extreme circumstances, analysts expect valuations to eventually return to this normal range. However, the ongoing macroeconomic downcycle has depressed average sector multiples near historical lows.
13.
Contrary evidence: Some low-cost asset regions have managed to preserve robust valuations and healthy target multiples despite the global petrochemical downcycle. For example, PTT Global Chemical was upgraded in July 2026 based on an elevated target multiple of 6.0x EV/EBITDA, up from its previous 5.5x benchmark, due to healthier refining margins. This selective resilience suggests that low-cost or highly integrated assets do not suffer identical valuation discounts to high-cost European facilities.
14.
Depressed outlook: The collection of asset transactions completed between 2024 and 2026 establishes that bottom-of-cycle commodity petrochemical assets command almost no premium. Strategic buyers are unwilling to pay historical book values, forcing sellers to accept vendor loans or cash contributions to execute transactions. Therefore, any planned divestment of underperforming assets is highly likely to trigger major impairments rather than generate premium cash proceeds.

Other side of the story: While recent European chemical disposals suggest that sellers must accept steep discounts or cash-negative terms, North American assets could attract significantly higher valuation multiples. High-capacity, ethane-integrated US Gulf Coast facilities remain highly attractive to buyers looking to expand in cost-advantaged regions. Consequently, Shell may succeed in securing a premium valuation closer to its targeted eight billion dollars if competitive bidding intensifies among strategic buyers.
