[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. PKO Bank Polski is a cause-for-concern-clearing case that has already cleared: Poland's largest bank, with a quarter of the loan market, a cost-to-income ratio near 30% and a return on equity of 18% to 19%, is now reporting almost clean of the Swiss franc legal provisions that masked its earnings for six years, and the market has re-read it from a legal risk to a growth franchise, the shares up 67% in twelve months. At PLN 116.34 the price pays for the clean earnings, the tax step-down and the operating growth together, so the modal return is about 7% a year over three years against a 20% bar. Below about PLN 90 the same path would clear the bar; here it does not.

  • The change is external and legal, and it has run its course.
    • The Swiss franc mortgage litigation cost PLN 24.3 billion of provisions since 2019 and PLN 4.4 billion in 2025 alone
    • The quarterly charge has fallen from PLN 1,249 million in Q2 2025 to PLN 297 million in Q2 2026, coverage of active contracts is 188%, over 90% of active contracts are settled, judged or in court, and management expects the charge to cease being material by the end of 2026.
    • That is hard clearing evidence, not hoped-for clearing, which is why the concern and its clearing are both rated A in Building Block 3.
  • The change inside the company is continuity, not change.
    • The chief executive took office in June 2024 and the "#1, full stop" strategy was adopted in October 2024
    • Both are now mature, and the 2027 targets have been reaffirmed rather than raised.
    • Guidance has been beaten by 3% to 10% in every year since 2023, but it is held, not lifted, so the internal forces are a C: one strong force (the balance sheet being put to work, loans +13.5% year on year) and no guidance increase.
  • The external forces ahead pull in both directions, so the net force is modest.
    • Loan demand is a tailwind with two-plus years of visibility: customer financing +13.4% year on year to PLN 328.5 billion at June 2026, consumer loans +21.5%, corporate financing +15%, Polish GDP +4% in Q2 2026, EUR 137 billion of EU funds behind corporate investment, and loans at only 32% of GDP against nearly 80% in the euro area.
    • Against it, the net interest margin compresses as Polish rates fall (management guides 15 basis points per 100 basis points of cuts; the sell-side models 56 basis points of compression through 2028), the corporate income tax rate for banks rose to 30% in 2026 from 19%, and a reserve tax is mooted.
    • Volume is in an upturn, margin in a downturn, and revenue is roughly flat in 2026 before consensus has it growing 6.9% in FY2027.
  • 'Financial Outputs' are improving in quality more than in rate.
    • The operating engine, pre-provision profit, grows about 5% to 6% a year as 9% loan growth is offset by the margin.
    • Earnings per share rise faster, about 10.7% a year from FY2026 to FY2029 on our path (PLN 9.0 to PLN 12.2), because roughly half of that growth comes from below the operating line: the corporate income tax rate stepping down from 30% in 2026 to 26% in 2027 and 23% in 2028, and the CHF charge falling from about PLN 1.3 billion to about PLN 0.4 billion.
    • The FY2027 step of about 17% to 20% is two-thirds tax and provisions, which the framework says investors do not extrapolate.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain.
    • The chief executive and strategy (layers 1 and 2) were set in 2024
    • The actions (digital onboarding, cost control, consumer lending, settlements) show in the key operating metrics (loans +13.5%, cost-to-income 28.7% in Q2 2026, cost of risk 31 basis points, CHF provisions PLN 297 million a quarter), in the financial statements (record FY2025 net profit of PLN 10,682 million) and in the sell-side's estimates (FY2028 consensus earnings per share +19.8% in twelve months).
    • There is no unpropagated change left for the market to discover.
  • The price has walked up the stairs ahead of the earnings.
    • The shares trade at 9.9 times consensus earnings 24 months forward, the 93rd percentile of their own five-year range (median 7.26 times), between the ten-year median of 9.0 times and the ten-year 75th percentile of 10.9 times, and at 2.4 times FY2026 consensus book value.
    • Of the 67% twelve-month return, about 43 points was the multiple moving from about 6.9 times to 9.9 times and about 17 points was estimate revision.
    • The consensus target of PLN 117.82 is now at the price, and the buy share has fallen 13 points in three months to 40%.
  • What we think it earns and returns.
    • Earnings per share of PLN 8.55 reported for FY2025 rising to about PLN 9.0 (FY2026), PLN 10.5 (FY2027), PLN 11.6 (FY2028) and PLN 12.2 (FY2029)
    • A dividend yield of about 5% a year at a 65% payout
    • And a multiple drifting from 10.3 times our 24-month-forward earnings to 9.5 times at eighteen months and 9.0 times at three years as the one-off steps pass.
    • That gives a value including cash returns of PLN 123.7 at eighteen months (4.2% a year) and PLN 142.6 at three years (7.0% a year), an F against the framework's anchors.
    • Holding the multiple where it is gives only about 11% a year over three years
    • The plausibly worse drop-through (the full 56 basis points of margin compression, cost of risk at 60 basis points, payout 60%) gives about 4.4% a year.
  • The pattern it fits is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', and it fits loosely.
    • Quality, named concern, clearing evidence and few concerns all pass
    • The oversold condition fails outright, because the entry would be after the clearing rather than before or during it.
    • The secondary 'Compounder' describes what is left: a proven beater of its own guidance with one named mis-modelled line (9% loan growth modelled against 13.5% delivered) whose earnings effect is halved by the margin.

  • The chain holds through the outputs and breaks at the price.

    • Adequate forces, adequate outputs, and a return that fails the bar because the re-rating has been earned by whoever bought a year ago.
    • The case is therefore a fair hold with a 5% yield, not a 20% a year case
    • It becomes one below about PLN 90, where the 24-month-forward multiple would be about 7.9 times our path.

  • Rating = D, because the expected change is not material enough at this price: 'Change Forces' (C) and 'Financial Outputs' (C) describe a strong franchise growing its operating profit at a mid-single-digit rate, lifted for two years by a tax step-down and the end of a legal charge, and the price already pays for that lift, so the 'Modal return' (F) is about 7% a year against a 20% bar.

    • The parts combine as a chain in which the last link breaks: adequate forces, adequate outputs, insufficient return.
    • Not C, because a C needs a modal return in the framework's 13% to 17% band, which requires the multiple to hold about 10.9 times at the three-year horizon, a branch we rate possible, not probable
    • Not F, because the franchise and the upgrade record are not those of a bottom-quartile case, and at about PLN 90 the same path would clear the bar.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against our view is the bull case in its strongest form.
    • PKO is the best bank in a fast-growing, underpenetrated economy that is the main beneficiary of EUR 137 billion of EU funds.
    • Loans grow 12% to 13% a year rather than 9%, because consumer credit is up 21.5%, corporate 15%, and the chief executive calls 2026 the strongest year in the bank's history.
    • The clean margin rose 4 basis points in Q2 2026 while three-month WIBOR fell 174 basis points year on year, so the hedging works and the margin holds at 4.3%, not 4.1%.
    • Costs fall year on year
      • The cost of risk is 29 to 31 basis points with unemployment at 3.1%
      • The CHF charge ends in 2026 and the tax rate falls seven points by 2028.

    • The sell-side's 9% loan growth, 56 basis points of compression and 60% payout are three conservative assumptions stacked, so FY2028 earnings per share are PLN 13 or more rather than PLN 11.8, and a bank compounding earnings at 15% a year on a 19% return on equity deserves the eleven to twelve times the market pays for such banks

      • At PLN 13 of FY2028 earnings that is PLN 150 or more, the Morgan Stanley bull case of PLN 151.

    • What we concede.

      • The operating momentum is real and the beats are consistent.
      • The 2.4 times book multiple is not high for a 19% return on equity.
      • Consensus has been too low on volume for a year.
      • If the margin holds at 4.3% rather than 4.1%, FY2028 earnings per share are about PLN 12.3 on our arithmetic (each 10 basis points of margin on about PLN 330 billion of loans is about PLN 330 million of net interest income, about PLN 0.19 of earnings per share after a 26% tax), and at 10.9 times that is a three-year return of about 15% a year, a C.
    • Why we hold our view.
      • The bull case needs three things at once: the margin holding, the tax step-down arriving and the multiple rising.
      • Each we rate plausible or possible, and their joint odds are low.
      • The re-rating leg has already been taken, the multiple having moved from 6.9 times to 9.9 times in a year.
      • The FY2027 step is tax and provisions, the kind of growth the framework says investors do not extrapolate.
      • And the environment has this week turned against bank multiples through the sovereign-bank loop, with Poland's own sovereign under a Moody's downgrade.
    • Where the bull case would win.
      • If the operating engine can grow at 10% a year rather than 6%, which needs the margin to stop falling while loans grow 9%.
      • That is the thing to watch, and it is why the recommendation is a park with a price trigger rather than an invalidation.
Change Forces: are the input change forces sufficiently strong? (E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the forces acting on PKO are many but they pull in both directions and none is new: the loan-growth tailwind (customer financing +13.4% year on year to PLN 328.5 billion at June 2026) and the end of the CHF overhang are real and measurable, but they are met by a rate-driven margin headwind the sell-side already models at 56 basis points through 2028 and by a fiscal and regulatory line that moves against the bank more often than for it.
  • Internal change: the chief executive has been in post since June 2024, the strategy was set in October 2024, and guidance has been reaffirmed, not raised, so the internal forces are mature and single-dimensional.
  • Not B, because no internal force is new or multi-dimensional and the external forces net to roughly neutral for earnings over three years.
  • The forces are strong enough to keep the franchise growing
    • They are not strong enough to produce a change the market has not already seen.
External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the external forces are strong but opposed: demand (loan growth 13.5%, EU funds, GDP +4% in Q2 2026) and the clearing of the CHF overhang are tailwinds with two-plus years of visibility, while the policy-rate path (the NBP at 3.75%, a consensus terminal rate of 3.25% by 3Q27) and the fiscal line (corporate income tax at 30% in 2026, a mooted reserve tax) are headwinds of similar size.
  • Net direction: roughly neutral for earnings over three years, positive for volumes, negative for margin.
Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because end-market demand is rising cyclically with at least two years of visibility: Poland's real GDP grew 4% in Q2 2026, PKO's economists project total investment +10% in 2026 against +4.4% in 2025, and the EUR 137 billion of EU cohesion and NextGenEU funds is named by the sell-side as the stimulus behind double-digit corporate loan expansion.
  • Loan growth: customer financing +13.4% year on year to PLN 328.5 billion at June 2026
    • Gross customer loans +13.5% year on year and +3.8% quarter on quarter to PLN 315.05 billion at Q2 2026.
  • Segments: consumer loans +21.5% year on year and PLN mortgages +13.9% year on year at Q2 2026
    • Corporate financing +15% year on year.
  • Structural room: loans are 32% of GDP against nearly 80% in the euro area
    • Mortgages 13% of GDP against 36%.
  • Against: the consumer-loan surge is described in the research as heavily driven by temporary, non-recurring legacy refinancing programmes
    • The 2% Safe Mortgage subsidy has expired
    • And management itself guides to mid-single-digit loan growth in 2026 across segments, below what it is delivering.
  • Direction: tailwind.
    • Not A, because the demand force is cyclical and partly subsidy-driven, and the sell-side's 9% a year for 2027 to 2028 is the sober path.
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = D
  • Rating = D, because the balance of regulatory change moves cost against the bank: the corporate income tax rate for banks rose to 30% in 2026 from 19% in 2025, scheduled to fall to 26% in 2027 and 23% in 2028, with the sell-side noting a distinct risk that the planned reductions may not materialise
    • A tax on mandatory reserves is proposed (worst case 5% to 6% of 2026 net income, about PLN 350 million on a market-share approach)
    • And the autumn 2027 election keeps credit-holiday talk alive.
  • Reliefs: the CJEU ruling of 12 February 2026 (Case C-471/24) that national courts cannot examine WIBOR methodology removes a systemic litigation threat
    • The KNF cut the long-term funding ratio target to 20% from 40% in July 2026
    • The WIBOR phase-out is postponed to 2036.
  • History: credit holidays cost PLN 3,111 million in a single quarter of 2022 and PLN 166 million to PLN 212 million in 2024 (two figures in the research).
  • Direction: headwind.
    • Not C, because the tax step-up is already in 2026 earnings and the reserve tax is live
    • Not F, because the WIBOR ruling and the funding-ratio cut are real reliefs.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the macro force that acts on PKO more than on the universe is the Polish policy rate, and its direction is now uncertain rather than down: the NBP cut to 3.75% in March 2026 and paused, and headline CPI rose to 4.0% in September 2026.
  • Rate views: one house expects caution on further cuts and another's economists expect 50 basis points of hikes in 1Q27, while Morgan Stanley models a terminal 3.25% by 3Q27 and PKO's own economists 3.0% by end-2027.
  • Sensitivity: management guides a 15 basis point margin decline per 100 basis point cut and a margin above 4% even at a 3% rate.
  • Direction: headwind for margin, tailwind for credit demand
    • Net a mild headwind.

  • Rating = C, because the company's own cycle is at a high plateau, not turning up: total revenue on the company's basis was PLN 7,616 million in Q2 2026, 1% ahead of company consensus
    • Net interest income was PLN 6,075 million, down 1.3% year on year but up 2.0% quarter on quarter, with the margin at 4.31%, down 61 basis points year on year and stable quarter on quarter.

Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is stable rather than easing: deposit pricing is disciplined under system excess liquidity (sector loan-to-deposit ratio 62%, PKO's 66.2% at Q2 2026), but loan pricing is competitive (Santander Bank Polska cut its nominal mortgage rate from 15% to 9.5% in early 2026) and Erste's entry via a 49% stake in Santander Bank Polska is intensifying corporate lending competition.
  • Market structure: the top five banks hold about 60% of assets
    • PKO 24% of loans, Pekao 16%, ING Bank Śląski 14%, mBank 11%.
  • Wealth distribution: ING Bank Śląski's acquisition of Goldman Sachs TFI directly challenges PKO's dominance in mutual fund distribution.
  • Direction: neutral.
    • Not B, because no competitor is retreating.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption force is being disproved for PKO rather than strengthening: BLIK transactions in the IKO app exceeded 460.2 million by 31 March 2026, PKO Pay Later reached 374 thousand customers with PLN 522 million of limits, machine learning underpins over 80% of consumer loan decisions, and over 30% of new customers are acquired digitally, double the prior year.
  • Against: Allegro Pay finances over 15% of platform GMV in-house
    • An expert call warns the Allegro partnership could train PKO's future disruptor
    • Revolut has a strong Polish presence.
  • Direction: mild tailwind.
    • Not A, because the Allegro dependence is a two-edged force within 24 to 36 months.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the bank's inputs, deposits and wholesale funding, are abundant and cheap: deposits +10.5% year on year to PLN 475.8 billion at Q2 2026, current accounts 74.9% of deposits, LCR 267%, NSFR 161%, a EUR 500 million covered bond priced at mid-swaps plus 38 basis points on a EUR 2.2 billion book, and EUR 700 million of senior non-preferred debt at 3.75% rated Baa1.
  • Direction: tailwind.
    • Not A, because IT supply is a local duopoly (Asseco, Comarch) and wage inflation of 5.8% in 2026 is the cost input that is rising.

[Robustness of] Foundations upon which the Investment Thesis is built upon (BB 2) Rating = B

The 'Foundations' make the expected change plausible and probable, and they make the thesis's weakness sharper rather than softer: Poland is an underpenetrated, consolidating, concentrated banking market in which the largest deposit franchise sets prices, and PKO is that franchise, so the operating earnings we expect are the earnings this business should produce; what the 'Foundations' cannot do is make the earnings grow faster than the margin allows or make the owner behave like a minority shareholder, and those two things, not the business, are where the thesis is fragile.

  • The value chain supports the thesis on every side but one.
    • Demand is strong and broadening (household lending +12.2% year on year across the sector in mid-2026; PKO's consumer loans +21.5%, PLN mortgages +13.9%, corporate financing +15%), funding is abundant and cheap (deposits 73% of liabilities, current accounts 74.9% of deposits, a retail deposit beta of about 13%), and the structure is concentrated and consolidating (the top five banks hold about 60% of assets; 38 banks have become 30 since 2014).

    • That is why the value-chain Element is a B with a D inside it.
  • The business is strong enough on every dimension but ownership.
    • PKO is the scale leader with a PLN 608 billion balance sheet against the next bank's PLN 368 billion, a funding moat that shows in the 13% retail deposit beta and a 4.31% net interest margin, a cost-to-income ratio of 28.7% in Q2 2026 that is in the bottom decile of European banking, a return on equity of 18% to 19%, and a credit record of 30 to 40 basis points of cost of risk.
    • The advantages are real, demonstrated and widening on the digital side, and they compound by mechanism: the largest deposit base gives the lowest funding cost, the largest data set the best underwriting, the largest app the lowest acquisition cost.
  • Management is good, and the owner decides who manages.
    • The team sets the right direction, has the operating resources, and has beaten its own guidance in every period since 2023 (by +10.04%, +3.38%, +6.82% and +5.80%).
    • But six of eight management board members were dismissed in February 2024, a deputy chief executive was dismissed in March 2026, executive pay is capped by statute at fifteen times the average enterprise salary, executive share ownership is nil, and the research itself rates execution capability at medium confidence.
    • The operator is a B
    • The governance that surrounds it is imposed, not chosen.

  • What the 'Foundations' therefore tell the thesis.

    • The operating path in Building Block 1 (pre-provision profit +5% to 6% a year, cost of risk normalising, the CHF charge ending) is well founded: a bank with these advantages in this market should deliver it.
    • The 'Foundations' also confirm the two things that cap the case, because they are structural: the margin is set by one country's policy rate, and the earnings leakage to the state (a 30% tax, a non-deductible levy, the possibility of a new holiday or tax) is set by one owner.
    • Neither can be managed away, which is why 'Foundations' B does not lift 'Investment Thesis' D.

  • Rating = B, because the value chain and the industry (B) and the business (B) combine as a strong business in a supportive structure with one owner-driven fragility: scale, digital lock-in and cost structure are durable advantages that generate a return on equity of 18% to 19%, and the fragility is the State Treasury's control and the political and fiscal line.

    • Not A, because that fragility is structural and recurs (six of eight management board members dismissed in 2024; four chief-executive changes in 2015-2023), and the business's growth is tied to one country's policy rate.
  • Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.
    • The strongest case against a B is that the 'Foundations' are not robust at all because the owner is the foundation: PKO is a state-controlled bank in a country whose government raised the sector's tax rate to 30% in one budget, legislated credit holidays twice, proposes a reserve tax, purged the board when the government changed, and faces an election in autumn 2027

The mechanism tying the 'Investment Thesis' and the 'Foundations' to the pattern is that a dominant deposit franchise earning 18% to 19% on equity had a transient charge of PLN 1 billion to PLN 4 billion a year laid over its reported earnings; as the charge ends, reported earnings rise to meet the franchise's earning power and the market re-reads the stock from a legal risk to a growth franchise.     • And the concerns are two, not more (B).

    • This entry would be after it.

  • PKO Bank Polski is a cause-for-concern-clearing case that has already cleared.
  • It is a high-quality domestic bank, Poland's largest with a quarter of the loan market, a cost-to-income ratio near 30% and a return on equity of 18% to 19%, whose reported earnings were masked for six years by Swiss franc mortgage provisions (PLN 24.3 billion since 2019, PLN 4.4 billion in 2025 alone) and which is now reporting almost clean (PLN 297 million in Q2 2026, coverage 188%, over 90% of contracts settled or in court).

  • It would be compelling below about PLN 90, where the same path clears 20% a year.
  • What we are betting on, in one line: that a bank earning 18% on equity with a 5% yield, growing operating profit 5% to 6% a year and reported earnings 10% to 11% a year for three years on a tax and provision step, and trading at 10 times earnings two years out, is fully priced for all of it.
