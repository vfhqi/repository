Despite Chief Executive Steven van Rijswijk leading the organization since July 2020 and serving internally since 1995, management has not articulated a concrete target to reduce this structural dependency or explained why such extreme retail concentration optimizes shareholder value relative to more diversified business models.

The concentration poses material earnings volatility risks during retail credit cycles or net interest margin compression events, yet public disclosures and strategic roadmaps remain silent on diversification timeframes or quantitative rebalancing thresholds. The group reports an average customer lifetime exceeding 13 years while simultaneously disclosing that mobile primary customers–representing only 39% of the base–generate double the product holdings of transaction-only accounts.

This creates a statistically unusual dispersion where the majority 61% of customers maintain extraordinarily long relationships but contribute disproportionately low product penetration and revenue intensity. Despite achieving positive operating leverage in 2Q2026 and serving 41 million customers with 39% classified as mobile primary, management has not disclosed conversion velocity targets, cohort-specific retention economics, or explained why conversion rates remain suppressed after decades of digital-first positioning.

The structural product gap between mobile primary and non-primary cohorts suggests either significant friction in the digital migration journey or fundamental product-market fit challenges that have persisted unexplained across multiple strategic planning cycles. Workload on the proprietary ING Private Cloud exceeded 70% in 2025, up from 63% in 2023, enabling near-zero downtime for global database security patching, yet the group simultaneously acknowledges structural operational reliance on US-based technology infrastructure and cloud software while only exploring–not executing–Dutch and European alternative suppliers.

This configuration represents a top-5% geopolitical concentration outlier among systemically important European banks, where peers have aggressively diversified critical infrastructure sovereignty. Despite CEO van Rijswijk's tenure since July 2020, neither annual reports nor investor presentations quantify the percentage of core banking workloads dependent on transatlantic providers, disclose concrete timelines for infrastructure regionalization, or explain the strategic rationale for accepting persistent single-jurisdiction technology risk in an era of escalating digital sovereignty regulation and cross-border data flow uncertainty.
