C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

F3031st of 32

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: competitive intensity is rising on both flanks, and the next six to twelve months are the most dangerous window of the last three years. Varian has started a new hardware cycle in the West exactly as Elekta's Evo cycle begins, and United Imaging has become the leader in new Chinese radiotherapy sales. The offsets are real but smaller: Evo has closed Elekta's adaptive-therapy gap for the first time in a decade, the third player (Accuray) is weakening, and Varian competes on premium and ecosystem rather than price cuts in the West.

  • Varian (Siemens Healthineers), the West: Accela launched at ASTRO in late September 2026, with a 2.5 revolutions-per-minute gantry (against about 1 for TrueBeam) and NeoArc arc therapy that can complete multi-site treatments in about sixty seconds; Jefferies calls it "a new hardware innovation cycle". DNB Carnegie judges the online adaptive capability unresolved and the price premium high, so Evo remains a viable lower-cost alternative. Varian grew 7.3 percent in the September 2026 quarter; Siemens Healthineers signed more than EUR 400m of partnership deals in that quarter, including a USD 150m-plus US academic partnership bundling imaging and radiotherapy, the form the "shark attack" takes in this industry. Varian holds 55 to 62 percent of the global market and has gained about ten points of share over the last several years.
  • United Imaging, China: radiotherapy share from under 10 percent five years ago to over 30 percent by 2025; its own annual report records an 18.1 percentage point gain in 2025 to rank first in new domestic sales value; its hospital procurement value rose 102 percent in September 2026. Its imaging systems are priced 15 to 35 percent below multinationals, its R&D intensity is above 20 percent of revenue, and it targets 50 percent overseas sales by 2030, which makes Elekta's emerging-market strongholds (TIMEA, APJ) a medium-term front.
  • New low-cost hardware: Leo Cancer Care and Intelliray push standard hardware toward commodity pricing.
  • Offsets: over 25 percent of Evo's US orders are competitive flips; Accuray's product revenue fell 26 percent in its latest quarter, freeing share; Elekta has itself stopped being the industry's discounter (floor prices; walking away from low-margin tenders), which lowers price intensity from its own side.
  • Last six months against the prior 12 to 18: worse. In the prior period the threat was Varian's ecosystem and an Elekta product gap; now it is a new Varian platform, an explicit bundling push and a Chinese leader in new sales.

For the model: assume flat global value share at best in the base case; do not assume Evo flips continue at the Q1 rate through the Accela launch; assume China value share erodes slightly even as Chinese volume recovers.

C-07

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

F3529th of 32

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: the macro force bites hardest at the company level through currency, and at the market level through the discount rate. Currency is the largest unguided transmission into Elekta's earnings, and it is moving against it again as the krona strengthens; the discount rate (US ten-year near 5 percent) caps how far a slow-growth medtech multiple can re-rate.

  • Company level (currency): reporting in SEK, about 2 percent of revenue in SEK, costs in GBP, EUR and SEK, revenue in USD (Americas 29 to 30 percent; US 21 to 23 percent), EUR and CNY (China 12 to 17 percent depending on source). Translation took 8 percent off FY25/26 net sales (+1 percent constant currency became -7.2 percent reported); Q1 FY26/27 had a SEK 33m translation hit to sales and 20 basis points to gross margin. Sensitivities: 1 percent move in SEK = about SEK 32m of net profit (about 2 percent of EPS); 10 percent USD depreciation = about SEK 300m of operating income before hedging (about 13 percent of EBIT); 10 percent GBP depreciation = about SEK 250m benefit; 5 percent FX = about 7.5 percent of EPS (DNB Carnegie). Hedging covers 50 to 100 percent of forecast flows on a rolling 24-month forward programme, so a lasting move reaches the P&L with a lag of up to two years. The Riksbank sits at 2.00 percent with further rises signalled, and Swedish exporters' reported results are understating operations as the krona strengthens (Geography Digest, 10 November 2026): a headwind into FY27/28 as hedges roll.
  • Sector level (hospital capital budgets): high rates raise hospital borrowing costs; US non-profit hospitals delay purchases and extend equipment life; the effect of 340B and reimbursement changes on capital budgets arrives in 2028-2029.
  • Industry and market level (discount rate and risk appetite): the Fitness for Fighting digest reads risk-off beneath an index at its 52-week high, with Fragility Double Underweight and Momentum Underweight; Healthcare and Medtech capital goods hold relative strength A (the market is buying defensives). Elekta, a mid-cap with SEK 23.9bn of market value, sits on the fragile side of that tilt.
  • China macro: a stimulus programme (RMB 300bn mentioned) is an upside; anti-corruption audits persist in hospital channels.

For the model: run currency as an explicit line (translation on revenue; transaction on gross margin with a 12-to-24-month hedge lag), not as noise.

C-01

To what extent is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically, and with how much visibility? (A cyclical swing counts when it has two-plus years of visibility.)

D6017th of 32

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: end-market demand is structurally healthy (mid-to-high single-digit growth) and has turned cyclically upward in the last six months in China, while US and European hospital capital budgets have tightened at the margin. End-market demand is not Elekta's constraint; its share of that demand is. The only cyclical swing that meets the two-year-visibility test is China's procurement recovery, and its effect on Elekta's sales arrives mainly in FY27/28 and FY28/29.

  • Structural (unchanged, positive): the global radiotherapy market of about USD 7 to 7.5bn grows 5 to 8 percent a year (to USD 8 to 11bn by 2030); cancer incidence rises from 20m cases (2022) to 35m (2050); radiotherapy is needed in over half of cancer cases yet takes under 10 percent of oncology budgets; emerging markets have about 2 linear accelerators per million people against 13 in the United States, and China about 2 against a WHO guide of 4. Emerging-market hardware is projected to grow 8 to 10 percent a year, mature markets 2 to 4.
  • China (cyclical, sharply better in the last six months): the anti-corruption campaign from July 2023 cut the Chinese radiotherapy market by 40 to 50 percent over two years; industry volumes fell from about 300 units (2021) to about 200 (2025) and are expected at about 240 in 2026. Hospital equipment procurement rose 20 percent year on year in August 2026 and 60 percent in September (third quarter +33 percent; year to date turned to +3 percent from -4 percent). Every province must run at least one round of centralised bidding for Class B equipment, which includes standard linacs, by the end of 2026. Elekta's China orders have grown for three consecutive quarters while its China sales fell 16 percent in Q1 FY26/27: the textbook signature of a volume recovery in the order book that has not yet reached revenue.
  • United States (structural replacement need, cyclically constrained budgets): more than 60 percent of the US installed base is older than eight years and the average linac is about 14 years old, so replacement is due. But hospital finances are under pressure (Medicare cuts, the 2026 coding overhaul, the proposed 2027 cut to 340B drug payments, long-term rates near 5 percent): hospitals add linacs only at tertiary sites, extend equipment life, and procurement of systems above USD 1m takes up to twelve months. Customers paused orders in early 2026 awaiting Evo's FDA clearance (January 2026); that pause has ended, and US solutions orders grew at a double-digit rate in Q1 FY26/27.
  • Europe: stable, demographically planned renewals at mid-single digits; Germany postponing large procurements; the EU4Health budget cut 35 percent. Elekta's Europe grew 4 to 5 percent in constant currency in Q1 FY26/27.
  • Middle East and Africa: installation delays from regional conflict (TIMEA -4 percent in Q1).
  • Visibility: high on the level of revenue, low on its timing. Orders convert in 12 months for standard linacs and 18 to 24 months for Gamma Knife and Unity; the backlog of about SEK 30bn-plus after the SEK 2.2bn cancellation (about two years of sales; Morgan Stanley put 82 percent of the pre-cancellation SEK 37bn as convertible within three years) and a service base of about 44 percent of sales give a floor. What is not visible is the quarter in which installations, especially in emerging markets, land.

For the model: treat market growth of about 5 to 6 percent as the ceiling for Elekta's organic growth at constant share, China volume recovery as an order-led input lagged 12 to 24 months, and US growth as tied to Evo share gains rather than to market growth.

C-03

To what extent is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved, and in which direction does it act on the company?

D5023rd of 32

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: the disruption forces are now roughly neutral for Elekta in the near term and modestly negative over the long term. The two that Elekta was losing (CT-guided online adaptive therapy and cloud software) it now competes in; the one it bet heavily on (MR-guided therapy) is being disproved as a mainstream product; and the slow, structural one (AI commoditising treatment planning and hardware) erodes the software lock-in on which both duopolists rely.

  • Online adaptive therapy, CT-guided (strengthening; now positive): Varian led with Ethos (2019; over 110 orders, 15,000 adaptive sessions) and HyperSight (2022); Elekta had no CT-guided adaptive linac until Evo (CE mark September 2024, FDA January 2026, India 2026). Over 120 abstracts at ESTRO 2026 on adaptive therapy. Elekta extended adaptive workflows to Harmony in September 2026; Prostate Guideway awaits FDA clearance, and the US adaptive workflow on Evo was not cleared with the hardware.
  • MR-guided therapy (being disproved as mainstream; negative for past capital allocation): Unity costs USD 7 to 10m, has no premium reimbursement code and low throughput; ViewRay's bankruptcy is the precedent; Unity took three years to reach 100 orders against 18 months for Ethos. Management has de-emphasised it and stopped disclosing Unity volumes. The disproof costs Elekta past returns (part of the SEK 1.4bn write-offs and a 6 percent reported return on capital) more than future revenue.
  • AI auto-contouring and vendor-neutral planning (strengthening; negative): contouring "has evolved into a commercial commodity"; TheraPanacea leads in Europe, Radformation in the US, RaySearch has about 13 percent of planning. This makes hardware more interchangeable and devalues a closed ecosystem. Elekta has turned part of it into channel (MIM, TheraPanacea, RaySearch distribution, Brainlab).
  • Cloud and SaaS (strengthening; positive): Elekta ONE planning cuts planning time by about 80 percent; SaaS reached 23 to 25 percent of oncology information system orders; conversions carry price uplifts of over 50 percent (up to 80 percent) with zero attrition so far.
  • Hypofractionation (strengthening; mixed): fewer, larger doses raise value per treatment (stereotactic body radiotherapy pays up to 3.2 times per fraction) but raise capacity per machine; favours fast, imaging-rich systems, where Accela's speed is the threat.
  • Radioligand and immunotherapy: complements over our horizon, not substitutes.
C-04

To what extent is political or regulatory change under way, and how does it bear on the company?

D5219th of 32

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: a lot is moving, and the net effect over the next six months is mildly positive (US tariff refunds, US reimbursement status quo preserved, China procurement unfreezing under rules Elekta's Beijing plant can meet), with two low-probability, high-impact tails: a Section 232 tariff on UK-made linacs and Chinese retaliation against European bidders.

  • US trade: the Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA duties in February 2026; Elekta booked a SEK 53m refund in Q1 FY26/27 (150 basis points of gross margin in the quarter), has a further claim in progress, and DNB Carnegie expects about 50 basis points on the full-year EBIT margin. US tariff exposure is a recurring gross-margin item (tariffs and currency took 230 basis points off Q3 FY25/26 gross margin).
  • US payment: CMS postponed the mandatory Radiation Oncology Alternative Payment Model indefinitely (removing a purchasing-delay risk); ASTRO's ROCR Act proposal (support from over 122 institutions) would favour hypofractionated care; the 2026 coding overhaul and the proposed 2027 340B cut weigh on hospital capital budgets with a lag into 2028-2029.
  • US clearance: Evo hardware cleared January 2026; the online adaptive workflow and Prostate Guideway await clearance, which delays the full US value of the platform.
  • China: centralised procurement splits equipment into Class A (systems above RMB 50m, national tenders) and Class B (standard linacs, provincial tenders), which increases price pressure but Jefferies describes pressure on the top class as "relatively modest" and the policy as emphasising quality; a 20 percent price preference for locally made equipment, which Elekta's 90 percent-localised Beijing production should meet; the 15th Five-Year Plan requires local research and domestic software integration (the AnSheng software joint venture answers part of this); data-security law forces software localisation. The EU's first use of its International Procurement Instrument prompted Chinese restrictions on European medical-device bids above about USD 6.3m; whether Elekta's locally made systems are caught is not established in the files and should be treated as a tail risk.
  • Europe: the EU4Health cut (EUR 1bn, 35 percent) and its proposed removal from the 2028-2034 budget weigh on public cancer infrastructure; the proposed European Public Procurement Act (at least 30 percent of tender scoring on quality, sustainability and security; a 50 percent European content threshold in strategic sectors) would favour European vendors against low-cost Chinese bids.
