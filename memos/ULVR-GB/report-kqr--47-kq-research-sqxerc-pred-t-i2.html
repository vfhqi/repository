Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Unilever's profound business separations and regional reorganizations will fundamentally alter its financial trajectory, resetting the earnings baseline while driving higher structural margins.

The Key Question

1.
The Key Question: "Unilever PLC (ULVR-GB), a global home and personal care company reporting Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care and Home Care, plus a Foods business being combined with McCormick through a Reverse Morris Trust (completion guided for mid-2027 at the latest); Ice Cream was demerged in December 2025 as The Magnum Ice Cream Company (19.9% retained); the company moves to six regional P&L units from 1-Jan- 2027. Key question: how comprehensively are these dynamics articulated in the current narrative of sell-side commentary and of investor commentary: the treatment of Foods in published FY2027 and FY2028 EPS and how large the post-Foods rebase will be; the EPS effect of using the Foods proceeds mainly for separation taxes and debt reduction rather than buybacks, and whether a share consolidation offsets it; the multiple on the post-Foods business (for example Deutsche Bank's published 18.4x FY2027 for the continuing business) against the headline P/E on status-quo consensus; the 2027 trade-off between price and volume and the developedmarket private-label risk; the size of currency translation drag; whether the 19.1% pro forma margin includes or excludes stranded costs; minority leakage at Hindustan Unilever; the US Wellbeing decline and its recovery timetable; the risks of the regional re-organisation; the spare balance-sheet capacity after completion and its likely use; the Brazil indirect tax contingent liability (€3,557m in the FY2025 accounts)?"
2.
Profound change: Unilever initiated its massive portfolio reshape by demerging its Ice Cream business as a standalone entity in December 2025. This spin-off created The Magnum Ice Cream Company, with Unilever retaining a 19.9% shareholding to maintain partial exposure to the category. The transaction removed lowgrowth and capital-intensive operations from the consolidated group to focus resources on core categories. This separation represents a major step in simplification but introduces near-term margin friction due to transitional service agreements.
3.
Strategic separation: Unilever followed the ice cream demerger by agreeing to combine its Foods business with McCormick in March 2026. The transaction is structured as a tax-free Reverse Morris Trust, which is guided to complete by mid-2027 at the latest. While the transaction unlocks a massive cash inflow of $15.7 bn, it will materially lower Unilever's pro forma earnings baseline once finalized. Investors are likely to remain wary of the timeline and costs of completion.

Section 2 – Foods Deal Mechanics and the Post-Separation EPS Rebase

Section summary
  1. Consensus models are slowly updating for the Foods separation, but several prominent firms remain anchored on the status-quo perimeter, creating a massive disconnect in forward earnings projections.
  2. The post-Foods earnings rebase will result in a substantial structural step-down in Unilever's EPS, though it will leave behind a highly focused, faster-growing beauty and personal care portfolio.
  3. McCormick continues to project significant strategic benefits and cost synergies from the transaction, contrasting with the near-term dilution and execution anxiety felt by Unilever shareholders.

RMT Structure and Valuation

1.
Structured details: Unilever announced a definitive agreement in March 2026 to combine its global Foods division with McCormick & Company. The Reverse Morris Trust transaction values Unilever's Foods division at an enterprise value of $44.8 bn. This valuation represents a trailing multiple of 13.8x EBITDA, which aligns with modern pure-play reshuffling trends across the staples sector. Unilever shareholders will retain a major economic stake by owning 55.1% of the newly combined food enterprise.
2.
Cash generation: Unilever will receive $15.7 bn in cash proceeds alongside a 65% economic interest in the combined entity. The cash proceeds are subject to typical closing adjustments and will be delivered upon completion, guided for mid-2027. Management intends to use this cash for debt paydown and separation costs rather than an immediate retail buyback expansion. This structure aims to offset substantial one-off separation liabilities and tax costs associated with the RMT format.

Section 3 – Capital Allocation Impact, Separation Proceeds, and Share Consolidation

Section summary
  1. Unilever's allocation of €11 bn in Foods proceeds toward taxes and debt paydown prioritizes balance sheet strength over immediate share buybacks.
  2. Sell-side analysts expect a share consolidation to be executed on or around completion to offset the severe pro forma EPS dilution.
  3. Historical precedents across consumer staples reveal that deleveraging and structural simplification are essential prerequisites for long-term valuation rerating.

Unilever Cash Proceeds and Debt Targets

1.
Proceeds allocation: Unilever outlines that its multi-billion cash proceeds from the Foods transaction will be strictly partitioned to manage balance sheet risks. The company reports in its November 2026 presentation that approximately €5 bn of the total €14 bn inflow is earmarked for taxes and separation costs. A further €6 bn is designated for debt paydown, with the remainder reserved for additional debt reduction or share buybacks. This structured plan ensures Unilever reaches a leverage ratio of 1.8x net debt to EBITDA.
2.
Broker perspectives: Bernstein Research highlights that the deployment of the cash proceeds is aimed at maintaining structural financial discipline. The broker notes in March 2026 that Unilever intends to reduce pro forma leverage to 2.0x while executing a €6 bn share buyback program. This buyback is scheduled to run from 2026 to 2029 to provide multi-year support to the capital structure. However, the broker cautions that the combined Foods entity will be leveraged at nearly 4.0x EBITDA post-transaction, which could pressure its valuation.
3.
Share consolidation: Unilever has indicated that it will execute a share consolidation to prevent technical pressure on its stock price post-transaction. The corporate presentation from November 2026 confirms that a share consolidation is scheduled to take place on or around the closing of the Foods transaction. This capital action is designed to stabilize the absolute share price as the asset base shrinks.

Share Consolidation and Dilution Mitigation

4.
Dilution offset: Rothschild & Co Redburn analyzes that a share consolidation is the primary mechanism available to mitigate massive EPS dilution. The broker reports in March 2026 that the divestment of the Foods business is expected to be 32% dilutive to pro forma EPS, or 21% excluding stranded costs. To address this gap, Unilever is expected to use a share consolidation similar to the mechanism deployed for its ice cream demerger.
5.
Precedent consolidation: Jefferies Research notes that Unilever has historically utilized share consolidations to manage capital transitions during large-scale separations. The broker reported in December 2025 that the group would implement a share consolidation during the Ice Cream spin-off to maintain its share price stability. This strategy demonstrates management's preferred route to protect the headline share value during structural perimeters changes.

Post-Separation Leverage and Spare Balance Sheet Capacity

6.
Balance sheet capacity: Unilever intends to preserve its balance sheet strength post-deal rather than pursuing highly aggressive immediate share buybacks. The company's corporate filing in November 2026 confirms that there will be no refinancing requirements until 2028, which creates a stable operational window. This conservative liquidity management maximizes spare balance sheet capacity post-completion.
7.
Broker evaluation: Deutsche Bank Research suggests that a focused capital allocation strategy will yield significant long-term strategic advantages. The broker notes in March 2026 that a complete separation will allow both the core and Foods portfolios to allocate capital to core priorities independently. However, they warn that the standalone Foods business must overcome replicated central costs, which could leave the remaining continuing business with stranded cost challenges.
8.
Acquisition strategy: Haleon PLC provides a comparable case of how post-separation capital allocation is scrutinized by institutional investors. Jefferies Research reported in November 2026 that Haleon's decision to launch a £500 mm share buyback was received poorly by investors. The market viewed this buyback as a setback to Haleon's strategic intent to consolidate its fragmented category through selective bolt-on acquisitions.
9.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that using transaction proceeds for share buybacks rather than debt reduction is highly dilutive and fails to create long-term shareholder value. DNB Carnegie notes in October 2026 that while Essity's plans to divest its Consumer Tissue business are structurally positive, the immediate impact of losing 20% of group earnings is highly dilutive. Investors will strictly require evidence of an attractive use of the transaction proceeds before re-rating the remaining business.
