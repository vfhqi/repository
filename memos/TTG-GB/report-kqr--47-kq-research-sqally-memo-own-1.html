Section 382 Regulatory Mechanics

1.
Regulatory threshold mechanics: According to the Morgan Stanley manual of 23 June 2023, Section 382 limits net operating losses if a company undergoes a 50% ownership change over a rolling three-year period. This annual limit is calculated by multiplying the equity value before the change by a long-term tax-exempt rate. This means TT Electronics faces potential usage restrictions if shareholder turnover crosses this threshold. Consequently, these limitations could reduce the net present value of its unrecognised tax assets.
2.
Income offset restrictions: Under the Morgan Stanley manual of 23 June 2023, net operating losses generated in 2021 or after have an indefinite carryforward but are limited to offsetting 80% of taxable income in any single year. This federal restriction operates independently of any ownership change and applies to all US corporate entities. Therefore, even without triggering a Section 382 change, TT Electronics cannot fully wipe out its taxable income in a single period. This rule means the continuing group's cash tax liabilities will remain elevated even during profitable turnaround years.
3.
Built-in gain exemptions: The Morgan Stanley manual of 23 June 2023 indicates that a company with a net unrealized built-in gain can temporarily increase its annual Section 382 loss limitation. If TT Electronics can establish that its US assets hold built-in gains, it might mitigate the immediate impact of an ownership change. This exemption provides some flexibility, unless the company lacks qualifying built-in gains at the change date. Therefore, tax planning around asset values could partially shelter the group's US income from immediate tax hikes.
4.
Comparable peer limitations: Sidoti Research reports on 16 September 2026 that Eastman Kodak Company holds approximately $1.9 billion of US net operating loss carryforwards to shelter income. However, the broker notes that any ownership change would trigger Section 382 limitations, raising Kodak's cash tax and impairing its valuation shield. Like Kodak, TT Electronics has a substantial pool of unrecognised tax losses that is vulnerable to shareholder ownership shifts.
