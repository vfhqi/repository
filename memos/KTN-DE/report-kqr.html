Section 3 – Outsourcing Dynamics in Defence and Medical Sectors

Section summary
  1. Highly regulated European OEMs are accelerating the structural transition toward regional EMS outsourcing, driven by strict localization requirements and rising geopolitical compliance burdens
  2. Defence contracts offer structurally superior margin profiles and working capital advantages compared to medical lines, though long qualification cycles of up to ten years create high initial barriers

Regulatory tailwinds and regional origin criteria

1.
Sustained outsourcing runway: Nordea Commissioned Research on 21 January 2026 reported that only ~40% of electronics in Europe are currently produced by contract manufacturers compared to a 50% global average, indicating significant room for structural outsourcing growth.
2.
Accelerating defense demand: Peter Nilsson, CEO of Kitron, stated in a 25 August 2026 press release that secure and regulated electronics manufacturing represents a strategic bottleneck in the European defence investment supercycle, prompting OEMs to expand secure local supply chains.
3.
Expanding defense relationships: A Cicor press release on 16 September 2026 announced major program wins worth up to EUR 30 million, demonstrating that the group now serves 16 of Europe's top 20 aerospace and defence contractors.
4.
Sovereign hardware expansion: In its half-year 2026 report, Kontron disclosed that its Aerospace and Defense division recorded 31.8% revenue growth to EUR 83.6 million, proving that localized NATOcompliant production is expanding rapidly.
5.
Increasing security mandates: In its half-year 2026 report, Kontron highlighted that upcoming Cyber Resilience Act and NIS2 regulations are fueling demand for secure platforms, allowing specialized EMS players to embed proprietary security software architectures.

Contract economics and medical versus defence margin profiles

6.
Value-based pricing power: Deutsche Bank Research on 7 August 2026 observed that defence projects carry higher, value-based profit margins because procurement prioritizes security over cost.
7.
Favorable cash conversion: Deutsche Bank Research on 7 August 2026 noted that defence contracts are front-end loaded with advanced milestone payments, which structurally improves working capital profiles and reduces free cash flow drags.
8.
Prolonged qualification cycles: Emkay Research on 16 July 2026 noted that testing and qualification periods for defence and industrial sectors typically last two to ten years, which creates exceptionally sticky client relationships.
9.
Persistent margin strength: A Cicor investor presentation on 22 July 2026 showed that its Aerospace and Defence segment revenue achieved a 45% compound annual growth rate since 2023, illustrating how defense outsourcing drives sustained margin expansion.
10.
Stable medical demand: In a 13 December 2022 Capital Markets Day webcast, Kitron management explained that medical device manufacturing is driven by recurring public sector investments, making the segment highly resilient and non-cyclical.

Sourcing preferences and compliance barriers for smaller players

11.
Burdensome compliance costs: A Jefferies note on 5 June 2026 estimated that complex "European preference" rules and environmental compliance frameworks carry a hefty administrative business impact of EUR 17 to 26 billion annually.
12.
Unfavorable scale dynamics: An expert regulatory consultant on 22 January 2026 observed that smaller medtech players face unpredictable, lengthy notified body approval timelines under European MDR, while larger OEMs exert greater pressure.
13.
National sourcing barriers: An expert procurement manager from Zollner Elektronik AG argued on 28 August 2026 that local sourcing preferences restrict international contract expansion for EMS providers unless they maintain domestic manufacturing facilities.
14.
Market consolidation pressure: Nordea Commissioned Research on 21 January 2026 noted that the fragmented European EMS market of 1,900 players is consolidating around larger full-service providers as OEMs seek complete value chain partners.
15.
Other side of the story: : Sceptical market analysis from DNB Carnegie on 10 July 2026 warned that large global EMS providers are prioritising high-volume artificial intelligence data center capacity, which could restrict production allocation for lower-volume regional defense customers.

  2. While the legacy corporate collaboration market faces structural declines, expanding software-led architectures in control rooms and medical quality assurance are structurally elevating the company's margin profile

Expanding recurring backlog: Barco's 10 February 2026 annual report disclosed that the total contract value of recurring Cinema-as-a-Service contracts signed in 2025 accumulated to €89 million, securing highly predictable long-term revenue.
5.
Extending hardware lifespans: Barco's sustainability statement published on 30 March 2026 revealed that the expected lifetime of its cinema projectors now averages 12 years, highlighting extended hardware cycles for budget-constrained exhibitors.
6.
Skeptical consensus views: Sceptical divisional analysis from Barco's 14 July 2026 interim report cautioned that cinema sales in China remain severely pressured by weak local box office trends, limiting nearterm recovery momentum.

Contrasting healthcare visualization and corporate collaboration

7.
Declining collaborative hardware: Barco's 14 July 2026 interim report noted that the corporate meeting room market is experiencing a double-digit decline in agnostic BYOD sell-out, representing a structural headwind for legacy ClickShare devices.
8.
Ramping integrated room systems: To counter BYOD softness, a Barco press release on 17 September 2026 highlighted the certification of ClickShare Hub Pro with Huddly and Shure to enter the Microsoft Teams Room segment, unlocking a vast unpenetrated workplace market.
9.
Improving control room mix: Barco's investor presentation on 15 July 2026 confirmed that software sales exceeded 40% of Control Rooms revenue via the Barco CTRL platform, driving divisional gross margin expansion to 52.9%
10.
Sustained medical visualization cycles: A Barco press release on 10 February 2026 highlighted that Diagnostic Imaging sales grew, supported by digital pathology and software-enabled compliance platforms that surpassed 100,000 connected displays.

Softening surgical demand: In its 14 July 2026 interim report, Barco reported that its Healthcare division EBITDA margin plummeted to 2.9% because the expiration of major long-term surgical contracts created massive operating deleverage.
12.
Value-engineered surgical response: To restore Healthcare profitability, Barco's 10 February 2026 press release announced the centralization of its modality business in Suzhou, China, to leverage local costengineering and counter intense regional pricing competition.

Non-consensus display technologies and lifecycles

topic-specific reports Late-stage niche dominance: In its 14 July 2026 interim report, Barco disclosed that it is capitalizing on late- stage replacement demand for rear projection video walls as the sole remaining global supplier, capturing high-margin niche control room revenues.
