Sandvik Peak Margins and Multiple Derating

7.
Favorable mining cycle: Sandvik achieved a record quarterly operating margin of 16.2% in the second quarter of physical year 2012 on high capacity utilization. This performance marked the peak of the mining super-cycle, driven by exceptionally strong equipment demand in North America. However, the forward valuation multiple began a sharp de-rating from its peak percentile as European manufacturing indicators weakened. This multiple contraction preceded the actual decline in operating margins by approximately two quarters.
8.
Margin contraction and bottoming: Sandvik's mining segment EBIT margin fell from 18.3% in the middle of 2012 to 14.2% by Q2 2013. The company's overall share price troughed approximately ten months after the volume roll-over in its machining business. This share-price low occurred after the first reported earnings decline, which was fully digested by the market. The trough multiple sat significantly below Sandvik's tenyear average as mining capital expenditure forecasts were slashed.
9.
Footprint consolidation milestones: Sandvik's share price bottomed only after management announced aggressive operational restructuring and capacity adjustments. In its business update call on 28 November 2012, the company announced structural cost-saving measures targeting one billion SEK and the reduction of fifteen hundred positions. Investors rewarded these self-help measures, which offset declining cyclical volumes.
10.
Short-cycle industrial lag: Sandvik experienced another margin peak in early 2022 before inflation and central bank interest rate hikes slowed global manufacturing. This pattern demonstrates that equity valuations often price in the bottom-line contraction long before it materializes in reported financial statements.

This structural gap means that Kennametal's forward P/E multiple is much more sensitive to raw material cost spikes.
12.
Cash generation during downturns: Historical precedents show that both Kennametal and Sandvik aggressively released working capital as earnings normalized. Jefferies reports that Sandvik's valuation fell to historic lows during its earnings contraction while its FCF conversion improved. This cash generation cushions the share-price fall and limits multiple contraction. Consequently, a company's ability to swiftly unwind working capital is highly scrutinized.
14.
Sum-of-the-parts discount: Covering brokers often transition from simple price-to-earnings multiples to sum-of- the-parts valuations as cyclical earnings decline. Morgan Stanley upgraded Sandvik in September 2013 by applying a sum-of-the-parts model to capture the resilience of its machining business relative to mining. This valuation methodology applied a 12x EV/EBIT multiple to Machining Solutions, while the more volatile Mining division was valued at 8x.
