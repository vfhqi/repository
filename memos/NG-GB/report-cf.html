National Grid presents a compelling regulated infrastructure investment case anchored by the appointment of CEO Zoë Yujnovich in November 2025, whose extensive large-scale capital execution experience at Shell directly addresses the company's transition to a £70 billion five-year capital framework targeting 8% to 10% underlying EPS CAGR through FY31. The leadership succession strengthens project execution capabilities through Yujnovich's proven track record managing capital budgets comparable to National Grid's investment plans, while comprehensive board committee restructuring in November 2025 consolidates governance over risk and capital allocation.

The company's strategic pivot to pure-play regulated electricity networks is complete following the disposal of National Grid Renewables for £1.53 billion and Grain LNG for £1.66 billion, eliminating non-core earnings volatility and focusing management bandwidth entirely on transmission and distribution asset compounding.

The change materiality is exceptionally high as National Grid enters a multi-generational infrastructure build cycle driven by explosive data center demand, industrial electrification, and Net Zero mandates. UK data center connection requests have reached 10GW in the active queue, with the RIIO-T3 business plan anticipating up to 19GW of new demand connections, representing a fourfold acceleration versus historical connection rates. The RIIO-T3 Final Determination acceptance in March 2026 upgraded the five-year financial plan to at least £70 billion, targeting around 10% annual asset growth through March 2031. This capital reallocation is buttressed by constructive US regulatory settlements including the New York PSC approval of a three-year Niagara Mohawk rate plan allowing a 9.5% return on equity, providing secure growth visibility across both UK and US jurisdictions. - Earnings Calls The top five strategic drivers of forward EPS trajectory are precisely defined with quantified impacts.

First, UK Transmission regulated asset base growth targeting c.10% asset CAGR under the newly accepted RIIO-T3 framework represents the primary earnings engine, with UK Electricity Transmission EBIT growing 18% to £1,682 million in FY26 on RAV expansion. Second, connection queue velocity addressing the 19GW demand pipeline creates substantial upside optionality as hyperscale data centers demand 200MW individual connections exceeding the load of entire medium-sized cities.

Third, US rate base expansions allocating £29 billion over five years across New York and New England networks provide diversified growth, though partially offset by Federal Energy Regulatory Commission cuts to New England base ROEs by 100 bps to 9.57%. Fourth, portfolio divestment impacts create near-term EBIT dilution of approximately £220 million but enhance strategic focus and balance sheet flexibility. Fifth, financing cost escalation projected at £200 million higher in FY27 represents the primary headwind, though mitigated by fixed interest rates covering 80% to 91% of gross debt.

National Grid's recent delivery track record demonstrates solid financial execution offset by mixed operational performance. However, US Regulated EBIT of £2.57 billion missed consensus by 3% due to FERC order impacts and storm costs, and the lost time injury frequency rate rose to 0.11, missing the internal target of 0.10.

The company's cash conversion remained robust with FFO to adjusted net debt reaching 13% in FY26, providing significant headroom above the 10% rating threshold. Management maintained disciplined capital allocation by completing £2.8 billion in asset divestments to fund the investment ramp-up while preserving regulatory gearing at 61%.

Forward credibility is rated high based on transparent multi-year frameworks and proven execution discipline. The group has established a consistent track record of delivering solid, in-line financial results, with FY25 underlying EPS of 73.3p successfully delivered within the guided 6% to 8% CAGR range. The upgraded 8% to 10% EPS CAGR through FY31 is underpinned by secured regulatory approvals covering three-quarters of the capital plan, with management de-risking delivery timelines by securing three-quarters of the global supply chain.

However, sensitivity analysis reveals material macroeconomic exposures, including a 10% dollar movement impacting post-tax income by £64 million and reducing fast-money ratios from 15% to 2% lowering EPS by approximately 10%. The company qualifies as an honest-tracker archetype that de-risks forward assumptions before upgrading targets, enhancing the reliability of medium-term guidance.
