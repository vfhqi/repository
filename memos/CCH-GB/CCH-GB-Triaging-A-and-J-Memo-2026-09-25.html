[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Coca-Cola HBC is a top-decile bottler using an under-geared balance sheet to make a step-change in its perimeter, and at 50.67 EUR the shares pay a fair return for that, not a compelling one. The modal path returns about 12% a year over eighteen months and about 14% a year over three years against the 20% bar; the case clears the bar only if the multiple re-rates once the CCBA acquisition is delivered and the two largest emerging-market currencies stay calm at the same time, a joint outcome we put at about 35%. Below about 45.6 EUR (about 39.2 GBP) the modal path itself clears the bar, so the company is better than its return at today's price.

  • The change is internal, broad and real, and it is the largest a bottler can make short of a merger.
    • The company is buying 75% of Coca-Cola Beverages Africa for US$2.6bn at about 10x EV to 2024 EBITDA, adding 14 markets and 493 million people and making it the second-largest Coca-Cola bottler
    • four of six antitrust clearances are secured and completion is on track for H2 2026.
    • The framework's screen of internal forces is passed on its first limb: new geographies and standards improvement are A, the acquisition, the strategy step, the guidance raise and the balance sheet are B, and none of the screen's red flags fits.
    • The balance sheet is being used, not talked about: 0.7x net debt to comparable EBITDA at end-2025 rising to about 1.6x on completion, with EUR 2.1bn of bonds issued in March 2026 and the bridge cancelled.
    • What is absent is a chief-executive change
      • the chief executive has led the group since December 2017, and his record is the foundation the case rests on rather than a change force.
  • The external forces are a net tailwind for now, but the largest of them is the pause of a structural headwind, not its removal.
    • Demand, competition and disruption are moving the company's way
    • the emerging-market currencies that have cost it most (translation drag averaged 3.8% of sales a year over 2019 to 2024) have turned to a small tailwind, with translation guidance moved from a EUR 0m to 30m headwind to a EUR 0m to 10m tailwind.
    • Egypt's urban inflation re-accelerated to 14.9% in July 2026, and 64% of revenue is earned in emerging-market currencies against inputs priced off the dollar
      • that is the exposed point of the whole case.
  • The change has travelled through the company's resources, choices and actions into its key operating metrics and its H1 2026 financial statements; only the last layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, the sell-side's estimates, has moved little.
    • H1 2026 organic revenue grew 9.6%, organic EBIT 15.2% and comparable EPS 15.2% to 1.51 EUR, with growth now volume-led (volume +7.5%, the thirteenth consecutive quarter of growth) rather than price-led, and guidance was raised.
    • Estimates have moved up only 1% to 3% since the results, because the full-year guidance implies a second half of just 1% to 3% organic EBIT growth and the sell-side reads that as the company's habit of conservatism
      • consensus FY2026 EBIT already sits at the top of guidance.
  • Our modal earnings path compounds at 10.6% a year, from revenue and margin rather than tax or buybacks, and that sits just below the framework's 12% to 20% band.
    • Comparable EPS of 2.96 EUR in FY2026, 3.30 EUR in FY2027 and 3.68 EUR in FY2028 from FY2025's 2.72 EUR, against consensus medians of 2.92, 3.21 and 3.52 EUR.
    • The legacy business gives about 9% a year (revenue about 6.5%, margin about 2.6%, a little taken below EBIT)
      • the acquisition adds about 2.5% a year net of dilution, minorities and interest, which is what management guides as low-single-digit accretion.
  • What it costs: 14.27x 24-month-forward earnings, near the middle of the stock's own ten-year range and above the middle of its five-year range, after a 16% fall from the 52-week high on no estimate cut.
    • The multiple sits at the 68th percentile of its five-year weekly series (median 13.13x) and between the ten-year 'conservative' (14.10x) and 'prudent' (15.60x) bands
    • on a one-year-forward footing the stock is about 15.8x consensus FY2027 EPS of 3.21 EUR.
    • The price already assumes a compounder growing about 10% a year with a 2.5% yield, the acquisition at the guided accretion and no more, and calm currencies this year.
      • It does not pay for a CCBA turnaround at the Egyptian rate, and it does not price a devaluation.
  • At that multiple, held, the return is a fair 12% to 14% a year, and the skew at today's price is against the case.
    • The cash return gives about 1.7% a year, earnings growth about 10 points a year, and the re-rating nothing at the 14.3x we hold, which is the blend of a re-rated branch at 15.6x (35%), a held branch (45%) and a de-rated branch at 12.2x (20%).
    • The upside branch adds about 6 points a year to the eighteen-month return
      • the downside branch, a 20% devaluation with the de-rating that would follow, takes 10 to 20 points and turns the eighteen-month return negative.
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', with 'Compounder' as a secondary fit, and the fit is good rather than crisp.
    • The mechanism is a management that turned one difficult emerging-market franchise (Egypt: value share +800 basis points, coolers tripled, gross margin +350 basis points since 2022) applying the same playbook to fourteen more markets
    • what keeps the fit from crisp is that the largest input has not closed and the acquired outputs are a year away.
  • The chain holds through the outputs and breaks at the return.
    • It would hold at the return on either of two things the case turns on: the deal's first full year delivering without a margin or cash surprise (probable, about 65%) and the naira and the Egyptian pound holding within a band local pricing can absorb (plausible, about 55%)
    • the re-rating needs both.

  • The case therefore is a clean, well-understood case for a fair return: park it, with an entry level.

    • At about 45.6 EUR over eighteen months (about 39.2 GBP), 10% below today, the modal path alone returns 20% a year.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the business and already visible in the outputs, but at 50.67 EUR the 'Modal return' is about 12% a year over eighteen months and about 14% a year over three years, below the 20% bar, and the modal earnings-per-share path of 10.5% to 11% a year sits just below the 12% to 20% target band

    • the return, not the change, sets the rating.
    • Not B, because a B needs a modal return of about 18% to 24% a year on the framework's anchors and the B case is the re-rating branch, a 35% outcome on our odds.
    • Not D, because the earnings path is of high quality (revenue and margin, not tax or buybacks), upgrades are more likely than downgrades over the next twelve months, and the three-year modal return of about 14% sits inside the framework's C band.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against: the market has priced this company correctly.
    • It is a 10% compounder, not a 15% one, and at 14x to 17x forward earnings it earns its holders a fair 12% to 14% a year and no more.
    • The second-half deceleration is demand, not habit: unit-cost guidance was raised to low-to-mid single digits, Q4 has four fewer selling days, and Developing-segment margins are already down 70 basis points under marketing spend.
    • The deal raises risk faster than return: a 7.4% EBITDA-margin asset, fourteen new currencies, 25% minorities, a 5.47% share issue, capital intensity toward 8% of sales and the buyback gone
      • low-single-digit first-year accretion is the whole of what the company will commit to.
    • The transactional currency mismatch (64% of revenue in emerging-market currencies against dollar-priced concentrate and packaging, with USD revenue of 1.2%) is the highest among soft-drink peers, and Egypt's macro is deteriorating.
    • The multiple has just shown, by falling 16% in seven weeks on no estimate change, that it can go down as easily as up
      • the sell-side's bear cases are 2,200p at 12.4x (Morgan Stanley) and 3,000p (Jefferies).
    • Every number in this hypothesis is real and in the evidence.
  • What we concede.
    • The modal return at today's price does not clear the bar
    • the second-half debate cannot be settled until the November 2026 update
    • the deal's return target is undisclosed
    • the currency exposure is structural and outside management's control
    • the multiple risk is live
    • and the skew at this price is against us on the branches.
  • Why we still hold our view, which is neutral with an entry level rather than bearish: the competing hypothesis proves the return is fair, not that the company is impaired.
    • The record it must explain away is thirteen quarters of volume growth, three years of initial guidance beaten by 6 to 15 points, share gains for six years, a comparable EBIT margin at a record 11.7%, return on invested capital up 830 basis points in five years to 19.4%, and free cash flow that covered the dividend nearly twice while capital expenditure rose 36%.
    • The deal was struck at 10x EBITDA with an option structure and a demonstrated precedent, and the sell-side's outer-year revenue lines have not yet been extended for it, so the market is not paying for it.
    • The entry level is arithmetic, not hope: at about 45.6 EUR the modal path alone returns 20% a year over eighteen months.
      • We make no claim that an asset value covers the enterprise value
      • the bounded-downside argument is the multiple's own floor of 12.0x, 16% below today.

  • Rating = B, because the step-up is strategically sensible and disciplined but not yet integrated: 75% of CCBA for US$2.6bn (US$1.6bn in cash and about US$1.0bn in new CCH shares, giving the seller, Gutsche Family Investments, 5.47% of the enlarged company), valuing 100% of CCBA at US$3.5bn of equity and about 10x EV to 2024 EBITDA, with an option on the remaining 25% between three and five years after completion.
