EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

LU-VE S.p.A. has upgraded its medium-term Industrial Plan targets to low-double-digit organic growth and 15-17% adjusted EBITDA margins through 2029, representing a material strategic acceleration from the previous highsingle-digit volume and 14-15% margin framework. This upward revision reflects management's projection that a major US hyperscaler framework agreement will deliver over EUR 100 million during the first two years, fundamentally transforming the company's data center cooling segment into a high-visibility growth engine.

The strategic pivot is underpinned by the expansion of the Jacksonville facility by 20,000 square meters to exceed 30,000 square meters, removing physical capacity constraints that previously limited North American scaling potential.

The five primary drivers of forward EPS trajectory rank as follows. First, the hyperscaler contract delivers estimated 15% revenue CAGR with high plausibility, given that deliveries commenced in May 2026 targeting EUR 20 million incremental second-half sales. Second, the Jacksonville plant expansion targets 200 additional employees within 18-24 months, providing approximately 20% capacity boost with high plausibility as construction was completed on schedule. Third, power generation remains the fastest growing business, strengthening total order backlogs in 2026, driving approximately 10% segment growth with high confidence backed by the record EUR 333.3 million backlog. Fourth, heat pump applications are expected to accelerate under the 2026-2029 plan leveraging green-tech first-mover status, contributing approximately 5% volume recovery with medium plausibility contingent on European climate subsidy stability. Fifth, indexed pricing formulas completely neutralize metal price increases through corresponding revenue adjustments, providing 100% margin insulation with high confidence as demonstrated by EUR 5.1 million in pricing benefits offsetting severe raw material inflation.

H1 2026 earnings delivery exceeded operational expectations across multiple dimensions. Product sales grew 10.5% in the first half of 2026, driven by robust organic volumes of 8.7%, while adjusted EBITDA margins reached 15.3% benefiting from operational leverage and automation, representing a 70 basis point improvement year-over-year. Geographic diversification accelerated dramatically, with the US market growing 92.1% year-onyear in H1 2026, validating the Texas capacity expansion thesis.

Segment performance was highly mixed, as air conditioning surged 41.9% while commercial refrigeration grew only 1.8% on delayed retail projects. Balance sheet strength improved substantially, with net financial debt to EBITDA leverage improving sharply to 0.7x in the first half of 2026, though net working capital on sales reached 18.6% representing EUR 118 million of tied-up cash, exceeding the mid-teens efficiency target due to seasonal inventory accumulation.

Forward guidance credibility is assessed as moderately high, supported by a conservative sandbagger management archetype. Historically, since 2018, LU-VE delivered a 15% revenue CAGR, outperforming its stated historical guidance, demonstrating a consistent pattern of under-promising and over-delivering on long-term objectives. However, near-term targets assume commercial refrigeration recovery, which remains delayed by retail caution, introducing execution uncertainty in the short-cycle portion of the portfolio.

The base-case scenario yields EPS of EUR 1.95 in FY26E rising to EUR 2.90 by FY28E, while the bull-case generates EPS of EUR 2.10 in FY26E rising to EUR 3.50 in FY28E under accelerated hyperscaler call-offs, and the bear-case constrains EPS to EUR 1.70 in FY26E stagnating at EUR 2.10 by FY28E if facility hiring delays emerge. Sell-side positioning exhibits unanimous bullish conviction but suffers from narrow coverage breadth.

TPICAP Midcap represents the primary active coverage pool with Alessio Olmi serving as lead research analyst, while the target price migrated from €50 to €70 in May 2026 following the hyperscaler contract announcement. LU- VE trades at 12.0x 2027E EV/EBITDA versus a peer multiple of 15-22x, representing a substantial valuation discount that brokers attribute to limited institutional awareness rather than fundamental shortcomings.

The bull narrative centers on the €100 million contract securing massive medium-term visibility, while the bear narrative highlights backlog predominantly reflecting short-cycle orders above 90% which inherently restricts long-term visibility. Peer-relative positioning places LU-VE in the mid-quartile for growth momentum but lagging on margin performance against best-in-class competitors. LU-VE secured 8.7% organic volume growth during the first half of 2026, outpacing Systemair's 6% organic growth rate but trailing Carel Industries' 23% organic revenue acceleration. Profitability metrics show LU-VE delivered a 15.3% adjusted EBITDA margin in H1-26, exceeding several regional cooling competitors but substantially below Carel's superior 22.5% H1 EBITDA margin. Working capital efficiency remains a relative weakness, as LU-VE's net working capital reached 18.6% of LTM sales totaling EUR 118 million, slightly below Nibe's 23.6% of sales but reflecting deliberate seasonal inventory build-ups.

Activist presence is structurally precluded by concentrated family control. Finami S.p.A. holds 44.3% of shares but commands 61.4% of total voting rights, while G4 S.r.l. controls 12.5% of share capital and holds 17.3% of voting power, ensuring that no active activist presence exists in the AS library for LU-VE S.p.A.. This governance structure provides strategic continuity but introduces minority shareholder governance risks if family interests diverge from public market priorities. Three material risks warrant continuous monitoring. First, global supply of energy-efficient EC electronic motors is highly concentrated in only two specialized global manufacturers, and any component procurement failures could prevent timely customer deliveries generating severe operational penalties, representing an unmitigated single point of failure cross-referenced in Section 3. Second, the primary execution threat remains finding skilled US workers to staff expanded Texas capacity, making Jacksonville hiring highly critical to achieving upgraded growth targets, detailed in Section 10. Third, a prolonged cyclical downturn in commercial refrigeration markets would strand excess manufacturing capacity, rapidly unwinding operating leverage advantages relative to pure-play HVAC competitors, as analyzed in Section 7. The compound probability of the joint bull-case scenario is estimated at 64%, assuming independent probabilities of 80% for US execution and power cooling, while acknowledging that realizing the hyperscaler contract is structurally dependent on completing Jacksonville plant staffing.
