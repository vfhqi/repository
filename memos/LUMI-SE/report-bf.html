EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Business model and strategic positioning

1.
Lundin Mining successfully transformed into a high-growth Latin American copper pure-play after divesting European zinc and US nickel assets, concentrating 85% of revenue exposure on copper and positioning the company to become a top-ten global copper producer targeting over 500,000 tonnes annual output. The flagship Candelaria Copper Mining Complex in Atacama, Chile represents the historical production core, while Caserones, Chapada, and the gigantic Vicuña joint venture with BHP provide decades of tier-one base metal growth. The company is advancing the high-altitude Vicuña joint venture with BHP to unlock multi-generational base metal volumes, modeled as a top-five global copper, gold, and silver mine.
2.
The company operates under a contractual sales structure with multi-year agreements to strategic Asian and European smelters, though customer concentration spiked severely in Q1 2026 with the largest customer representing 51% of total sales, creating elevated counterparty risk. Rising market prices unlocked $268.80 million in positive provisional adjustments in FY2025, demonstrating significant revenue leverage to commodity price movements. Strategic leadership under CEO Jack Lundin since December 2023 has executed aggressive portfolio streamlining and launched company-wide Full Potential operational excellence programs delivering measurable unit cost savings and throughput gains.

Financial profile and peer benchmarking

3.
Lundin Mining delivered record FY2025 revenue of $4.05 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $1.92 billion, representing a 46% EBITDA margin that significantly outperforms Freeport-McMoRan's 35% and Teck Resources' estimated 38%. Revenue grew at a calculated 3-year CAGR of 10.04% from FY2022 to FY2025, exceeding Freeport-McMoRan's 4.40% and Vale's estimated 2%, driven by copper price strength and strategic M&A. Return on capital employed reached 14.70% in FY2025, outperforming several large diversified miners, while adjusted return on equity stood at 12.50%, tracking in line with mid-cycle peer averages.
4.
The company achieved a net cash position of $249.40 million by March 2026 after fully repaying its $1.15 billion term loan, transitioning to zero financial leverage while upsizing its revolving credit facility to $4.50 billion to fund Vicuña construction. Capital expenditure reached $669 million for FY2025, with sustaining capital at $477.80 million and expansionary spending of $191.20 million focused on Saúva and Vicuña development, reflecting a capital intensity of 16.51% that remains competitive versus Teck Resources' elevated 25%. Group C1 cash costs averaged $1.87 per pound in FY2025, positioning Lundin favorably against First Quantum's elevated $2.51 per pound.
5.
However, headline free cash flow in FY2025 was significantly impacted by a massive $414 million working capital build-up, illustrating severe liquidity strain from shipment delays and concentrate inventory accumulation risks.

Value chain position and margin distribution

6.
Lundin Mining operates exclusively at the upstream mining and milling stage, capturing the highest value-chain margin accrual of 40-60% gross margin and 25-45% EBIT margin, while avoiding downstream smelting and refining stages that generate only 5-15% gross margin and 2-8% EBIT margin. The company maintains high vertical integration at Candelaria through wholly-owned desalination plants and the Punta Padrones port, while strategically outsourcing Vicuña's Stage 3 offsite infrastructure to conserve upfront capital but accepting permanent opex exposure to third-party tariff inflation.
7.
Severe concentrate scarcity pushed Chinese spot treatment charges into unprecedented negative territory at minus US$55 per tonne, demonstrating that structural global copper concentrate deficits have shifted pricing leverage decisively to upstream miners. Severe concentrate deficits have shifted structural pricing leverage heavily in favor of upstream copper miners, while Chinese smelting expansion flooded the custom processing market, depressing treatment charges, allowing miners to capture expanding economic rent.

Customer and supplier dynamics

8.
The four largest customer accounts represented 16%, 16%, 14%, and 10% of group revenue in FY2025, but concentration spiked with the largest customer representing 51% of total sales in Q1 2026 following portfolio streamlining, creating extreme near-term customer concentration and elevated counterparty credit risk. Japan stands as the largest destination, generating $1.22 billion of FY2025 contracts, while smelters in China accounted for $1.14 billion and Spain contributed $679.70 million, demonstrating heavy geographic concentration in Asia.
9.
The company faces localized input cost pressures with operating cost inflation ranging from 3.50% in Chile to 31.50% in Argentina in 2025, though management successfully renegotiated contractor agreements at Caserones to lower operating costs by $18 million annually. Sulfuric acid spot prices in Chile more than doubled, representing a localized inflation risk, though Caserones' acid costs exceeded $300 per tonne, supply contracts remain secure, mitigating immediate supply disruption risk. Lundin secured 100% renewable power contracts across all operating assets, insulating operations from fossil fuel price volatility.

Competitive landscape and consolidation

10.
The global copper mining market remains highly fragmented relative to iron ore, with no dominant players controlling consolidated scale, while regional production exhibits higher concentration with Peru's top four mines contributing 58.70% of national output. Extreme scarcity of high-grade copper deposits in stable jurisdictions has made corporate M&A the default growth strategy, with global copper mine mergers reaching record-high transaction volumes during 2025, including the announced Anglo American and Teck Resources merger, driving aggressive corporate consolidation at record premiums.
11.
Lundin Mining exhibits competitive cost curve positioning with C1 cash costs expected to increase to $1.90-2.10 per pound in 2026, comparing favorably to First Quantum's $2.51 per pound but trailing Ero Copper's exceptional $0.30 per pound. Competitors are pursuing technological innovation including autonomous drilling fleets, ultra-fine flotation, and advanced CuproChlor-T chloride-based leaching technology to process previously untapped primary sulfide ores, though leaching hypogene sulfides can incur elevated C1 processing costs. Downstream smelters are aggressively securing mining equity to guarantee concentrate feed amid structural deficits, creating potential vertical integration threats.

Operator quality and execution track record

12.
The refreshed leadership team under CEO Jack Lundin demonstrates exceptional execution capabilities, rapidly streamlining the asset portfolio through accretive divestments of Neves-Corvo and Zinkgruvan for $1.37 billion and Eagle for equity in Talon Metals, transitioning into a pure-play copper giant. Lundin Mining has met its consolidated production targets for three consecutive years, with Q1 2026 operations tracking firmly in line with reaffirmed annual production and cost guidance, demonstrating consistent operational delivery.
13.
The company-wide Full Potential program delivered measurable unit savings, dropping general operating costs from $11.80 to $10.60 per tonne, while operational enhancements at Caserones expanded active copper cathode production capacity by 37% and boosted average truck speeds by 19%. Management exhibited prudent capital allocation discipline, fully repaying its $1.15 billion term loan to achieve a net cash position while upsizing the revolving credit facility to $4.50 billion, providing ample liquidity for Vicuña construction without jeopardizing financial flexibility.
14.
Continuing operations generated a record adjusted EBITDA of $1.92 billion during FY2025, delivering a strong 46% adjusted EBITDA margin and group C1 cash costs averaging a highly competitive $1.87 per pound, while return on capital employed reached 14.70%, outperforming key base metal peers, and adjusted return on equity stood at 12.50%, demonstrating best-in-class financial outputs and attractive returns profile.

Structural growth outlook and long-term opportunities

15.
Growth in global copper demand has exceeded growth in real global GDP by more than 20% since 2011, driven by electrification, renewable power grids, and digital infrastructure, while global copper mine supply will remain tight as older operations experience steadily declining ore grades, falling from 1.60% in 1990 to roughly 0.87% in 2022, with recent acceleration downward to 0.55%, creating sustained structural copper deficits. The average lead time to bring a discovered copper deposit into production has reached 17.90 years, extending from 12.70 years in 2009 due to complex permitting and social studies, creating severe entry barriers that protect incumbent producers.
16.
Lundin targets adding 30,000 to 40,000 tonnes of annual copper via low-capex brownfield projects including Saúva Phase 1 development adding 15,000 tonnes of copper and 45,000 ounces of gold annually targeting first ore in Q1 2029, Candelaria underground expansion to approximately 22,000 tonnes per day throughput, and Caserones cathode capacity expansion to 40,000 tonnes per year. The transformational Vicuña district provides decades of tier-one base metal growth, with Stages 1-3 targeting average annual production of 400,000 tonnes of copper and 700,000 ounces of gold, while securing unprecedented regulatory and fiscal stability under Argentina's RIGI PEELP regime covering $9.70 billion in capital expenditure with a 40-year benefit period.
17.
However, the ambitious growth profile is concentrated entirely into the high-altitude Vicuña district, meaning any permitting delays, capex overruns beyond the $18 billion estimate, or Argentine fiscal instability could permanently impair the multi-billion dollar phased development timeline, stretch the upsized balance sheet, and dilute group return on capital, while sustained high copper prices could accelerate aluminum substitution and secondary scrap recycling loops.
