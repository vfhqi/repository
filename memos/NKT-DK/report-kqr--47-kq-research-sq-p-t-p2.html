Section 9 – Second and third-order implications 9 findings

Section summary
  1. Pricing power shifts will accelerate developer qualification of Asian manufacturers as grid connections face multi-year infrastructure delays
  2. Structural labor and auxiliary equipment bottlenecks risk delaying multi-billion euro transmission projects regardless of cable supply

Actor Motives and Turnkey Evolution

1.
Accelerating procurement shifts: Far East competitors have the means and motive to establish local footprints as TSOs secure multi-sourcing partners, allowing entrants to build local European manufacturing track record and execution experience
2.
Expanding market access: Sumitomo possesses the technology and shipyard capabilities to bypass historical European barriers, already winning major 525 kV European work while constructing local production facilities in the United Kingdom
3.
Rising developer optionality: Large utility customers face massive capital constraints, driving a high willingness to experiment with new suppliers on less critical high-voltage alternating-current or land- based projects
4.
Tightening local protection: Despite lower pricing models from Asian entrants, Western developers still prioritize political acceptability and strategic independence, meaning Chinese competitors face increasing barriers in securing key European public procurements

Structural Grid Bottlenecks and Outliers

5.
Escalating grid curtailment: Slower-than-expected transmission line builds are causing severe renewable energy curtailments, meaning up to 20 GW of RE projects could face connectivity delays in the near term
6.
Shifting demand centers: Power constraints on traditional grids are forcing developers to bypass offshore networks, as localized data centers are creating demand faster than utilities can deploy physical transmission infrastructure
7.
Constraining labor capacity: Turnkey execution risks are compounding because the skilled labor shortage is getting worse, particularly for high-voltage electricians, commissioning specialists, and substation construction crews
8.
Severe equipment bottlenecks: The critical path of major transmission programs is increasingly determined by long lead equipment availability, with large transformers and high-voltage switchgear now commanding lead times of up to four years
9.
Other side of the story: Other side of the story: Sceptics argue that the market's high pricing expectations ignore near-term project timeline delays, which indicate that HVDC orders face an air pocket before the next wave of grid reinforcements is approved
