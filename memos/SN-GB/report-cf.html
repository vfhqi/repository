Appendix

Outliers worth noting

Smith & Nephew's Orthopaedics inventory holding period of over 600 days drastically trails global peer medians, placing the company in the bottom ~5% of the medical technology sector for working capital efficiency. This extreme outlier directly stems from the Ortho 360 operating model's aggressive SKU rationalization program, which targets reducing gross inventory by $500 million but has simultaneously triggered a $159 million inventory obsolescence provision in 2025.

The magnitude of this inventory bloat is unprecedented among European MedTech peers, with Coloplast targeting working capital-to-sales ratios of around 24% and ConvaTec demonstrating approximately 100% equity cash conversion for FY26, highlighting the structural nature of Smith & Nephew's working capital dysfunction referenced in Section 4 and Section 7.
