  1. The evidence does not allow the Key Question to be fully resolved today. It does support a directional answer. Elekta trades near historical valuation lows as market participants await concrete evidence of a turnaround. The report's overall confidence in what the evidence shows is medium. The main limit is that research documents from Goldman Sachs, UBS, and Barclays on Elekta are not present in the captured sources. The bear case from those three brokers is therefore only partly verified. Premium periods were rarely backed by fundamentals. Elekta's elevated multiples cluster in three windows: late 2019, fiscal 2023/24 and fiscal 2024/25. The evidence therefore favors the view that premium multiples were driven by optimism and temporary tailwinds, not sustained acceleration.

There is some contrary evidence. Not every elevated multiple coincided with weak execution. Adjusted EBIT margins improved as cost-saving measures took effect even while sales growth was slow. This suggests efficiency gains can support a re-rating, though the case is thinner than the sell-side skeptics' record.

The peer group has de-rated, so Elekta's discount is partly sector-wide. Historical valuation premiums have shifted as structural market dynamics and macroeconomic shocks altered earnings visibility. External shocks such as China's anti-corruption measures caused a synchronized derating across imaging and radiotherapy peers. Peer comparisons are sensitive to the source, date and metric used, which limits precision.

Turnaround precedents show that re-rating follows delivered execution, with a lag. Its multiple peaked nearly twelve months after its restructuring plan. Demant peaked approximately eight months after its first upgraded guidance, after reversing its historical trend of guidance cuts. On this evidence, Elekta resembles an early-stage turnaround without the guidance-beat sequence that preceded the Demant re-rating.

Section 3 – Analysis of Elekta's Premium Valuation Periods

Section summary
  1. Elekta's valuation multiples historically exceeded 20 times forward earnings during periods of temporary growth acceleration and speculative expansion.
  2. Operational metrics during premium phases reveal a consistent pattern of margin deterioration, guidance cuts, and severe sell-side estimate revisions.
  3. Confidence is high that premium valuations were fundamentally unsupported, based on historical guidance downgrades and consistent consensus earnings cuts from Deutsche Bank and DNB Carnegie.
  4. Medtech turnarounds demonstrate that product-led cycles and cost cutting drive significant multiple expansion.
  5. Confidence in these turnaround precedents is high based on verified multiples and execution timelines for Philips and Demant.

Premium Valuation in Late 2019

1.
Elevated multiples: Elekta experienced a premium valuation phase during the late months of calendar year 2019. Sell-side analysts noted that the company traded at an elevated forward earnings multiple of 26x during this prepandemic period. This multiple stood well above historical averages despite ongoing competitive risks. These high multiples showed how optimistic growth expectations inflated valuations before structural shocks occurred.
2.
Strong growth: Operational metrics in the first half of fiscal year 2019/20 initially supported this premium multiple. Management reported solid net sales growth of 7% in constant currency for the first half of the year. Additionally, the company recorded an organic net sales growth of 9% in the first quarter. This strong acceleration in order momentum initially convinced investors that revenue growth would remain robust across all core regions.
3.
Falling margins: Profitability struggled to keep pace with revenue growth despite the high multiples. The company reported a weaker gross margin development due to unfavorable product mix and Unity ramp-up costs. This pressure resulted in an EBITA margin of 15.2% in the third quarter of fiscal year 2019/20. Consequently, the multiple faced correction risks as margins did not meet expectations of twenty percent.
4.
Missed guidance: Management was ultimately forced to adjust its financial targets during this premium period. In early 2019, the company reduced its full-year EBITA margin outlook from 20% to around 18%. This guidance downgrade was followed by a complete withdrawal of mid-term scenarios in early 2020 due to pandemic-related market disruptions. These successive adjustments proved that premium valuations lacked fundamental backing from consistent operational execution.

Valuation Peaks in Fiscal Year 2023/24

5.

These realized improvements initially suggested that operational momentum supported the premium multiple of over twenty times.

Analysts from Bernstein Research highlighted that a significant portion of this outperformance was short-lived. A major earnings miss in the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2023/24 immediately renewed investor concerns about execution.
