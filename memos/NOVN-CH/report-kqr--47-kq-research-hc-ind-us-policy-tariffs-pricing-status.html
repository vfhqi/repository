  2. Implementation of US drug pricing negotiations and Section 232 tariffs significantly pressures the margins of European drugmakers.
  3. While onshoring agreements and voluntary pricing pacts mitigate tariff risks through 2028, political shifts create long-term uncertainty.

Global Policy Pressures

6.
Increasing regulatory scrutiny: Lengthening approval times and complex reimbursement processes across Western Europe and the United States are creating sudden commercial headwinds for medical technology developers. Deutsche Bank analysts noted in November 2026 that more rigorous scrutiny of high-value medical products is causing customer destocking as patient care clinics face cash flow strains. This structural pressure directly moderates B2B sales growth and squeezes operating margins. Strained commercial reimbursement will ultimately dilute earnings per share unless developers can demonstrate exceptional clinical cost-effectiveness.
7.
Proposed European Union: Pending European Union pharmaceutical legislation threatens to weaken market exclusivity periods and intensify pricing pressures for innovative drugmakers. Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals reported in its fiscal 2026 annual filing that the EU Pharmaceutical Package could allow member states to disapply market protection if a product is not launched widely within three years. This stricter framework contrasts with the historically high-margin US market structure, increasing commercialization risks. Consequently, reduced regulatory data protection will likely accelerate generic erosion and diminish long-term earnings per share.
8.
Other side of the story: Despite severe political rhetoric and fears of sweeping legislative changes, historical precedents suggest that the actual operational impact of healthcare policy reforms is often far more manageable than initial market reactions imply. Redburn analysts highlighted in December 2025 that political risk is routinely over-priced, whereas real-world outcomes rarely match the most terrifying warnings. Easing policy uncertainties and steady execution by high-quality commercial compounders will likely support valuation multiple expansion, mitigating near-term drug pricing headwinds to drive resilient earnings per share growth.

Medicare Negotiation Impacts

9.
Stricter drug pricing: The US Inflation Reduction Act allows the government to directly negotiate prices for high- expenditure Medicare medications. This federal pricing oversight has already targeted ten blockbuster drugs in its initial wave, forcing list price discounts of up to 79% beginning in 2026. These steep statutory cuts threaten to depress global brand profitability and squeeze European drugmaker margins.
10.
Escalating revenue headwinds: An additional fifteen therapies will face Medicare price cuts averaging 65% beginning in January 2027. This next wave of negotiations represents a severe commercial barrier for exposed multinational developers. Consequently, these mandated list price reductions are expected to negatively alter the revenue mix for several European companies.
11.
Restrictive regulatory updates: The US Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services issued final rules in November 2026 that include subcutaneous formulations in federal price negotiations. This pricing mechanism means that manufacturers of biologics face accelerated price erosion. Consequently, the rule is poised to compress operating profits for multinational drugmakers unless they raise prices internationally.
12.
Contested historical precedents: A similar pricing rule proposed during President Trump's first term sought to tie Medicare Part B reimbursement rates to low foreign prices. That past case was heavily litigated by the pharmaceutical lobby and ultimately rescinded in August 2021 under the subsequent administration. This historical outcome suggests that aggressive federal pricing models face significant judicial barriers before they can permanently depress long-term earnings.

Tariff Implementation And Onshoring

13.
Severe import penalties: President Trump issued a Section 232 proclamation imposing a default 100% tariff on patented pharmaceutical imports. This restrictive trade policy represents a unprecedented cost risk for drugmakers exporting finished products into the highly profitable US market. However, the Department of Commerce allows manufacturers to avoid these steep penalties by committing to expand domestic US operations.
14.
Mitigated tariff exposure: The US government established preferential agreements that cap pharmaceutical tariffs at 15% for the European Union. This capped framework substantially limits the downside relative to initial worst-case fears of universal import duties. Furthermore, products imported solely for clinical trials or research are completely excluded from these duties to protect drug development pipelines.
15.
Constructive policy exemptions: Drugmakers can secure complete exemptions from Section 232 tariffs through January 2029 by executing dual agreements with the US government. These bilateral deals require companies to implement most-favored-nation pricing and invest in domestic manufacturing. Easing policy uncertainty has already encouraged twenty-six major companies to sign these pricing pacts, derisking the near-term earnings outlook.
16.
Accelerating onshoring investments: European pharmaceutical companies are shifting significant capital expenditures to the United States to secure import exemptions. This trade pressure has helped drive nearly 670 billion dollars in announced domestic manufacturing commitments. While these localization efforts will eventually support long-term margins, they will likely strain corporate free cash flows over the next three years.

Political Shifts And FDA Evolution

17.
Pronounced political pressure: The November 2026 US midterm elections resulted in a Democratic clean sweep that captured both chambers of Congress. This legislative shift places Senator Ron Wyden as the presumptive Senate Finance Committee chair. Consequently, the new Democratic majority is expected to push for expanded Medicare negotiations and challenge existing most-favored-nation contracts.
18.
Strained administrative capacity: Massive staffing cuts at the Department of Health and Human Services have reduced the Food and Drug Administration workforce by roughly 3,500 personnel. This administrative downsizing has led to far fewer advisory panels, which fell from 5.5 to 1.8 sessions per quarter. These capacity strains threaten to delay marketing approvals and disrupt product launch timelines for European developers.
19.

Furthermore, the implementation of fast-track programs continues to streamline domestic approvals for compliant manufacturers. This operational efficiency suggests that staffing cuts have not created the approval backlogs that the industry initially feared.
20.
Heightened advertising scrutiny: The Food and Drug Administration has aggressively deployed new enforcement tools to regulate direct-to-consumer pharmaceutical promotions. This regulatory push includes issuing larger volumes of warning letters and eliminating loopholes that previously allowed companies to obscure safety risks. Consequently, compliance costs will likely rise as companies are forced to restructure marketing materials across social media channels.

Tariff Authority And Legal Battles

1.
Unstable trade policies: The US Supreme Court in February 2026 invalidated executive tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, prompting the administration to pivot to Section 122 surcharges of 10% to 15%. This legal volatility, reported by Jefferies in July 2026, has shifted the trade landscape toward a more fragmented and legally constrained phase. Consequently, European exporters face prolonged supply chain policy uncertainty that could depress valuations and earnings per share unless they regionalize manufacturing.
2.
Challenged margin targets: Getinge reported a negative 104 million Swedish kronor tariff impact in the first quarter of 2026, which dragged down its adjusted EBITA margin by 140 basis points. Analysts at Bernstein Research reported in April 2026 that the company faces an estimated 380 million kronor full-year tariff headwind for fiscal year 2026. While price increases of 2% to 3% can partially offset this cost, the ongoing tariff headwind could hamper Getinge's operating leverage and make its 2028 adjusted EBITA margin target of 16% to 19% difficult to achieve.
3.
Persistent margin dilution: Elekta continues to experience significant profit margin compression due to a combined 230 basis point drag from foreign exchange and import duties. Analysts at Deutsche Bank reported in March 2026 that Elekta faces a 100 basis point headwind on its gross margin because its US hardware shipments rely on manufacturing facilities in the United Kingdom, Sweden, and the Netherlands. This trade pressure is poised to suppress Elekta's near-term earnings growth until the company can successfully pass cost increases to hospital clients.
4.
Contrary evidence: Getinge announced on June 3, 2026, that it received a massive tariff refund of 36 million dollars, which represents approximately 337 million Swedish kronor, to be recognized as a cost reduction. Analysts at DNB Carnegie reported in July 2026 that this non-operating windfall will significantly bolster reported margins and cash flows. This positive development suggests that actual tariff headwinds are far smaller than the market initially feared and will not permanently dilute long-term earnings per share.
