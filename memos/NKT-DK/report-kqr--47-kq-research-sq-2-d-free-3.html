Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. NKT's earnings trajectory from its ambitious 2028 EUR 700m+ and 2030 EUR 900m+ EBITDA targets is partially resolvable from current broker disclosures despite underlying uncertainties in tax normalization, depreciation timing, and value-chain execution, with the translation to bottom-line EPS materially dependent on the synchronized commissioning of the Karlskrona expansion, Eleonora vessel deployment, and converter-station supply-chain capacity during the critical 2027-2029 ramp-up period. NKT operates through three structural segments–Transmission delivering turnkey high-voltage submarine and onshore cable systems, Grid Solutions and Accessories combining legacy services with high-voltage onshore AC projects, and Distribution specializing in medium- and low-voltage cables supporting European grids –with the Transmission backlog of EUR 13bn providing strong multi-year earnings visibility that underpins management's strategic financial targets.

The resolvability of the earnings question hinges on how capital intensity, depreciation profiles, tax normalization, hybrid coupon treatment, metal price volatility, legal provisions, and transmission operator funding dynamics collectively shape the path from operational EBITDA to reported earnings per share over the next four fiscal years.

Covering brokers including Jefferies project significant adjusted EPS acceleration to EUR 7.58 by 2028 as the EBITDA growth translates through improved fixed cost absorption, yet this conversion faces near-term margin pressure from factory ramp-up costs and elevated depreciation charges as major expansion assets enter commercial service.

The analysis examined 10 distinct primary documents and broker research notes directly covering NKT A/S, utilizing all sources without exclusions, alongside peer disclosures from Prysmian and Nexans to anchor depreciation benchmarks, tax rate comparisons, and revenue reconciliation methodologies in the European power cable sector. The EUR 2bn capital expenditure program spanning 2025-2028 represents the largest single determinant of nearterm earnings trajectory, as the Karlskrona factory expansion commencing commercial operations in Q2-2027, the NKT Eleonora cable-laying vessel launching operationally in 2027, and the Cologne high-voltage capacity expansion becoming operational during 2027 will simultaneously increase the depreciable asset base and unlock superior operational leverage.

However, the gradual sequenced ramp-up of Karlskrona's two new extrusion lines means the assets will not achieve full financial impact until the 2029 fiscal year, creating a two-year transitional period during which depreciation charges rise faster than revenue contribution, compressing reported EBIT margins before the full earnings inflection materializes.

Margins in Transmission during 2026 already reflect a 200 basis point headwind from factory capacity ramp-up costs as NKT hires personnel ahead of the 2027 commissioning, demonstrating that the pre-commissioning investment phase creates tangible near-term margin dilution that must be weighed against the post-2028 fixed cost absorption benefits.

The tax rate trajectory presents one of the most material yet uncertain variables in the EBITDA-to-EPS conversion, as NKT experienced a low 6% tax rate in 2025 due to significant deferred tax capitalization while DNB Carnegie models a steady-state paid tax rate of 21% for the 10-year period and German tax losses carried forward increased to EUR 546m in 2025 supporting a recognized deferred tax asset of EUR 160m.

The German tax loss carryforwards benefit from having no legal expiry date under local rules, yet the capitalization of these losses remains subject to significant management estimates regarding future high-voltage profitability, introducing valuation uncertainty that could force asset write-downs if the high-voltage market slows.

Broker treatment of the EUR 150m green hybrid bond refinanced at a 5% annual coupon in March 2026 reveals meaningful methodological divergence, as DNB Carnegie deducts the annual hybrid coupon to calculate adjusted earnings metrics while Jefferies treats the EUR 150m hybrid capital as a net debt subtraction in its sum-of-theparts framework, with specific modeling parameters for Morgan Stanley, Danske Bank, and SEB remaining undisclosed in available research materials.

This EUR 7.5m annual coupon represents a material earnings adjustment that, if treated inconsistently across broker models, could explain variance in consensus EPS estimates and create artificial dispersion in the perceived earnings trajectory.

The capex-driven cash depletion is systematically reducing net financial income as a contributor to bottom-line earnings, with group cash declining from EUR 1,518m at end-2024 to EUR 1,214m at end-2025 and further to EUR 850m by mid-2026 while net financial items fell from EUR 24m in H1 2025 to EUR 12m in H1 2026 and turned into a cost of EUR 3m in Q2 2026.

This progressive erosion of interest income represents a tangible EPS headwind through 2027, yet Jefferies projects that the post-capex inflection will allow NKT to accumulate EUR 2.7bn net cash by 2030, creating substantial financial income optionality in the outer years that partially offsets the nearterm earnings dilution.

Metal price reconciliation mechanics introduce significant revenue volatility that brokers neutralize through standardized pricing assumptions, as NKT defines standard metal prices for copper at EUR 1,550/tonne and aluminum at EUR 1,350/tonne, with FY2025 reported market revenue of EUR 3,565m reconciling to EUR 2,722m at standard prices after a EUR -843m metal adjustment.

The EUR 843m annual metal price swing in reported revenues demonstrates that investors relying on headline revenue growth rates without adjusting for commodity price movements risk fundamentally misinterpreting the underlying operational trajectory, as unit economics and EBITDA margins remain insulated from these pass-through metal costs.

The regulatory and legal overhang from ongoing antitrust investigations in Slovakia, Czech Republic, and Germany represents an unquantified earnings risk, as NKT's Czech subsidiary received a first-instance decision from the Slovak Antimonopoly Office in February 2026 regarding coordinated metal surcharge practices, with a final administrative ruling expected during 2027 and a Czech decision anticipated by H1-2027 while management maintains a zero-provision stance, creating material earnings risks because adverse final rulings in 2027 could force sudden cash outflows.

This working capital volatility introduces quarterly earnings noise that obscures the underlying operational trajectory, requiring investors to focus on multi-year cumulative metrics rather than period-over-period comparisons. manufacturing reshoring offset the margin pressure from increased competition. If the key question is fundamentally unresolvable, investors risk mispricing NKT's capital efficiency because elevated post-2028 capital expenditures would permanently depress free cash flow yields, suggesting that the terminal value assumptions embedded in current valuations may prove overly optimistic if NKT cannot transition from growth capex to maintenance-level spending after the current expansion cycle.

The outlier scenario of NKT accumulating EUR 2.7bn net cash by 2030 through successful backlog conversion presents massive capital return optionality that consensus has not yet priced in, yet this optimistic outcome requires flawless execution across depreciation management, tax optimization, working capital discipline, and value-chain synchronization over a four-year period fraught with external dependencies.
