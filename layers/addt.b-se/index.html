Group judgement

External change forces

Layer 1 · External change forces

Written last by the Investment Analyst for its group. The rating above stands for it, so it carries no number of its own.

The external forces now favour Addtech's operations and disfavour its valuation. Demand is turning up cyclically with two to four quarters of visibility, on top of a structural layer (grid, defence, data centres, electrification, about a quarter to a third of revenue) whose direction is visible for years but whose timing is lumpy. Competition in product markets is benign and stable; competition for acquisitions is creeping up but has not yet moved Addtech's 6.8x entry multiple. Input inflation is a modest nominal tailwind with a one-to-two quarter lag risk. The decisive external force for the share price is the discount rate: the October 2026 turn to a rising-rate regime compresses the multiples of mature Swedish compounders as a group. For the Consumer's model, the external picture supports an organic recovery from about 2% to 3% to 5%, partly price and refill, with an uncertain hand-off to sustained end-use growth in FY2029, and modest macro sensitivity in the interest and currency lines.

C-02

To what extent, and in which direction, is competitive intensity changing?

B4779th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: competitive intensity is stable and benign in Addtech's product markets, and rising slowly in its acquisition market; the second matters more for the model because acquisitions supply about two-thirds of earnings growth.

  • Product markets: stable, benign. Thousands of niche markets, mostly family-owned local competitors, conduct margin-first; Addtech subsidiaries held and raised gross margin through eleven flat organic quarters (FactSet gross margin 26.9% FY2023 to 29.5% FY2026; company basis 33.3%), which is the clearest evidence that prices did not fall when volume fell. No price war is visible anywhere in the files.
  • Acquisition market: intensifying at the edges, not yet in Addtech's prices. New listed and private Nordic acquirers (Röko, Idun, Salix, InstallatørGruppen, Tången, Auroora) and the newly activated engines at OEM International (under EQT) and Bufab compete for the same small private targets; an expert says Nordic EBITDA multiples have moved from 4-6x to 8-10x at the larger end. Against that, private equity has eased because of higher borrowing costs, deals under EUR 50m stay largely un-intermediated, and Addtech's median entry multiple is stable at 6.8x EBITA (Lagercrantz 6.6x, Lifco 6.7x, Indutrade 8.6x). About 80% of Addtech's deals are sourced bilaterally through local networks.
  • Direction over the next 6 to 12 months: product markets unchanged; acquisition-market intensity rising modestly. Rising interest rates cut both ways: they keep private equity out (helpful) but make sellers' price expectations stickier.
  • Model implication: hold the entry multiple at about 7x EBITA in the base case and run a sensitivity at 8x to 8.5x; the research's own warning line is an average above 8x EBITA. Note that Addtech rarely discloses individual deal multiples, so drift would show first in goodwill per unit of acquired revenue and in falling acquired-margin accretion.
C-05

How are input costs, input availability and supplier concentration changing, and is the net effect a headwind or a tailwind?

B5277th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: input costs are rising again (energy-led, plus metals, plastics and freight) and availability is tightening in specific components; for Addtech the net effect is a modest tailwind to nominal revenue and roughly neutral to margin, because supplier concentration is negligible and pricing is passed through within weeks in most units and within about two quarters in project-heavy Process; the risk is a one-to-two quarter pass-through lag, as Lagercrantz has just shown.

  • Supplier concentration: largest supplier about 1% of purchasing; 49% of purchases from continental Europe, 27% Nordics; high-risk countries 2% to 3%. Subsidiary-level single-source dependencies exist (Battery Systems on global OEMs) but are small in group terms.
  • Costs and availability now: Lagercrantz's July to September 2026 margin lost about 40 basis points to a lag in passing on raw material and freight increases; Addtech's inventories rose in Q1 and Q2 FY2027 on supply disruptions (Middle East conflict) and high order intake; automation component lead times lengthened to six to eight months on AI data-centre demand.
  • Why the net is a tailwind for a value-added distributor: cost inflation, when passed through, lifts nominal sales and gross profit in absolute terms; Addtech's FactSet gross margin still rose about 110 basis points year on year in Q2 FY2027. The sector digest's rule applies: in a restock with inflation a distributor gains three ways (volume, price, mix) for 6 to 18 months, and the value-added distributor's pricing window is 24 to 36 months.
  • Watch: year-on-year gross-margin expansion has already slowed from about +220 basis points (Q4 FY2026) to about +100 to +110 basis points (Q1 and Q2 FY2027); a further slowdown to zero alongside rising input costs would signal pass-through strain.
  • Cash cost: rising inventories absorb working capital (H1 FY2027 operating cash flow flat at SEK 1,332m against EBITA up 14%).
C-07

To what extent is a macro force acting on the company, on its sector and on its industry, and at which level does it bite hardest?

B+4885th percentile

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: three macro forces act on Addtech. The one that bites hardest is the discount rate, and it bites at the sector (cohort) level through the valuation multiple rather than at the company level through earnings; currency translation bites at the company level on reported numbers; the industrial cycle is now a tailwind at all three levels.

  1. Rates and yields (the binding force). The regime turned in October 2026: a Riksbank hike expected in November 2026, markets pricing up to three European Central Bank rises by mid-2027, the US ten-year above 5%, euro high-yield 90 basis points wider since September. For Addtech's earnings the effect is small: about SEK 7.1bn to 7.7bn of variable-rate debt, a 1 percentage point rate move costs about SEK 57m after tax (about 2.6% of FY2026 net income). For the multiple the effect is large: the Swedish compounders "trade on their multiple more than their earnings", and DNB Carnegie cut its target from SEK 410 to SEK 395 on 5 October 2026 purely because the spread between the cohort's earnings yield and the ten-year euro swap rate narrowed. Addtech's 24-month-forward P/E fell from 28.6x (2 October) to about 25.5x to 26.1x (early November) with estimates roughly unchanged. The sector digest is explicit: premium compounders "give the cycle back through their multiples when yields rise", and mature engines (Addtech, Indutrade, Lagercrantz) need a re-rating while newly lit engines can outrun a de-rating.
  2. Currency (company level, reported figures). About 75% of sales are outside SEK (EUR about 36%, DKK 13%, NOK 11%, GBP 6%); translation is unhedged by policy. A 5% SEK appreciation cuts sales by SEK 810m and operating profit by SEK 98m (about 2.5% of EBITA). Translation was -4% on FY2026 sales, +1% (SEK 53m) in Q2 FY2027 as the krona weakened. The consensus forecasts collected in mid-2026 expect EUR/SEK toward 10.40 to 10.50, but the krona traded near 11.0 to 11.2 in October 2026; a Riksbank hike could restart krona strength. This adds noise of about ±2% a year to reported EBITA without changing the business.
  3. Industrial cycle (all levels, now positive). PMI above 53, German capital goods utilisation rising; construction the lagging exception.

Model implication: keep the earnings model's macro sensitivity modest (interest and FX lines), and carry the rates force mainly in the valuation work of later sets.

To what extent is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically, and with how much visibility? (A cyclical swing counts when it has two-plus years of visibility.)

Layer 1 · External change forces

Judgement: demand is turning up, but the turn is mostly cyclical with two to four quarters of visibility; only about a quarter to a third of revenue sits on structural demand with genuine multi-year visibility, and that structural part is lumpy and timing-uncertain. The near-term organic acceleration is also likely to be partly restocking and price rather than end-use, which matters for how long it lasts.

Reasoning, by demand layer:

  1. Structural, multi-year visible (roughly 25% to 30% of revenue). Grid transmission and distribution (about 21% of revenue, Energy business area, about 30% of group EBITA): Svenska Kraftnät plans about SEK 225bn of grid investment over 2026 to 2035 and a 50% investment increase in 2026; Q1 FY2027 grid order intake recovered and management says grid projects are "tilted towards the second half" of FY2027. Defence (about 4% of group sales, about 15% of Automation sales; ten subsidiaries exposed): NATO commitments (Jefferies: more than USD 800bn a year of additional spending) and EU SAFE/ReArm (up to EUR 800bn by 2030). Data centres (Jefferies: European capacity up about 75% by 2030). Electrification of off-highway and marine (RAMME). This layer passes the "two-plus years of visibility" test on direction, not on timing: Energy sales fell 7% in Q1 FY2027 against an exceptional project base, and the research repeatedly flags transmission operator permit and contractor bottlenecks. Structural demand here is real but arrives in lumps.
  2. Short-cycle industrial, cyclically turning (roughly 55% to 60%). About 60% of the top 20 subsidiaries have short-to-mid-cycle exposure. The 33/66-weighted Swedish and eurozone manufacturing PMI crossed 53 in June 2026 (Sweden's PMI 58.1 in September 2026); Addtech's organic growth has historically accelerated two to five quarters after that crossing and outgrown industrial production by about 1.5 percentage points a year. That puts the acceleration window at Q3 FY2027 (October to December 2026) to Q2 FY2028. Book-to-bill was slightly above 100% in Q1 and positive in Q2 FY2027, "well above 1.0x" excluding sawmills; Automation orders improved sequentially for five quarters. Visibility is two to four quarters, not two years, so under the question's own rule this is a cyclical swing without long visibility.
  3. Cyclical drags, bottoming but not recovering (roughly 10% to 15%). Sawmill equipment (about 5% of EBITA; some subsidiaries' sales down 30% to 50% from peak; a 40% earnings drop modelled in the niche) and Nordic construction and installation (hits Safety and parts of Industry; building permits show no recovery; European building recovery deferred into 2027). Order activity in sawmills "shows gradual signs of stabilizing from low levels".
  4. Composition caution (use, refill or price). Customers are placing long-term framework orders "to hedge against geopolitical uncertainties and potential price increases", with enforceable obligations only on call-off; inventories rose in Q1 and Q2 FY2027 on "high order intake and supply chain disruptions"; energy-led inflation (Brent near USD 115 in the late-October environment read) and rising metals, plastics and freight costs are pushing prices up across the cohort (Lagercrantz, Bufab). Some of the coming organic acceleration will therefore be refill and price, which fade within a few quarters, rather than end-use, which lasts. The Consumer should not extrapolate a strong H2 FY2027 organic print into FY2029 at the same rate.

Peer cross-check: the cohort's organic lines have already turned (Lagercrantz +12% in its July to September 2026 quarter, Indutrade +5% in both Q2 and Q3 2026, Diploma 15%); only Lifco (+1.6%, with a customer loss) is weaker than Addtech (+2%). This confirms the cycle is real and says Addtech's lag is mix and comparison (Energy, sawmills, Process, construction) rather than an absent cycle, though local share loss is not disproved.

Skew and cone: over twelve months the skew is mildly positive on volume (comparisons are easy: organic was flat to negative for most of FY2026) and the cone is moderately wide (organic +1% to +6% for H2 FY2027), with the downside branch being a PMI relapse below 50 under rising rates.
