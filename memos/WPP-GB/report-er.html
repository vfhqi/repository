Section 16 – Conclusion

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

WPP's H1 2026 results delivered a resounding operational beat against historically low expectations, marking the first tangible evidence that the Elevate28 turnaround program is stabilizing the business after years of severe underperformance relative to peers like Publicis Groupe and Omnicom. The company entered the reporting period with a deeply pessimistic sell-side consensus expecting Q2 organic net revenue to decline by 6.0%, yet WPP delivered a contraction of just 2.8%, representing a substantial sequential acceleration from the 6.7% organic decline registered in the first quarter. Major brokers including Bernstein and Deutsche Bank highlighted that this performance was comfortably ahead of consensus expectations and demonstrated the first clear signs of stabilization.

The financial delivery exceeded expectations across multiple dimensions, with headline operating profit of

£398 million materially surpassing the consensus estimate of £351 million, while the headline operating margin of 8.4% expanded by 0.2 percentage points year-on-year despite negative operational leverage from declining net sales. This margin resilience unexpectedly offset negative operational leverage and was driven by lower yearon-year headline severance costs and the early execution of Elevate28 cost-saving initiatives. Average adjusted net debt fell by £326 million year-on-year to £2,935 million, while the Board declared an unchanged interim dividend of 7.5 pence per share, keeping WPP firmly on track to deliver its planned flat annual payout of 15.0 pence.

CEO Cindy Rose and CFO Joanne Wilson emphasized that Phase 1 of the Elevate28 plan, titled Stabilise, is successfully transitioning WPP from a complex holding company structure into a single integrated operating company organized around four streamlined global divisions: WPP Media, WPP Creative, WPP Production, and the newly launched WPP Enterprise Solutions. This structural transformation consolidates operations under four global operating units and aims to eliminate duplication and enable a more effective client proposition by establishing four regional profit and loss structures. Management stated that WPP is firmly on track with Phase 1 to stabilize the business, having successfully put in place the building blocks of its new organizational structure, and confirmed that the group is on track for £100 million savings in FY26 as part of its target to deliver £500 million in gross annualized cost savings by 2028.

WPP's proprietary AI-powered agentic marketing platform, WPP Open, emerged as a critical competitive differentiator in new business pitches, with management highlighting that the platform has achieved significant scale with 69,000 active users and is credited with delivering a 5% to 10% uplift in new business pitch conversion rates when compared directly to pitches where the platform was not utilized. The deployment of WPP Open across 69,000 users, representing approximately 85% of WPP's global client-facing workforce, has delivered a tangible operational benefit by improving pitch conversion rates. Management positioned WPP Open as a key differentiator that connects all agency functions and is powering strong new business while resonating strongly with global clients. & Co Redburn - Earnings Calls The sell-side response was overwhelmingly positive, with major brokers upgrading earnings estimates and price targets for the first time in years, although structural concerns regarding WPP's competitive positioning relative to Publicis Groupe's integrated Epsilon data capabilities and Omnicom's scale advantages persist. Deutsche Bank's Steve Liechti raised WPP's price target from 425 pence to 555 pence and upgraded the FY26 likefor-like net revenue decline forecast to negative 4.1%, using a sum-of-the-parts EV/EBIT methodology to reflect recent agency peer re-ratings.

Bernstein's Annick Maas maintained a Market-Perform rating but raised the price target from 332 pence to 400 pence, adjusting her valuation models to reflect encouraging signs of near-term stabilization while choosing to remain on the sidelines. However, analysts continued to warn that WPP faces a less coherent data strategy than its high-performing peers, with its AmeriLink database remaining competitively disadvantaged against Publicis Epsilon, which boasts transactional personally identifiable information on 250 million US individuals.

Major client wins including the global media account for The Estée Lauder Companies valued at approximately 680 million dollars and the US media mandate for Wendy's representing 319 million dollars in media spend validated WPP's unified operating model and integrated pitch strategy, although the group continues to defend 3.5 billion dollars in media billings currently under review, including the critical global Coca-Cola media account representing 1.4 billion dollars in active billings. WPP successfully won major integrated pitches including the global media account for The Estée Lauder Companies and the US media mandate for Wendy's, demonstrating the competitive efficacy of its unified approach. However, WPP faces the highest defensive exposure in the industry, actively defending 3.5 billion dollars in media billings currently under review in a pitch environment that remains exceptionally brutal and fiercely contested.

The investment thesis shift centers on whether WPP can sustain sequential organic revenue improvement through 2027 to achieve Phase 2 of Elevate28, titled Build, which targets a return to positive organic growth sometime during 2027, followed by Phase 3 Accelerate from 2028 onwards, which is designed to establish WPP as a simpler, lower-cost, AI-enabled business that wins market share. CEO Cindy Rose explicitly declared that returning the group to consistent positive organic growth represents WPP's strategic North Star as a management team, with the turnaround executing through three distinct operational phases starting with Phase 1 Stabilise in 2026. The critical near-term execution risk is whether management can navigate the expected 200 basis point margin contraction in H2 2026 driven by the necessary rebuilding of employee incentive pools, which increased 120% to £130 million in H1, while delivering on the full-year headline operating margin guidance of 12% to 13%. Despite delivering a resilient operating margin in H1 2026, WPP expects its second-half operating profit margins to decline with up to a 200 basis point margin contraction year-on-year in H2, driven by the necessary rebuild of the employee incentive pool and ongoing AI and data investments.

The structural headwinds remain formidable, with WPP continuing to face a 500 to 600 basis point drag on

FY26 net revenues from previously announced global account losses, severe organic growth underperformance relative to Publicis Groupe's 4.8% H1 growth and Havas's 2.5% expansion, and persistent client-side pressures from in-housing trends and AI-driven pricing deflation that threaten traditional headcount-based billing models. Historical client churn continues to act as a significant barrier to short-term organic growth inflection, with WPP's management continuing to project a 500 to 600 basis point headwind on 2026 net revenues from previously announced global account losses.

Bernstein's Maas highlighted that WPP's Q2 like-for-like revenue less pass-through costs decline of 2.8% was materially worse than high-performing peers like Publicis, which delivered strong organic growth of 4.8%, and Havas, which grew 2.5% in the same quarter, underscoring WPP's ongoing battle to recover lost market share.

Section 16 – Conclusion

WPP's H1 2026 results delivered a resounding operational beat against historically low expectations, with Q2 organic net revenue declining only 2.8% versus consensus expectations of a 6.0% contraction, representing the first clear evidence of stabilization under the newly launched Elevate28 turnaround plan. The appointment of CEO Cindy Rose, with her deep Microsoft technology background, has established unprecedented corporate alignment and operational discipline across WPP's historically fragmented agency network, driving the transition from a legacy holding company structure to a unified single-company model organized around four streamlined operating units.

The H1 headline operating margin of 8.4% rose 0.2 percentage points year-on-year and headline operating profit of £398m materially surpassed consensus estimates of £351m, demonstrating that management's aggressive cost-saving execution is delivering tangible results despite negative operational leverage from declining net sales.

The single most important change to the investment thesis is that WPP has successfully arrested its multi-year organic growth deterioration and begun to close the competitive gap with high-performing peers like Publicis Groupe. The sequential acceleration from a 6.7% organic decline in Q1 to a 2.8% decline in Q2 represents the first organic growth consensus upgrades since 2022, with full-year consensus LFL net revenue declines expected to moderate to negative 3.5% or 4.0%, representing a constructive shift in analyst sentiment.

WPP's unified operating model has validated its strategic efficacy through major integrated client wins, including the global media account for The Estée Lauder Companies valued at approximately 680 million dollars, the Henkel global creative partnership, and the US media mandate for Wendy's representing 319 million dollars in media spend. The deployment of WPP Open has achieved significant scale with 69,000 active users representing approximately 85% of WPP's global client-facing workforce, and crucially is credited with delivering a 5% to 10% uplift in new business pitch conversions when compared directly to pitches where the platform was not utilized.

& Co Redburn The strongest counter-hypothesis is that WPP's operational stabilization represents a temporary cyclical bounce rather than a sustainable structural recovery, and that the group remains structurally disadvantaged against Publicis Groupe's integrated data ecosystem and faces severe margin compression from AI-driven pricing deflation. WPP's Q2 LFL revenue decline of 2.8% was materially worse than high-performing peers like Publicis which delivered strong organic growth of 4.8% and Havas which grew 2.5% in the same quarter, underscoring WPP's ongoing battle to recover lost market share and close the widening revenue performance gap with its main European competitors.

WPP continues to face a less coherent data strategy than its high-performing peers, with its AmeriLink database remaining competitively disadvantaged against Publicis Epsilon which boasts transactional personally identifiable information on 250 million US individuals. WPP faces the highest defensive exposure in the industry, actively defending 3.5 billion dollars in media billings currently under review, compared to peer Omnicom defending 2.1 billion dollars and Publicis enjoying a highly insulated position with only 300 million dollars of billings under review.

Second-half margin headwinds remain a massive risk, as management warned that a 200 basis point year-on-year margin contraction in H2 will be required to fully rebuild the employee incentive pools, with a 120% increase in employee incentives to £130m severely testing the company's ability to hit its full-year margin targets if H2 revenues do not inflect.
