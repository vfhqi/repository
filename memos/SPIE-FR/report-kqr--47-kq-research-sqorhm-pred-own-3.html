Section summary

Term Loan Structure and Pricing

1.
Floating facility details: SPIE’s banking liabilities include a €600 million senior term loan, historically referred to as Facility A, which was originally drawn down in October 2022. This facility carries a five-year maturity, making it due in full on October 17, 2027. The loan is currently outstanding at its full nominal value of €600 million, represented on the balance sheet at an amortized cost of €599.1 million as of June 30, 2026.
2.
Grid-based margin pricing: The interest rate on the term loan is structured as a floating rate based on Euribor plus an applicable margin. This margin is determined dynamically according to a grid linked to SPIE's year-end leverage ratio excluding IFRS 16. The applicable pricing scales from a low of 1.20% per annum when leverage is below or equal to 3.0x, up to a maximum of 2% per annum if leverage exceeds 3.5x. Based on the company's leverage ratio of 1.3x at the end of December 2025, the margin was established at 1.40% above Euribor.

Hedging Rate and Swap Instruments

3.
Fifty percent swap ratio: SPIE actively manages the interest rate volatility of this floating-rate obligation through derivative financial instruments. Specifically, the group has hedged exactly 50% of the term loan's nominal exposure, corresponding to a hedged value of €300 million. This leaves the remaining €300 million of the term loan exposed to floating market interest rates.
4.
Swap transaction specifications: In October 2022, SPIE established two separate interest rate swap instruments to execute this coverage. These derivative instruments are designated as cash flow hedges under IFRS 9 and have a maturity matching the term loan in October 2027. Under the terms of these swaps, the group pays a fixed rate against receiving a floating 1-month Euribor rate on a combined notional amount of €300 million. As of June 30, 2026, the fair value of these interest rate swaps was recognized on the balance sheet as a liability of €2.23 million.
5.
Sensitivity to rate shifts: The partial hedging of the term loan directly mitigates the risk of escalating finance costs on SPIE's net income. Based on the group's debt profile at mid-2026, a 0.50% parallel shift in market interest rates would impact unhedged floating debt by €3.0 million over a 12-month period.
