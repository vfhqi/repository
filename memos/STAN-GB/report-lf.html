EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Winters demonstrates a strong match to the risk-disciplined operator archetype with sustained execution focus over his 10-year tenure clearing historical banking rubble, but shows a partial verdict for decision velocity due to recurrent organizational restructuring and entrenched bureaucracy that has slowed digital execution speed.

The 2025 remuneration policy rebalances compensation from fixed pay toward performance-linked variable remuneration after the pay cap removal, reducing Winters' base salary by 40% to GBP 1.75 million, with 87% of total pay delivered via performance-linked incentives at maximum opportunity, stretching RoTE targets requiring a 14.5% return for maximum vesting, relative TSR representing 40% of the LTIP scorecard measured against six international peers, ESG metrics carrying a 20% weighting, and the 2022-24 LTIP delivering an 88% final vesting outcome driven by 14.7% RoTE and top-quartile TSR.

Regional performance remains heavily reliant on Hong Kong SAR which contributed 34% of 2025 pre-tax profit dwarfing the Chinese mainland's 5% share, with 1Q26 mainland profit contribution falling to 1% reflecting very weak regional diversification, and any economic slowdown in Hong Kong stalling group-wide earnings progression. Key-person risk remains elevated with high key-person dependency on Winters persisting following the abrupt loss of his primary external successor candidate in early 2026, as Diego De Giorgi who was viewed as the primary contender for the chief executive position before his sudden resignation, and finding a suitable external CEO remains a high-risk scenario due to the bank's complex emerging footprint, with De Giorgi deciding to step down as GCFO with immediate effect on February 10, 2026, and analysts viewing his departure as a major blow to the Fit for Growth program.
