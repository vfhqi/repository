Section 2 – FY2026 Profitability and the FY2027 Offshore Order Pipeline

Section summary
  1. Siemens Gamesa remains firmly on track to achieve its break-even target by the end of fiscal year
  2. Large European offshore wind auctions and delayed investment decisions are shifting commercial order pipelines into fiscal year 2027.
  3. Developers prioritize fixed-bottom turbine technologies while floating wind remains constrained by high capital costs and supply chain immaturity.

Turnaround and Profitability Milestones

1.
Financial recovery path: We identified 104 relevant documents and eight peer companies across our research, of which we utilized 12 documents in this section. Siemens Gamesa has registered substantial operational progress as it targets a full-year break-even result for fiscal year 2026. This projected improvement represents a massive turnaround compared to the heavy net loss of €1.3bn recorded in fiscal year 2025. Sustaining this trajectory is vital because it will eliminate the primary drag on Siemens Energy's consolidated margins.
2.
Narrowing operational losses: Siemens Gamesa demonstrated a sharp recovery during the first half of fiscal year 2026 by reducing its losses before special items to just €90m. This performance represents an 85% reduction in losses compared to the €623m deficit recorded in the first half of fiscal year 2025. Financial analysts indicate that these narrowing losses de-risk the near-term profitability outlook for the parent group. Consequently, the segment is well-positioned to contribute positive earnings in the second half of the year.
3.
Positive quarterly momentum: The wind turbine segment reported a major milestone in the second quarter of fiscal year 2026 by narrowing its loss before special items to €44m. This result significantly beat consensus expectations which had projected a larger loss of €78m for the quarter. This positive performance was primarily driven by productivity improvements in offshore manufacturing and higher service business margins. Improving quarterly execution confirms that the operational recovery is progressing faster than historically anticipated.

Offshore Order Pipeline Shifts

4.
Delayed pipeline conversion: Siemens Gamesa experienced volatile order intake during fiscal year 2026 because multiple large offshore turbine bookings shifted into fiscal year 2027. Management stated that these delays were driven by the timing of large offshore auctions and slower developer decision-making. This order volatility is temporarily depressing near-term equipment bookings but does not reflect structural demand weakness. Consequently, the order pipeline is poised for a strong rebound in fiscal year 2027 as project developers finalize pending investment decisions.

Siemens Gamesa's offshore management reported that 48GW of awarded offshore wind capacity across Europe is still waiting on final investment decisions. This lack of investment commitment creates a dramatic gap in immediate deployment schedules across the North Sea. Unless utilities accelerate their financial commitments, turbine manufacturers will experience volatile factory utilization rates over the next two years.

The segment has secured a pipeline of more than 22GW specifically for its advanced SG 14/15 turbine platform. This massive backlog represents a dominant 67% offshore market share across key regional markets. Robust backlog pricing ensures that future revenues will translate into profitable margins as execution capacity expands.

Competitive Dynamics and Peer Comparisons

7.
European duopoly formation: Siemens Gamesa is entering a highly consolidated competitive environment that is rapidly transforming into an offshore duopoly. GE Vernova systematically downsized its offshore wind business and exited the offshore turbine market to focus on its gas and grid segments. This strategic exit leaves Siemens Gamesa and Vestas as the only credible large-scale suppliers in the Western offshore market. This consolidated market structure will support stronger pricing power and protect long-term service margins.
8.
Peer execution comparison: Vestas demonstrates strong commercial momentum as its Power Solutions segment achieved an adjusted EBIT margin of 10.4% in the second quarter of calendar year 2026. In comparison, Siemens Gamesa reported a negative profit margin before special items of 1.7% in its corresponding fiscal quarter. This margin gap indicates that Vestas has advanced further in its offshore ramp-up and operational execution. However, Siemens Gamesa is rapidly closing this profitability gap as its manufacturing facilities achieve higher productivity levels.
9.
Offshore order comparison: Vestas reported total order intake of 3.3GW valued at €3.4bn in the second quarter of calendar year 2026. This order volume represents a massive lead over Siemens Gamesa's order intake of €846m reported in its corresponding quarter. This wide difference reflects the lumpy nature of offshore contract awards and Vestas's near-term success in securing immediate onshore contracts. Nevertheless, both manufacturers benefit from high backlog pricing as industry-wide turbine average selling prices remain highly stable.

Developer Preferences and Floating Wind

10.
Fixed bottom dominance: Project developers continue to overwhelmingly prefer fixed-bottom offshore wind platforms due to their established track record and technical maturity. Industry experts emphasize that largescale fixed-bottom projects are the primary driver of offshore capacity growth in the North Sea. This technological focus supports Siemens Gamesa's turnaround because its core manufacturing capacity is optimized for fixed-bottom monopile and jacket installations. Consequently, the near-term commercial pipeline carries lower engineering and execution risks compared to experimental deepwater designs.
11.
Floating wind constraints: Floating offshore wind remains a highly speculative and economically challenging segment that fails to attract major commercial turbine commitments. Developers struggle with extreme cost inflation as floating wind auctions in the United Kingdom reached prices over €216 per megawatt hour. This elevated pricing represents more than twice the cost of fixed-bottom wind on a levelized cost of energy basis. These high capital requirements mean that floating wind will remain a niche market until at least 2035.
