Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. Salzgitter's H1 2026 earnings delivered a powerful operational turnaround that validates the structural recovery thesis, driven by surging Aurubis contributions, robust P28 cost savings, and tightening EU trade protections that are restoring domestic pricing power despite persistently weak German industrial demand. EBITDA VX surged to €459.0 million in H1 2026, a massive increase from €116.8 million in the prior-year period, while EBT VX showed a significant turnaround to €257.6 million compared to a pre-tax loss of €-83.8 million in the first half of 2025. This operational inflection prompted management to raise full-year EBITDA guidance twice to a range of €725 million to €825 million, representing a material upgrade from consensus expectations. The group's result was heavily supported by a €193.0 million equity contribution from its Aurubis stake, surging from €71.5 million recorded in the first half of 2025.

The most important strategic delta was the complete acquisition of Hüttenwerke Krupp Mannesmann in July 2026. Salzgitter completed the acquisition of the remaining 70% of Hüttenwerke Krupp Mannesmann, transitioning the asset from a 30% joint-operation with thyssenkrupp and Vallourec to a wholly-owned subsidiary. The buyout of the joint venture was structured on highly favorable terms as thyssenkrupp transferred its 50% stake for a nominal €1.

This transaction secures critical slab feedstock supply as Russian import quotas phase out by September 2028, positioning Salzgitter with structural slab market control that peers lack. However, the integration requires a massive workforce reduction from 3,000 to 1,000 employees by end-2028 and demands substantial restructuring capital. Trade policy developments emerged as the single most powerful external catalyst.

The newly implemented European Union steel safeguard framework that took effect on July 1, 2026, capped annual duty-free imports across 26 steel categories at 18.3 million metric tons. This cap represents a massive 47% reduction compared to the 2024 steel quotas and doubles the out-of-quota tariff from 25% to 50%. ArcelorMittal successfully raised its hot-rolled coil offers by €50 per ton to €770 per ton, reporting full order books for the entire third quarter.

European hot-rolled coil prices rebounded by approximately 9% from their June lows to reach €720- 730 per ton in early September 2026. This swift pricing recovery occurred despite seasonally soft summer conditions, proving that pricing power has shifted back to domestic integrated mills. The P28 performance program substantially exceeded expectations. The P28 performance program generated €97.0 million in sustainable savings, enabling Salzgitter to achieve most of its 2026 target of €122 million by midyear.

Management expanded the program's ultimate target to €575 million in cumulative benefits by 2028, providing crucial margin protection against cyclical headwinds. Divisional performance revealed critical mixed signals. Steel Production EBITDA of €80 million was 13% below Morgan Stanley's forecast and came in 6% below Visible Alpha consensus due to shipments lagging forecasts. However, Trading EBITDA of €24 million came in 47% ahead of Morgan Stanley's forecast and was 70% ahead of Visible Alpha consensus of €14 million due to temporary margin gains.

The Technology division disappointed slightly due to weak international beverage machinery demand. Balance sheet metrics showed resilience under heavy transition capital demands. Net financial debt of €792 million was substantially better than Visible Alpha consensus of €1.04 billion and remained perfectly stable versus prior year despite heavy SALCOS investments. Q2 free cash flow to equity outperformed Morgan Stanley's expectations by €74 million, driven by favorable timing of capital expenditures.

The sell-side response was constructive but nuanced. Following the full Q2 release, Deutsche Bank analyst Synagowitz trimmed the price target from €65 to €63 due to near-term capex volatility, whilst maintaining a Buy rating. The broker noted that although the Q2 results were strong, the bumpiness of the capex trajectory warranted temporary price target caution. However, in a subsequent September sector update, Deutsche Bank's Synagowitz raised the target price from €63 to €70 and reiterated the Buy rating. This upward revision reflected a positive outlook on carbon steel pricing dynamics, with Salzgitter highlighted as a prime beneficiary of high operating leverage.

Management's decarbonization strategy revealed pragmatic capital discipline. Under its Salzgitter AG 2030 strategy, management has decided that while SALCOS Stage 1 remains secured for a 2027 start-up, the investment decisions on subsequent implementation steps will be postponed by three years due to challenging political and economic framework conditions. This three-year postponement of SALCOS Phases 2 and 3 conserves over €1 billion of capex during weak demand, validating a modular approach.

The federal government of Germany and the state of Lower Saxony increased the public subsidies for Salzgitter's SALCOS Phase 1 transformation program in early 2026 by €322 million, bringing the total approved public transition funding to €1.322 billion. Out of the committed €1.3 billion in public transition grants for the SALCOS program, Salzgitter has already successfully received €1.076 billion in disbursements by June 30, 2026, to materially reduce immediate cash burn. 8 headwinds remain structurally severe.

Regional macroeconomic forecasts have been revised sharply downward, with Germany's expected growth cut to 0.6% and the eurozone's to 0.9% as persistent industrial stagnation continues. This subdued industrial backdrop directly limits private household purchasing power and dampens consumer demand across core construction and appliance sectors. Consequently, Salzgitter's domestic carbon steel shipments face persistent volume headwinds because economic stagnation shows no signs of a near-term cyclical rebound.

German automotive and construction demand remains deeply depressed, forcing the group to rely entirely on pricing power rather than volume recovery. Raw material inflation posed unexpected cost pressures. Coking coal prices defied expectations of normalization, with weather-related disruptions in Australia and strong Indian demand driving the Q2 2026 benchmark price nearly 30% higher year-on-year to average $238 per metric ton.

Scrap prices also escalated as domestic mills competed for premium feedstock ahead of SALCOS commissioning. Leadership transitions stabilized successfully. CFO Birgit Potrafki successfully assumed control of the finance department on April 1, 2024, following a dual-service transition period with her predecessor Burkhard Becker. This transition resolved leadership uncertainty at a critical juncture of the group's decarbonization push.

Potrafki is appointed through to January 31, 2027. The Executive Board was streamlined from five members in 2024 during transition phases down to three members in 2025. This lean team consists of CEO Gunnar Groebler, CFO Birgit Potrafki, and CPO Birgit Dietze. The consolidation was designed to accelerate decision-making efficiency and improve execution of the P28 savings program during peak capital expenditure.

Disruptive technology forces validated strategic caution. Transitioning to the H2-DRI-EAF route reduces carbon emissions by 99% but demands fifteen times more electricity than traditional steelmaking. This technology's viability remains hostage to cheap green hydrogen and extensive renewable power generation. The rollout of the planned European Hydrogen Backbone has slowed significantly. TSO projections now show that only 23% of planned projects will be commissioned by 2029 compared to the 74% previously expected. The proposed Norway-to-Germany hydrogen pipeline was entirely abandoned by developers Equinor and Shell. This setback underlines chronic delays to cross-border networks connecting low-carbon supply to German industrial hubs. Regulatory tailwinds strengthened materially. The European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism officially entered its definitive enforcement phase on January 1, 2026, creating a more level playing field by imposing carbon-linked import levies equivalent to the domestic costs borne under the emissions trading system. The European Commission officially established the first quarterly reference price for CBAM certificates on April 7, 2026, at €75.36 per ton of carbon dioxide, which structurally lifts the European import cost floor to benefit lowcarbon domestic producers. However, under the European Commission's latest reform proposal published on July 17, 2026, the European Union Emissions Trading System will restore 15% of free allowances for CBAM sectors and postpone the full phase-out to 2038 to partially protect energy-intensive industries.

Salzgitter CEO Gunnar Groebler strongly criticized the European Commission's proposals to ease emissions trading rules, warning that retroactively changing the rules of the game would undermine pioneering green steel investments and penalize companies that already transitioned.

topic-specific reports Agency Portfolio optimization accelerated through active management. Salzgitter successfully signed an agreement to sell Klöckner DESMA Elastomertechnik GmbH at the end of July 2026. This transaction comprises the sale of the core manufacturing company in Friedingen alongside its four foreign subsidiaries. The disposal of Klöckner DESMA Elastomertechnik marks another deliberate step in active portfolio management and simplification. The business is scheduled to be fully deconsolidated from the Technology Business Unit by the end of the 2026 financial year.

Defense sector penetration emerged as a strategic growth vector. Salzgitter is actively accelerating its downstream footprint in secure protection and defense steels under its specialized "Salzgitter AG Defence" brand. Management is expanding material approvals and deepening its value chain to serve civilian armored and military vehicle manufacturers. CEO Gunnar Groebler stated that defense applications will generate a single-digit percentage of overall sales within the next two to three years.

The group expects 2026 to be a transitional permitgathering year before ramping up to mid-to-high single-digit percentages in the years that follow. dynamics shifted decisively in favor of domestic mills. Salzgitter's internal monitoring of strip and heavy plate steel deliveries in early 2026 confirmed that domestic shipments rose while imports fell. This shift indicates that customers are reallocating their purchase volumes and increasingly sourcing from local producers.

High-frequency trade data from late August 2026 shows that key exporting nations are rapidly exhausting their quotas, with Turkey completely filling its hot-rolled coil allocations. This rapid quota exhaustion materially restricts spot market availability and prevents foreign producers from dumping excess volumes into Europe. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the bullish thesis centers on persistent demand destruction risk.

If German economic stagnation deepens into a structural multi-year recession, the destruction of domestic industrial demand would overwhelm the pricing benefits of EU safeguards, forcing mills into aggressive volumechasing and severe pricing concessions that would completely erode P28 cost-saving initiatives and expose the balance sheet to severe strain under dual transition capital burdens. The specific data points that would change this assessment include: quarterly German automotive production falling below 850,000 units for three consecutive quarters, European hot-rolled coil prices breaking below €650 per ton on a sustained basis, SALCOS Phase 1 hydrogen supply costs exceeding €4.50 per kilogram, or HKM workforce restructuring costs surpassing €400 million.

The outlier scenario that markets are underpricing involves accelerated NATO defense spending triggering a structural step-change in specialty armor plate demand, where Salzgitter's newly acquired Thyrolf & Uhle capabilities and Ilsenburger Grobblech capacity could generate €200-300 million of incremental high-margin EBITDA by 2028-2029, fundamentally re-rating the ex-Aurubis stub valuation. Two critical areas requiring verification next: First, validate whether HKM's €100 million cumulative negative cash flow guidance through 2028 accurately captures full workforce severance liabilities and electric arc furnace cost overruns, as any material escalation would jeopardize the group's deleveraging trajectory.

Second, monitor the actual commissioning timeline and hydrogen cost structure for SALCOS Phase 1 in summer 2027, as delays or cost inflation would fundamentally undermine the modular decarbonization strategy and force unplanned reliance on natural gas that would destroy the green steel premium pricing thesis. Other side of the story: While management successfully executed a portfolio realignment and captured trade protection tailwinds, the combination of massive HKM integration complexity, persistently weak German demand, and chronic hydrogen infrastructure delays creates a narrow path to sustainable free cash flow generation, meaning that any miss on the 2027 steel pricing assumptions or SALCOS execution would trigger severe balance sheet distress and force dilutive capital raises.
