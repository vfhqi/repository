EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

The FALSE FRIEND hypothesis emerges as the winning thesis profile with the absolute fewest structural inconsistencies across fifteen core evaluated indicators, revealing that Proximus presents a deceptively stable operational façade that masks severe underlying cash generation deterioration. The bear case against Proximus demonstrates medium-to-high plausibility, supported by validated fact-based evidence on capital expenditure escalation and dividend rebasing, though contradicted by resilient domestic subscriber metrics that prevent an outright collapse scenario.

The bear-case credibility assessment reveals a differentiated risk landscape where fact-based allegations on capex escalation and dividend cuts are highly validated by official corporate and broker reporting, with uncompromised filings confirming capital expenditure will rise to an estimated EUR 1.3bn in FY26 due to Unifiber consolidation, while ING confirms management halved the dividend to EUR 0.30 per share to preserve critical balance sheet liquidity.

However, speculative claims around immediate DIGI Belgium disruption overstate the threat given muted subscriber momentum, while fears of catastrophic structural margin deterioration remain largely unsubstantiated by recent results. Internal contradictions emerge as bears simultaneously warn of intense DIGI disruption while highlighting stalled DIGI operational momentum, and argue Flanders fiber overbuilding destroys long-term network utilization while ignoring BMA regulatory approvals.

The Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix definitively identifies the FALSE FRIEND profile through critical diagnostic indicators. The uncovered rebased dividend represents a key diagnostic inconsistency for the YES profile, while the loss of 42,000 fixed voice lines in Q2 2026 disproves the stable domestic return narrative. Wyre securing EUR 4.35bn of external funding is diagnostic of aggressive overbuild capabilities, while DIGI launching 5G FWA at seven euros per month directly threatens premium home internet margins.

The matrix reveals six hard inconsistencies for the bull YES hypothesis against elevated capex guidance and rebased dividends, while the bear NO hypothesis faces contradictions from resilient domestic postpaid additions of 25,000 in second quarter 2026.

The highest-plausibility failure modes cluster around three correlated root causes that compound to create joint failure scenarios. Severe labor escalation through mandatory wage indexation automatically erodes margins, while intense B2B mobile competitive pressure drove Q2 2026 business mobile service revenue down by 3.5% and business mobile ARPU plummeted 5.2% to EUR 17.7. Unifiber reconsolidation increases FY26 capex to up to EUR 1.3bn severely restricting organic FCF to approximately EUR 50m, while civil works constituting 70% of capex expose the fiber rollout directly to inflationary subcontracting costs.

The execution cluster reveals buying Fiberklaar at twice book value required EUR 246m forcing debt reconsolidation of EUR 290m, with painful partner negotiations proving highly expensive, while Unifiber funding required EUR 130m in cumulative equity injections. Competitive disruption manifests through DIGI launching aggressive low-cost mobile plans at EUR 9 per month severely undercutting premium incumbent portfolios, while Orange achieved EUR 47.5m in run-rate VOO synergies during 2024 far ahead of schedule, threatening premium share in Wallonia where Proximus historically enjoyed dominant market share.
