  1. Unilever is undergoing a major structural shift to become a focused Household and Personal Care pureplay while retaining its high-growth Indian business.
  2. The company's financial performance has been steady but lagged premium peers like L'Oreal and Procter & Gamble over the last three years.
  3. Broker expectations and ratings have shifted positively as the structural demergers and partnerships reduce complexity and unlock gross margin upside.
  4. Broker analysts focused heavily on Unilever's projected commodity cost inflation and the sustainability of its volume-led growth targets.
  5. The planned demerger of the ice cream business and combination of foods with McCormick were viewed as critical steps to simplify the portfolio.
  6. Sell-side expectations remained cautious regarding developed market volume declines and intense private label promotional competition.
  7. Successful execution of the SASSY marketing framework and the regional operating model is required to drive multiple expansion.

The single most critical structural shift. The most important change is the emergence of a pureplay Household and Personal Care (HPC) portfolio with India Foods retained, in which volume-led growth is demonstrable in the core. The ice cream business was demerged in December 2025, and the Foods combination with McCormick is expected to close by mid-2027. The Q3 split shows why this matters.

The portfolio change is meant to deliver a premium mix rising from roughly 35% to 50% and an underlying operating margin moving from 19% toward 22% to 23%. In effect, the earnings suggest the separation is revealing a faster-growing core rather than only simplifying the group.

The strongest counter-argument. The best case against this view is that the volume strength is geographically narrow and may not be structural. In Q3, emerging market volume grew 6.0% while developed market volume fell 1.7%. Western European home care is highly exposed to private label penetration approaching about 20%.

Jefferies notes volume and mix growth has averaged 2.7% over eleven quarters, so tougher comparables could constrain growth over the next eighteen months. Margin conversion is also at risk. Second-half commodity inflation of about €550m brings the full-year total to about €850m. Deutsche Bank held its FY27 operating margin forecast flat at 20.1% because of accelerating raw material inflation.

Marketing investment above sixteen percent of sales post-separation would absorb much of the productivity benefit. Finally, the €1bn productivity program is primarily intended to offset the €500m in stranded costs, so less than half of it is net new capacity. On this reading, the pureplay shift raises the quality of the portfolio but does not by itself guarantee EPS acceleration. What would change my mind.
