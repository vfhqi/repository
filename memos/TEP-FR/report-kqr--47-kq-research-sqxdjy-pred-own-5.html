Working Capital Requirements

1.
Decelerating performance: Teleperformance reported a working capital outflow of EUR 172 million during the FY2022 period as operational expansion continued. This outflow represented a significant increase compared to the EUR 75 million outflow recorded in FY2021. The rising cash consumption reflected the post-pandemic operational ramp-up and extended collection periods across regional divisions. This working capital drag restricted the translation of operating profits into ultimate free cash flow.
2.
Improving efficiency: Teleperformance achieved a positive working capital turnaround in FY2023 by generating a cash inflow of EUR 24 million. This favorable movement contrasted sharply with the EUR 172 million cash outflow recorded in the previous fiscal year. Stronger cash management policies and disciplined collection efforts across major customer accounts drove this operational improvement. Consequently, the group successfully boosted its net cash flow from operating activities to EUR 1,375 million in FY2023.
3.
Favorable conversion: Teleperformance sustained its positive working capital momentum in FY2024 with a cash inflow of EUR 103 million. This cash generation was supported by disciplined collections and optimized payments during the integration of Majorel. According to Bernstein Research on November 9, 2026, the company reported total cash from operating activities of EUR 1,813 million for the year. This efficient working capital management allowed the company to deliver record-high free cash flow of EUR 1,084 million.
4.
Slower optimization: Teleperformance experienced a smaller working capital inflow of EUR 52 million in FY2025 as integration activities normalized. This reduced cash generation reflected ongoing adjustments to billing cycles and payment terms following major acquisitions. The group's total cash flow from operating activities declined to EUR 1,614 million from the peak recorded in the previous year. Over the period, working capital became less of a supportive driver for net cash generation.

This cash utilization was much lower than the EUR 122 million outflow reported in the first half of 2025. Management attributed the improved performance to enhanced credit control systems and tighter inventory management. Stable working capital control remains critical to support full-year FCF objectives of EUR 800 million to EUR 850 million.

The group reported outstanding receivables transferred under these agreements of EUR 110.1 million at end-2025 compared to EUR 105.0 million at end-2024. These transactions were executed without recourse, allowing the company to derecognize the assets from its balance sheet. This factoring strategy temporarily reduced outstanding trade receivables and boosted reported operating cash flows at the closing dates.

This total included write-downs of EUR 32 million, leaving a net trade receivables position of EUR 2,099 million. The bulk of these receivables was classified as not yet due, representing EUR 1,728 million of the net balance. Tighter DSO monitoring helped prevent unfavorable working capital spikes across Core and Specialized segments.

Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that the group's cash flow quality is lower because factoring programs obscure the true underlying working capital performance. Unseen volatility: The first critical gap relates to the intra-quarter factoring program balances which remain undisclosed in annual reports. Reviewing quarterly cash flow statements or conducting expert calls with regional treasury heads would resolve this mystery.

Aggregated leasing: The second gap concerns the highly aggregated lease commitment disclosures across Teleperformance's global operations. While the company lists a total lease liability of EUR 783 million, it does not detail local contract terms. This aggregation hides regional renewal risks in its 91 operating countries. Obtaining country-specific property lease schedules from upcoming annual reports would close this knowledge gap.

Unclear tax: Another notable deficiency is the lack of detailed regional cash tax paid breakdowns under Pillar Two rules. Analyzing future country-by-country tax reports submitted to French authorities would resolve this uncertainty.

Vague scheduling: There is a significant lack of detailed restructuring cash flow schedules for the Future Forward strategy. Although management guides for massive restructuring charges, the precise timing of cash outflows remains vague. This vagueness makes it difficult for analysts to project short-term free cash flow impacts. Waiting for the full-year audited financial statements of FY2026 would resolve this scheduling gap.
5.
Incomplete commitment: The disclosures are thin regarding off-balance sheet lease commitments and variable lease payments. The group states that variable lease payments are not significant but provides no further numerical details. This lack of detail leaves analysts unable to model total operational commitment risks. Accessing the internal leasing database through a corporate presentation would close this information gap.

Hidden overhead: The company does not provide separate disclosures for low-value and short-term leases across its segments. Reviewing segment-level notes in future Universal Registration Documents would resolve this issue.

Obscured segment: There is no distinct reporting of segment-level working capital movements between Core and Specialized Services. Organizing dedicated expert calls with divisional chief financial officers would close this operational gap.

Shorter durations: There is a critical information gap regarding average customer contract durations and annual renewal rates. Undisclosed development: The financial statements do not break out capitalized software and AI development spend in interim reports. Section 7 – If the Answer Differs from the Common View

Section summary
  1. Structural AI-driven deflation and rigid lease liabilities represent a severe threat to Teleperformance's cash conversion and ultimate earnings.
  2. Should automation accelerate faster than historical patterns, pricing pressure could trigger a rapid erosion of the group's working capital.

AI Deflationary Risks

1.
Severe pressure: Traditional seat-based contact center contracts face structural volume deflation as automated conversational agents replace human operators. Corporate clients actively challenge historical BPO pricing structures and demand immediate efficiency savings during contract renewals. This volume cannibalization is expected to severely depress core organic growth and cash generation. Consequently, the common consensus view of stable cash conversion could fail if clients shift to cheaper digital self-service.
2.
Accelerated decline: Morgan Stanley reports that a severe downside scenario could see Teleperformance's organic growth drop to negative 3% if the company fails to capitalize on agentic AI transformation. In this alternative outcome, the group becomes a low-value-added staff provider with EBITA margins contracting toward 11%. This structural margin compression would directly impair the group's ability to generate the guided EUR 3 billion in three-year cumulative free cash flow. Ultimately, lower cash generation would halt the planned de-leveraging trajectory and damage future earnings per share.
3.
Margin compression: Deutsche Bank Research suggests that the BPO industry is undergoing a structural margin compression cycle because of the rapid adoption of artificial intelligence. While automation makes operations more efficient, the resulting financial benefits are mostly shared with cost-sensitive enterprise clients. This value compression forces BPO operators to aggressively invest in scale and capability to capture the remaining complex interactions. Under these conditions, the group's cash flow margins will remain depressed over the medium term.
4.
Technological disruption: Jefferies Research notes that Teleperformance and its main peers are highly vulnerable because they sell easily automated, call-priced services. There are few structural barriers to prevent clients from demanding steep rate cuts or dropping traditional services entirely. This low-barrier environment accelerates the long-term disruptive impact of generative AI on outsourcing economics. Consequently, these rapid technological changes could permanently impair BPO cash flow generation.

Financial Commitments and EPS Impact

5.
Rigid obligations: High lease and interest expenses could severely restrict net cash flow if operational volumes fall faster than expected. Rothschild & Co Redburn models rigid annual lease payments of EUR 319 million and cash interest paid of EUR 169 million for FY2026. These fixed cash commitments do not scale down in line with falling seat volumes. Therefore, any top-line contraction will disproportionately impact the remaining cash available to equity holders.
6.
Squeezed liquidity: Bernstein Research shows that rigid lease payments of EUR 315 million and interest paid of EUR 179 million in FY2026 will continuously pressure the net cash position. If organic revenue growth deteriorates further, these undiscounted lease liabilities will restrict the capital available for necessary AI reinvestment. Consequently, the group may be forced to divert cash away from debt reduction and tactical share buybacks. This cash diversion would directly impair adjusted earnings per share and delay balance sheet de-leveraging.
7.
Restructuring strain: Massive non-recurring restructuring cash outlays under the Future Forward strategy represent an additional near-term cash drain. The group revised its expected FY2026 restructuring costs upward to a range of EUR 120 million to EUR 140 million. While management expects these measures to eventually yield annualized cost savings, the immediate cash-outs reduce available liquidity. If these expensive restructuring programs fail to restore top-line momentum, reported earnings per share will suffer.

Customer and Technological Moats

8.
In-sourcing threat: Former contact center executives observe that BPOs struggle to replace lost seat-based revenue with proprietary technology sales. Rather than purchasing BPO-branded AI software, major corporate clients prefer to build and deploy custom solutions internally. These corporate clients frequently require their multi-vendor BPO partners to utilize the clients' own bespoke technology stacks. Consequently, the BPO industry's traditional volume-based revenue generation models face severe structural disruption.
9.
Contrary evidence: Morgan Stanley reports that the threat of full customer service automation remains highly exaggerated because voice interactions require significant human empathy. For instance, Klarna recently reversed its chatbot-only strategy and restarted hiring human agents to protect customer satisfaction levels. This reversal highlights that 100% automated customer care often takes a severe toll on service quality and brand reputation. Therefore, human-in-the-loop hybrid models remain the most viable long-term operational strategy.
10.
Other side of the story: While structural AI-led pricing pressure and rigid financial leases pose severe long-term risks, the group's global scale and vendor consolidation tailwinds provide a strong defensive cushion. Teleperformance continues to leverage its FCF generation and low leverage as strong differentiators when clients consolidate their supplier panels. Additionally, emerging data annotation services and outcome-based pricing models offer new avenues to protect overall profitability. Thus, the group's robust cash conversion profile is expected to prevent a sudden collapse in earnings.
