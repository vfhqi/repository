What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: the business is intact and cheap, and the share price now depends on proving that volume replaces price in 2027 at a cost base growing about 3%. Six judgements decide that, and the call can test five of them.

  • Judgement 1, costs. In the third quarter of 2026 (Q3), EUR 41m of extra gross profit produced only EUR 21m of extra EBITA; operating costs rose about EUR 20m, or 11%, including acquisitions and currency (our arithmetic). The explanation on record is digital roll-out and more front-line salespeople. The case assumes organic cost growth of 2.75% to 3.0% a year. If the spend is a step that recurs, costs at 4% take EUR 9m from FY2027 EBITA and EUR 0.11 from cash EPS, and the conversion recovery towards 45% is slower. If it is accruals or offset by back-office cuts, the case holds.
  • Judgement 2, the make-up of growth. Third-quarter organic revenue growth slowed to 5.6% while the gross margin rose to 25.1% from 24.3%; brokers credit mix. FY2027 needs volume and mix to go from +0.7 points to +4.0 points of organic gross profit growth while price goes from +3.5 points to (1.0). The only volume statement so far is "strong volume growth in Asia-Pacific". Volume spreading beyond Asia-Pacific makes the FY2027 consensus of EUR 6.40 reachable against our EUR 6.15; no volume means cuts of 3% to 5% or more.
  • Judgement 3, customers' inventories. Jefferies reports customers' inventory days 6% above a year ago and above ten-year levels in food, construction and household care. The case assumes channel stocks at mid-cycle and no pre-buying, which is why its bear case is FY2027 EBITA (7)% and its downside 17% to 23%. If customers hold excess stock, the bear looks more like 2023 (volume about (6)%) and the downside is less limited.
  • Judgement 4, how durable the gross margin is. Either the third-quarter margin gain is share and mix (Asia-Pacific organic gross profit +20.1%; EMEA +8.3% against Azelis' +2.1%) or it is stock bought at earlier cost and sold at higher prices. Mix means the margin need not drift down as the case models; a stock gain means a larger give-back, up to 100 basis points for a quarter or two, when supplier prices fall.
  • Judgement 5, the lines below EBITA in FY2027. About two-thirds of our gap to FY2027 consensus sits below EBITA: the March 2027 bond refinancing, currency and hyperinflation results, and a 25% tax rate. These are factual questions investor relations can answer directly, and each feeds straight into the model.
  • Judgement 6, the February 2027 results and the new chief financial officer. Dividend (our EUR 2.10 against EUR 1.97 consensus), the unused buyback authority, disclosure, a cost programme, the group's enterprise resource planning computer system. This is the one dated moment when an internal change could appear; mostly for the 2-3 December meetings.

Order of play. Ask the cost questions (A2, A3) first, then volume (A4), then the lines below EBITA (A7 to A9): they are answerable and move the numbers most. Then customers' inventories (B2.1). Leave people, disclosure and capital allocation to the end, or for 2-3 December.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the fourth quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2027)

A1 ★ The fourth quarter and the outlook statement. "You now expect positive organic operating EBITA growth for FY2026 and called the start of the fourth quarter promising. What does 'promising' refer to: gross profit, volumes or orders? And is there anything in last year's 'very soft November and extremely soft December' that you would not expect to repeat?"
- Why: the case has Q4 2026 EBITA of about EUR 121m at a 40% to 41% conversion margin; fourth quarters missed in 2023 and 2025 (Q4 2025 by 8%). After nine months up 8%, "positive for the year" is a low bar.
- Tell: "volumes and orders" = upside to EUR 121m; "an easy comparison" = the fourth-quarter pattern stands.

A2 ★ Organic cost growth. "On the reported third-quarter figures, operating costs rose about EUR 20 million, around 11%, including acquisitions and currency. What was organic cost growth in the third quarter and over the nine months, and how much of it was the digital roll-out and the added sales headcount?"
- Why: the case uses 2.75% to 3.0% a year; each point of cost growth is EUR 7.3m of EBITA and EUR 0.09 of cash EPS. On 5-Nov-2026 we estimated third-quarter organic cost growth at roughly 6% to 7% (approximate).
- Tell: "very low single digit over nine months, the third quarter was a bonus true-up" = one quarter, case intact; "we are investing for growth" = costs leading volume, the 17-Nov-2026 summary's strongest challenge, confirmed.

A3 ★ A one-off step or a run-rate. "Is the increase in front-line salespeople and digital spend a step that is now complete, or a rate of investment we should carry into 2027? Does back-office automation offset it, and by how much?"
- Why: the case has headcount falling after the late-2025 restructuring (EUR 15m of severance) and automation holding cost growth near 3%. The third quarter shows the opposite.
- Tell: "complete, offset by order-to-cash automation" = conversion recovers on the case's path; "continuing through 2027" = take cost growth to 4% and FY2027 EBITA down about EUR 9m.

A4 ★ Volume beyond Asia-Pacific. "You described third-quarter pricing as back to normal and product-specific, and named strong volume growth in Asia-Pacific. Can you say, even directionally, whether volumes grew in EMEA and the Americas in the third quarter, and in October?"
- Why: FY2027 needs volume and mix of +4.0 points; a volume claim exists for one region only, and the Americas grew organic revenue only 1.5%.
- Tell: "volumes up in EMEA too" = the first evidence of the second leg; "stable" = Jefferies' "no genuine volume recovery yet" stands.

A5 ★ How semi-specialty prices reset. "How quickly do semi-specialty prices follow supplier prices down: what share of that business resets monthly or quarterly, and roughly what share of gross profit is semi-specialty today?"
- Why: semi-specialties are 15% to 20% of revenue; their prices rose 10% to 30% after the Strait of Hormuz closed in March 2026. The model has price turning from +3.5 points to (1.0) in 2027, with frame agreements of three years and customer commitments of two to six months.
- Tell: a short reset cycle and a larger share = the give-back is faster and bigger than modelled; "specialty prices are negotiated customer by customer and held in 2023" = the case's milder bear is supported.

A6 ★ The quarter of the turn. "You took inventory write-downs of EUR 14 million in Q4 2022 and EUR 16.6 million in 2023. How is inventory valued, and did selling stock bought at earlier cost add to the third-quarter gross margin? Is there a rule of thumb for the margin effect when supplier prices fall?"
- Why: the case allows up to 100 basis points of gross margin and a EUR 14m to EUR 28m inventory loss in the quarter of the turn. Working-capital days were 74 at end-September against 71 a year earlier.
- Tell: "the margin gain was mix, not stock" = supports the more durable reading; "some benefit from timing" = expect the larger give-back.

A7 ★ The bond refinancing. "The EUR 300 million 2.125% bond falls due in March 2027. How and when do you expect to refinance it, and what cost is it reasonable to model?"
- Why: the case assumes about 4.5%: about EUR 7m a year more interest, EUR 0.09 a share at run-rate. It is part of the gap to consensus that no broker models.
- Tell: a cost near 4.5% confirms the model; refinancing from cash or a cheaper facility narrows our gap to consensus.

A8 ★ Currency and hyperinflation results. "Currency and hyperinflation results cost about EUR 27 million in 2025. What should we model for 2026 and 2027, and are you considering changing the Turkish subsidiary's functional currency, as Azelis did?"
- Why: a repeat of 2025 is EUR 0.27 a share in any year; the memo calls it the second-largest source of earnings per share variance after operating leverage, and nobody forecasts it.
- Tell: "a change of functional currency" removes the hyperinflation line; "we cannot predict it" keeps it as an unpriced risk to the FY2027 consensus.

A9 Tax. "What effective and cash tax rate should we use for 2027? Some brokers use 22% to 23%."
- Why: the model uses 25%; each point is EUR 0.08 of cash EPS.
- Tell: 25% = consensus earnings per share is too high; 22% to 23% = half the below-EBITA gap closes.

A10 ★ Full-year cash conversion. "You expect full-year cash conversion in the high 80s to low 90s per cent, from 73% at nine months and 66% in the third quarter. What has to happen in the fourth quarter to get there, and on what definition of free cash flow?"
- Why: it tests whether IMCD's own stock turns into cash. The memo also expects a free cash flow "miss" in February against the FactSet consensus of EUR 336m that is a difference of definition.
- Tell: "inventory release as prices ease" = the stock-gain reading of judgement 4 gains weight; a definition that excludes acquired working capital = the "miss" is only a definition, worth saying before February.

A11 How consensus is set. "With no guidance, which consensus do you track, and on what earnings per share basis? Is most of the spread in brokers' 2027 numbers in the conversion margin?"
- Why: three brokers on a different earnings per share basis pull FactSet's mean below the median (FY2027 EUR 6.07 against EUR 6.40), so a move in the mean can look like a revision and not be one. The memo finds the spread among brokers is in conversion, not gross profit.
- Tell: a company-collected consensus near EUR 6.40 on the cash basis confirms the median is the number to track.

A12 ★ The February 2027 results (or save for 3 December). "Floris Lagerwerf takes over as Chief Financial Officer on 1 January. Should we expect any change at the full-year results in what you disclose or how you talk about the outlook? Is a capital markets day planned?"
- Why: the memo's probabilities for February: a cost programme 25%, a buyback 20%, a volume and price split 15% to 20%, guidance 10% to 15%, an ERP announcement 20% to 30%. Peers' new chief financial officers took write-downs at their first results (Brenntag EUR 248m; Azelis EUR 110m).
- Tell: any signal of new disclosure lowers surprise risk on FY2027; "continuity" keeps the February print a single, unguided event.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Costs, digital and the group computer system

B1.1 What the digital spend buys. "What results are you measuring from SalesAssistant and MyIMCD, which has 28,000 users: gross profit per salesperson, cross-selling, cost to serve?"
- Why: if the spend shows a return, rising costs are investment; if not, they are cost inflation.
- Tell: a named measure with a payback = the cost step is defensible; none = treat it as cost.

B1.2 Variable pay. "Board variable pay was 32% of remuneration in 2025 against 44% in 2024. How much rebuild of variable pay is in 2026 costs, and does it rise again in 2027?"
- Why: the memo estimates EUR 5m to EUR 10m of rebuild over 2026 and 2027, which partly explains costs following gross profit up.
- Tell: "mostly in 2026" = 2027 cost growth can ease; "rises with targets" = costs follow volume, as in 2021.

B1.3 The ERP. "Is replacing the group ERP system on the agenda after the chief financial officer handover, and how would its cost be treated?"
- Why: the memo estimates EUR 30m to EUR 60m over 2028 to 2030, not in the model's operating costs.
- Tell: expensed and soon = a cost the case does not carry; capitalised or not planned = no change.

B2. Customers' inventories and demand

B2.1 ★ Customers' inventories. "Jefferies reports customers' inventory days 6% above a year ago and above ten-year levels in food, construction and household care. You and Morgan Stanley have described customers refusing to hold stock. What do your order patterns show: order size, frequency, lead times, and is the order book still about six to eight weeks?"
- Why: both descriptions cannot be true of the same customers, and the case's limited downside rests on lean customer stocks.
- Tell: short, frequent orders and an unchanged order book = the case's lean-channel view holds; larger orders and lengthening books = treat the destocking leg as live.

B2.2 Pre-buying. "When supplier prices rose this year, did any customers buy ahead? And what would tell you a restocking is beginning?"
- Why: the memo names evidence of pre-buying as what would change its view of the downside.
- Tell: "no pre-buying" supports the milder bear; any pre-buying re-opens the 2023 comparison.

B2.3 End markets. "Which end markets are improving and which are not: coatings and construction in the Americas, pharmaceuticals after the tariff disruption, food and personal care?"
- Why: coatings, adhesives, sealants and construction are about 10% of the group and wait for lower US mortgage rates; the model asks little of the Americas (EBITA margin 9.8% in 2026 to 10.6% in 2028, against 12.2% in 2022).
- Tell: broad improvement = volume on the case's path or better; improvement only in pharmaceuticals = a rebound from a low base, not a recovery.

B3. The regions

B3.1 ★ Asia-Pacific. "Asia-Pacific organic gross profit grew 20.1% in the third quarter. How much was new principal mandates, how much pharmaceuticals, including Signet in India, and how much an easy comparison? How do the comparisons change over the next two quarters?"
- Why: the case had IMCD losing the regional race to Azelis and Asia-Pacific margins drifting from 12.6% to 12.0%; Morgan Stanley notes the comparisons were light.
- Tell: mandate wins = more durable gross margin (judgement 4); a tariff rebound = it does not repeat.

B3.2 The Americas. "Americas organic gross profit grew 3.4% against Azelis' 14.9%, partly because you are leaving some supplier relationships. Which, how much gross profit, and when is the exit lapped?"
- Why: it separates a choice from a loss of share; the coast-to-coast integration is "several quarters" from bearing fruit.
- Tell: a dated, sized exit = the Americas gap is temporary; vague = a share problem the model does not carry.

B3.3 EMEA. "EMEA organic gross profit grew 8.3% against Azelis' 2.1%. How much came from mandates won from competitors, for example lines BASF moved from Brenntag Specialties?"
- Why: principals outsourcing smaller accounts adds about one point a year of organic gross profit in the case; mandate wins would be on top.
- Tell: named mandate wins = the structural driver is accelerating.

B3.4 Currency. "With 56.5% of revenue outside the euro and unhedged, and Latin America and Asia often quoted in dollars but invoiced in local currency, what is the sensitivity to a 10% move in the dollar?"
- Why: the memo puts translation at EUR 0.11 of cash EPS per 10% dollar move, and Brazil lost about 10% of local sales in 2025 on a weaker dollar.
- Tell: a larger transaction effect than translation alone = another unmodelled swing.

B4. Principals and the moat

B4.1 ★ Exclusivity. "Are principals' contract reviews getting shorter, and are more appointing two or three distributors per region? What share of gross profit is under exclusive mandates today?"
- Why: exclusivity covers more than 90% of contracts and underpins the moat on the 80% specialty book; a Caldic managing director says principals increasingly split regions.
- Tell: "stable" = the moat holds; any trend to split regions narrows the case's foundations.

B4.2 Chinese principals. "How do you approach mandates from Chinese producers, given that a Western principal pulled lines from Azelis over a Chinese exclusive?"
- Why: the memo names that precedent as what would change its view of the moat.
- Tell: a clear policy = low risk; opportunistic = a mandate risk.

B4.3 Chinese substitution in specialties. "Where are you seeing Chinese producers reach customised specialties, not only semi-specialties?"
- Why: a BASF director says Chinese producers reach customised specialties at 30% lower cost; customers cap substitution at 20% to 30% of a formulation's volume because of re-approval risk.
- Tell: "only in semi-specialties" = the 80% is protected.

B5. Competition and the acquisition market

B5.1 Brenntag's new platform. "Brenntag's new platform targets smaller and mid-sized accounts. How much does that overlap with your customers, and has anything changed since its capital markets day?"
- Why: small and mid-sized accounts outsourced by principals are IMCD's structural growth source.
- Tell: little overlap = no change; "they are coming after the same accounts" = a new competitive risk the memo does not carry.

B5.2 ★ Acquisition prices (or save for 2 December). "With Azelis and others constrained by leverage, are sellers' price expectations falling? What multiples have recent bolt-ons cost compared with 2025?"
- Why: at today's valuation a EUR 100m buyback adds about 0.75% to cash EPS against 0.35% for a deal priced like 2025's average (1.3 times sales). A deal at 9 times EBITDA adds about 1.2%.
- Tell: cheaper deals = the acquisition window, the strongest mid-term driver, is opening; "sellers still have Covid-era results in mind" = the window is later.

B5.3 Mandate losses. "Has any principal moved business away from IMCD to Brenntag or Azelis this year?"
- Why: the case records IMCD winning mandates from both; a loss would be the first contrary evidence.
- Tell: none = the share argument holds.

B6. Capital allocation and the balance sheet

B6.1 ★ Buyback against acquisitions (or save for 3 December). "The 10% buyback authority to October 2027 is unused. How does the board compare a buyback with an acquisition at today's valuation, and what leverage would it be comfortable at?"

- Tell: any openness to a buyback is upside nobody models; "acquisitions first" keeps it out of the case.

B6.2 Working capital. "Is the rule that inventory buying must be backed by firm customer orders still in force, given working-capital days of 74 at end-September?"

B6.3 The dividend. "Is paying out about 35% of cash EPS still the dividend practice?"
- Why: that practice gives EUR 2.10 for FY2026 against a EUR 1.97 consensus, the one line where the case is above consensus.
- Tell: "yes" = a likely positive in February.
