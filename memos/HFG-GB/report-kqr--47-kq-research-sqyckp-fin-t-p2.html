Section 2 – Contract Structures and Pass-Through Mechanics

Section summary
  1. Hilton's main contracts use either transparent cost-plus or fee-per-kilogram structures to pass through raw material costs.
  2. Structural pricing lags remain for overheads like labour and energy, but customer-provided supply chain finance reduces financing costs.
  3. Peer models like Cranswick's quarterly cost-of-production resets offer a benchmark for rapid pass-through speed in the protein sector.

Contractual structures and retail agreements

1.
Stable retail agreements: Former employees note that Hilton's contract with Tesco in the UK and Ireland operates on an open-book model with processing costs fixed annually. According to Deutsche Bank's report of 19 May 2026, Hilton recently extended its commercial partnership with Tesco, providing substantial volume visibility. Therefore, these agreements insulate Hilton from long-term raw material price volatility but expose it to annual renegotiation risks.
2.
Variable fee structure: Hilton's agreements in Australia and New Zealand with Woolworths utilize a fee-per-kilogram structure to protect core packing margins. A Deutsche Bank note on 3 September 2026 explains that this fee structure can lead to margin shifts during periods of high beef prices. This means that while cash profits are protected, operating margins are mathematically squeezed when raw material prices rise.
3.
Secured european partnerships: Hilton has successfully delivered critical contract renewals for core retail meats with major customers like Coop Danmark, Salling Group, and Albert Heijn. Deutsche Bank's report of 29 January 2026 confirms these renewals demonstrate the resilience of Hilton's core relationships in northern Europe. These long-term retail agreements underpin the group's volume stability across its continental packing facilities.

Deutsche Bank's report of 14 February 2025 highlights that volumes with Zabka have tripled over three years and now support Romania expansion. This relationship demonstrates Hilton's ability to diversify away from traditional red meat into fast-growing prepared food categories.

According to Hilton's interim report on 2 September 2026, operations are on track to commence in January 2027. Hilton's interim report of 2 September 2026 expects operations at the new meat facility to commence in late 2026. The venture combines Hilton's packing expertise with NADEC's local livestock supply to generate high return on capital in a growing market.

Section 3 – Pricing Behavior Under Material Cost Inflation and Deflation

Section summary
  1. Hilton's adjusted operating margins underwent significant compression during the 2022 and 2025 raw material inflation spikes.
  2. Rapid cost pass-through was severely challenged by high white fish and smoked salmon inflation.
  3. Australian livestock deflation in 2023 and 2024 supported strong APAC volume growth and stable packing fees.
  4. Peer comparisons show Hilton's fee-per-kilogram model faces different margin dynamics than integrated processors.

Impact of historical inflation cycles

1.
Compressed operating margins: Hilton experienced severe profitability pressure during the 2022 inflationary spike because of lagging cost pass-through mechanisms. Shore Capital's note of 8 November 2022 indicates that a perfect storm of soaring seafood costs and delayed retail resets forced significant earnings downgrades. This structural pricing lag means that rapid input inflation temporarily compresses intermediate margins before retail recovery is achieved.
2.
Persistent inflationary pressure: The 2025 inflation cycle highlighted continued vulnerabilities in Hilton's West region due to sustained input cost spikes in core red meat. Deutsche Bank's report of 3 September 2025 explains that UK and Ireland revenues rose 12.4% primarily driven by beef price inflation. Because retail meat volumes only grew 1.7%, Hilton had to actively rely on product reformulations and promotional strategies to prevent severe volume erosion across major supermarket partners.
3.
Severe seafood inflation: Extreme price spikes in white fish during 2025 severely impacted Hilton's Seachill business and dampened consumer demand. Deutsche Bank's report of 3 September 2025 highlights that cod prices soared 30% and haddock surged 90% due to quota cuts. This raw material surge compressed UK seafood margins to 2.80% in the first half of 2025. Consequently, Hilton had to introduce alternative species like hake to protect product affordability.
4.
Smoked salmon setbacks: Operational disruptions at Foppen's Greek facility in 2025 compounded Hilton's seafood division losses and disrupted US export channels. Hilton's interim report of 3 September 2025 shows that regulatory export restrictions forced a temporary transition of production from Greece to the Netherlands. Consequently, air-freighting products to the US to maintain customer service incurred non-underlying logistics costs of £2 million, dragging down overall European segment profitability.

Post-inflation recovery and livestock cycles

5.
Gradual margin recovery: Hilton achieved a gradual recovery in its financial performance during 2023 and 2024 as historic input inflation began to moderate. Hilton's earnings call transcript of 8 April 2025 highlights that group operating profit margins successfully improved from 2.40% in 2023 to 2.60% in 2024. This positive development indicates that Hilton's business model can rebuild its core packing margins once the unprecedented cost spikes of previous cycles stabilize.
6.
Defensive profit insulation: Deflation in Australian raw material prices during 2023 and 2024 protected Hilton's regional cash profits despite declining revenues. Deutsche Bank's report of 3 September 2026 notes that East region operating margins remained stable at 2.45% because of fee-per-kilogram contract structures in Australia. This contractual mechanism ensures that while regional turnover fluctuates alongside livestock prices, Hilton's unit packing fees remain insulated from underlying agricultural deflation cycles.
7.
Pork oversupply mitigation: Oversupply in European pork markets during historical cycles tested Hilton's ability to maintain processing fees across continental facilities. Shore Capital's report of 8 November 2022 notes that single-customer continental facilities delivered solid volume growth and outperformed domestic markets. Because Hilton's open-book model passes raw material movements directly to retailers, pork deflation benefited retail volumes while protecting the packer's underlying fee per package.
8.
Unsurfaced livestock forecasts: Research did not surface the specific Morgan Stanley notes of August and September 2026 regarding Australian cattle declines. However, Deutsche Bank's note of 3 September 2026 indicates that Hilton's Australian fee-per-kilogram contracts protect cash profits during beef cycles. Consequently, intermediate margins may shift mathematically when raw prices fluctuate, but long-term cash generation remains insulated.
