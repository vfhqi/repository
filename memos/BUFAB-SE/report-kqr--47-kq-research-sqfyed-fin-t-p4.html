Section 2 – Net Financial Items and Debt Sensitivity

Section summary
  1. Bufab faces elevated net financial items due to its high reliance on floating-rate credit facilities that expose its earnings directly to interest rate cycles.
  2. Comparing Bufab with Indutrade reveals similar floating-rate sensitivities, while OEM International remains entirely insulated from interest rate hikes due to its net cash position.

Capital Structure and Facility Costs

1.
Stable financing: Bufab secured its primary funding through a large SEK 4,800 million revolving facility from Handelsbanken and SEB that runs until July 2028. This credit facility replaced the previous SEK 4,000 million facility and provides essential capital for future acquisitions or operations. This long-term framework reduces refinancing risks but keeps Bufab exposed to variable banking margins.
2.
Short-term exposure: Bufab maintains bank loans with an average remaining fixed-rate interest period of only three months. The company notes in its 2025 Annual Report that this floating-rate structure leads to immediate impacts on interest expenses when base rates shift. Rapid changes in Swedish or European central bank rates will quickly pass through to the income statement.
3.
Upward pressure: Bufab saw its adjusted net debt rise to SEK 2,785 million in September 2026 due to recent acquisitions and dividend payments. This level increased from SEK 2,330 million in the previous year, which reflects substantial capital outflows for expansion during 2026. Higher debt combined with sticky interest rates threatens to elevate the net interest burden over the next two years.
4.
Moderate leverage: Bufab reported an adjusted net debt to EBITDA ratio of 2.2 times in September 2026. This metric improved slightly from 2.3 times in September 2025, which indicates stable operating cash generation despite the increased absolute debt. Maintaining this leverage ratio within its target range of 2.0 to 3.0 times is critical for securing favorable interest rates.
5.
Refinancing risk: Bufab reduces its immediate liquidity pressure by securing an option to extend its main credit facility for two additional years. The company explains in its 2025 Annual Report that executive management regularly monitors these refinancing needs at least twelve months prior to maturity. This proactive risk management ensures that Bufab can maintain stable access to bank loans during tight credit cycles.

Rate Sensitivity and Policy Impact

6.
Symmetrical sensitivity: Bufab disclosed that a one percentage point change in interest rates affects its profit after financial items by SEK 34 million. The company confirms in its 2025 Annual Report that this sensitivity reflects its closing net debt structure. Any future monetary easing will directly boost earnings per share by lowering interest expenses.
7.
Non-linear outcomes: Bufab estimated that a five percentage point change in market rates would impact its financial profit by SEK 172 million. This figure from the 2025 Annual Report shows that extreme rate spikes risk causing severe earnings contraction unless hedging is increased. The non-linear progression of this interest sensitivity underscores the risk of unexpected inflation shocks.
8.
Monetary headwind: Riksbank policy rates are projected to peak higher as Swedish core inflation begins to pick up significantly from December 2026. Morgan Stanley expects a second rate hike in February 2027 to reach 2.25 percent, up from previous assumptions. This hawkish monetary path will postpone any anticipated interest expense relief for Bufab during fiscal year 2027.
9.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie projected that Bufab net interest expense will actually fall to SEK 150 million in 2027 and SEK 115 million in 2028. This optimistic forecast is based on expected rate cuts and strong operating cash flow that will allow the company to deleverage rapidly. If these projections prove correct, Bufab will see substantial earnings per share accretion regardless of near-term central bank pauses.

Insulated peer: OEM International provides a stark contrast with a net cash position of SEK 178 million as of September 2026. This conservative balance sheet allows the peer to fund acquisitions through internal cash rather than costly bank facilities.

Section 3 – Amortisation, PPA, and Earn-Out Liabilities

Section summary
  1. Bufab uses straight-line amortisation over ten years for acquired customer relationships which regularises its intangible asset schedules.
  2. Comparing peer structures shows Addtech and Lagercrantz rely heavily on ten-year amortisation periods for customer relations and structured level three contingent considerations.

Purchase Price Allocation and Reallocation

1.
Strategic reallocation: Bufab adjusted its initial accounting for the novia Group acquisition during the first quarter of 2026. The company reallocated SEK 180 million from goodwill to customer relationships after obtaining new valuation information. This newly identified asset is amortised over a ten-year useful life and added SEK 5.7 million in amortisation expenses during the quarter. This adjustment reduces reported earnings but leaves cash flows entirely unaffected over the period.
2.
Brand identification: Bufab completed the preliminary purchase price allocation for D.C. Handrails and Ironwork Parts in July 2026. Out of the SEK 420 million purchase consideration, the company allocated SEK 44 million to identified brand value and SEK 184 million to goodwill. The remaining acquired net assets of SEK 236 million included cash and cash equivalents of SEK 148 million. This transaction indicates that Bufab continues to pay premium goodwill multiples for niche component distributors.
3.
Goodwill testing: Bufab allocates its accumulated goodwill to its lowest cashgenerating units to perform mandatory annual impairment tests. The company holds SEK 959 million in goodwill within the Europe West segment and SEK 652 million in the UK and Ireland segment. Management notes that even if sales growth drops to zero percent, no impairment would be required. This suggests that goodwill carries low near-term write-down risks for investors.
4.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reported that Bufab trades at a motivated valuation discount to peers due to its lower return on capital and weaker balance sheet. This valuation discount exists despite the fact that Bufab achieved an EPS compound growth rate of 17 percent between 2014 and 2024. This historical record indicates that M&A accounting and amortisation schedules are less critical to valuation than improving underlying operational returns.
5.
Fair value adjustments: Management remeasures outstanding contingent considerations on an ongoing basis using Level 3 inputs based on business plans and forecasts. Outstanding additional purchase considerations were SEK 225 million at the end of 2025, down from SEK 324 million in 2024. Any discrepancies between estimated performance and actual results are recognized in consolidated profit or loss. These accounting adjustments can introduce unexpected volatility into reported net income figures.

This additional consideration may become payable in installments across 2026, 2027, and 2028 depending on earnings performance. The company initially recognized a contingent consideration liability of SEK 65 million for this agreement. These structured payments help Bufab mitigate post-acquisition performance risks on large transactions.
8.
DC Iron liabilities: Bufab established a performance-based contingent consideration for D.C. Iron that is linked to revenue and profit growth. This contingent liability could result in cash payments of up to SEK 103 million between 2027 and 2030. The transaction was financed through existing credit facilities, which will increase net debt until these liabilities are resolved. This earn-out profile will extend cash flow pressure into the late part of this decade.

This outcome compared with a cash outflow of SEK 3 million in the first quarter of 2025. The lack of immediate outflows allowed Bufab to generate SEK 201 million in operating cash flow during the quarter. Managing these payment schedules is crucial for Bufab to sustain solid cash conversion metrics.

  1. Bufab faces SEK 72 million in CBAM cash outflows during 2027, but the downstream extension in 2028 will protect its regional pricing power.

  3. Acquisition-driven geographic shifts will keep Bufab's effective tax rate near 25 percent, while its overall Pillar Two minimum tax exposure remains minimal.

Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism Cash Impacts

1.
Elevated cash provisions: Bufab recognized SEK 72 million in CBAM provisions during the first nine months of 2026. These non-cash provisions grew from SEK 50 million in June 2026 as import obligations accumulated under the new framework. This accounting accrual suppresses reported earnings but temporarily inflates operating cash flows until cash outflows commence.
2.
Delayed cash settlements: Bufab expects the accumulated CBAM liabilities to be settled in cash during the first three quarters of 2027. Group management confirmed in its July 2026 earnings call that these payments will create direct cash outflows next year. Consequently, this timing mismatch will reduce free cash flow in fiscal 2027 even as operating margins normalize.
3.
Varying compliance costs: Addtech also reported that complying with stricter EU regulations like CBAM requires significant resource allocations. These regulatory demands increase the administrative burden on decentralized distributors who must audit numerous global supplier networks. The rising cost of compliance threatens to drag on operating efficiency unless internal audit systems are automated.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some analysts argue that CBAM will act as a supportive regional pricing factor that helps distributors maintain their spreads. Deutsche Bank reported in October 2026 that a firmer European price floor can support inventory values and offset compliance costs. Therefore, the financial penalty of CBAM might be smaller than raw provision figures suggest because domestic price levels will adjust upward.
5.
Accumulating trade barriers: Bufab continues to actively manage several international trade barriers including tariffs in the United States and European environmental regulations. The group notes that managing these complex regulatory requirements actually favors scaled distributors over smaller competitors. Therefore, the rise in trade friction can help Bufab capture market share despite near-term cost pressures.

Importers of these downstream goods will be forced to track emissions or pay heavy penalties.
8.
Altered sourcing dynamics: Non-EU suppliers who cannot provide verified actual emissions data will face punitive default markups under the new rules. Morgan Stanley reported in September 2026 that default values carry a 20 percent markup in 2027. This default framework will raise the cost floor of imports and squeeze unverified supply chains.

Section 5 – Currency, Share Count, and Dividends

Section summary
  1. Bufab faces transaction headwinds from its structural USD purchase mismatch and translation risks on EUR and GBP sales.
  2. The group's share count remains tightly controlled through targeted buybacks that hedge employee option programs and prevent share dilution.
  3. Bufab’s proposed dividends align closely with its historical payout ratio and remain highly sustainable within its established financial policy.

Currency Exposures and Mismatch Risks

1.
Structural transaction exposure: Bufab faces significant transaction exposure due to a structural currency mismatch in its sourcing and sales operations. The company notes in its 2025 Annual Report that its largest net currency exposure relates to the US dollar because merchandise sourcing from Asian markets is predominantly conducted in USD, whereas USD-denominated sales are comparatively small. During the 2025 financial year, Bufab’s gross USD costs reached SEK 2,228 million against gross USD sales of only SEK 1,290 million. This mismatch creates a persistent short position in USD that directly penalizes operating margins when the Swedish krona weakens against the US dollar.
2.
Quantifiable sensitivity thresholds: The financial impact of exchange rate fluctuations on Bufab's operational profit is highly sensitive to USD movements. The company disclosed in its 2025 Annual Report that a 1 percentage point strengthening of the USD against the SEK has a negative impact of SEK -9 million on operating profit. Furthermore, a 5 percentage point strengthening of the USD against the SEK leads to a negative impact of SEK -47 million on group operating profit, assuming all other variables remain constant. This sensitivity makes the forecasting of Bufab's near-term earnings highly dependent on USD/SEK forward curves.
3.
Translation exposure dynamics: Bufab’s translation exposure is primarily driven by its extensive sales network across the Eurozone and the United Kingdom. The group reported in its 2025 Annual Report that its gross EUR sales reached SEK 3,561 million against EUR costs of SEK 2,683 million, while gross GBP sales reached SEK 1,415 million against GBP costs of SEK 660 million. A 1 percentage point strengthening of the EUR against the SEK has a positive impact of SEK 9 million on operating profit, while a 1 percentage point strengthening of the GBP has a positive impact of SEK 8 million. These translation effects can partially offset USD transaction headwinds during periods of broad-based SEK weakness.
4.
Contrary evidence: DNB Carnegie reported in February 2026 that foreign exchange headwinds from a stronger SEK versus the EUR, USD, and GBP offset positive operational revisions, lowering 2027–2028 adjusted EPS estimates by approximately 1%. This demonstrates that a strengthening Swedish currency represents an earnings headwind rather than a tailwind because translation losses on foreign sales can outweigh transaction savings on USD sourcing.

Peer Currency and Hedging Comparisons

5.
Bossard risk mitigation: Peer distributor Bossard Holding AG manages its currency risks through a localized purchasing model that contrasts with Bufab's centralized exposure. Bossard noted in its 2025 Annual Report that its operating transactions are predominantly conducted in the local currencies of its individual group companies, keeping transactional currency risks relatively low. For payments to foreign suppliers that fall outside local currency zones, Bossard utilizes targeted foreign exchange forward contracts. This decentralized localcurrency alignment shields Bossard's gross margins from the severe transactional mismatches experienced by Bufab.
6.
Translation hedging strategies: Bufab utilizes structural financing hedges to mitigate the translation impact of foreign net assets on its consolidated equity. The company notes in its 2025 Annual Report that it raises loans in EUR, GBP, and USD to match the currency of its foreign subsidiaries' net assets. These currency loans are designated as effective hedges under IFRS, allowing exchange rate gains and losses to be recognized in other comprehensive income. This hedging structure reduces capital volatility but does not protect the transactional operating flows from currency swings.

Share Count, Option Dilution, and Buyback Programs

7.
Strict share count preservation: Bufab maintains a highly controlled share capital structure and actively hedges against any dilution from its employee incentive programs. The group's total outstanding shares remained stable at 190,552,665 during the first nine months of 2026. The Board of Directors proposed at the 2026 Annual General Meeting that all obligations under the long-term incentive program LTI 2026/2029 be fulfilled through the acquisition and transfer of treasury shares, ensuring no new shares are issued. This structural choice guarantees that option exercises do not dilute existing equity holders.
8.
Targeted share repurchases: Bufab executes programmatic share buybacks specifically to secure its commitments under outstanding share savings programs. During the second quarter of 2026, the company repurchased 580,000 shares on Nasdaq Stockholm under the LTI 2026/2029 authorization. Following these transactions, Bufab’s total treasury holdings rose to 1,358,240 shares as of September 2026, representing an increase from 765,575 shares at the end of 2025. These repurchased treasury shares do not carry dividend rights, slightly reducing the dividend-paying share base.
9.
Incentive cost and EPS impact: The operating cost of Bufab's long-term incentive programs remains marginal relative to its consolidated earnings. The board estimated in March 2026 that the total cost of LTI 2026/2029 would amount to approximately SEK 32.04 million over its three-year vesting period, assuming a 10% annual share price increase and 50% target achievement. This program's estimated impact on earnings per share was only SEK -0.17 for the year. Consequently, option programs present a negligible risk to Bufab's near-term EPS growth trajectory.

Dividend Policy and Capital Sustainability

10.
Established payout targets: Bufab's dividend policy targets a payout of 30% to 60% of annual net profit. For the 2024 financial year, the Board of Directors proposed a dividend of SEK 5.25 per share, representing approximately 36% of net profit. This dividend proposal resulted in a total cash distribution of SEK 199 million, which was settled in May 2025. The group’s historical payout ratio has remained highly consistent, averaging 28% of profit after tax since 2014.
11.
Cash flow sustainability: Bufab's dividend distributions are fully covered by its robust operational cash generation and low capital intensity. The company reported a cash conversion ratio of 111% for the full year of 2024, with cash flow from operations reaching SEK 1,101 million. During the first nine months of 2026, cash flow from operations remained strong at SEK 989 million, easily covering the paid dividend of SEK 247 million. This cash generation ensures Bufab can comfortably fund organic reinvestment, M&A, and dividends.
12.
Other side of the story: While Bufab's historical dividend payout has remained near the lower bound of its 30% to 60% target to prioritize M&A deleveraging, some brokers project that the distribution mix will shift significantly as acquisition growth moderates. DNB Carnegie estimated in April 2026 that Bufab's near-term payout ratio will remain conservative at 30% to 40% during the peak M&A phase, but will sustainably expand to 80% after 2033 as the business matures and net debt to EBITDA falls to 1.5x. This long-term outlook suggests that current dividend levels represent a floor, with significant potential for payout expansion as capital allocation priorities shift.
