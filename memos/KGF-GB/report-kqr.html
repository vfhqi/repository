What do brokers, former retail media and marketplace heads and the companies expect for 2027 and 2028, and are suppliers moving advertising budgets toward or away from these networks as artificial intelligence shopping agents start to handle product search?"
2.

Over the next three years, these streams will be the primary driver of CECONOMY's adjusted EBIT expansion to €800 million.
4.

  1. European store retailers are rapidly scaling high-margin retail media networks to secure new digital advertising streams from suppliers.
  2. High-margin digital ad income typically flows through to operating profit at rates far exceeding traditional brick-and-mortar retail margins.

Consequently, the expanding digital network is unlocking high-margin alternative revenue streams to support overall profitability.

The retailer reported that its retail media income reached 91 million euros in fiscal year 2024/25. These initiatives are driving structural margin expansion ahead of traditional consumer electronics sales.

Digital advancement: Dunelm has prioritized investments in its digital ecosystem to lay the groundwork for future monetization opportunities. The homewares retailer reported that digital sales increased to 41% of total sales in the first quarter of fiscal year 2027. Section summary
  1. European store retailers are rapidly scaling third-party marketplaces to capture high-margin commission revenues.
  2. Asset-light marketplace platforms require minimal incremental capital expenditure compared to traditional inventory-holding retail models.
  3. While marketplace drop-through margins are highly attractive at maturity, initial platform development costs can limit near-term earnings contributions.

This platform development is converting CECONOMY into a service-led platform that reduces dependency on low-margin inventory sales.

Section summary
  1. Rapidly evolving artificial intelligence shopping agents pose a dual threat of traffic disintermediation and retail media disruption for European store retailers.

  3. Supplier budgets remain anchored to closed retail networks for now due to the superior transaction intent and closed-loop measurement of first-party purchase data.

If other retailers follow suit, it could protect the direct customer relationship at the expense of external traffic.

Morgan Stanley reported that strict European regulations, including GDPR and the Digital Markets Act, could delay or constrain agentic AI disruption relative to the US.
