2.
Rising monetary hawkishness: the Riksbank's signal for imminent rate hikes in Q4 2026 provides a late- cycle NII cushion against earlier normalization fears
3.
Structural fee recovery: double-digit growth in asset management and card fees is beginning to offset the normalization of deposit margins across the Nordic footprint
4.
Refined capital allocation: the shift toward higher special dividends over buybacks in Q4 2025 marks a tactical change to reach the 300bps target buffer

Key drivers ranked by EPS impact

5.
Dominant driver (1): higher-for-longer Swedish policy rates reaching 2.07% by Q1 2027 provides a higher floor for structural NII than prior cycles
6.
Critical driver (2): normalization of corporate investment activity is essential to drive the high-single- digit volume growth required to sustain double-digit ROE targets
7.
Efficiency driver (3): execution on the SEK 33.3bn cost target for 2026 is the primary internal lever to defend margins against wage-driven inflation pressures
8.
Asset driver (4): monetization of the pension surplus, currently 10% of market cap, remains a significant but timing-uncertain catalyst for total shareholder return
9.
Regulatory driver (5): finalization of Baltic IRB models remains a 50bps pro-forma capital headwind that constrains the absolute magnitude of near-term share buybacks

Recent delivery and credibility

11.
Superior dividend yield: total DPS of SEK 11 significantly exceeded the SEK 9.10 consensus, reinforcing management's commitment to returning capital despite P&L noise
12.
Honest-tracker archetype: if management sustains its 15% ROE aspiration, its track record suggests guidance is a floor rather than a maximum stretch target
