Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The concentration of Greggs' near-term earnings drivers will determine whether its historically high returns can recover or will remain depressed by rising supply chain depreciation and wage inflation.

Related financial questions concerning capital returns, physical expansion limits, and business-to-business growth will shape the skew of potential earnings per share outcomes.

Financial Significance of Earnings Drivers

1.
Critical inquiry: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Pressured margins: Greggs faces a pivot where its operating margin is heavily influenced by the execution of its supply chain industrialisation. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 4 March 2025 that new supply chain investments are expected to dilute operating margins by 40 basis points in both 2025 and 2026. This means that any delays or underutilisation in these major projects directly threatens the recovery of earnings per share over the forecast horizon.
3.
Escalating wages: Greggs remains highly exposed to regulated wage cost developments due to its labour-intensive service model. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 10 February 2026 that the company has faced unprecedented inflation in regulated labour costs from the National Living Wage. If wage cost growth continues to outpace pricing recovery, operating deleverage could severely depress future earnings per share outcomes.
4.
Strategic pricing: Greggs must balance potential price increases against volume-driven demand elasticities to maintain healthy margins. Rothschild and Co Redburn warned on 22 July 2025 that relying too heavily on pricing to drive growth could trigger adverse elasticity effects and hurt margins. Conversely, shifting toward volume-led growth in an inflationary environment would exert incremental pressure on margins due to higher labor and distribution costs.
5.
Rising cashflows: Greggs is approaching a financial inflection point as its major capital expenditure cycle draws to a close. Jefferies Research highlighted on 24 April 2026 that capital requirements will drop off significantly after the Derby and Kettering projects complete, boosting free cash flow. This cash generation creates potential for special dividends and share buybacks that could enhance earnings per share.

Section 2 – Financial Drivers of the Mid-Term Forecast vs. Historical Base Case

Section summary
  1. Greggs faces a transition from historical price-led growth to a more capital-efficient model focused on volume recovery and B2B expansion.
  2. Declining transaction volumes and elevated cost inflation pose near-term headwinds to the group's retail operating margin.
  3. Easing capital expenditure requirements will support enhanced cash generation and potential capital returns over the next four years.

Retail Growth Dynamics

1.
Slowing transaction: Greggs experienced a deceleration in retail volume momentum as consumer spending became increasingly constrained during the historical base case. Although total sales rose by 6.8% to £2,151 million in 2025, company-managed like-for-like sales growth slowed to 2.4% due to challenging market conditions. This retail slowdown has dragged retail operating margins downward because of operating deleverage across the group's fixed store network.
2.
Persistent volume: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted that Greggs' sales growth slowed in early 2026, pointing to negative underlying volume trends. The bank noted that average pricing was increased by another 3% in December 2025 to achieve a 6% annualised price run-rate. High street footfall remained weak, and like-forlike volumes remained in decline because consumer demand showed high price elasticity.
3.
Muted volume: Jefferies Research warned that structural headwinds from weight-loss drugs are beginning to affect transactional demand. The broker downgraded the stock to Hold because of concerns that GLP-1 usage will structurally depress retail consumption patterns over the next 18 months. This structural shift implies that historical volume drivers cannot easily be replicated in the mid-term forecast.
4.
Steady expansion: Greggs continues to execute its store-rollout strategy with a target of 100 to 110 net new shops in 2026. Management reiterated its long-term ambition to establish a network of at least 3,500 shops in the UK. The company plans to maintain a shop opening rate of at least 100 net openings annually over the medium term to expand geographical coverage.

Channel and B2B Diversification

5.
Rapid expansion: Greggs expanded its business-to-business channel to £137.5 million in the first half of 2026, representing an 18.2% growth compared to the prior year. This segment includes franchise sales and wholesale supply to partners like Iceland Foods and Tesco. Business-to-business sales act as a critical driver to diversify the group's retail risks and capture incremental out-of-home snacking occasions.
6.
Resilient partner: Franchised shops managed by partners showed superior resilience compared to company- managed shops during the peak inflation cycle. Franchised units delivered a 4.3% like-for-like system sales growth in 2025, outperforming company-managed shops by 1.9 percentage points. Management aims to leverage these franchise channels because they generate high-margin royalty streams without significant direct capital investment.
7.
Temporary headwinds: Deutsche Bank Research reported that Greggs encountered operational issues with a major petrol-forecourt franchise partner in 2026. The franchise partner is currently going through a structural restructuring that has temporarily dampened the segment's overall performance. Excluding this specific partner, franchised like-for-like sales growth remained above company-managed shops at more than 2% in the first half of the year.
8.
Innovative formats: Greggs is trial-testing smaller formats such as "bitesize Greggs" and "Greggs Express" to access locations with physical constraints. These smaller-footprint formats allow the group to open new outlets in railway stations and supermarkets where standard shops are unviable. Developing these flexible formats will enhance store-opening potential and mitigate high street footfall declines.

Cost Pressures and Supply Chain

9.
Easing inflation: Greggs managed its cost profile tightly as like-for-like cost inflation reduced to circa 2% in 2026 from 5.5% in 2025. This reduction was driven by structural cost savings of £11 million targeted for the full year. However, management has warned that the business faces greater inflationary pressures in 2027 due to rising national wage mandates.
10.
Strategic restructuring: Deutsche Bank Research detailed that Greggs is proposed to close four manufacturing sites at a cash cost of £60 million. This consolidation is designed to generate £20 million in annual cost savings from 2028 or 2029. Although this restructuring will improve long-term efficiency, incremental overhead costs in 2027 will act as a temporary drag on profit margins.
11.
Challenging progression: Jefferies Research cautioned that while 2026 profit outcomes have modestly improved, 2027 will remain challenging. The upcoming start-up costs from Kettering and Derby distribution centres are expected to pressure operating margins next year. Consequently, the group's mid-term profit progression is highly dependent on volume recovery to absorb these fixed logistics overheads.
12.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research argued that the mid-term margin recovery remains highly constrained by the group's rising depreciation expenses. The bank noted that the launch of the Derby warehouse and Kettering facility will trigger elevated depreciation charges in late 2026 and 2027. This depreciation, combined with a later wage award, is expected to dilute profit margins by 40 basis points in the upcoming fiscal years.

Capital Allocation and Returns

13.
Normalising investment: Greggs is passing the peak of its multi-year capital expenditure program that supported logistics expansion. Capital expenditure guidance was cut to £180 million for 2026 from a record £287.5 million in 2025. Management expects annual capex to normalise to between £150 million and £170 million starting in 2027.
14.
Growing cash: Jefferies Research estimated that Greggs will generate over £150 million per annum in free cash flow as infrastructure investments conclude. This substantial cash flow generation is expected to support stable valuation multiples and limit the downside risk to the company's share price. Investors are increasingly looking to this cash generation as the core driver of mid-term shareholder value.
15.
Enhanced returns: Rothschild & Co Redburn projected that the completion of the major supply chain investments will enable capital returns to expand. The broker highlighted that capital expenditure had been highly elevated for several years to support store rollout targets. As capex falls, Greggs will enter a highly cash-generative phase that can fund special dividends and buybacks under its capital allocation policy.

Section 3 – Operating Margin Dynamics and the 50 Basis Point Sensitivity Rule

Section summary
  1. Greggs' operating margin fell to 8.7% in 2025 as structural wage inflation and logistics investments broke the historical 50 basis point sensitivity rule.
  2. Centralized production and manufacturing consolidation will protect medium-term operating margins against escalating National Living Wage pressures.
  3. Peer comparisons with travel concessionaires and pub operators highlight Greggs' superior margin stability despite its high labor intensity.

Historical Margin Dynamics

1.
Decreasing profitability: Greggs saw its operating margin decline to 8.7% in 2025 from 9.7% in 2024 due to significant cost pressures. Management reported in their 2025 annual report on 28 September 2026 that this 100 basis point contraction resulted from higher fixed manufacturing and logistics overheads. This margin pressure reduced underlying pre-tax profit by 9.4%, showing that lower volumes can quickly dilute earnings.
2.
Historical compression: Greggs experienced its most severe margin disruption during the 2020 pandemic period. Rothschild & Co Redburn showed in their October 2026 report that EBIT margin recovered strongly during 2021 before stabilizing around 9.7% in 2024. This demonstrates that margins are highly resilient once store volumes normalize after external disruptions.
3.
Stable performance: Greggs achieved a resilient 9.7% operating margin in 2024 despite high inflation. The group reported in their 2025 preliminary results on 3 March 2026 that 2024 benefited from cost-saving initiatives worth £10.6 million. This indicates that strong cost control and vertical integration can offset initial wage inflation.
4.
Operational deleverage: Greggs experienced severe profit pressure in 2025 as wage inflation outpaced volume growth. Management stated on their 2025 earnings call on 3 March 2026 that volume growth was negative in 2025. This negative trend meant the group suffered from significant store-level operational deleverage because fixed shop rent and occupancy costs remained stable.

Testing the Sensitivity Rule

5.
Disrupted relationships: Greggs broke the historical 50 basis point sensitivity rule in 2025 because of extreme cost-push inflation. Jefferies Research had found in May 2020 that Greggs' margin moved by 50 basis points or less when like-for-like sales were between 0% and 4.5%. However, the operating margin fell by 100 basis points in 2025 despite like-for-like sales growing by 2.4% in company-managed shops.
6.
Revised expectations: Jefferies Research downgraded Greggs to Hold on 8 February 2026 after recognizing structural volume headwinds. The broker modeled a -50 basis point margin contraction for fiscal year 2026 and -30 basis points for 2027. This indicates that operating leverage is turning negative because samestore sales no longer comfortably outpace high cost inflation.
7.
Defensive pricing: Greggs maintained a relatively flat gross margin through active pricing despite escalating costs. Management explained on their 2025 earnings call on 3 March 2026 that price-led revenue is highly margin-accretive with 100% drop-through to gross profit. However, relying on pricing to offset cost inflation has triggered negative transactional volumes, which dilutes overall operating margin.
8.
Volume drop-through: Greggs' margin recovery is highly sensitive to the balance between volume and price growth. Rothschild & Co Redburn explained on 22 July 2025 that price increases have 100% drop-through to profit, whereas volume growth carries ingredient and labor costs. This means that volume-led growth exerts margin pressure under high supply chain inflation, contradicting the simple assumptions of the historical sensitivity rule.

Capacity Overheads and Cost Savings

9.
Phased headwinds: Greggs faces persistent margin dilution over the next two fiscal years from new industrial capacity. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 5 March 2025 that the Derby and Kettering facilities will dilute operating margins by 40 basis points in 2025 and 2026. This means that capacity underutilisation will drag margins until store network expansion can fully absorb the heavy fixed depreciation charges.
10.
Structural savings: Greggs announced a major restructuring in September 2026 to consolidate its manufacturing footprint. Management reported in their Q3 trading update on 30 September 2026 that closing four sites will cost £60 million but generate £20 million in annual pre-tax savings by 2029. This restructuring is vital because it alleviates severe labor cost pressures arising from the National Living Wage.
11.
Escalating overheads: Greggs faces persistent pressure from regulated labor cost increases that restrict margin recovery. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 30 September 2026 that wage costs represent approximately one-third of the group's total cost base. This high labor intensity makes the operating margin vulnerable to the National Living Wage expansion, which continues to grow faster than baseline sales.
12.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted on 30 September 2026 that Greggs expected a modestly improved outcome for 2026 due to strong cost control. The bank noted that cost inflation for 2026 had reduced to 2% from the 3% previously guided, which provides short-term margin relief. This indicates that near-term margins are more resilient than bear cases suggest, although severe wage pressures are still anticipated for 2027.

Peer Operating Leverage Comparisons

13.
Superior gearing: SSP Group exhibits higher operating leverage than Greggs due to its travel-sector concessions model. Morgan Stanley reported on 9 October 2026 that a 1% change in like-for-like sales at SSP Group impacts EBIT by 3% to 4% due to high fixed concession costs. This high operational gearing means SSP Group has more volatile margins than Greggs' vertically integrated retail estate.
14.
Divergent recoveries: SSP Group has struggled to restore pre-pandemic margin levels because of operational issues in Europe. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 9 October 2026 that US airport sales had soft 2% like-for-like growth, which has high operational gearing at the EBIT line. This reveals that travel catering operators face localized demand risks, whereas Greggs maintains superior margin stability through its nationwide everyday value proposition.
15.
Fixed cost coverage: J D Wetherspoon represents another high-gearing hospitality peer whose margins suffer from cost-push dynamics. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 6 January 2026 that both Greggs and J D Wetherspoon experienced volume pressure after raising prices. This indicates that price-volume elasticity limits margin protection because fixed cost recovery is highly dependent on transaction frequency.
16.
Labor exposure: Mitchells & Butlers and J D Wetherspoon experience similar margin pressures from labor inflation as Greggs. Deutsche Bank Research noted on 10 February 2026 that these labor-intensive operators are heavily exposed to the National Living Wage. This exposure implies that margins across the hospitality sector will contract unless operators can pass price increases without triggering a massive drop in customer volume.
17.
Other side of the story: Greggs' vertical integration provides unique margin defense that pure-play retailers cannot match. A Pret A Manger operations manager stated on 9 November 2026 that Greggs' centralized production eliminates in-store kitchen labor and food waste, allowing it to maintain competitive pricing. Although capacity investments initially dilute margins, this centralized model ensures that long-term operating leverage remains superior to fragmented high street competitors.
