Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Medartis established a strong global position in extremity and head surgery, leveraging Swiss quality to address an estimated USD 8.5 billion addressable market.
  2. Historical sales grew at double-digit organic rates over the three years prior to August 2025, supported by the strategic acquisitions of Nextremity and Keri Medical.
  3. Despite robust operational growth, the group's share price underperformed the Swiss Performance Index Extra due to integration costs and regional pricing headwinds.
1.
Global leader: Medartis Holding AG, founded in 1997 and headquartered in Basel, operates as a public medical device manufacturer listed on the SIX Swiss Exchange. The company specializes in developing and selling implant solutions for internal surgical fixation of bone fractures. The core business addresses high- growth segments in extremities and craniomaxillofacial reconstruction.
2.
Specialized focus: Medartis delivers its medical products to hospitals and surgical centers in customized, pre-configured containers. The modular kits contain implants and necessary fixation instruments tailored to specific surgical specialties. The precise distribution model minimizes clinical preparation time and ensures that surgeons have reliable access to high-quality tools.
3.
Large market: The company addresses a total global orthopedic market estimated at USD 50 billion. Within this field, Medartis targets a total addressable market of approximately USD 8.5 billion across head and extremity surgery. The target market structure is heavily concentrated in the United States, which represents 54% of the global opportunity.
4.
Broad footprint: The group sells its innovative fixation solutions directly through its own subsidiaries in 12 major countries. Additionally, Medartis reaches patients in a further 52 distributor markets globally. The dual commercial model balances high-touch direct customer service with cost-efficient international distribution partners.
5.
Strategic shift: Medartis has successfully transitioned from a highly centralized Swiss precision manufacturing model to a decentralized global network. The group now controls five diverse production facilities across three continents, including Basel and Warsaw. The geographic expansion has enhanced operational redundancy and positioned manufacturing closer to critical target markets.
6.
Accelerating growth: The business reported total sales of CHF 182.8 million in FY 2022 compared to CHF 159.9 million in FY 2021. The robust sales performance represented an organic revenue increase of 17.8% at constant exchange rates. Topline expansion was primarily supported by a 14.0% constant exchange rate growth in the flagship upper extremities division.
7.
Healthy demand: Total group sales grew further to CHF 212.0 million in FY 2023 compared to CHF 182.8 million in FY 2022. The positive sales trend reflected an organic growth rate of 20.5% at constant exchange rates. Top-line momentum was supported by double-digit organic expansion across all geographic regions except Asia Pacific.
8.
Robust expansion: Medartis delivered total sales of CHF 224.8 million in FY 2024 compared to CHF 212.0 million in FY 2023. Core business sales reached CHF 219.6 million, growing by 11.7% at constant exchange rates from CHF 201.2 million. The consistent sales progression showed that customer demand remained strong despite foreign exchange headwinds.
9.
Improved efficiency: The core EBITDA margin improved significantly to 19.0% in FY 2024 compared to 17.1% in FY 2023. The profitability expansion was driven by disciplined global cost management and manufacturing efficiencies. Enhanced operational leverage offset temporary price cuts in Australia and elevated regional customs tariffs.
10.
Upper dominance: The upper extremities product segment remained the largest revenue driver, generating CHF 148.2 million in core sales during FY 2024. Meanwhile, the lower extremities segment contributed CHF 40.7 million in sales, and head surgery added CHF 30.7 million. The product sales distribution illustrates that hand, wrist, and elbow products continue to represent the core of the business.
11.
Transformative acquisition: Medartis acquired Nextremity Solutions in May 2022 to accelerate commercialization in the highly attractive United States extremity market. The strategic acquisition provided the group with a local manufacturing footprint in Warsaw, Indiana. The transaction expanded the design surgeon network and boosted local commercialization efforts.
12.
Underperforming equity: The five-year relative share price development shows that Medartis peaked in early 2022 and subsequently entered a down-trend. The stock consistently underperformed the Swiss Performance Index Extra and the Euro Stoxx 50 during 2023. The negative equity trend reflected market concerns over regional price cuts in Australia and high integration costs.
13.
High expectations: Prior to the H1 2025 earnings update, sell-side analysts were heavily focused on US sales channel restructuring. Brokers anticipated that the strategic decision to replace 20 underperforming distributors would temporarily disrupt local sales. However, the market maintained positive expectations for the subsequent commercial rollout of the TOUCH prosthesis.
14.
Clinical leadership: Medartis maintains a top-three market share position in hand and wrist indications across Switzerland, Germany, Austria, France, and Australia. The company systematically expands its portfolio to close remaining gaps in lower extremities and shoulder fixation. Close collaboration with leading surgeons and the International Bone Research Association supports high-quality clinical training and education.
15.
Regulatory compliance: The transition from older legacy products to next-generation systems has supported steady growth in craniomaxillofacial surgery. Medartis successfully migrated European customers to the next-generation Modus 2 system to meet strict European Medical Device Regulation standards. The updated surgical platform attracted new medical accounts and bolstered regional market share.
16.
Other side of the story: The company's optimistic focus on rapid US market expansion could be hindered by prolonged distributor onboarding and training. If the direct sales force transition in critical areas like Florida takes longer than expected, local revenue growth may stall. The commercial delay would ultimately prevent Medartis from achieving its ambitious sales targets in the region.
