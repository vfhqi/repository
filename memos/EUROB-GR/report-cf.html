EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF) 27 findings

Eurobank operates under highly stable executive leadership anchored by CEO Fokion Karavias since February 2015, with substantial insider equity alignment totaling 1,311,488 shares held by the CEO alongside experienced deputies Stavros Ioannou and Konstantinos Vassiliou managing international activities and corporate banking operations respectively. Board governance has been strengthened through digital and technological expertise, exemplified by Alexandra Reich's March 2026 appointment bringing telecom transformation capabilities from ING Group following the regulatory departure of Jawaid Mirza. The organization demonstrates robust strategic execution capacity across three core geographies spanning Greece, Cyprus, and Bulgaria, though notable retail leadership departures such as Deputy CEO Andreas Athanasopoulos in December 2023 warrant monitoring for potential execution bottlenecks in transformation delivery and digital network integration.

Plausible problems to monitor

Cypriot loan growth improved gradually to only 4% YoY in 2Q26, lagging behind Bulgarian lending momentum and indicating geographic disparities in credit demand dynamics (see Section 7 – Peer-Relative Earnings Momentum). Alpha Bank delivered a 14.5% Return on Tangible Equity in 2Q26, trailing Eurobank's leading return profile but nonetheless representing competitive pressure from peers closing the profitability gap (see Section 7 – Peer- Relative Earnings Momentum).

Eurobank maintains 30% of deposits in term structures, representing the highest mix among systemic peers and potentially limiting funding flexibility, while the Cyprus division operated with a low loan-to-deposit ratio of 38%, indicating substantial idle liquidity that remains undeployed (see Section 7 – Peer- Relative Earnings Momentum). The risk of accelerated decline in ECB rates could disproportionately compress Eurobank's margins, unwinding regional net interest income advantages faster than peers with different balance sheet structures (see Section 7 – Peer-Relative Earnings Momentum).

Outliers worth noting

Eurobank's Cypriot operations maintain an exceptionally low loan-to-deposit ratio of approximately 37.5% in 2Q26, representing a statistical outlier that leaves massive idle liquidity relative to both domestic Greek banking operations and regional European peers. This structural anomaly creates unique reinvestment optionality but also signals potential underutilization of balance sheet capacity compared to the sector median loan-todeposit ratio of approximately 65% observed across Greek systemic banks.

Management has actively deployed this surplus liquidity into high-yielding debt securities, purchasing €1.1 billion in bonds during recent quarters to optimize net interest income generation. The extreme liquidity position appears partially attributable to the legacy deposit franchise inherited from Hellenic Bank consolidation, yet the magnitude of the divergence from peer norms remains unexplained by management commentary and warrants ongoing monitoring as a potential drag on capital efficiency metrics.

This outlier is cross-referenced in Section 4 Financial Profile Change and Section 7 Peer- Relative Earnings Momentum. Eurobank achieved organic loan growth of €2.7 billion in 1H26, representing an 11% year-on-year expansion that significantly outpaced domestic Greek banking peers. This growth rate substantially exceeded closest competitors, with Postbank's Bulgarian operations recording 17% lending growth but Greek domestic peers registering materially lower expansion rates.

The velocity and consistency of this loan growth trajectory represents a multi-standard-deviation outperformance relative to the broader European banking sector, where loan growth averaged approximately 4% across comparable markets. Management upgraded its FY26 loan growth target to €4.5 billion from €3.8 billion previously, with sequential quarterly acceleration from €1.1 billion in 1Q26 to €1.6 billion in 2Q26. While the Greek National Development Program and EU Recovery and Resilience Facility provide plausible structural explanations for elevated corporate credit demand, the magnitude and persistence of Eurobank's outperformance versus regional peers suggests either superior origination capabilities or potential underwriting standard relaxation that requires continuous credit quality surveillance.

This anomaly is cross-referenced in Section 6 Most Recent Earnings Delivery and Section 9 Key Drivers Synthesis. Eurobank's international operations delivered a net interest margin of 280 basis points in 2Q26, representing a 60 basis point premium over Greek domestic operations at 220 basis points. Bulgarian lending spreads reached approximately 1.5 times those of Greek domestic corporate lending, creating a structural margin advantage that significantly exceeds typical regional banking variance.

This geographic margin differential appears unusually wide when benchmarked against European cross-border banking operations, where international divisions typically generate 10-30 basis point premiums rather than the 60+ basis point gaps observed at Eurobank. The sustainability of this outlier margin performance remains uncertain given competitive dynamics in Bulgarian and Cypriot markets, where commercial spreads have been gradually converging to the 3.1% to 3.4% range by early 2026.

The absence of detailed management commentary explaining the structural drivers of this persistent margin premium creates transparency concerns regarding potential unsustainable pricing dynamics or concentrated exposure to higher-risk lending segments. This outlier is cross-referenced in Section 3 External Change and Section 7 Peer-Relative Earnings Momentum. Eurobank's NPE coverage ratio declined sharply from 94% in 1Q26 to 82.4% in 2Q26, representing a 1,160 basis point sequential drop driven by provisions utilized for CHF loan conversions and asset reclassifications to heldfor-sale.

This quarterly volatility magnitude significantly exceeds typical provision movement patterns observed across Greek systemic banks, where coverage ratios generally fluctuate within 200-300 basis point ranges between reporting periods. While Eurobank stood out as the only systemic Greek bank reporting sequential Stage 3 stock declines, the mechanics and economic substance of the coverage ratio compression remain inadequately explained in public disclosures.

The timing and magnitude of this adjustment coincides with active portfolio disposal initiatives including Project Moon II and Project Sun, yet the aggregate impact on forward provisioning requirements and potential residual credit losses lacks quantitative clarity. The sharp deviation from peer provisioning stability and the concentration of adjustments within a single quarter creates earnings quality questions regarding the sustainability of the reported 50 basis point cost of risk performance.
