Section 2 – Current Status and Likely Path of Section 301 Port Fees

Section summary
  1. The USTR Section 301 port fees remain suspended through January 9, 2027, following a series of temporary bilateral trade truces between the United States and China.
  2. Port fees of USD 46 per net ton on foreign-built car carriers pose substantial cost risks if reinstated, though near-term implementation remains unlikely.

Chronology of Port Fee Implementation

1.
Systemic regulatory action: The United States Trade Representative launched a Section 301 investigation in April 2024 to address China's growing dominance within the global maritime, logistics, and shipbuilding sectors. This probe led to a January 2025 finding by the Biden Administration that Chinese policies heavily burdened American commerce. Consequently, the administration proposed robust trade actions to limit this maritime influence and protect domestic industries.
2.
Rapid industrial dominance: The primary target of the United States Section 301 investigation was China's rapid rise in global shipbuilding and maritime services. In 1999, Chinese shipyards built less than 5% of all global shipping tonnage, but this market share surged to over 50% by 2023. This dramatic growth led to China owning over 19% of the commercial world fleet by January 2024.
3.
Strategic policy objectives: The United States administration designed the Section 301 measures to actively discourage shipowners from ordering vessels at Chinese shipyards. To incentivize domestic shipbuilding, the revised proposition allows for fee remissions if the vessel owner orders a United States-built vessel of equivalent capacity. This policy attempt highlights the administration's strategic focus on rebuilding its domestic maritime industrial base.
4.
Escalating fee proposals: The Office of the United States Trade Representative initially proposed a massive fee of USD 150 per car equivalent unit on all non-United States built car carriers entering the country. This aggressive proposal threatened to add significant costs of USD 1 million to USD 1.35 million for each voyage calling at United States ports. Shipping lines expressed deep concern because only one of the more than 800 car carriers globally was built in the United States.
5.
Adjusted final fees: The regulatory authorities modified their initial fee structure by establishing a finalized fee of USD 46 per net ton on foreign-built car carriers. This revised fee on roll-on roll-off vessels was scheduled to apply up to five times per calendar year for each arriving vehicle carrier. Non-Chinese operators of standard Chinese-built container vessels faced a separate fee structure starting at USD 18 per net ton or USD 120 per discharged container.
6.
Reciprocal Chinese actions: The Chinese Ministry of Transport quickly retaliated in October 2025 by introducing reciprocal port fees on United States-linked vessels. These retaliatory fees targeted vessels built, flagged, or owned by entities with 25% or more United States ownership. The Chinese measures established a fee of RMB 400 per net ton, which was structured to scale up to RMB 1,120 per net ton by 2028.

Suspensions and Current Regulatory Pathways

7.
Collaborative trade truces: The United States and China paused their port fees on November 10, 2025, as part of a broader trade agreement. This agreement, negotiated during the Busan Summit in South Korea, suspended all port fee liabilities for twelve months to facilitate further negotiations. This initial suspension provided immediate financial relief to shipping lines that had faced sudden cost liabilities.
8.
Extended fee suspensions: The Office of the United States Trade Representative finalized an additional two-month suspension of these Section 301 port fees on November 9, 2026. This extension, which pushes the suspension period to January 9, 2027, was implemented to allow ongoing discussions with Chinese officials. During this extension period, no shipping party will accrue liability or be required to pay any maritime service fees.
9.
Reciprocal eastern extension: The State Council Tariff Commission of China mirrored the American extension on November 9, 2026, by delaying its own retaliatory measures. China extended its suspension of special port fees on United States ships and paused its domestic investigation until January 10, 2027. This parallel action preserves the temporary trade truce and keeps the additional tariff on United States goods at a reduced rate of 10%.
10.
55 of Agriculture (USDA) Office of the People's Republic of China Projected regulatory path: The consensus among political analysts in Washington indicates that the United States will likely utilize further short extensions of two to three months rather than immediate fee resumption. Regulatory authorities view these suspended port fees primarily as critical negotiating leverage during bilateral trade discussions. The future of these tariffs remains uncertain, leaving shipping companies with limited longterm commercial visibility.

Stakeholder Perspectives and Industry Opposition

11.
Vocal association resistance: The World Shipping Council and the International Chamber of Shipping strongly support the extended suspensions and urge a permanent elimination of these fees. These groups argue that the proposed Annex III fees on car carriers are detached from Section 301's focus on Chinese maritime practices because they penalize all foreign-built vessels regardless of origin. They emphasize that these fees act as a regressive tax on imports and unnecessarily inflate global trade costs.
12.
National shipbuilding lobbying: The USA Shipbuilding Coalition opposes the ongoing suspensions and argues that the delays create severe investment uncertainty for domestic shipyards. They contend that the temporary pauses have allowed Chinese orderbooks to recover at the expense of American maritime revitalisation. Consequently, they advocate for the immediate application of Section 301 fees to force the rebuilding of the domestic fleet.
13.
Contrary evidence: The threat of Section 301 fees may be entirely eclipsed by the proposed introduction of a universal infrastructure or security fee under the second Trump administration's Maritime Action Plan released on February 13, 2026. This new policy proposal would apply more broadly than the Section 301 fees by targeting all internationally built vessels entering United States ports. This potential fee would be calculated based on the weight of imported tonnage, which could render the specific Section 301 car carrier fees redundant.
14.
Historical trade precedents: The temporary imposition and subsequent deferral of these maritime tariffs closely mirror the trade disruptions experienced during the 2019 US-China tariff escalations. During that historical precedent, initial trade volumes dropped on direct mainlanes, forcing liner operators to reorganize their shipping networks and diversify global supply chains. Ultimately, that disruption led to increased supply chain inefficiencies, which historically drove up global demand for shipping capacity.
15.
Comparative peer exposure: The annual financial risk from these port fees varies widely among peers, with Wallenius Wilhelmsen disclosing an unmitigated gross exposure of USD 350 million to USD 400 million. In contrast, Höegh Autoliners faces a much lower annual unmitigated exposure estimated in the range of USD 60 million to USD 70 million. This significant difference in financial exposure reflects the relative size of each carrier's operating fleet and their specific trade lane exposure to the United States market.

If diplomatic negotiations fail, the resumption of both American port fees and China's retaliatory tariffs will severely disrupt shipping profitability. Under such conditions, operators would face immediate margin pressure unless they can rapidly pass these regulatory costs to customers.

This financial impact was recorded as an adjustment to the group's shipping services EBITDA because the fees were active for only four weeks before the suspension. 7.
Successful fuel surcharges: Maritime transport operators have historically utilized fuel surcharge mechanisms to mitigate extreme operating cost increases. During the implementation of the IMO 2020 sulfur cap regulations, shipping firms faced extreme fuel price volatility. The carriers successfully transferred the increased costs of low-sulfur fuel to shippers through spot freight rate adjustments. This precedent demonstrates that the global shipping industry can successfully implement broad surcharges to protect net earnings.
8.
Gradual market adaptation: Shipping companies proved during the IMO 2020 transition that environmental compliance costs are eventually absorbed by global cargo owners. The new fuel standards tested the sector's operating capabilities, but larger carriers leverage their scale to manage these cost pressures. While initial volatility temporarily squeezed margins, contract rates adjusted to the new baseline within two quarters. This historical precedent suggests that Section 301 port fees will also follow a similar pass-through pattern.
9.
Parallel contract renewals: Wallenius Wilhelmsen continues to secure favorable contract renewals with key high-and-heavy and automotive customers. These multi-year agreements, including a major contract signed with a leading equipment manufacturer, incorporate updated cost-sharing clauses. The carrier's capability to lock in supportive freight rates is enhanced by the tight supply of specialized car carriers. Thus, the current supportive market structure increases the probability of passing through port fee liabilities.
