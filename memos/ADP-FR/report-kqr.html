Section 2 – Tax Pass-Through Mechanics and Infrastructure Tax Coverage

Section summary
  1. ADP's proposed 75% tax pass-through clause faces critical legal headwinds as transport infrastructure taxes are historically categorized outside corporate tax definitions, creating high uncertainty over complete cost recovery.

Tax Pass-Through Legal Mechanics

1.
Tariff recovery: The transport regulator ART on 18 January 2024 officially confirmed that the long-distance transport infrastructure tax could be covered by airport charges.
2.
Declining impact: In its 27 September 2023 press release, ADP SA estimated that tariff pass-throughs would ensure the tax's cash impact is limited to its non-regulated portion starting in 2027.
3.
Regulatory oversight: Bernstein Research on 9 July 2026 reports that because the regulator sets tariffs, any lower-than-expected allowed return or restricted pass-through would impact the group negatively.
4.
EBITDA erosion: ADP SA's 27 September 2023 disclosure calculated that before any tariff adjustments, the long-distance transport tax would reduce the group's consolidated EBITDA by approximately 120 million euros.
5.
Constitutional validation: In its 24 October 2024 filing, ADP SA noted that the French Constitutional Council declared the long-distance transport infrastructure tax fully compliant with the Constitution on 12 September 2024.

Applicability to Infrastructure Tax

6.
Tax classification: In its 23 July 2024 half-year report, ADP SA classified the long-distance transport infrastructure tax as taxes other than income taxes, which operating expenses absorb before corporate tax calculations.
7.
Proposed escalation: Atlas Arteria on 30 September 2026 reported that the draft Finance Bill for 2027 seeks to increase the infrastructure tax rate from 4.6% to as much as 12.2% of revenues.
8.
Profitability linkage: Atlas Arteria's 30 September 2026 release stated that the French government intends to set the final rate based on the company's profitability, targeting transport infrastructure operators with revenues exceeding 120 million euros.
9.
Legal distinction: Experts in French airport regulation suggest that because the 75% pass-through clause refers to standard corporate income taxes, a tax levied directly on revenues may fall outside the clause's scope.
10.
Tariff risk: Bernstein Research on 18 February 2026 notes that arbitrary changes to the tax regime in France continue to negatively impact the group's outlook and complicate tariff negotiations.
