One point for Richard before the questions. The memo of 3-Nov-2026 reads the case as "a dated watch-list case, not a current position", while the Sector Digest for Gambling and the Industry Digest for Consumer discretionary, both written by the Assistant Portfolio Manager, record the stock as held. The questions are therefore weighted to what a holder most needs from this call: the size and timing of the cuts to guidance and estimates, and how close the balance-sheet tail is.

What the call should settle, and why

The Investment Analyst's case in one line: an engine better than the group, and an equity outcome decided below EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation). The UK online casino business has grown through a 40% duty. The group around it carries 4.33 times leverage, a parent with a going-concern warning, guidance above its own arithmetic and an acquisition, Evoke, whose return to shareholders is a geared bet on GBP 180m of synergies. On the Investment Analyst's reading the next six months are skewed to cuts in guidance and estimates, and the six months after that to conditional, modest upgrades. The nine-month results of 10-Nov-2026 confirmed demand and worsened margin and leverage.

The call can test six judgements. The first four decide the size and timing of the cuts; the last two decide whether the equity is worth holding through them.

  • Judgement 1, whether the third-quarter margin is the new level. The group margin was 31.1% in the third quarter (30.6% in the second) with a full quarter of mitigation running; our arithmetic puts the interactive business near 31%, against the 36% to 37% the case assumed for the second half. Each EUR 10m of EBITDA is about 8% of FY2027 adjusted earnings per share and about 4% of the share price. The case had weighed a loss of volume with the margin held; it had not weighed a margin shortfall with the volume held.
  • Judgement 2, when and how the guidance is rebased. FY2026 guidance of about EUR 422m needs EUR 148.4m in the fourth quarter, 67% above the third. The FY2027 target of EUR 454m is 12% above the Investment Analyst's EUR 400m. The Investment Analyst expects FY2026 estimates to fall 3% to 8% and FY2027 standalone estimates 5% to 12%, and expects the company to reframe before it cuts.
  • Judgement 3, UK volume after the cuts to player generosity. The base is UK growth of about 6% in constant currency in FY2027, against 12.2% in the third quarter. A 10% loss of UK volume takes about EUR 35m off EBITDA, 27% off adjusted earnings per share and 13% off the share price; the consolidation of smaller operators' customers adds about EUR 15m and 6%.
  • Judgement 4, leverage and the covenants. Leverage rose from 4.05 to 4.33 times on flat net debt, because a pre-duty quarter dropped out of twelve-month EBITDA; two more have still to drop out. Against the 4.75 times test the case cites, headroom is about 0.4 times and narrowing mechanically. The definitions are unknown, and an equity issue of 450m shares at EUR 0.80 would cost about 13% of the share price.
  • Judgement 5, Evoke: completion, synergy phasing and financing. Half the synergies delivered or not is about EUR 0.26 a share either way, 24% of today's price. It is the largest judgement in the case and the least evidenced.
  • Judgement 6, the parent. Bally's Corporation carries a going-concern note, must refinance by 31-Mar-2027, has pledged its 58% stake and shares its chief executive with the company. The Investment Analyst's view is that the ring-fence holds for debt and not for cash.

Order of play. Put the definitional questions to the chief financial officer first (the third-quarter margin, the mitigation measure, the covenant tests, the lines below EBITDA): they are factual, our numbers and the company's diverge most there, and a chief financial officer can answer them without giving a forecast. Then put the judgement questions to the chief executive (player behaviour after the generosity cuts, the Evoke synergy plan, the parent). Leave lottery tenders, the shareholder register and governance to the end.

Expect two limits on the answers. The full third-quarter release is dated 26-Nov-2026, so some answers will be deferred to it; where that happens, ask what the release will contain. On Evoke, the company may decline to go beyond its published documents; where that happens, ask where and when the missing item will be published.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the fourth quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2027)

A1 ★ The FY2026 guidance. "Nine-month adjusted EBITDA is EUR 273.6m, so the guidance of about EUR 422m needs about EUR 148m in the fourth quarter against EUR 88.9m in the third. Is EUR 422m still the guidance? If it changes, will that be at the full release on 26 November, and will you give a group number or the two divisional ranges (the interactive business at EUR 280m to 295m and lottery at EUR 100m to 105m)?"
- Why: the Investment Analyst's FY2026 is EUR 385m (range 375 to 398), which needs about EUR 111m in the fourth quarter. Even the chief executive's own divisional outlook (EUR 380m to 400m in total) needs EUR 106m to 126m, which is 20% to 42% above the third quarter (our arithmetic). The Investment Analyst put a 65% probability on a cut or reframe at the third-quarter results. We have no record of what the company said about guidance on 10 November.
- Tell: a divisional framing counts as a cut, whatever it is called. "On track" with no bridge to the fourth quarter moves the cut to the FY2026 results in March or April 2027. A number at or below EUR 385m to 400m is the first of the three conditions the Investment Analyst set for the skew to turn positive.

A2 ★ The third-quarter margin: a lag or the new level. "The group margin was 31.1% in the third quarter against 30.6% in the second, with a full quarter of mitigation in it. On our arithmetic the interactive business earned about EUR 63m on about EUR 202m of revenue in the quarter, a margin near 31%, against 35.2% in the first half. Is that arithmetic right? Was anything one-off in the quarter, were the second-half cuts to bonuses and return-to-player fully in it, and at what margin did the business leave the quarter?"
- Why: this is the first of the three readings in the earnings summary of 10-Nov-2026: growth may be being held by giving margin back. The case assumes that the interactive business keeps 40% to 45% of each extra euro of revenue and earns a 36% to 37% margin in the second half. If 31% is the level, the Investment Analyst's standalone EBITDA of EUR 385m, 400m and 408m for FY2026 to FY2028 is too high even if volume holds.
- Tell: a named one-off, or cuts that began late in the quarter, means a mitigation lag and the margin path stands. "This is the run-rate until volume builds" means the margin, not the volume, is the open question, and the FY2027 number falls with it.

A3 ★ The mitigation measure, on one definition. "You reported a gross duty impact of EUR 68.0m over nine months with 48.5% offset. In the second quarter you described about 65% offset, EUR 11.3m from costs and EUR 10.1m from growth. On one consistent definition, what was the offset in the third quarter alone, split between cost savings, generosity and growth? What rate should we use for the fourth quarter and for 2027?"
- Why: the measure has been defined four ways (40%, 53%, 65%, 63%). If the second-quarter gross impact was about EUR 34m, as our files hold, the third quarter's offset was only about a third (our arithmetic). The Investment Analyst counts each lever once and treats two-thirds of the second-quarter figure as growth that would have happened anyway. The annualised gross duty is about EUR 127m, and about EUR 32m of it still annualises in the first quarter of 2027.
- Tell: a third-quarter rate near a third on the old definition means the levers are slower than described. A fifth definition means we keep our own count.

A4 ★ UK trading since September. "UK online revenue grew 12.2% in constant currency in the third quarter. How did August, September, October and the first weeks of November run? How much of the growth is more active players and how much is yield from lower bonuses and return-to-player? Are active players and first-time depositors rising?"
- Why: the engine accelerated into the duty (+6.3%, +10.5%, +11.6%, then +13.0% in July), but part of the growth is price taken from players, and the comparatives turn hard from the second quarter of 2027. Our files also hold a second third-quarter figure of 11.4% with no clear period.
- Tell: active players and first-time depositors rising means share is being won and the Investment Analyst's FY2027 growth of 6% is too low (each point above 6% is about EUR 3.5m of EBITDA). Revenue up on flat active players means price-led growth that annualises.

A5 ★ The FY2027 target and what it will include. "Is EUR 454m still the FY2027 target for the current group? When will you give FY2027 guidance, and will you show the current group separately from Evoke, with the synergies outside the standalone number?"
- Why: the Investment Analyst's FY2027 is EUR 400m (range 370 to 425) and expects the EUR 454m to be folded into a combined figure at the FY2026 results, not cut in the open. The memo calls separate guidance for Evoke "the one disclosure to insist on", because rebased standalone numbers can be beaten only if synergies are kept out of them.
- Tell: a standalone target at or below EUR 400m to 430m, with Evoke guided separately, would be the first conservative number the group has published. One combined figure hides the rebase, and the screens will then show "upgrades" that are only the change of perimeter.

A6 ★ The lines below EBITDA. "You guide only to EBITDA. For 2026 and 2027, what should we model for depreciation and amortisation, including the amortisation of acquired intangibles, for cash interest, the tax rate, capital expenditure and exceptional items? Will you define an adjusted earnings per share?"

The only visible broker earnings per share (Barclays: EUR 0.020, 0.030, 0.040) sit near the reported basis, which is why the multiple of 30 times on the screens carries no information.
- Tell: numbers given now narrow the earnings per share range (about 20% to 25% either way standalone). A refusal leaves it to the FY2026 annual report.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Evoke: the largest judgement and the least evidenced

B1.1 ★ What the synergies are made of, and when they land. "The GBP 180m target is about a fifth of combined EBITDA, against 5% to 10% in typical mergers in the sector. How does it split between marketing, technology and platform, content and overheads? What is the phasing by half-year? How much depends on moving Evoke's customers onto your platform, and what loss of customers have you assumed in that move?"
- Why: each GBP 10m of delivered synergy is about EUR 0.044 a share. The Investment Analyst's cases are 85% delivered (30% probability), half (42%), absorbed by volume loss (13%) and no completion (15%). Taking Evoke's marketing from 23% of revenue toward 16% to 17% over two years is feasible on precedent; taking it to the company's own 11% is not. Our files carry a guide of 75% in the first year, and a target date of year two at a GBP 25m cost.
- Tell: marketing-led synergies arrive fast and put revenue at risk; platform-led synergies arrive slowly and stick. Phasing at or above 40% of the GBP 180m within two quarters of completion would move the Investment Analyst's probabilities toward 45%, 40% and 5%, and the probability-weighted value at end-2027 from about EUR 1.26 to about EUR 1.37 a share.

B1.2 ★ Whether delivery will be visible. "Will you report delivered synergies and integration costs each quarter, separately from the current group's results?"
- Why: the Investment Analyst's position is that the case belongs after the first synergy phasing is in print.
- Tell: a yes gives the case its tracking measure; a no means synergies and standalone performance can each hide the other.

B1.3 ★ Completion. "What is still outstanding before completion: the Gibraltar court sanction, which gaming approvals, and on what dates? Is the first quarter of 2027 still the expectation? Is any condition tied to UK duty or to the financing?"
- Why: the Investment Analyst's overlay assumes completion on 1 April 2027 and puts 15% on no completion, where the shares are worth about EUR 0.90 at the fair multiple against EUR 1.27 with half the synergies. The Assistant Portfolio Manager found no condition tied to UK duty in the announcement.
- Tell: a slip past the first quarter moves the synergy clock and puts completion in the same months as the leverage tests in B4.1.

B1.4 ★ What connects Evoke's debt to the rest of the group. "After completion, what links Evoke's borrowings to the current group: the GBP 200m prepayment you have committed to support by December 2027, any guarantees, and the cross-default language? How will the GBP 200m be funded? What is the plan for Evoke's GBP 769m due in July 2028 and its 11% cash coupon?"
- Why: GBP 243m of equity controls GBP 2.2bn of enterprise value at 5.2 times leverage, adding EUR 2.1bn to 2.2bn of debt and a coupon of about EUR 112m a year. The memo calls the separation of the two credits "porous", and Fitch caps the rating two notches above the parent's B-. Deutsche Bank puts pro forma leverage at 4.50 times.
- Tell: "no recourse, no guarantee, funded from Evoke's own cash flow" means two credits. Group cash or new group debt for the prepayment means one credit, and the leverage that matters is the combined one.

B1.5 The cash alternative and the new shares. "What take-up of the 52p cash alternative do you expect against the GBP 117.1m cap? About 252m new Athens-listed shares will go to UK holders: what are you doing to place them with long-term owners?"
- Why: maximum cash adds about EUR 135m of debt and halves the flow of shares back to the market. The free float is 41%, daily turnover is about USD 3m, and arbitrage funds are short the shares until completion. An all-share outcome dilutes the parent toward 51%.
- Tell: a plan for a UK shareholder base (a listing, index eligibility, a marketed placing) turns a supply problem into new demand; no plan leaves the overhang.

B1.6 Evoke's own tax bill and the William Hill shops. "Evoke has put its tax headwind at GBP 125m to 135m before mitigation. What offset do you expect there? Does your plan for the William Hill shops assume Machine Games Duty at 25% from April 2027, and are the shops a long-term part of the group?"
- Why: the Assistant Portfolio Manager estimates the machine duty at GBP 9m to 12m a year on about 860 to 900 shops, about 3% of Evoke's 2025 EBITDA of GBP 356m. The larger April 2027 item is the betting duty on William Hill's online sports book.
- Tell: a sale or closure plan for the shops is optionality not in our numbers; "core" means the duty is absorbed inside the synergy case.

B1.7 Who does the work. "Who runs the integration day to day? You have one chief operating officer holding four functions and no chief technology officer, and the EUR 41m of synergies from the first combination are still landing. What must go right in the first hundred days?"
- Why: the second deal was launched before the first was proven. The memo reads management as forceful and thin, with staff turnover of 19% and two cultures being merged under cost-cutting.
- Tell: named leaders, a dated migration plan and retention terms for Evoke's key people are evidence; "the same team that did Gamesys" is not.

B2. The UK online engine

B2.1 ★ Player behaviour after the generosity cuts. "What have the second-half cuts to bonuses and return-to-player done so far to active players, deposit frequency and your highest-value players? What would tell you that you had gone too far?"
- Why: the elasticity is untested. High-value players notice a 0.5% change in return-to-player; Rank's growth slowed from 12% to 6% when it cut marketing; the Netherlands lost 23% to 25% of regulated revenue after its tax rise. Regulus's -12% for 2027 is the bear marker and Entain's +14% the bull.
- Tell: stable high-value cohorts through October and November support the base. Any mention of "rebalancing generosity" in the fourth quarter means the margin lever is being handed back.

B2.2 ★ Winning the customers of operators that leave. "Your UK share has been 14% to 15% since 2020, and this year you grew with the market, not ahead of it. Where is the business of the departing operators going? Have you bought any customer databases, on what terms, and what is in the pipeline?"
- Why: about a dozen operators are seeking to sell, and databases are changing hands on royalty-style terms. Entain has taken three points of share in two quarters. This is the largest tailwind in the case, it is not yet in the company's own numbers, and it is being bought through Evoke.
- Tell: named database deals at marginal marketing cost are the chief executive's own upper scenario (about EUR 15m of EBITDA). "Evoke is our consolidation" means the standalone engine has no share gain to come.

B2.3 The unlicensed market. "What have you seen in the unlicensed market since April, in your own data and the regulator's, and what enforcement do you expect?"
- Why: the UK black market is put at 8% to 10% of the industry and possibly growing 50% to 100% after the tax; our files record about 2m visits to unlicensed sites in June 2026. Each price step the licensed market takes raises the risk.
- Tell: evidence of high-value players leaving for unlicensed sites is the Netherlands case arriving.

B2.4 Incremental margin and content costs. "After the 40% duty, content, payments and marketing, what does an extra pound of UK casino revenue keep? Are your content suppliers giving discounts?"
- Why: about 60% is kept after duty (79% before) and about 40% to 45% after other costs. Content fees are about 22% of digital revenue; discounts of 5% to 10% would be worth up to EUR 10m. Marketing is about 11% of revenue against Evoke's 23%.
- Tell: an incremental margin above 45%, or content discounts signed, is an upgrade source the Investment Analyst has sized and not counted.

B2.5 Concentration and new markets. "The UK was about 65% of first-half revenue. You have spoken of two new consumer markets a year: which, at what cost, and when do they matter? Spain grew 18.9% to EUR 13.3m in the quarter."
- Why: the Assistant Portfolio Manager's note reads the holding as two-thirds exposed to the one market our gambling Underweight is built against.
- Tell: a new market named with a revenue ambition and a date would be new information; none is in our files.

The case was written on year-end net debt of EUR 1,550m; September was EUR 1.63bn including the Victoria licence.

B4.3 ★ Equity. "In what circumstances would you issue new shares, beyond those for Evoke? You hold an authorisation of EUR 135m nominal."

B5. The parent, governance and alignment

B6. Optionality not in our numbers
