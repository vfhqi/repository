Section 16 – Conclusion

The H1 2026 results fundamentally altered the investment narrative for Fuchs SE by demonstrating that the company possesses exceptional operational resilience and pricing power during periods of extreme supply-chain volatility, yet simultaneously exposed a critical vulnerability in its cash-generation model that threatens the core capital-light thesis underpinning its historical valuation premium. The group delivered record-breaking first-half sales of €2,003 million and EBIT of €260 million, representing an 11% and 24% increase respectively versus the prior-year period, with Q2 2026 EBIT of €135 million beating the Vara consensus estimate of €108 million by a substantial 25%.

This exceptional delivery validated management's ability to execute multiple rapid pricing rounds globally while leveraging its decentralized sourcing network to capture market share from competitors struggling with raw material shortages.

The single most important change to the investment case centers on the revelation that Fuchs can sustain and potentially defend higher-than-expected gross margins even during severe input cost inflation cycles. The group's EBIT margin improved to 13.0% in H1 2026 from 11.6% in H1 2025, demonstrating significant top-line operating leverage as sales grew 11% while functional costs rose only 5%, confirming that the company's transition toward its historical 14% EBIT margin target under the FUCHS100 strategy is structurally achievable rather than aspirational.

This operational performance directly contradicts the pre-earnings sell-side concern that rising base oil inflation driven by the Middle East conflict would squeeze near-term margins, proving instead that Fuchs' shortened pricing adjustment cycles and contract indexation mechanisms provide far more effective margin protection than previously modeled.

However, the results simultaneously revealed a severe structural weakness in the company's cashgeneration profile that materially undermines its capital-light investment thesis. Free cash flow before acquisitions declined 25% to €61 million from €81 million in H1 2025, reflecting a massive €139 million outflow from inflation-driven net operating working capital buildup, which caused group net liquidity to fall significantly to €13 million in Q2 2026 compared to €151 million at fiscal year-end 2025.

This working capital drain directly contradicts the historical narrative that Fuchs maintains an asset-light model that consistently converts approximately 80% of net income into free cash flow, exposing a critical vulnerability where raw material price inflation mechanically destroys cash conversion regardless of the company's ability to pass through costs via sales price increases. The strongest counter-hypothesis to the positive investment case argues that the massive Q2 volume surge represents an unsustainable illusion driven by temporary customer pre-buying and competitor supply failures that will violently reverse in the second half of 2026.

Management explicitly disclosed that only one-third of the strong Q2 sales expansion came from organic business growth, with the remaining growth driven by pre-buying effects and competitor supply constraints that inflated temporary volumes, while warning that customer pre-buying observed during Q2 will act as a material drag on sales volumes in the second half as these pull-forward effects reverse. This interpretation suggests that Fuchs faces a structural volume cliff in H2 2026 as customers aggressively destock their inflated inventories and as competitors like Shell restore their raw material supply chains, potentially triggering a severe EBIT contraction that overshoots even the company's conservative upgraded guidance range of €460-480 million for the full year.

The bear case gains further credibility from management's own cautious messaging, as CEO Stefan Fuchs explicitly cautioned analysts that the exceptionally strong first-half results cannot simply be extrapolated into the second half, highlighting that multiple temporary tailwinds supporting H1 performance will likely dissipate or reverse. Additionally, a portion of Q2 volume growth came from customers switching to Fuchs because other lubricant suppliers struggled with raw material availability, which management considers to be largely temporary, indicating that competitive share gains are vulnerable to immediate reversal once peer operations normalize.
