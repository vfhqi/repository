EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Ambu has completed a comprehensive strategic turnaround under experienced leadership, transitioning from a low-growth, overleveraged position to a focused single-use endoscopy platform targeting double-digit organic revenue expansion and operating margins above twenty percent by fiscal 2029/30. The company achieved thirteen percent organic revenue growth in fiscal 2024/25 while expanding EBIT margins to thirteen percent, significantly outperforming mature European MedTech peers.

The balance sheet transformed from highly leveraged (3.9x NIBD/EBITDA in fiscal 2021/22) to debt-free status with negative gearing of minus 0.3x, providing substantial financial flexibility for strategic acquisitions and capital returns.

1.
MATERIALITY OF CHANGE Leadership inflection: Britt Meelby Jensen assumed the CEO role to execute the focused turnaround strategy, stabilizing performance through cost discipline and asset prioritisation. Executive changes drove comprehensive operational stabilization under a highly experienced team with proven MedTech scaling expertise.
2.
Strategic pivot execution: The ZOOM IN strategy tripled organic revenue growth and stabilized the financial platform ahead of schedule. ZOOM AHEAD increased organic revenue CAGR targets to eleven to thirteen percent through 2030. Management successfully transitioned from defensive turnaround operations to aggressive growth expansion while maintaining profitability discipline.
3.
Manufacturing localization: The newly constructed Juárez factory in Mexico increased volume output by three times in a single year and achieved landed cost parity with Malaysia production, excluding tariffs. Mexico factory scaling provides structural tariff mitigation and supports North American commercial expansion with substantial remaining capacity headroom.
4.
Capital structure transformation: Strong operational cash flow eliminated all net interest-bearing debt, enabling capital redeployment, while leverage dropped from 3.9x NIBD/EBITDA in fiscal 2021/22 to negative gearing. Debt-free status unlocks inorganic growth opportunities and shareholder returns without balance sheet constraints.
5.

Deliberate portfolio pruning eliminated manufacturing complexity and concentrated resources on high-return specialties.

Plausible problems to monitor

Despite Ambu's strong 16% organic growth in Endoscopy Solutions, the Anesthesia and Patient Monitoring segment contracted or slowed significantly, limiting overall organic revenue gains and revealing structural weakness in legacy divisions that are significantly underperforming the company's growth targets. This creates a dual-speed business model where the flagship endoscopy platform must carry an increasingly burdensome legacy portfolio, a problem that peers like Boston Scientific have largely avoided by maintaining more balanced divisional momentum. Ambu's 13.5% EBIT margin in Q3 2025/26 lagged substantially behind Coloplast's 26.2% adjusted operating profitability, exposing a persistent profitability gap of more than 1,200 basis points relative to mature Danish MedTech peers, despite operating in ostensibly similar regulatory and cost environments. [Cross-ref: Section 7]

Net working capital reached 22% of revenue in Q2, exceeding the 20% long-term target, as management deliberately maintains elevated inventory levels to mitigate geopolitical risks and tariff actions. This elevated inventory position reflects deliberate supply buffer building, yet Ambu experienced persistent downward consensus revisions from mid-2025 due to tariff drags before Q3 results stabilized expectations.

The company's working capital intensity is materially higher than Boston Scientific or Coloplast, both of whom operate closer to 15-17% of sales, suggesting that Ambu faces structurally higher cash conversion friction that may prevent it from achieving stated +40% cash conversion targets if geopolitical tensions persist. [Cross-ref: Section 4, guidance of steady progression toward 20% EBIT margins by FY 2029/30. This represents a credibility risk that is significantly higher than peers pursuing purely organic scale strategies, as the integration complexity and execution uncertainty surrounding the TIMS Medical acquisition and broader EndoIntelligence platform create binary outcome scenarios where margin targets could be missed by 200-300 basis points if synergies fail to materialize.
