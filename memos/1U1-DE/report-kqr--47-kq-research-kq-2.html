Executive Summary

Section summary
  1. BNetzA's low-band shared spectrum hearing is highly resolvable within a spring 2026 decision window, providing the primary catalyst for 1&1 AG's 18-month to 3-year EPS recovery and 25%+ IRR potential, though extended administrative court litigation from incumbent MNOs or regulatory compromise could leave 1&1 stranded without low-band access, depressing near-term EPS and forcing sustained reliance on high-cost Vodafone roaming. BNetzA formally launched the written hearing on 17 February 2026 with a firm deadline of 18 March 2026 for operator submissions, targeting an official order during spring 2026 that represents a compressed two-month window from comment closure to final decree. Historical turnaround base rates under the Telecommunications Act confirm BNetzA typically completes complex spectrum consultations within eight to twelve weeks. This procedural velocity aligns with 1&1 AG's 18-month to 3-year EPS trajectory, which depends directly on transitioning from costly Vodafone national roaming to low-band open RAN deployment to eliminate the severe opex drag from Vodafone's capacity-based roaming model that eroded 1&1's 2025 EBITDA.

The legal mechanics governing mandatory spectrum sharing create significant complexity. Incumbents contend that mandatory spectrum sharing under Section 105 of the Telecommunications Act infringes Article 14 Grundgesetz constitutional property protections, while 1&1 signaled it will demand financial indemnification from the regulatory authority for higher wholesale costs if low-band sharing is denied. Incumbents are expected to file emergency petitions for administrative stays in Cologne Administrative Court immediately following order publication, though BNetzA can enforce immediate execution under administrative law, allowing 1&1 to access low-band frequencies while court appeals remain pending. Judicial annulments of past 5G auctions create legal instability, as the Cologne Administrative Court declared the 2018 Presidential Chamber decisions governing 5G auction rules unlawful due to political influence, with the Federal Administrative Court in

Leipzig rejecting BNetzA's appeal on October 16, 2025.

Incumbent opposition strategies reveal calculated resistance designed to preserve competitive advantages and create settlement leverage. Commercial negotiations between 1&1 AG and incumbent operators for voluntary lowband spectrum sharing broke down completely by year-end 2025, with incumbent operators resisting voluntary access to preserve proprietary indoor coverage advantages in frequencies below 1 GHz where 80% of data traffic occurs. Non-consensus analysis indicates Telefónica Deutschland's legal resistance aims to drive 1&1 into in-market M&A consolidation negotiations, while prolonged administrative litigation risks provide mutual incentive for operators to negotiate commercial consensus before final judicial rulings. Incumbent legal filings seek mandatory financial compensation or expanded reciprocal roaming access to offset ordered spectrum sharing.

The financial imperatives for 1&1 AG's network independence are substantial and quantifiable. Network deployment reached approximately 34% household coverage in the first half of 2026 using 2.0 GHz and 3.6 GHz spectrum, but lacking spectrum below 1 GHz severely limits indoor signal penetration and suburban reach where 80% of consumer mobile data is consumed. The national roaming contract with Vodafone calculates wholesale fees based on 1&1's percentage share of total data volume on Vodafone's network, and slower-thanexpected data growth on Vodafone's own network elevated 1&1's relative usage share, driving unexpected wholesale cost inflation. The agreement establishes a fixed minimum annual payment obligation of €50 million across its initial five-year term.

Strategic pathways and valuation implications diverge dramatically based on regulatory outcomes. BNetzA possesses the statutory means under Section 105 of the Telecommunications Act, a strong regulatory motive to defend its 4th MNO framework, and an immediate spring 2026 opportunity to order shared sub-1 GHz access. Forced low-band spectrum sharing supports 1&1's financial guidance of €800 million EBITDA in 2026 and €100 million annual EBITDA growth through 2028, whereas roaming status quo creates severe margin drag.

Valuation models show 1&1 is worth €13.50 to €17.50 per share under a standalone rollout, but re-rates to €34.50 to €43 under a wholesale model and up to €55 in consolidation. Persistent spectrum denial could force a strategic pivot toward market consolidation or a wholesale-only model, potentially unlocking substantial equity value.

Material evidence gaps remain across technical parameters, financial structures, and judicial timelines. The exact operational framework for mandatory sub-1 GHz spectrum sharing remains undefined pending BNetzA's final order publication, which can be resolved efficiently by monitoring BNetzA's official order publications during late spring 2026. The financial terms for cooperative low-band access are unconfirmed, leaving a wide variance between recurring spectrum lease payments and an estimated €800 million spectrum capital outlay, which will be resolved through regulatory fee determinations or expert consultations with German telecom policy specialists. The exact window and likelihood of Cologne Administrative Court granting an emergency administrative stay to incumbent operators remains unknown, though this risk can be monitored directly through administrative court docket filings following BNetzA's formal order.

The regulatory resolution window carries asymmetric risk-reward dynamics for concentrated equity investors targeting high IRR returns. BNetzA's accelerated spring 2026 timeline creates decisive catalyst potential, though non-consensus regulatory tracking suggests an expedited ruling by late May 2026 to preempt further market distortion remains vulnerable to incumbent litigation creating procedural bottlenecks and potential administrative stays. The interplay between statutory enforcement authority, constitutional property challenges, and strategic consolidation incentives establishes BNetzA's low-band determination as the critical inflection point governing whether 1&1 AG achieves independent network profitability or transitions toward wholesale restructuring or market consolidation by late 2026.
