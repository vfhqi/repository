EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Gek Terna SA is executing a landmark structural transformation from a cyclical construction contractor into a high-margin concessions cash-flow compounder, fundamentally resetting its earnings profile and competitive positioning. The group has successfully onboarded two transformational motorway concessions –Attiki Odos generating average annual EBITDA exceeding 300 million euros and Egnatia Odos expected to reach 220 million euros in annual EBITDA by 2030 –which together represent the core of a portfolio projected to deliver over 11 billion euros in cumulative cash distributions to the parent company over asset lifetimes.

This strategic pivot is supported by the divestment of the Terna Energy renewables stake to Masdar for 880 million euros in total proceeds, crystallizing substantial liquidity for reinvestment while simplifying the corporate structure. The materiality of change is unambiguously high, with concessions projected to generate approximately 80% of consolidated operating profitability by 2030, marking a decisive shift from construction-driven volatility to regulated, inflation-protected toll revenues.

Recent financial delivery strongly validates management's execution capability and establishes the credibility of forward targets. Group adjusted EBITDA surged 56.30% to 631.40 million euros in FY25, substantially outpacing peer growth trajectories and demonstrating the operational leverage inherent in the concessions model. Adjusted

EBITDA increased 22.44% to 165.9 million euros in Q1 2026, with concessions segment EBITDA rising 45% to 108.6 million euros and representing 65.46% of group operating profitability. The earnings quality is exceptionally robust, driven by average daily traffic on Attiki Odos growing 4.6% in FY25 to 286,278 vehicles and supported by contractually mandated CPI-indexed tariff adjustments that provide automatic inflation protection.

Management's historical posture qualifies as an honest-tracker to conservative sandbagger, with the group routinely exceeding intermediate targets on concessions during recent quarters, establishing a high-credibility foundation for the ambitious medium-term guidance.

- Earnings Calls The forward guidance framework centers on management's target for group adjusted EBITDA to exceed 1.0 billion euros by 2030, representing a 58% expansion from the FY25 baseline and reflecting the full-year contribution of both major motorway assets alongside a massive 8.8 billion euro construction backlog providing over five years of visible revenue. This target appears highly achievable given the operational maturity of Attiki Odos, the December 2025 commencement of Egnatia Odos, and the strategic UtilityCo joint venture with Motor Oil Hellas merging retail supply and thermal assets to stabilize the energy segment.

The balance sheet architecture provides exceptional downside protection, with non-recourse project finance debt comprising 95% of total group net debt at 4.086 billion euros and non-project finance leverage standing at a modest 0.60x in FY25. The structural insulation of parent-level leverage from project-level debt represents a critical risk-mitigation feature that distinguishes Gek Terna from highly leveraged construction peers.

Peer-relative positioning confirms Gek Terna's emerging leadership within the Greek infrastructure sector and favorable benchmarking against international operators. The group reported a concessions EBITDA margin of 57.50% in FY25, substantially exceeding the low single-digit EBIT margins typical of pure-play construction competitors and demonstrating the fundamental margin reset underway. Against domestic peers, Gek Terna ranks first in overall momentum due to structural concession onboarding, while peer Metlen targets doubling midterm EBITDA to 2.0 billion euros, validating sector-wide growth dynamics.

The external demand environment is exceptionally favorable, with the Greek infrastructure sector expanding rapidly with 8 to 10 billion euros of projects expected soon and structural underinvestment creating an estimated 80 billion euro infrastructure gap. Regulatory frameworks provide decades of visibility, with concessions boasting a weighted average remaining lifetime exceeding 25 years and operating under highly predictable inflation-indexed toll adjustment mechanisms that eliminate near-term pricing risk.

Sell-side positioning reveals concentrated domestic coverage with limited international analyst engagement, creating potential for future discovery as the concessions story matures. Gek Terna is actively covered by Axia Ventures Group with Constantinos Zouzoulas serving as lead analyst, maintaining a Buy recommendation with a target price of 27.40 euros per share that implies approximately 48% upside from the trading level of 18.52 euros.

Axia notes the market discounts Gek Terna's awarded concessions portfolio with an unjustified discount rate exceeding 16%, highlighting substantial valuation compression relative to intrinsic cash-flow value. The absence of broad international coverage represents both a near-term liquidity constraint and a medium-term catalyst opportunity as strategic execution de-risks the investment thesis and attracts broader institutional attention.

Activist presence has recently diminished but historically provided constructive engagement and validation of the transformation thesis. Marble Bar Asset Management increased its holding to 6.42% of share capital on June 4, 2025, before decreasing below the 5% threshold on January 22, 2026, retaining a 4.93% stake. The activist reduction likely reflects profit-taking following substantial share-price appreciation rather than fundamental concerns, and the retained position suggests ongoing conviction in the long-term value proposition. Corporate governance remains highly concentrated with Chairman and CEO Georgios Peristeris holding a dominant 31.80% stake, ensuring strategic continuity but potentially limiting activist influence on capital allocation decisions. The reconstituted fifteen-member Board of Directors approved in June 2025 with 35.7% female representation demonstrates commitment to governance best practices, although the dual role of Peristeris as both Chairman and CEO concentrates power and could weaken independent oversight.

The investment case synthesizes into a compelling setup characterized by structural earnings inflection, exceptional margin expansion, and decades of visible cash-flow generation insulated from construction cyclicality. Key risks center on traffic volume vulnerability to prolonged macroeconomic recessions, severe labor shortages and construction cost inflation pressuring backlog margins, and high initial maintenance requirements during the first five years of Egnatia Odos that could delay the full profitability ramp.

However, these risks are substantially mitigated by contractual inflation protection, diversified revenue streams across construction and concessions, and the non-recourse debt architecture that ring-fences project-level risks from parent solvency. The forward trajectory appears highly de-risked with management demonstrating consistent delivery against conservative guidance, establishing Gek Terna as a rare high-conviction compounder within European infrastructure markets.

Outliers worth noting

The most striking statistical outlier in Gek Terna's financial profile is the extreme concentration of non-recourse project finance debt, which comprised 95.00% of total group net debt at 4.086 billion euros in FY25, rendering this debt entirely non-recourse to the parent company. This debt architecture represents a profound deviation from typical European construction and infrastructure operators, where consolidated leverage metrics conventionally reflect parent-level credit risk.

Peer Metlen reported a net debt to EBITDA ratio of 2.40x in FY24, or 1.70x excluding non-recourse debt, illustrating that even when peers isolate project finance, the proportion rarely approaches Gek Terna's extreme 95% threshold. This bifurcated leverage structure creates significant analytical complexity, as traditional consolidated leverage ratios dramatically overstate parent-level financial risk and fail to capture the operational reality of cash-generative concessions insulated from corporate default.

The second major outlier is Gek Terna's concessions EBITDA margin of 57.50% in FY25, which vastly exceeds both domestic construction peers and international infrastructure operators. This margin profile compares favorably to historical low-margin construction peers, and the Egnatia Odos concession is modelled with an average EBITDA margin above 75% over its 35-year life, representing an extreme profitability outlier even within the toll-road subsector. While peer Metlen's ambition to double mid-term EBITDA to 2.00 billion euros showcases sector-wide growth, the margin differential remains stark, with Gek Terna's concessions segment delivering profitability levels typically reserved for monopolistic utility assets rather than competitive infrastructure operators. This margin outlier is directly attributable to the unique regulatory frameworks governing Greek motorway concessions, particularly inflation-indexed toll adjustments and congestion pricing provisions unavailable to European peers.

The third significant anomaly is the 61.7% workforce expansion in 2025 to 14,094 individuals, including subcontractors, which represents an extraordinary scaling velocity inconsistent with the group's modest topline revenue growth. Group revenues grew by only 0.33% year-on-year to 992.6 million euros in Q1 2026, creating a puzzling disconnect between workforce scaling and revenue generation. This deviation suggests either substantial forward hiring in anticipation of imminent backlog execution, inefficient labor deployment during concession integration phases, or structural changes in subcontractor engagement models that inflate headcount metrics without proportional revenue contribution.

The absence of peer-specific headcount benchmarks in the available documentation prevents definitive assessment of whether this represents an industry-wide phenomenon or a company-specific execution challenge. A fourth outlier emerges in the traffic performance on Attiki Odos, where average daily traffic grew 4.60% in FY25 to 286,278 vehicles, beating initial internal estimates and representing structural demand exceeding feasibility study projections.

This sustained traffic outperformance, combined with the asset's 4.6% growth in 2025 outperforming early feasibility study expectations, suggests either conservative initial modeling or unanticipated demand catalysts specific to the Athens metropolitan area. The deviation becomes more pronounced when contextualized against broader European toll-road operators, where mature concessions typically exhibit lowsingle-digit traffic growth constrained by population dynamics and modal shift to public transit.

The fifth notable anomaly is the rapid reduction of Marble Bar Asset Management's activist position from 6.42% to below the 5% threshold in early 2026, occurring during a period of strong operational delivery and strategic execution. Axia Ventures Group maintains a Buy recommendation with a target price of 27.40 euros per share, implying approximately 48.00% upside from the trading level of 18.52 euros, yet the activist investor chose to exit despite material valuation upside remaining.

This counterintuitive position reduction suggests either portfolio rebalancing constraints, liquidity concerns in the Greek equity market, or disagreement with the market's persistent discount to intrinsic value that the activist concluded was structurally unresolvable within their investment horizon.

A sixth outlier appears in the extreme volatility of the Conventional Energy segment, where revenue fell 12.14% to 464.7 million euros and segment EBITDA plummeted 45.79% to 11.6 million euros in Q1 2026. This profitability collapse stands in stark contrast to the group's overall operating momentum and highlights the structural challenge of merchant energy exposure even within a joint venture framework. The magnitude of this margin compression, occurring during a single quarter, raises questions about the sustainability of energy contributions and the effectiveness of the Motor Oil Hellas joint venture in stabilizing thermal generation economics.

Finally, the magnitude of capital deployment for major concessions represents a financial outlier, with 3.27 billion euros deployed for Attiki Odos including a 671.00 million euro parent equity ticket, and a 1.30 billion euro upfront payment for Egnatia Odos in late December 2025. These equity commitments, totaling close to 2.00 billion euros across the concessions portfolio, represent an extreme concentration of parent-level capital in illiquid, longduration assets with minimal secondary market tradability.

While concession business models inherently require substantial upfront equity, the absolute scale of Gek Terna's commitments relative to parent-level cash resources creates potential liquidity constraints that exceed typical infrastructure operator profiles, particularly given the absence of diversified geographic or asset-class exposure to mitigate concentration risk.
