Section 3 – OEM Propulsion Economics and Spare Parts Dynamics

Section summary
  1. Commercial spare parts remain the primary driver of MTU Aero Engines' OEM profitability due to their massive pricing premium over new engine series deliveries.
  2. The progressive normalization of spare engine delivery ratios at Pratt & Whitney and Safran will present a gradual margin headwind over the next eighteen months.
  3. Supply chain constraints have restricted new engine series growth, limiting short-term fixed- cost absorption but allowing high-margin aftermarket revenues to dominate the mix.

Spares Revenue and Premium Pricing

1.
Robust aftermarket sales: MTU Aero Engines experienced substantial revenue growth in its commercial spare parts segment during the first nine months of 2026. The company reported on 29 October 2026 that organic commercial spare parts sales grew by a mid-teens percentage in US dollar terms. This strong expansion was heavily underpinned by sustained demand for the V2500, the PW1100G-JM, and Pratt & Whitney Canada business jet programs. This highly profitable revenue stream will continue to propel absolute earnings growth for the group.

Resilient lifecycle demand: MTU Aero Engines benefits from delayed aircraft retirements which keep mature propulsion programs active for longer than previously planned. This extensive fleet age supports stable spare parts demand and stronger contract pricing tailwinds for the company. Bernstein Research estimated on 16 July 2026 that the GTF spares pool represented roughly 23% of the active fleet, compared to a desired ratio of 12%. While this elevated pool initially supported airline customers, the eventual normalization of this ratio represents a key headwind to future margins. This situation has temporarily boosted high-margin V2500 aftermarket demand as airlines extend the lives of legacy narrowbody fleets.

Peer Spares Dynamics and Ratio Normalization

5.
Gradual margin pressure: Safran has experienced a similar normalization in its spare engine delivery ratios as fleet disruptions gradually resolve. Jefferies Research reported on 13 August 2026 that the spare engine ratio for both the LEAP and GTF stood at roughly 20% of total deliveries. The broker expects this elevated ratio to progressively normalize toward a long-term target of 12% to 14% for both platforms by 2030. This normalization will likely weigh on near-term OEM profitability as the share of highly profitable spare engine shipments declines.
6.
Volatile program margins: Pratt & Whitney's segment margins have been heavily influenced by the shift from spare engine deliveries to core MRO output. Rothschild & Co Redburn estimated on 24 July 2026 that Pratt & Whitney's commercial OE sales fell 8% despite a 15% rise in overall engine deliveries, indicating a 25% to 35% decline in GTF spare engine shipments. This shift occurred because management prioritized directing material to MRO shops to reduce grounded aircraft. This operational decision impacts near-term profitability margins but reduces future compensation liabilities to airlines.
7.
Softening services momentum: Safran reported a notable deceleration in its high-margin aftermarket growth rates during the third quarter of 2026. Bernstein Research noted on 26 October 2026 that Safran's spare parts sales growth slowed to 17% in the third quarter compared to approximately 30% in the first half of the year. This sudden easing of services growth reflects changing workscopes and a normalization of fleet maintenance schedules. Such slower growth could restrict future margin expansion if the trend persists across the narrowbody market.
8.
Solid aftermarket backup: GE Aerospace has maintained strong services profitability despite broader supply chain constraints impacting new engine production. Bernstein Research indicated on 9 July 2026 that robust demand for legacy engine overhauls has provided strong support to propulsion margins. Additionally, the high backlog of shop visits and the low rate of aircraft retirements protect engine manufacturers from cyclical downturns. This deep backlog will underpin mid-term earnings stability across the major engine joint ventures.

Supply Constraints and EBIT Sensitivity

9.
Constrained production growth: MTU Aero Engines adjusted its full-year guidance on 29 October 2026 to reflect persistent supply chain bottlenecks. The company lowered its organic commercial series revenue growth guidance to mid-to-high single digits from the previous mid-to-high teens range. Morgan Stanley noted on 29 October 2026 that organic commercial series growth was cut to mid-to-high single digits on supply-chain bottlenecks. Slower new engine shipments limit the absorption of fixed costs within the commercial OEM production facilities.
10.
Outsized leverage effects: MTU Aero Engines' overall operating profit is highly sensitive to the volume mix between commercial spare parts and commercial maintenance. Rothschild & Co Redburn noted on 29 October 2026 that stronger-than-expected MRO growth was margin dilutive and failed to raise group adjusted EBIT guidance. This occurs because commercial MRO carries an operating margin of only 8.0%, whereas the OEM and military segment achieves margins exceeding 30%. Consequently, a small change in high-margin spare parts volume has a far greater impact on EBIT than a corresponding change in MRO volume.
11.
Stable segment margins: MTU Aero Engines' OEM segment has maintained robust profitability despite the headwind of a lower spare engine ratio. Morgan Stanley reported on 29 October 2026 that MTU's OEM adjusted EBIT margin fell to 28.6% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to 34.8% in the prior year period. This margin contraction was driven by a higher share of installed engine deliveries and a normalizing spare engine ratio. However, the segment margin remains highly resilient at 30.3% for the first nine months, allowing MTU to sustain high levels of profitability despite the shifting delivery mix.
12.
Ambitious long-term targets: MTU Aero Engines has established clear long-term margin targets that rely on the profitable expansion of its aftermarket business. Deutsche Bank noted on 12 August 2026 that the company guides to a 2030 OEM and military segment margin of 28% to 30%. This target is supported by the expectation that profitable commercial aftermarket sales will outgrow series production over the decade. Meeting this target will allow the group to expand EPS substantially as the installed engine base matures.
13.

If passenger traffic slows or older fleets are retired faster than anticipated, the high-margin spares tailwind will fail to offset series delivery drags, directly threatening MTU's mid-term earnings projection.
14.
Other side of the story: While supply chain constraints and declining spare engine ratios present immediate headwinds to OEM segment margins, the massive backlog of deferred maintenance ensures long-term profitability remains secure. Bernstein Research argued on 7 September 2026 that MTU will continue to benefit from an extended life cycle on legacy narrowbody engines like the V2500. The persistent shortage of new aircraft forces airlines to fly older platforms longer, guaranteeing a highly profitable spare parts tailwind that will insulate MTU's earnings from typical cyclical downturns.

Section 4 – Commercial MRO Network Expansion and Ramp-up Maturation

Section summary
  1. The rapid ramp-up of global MRO joint ventures like EME Aero and Zhuhai Jinwan is critical to expanding narrowbody engine aftermarket capacity.
  2. Severe margin dilution persists during the early phases of new shop maturation due to high start-up expenses and a lack of proprietary repair content.
  3. Long-term MRO profitability targets rely on the optimization of workscopes and the transition of labor-intensive activities to bestcost countries.
  4. MTU Aero Engines possesses substantial flexibility in managing its company-funded research and development spending.
  5. Structural cost advantages are being successfully secured through the transfer of labor-intensive work to Poland, Serbia, and China.
  6. Operational precedents from peers like Rolls-Royce demonstrate that aggressive contract and overhead restructuring can drive rapid margin expansion.

The joint venture inaugurated its second test cell on June 30, 2025, after investing over thirty-seven million US dollars in the site expansion. This strategic investment will enable the Polish facility to service over 500 engines annually from 2028 onwards. This capacity expansion is essential to ease the current backlog of grounded aircraft and support long-term segment earnings.
2.
Efficient operational ramp-up: MTU's secondary Chinese facility in the Jinwan district has demonstrated a remarkably fast operational acceleration since its opening in March 2025. The site successfully completed its 100th geared turbofan engine overhaul in less than 18 months, according to a company press release from September 21, 2026. Once fully ramped up, the facility will provide up to 260 annual shop visits to support growing regional demand. This high volume will help dilute fixed overhead costs and gradually improve MRO segment margins.
3.
Critical Asian hub: The combined capacity of MTU's main Zhuhai site and the new Jinwan facility positions the joint venture as a dominant global narrowbody maintainer. China Southern Airlines and MTU announced that the two sites will eventually support over 700 combined annual shop visits after full maturation. This massive scale helps secure long-term market leadership in the fastest-growing aviation region.

MacroAsia Corporation reported in its quarterly statement on November 10, 2026, that the associate employed 1,812 personnel, compared to 3,438 workers prior to the pandemic. The company is expanding its operations in Clark Pampanga by 2028 to capture growing third-party demand for heavy structural maintenance. Resolving labor constraints is critical because labor represents a major portion of MRO costs.

Path to the Target Margin Corridor

Bernstein Research reported on September 10, 2026, that the company aims to restore core maintenance profitability by optimizing its capacity mix. Achieving this target requires shifting 60% of MRO work to best-cost locations like Poland, Serbia, and China, up from 40% historically. 10.
High pass-through dilution: The dominance of zero-margin OEM material in shop visits remains the primary structural barrier to higher MRO margins. Bernstein Research explained on September 8, 2026, that approximately 60% to 70% of MTU's commercial MRO revenues are low-margin or zero-margin spare parts pass-throughs. Excluding this pass-through material, MTU's actual engine servicing activities generate solid operating margins of approximately 20%. Consequently, the underlying business model carries sufficient pricing power to offset rising material inflation.
