EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The central question for Tenaris is how plausible is the change now expected for this company, given its record of delivery and the strengths the business demonstrably has, rather than strengths it would need to acquire. This report finds that Tenaris's projected 2027 EBITDA margin recovery to the 20-25% corridor is plausible but faces material execution risks, particularly around the timing of domestic steel self-sufficiency and the resolution of Middle East shipping bottlenecks.

The assessment yields medium confidence in the company's ability to achieve consensus expectations, reflecting a balance between demonstrated historical resilience and significant operational uncertainties that remain unresolved. Tenaris has consistently demonstrated exceptional pricing power, achieving realized prices thirty-four percent higher than the standard PipeLogix Seamless Index, and its Rig Direct system, which manages the entire supply chain from mill to well site, accounted for seventy percent of United States sales by the first quarter of 2025.

These are strengths the business demonstrably has. During severe downturns, the company has proven its operational discipline: Tenaris maintained a reported EBITDA margin of thirteen point nine percent in 2016 despite a thirty-eight percent plunge in net sales, and management achieved annualized fixed-cost savings of two hundred thirty million dollars by the end of 2020. More recently, the company reported a reported EBITDA margin of twenty-four point four percent in 2024, demonstrating that structural efficiency gains now protect profitability during softer pricing cycles.

These cycle-tested capabilities provide a solid foundation for margin recovery. However, the path to 2027 recovery depends critically on strengths the company would need to acquire, specifically domestic steel self-sufficiency to escape the crushing burden of fifty percent Section 232 tariffs. Effective March 12, 2025, the US government extended Section 232 tariffs of twenty-five percent to virtually all steel imports, which subsequently increased to fifty percent on June 4, 2025, forcing the company to absorb high tariff duties on imported steel bars used for its seamless pipe operations.

Bernstein Research estimated on 21 July 2025 that the fifty percent tariff causes a seventeen percent decline in US EBITDA. The company's response involves major capital programs: on 24 June 2026, Tenaris announced a more than $90 million investment program for its Koppel steel shop and Ambridge seamless pipe plant to streamline production and improve productivity, and on 22 May 2026, the company announced a landmark CAD 306 million joint investment to modernize its Sault Ste. Marie Industrial Centre. Yet these projects are scheduled to extend into the second half of 2027, meaning structural margin benefits will lag behind consensus recovery timelines.

The competitive landscape has shifted materially against Tenaris. Vallourec has closed its historical margin gap with Tenaris after completing the closure of its high-cost German rolling mills, and Vallourec produces 100% of its steel domestically in the United States through its Youngstown, Ohio electric arc furnace facility with a capacity of 750,000 tons, matching its rolling requirements. This structural advantage means Vallourec is a net beneficiary of US steel tariffs while Tenaris continues paying duties on imported semi-finished steel.

Furthermore, Tenaris's Koppel facility has a limited capacity of 432,000 tons compared to total US steel requirements of 1,571,875 tons, requiring the company to purchase 1,139,875 tons externally, half of which has historically been imported. Until this massive sourcing gap closes, Tenaris will face persistent margin compression. Near-term recovery depends on three interconnected catalysts: lagged US seamless pricing realization, normalization of Middle East logistics, and accelerating offshore deepwater volumes.

The benchmark PipeLogix index has a one-to-two quarter lagged impact on realized pricing, meaning that the sharp pricing recovery observed in late 2025 and 2026 will not fully support corporate earnings per share until mid-2026. Importantly, US seamless prices jumped nine percent in October 2026, marking the largest monthly gain since July 2008. On the logistics front, the armed conflict effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz for most of the second quarter 2026 to commercial shipping, causing sequential revenue and EBITDA margin contractions, but the Strait of Hormuz closure deferred approximately one hundred thirty million dollars of premium Middle East tube sales in the second quarter, and because these orders are contractual, they are expected to be invoiced rapidly once shipping routes reopen.

Finally, deepwater offshore spending is expected to reach one hundred twenty billion dollars by 2028, providing a high-margin backlog that buffers North American onshore volatility. & Co Redburn Broker models reflect cautiously optimistic assumptions about Tenaris's 2027 outlook. Rothschild & Co Redburn's report dated 20 August 2026 projects Tenaris's EPS will rise from $1.96 in 2026 to $2.60 in 2027, with this expected earnings expansion hinging on the successful pass-through of US pricing and Middle East volume recovery.

Bernstein Research projects that Tenaris's revenues will grow to $12,400 million by 2027 while keeping reported EPS stable at $1.93, assuming that global deepwater offshore activity and regional pricing remain favorable through 2027. Tenaris traded on a forward valuation of 9.30x EV/EBITDA and 12.40x P/E based on 2027 estimates, multiples that embed significant margin recovery expectations. However, Rothschild & Co Redburn stood 11% below consensus EBITDA estimates for 2026 due to rising unit costs, with these rising inputs including US hot-rolled coil and scrap prices that threaten to compress tubular manufacturing margins.

Critical evidence gaps constrain confidence in the 2027 recovery thesis. The exact formulas used in Rig Direct long-term contracts remain undisclosed, limiting visibility on pricing pass-through timing. The precise monthly production growth curve for the Koppel steel mill through 2027 has not been specified, creating uncertainty around self-sufficiency achievement. Geothermal contract margins are unavailable, preventing assessment of whether this emerging segment matches traditional tubular profitability.

Most importantly, the timeline for clearing the Middle East shipping backlog remains speculative, tied to geopolitical factors beyond Tenaris's control. Tenaris experienced an increase in its operating working capital days to 143 days as of June 30, 2026 compared to 128 days in the prior year, with total operating working capital rising to $4,648,386 thousand from $4,328,475 thousand in June 2025. This elevated working capital absorbs cash and reduces capital deployment flexibility until shipments normalize.

The net assessment is that Tenaris possesses formidable demonstrated strengths–premium pricing power, customer integration through Rig Direct, and proven cost discipline during downturns–that support a credible path to margin recovery. However, achieving consensus 2027 targets requires successfully acquiring new operational capabilities, specifically domestic steel self-sufficiency, that will only materialize in late 2027 at the earliest.

The recovery also depends on external factors outside management control: the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, sustained offshore investment momentum, and the absence of further tariff escalation. If domestic steel expansions experience delays, or if Middle East volumes are permanently lost rather than deferred, EBITDA margins could trough near 20% rather than recovering to 25%, triggering substantial EPS downgrades and valuation multiple contraction.

Conversely, if Koppel and Sault Ste. Marie ramp successfully while offshore growth accelerates, Tenaris could exceed current consensus, supported by its robust net cash position that enables continued shareholder returns. The balance of evidence supports medium confidence: the demonstrated cycle strengths are real, but the required operational transitions face material execution risk over the investment horizon.
