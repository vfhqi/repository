APM Mini-Memo: Attendo

The one-line view. Attendo is lifting earnings per share from 6 Swedish kronor towards 9 by 2028 through fuller care homes and buybacks, and the story depends on staffing those homes at wages that municipal prices repay.

What the company does

Attendo is the largest private care provider in the Nordic region. Attendo runs about 770 local units for about 28,000 customers, with care for older people at 72% of sales and disability care at 14%. Municipalities and Finnish welfare regions pay for the care, and Attendo leases almost all of its buildings.

Attendo is two businesses with very different margins. Finland is 58% of sales and earned a 9.0% operating margin after rent costs in 2025; Scandinavia, almost all of it Sweden, is 42% of sales and earned 4.5%. Group sales of 18,991 million kronor were flat in 2025 because Attendo has been handing back low-margin outsourcing contracts, and homes that Attendo operates itself are now 92% of revenue.

A plausible investment thesis

The story is arithmetic rather than sales growth. The research on file reduces Attendo's earnings to occupancy, multiplied by the share of own-operated homes, multiplied by price indexation, on a leased base with a falling share count. Rent and staff are largely fixed in a care home, so most of the revenue from each extra resident becomes profit. The research puts that gearing at about 7% of adjusted earnings per share for each half point of operating margin.

Scandinavia is what is improving now. The Scandinavian operating margin rose to 5.4% from 3.3% in the second quarter of 2026, against historical margins of 7% to 8%, and DNB Carnegie models only 5.5% for 2027. Group occupancy is 88% against a target of at least 92%, so the recovery has room to run for the next six to twelve months and beyond.

The record makes the target believable. Adjusted earnings per share doubled from 3 kronor in 2023 to 6 in 2025, and the 2026 target of 5.50 was reached a year early. The company now targets at least 9 kronor by 2028, and DNB Carnegie forecast 8.77 for 2027 and 9.72 for 2028 on 8 October 2026. Projected buybacks of 1.6 billion kronor through 2028 add to the step-up, funded by debt of 1.1 times profit after rent costs against a target range of 1.5 to 2.5 times.

Three drivers carry the case: group occupancy each quarter, the Scandinavian margin, and the pace of buybacks.

The Target Setup

No Investment Analyst memo exists for Attendo, so there are no Bottom-up/Stock-driven ratings and the Target Setup read here is my own. The Target Setup is 'Big Corporate Change-driven EPSU'. The step-up in earnings per share comes from the company's own actions: the exit from outsourcing, the repair of the Scandinavian margin and the buybacks.

Demand does not yet drive the numbers. Organic growth was 1.2% in the second quarter of 2026, so 'Demand-driven EPSU' would join only if the 1,250 beds under construction, adding 2% to 3% to capacity a year through 2027, fill quickly. None of the cause-for-concern setups applies, because the shares stand within 1.3% of their 52-week high.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Simple: the case is one mechanism with a handful of observables, although no Case Riskiness rating exists without a memo. What needs to go right is plain: Attendo must hire enough permanent care staff to fill its beds, and municipal prices must catch up with wages.

The main risk is that a staff shortage and slow indexation arrive together. Vacancies force Attendo onto expensive agency staff, while municipal contracts reprice about twelve months after costs rise. The Finnish wage agreement adds 3.5% from September 2026 and 2.7% from September 2027. The research's case of no margin improvement gives earnings per share of 6.17 kronor and a value of 70 kronor a share, against a price of 132.7.

Four things show early whether the risk is biting: occupancy moving towards 92%, the share of agency staff, Finnish prices against the wage rise, and the new chief executive. Virpi Holmqvist was appointed from 14 September 2026 and has committed to the strategy; Martin Tivéus, who led the Finnish turnaround, leaves by the turn of the year. Her first results are the test of continuity.

Business quality

Good, for a business paid by the state. Residents rent their rooms directly from Attendo, so a municipality cannot easily cancel a home, and private operators run care at about 10% lower cost than public provision in Sweden and close to 20% lower in Finland. Capital spending is about 1% of sales, and free cash flow of 1,041 million kronor in 2025 funded 453 million of buybacks and a dividend of 1.80 kronor a share.

The limits are a thin margin and large leases. The group operating margin after rent costs was 6.7% in 2025 against 8.0% at Ambea, and lease liabilities of 13,284 million kronor take debt to 3.9 times profit on the reported figures. Finland shows the resilience: its margin rose to 8.9% from 6.8% in the first half of 2026 even after regions cut prices.

Plausible returns and valuation

Fair, not cheap. At 132.7 kronor on 13 November 2026, Attendo trades on 14.2 times earnings two years forward, the 58th percentile of its own history, with a market value of about 1.6 billion euros. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 13 November is C, a reading of valuation alone.

The plausible return clears your bar only with a higher multiple. The weekly routine's figure of 12 November is 27.4% a year, an A against your anchors, built from consensus earnings growth of 12.3% a year and a multiple rising from 14.2 to 17.8 times. Earnings growth and the dividend alone would sit nearer the C anchor of 13% to 16%, in my reading.

The market has reasons to pay more. Our Sector note judges that investors are starting to read these companies as owners of scarce care beds rather than low-margin contractors, and a Scandinavian margin above forecast would lift estimates. The weekly Sell-side Momentum rating of 12 November is A and the weekly Technical Momentum rating of 13 November is B, though the chart's uptrend reading switched on and off twice in the look-back.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

Pros: demand is assured and supply is scarce. Scandinavia's population over 80 grows by about half between 2020 and 2030, Sweden needs over 400 new nursing homes by 2032, and municipalities cannot build fast enough. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 13 November is A, read from the Sector's environment only. Finland's reform of 1 January 2026, letting regions buy care privately, is an option for 2027 and later.

Cons: the theme is largely known and the customer is short of money. The Nordic care operators rose 64.7% over the twelve months to 22 October 2026; our Sector note judges that only Attendo's Scandinavian margin and the Finnish reform are not yet recognised. Finnish regions may take care back in house and Sweden debates limits on welfare profits; the outcome of the September 2026 Swedish election is not in our files.

In short

  • Interesting because the step-up is self-made: fuller homes, a better mix and buybacks take adjusted earnings per share from 6 kronor in 2025 towards at least 9 by 2028
  • Credible because management delivers early: the 2026 target was reached in 2025 and debt is below the target range
  • Scandinavia is the upside to watch: its margin is 5.4% against a history of 7% to 8% and a 2027 forecast of 5.5%
  • Staffing and indexation decide the case: track occupancy towards 92%, agency staff and Finnish prices against the 3.5% wage rise
  • A full memo is the next step: the 27.4% plausible return needs the multiple to rise from 14.2 to 17.8 times, and no Bottom-up/Stock-driven rating yet tests that
