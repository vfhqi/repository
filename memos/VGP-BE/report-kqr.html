Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Grid capacity and connection timelines will dictate whether European logistics landlords can successfully transition to high-yield data center JVs, driving substantial EPS and NAV growth over the next three years.

Power as an Earnings Catalyst

1.
Crucial inquiry: "For the listed European logistics landlords other than Segro (VGP, CTP, Warehouses De Pauw, Tritax Big Box, Montea, LondonMetric and Argan), how much grid capacity, in megawatts or megavolt-amperes, does each hold with a firm connection date, and by when? What rent, yield on cost and value per megawatt do brokers and data-centre developers put on that powered land, whether it is leased as powered shells, sold, or built out in joint ventures, and how much of that value sits in current broker net asset values? Are new commitment fees and queue reforms in Germany, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom shortening the five-to-ten-year wait for connections, which would end the landlords' advantage sooner?."
2.
Accelerating growth: Rising demand for artificial intelligence and cloud computing has positioned powered land as a critical driver of future earnings per share and net value growth for European logistics developers. These landlords can generate substantially higher returns by securing large-scale grid connections compared to traditional warehousing development. This power-first strategy addresses the extreme scarcity of utility infrastructure across Europe, creating a major barrier to entry that supports high yields on cost. Consequently, securing these connections is a fundamental driver of capital value gains and long-term rental income.
3.
Insatiable demand: Hyperscalers and data-centre developers are increasingly prioritizing immediate access to energy and the availability of powered land over traditional location factors when planning new facilities. Global data centre power demand is projected to rise from seventy-eight terawatt-hours in 2025 to nearly two hundred terawatt-hours by 2030, but grid access remains the largest constraint. Because technology tenants have strict deployment schedules, sites with secured power and planning permits command significant valuation premiums. This urgency shifts pricing power to landlords who can deliver ready-built powered shells or clear land parcels on a bankable timeline.
4.
Elevated risks: Unlocking the massive optionality of digital infrastructure projects introduces substantial operational and financial risks for mid-cap logistics landlords. These developers face severe constraints including grid equipment lead times, complex permitting procedures, and rising development costs. Additionally, funding large-scale capital expenditures is subject to external factors like volatile energy prices and interest rate shifts. Any delays in power delivery or failure to secure anchor tenants could result in significant capital impairment and dilutive equity raises.

5.
Underappreciated upside: Current broker net asset valuations largely fail to recognize the unrecognized option value of powered land held by developers outside of Segro, such as VGP, CTP, and Tritax Big Box. Because these pipelines are valued under historical cost accounting, successful pre-letting represents a material near-term catalyst for net tangible assets. For instance, developers can capture development margins of over thirty percent by utilizing a highly capital-efficient joint venture structure. This strategic shift from standard warehousing to digital infrastructure is expected to drive a rapid earnings inflection over the next three years.

However, regulatory reforms aiming to replace first-come queues with project-readiness tests could threaten this defensive advantage. Consequently, understanding the local policy landscape in Europe's major markets is critical to sizing the duration of this investment opportunity.

Section 4 – Grid Connection Queue Reforms and Commitment Fees

Section summary
  1. Regulatory reforms across Europe are transitioning grid connection queues from chronological order to maturity-based systems to eliminate speculative applications.
  2. Increased financial hurdles and stricter milestone enforcement will filter out unviable projects but introduce near-term execution delays during the transition phase.

United Kingdom Connection Reforms

1.
Regulated acceleration: The British energy regulator Ofgem approved a comprehensive connection queue reform run by the National Energy System Operator to replace the old chronological allocation method. Under this new framework, projects must demonstrate objective readiness milestones to transition to Gate 2 and secure priority. This regulatory shift prioritizes viable developments over speculative applications to streamline the grid. Consequently, this reform could ultimately shorten connection timelines for ready projects across the United Kingdom.
2.
Substantial penalties: Ofgem proposed a new Data Centre Commitment Fee of £237,500 to £712,500 per megawatt to penalize speculative connection requests. The proposed fee represents roughly two point five to seven point five percent of average data center development costs. Developers would forfeit these substantial fees if their projects exit the connection queue early. This financial hurdle is designed to weed out unviable data center projects that congest the network.
3.
Transitional bottlenecks: Gresham House Energy Storage Fund experienced direct delays to its battery project construction timelines due to the lengthy queue reform process. The national review process analyzed more than six thousand grid connection offers across multiple technologies to restructure the queue. These transitional delays pushed the completion of some key pipeline projects beyond original target dates. Consequently, these regulatory changes introduce significant near-term execution risks for energy infrastructure developers.
4.
Contrary evidence: The ongoing British grid connection queue reform has actually prolonged project waiting times by pausing and re-assessing the entire pipeline of applications since 2024. National Energy System Operator updates delayed binding connection offers for prioritised Gate 2 projects from early 2026 to late September 2026 due to unresolved technical and engineering issues. This system-wide delay proves that regulatory changes can temporarily worsen grid bottlenecks instead of resolving them.

Continental European Policy Updates

5.
Proactive enforcement: TenneT is implementing early financial commitments and a priority framework in the Netherlands to reduce speculative grid applications. The Dutch regulator ACM authorized TenneT to prioritize connections based on safety and congestion mitigation rather than reservation order. Furthermore, TenneT is utilizing flexible transmission agreements to unlock up to nine gigawatts of off-peak capacity. These measures aim to accelerate Dutch grid connection access for viable industrial consumers.
6.
Mature prioritisation: The German Federal Network Agency and transmission operators introduced maturity-based procedures called AgNes and Reifegradverfahren starting in April 2026. This reform replaces the traditional chronological grid queue with a readiness-based model for large power consumers. Additionally, the Netzpaket draft act introduces monthly grid-capacity maps and unused-capacity clawbacks after three years. These policies could successfully clear dormant connection permits across major German industrial zones.
7.
Eroding premiums: The successful rollout of readiness-based reforms across Europe threatens to undermine the competitive advantage of early-stage logistics landlords. If these policies successfully shorten the traditional five-to-ten-year wait times, competitors can obtain power connections much faster. This accelerated access would quickly diminish the high scarcity premiums currently commanded by first-mover landlords. Thus, rapid queue clearance represents a structural threat to the long-term asset valuations of mid-cap developers.
