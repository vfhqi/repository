What the company does

SBM Offshore is the global leader in deepwater floating production. It builds and operates floating production, storage and offloading vessels in two segments: Lease & Operate, a fleet of long-term charters, and Turnkey, design and construction. Its 16 units produce over two million barrels of oil equivalent a day, 14 of them leased against MODEC's 16. Revenue in 2025 was 5,066 million dollars on the cash-aligned Directional basis, with operating profit before depreciation of 1,709 million, a 33.7 percent margin; 2026 guidance is about 7.6 billion and about 1.9 billion. Guyana and Brazil produce 93 percent of that profit and three clients 88 percent of revenue.

A plausible investment thesis

The change is real and has already paid. Since an insider succession in April 2024 the company has moved from owning its vessels to Sale & Operate and Build-Operate-Transfer contracts. ExxonMobil bought the One Guyana vessel for 2,321 million dollars in February 2026, Petrobras awarded the two Sergipe units for 8 billion dollars in May, and thirteen standardised Fast4Ward hulls were ordered ahead of contracts. Net debt fell 35 percent to 3,680 million dollars, backlog rose 14 percent to a record 35.6 billion, and distributions step up 57 percent to 440 million dollars this year inside a minimum 2.1 billion framework to 2031.

From here the mechanism to earnings per share is awards, not propagation. Consensus 2026 earnings per share of about 6 dollars carry a 608 million dollar gain on One Guyana and fall to 4.47 dollars in 2027, while the engine beneath grows from about 2.8 dollars excluding the gain to about 4.6 on lower interest and a share count falling about 4 percent a year. The sell-side models from booked backlog, so each award is an upgrade. Track award velocity, above all the Longtail final investment decision expected late in 2026, Turnkey margin recognition past the 25 percent completion threshold, and the 24-month-forward multiple.

Business quality

Good rather than great, with a narrow moat. Uptime of 98.9 to 99.1 percent, the standardised hull programme and the deepest gas-handling engineering make this the operator clients re-hire. Against that, return on invested capital is about 15 percent, cash conversion is below 80 percent, and there is no pricing power, because Petrobras tenders start every bid at zero. The blended margin compresses by design from 33.8 percent toward 24.7 percent as Turnkey rises against Lease & Operate. The memo's Foundations' Robustness rating is B, capped by two clients, two basins and three Chinese yards.

The strongest counter-case

SBM Offshore is a false friend: the transactions that drive the headline deplete the annuity. The Lease & Operate margin is 46 percent from 50, and the mix shifts to lump-sum construction at 15 to 20 percent margins won on price (Sergipe at 3.7 billion dollars against MODEC's 5.3 billion) and executed in Chinese yards running at nine times their historical output. The history holds a 35 percent overrun on Frade in 2008 and a 470 million dollar termination on Yme in 2013. A stacked Sergipe and Longtail delay could compress forward earnings per share by up to 25 percent, and with 93 percent of profit on two basins, one lost award is a case event.
