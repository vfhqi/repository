Section summary

Overall confidence is medium-low because Instalco's 2026 margin recovery is real, but the evidence still cannot distinguish durable price retention from volume, mix, execution, selectivity and temporary competitor-capacity effects.

The research base drew on more than one hundred relevant documents covering Instalco and at least eight named peers or comparators, including Bravida, Assemblin Caverion, SPIE, Burkhalter, Fasadgruppen, Nimlas and InstallatørGruppen, with evidence spanning company filings, earnings transcripts, investor presentations, broker research, expert interviews and regulatory material across Sweden, Norway, Finland and Germany.

1.
Best evidence for resilience: Instalco's reported FY2025 compensation mix of 43 percent fixed-price projects and 57 percent cost-plus or other remuneration provides meaningful protection against input-cost volatility, while project type comprising 37 percent service, 33 percent renovation and 30 percent new production offers recurring or shorter-cycle work that is less correlated with the construction cycle. Q2 2026 organic growth of 8.4 percent, EBITA of SEK274 million and an EBITA margin of 7.1 percent, versus 6.4 percent a year earlier, demonstrates improving economics, with management attributing the result to higher volumes, operational improvements and a gradually improving market, while order backlog rose 17 percent overall and 14.8 percent organically.

The clearest pass-through precedent comes from Instalco's Q3 2021 earnings call, when management said cost-plus arrangements allowed the company to push much of the price increase to customers over time, although older fixed-price projects were problematic and the impact would remain for several quarters.

2.
Key uncertainty and the central gap: Country-level contract mix, practical pass-through lag, service churn, tender win rates and price-volume decomposition remain undisclosed, preventing a clean separation of whether 2026 organic growth is price or volume and whether Swedish tender margins can survive the 2027 collective wage round and a metals spike on un-indexed fixed-price work.

The available evidence does not establish a documented automatic pass-through mechanism for the spring 2027 Swedish collective wage round on Instalco contracts priced during 2026, because Instalco defines its project revenue as generally fixed price and does not disclose country-level revenue covered by wage indexation, leaving the main risk concentrated in the fixed-price order book signed before wage rates are known.

The 2026 metals scenario is more concerning than a normal wage increase because copper and aluminium can move abruptly while project prices remain fixed, with copper approximately 21 percent higher year on year in 2026, suggesting that a future metal shock could be more concentrated in electrical and HVAC inputs than the original 2022 inflation episode.

3.
Peer pricing-power benchmarks: SPIE is the clearest positive benchmark for contract-based inflation protection, with explicit identification of indexation clauses, shorter offer validity, short activity cycles and daily updates to procurement bases and pricing tools as mechanisms for absorbing inflation, and management stating in July 2024 that it had passed inflation through to customers and achieved an additional price increase beyond inflation.

InstallatørGruppen provides the clearest contractual contrast with Sweden, because Danish AB18 contracts longer than 12 months normally include automatic price indexation, while some contractors instead accept fixed prices, and contracts shorter than 12 months generally price expected wage inflation into the initial tender but do not receive subsequent indexation.

Fasadgruppen is a useful negative comparator because its renovation exposure has not insulated it from price givebacks, with around 80 percent of 2025 sales from renovation yet adjusted EBITA margin declining to 8.8 percent from 9.5 percent in 2022 as new-build contractors moved into renovation, and most projects being fixed price so rising material costs and time overruns affect margins directly, particularly in Sweden.

4.
Service and partnering resilience: The Skanska framework running from 2026 to 2032 for electrical and heating- and-plumbing installations under Adda-based preschools, schools and sports halls demonstrates customer stickiness more clearly than pricing power, with Instalco having worked successfully with Skanska for many years and the new agreement expanding the relationship, although no call-off volume, price-escalation clause, minimum order or margin was disclosed.

The VAROPreem framework for 2026–2029 with two one-year extension options to provide industrial scaffolding at all Swedish facilities, including refinery and energy operations, offers a different form of customer quality, with national scope, site familiarity and safety requirements increasing switching costs relative to a single-site tender, although the contract is still a framework rather than a disclosed revenue guarantee.

Service and partnering could stabilise EPS through the wage round because they allow more frequent repricing, customer dialogue or cost visibility than a long fixed-price turnkey project, with the Skanska framework covering standardised concepts and the Region Blekinge agreement placing strong emphasis on quality and expertise rather than price alone, reducing tender volatility and selling costs.

5.
Competitor capacity allocation and temporary relief: The capacity-diversion hypothesis is credible but not sufficient to explain all of Instalco's 2026 improvement, because Bravida's first-half 2026 order intake increased 25 percent to SEK19,926 million and backlog increased 21 percent to SEK20,372 million, primarily due to a single Norwegian data-centre order worth more than SEK4.3 billion to its joint venture, with DNB Carnegie estimating that Bravida's data-centre backlog could reach SEK8–9 billion and Deutsche Bank arguing that strong data-centre demand could remove capacity from non-data-centre technical services, supporting margins for companies competing in ordinary work.

Company-specific selectivity is the stronger primary explanation for current relief, because Instalco had already adopted cautious order-taking, prioritising the right projects, customers and prices before the recovery, with the 2025 Q4 report saying the improvement was primarily its own performance rather than reliance on external market conditions, and the 2026 report saying that more projects enabled greater selectivity, meaning improved margins may reflect refusing low-priced work, better execution and mix rather than achieving higher prices on comparable tenders.

6.
Swedish tender conditions and regional pressure: Swedish installation competition is not uniform geographically, but is particularly intense where project supply has fallen short of installer capacity, with management in February 2025 describing southern Sweden as experiencing a "perfect storm" because many new competitors had entered the region while large projects had not materialised, creating overcapacity, supporting the broader claim of a sharp increase in bidder numbers though the precise increase from five to fifteen bidders across Swedish mid-sized tenders cannot be independently verified from the supplied evidence.

The anticipated Swedish rate-hiking cycle creates a counterforce to the construction recovery because higher financing costs can delay private projects even as public and industrial investment improves, with Nordea expecting the Riksbank to raise the policy rate to 2 percent in November 2026 and Morgan Stanley's September 2026 analysis pricing approximately 27 basis points of Swedish hikes in the fourth quarter and another 35 basis points in the first half of 2027.

7.
Germany and geographic diversification: Geographic diversification reduces Instalco's dependence on Swedish tender conditions, but it does not uniformly reduce earnings volatility, because Norway adds public and infrastructure demand but carries the highest current construction-cost inflation at 5.4 percent and policy rates of 4.50 percent, Finland adds data-centre, industrial, renovation and energy-storage exposure, but residential activity and backlog remain weak, and Germany adds Fabri's 12.7 percent historical EBITA margin and a large structural market, but introduces subsidy, labour-cost and integration uncertainty.
8.
Principal indicators and confidence-ranked range: The highest-confidence hypothesis is selective resilience rather than unrestricted pricing power, with Instalco likely to protect service, partnering, specialist and technically complex work while remaining exposed on ordinary unindexed fixed-price tenders, with a reasonable mediumconfidence operating range of an EBITA margin around 7 percent to 8 percent through 2027, with the lower end more likely if wages and metals rise before repricing and the upper end requiring continued execution gains and selective tendering, consistent with current LTM EBITA margin of 6.6 percent, the 8 percent company target and DNB Carnegie's 7.8 percent 2027 consensus reference.

The higher-pricing-power hypothesis would require Swedish margins to remain near or above 7 percent after the 2027 wage round, backlog per FTE to stay elevated, and organic growth to continue without a deterioration in project award margins, with cash conversion recovering from 90 percent toward 100 percent while leverage falls below 2.5x despite Fabri consolidation and minority interests, but confidence in this hypothesis is medium-low because the company has not yet separated price from volume or disclosed tender-level margin data.

Section 2 – Instalco Contract Mix and Pricing Mechanics

Contract architecture and disclosed mix Instalco’s disclosed contract economics provide meaningful protection against input-cost volatility, but they do not establish that 57% of revenue is automatically indexed or fully cost-pass-through. For FY2025, 43% of projects were fixed-price and 57% used cost-plus or other remuneration, including service assignments. Instalco separately states that variable components are generally absent except for fines, so the 57% category should be read as a broad commercial classification rather than as a quantified index-linked share.

The mix has become less fixed-price over time, although the disclosures are not a perfect revenue-weighted time series. Fixed-price projects represented approximately 46% in 2021, approximately 42% in 2023, approximately 44% in 2024, and 43% in FY2025. Partnering was approximately 32% in 2021, approximately 33% in 2023, and just over 30% in 2024, while the remainder comprised service and other collaboration or payment forms. The evidence therefore supports a gradual shift away from fixed-price exposure, but not a precise conclusion about how much revenue has become index-linked.

Instalco’s partnering model is economically closer to time-and-materials or cost-plus contracting than to conventional fixed-price work. The customer receives visibility into project costs, while Instalco secures remuneration through a mark-up or fixed component within the partnership. This structure reduces direct exposure to wage and material overruns, but it does not guarantee that the mark-up itself rises with every cost increase or that the customer accepts all proposed scope changes.

company’s larger projects are generally handled through partnering, which is important because project duration and execution complexity are likely to be greatest in that category. Instalco’s 2024 presentation said that it limits exposure to large fixed-price projects and uses partnering to reduce risk, supported by second opinions, project forecasts and subsidiary board oversight. This suggests that the reported 43% fixed-price share may overstate the group’s exposure to the most complex projects, although the company does not disclose fixed-price revenue by project size, country or discipline.

Segments, service economics and customer stickiness The project mix is another form of contractual diversification, with FY2025 sales comprising 37% service, 33% renovation and 30% new production. Service and renovation together represented approximately 70% of sales, providing more recurring or shorter-cycle work than new production. The company’s service work is not necessarily contractually bound in every case, because its accounting policy describes service as occasional customer needs, but the repeat relationship can still support pricing and renewal economics.

Instalco’s discipline mix reduces dependence on any single technical market, with FY2025 sales comprising electrical 33%, heating and plumbing 28%, industry 19%, ventilation 15%, and technical consulting 5%. Industrial and consulting work may be less directly tied to residential construction than conventional building installation, while technical complexity can make supplier replacement more difficult. However, the evidence does not show separate contract structures or achieved price increases for these disciplines, so their apparent resilience should not be treated as measured pricing power.

Customer concentration is low enough to support local bargaining power, but construction companies remain the largest customer group. In FY2025, construction companies represented 48% of sales, followed by industrial companies at 20%, public-sector customers at 9%, property companies at 8%, and other customers at 15%. Approximately 2,000 customers were reported in the 2025 annual report, and no single customer represented 10% or more of sales. This limits single-customer risk, but the high construction-company share means that large contractors can still exert substantial tender and payment pressure across many local subsidiaries.

Switching costs arise primarily from technical competence, local relationships, lead times and the ability to coordinate several disciplines rather than from contractual exclusivity. Instalco cites long-term local relationships and short lead times as competitive advantages in its mid-sized project market, where more than 80% of project revenue excluding services comes from assignments between SEK 1 million and SEK 75 million. A long relationship can support price retention, but public procurement and standardised construction specifications can make the installation component more substitutable when several qualified bidders have capacity.

Stakeholders able to shift the outcome

NCC, Skanska and Peab have the incentive and procurement scale to resist installer price increases, particularly where projects are standardised and several contractors can bid. They also have the power to improve outcomes by using partnering, early design involvement, risk-sharing mechanisms and escalation clauses that reduce the need for installers to price a large contingency into tenders. Instalco’s SEK100 million NCC renovation assignment in central Stockholm illustrates the value of long-standing relationships and technically demanding work, but the press release does not disclose the contract’s indexation or margin terms.

Public buyers are another decisive stakeholder because they can choose lowest price, quality-weighted criteria or multi-supplier frameworks. Region Blekinge’s selection of Inmatiq on quality and expertise is an early positive signal for specialist pricing, while the Skanska Adda framework shows how standardisation can reduce delivery risk but also enable cost comparison. A shift toward quality-weighted procurement would favour technically capable groups, whereas continued lowest-price awards would keep fixedprice margins under pressure.

Suppliers can push the result in either direction. Morgan Stanley’s 19 October 2026 industrial survey found healthy pricing in switchgear, electrical equipment and transformers, but warned that the timing mismatch between supplier price increases and customer price realisation could create near-term margin pressure. Instalco’s suppliers therefore have the power to raise input costs before the group can reprice customer contracts, particularly in electrical and HVAC work exposed to copper, aluminium and specialist equipment.

Confidence-ranked hypotheses and decisive indicators The highest-confidence hypothesis is selective resilience rather than unrestricted pricing power, with Instalco likely to protect service, partnering, specialist and technically complex work while remaining exposed on ordinary unindexed fixed-price tenders. A reasonable medium-confidence operating range is an EBITA margin around 7% to 8% through 2027, with the lower end more likely if wages and metals rise before repricing and the upper end requiring continued execution gains and selective tendering. This range is consistent with current LTM EBITA margin of 6.6%, the 8% company target and DNB Carnegie’s 7.8% 2027 consensus reference.

The higher-pricing-power hypothesis would require Swedish margins to remain near or above 7% after the 2027 wage round, backlog per FTE to stay elevated, and organic growth to continue without a deterioration in project award margins. It would also require cash conversion to recover from 90% toward 100% while leverage falls below 2.5x despite Fabri consolidation and minority interests. Confidence in this hypothesis is medium-low because the company has not yet separated price from volume or disclosed tender-level margin data.

The lower-pricing-power hypothesis would be confirmed by renewed Swedish backlog declines, falling order backlog per FTE, more project deviations, deteriorating receivables and a margin below 7% despite improving activity. A further sign would be competitors redeploying capacity from data centres into ordinary Swedish tenders, accompanied by lower win prices or Instalco’s renewed caution about accepting orders. The most decisive evidence would be a post-wage-round disclosure showing whether contracts priced in 2026 absorbed or recovered labour and metals inflation. Other side of the story: The common view may ultimately prove too cautious if Instalco 2.0 produces a lasting improvement in tender discipline, if public and industrial demand displaces weak residential work, and if service, partnering and German operations raise the group’s effective pricing resilience.

Fabri’s majority consolidation, the Skanska framework, the VAROPreem agreement and the Finnish ten-year maintenance tail all broaden the sources of future earnings beyond Swedish open tenders.
