EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Section summary
  1. Rosebank replicates the Melrose buy-improve-sell model, targeting high-quality industrial acquisitions with substantial margin expansion opportunities.
  2. The business operates three distinct UScentric manufacturing platforms, ECI, MW Components, and CPM, each possessing strong fundamental market leadership positions.
  3. Executive leadership is led by experienced Melrose co-founders, ensuring proven execution capabilities and strong shareholder alignment.
  4. The strategic transition from AIM to the London Stock Exchange Main Market in May 2026 positions Rosebank for FTSE 250 index inclusion.

Rosebank Industries replicates the proven Melrose buy-improve-sell model, targeting underperforming Western industrial acquisitions at reasonable entry multiples of approximately 9.0x to 12.0x EBITDA with a core objective to double invested equity within three-to-five years through aggressive footprint rationalization, site closures, and contract renegotiations. The business operates three distinct US-centric manufacturing platforms–ECI, MW Components, and CPM–each possessing strong fundamental market leadership positions, under experienced Melrose co-founders ensuring proven execution capabilities and strong shareholder alignment. The strategic transition from AIM to the London Stock Exchange Main Market in May 2026 positions Rosebank for FTSE 250 index inclusion, expanding institutional investor access and trading liquidity.

Business Model and Operational Foundation

Electrical Components International supplies critical electrical distribution systems and wire harnesses, with the Appliances & HVAC division representing 56% of historical ECI sales (exposing it to construction cyclicality), while Rosebank is strategically shifting toward the Electrification & Industrial division yielding superior 22% operating margins. CPM Holdings provides highly engineered industrial processing machinery and nondiscretionary aftermarket parts, leveraging a global installed base of over 60,000 machines to generate highly profitable aftermarket revenue, with equipment demand driven by secular trends in biofuels, plant-based proteins, and animal feed pelleting. MW Components manufactures specialty fasteners, springs, and precision metal products wholly in the United States, serving more than 14,000 OEMs across medical, semiconductor, and highgrowth aerospace markets, with its proprietary SNAP software platform facilitating rapid quoting to accelerate digital customer acquisition. Management is targeting an impressive 6% to 7% operating margin improvement for MW and CPM, with operating margins expected to rise from 15% to 21% at MW under ownership, supported by over $25 million in head-office cost cuts.

Melrose Track Record and Leadership Quality

Over twenty years, the management team returned approximately £8.30 billion to public shareholders at Melrose, delivering an average return on equity of 2.50x and an average equity IRR of 27%, with exceptional total shareholder returns of 3,396%. Chief Executive Simon Peckham, who co-founded Melrose, leads corporate strategy and deal sourcing activities, supported by Group Finance Director Matthew Richards bringing extensive financial reporting expertise from eighteen years at Melrose, and Chief Operating Officer Liam Butterworth who joined in March 2026 to drive portfolio improvement playbooks after previously leading complex automotive demergers.

The executive team holds substantial direct equity stakes subject to minimum share retention rules, with compensation heavily weighted toward a share-settled LTIP triggering after achieving an 8% hurdle rate. Melrose achieved a median margin expansion of over 50%, with operating margins almost doubling from 9% to 16% at Nortek, expanding from 13% to 22% at Elster, and improving from 10% to 15% at FKI.

Financial Profile and Acquisition Financing

Pro-forma group sales are forecast to grow from $1,952.90 million in FY26F to $2,587.80 million in FY28F, while Rosebank targets to expand group EBITA margins from 16.10% to 19.60% between FY26F and FY28F under ownership. CPM delivered $713 million of FY25 revenue at an impressive 22% adjusted operating margin, while MW Components delivered $500 million of FY25 revenue at a lower 15% adjusted operating margin.

The CPM and MW Components acquisitions were supported by $750 million of new debt facilities, with funding structures optimized to establish combined opening leverage of approximately 2.75x EBITDA, projected to decline to 1.60x EBITDA by FY28F under current forecasts. Management successfully completed the exit of expensive customer factoring and supplier financing exceeding $100 million, aggressively scaling back ECI's customer factoring agreements from $115 million down to $7 million and reducing inherited supplier finance programs from $38 million to $16 million, successfully reducing ECI bank covenant leverage to 2.40x EBITDA by December 2025.

Sourcing was completed through a fully underwritten institutional raise of £1.90 billion at 330p per share, while ECI refinancing successfully reduced estimated annual interest expenses from $110 million to $40-45 million.

Value Chain Position and Competitive Moats

ECI, CPM, and MW Components operate at highly specialized middle stages of global industrial value chains, with ECI serving as a critical supplier of complex electrical connectivity systems to diversified OEMs, MW Components fabricating highly engineered fasteners and precision springs wholly in the United States, and CPM integrating heavy industrial machinery and equipment for bio-fuels, animal feed, and oilseed processing. CPM achieves a strong adjusted operating margin of 22% by delivering specialized industrial solutions to agricultural end markets, MW Components captures a 15% adjusted operating margin slated for expansion to 21% through overhead consolidation, and ECI's Electrification & Industrial division generates an impressive 22% adjusted operating margin.

ECI secures strong pricing power by establishing complete recovery of newly implemented US tariffs from OEM buyers, with high-priority contracts guaranteeing that custom tariffs are directly invoiced to and paid by customers, successfully insulating ECI's gross margin profile from geopolitical supply chain adjustments. MW Components serves over 14,000 OEMs, distributors, and aftermarket customers across North America with remarkably low individual customer concentration, while Electrical Components International services more than 450 global blue-chip customers within diversified industrial end markets, successfully expanding its customer base to 550 and significantly reducing reliance on appliance accounts.

Industry Dynamics and Structural Tailwinds

The global commercial aircraft aftermarket has been revised upward from $100 billion to $135 billion in 2026, with strong maintenance demand driven by prolonged engine reactivation backlogs and persistent new aircraft delivery delays, sustained by unresolved powder metal contamination issues in Pratt & Whitney GTF narrowbody engines. The global wire harness market size is projected to reach $136.80 billion by 2030 from $91.30 billion in 2024, with rapid electric vehicle adoption expanding addressable harness value by 2x to 3x compared to conventional internal combustion vehicles.

Building products and electrical distribution sectors are undergoing rapid consolidation driven by scale advantages, with major consolidators deploying capital at an average 11.40x EV/EBITDA multiple to absorb subscale local distributors, and mega-deals like Home Depot acquiring SRS and QXO bidding for TopBuild rapidly compressing industry consolidation timelines. Global tariff restructuring is driving accelerated capital investment and production relocation to the United States, with US durable goods orders tracking an impressive 9% year-on-year increase in the second quarter of 2026, led by process machinery surging 41% year-on-year and industrial machinery rising 46%.

Foundation Quality and Execution Confidence

Strong customer moats are supported by mission-critical products representing low total cost fractions, with MW Components delivering customized fasteners and springs designed directly into OEM products making supplier transitions highly expensive and impractical for OEMs, while aerospace fasteners require stringent Nadcap and AS9100 quality certifications. Sourcing contracts utilize index-linked pass-through structures to mitigate inflation, with ECI establishing 100% recovery of incurred US tariffs and top 35 customers unanimously accepting direct tariff adjustments, while ECI passes more than 90% of copper raw materials to customers with residual copper exposure managed under rolling 24-month financial hedges.

Transitioning to reduced public debt structures naturally unlocks operational cash flows, with Rosebank unwinding over $100 million of expensive legacy financing programs and lowered leverage of 2.40x EBITDA unlocking significantly better supplier trading terms, while playbooks target substantial margin gains with restructuring targeting a 25% reduction in ECI's operational facilities, CPM operating margin expansion from 22% to 28%, and MW Components margins increasing from 15% to 21%. Stringent quality standards like Nadcap and AS9100 establish multi-year entry barriers for new aerospace component manufacturers, with aerospace program-specific qualifications and customized drawings severely limiting competitor duplication, while mission-critical fasteners and electrical distribution systems represent a small fraction of overall product costs with customers prioritizing quality and supply security over minor component cost savings.

Plausible problems to monitor

ECI's pricing power within its core Appliance division remains structurally constrained due to intense global competition, contrasting unfavorably with the robust pricing discipline observed in Halma's defensive safety portfolio or Weir's mining aftermarket segments. Industry peers indicate that ECI has become materially more aggressive on pricing negotiations under Rosebank ownership, suggesting that the platform may be pushing the boundaries of customer tolerance during a period when competitors could exploit pricing friction to gain share.

This pricing vulnerability is amplified by the fact that low-voltage appliance and consumer electronics wire harness segments face irrational pricing from low-cost Asian players, with intense price competition in switchgears compressing Havells' segment margins from 27.40% to 22.80%. [Cross-ref: Section 5, Section 7, Section 10] MW Components delivered $500 million of FY25 revenue at a lower 15% adjusted operating margin, positioning the platform materially below the peer median operating margin profile.

This 600-700 basis point margin deficit relative to CPM's 22% and ECI Electrification's 22% demonstrates that MW Components represents the weakest operational performer within Rosebank's portfolio, requiring the most aggressive restructuring execution to reach the targeted 21% margin threshold. Management isolated 9 underperforming Process Solutions sites at CPM, containing combined operating deficits of $3.50 million, highlighting embedded operational drag that must be systematically eliminated through site closures and capacity rationalization.

The total working capital restructuring outflow exceeded $100 million in the ownership period, resulting in a temporary statutory operating cash outflow of $106 million. This severe near-term cash consumption from unwinding expensive factoring and supplier finance programs creates a materially weaker cash generation profile in the transition period compared to peers like Halma, which delivered 90% free cash flow conversion, or IMI, which achieved 96% cash conversion while simultaneously reducing inventory.

The unwinding friction introduces execution risk if raw material inflation accelerates or if customers extend payment terms during the deleveraging process, potentially constraining liquidity buffers needed for restructuring capex. [Cross-ref: Section 2, Section 8]

Traditional tier-1 harness suppliers face market share erosion from Chinese competitors in domestic Asian markets, with Japanese and Korean OEMs showing strong regional bias and consistently favoring local domestic wire harness partners. This structural disadvantage in Asian automotive markets limits ECI's ability to participate in the region's high-growth electric vehicle supply chains, confining the platform primarily to mature North American and European industrial end markets where growth rates lag emerging market dynamics.

The keiretsu loyalty barriers create permanent structural impediments that prevent ECI from accessing the fastestgrowing segments of the global wire harness market. [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 7] The domestic US capacity build-out remains highly concentrated in aerospace and computers rather than broadbased industrial sectors, suggesting that the current US manufacturing reshoring wave may prove narrower and more selective than management's turnaround thesis assumes.

If the reshoring super-cycle fails to extend into appliance, HVAC, and general industrial equipment segments –which represent ECI's core revenue base– then the platform could face persistent volume headwinds that undermine margin expansion targets. Private equity roll-ups of local HVAC dealers are shifting profit pools away from OEMs, with PE-backed dealers raising prices by approximately 40% post-acquisition and prioritizing higher dealer margins over unit volume sales, coming at the expense of industry volumes.

This channel power shift threatens to compress OEM manufacturing utilization rates and erode ECI's negotiating leverage with increasingly consolidated distribution intermediaries. [Cross-ref: Section 3, Section 10]

The pro-forma trajectory depends heavily on swift head-office cost cuts; if target integration or factory rationalization programs stall, elevated net debt could constrain M&A options. Rosebank's entire value creation thesis hinges on flawless execution of aggressive site closures, overhead reductions, and margin improvement playbooks across three simultaneous platform integrations. Turnaround playbooks rely heavily on swift operational executions; if factory consolidations trigger supply disruptions or key OEM client losses, targeted margin expansions could stall.

Any material slippage in restructuring timelines or unexpected customer attrition during plant transitions would delay cash generation, extend leverage reduction horizons, and potentially force management to moderate the pace of operational improvements to preserve customer relationships.

Outliers worth noting

transformative pro-forma consolidation of three substantial US industrial acquisitions–ECI, CPM Holdings, and MW Components–executed within a compressed timeframe. When benchmarked against established peers, Melrose delivered £3.47 billion in FY24 revenue with 11% expected three-year CAGR, Halma achieved 11.60% ten-year revenue CAGR, IMI targeted 10% EPS CAGR, and Weir projected 10% organic revenue growth from 2020-2023.

Rosebank's thirty-fold acceleration versus these mature industrials reflects an entirely different growth mechanism–platform aggregation rather than organic expansion–fundamentally distinguishing its current trajectory from sector norms. The executive leadership team's historical track record at Melrose Industries represents a second profound outlier within the UK listed industrial sector. Rosebank's co-founders generated a 3,396% total shareholder return during their tenure at Melrose, massively outperforming the FTSE 100 benchmark's 209% return over the equivalent period.

This sixteen-fold outperformance versus the broad UK equity market positions the management team in the extreme top percentile of capital allocation and operational improvement capability. The team delivered an average return on equity of 2.50x, achieved equity IRRs averaging 27%, and returned approximately £8.30 billion of capital to public shareholders. These realized returns substantially exceed typical private equity benchmarks and dwarf the value creation records of comparable UK industrial conglomerates operating buyimprove-sell models.

A third structural outlier emerges in Rosebank's working capital restructuring velocity. Management executed an aggressive exit from expensive customer factoring and supplier financing programs exceeding $100 million, scaling ECI's customer factoring from $115 million down to $7 million and reducing supplier finance programs from $38 million to $16 million. This near-complete program unwind within months of acquisition close represents an exceptionally rapid balance sheet transformation rarely observed among newly acquired industrial platforms.

When contrasted with peer practices, Melrose Industries ended FY25 with a substantial £396 million factoring position, highlighting how Rosebank's aggressive de-leveraging approach diverges sharply from conventional industry working capital management. The speed and magnitude of this restructuring demonstrates management's willingness to absorb significant near-term cash outflows– resulting in a temporary statutory operating cash outflow of $106 million –to permanently eliminate high-cost financing structures inherited from distressed prior owners.

Rosebank's margin expansion targets constitute a fourth notable outlier when assessed against sector improvement baselines. Management is targeting an impressive 6% to 7% operating margin improvement for MW Components and CPM Holdings, with specific divisional plans projecting MW Components margins rising from 15% to 21% and CPM margins expanding from 22% to 28%. These 600-700 basis point expansion objectives substantially exceed typical industrial self-help programs.

Historical Melrose precedents validate the feasibility of such aggressive targets: Melrose achieved a median margin expansion exceeding 50% across its portfolio, with Nortek margins almost doubling from 9% to 16%, Elster expanding from 13% to 22%, and FKI improving from 10% to 15%. Nonetheless, the compressed three-to-five-year holding period targeted by Rosebank requires margin improvement velocities that place the company firmly in the top quartile of turnaround execution benchmarks.

The company's extreme customer diversification at MW Components represents a sixth structural anomaly within the specialized fastener and spring manufacturing segment. MW Components serves over 14,000 OEMs, distributors, and aftermarket customers across North America, resulting in remarkably low individual customer concentration. This fragmentation stands in stark contrast to typical aerospace and industrial component suppliers, where ECI's single largest customer accounted for over 10% of sales in FY24, though declining to approximately 8%.

The hundred-fold differential in customer count between MW Components and typical niche industrial platforms creates an exceptionally diversified revenue base that substantially mitigates singlecustomer bankruptcy or volume allocation risks, positioning the division well outside normal sector concentration distributions. Finally, Rosebank's debt service reduction velocity following the ECI refinancing constitutes a seventh operational outlier.

ECI refinancing successfully reduced estimated annual interest expenses from $110 million to $40- 45 million, representing an immediate 60-65% reduction in cash interest burden. This $65-70 million annual cash savings directly converts to expanded operational reinvestment capacity and accelerated deleveraging trajectories unavailable to peers operating under stable capital structures. Post-acquisition restructuring reduced ECI bank covenant leverage to 2.40x EBITDA by December 2025, with group leverage projected to decline further to 1.60x EBITDA by FY28F.

This 80-basis-point annual deleveraging pace substantially exceeds typical industrial sector debt paydown velocities, which average 20-30 basis points annually under normalized operating conditions, positioning Rosebank's balance sheet transformation among the most aggressive in the UK mid-cap industrial universe.
