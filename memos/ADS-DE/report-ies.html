adidas (ADS-DE): what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the existing case

On net these results modestly strengthen the existing case, because the sell-side has stopped cutting and a pinned Headline Rating would move up two grades; on the substance the case was waiting for, footwear and overheads, they weaken it, and the gap between those two readings is what this note is about.

What changed

The third quarter, reported on 29 October 2026, beat consensus on every headline line. Net sales were EUR 7,252m against consensus of EUR 7,150m, up 7% currency-neutral and 9.4% reported on EUR 6,630m a year earlier. Reported gross margin was 54.3% against consensus of 51.3% and 51.8% a year earlier. Operating profit was EUR 1,008m against consensus of EUR 742m and EUR 736m a year earlier, an operating margin of 13.9%. Diluted earnings per share were EUR 4.08 against consensus of EUR 2.82 and EUR 2.57 a year earlier.

The beat is a one-off. A EUR 200m US tariff refund added 250 basis points to gross margin and about EUR 0.86 to earnings per share. Underlying operating profit was EUR 808m and underlying earnings per share about EUR 3.20. Management raised full-year 2026 operating profit guidance to about EUR 2.5bn from EUR 2.3bn; the raise includes the EUR 200m refund and a EUR 100m fourth-quarter reinvestment in software write-offs and organisational simplification. The 10% operating margin target for 2027 was reiterated. The case already carried named broker 2026 operating profit estimates of EUR 2,417m to EUR 2,520m, so the new guide lands where the sell-side already sat, with a one-off inside it.

Against what the case assumed:

  • Footwear was flat currency-neutral. The case carried footwear at +1% in the second quarter and named footwear below 2% for a second consecutive quarter as the first leg of its park trigger. That leg has fired.
  • Apparel grew 19%, running 19% and the performance division 14% currency-neutral, the sixth consecutive quarter of performance outgrowing lifestyle. The case's lead quantified driver was performance running growing above 30% on a EUR 2.0bn to EUR 2.5bn base.
  • Direct-to-consumer grew 17% (e-commerce 18%, own retail 16%) to 40% of sales; wholesale grew 2%. The case carried direct-to-consumer at +25% and expected 20% to 25%; its warning level is below 10%.
  • Europe fell 4% currency-neutral, of which 2 points was a EUR 50m shipment delay from the SAP migration; European wholesale fell high single digits by deliberate restriction and European direct-to-consumer grew 7%. The case's base held Europe at +6%. Greater China grew 13%, North America 12%, Japan and Korea 19%.
  • Inventories were EUR 5.74bn, up 5% year on year and down 4% on the quarter, from 13% growth a quarter earlier. The case carried EUR 5,969m at 180% of next-quarter cost of goods sold. Net debt fell about EUR 200m year on year.
  • Marketing fell to 11.5% of sales from 12.0% a year earlier. The review carries no operating-overhead ratio for the quarter.
  • The tariff refund the case held as a USD 250m to USD 300m contingent asset has landed, at EUR 200m, in this quarter.

The sell-side response was favourable. Bernstein held its EUR 245 target and Outperform, raising 2026 earnings per share to EUR 10.17 from EUR 9.98 and trimming 2027 to EUR 12.27. Deutsche Bank raised its target to EUR 210 from EUR 205, 2026 earnings per share by 12% to EUR 9.70 and 2027 to EUR 12.30. Jefferies raised its target to EUR 200 from EUR 187. Rothschild Redburn trimmed its target to EUR 230 from EUR 235 with 2027 earnings per share at EUR 12.0. Morgan Stanley kept Overweight but models 2027 earnings per share at EUR 9.35. The shares rose 5.76% on the day; the post-results close cited was EUR 158.05, and the price is EUR 172.85 at 2 November, 12.3% above the 200-day average of EUR 153.96 and above the 50-day average of EUR 150.59. The dashboard's net revisions are now minus 1% over one month, minus 2% over three and flat over six on earnings per share, a revision score of zero, against the cuts of 8%, 11% and 13% the memo recorded.

The challenge

First reading: this was a flat quarter wearing a refund, and the case's own park trigger is built so that it cannot see that. Take the review's figures at face value. Gross margin of 54.3% less the 250 basis points of refund is roughly 51.8%, the same as a year earlier. Underlying operating profit of EUR 808m on EUR 7,252m of sales is an operating margin a shade above 11%, against 11.1% a year earlier. That flat underlying margin was delivered with direct-to-consumer growing 17% against wholesale at 2%, a mix that should lift gross margin, and with marketing 50 basis points of sales lighter. If both tailwinds were present and the margin still did not move, something else moved against them by about the same amount: product margin, non-marketing overheads, or both. The false-friend profile the risks report scored at zero inconsistencies predicts exactly this, apparel and jersey growth at structurally lower margins than mature footwear, and overheads growing ahead of sales. The case's park trigger looks for operating expenses outgrowing sales; marketing normalisation after the World Cup can hold total operating expenses in line while the overhead ratio the memo actually cares about, 29.9% in the first half, gets worse. For this reading to be right, the third-quarter overhead ratio must be at or above the first-half level and the underlying gross margin flat rather than up. The review does not carry the first; the second is arithmetic. We cannot knock this reading down on the evidence in hand, and the sell-side has not tried: the 12% Deutsche Bank upgrade to 2026 earnings per share is roughly the refund.

Second reading: the Greater China growth that anchors the case's largest external force is distribution-led, and the mechanism the case named is contradicted in the quarter. The case's single largest change force is Nike's retreat, with Topsports named as reallocating shelf space and marketing capital to adidas from January 2027. The review carries, dated 30 October, that Topsports is deliberately restraining its purchases of adidas product because of market inventory saturation. The 13% growth in Greater China is attributed to partner appetite for mono-brand stores, with HLA Group managing the majority of net store additions. Those two facts sit together only if growth is coming from new doors rather than from the existing distributor's reorders. That is still growth, but it is sell-in ahead of a January 2027 event, into a market where the review records sportswear demand decelerating and domestic brands at low-single-digit growth. For this reading to be right, Greater China growth would need to slow as store openings annualise, and Topsports' restraint would need to persist into the Nike exit rather than reverse at it. The case does not hold this reading; it holds the opposite.

Third reading: the supplier power the case grades as its clearest structural advantage is being spent in this quarter, not banked. The case counts about 100 basis points of sourcing gross margin from forcing competitive bidding, a largest factory partner at 6% of volume and a new tier-one partner bidding costs down. The review puts the other side of that trade on the page: Yue Yuen, the primary footwear supplier, warned on 26 October of a 50% to 55% fall in nine-month net profit, citing tariff-sharing arrangements in which brands forced suppliers to shoulder import duty; Huali's adidas orders are up over 50%; suppliers are being asked to carry redundant capacity; cotton is up 13% year on year and oil-linked inputs are rising, and manufacturers cannot pass it through. If underlying gross margin is flat while sourcing is contributing a tailwind of this size, the tailwind is already being consumed by markdowns and mix. A supplier base at these margins is a different counterparty from the one the memo described: Vietnam and Indonesia make 72% of footwear, and a distressed supplier either cuts capacity or recovers terms. For this reading to be right, the sourcing gain would have to be a one-time transfer that has largely happened, and supplier terms would have to tighten from 2027. The review itself calls supplier profitability a consensus blind spot. The case accommodates supply disruption as a risk; it does not accommodate the sourcing driver being already in the base.

Fourth reading: the valuation offset that rescued thematic fit and justified progression rests on a 2027 consensus that one named broker disputes by a quarter. The case used 12.5x forward earnings at the 3rd percentile as the genuine offset to a [D] thematic fit. The dashboard now pins valuation A at 13.2x and the 1st percentile, on 24-month forward earnings per share of EUR 13.12. Bernstein, Deutsche Bank and Rothschild Redburn model 2027 at EUR 12.0 to EUR 12.30; Morgan Stanley models EUR 9.35 because it expects wholesale inventory and promotions to take time to clear. The quarter supplied evidence for both: 19% growth in performance and apparel for the first group, flat footwear and a European wholesale decline for Morgan Stanley. On EUR 9.35 the price of EUR 172.85 is about 18 times, which is not a 1st-percentile multiple. For this reading to be right, the three bulls' 2027 estimates would have to drift toward Morgan Stanley's, which means the promotional environment not normalising by early 2027 as management expects. The case holds the multiple as a fact; it is a function of a contested number.

What we are not putting forward: the obvious bear reading that Europe signals demand collapse. The review's own figures make it weak. European direct-to-consumer grew 7%, two points of the 4% decline was a dated shipment delay management expects to recover in the fourth quarter, and the wholesale restriction is the pricing discipline the case already credits. Europe is a watch item, not a reading.

What would settle it

The third-quarter operating-overhead ratio against the first-half 29.9%, and the gross margin excluding the refund as stated by the company rather than derived. The review in hand carries neither. The second Earnings Review, four working days from now, will carry the consensus estimates and targets that have landed since 29 October, so it will show whether the dashboard's revision figures move with the upgrades or whether 2027 estimates are drifting toward Morgan Stanley's, and it will carry the pinned sell-side momentum letter on a window that includes the print. It will not carry a disclosure the company has not made: the quarterly overhead ratio if it is not in the interim report, or anything on Topsports' reorder behaviour.

What settles the China reading is fourth-quarter Greater China growth against the 13% printed, and whether Topsports' restraint reverses into the January 2027 Nike exit. The former arrives with the full-year results in early 2027; the latter has no committed disclosure and is likely to be visible only through distributor commentary.

What settles the supplier reading is Yue Yuen's full-year result and any change in adidas's stated sourcing terms. Neither is a company commitment.

What settles the footwear question is the fourth quarter: management expects the delayed European shipments to recover in full and Europe to return to high-single-digit growth. A fourth quarter with footwear still below 2% would fire the first leg a third time and remove the timing explanation. The first-half 2027 gross margin against the 51.5% floor the review names is the longer test.

The condition the memo set for writing the Early-Stage Assessment, after the third-quarter print, has been met in time. Whether it has been met in substance is the question above.
