Agency & Peer Benchmark Perspective High IFRS Interpretations Committee guidance, European Banking Authority nonrecourse cure rate data (93-100%), peer filings from Prysmian (€419M factoring), Schneider Electric (€103M supplier finance), Deutsche Bank adjustments for Alstom and Mahle

The High confidence classification across all four technical sections reflects several structural strengths in the underlying evidence base. Non-recourse factoring arrangements meet strict IFRS 9 true-sale derecognition criteria because substantially all credit and collection risks transfer entirely to financial institutions, a standard validated by European Banking Authority documentation showing 93% to 100% cure rates across EU jurisdictions.

The leverage recalculation framework in Section 3 draws directly from audited balance sheet line items specifying €22.8M reported Net Financial Debt against €80.5M EBITDA, with off-balance-sheet facility inventory totaling €12.1M in non-recourse factoring and €25.2M in supplier reverse factoring, eliminating estimation uncertainty in the sensitivity scenarios.

The covenant analysis benefits from explicit disclosure that financial covenants in EIB, ICO, and bank agreements evaluate leverage based strictly on reported Net Financial Debt to EBITDA ratios, with full compliance confirmed at June 30 2026. Furthermore, capital policy guarantees shareholder payouts above 30% of net profit as long as NFD/EBITDA remains below 2.0x, leaving over 1.0x EBITDA in covenant headroom even after adding €37.3M of off-balance-sheet facilities.

The rating agency and peer benchmark section achieves High confidence through triangulation across multiple comparable case studies, including Deutsche Bank Research adjustments adding €500M in working capital financing to Alstom's debt, Prysmian's €419M non-recourse factored receivables reported without covenant reclassification, and Schneider Electric's €103M supplier financing classified as immaterial by S&P Global Ratings and Moody's when reaffirming A-grade ratings.

One interpretive limitation emerges from the absence of granular subsidiary-level covenant definitions and recourse carve-outs in publicly available filings, particularly concerning Latin American reverse factoring expansion that creates vulnerability to sudden banking credit limit contractions if sovereign risk spikes. Additionally, the analysis relies on management presentations and broker research for peer leverage comparisons rather than direct access to competitor credit agreements, introducing minor estimation variance in the relative covenant buffer calculations.

The High confidence verdict nonetheless holds because the core question on whether reported leverage materially understates true indebtedness can be definitively answered: even under the most aggressive full consolidation scenario adding both €12.1M factoring and €25.2M reverse factoring, adjusted FY2025 net leverage reaches only 0.75x EBITDA, expanding to a peak 0.91x EBITDA at H1 2026, both figures remaining well within investment-grade capital goods thresholds.

Other side of the story: Sceptical credit analysts argue that High confidence assignments may be premature given that Latin American subsidiaries face commercial dispute carve-outs during supply disruptions where banks can legally seek recourse from the operating entity, and that rating agency adjustments reclassifying all €37.3M of working capital lines as debt expose Arteche to multiple compression if capital goods peers face broader credit rating downgrades.

Furthermore, the confidence framework does not fully account for secondorder refinancing risks where combining €15.5M in short-term MARF paper with €37.3M of off-balance-sheet trade financing exposes the company to heightened refinancing friction if commercial paper markets freeze during macroeconomic stress periods.
