Financial Leverage and EPS Impact

2.
Significant earnings sensitivity: Nexans' complete exit from non-electrification activities in July 2026 places its financial performance squarely on its three core electrification segments. Analysts from Bernstein Research expect this pure-play strategy to drive Adjusted EPS from €5.15 in 2025 to €9.95 by 2027, highlighting the steep trajectory of its financial recovery. This strategic shift makes Nexans highly sensitive to fixed overhead absorption in subsea plants such as Halden, where output volumes dictate segment profitability. Management expects to deliver €23m in runrate synergies by year three, representing a significant portion of the business's standalone profit. Achieving these targets requires successful integration of North American operations to maximize purchasing scale and commercial cross-selling. Consequently, the operational execution of M&A synergies represents a primary driver of the group's medium-term margin expansion.

By focusing entirely on electrification, management aims to re-rate the stock, which currently trades at 7.5x 2027 EV/EBITDA compared to Prysmian's 10.7x multiple. The success of this re-rating depends on improving visibility in Transmission EBITDA and executing the 2028 roadmap successfully. Critical operational risks: Complex turnkey subsea projects carry significant execution challenges that can quickly erode operating profits. Nexans' 2025 universal registration document identifies portfolio rotation as a main risk, especially since M&A execution has historically been volatile for the group. Halden Mass-Impregnated Line Under-Absorption

1.
Severe revenue disruption: Nexans faced a material threat of fixed-cost under-absorption at its Halden plant when the Great Sea Interconnector was paused in late 2025. An analysis by Bernstein Research on 19 June 2026 stated that the halt of this project created a lost annual sales run-rate of approximately €100m at Halden. This idling threat puts heavy pressure on the Transmission segment's margin targets unless alternative contracts can be secured quickly. Without replacement orders, the high depreciation costs of the mass-impregnated line would severely dilute earnings per share, proving that subsea assets carry significant operating leverage.
2.
Elevated margin pressure: Bernstein Research warned on 24 February 2026 that the under-utilisation of the Halden subsea cable line in Norway would challenge Nexans' profit margins. The analyst argued that replacing lost GSI volumes required finding and delivering alternative contracts on a very tight schedule. This timing pressure forces Nexans into a weakened bargaining position, effectively acting as a forced seller. Consequently, any failure to rapidly secure high-margin replacement orders directly impacts group profitability.
3.
Mitigating cash structures: The financial drag of the GSI delay was partially offset by customer downpayments that protected Nexans' near-term revenue recognition. According to a Bernstein Research update on 10 January 2025, Nexans continued to secure quarterly GSI cash inflows despite the lack of a final investment decision. These downpayments allowed Nexans to record sales and margins on reservation fees and sea bed surveys during the early delay phase. Consequently, structured commercial terms can cushion early-stage under-absorption before full factory loading is achieved.

4.

The company's 2019 annual report confirmed this closure was part of a major €210m group-wide cost savings initiative. This closure reflects how land high-voltage assets can become structurally uncompetitive when demand shifts or capacity remains underutilised. Such dramatic footprint rationalization is necessary to eliminate heavy fixed-overhead drag during periods of market overcapacity.

Therefore, mitigating under-absorption through closures offers a slow and expensive route to protecting group operating margins.

Section 5 – Installation Vessels Fleet Ownership vs Charter Dynamics

  1. Owning advanced subsea vessels provides cable makers with significant margin protection by eliminating high third-party charter fees.
