Section 3 – Ownership Dynamics, Short Interest, and Insider Activity

Section summary
  1. Elekta's ownership remains highly concentrated under its founder and Swedish institutional funds. Net read: Verified insider transactions are limited to small board purchases, leaving the chief executive's rumored share purchase unconfirmed.
  2. High free floats and parent divestments create distinct technical and overhang dynamics for peers Philips and Siemens Healthineers.

Institutional Shareholder Dynamics

1.
Stable voting control: Laurent Leksell has maintained dominant voting control of Elekta since its initial listing on Nasdaq Stockholm. As of April 30, 2026, he controlled 30.23% of the total voting rights through own and related party holdings, representing a slight increase from the 30.21% controlled on April 30, 2025. His voting power is amplified because Series A shares carry ten votes each, whereas Series B shares carry only one vote. This concentration ensures that the founding family retains significant long-term strategic influence over corporate governance decisions.
2.
Large institutional holdings: The Fourth Swedish National Pension Fund remains the single largest capital holder in Elekta. According to the company's annual report, this pension fund held 9.42% of the capital and 6.97% of the voting rights on April 30, 2026. Global asset managers also command sizable positions, with Vanguard holding 3.77% of total capital and Nordea Funds holding 3.48%. These significant institutional stakes indicate that major funds are deeply committed to Elekta's transition under its new leadership team.
3.
High ownership concentration: The combined holdings of the top ten institutional shareholders represent a substantial portion of Elekta's equity. On April 30, 2026, the ten largest institutional investors held 40.65% of the capital and 56.08% of the total votes. The remaining capital of 59.35% is distributed among other retail and institutional investors. This tightly held structure limits the overall free float and can increase stock price volatility during market downturns.
4.
Low treasury holdings: Elekta maintains a minimal amount of treasury shares on its balance sheet. On April 30, 2026, treasury shares amounted to 1,485,289 shares, which is equivalent to 0.40% of the total outstanding share capital. This level has remained unchanged since April 30, 2025, indicating that no active share buyback programs were executed during the fiscal year. The lack of buyback activity suggests that management is prioritizing preserving cash for operational restructuring and debt repayment.

Insider Activity and Governance

5.
Strategic leadership transition: Elekta announced the appointment of Jakob Just-Bomholt as President and Chief Executive Officer on June 9, 2025. Jakob Just-Bomholt officially assumed his executive duties on September 1, 2025, succeeding Jonas Bolander who had served as acting CEO. He is a highly experienced Danish executive who previously led 3Shape, Falck, and A.P. Moller Maersk. Investors expect his external perspective to drive a strong strategic reset and improve operational efficiency across the company's global regions.
6.
Thin transaction disclosure: Publicly available reports present limited evidence regarding executive share ownership at Elekta. While Jakob Just-Bomholt was appointed as CEO in late 2025, his shareholdings were not listed in the company's remuneration report for the fiscal year ended April 30, 2026. This absence of data makes it difficult to verify the claim that he purchased one million shares upon joining. Confirming this large transaction is vital because substantial insider buying serves as a powerful signal of alignment with minority shareholders.
7.
Small board purchases: Other Elekta insiders have made modest share purchases in the open market. Board member Jan Kimpen acquired 10,000 Series B shares on Nasdaq Stockholm on June 7, 2026. He executed the transaction at an average price of SEK 51.82 per share. This insider activity shows that board members are willing to deploy personal capital at current discounted valuation levels.
8.
Contrary evidence: Regulatory filing research failed to locate any notifications confirming the chief executive's purchase of one million shares between September 2025 and October 2026. Although the CEO received a total remuneration of TSEK 11,799 in fiscal year 2025/26, the company's official corporate governance and remuneration disclosures list his direct shareholdings as zero. This contradiction suggests that the market's assumptions regarding massive CEO share purchases may be entirely unfounded or deferred to future incentive cycles.

Peer Ownership Structure

9.
Strong anchor shareholding: Philips exhibits a different ownership dynamic characterized by a single massive strategic anchor investor. The investment firm Exor NV purchased a 15% stake in Koninklijke Philips NV for €2.60 bn on August 14, 2023. Exor has since increased its permitted ownership limit from 20% to 22% as of September 2026, though no immediate purchases were announced. This stable corporate backing provides Philips with substantial long-term governance support that pure-play competitors like Elekta currently lack.
10.
Fragmented institutional float: Aside from its major strategic anchor, Philips has a highly fragmented institutional shareholder base. Vanguard is the only other institutional shareholder holding more than 1% of Philips' outstanding shares, with a 2.49% stake reported in late 2021. This highly dispersed free float of 98.80% contrasts sharply with Elekta's more concentrated Swedish institutional base. The high float makes Philips shares highly liquid but also susceptible to rapid changes in passive index fund positioning.
11.
Looming parent divestment: Siemens Healthineers faces a unique ownership overhang due to its relationship with its German parent company. Siemens AG reduced its ownership stake in Siemens Healthineers by 2% in February 2025 to help finance its acquisition of Altair Engineering. The parent company plans to distribute a substantial 30% stake to its shareholders via a dividend spin-off in late 2027. This planned divestment is expected to create a persistent trading overhang for Siemens Healthineers.
12.
Thin short data: Precise and reliable data regarding current short interest levels and borrow costs for Elekta and its peers is extremely thin. While Dutch law requires the disclosure of gross short positions to the regulator, public databases do not provide real-time short interest for Elekta. Jefferies reported Elekta's free float at 94.30% in November 2026, which indicates ample liquidity for short sellers. This lack of visible short data prevents a comprehensive technical assessment of bearish sentiment across the med-tech sector.
13.
Other side of the story: While the absence of clear regulatory filings regarding the chief executive's purchase of one million shares raises immediate disclosure concerns, Elekta's technical dynamics remain highly supportive. The strong concentration of 56.08% of voting power among the top ten Swedish institutional holders protects the company from aggressive activist campaigns. Furthermore, the open-market purchase of 10,000 shares by board member Jan Kimpen in June 2026 signals positive internal sentiment. These elements provide a stable foundation for equity execution as the new operating model begins to deliver annual cost savings.
