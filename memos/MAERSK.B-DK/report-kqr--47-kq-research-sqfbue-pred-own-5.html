Section 2 – Congestion-Linked Income in Maersk's Ocean Segment

Section summary
  1. Maersk's Ocean segment experienced substantial revenue expansion in 2025 and 2026 due to persistent congestion-linked surcharges.
  2. The Gemini Cooperation with Hapag-Lloyd optimized vessel networks but did not insulate carriers from looming rate normalizations.

Elevating Ocean Segment Revenue

1.
Strong revenue: Maersk reported total Ocean segment revenue of USD 18,704 million in the first half of 2026, up from USD 17,482 million in the first half of 2025. This top-line expansion was heavily supported by elevated spot rates and various congestion-linked surcharges. Analysts attribute this positive performance to robust export demand and persistent port bottlenecks. These factors directly helped drive near-term earnings above consensus expectations. g p Q Maersk segment revenue and EBITDA breakdown for the first half of 2026.
2.
Immense leverage: Maersk's financial results are highly sensitive to freight rate fluctuations due to significant operational leverage. A minor USD 100 per FFE change in freight rates impacts Maersk's full-year EBIT by approximately USD 1.3 billion. This immense sensitivity means that short-term rate hikes driven by port delays translate directly into high margins. Consequently, these temporary surcharges serve as the primary driver of Maersk's upgraded profit guidance.
3.
Elevated surcharges: Shippers faced peak season surcharges on Asia-Europe routes ranging from USD 400 to USD 2,000 per container. Maersk and CMA CGM priced these surcharges at the higher end of the spectrum due to capacity constraints. These elevated commercial fees bolstered Maersk's average loaded rates significantly. 40 41 44 resulting operating leverage remains the primary driver of Maersk's short-term EPS growth.
4.
Persistent congestion: Port bottlenecks in China and Europe significantly constrained effective shipping capacity, supporting high spot rates. Maersk management noted that terminal bottlenecks are likely to become a frequent market feature. Shippers have experienced delays of up to 12 days in Shanghai. These persistent delays prolonged the collection of detention fees and demurrage revenue.
5.
Contrary evidence: Maersk has cautiously resumed transits through the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, returning about one-third of its schedule to the Red Sea route. This routing change increases effective vessel capacity and threatens to undermine emergency and war-risk surcharges. A persistent return of liner trades to this route remains the largest downside risk to freight rates and carrier earnings power. Management expects that this recovery of transits will gradually normalize global networks over time.
6.
Superior pricing: Maersk achieved an impressive 22% year-on-year loaded freight rate increase in Q2 2026, outperforming peer averages. 48 49 52 - Earnings Calls 53 comparison, peer Hapag-Lloyd recorded a more modest 9% loaded rate increase during the same quarter. This discrepancy reflects Maersk's higher exposure to volatile spot rate dynamics and congestion surcharges. Such exposure amplifies near-term profitability but increases risk of a sharp earnings cliff.

Gemini Alliance Operational Dynamics

7.
Strategic alliance: Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd initiated the Gemini Cooperation in February 2025 to optimize East-West trade routes. The alliance utilizes a unique hub-and-spoke model designed to enhance overall schedule reliability. Improved network reliability helps shippers minimize inventory buffers and lower overall logistics costs. By streamlining operations, the partnership enables both carriers to realize efficiencies just above the upper end of initial guidance.
8.
Operational savings: Maersk management confirmed that Gemini cost benefits have been successfully realized through optimized bunker consumption. These efficiency gains helped offset some of the higher container handling costs. Shippers observe that carriers are pocketing these network cost savings rather than passing them through. Consequently, these cost reductions directly support Ocean segment EBITDA margins during periods of high demand.
9.
Centralized hubs: The Gemini alliance structure utilizes dedicated transhipment hubs to improve reliability and asset utilization. This centralized model helps concentrate container moves within Maersk-owned facilities. However, the model requires robust volumes to achieve optimal efficiency. Any material deterioration in global trade would prevent Gemini from delivering targeted margins and cost advantages.
10.
Strained relationships: Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd have struggled to consistently honor contract allocations during peak periods. Shippers faced weekly cargo rollings, driving frustrated customers to seek more reliable alternatives. This friction occurs because carriers prioritize highly profitable spot cargo and associated congestion fees. This behavior strains long-term customer relationships despite bolstering short-term container earnings.

Rate Normalization and Future EPS Risks

11.
Historical precedent: Maersk experienced a substantial decline in profitability during 2025 when previous congestion-linked tailwinds cleared. Lower average freight rates eroded Ocean segment revenue and compressed overall margins. This historical decay indicates how rapidly surcharge revenue can evaporate once market bottlenecks are resolved. Shippers must anticipate a similar sharp correction in container earnings as capacity normalizes.
12.
Severe oversupply: Global fleet capacity is projected to expand significantly as record vessel deliveries hit the water through 2028. Analysts expect the container orderbook to reach nearly 39% of global fleet capacity. This structural oversupply is expected to outpace underlying trade demand. The widening supply-demand imbalance will inevitably trigger intense rate wars among carriers.
13.
Steep decline: Bearish analysts maintain a cautious view on Maersk's medium-term earnings outlook. Effective supply growth has been temporarily suppressed by port bottlenecks, slow vessel speeds, and longer routings. Once these supply chain disruptions normalize, the release of capacity will put severe downward pressure on rates. This normalisation would result in substantial EBITDA declines for the Ocean segment.
14.
Volatile costs: Maersk continues to operate under high operating costs due to elevated fuel and charter expenses. 83 84 - Earnings Calls Leading-cost liner operators carry high break-even costs of roughly USD 1,100 per TEU. High bunker consumption from rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope has increased operating costs. This cost pressure raises the break-even threshold for Maersk's container shipping operations.
15.
Strategic mitigation: Maersk plans to manage its future fleet capacity with a keen focus on overall profitability. The company intends to reinvest some vessel tonnage into slow steaming and return optional chartered capacity to tonnage providers. 16.

If freight markets continue to outperform expectations, carriers will maintain elevated pricing power near term. Under these conditions, Maersk's elevated surcharge income could persist, delaying the anticipated consensus earnings cliff.

Section 3 – Congestion-Linked Income in APM Terminals

Section summary
  1. Port and terminal operators leverage progressive storage tariffs during bottlenecks to maintain yard fluidity and capture high-margin revenue.
  2. APM Terminals realized record earnings in 2025 and 2026 driven by volume growth and elevated congestion-linked storage fees.

Dynamic Revenue Drivers in

1.
APM Terminals Strong growth: APM Terminals achieved record-high financial performance in 2025 due to robust global demand and elevated storage fees. Segment revenue grew 20% year-on-year to USD 5.3 billion as port congestion and operational disruptions intensified. This exceptional top-line expansion was propelled by higher storage revenue alongside volume growth of 8.9%. Management highlighted that these ancillary revenues directly supported the segment's record profitability during peak operational bottlenecks.
2.
Expanded margins: The terminal segment maintained its strong financial momentum into the second quarter of 2026 with record-high EBITDA. APM Terminals recorded an improved EBITDA margin of 35.7% as rates normalized and operations stabilized. Under pressure from Middle East conflicts, the terminal operator managed to improve revenue per move by 7.7% via rate hikes and higher storage collections. This performance demonstrates that congestion-linked income remained a primary lever for sustaining elevated terminal margins.
3.
Distinct segments: APM Terminals operates a diversified commercial model where ocean carriers and landside customers split total service billings. Gateway terminals and hubs across 34 countries generate roughly 75% of segment revenues directly from ocean shipping lines. Landside customers account for the remaining 25% of total revenue, which includes storage, cargo handling, and landside demurrage. This commercial exposure makes landside revenue highly sensitive to import dwell times during network disruptions.
4.
Gateway operations: The Gothenburg terminal in Sweden serves as a key gateway connecting Baltic trade with international ocean networks. APM Terminals manages the container terminal which recently welcomed a direct CMA CGM service to Asia. To optimize the terminal's flow, progressive storage tariffs are applied to imported and exported containers. These tariffs scale sharply after the initial free time to incentivize rapid cargo evacuation by local shippers.

Terminal storage tariff structures and scaling

5.
Steep escalations: Terminal storage tariffs at APM Terminals Gothenburg are structured to penalize cargo owners who leave containers in yards. Imported standard containers are granted a free storage period of five calendar days to facilitate customs clearance. From day six to eight, the daily storage fee starts at SEK 133 for a twenty-foot container. The rate then escalates to SEK 578 daily from day eleven onwards, illustrating how bottlenecks trigger exponential tariff scaling.
6.
Heavy penalties: Out-of-gauge and hazardous cargo storage tariffs at Gothenburg scale even more aggressively than standard container rates. Shippers face immediate daily fees of SEK 437 per container from the second day of storage. This tariff escalates to SEK 1,015 for twenty-foot units and SEK 1,593 for forty-foot units from day fifteen onwards. These steep charges generate substantial ancillary revenue spikes for APM Terminals when regional logistics chains break down.
7.
Digital integration: Port terminal operators in India utilize unified digital platforms to streamline customer invoicing and payment processes. Allcargo Terminals deployed its myCFS portal to digitize import and export container tracking and tariff billing. This digital portal provides online proforma and e-tariff access to reduce manual touchpoints. While digital tools accelerate cargo clearance, they also ensure terminal operators can transparently collect escalating storage and demurrage fees.
8.
Moderate utilization: Allcargo Terminals operated at 76% capacity utilization in late 2025 as it actively expanded its logistics network. The company is executing a three-year plan to increase container capacity to 1.3 million twenty-foot equivalent units. This expansion aims to release packed facilities and reduce average container dwell times. Consequently, expanding physical capacity remains a primary defense against the severe yard bottlenecks that drive congestionlinked revenue.

Comparing APM Terminals with industry peers

9.
Swift normalization: The Port of Virginia experienced a notable shift in cargo velocity during fiscal year 2026 as logistics bottlenecks eased. The port recorded a decline in loaded container storage revenues due to shorter container dwell times. Importers and shiplines recalibrated their sourcing, which reduced average yard storage exposure across the terminal's facilities. This trend demonstrates how quickly terminal congestion-linked revenues normalise once global supply chain bottlenecks clear.
10.
Tightened controls: Malaysian peer Westports Holdings implemented aggressive measures to manage yard density during operational peaks in late 2025. Jefferies Research reported that the group reduced free storage days for laden containers from five to four days. The broker noted that Westports also increased overstaying charges and restricted cargo to resolve yard congestion. These tighter controls helped normalise yard utilization to 75% but reduced the duration of high-margin storage collections.
11.
Contrary evidence: Port of Tanjung Pelepas, a joint venture of APM Terminals, maintained seamless operations throughout 2025 without experiencing severe congestion. The terminal handled record volumes exceeding 1.2 million TEUs monthly during peak shipping seasons. Operational efficiency allowed the hub to grow volumes without collecting elevated congestion fees or storage surcharges. This reveals that some major transshipment hubs can expand throughput while keeping storagelinked revenues minimal.
12.
Stable charges: The Thessaloniki Port Authority in Greece maintains standard non-escalating tariff structures for empty containers to prevent disruptive behaviors. Empty containers are granted five free days before attracting a flat daily storage charge. Shippers face fixed rates of 3.30 Euro for small containers and 7.70 Euro for larger units. Unlike progressive import tariffs, these flat structures prevent spikes in congestion-linked income during periods of equipment backlogs.
13.
Performance buffer: APM Terminals Bahrain achieved highly stable financial performance in 2025 despite localized shifts in shipping patterns. Container-related revenue fell marginally by 1% as local exporters converted shipments to breakbulk. The port operator also noted that reduced container dwell times impacted overall yard storage collections. This trend confirms that terminal operators face direct revenue compression when shippers accelerate cargo clearance and bypass storage tariffs.
14.
Other side of the story: While easing congestion suggests a normalization of storage revenues, permanent terminal capacity constraints may support elevated tariffs. Bernstein Research reported that Maersk management expects terminal bottlenecks to become a more frequent feature of the market. The broker highlighted that global ports lack sufficient capacity to absorb expanding vessel fleets. If these terminal capacity constraints persist, APM Terminals will likely maintain elevated storage fee income, providing a structural hedge to declining ocean freight rates.

Section 4 – Peer Performance and the 2021-2022 Post-Congestion Decay

Section summary
  1. Easing ocean and terminal congestion after 2022 triggered a rapid normalisation of lucrative storage, demurrage, and detention revenues across global liner networks.
  2. Ocean carriers saw a massive collapse in non-freight ancillary revenues while diversified gateway terminal operators demonstrated greater financial resilience.

Normalisation of Carrier Ancillary Revenues

1.
Rapid deceleration: Maersk experienced a substantial decline in Ocean segment profitability in 2023 as pandemic-driven network disruptions resolved. The carrier saw a negative impact on its container handling and network costs from decreased demurrage and detention revenue during the year. This decline coincided with a 50% year-on-year drop in average freight rates as market capacity returned to service. Consequently, the disappearance of these congestion-linked fees severely pressured the group's net earnings.
2.
Steep contraction: Hapag-Lloyd reported a massive drop in its Liner Shipping segment revenue, which fell 47.2% to USD 19,209.9 million in 2023 from USD 36,380.4 million in 2022. This contraction was heavily driven by "revenue not assigned to trades," representing demurrage and detention, which declined 45.6% to USD 1,345.6 million in fiscal year 2023. This rapid normalisation underscores the extreme volatility of congestion-linked income for ocean liners.
3.
Significant reduction: Hapag-Lloyd's demurrage and detention earnings fell from EUR 2,349.1 million in 2022 to EUR 1,244.2 million in 2023 in its primary reporting currency. This drop was caused by the steady normalisation of global supply chains and shorter container dwell times at major marine gateways. The loss of this high-margin income contributed to Hapag-Lloyd recording an EBIT loss of USD 251 million in the final quarter of 2023.
4.
Lower costs: Hapag-Lloyd benefited from an 11% decline in transport expenses to USD 12.9 billion in 2023 as global congestion cleared. This cost reduction was primarily driven by lower demurrage and storage expenses incurred by the carrier for external equipment. These easing operational bottlenecks allowed the carrier to lower its unit transport expenses by 9.8% to USD 1,255 per TEU. 156 - Earnings Calls 158 159 160 164 - Earnings Calls 167 g Q y p Hapag-Lloyd FY 2023 revenue breakdown showing a 45.6% drop in revenue not assigned to trades.
5.
Contrary evidence: International Container Terminal Services Inc did not benefit from high congestion-linked storage revenues during the pandemic because its gateway terminals did not experience material bottlenecks in 2021 and 2022. In fact, persistent delays in vessel arrivals actually depressed container volumes across the company's port network. Therefore, the group did not suffer a severe earnings correction from storage normalisation, unlike its European liner and terminal peers.

Port Terminal Storage Surcharges Normalisation

6.
Severe normalisation: APM Terminals experienced a sharp drop in congestion-linked storage revenues in 2023 as global trade demand weakened. Container dwell times returned close to the pre-pandemic levels of 2019, erasing the extraordinary storage income that supported earlier years. Consequently, revenue per move decreased by 8.2% during 2023 as lower storage collections outweighed tariff increases. This normalisation represents a direct headwind to Maersk's overall terminal segment profitability.
7.
Substantial growth: APM Terminals previously generated record storage fees in 2021 as severe congestion along all supply chain nodes, particularly in North America, escalated. Gateway revenue per move had spiked to USD 312 in 2021 as congestion-driven storage revenue expanded by approximately USD 220 million. The subsequent deflation of these fees back to 2019 baseline levels highlights how quickly terminal margins contract when supply chain bottlenecks resolve.
8.
Resilient growth: DP World reported a significant increase in 2022 revenues driven by market condition-specific elements including elevated terminal storage fees. The operator's Ports and Terminals business generated USD 6,058.17 million in contract revenue in 2022, supported by strong global demand. While storage fees normalized thereafter, DP World protected its earnings by introducing variable pricing clauses linked to consumer price inflation indexes.
9.
Stable performance: DP World demonstrated high financial resilience in 2023 as its adjusted EBITDA remained stable at USD 5,108 million. The group's reported revenue grew by 6.6% to USD 18,250 million, driven by strong performance in Drydocks and key acquisitions. This diversified business model insulated the company from the severe congestion-linked revenue decay that impacted pure-play ocean shipping lines.
10.
Moderate recovery: International Container Terminal Services Inc reported that terminal storage and ancillary revenues were largely absent in 2023 at its key Manila and Melbourne ports. Despite this headwind, the company's gross revenues from port operations rose 6% to USD 2.39 billion in 2023. This top-line expansion was driven by tariff adjustments, general cargo growth, and the consolidation of Manila North Harbour.

Section 5 – Speed and Dynamics of Surcharge Disappearance

Section summary
  1. Easing global port congestion after 2022 triggered a rapid collapse in spot rates and the evaporation of lucrative carrier surcharges.
  2. Ocean carriers utilize aggressive blank sailings to temporarily support pricing, but structural overcapacity limits the long-term effectiveness of capacity discipline.
  3. The normalization timeline for the 2025-2026 cycle remains gradual due to ongoing maritime disruptions and rigid contract surcharge mechanisms.

Historical normalization after the 2022 peak

1.
Rapid collapse: Easing of port congestion post-2022 led to a swift normalisation of global container freight rates and the erasure of congestion-linked surcharges. According to Vietcombank Securities, global container freight rates plummeted by approximately 85% from their peak as supply chain bottlenecks resolved. Delivery delays and widespread strikes in ports that had characterized the height of the pandemic also quickly disappeared. Consequently, the highly profitable ancillary revenues that had bolstered carrier earnings vanished within a few quarters.
2.
Swift capacity: Easing port bottlenecks freed substantial vessel capacity back into the global shipping market, accelerating the downward pressure on surcharges. Jefferies Research observed that the unwinding of heavy congestion across ports worldwide created immense pressure on spot rates as shipping space became increasingly available. The gradual release of the estimated 10% of the global fleet previously stuck at ports eliminated the capacity deficits that had justified emergency fees. This rapid influx of active capacity effectively ended the extraordinary period of carrier-dominated pricing.
3.
Delayed contracts: Long-term agreements temporarily shielded ocean liners from the immediate financial impact of falling spot rates but eventually succumbed to market pressures. DNB Carnegie reported that while significant declines in the spot market did not immediately affect long-term contract rates, these contracts eventually adjusted downward as shippers renegotiated terms. For example, some retailers successfully negotiated massive rate reductions of up to 50% in their revised agreements. This contract lag delayed the full impact of rate normalization on carrier earnings.
4.
Contrary evidence: Easing geopolitical tensions may not immediately normalize shipping networks or erase surcharges because carriers and vessel owners require several months to fully reassess safety and security risks before resuming historical routes. Morgan Stanley reported that even if Middle East tensions unwind, operators may need more than two to three months to return to normal routings. This suggests that congestion-linked emergency surcharges could persist much longer than fundamental supply and demand models predict.

Contractual structures and surcharge rigidity

5.
Rigid pricing: Dynamic contract structures in the 2025-2026 cycle have introduced more rigid surcharge mechanisms that are harder for shippers to quickly dismantle. Shippers observe that ocean carriers have grown highly adept at inventing novel surcharge categories, such as emergency situation and emergency volume surcharges, to protect their margins. These ancillary fees can instantly increase total contract shipping costs by 20% to 40% depending on the baseline rate. Consequently, these rigid contractual add-ons prevent shippers from realizing the full benefits of falling baseline spot rates.
6.
Elevated costs: Rising fuel expenses and prolonged transit routes have forced carriers to maintain high baseline surcharges to offset elevated operational expenditures. Water Tower Research reported that rerouting vessels around the Cape of Good Hope adds up to $1 million in fuel and operating costs per voyage. These substantial cost increases are passed directly to shippers through well-established bunker adjustment factors and emergency routing surcharges. As a result, these fuel-linked surcharges remain highly sticky and continue to elevate total landed costs.
7.
Gradual negotiation: Shippers are increasingly locking in long-term contracts to secure capacity but find themselves exposed to persistent non-negotiable ancillary fees. Expert insights from a global logistics coordinator show that while contract rates are relatively stable, carriers continue to levy heavy fuel and emergency surcharges regardless of contract commitments. These surcharges typically contribute between 8% and 10% of the total ocean freight cost paid by cargo owners. This structural rigidity means that a significant portion of congestion-linked income remains exposed to persistent non-negotiable fees.
8.
Slow transition: The transition of freight contracts from high pandemic levels to lower baselines is historically a slow process due to staggered renewal cycles. Morgan Stanley noted that while spot rates can fall precipitously, contract rates adjust on a delayed timeline as yearly agreements roll over. Shippers who structurally increased their contract exposure to avoid spot volatility are locked into higher rates for longer periods. This slow contract decay cushions carrier earnings and delays the negative impact on EPS during the initial stages of a market downturn.

Capacity management and blank sailing dynamics

9.
Strategic blanking: Ocean carriers actively manage effective vessel supply through tactical blank sailings to prevent spot rates and surcharges from collapsing. Expert insights show that carriers have successfully kept shipping rates high by coordinating blank sailings and restricting capacity during periods of soft demand. This deliberate withholding of capacity forces shippers to compete for limited space, thereby propping up spot pricing and associated peak season surcharges. Consequently, capacity discipline remains the primary tool for carriers to manage effective vessel supply through soft demand periods.
10.
Squeezed capacity: Aggressive capacity management by shipping alliances can engineer artificial space shortages that temporarily drive spot rates upward. 11.
Strained reliability: The reliance on blank sailings to support rates often comes at the expense of schedule integrity and customer satisfaction. Shippers frequently face weekly cargo rollings and delayed deliveries when carriers tactically cancel scheduled voyages to manage capacity. While these capacity adjustments protect carrier margins in the short term, they severely strain long-term customer relationships. Shippers increasingly favor carriers that avoid rate manipulation at the expense of schedule integrity and reliability.

If successful, this strategic shift could reduce the industry's reliance on tactical capacity disruptions.

Consequently, elevated emergency surcharges are projected to follow a timeline more gradual and volatile than past normalizations.

Once geopolitical disruptions ease and vessels resume transiting through the Suez Canal, this massive influx of capacity will quickly flood the market. This structural supply-demand imbalance is expected to trigger intense rate competition and a rapid collapse of surcharges.

Other side of the story: While a rapid normalisation of surcharges is widely anticipated once geopolitical tensions ease, persistent terminal constraints and port bottlenecks could sustain elevated pricing power. Under these conditions, carriers and terminal operators will likely maintain elevated storage and detention fees, providing a cushion to overall EPS.
