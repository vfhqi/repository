Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

Section summary
  1. OTE's outlook is defined by its strategic shift to a pure-play Greek operator with enhanced capital returns and structural growth in mobile and fiber.
1.
Simplified corporate profile: Hellenic Telecom completed the sale of its mobile operations in Romania to focus exclusively on Greece. This divestment eliminates a major financial drag and creates a simplified equity story for global investors.
2.
Strategic rural expansion: The group completed the acquisition of Terna Fiber in January 2026. This acquisition allows OTE to deploy subsidized fiber networks to semi-urban and rural areas.
3.
Enhanced capital returns: Management introduced a new shareholder remuneration policy linked directly to actual ex-post cash flow. The company committed to distributing virtually 100% of free cash flow through dividends and buybacks.
4.
Declining wholesale revenues: Hellenic Telecom began winding down its low-margin international wholesale operations. This transition is expected to reduce international wholesale revenues by €170 million in fiscal year 2026.
5.
Accelerating mobile migration: The group is actively migrating prepaid users to high-value contract packages to drive ARPU expansion. This transition serves as a key mechanism to monetize growing mobile data volumes across Greece.
6.
Regulatory copper retirement: Retail fixed service revenues are projected to return to positive growth as copper lines are retired. The regulator now permits OTE to discontinue inferior copper connectivity where fiber is available.
7.
Expanding systems solutions: The group achieved massive growth in its systems solutions segment through public sector contracts. However, this rapid ICT expansion represents a lower-margin revenue stream compared to traditional telecom services.
8.
Favorable tax changes: The Greek government removed the 10% special pay-TV tax in January 2026 to boost legitimate media services. This regulatory change is expected to drive pay-TV penetration higher over the next three years.
9.
Defensive competitor venture: A proposed joint venture between PPC and Vodafone Greece could alter the competitive landscape. Analysts believe this venture will ease competitive overbuild worries in the fiber space.
10.
Developed market transition: The upcoming reclassification of Greece to developed market status will impact equity flows. While the transition creates temporary passive index selling risks for OTE, it enhances long-term valuation prospects.
11.
Romanian exit distributions: The company planned to distribute the net positive price from its Romanian asset sale to shareholders. This cash distribution edges up recurring operating cash flow while simplifying the group's geographical focus.
12.
Strategic wholesale agreements: Vodafone Greece and Nova signed up to OTE's new fiber-to-the-home wholesale pricing framework. The framework offers volume-linked discounts to boost high-margin wholesale revenues for OTE.
13.
Accelerating fiber take-up: The Greek government launched an €80 million Gigabit Voucher scheme to hasten fiber take-up. These subsidies directly support OTE's long-term fiber monetization plans by reducing consumer installation barriers.
14.
Expanding television content: The company implemented a content-sharing agreement with peer Nova to expand pay-TV penetration. The agreement helps both operators reduce content acquisition costs while combating illegal streaming platforms.
15.
Emerging competitor threat: Greek utility provider PPC has built an alternative fiber network to compete in the broadband space. However, PPC's telco venture is unlikely to destroy material value for OTE due to high build costs.
16.
Other side of the story: Aggressive price competition from Vodafone Greece or Nova could disrupt OTE's domestic pricing power. Such competitive actions could undermine retail fixed growth and negate the benefits of the new wholesale pricing framework.
