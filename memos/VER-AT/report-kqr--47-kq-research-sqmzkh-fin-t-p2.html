Mechanics of the Austrian EKB-S Levy

5.
Strict revenue: The Austrian EKB-S levy imposes a heavy tax burden on electricity generation revenues to curb windfall profits from the European energy crisis. The Austrian government lowered the market revenue cap from one hundred and twenty euros per megawatt-hour to ninety euros per megawatt-hour for existing generation plants. For plants commissioned after April 2025, the cap is set at one hundred euros per megawatt-hour. This restrictive regime forces a ninety-five percent levy on excess revenues above these thresholds.
6.
Beneficial deductions: VERBUND utilizes statutory investment deductions to elevate its effective revenue cap and shelter earnings from the windfall regime. The EKB-S regulation allows companies to deduct seventy-five percent of qualifying renewable energy investments in Austria, capped at twenty-five euros per megawatt-hour. Bernstein Research reported on 14 September 2026 that this offsetting mechanism can increase the effective threshold to between one hundred and five and one hundred and ten euros per megawatt-hour. Furthermore, the levy explicitly excludes pumped-storage plants and control power from the revenue cap.
7.
Contrary evidence: VERBUND maintained comfortable full-year earnings visibility despite experiencing record-low hydro levels and severe windfall tax headwinds throughout the year. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 3 September 2026 that planning buffers from Austrian Power Grid and the social tariff supported the company's EBITDA guidance. This regulatory buffer indicates that the windfall tax system was less damaging to net income than initial pessimistic market models suggested.

Section 3 – Retail and Industrial Tariffs Under Political and Inflationary Pressure

Section summary
  1. VERBUND AG faces persistent retail margin pressure as political interventions, social tariffs, and consumer litigations restrict its capability to pass through volatile wholesale costs.
  2. Domestic peer EVN AG and international competitors like Hidroelectrica and Iberdrola employ aggressive retail expansions and structured customer contract hedges to mitigate state price caps and margin squeezes.
  3. Despite severe regulatory headwinds, new commercial distribution partnerships provide VERBUND with defensive customer retention channels that safeguard its core retail market share.

Political Fallout and Consumer Litigations

1.
Unprecedented litigation: VERBUND AG faced severe legal challenges from consumer protection groups following its major retail tariff hikes in 2022 and 2023. The company reported in its 2023 annual report that it increased existing customer rates in May 2022 based on indexation clauses and in March 2023 under statutory rights. These pricing decisions triggered class-action lawsuits challenging the adjustment mechanisms, which eventually forced retroactive billing modifications and customer settlements.
2.
Settlement credits: VERBUND AG resolved the legal uncertainty surrounding its retail contracts by distributing direct monetary credits to its consumer base. Under the price adjustment agreements, customers received flatrate credits based on their electricity consumption. For example, the May 2022 agreement provided a 90 euro credit for average consumption, while the March 2023 agreement refunded between 20 and 85 euros per household. This practical compromise helped the utility avoid prolonged supreme court litigation.
3.
Contrary evidence: VERBUND AG maintained a resilient retail supply segment and customer retention rate despite the negative publicity and financial outlays from these consumer lawsuits. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 28 July 2022 that the price indexation lawsuits possessed limited long-term damage potential because indexation strictly followed stated rules. Indeed, the company successfully transitioned over 73% of eligible customers to new predictable loyalty contracts by December 2023, minimizing churn. This high rate of opt-in confirms that customers valued supply security over switching suppliers.
4.
Depressed margins: VERBUND AG experienced severe profitability pressure in its retail sales segment during the peak of the European energy crisis. The company's sales segment EBITDA plummeted to a negative 197 million euros in 2023, compared to more positive historical averages. Within this segment, the retail business alone posted minus 380 million euros in EBITDA due to elevated procurement costs and litigation provisions. This dramatic deficit illustrates how political pressure prevented the company from passing through high wholesale market prices to its retail base.

Domestic Social Tariffs and Price Caps

5.
Social provisions: VERBUND AG faces ongoing political demands to subsidize vulnerable household electricity bills through the implementation of mandatory social tariffs. Deutsche Bank Research reported on 22 July 2026 that the company expects a social tariff impact of approximately 40 million euros in its full-year guidance. Management subsequently guided to a thirty million euro social tariff cost in the second half of 2026 alone. These social mandates directly reduce the utility's retail profitability during prolonged inflationary cycles.
6.

Deutsche Bank Research reported on 19 January 2026 that the state agreed to cap industrial bills at 50 euros per megawatt-hour. Because the funding mechanism must be budget-neutral, the government may expand the generator levy or extract extra state company dividends. This regulatory overhang creates significant earnings uncertainty for VERBUND as industrial price relief is shifted onto energy producers.
7.
Underestimated costs: VERBUND AG faces a cumulative industry burden from these various state price relief interventions as they stretch toward the end of the decade. Chief Executive Officer Michael Strugl warned that the combination of crisis levies, social tariffs, and industrial price caps will cost the energy sector approximately 500 million euros starting in 2027.
