Section 16 – Conclusion

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Attendo's Q1 2026 results delivered a materially stronger-than-expected lease-adjusted operating profit of SEK 326 million versus the SEK 277 million sell-side consensus, representing an 18% beat driven by simultaneous margin expansions across both Finland and Scandinavia. This performance fundamentally alters the investment case by providing the first definitive proof that the company's dual-market operational turnaround is structural rather than cyclical, validating management's margin-over-volume strategic pivot.

The core deviation from consensus centered on geographic margin trajectories, with Scandinavia's lease-adjusted EBITA margin expanding to 4.7% from 3.4% year-over-year while Finland reached 9.4% from 6.9%. Sell-side analysts immediately upgraded the 2026 lease-adjusted EBITA margin forecast by 40 basis points to 7.60% to reflect superior cost control execution. [IT]The simultaneous profitability inflection across both regions eliminates the prior narrative that Attendo's turnaround remained overly dependent on Finnish staffing ratio benefits[/IT], significantly de-risking the investment thesis. Despite robust underlying momentum, reported organic sales growth remained muted at 0.4% due to deliberate exits from low-margin outsourcing contracts in Sweden and strategic divestments in Finland. Excluding these portfolio adjustments, continuing operations grew by 5.2%, demonstrating that core customer-payor demand remains fundamentally strong. This strategic trade-off underscores management's unwavering commitment to prioritizing sustainable profitability over headline volume metrics.

5 The company's rapid balance sheet deleveraging to a lease-adjusted net debt ratio of 1.1x from 1.8x, combined with exceptional free cash flow generation of SEK 211 million versus SEK 50 million in the prior year, enabled the board to authorize a new SEK 275 million share buyback program. DNB Carnegie calculated that Attendo theoretically possesses up to SEK 4.50 billion in capital allocation flexibility for buybacks or acquisitions through 2028 while remaining within its target leverage corridor. This massive unused borrowing capacity provides a powerful floor to the valuation and supports aggressive shareholder returns.

9 The most significant operational disruption emerged post-quarter when CEO Martin Tivéus announced his resignation on June 30, 2026, to accept an international role outside Sweden. While the extended notice period through end-2026 or early 2027 allows for structured succession planning, the departure introduces near-term execution uncertainty during a critical margin recovery phase. DNB Carnegie viewed the timing as reasonable and does not expect this transition to disrupt the underlying earnings trajectory.

However, the incoming CEO's ability to maintain Tivéus's strict contract discipline and capital allocation framework will be the single most important determinant of whether the SEK 9.00 adjusted EPS target remains credible. The competitive landscape shifted dramatically following Ambea's late-June 2026 public takeover bid for Humana, creating a combined Nordic care colossus with over SEK 26,000 million in sales.

This consolidation validates Attendo's own-operations model and margin-led strategy while potentially reshaping pricing dynamics and municipal relationships across the region. Simultaneously, Finland's regulatory adjustment to a 0.60 staffing ratio from 0.65 effective January 2025 has successfully translated into lower personnel costs and improved operational efficiency, ahead of initial conservative expectations.

The strongest counter-hypothesis to the positive case centers on persistent municipal budget constraints and lagging price indexation mechanisms failing to offset accelerating wage inflation. The structural timing mismatch in contract indexation, which adjusts annually in October, creates acute vulnerability during periods of rapid cost spikes. Despite operational deleveraging, Attendo's massive SEK 13,284 million in real estate lease liabilities under IFRS 16 exposes the group to sustained interest rate pressure and covenant risks. If local welfare regions enforce strict budget cuts or delay patient referrals to private operators, the high-leverage occupancy recovery driving current margins could rapidly reverse. 15
