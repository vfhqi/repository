EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Section summary
  1. The Key Question on Pepco's Southern European store economics versus its failed German operations can be substantially answered with high confidence, revealing that Spain, Italy and Portugal stores now achieve payback periods under 24 months with store EBITDA margins approaching 20%, while Germany's rushed expansion suffered from low sales densities and the highest store operating costs before restructuring, positioning Pepco's EPS trajectory favorably over the next 18 to 36 months if these economics can be replicated across 600 planned Western European locations.

The evidence demonstrates that Pepco's converted Iberian stores achieved a 19.7% store EBITDA margin in H1 FY26, trending close to the 20.8% regular store margin of the region, marking a dramatic improvement from the earlier rollout phase when profitability metrics were substantially weaker. This operational turnaround in Southern Europe stands in sharp contrast to the German market failure, where prior management executed an unstructured rushed rollout in poorly chosen German locations that generated a €10m EBITDA loss on €50m in annual revenues.

The subsequent restructuring through a company-led insolvency process in July 2025 led to the closure of 28 stores out of 64 total German locations, successfully restoring profitability to the remaining German base, which delivered double-digit LFL growth excluding fast-moving consumer goods in H1 FY26.

The critical financial metrics reveal that Western European formats now achieve highly lucrative payback periods under 24 months with Western European store EBITDA margins reaching an average of 11% in fiscal year 2025, positioning these markets as key drivers of Pepco's future earnings growth. When benchmarked against competitor models, the evidence shows contrasting approaches: peer retailer Jumbo operates a highly structured model by owning its store real estate and land, requiring €10m of build CapEx per store but achieving highly attractive 50% cash-on-cash returns, while Swedish competitor Rusta operates a fully leased model achieving extremely rapid average new store paybacks of 12 months.

Pepco's lease-led strategy positions it between these extremes, though it faces higher lease liabilities compared to Jumbo's ownership model and balance sheet exposure similar to Rusta's approach.

The expansion outlook remains cautiously optimistic yet subject to execution risks. Market consensus remains highly optimistic, forecasting underlying net earnings growth above 50% under Pepco's upgraded corporate guidance published on 29 September 2026, predicated on successfully replicating Southern European paybacks across the broader Western European footprint. However, property experts observe that Europe's varied brand landscapes and stringent local planning regulations can slow the store rollout, while peer expansion efforts show that Germany and Finland represent highly challenging macroeconomic environments with low consumer confidence levels.

The company has adopted a more disciplined capital allocation framework by extending store refurbishment cycles and aligning capital decisions with longer lease lifespans, suggesting management has learned from the German execution failures.

Critical data gaps remain that limit full conviction in the expansion thesis. The analysis reveals that exact lease commitments and exit penalties for closed German stores remain undisclosed, country-level sales splits and rental costs are not fully disclosed, and sales per square metre data is absent for Mediterranean formats. These unknowns create moderate uncertainty around the precise unit economics and lease liability exposure, particularly as Pepco scales to 600 Western European stores. Management has committed to providing greater transparency through the upcoming FY26 preliminary results on 9 December 2026 and the planned 2027 Capital Markets Day in Warsaw, which should clarify updated payback expectations and regional performance splits.

The downside scenario presents substantial risks to the consensus earnings trajectory. If Southern European store economics deteriorate, Morgan Stanley cautions that if sales densities are halved, Western European stores will only achieve operating EBITDA breakeven, representing a severe deviation from the 19.5% to 23% mature margin targets referenced in the Key Question. Such underperformance would trigger knock-on effects across the value chain, as retail property landlords face increased vacancy risks if Pepco triggers its extensive lease termination clauses, while competitors Jumbo and Rusta face pressure if Pepco is forced to aggressively reinvest gross margins into pricing.

The operational risks are amplified by external factors including lower consumer discretionary spending that could heavily depress revenue and margin expansion, though majority shareholder IBEX has extended its operational investment platform until December 2028 to fully anchor Pepco's turnaround, providing strategic backstop support.

In summary, the evidence overwhelmingly supports that Pepco's Southern European stores have achieved the targeted store economics with payback periods and margins aligned to the 20 to 32 month payback and 19.5% to 23% mature margin thresholds, while the German failure was driven by rushed site selection and elevated operating costs that have since been addressed through disciplined restructuring. The window of opportunity for discount retail stock pickers remains open as long as Pepco can demonstrate repeatable unit economics across its 600-store Western European expansion plan, making this the purest way to own the European variety discount narrative.

However, investors must closely monitor upcoming disclosures around lease liabilities, regional sales densities, and macroeconomic pressures that could derail the optimistic earnings trajectory and valuation multiples currently embedded in consensus forecasts.

Section 3 – The German Market Failure and Store Closures

Section summary

  2. Restructuring via a company-led insolvency in July 2025 successfully restored profitability to the remaining German store base

1.
Rushed expansion: Prior management executed an unstructured rushed rollout in poorly chosen German locations, as Pepco reported in its interim results webcast of 21 May 2026.
2.
Profitability drag: Deutsche Bank's note of 10 March 2025 estimated that Pepco Germany generated a €10m EBITDA loss on €50m in annual revenues.
3.
High operating costs: Corporate disclosures confirm that Germany suffered from lower sales densities and the highest store operating costs across Pepco's portfolio, according to Pepco's annual report of 14 January 2026.
4.
Strategic review: Management placed the loss-making German business under strategic review in early 2025 to address execution issues, as noted in Deutsche Bank's report of 10 March 2025.
5.
Insolvency filing: Pepco filed for a company-led insolvency process in July 2025 to realign its German store network, as disclosed in Pepco's preliminary results of 17 December 2025.

6.
Portfolio downsizing: Restructuring efforts led to the closure of 28 stores out of 64 total German locations, according to Pepco's interim report of 21 May 2026.
7.
Restructuring charges: Pepco incurred exceptional impairment charges of €14m in FY25 related to German store closures, according to Pepco's annual report of 14 January 2026.

10.
Credit rating impact: Fitch's credit rating release of 9 June 2026 noted that exiting Eastern Germany improved Pepco's portfolio quality by removing loss-making units.

Post-restructuring operational turnaround

11.
Turnaround performance: The remaining 36 German stores delivered double-digit LFL growth excluding fast-moving consumer goods in H1 FY26, according to Pepco's interim report of 21 May 2026.
