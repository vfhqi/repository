What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: Fevertree is now a brand-royalty company with a soft-drinks business attached, whose earnings recovery to a 15% margin comes almost entirely from a floored United States royalty set by a contract nobody outside the two companies can see, while the Rest of Group grows about 3% a year and carries the glass-energy risk in 2027 and 2028. Seven judgements decide whether to add, hold or cut.

  1. Judgement 1, the floor and its 2027 step. Fevertree booked £4.1m of United States EBITDA in H1 2026, while Redburn's 5.4% joint-operation margin implies only £3.5m to £4m for its half of the whole year: the guarantee, not the profit share, is setting the royalty. We model £20.0m for FY2027 and £27.0m for FY2028 (from £8.2m in FY2025). If the 2027 step is £24m, FY2027 EPS rises 2.8p (+8.5%) and the gap to consensus halves; if £17m, it falls 2.1p (6%).
  2. Judgement 2, United States shipments against takeaway. Consensus's £150m of United States H2 2026 revenue needs about +20%; the scanner rate was 16% in the third quarter, and H1 shipments (+11%) ran slightly ahead of tracked takeaway (8% to 9%). A destock half costs little EBITDA while the floor binds, but the memo puts the multiple at 16 to 18 times on it, 590p to 670p; a rate above 15% through the April 2027 lap supports 28 to 30 times on consensus EPS, 1,450p to 1,560p.
  3. Judgement 3, FY2026 and the base for 2027. Guidance is brand revenue £402.4m and EBITDA £49.9m, reaffirmed 10-Sep-2026. About £3.5m of one-off tariff refunds sits in H2, so underlying FY2026 EBITDA is about £45m. We expect EBITDA met within about £1m (65%) and revenue about 1% light (55%); FY2027 growth must be measured from £45m, not £49.9m.
  4. Judgement 4, the Rest of Group margin and glass energy. Glass energy is 100% hedged for 2026, 66% for 2027 and 33% for 2028; calendar-2027 gas forwards near €80 per megawatt hour and spot above €100. One point of Rest of Group margin is £2.6m to £2.7m of EBITDA, 1.8p to 1.9p of EPS. We hold the margin at 24.3% in FY2027 and FY2028; consensus needs about 26% to 27% in FY2028, which has no precedent in six years.
  5. Judgement 5, United States marketing. Marketing doubled to about 20% of United States revenue with the first national campaign from April 2026. We take 15% by FY2028; Redburn and consensus about 11%. Each £4m is 2.85p of FY2028 EPS (7%); the branch in which marketing falls and velocity holds is the only one that reaches consensus.
  6. Judgement 6, the FY2027 outlook and the slow drift of consensus. No guidance raise since 2022; FY2027 is unguided apart from "at least £100m" of free cash flow over 2027 and 2028 and "double-digit" United States growth. Consensus FY2027 EBITDA is £63.9m, ours £58.3m. The memo puts an outlook endorsing £63m or more at 30% and an endorsement of a consensus drifted to £58m to £62m at 50%: a slow reset, not a cut.
  7. Judgement 7, the options. The levy ruling (a win is +2.1p a year, 6% of FY2027 EPS, plus a £5.4m reversal), and the standstill's end around February 2028, a date-certain option on a bid from the only party that sees the United States numbers. Neither is in our base; either could turn the skew.

Order of play. Ask the floor and its disclosure (A4, B2.1) and United States H2 shipments (A2) first: they decide whether the case can turn. Then FY2026 and the refunds (A1, A3), the FY2027 outlook (A5), glass energy and pricing (A6, B3.3), then Molson Coors' 2027 plans (B1.1, B1.2). Then the centre and cash (A8, A9), the levy (B4.1) and the stake (B6.1). Leave people and pay to the end, or for management.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the pre-close in January 2027, the FY2026 results on 30-Mar-2027, the annual general meeting in June 2027 and the H1 2027 results in September 2027)

A1 ★ The FY2026 guidance. "Guidance of £402.4 million of brand revenue and £49.9 million of EBITDA needs about +7% revenue and +24% EBITDA in the second half. As we read it, the EBITDA step is helped by the levy provision in last year's base, by tariff refunds and by the United States minimum. Is that the right reading, and is revenue the line with less headroom?"
- Why: FY2026 is settled within about plus or minus £1.5m of EBITDA in the memo. Revenue needs United States H2 growth near +20% against a 16% scanner rate. Each £1m of EBITDA is about 0.7p of EPS.
- Tell: "comfortable on both" = in line; any hedging on revenue = our "revenue slightly below, EBITDA in line" reading, which the company would report as met.

A2 ★ United States shipments against consumption. "In the first half, United States shipments grew about 11% while tracked takeaway averaged about 8% to 9%. How do you and Molson Coors judge distributor and retailer stock going into the holiday season and the April lap, and how should we model second-half shipments against the 16% scanner rate?"
- Why: consensus needs about +20% in H2; Celsius's shipments to PepsiCo fell USD 124m in one quarter of 2024 while its scanner sales grew 7%. A destock half is a 3% to 5% group revenue miss, small for EBITDA under the floor, large for the multiple (the memo's (27%) to (35%)).
- Tell: "shipments in line with takeaway, stocks normal" = Judgement 2 passes for now; "some phasing into 2027" = the destock risk the memo dates to the April 2027 lap.

A3 ★ The tariff refunds. "We estimate your share of the United States tariff refunds at about £3.5 million, booked in the second half. Is that the right order of size, will they sit inside adjusted EBITDA or as exceptional, and should we take them out of the base for 2027?"
- Why: underlying FY2026 EBITDA is then about £45m (11.4% margin), not £49.9m; consensus FY2027 EBITDA of £63.9m is +42% on the underlying figure, against our £58.3m (+30%, most of it the floor's step).
- Tell: "inside adjusted, one-off" = brokers who grow from £49.9m overstate 2027; "recurring savings as well" = tariffs gone add about £2.5m a year, as the memo has from H2 2026.

A4 ★ How to model the United States line. "You booked £4.1 million of United States EBITDA in the first half. Is it right to read that as the guaranteed minimum rather than half the joint operation's profit, and how should we model the step in 2027? Will the FY2026 accounts show the royalty and the profit share separately?"
- Why: the largest single unknown. Our base: £12.8m (FY2026), £20.0m (FY2027), £27.0m (FY2028). Plus or minus £4m in FY2027 is plus or minus 2.8p of EPS (8.5%). Deutsche Bank's £29m by FY2028 and Redburn's September upgrade on "better-modelled guaranteed EBITDA" suggest the schedule may be steeper than ours. H2 2026 royalty EBITDA above about £8.5m in the March accounts would imply a higher floor.
- Tell: "the minimum is binding this year and next" = United States revenue does not reach EPS until the joint operation's profit passes it, as the memo argues; any steer that 2027 steps up sharply = upside to our FY2027 EPS; "we earn half the profit already" = the floor matters less and United States growth matters more than we model.

A5 ★ The FY2027 outlook. "At the March results, will you give an FY2027 EBITDA figure, or frame the outlook around the cash target and United States growth as before? When do you next compile consensus, and do you publish it?"
- Why: consensus FY2027 EBITDA £63.9m against our £58.3m (a gap of £5.6m, about 3.9p of EPS); FY2027 EBITDA consensus has moved (2%) over three, six and twelve months beneath a flat EPS line held up by buybacks. The memo puts an endorsement of £63m or more at 30%.
- Tell: "a figure for the first time" = the floor gives management a number it trusts, upside to the memo's 10% chance of a raise; "consensus, as usual" = the slow drift the memo expects.

A6 ★ Glass energy in 2027. "Your glass energy is about two-thirds hedged for 2027. When is the 2027 glass price set with your suppliers, and is our reading right that the unhedged third meets forward gas near €80 per megawatt hour, so 2027 carries cost risk as well as 2028?"
- Why: the memo's 2027 pass-through on the unhedged third is 0.5 to 1.0 points of Rest of Group margin, 0.9p to 1.8p of FY2027 EPS. A 10% move in the glass price is about £5m of EBITDA (10%). Glass makers set most contracts annually over the winter.
- Tell: "2027 glass priced below 2026" = upside to our 24.3% margin; "agreed, up mid-single digits" = our base; "not yet agreed" = the risk the sell-side has not dated.

A7 Hedging for 2028. "Cover for 2028 is about a third. Has it moved, and with 2028 forward gas near €49, do you intend to lock more in?"
- Why: a 2022-type move on the unhedged two-thirds in 2028 is 1.5 to 2.5 points of margin, £4.0m to £6.7m, (7%) to (12%) of FY2028 EPS. The 2022 to 2023 precedent cost 600 basis points of gross margin in a half.
- Tell: "cover raised toward two-thirds at today's forwards" = the FY2028 risk most brokers name is smaller; "policy unchanged" = the risk stays.

A8 ★ Central costs and exceptional costs. "Central costs were £23.2 million in 2025, up 7%. What should we model for 2026 and 2027, and when do the exceptional costs of about £5 million a year end?"
- Why: we grow central costs at 2.5% a year (£23.8m, £24.4m); each £1m is 0.7p of EPS (2%). Exceptional costs ran at about £5m for three years; our cash flow deducts £3.0m, £1.5m and £1.0m in FY2026 to FY2028. Central costs below 5.5% of revenue is one of the memo's tests of a better-run company.
- Tell: "flat, systems programme done" = a small upgrade; "rising with the brand investment" = the Rest of Group adds almost no margin, as the memo says.

A9 ★ Cash, buybacks and interest income. "After the £60 million buyback this year, how much net cash do you want to hold, and what should we model for buybacks and interest income in 2027?"
- Why: net cash fell from £130m (H1 2025) to £68m (H1 2026) and we expect about £44m at end-2026. Consensus has pre-tax profit above EBIT by £2.7m and £3.9m in FY2027 and FY2028 while its share count falls to 104m to 107m: it counts the cash twice, about 0.9p and 1.9p of EPS against our £1.4m and £1.3m. Buybacks of about £30m a year add only 1% to 2% a year to EPS net of interest forgone.
- Tell: "continued buybacks from free cash flow" = interest income falls and consensus EPS loses 2% to 4%; "pause to rebuild cash" = consensus share counts are too low.

A10 The free cash flow target. "Is our reading right that the 'at least £100 million' over 2027 and 2028 is helped by working capital released as United States goods stop being recharged at cost, and is it after exceptional costs?"
- Why: we reach £99m (£48.2m and £50.9m) at EBITDA 12% below consensus, with about £12m of working capital released over the two years as statutory United States revenue falls from £128m toward about £29m; consensus is about £118m (Jefferies £117.6m). The Buy houses read the target as conservative; the memo reads it as realistic.
- Tell: "yes, the release is in it" = the target is realistic, not a sandbag; "on a lower EBITDA path than consensus" = the drift has already begun inside the company's own numbers.

A11 Tax. "What tax rate should we model for 2026 and 2027?"
- Why: we use 25%; consensus net income of £31.0m on £39.0m of pre-tax profit implies 20.5% for FY2026. Five points is about 1.7p of FY2026 EPS.
- Tell: "about 25%" = consensus FY2026 net income is high.

A12 The second half in the United Kingdom and Europe. "The United Kingdom grew about 3% in the first half with good weather; Europe had some phasing benefit. How should we think about the second half for each, given the on-trade and the European phasing?"
- Why: 1% of Rest of Group revenue (about £2.5m) moves EBITDA about £0.8m; incremental revenue drops through at 30% to 35%. We model the United Kingdom flat to +1% and Europe +3% to 3.5%.
- Tell: "European phasing gives back in H2" = the January pre-close shows Europe soft, as the memo expects.

A13 The January pre-close. "What will the pre-close statement in late January cover: regional growth at constant currency, a statement on EBITDA, net cash and the completion of the buyback?"
- Why: it is the first of the four events. The memo's reading: United States H2 growth at or above 15% with EBITDA at or above £49m is "met"; United States growth below 12% with EBITDA still near £49.9m would itself show the floor binding. It expects a (1%) to (3%) trim to FY2027 EBITDA consensus there.
- Tell: a regional split and an EBITDA statement = Judgements 2 and 3 tested in January rather than March.

A14 The dollar. "The United States royalty is earned in dollars. How should we model the currency effect on it and on the group, and is any of it hedged?"
- Why: the United States is 35% of brand revenue; a stronger dollar in 2027 on a dollar-denominated royalty is one of the memo's routes to an upgrade, which Morgan Stanley expects.
- Tell: "translated at the average rate, unhedged" = sterling moves the royalty one for one; "hedged" = no currency upside.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. The Molson Coors partnership

B1.1 ★ Molson Coors' plans for 2027. "What did Molson Coors' third-quarter call on 5 November mean for Fevertree: are its 2027 distributor incentives on Fevertree volume renewed, and does its cost programme touch the people or money behind the brand?"
- Why: Molson Coors has a new chief executive (Rahul Goyal), a USD 450m cost programme, 400 salaried roles cut, Americas volumes down 4.8% in H1 2026 and a USD 275m ready-to-drink purchase (Monaco) competing for the same sales calls. Deprioritisation is the memo's partner risk: earnings protected by the floor to 2030, the multiple not (a 25% to 35% de-rating).
- Tell: "incentives renewed, the brand is a priority" = supports the base; any sign that savings include the brand's marketing = the warning the memo names.

B1.2 ★ United States marketing. "Marketing is about 20% of United States revenue this year. Who decides the 2027 and 2028 budgets, and is the plan to step it down as distribution matures, or to hold it while growth needs it?"
- Why: plus or minus £4m is plus or minus 2.85p of FY2028 EPS (7%). Deutsche Bank warns growth may need permanently elevated marketing; Monster and Celsius spend about 20% permanently. The same money is credited with the 2026 scanner acceleration and with the FY2028 margin.
- Tell: "stepping down from 2027" = toward consensus, if velocity holds; "held while we build" = our 15% or lower, and consensus FY2028 EBITDA falls about £4m.

B1.3 ★ Can localisation and the glass slip. "Only cans are now expected to be produced in the United States in 2027. Is our reading right that the guarantee was sized consistent with full localisation, so the cost of the glass slip falls on Molson Coors rather than on your royalty?"
- Why: can localisation is worth about £2.5m to £3m from 2027 in our base; Deutsche Bank reports the guarantee delivers profit "consistent with full localization assumptions". It is one of the memo's unpriced protections.
- Tell: "yes" = a protection the market does not price; "the profit share bears it" = a cost above the floor, later.

B1.4 When the profit share passes the floor. "How should we think about when half the joint operation's profit would exceed the guaranteed minimum: is it a 2028 or a 2030 question?"
- Why: Redburn's 30% joint-operation margin by 2028 would lift the profit share above the floor and give Fevertree real United States operating leverage for the first time; until then plus or minus 5 points of FY2027 United States growth is only plus or minus 0.5p of EPS.
- Tell: "later in the period" = United States growth stays a multiple driver, not an earnings driver, as the memo says.

B1.5 The on-trade in the United States. "The scanner data cover none of the 250,000 on-trade accounts. How do you and Molson Coors track the on-trade, and how are beer sales teams doing at selling a mixer to bartenders?"
- Why: the Snapple precedent: a specialist on-premise heartland diluted by a mass network. Distributors rate execution positively (73% to 75%) and 60% expect the brand to gain share.
- Tell: a measure shared = a second monthly test beyond the scanner data.

B1.6 United States pricing. "Has any price change been agreed through the joint governance committee for 2027, and how much does the growth of eight-pack cans dilute value per unit?"
- Why: tracked value grows slower than volume as eight-packs grow; Keurig Dr Pepper took 8% net price. A United States price rise is one of the memo's tests for real operating leverage.
- Tell: "a price rise in 2027" = upside to joint-operation profit.

B1.7 Who runs the relationship. "With the North American team gone, who inside Fevertree manages Molson Coors day to day, and how often does the joint governance committee meet?"
- Why: the United States-specific capability (North America chief executive, distribution director, team) left without replacement inside Fevertree, while three-quarters of forecast revenue growth runs through the partner.
- Tell: a named owner and a regular cadence = the dependency is managed; "the founder and finance director directly" = more weight on a thin bench (B7.1).

B2. Disclosure

B2.1 ★ The floor and the joint operation. "Would you consider disclosing the minimum royalty schedule, or the joint operation's profit, so that the market can see what reaches your earnings?"
- Why: the memo names disclosure of the floor as the single item that would collapse the forecast error around the largest driver and could take the multiple from 24.5 times toward 28 to 30 times on our EPS (+15% to 20%). No covering house states the floor's size or that it appears to be binding.
- Tell: "the segment note will show it from March" = Judgement 1 is tested in four months; "confidential, by agreement" = brokers keep guessing and the stock keeps trading the scanner data.

B2.2 The two revenue bases. "Will guidance and segment reporting stay on brand revenue, and will you keep giving the statutory bridge?"
- Why: FactSet carries statutory revenue (£325.0m in FY2025) while guidance and consensus use brand revenue (£375.3m); our own model summary mixed the two and understated EBITDA 13.4%. A clear bridge stops the same error in screens.
- Tell: "brand revenue, bridge kept" = no change.

B3. The Rest of Group: competition, pricing and the portfolio

B3.1 ★ Share in Europe. "Nielsen showed a value-share loss of about 20 basis points in Europe in the four weeks to 4 October, the first in our files. Is it a timing effect or a competitive one, and where is private label hurting most?"
- Why: Europe is 26% of brand revenue; private label is 38% to 45% of mixers in Spain and the Netherlands; London Essence (Carlsberg-Britvic), Fentimans and Frobishers (AG Barr) and Schweppes campaigns are active. Two consecutive twelve-week share gains would change the memo's view.
- Tell: "promotional timing, recovered" = noise; "private label in the Netherlands and Spain" = share at risk, as the memo has it.

B3.2 The United Kingdom on-trade. "With the on-trade about 35% below 2019 and now 40% of your United Kingdom sales, how is the on-trade trading this autumn, and how far does the off-trade make up for it?"
- Why: the United Kingdom is 29% of brand revenue; inflation is 3.5%, the energy cap is forecast up about 16% in January and 2027 growth was cut to 1.0%. We model the United Kingdom flat to +1%.
- Tell: "on-trade stable through the winter" = removes one of the memo's environmental risks.

B3.3 ★ Price in 2027. "Are you planning price increases in the United Kingdom and Europe for 2027 to recover glass costs, and how did the trade react to the 2024 increase?"
- Why: no real price rises in 2022 to 2023, a 300 basis-point catch-up in 2024, Europe price and mix +1.6% over 52 weeks. A net price rise above inflation without share loss is the memo's first test of pricing power; each point of Rest of Group margin is 1.8p to 1.9p of EPS.
- Tell: "price in 2027, holding share" = upside to our 24.3% margin; "we share the margin with the trade" = the margin stays near 24%, as we model.

B3.4 Beyond tonic. "The beyond-tonic platforms are about 47% of sales and grew 13% in the first half. How are the non-alcoholic ready-to-drink cans and draught doing, and what share of growth do they give in 2027?"
- Why: five flavour platforms produced 85% of H1 2026 growth; non-alcoholic ready-to-drink cans in over 4,000 United Kingdom points at £1.90; draught up 185% in trial. Tonic falls about 3% a year in our base.
- Tell: "beyond tonic offsets tonic with room to spare" = United Kingdom growth above our flat to +1%.

B3.5 The deposit return scheme and the sugar threshold. "Is our reading right that the deposit return scheme from October 2027 and the lower sugar threshold from January 2028 are roughly neutral for you?"
- Why: cans enter the deposit scheme with no producer fee until 2029; the portfolio sits above 5 grams and below 4.5 grams per 100 millilitres, so the threshold change adds about nothing. Both are in the Sector Digest's dated tests.
- Tell: "neutral" = no change.

B3.6 Rest of World. "Rest of World is about 10% of brand revenue. Which markets drive it, and is high single-digit growth the right way to model it?"
- Why: we model +7% to 8% a year, the fastest Rest of Group region; it is the least covered part of our files.
- Tell: "a few markets, distributor-led" = lumpy; "broad-based" = our rate holds.

B4. The levy and input costs

B4.1 ★ The packaging levy case. "Where does the court case on the packaging levy stand, when do you expect a ruling, how much is provided, and what does it cost a year?"
- Why: our base carries about £3m a year; a loss changes nothing, a win is +2.1p a year (6% of FY2027 EPS) plus a one-off reversal. Our files disagree on the provision: £5.4m (the memo), £2.8m charged in FY2025 (the first set of answers) and £2.6m (the United Kingdom Geography Digest). Jefferies puts a win at about 5% of EBIT and leaves it out of its forecasts.
- Tell: "a ruling in 2027" = a dated upside option; the provision figure settles our files.

B4.2 Other inputs. "How hedged are aluminium and freight for 2027, and are the two European co-packers fully back?"
- Why: 1% on cost of goods is about £2.2m (4.4% of FY2026 EBITDA); co-packing is 32% of cost of goods, glass 24%, logistics 19%.
