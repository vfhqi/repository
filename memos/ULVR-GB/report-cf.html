Portfolio simplification and ice cream demerger

1.
Bold transformation: Unilever successfully completed the demerger of its underperforming ice cream division on December 6, 2025, creating a standalone corporate entity. The newly formed entity, The Magnum Ice Cream Company N.V., began trading independently on European and American stock exchanges on December 8, 2025. Former parent company shareholders automatically received one share of the new ice cream business for every five original shares they owned. Portfolio pruning represents the first phase of a broader strategic initiative to eliminate operational complexity and boost long-term margins.
2.
Drastic restructuring: Unilever executed an eight-for-nine share consolidation to streamline its capital base immediately following the ice cream demerger. Executive leadership subsequently cancelled over 51 million treasury shares to adjust voting rights and stabilize market valuation. Consolidation efforts seek to mitigate technical dilution and adjust the capital structure for the remaining business. Shareholder sentiment remains sensitive to these technical changes as the company shifts its primary focus to personal care.
3.
Orderly divestment: Unilever retained a minority stake of approximately 19.9% in the newly listed ice cream standalone business post-demerger. Strategic plans outline a gradual sell-down of this residual exposure to cover transaction expenses and enhance overall financial flexibility. Measured exit strategies avoid immediate market flooding while securing capital for growth. Proceeds will support the core business as management targets higher-margin premium segments over the next three years.
4.
Analytical caution: Bernstein Research highlights that the ice cream demerger created significant technical selling pressure on Unilever shares. Domestic European holders are actively debating whether to retain the newly listed food and ice cream entities or exit their positions. Capital shifts risk depressing short-term valuations despite the positive long-term strategic logic of the split. The resulting selling pressure could weigh on absolute performance until institutional portfolios complete their rebalancing.

Foods divestment and McCormick combination

5.
Decisive pivot: Unilever announced a historic agreement in March 2026 to combine its global foods business with McCormick to form a major flavour business. The landmark transaction values the divested foods business at 45 billion dollars, representing a premium valuation multiple of 13.8 times EV to EBITDA. Under the agreed terms, Unilever and its shareholders will receive McCormick shares valued at 29.1 billion dollars alongside 15.7 billion dollars in cash. Cash proceeds will support subsequent balance sheet restructuring and targeted share buyback programs.
6.
Concentrated focus: Unilever will transform into a pure-play home and personal care business once the McCormick deal closes in mid-2027. The combined beauty, wellbeing, and personal care segments will contribute 67% of pro forma turnover compared to 51% in fiscal year 2025. Concentration of assets directs corporate resources toward structurally high-growth categories that generate superior returns. Pure-play status is expected to elevate the underlying sales growth profile to a historical average of 5.4%.
7.
Indian ring-fencing: Hindustan Unilever Limited has deliberately excluded the high-growth Indian food business from the global divestment transaction. Indian foods contribute a modest 3% to total group revenue but offer high margins and strong local growth dynamics. Retaining these assets preserves local market dominance while the parent entity completes its global restructuring. The separation of global food operations has no direct impact on the Indian operating subsidiary's immediate expansion plans.
8.
Synergistic outcomes: Unilever expects the combined food entity to deliver 600 million dollars of annual run-rate cost synergies. An additional 100 million dollars of incremental synergies will be fully reinvested to support future product growth.
