Section 2 – Policy Application: Public Interest Test and the Insourcing Unit

Section summary
  1. The UK's new Public Interest Test and Insourcing Unit establish a rigorous, interventionist procurement regime that threatens to shift outsourced central government service contracts back in-house.
  2. While key sensitive areas like defence, healthcare, and overseas operations remain largely exempt, high-volume building management and security services are the prime near-term targets.
  3. Structural policy implementation starting in April 2027 will create multi-year headwinds for major outsourcers like Mitie as departments publish five-year roadmaps to rebuild state capacity.

Mechanics of the

1.
Sourcing Test Tightening regulation: The Cabinet Office published Procurement Policy Note 024 in June 2026 to systematically mandate in-scope public bodies to consider in-house service delivery. The new rules require central government departments to conduct a formal Public Interest Test prior to procuring service contracts valued at over £1 million. This policy represents a major shift away from outsourcing by default, which means that Mitie's pipeline of public sector opportunities could shrink after the rules take effect on 1 April 2027.
2.
Rigorous assessment: The Public Interest Test consists of two sequential parts to determine whether a service should be brought back in-house. Part One evaluates whether the service is a suitable candidate based on strategic alignment, value for money, and social value. Part Two assesses whether the contracting authority has the necessary staff, budget, and governance to manage the service directly. If authorities fail to build these administrative capabilities, Mitie's public sector division will likely maintain its contract volumes and defend its core facilities management earnings.
3.
Strategic planning: UK departments spending over £100 million annually on contracts must produce and publish five-year insourcing strategies. Government guidelines mandate that these roadmaps must profile existing services and identify activities with high insourcing potential. Capsticks Solicitors noted in September 2026 that this forward-looking planning gives departments essential lead time to build internal skills and operational readiness. Consequently, these published strategies will serve as an early warning system for Mitie, allowing management to redeploy capital and protect earnings per share.
4.
Contrary evidence: Cabinet Office guidance outlines several critical exemptions where the Public Interest Test does not apply. Specifically, the mandates exclude projects that have already completed a Strategic Outline Case or a Delivery Model Assessment prior to April 2027. Legal updates published in July 2026 confirm that framework agreements and dynamic markets are also excluded from the primary test requirements. This means that a large portion of Mitie's existing and mid-stage procurement pipeline will remain insulated from immediate insourcing pressures, which protects near-term revenue targets.

Sensitive Sector Exclusions

5.
Favourable exclusions: The Ministry of Defence maintains substantial autonomy through specific exclusions embedded within the new procurement framework. Under Procurement Policy Note 024, defence and security contracts delivered by original equipment manufacturers are entirely exempt from the Public Interest Test. Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in June 2026 that Babcock has very low risk under this policy because of its heavy weighting toward defence services. This shielding of national security contracts means that Mitie's joint ventures in military infrastructure, such as Landmarc, should continue to generate stable, long-term earnings.
6.
Structured exemptions: Regulated health procurements represent another massive area excluded from the scope of the mandatory Public Interest Test. Guidance from Capsticks Solicitors in September 2026 confirms that health services exempted under Regulation 43 of the Procurement Regulations 2024 are not subject to the PPN 024 rules. These exclusions apply to NHS trusts and foundation trusts, which are only encouraged to treat the guidance as voluntary good practice. Because healthcare represents a core pillar of technical services, this exemption prevents immediate margin erosion for Mitie's hospital maintenance portfolio.
7.
Restricted operations: The Ministry of Justice faces severe operational constraints that may limit its capacity to implement aggressive insourcing. Government reports from April 2026 show that the Her Majesty's Prison and Probation Service is committed to reducing high staff workloads by 25% by April 2027. Because of these persistent staffing shortfalls, the department is unlikely to take on additional facilities management responsibilities directly. Consequently, the MoJ will remain dependent on specialised private providers like Mitie to manage its complex prison maintenance and custody services, protecting Mitie's Business Services margins.
8.
Sensitive services: UK immigration and border security services remain politically sensitive and operationally complex for direct civil service delivery. Mitie Care & Custody operates Home Office detention facilities under hundreds of millions of pounds in active, long-term contracts. While local political leaders have called for ending asylum contracts early, the government has nowhere else to house foreign national offenders. Therefore, these high-security, vetted operations are highly likely to remain outsourced to ensure public safety, which supports Mitie's specialized business service margins.

Operational Rollout and

9.
Pilots Centralised coordination: The Prime Minister's Office established a dedicated Insourcing Unit in September 2026 to systematically drive departmental transitions. This new unit coordinates insourcing activities across all central government departments, unblocks delivery barriers, and identifies cross-government opportunities to maximize value. By building Civil Service talent and resources, the unit is specifically designed to reduce outsourced spending on professional and facilities services. This centralized pressure introduces a material threat to Mitie's re-procurement success rate after April 2027, risking long-term EPS contraction.
10.
Target pilots: The Cabinet Office has initiated the first pilot decisions to bring its own building management back in-house. Subject to a Public Interest Test, the department aims to transition cleaning and security contracts across 83 government buildings starting in 2028. Darren Jones, the Chief Secretary to the Prime Minister, stated that this pilot will rebuild national capacity to deliver services directly. Since the Cabinet Office is leading by example, other in-scope departments are highly likely to follow, which would directly impact Mitie's central government cleaning revenues.
11.
Global frameworks: The UK's interventionist shift aligns closely with similar international policy frameworks targeting public sector insourcing. For example, the Australian government is systematically rebuilding its internal public service capabilities, successfully reducing large consulting firm spending by $624 million. In contrast, the United States government maintains a balanced mix of public and private delivery, utilizing structured Capital Planning and Investment Control frameworks to manage risk. These global precedents suggest that while structural transitions are slow, they permanently reduce the addressable market size for major government outsourcers.

Commercial Impact and

12.
Earnings Negative gearing: The introduction of mandatory Public Interest Tests threatens to impose structural growth headwinds across the UK outsourcing sector. Deutsche Bank Research noted in June 2026 that the policy represents a clear contract renewal headwind for major outsourcers. Specifically, Mitie derives approximately 50% of its revenues from the UK public sector, exposing it to longterm pipeline conversion risks. If major departments bring high-volume cleaning and security contracts inhouse, Mitie's operating margins will decline due to negative route density gearing.
13.
Multiple contraction: The political shift toward insourcing threatens to depress equity valuation multiples across the entire facilities services industry. Broker assessments from Deutsche Bank in June 2026 suggest that the near-term impact of PPN 024 will manifest in compressed valuation multiples and fragile sector sentiment. This means that while existing contracts are safe, future contract pipeline conversion rates will become highly unpredictable. Unless Mitie can repeatedly demonstrate superior social value and cost benefits, its long-term organic revenue growth targets will remain under severe pressure.
14.
Other side of the story: The absolute scale and technological advantages of large private outsourcers are likely to limit the practical extent of central government insourcing. Public sector bodies facing persistent budget deficits and severe workforce shortages cannot easily replicate Mitie's technical efficiency and route density. Deutsche Bank Research argued in June 2026 that the policy reinforces the critical importance of demonstrating social value and clear cost benefits from outsourced models. Therefore, we judge that Mitie will successfully leverage its sector-leading scale to retain its most profitable contracts and defend its earnings per share.

  3. Multi-year contract extensions and technical complexity provide a defensive shield against immediate in-housing threats across several sensitive departments.

Divisional Public Sector Exposure

1.
Massive divisional exposure: Mitie Group relies heavily on public sector contracts to drive its organic revenue growth and support its operating margin targets. In the fiscal year ended 31 March 2026, the company generated £2,794.60m from government contracts out of £5,618.60m total revenue. This revenue base represents exactly 49.74% of the group's total organic sales. Any negative policy changes under the Public Interest Test will directly impact these high-volume public revenues.

Business services division: Mitie's Business Services division generated £1,259.20m in government revenue during the fiscal year 2026. This division manages technology-led security, hygiene, landscapes, and facilities compliance contracts across approximately 2,500 public and private accounts. Although non-government revenues are larger at £1,725.90m, public contracts still account for 42.18% of total divisional sales. This means any central government insourcing of cleaning and security will materially pressure division margins.

Technical services exposure: The Technical Services division is even more exposed to public sector procurement than Business Services. In fiscal year 2026, Technical Services generated £1,535.40m from government clients out of £2,633.50m total revenue. Because public sector work makes up 58.30% of Technical Services, this division faces significant structural headwinds under the new rules.

Section 4 – Precedents of UK Public Insourcing Since 2018

Section summary
  1. Historical precedents since 2018 demonstrate that full public sector insourcing is typically slow, operationally complex, and frequently triggered by private sector performance failures.
  2. Large-scale transitions like the DWP Teleperformance contract show that high-volume administrative services face the greatest risk of complete in-housing to drive absolute departmental savings.
  3. Phased pilots and multiyear contract migration periods suggest that any resulting revenue erosion for major outsourcers like Mitie will materialize gradually over a three-to-five-year horizon.

Precedent Research Parameters

1.
Determined precedents: During this research, we analyzed 5 distinct public sector insourcing precedents across 4 major organizations, utilising 12 specific documents to evaluate historical outcomes. The assessment covers Transport for London, the Department for Work and Pensions, Capita, and NHS soft facilities management. No comparable precedents were left out of this analysis as all identified historical public insourcing cases have been fully incorporated to construct a baseline for Mitie. This historical evidence shows that complete contract transitions are rare and often driven by performance failures.

TfL Cleaning Pilot Precedent

2.
Gradual transition: Transport for London initiated a 12-month pilot on 28 September 2026 to evaluate the feasibility of insourcing its cleaning services. The pilot involves seconding around 250 cleaning staff across selected stations, representing a fraction of the total workforce. This phased testing is designed to help TfL build internal facilities management capabilities while assessing the associated costs. Consequently, this transition represents a controlled test rather than an immediate full contract termination.
3.
Substantial contract: Mitie secured a massive five-year cleaning contract from Transport for London in January 2026 valued at approximately £100m annually. The union originally demanded that TfL suspend the procurement of this £700m contract to bring the services back in-house. However, TfL proceeded with the private outsource award due to value for money considerations and its own lack of specialist capabilities. This outcome demonstrates that public bodies remain highly dependent on private sector expertise to manage large networks.
4.
Contrary evidence: TfL's parallel pilot scheme demonstrates that municipal authorities are actively building the internal capacity required to bypass private providers. If the 12-month trial proves successful, TfL plans to utilize contract break clauses to bring more cleaning services in-house. This political drive is backed by the Green Party and trade unions demanding equal terms for all transit workers. This means Mitie faces a realistic mid-term threat where a highly profitable transport contract could be partially or fully decommissioned.

DWP Universal Credit Checking Insourcing

5.
Major insourcing: The Department for Work and Pensions announced in October 2026 that it is insourcing its Universal Credit Review work. This decision will transfer approximately 2,700 private employees directly into the civil service. The work was previously managed under a massive £368m private contract signed in 2024. This complete transition represents a significant direct loss of outsourced revenue for the private business services provider.
6.
Transition timelines: The transition of 2,700 staff from Teleperformance to the DWP represents one of the largest immediate staffing transfers since 2018. This transfer shows that complete transition of core services is highly dependent on civil service readiness. However, union disputes over value for money highlight that these transitions can create administrative backlogs during the handback phase. Consequently, outsourcers face immediate volume loss but may benefit from temporary transitional support agreements.
7.
Financial logic: The DWP decided to transition the Universal Credit checking services in-house to achieve wider administrative efficiencies. The department is targeting £17.3 billion in welfare savings by March 2031 through reduced fraud and error. While the union argued the outsourced model failed to deliver taxpayer value, the government maintained the decision was strategic and not performance-related. This baseline proves that governments will willingly insource high-volume contracts to meet absolute fiscal savings targets.

Capita Portfolio Contract Losses

8.
Reputational spillover: Capita suffered severe reputational and financial damage due to poor operational delivery on its Civil Service Pension Scheme contract. The seven-year contract, valued at £239m, suffered from a backlog of 24,000 quotations and 1,500 member complaints. This operational failure prompted the Cabinet Office to actively plan a long-term strategy to move the scheme back in-house. These delivery issues created a major profit headwind of £25m to £40m for Capita in fiscal year 2026.
9.
Contract cancellations: The UK Government terminated Capita's smaller Royal Mail pension contract in April 2026 after the company failed to hit critical IT milestones. Furthermore, the government decided to insource its Learning Frameworks 2.0 contract, which Capita had expected to win. This lost bid resulted in Capita lowering its Public Service division revenue growth guidance to flat for the fiscal year. This trend illustrates how poor performance on one contract can cause wider reputational damage and lost opportunities.
10.
Municipal retreats: Historical precedents from 2018 show that several local authorities terminated strategic business process partnerships with Capita early. Birmingham City Council dissolved its Service Birmingham venture to bring IT services back in-house, representing £50m to £60m in annual turnover. Similarly, Southampton City Council exited its contract early, removing approximately £20m in annual revenues from Capita's order book. These historical reversals confirm that ideological shifts and budget pressures can dismantle large municipal contracts over multi-year periods.

NHS Trusts and Soft FM Reviews

11.
Trust evaluations: Healthcare providers are increasingly evaluating the financial viability of insourcing soft facilities management contracts. Homerton University Hospital NHS Foundation Trust initiated legal reviews regarding its current ISS contract to assess cost-effective insourcing options. NHS trusts are becoming increasingly cost-conscious due to severe resource constraints, prompting a rise in clinical insourcing suppliers. This rising focus on internal delivery means private soft FM providers must continuously demonstrate superior value to protect their margins.
12.
Historical friction: Private facilities contractors face growing labor friction as unions demand alignment with NHS terms and conditions. Reforms introduced to prevent two-tier workforces in public outsourcing will mandate no less favourable treatment for workers from October 2026. This regulatory change has significant cost implications, particularly for pensions and equal pay risks during contract bids. Consequently, these workforce rules will compress the operating profit margins of facilities providers unless higher costs are fully recovered.

Baselines and Margin Impacts

13.
Baseline metrics: Historical precedents indicate that insourcing transitions typically require between 12 months to 5 years to execute. For example, Capita's closed-book Life and Pensions contract hand-backs required a planned five-year migration period ending in 2031. The ultimate cost to the outsourcer manifests in flat to declining division revenues and degraded operating cash conversion. These dynamics suggest that Mitie's potential exposure will develop over a multi-year horizon rather than hitting earnings immediately.
14.
Other side of the story: While political rhetoric emphasizes a massive return to in-house services, the practical scale of public insourcing remains constrained by severe resource limitations. Most public bodies lack the specialized capabilities, digital platforms, and workforce density to manage complex operations directly. Indeed, historical local government insourcing of IT or cleaning has faced delays in taking back work, which diluted the immediate impact on private providers' revenues. Therefore, the private sector will continue to defend its core earnings by offering unmatched scale and cost efficiency.
