Section 1 – Brief company description and history

Section summary
  1. Market attention was dominated by the upcoming separate listing of BioMar as a major valuation catalyst.
  2. Operational discussions focused on restructuring initiatives across weaker segments to offset persistent European demand headwinds.
  3. Peer comparisons highlighted how analysts favored margin-led corporate separations and integrations over complex conglomerate structures.
1.
Long industrial heritage: Founded in 1878, Schouw & Co. evolved from a packaging company into one of Denmark's largest industrial conglomerates, listing on the Copenhagen Stock Exchange in 1954.

Core aquaculture segment: BioMar Group, acquired in 2005, is the largest portfolio business and the world's third-largest provider of high-quality feed solutions for fish and shrimp farming.

Advanced materials division: Fibertex Personal Care and Fibertex Nonwovens, acquired in 2002, manufacture spunmelt nonwovens for hygiene and specialized nonwovens for industrial applications.

Stable financial performance: Standing in contrast to volatile peers, the group leveraged its diversified business-to-business model to deliver resilient operational profit and cash flow over the 2023- 2025 period.

Resilient share price: In the period around the Q1 2026 release, Schouw & Co.'s share price remained highly resilient, ending the quarter at DKK 661 on May 1, 2026, compared to DKK 614 in the same period last year.

Historical broker optimism: Prior to the coverage suspension, DNB Carnegie analysts Emil Haargaard and Lars Topholm had reiterated their positive outlook, highlighting double-digit earnings per share growth potential driven by BioMar's margin expansion.

Section 2 – Sell-side and market focus prior to these recent earnings

Section summary
  1. The successful listing of BioMar and the acquisition of Spectre represent transformational portfolio shifts that unlock shareholder value.
  2. Leadership transitions at parent and segment levels establish a capable senior management foundation for long-term growth.
  3. Capital allocation is increasingly focused on disciplined organic expansion and strategic bolt-on acquisitions.
  4. Persistent external pressures from Chinese imports and macro volatility require constant operational vigilance to safeguard margins.

BioMar IPO and Valuation Unlock

1.
Anticipated listing timeline: Sell-side interest centered heavily on the separate stock exchange listing of BioMar Group on Nasdaq Copenhagen, which DNB Carnegie analyst Emil Haargaard expected could take place in first half of 2026 to unlock hidden conglomerate value.
2.
Strategic scale expansion: Analysts closely tracked BioMar's potential consolidation plays, with DNB Carnegie's Haargaard highlighting the outcome of Mowi's strategic feed review as a secondary near-term catalyst that could present a compelling acquisition opportunity for BioMar to enhance its market scale.
3.
Margin sustainability focus: The core debate on BioMar's stand-alone investment case remained its ability to sustain operating margins over the long term, which DNB Carnegie's Haargaard flagged as a critical factor for securing premium valuation multiples during the carve-out process.
4.
Valuation discount concerns: Conglomerate structures historically faced skepticism from investors, and DNB Carnegie's Haargaard argued that the market would highly appreciate a more active exit strategy to dismantle an unwarranted conglomerate valuation discount of 21% relative to pure-play peers.
5.
Robust feed volume delivery: From a corporate perspective, BioMar's operational focus going into 2026 was maintaining its strong volume-led growth strategy after delivering a 14% increase in volumes to 382,000 tonnes in previous periods.

Segment Recovery and Footprint Optimization

6.
GPV integration challenges: Sell-side expectations for GPV Group revolved around the ongoing operational integration of Enics, with DNB Carnegie's Haargaard focusing on whether GPV could improve Enics' lower profitability levels to historical group averages.
7.
GPV order book recovery: Despite soft global demand, GPV sought to reassure the market with a gradual recovery in order intake while executing site consolidations across its manufacturing footprint.
8.
Borg Refine4Future program: Borg Automotive's profitability collapse in 2025 led management to establish the 'Refine4Future' strategy, a major transformation program intended to safeguard and optimize logistics footprint and manufacturing facilities by 2027.
9.
UK manufacturing closure: Borg's footprint adjustment was highlighted by the board's decision to close its UK operations by April 2026, ceasing all domestic manufacturing and research activities to transfer steering product lines to Poland.
10.
Chinese competitive pricing: The market remained highly cautious about Borg's automotive aftermarket recovery due to intense competitive pricing from China on key products like brake calipers, which DNB Carnegie's Haargaard expected to persist well into 2026.
11.
Fibertex overcapacity headwinds: In the advanced materials segment, Fibertex Personal Care experienced severe pricing pressure in Asia, where significant overcapacity of spunmelt nonwovens in China depressed regional selling prices below historical sustainable levels.
12.
Fibertex US startup costs: Investors monitored the operational startup of Fibertex Nonwovens' new formulation line in the United States, which had previously suffered profitability pressure from hurricane disruptions and lower initial volumes.

Macro Pressures and Peer Comparison

13.
Pass-through cost dynamics: The sell-side debated Schouw's resilience to fluctuating input costs, noting that while BioMar and Fibertex Personal Care utilized automatic raw material pass-through mechanisms , energy cost fluctuations remained harder to pass directly to customers.
14.

SKF transition cost risks: In contrast, Redburn's analyst team downgraded SKF due to fears of short-term margins missing from separation dyssynergies and stranded logistics costs that consensus had under-communicated.
16.
Coloplast strategic integration: For healthcare peer Coloplast, the primary analytical debate focused on the integration of its acquired Kerecis business, which DNB Carnegie's analyst team flagged as reducing earnings stability and visibility following weak margin delivery.
17.
Coloplast margin recovery path: Alternatively, Jefferies analysts took a more constructive stance on Coloplast's outlook, arguing that normalizing input costs and cost discipline would support clear margin recovery and top-line acceleration.

Section 3 – Key moving parts and changes

1.
Successful CFO transition: Parent company Schouw & Co. executed a smooth transition by appointing Thomas Hansen to take over as Chief Financial Officer on April 1, 2024, following the retirement of Erik Weimar Rasmussen who served for over 27 years. Hansen, who brings 18 years of wind manufacturing experience, took over the parent group's finance function to lead the next phase of capital control.
2.
Executive board consolidation: Under a planned transition, Executive Vice President Peter Kjær stepped down from the Executive Board on August 15, 2025, ahead of his scheduled retirement in 2026. Following this change, President and Chief Executive Officer Jens Bjerg Sørensen became the sole member of the Executive Board to streamline corporate decision-making.
3.
Segment leadership renewals: To drive future operational recoveries, Borg Automotive appointed Jesper Møberg as Chief Executive Officer in 2025, while Spectre welcomed Lars Christensen as Chief Executive Officer. These appointments highlight Schouw & Co.'s approach to placing highly autonomous leadership teams at the helm of its core businesses.
4.
Transformational BioMar listing: On May 28, 2026, Schouw & Co. successfully completed the Initial Public Offering of BioMar Group on Nasdaq Copenhagen at a price of DKK 108 per share. This milestone transaction raised DKK 2.7 billion in gross proceeds and represents a textbook value unlocking catalyst for Schouw & Co. shareholders.
5.
Strategic platform acquisition: Immediately following the IPO, Schouw & Co. expanded its portfolio by signing an agreement in July 2026 to acquire a 75% stake in technical outdoor apparel manufacturer Spectre. The transaction represents an upfront enterprise value of DKK 1.1 billion, acting as a new platform for strategic expansion into premium niches.
6.
Stable mid-term margin targets: During the October 2026 Capital Markets Day, Schouw & Co. introduced its "Expand 2030" strategy, targeting a consolidated group EBITDA margin of around 10% by 2030. This goal is supported by segment-level ambitions, such as BioMar targeting an EBITDA margin of 7% to 9% and GPV targeting 10%.
7.
Significant leverage reduction: Following the receipt of BioMar listing proceeds, Schouw & Co. successfully lowered its financial gearing ratio (NIBD/EBITDA) from 1.9 down to a highly comfortable 0.7x EBITDA as of June 30, 2026. This provides the group with substantial financial flexibility to execute its longterm investment goals.
8.
Increased share buybacks: Underpinning its commitment to capital distribution, the Board of Directors increased its 2026 share buy-back programme by DKK 170 million in August 2026. This adjustment brings the total potential share buyback amount to DKK 410 million, demonstrating highly disciplined capital allocation.
9.
Sustained dividend growth: Reflecting its core policy of stable or rising returns, Schouw & Co. increased its dividend for the 2025 financial year by DKK 1 to DKK 17 per share. This continues a 25-year upward trend in distributions, where total dividends paid have more than 30-doubled.
10.
Strong feed volume momentum: Driven by rapid growth in its Ecuadorian shrimp business, BioMar lifted its full-year 2026 revenue expectations by DKK 1 billion to a range of DKK 17 to 18 billion. Volume trends remain highly supportive as the Norwegian salmon market also begins to normalize.
11.
Sluggish automotive demand: Borg Automotive continues to struggle with weak aftermarket conditions, leading to a H1 2026 revenue contraction of 9% compared to the same period last year. Despite lower sales, the implementation of the "Refine 4 Future" recovery plan helped lift EBITDA to DKK 38 million in Q2 2026.
12.
Fibertex demand recovery: Demand for specialized nonwovens showed significant improvement, with Fibertex Nonwovens delivering a 20% revenue increase to DKK 698 million in Q2 2026. This recovery was primarily supported by strong volume growth in the US wipes segment and European automotive markets.
13.
Aggressive Chinese competition: Borg Automotive's profitability remains heavily pressured by intense competitive pricing from China in key product categories like brake calipers. This trend requires ongoing footprint optimization and cost-cutting initiatives under the Refine 4 Future strategy.
14.
Biological and climate risks: BioMar remains exposed to biological volatility in fish farming, which was illustrated by severe biological headwinds in Norway during late 2025. DNB Carnegie analyst Emil Haargaard noted that such biological disruptions pose a persistent risk to feed volumes.
15.
Elevated macroeconomic uncertainty: Global operations are increasingly threatened by geopolitical tensions, including potential disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz that could trigger severe energy and raw materials shortages. Consequently, management maintains a cautious approach by prioritizing regional footprint diversification.
16.
Other side of the story: While separate listings like the BioMar IPO are structured to unlock substantial valuation upside, they risk diluting the defensive cash flows that have historically insulated the parent company during cyclical downturns. Furthermore, the addition of new platform businesses like Spectre could increase operational integration risks.

Section 7 – Changes in leadership

Section summary
  1. Parent and segment leadership transitions have established a streamlined governance structure to execute long-term strategic recoveries despite rising organizational restructuring demands.
  2. Schouw & Co. executed a transformational portfolio restructure through the separate listing of BioMar and disciplined platform acquisitions.
  3. Broad-based footprint optimization projects are consolidating manufacturing across core industrial segments to protect long-term margins.
1.
Executive board streamlining: Parent company Schouw & Co. executed a planned leadership transition as Executive Vice President Peter Kjær stepped down from the Executive Board on August 15, 2025, ahead of his formal retirement in 2026. This structural adjustment left President and CEO Jens Bjerg Sørensen as the sole member of the Executive Board to accelerate corporate decision-making and enhance group agility.
2.
Borg segment CEO transition: To spearhead its commercial and operational turnaround, Borg Automotive initiated a major generational change by appointing Jesper Møberg as Chief Executive Officer in 2025 to replace Kim Kruse Andersen. Having served as Borg's Chief Financial Officer for 15 years, Møberg brings deep internal operational expertise to drive the group's "Refine4Future" recovery plan.
3.
Borg executive team expansion: Alongside the CEO transition, Borg Automotive promoted Ernst Kildegaard to Executive Vice President to strengthen the senior leadership team during its footprint transition. This reinforced management layer is directly tasked with addressing sluggish demand and rising production costs across European aftermarket operations.
4.
Segment financial governance intervention: Operational management at Borg Automotive intervened directly in its French legal entity during 2025 after transferring its bookkeeping to the group's shared service centre. Segment leaders identified major accounting discrepancies, including previously unaccounted customer bonuses and non-agreed invoices, which forced corrective actions to align the subsidiary with strict group governance standards.
5.
GPV organizational rightsizing: To optimize its factory network, GPV Group's leadership, led by long-serving CEO Bo Lybæk since 2008, executed a significant white-collar workforce reduction across select group functions. This restructuring included closing offices across Europe and dismantling the de facto Enics headquarters to build a highly efficient organizational model.
6.
Outstanding BioMar employee engagement: BioMar Group's management demonstrated strong cultural health, achieving a remarkable Employee Net Promoter Score of 56 in 2025, up from 51 in 2024. Based on an impressive 94% participation rate, this score positioned the aquafeed specialist in the top 5% of the manufacturing industry benchmark.
7.
Other side of the story: While long-term leadership stability supports strategic continuity, DNB Carnegie analyst Emil Haargaard cautioned that the conglomerate has a high level of management dependence on CEO Jens Bjerg Sørensen, who has steered the group's active ownership and M&A agenda for over 25 years.

Section 9 – Change in strategic priorities, ambition and goals

Section summary
  1. Schouw & Co. raised its long-term operational intensity by introducing the Expand 2030 strategy, targeting consolidated EBITDA of DKK 4.5 billion through disciplined active ownership.
  2. Management has shifted its focus from volume-driven expansion to margin-led capital efficiency and cash discipline to restore historical returns.
  3. Despite elevated sustainability targets, the group faces sluggish progress in gender diversity and rising environmental processing risks.
  4. Schouw & Co. faces divergent demand cycles across its businesses as solid aquaculture and hydraulic volumes contrast with sluggish automotive and electronics demand.
  5. Structural growth in global protein and wind energy supports strong order book expansion for the group's leading business units.
  6. Persistent Chinese export competition and customer destocking continue to depress aftermarket pricing power in secondary divisions.
1.
Ambition expansion under Expand 2030: During the October 2026 Capital Markets Day, Schouw & Co. launched its "Expand 2030" strategy to drive long-term value. This new strategy establishes an ambitious target of reaching consolidated EBITDA of DKK 4.5 billion by 2030.
2.

Schouw & Co's Q1 2026 results and the subsequent successful Initial Public Offering of BioMar Group on May 28, 2026, at DKK 108 per share, raising DKK 2.7 billion in gross proceeds, represent a textbook value unlocking catalyst for shareholders that fundamentally transformed the 18-month to 3-year EPS outlook. The single most critical driver reshaping Schouw's long-term earnings trajectory is the structural portfolio transformation executed through the BioMar carve-out combined with disciplined capital redeployment. Following the receipt of BioMar listing proceeds, Schouw & Co. successfully lowered its financial gearing ratio from 1.9 down to a highly comfortable 0.7x EBITDA as of June 30, 2026, providing unprecedented financial flexibility.

The group immediately capitalized on this by signing an agreement in July 2026 to acquire a 75% stake in technical outdoor apparel manufacturer Spectre for an upfront enterprise value of DKK 1.1 billion, acting as a new platform for strategic expansion into premium niches. This demonstrates management's ability to rapidly redeploy capital into accretive growth opportunities rather than simply returning cash to shareholders, a critical factor that separates Schouw's strategic execution from passive conglomerate peers.

The market's 18-month to 3-year outlook has been fundamentally reset by three interconnected developments. First, Schouw & Co. launched its "Expand 2030" strategy during the October 2026 Capital Markets Day, establishing an ambitious target of reaching consolidated EBITDA of DKK 4.5 billion by 2030, representing a 55% increase from the 2025 baseline of approximately DKK 2.9 billion. Third, management shifted from volume-driven expansion to margin-led growth and reinforced capital discipline, reconfirming a strict target of achieving ROIC of 15% in 2030, fundamentally altering the quality and sustainability of future earnings. This optionality is particularly valuable given Bernstein Research's analysis that battery electric vehicles require structurally less maintenance generating up to 50% lower replacement parts revenue than legacy diesel variants, though this headwind is expected to materialize slowly over a 12-year cycle, providing a defined window for Schouw to either fix or exit Borg before structural obsolescence crystallizes. forward, two high-priority questions for future disclosures that would materially impact the investment thesis are: (1) What specific contractual mechanisms and exclusivity provisions does BioMar maintain with its top 10 salmon and shrimp farming customers to prevent replication of the Mowi-Skretting integrated partnership model, and what percentage of BioMar's 2030 volume target depends on customers who could credibly establish internal feed production capabilities? This question directly addresses the structural disintermediation risk that management under-communicated during Q1 discussions. (2) What is the detailed cash flow profile and ROIC trajectory of the Spectre acquisition over the 2026-2030 period, including any earnout structures tied to the remaining 25% ownership stake, and how does Schouw plan to balance organic capex requirements at GPV, HydraSpecma, and Fibertex Nonwovens against additional platform acquisitions given its stated 1-2 new platform investment target?

This question forces transparency on capital allocation discipline and tests whether management can truly compound shareholder value through M&A or will dilute returns by overpaying for growth. In synthesizing the Q1 results, the fundamental shift in Schouw's 18-month to 3-year outlook centers on a deliberate strategic evolution from passive conglomerate holding company to active portfolio architect. The group aims to achieve average organic revenue growth of 5-7% per year against the 2025 baseline, supported by plans to add 1-2 new platform investments and execute 1-2 structural changes by 2030.

The Q1 results provided validation that management possesses the operational discipline to protect margins during transitional periods, as evidenced by ROIC excluding goodwill improving by 0.9 percentage points to 13.4% in the first quarter, up from 12.5% in Q1 2025. However, the path to achieving the ambitious DKK 4.5 billion EBITDA target by 2030 remains highly execution-dependent and vulnerable to external shocks across at least three of the six operating segments, creating a binary risk-reward profile where successful execution could drive 40-50% upside to current valuations while operational stumbles could trigger renewed conglomerate discount compression toward historical 20-25% levels below sum-of-the-parts fair value.
