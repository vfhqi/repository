Evolution (EVO-SE): what the third-quarter 2026 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case modestly on its own tests: the headline improved, but two of the case's four park triggers now sit at or across their lines, and the share price has moved further from the entry the case described.

1. What changed

The case assumed four consecutive quarters of falling revenue, both big regions declining, and a 66% margin floor still to be proven. Reported on 23 October 2026:

  • Revenue returned to growth. Third-quarter 2026 net revenue was €526.13 million, up 3.7% year on year, in line with consensus of €526.10 million. Live casino was €443.04 million, up 2.6%, 0.9% short of consensus. Slots were €83.09 million, up 10.1%, 5.0% ahead and a second consecutive double-digit quarter.
  • Margin slipped. Adjusted EBITDA was €345.93 million at a 65.75% margin, against 66.43% a year earlier and 65.86% in the second quarter. Year to date is 65.7%; the full-year target of approximately 66.1% was maintained.
  • Per-share growth came from share count again. Profit for the period fell 3.6% to €243.30 million, with €12.10 million of one-off costs; basic earnings per share rose 4.6% to €1.31. Diluted earnings per share of €1.29 missed consensus of €1.34.
  • Regions. By player location, Asia was €192.10 million, up 1.6% year on year and slightly up on the quarter. Europe was €173.70 million, down 4.7% year on year and roughly flat on the quarter; by customer location Europe missed consensus by 10.7%. Latin America was €49.00 million, up 23.1%. Regulated markets were 49% of revenue.
  • Volume ran far ahead of revenue. The company's game rounds index rose 22.5% year on year and 11.3% on the quarter. Revenue rose 3.7% and 1.6%.
  • Brazil. The local studio closed on 5 October 2026 after a federal ban by provisional measure. Management puts Brazil at well below half of Latin American revenue and calls the outcome highly unclear.
  • Buyback. 11.78 million shares were bought for €823.92 million in the quarter, against operating cash flow of €366.07 million. Cash was €656.41 million at 30 September 2026. 16.17 million shares were cancelled in late September. About €1.32 billion of the €2 billion programme was deployed by early November. Management signalled a more moderate pace, funded from cash flow.
  • Sell-side. Targets rose on share count (Morgan Stanley SEK 780 from SEK 740; Jefferies SEK 780 from SEK 630; DNB Carnegie SEK 1,000 unchanged; Redburn SEK 590 from SEK 585). Operating estimates fell on Brazil: Redeye cut 2027 and 2028 revenue and EBITDA by 4% to 5%; Redburn cut 2028 diluted earnings per share by 6% to €6.32; Morgan Stanley cut earnings per share 2% to 3% on fewer assumed repurchases.

2. The challenge

Reading one: game rounds have stopped measuring demand. The case treats the gap between record rounds and flat revenue as its load-bearing tension and allows two explanations: a temporary lag, or leakage to unlicensed mirrors. These results offer a third that the case does not hold. Morgan Stanley attributes recent player growth mainly to low-staking game shows such as Ice Fishing, which dilute revenue per user. On our arithmetic, revenue per game round fell about 9% in the quarter and about 15% year on year. Taking the case's own index figures for the first half with reported revenue, this crude measure has now fallen three quarters running, which is the case's park trigger on its face. A lag is the least likely explanation, because a lag narrows and this gap widened sharply. For the mix reading to be right, classic-table monetisation must be steady while game shows add cheap rounds on top; DNB Carnegie's tracker showing Baccarat and Roulette back to year-on-year growth is consistent with that. If so, the reading cuts two ways. The trigger misfires, because falling revenue per round is arithmetic and not impairment. But the demand evidence is also weaker than the case scored it, because volume no longer forecasts revenue, and the share price recovery has tracked volume.

Reading two: regulated revenue is not the safe leg. The case's one departure from the bear analysis was that the regulated-mix shift is real compliance progress. Brazil shows a licensed, regulated market removed by decree within days, in the region supplying the fastest growth. The regulated share also stood at 49%, no higher than the level the case recorded a quarter earlier. For this reading to be right, Brazil has to be a pattern and not an accident: other newly regulated markets would need to prove equally reversible, or the rest of Latin America would need to slow. If Brazil stays isolated and the region offsets it, as management expects, the reading fails.

Reading three, cutting the other way: the inflection is real and the case is too cautious. Every region grew on the quarter, Asia stabilised, classic tables recovered and slots repeated. That is the organic inflection the case's hold-and-watch stance waits for. It requires the growth to be organic. Redburn's note attributes the in-line quarter to currency tailwinds, and the evidence gives no constant-currency figure. Reported live growth of 2.6% is far from the case's conviction-raising test of constant-currency live growth above 10%.

A sharpening of what the case already holds. The false-friend signature repeated, and the buyback was probably at its fastest in this quarter: spend ran at more than twice operating cash flow, at an average price about 19% above the second-quarter average the case recorded. Each euro now retires fewer shares, so per-share growth leans more on operating profit from here.

4. What would settle it

  • Revenue per round by product. This separates mix from leakage. The company does not disclose it and has not committed to. The nearest substitutes are broker trackers split between classic tables and game shows. This is an open question that nothing scheduled answers.
  • The fourth-quarter report, whose date the evidence does not give. It settles the margin trigger and the guidance test, shows the first quarter without Brazil, and shows whether the regulated share falls.
  • Constant-currency live growth. Not in the evidence; needed to test reading three.
  • Brazil. No date exists for a resolution.
  • Game Night, due late 2026: the first test of whether the large game shows lift revenue as well as rounds.
  • The second review, four working days on. It is likely to carry settled consensus estimates and targets. Broker changes to 14 November 2026 are already in hand, so it is unlikely to add much. It carries no new company disclosure and settles none of the points above.
