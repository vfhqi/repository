Section 2 – Spanish Corporate Income Tax Framework and Loss Limitations

Section summary
  1. The elimination of time limits under Law 27/2014 allows Vodafone Spain to carry forward its €5bn NOLs indefinitely
  2. Stricter annual offset limits for large companies restrict how quickly these tax losses can be utilized
  3. The Constitutional Court's 2024 ruling on Royal Decree-Law 3/2016 has opened pathways for reclaiming historical tax credits

Overview of Spanish Corporate Tax Act

1.
Tax rate reductions: Spanish Law 27/2014 enacted on 27 November 2014 progressively reduced the general corporate income tax rate to 25% starting in 2016.
2.
Unlimited carryforward: The same Spanish Law 27/2014 removed the previous 18-year time limit, allowing net operating losses to be carried forward indefinitely without expiration.
3.
Offsetting caps: To balance this change, Spanish Law 27/2014 established clear caps on offsetting tax loss carryforwards at 70% for subsequent years.
4.
Stricter limits: For large companies, Spanish Law 27/2014 maintained a transitional limitation on offsetting tax losses of 25% in 2015.
5.
Constitutional Court ruling: On 18 January 2024, the Spanish Constitutional Court declared unconstitutional several corporate income tax measures introduced under Royal Decree-Law 3/2016.

Constitutional Court Ruling and Reclaims

6.
Reclaiming tax credits: Following this unconstitutionality, Telefónica reported on 27 February 2025 that it could once again have tax credits available.
7.
Rectification filings: Telefónica avoided local declaration limits by submitting letters of request for rectification for financial years 2016 onwards.
8.
Vodafone Spain losses: Across nine relevant sources covering three Spanish telecom companies and one comparable past case, Zegona Communications disclosed gross historical losses and tax credits of approximately €5.13bn in its interim financial statements published on 27 September 2024.
9.
Unrecognized deferred tax assets: Zegona Communications has left €1.23bn in deferred tax assets unrecognized due to lack of sufficient certainty.
10.
Comparable peer MasOrange: In its interim report dated 28 July 2026, Orange SA reported that MasOrange had €2.202bn in unrecognized deferred tax assets.

Utilization of NOLs and Peer Comparisons
