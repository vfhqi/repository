EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Sampo has completed its multi-year transformation into a highly focused, pure-play non-life P&C insurance champion through key strategic divestments and integrations, leveraging unmatched regional scale and digital direct distribution to drive predictable underwriting profit and agile, inflation-indexed repricing, while leadership succession has transitioned smoothly to highly experienced internal operators focused on executing run-rate synergies and tightening cost controls. The business model delivers consistent underwriting outperformance and combined ratio stability over the past decade, with operating earnings underpinned by high-quality underwriting results rather than volatile investment returns, enabling robust capital generation and flexible balance sheet leverage to sustain high shareholder distributions. The Nordic P&C insurance market represents a highly profitable NOK 400 billion premium pool characterized by extreme market concentration where the top four players hold up to 93 per cent of local markets, with competitor pricing behavior operating with exceptional discipline prioritizing underwriting margins and stable combined ratios over volume expansion, while minimum scale thresholds present formidable barriers to entry requiring massive investments in proprietary claims data and unified core IT platforms.

Strategic transformation and operational platform

1.
Sampo finalized its transition from a financial services conglomerate into a pure-play P&C insurer between 2020 and 2025, successfully completing its full exit from Nordea in April 2022 to substantially reduce balance sheet market risk, demerging Mandatum in October 2023 to optimize capital deployment efficiency, and consolidating its Nordic platform through the acquisition of remaining non-controlling interests in Topdanmark in late 2024, with Topdanmark legally merged with If P&C on 1 July 2025 creating one single Nordic operating platform.
2.
Operating activities are managed across four main customer segments to reflect distinct risk profiles and target customer bases, with the Private Nordic division serving 3.7 million households under brands like If and Topdanmark, the Private UK division through Hastings supporting over 4 million customer policies as of 2025, Commercial and Corporate markets split into distinct divisions with the Nordic Commercial segment focusing on SMEs including the recently acquired Oona Health business, and the Nordic Industrial segment operating as the leading insurer for large corporate clients with turnover exceeding SEK 500 million.
3.
The group's primary revenue driver remains If P&C's balanced presence across four highly consolidated Scandinavian countries, with Sweden and Finland acting as primary earnings engines supported by high-margin operations in Norway and consolidated Danish markets, with approximately 75 per cent of total group insurance revenue generated in the Nordics while the UK provides roughly 20 per cent, and If holding balanced shares across Finland at 25 per cent, Sweden at 30 per cent, and Norway at 27 per cent.
4.
Torbjörn Magnusson retired as Group CEO on September 30, 2025, after successfully executing the pure-play P&C strategy, with Morten Thorsrud appointed Group CEO on October 1, 2025, to ensure strategic continuity, and Thorsrud's operational background highly aligned with executing steady growth in underwriting profit across regional platforms.
5.
Underwriting profit grew consistently over the last three years, culminating in a strong EUR 1.485 billion result in 2025, with Sampo achieving 8 per cent like-for-like premium growth in fiscal year 2025 driven by Private Nordic retail lines, while underwriting discipline maintained the group combined ratio at an impressive 82.1 per cent for fiscal year 2025.

Sampo has shown resilient top-line premium growth throughout its non-life consolidation phase, with gross written premiums reaching EUR 10,738 million in fiscal year 2025 representing an 8 per cent reported growth rate against EUR 9,931 million in 2024, while net insurance revenue amounted to EUR 9,078 million in 2025, expanding 8 per cent year-on-year from EUR 8,386 million in fiscal year 2024.
7.
Underwriting profitability has been highly stable, consistently outperforming the broader European non-life sector, with Sampo recording an excellent group combined ratio of 83.60 per cent in 2025, improving 0.70 percentage points compared to 84.30 per cent recorded in 2024, and this performance matching Tryg's 80.30 per cent and outclassing Alm. Brand's 2025 targets.

Technical underwriting results have expanded to become the primary engine of group earnings, with underwriting profit growing 13 per cent to EUR 1,485 million in 2025 from EUR 1,316 million in fiscal year 2024, while net financial result grew to EUR 1,210 million in 2025 from EUR 636 million in 2024, though volatile equity mark-to-market movements drag on reported earnings during turbulent market periods.

Market structure and competitive positioning

12.
The total general P&C insurance market in the Nordic region represents approximately NOK 400 billion in premium volume, with strong economic prosperity in Scandinavia meaning residents have large, high-value assets requiring P&C coverage, and the region delivering structurally lower cost shares and stronger profitability than other European non-life markets.
13.
The Nordic P&C insurance industry has consolidated into a highly stable regional oligopoly, with the four largest insurance companies in each country controlling between 60 and 85 per cent of the market, and highly concentrated structures resulting in lower competition levels, exceptionally stable market shares, and lower combined ratios.
14.
Finland represents the most concentrated Nordic country, with the top five players controlling 93 per cent share, the top three non-life insurers commanding a massive 81.10 per cent combined share, Pohjola Insurance leading the Finnish P&C market at 32.60 per cent, and If P&C holding the second-largest position in Finland with a stable 21.80 per cent market share.
15.
The majority of Nordic non-life insurance participants prioritize underwriting profitability and combined ratios over volume expansion, with competitors matching peer rate hikes to offset claims inflation rather than discounting to aggressively capture market share, and shared pricing discipline allowing the industry to recover rapidly from unexpected weather or claims inflation events.
16.
Larger insurers exploit significant economies of scale to protect and expand underwriting margins over smaller players, with large scale allowing insurers to spread rising regulatory compliance and technology infrastructure costs over a broader premium base, and operating a unified cross-border IT structure generating massive operational synergies and improving cost ratios, exemplified by Tryg's platform consolidation post-RSA transaction targeting DKK 500 million in annual service result improvements.

Customer dynamics and retention economics

17.
Sampo maintains a highly stable retail and commercial customer base across the Nordics anchoring predictable underwriting cash flows, with premium retention remaining consistently above the 89 per cent target shielding the group from high customer acquisition costs, while bundling and automated digital indexation create powerful customer switching barriers though regulatory scrutiny of pricing is increasing.
18.
If P&C targets and consistently delivers a stable retail premium retention rate exceeding 89 per cent, with Private Nordic retention remaining resilient despite premium rate increases of 13 per cent in early 2026, and high retention levels allowing Sampo to avoid aggressive corrective pricing actions unlike volatile regional peers.
19.
Low market churn leads to materially lower customer acquisition costs for listed Nordic insurers, with Nordic customer acquisition cost ratios approximately 9 percentage points lower than European multi-line peers according to Morgan Stanley estimates, and high market concentration and rational pricing between listed peers preserving this cost advantage over long horizons.
20.
Nordic insurers benefit from annual index-linked price increases that do not require customer notification, with this automated mechanism leading to fewer customers reacting or shopping around during yearly renewals, though regulators highlighted that insurers earn greater margins on customers with over 10 years of tenure.
21.
The Danish Competition Authority is considering a market investigation into general P&C competition, with previous FCA pricing reviews proposing periodic automatic switching for long-retained retail customers, and regulatory rules potentially forcing insurers to make auto-renewal opt-in only.

Operator quality and foundation strength

22.
Morten Thorsrud's appointment ensures seamless operational continuity following Torbjörn Magnusson's successful completion of the pure-play P&C strategy, with prudent risk management maintaining exceptional underwriting discipline and conservative reserve building throughout volatile seasonal weather cycles, If P&C leveraging unmatched regional scale and proprietary claims data to drive systematic cost ratio improvements over sub-scale peers, and operational efficiency generating best-in-class combined ratio stability and highly predictable double-digit operating return on equity profiles.
23.
If improved its combined ratio from 89 per cent in 2012 to 81.30 per cent in 2021, consistently delivering underwriting results well ahead of its historical 85 per cent target, with management maintaining highly prudent reserving practices refusing to weaken technical reserves during high-margin quarters, and all employee incentive programs tied directly to group combined ratio performance.
24.
If delivered the lowest standard deviation of only 2 per cent over 21 quarters compared to Tryg at 3.20 per cent and Gjensidige at 4.70 per cent, with If and Topdanmark historically delivering much lower combined ratios than any other listed European peer, and this underwriting outperformance reinforced by high and stable premium retention rates exceeding 89 per cent.
25.
Entering the Nordic P&C market requires substantial regulatory capital reserves to comply with Solvency II directives with compliance requiring capital comfortably above 150 per cent of requirements, strict capital regulatory thresholds making greenfield entry costly and disincentivizing speculative capacity additions from foreign operators, while direct distribution models and established brands create insurmountable customer acquisition barriers for new competitors.
26.
Autonomous vehicles could pose a long-term structural challenge to traditional retail P&C premium volumes with improving accident frequencies from safer driving expected to offset rising vehicle repair severities long-term, though current electric vehicle data shows repair costs outweigh the benefits of lower accident frequencies, and advanced technology replacement costs keep average motor claims severity structurally elevated across the region.

Plausible problems to monitor

Hastings operates as a pure-play UK digital insurer generating 100 per cent of its customer policy base through price comparison websites, exposing Sampo to a structurally more price-sensitive and cyclical customer base compared to the consolidated Nordic retail foundation. Management acknowledges that short-term premium volatility remains normal for Hastings because if competitive price comparison pressures persist, achieving growth at target margins becomes highly challenging.

This stands in sharp contrast to If P&C's Nordic operations, which consistently deliver stable retail premium retention rates exceeding 89 per cent even during aggressive premium rate increases. The UK exposure introduces meaningful combined ratio volatility risk during soft pricing cycles, creating a structural earnings drag that Nordic peers like Tryg and Gjensidige avoid entirely through minimal UK exposure. [Cross-ref: Section 1, Section 5]

Net investment results remain highly sensitive to financial market swings despite a conservative profile, with the reported investment result growing to EUR 1,210 million in 2025 from EUR 636 million in 2024, while volatile equity mark-to-market movements drag on reported earnings during turbulent market periods. Although focus on non-life lines ensures earnings are driven by predictable underwriting profit rather than investment yields, the balance sheet still carries material investment exposure that introduces earnings volatility risk during periods of macroeconomic stress.

Demerging wealth management assets substantially reduced balance sheet market risk from equity volatility, but residual investment book fluctuations remain a non-trivial drag on reported EPS stability compared to underwriting-only earnings streams. This investment sensitivity underperforms the pure technical result focus that characterizes best-in-class non-life operators. [Cross-ref: Section 2]

The Danish Competition Authority is considering a market investigation into general P&C competition, with previous FCA pricing reviews proposing periodic automatic switching for long-retained retail customers and regulatory rules that could force insurers to make auto-renewal opt-in only. Nordic insurers benefit from annual index-linked price increases that do not require customer notification, leading to fewer customers reacting or shopping around during yearly renewals.

Regulators highlighted that insurers earn greater margins on customers with over 10 years of tenure. The regulatory intervention threat surrounding automatic renewals and price indexation practices represents a structural vulnerability to Sampo's high-margin retention economics. If Danish or broader Nordic antitrust authorities enforce mandatory explicit renewal notifications or ban index-linked automatic escalation, Sampo would face material customer churn acceleration and retention margin compression.

This risk vector is particularly acute given the oligopolistic market structures and pricing coordination dynamics that have historically underpinned Nordic P&C profitability. [Cross-ref: Section 5]

Downstream suppliers are increasingly owned by private equity firms, intensifying EBITDA-driven negotiation pressures on Sampo, while automotive physical damage claim severity remains elevated, driven by parts complexity and rising labor rates, with embedded vehicle technology forcing repair workshops to replace rather than repair damaged components and Advanced Driver Assistance Systems (ADAS) calibration growing to impact more than 25 per cent of claims.

Suncorp management estimates that insurance inflation runs 3 to 4 per cent higher than general CPI, with labor rates across automotive workshops remaining elevated and keeping physical repair costs sticky. This persistent claims severity inflation combined with consolidating supplier bargaining power creates a structural margin squeeze risk that could outpace Sampo's ability to implement offsetting premium rate increases, particularly if regulatory scrutiny on pricing intensifies concurrently.

The private equity ownership trend in repair networks fundamentally shifts value-chain power dynamics away from insurers, representing a multi-year headwind to claims procurement leverage that peers face equally but which threatens industry-wide combined ratio expansion. [Cross-ref: Section 6, Section 8]
