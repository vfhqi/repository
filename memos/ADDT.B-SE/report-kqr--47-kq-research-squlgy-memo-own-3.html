Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Delivering on the tilted H2 order book is critical for Addtech to reverse its H1 sales decline and meet full- year earnings expectations.

Defining the critical grid inquiry

1.
Critical inquiry: "Addtech AB (ADDT.B-SE), a Swedish decentralised acquirer and owner of more than 150 B2B technical-trading and niche-manufacturing companies in about 20 countries, reporting six business areas since 1 October 2025 (Automation, Electrification, Energy, Industry, Process, Safety), with a fiscal year ending 31 March. For Addtech AB (ADDT.B-SE), what is the evidence on deliveries and orders in its Energy business area for the six months from October 2026 to March 2027, given that the company reported Energy sales of SEK 2,089m for April to September 2026 against SEK 2,156m a year earlier and said its order book was tilted towards the second half of the fiscal year? Specifically: what has Svenska Kraftn\u00e4t actually invested in 2026 against its plan (it has stated a SEK 225bn investment plan for 2026 to 2035), which transmission and distribution projects in Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland and the United Kingdom have received or been refused permits or been delayed since July 2026, what are regional grid operators and contractors saying about equipment orders, lead times and contractor capacity, and what are wind-turbine manufacturers such as Vestas (reported by DNB Carnegie as the Energy business area's largest customer) saying about European installation volumes and supplier pricing for 2027? Which Addtech Energy subsidiaries are named in tenders, framework agreements or customer announcements in this period, and what do brokers expect for Energy's organic growth and margin in the quarters ended 31 December 2026 and 31 March 2027? If these deliveries are successfully executed, it will significantly boost the group's full-year earnings per share.

Section 2 – Transmission Grid Investment and Project Permits

Section summary
  1. Actual Nordic grid investments in 2026 show mixed progress with Svenska Kraftnät and Statnett expanding spend while Fingrid experiences near-term capex declines.
  2. Permitting remains the primary bottleneck for transmission projects across Europe despite recent legislative actions to fast-track grid connections.

Nordic grid investment trajectories

1.
Substantial expansion: Svenska Kraftnät is accelerating its grid infrastructure deployment as part of its long-term investment plan of SEK 225bn spanning 2026 to 2035. To meet this ten-year plan, the public utility expects annual project investments to surge from SEK 9bn in 2025 to SEK 20bn per year in both 2026 and 2027. This massive ramp-up is designed to address domestic capacity constraints and will directly support sales growth within Addtech's high-voltage energy subsidiaries.
2.
Accelerated spending: Statnett has reported a significant step-up in its grid and electricity system capex during the first half of the year. The Norwegian state-owned operator invested NOK 6.4bn in the first half of 2026, representing an increase of NOK 1.7bn compared to the same period in 2025. This accelerated capital expenditure will boost high-voltage equipment demand and should positively impact Addtech's regional component orders.
3.
Muted execution: Fingrid has reported a slight year-on-year decline in its capital deployment despite maintaining its extensive long-term spending plans. The Finnish transmission system operator recorded gross capital expenditure of EUR 313m for the nine months ended September 2026, which is lower than the EUR 342m reported in the prior year period. This near-term contraction shows that project delays can temporarily slow down delivery of grid components and delay related supplier revenues.
4.
Utility reinvestments: Vattenfall is implementing a massive investment plan that allocates substantial resources toward reinforcing local and regional networks in Sweden. In February 2026, the utility set out details of its SEK 165bn group investment program for the 2026 to 2030 period. Within this budget, Vattenfall has allocated SEK 47bn specifically to electricity networks in Sweden, with 70% directed to replacement and maintenance capital expenditure. Regulatory uncertainties: E.ON is executing a substantial network capital program in Sweden despite facing limited protection against macroeconomic volatility. The operator is investing SEK 27bn into the country's grid infrastructure over the 2024 to 2027 regulatory period. However, the current regulatory WACC of 4.53% offers limited protection against market changes and may restrict future investment appetite.

Regulatory permitting decisions and bottlenecks

5.
Severe rejections: Swedish offshore wind projects faced a major setback when the national government issued sweeping project denials. On July 16, 2026, the state rejected eleven offshore wind applications, including Eolus's Västvind and Najaderna projects, citing critical national security concerns. This decision restricts the near-term pipeline for sea-based turbine installations and dampens component demand expectations for Nordic electrical equipment suppliers.
6.
Strict rationing: Denmark has enacted emergency legislation to manage grid capacity limits as connection requests overwhelm existing infrastructure. Climate and Utilities Minister Samira Nawa presented the emergency grid bill to parliament on August 20, 2026, following a consultation with stakeholders. This emergency legislation is designed to ration limited grid capacity by prioritizing connections that support the green transition.
7.
Policy interventions: Finland is preparing to restructure its grid capacity allocation rules to address the severe network bottlenecks resulting from surging electrification. The Finnish economy ministry announced in late August 2026 that it had initiated legislative changes to resolve current connection queues. These regulatory changes are designed to streamline grid connection procedures, which will improve long-term visibility for technical trading firms.
8.
Regional opportunities: Infrastructure contractors continue to secure major public works that expand transport and energy networks outside traditional northern strongholds. Civil contractor NYAB obtained the Phase 2 contract for the Uppsala Tramway project, securing an estimated EUR 150m share of the EUR 300m total project. These large-scale projects provide a steady volume of civil work that supports regional grid equipment installations.
9.
Concession barriers: Norway is applying tighter regulatory restrictions on grid connections that introduce significant delays for renewable energy developers. The Norwegian Water Resources and Energy Directorate implemented a rule requiring a formal grid reservation before starting concession applications. This reservation requirement has introduced significant project delays that postpone the delivery dates for electrical infrastructure suppliers.

United Kingdom and European grid expansion

10.
Rapid acceleration: National Grid has recorded a major spike in its capital investment program to support transmission network reinforcement. The utility deployed capital investment of GBP 6.7bn during the six months ended September 2026, which represents an increase of 33% at constant currency compared to the prior year period. This significant investment program will underpin transmission infrastructure growth and drive positive earnings development for major high-voltage hardware suppliers.
11.
Permitting milestones: National Grid has made steady progress on its critical transmission projects by securing long-awaited planning approvals. The operator obtained a Development Consent Order for the Sea Link project in late October 2026, moving the offshore connection project into its construction phase. Securing these planning permits is a critical catalyst for equipment deliveries and helps mitigate potential financial penalties from grid execution delays.
12.
Contrary evidence: Project delays and legal disputes continue to disrupt onshore wind connections and postpone planned developer transactions in the United Kingdom. Energiekontor adjusted its 2026 earnings forecast downward on August 13, 2026, because an investor walked away from acquiring several Scottish wind projects following grid connection postponements. This disruption illustrates how ongoing litigation can delay connections and temporarily freeze expected equipment orders for regional component manufacturers.
13.
Consenting progress: Scottish power operators are making positive headway on securing planning consents for their multi-billion network investment programs. SSE reported that its transmission business has secured 27 out of 34 major consents required for its key investment projects as of late 2026. Sustained consenting progress is critical to minimize grid bottleneck costs and ensure timely hardware delivery for grid suppliers.
14.
Fast-track reforms: European Union lawmakers have established a new regulatory framework to bypass administrative grid hurdles and accelerate clean energy deployment. On November 18, 2026, the European Parliament and the Council reached a provisional agreement that introduces a dedicated EU-wide permitting framework and shortens grid connection deadlines. This fast-track framework is expected to unlock mature renewable projects and accelerate component deliveries for electrical equipment suppliers.
15.
side of the story: While national state-led capex increases and European permitting reforms point to a significant market acceleration, regional grid operators still face severe near-term execution risks. Permitting procedures frequently delay project development through environmental assessments, land-use approvals, and legal appeals even after investment plans are approved. Consequently, Addtech's high-voltage equipment sales are highly vulnerable to project slippage because permitting remains the largest bottleneck to grid upgrades.

  1. Extended transformer lead times and severe labor shortages remain the primary bottlenecks for regional grid operators.
  2. Record backlogs at European contractors highlight robust demand, although execution risks are rising due to project delays.

Specifically, Minnesota Power reported in November 2026 that procurement lead times for large power transformers have stretched up to four years. Consequently, some operators must order these critical components while still seeking necessary route and site permits to protect project in-service dates. These extended timelines delay actual equipment deliveries and defer related revenue recognition for suppliers.

This softening could shift the market dynamics back toward a buyer's market.

Project delivery risks and impacts

11.
Delayed starts: Infrastructure developers are experiencing a widening window between securing project awards and actually initiating site work. AECOM reported in November 2026 that its backlog grew to a record $28.02 billion, yet conversion to revenue was slowed by longer waits to get projects underway. This trend is driven by complex project characteristics and prolonged supply chain lead times. For component suppliers, these delayed kickoffs push expected equipment deliveries and subsequent revenue recognition into later quarters.
12.
Margin erosion: Rising material and logistics costs are severely eroding the profitability of contractors tied to long-term fixed-price contracts. Over the past year, prices for aluminum and steel have climbed far faster than contractors' bid prices. On a contractor's typically thin margin, even minor shifts in material lines can erase the profit for a whole job. Consequently, contractors are becoming more selective in bidding to avoid absorbing these inflation risks.
13.
Financial penalties: Schedule overruns on grid connection projects introduce severe financial penalties and compound capital costs for developers. For projects with delayed in-service dates, capital costs are compounded forward over a longer horizon, which drastically inflates total project costs. These delays also postpone the realization of project benefits, which harms the overall economic return on investment. Ultimately, these schedule overruns raise the total cost burden for both utilities and ratepayers.
