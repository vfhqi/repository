[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. IMCD is a good business coming out of a three-year earnings trough, and the trough is clearing for a cyclical reason, the end of destocking, that its foundations did not cause. At 95.46 EUR the shares pay the framework's 20 percent bar over eighteen months only if we credit a partial re-rating at near-even odds, and they do not pay it over three years. The whole case turns on one thing: whether organic gross profit growth stays positive as the Middle East pricing spike of 2026 unwinds.

  • The change is external and cyclical, and it has turned.
    • The post-pandemic destocking cycle that ran through 2024 and 2025 has ended, and IMCD's own cycle has turned with it.
    • Organic gross profit, the line IMCD earns on, went from -7.2 percent in Q4 2025 to -3.7 percent in Q1 2026 to +7.3 percent in Q2 2026.
      • Organic revenue went from -2.8 percent to -0.8 percent to +7.4 percent over the same three quarters.
    • The turn is sector-wide: Azelis printed organic gross profit of +7.2 percent in the same quarter.
      • Customer inventories are lean and buying is just-in-time on a six-to-eight-week order book.
    • Part of the Q2 2026 growth was price, passed through from the Middle East supply disruption that began in late February 2026, and that pricing is already reversing: European ethylene and propylene prices fell 12.9 percent and 14.2 percent in July 2026.
      • How much was volume is not disclosed, and that is the gap in the evidence.
  • The internal change is continuity, not change.
    • A 26-year insider replaced an abruptly departed chief executive on 24 April 2025 without a strategic pivot
    • the six pillars of the 2024 investor day stand.
    • The one internal force that reaches the outputs is cost discipline: organic operating expenses were flat year on year in H1 2026 after a late-2025 restructuring, and the CFO says the cost base can take significantly higher volumes without a proportional headcount increase.
    • There is no guidance to raise, because the company gives none.
      • Acquisitions run at about a fifth of the 2025 pace (about 70 EURm of annualised revenue in H1 2026 against about 320 EURm added in 2025).
      • The dividend was cut 16 percent for FY2025 and buybacks are mooted, not begun.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have turned decisively, on one quarter of evidence.
    • Q2 2026 EBITA rose 17 percent to 155.2 EURm, 6 percent above consensus, and the conversion margin (EBITA over gross profit) reached 44.9 percent against 43.1 percent expected.
    • Consensus FY2026 cash EPS is 5.87 EUR, +13.1 percent on FY2025's 5.19 EUR, and revenue consensus is +8.5 percent after +1.1 percent in FY2025.
    • The sell-side's revision cycle turned in August 2026: FY2026 consensus EPS is +3.2 percent over three months to 2026-09-11, after twelve-month cuts of 10.6 percent.
      • The dashboard's pinned earnings-momentum rating of F rests on data from 2026-07-16, before the results, and is stale.
    • The margin evidence is the weak part: the H1 2026 EBITA margin of 10.8 percent is still below H1 2025's 11.1 percent, and the H1 conversion margin of 43.3 percent is flat on 43.4 percent.
      • Only Q2 2026 shows expansion.
  • The change has reached all six layers of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, the last only just.
    • It shows in the company's actions (restructuring, flat costs, digital tools), in the key operating metrics (organic gross profit growth, conversion margin), in the financial statements (H1 2026 EBITA +8 percent at constant currency) and, since August 2026, in the sell-side's estimates.
    • The share price moved first, +43 percent from the 52-week low of 66.942 EUR, two months before the estimates turned.
      • Propagation from the operating metrics is one quarter old.
  • What we think it earns: cash EPS of about 5.90 EUR in FY2026, 6.55 EUR in FY2027 and 7.10 EUR in FY2028 on our modal path, about 11 percent a year over three years, against consensus medians of 5.87, 6.40 and 6.83 EUR.
    • The path is revenue growth of about 6 percent a year (organic 4 to 5 percent, acquisitions 2 to 3 percent, less currency) and a conversion margin recovering from 41.7 percent in FY2025 to about 44.5 percent in FY2028 on flat organic costs, against a historical normal of 45 to 46 percent.
      • Our view sits above consensus only on the margin, by about 1.4 points a year of EPS growth.
    • Beyond FY2028 the path grows about 8 percent a year, which is why the three-year rate is lower than the eighteen-month one.
  • What it costs on the valuation: 95.46 EUR is 14.9x consensus cash EPS 24 months forward of 6.42 EUR.
    • That is the 19th percentile of the stock's own five-year range (11.88x to 39.64x, median 20.00x, 25th percentile 16.66x) and the 8th percentile of its ten-year history.
      • Over the last two years alone the multiple sits at its median of 15.49x, so "cheap" depends on the window.
    • Against the two peers the shares are not cheap: a 14 percent premium to Azelis at 13.1x and 8 percent to Brenntag at 13.8x, and about 32 percent on EV/EBITDA.
      • The multiple is industry-driven: all three listed distributors de-rated by more than 45 percent in the destocking cycle.
  • The return against the bar is about 20.6 percent a year over eighteen months and about 15.1 percent a year over three years, on the framework's convention of valuing the shares at each horizon on the earnings two years beyond it, at a blended multiple of 16.0x.
    • The blend is a cleared branch at 17.5x and an uncleared branch at 14.5x at 52 to 48 odds.
      • The cleared branch alone gives about 27.9 percent a year over eighteen months (an A)
      • the uncleared branch alone gives about 13.1 percent a year (a C at its floor).
      • The re-rating is the swing factor
      • the earnings growth carries the return.
    • 16.0x costs nothing on the valuation, because it is still below the stock's five-year 25th percentile of 16.66x.
      • It is a 22 percent premium to Azelis, narrower than the historical gap.
    • On the same path with a plausibly worse drop-through from EBITDA to cash EPS (other finance items staying at 25 EURm a year, net interest at 75 EURm, tax at 26.5 percent), FY2028 cash EPS is 6.78 EUR rather than 7.10 EUR and the eighteen-month return falls to 16.9 percent a year, a C
      • the 'Modal return' Element and this Building Block stay C, but the recommendation would move from bullish to neutral, because the modal path would no longer clear the bar at 95.46 EUR.
  • The pattern it fits is 'OSHQC CfC Clearing', an oversold high-quality compounder whose transient cause for concern is clearing
    • the fit is moderately crisp (C), because the clearing evidence is one pricing-assisted quarter and the framework asks for hard evidence.
  • Where the chain breaks: if the Middle East pricing tailwind reverses onto volumes that are still falling underneath it, organic gross profit turns negative in H2 2026, estimates are cut a fourth year, and the multiple returns to 13x or below.
    • Behind that sits Chinese price competition on the semi-specialty fifth of revenue and a weak Americas (organic revenue +1.6 percent in Q2 2026).
    • The nine-month results on 5 November 2026 are the test: organic gross profit growth still positive with the group gross margin at or above 24.5 percent confirms the case
      • below zero, or a gross margin below 24.5 percent, or a conversion margin below 42 percent, invalidates it.

  • What the case therefore is: a cyclical clearing in a good business, priced at the uncleared multiple plus a fraction, worth progressing now at a working-hypothesis conviction, and worth more below about 84 EUR, where the three-year path also clears the bar.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material enough to carry an eighteen-month return of about 20 percent a year and no more: the parts combine as a real cyclical turn in the outputs ('Financial Outputs' at B) driven by modest and partly transient forces ('Change Forces' at C) into a return that meets the bar only on a blended multiple at near-even odds ('Modal return' at C)

    • the forces and the return bind, and the outputs do not lift them.
    • Not B, because a B needs either a stronger force (a demand inflection with two years' visibility, or an internal programme with guidance) or a return that clears the bar on the modal branch alone, and neither is present.
    • Not D, because the cyclical turn is evidenced in three quarters of sequential improvement, confirmed by the peer, and the price of the case (a 16.0x multiple, below the stock's own 25th percentile) is not demanding.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the whole recovery is inflation, not volume, and it is already reversing.
    • The Q2 2026 acceleration was price and pre-buying from the Strait of Hormuz disruption (Brent above 100 USD a barrel from late February 2026, container freight two to three times normal)
    • Jefferies' own title for the quarter is "inflation-driven"
    • Azelis printed the identical organic gross profit growth of 7.2 percent, so nothing company-specific was proved.
    • European petrochemical prices fell 12.9 to 14.2 percent in July 2026, so the tailwind is turning into a headwind now.
    • Underneath, volumes are structurally weak in the Americas (organic revenue +1.6 percent in Q2 2026; EBITA margin 8.8 percent in Q1 2026) and in the semi-specialty fifth of revenue under Chinese pressure
      • exclusivity terms are shortening from five-year commitments to twelve-month reviews
      • the 91.4 percent cash conversion of FY2025 is a counter-cyclical working-capital release that must reverse (net working capital is already 24.5 percent of revenue at H1 2026 against 21.6 percent)
      • the acquisition engine is throttled at 2.8x leverage.
    • So H2 2026 organic gross profit turns negative, estimates are cut a fourth year, and the multiple, still a 32 percent premium to peers on EV/EBITDA, goes to 13x or below, toward the sell-side's downside of 70 EUR, a fall of 27 percent.
      • IMCD does not disclose volumes, so this cannot be disproved from the accounts.
  • What we concede.
    • The Q2 print was pricing-assisted.
    • The durability of organic gross profit growth is unproven and is the whole bet.
    • The Americas is a real problem.
    • Cash conversion will fall to the mid-80s.
    • On the uncleared branch with earnings also cut the downside is 20 to 30 percent, and no separable asset of IMCD bounds it
    • we make no such claim.
  • Why we still hold our view.
    • The case does not need the price rises to last.
    • The sector-wide destocking has demonstrably ended: inventories are lean, buying is just-in-time, and the company's ten-year organic gross profit growth of 6 to 7 percent a year resumed as soon as it did.
    • EMEA organic revenue of +10.3 percent against Azelis' +0.4 percent in Q2 2026 is share gain from new supplier mandates
      • inflation does not explain a ten-point gap between two distributors in the same region.
    • Organic operating costs are flat, so even 4 percent organic gross profit growth delivers EBITA growth of 8 to 10 percent.
    • Management restrained the specialty price rises to low single digits, far below the semi-specialty rises, so the give-back as pricing normalises is small.
    • The multiple at the 19th percentile of five years already discounts a non-recovery
      • on the uncleared branch at 14.5x the eighteen-month return is still 13.1 percent a year on our earnings, and it is negative only if earnings are also cut.
  • The odds we give the case against are the 48 percent of the uncleared branch.
    • What would change our mind: nine-month organic gross profit growth below 0 percent with a group gross margin below 24.5 percent, or a conversion margin below 42 percent in H2 2026.

    • Combination red flag: one of the framework's ten patterns in which two things together invalidate a case; "underperformance" in those patterns means 15 percent or three months of share-price underperformance.
