EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Orange S.A. has successfully executed its Lead the Future strategic plan and transitioned into Trust the Future, demonstrating orderly governance transitions, aggressive portfolio pruning of non-core assets like retail banking, full takeover of MasOrange in Spain, and confident executive tone shifts emphasizing industrial consolidation over legacy growth avenues. The overarching investment case centers on structural margin expansion driven by descending capital intensity, European consolidation synergies, and hyper-growth in Africa and Middle East operations, though execution risks persist in the enterprise segment turnaround and Spanish lowend competitive dynamics.

Net Signal of Change The financial profile exhibits stable organic revenue growth of 0.90% to €40.40 billion in FY25, with management targeting a 3% EBITDAaL CAGR from 2025 to 2028 supported by MasOrange consolidation, while organic cash flow rose 8% year on year to €3.70 billion and net debt/EBITDAaL finished at a solid 1.80x baseline leverage. The external environment reflects convergent consumer bundling driving high-value migration, near-complete fiber and 5G rollouts transitioning Orange from infrastructure builder to monetization phase, descending capex cycle declining 3.40% historically during 2025, and moderate wage inflation moderating to approximately 3% recently. Recent Q1 2026 earnings demonstrated strong top-line and EBITDAaL beats with revenues of €10,095 million up 3.50% year on year and quarterly EBITDAaL growing 6.60% to €2,601 million, prompting management to upgrade 2026 EBITDAaL growth guidance to above 3%. The net signal is constructive acceleration into the new strategic cycle, though temporary leverage spikes and lumpy wholesale timing effects require close monitoring.

Key Drivers Ranked by Impact and Plausibility The Africa and Middle East hyper-growth engine represents the highest-impact driver, delivering its 12th consecutive quarter of double-digit revenue growth expanding 12.70% to €2,212 million with EBITDAaL margins of 39.20% well above the group average, driven by 13.10% retail services growth and Orange Money active user base expansion of 18.30% year-on-year reaching 47 million customers. Plausibility verdict: HIGH with low dependency given structural market penetration tailwinds and mobile data monetization momentum across 16 countries.

MasOrange Spanish synergy capture serves as the second-ranked driver, with the joint venture surpassing 40% market share as the leading operator following Orange's completion of the acquisition of Lorca's 50% stake on June 8, 2026, consistently delivering upside to synergy targets and maintaining a run-rate synergy target of at least €500 million by 2028. Plausibility verdict: HIGH with medium dependency on regulatory approval timelines and integration execution discipline.

French copper decommissioning and fiber co-financing dynamics constitute the third driver, with FTTH rollout virtually complete across France and Spain with coverage exceeding 80% by early 2026, Group economic capital expenditure declining 3.40% historically during 2025, capital expenditure projected to decrease to 14% of sales by 2028 from 15.40% in 2025, and economic capex dropping €350 million below prior estimates. However, Morgan Stanley estimates copper wholesale will be a -€500 million cumulative EBITDAaL headwind by 2028. Plausibility verdict: MEDIUM with high dependency on managing the transition opex burden and offsetting wholesale revenue declines through retail upselling.

Operational efficiency and cost savings programs rank fourth, with the group successfully capturing €600 million in net opex savings over the three-year Lead the Future plan, achieving a 30.87% EBITDAaL margin in FY25 up 0.60 percentage points, and management expecting a 2 percentage point improvement in EBITDAaL margins by 2028. Plausibility verdict: HIGH with low dependency as programs are already delivering structural benefits. French market consolidation via the SFR transaction represents the fifth driver, with Orange signing an MoU in June 2026 to acquire 27% of SFR assets for €5.60 billion adding 4 million mobile and 1 million broadband subscribers, with Morgan Stanley noting that French consolidation can structurally repair domestic EBITDAaL growth from its flattish run-rate and calculating that the SFR carve-up could deliver €900 million in annual EBITDAaL synergies. Plausibility verdict: MEDIUM with high dependency on lengthy H2 2027 antitrust investigations and potential remedies. Orange Business Services turnaround ranks as the lowest-plausibility driver, with management targeting B2B EBITDAaL turnaround by 2025 or 2026, though Morgan Stanley models weaker enterprise profitability lowering 2026 B2B growth to -3% on persistent IT market headwinds and noting that Orange Business Services faces sustained opex declines through 2026.

Plausibility verdict: LOW with medium dependency on uncertain global enterprise IT spending recovery. Recent Delivery Track Record Orange met or exceeded its consolidated annual guidance targets for EBITDAaL and organic cash flow from fiscal year 2023 to 2025, with Group EBITDAaL growth accelerating to 3.80% in FY25 outperforming the initial guided floor of 3.50%, and organic cash flow reaching €3.70 billion in FY25 exceeding the guidance target of at least €3.60 billion.

Strategic execution remained robust, with MasOrange achieving €356 million in cumulative synergies in FY25 outperforming its €300 million target, management completing the official closure of Orange Bank retail operations in Europe on December 15, 2025, and successfully finalizing the sale of OCS and Orange Studio in January 2024. However, Orange Business experienced a -6.30% EBITDAaL decline in FY25, representing a persistent operational miss.

Overall delivery performance demonstrates consistent financial outperformance offset by enterprise segment execution challenges. 19 - Earnings Calls Forward Guidance Credibility and Management Posture Orange consistently achieved its financial targets over the last four consecutive fiscal years, upgraded its FY25 EBITDAaL target twice achieving 3.80% growth against a 3.50% initial floor, achieved low-single digit EBITDAaL growth of 2.70% in FY24, and consecutive guidance upgrades and conservative initial targets classify management as a classic sandbagger archetype, with management raising its FY26 EBITDAaL growth target to above 3% during Q1. The Spanish market remains intensely competitive as low-cost operator Digi Spain announced plans for an IPO, and Digi Spain continues to charge €17 for convergent services substantially undercutting Orange's €50 equivalent.

Gearing rises to 2.40x following the €13.65 billion MasOrange takeover, and lengthy regulatory delays and heavy integration opex could deplete FCF driving leverage above 2x. Orange has not implemented a wide back-book price increase in France since fiscal year 2023, and severe low-cost challenger discounting from Digi Spain could derail MasOrange synergies while the absence of broad French price indexation risks margin compression if opex inflation outpaces the current 1.10% retail service revenue growth run-rate.

Expert feedback reveals a highly formal results-driven culture with burnout pressures limiting employee agility, Executive Committee members own 283,800 shares representing a minimal 0.010% of total share capital, and the CEO's performance-based LTIP shares are capped at 70,000 shares per plan. Key plausible problem to monitor center on temporary leverage spikes, Spanish price competition intensity, limited French pricing power, and weak insider ownership alignment.

Investment Case Verdict Orange S.A. presents a constructive structural margin expansion story anchored by descending capital intensity, proven MasOrange synergy delivery, and double-digit Africa Middle East momentum, though execution hinges on successfully navigating temporary leverage spikes from Spain consolidation, managing the €500 million copper decommissioning headwind, stabilizing the enterprise segment turnaround, and defending against Spanish lowend price competition–management's sandbagger posture and peer-leading consensus revision momentum support confidence in the €5.20 billion organic cash flow target by 2028 and progressive dividend floor of €0.85 per share, positioning Orange as a defensive European telecom consolidation play with attractive 5% dividend yield but requiring close monitoring of French SFR transaction regulatory timelines and Spanish market-share defense against Digi Spain's aggressive convergent pricing strategy.
