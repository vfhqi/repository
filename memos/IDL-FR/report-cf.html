EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Earnings expectations. The only fully documented estimate set is from Bernstein, which as of October 2026 projects reported earnings per share of EUR 11.61 for FY26e and EUR 14.10 for FY27e. The FY26 figure is a 27.58% rise from EUR 9.10 in 2025. Bernstein is marginally ahead of consensus at the sales level and up to one percent higher at the adjusted operating profit level, so the true Bloomberg consensus EPS is likely slightly lower.

A verified aggregator consensus EPS was not available in the research, so Bernstein is used as the proxy. Main drivers and plausibility. Five drivers support the earnings path. Their plausibility ratings are: E-commerce and North American scaling, driven by like-for-like growth of 48.1% in North America in Q3 2026, rated high plausibility.

Gradual margin progression through the J-curve contract cycle, rated medium plausibility. AI4ID and automation, rated high plausibility.

North American M&A, rated medium plausibility. Geographic diversification, rated high plausibility. Taken together, the cumulative probability that all major earnings drivers succeed together is medium. Latest results.

Q3 2026 was strong on the top line. Revenues were EUR 1,177.9 mm, up 25.6%. They exceeded Bloomberg consensus by 7%, with like-for-like growth of 25.0%. In the first half, underlying EBIT grew 23.3% to EUR 81.1 mm and the underlying margin improved 20 basis points to 3.9%.

Operating cash flow rose 69.9%, helped by an EUR 85 mm positive working capital swing. This allowed pre-IFRS 16 leverage to fall to 0.6x EBITDA. Part of the Q3 outperformance may be cyclical rather than structural, since it was supported by inflationary price adjustments and favorable base effects. Reliability of guidance.

Management historically avoids publishing formal quantitative earnings guidance, so consensus is the practical benchmark. On that measure, delivery has been consistent. Q3 2026 sales beat consensus by 7% following a second-quarter beat of 8%. The weakness is that the absence of quantitative guidance could mask execution shortfalls and force analysts to rely on volume assumptions that may prove too optimistic in a downturn.

Free cash flow in 2025 also showed that rapid contract launches can trigger unexpected working capital pressure. Broker positioning. Coverage is thin and concentrated. Bernstein's Philippe Lorrain maintains an Outperform rating as of October 2026, while Redburn is Not Rated.

Bernstein's target path has been uneven, with a rise in November 2025, cuts in March and April 2026, and a recovery by October 2026. Recent revisions have been upward: sales and operating profit estimates were raised by 3% after Q3. With a single dominant source, the broker view carries limited cross-validation. Fundamental peer positioning. ID Logistics ranks at the top of the peer group on growth and balance sheet strength.

Its organic growth is also well above peers: GXO reported 6.4% organic growth in the same period. On leverage, GXO's net leverage was 2.5x at the end of 2025, against 0.6x at ID Logistics. Demand

Bernstein analyst Lorrain reports in October 2026 that e-commerce penetration and higher outsourcing rates directly support the sales trajectory of this growth compounder. Securing multi-year contracts with major online merchants protects future earnings per share.
2.
Moderating pricing power: Contract logistics providers face shifting pricing dynamics as inflation cools. Bernstein analyst Lorrain highlights that 3Q26 organic growth was driven primarily by strong volumes and, to a lesser extent, price increases from cost inflation. Better volumes reflect a recovery in retail activity rather than structural shifts. Consequently, future operating margins depend heavily on continuous volume growth rather than aggressive price increases.

Resilient outsourcing momentum: Multinational corporations continue to outsource complex supply chain operations to third-party logistics specialists. Shippers frequently struggle to manage highly manual operations internally and prefer to outsource to mitigate labor risks. Continuous outsourcing activity provides a highly predictable revenue stream that directly supports future earnings per share.
