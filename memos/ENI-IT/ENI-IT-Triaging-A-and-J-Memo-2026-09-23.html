[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = D

Thesis. Eni is a self-help and capital-return case on a commodity-levered integrated major, with the oil cycle at its back, that the market has already paid for. At EUR 23.38 (2026-09-22) the shares price a completed internal change and a supply-shock oil price, so the modal return from here is about 9 percent a year against the framework's 20 percent bar. The case clears the bar only below about EUR 20.

  • The internal change is real, broad and mature.
    • Over fifteen years Eni insourced its exploration and engineering, and in the last two years it has turned that into peer-leading organic volume growth, a satellite model, an exit from basic chemicals, a record-low balance sheet and a rule-based distribution.
    • Volumes: underlying production rose 11 percent year on year in H1 2026
      • 2026 guidance was raised to about 5 percent growth
      • the plan is 3 to 4 percent a year to 2030 on 54 projects in execution adding 850 kboe/d
    • Satellites: minority stakes in Enilive (to KKR) and Plenitude (to Ares) were sold at 11 to 15 times EBITDA against a group at about 4 times consolidated EBITDA
      • Plenitude deconsolidates in Q3 2026
      • the Searah venture with Petronas closed in June 2026 with over 300 kboe/d
    • Balance sheet and distributions: the Brindisi and Priolo crackers are closed
      • pro-forma gearing is a record-low 10 percent at June 2026
      • the 2026 buyback went from EUR 1.5bn to EUR 3.4bn under a published rule
  • An external force did at least as much of the lifting, and it is the one force the company does not control.
    • Realised Brent of USD 92.57 a barrel in H1 2026 against USD 71.74 a year earlier, on a Middle East supply shock that shut in about 10 million barrels a day, is worth about EUR 2.7bn of adjusted net profit on the company's own sensitivity of EUR 130m per dollar a barrel.
    • That is larger than any internal change in the year.
  • The change has travelled the whole 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, so nothing is left to propagate.
    • It shows in the operating metrics (volumes, unit costs), in the financial statements (Q2 2026 adjusted net profit EUR 2.33bn, double the prior year and 12 percent above consensus; cash flow from operations before working capital EUR 4.47bn, 15 percent above), in the sell-side's estimates (FY2026 consensus EPS up 82 percent in twelve months) and in the share price (up 60.9 percent in twelve months).
  • From here the outputs are flat, because the brokers already assume the commodity backdrop normalises.
    • Consensus EPS is EUR 2.69 for FY2026, EUR 2.44 for FY2027 and EUR 2.45 for FY2028 (20-Sep-26).
    • Our modal path sits at consensus and grows earnings per share by about 5 percent a year from the current 24-month-forward figure of EUR 2.44, of which about 3.5 points is the buyback and the rest is volume growth and self-help net of commodity and currency drift.
  • The cash return is the dependable leg.
    • A dividend of EUR 1.10 for 2026, rising to EUR 1.19 by 2028, is a 4.7 percent yield at EUR 23.38.
    • The EUR 3.4bn buyback is a further 4.9 percent of the market capitalisation while Brent stays near the company's USD 85 deck, and it falls by rule if Brent does not.
  • What the thesis costs on the valuation is the problem.
    • At 9.6 times 24-month-forward earnings (dashboard, 2026-09-22) the shares sit at the ten-year median of 9.5 times and at the 90th percentile of the last five years (9.82 times on the weekly series, 2026-09-18, against a five-year median of 7.01).
    • The shares have returned 60.9 percent over twelve months and 0.0 percent over six.
    • There is no re-rating leg to earn, and the multiple is the risk rather than the reward.
  • The return does not clear the bar on any reading of the multiple.
    • We value the shares at each horizon at a multiple of the earnings two years beyond it, at the ten-year median of 9.5 times.
    • Our modal 24-month-forward EPS of EUR 2.62 at eighteen months gives EUR 24.89, plus EUR 1.70 of dividends, EUR 26.59: plus 13.7 percent, or 9.0 percent a year.
    • At three years EUR 2.82 gives EUR 26.79 plus EUR 3.50 of dividends, EUR 30.29, also 9.0 percent a year.
    • At an unchanged 9.6 times the rate is 9.7 percent a year
    • at the arithmetic blend of the multiple branches, 9.3 times, it is 7.5 percent.
    • On a one-year-forward footing the answer is the same, because the earnings path is flat.
    • Drop-through: on a plausibly worse conversion of EBITDA into earnings per share (the adjusted tax rate back to the 42 percent pro-forma upstream rate; the buyback at the EUR 1.5bn baseline because the same lower price cuts the upside) the eighteen-month EPS is about EUR 2.37 rather than EUR 2.62 and the return is about 2.3 percent a year
      • the 'Modal return' rating is F either way
  • The pattern is 'Major Corporate Change', fitted crisply, and its promised output has already arrived.
    • A fifteen-year insourcing decision became a satellite-and-growth strategy, executed through Searah, the stake sales, the cracker closures and the distribution rule, and produced an 82 percent upgrade to FY2026 consensus.
    • The upgrade phase is behind the shares, not ahead of them.
  • The chain holds from the company's resources to the sell-side's estimates, and it breaks at the return.
    • The volume growth, the self-help and the distribution framework rest on a top-quartile operator and are probable.
    • The earnings leg is mostly buyback and is conditional on Brent by the rule that sets it
    • the multiple is at fair on the generous window and dear on the strict one.

  • The case is therefore compelling as a business and not compelling as an investment at this price.

    • It is a park at EUR 23.38, with a re-entry level of about EUR 20 on the eighteen-month modal path, where a 4 percent volume grower with a 5 percent cash yield through a commodity normalisation would clear the bar.

  • Rating = D, because materiality is tested at the return and the return is about 9 percent a year on the modal path, well short of 20 percent

    • the parts combine so that the weakest binds: the change forces are strong (E 1 at B) and fully propagated, the outputs have improved sharply and are now flat on consensus (E 2 at C), and the 'Modal return' (E 3 at F) carries a dependable cash leg of under 5 percent, an earnings leg of about 5 percent that is mostly buyback and no re-rating leg because the multiple is at the ten-year median and the five-year 90th percentile
    • the change is material to the company and immaterial to the return at EUR 23.38.
    • Not C: C needs a modal return of 13 to 16 percent, which needs a re-rating to about 11 times or Brent held at the deck with the buyback at EUR 4.0bn and no de-rating, a branch we put at about one in twelve
    • Not F: the change is real, mature and cash-generative
      • the failure is of price, not of substance, and the case clears the bar below about EUR 20

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The case against, at its strongest: the Q2 2026 print fundamentally re-rated the thesis, and the shares are still cheap.
    • EUR 0.7bn of the EUR 1.2bn cash-flow upgrade was operational, not commodity
    • production grows about 5 percent this year and 4 percent a year to 2030 on 850 kboe/d of sanctioned projects
    • upstream free cash flow per barrel rises more than 50 percent by 2030 and group cash flow from operations reaches about EUR 17bn under normalised conditions
    • the satellites return EUR 16bn of cash in 2026 to 2030
    • the buyback is a structural floor that has only ever been raised
    • the total cash yield is 9 to 10 percent
    • the stock trades at 9.6 times, below Shell, BP and Equinor and at the 54th percentile of its own ten-year history
    • consensus systematically under-models GGP
    • and the sell-side is still upgrading.
    • On that reading the return is a 9 to 10 percent cash yield, plus 5 percent volume growth, plus a re-rating toward the 75th percentile of 11.2 times as the market pays for a growth-and-return combination unique in European oil: 20 percent-plus a year.
  • What we concede.
    • The operational delivery is genuine and top-quartile
    • the cash yield is the highest in the peer set
    • the satellite valuations are external, transacted marks
    • the multiple against ten years is at fair, not dear
    • and GGP is probably under-modelled by a few hundred million euros of EBIT.
  • Why we hold our view: the arithmetic does not reach the bar without a condition that is not our modal.
    • Reaching 20 percent a year needs either a re-rating to the 75th percentile, which the stock has not traded at on a 24-month-forward basis in five years (its maximum being 11.83 times), or Brent held at USD 85 for two years, which we put at about four in ten.
    • The bull's cash yield counts the buyback twice, once as yield and once in the per-share growth it produces.
    • Consensus already embeds a share count falling about 3.9 percent a year and still has FY2027 EPS down 9.4 percent, which is the brokers' own commodity normalisation.
    • A 10 percent cash yield at flat earnings and a flat multiple is a 10 percent return, not 20.
  • Where the case against would win.
    • Brent above USD 85 into 2027 with the Gulf disruption persisting, the buyback at EUR 4.0bn and the market paying 11.2 times: about 25 percent a year on that branch, which we weight at about one in twelve.
    • The research's own tests (Brent below USD 70 for two quarters; Searah short of 400 kboe/d by Q4 2027; a Versalis quarterly loss above EUR 200m) are the right monitors, and none of them is needed to hold our view: the price does the work.

  • This is a self-help and capital-return case on a commodity-levered integrated major, with the commodity cycle at its back, that the market has already paid for
    • it is not a compounder, not a recovery still to come and not a demand inflection.
  • The change is real: over fifteen years Eni insourced its exploration and engineering, and in the last two it has turned that into peer-leading organic volume growth (underlying production up 11 percent in H1 2026; guidance raised to about 5 percent for the year and 4 percent a year to 2030), a satellite model that sold minority stakes in Enilive and Plenitude at 11 to 15 times EBITDA and is deconsolidating Plenitude in Q3 2026, the exit from basic chemicals, a record-low pro-forma gearing of 10 percent and a buyback raised from EUR 1.5bn to EUR 3.4bn under a published rule.
