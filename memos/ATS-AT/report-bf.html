Section 5 – Customer Side Analysis

Section summary
  1. AT&S exhibits extreme customer concentration with five accounts representing 76% of sales, making it highly dependent on Apple and Intel.
  2. Financial risk is partially mitigated by more than €0.80 billion in customer prepayments de-risking high-end Malaysian and Chinese capacity expansions.
  3. Switching costs remain highly structural and high-barrier, driven by multi-month qualification cycles, reliability validation, and deep co-development integration.
  4. Pricing pass-through is actively utilized but challenged by severe customer pushback and regional under-utilization as clients diversify away from China.

Customer Concentration and Core Accounts

1.
High customer concentration: The revenue generated by the five largest customers accounts for 76% of total revenue.
2.
Stable concentration range: Individual major customer shares range from 3% to 31% of total group sales.
3.
Historical peer comparison: The top five concentration rose slightly from 75% in the prior fiscal period.
4.
Key account - Apple: Deutsche Bank estimates that smartphone giant Apple accounts for 30% to 40% of AT&S revenues.
5.
Alternative broker estimate: Jefferies research separately models Apple's historical revenue contribution at 20% to 30% of group sales.
6.
Key account - Intel: Jefferies estimates that legacy semiconductor leader Intel represents approximately 40% of total AT&S revenues.
7.
Secondary broker estimate: Deutsche Bank indicates that Intel's historical contribution sits closer to 20% to 30% of sales.
8.
Key account - AMD: High-performance processor designer AMD is actively expanding its high-end IC substrate business with AT&S.
9.
Advanced facility ramp: AMD's volume expansion is primarily focused on reserved capacity at the Kulim plant.

Contractual Frameworks and Prepayment Mechanisms

10.
Massive customer prepayments: AT&S secured more than €0.80 billion in customer prepayments to fund its advanced Kulim capacity.
11.
Substantial risk sharing: Prepayments represent approximately 50% of the total €1.80 billion investment in Malaysia.
12.
Shared account funding: Prepayment commitments are estimated at €0.40 billion each from core partners Intel and AMD.
13.
Far-reaching capacity guarantees: Contractual agreements link capital expansions to rigid capacity reserves and volume guarantees for clients.
14.
Strict delivery penalties: Failure to meet guaranteed volumes can result in material damages and contractual penalties.
15.
Custom revenue recognition: Tailored product specifications allow over-time revenue recognition under IFRS 15 for certain accounts.
16.
Extended shipping timelines: Revenue recognition is offset by 6 to 8-week delivery cycles from production to final shipment.

Switching Costs and Technical Qualification Barriers

17.
Intrenched mSAP position: AT&S is one of only three global suppliers capable of delivering high-volume mSAP substrates to Apple.
18.
Low customer substitution: Entrenched mSAP positions create high technical barriers that prevent rapid supplier switching by smartphone OEMs.
19.
Rigorous qualification cycles: Semiconductor customers require extensive qualification processes, often lasting several months before commercial production begins. 199 10Q System reliability validation: Qualification involves rigorous testing of bare boards within integrated hardware and software environments. 200 10Q High redesign costs: Replacing specialized interconnect components introduces severe operational risks, redesign costs, and testing delays.
20.
Collaborative R&D locks: Custom ASIC developers co-optimize high-speed 3D substrate solutions directly within Leoben's R&D facilities.
21.
Mutually approved processes: Close physical co-development ensures AT&S processes are approved prior to volume production launches.

Pricing Pass-Through and Customer Pushback Dynamics

22.
Input cost pass-through: High-end substrate fabricators successfully implement pricing pass-throughs to offset escalating raw material inflation.
23.
Broad material hikes: Raw material price increases are implemented globally across applications rather than targeting specific accounts. short-term pricing lags: Margin pressure persists in the short term due to negotiated lag times in commercial price adjustments.
24.
China diversification pushback: Key customers are actively reducing manufacturing exposure to China, impacting Chongqing utilization.
25.
Under-utilization margins drag: Chongqing utilization remains restricted as clients substitute domestic volumes with Southeast Asian capacity.
26.
Substituted volume flows: AMD is shifting a portion of its China substrate volume to reserved Malaysian capacity.
27.
Local substitution risks: Intel's footprint in China faces localized competitive threats from rapid domestic CPU substitution.
28.
Other side of the story: The concentration of 76% of revenues in five accounts means that any volume reduction or supplier diversification by Apple or Intel would immediately cause severe, unmitigated under-utilization across AT&S's high-fixed-cost manufacturing base.

Section 6 – Supplier + Distributor + Channel Analysis

Section summary
  1. raw material procurement remains heavily exposed to monopolistic Japanese suppliers for T-glass and Ajinomoto build-up dielectric films
  2. high concentration of production in Asia leaves the group vulnerable to escalating freight rates, tariff barriers, and localized gas supply shocks
  3. rising input costs are passed to clients with a multi-month lag, while direct-to-OEM distribution eliminates third-party distributor disintermediation risks

Critical Input Sourcing and Supplier Concentration

1.
Monopolistic dielectric film: AT&S relies on Ajinomoto as the monopoly supplier of build-up film, holding a 95% global market share.
2.
Extended qualification timelines: The deep integration of Ajinomoto film makes qualifying alternative suppliers highly slow and risky.
3.
Acute T-glass bottleneck: High-end server substrates get dimensional stability from low-expansion T-glass, dominated globally by sole supplier Nittobo.
4.
Capacity running full: Nittobo is operating at maximum capacity, creating severe industry-wide supply deficits in 2026.
5.
Joint customer qualification: AT&S is partnering with key clients to qualify secondary fiberglass options like Taiwan Glass.
6.
Specialized copper sourcing: Mitsui Mining acts as the dominant supplier for ultra-thin carrier-supported copper foil.
7.
High performance requirements: Advanced server applications drive a massive volume surge for hyper-very-low-profile copper foils.
8.
Customer-mandated dependencies: Sourcing options are restricted for certain chemicals where customers specify a single-source composite supplier.
9.
Production delay risks: Bottlenecks at these customer-specified suppliers can cause unmitigated manufacturing delays and cost increases.
10.
Sourcing strategy objective: Sourcing policies aim to build a diversified base to systematically reduce single-source raw material dependencies.
11.
Strategic supplier assessments: Over 90% of strategic suppliers undergo structured ESG and operational risk reviews.

Supply Chain Vulnerabilities and Geopolitical Risk Profiles

12.
Geopolitical trade disruptions: Trade tensions and protectionist tariffs disrupt global material flows, creating localized cost and shipment imbalances.
13.
Extended transit times: Middle East conflicts force logistics rerouting, which increases freight costs and safety stock requirements.
14.
Regional manufacturing concentration: Sourcing is highly dependent on Asian hubs, with Southeast Asian supply lines still developing.
15.
Localized procurement pivots: In preparing Malaysian operations, AT&S is working to expand its regional purchasing footprint.
16.
Energy supply hazards: Domestic European production sites remain highly exposed to unplanned gas supply disruptions or cut-offs.
17.
Quick-response measures: AT&S implemented contingency actions and a group-wide energy security strategy to protect facility operations.
18.
Environmental regulation risks: The prohibition of specific chemicals under REACH and RoHS directives can materially increase manufacturing expenses.
19.
Supply chain monitoring: Human rights and ethical sourcing risks are managed via audits of 153 suppliers representing 60% of purchases.
20.
Smelter verification programs: Suppliers committed 100% to sourcing conflict-free minerals verified through the Responsible Minerals Initiative.

Input Cost Trends, Pricing Pass-Through, and Distribution Channels

21.
Raw material price hikes: Rising AI demand and geopolitical tensions drove copper and gold prices to record highs.
22.
AT&S resource inflows of key raw materials for fiscal years 2024/25 and 2025/26 High laminate volatility: Surging copper costs directly impact copper-clad laminate prices, driving industry-wide product cost increases.
23.
Resin cost inflation: Escalating oil prices put significant upward pressure on essential chemical, plastic, and resin inputs.
24.
Lagged cost pass-through: AT&S negotiates price adjustments with clients to pass on raw material cost inflation.
25.
Profitability squeeze periods: Cost pass-through occurs with a multi-month delay, leaving margins vulnerable to sudden price spikes.
26.
Direct-to-OEM sales model: AT&S distributes highly customized substrates directly to Tier-1 technology innovators like Apple, AMD, and Intel.
27.
Multi-location sales support: Sourcing and commercial negotiations are managed through six global sales support offices.
28.
Negligible disintermediation risk: Direct sales eliminate intermediaries, as substrates are co-designed and qualified directly with OEMs.
29.
Customized design lock-in: Technical design specifications and long qualification cycles prevent independent distributor channel replacement.
30.
Other side of the story: Severe customer-specified supplier restrictions and T-glass shortages could disrupt feedstock access, capping substrate capacity expansions regardless of market demand.

Section 7 – Competitive Landscape + Adjacent Stages + Vertical Integration Threats

Section summary
  1. AT&S faces intense technological competition from consolidated Japanese and Taiwanese giants, but retains a defensible niche in advanced high-end HDI and EMIB-T packaging
  2. Market share continues to consolidate towards tier-1 players with superior yields, while pricing remains cyclical and tied to raw material shortages
  3. Vertical integration threats from customers are credible but limited by extreme substrate fabrication complexity and capital barriers

Competitive Mapping and Technology Vectors

1.
Technology tiering structure: the high-end packaging substrate landscape is highly consolidated around three dominant Asian players.
2.
Dominant market leader: Japan's Ibiden leads in technology complexity, capturing an 80%+ share in Nvidia's high-end AI GPU substrates.
3.
Aggressive scale challenger: Taiwan's Unimicron leverages massive capacity, maintaining the top global substrate market share at 17%.
4.
AT&S competitive position: AT&S holds the fourth position globally with a 6% share of the global IC substrate market.
5.
Niche capability advantage: the group competes on high complexity, being one of few qualified suppliers for Intel's advanced EMIB platform.
6.
HDI market positioning: AT&S holds a strong global position in high-tech HDI PCBs, consistently ranking second behind Taiwanese competitor Compeq.
7.
Broad consumer exposure: the group's consumer electronics exposure (>70% of sales) is more vulnerable to volume declines than AI-focused peers.
8.
Strategic capacity expansion: the new production facility in Kulim, Malaysia, is designed to expand AT&S's high-end IC substrate capacity.
9.
Underperforming return metrics: AT&S's return profile underperforms peers, with ROA lagging at 2.80% in fiscal year 2025/26.
10.
Superior peer returns: peer Ibiden achieved a superior return on equity of 13.50% in 2025, driven by richer Nvidia GPU exposure.
11.
Western niche player: peer TTM Technologies is strong in North American backplanes and military apps, but faces cost and R&D disadvantages.
12.
High cost structure: TTM's higher Western cost base prevents it from competing effectively in high-volume, cost-sensitive AI or mobile substrates.
13.
Automotive substrate specialists: smaller European peers like Schweizer Electronic focus on niche power electronics, but lack the scale for advanced ABF substrates.

Market Share Trends and Pricing Discipline

14.
Substrate share dynamics: Ibiden is expected to capture 100% share of next-generation Rubin GPU substrates due to extreme design complexity.
15.
Competitor share gains: Unimicron is gaining share in Blackwell substrates, targeting a 20-30% share as production ramps.
16.
Optical transceiver competition: Unimicron and Shennan dominate the optical transceiver PCB market, but face aggressive share gains from Zhen Ding.
17.
Escalating optical penetration: Zhen Ding's supply share in 800G transceivers is projected to reach 20% by calendar year 2028.
18.
R&D intensity barriers: high-end substrate manufacturing demands massive, continuous capital and research investments to sustain industry-leading yields.
19.
Peer capex expansion: Ibiden raised its three-year capex plan to ¥500 billion in February 2026 to support advanced packaging.
20.
Substantial Taiwanese spending: Unimicron guided 2026 capex to NT$34 billion, with 70% allocated to high-end ABF expansions.
21.
Material-driven pricing environment: recent substrate price hikes are driven by upstream material cost inflation rather than pure structural pricing power.
22.
Shortage price spikes: tight supply of Nittobo's T-glass and Resonac's CCL supports a strong pricing environment for substrates.
23.
Substrate price escalation: Unimicron's quarterly ABF substrate ASP hikes are estimated to accelerate to 7-10% QoQ through 2026.
24.
Nvidia substrate dominance: Ibiden secures dominant share due to superior post-packaging yield rates compared to peers like Unimicron.
25.
Substrate scaling leadership: Unimicron's position as a scaling leader allows it to produce high-end substrates at a lower price point.
26.
Intel pricing renegotiations: substrate suppliers successfully renegotiated better pricing with Intel, following aggressive price cuts in calendar year 2024.

Vertical Integration and Consolidation Threats

27.
Conglomerate resilience edge: several IC substrate competitors are backed by larger, diversified conglomerates with superior capital flexibility.
28.
Integrated raw materials: competitor Nan Ya Plastics benefits from upstream integration into glass cloth and laminates, reducing cost volatility.
29.
Customer internalization threats: hyperscale customers are designing custom ASICs, but remain fully dependent on external substrate fabricators for manufacturing.
30.
Extreme yield barrier: building in-house substrate fabrication is highly economically unviable for chip designers due to low initial yields.
31.
Customer capacity co-financing: AT&S mitigates capital risk by utilizing significant customer prepayments to co-finance new plants.
32.
Intel capacity priority: Intel's prepayments of ~€400 million secure dedicated high-end substrate supply amid structural industry-wide shortages.
33.
Upstream consolidation risk: upstream materials consolidation, such as Resonac's 30% price hike on CCLs, threatens fabricator margins.
34.
Delayed cost recovery: AT&S passes on input cost increases, but typically with a delay of a few months.
35.
Transition to glass substrates: the medium-term commercialization of glass-core substrates by 2028-2029 represents a potential structural threat to organic ABF.
36.
Entrenched organic dominance: organic substrates will remain the de facto standard through 2027, as glass core remains in prototype phases.
37.
Glass substrate initiatives: AT&S is actively engaging with partners to develop future technologies like glass-core substrates.
38.
Strategic technology shift: the migration to EMIB-T packaging means advanced packaging complexity shifts the primary value capture from silicon interposers directly to the substrate.
39.
Other side of the story: conglomerate-backed Chinese players like Zhen Ding and Shennan could trigger severe overcapacity and price wars if they aggressively expand into advanced ABF substrates.
