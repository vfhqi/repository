Section 2 – Growth-Margin Dynamics and Staffing Disclosures

Section summary

Contractor Reliance and Growth Gearing

1.
Delayed recovery: Kainos management confirmed during the November 2026 analyst briefing that overall profit expansion will lag top-line growth because of short-term contractor reliance. The business expects margins to normalize as temporary contractors are replaced with full-time employees when revenue growth moderates below 20%. If growth stays above this threshold, the company will continue to rely on contract staff to scale its delivery capability. This relationship suggests that faster growth delays overall margin recovery.
2.
Limited gearing: Deutsche Bank Research highlighted in November 2026 that high-growth scenarios will force the company to sustain elevated contractor levels. The analyst noted that group gross margin declined to 43.1% because of this reliance, meaning that near-term operational gearing is unlikely to come through. This perspective suggests that short-term revenue upgrades will not immediately translate into higher earnings per share. Investors must understand that operational gearing remains limited near-term.
3.
Transient headwinds: Deutsche Bank Research noted in August 2026 that the near-term margin impact of high contractor levels is a transient issue. The analyst explained that positive contract momentum provides longterm support for the business as utilization levels normalize. The firm expects better operational gearing to emerge as the staffing mix improves in future periods. This outlook suggests that contractor headwinds are merely a transient issue.
4.
Capacity constraints: Kainos reported that contractor usage quadrupled to 259 people in fiscal 2026 from 69 in the prior year. This rapid staffing was necessary to support strong bookings growth but reduced overall margins by approximately 1%. Management anticipates that these contractor numbers will normalize as growth moderates over the current period. This trend shows that rapid scaling requires short-term contractor reliance.
5.
Contrary evidence: Kainos delivered a 30% increase in adjusted pre-tax profit to 41.7 million pounds despite the significant gross margin contraction in the first half of fiscal 2027. This absolute profit expansion was supported by direct expenses and central costs growing much more slowly than revenue. This outcome demonstrates that rapid top-line growth can still drive robust earnings growth even if it temporarily compresses the gross margin percentage.
