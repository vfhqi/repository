EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Getlink SE operates as a highly stable, execution-focused infrastructure concession under CEO Yann Leriche and Deputy CEO Géraldine Périchon, delivering H1 2026 revenue of €824 million (up 13% recalculation-adjusted) and EBITDA of €404 million despite entering a high capex cycle of €170-220 million annually through 2032. The investment thesis centers on defensive structural advantages in the cross-Channel transport monopoly, exceptional EBITDA margins of 49-54%, and highly visible interconnector cash flows that together create a compelling long-duration infrastructure play with moderate operational leverage to European macro cycles.

The core change forces reshaping Getlink's forward trajectory include the unbundled fare structure launched in 2025 driving premium mix expansion to 19%, rising ferry environmental surcharges (approximately €50 per crossing) that narrow competitive pricing gaps and support market share gains, potential high-speed rail competitor entrants (Virgin, Trenitalia) threatening Eurostar's long-term monopoly on tunnel capacity, and ElecLink representing the highest margin growth driver with pre-sold revenue visibility extending through 2027.

External headwinds center on UK business rates escalating to €24-27 million by 2028 following failed VOA negotiations, ARENH mechanism expiration increasing direct electricity exposure and raising baseline opex risk, and subdued cross-Channel freight trade suffering from economic slowdowns and structural Brexit-related trade drags. The top six ranked EPS drivers in order of estimated impact and plausibility are: Eurotunnel yield management (approximately +4% impact, HIGH plausibility) leveraging unbundled ticketing to capture leisure customer willingness-to-pay dynamically; ElecLink electricity spreads (approximately +15% impact, HIGH plausibility with MEDIUM confidence) underpinned by €305 million in secured 2026 capacity representing 98% pre-sold revenue; EU EES border rollout (approximately -5% impact, MEDIUM plausibility) with implementation delays easing nearterm friction but passenger volume risks persisting; UK business rates dispute (approximately -6% impact, HIGH plausibility) triggering multi-year tax increases despite active court proceedings; Europorte acquisitions (approximately +2% impact, HIGH plausibility) successfully expanding rail infrastructure maintenance capabilities through Renofer, Giravert, and Electrofer bolt-ons; and fuel and energy cost inflation (approximately -3% impact, MEDIUM plausibility) with structured rolling hedges and Power Purchase Agreements containing opex volatility after French ARENH expiration.

Cross-driver dependencies reveal that Eurotunnel yield improvements are highly dependent on ferry price hikes driven by rising fuel surcharges, the joint probability of concurrent maximum ElecLink spreads and volume rebounds remains moderate near 35%, and a stacked downside scenario of EES border friction and contracting power spreads could reduce group EBITDA by 15%.

H1 2026 delivery modestly exceeded expectations with revenue reaching €824 million (versus €818 million consensus, a +0.73% beat), EBITDA hitting €404 million (versus €402 million consensus, a +0.50% beat), but net profit missing consensus at €118 million (versus €125 million, a -5.6% miss) despite exceptional free cash flow of €295 million compared to €218 million in H1 2025. Performance was anchored by ElecLink revenue surging 71% to €157 million with outstanding EBITDA growth of 79% to €93 million post-profit sharing, Europorte revenue increasing 12% to €93 million propelled by Socorail contract wins, and optimized shuttle yields growing 4% through active yield management offsetting sluggish volume trends.

Cost pressures emerged as group opex rose 7% to €375 million with Fixed Link opex increasing 6% to €280 million driven by €3 million in H1 business rates taxes and €9 million in transitory maintenance costs for specific rolling stock maintenance.

Management's guidance archetype exhibits a consistent sandbagging pattern with upgraded FY26 EBITDA guidance of €835-870 million reflecting robust secured ElecLink revenue of €305 million representing 98% of 2026 capacity, assuming limited near-term traffic disruption if EU EES implementation remains delayed until late 2026, and validated by historical outperformance as FY23 EBITDA reached €979 million (beating the conservative floor of €910 million), FY24 delivered €833 million (hitting the absolute ceiling of €780-830 million guidance), FY25 achieved €859 million (beating €780-830 million despite interconnector suspensions), and the company has surpassed EBITDA targets for three consecutive years establishing clear conservative communication credibility.

Forward guidance remains highly sensitive to volume swings with Le Shuttle volume shifts of 1% impacting group EBITDA by 0.8%, Eurostar passenger shifts of 1% impacting EBITDA by 0.4%, and long-duration asset valuations declining by 7% for every 50 bps shift in real European bond yields.

Sell-side positioning remains constructively positioned with Bloomberg consensus featuring 53% Overweight ratings as of July 2026, with Morgan Stanley's Nicolas Mora reiterating Overweight rating with €20.20 price target projecting €891 million FY26 EBITDA, Jefferies' Graham Hunt maintaining Hold rating with €18.50 price target noting consensus is already well-calibrated, and Bernstein's Victor Acitores keeping Market-Perform rating with €19.10 price target citing complex cross-Channel ferry overcapacity, while price targets span from €14 to €23 with a mean consensus centered near €19.66.

Fundamental peer positioning demonstrates that Getlink's H1 2026 revenue growth of 13% actual vastly outperforms Vinci's 2% revenue growth, its 49% H1 EBITDA margin remains structurally superior vastly exceeding Vinci's 18.0% margin, €1.4 billion cash reserves remain highly defensive dwarfing Mobico's tight liquidity position, Eurotunnel EBITDA grew 0.3% despite soft volumes demonstrating superior margin resilience, projected 5.2% yield offers solid income returns outperforming mature toll-road peers, and €295 million H1 free cash flow inflected sharply upward beating Eiffage's seasonally negative cash flow.

Activism presence centers on Eiffage's strategic stakebuilding trajectory, having acquired TCI's 13.71% block in October 2022 to reach an 18.79% holding, increased its shareholding to 27.66% in October 2025 paying €17.70 per share on the transaction, reached 29.4% in March 2026 remaining just below the 30% mandatory bid trigger, secured two seats on Getlink's board in April 2023 to actively support the strategic roadmap, while seller protections on the €17.70 block restrict further premium transactions for approximately 18 months post-deal and Jefferies expects the shareholder structure will remain stable with no near-term takeover bid planned.

This long-term positioning by Eiffage extends its concession maturity profile to hedge against APRR's 2035 expiry, providing strategic portfolio diversification for the construction and concessions giant. The two to three material risks confronting the investment thesis are: severe technical cable suspensions as a structural fault halted ElecLink for over four months disrupting high-margin power transmission revenue in FY24 followed by a two-week preventive suspension in May 2025 to verify cable shielding, though management successfully secured €55 million in insurance compensation offsetting the bulk of operating losses from outages but future technical faults without insurance coverage would expose unhedged execution deficits; and escalating UK business rates with failed VOA negotiations adding €6 million in 2026 climbing to €24-27 million by 2028 despite active legal proceedings to challenge the rateable value assessment, as failed court appeals would permanently raise property taxation discouraging future private UK infrastructure investments.

A compound downside scenario emerges if a collapse in wholesale power spreads combined with severe EES passenger border gridlock would completely offset Eurotunnel yield gains and trigger severe earnings downgrades, while unexpected technical interconnector outages combined with severe biometric processing border congestion will break the conservative guidance floor and trigger severe sell-side consensus downgrades.

Additional competitive risks surface if unexpected fuel price drops or delayed high-speed rail entrants cause competitor pricing to collapse, leading LeShuttle to lose premium market share to resurgent ferry operators.

Outliers worth noting

Getlink SE exhibits several statistical outliers and operational anomalies that deviate substantially from infrastructure peer norms and historical base rates across the transport concession sector. These outliers span governance concentration, asset duration, profitability margins, and technical performance characteristics that warrant investor attention. The most dramatic outlier is Eleclink's +79% EBITDA growth in H1 2026, reaching €93 million post-profit sharing, a performance trajectory that vastly exceeds typical interconnector operators and mature infrastructure peer growth rates.

While mature European power interconnectors typically generate mid-single-digit organic EBITDA growth during periods of stable electricity spreads, Getlink's high-voltage cable delivered double-digit expansion driven by exceptional France-UK price differentials and near-perfect 99.7% cable availability. This growth rate sits multiple standard deviations above the European utility sector median of approximately 3-5% EBITDA growth and infrastructure peer median growth of roughly 4-6%, as detailed in Section 6.

The sustainability of this exceptional margin expansion remains highly dependent on volatile wholesale electricity markets and the absence of further technical suspensions.

A second striking outlier is the concentration of shareholder ownership, with Eiffage's stake reaching 29.4% in March 2026, positioning just below the 30% mandatory takeover threshold. This ownership concentration dramatically exceeds typical free-float patterns for listed European infrastructure assets, where institutional shareholder blocks rarely exceed 10-15% and strategic industrial stakes above 25% are exceptional outside of controlled company structures.

The Eiffage-Mundys combined anchor shareholder position creates an unusual governance dynamic that limits potential hostile takeover scenarios but also constrains management's strategic flexibility, as explored in Section 2. Most comparable listed European toll-road and rail concession operators maintain dispersed shareholder bases with no single blockholder exceeding 15%, making Getlink's concentrated ownership structure a statistical anomaly within its peer set.

The ultra-long concession duration extending to 2086 represents a third fundamental outlier in the infrastructure landscape. This concession maturity profile extends Eiffage's portfolio exposure significantly beyond typical infrastructure asset durations, particularly serving to hedge against APRR's 2035 expiry. While most European toll-road and tunnel concessions operate on 25-50 year horizons with typical expiries falling between 2035-2060, Getlink's concession extends over six decades beyond the current infrastructure peer median.

This exceptional duration creates unique valuation dynamics, as long-duration concession cash flows remain highly sensitive to WACC increases, compressing equity multiples mechanically as detailed in Section 3. The extreme duration amplifies discount rate sensitivity and introduces multi-generational regulatory and political risk exposures that are difficult to model using standard infrastructure valuation frameworks. Getlink's 49% H1 EBITDA margin demonstrates a fourth outlier characteristic, remaining structurally superior and vastly exceeding Vinci's 18.0% margin and the broader diversified infrastructure peer median of approximately 16-20%.

This exceptional profitability stems from the asset-light, high-fixed-cost operating model of the Channel Tunnel, where incremental traffic generates minimal variable costs once baseline infrastructure maintenance is covered. As discussed in Section 7, pure-play tunnel and bridge concessions typically generate 35-45% EBITDA margins, but Getlink's margin profile sits at the absolute upper end of this range, amplified by high-margin Eleclink interconnector contributions. The margin sustainability depends on maintaining pricing power against ferry competitors and avoiding major capital-intensive refurbishment overruns. The company's 60.2% car market share and 35.8% truck market share in the Dover Strait crossing market represent a fifth outlier dynamic, as monopolistic infrastructure assets rarely face direct modal competition of this intensity.

Unlike most toll-road or rail concessions that operate as de facto monopolies within their geographic corridors, Getlink competes directly with eight active ferry operators providing substitute crossing capacity. This market structure is highly unusual for regulated infrastructure concessions, where competitive intensity typically remains modest and stable. The competitive overlay introduces demand elasticity and pricing power constraints that are atypical for infrastructure peers, as outlined in Section 7, creating revenue volatility patterns more commonly associated with unregulated transport operators than with concession-based infrastructure assets.

A sixth anomaly emerges in the credit rating upgrade of the CLEF debt securitisation to Baa1 with stable outlook, up from Baa2 previously, which represents an unusual positive migration trajectory during a period of elevated European interest rates and infrastructure sector repricing. Most infrastructure operators experienced rating pressures or downgrades between 2022-2025 as monetary tightening compressed valuations and increased refinancing costs.

Getlink's rating upgrade trajectory, detailed in Section 4, reflects the exceptional cash flow visibility provided by secured Eleclink revenues and rapid deleveraging, but positions the company as a statistical outlier relative to broader European infrastructure rating trends during this cycle. The elevated annual capex guidance of €170-220 million through 2032 creates a seventh outlier pattern, substantially exceeding mature rail peer averages of approximately €130 million and representing a higher capital intensity ratio than comparable single-asset infrastructure concessions.

This extended high-capex cycle, driven by mid-life passenger shuttle refurbishment, temporarily compresses free cash flow yields and distinguishes Getlink from infrastructure peers that have largely completed major capital programs. As discussed in Section 4, most mature European tunnel and bridge concessions operate on maintenance capex of 3-5% of revenues, while Getlink's program represents approximately 11-14% of revenues during the peak investment years, creating an exceptional capital burden relative to base rates. - Earnings Calls These seven outliers collectively create a unique risk-return profile that differentiates Getlink from standard infrastructure investment cases.

The combination of exceptional Eleclink profitability growth, ultra-long concession duration, concentrated ownership, superior margins, direct modal competition, counter-cyclical credit strength, and elevated capital intensity creates pattern dependencies that limit the applicability of peer-based valuation frameworks. Investors must recognize that Getlink operates at the statistical extremes across multiple dimensions simultaneously, compounding both upside potential and downside vulnerability relative to infrastructure sector norms.

The sustainability of these outlier characteristics depends critically on management's ability to navigate volatile energy markets, maintain competitive pricing power against ferries, execute complex refurbishment programs, and preserve stakeholder alignment across an exceptionally long concession horizon.
