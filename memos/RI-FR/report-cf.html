EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Bottom Line

EPS is at a cyclical low, and the near-term path depends more on cost control than on sales. Recurring diluted EPS fell to €5.85 in FY26 from €7.26 in FY25. Management projects broadly stable organic net sales in FY27. Jefferies models organic growth of 1.6% in FY28 and 3.0% in FY29. Recovery is therefore a FY28-onward story in most broker models.

The report body contains no explicit numerical consensus EPS for FY27 or FY28. The most reliable anchors are the FY26 outturn above and the revision trend described below. Forward EPS levels are inferred qualitatively and should not be read as consensus figures. The risk skew is toward downside revisions in the near term, with upside concentrated in a few identifiable catalysts. Sell-side analysts repeatedly downgraded organic growth forecasts after overestimating the speed of recovery in the United States and China. Rapid EPS recovery is conceivable if currency translation and self-help offset mature-market declines.

Current and Next-Year Consensus Position

FY26 EPS beat a lowered bar. Adjusted recurring EPS of €5.85 beat the €5.71 consensus. Lowered expectations and accelerated efficiencies drove the beat, which has a structural limit while the top line contracts. Reported profit fell much faster than EPS quality metrics suggest. Group share of net profit declined 26% to €1,203 million. Statutory operating profit of €2,162 million sat below recurring profit because of restructuring charges and write-downs.

FY27 estimates are still being trimmed. Morgan Stanley cut FY27 organic sales growth to negative 0.9%. It also forecasts a 54 basis point organic operating margin decline.

Recent Results

Q1 FY27 showed sequential improvement but not a clean recovery. Organic net sales fell 0.9%, against a 2.3% decline in Q4 FY26. This beat the consensus expectation of a 1.7% decline. The Q1 beat has quality caveats.

Morgan Stanley attributes it mainly to temporary stocking benefits in Asia that are likely to unwind. India's 19% growth was flattered by pipeline stocking ahead of a potential listing. The regional split is wide. Americas fell 9% organically.

Asia-Rest of World grew 9% while China fell 25%. Europe fell 6%. Margins and cash held up better than sales. The organic recurring operating margin contracted 35 basis points to 25.8% in FY26.

This came despite a 109 basis point benefit from lower advertising and promotion spend. Free cash flow rose to €1,197 million and cash conversion reached 91%.

Ranked Future Drivers

1. Fit-for-Future efficiencies are the primary EPS shield. Half of the €1 billion target was delivered in FY26, and the program is expected to protect operating margin. Morgan Stanley doubts it can fully offset negative operating leverage.

2. India growth and a potential partial listing rank second. A listing is seen as a crucial catalyst to accelerate debt reduction. Redburn argues it could raise EPS estimates by lowering net financing costs. The trade-off is lost exposure to a high-growth market and long-term dividend leakage to minorities.

3. US distributor destocking easing is the largest swing factor on top-line leverage. Redburn expects the inventory correction to turn into a second-half tailwind that unlocks operating leverage. Morgan Stanley warns that peer price cuts could dilute premium margins and delay the volume recovery.

4. China depletion recovery ranks fourth. Underlying depletions turned positive, driven by Martell off-trade campaigns. Morgan Stanley notes the gains came against very soft comparisons. 5. Capital discipline and cash conversion rank fifth. Strategic investment is capped at €700 million, down from €800 million. The cash conversion target is about 90%. Together these support deleveraging and interest savings. The drivers are not independent. US and China recovery tend to occur together because both are postpandemic corrections. Capping marketing to defend margins risks diluting premium brand equity. The combined probability of a full macro-led rebound is assessed as medium.

Guidance Reliability

Management has been far more reliable on margins than on sales. FY24 organic sales were flat against initial guidance of 4% to 7% growth, and FY26 sales fell 3.9%. Management did deliver a 64 basis point organic margin expansion in FY25, meeting its target. Current guidance rests on assumptions that are not yet proven.

Destocking in the United States and China must ease after Q1, and emerging markets must sustain robust double-digit momentum. Deutsche Bank notes that reiterated flat organic sales guidance implies downbeat operating profit revisions. The medium-term growth ambition has already been tempered. Management now points to the lower end of its +3% to +6% organic range.

EPS is sensitive to a few variables. A 1 percentage point drop in organic revenue growth reduces recurring operating profit by about €24 million (calculated). A 0.50 point margin contraction reduces it by about €47 million (calculated). A 1% US dollar appreciation adds €12 million.

Broker Positioning

Sell-side sentiment is cautious and divided. The ratings distribution is 35% Overweight, 50% Equal-weight and 15% Underweight. Price targets show a very significant spread. Dissent is concentrated on leverage, mix and US share loss.

Morgan Stanley's Sarah Simon cites persistent US share losses and weak Chinese consumption. Deutsche Bank's Mitch Collett cites intense US price competition. Redburn's Chris Pitcher highlights the weakest growth period since 1975. & Co Redburn The constructive camp argues the trough has passed. Jefferies' Edward Mundy argues that Q1 marked the cyclical trough. Bernstein points to India as a growth engine that could drive a re-rating once US inventory corrections subside.

Peer Position

Pernod Ricard ranks weak on earnings momentum. Diageo's latest quarterly organic sales grew 1.4%. Campari's grew 3.4%. Pernod Ricard's consensus earnings estimates fell 3.3% in Q3 2026, against positive revisions of 4.6% for Diageo and 3.2% for Rémy Cointreau. Leverage and pricing are relative disadvantages. Pernod Ricard's 3.70x net debt/EBITDA exceeds Diageo at 3.10x and Brown-Forman at 1.80x. Diageo cut prices on 71% of key SKUs, versus 44% for Pernod Ricard. Rémy Cointreau holds a 26% share of China cognac retail sales. Much of the underperformance looks cyclical rather than idiosyncratic. Peer guidance misses indicate that external cyclical headwinds drove the shortfalls.

Key Outside Forces

Demand and the inventory cycle dominate. Shipments have underperformed depletions as distributors manage cash flow tightly. High interest rates push distributors and retailers to hold less stock. Structural disruption and regulation add pressure. Ready-to-drink cocktails have grown at double-digit rates while traditional spirits face volume pressure. EU-China trade disputes have previously disrupted cognac imports. The India-UK agreement halved Scotch import tariffs to 75%. A bulk Scotch tariff dispute is holding containers at Indian ports.

Currency, rates and input costs matter for the euro-reported bottom line. Adverse currency translation significantly reduced reported net sales. The weighted average cost of debt rose to 3.40%. Gross margins are hit by past inflation on aged wet goods.

Critical Risks

Operating deleverage may overwhelm self-help. Bears expect organic declines in the United States and China to pressure recurring profit. Capping brand investment helps near-term margins but risks accelerating share losses. Elevated leverage limits flexibility.

Leverage rose to 3.70x, against a target below 3.0x by FY29. Morgan Stanley projects 3.70x again in FY27 and says failure to list the Indian subsidiary would limit financial flexibility. Organizational and governance execution is a secondary but real risk. Restructuring and layoffs have diminished staff morale.

Staff exits could erode execution capability and delay the earnings recovery. The CFO handover to internal successor Mauve Croizat appears orderly. The compensation shift from an operating margin criterion to net sales for FY27 changes incentives toward growth.

Younger consumers increasingly favor convenient, lower-alcohol formats that fit modern lifestyles. Wholesalers are diversifying their portfolios to include these high-growth convenience products. Jefferies expects ready-to-drink options to remain the primary growth driver for beverage alcohol.

8.

Ready-to-drink cocktails have grown at double-digit rates while traditional spirits face volume pressure. This convenience trend has forced major spirits manufacturers to adapt their portfolios. Consequently, failing to secure a leading position in convenience categories will limit future volume growth prospects.
10.
Moderation trend: Health-conscious consumers are seeking low-alcohol and sugar-free alternatives to traditional high-strength spirits. Younger demographics are increasingly wellness-oriented and drinking less alcohol overall. DNB Carnegie reports that non-alcoholic beverages are growing faster than mature alcoholic categories. To remain relevant, Pernod Ricard is expanding its portfolio of innovative non-alcoholic product offerings.

Regulation Tariff exposure

Pernod Ricard's international business remains highly sensitive to trade tariffs and indirect tax changes. Trade disputes between the European Union and China have previously disrupted cognac imports. Although the cognac suspension was resolved, regulatory measures continue to impact on-trade demand. Jefferies reports that international regulators are calling for stricter rules on spirits marketing.

11.

These constraints are intended to minimize alcohol abuse but increase corporate compliance costs. Morgan Stanley expects changing alcohol guidelines to present persistent downside risks. Consequently, strict regulatory oversight will continue to limit promotional flexibility across key markets.
12.

This bottleneck stems from an ongoing dispute regarding tariff treatment under the new trade agreement. While the pact halved import tariffs on bottled spirits, bulk Scotch remains subject to disagreement. Prolonged delays in resolving this tax dispute could cause near-term supply chain disruptions.
13.

Furthermore, Brazil has implemented a selective tax that charges the highest rate on spirits. Jefferies expects these statelevel tax revisions to create margin volatility for international importers.

Pernod Ricard relies on secure access to high-quality agricultural raw materials to produce its premium brands. Extreme weather events driven by climate change threaten agricultural yields in key sourcing regions. Dry goods, including glass bottles and cardboard, represent over ninety-five percent of product weight. Jefferies reports that packaging costs have not seen any significant moderation in inflation.

14.
Environmental stress: Water scarcity and rising temperatures pose significant operational risks to distilleries and bottling plants. Pernod Ricard is implementing water reduction roadmaps to mitigate scarcity in high-risk areas. 215 topic-specific reports Morgan Stanley expects supply chain disruptions to impact overall production capacity. Consequently, climate transition investments are necessary to secure long-term supply chain resilience.
15.
Packaging constraints: Disruptions in packaging material supply chains can force temporary shutdowns of strategic industrial facilities. Glass manufacturing remains highly energy-intensive and exposed to global energy price inflation. Bernstein research reports that European glass market dynamics are gradually stabilizing as capacity declines. However, suppliers face ongoing cost pressures that could impact future packaging economics.
16.
Cost pressures: Purchase price inflation for raw materials and services is closely monitored to protect margins. Pernod Ricard's gross margins are negatively impacted by past inflation on aged wet goods. Morgan Stanley expects these elevated costs to flow through the profit and loss account.
