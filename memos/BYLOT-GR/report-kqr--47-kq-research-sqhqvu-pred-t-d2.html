Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The concentration of operational drivers determines whether Ballys Intralot can successfully integrate evoke and manage high debt.

Defining the Key Question

1.
Key Question: "how concentrated or diffuse are the drivers that separate the next 18 months to four years' financials from the last four to six years, and how wide and how skewed is the range of plausible outcomes over the next 18 months?"
2.
Significant transformation: Bally's Intralot has undergone a massive restructuring following the €2.7 billion acquisition of Bally's International Interactive in October 2025. This transaction has nearly tripled group revenue and shifted the company's concentration, with two-thirds of group revenue now generated by the UK iGaming segment. Understanding the concentration of future financial drivers is vital for investors because this business mix creates substantial exposure to regulatory changes and integration dynamics.
3.
Highly sensitive: The future earnings per share of the combined group are highly dependent on the success of the recommended evoke plc acquisition. Jefferies Research noted in December 2025 that the company possesses sector-leading 40% EBITDA margins and high cash conversion, which support deleveraging and future dividend payments. Consequently, these favorable cash flow characteristics are expected to protect the combined entity from severe downside during the integration period.

Elevating leverage: Pro-forma consolidated net leverage for the combined group is expected to reach 4.5x net debt to EBITDA upon completing the evoke acquisition. Although Bally's Intralot had no active financial covenants to comply with in its loan agreements as of June 2026, its long-term financial health depends on successful integration. Deutsche Bank Research warns that net leverage exceeding 7.0x for an extended period represents a severe downside risk to credit stability and cash liquidity.

If consumer volume elasticity is higher than expected, these tax changes could severely damage group EBITDA margins and cash flows.

Section 2 – UK Online Volume Elasticity, Bonus Cuts, and Casino Duty Step-Ups

Section summary
  1. Higher remote gaming taxes force operators to implement promotional cuts and lower payout rates to preserve profitability.
  2. Historical precedents show that aggressive tax hikes can trigger black market migration and contract the regulated market size.

Regulatory Headwinds and Operator Actions

1.
Significant regulatory shifts: The UK remote gaming duty increased from 21% to 40% in April 2026, creating a severe margin headwind for online operators. In response, Ballys Intralot and peer operators are reducing bonus generosity and cutting customer promotions to protect cash flows. This strategy aims to mitigate the gross financial impact of the tax increase through commercial optimization.
2.
Dropping payout margins: Operators are increasingly lowering their return-to-player rates on slot games to claw back profitability. A industry expert from Ballys Interactive noted that U.K. online gaming growth is increasingly driven by margin expansion rather than player activity. Consequently, these product adjustments protect operating margins at the expense of customer value.
3.
Steep step up ahead: The next critical regulatory milestone is the April 2027 duty step-up, which increases the remote betting tax from 15% to 25%. Deutsche Bank Research indicates that this step-up creates another structural constraint on returns across the UK market. This transition will test player volume elasticity as operators further reduce promotional spending to absorb the duty.
4.
Contrary evidence: Robust online trading during the initial quarters after the April 2026 tax rise suggests high customer resilience. Regulus Partners reported that UK online gaming revenues actually grew 12% year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026. This near-term strength indicates that volume elasticity was initially lower than feared, though long-term impacts remain unconfirmed.

Comparable Cases and Peer Performance

5.
Severe Dutch contraction: The Netherlands case serves as a warning where a tax hike to 34.2% led to a 23% market contraction in early 2025. Operators in the Dutch market reacted by worsening odds and cutting marketing, which fueled black market migration. This precedent suggests that tax increases beyond a certain threshold can ultimately diminish total regulated market volumes.
6.
Preemptive safer measures: The UK iGaming sector possesses a highly recreational player base that may prove more resilient than Dutch players. Major peers like Flutter Entertainment implemented preemptive safer gambling measures, such as slot staking limits, to soften the blow of regulatory changes. These historical actions mean the residual player base is less sensitive to changes in odds.
7.
Varied peer outcomes: The financial dispersion among UK peers is evident as tax rises pressure earnings across the sector. Morgan Stanley reported that remote gaming duty increases are more benign for Flutter due to its balanced sports and gaming mix. Conversely, gaming-heavy operators like Entain face a much steeper margin squeeze before accounting for mitigation.

Mitigation and Volume Elasticity

8.
Drastic cost mitigation: To combat the online tax burden, Flutter Entertainment announced a major restructuring in October 2026, targeting up to 1,400 redundancies. The operator expects this initiative to support material cost savings and preserve investment in customer proposition. These decisive cost actions are designed to protect the group's long-term profitability despite regulatory headwinds.
9.
Divergent retail challenges: Mitigating retail tax hikes represents a greater operational challenge than offsetting online gaming duties. Deutsche Bank noted that Entain's online mitigation leverages highly flexible marketing spend, which retail operations largely lack. As a result, retail estate rationalization remains the primary lever for land-based mitigation after the recent machine games duty rise.
10.
Stable cash expectations: Entain plc remains confident in its financial targets despite digesting an annualized cost of £25 million from the April 2027 machine games duty rise. Management maintains its target of generating at least £500 million of annual adjusted cash flow by 2028. This resilience indicates that scaled operators can navigate regulatory shocks through proactive optimization.

Mechanics

Investors expect this combination to stabilize the long-term capital structure of the group.

A higher cash election will reduce the pro-forma share count and improve earnings per share. However, this will also increase initial debt and interest expenses.

Contrary evidence: Proactive mitigation measures by evoke plc managed to offset over half of the gross gaming duty increases during the first half of 2026. Consequently, early operational results might prove more resilient than expected before full integration.

4.
Severely elevated: The combined group will face substantial leverage with pro-forma net leverage reaching 4.5 times EBITDA. This compares to evoke plc's standalone net leverage of 5.6 times recorded in June 2026. Both companies possess debt structures with high interest burdens. This high leverage reduces financial flexibility during the integration phase.
