Defining the Key Question

1.
Defined focus: "Alfen N.V. (ALFEN-NL; Euronext Amsterdam), Dutch maker of energy-transition equipment in three business units: Smart Grid Solutions (secondary transformer substations and turnkey transport distribution stations, mainly for Dutch grid operators Enexis, Liander and Stedin, plus private grid projects and the Finnish unit Alfen Elkamo), Energy Storage Systems (utility-scale and mobile battery systems built on CATL cells) and EV Charging (AC chargers for home, business and public use, sold through distributors). How does Alfen N.V. (ALFEN-NL) account for depreciation, amortisation and impairment (property, plant and equipment, right-of-use assets, capitalised development with its five-year life and development in progress), how have these lines moved in FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 and H1 2026, how does FactSet classify them in its annual and semi-annual records, and what depreciation and amortisation does each covering broker (Jefferies, Deutsche Bank, ING, KBC Securities, Stifel, Oddo BHF) assume for FY2026-FY2028?" Significance to Investors

3.
Direct earnings impact: Depreciation and amortization policies directly impact Alfen N.V.'s reported earnings per share by spreading development costs over a five-year life. When Alfen N.V. altered its strategy in February 2025, it wrote down €7.40 million in capitalized development costs, severely depressing FY2024 earnings per share. Understanding these accounting policies is critical because capitalization rates defer current R&D expenses to future periods, artificially boosting near-term margins. Thus, tracking D&A movements reveals the true economic cost of Alfen's clean-tech product cycles.
4.
Contrary evidence: Alfen N.V.'s management contends that capitalizing €10.20 million in development costs during FY2025 reflects ongoing, high-value product innovation that will generate future cash flows. However, the massive €7.40 million strategic impairment in FY2024 demonstrates that capitalized development is vulnerable to rapid obsolescence in the fast-evolving EV charging market. This historical writedown suggests that Alfen's intangible assets may remain overvalued if market demand shifts again. Therefore, capitalization carries substantial write-down risks that can suddenly wipe out reported profits.

Covenant and Leverage Risks

5.
Crucial debt compliance: Alfen N.V.'s financial health relies heavily on complying with its bank covenants, which mandate a Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA ratio below 3:1. After breaching this covenant in Q3 2024, Alfen N.V. secured crucial bank waivers and negotiated adjusted terms that exclude one-off strategic restructuring costs from its leverage calculations. Management successfully met this ratio at 1.10x in H1 2025 and 1.20x at the end of 2024, avoiding a catastrophic credit event. Maintaining this headroom is essential to prevent debt acceleration and protect future earnings per share.
6.
Stable credit facilities: Alfen N.V. reinforced its financial stability in October 2026 by renewing its group credit facility with Rabobank until October 2029. This renewed agreement preserves Alfen's €50 million revolving credit facility and €80 million bank guarantee facility under unchanged leverage covenants. Because the covenants are based on Adjusted EBITDA, any changes in depreciation or non-cash impairments will not directly trigger a breach but will signal underlying asset deterioration. Thus, monitoring D&A is vital for evaluating creditworthiness and long-term liquidity.

Operating Leverage and R&D

During periods of volume expansion, this fixed cost structure accelerates earnings per share growth as seen in historical cycles. Managing this capital expenditure intensity is essential to stabilize margins through future industry downturns.

CapEx declined from €24.60 million in FY2024 to €14 million in FY2025, in line with this strategic spending limit. This disciplined capital intensity is critical to maintaining positive free cash flow and supporting earnings recovery.

Section 2 – Accounting Policies for Depreciation, Amortisation and Impairment

Section summary
  1. Alfen N.V. utilizes straight-line depreciation across all tangible and intangible assets, aligning its capitalised development amortisation with a strict five-year useful life to reflect rapid technology lifecycles. Net read: The company applies selective impairment triggers under IAS 36 and IAS 38, conducting annual evaluations on development in progress and goodwill while testing other assets only upon specific operational indicators. Net read: Alfen's accounting estimates compare conservatively with European clean-tech peers, although peer capitalization parameters and useful lives diverge depending on product-specific commercial durability.

Tangible Asset Depreciation Policies

1.
Conservative policies: Alfen N.V. depreciates its property, plant and equipment using the straight-line method to systematically allocate costs over estimated useful lives. According to the 2024 annual report, the company applies useful lives of 5 to 30 years for buildings, 5 to 10 years for furniture, fittings, and equipment, and 5 years for other fixed operating assets. This systematic approach ensures that asset values are written down in proportion to their economic consumption, directly reducing earnings per share as the capital base expands.
2.
Structured lease depreciation: Alfen N.V. accounts for right-of-use assets under IFRS 16 by depreciating them on a straight-line basis from the lease commencement date to the end of the lease term. In the 2025 annual report, the company details that these assets, including land, buildings, and vehicles, are written down over periods typically ranging between 3 and 10 years. This leasing framework stabilizes the operating expense line but exposes the income statement to accelerated depreciation charges if lease terms are renegotiated or terminated early.
3.
Comparative peer parameters: Schneider Electric SE adopts similar straight-line depreciation policies for property, plant and equipment to ensure sector comparability. In its 2025 financial statements, Schneider Electric outlines useful lives of 20 to 40 years for buildings and 3 to 10 years for machinery and equipment. Because Alfen's building useful life of 5 to 30 years is substantially shorter than Schneider Electric's maximum of 40 years, Alfen exhibits a more conservative depreciation schedule that front-loads operating expenses.
4.
Broader industry comparison: Legrand SA applies straight-line depreciation across its global manufacturing footprint, setting standard benchmarks for electrical equipment manufacturers. According to Legrand's 2025 annual statement, standard buildings are depreciated over 40 years, lightweight buildings over 25 years, and machinery over 8 to 10 years. Alfen's shorter 5 to 10 year range for equipment depreciation aligns closely with Legrand's machinery life cycle but reflects a tighter timeline for real estate assets.

Intangible Development Cost Amortisation

5.
Systematic product amortisation: Alfen N.V. capitalizes qualifying development costs and amortises them on a straight-line basis over a fixed useful life of 5 years. In its 2025 annual report, the company explains that amortisation commences immediately when the developed product or system is ready for commercial use. This amortization schedule directly reduces reported profits over the five-year period, representing the expected commercial lifecycle of energy-transition innovations.
6.
Strict capitalisation criteria: Alfen N.V. applies the rigorous requirements of IAS 38 to determine whether development expenditures can be capitalised as intangible assets. The company requires proof of technical feasibility, intention to complete, and a clear probability of future economic benefits before capitalizing any project hours. If a project fails to meet these criteria, Alfen must immediately expense all related R&D costs, which would severely suppress operating margins in that fiscal period.
7.
Prudent capitalization audits: Alfen N.V.'s capitalization of development costs is highlighted as a key audit matter because of the high level of management judgment involved in evaluating technical and economic feasibility. In the 2025 annual report, the statutory auditor notes that they verified capitalized hours against time registrations and assessed future economic benefits against management business plans. This strict auditing oversight reduces the likelihood of arbitrary capitalization but cannot eliminate the risk of subsequent strategic impairments if market dynamics turn negative.
8.
Contrary evidence: Alfen N.V. maintains that its five-year useful life for capitalized development is highly accurate based on product lifecycle expectations. However, rapid technological shifts and intense competitive pressures in the EV charging market can unexpectedly shorten actual asset lives, rendering the five-year assumption obsolete. This risk was realized in FY2024 when Alfen had to write down €7.40 million in development assets due to strategic shifts, showing that standard amortisation schedules often fail to capture rapid commercial depreciation.

Segmental Lifetime and Impairment Testing

9.
Distinct business lifecycles: Alfen N.V. manages different asset lifetimes across its three primary business units based on supplier data and government standards. The company assumes a 40-year technical longevity for Smart Grid transformer stations, a 10-year lifetime for EV Charging AC units, and a 20-year operational life for Energy Storage Systems. These disparate product lifecycles influence capital expenditure planning, as shorter product cycles require faster technological reinvestment and more frequent capitalization updates.
10.
Rigorous trigger evaluations: Alfen N.V. conducts impairment testing under IAS 36 at each reporting date to ensure assets are carried at recoverable amounts. Capitalised development projects still in progress are not amortised but must undergo mandatory annual impairment testing, regardless of whether trigger events have occurred. This preemptive testing is highly critical for earnings because any delay in product launches can prompt immediate write-downs of development in progress, impacting reported EPS.
11.
Consistent impairment metrics: Alfen N.V. utilizes value-in-use calculations under IAS 36 to estimate the recoverable amount of its cash-generating units during impairment tests. In the 2023 annual report, the company details using key assumptions such as a 17.6% discount rate, expected revenue growth, and a 2.0% terminal growth rate. These valuation models directly dictate whether non-current assets must be written down, directly impacting the balance sheet and future depreciation baselines.
12.
Historical peer precedent: SolarEdge Technologies Inc offers a comparable Clean-Tech precedent of asset impairment due to market downturns and strategy changes. In its amended 2025 annual report, SolarEdge recorded property, plant, and equipment impairment and abandonment charges of $224.77 million in 2024 and $5.80 million in 2025. This precedent shows how rapidly market changes can trigger massive non-cash write-downs across clean-tech production networks, demonstrating that Alfen's asset bases are highly vulnerable to sudden demand shocks.
