The FY26 EBITDA target of ~€800m is partially resolvable through structural Versatel consolidation, but verifying the ~€150m operational residual requires tracking national roaming cost stabilization and Open RAN offloading performance. The investment thesis hinges on whether management can execute operational cost reductions after two consecutive years of material guidance misses, with substantial upside emerging either through successful network deployment or strategic pivot to consolidation.

The central analytical challenge for concentrated long-only investors centers on decomposing the €262.5m EBITDA bridge from FY25 actual of €537.5m to the FY26 target of approximately €800m. Management established a like-for-like FY25 EBITDA baseline of €689m including full-year Versatel, bridging €151.5m of mechanical step-up above the reported €537.5m actual, with achieving the ~€800m target requiring a residual €111m operational EBITDA expansion off the €689m baseline rather than a full €262.5m organic leap.

This structural consolidation accounting mechanism renders over half of the guided EBITDA increase highly predictable, shifting analytical focus to operational execution risk embedded in the residual expansion.

The 1&1 Versatel acquisition at an enterprise value of €1.3bn unified mobile and fiber assets while introducing €398.3m in goodwill and a variable performance earn-out mechanism permitting purchase price adjustments up to €300m based on EBITDA performance between 2027 and 2029. However, the integration carries near-term cash flow friction, with analysts projecting a combined €100m free cash flow drag across FY26 and FY27 from ongoing

Versatel fiber network buildout, while the entity simultaneously services €950m in intra-group debt to United Internet.

The Vodafone national roaming transition eliminated dual-stack migration costs but introduced structural accounting drags and wholesale pricing volatility. Management confirmed that FY25 earnings absorbed approximately €100m in temporary customer migration and upstream network costs that completely disappear starting in FY26, establishing Vodafone as the exclusive roaming provider for all 12 million mobile contract customers from January 2026.

The capacity-indexed pricing model creates long-term cost predictability through automatic inflation and technology adjustments, yet exposes 1&1 to earnings swings when total network data growth diverges from planning assumptions. Critically, FY25 wholesale cost overruns occurred because overall traffic on the Vodafone network grew slower than expected, mechanically inflating 1&1's relative usage share, though management reported that data growth rates between the two operators began converging in Q1 2026.

Open RAN infrastructure deployment represents the primary mechanism for reducing third-party roaming dependency and capturing wholesale savings. Operating Europe's first fully virtualised 5G Open RAN network, 1&1 expanded household coverage from 27% at year-end 2025 to >34% in H1 2026 using exclusively mid-band 2 GHz and high-band 3.6 GHz frequencies, maintaining a steady rollout cadence by commissioning approximately 300 antenna sites per quarter, targeting ca. 36% to 40% household coverage by year-end 2026.

The architecture leverages four fully redundant core data centers and approximately 350 decentralised edge data centers out of more than 500 planned through 2030, with every gigabit antenna site connected via fiber optics supplied by 1&1 Versatel's 70,000 kilometer proprietary fiber network. Independent testing confirmed the cloud-native architecture achieves 10% to 30% lower energy consumption compared to conventional monolithic mobile networks, while the diversified vendor ecosystem uniquely eschews Chinese equipment suppliers.

Retail dynamics contribute zero to the residual €150m operational EBITDA uplift, as management guided strictly stable full-year 2026 service revenues at €3,661.8m. Management made a conscious strategic decision to abandon low-margin subscriber chasing in the discount segment, discontinuing budget tariffs across sub-brands like winSIM and yourfone, with discontinuing low-cost tariffs and trimming data allowances causing a net loss of 150,000 mobile internet contracts in H1 2026, with 100,000 contract cancellations occurring in April 2026 alone.

This disciplined commercial pivot intentionally sacrificed volume to prioritize higher-margin contract retention and defend core service revenue margins during network migration, yet mobile consumer ARPU slipped approximately 1% year-on-year through Q1 2026, while broadband ARPU similarly decreased 1% year-on-year, reflecting persistent market price sensitivity. The strategic consequence is unambiguous: the entire earnings burden for achieving the FY26 target rests on wholesale cost reductions rather than top-line expansion.

Management execution credibility represents the most substantial risk factor after two consecutive years of severe guidance misses. Management guided FY24 group EBITDA to €686m before reporting an actual figure of €591m, representing a 17.94% earnings miss driven by mobile network development bottlenecks, stemming from a major mobile network outage in May 2024 that revealed core data center components were critically undersized for subscriber volume, forcing an emergency migration freeze.

Management issued a June 2025 profit warning cutting guided EBITDA from €571m to €545m, ultimately delivering €537.5m for a 5.87% full-year miss, with national roaming charges exceeding budget because total data volume on the Vodafone network grew slower than planned, mechanically inflating 1&1 relative usage share. Guidance aggressively incorporated €50m to €100m in expected Rakuten supplier outage compensation that failed to materialize before annual accounts closed, while FY24 reported expenses included €14.3m in out-of-period billings for historical network rollout work performed during 2022 and 2023. - Earnings Calls The residual €150m operational step-up to achieve the FY26 target requires flawless execution across Open RAN traffic offloading and roaming cost stabilization, where historical precedent signals persistent execution risk.

Delivering this expansion demands 100% elimination of dual-running migration costs, on-time Open RAN site commissioning at approximately 300 antenna sites per quarter, and sustained data growth convergence between 1&1 and Vodafone networks to stabilize capacity-based wholesale charges. Management established aggressive medium-term guidance promising €100m annual EBITDA growth across FY27 and FY28, supported by guided FY26 cash capex reduction to €500m to €550m from €652m in FY25, yet unresolved access to low-band sub-1 GHz spectrum threatens indoor coverage quality and risks structural reliance on expensive national roaming.

Critical data gaps constrain precision in forecasting the operational residual, particularly regarding confidential Vodafone roaming unit rates, undisclosed antenna site operating expenses across 3,000 active Open RAN physical locations, and uncertain intercompany fiber pricing mechanics between Versatel and the Enterprises and Networks segment. These analytical limitations can be resolved through targeted expert interviews with former Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone procurement directors to establish precise benchmark ranges for German wholesale roaming rates, physical antenna site audits to clarify actual commissioning velocity and co-location opex, and monitoring upcoming Q3 2026 quarterly disclosures for segment elimination validation.

The broader market implications extend beyond 1&1's standalone trajectory. United Internet, holding an 86.46% controlling stake, possesses the means, motive via holding company discount elimination, and opportunity to acquire remaining minority shares or merge 1&1 network assets, while Vodafone maintains incentives to capture high-margin wholesale cash flows under the 18-year roaming agreement as 1&1 capacity usage scales.

Deutsche Bank Research highlights that if physical rollout execution fails, pivoting to network-sharing joint ventures or German market consolidation could trigger over 100% equity upside, as the transaction would enable a transformative 4-to-3 German telecom market consolidation. Conversely, missing EBITDA guidance risks permanent equity valuation impairment for United Internet under a structural holding company discount while carrying €1.94bn in intra-group debt exposures, creating asymmetric downside protection through strategic optionality.

The fundamental investment decision for concentrated long-only investors targeting 25%+ internal rates of return hinges on whether the residual operational bridge materializes through sustained wholesale savings or whether chronic execution friction forces a strategic pivot to consolidation. The Versatel consolidation mechanics are structurally predictable and quantifiable, rendering approximately 60% of the EBITDA bridge resolvable with high confidence, yet the remaining €150m operational residual embeds material execution risk absent definitive evidence of sustained roaming cost stabilization and accelerating Open RAN traffic offloading.

Historical guidance misses of 17.94% in FY24 and 5.87% in FY25 severely weaken management forecast credibility, though the complete elimination of temporary migration costs and data growth convergence signals in Q1 2026 provide early validation of operational stabilization. The equity case ultimately depends on verifying whether Open RAN household coverage expansion from 34% in H1 2026 toward the year-end target of 36% to 40% translates into proportional wholesale cost relief, a determination requiring continuous monitoring of quarterly segment disclosures and physical site commissioning velocity through the remainder of FY26.
