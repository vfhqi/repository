EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Vodafone's capacity to resume share buybacks in fiscal year 2028 depends critically on balance sheet deleveraging to the bottom half of its 2.25x to 2.75x target range, which will directly determine equity valuation and earnings accretion. The reconstructed free cash flow bridge reveals that Group adjusted EBITDAaL is projected to rise to €13.9 billion by fiscal year 2028, driven primarily by the consolidation of Safaricom which adds approximately €1.1 billion to the target, alongside the integration of VodafoneThree in the United Kingdom.

Leverage temporarily expanded to 2.4x in fiscal year 2027 following the buyout of Three UK minority shareholders, but is projected to organically drop below 2.25x by fiscal year 2028, creating substantial financial headroom to resume suspended share buybacks without breaching conservative leverage parameters. The fiscal year 2027 adjusted EBITDAaL guidance range of €13.0 billion to €13.3 billion reflects the nine-month consolidation of Safaricom, which management notes has a nil effect on fiscal year 2027 adjusted free cash flow while adding significant headline profitability.

Regional capital additions are forecast to peak as VodafoneThree capital expenditure hits €1.6 billion in fiscal year 2027, coupled with €0.4 billion of integration costs, temporarily diluting near-term European operating cash before synergies materialise. However, the annual cost savings target has been raised to £1.0 billion by fiscal year 2032, with adjusted UK EBITDAaL expected to grow by a mid-to-high single-digit compound annual growth rate through fiscal year 2032, underpinning long-term free cash flow expansion.

Cash interest paid reached €2,257 million in fiscal year 2026, including a substantial lease interest component of €577 million, and managing these massive interest obligations is critical because higher debt servicing directly compresses the Group's forward earnings per share. Luxembourg tax loss carryforwards represent a massive potential asset with €15,248 million in deferred tax assets recognised on €63,881 million of pre-2017 losses, though these are projected to be utilized over 46 to 50 years, providing only gradual cash relief to the Group. The contrary evidence indicates that the Safaricom consolidation will introduce €300 million to €350 million in additional cash taxes annually, which will restrict overall free cash flow expansion and delay Group buyback funding.

Dividends received from associates and joint ventures reached €557 million in fiscal year 2026, primarily driven by Oak Holdings which contributed €312 million, and these steady cash repatriations directly offset corporate financing costs and fund Group-level shareholder returns. Restructuring and integration costs will peak at approximately €0.7 billion in fiscal year 2027, including about €0.4 billion in integration capital additions directly related to the VodafoneThree merger, diluting near-term free cash flow and delaying the benefits of merger synergies.

The strategic asset optionality remains significant, as Vodafone's 50% stake in Oak Holdings is valued at approximately €6.4 billion, and selling down this stake would accelerate balance sheet deleveraging and fund future share buyback programs.

Covering brokers project that Vodafone will generate clean free cash flow of €2.2 billion for the twelve months ending March 2028, calculated after accounting for restructuring charges and regional spectrum outlays, with strong cash flow growth of 8% annually funding progressive shareholder returns from organic sources. Alternatively, Deutsche Bank argues that Vodafone will utilize its substantial debt capacity within the 2.25x to 2.75x range to accelerate buybacks, suggesting that maintaining leverage at the midpoint rather than deleveraging would release up to €5.0 billion in incremental capital and allow the company to execute buybacks during periods of low equity valuation.

This funding debate reflects differing assumptions on the optimal balance between organic cash generation and balance sheet capacity utilization. Historical precedents from European telecommunications operators provide compelling evidence of successful buyback resumptions following deleveraging. Telenor's net leverage fell to 1.2x, well below its 1.8x to 2.3x target range, allowing management to accelerate its NOK 15bn share buyback to complete in two years instead of three, boosting investor sentiment while driving substantial near-term earnings per share accretion.

Similarly, Telia's leverage reached 2.0x at the very bottom of its target range, enabling the board to contemplate new buyback opportunities alongside its core dividend while maintaining low leverage to offset rising interest rate pressures on its long-term equity valuation. Tele2's financial leverage ended at a low 1.7x, significantly below its target range of 2.5x to 3.0x, opening up immediate opportunities for extraordinary shareholder remuneration and representing a proven method to accelerate equity value creation for shareholders. & Co Redburn The overall confidence in the reconstructed free cash flow bridge remains medium due to regional regulatory volatility and unresolved operational variables.

The ultimate customer churn behavior following the German fixedline pricing contract reversal remains highly uncertain, with 116,000 customers opting into the class action lawsuit and an expected €5 monthly price cut impacting 2.5 million customers, though the long-term impact on churn is still unquantified. Additionally, the exact timing of Safaricom's parent-level dividend repatriation remains a critical unknown for fiscal year 2027 modeling, as the nil impact on near-term adjusted free cash flow is highly dependent on when minority dividends and local cash repatriations are cleared by local regulators.

Downside risks to the consensus view remain material and could delay the resumed share buyback program. The European Court of Justice ruling on fixed-pricing contract clauses will immediately lower German EBITDAaL by 1.5%, projected to reduce aggregate Group free cash flow by approximately 1.5% in both fiscal year 2027 and fiscal year 2028, constraining the Group's deleveraging speed. Furthermore, Vodafone's current capital policy prioritizes maintaining a solid investment-grade credit rating, and if organic free cash flow underperforms, leverage will expand and threaten a credit rating downgrade, forcing management to postpone or significantly downsize the planned fiscal year 2028 share buybacks.

The power of rating agencies and regulators to intervene represents a structural constraint on capital allocation flexibility. Conversely, successful execution of self-help initiatives could significantly accelerate earnings per share growth beyond current consensus expectations. Rapid cost reductions and balance sheet simplification are already driving an equity re-rating, and the full integration of VodafoneThree is projected to deliver £1.0 billion in synergies, meaning successful execution will accelerate organic cash generation and fund substantial share buybacks from fiscal year 2028.

The key judgment for investors centers on whether Vodafone can navigate German regulatory setbacks and African cash leakage while delivering on its ambitious UK integration program, thereby creating the organic cash flow and balance sheet capacity necessary to resume meaningful capital returns without compromising its investment-grade credit rating or long-term network competitiveness.
