Overview

1.
Sector-Wide Overview: De-Rating of European Staples & Household/Personal Care (2021 to Mid-2026) From 2021 through mid-2026, European consumer staples, food producers, and household and personal care (HPC) companies traded through a challenging sector-wide de-rating channel. This structural multiple compression was driven by several overlapping macroeconomic and operational headwinds:
2.
Extreme Input Cost Inflation: Unprecedented price spikes across raw agricultural commodities (cocoa, green coffee, milk proteins, wheat), crude oil derivatives, palm oil, packaging, logistics, and energy severely eroded gross margins. Volume Elasticity and Pricing Friction: Although companies initially passed through these costs via aggressive double-digit price increases, this eventually triggered severe volume (or Real Internal Growth) contraction and market share losses as cash-strapped consumers downtraded to private labels. Portfolio Complexity and Conglomerate Discounts: Corporate structures containing highly divergent assets (e.g., fast-growing retail retail concepts combined with volatile commodities, or underperforming regional brands) faced significant investor pushback.
3.
Litigation and Geopolitical Overhangs: Substantial legal liabilities (such as infant formula and hair straightener litigation) and the complex operational unwind of Russian business units weighed heavily on multiples.
4.
Macroeconomic Headwinds: Rising global interest rates and currency volatility (notably Swiss Franc strength and Euro/GBP fluctuations) depressed absolute returns and compressed forward P/E and EV/EBITDA valuation multiples. 2025 Leadership Transitions & Strategic Remodeling: Nestlé and Unilever The mid-to-late-2020s landscape has been deeply shaped by pivotal 2025 CEO appointments at the sector's two anchor companies, Nestlé and Unilever. While historical analyses focus on the onset of private label pressure, contemporary broker assessments outline highly divergent restructuring agendas:

Unilever: Fernando Fernandez (Appointed February 2025)

Under the leadership of Fernando Fernandez, who was promoted to Chief Executive in February 2025, Unilever has executed a aggressive structural shift: & Co Redburn Operational Restructuring: Fernandez dismantled the category-first organizational structure in favor of a regional-first country operating model, dividing global performance across six regional country-led units.

To support this, he appointed a new Chief Growth and Marketing Officer and a Chief Corporate Strategy Officer. Portfolio Reshaping: Fernandez accelerated a pivot toward high-growth beauty, personal care, and wellness categories. The company successfully completed the demerger of its dilutive Ice Cream business (TMICC) in December 2025 and finalized the McCormick Foods combination in March 2026. Portfolio Reshaping (cont.): He has turned over more than 15% of the portfolio and consolidated underperforming tail markets into a centralized "One Unilever Markets" division. & Co Redburn Cost Programmes: Fernandez initiated a major workforce streamlining, cutting over 20% of Unilever's non-

Cost Programmes (cont.):. The overarching productivity program targets €1 bn in gross savings by the end of 2029. While €500 mm of these savings will be absorbed by stranded costs stemming from the Foods McCormick divestiture, brokers expect €500 mm in net cost savings to flow directly to the bottom line. Capital Returns: With leverage projected to decline steadily toward 1.7x net debt/EBITDA, brokers expect Unilever to accelerate capital returns through expanded share buyback programs.

Nestlé: Philipp Navratil (Appointed September 2025)

Following the sudden dismissal of Laurent Freixe in September 2025 due to a Code of Business Conduct breach, Philipp Navratil was appointed Group CEO:

1.
Operational & Cultural Shifts: Navratil immediately initiated thorough operational reviews across category headquarters to re-establish retail pricing competitiveness Operational & Cultural Shifts (cont.):. He has worked to instill a leaner, performance-driven culture. To enhance free cash flow generation, Navratil trimmed Nestlé’s long-term tangible capital expenditure guidance to 5.2% of sales by 2029 (down from the historical 6% run-rate). Portfolio Reshaping: Nestlé has pursued targeted portfolio simplification, selling its Nestlé Waters & Premium Beverages business to Platinum Equity and selling non-core segments of its Vitamins, Minerals, and Supplements (VMS) portfolio to Yellow Wood Partners. Crucially, the company completed the complex deconsolidation of its Russian operations in late September 2026.
2.
Cost & Margin Expectations: Brokers expect Navratil's self-help and cost-containment measures to drive operating margins back toward 17% by FY2027.
3.
Capital Returns: Due to higher relative balance sheet leverage and tighter free cash flow dividend coverage, discretionary share buybacks are expected to remain constrained. Capital returns will prioritize maintaining the core progressive dividend, with divestment proceeds used to pay down debt. Capital Returns (cont.): Brokers note a potential monetization of Nestlé’s stake in L'Oréal in 2027 as a key liquidity event.
