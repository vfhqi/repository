ABB (ABBN-CH) — Q3 2026 results, 20 October 2026: what they do to the existing case

These results weaken the existing case by a modest but real amount: they confirm the demand the case rests on, and at the same time show that demand is now arriving faster than ABB can ship it, that the corporate-change programme has grown into something larger and noisier than the case sized, and that the de-rating the case feared has started without any of the triggers the case named.

What changed

The quarter is Q3 2026, the three months to 30 September 2026, reported 20 October 2026, in US dollars unless stated.

  • Group orders $12,055m, up 40% comparable, about 6% above company-compiled consensus. Revenue $9,701m, up 14% comparable, in line. Operational EBITA $2,026m, up 24%, at a 20.8% margin, up 160 basis points year on year and 20 basis points above consensus. Basic EPS $0.65, down 2% year on year and 18% below DNB Carnegie's consensus, because of a $115m non-cash loss on the deal-contingent FX hedge for Rotork. Free cash flow $1,576m against a Visible Alpha consensus of $1,224m. Backlog $32,256m, up 37% comparable; group book-to-bill 1.24x.
  • Electrification: orders $7,671m, up 70% comparable and 12% above consensus, the seventh consecutive sequential increase; revenue $5,424m, up 21%, about 1% below consensus; margin 25.6%, up 110 basis points; backlog $16bn from $13.7bn at the prior quarter-end; book-to-bill 1.41x. Data-centre orders grew at a triple-digit rate again. Management reported double-digit order growth across utilities, grid refurbishment, tunnels, bridges, airports and semiconductors, with no quantification by end market and no data-centre backlog share or customer-concentration figure.
  • Automation: orders $2,114m, flat comparable and 9% below consensus; revenue $2,126m, up 5%, 1% below consensus; book-to-bill 0.99x; margin 14.8%, up 90 basis points. Management cited large-project timing and the Middle East, and acknowledged persistent softness in chemicals, pulp and paper, metals, cement and mining.

Q4 comparable growth guided at about 14%, against Deutsche Bank's backlog-implied 16% or more. Reported operational EBITA margin improvement of at least 270 basis points for 2026, of which about 80 basis points is the CHF240m gain on the Swiss property sale completed 13 October; pre-gain margin at least 20.9% against consensus of 20.8%. The challenge

The case's own two tracking metrics were met: Electrification book-to-bill above 1.10x (1.41x) and data-centre order growth above 50% (triple-digit). On its own terms, the quarter confirms. The readings below are the ones the case does not hold.

Reading one: the bottleneck is ABB, and the case's tracking metric now measures it. Electrification orders beat by 12% and revenue missed by 1%; group book-to-bill 1.24x; Q4 guided at about 14% when backlog phasing implies at least 16%; management conceded high utilisation in some product lines and chose to hold capex at about $1bn a year. The case treated a rising book-to-bill as proof that demand converts (the secondary setup, Element E8 Required Attribute 2) and credited a "well-invested base" that deploys capital ahead of demand (Element E1 RA2 CQ2, [B]). The strongest form of this reading is that a 1.41x book-to-bill with revenue only in line is the signature of a throughput-constrained business; that the 2027 and 2028 EPS brokers now underwrite (Morgan Stanley's base case needs 16.3% organic growth in 2027 and 22% Electrification growth) depend on a conversion rate ABB has just declined to invest for; and that every extra quarter spent in backlog is deferred revenue exposed to architecture change (the Infinitus direct-current portfolio, with inventory already up about $1.2bn this year) and to customer grid-connection delays the company does not control. For it to be right: Q4 revenue growth lands at or below the 14% guide while orders stay strong, the utilisation language survives into January, and capex stays at $1bn. The case has no place for a quarter in which the demand evidence is unambiguous and the earnings do not follow, because it tests orders, not shipments.

Reading two: the corporate-change setup has turned into an integration load, and its first EPS effect was negative. The case's primary setup is corporate-change-driven EPS upgrade (Element E8 RA3, [A]), with the M&A pivot sized at USD 500m-1bn deals and "the first transaction under the new framework" named as the clearest test. Rotork is about $5.5bn, more than five times the top of that range; the Mitsubishi Electric joint venture is a second deal closing H2 2027; Robotics is being separated at the same time; and the only EPS line Rotork has produced so far is a $115m hedge loss that turned a 24% operational EBITA increase into a 2% EPS decline. The strongest form: the setup the case rated [A] is now the main source of reported-EPS noise, management attention becomes the binding constraint before balance-sheet capacity does, and the honest-tracker beat record the case leans on (Element E7 RA5, [A]; Element E1 RA2 CQ3, [B]) is harder to read for at least three more quarters. For it to be right: Rotork clearance slips or carries remedies; the joint venture's low-double-digit margin does not move toward its 20% target; and Automation, the division Rotork goes into, keeps missing on orders while integration begins. The case did not size a deal of this scale and could not have weighed a negative first effect.

Reading three: the false friend has started to play out, with none of the triggers the case named. The case's single most consequential flag (Element E8 RA8, [D]) is multiple compression in a business that keeps delivering, triggered by doubt about AI capex. What happened is the compression without the doubt: data-centre orders grew at a triple-digit rate, brokers raised 2027 EPS by 2% to 3%, and targets were cut because the peer group de-rated. Two readings follow, and the case holds neither. Either the market is pricing the 2028-29 data-centre capex slowdown ahead of the evidence, Deutsche Bank's view, in which case the operating beats are not the variable and the premium, still about 25% to Electrification peers on Morgan Stanley's numbers, is what gets removed; or the reset the case said would move conviction toward high has begun (P/E 22.0x and the 90th percentile against the case's 30.1x and 100th; price about 14% below the case's own CHF 83.58; consensus target above spot), but as a sector move that leaves ABB's relative premium intact, so it closes the absolute-valuation gap the case flagged while leaving the false-friend evidence untouched. What would make the first right: data-centre order growth below 50% in Q4 2026 or Q1 2027 as the 33% comparison bites, or visible hyperscaler credit deterioration. What would make the second right: the premium narrows toward the 20x to 22x 2027 earnings the review names, with ABB not underperforming peers on the way.

A fourth reading, weaker but not dismissible: the group is becoming a one-division company. Electrification was $1,394m of $2,026m of operational EBITA and $7,671m of $12,055m of orders; Automation's book-to-bill is 0.99x; Motion's margin fell 100 basis points. The case's segment-level test called Automation "a secondary confirmation" of the setup fit on a 3% order beat; it has just missed by 9%. This cuts both ways. It moves the case toward the two-fulcrum-driver distillation it wanted (Element E7 RA1), but it gets there by concentration rather than design, which is precisely the single-vector dependency RA8 warns about. The case holds those as two separate findings; the results show them as one fact.

What the results do not admit is a reading in which demand is weakening. Orders were broad across regions (Americas up 70% comparable, China up 23%, Europe up 10%) and end markets, no evidence of pre-ordering emerged, and no accessible broker argued otherwise. The bear case runs through conversion, concentration, integration and the multiple, not through demand.

That is the sell-side and valuation half of reading three. It will not carry evidence on conversion, capacity, Automation or integration, because no company disclosure falls in that window.
  • The Q4 2026 results in January 2027 settle reading one and most of the fourth: comparable revenue growth against the 14% guide and the 16% backlog-implied figure; formal 2027 guidance and whether it clears 12%; Automation orders and a book-to-bill back above 1.0x; data-centre order growth against a 33% comparison.
  • Rotork closing, expected H1 2027, and the joint venture closing, expected H2 2027, are the first hard tests of reading two; nothing before then can be.
  • Data-centre share of backlog, customer concentration, capacity utilisation by product line and regional Electrification margins would resolve the concentration and bottleneck questions, and management has committed to none of them. On current disclosure those questions stay open whatever January brings.
  • The Early-Stage Assessment's own planned tests, the fulcrum-driver distillation and the false-friend stress test, now work from a harder set of facts than the case anticipated.
