Section summary
  1. The proposed corporate tax hikes and energy-sector surcharges represent a major headwind for PGE's future earnings per share and capital expenditure programs.
  2. Financial returns on key transition projects like the Baltica 2 offshore wind joint venture face dilution from rising effective tax rates.

Critical uncertainty: "For PGE Polska Grupa Energetyczna (PGE-PL), what will the effective tax rate be from 2027 under the corporate income tax rise from 19% to 22% and any energy-sector surcharge under Bill UD459 or the Ministry of Finance draft of up to 30%? Section 2 – The 22% Corporate Income Tax Rise and Deferred Tax Remeasurement

Section summary
  1. The Polish corporate tax rise to 22% will increase PGE's long-term tax liabilities while triggering positive non-cash deferred tax asset adjustments.
  2. Upward revaluation of PGE's massive mine rehabilitation provisions will partially cushion the immediate impact of the tax rate hike on short-term net book value.

Legislative Action and Direct Rate Impact

1.
Significant legislative: The Polish Senate passed a major corporate income tax rate increase from 19% to 22% on November 6, 2026. This policy change directly raises the baseline corporate tax rate for all Polish domestic enterprises starting in fiscal year 2027. For the country's energy groups, this legislative action establishes a higher fiscal burden that will dilute future earnings per share across the sector.
2.
Substantial upward: PGE's future effective tax rate is expected to experience upward pressure starting in 2027 due to this nominal rate adjustment. Because certain utility expenditures and conventional generation assets are non-deductible for tax purposes, the effective rate typically exceeds the nominal statutory baseline. This structural mismatch means PGE's ultimate tax expense will expand faster than the raw 3% increase in statutory corporate rates.

Deferred Tax Assets and Mine Rehabilitation

3.
Calculated balance: PGE reported a massive provision of PLN 7,566 million for mine rehabilitation expenses as of June 30, 2026. This long-term liability, primarily tied to the Bełchatów and Turów lignite mines, acts as the primary driver for PGE's deferred tax assets. Adjusting the statutory rate from 19% to 22% will trigger a oneoff non-cash remeasurement of this asset.
4.
Positive earnings: This deferred tax asset revaluation will generate a substantial accounting benefit on PGE's balance sheet. The remeasurement of these assets at 22% will yield an estimated PLN 226.98 million (calculated) upward adjustment based on historical mine decommissioning liabilities. This accounting adjustment will directly boost short-term net profit upon formal enactment.
5.
Contrary evidence: PGE's anticipated deferred tax gains could be severely restricted if future taxable income projections do not justify full asset recognition. If future cash flows from conventional power generation deteriorate further, management may be required to write down these assets. This potential write-down would eliminate the balance sheet benefits and exacerbate net profit declines during the transition.

This adjustment will improve Enea's equity position and partially offset its transition liabilities in the coming quarters.

Section 3 – Energy-Sector Surcharges under Bill UD459 and Ministry of Finance

Section summary
  1. Proposed energy-sector surcharges under Bill UD459 threaten PGE's segment earnings, although the vertically integrated status of its distribution unit may offer an exclusion from the levy.
  2. Rising corporate tax rates of up to 30% on fuel and energy firms reflect growing fiscal deficits that squeeze PGE's cash flows.

Drafts

Legislative Framework of Bill UD459

1.
Proposed legislative: The Polish government draft Bill UD459 introduces a temporary corporate tax surcharge targeting large energy entities. This proposed tax reform applies higher tax rates to entities with revenues exceeding EUR 50 million that engage in fossil fuel extraction, fuel trading, or electricity distribution. The legislation specifically structures this surcharge as a temporary corporate income tax increase that will escalate the effective statutory tax rate up to 30% in 2027.
2.
Proportionate tax: The surcharge calculation base is defined using a proportionate share of corporate income rather than flat EBITDA thresholds. Under the draft rules, the higher tax rates apply only to a proportionate part of the tax base if the covered activities account for 33% to 75% of revenues. This net income-based structure means PGE's ultimate tax exposure is directly tied to taxable net profit rather than consolidated cash generation.
3.
Increased domestic: Tax capital groups in Poland are also heavily impacted by the proposed CIT restructuring linked to global minimum tax rules. Bill UD458 dictates that any constituent entities of multinational or domestic groups subject to Pillar Two must comply with the new CIT rates regardless of their individual revenue. This systemic integration means PGE's consolidated tax strategy must adapt to overlapping minimum taxes.
4.
Proposed revenue: The Polish Ministry of Climate and Environment is also exploring direct revenue caps on generation units to limit excess profits. These caps operate independently of the corporate income tax and are modeled after previous Price Difference Fund contributions. Combining these caps with a 30% surcharge would severely damage generation segment economics.

PGE Segment Scope and Exclusions

5.
Significant operating: PGE's core electricity distribution segment may escape the direct surcharge scope due to its specific corporate structure. Draft Bill UD459 explicitly excludes electricity distribution activities from the tax surcharge if they are carried out by vertically integrated undertakings. Because PGE operates as a fully integrated national utility, its highly profitable grid operations should avoid these specific surcharge burdens.
6.
Regulated revenue: PGE's exposure to volatile corporate tax changes is partially mitigated by the national tariff-setting mechanism administered by the Polish Energy Regulatory Office. Under Polish Energy Law, regulated tariffs are designed to cover justified operating costs, depreciation, and taxes while ensuring a reasonable return on capital. This regulatory framework ensures that tax increases are eventually passed through to end consumers.
7.
Elevated segment: PGE's reported operating results demonstrate that its distribution and conventional generation units generate the bulk of group profits. The distribution segment contributed PLN 2,769 million to the group's total H1 2026 EBITDA of PLN 8,195 million. Surcharges targeting these segments would significantly reduce PGE's cash generation capabilities and its capability to fund grid modernization.
