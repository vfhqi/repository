Kainos (KNOS-GB): what the H1 FY27 results do to the existing case

These results weaken the case, and by more than a nuance: the one test the case set for this date came back on the alarm side, the company has changed its own account of when the margin recovers, and the 12% fall that took the shares below our accumulate level happened for the reason that undermines the path on which that level was calculated.

What changed

The case set four confirming tests for 9 November 2026. Two passed strongly and two failed.

  • Passed. Revenue for the six months to 30 September 2026 was £268.8m, up 37% (the case's test: 20% or more), against a Deutsche Bank forecast of £256.1m. Adjusted pre-tax profit was £41.7m, up 30% (the test: 10% or more), against £39.5m forecast. Adjusted diluted EPS was 27.4p, up 45%.
  • Failed. Contractors were to be below 259 and falling. Technical contractors in Digital Services were 316 at H1 FY27, from 139 a year earlier, and contractors were 8% of total staff of 3,885. Group gross margin was to be above 46.1%; it was 43.1%, from 48.0% in H1 FY26.
  • Not confirmed. Workday Services gross margin was 48.5%, from 49.8%, with utilisation at 73% from 75%. The review gives no like-for-like figure, so the case's 47.7% test cannot be read cleanly; the direction is down.
  • Digital Services revenue was £162.5m, up 57%, at a gross margin of 31.9%, from 35.8%. That is below the 35.6% the case uses as its framework-pricing alarm. Partner and contractor spend in the division was £49.2m, from £15.1m.
  • Management's account of the margin has changed in two ways. About 60% of the partner and contractor increase is described as temporary capacity and about 40% as a structural result of bidding as prime contractor. And high contractor levels are now expected to persist for as long as Digital Services revenue growth stays above 20%.
  • Workday Products revenue was £48.0m, up 22%; ARR was £96.0m, up 24%, with £100m expected in December 2026. Gross margin fell to 74.3% from 77.7%, of which 1.4 points is a bonus-allocation change and the rest implementation cost on new products.
  • Contracted backlog was £853.0m, up 26%, on a new total-contract-value basis that is not comparable with the £433.9m the case recorded. Awards of £111.0m (Home Office) and £84.0m (Foreign Office) are pending. Existing customers were 82% of revenue, exactly at the case's alarm line. Cash conversion was 62%; cash and deposits were £86.5m, from £105.5m.
  • Sell-side response, Deutsche Bank only so far: Hold and a 1,260p target, both unchanged; revenue forecasts raised 2% to 3% for FY27 to FY29 (£550m, £610m, £672m); adjusted pre-tax profit unchanged at £84.4m, £92.7m and £103.2m; margins flat at 15.2% to 15.4%; EPS 53.14p, 57.37p and 63.69p. The shares fell 12% to 1,084p on the morning; the broker called the fall unwarranted. The shares were 1,090p on 11 November 2026.

The challenge

We see three readings the case does not hold. None is easy to dismiss.

1. Growth and margin recovery are now alternatives, not companions. The case's first two Key Drivers are bookings converting to revenue at the guided pace and contractor replacement restoring the services margin from H2 FY2027. Its timing mechanism was mechanical: a contractor deploys in four weeks, a permanent hire takes five months, so the margin follows the revenue with a lag. Management's new rule breaks that link. The margin recovers when Digital Services growth falls below 20%, and the division is growing at 57% with the backlog expected to pass £1bn. On that rule the case's two mis-modelled lines cannot both be right: if FY2028 revenue beats consensus as the case expects, the FY2028 margin does not recover; if the margin recovers, it is because growth has slowed. Deutsche Bank's unchanged full-year profit after a £2.2m first-half beat is an implied second-half cut of the same size, to about £42.7m on about £281m of revenue, a 15.2% margin against 15.5% in H1 (calculated). That is the opposite of the H2 FY2027 improvement the case was waiting for. For this reading to be right, public-sector awards need only keep arriving at the present rate, which is the case's own demand view.

2. The margin has not been delayed; the business has changed shape. The case treated the prime-contractor pivot as an internal change that raises what Kainos earns on the recovery. The staff-type split says it lowers the percentage margin by design. In Digital Services, partner revenue was £29.1m at a 9.6% gross margin and contractor revenue £20.1m at 24.9%; employee-delivered revenue was £113.3m at 39.0%, against 39.5% a year earlier. Two things follow. First, employee-delivered revenue grew about 28%, not 57%; some 58% of the division's revenue growth was delivered by other people's staff (calculated). Second, the in-house margin is intact, so the breach of the 35.6% alarm is mix, not price discounting. This reading cuts both ways and the case holds neither side. On the hard side, about one point of gross margin was the prize for replacing contractors; if 40% of the increase is permanent, full replacement does not return the margin to where the case's path needs it, and a 17.0% FY2029 margin is unlikely on services alone. On the kind side, gross margin may be the wrong instrument: group gross margin fell 4.9 points while the adjusted pre-tax margin fell 0.8 points, so overheads absorbed about four points, and Digital Services gross profit rose about 40%. A larger, lower-gross-margin, lower-overhead prime contractor can compound profit at a flat margin. What it probably cannot do is hold a premium services multiple, because the market pays less for pass-through revenue. What would have to be true: partner share keeps rising with each large award, and overheads keep growing slower than revenue.

3. The software mix is running backwards. The case's second reason for holding against its false-friend hypothesis was arithmetic: Workday Products at a 77.8% gross margin growing about twice the group rate lifts group gross margin about 0.3 points a year whatever services do. In this half Products grew 22% against 37% for the group, its share of revenue fell to about 18% from 20% (calculated), and its gross margin fell 3.4 points. The arithmetic now subtracts. The software line is healthy on its own tests; what has changed is that the faster Digital Services grows, the more the group is a services company, and the case names the software mix as the reason the market holds this multiple while it sells the sector. For this to matter, Digital Services need only keep outgrowing Products, which is probable for at least the next two halves.

The obvious fourth reading, that the fall is simply the case's entry point arriving, fails on the case's own construction. The roughly 1,160p level is where the modal path clears the bar, and that path carries margins of 16.4% in FY2028 and 17.0% in FY2029. The one broker to respond has FY2029 EPS at 63.69p, beside the case's drop-through figure of 63.4p rather than its modal 70.0p. The case valued the false-friend path at about 980p to 1,050p, 4% to 10% below the 11 November price.

What it does to the case

  • 'Financial Outputs' (E 2, BB 1), B. Revenue growth under less pressure. Margin under more: the level is lower and the recovery is now conditional. Cash conversion under more.
  • 'Modal return' (E 3, BB 1), C. Pulled both ways. The lower price lifts the return on the modal path; the evidence shifts weight from the modal path toward the conceded lower anchor, to which the case gave about 30%. The case's trigger for the drop-through world, contractors above 200 at 31 March 2027, is now probable on management's own rule.
  • Building Block 3, 'Major Corporate Change', B. More pressure. The case called the margin the test that separates internal change from demand recovery; this half shows demand without the margin. The competing 'Demand Inflection' reading gains. The stated condition for a fall to C, no margin movement by FY2028, is more likely to be met.
  • 'Compounder' mis-modelled lines, B. More pressure on the margin line, less on the revenue line, and the two now conflict.
  • Building Block 4, C, and Master Rating 4, 'Case Riskiness', C. 'Limited multiple risk' under less pressure at 1,090p; the case said this Building Block would be B near 1,100p, written when nothing but a dated margin was going the wrong way. The market catch-up red flag has fired as the case said it would. 'No estimate cuts' under more pressure: no stated cut, an implied one.
  • Competition and the framework-pricing risk. The same or slightly less: the alarm level was breached by mix, and the in-house margin held.
  • The pinned letters, as context. TSR/Valuation stands at B (11 November 2026) against the memo's C; Technical Momentum at A against B; Thematic at C against D; Sell-side Momentum unchanged at C. The valuation field still shows 19.2x on 62.63p, the figures the case recorded at 1,211p; at 1,090p that arithmetic is 17.4x, so we would not lean on the B until it is rebuilt.
  • Price discipline. 1,090p is 6% below the accumulate level of about 1,160p, 5% above the case's de-rating branch at about 1,033p, and above its three-year bar-clearing price of about 1,005p. The price condition is met; the evidence condition the case attached to this date is refuted.

What would settle it

  • The second review, four working days on. It will carry the other brokers' estimates and targets. Three things to read: whether FY2028 EPS consensus holds above the case's 59.1p confirm level; whether anyone other than Deutsche Bank models a margin above 15.4% for FY2028; whether targets come down toward the price. It will carry no new company disclosure. It cannot tell us whether contractors are falling.
  • December 2026. ARR passing £100m. It confirms the third Key Driver and says nothing about the challenge.
  • FY2027 results, May 2027. The first hard evidence: contractor numbers at 31 March 2027 against the case's 200, Digital Services gross margin against 31.9%, and FY2028 guidance. The sell-side's own marker for a change of view is contractors below 5% of headcount and a services gross margin above 35%.
  • What nothing yet promises to answer. The staff-type split that separates in-house from partner margin was given this time; we have no commitment that it is repeated, and without it the first two readings cannot be told apart. Management has put no date and no number on the recovery, only the 20% growth condition. Workday Services like-for-like margin is not in the evidence we have. These are open questions of the second kind: the company may never close them, and we would have to infer the answer from gross profit growth against revenue growth.
