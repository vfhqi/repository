Earnings momentum rank: Fortum sits in the 40th percentile against its five closest peers on consolidated earnings momentum, with a flattish earnings growth profile compared to high-growth peers, while RWE leads with an estimated 13% earnings CAGR. [Cross-ref: Section 7 + Section 5]
2.
Hedge-driven revision pressure: Fortum hedged 45% of its 2028 output at EUR 42/MWh, below the expected EUR 44/MWh, and Morgan Stanley warned this could trigger a 5% to 10% downgrade in consensus EPS, whereas peer RWE has seen upward estimate revisions. [Cross-ref: Section 6 + Section 7 + Section 8]
3.
Broker sentiment skew: Bloomberg consensus dated 3 September 2026 shows 52% of analysts at Underweight or Sell, 43% at Equal-weight or Hold, and only 5% at Overweight or Buy, a distribution weighted heavily toward neutral and negative views. [Cross-ref: Section 8]
4.
Geographic concentration: Fortum derives 88% of its comparable EBITDA from power generation, concentrated in Sweden and Finland, whereas Vattenfall maintains a more integrated profile that provides superior geographic diversification, so any regional power price drop will impact Fortum more severely than its diversified peers. [Cross-ref: Section 7 + Section 8]
5.
Growth visibility gap: RWE's diversified technology pipeline provides far greater growth visibility than Fortum's pure-play Nordic focus, while the plausibility of Fortum's own rapid capacity growth is medium because current market power prices make unhedged projects uneconomical. [Cross-ref: Section 7 + Section 9]
6.
Valuation premium without growth support: Fortum trades at a premium to the sector median on a 2027 enterprise value to operating profit basis even though it shows a flatter intermediate growth profile than its European utility peers. [Cross-ref: Section 4 + Section 8 + Section 11]
7.
Volume and consensus execution: Fortum's second-quarter 2026 comparable EBIT missed consensus by 13% due to severe nuclear generation outages and poor hydrology, and 2026 nuclear output guidance narrowed to approximately 23 TWh, below the normal baseline of 26 TWh. [Cross-ref: Section 5 + Section 6 + Section 10]
