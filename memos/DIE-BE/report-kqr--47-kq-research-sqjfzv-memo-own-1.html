Historical precedents of European and emerging-market holding companies listing or partially selling core assets while maintaining control reveal sharply divergent outcomes for holding company discount trajectories over the subsequent twelve to twenty-four months. Fiat Chrysler Automobiles completed the listing of Ferrari in late 2015 before spinning off the remaining asset in early 2016, with Exor retaining control through a 23.5% common share stake and leveraging a loyalty voting mechanism under Dutch law to secure approximately 33.4% of total voting power, allowing Exor to maintain dominant influence without requiring majority equity ownership. This structural approach successfully established transparent market pricing while preserving family control, demonstrating a viable template for D'Ieteren's pending transaction.

The quantitative development of net asset value discounts across comparable precedents demonstrates that capital allocation strategy heavily dictates whether listing transactions succeed in compressing valuation gaps. Investor AB experienced a temporary widening of its net asset value discount following the IPO of EQT in late 2019, trading at a 22.5% discount to net asset value in line with its five-year average of 24%, but proactive asset management and solid earnings growth subsequently allowed the company to narrow the holding discount to approximately 12% over the next twenty-four months.

By sharp contrast, Prosus NV witnessed its holding discount expand significantly in the twenty-four months following its Amsterdam listing in September 2019, with initial optimism quickly withering as the discount widened to 26% and subsequently expanded to 40% due to ongoing investments, demonstrating that listing a core asset without simplification can deepen the holding company discount. critical distinction between successful and unsuccessful discount compression centers on whether holding companies pair asset monetization events with aggressive shareholder distributions or capital returns.

Naspers and Prosus successfully executed a programmatic, open-ended share buyback program starting in June 2022, funding this massive capital return through orderly, on-market partial sales of their liquid Tencent stake, with this continuous buyback program critical to supporting Prosus's valuation and adding 16% in incremental net asset value per share accretion for continuing shareholders over the program's multi-year duration.

However, Prosus's massive 10 billion dollar share buyback program in fiscal year 2026 failed to compress its combined holding company discount, with the net asset value discount remaining flat at approximately 43% despite returning billions of dollars to free-float shareholders, as persistent structural tax leakages and complex crossshareholding relationships continue to offset the benefits of buybacks. This mixed outcome demonstrates that aggressive capital distributions alone cannot resolve deep holding discounts if fundamental governance and structural complexities remain unaddressed.

Alternative precedents further illustrate the importance of distribution pairing strategies. Kinnevik completed the divestment of its mature, listed Tele2 stake in 2024 to pivot toward early-stage private assets, with the board deciding to distribute the gross sales proceeds of 12.3 billion SEK back to shareholders through an extraordinary cash distribution of 23 SEK per share, but this radical shift in capital allocation ultimately drove Kinnevik's net asset value discount wider because the unlisted portfolio lacked public market transparency. Conversely, Investor

AB has historically avoided pairing major asset listings with large-scale share buybacks or extraordinary distributions, instead utilizing its robust cash flows to reinvest in unlisted holdings and fund regular dividend growth, with its active ownership model and consistent net asset value outperformance allowing the company to sustain a narrow valuation discount of only 11% without programmatic share repurchases. This demonstrates that holding companies can achieve premium valuations through superior long-term returns on unlisted assets rather than purely through distribution mechanics. assumptions embedded in valuation models explicitly incorporate expectations about capital allocation following the Belron liquidity event.

Jefferies Research increased its discounted cash flow enterprise valuation for Belron to 33.8 billion euros, implying a multiple of twenty times fiscal year 2026 expected EBIT, while an October 2024 minority transaction of a 1.4% stake valued Belron at 32.2 billion euros, indicating that private market valuations remain robust despite high leverage. These broker figures assume that D'Ieteren will successfully maintain its controlling stake while unlocking partial liquidity for institutional backers, but elevated leverage and high interest rates could depress Belron's public market valuation, with the group's substantial debt of over eight billion euros remaining a key concern, suggesting that if public markets penalize the company for its debt load, the holding discount may not narrow and the anticipated liquidity event might disappoint investors by failing to rerate the shares.

Critical evidence gaps persist regarding the specific transaction structure, governance terms, and capital allocation framework that will accompany any Belron listing. D'Ieteren has not officially confirmed adviser appointments beyond market reports of Rothschild involvement, has not specified whether Amsterdam or New York will serve as the listing venue, and has provided no public guidance on post-transaction lock-up provisions, loyalty voting structures, or share buyback commitments.

Future expert calls with capital markets advisers would help clarify whether a primary raise will occur, which would directly impact Belron's interest expenses, while historical precedents such as Exor and Naspers demonstrate that pairing listings with distributions is essential to narrow the persistent holding discount, with future investor presentations during the annual meeting in May 2027 expected to outline capital allocation priorities that will determine long-term earnings per share accretion.

Without transparent disclosure of these structural elements, investors face substantial uncertainty about whether the transaction will successfully compress D'Ieteren's 45% holding discount or instead replicate the disappointing Prosus precedent where persistent governance complexity sustained wide valuation gaps despite aggressive capital returns. The domestic automotive segment presents an additional earnings headwind that complicates the group's overall valuation trajectory regardless of Belron transaction outcomes.

D'Ieteren Group reported on September 9, 2026, that its automotive segment suffered a 66.6% decline in adjusted pre-tax profit, with the segment's operating margin declining materially to 2.1% due to negative volume and distribution margin pressure, directly reducing group earnings per share unless offset by stronger performance in aftermarket segments. This operational deterioration underscores the strategic imperative of successfully executing the Belron liquidity event to fund capital recycling into higher-margin unlisted growth platforms like Parts Holding Europe, which could fundamentally regear group earnings toward defensive distribution businesses and offset structural weakness in vehicle retailing.

Successful execution of the Belron liquidity transaction paired with transparent governance improvements and shareholder-friendly capital allocation represents the most credible pathway to narrow D'Ieteren's deep holding company discount over the next twelve to twenty-four months, but precedent evidence demonstrates that structural complexity, reinvestment risks, and macroeconomic headwinds could prevent discount compression even if the transaction closes successfully, requiring investors to monitor upcoming corporate disclosures regarding transaction structure, distribution commitments, and unlisted asset performance to assess whether the group can replicate Investor AB's successful narrowing trajectory rather than Prosus's persistent wide discount.

Evergreen investments: Sofina SA operates as a listed Belgian holding company that specializes in minority participations and private equity funds.
