Section 2 – Shaper-Class Delivery, Financing, and Capex Schedules

Section summary
  1. Wallenius Wilhelmsen is rapidly executing its newbuilding program to secure modern shipping capacity and expand its fleet size.
  2. Post-delivery bank financing is secured for most vessels, which reduces the immediate cash flow strain on the carrier.
  3. We have high confidence in this segment because the capital schedules are fully documented in recent company filings.

Shaper-Class capital commitments

1.
Rapid execution: Wallenius Wilhelmsen is aggressively expanding its maritime capacity through a highly structured vessel acquisition program. The company currently has a massive orderbook that was expanded to up to 26 vessels, with two already delivered as of November 2026. This fleet expansion is critical for the operator to capture growing automotive volumes out of East Asia. Consequently, early delivery of these vessels will support the company's medium-term earnings per share growth.
2.
Declining obligations: Wallenius Wilhelmsen has successfully reduced its near-term capital expenditure commitments through scheduled yard payments. The remaining capital commitment for the remaining 13 contracted newbuilds stood at approximately USD 1.2 billion at September 30, 2026. This represents a solid reduction from the USD 1.4 billion of capital expenditure remaining at the end of June 2026. Managing these cash outflows is essential to protect free cash flow to equity metrics.
3.
Structured installments: Wallenius Wilhelmsen is operating under a strict payment schedule designed to minimize liquidity volatility. The carrier had previously outlined a remaining installment schedule of USD 430 million for 2026, USD 665 million for 2027, and USD 342 million for 2028 at the end of 2025. The group has secured post-delivery bank financing for 11 of the 14 vessel newbuilds on 18-22 year profiles. This long-term debt profile aligns perfectly with the operational life of the modern vessels. Securing this longterm financing limits immediate refinancing risks and supports dividend distributions.

Section summary

Fleet depreciation and useful lives

1.
Longterm useful lives: Wallenius Wilhelmsen employs a depreciation policy of 27-30 years for its vessels to reflect their long operational utility. This policy is slightly longer than the 25-year standard applied by peers like Diana Shipping to their dry bulk fleets. Amortizing these assets over a longer period reduces annual non-cash depreciation charges on the income statement.
2.
Incremental vessel depreciation: Wallenius Wilhelmsen reported vessel and dry-docking depreciation of USD 221 mm for the first nine months of 2026. This represents a massive expense base compared to smaller pure-play peers such as Höegh Autoliners, which also utilizes a 30-year useful life for its roll-on/roll-off fleet. The arrival of multiple Shaper-class vessels will inevitably drive a steep depreciation step-up over the next four years.
3.
Peer depreciation benchmark: Mitsui O.S.K. Lines similarly utilizes straight-line depreciation for its massive global vessel fleet. Mitsui O.S.K. Lines experienced a positive earnings impact when it extended the useful life of its ferry vessels from 15 to 20 years in 2024. This peer adjustment demonstrates how accounting policy shifts can materially alter shipping operating profit metrics.
4.
Contrary evidence: Historical precedents from the offshore sector show that depreciating vessels over 5 to 25 years can lead to sudden, massive impairment charges if market demand shifts prematurely. If Wallenius Wilhelmsen faces rapid technology obsolescence from alternative fuels, its long useful lives could be compromised. However, management believes that its fuel-flexible design mitigates these long-term asset impairment risks.
5.
Rising asset base: Wallenius Wilhelmsen saw its total vessel and tangible asset base grow to USD 6.96 bn by the end of September 2026. This asset growth was driven by continued installments and the delivery of the first Shaper-class newbuilds. As more high-value vessels join the active fleet, the rising asset base will weigh on return on capital unless freight rates remain elevated.

Lease liabilities and vessel chartering

6.

The company paid USD 130 mm in lease repayments during the third quarter of 2026 alone. These large recurring cash outflows represent a significant prior claim on operating cash before free cash flow to equity is calculated.
7.
Peer charter strategies: Höegh Autoliners maintains a much lower lease liability profile, with current lease liabilities of only USD 5 mm at the end of 2025. Höegh Autoliners had only one vessel chartered-in on a longterm time charter at year-end 2025, preferring an owned-fleet model. By contrast, Wallenius Wilhelmsen relies heavily on chartered-in tonnage, which increases operational and lease liability leverage compared to its pure-play competitor.
8.
Diversified peer commitments: Mitsui O.S.K. Lines manages its extensive vessel chartering commitments through both short-term and long-term operating leases. The Japanese shipping giant reported lease liabilities of JPY 161.69 bn on its balance sheet as of September 30, 2026. This massive lease portfolio highlights how major global car carriers utilize off-balance and on-balance financing to maximize active fleet capacity flexibility.
9.
Operational cash pressure: Wallenius Wilhelmsen faces rising charter-in rates as global vessel capacity remains tight across major long-haul trade routes. High time-charter rates, which exceeded USD 80 k per day for certain newbuilds in late 2026, will drive lease additions higher. These rising lease costs will restrict margin expansion in logistics and shipping segments over the next 18 months.
10.
Rising lease additions: Wallenius Wilhelmsen recognized USD 194 mm in lease additions for right-of-use vessel assets during the first nine months of 2026. This rate of additions exceeds the lease activity of peers such as International Seaways, which maintains highly restricted charter-in commitments. These rising capitalized leases will inflate the reported lease liabilities on the balance sheet over the coming years.

Section 4 – EUKOR Minority Share, Put Option, and Tonnage-Tax Regimes

Section summary
  1. Wallenius Wilhelmsen has deferred the earliest exercise date of the EUKOR put option to 2031, significantly reducing near-term liquidity pressure.
  2. The group maintains a highly predictable and low tax expense under the Norwegian tonnage-tax regime.
  3. We have high confidence in this segment because the option deferral and tax policies are legally documented in recent filings.
  4. Wallenius Wilhelmsen raised USD 300 million in October 2026 to support its ambitious Shaper-class fleet expansion program.
  5. The carrier manages its dividend payouts under a strict USD 1 billion minimum liquidity buffer.
  6. Unmitigated US port fee exposure represents a major risk, but active customer recovery strategies aim to protect margins.

EUKOR minority profit flows

1.
Significant earnings allocation: Wallenius Wilhelmsen allocates a large portion of its consolidated net income to the 20% non-controlling interest in EUKOR Car Carriers. In 2022, the group recorded a total net profit of USD 794 mm, of which USD 116 mm was attributable to this minority shareholder. This substantial cash leakage directly reduces the remaining income available to the parent company's shareholders. Consequently, monitoring these minority distributions is essential to forecast net earnings per share accurately.
2.
Continuous dividend flows: EUKOR Car Carriers distributes regular dividends to its shareholders, which include the Hyundai Motor Group. For the fiscal year ended December 31, 2022, Wallenius Wilhelmsen reported that dividends paid to non-controlling interests reached USD 16 mm. These payments represent a direct outflow of cash from the joint venture's net assets. This cash diversion limits the capital that Wallenius Wilhelmsen can reinvest into its primary operations.
3.
Stable joint ownership: Wallenius Wilhelmsen has maintained its 80% control share in EUKOR Car Carriers since the joint venture was established in 2002. The remaining 20% interest is held by Hyundai Motor Group, creating a strong commercial alignment. In contrast, some smaller peers do not have similar large-scale automotive joint ventures. This structural alliance secures stable cargo volumes for the fleet.
4.
Strategic option deferral: Wallenius Wilhelmsen and Hyundai Motor Group agreed in April 2026 to defer the earliest exercisable date of the EUKOR put and call options to January 1, 2031. This deferral ensures that the option remains non-exercisable during the term of the current ocean carrier contract which runs until December 31, 2029. The agreement provides the group with a long-term operational window. This extended timeline effectively removes short-term liquidity risks.

Option deferral and valuation

5.
Complex valuation methodology: The exercise price for the EUKOR put option is determined by a strict formula dictated by the Korean Inheritance and Donation Tax Act. This statutory valuation is based on the highest of the earnings value per share and the net asset value per share. Historically, the earnings value has exceeded the net asset value because of high cyclical shipping profits. This calculation formula creates significant balance sheet liability volatility.
6.
Altered accounting treatment: Wallenius Wilhelmsen transitioned to recognizing the EUKOR put option as a full financial liability following auditor recommendations. From the second quarter of 2026, this liability is measured at the net present value of the future exercise price. The restated accounting approach reduced the group's reported equity by USD 929 mm at the end of the first quarter of 2024. This non-cash adjustment lowered the reported equity ratio.
7.
Massive currency sensitivity: The EUKOR put option liability is calculated in Korean Won, making it highly sensitive to foreign exchange rate movements. Financial disclosures indicate that a 10% movement in the USD/KRW exchange rate results in a USD 81 mm valuation impact. These currency-driven valuation shifts are recognized directly within equity rather than through the income statement. Consequently, exchange rate fluctuations will cause ongoing equity balance changes.

Regulatory tax regime dynamics

8.
Favorable tonnage taxation: Wallenius Wilhelmsen benefits from the Norwegian tonnage-tax regime, which exempts qualifying shipping income from ordinary corporate tax. Instead of paying 22% tax on shipping profits, the company pays a fee based on the net tonnage of its active vessels. This tax structure provides the shipping services segment with a highly predictable cost base. This favorable tax regime supports high net profit margins.
9.
Managed Pillar Two exposure: Wallenius Wilhelmsen faces limited financial exposure under the newly enacted OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax regulations. The group recognized a Pillar Two top-up tax provision of only USD 0.8 mm for the third quarter of 2026. This small tax liability is calculated based on a 15% minimum tax rate on profits from stateless entities. These low tax figures suggest that Pillar Two will not depress EPS.
10.
Peer tax comparison: Höegh Autoliners also operates under the Norwegian tonnage-tax regime and enjoys a similar exemption on international shipping income. The pure-play peer expects no additional tax liabilities under Pillar Two because shipping profits are excluded from the global minimum tax base. Similarly, other maritime entities like DOF Group report a low tax rate under these regimes. This industry-wide protection preserves capital for fleet renewal.
11.
Contrary evidence: Unexpected regulatory audits or changes in tax interpretations could strip shipping companies of their tonnage-tax eligibility. In 2022, the Norwegian Tax Administration ruled that Ugland Shipping had to pay ordinary taxes instead of tonnage-tax, creating a temporary NOK 77 mm liability. Although Ugland reversed this on appeal in 2025, the case proves that tax compliance errors can trigger massive retroactive tax expenses.

Peer currency and tax comparisons

12.
Divergent peer reporting: Hyundai Glovis operates under a different financial framework because it uses the Korean Won as its functional currency. This accounting policy contrasts with Wallenius Wilhelmsen and other international carriers that report in US Dollars. Denominating depreciation and amortization in Korean Won while earning revenue in US Dollars introduces exchange rate disparities. This currency mismatch creates unique margin volatility patterns.
13.
High currency exposure: Hyundai Glovis experiences significant operating profit fluctuations due to its functional currency and shipping contract structures. Jefferies reported in 2025 that Korean Won depreciation boosts the carrier's operating profits because its shipping revenues are mostly dollar-denominated. Conversely, Won appreciation historically depresses its logistics and distribution margins. This sensitivity demonstrates how foreign exchange trends dictate peer profitability.
14.
Strategic partnership stability: Wallenius Wilhelmsen's joint venture structure is highly protected by the ocean carrier contract with Hyundai Motor Group. Because the contract commits at least 50% of the customer's volumes, the joint venture remains highly viable. This solid commercial arrangement protects the 80% stake from sudden cargo losses. This strong alignment guarantees long-term earnings stability.
15.
Other side of the story: Wallenius Wilhelmsen could face severe balance sheet pressure if the USD/KRW exchange rate moves unfavorably, inflating the put option's value. Although the option's exercise date has been deferred to 2031, any massive escalation in EUKOR's taxable earnings will increase the future exercise price. If the option is eventually exercised, the resulting cash outflow of hundreds of millions of dollars would severely restrict future capital allocation.

Share capital and October private placement

This placement added 17.15 million new shares, which expands the outstanding equity base and improves stock liquidity. Consequently, the expanded share count will dilute near-term earnings per share but provides critical funding for fleet expansion capital expenditure.

Although the dilution from the 17.15 million new shares initially pressures the bottom line, the fresh cash helps preserve the firm's USD 1 billion liquidity target. Ultimately, this equity cushion supports the group's massive shipyard commitments without forcing additional expensive debt drawdowns.

Moderate dilution effect: Wallenius Wilhelmsen must balance its capital increases against the immediate dilution felt by existing minority holders. While the USD 300 million private placement dilutes earnings metrics, it provides immediate non-debt funding for the Shaper-class fleet expansion. The capital raise is crucial because it ensures the carrier can fund its advanced vessel program. This balanced funding mix is expected to protect long-term equity returns.

Dividend policy and parent company interplay
