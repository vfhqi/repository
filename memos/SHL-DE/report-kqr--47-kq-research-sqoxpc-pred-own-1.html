Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. The planned carve-out of Olyrian Diagnostics and the upcoming spin-off of a 34.1% stake by Siemens AG will significantly reshape Siemens Healthineers' risk profile and earnings over the next three years.

Defining the

1.
Key Question Key question: "For Siemens Healthineers' planned separation of its Olyrian Diagnostics business, what do precedents of in-vitro diagnostics and medtech divisional separations (for example Johnson & Johnson's sale of Ortho Clinical Diagnostics to Carlyle in 2014, and other diagnostics sales, listings and spin-offs since 2015) show about the valuation multiples achieved, the time taken, the stranded costs left with the parent and the effect on the parent's earnings per share; what valuations have brokers published for Olyrian, and what have management and brokers said about the intended use of proceeds? "
2.
Strategic transformation: Siemens Healthineers has officially initiated the carve-out of its diagnostics division into Olyrian Diagnostics, aiming for complete standalone status by mid-calendar year 2028. This separation process will allow the company to focus on its high-margin Imaging and Precision Therapy segments, boosting group profitability. Management believes this portfolio reshaping will be viewed positively by the financial markets.
3.
Growth acceleration: Redburn argues that the long-awaited exit from diagnostics should enhance the parent company's growth rate by approximately 150 basis points. It will also improve group margins and free cash flow as the core business Radiology market maintains market leadership. The clear timeline offsets immediate margin pressure and provides long-term visibility for double-digit earnings growth starting in fiscal year 2028.
4.
Transition headwinds: Siemens Healthineers' management expects Olyrian Diagnostics to continue its internal transformation program to achieve mid-teens margins over the medium term. The division has already demonstrated decisive action by expanding its margins by seven percentage points from fiscal year 2023 to fiscal year 2025. Standalone status is targeted by the middle of calendar year 2028 at the earliest.

Ownership Structure and

5.
Parent Dynamics Productivity initiatives: Deutsche Bank Research reports that Siemens Healthineers is launching its Transform4Future productivity program to counter over €300 million in stranded costs. These stranded costs stem from the diagnostics carve-out, ultrasound refocusing, and the deconsolidation from Siemens AG. The cost-saving program is designed to become fully effective from fiscal year 2028 through fiscal year 2030.
6.
Parent spin-off: Deutsche Bank Research notes that Siemens AG plans to spin off a 34.1% stake in Siemens Healthineers to its own shareholders in May 2027. This transaction will reduce Siemens AG's ownership to 33%, triggering the official deconsolidation of the medical technology unit. Siemens AG aims to eventually reduce its holding below 20%, turning its residual interest into a financial asset.
7.
Overhang risks: Bernstein Research reports that while the parent spin-off removes near-term structural uncertainty, Siemens AG's plan to reduce its stake below 20% remains an overhang. This long-term disinvestment target creates a persistent share overhang that could suppress multiple expansion over the next three years. Shares are expected to face technical pressure as further market sell-downs occur.

Contrary Evidence and

8.
Alternatives Financial adjustments: Morgan Stanley expects Siemens Healthineers to incur about €1.1 billion in adjustment items during fiscal year 2027, with the majority driven by €600 million of separation-related items. These separation costs cover the deconsolidation from Siemens AG, the Olyrian Diagnostics carve-out, and the repositioning of the ultrasound business. These temporary financial burdens are expected to suppress near-term reported profitability before the separation yields operational efficiencies.
9.
Contrary evidence: Bernstein Research warns that an earlier-than-expected trade sale of the Olyrian Diagnostics division is highly unlikely due to a lack of strategic buyers in the centralized laboratory market. The top market players already possess competitive in-house testing solutions and would gain few synergies from acquiring Olyrian. Consequently, Siemens Healthineers must commit to the full, multi-year carve-out timeline to demonstrate margin progression first.
10.
Other side of the story: While the carve-out is expected to eliminate earnings dilution, the immediate transition period will be characterized by severe margin headwinds, stranded corporate costs, and massive refinancing requirements. To ensure a smooth transition, Siemens Healthineers agreed to pay Siemens AG a fixed fee of €300 million over a 36-month transition period to use the Siemens brand. These non-recurring transition expenses will be adjusted in the reported figures to avoid impacting adjusted earnings per share.

Section 2 – Precedents of Medtech and Diagnostics Separations

Section summary
  1. Medtech divisional separations historically achieve valuations ranging from two to seventeen times key financial metrics depending on transaction structures.
  2. Parent companies frequently face substantial stranded costs and earnings dilution that require post-separation productivity programs or share buybacks.

Scope of

1.
Historical Analysis Systematic analysis: The historical record provides a deep repository of benchmark data for medical technology and diagnostic carve-outs. The researcher identified 22 relevant documents, eight distinct companies, and five comparable past cases across these materials, utilizing all of them to analyze historical separations. These precedents show that corporate parents must carefully manage stranded overhead and dilution to protect long-term earnings per share after divestitures.

Valuation Multiples of

2.
Past Deals Modest valuation: Johnson & Johnson achieved a valuation of approximately $4 billion when it completed the sale of its Ortho Clinical Diagnostics business in June 2014. This transaction price represented an enterprise value of approximately 2.1x annual sales based on the division generating $1.9 billion in revenue during 2013. The private equity buyer, The Carlyle Group, acquired the business to capitalize on corporate carve-out opportunities in the diagnostics market.
3.
Elevated valuation: Novartis secured a significantly higher multiple during the spin-off of its Alcon ophthalmology division in April 2019. Jefferies Research ascribed Alcon an equity value of $28 billion to $29 billion in December 2018. This valuation represented approximately seventeen times forward EBITDA, which was substantially above standard diagnostic divestiture levels.
4.
Robust capitalization: Becton Dickinson structured the spinoff of its Embecta diabetes unit in April 2022 to optimize capital allocation.
5.
49 Morgan Stanley notes that Embecta paid Becton Dickinson a $1.44 billion distribution payment, which was funded entirely by new debt raised by the spin-off entity. The transaction left Embecta with an estimated EBITDA margin of approximately 40% on its standalone business.

Transaction Structures and

6.
Timelines Prolonged preparation: Novartis spent nearly two years planning and preparing for the tax-neutral Alcon spin-off before securing shareholder approval. The parent company officially announced its intention to seek approval in June 2018 and completed the transaction in April 2019. This long timeline highlights the complexity of separating shared global assets and setting up independent corporate entities.
7.
Rapid execution: Johnson & Johnson executed the sale of its Ortho Clinical Diagnostics business on a more accelerated timeframe. The parent company accepted Carlyle's binding offer in March 2014 and closed the deal in June 2014. This swift exit successfully mitigated near-term operational disruptions but required immediate capital allocation plans to address the lost earnings.
8.
Contrary evidence: Deutsche Bank Research highlights that private trade sales in diagnostics can face highly challenging comparables during periods of intense currency volatility. Johnson & Johnson struggled with tough clinical diagnostics growth comparisons prior to its 2014 sale because of severe competitive pricing dynamics and foreign exchange headwinds. This suggests that trade sales may achieve lower multiples if executed during unfavorable macroeconomic cycles.

Stranded Costs and

9.
Parent Burden Persistent overhead: Zimmer Biomet experienced substantial stranded corporate costs following the spin-off of its under-scale ZimVie spine and dental business in March 2022. The parent company had to absorb $40 million to $50 million in stranded costs for the year. This burden illustrates how parent companies often retain unallocated expenses that depress adjusted operating margins post-separation.
10.
Heavy dilution: Baxter International faced intense margin contraction when it separated its Baxalta biopharmaceuticals business in 2015. The parent company retained unallocated corporate overhead that created 170 basis points of stranded costs immediately after the spin-off. Baxter relied on transition service agreements to temporarily offset some expenses, but ultimately had to absorb these costs as the arrangements expired.
11.
Severe margin impact: Baxter International encountered similar stranded cost headwinds during its preparation for the Kidney Care business separation. The corporate costs previously allocated to the Kidney Care segment created a 240 basis points negative impact on thirdquarter 2024 adjusted operating margins. Management launched restructuring initiatives to fully offset these unallocated expenses and mitigate long-term margin erosion.
12.
Strategic benchmarking: Morgan Stanley reports that typical medical technology spin-offs incur one-time separation costs equal to 3% to 4% of sales. Additionally, the newly formed standalone companies face recurring stand-up expenses of 1% to 2% of sales to replicate corporate functions. These benchmark figures indicate that divisional separations require substantial up-front and ongoing investments that dilute initial synergy savings.

Effect on Parent Earnings and

13.
Mitigation Neutralized dilution: Johnson & Johnson managed the immediate earnings dilution from its Ortho Clinical Diagnostics divestiture by deploying cash proceeds strategically. The division's exit created an initial earnings drag of approximately $0.05 to $0.08 per share. Johnson & Johnson offset this dilution by launching a $5 billion share repurchase program to reduce its outstanding share count.
14.
Operational realignment: Becton Dickinson utilized the spin-off of its diabetes business to refine its focus and accelerate growth. Morgan Stanley expects the transaction to allow Becton Dickinson to deliver consistent double-digit earnings per share growth. The parent company reinvested its capital into high-growth opportunities like connected care to replace the lost mature revenue.
15.
Comprehensive restructuring: Becton Dickinson initiated comprehensive restructuring plans to streamline its cost structure after the Embecta separation. These initiatives aimed to significantly reduce post-separation operational complexity and optimize the supply chain across continuing segments. The corporate realignment was designed to protect the parent's continuing operating margins from lingering transitional inefficiencies.
16.
Other side of the story: While separations are designed to unlock shareholder value, they often leave the parent company with complex financial entanglements and multi-year restructuring burdens. Becton Dickinson incurred significant ongoing post-separation transition expenses and foreign tax liabilities long after the official transaction date. Similarly, Zimmer Biomet had to manage post-separation costs and supply agreements that continued to impact its reported financial results for several quarters.

Section 4 – Intended Use of Proceeds and Parent Capital Allocation

Section summary
  1. Siemens Healthineers management has committed to structuring the Olyrian Diagnostics separation to maximize shareholder value rather than treating it as a traditional cash-generating divestment.
  2. Postseparation capital allocation will prioritize de-leveraging to a 2.5x net debt to EBITDA target and executing the Transform4Future productivity program to offset substantial stranded costs.

Strategic Intent and Separation Routes

1.
Clear strategic positioning: Siemens Healthineers management has clarified that the planned Olyrian Diagnostics separation will not be treated as a typical asset divestment to generate liquidity. Chief Financial Officer Jochen Schmitz emphasized that shareholders already own the diagnostics business directly, meaning any transaction is not a source of funds for separate corporate ventures. Instead, management intends to focus any changes in capital structure solely on maximizing shareholder value creation.
2.
Open optionality: Jefferies Research reports that Siemens Healthineers is keeping all separation pathways open, balancing speed against the ultimate achieved valuation. Although direct trade sales remain possible, strategic buyers face high antitrust hurdles, whereas private equity acquisitions appear highly credible given recent market transactions. The broker expects the carve-out to achieve standalone status by mid-calendar year 2028, with capital return decisions prioritizing direct share distributions.
