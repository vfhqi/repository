[Materiality of] Investment Thesis (BB 1) Rating = C

Thesis. Arteche is a 'Demand Inflection' case that has already happened: policy-mandated grid spending has filled the order book of a homologation-protected niche manufacturer, and scarcity has turned that demand into margin, a doubling of earnings and a raised guidance range. At €29.5 the shares have paid for that in advance, so our modal return from here is about zero over eighteen months and about 4% to 8% a year over three years. It is a business to own at about €22 or below, not at €29.5.

  • The external change is policy-mandated, sector-wide and intensifying, which is as strong as an external force gets in this universe.
    • Spain authorised a further €17.9bn of grid investment to 2030 on 28-Jul-2026, with 20% of the distribution budget earmarked for voltage control, which is the equipment Arteche sells
    • the US Defense Production Act determinations of 21-Apr-2026 named transformers, substations and protective relays as critical resources
    • high-voltage transformer lead times run 2.5 to 4.5 years.
    • Order intake rose 15.7% to €338m in H1 2026 and the backlog reached a record €382m at Q1 2026, so the force has reached the company's own order book and not only the sector's.
  • The internal change is broad rather than deep, and it responds to the external force rather than originates it.
    • Guidance was raised at every line on 28-Jul-2026
    • three acquisitions and two joint ventures moved the company from hardware into automation software
    • the payout doubled to 50%
    • the main-market listing of 02-Feb-2026 and the €100m placement of 25-Jun-2026 built a float.
    • There is no chief-executive change, and the acquisitions are small against a revenue base of about €577m.
  • 'Financial Outputs' have improved, and most of the improvement is delivered rather than forecast.
    • The EBITDA margin rose from 10.1% in FY2023 to 18.4% in H1 2026
    • earnings per share roughly doubled to €0.79 in FY2025 and rose 44% to €0.54 in H1 2026
    • FY2026 EBITDA guidance now reads €92.1m to €100.4m.
    • The improvement is concentrated in one lever, margin.
      • Revenue growth of 10.7% in H1 2026 is inside the target band but below FY2025's 15.3%, and cash conversion went backwards in FY2025 before recovering in H1 2026.
  • The change has travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, including the share price.
    • It shows in the company's choices and actions, its key operating metrics, its financial statements, the sell-side's estimates (earnings per share revisions +14% over six months and price targets +38% to 2026-07-16) and a share price up 81.7% over twelve months.
    • A change that has reached the last layer leaves less ahead: the major upgrade to the earnings path that the pattern describes is behind us, not in front of us.
  • From here the earnings path is ordinary, about 10% to 13% a year.
    • The below-EBITDA boost that turned EBITDA growth of 44% into net profit growth of 93% in FY2025 is spent and turns slightly negative
    • most of the margin gain is behind us
    • revenue growth of 10% to 13% a year is the engine.
    • Our modal earnings per share are about €1.00 to €1.05 in FY2026, about €1.13 in FY2027 and about €1.39 in FY2029.
  • What the case costs on the valuation is the whole problem.
    • The shares trade at 31.5x current earnings on the dashboard (2026-07-16), the 100th percentile of the stock's own ten-year history, above every peer in the grid cohort (10.4x to 27.0x) and above the July consensus target of €24.83
    • at €29.5 on the same consensus earnings the multiple is about 26x.
  • So the 'Modal return' fails the bar, because the de-rating more than offsets the growth.
    • A cash yield of 1.5% to 2%, earnings growth of 10% to 12% a year and a forward multiple moving from about 26x to 20x to 22x sum to between −8% and zero over eighteen months and to about 4% to 8% a year over three years, against a 20% bar.
    • The bull path (margin 19% to 20%, revenue +14% a year, the multiple held at 25x) gives about 15% a year, still a C on Richard's anchors.
  • The chain holds through the outputs and breaks at the price.
    • 'Demand Inflection' fits as the primary pattern at B, with 'Major Corporate Change' as the secondary.
    • The price at which our modal path clears 20% a year is about €22 over eighteen months and about €20 over three years, against a listing reference price of €22.40 in February 2026.

  • The case therefore is a good business at the wrong price: compelling and mostly delivered as a business case, and not compelling as an investment case at €29.5.

  • Rating = C, because the change is material to the outputs and has propagated through every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, but the expected total shareholder return in the modal case is far below 20% a year at €29.5

    • the Building Block asks both questions and the second fails.
    • The rating diverges from its Elements (E 1 at A, E 2 at B, E 3 at F) by judgement rather than by average, because the forces and the outputs would carry a B or better at a price 25% to 30% lower.
    • Not B, because a B would say the case is material at this price, and it is not
    • not D, because the fundamental chain is intact and the failure is the price alone.

Most plausible competing hypothesis, and our response.

  • The strongest case against: H1 2026 was the peak, not a plateau, and the reported numbers flatter.
    • Pricing power is a product of scarcity, and scarcity is being engineered away.
    • Siemens Energy is adding 50% to transformer and switchgear capacity
      • Hitachi Energy is doubling transformer capacity
      • Fortune Electric grows output 30% a year through 2027
      • JST Power Equipment is building in Poland and Mexico
      • Chinese suppliers with up to a 32% cost advantage are already taking share in European power transformers
      • ABB's $5.5bn Rotork acquisition bundles automation against independent component makers.
    • Transmission downcycles have lasted up to seven years, with price wars.
    • The accounts flatter the earnings base: receivables grew 26% against sales growth of 15% in FY2025
      • days payable compressed from 87 to 73
      • reverse factoring stands at €25.2m
      • capitalised development doubled to €5.26m while ABB and Hitachi expense theirs
      • intangibles of €122.4m exceed total equity
      • SEG's acquisition accounting is provisional to Q1 2027
      • about a third of FY2025's growth in earnings per share came from below EBITDA.
    • The research's stress case takes revenue growth to 3%, margins down 250 basis points, earnings per share to €0.25 and fair value down 35%.
      • Add a thin float, insider selling planned to lift the float further, and a 100th-percentile multiple, and the shares are set up to de-rate hard on the first miss.
  • What we concede: the multiple and the cash-conversion point, in full.
    • They are why our 'Modal return' is F and our mark-to-market risk is F, and why we do not recommend the shares at €29.5.
  • What we do not concede: a margin peak inside eighteen months.
    • The backlog covers most of FY2026
    • the Spanish and US money is authorised and time-bound
    • Tier-1 capital is going to mega-transformers and HVDC rather than instrument transformers, and the key-question research rates active Tier-1 entry into the niche as low plausibility
    • homologation takes two to five years per utility
    • and pass-through held through a 40% copper spike.
    • The bear case is right about the price and wrong about the business over our horizon, which is why the recommendation is a park and not a short.
  • The case against our park, at its strongest: the liquidity discount is still unwinding.
    • Coverage is Spanish and stale, the Strategic Plan 2030 resets targets upward in October or November 2026, and the sell-side moves the stock into the Eaton and ABB comparison set at 30x-plus (ABB trades at 32.9x 2026 earnings).
    • Possible
    • but it requires the multiple to stay at a level from which three of the four parts of our return arithmetic already fail, and it would make the shares more expensive, not the case better.
  • Where the case against would win: a half-year EBITDA margin below 16.5% with revenue still growing, which would say H1 2026 was mix rather than structure
    • or receivables outgrowing sales by 10 points for two quarters, which would put the earnings base itself in question.

External forces: are there sufficiently strong external forces acting on the company, new or intensifying, and in which direction? (RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because demand and regulation are strong tailwinds that intensified in 2026, and the headwinds (Tier-1 capacity, Chinese pricing, tariffs, input costs, currency) are second-order within eighteen months.
  • Direction by force: demand a tailwind (A)
    • political and regulatory a tailwind with a tariff cost (A)

Demand: is end-market demand changing, structurally or cyclically (a swing with two-plus years of visibility)? (KQ 1, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because end-market demand is rising structurally at sector level with multi-year visibility, and 2026 added dated policy commitments that directly fund Arteche's product lines.
  • Global spending: grid investment is estimated at a record $1.7 trillion in 2026
    • the European Commission targets €580bn of network investment by 2030
    • US power-infrastructure spending is planned above $1 trillion over the next decade (as of 2026-07-30).
  • Spanish decree: Spain authorised an additional €17.9bn of grid investment through 2030 on 28-Jul-2026 (€10.2bn distribution, €7.7bn transmission), lifting the prior caps of 0.13% and 0.065% of GDP, with 20% of the distribution budget earmarked for voltage control and remote grid management.
  • US determinations: the Defense Production Act determinations of 21-Apr-2026 designate transformers, substations, protective relays and capacitor banks as critical resources and authorise direct federal purchases.
  • Utility programmes: Iberdrola's €1.5bn senior green bond for networks (July 2026)
    • Neoenergia's €9bn for Brazilian networks to 2030
    • ScottishPower's £28bn by 2028.
  • Lead times: high-voltage transformers take 2.5 to 4.5 years
    • medium-voltage equipment lead times worsened by 25% in Q2 2026
    • Fortune Electric is booked out through 2027.
  • Arteche's orders: order intake rose 15.7% to €338m in H1 2026 (+12.3% at constant currency).
Political and regulatory: is a change moving demand, pricing or cost? (KQ 4, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = A
  • Rating = A, because regulation moved demand and pricing decisively in Arteche's favour in 2026 (Spain's decree, connection-rule reform, the US determinations, the F-gas rules), and the cost-side changes (US tariffs) are partly neutralised by the Mexican and Spanish footprint.
  • Connection rules: Royal Decree Law 7/2026 replaced chronological grid-access queues with priority for mature projects
    • Royal Decree Law 18/2026 (late June 2026) added dynamic reallocation of capacity.
  • Tariffs: on 24-Jul-2026 the US moved to Section 301 tariffs of 10% on non-exempt EU goods
    • in April 2026 Section 232 duties of 50% were applied to the full customs value of steel, aluminium and copper articles.
    • A headwind on the European export line into the US.
  • Spanish risks: parliament rejected blackout-prevention rules in July 2025
    • windfall-tax proposals on clean energy are live
    • only 12% of grid-access requests were approved in 2025, with distribution saturation at 83.4%.
  • Not B, because the alignment between the 20% voltage-control earmark in the Spanish decree and Arteche's measurement and automation lines is unusually direct.
Revenue and demand cycle: where is the company's own cycle, and is it turning? (KQ 6, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because Arteche's own cycle is high and still rising (book-to-bill above 1.15x for three years, a record backlog) but revenue growth has slowed from FY2025's pace and Latin America is contracting.
  • Book-to-bill: 1.16x in FY2025 and 1.15x in FY2024
    • backlog €355m at end-2025 and a record €382m at Q1 2026, about 64% of guided FY2026 sales.
  • Revenue by region: H1 2026 revenue +10.7% to €277.9m against +15.3% for FY2025
    • EMEA +18.8%, APAC +17.3%, LATAM −8.6%
    • LATAM order intake −17.9% on recloser-contract timing in Brazil.
  • Not A, because growth is not accelerating and one region is shrinking
    • not C, because orders (+15.7%) run ahead of revenue and guidance was raised.
Competition: is competitive intensity easing or tightening? (KQ 2, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because competitive intensity is tightening at the margins (Tier-1 capacity, Asian entrants, Tier-1 automation bundling) while pricing in the niche is still rising and Tier-1 capital is aimed at mega-transformers rather than instrument transformers.
  • Tier-1 capacity: Siemens Energy is expanding transformer and switchgear capacity by 50%
    • Hitachi Energy is spending $180m on a Finnish transformer plant to double capacity
    • GE Vernova is ramping switchgear to 10,500 units
    • Fortune Electric grows output 30% a year through 2027 (May to July 2026).
  • Where the capital goes: Siemens Energy's €2.2bn FY26 capital expenditure goes to large power transformers and switchgear, and Hitachi Energy's ₹4,000 crore to Indian power-transformer plants
    • the only Tier-1 instrument-transformer capacity found is GE Vernova's $138m Charleroi expansion.
    • The key-question research gives active Tier-1 entry into the niche a low plausibility.
  • Chinese entrants: Chinese competitors carry up to a 32% cost advantage and are gaining share in European power transformers
    • JST Power Equipment is building factories in Poland and Mexico.
  • Industry pricing: up 5% to 8% over the prior year, 8% to 12% for high-voltage hardware (expert call, 31-Jul-2026).
  • Management's own limit: management has said it cannot compete with Hitachi or Siemens on low-cost products made in India or China (analyst meeting, 20-Mar-2024).
  • Not D, because no share erosion is visible in any region, and the ABB and Rotork bundling threat is in automation, where SEG adds to Arteche's own offer.
Disruption: is a disruption force strengthening or being disproved? (KQ 3, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = B
  • Rating = B, because the disruption forces now in motion (SF6-free equipment, digital and virtualised substations, optical sensing) favour Arteche, and the one that could hurt (solid-state transformers) sits beyond the eighteen-month horizon and is four to five times more expensive today.
  • SF6-free mix: SF6-free products were 39.33% of Arteche's mix in 2025, from 22.8% in 2024
    • the F-Gas Regulation 2024/573 phases SF6 out of medium-voltage equipment from 2026 and high-voltage by 2030
    • SF6-free alternatives can price 20% to 30% higher.
  • Arteche's positions: it launched the first 400 kV SF6-free instrument transformer
    • it holds 54.15% of Arin Technologies (virtualised substations, with Redeia's Elewit)
    • it bought 35% of Uptech Sensing (optical sensing) on 18-Mar-2026.
  • Solid-state: alternatives remain four to five times more expensive
    • GE Vernova has delivered pilot units to hyperscalers
    • Bernstein places a breakthrough after 2027.
    • Modular data-centre power pods could bypass component homologation.
Supply side: input costs, availability, concentration? (KQ 5, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because input costs are a live headwind (copper +40% year-on-year in Q2 2026, steel +34%) that the company has so far passed through in full, with indexation lag as the residual risk.
  • Metal prices: LME copper averaged $13,329 per tonne in Q2 2026, +40% year-on-year
    • Morgan Stanley models $13,500 in 2026 and $12,900 in 2027
    • German hot-rolled coil was +34% by late July 2026.
  • Pass-through: most supply contracts carry copper, aluminium and steel indexation
    • direct margin rose 170 basis points in FY2025 and 380 basis points in H1 2026 despite the spike.
  • Bottlenecks: grain-oriented electrical steel, bushings, tap changers and protection components remain tight
    • cast-resin parts and digital relay modules are single-sourced.
  • Supplier power: days payable fell to 73 in 2025 from 87 in 2024, a sign of weakening bargaining power upstream.
Macroeconomic: is a macro force acting on this company more than on the universe? (KQ 7, RA 1, E 1, BB 1) Rating = C
  • Rating = C, because the macro forces that bite hardest here are currency (about 49% of revenue in non-euro currencies, several of them emerging-market, with little or no hedging) and high rates delaying marginal grid projects
    • both are headwinds, and neither is large enough to move the case inside eighteen months.
  • Currency exposure: non-euro sales were 48.73% of 2025 turnover (€247.77m)
    • the translation reserve fell to −€21.86m at end-2025, a €13.12m drain over 2024 to 2025 led by the Turkish lira and the Argentine peso
    • a 10% euro devaluation adds €2.0m to net results, a 10% Mexican peso move €1.1m.
  • Hedging: the sources contradict each other: one records zero active FX derivative contracts at the FY2025 close and elsewhere €44.3m notional of exchange-rate insurance on US dollar sales
    • another says USD, MXN and BRL exposure is managed with derivative insurance.
    • Consensus EUR/USD forecasts of 1.16 to 1.25 by 2027 are a headwind.
  • Rates: experts report persistently high capital costs delaying marginal projects while mission-critical grid work proceeds.

Summary: Written Summary of the Investment Case (not rated) (E 3, BB 4)
The case in 8 to 10 sentences (the thesis as tested), kept in full at both ends of the record (Part 1, E 3, BB 4)
  • This is a demand-inflection case that has already happened, in a good business, at a price that has paid for it.
  • Grid capital expenditure, mandated by policy in Spain, the EU and the US and funded by utilities with multi-year programmes, has filled the order book of a homologation-protected niche manufacturer: orders +15.7% to €338m in H1 2026, a record backlog of €382m at Q1 2026, and book-to-bill above 1.15x for three years.
  • Scarcity gave the company pricing power, and its fixed-cost base turned volume into margin: an EBITDA margin of 10.1% in FY2023, 15.8% in FY2025 and 18.4% in H1 2026.
  • Earnings followed: earnings per share about doubled to €0.79 in FY2025 and rose 44% to €0.54 in H1 2026, and management raised FY2026 EBITDA guidance to €92.1m to €100.4m on 28-Jul-2026.
  • The company then converted the cycle into a re-rated equity: a main-market listing in February 2026, a €100m float placement in June 2026, three acquisitions and two joint ventures that add software to hardware, and a payout doubled to 50%.
  • The change has therefore travelled every layer of the 'Six Layer Inputs-outputs' chain, from the external force through choices and actions to operating metrics, financial statements, sell-side estimates and, last, the share price, which rose 81.7% in twelve months and reached €46.0 before falling 19.2% in the last three months to €29.5.
  • The business case is compelling and mostly delivered
    • from here the modal earnings path is 10% to 13% a year, the multiple is at the 100th percentile of the stock's own history and above every cohort peer, and the 'Modal return' is about zero over eighteen months and 4% to 8% a year over three.
  • It is a case to own at about €22 or below, not at €29.5.
