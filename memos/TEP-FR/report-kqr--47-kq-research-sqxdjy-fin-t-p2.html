Critical uncertainties remain around the velocity of AI cannibalization and the exact margin impact of statutory wage reforms across India, Colombia, and the Philippines. Section summary
  1. The pricing power of Teleperformance SE faces severe structural headwinds from AI automation and offshore migration, directly impacting its margin profile and EPS trajectory over the next three years.

Section 2 – AI Automation, Contract Renewals, and Price Give-Backs

Section summary
  1. Legacy BPO providers face severe contract renewal pressure as enterprise clients leverage generative and agentic AI to demand direct price concessions.
  2. Industry pricing is bifurcated with legacy laborbased contract renewals declining while premium AI-enabled services maintain stable pricing.

AI Renewal Pressures

1.
Sustained pressure: Global customer experience providers face structural pricing challenges as corporate clients demand significant concessions during renewal negotiations. The research analyzed 15 relevant sources, covering 5 peer companies and 2 comparable historical pricing precedents. This ongoing assessment indicates that AI-driven automation is directly reducing billable agent hours and deflating contract values, which ultimately threatens long-term earnings per share unless providers pivot to higher-value technical services.
2.
Accelerated deflation: Nearshore and offshore delivery models face immediate revenue deflation as enterprise clients demand immediate pass-through of productivity savings. Nuvama Institutional Equities reports that productivity-led cannibalisation has historically been 1% to 1.5%, but it currently averages 2% and is projected to rise to 3% to 4% as generative AI adoption scales. This escalating headwind directly threatens traditional volume-linked revenue streams and could compress operating margins unless offset by volume increases.
3.
Aggressive concessions: Technology buyers are successfully securing larger price reductions at contract renewal by leveraging automated self-service tools. A computer electronics lead stated in November 2026 that direct pricing discounts of 5% are rising to 12% in total contract value once productivity commitments and AI savings are integrated. These concessions are significantly larger than legacy single-digit adjustments, which will depress core revenue growth and impact near-term earnings per share.

Upfront Discounting Trends

4.
Rising discounts: Enterprise deals increasingly require substantial upfront concessions from customer service providers to secure new logos or consolidate vendors. An expert call from mid-2026 reveals that upfront pricing flexibility has increased, with vendors commonly offering competitive bid discounts of 8% to 15% to win multi-year transformation deals. This defensive pricing trend represents a clear escalation from historical benchmarks and limits the initial profitability of newly secured client portfolios.
5.
Steep reductions: Flexible outcome-based pricing models are driving substantial cost savings for large enterprise clients while compressing traditional BPO revenue. An August 2026 Foundever whitepaper indicates that implementing flexible outcomebased pricing decreases cost-per-contact by 15% to 25% when human agents are augmented by AI. This structural decline in contract values requires service providers to aggressively scale digital offerings to prevent persistent top-line erosion and protect operating margins.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some software providers are maintaining stable contract values by restructuring their renewals toward high-value technology integration instead of giving outright discounts. A strategic director noted in July 2026 that while conventional BPO renewals are flat to down 1% to 3%, AI-enabled services actually support price increases of 3% to 7% because clients are willing to pay a premium for demonstrable return on investment.

Peer Group Comparisons

7.
Defensive adjustments: Concentrix is utilizing selective commercial flexibility to protect its massive enterprise footprint during highly competitive vendor consolidation bids. A software company executive reported in November 2026 that Concentrix faces traditional BPO pressure, leading to mature renewal spend being flat to 5% lower as customers trade legacy labor hours for automated software. This shift cannibalizes high-volume legacy revenue and restricts organic growth, which negatively impacts near-term earnings per share projections.
8.
Consolidated bidding: Large BPO competitors are conceding on renewal rates to defend market share as buyers reduce their vendor rosters. An industry manager reported in May 2026 that Concentrix concede on price to retain scope on technology and financial renewals, which are contracting by 8% to 12% annually. This aggressive bidding behavior demonstrates that even market leaders must sacrifice short-term pricing power to secure long-term contract volumes.
9.
Guaranteed savings: TaskUs is responding to the automation headwind by restructuring its digital customer experience contracts around immediate cost reduction. The company announced in its fourth-quarter 2025 earnings call that TaskUs will guarantee meaningful per contact savings from day one in exchange for deployment rights for its autonomous AI agents. This strategy puts the burden on TaskUs to expand margins over time through agentic efficiency, introducing operational execution risk to its EPS trajectory.
10.
Volume chasing: Sub-scale peers such as TELUS Digital are experiencing significant pricing headwinds as they attempt to compete with scale leaders. Morgan Stanley reported in March 2026 that sub-scale providers face vulnerability in price-led commodity deals, which forces them to discount heavily during rebids to protect utilization. This competitive dynamic limits their ability to fund necessary AI investments, widening the technological gap between smaller players and market leaders.

Structural Price Safeguards

11.
Contractual rigidities: Customer experience providers rely on multi-year commitments to insulate their operational margins from immediate pricing resets. An expert call in August 2026 indicated that strategic enterprise relationships tend to renew because switching customer experience providers involves severe operational disruption and substantial migration costs. This inherent stickiness provides a temporary cushion for Teleperformance and its peers, delaying the full margin impact of AI-driven deflation.
12.
Productivity sharing: Enterprise BPO contracts increasingly incorporate formalized efficiency clauses that mandate the pass-through of technology-driven cost savings over time. Morgan Stanley notes that clients are demanding that AI-driven productivity savings be passed through immediately, which threatens to deflate contract economics unless providers can expand their program scope. These clauses force service providers to continuously innovate to maintain their baseline profitability and avoid severe top-line degradation.
13.
Declining duration: The shift toward shorter contract terms is accelerating the frequency of pricing renegotiations across the customer experience sector. Expert calls in 2026 highlight that traditional three-year agreements are increasingly being renegotiated as clients demand annual reviews to assess the operational impact of AI automation. This trend reduces revenue visibility and exposes providers like Teleperformance to continuous pricing pressure, which dampens long-term earnings per share stability.
14.
Strategic transition: Teleperformance is actively restructuring its operations to transition from labor-based billing toward structurally higher-margin digital solutions. The group announced in November 2026 that its outcome-based and back-office solutions achieved strong double-digit performance, successfully decoupling revenue from headcount. This deliberate operational pivot represents the primary path for Teleperformance to defend its earnings per share profile against ongoing AI-driven contract deflation.
15.
Other side of the story: While aggressive bearish narratives forecast rapid revenue cannibalization from AI, leading customer experience providers possess significant defensive levers to protect their profitability. A former executive noted in December 2025 that margins on technology deployments are structurally higher, meaning that even as revenue bases contract from fewer agent hours, the net impact on operating margin can remain positive. If Teleperformance and its closest peers successfully scale these higher-value digital services, their long-term earnings per share may remain highly resilient.

Section 3 – Client-Led Offshore Migration and Delivery Hub Gaps

Section summary
  1. Client-led offshore migration significantly deflates top-line revenue for customer-experience providers due to massive billing-rate differentials.
  2. Scale operators face multi-year organic growth drags from offshoring that temporarily compress revenues before delivering long-term margin benefits.
  3. Emerging offshore hubs and specialized delivery networks dictate the pricing power of global customer experience providers during structural transitions.

Billing Rate Differentials

1.
Massive discount: Morgan Stanley reports that the average hourly call center billing rate in India is approximately 70% lower than in onshore delivery hubs. The broker notes that while hourly rates reach $22 to $35 in the US, they decline to $6 to $10 in India, creating a steep price differential. This massive arbitrage deflates customer experience revenues upon migration but supports higher long-term operating margins.
2.
Bifurcated pricing: Hinduja Global Solutions notes that client-led transitions from onshore delivery centers to lower-cost offshore hubs trigger immediate revenue drops. The company reports that billing rates decline by approximately 50% when clients transition their service delivery to offshore countries such as India or the Philippines. This dynamic requires providers to expand transaction volumes to offset the structural deflationary impact on their absolute revenues.
3.
Regional savings: Liveops highlights that modern multi-region customer operations models utilize geographical pricing gaps to optimize client budgets. The company reported that a blended delivery model achieves an estimated 30% cost savings through nearshore Mexico and 50% through offshore Philippines compared to U.S.-only delivery. These pricing dynamics force global providers to actively expand their international footprints to remain cost-competitive.
4.
Nearshore discounts: Ambit Capital highlights that geographic expansion into nearshore hubs serves as a critical cost-saving mechanism for European clients. The broker notes that routing customer operations through South Africa achieves a 30% lower delivery cost compared to onsite UK delivery while maintaining the same time zone. This geographic optimization pressures average billing rates but allows providers to retain higher margins than onshore operations.

Organic Growth Drags

5.
Persistent headwind: Morgan Stanley estimates that client-led offshoring shifts act as a deflationary drag of approximately 150 basis points on Teleperformance's annual organic growth. The broker notes that continued customer migration to offshore delivery centers pressures the core services division, offsetting volume gains in digital services. This structural shift requires Teleperformance to accelerate its specialized offerings to defend its top-line trajectory.
6.
Deliberate transition: Concentrix Corporation reported that its proactive effort to move onshore programs to offshore locations created a near-term headwind. The company noted that transitioning 4% of its onshore business to offshore in 2025, combined with weeding out low-complexity work, created an aggregate 3% drag on growth going into 2026. This operational transition depresses short-term revenue but establishes a more profitable, technology-enabled footprint.
7.
Historical deflation: Morgan Stanley outlines how client-led migration in 2022 compressed average bill rates across the customer experience sector. The broker reported that TaskUs faced severe revenue pressure when its two largest onshore clients simultaneously shifted workflows from the US to lower-cost offshore centers in the Philippines and India. This transition caused TaskUs to lower its near-term growth outlook despite expanding total headcount and operating margins.
8.
Contrary evidence: Concentrix Corporation reports that a substantial portion of legacy customer operations is shielded from offshore deflation due to strict brand and regulatory constraints. The company noted that about 15% of its onshore revenue has the potential to move offshore, but the actual shift is limited because clients maintain brand promises to retain onshore staff or face strict sovereignty and compliance mandates. This natural barrier suggests the total pricing drag from offshoring is smaller than the worst-case estimates.

Scale and

9.
Sub-Scale Peers Scale advantages: Morgan Stanley notes that smaller customer experience competitors face severe pricing pressure because they lack the offshore capacity of market leaders. The broker argues that sub-scale peers like TELUS International face a tough competitive environment where they must discount onshore rates to protect agent utilization. In contrast, global giants like Teleperformance leverage their deep offshore footprints to provide immediate cost optimization to financially constrained corporate buyers.
10.
Offshore heavyweights: IBEX Limited maintains a highly optimized, low-cost delivery footprint that shields its profitability from onshore wage inflation. The company reported that approximately 97% of its total workstations are located in nearshore and offshore geographies as of September 30, 2026. While this high offshore mix limits absolute per-agent revenue, it structurally expands operating margins compared to onshore-focused peers.
11.
Accelerated rightshoring: Firstsource Solutions is aggressively shifting its recruitment to low-cost international hubs to optimize its delivery margins. Anand Rathi Securities reports that approximately 80% of Firstsource's gross hiring is focused in offshore and nearshore locations, with India and the Philippines driving over 85% of net additions. This aggressive rightshoring strategy expands operating margins despite rising wage inflation in emerging delivery hubs.
12.
Segment headwinds: IIFL Research reports that Firstsource's offshore and nearshore delivery mix slipped 120 basis points sequentially to 42.3% in mid-2026. The broker attributed this minor shift to rapid growth in onshore-centric banking and financial services accounts. This highlights how vertical-specific requirements can temporarily alter delivery mixes, slowing the margin-accretive transition to offshore locations.
13.
Onsite decline: Ambit Capital reports that eClerx Services experienced a sequential contraction in its high- rate onsite delivery segment. The broker noted that eClerx's onsite revenue fell to 16.8% of its mix as the company expanded its offshore delivery headcount by 724 people. This shift reflects a broader industry trend where clients actively trade high-cost domestic labor for cost-effective digital delivery networks.
14.
Balanced footprint: Fusion CX maintains a highly diversified delivery mix across onshore, nearshore, and offshore delivery hubs to manage geopolitical risks. The company's filings show that its delivery center revenue mix for the three months ending June 30, 2026, consisted of 28.2% offshore, 39.9% nearshore, and 32% onshore. This multi-location delivery model allows Fusion CX to balance client-led offshore pricing demands with regulatory onshoring compliance.
15.
Other side of the story: While client-led offshore migration deflates short-term organic revenue growth, it acts as a powerful margin defense and client retention tool for scale operators. Morgan Stanley notes that offshore delivery models yield structurally higher gross margins because the steep labor cost discount far exceeds the billing rate reduction. If Teleperformance and Concentrix successfully shift legacy onshore clients to India and the Philippines, they will permanently improve their profitability and protect their EPS from AI-driven contract termination.

Section 4 – Delivery Hub Wage Shocks and Indexation Recovery

Section summary
  1. Major wage hikes in key offshore hubs like Colombia and the Philippines exert severe cost pressures on customer experience providers.
  2. Contractual indexation and cost-of-living clauses provide partial protection against wage shocks but suffer from lag and limited recovery of statutory benefits.
  3. Scale operators leverage geographically diversified footprints and advanced automation to absorb structural margin impacts from statutory reforms.

Latin American Cost Pressures

1.
Severe escalation: Colombian operations face massive margin headwinds due to a dramatic statutory wage increase implemented at the start of 2026. Tecnoglass reported in November 2026 that labor costs rose significantly following a 23% minimum wage increase in Colombia. This massive regulatory shock was compounded by a simultaneous reduction in the standard maximum workweek from 44 to 42 hours, making domestic delivery models substantially more expensive. These combined labor reforms increase the baseline cost of services for all operators delivery-focused in the region.
2.
Healthy expansion: Teleperformance's regional operations achieved positive growth despite these severe structural labor cost increases in the region. An expert call in February 2026 revealed that Teleperformance's operations in Brazil grew 7% in local currency year-over-year. This positive growth trajectory was largely driven by the execution of contractual cost-of-living adjustment clauses. However, the expert noted that global clients are highly resistant to recognizing these inflation costs, meaning that absolute pricing power remains constrained.
3.
Stiff resistance: Global customer experience providers face intense commercial pushback when attempting to enforce annual inflation-linked pricing increases. An expert call from February 2026 highlights that global buyers are highly aggressive during procurement negotiations and rarely recognize inflation costs automatically. This commercial friction is especially severe in mature markets where clients are unfamiliar with double-digit cost adjustments. Consequently, service providers must frequently absorb initial wage shocks before negotiating partial recoveries.
4.
Vexing expectations: Morgan Stanley reported in November 2026 that Colombia's inflation outlook remains highly vexed due to unanchored expectations and indexation pressures. The broker noted that unindexed services inflation remains persistent at over 9% year-over-year, which continues to point to lingering labor-cost pressures. These persistent cost pressures complicate the monetary easing path for the local central bank. Service providers operating in Colombia must therefore manage continuous costpush pressures on their operating bases.

Philippine Wage Adjustments

5.
Significant adjustment: The Regional Tripartite Wage Board of the National Capital Region granted a substantial increase to the daily minimum wage. Regis Partners reported via Jefferies in July 2026 that the board authorized a staggered P85 per day wage hike, representing a 12.2% increase from the previous base. This major adjustment is implemented in two phases, with P60 taking effect in July 2026 and the remaining P25 in January 2027. This statutory change pressures operating margins because manpower expenses account for a significant portion of local profits.
6.
Delayed propagation: The impact of statutory wage adjustments on the broader Philippine economy tends to materialize with a substantial time lag. Deutsche Bank Research noted in July 2026 that a traditional wageprice spiral is not the primary mechanism at play in the Philippines. Instead, firms experience a delayed pass-through because existing client contracts remain structurally sticky. This delayed adjustment means that operators must initially absorb the higher labor costs, which temporarily compresses operating margins.
7.
Mitigating exposure: TaskUs utilizes strategic forward contract arrangements to manage the financial volatility of its large offshore delivery footprint. The company reported in its third-quarter 2026 filing that a 10% appreciation of the Philippine Peso would have increased expenses by $32.65 million without hedging. To insulate its margins, the company maintains outstanding forward contracts of $160.81 million in Philippine Pesos as of September 30, 2026. This hedging program protects near-term earnings per share from currency shocks but does not recover structural wage inflation.
8.
Extensive concentration: Concentrix Corporation continues to expand its massive delivery footprint in the Philippines to leverage its established operating depth. The company highlighted in October 2026 that the country represents the world's second-largest delivery hub, combining institutional expertise with rising talent in enterprise services. However, having significant employee concentrations in Metro Manila exposes Concentrix's operating margin to statutory wage decisions. This concentration requires proactive labor management and advanced digital solutions to offset localized margin compression.

Indian Labour Code Reforms

9.
Structural overhaul: The Government of India initiated a massive consolidation of its historical labor regulations to streamline statutory compliance. The Ministry of Labour and Employment notified the implementation of four major Labour Codes, effective November 21, 2025, which consolidate 29 existing central enactments. These codes introduce a uniform definition of wages to be applied across all employee benefit calculations. This regulatory shift impacts all major IT and BPO providers with significant delivery footprints in India.
10.
Elevated liabilities: The new uniform wage definition under the Indian Labour Codes drives a significant increase in recurring employee benefit costs. Jefferies Research reported in January 2026 that the new codes mandate that the compliance wage must equal at least 50% of total remuneration. This change increases corporate liabilities for long-term benefits such as provident fund contributions, gratuity, and leave encashment. Consequently, companies with low basic salary structures face a recurring wage cost impact of 2% to 5%.
11.
Substantial liabilities: Genpact Limited recorded material employee benefit obligations as a direct result of the Indian regulatory transition. The company reported in May 2026 that the implementation of the new codes had a $19.06 million impact on its India Gratuity Plan. This amount was recognized as a prior service cost in other comprehensive income. The company continues to monitor state-level rule-making because local implementations could further escalate recurring compensation costs.

Indexation and Margin Protection

12.
Incomplete recovery: Traditional customer experience contracts incorporate indexation clauses that provide only limited protection against sudden statutory cost spikes. Genpact UK Limited reported in October 2026 that client agreements often fix pricing for extended periods. This structural rigidity limits the group's ability to pass on immediate cost increases, including wage inflation and higher statutory contributions. This lag in price adjustments means that unexpected regulatory wage shocks directly compress operating margins.
13.
Successful negotiations: Ibex Limited has utilized contractual cost-of-living adjustments to partially offset persistent wage inflation across its global delivery footprint. The company reported in October 2026 that it successfully negotiated price increases or COLA with many of its clients. These price increases were crucial to offsetting rising compensation levels brought on by global labor shortages. However, the company noted that margins remain depressed unless these pricing adjustments are paired with significant productivity gains.
14.
Contrary evidence: Some highly organized service providers in India have achieved immediate, margin-accretive pass-throughs of statutory wage cost increases. SIS Limited reported in November 2026 that higher wage and statutory costs under the new Labour Codes are being fully passed through to customers. This immediate repricing led to an EBITDA margin expansion of 20 basis points over six months. This demonstrates that in highly structured, compliance-oriented sectors, labor reforms can actually accelerate top-line revenue and margin growth.
15.
Other side of the story: While statutory wage shocks in Colombia, the Philippines, and India present immediate margin headwind risks, scale operators are wellpositioned to defend their earnings through geographic rebalancing and automation. Expert insights indicate that contractual cost-of-living adjustments combined with nearshore-to-offshore migration allow giants like Teleperformance to absorb localized wage shocks. Section 5 – Cyclical Budget Cuts and Client Volume Elasticity

Section summary
  1. Customer experience providers rely on diversified vertical portfolios and long-term client stickiness to buffer the impact of cyclical volume contractions.
  2. Historical precedents demonstrate that while transaction volumes are highly elastic during economic downturns, legacy contract structures delay immediate pricing erosion.
  3. Highly leveraged and sub-scale peers face severe margin compression during spending slowdowns, forcing defensive balance sheet recapitalizations.

Client Concentration and

1.
Switching Barriers Diversified portfolio: Teleperformance SE maintains a highly diversified client portfolio that significantly insulates the group from individual client budget decisions. The company's 2025 Universal Registration Document published on March 13, 2026, reports that its largest client contributed only 6% of FY2025 revenue, while its top ten clients represented 31% of total sales. This broad base prevents localized client budget cuts from severely impacting consolidated earnings per share.
2.
High stickiness: Teleperformance clients exhibit high historical stickiness due to the steep operational complexities associated with changing service providers. The group's 2025 annual report released on March 13, 2026, notes that the average relationship length is 14 years for its top 100 clients. A complete vendor transition typically requires multiple months of agent retraining and compliance audits, creating high switching barriers that defend baseline volumes during economic slowdowns.
3.
Low concentration: Concentrix Corporation shows a similarly defensive, low-concentration client footprint that helps mitigate the risk of cyclical vertical volume contractions. In its annual report filed on January 28, 2026, Concentrix disclosed that its five largest clients represented 19% of FY2025 revenues, with no single customer exceeding 10%. This robust diversification, coupled with a 16-year average tenure for its top 30 accounts, stabilizes cash flow generation during periods of broad-based spending pullbacks.
