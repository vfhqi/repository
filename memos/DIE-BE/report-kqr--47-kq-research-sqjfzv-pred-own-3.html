Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Understanding the financial contribution of ADAS calibrations and Safelite's third-party administration contracts is essential because Belron constitutes the primary earnings and valuation engine for D'Ieteren Group.

The Core Query

1.
Significant investigation: "For D'Ieteren Group (DIE-BE), what share of Belron's and Safelite's revenue and operating profit comes from ADAS calibration, how have US calibration prices (static, dynamic and dual) and NAGS mark-ups on complex windscreens moved since 2024, exactly what have State Farm (ADAS Map requirement), GEICO, Progressive, Travelers (hourly or mechanical-rate pricing), Liberty Mutual and USAA changed or proposed in calibration billing in 2025 and 2026, when do Safelite's main insurer contracts and administration mandates come up for renewal, and has any insurer moved volume away from Safelite or reopened its third-party administration arrangements?"
2.
High contribution: D'Ieteren Group relies heavily on Belron because it generates approximately 70% of the holding company's adjusted operating income. Accelerating ADAS calibration penetration, which reached 52.1% in the first half of 2026, serves as the primary driver of margin expansion toward the 25% target. Consequently, any disruption in US calibration pricing or billing rates would directly suppress group earnings per share.
3.
Elevated concentration: Valuation models by certain financial analysts demonstrate that Belron accounts for roughly 76% to 78% of D'Ieteren's sum-of-the-parts net asset value. These estimates assume that Belron deserves a multiple of 20x operating EBIT due to its structural earnings acceleration. Therefore, clarifying calibration margins and third-party administration dominance is critical to defending the valuation of the holding company.
4.
Contrary evidence: Some expert witnesses suggest that auto insurance carriers are increasingly seeking to regulate and legislate ADAS calibration prices because current margins of 85% to 90% are viewed as unsustainably high. These customers are actively pushing preferred repair networks to cap reimbursement rates or transition to lower mechanical-rate structures. Such actions threaten to compress average job values and disrupt the positive price-mix algorithm that has supported historical profitability.
5.
Strategic integration: Safelite's dominant position in the United States auto glass market is protected by its horizontally integrated third-party claims administration model. Winning the massive State Farm contract in late 2025 consolidated Safelite's administration footprint across virtually all major US insurers and secured a multi-year volume tailwind. However, this centralized position heightens regulatory scrutiny and increases vulnerability to contract renewals or potential antitrust challenges.
6.
Unresolved concerns: Closely related operational questions focus on the long-term threat of self-calibrating camera systems that could eliminate the need for manual recalibration. Additionally, analysts are closely monitoring whether a successful initial public offering of Belron will narrow the historical holding company valuation discount of 30%. Understanding these auxiliary dynamics is essential for predicting the stability of D'Ieteren's cash flows over the next three years.
7.
Other side of the story: While billing conflicts with insurers present real operational headwinds, Safelite's nationwide footprint and proprietary technology systems make it nearly impossible for insurers to bypass their network. Large carriers prioritize faster claim turnaround times and geographic consistency over minor pricing disputes, which keeps Safelite's volume secure. Thus, the risk of structural volume loss or TPA contract termination remains extremely low despite ongoing pricing friction.
