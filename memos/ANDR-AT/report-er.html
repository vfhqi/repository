Section 1 – Brief company description and history 10 findings

Section summary
  1. Andritz is a resilient capital goods leader whose structural pivot toward high-margin service revenues and green transition technologies has offset cyclical headwinds in its legacy pulp and paper operations.
1.
Business model and sector: Headquartered in Graz, Austria, Andritz is a global technology and engineering group operating in the capital goods and engineering sector. The company designs and supplies industrial plants, equipment, automation systems, digital solutions, and lifecycle services to a diversified range of industrial end markets
2.
Pulp and Paper business area: Pulp & Paper is the group's largest division, representing approximately 38% of group revenue and 46% of adjusted EBIT in 2025. This segment supplies technologies, equipment, automation, and services for producing pulp, tissue, paper, board, textile fibers, and nonwovens, with a strong focus on resource efficiency and circular-economy fiber production
3.
Metals business area: The Metals division contributes about 21% of group revenue and provides metal forming and processing systems, including advanced Schuler press lines. This business area serves the steel, aluminum, and automotive industries, and has expanded into green solutions like battery manufacturing equipment and lightweighting systems
4.
Hydropower business area: Representing 22% of group sales, Hydropower supplies electromechanical "water-to-wire" systems, turbines, and generators. This division is a major beneficiary of global grid stabilization trends, showing growing momentum in pumped-storage and refurbishment projects. Environment and Energy business area: Generating 19% of group revenue, Environment & Energy was reorganized in 2024 to consolidate solid/liquid separation technologies with environmental and green products. The segment focuses on sustainable growth pockets, including municipal and industrial waste treatment, animal feed pelleting, carbon capture, and green hydrogen systems
5.
History and evolution: Founded in 1852 as a small iron foundry, the company expanded through decades of organic development and more than 90 acquisitions since 2002, notably acquiring VA Tech Hydro in 2006 and Schuler AG in 2013. This disciplined strategy transformed Andritz from a local licensee into a global technology supplier operating in over 40 countries
6.
Three-year absolute performance: Absolute group revenue stood at €8,660 million in FY23, declined slightly to €8,314 million in FY24 due to muted capital project activity, and finished at €7,883 million in FY25 due to continuing cyclical market challenges. Group EBITA tracked this progression, reporting at €742 million in FY23, €713 million in FY24, and €648 million in FY25
7.
Operating margin progression: Group operating profitability remained remarkably stable despite lower

volume, with reported EBITA margins holding at 8.6% in FY23, 8.6% in FY24, and dropping slightly to 8.2% in FY25 due to restructuring provisions. However, group comparable EBITA margin actually expanded, rising from 8.7% in FY23 to 8.9% in both FY24 and FY25, driven by a growing share of high-margin service revenues.

8.
Relative performance versus peers: Andritz has historically achieved superior operating margins in its biomaterials and pulp equipment division compared to its primary peer, Valmet. In 2024, Andritz's process technology margins of 8.2% compared highly favorably to Valmet's Process Technologies adjusted EBITA margins of approximately 4.2% over the same period
9.
Absolute share price performance: As of late September 2026, Andritz's absolute share price performance showed positive trajectory over multiple time horizons. The stock delivered an absolute return of approximately 3.7% over the last 3 months, accelerated to 32.5% over the last 6 months, and achieved 39.4% over the last 12 months. Shifting sell-side forecasts: Over the past two years, sell-side consensus has kept group order intake expectations for 2026–2028 mostly unchanged, but has trimmed its EPS estimates by low double-digit percentages. Shifting sell-side forecasts (cont.): This cautious revision reflected the slower-than-expected recovery of large greenfield pulp projects and delays in green hydrogen and carbon capture capital investments
10.
Valuation and multiples gap: Despite its strong operational execution, Andritz has historically traded at a

substantial valuation discount to its capital goods peers.
