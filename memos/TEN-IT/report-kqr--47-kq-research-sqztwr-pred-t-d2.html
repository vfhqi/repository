Section 4 – Historical Cycles and Price Premium Durations

Section summary
  1. Distributor inventory gluts and import spikes historically delayed producer margin troughs by several quarters after restocking cycles peaked.
  2. Hard import quotas under Section 232 successfully supported a robust domestic seamless pricing premium starting in 2018.
  3. Aggressive local capacity expansions by Welspun and U.S. Steel are restructuring the domestic supply chain to bypass tariffs.

Historical Restocking Peaks and Inventory Gluts

1.
Cyclical demand collapse: The US onshore market experienced a severe correction when the active rig count dropped over 50% after peaking in September 2008. This drop initiated a massive destocking cycle where distributors halted new orders to clear excess pipe. Distributors held high levels of stock, which took several quarters to work off before mills could recover pricing. This delay meant that tube manufacturers faced depressed capacity utilization well into 2010.
2.
Inflated distributor inventory: The US market faced a supply glut as industry inventory expanded to 5.4 months of supply in late 2012. Overproduction from domestic mills and high import volumes pushed distributor months of supply higher than healthy levels. Consequently, OCTG prices experienced a slow bleed throughout 2012. Producers guided to very weak margins in early 2013 as a result of this inventory overhang.
3.
Rapid demand contraction: The collapse of oil prices in late 2014 severely reduced drilling investments among global operators. Tenaris estimated that total OCTG demand plummeted by 30% in 2015 due to immediate CAPEX retrenchment and de-stocking. This severe demand contraction forced producers to optimize their global manufacturing footprints to lower costs. This optimization process delayed the margin recovery of leading tubular players until late 2017.
4.
Persistent channel destocking: The US steel sector entered a transition phase in 2019 due to a sudden pullback in operator spending. Jefferies reported that prolonged channel destocking consistently dragged down steel distributor shipments throughout the year. This destocking trend meant that pipe and tube shipments contracted by 5.1% year-on-year in November 2019. I analyze that this persistent inventory clearing capped North American pricing gains despite ongoing trade protections.
5.
Volatile normalization cycle: US seamless steel pipe prices peaked at $4,308 per tonne in December 2022. Prices subsequently normalized and troughed at $2,110 per metric ton in August 2024. This steep price correction occurred because elevated import volumes and softer drilling demand overwhelmed the domestic supply. Distributor inventories subsequently climbed to six months, which delayed the pricing recovery until late 2025.

Section 232 Price Premiums and Trade Barricades

7.
Drastic import reduction: The introduction of Section 232 in 2018 radically reshaped US trade flows by implementing hard quotas. Quota agreements capped imports from South Korea, Argentina, and Brazil at 70%, 135%, and 70% of historical averages. These strict caps successfully removed 0.7 million tonnes of foreign pipe supply from the market. This supply reduction created pockets of extreme market tightness that supported domestic pricing.
8.
Significant price premium: The sudden restriction of imports under Section 232 triggered a 19% surge in US seamless prices. Seamless prices commanded a substantial premium of $244 per ton over welded products in 2018. This premium reflected the superior quality of seamless pipe, which allows it to withstand extreme temperature and pressure. Consequently, domestic producers realized improved margins as the pricing upcycle extended through 2019.
9.
Contrary evidence: Tenaris faced severe margin headwinds under Section 232 because its cross-border logistical network was heavily penalized. Although the policy boosted domestic prices, Tenaris was forced to pay a 25% tariff on steel billets imported from Mexico to its Bay City processing plant. This tariff created a painful $50 million quarterly headwind that restricted the company's near-term profitability advantages. This cost drag illustrates how trade protections can backfire on integrated international producers.
10.
Eroding price premium: The domestic price premium created by Section 232 eventually encouraged alternative supply routes to enter the market. Incumbent producers faced rising competitive pressures as some steel importers rushed to bring material before tariffs took effect. Furthermore, some US buyers easily absorbed the 25% tariff costs to compensate for domestic shortages. I judge that this import elasticity capped the longevity of the tariff-driven premium before domestic mills fully expanded.
11.
Severe duty exclusions: The US Department of Commerce issued heavy anti-dumping duties on welded pipe imports from South Korea in 2018. While some exporters received low duties, others faced punitive rates up to 75.8%. These tariffs forced foreign players to restructure their US supply chains and warehouse merchandise at the border. This regulatory pressure protected domestic welded manufacturers but increased overall project costs for US midstream operators.

The company committed $100 million to expand and upgrade its Little Rock HFIW mill to 350,000 metric tons. This upgrade was successfully completed in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. Additionally, Welspun is on track to commission a new LSAW plant by end-FY27 to capture premium midstream demand.

Massive capital commitment: U.S. Steel restructured its Tubular footprint by consolidating production at its Fairfield facility. The company announced a combined $550 million investment, including $475 million for a Quench and Tempering line. While Fairfield adds premium capacity, the Lorain and Lone Star plants remain indefinitely idled in 2020. This consolidation allows the company to improve operational efficiencies during downcycles.
14.
Strategic corporate partnership: Nippon Steel finalized a strategic agreement to purchase U.S. Steel and invest in its domestic assets. Under the agreement, Nippon Steel committed to make an $11 billion investment in U.S. Steel assets by 2028. This capital injection aims to modernize existing operations and maintain domestic production capacity across US facilities. This transaction ensures that domestic premium connection capabilities are preserved under a unified global platform.
15.
Other side of the story: Aggressive domestic capacity additions from players like Welspun and U.S. Steel risk creating severe local oversupply during future market downturns. Historically, excessive domestic supply responses have cut short lucrative pricing cycles even when import protection remains high. If operators reduce drilling activity, this added capacity will trigger intense margin compression across the sector. Consequently, future earnings per share could fall sharply despite structural tariff barriers.
