Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Petrelintide's market path defines Zealand's long-term EPS trajectory.

Framing the Core Inquiry

1.
Investigating clinical potential: The research centers on the inquiry "After the petrelintide results of 7 October 2026, with a discontinuation rate of about 2% but lower weight loss than Eli Lilly's eloralintide, how do obesity specialists, payers and brokers expect petrelintide to win share: in maintenance after weight loss, in cash-pay channels, in combination with Roche's enicepatide, or not at all? What peak sales and probability of success do brokers now assign to petrelintide, how has Roche described its commitment to the programme, and when are the first Phase 3 data and the next Roche milestones due?."
2.
Developing earnings projection: Resolving petrelintide's commercial positioning is critical because clinical-stage biotechs see their valuations heavily tied to peak asset potential, which directly drives future licensing fees and milestone royalties. For example, DNB Carnegie's report of 20 October 2026 highlights that Zealand's near-term adjusted EPS is expected to remain deeply negative, projecting DKK -47.33 for 2028e. This means clinical success or failure in obesity trials will dictate whether the firm transitions to positive cash flows or remains reliant on capital raises.
3.
Shifting treatment paradigms: The obesity market is evolving from a pure focus on absolute weight-loss efficacy to long-term tolerability and patient persistence. A former unit lead at Novo Nordisk noted on 1 April 2025 that while injections drive initial weight loss, highly tolerable maintenance therapies will capture significant market share. This suggests that petrelintide's low discontinuation rate of 1.9% could unlock a massive niche if patients prioritize mild side effects over maximum weight reduction.

This means petrelintide's lower standalone efficacy relative to GLP-1/GIP dual agonists could severely restrict its market uptake if payers demand high weight-loss thresholds before reimbursement.

Leveraging delivery technologies: European clinical-stage peers like Medincell are also securing non-dilutive capital to progress their long-acting pipelines, showing the sector's focus on funding R&D through partnerships rather than dilutive equity. This highlights that a shared industry trend among European biotechs is strategic capital preservation as they navigate the long clinical path before hitting commercial EPS.

Section 2 – Commercial Channels: Maintenance and Cash-Pay

Section summary
  1. Maintenance channels offer the clearest commercial entry point for petrelintide.
  2. Payer coverage hurdles will heavily restrict direct cash-pay consumer channels.

Maintenance Market Viability

1.
Promising positioning: Zealand Pharma's petrelintide could secure a unique role as a chronic maintenance therapy after initial GLP-1 weight loss. Jefferies Research argues on 17 July 2024 that amylin's activity as a leptin sensitiser can restore satiety rather than inducing food aversion, which supports high-quality, long-term weight maintenance. This specific physiological profile suggests that patients transitioning from intensive GLP-1 induction can utilize petrelintide to preserve lean body mass and avoid rebound weight regain.
2.
Chronic maintenance: Clinical evidence shows that discontinuing incretin therapies results in rapid weight regain, highlighting the necessity of long-term maintenance regimens. Morgan Stanley highlights on 27 May 2026 that patients in Novo Nordisk's STEP-1 extension regained approximately 67% of lost weight within one year of stopping semaglutide. Consequently, payers and specialists are actively looking for tolerable chronic maintenance options to sustain therapeutic benefits over a patient's lifetime.
3.
Favorable views: Medical specialists expect petrelintide to fit well as a post-induction step-down therapy. An endocrinologist at Optum stated on 27 March 2026 that petrelintide's moderate efficacy but superior tolerability makes it particularly suitable for the weight maintenance population. This means the asset can capture significant long-term market share without competing directly against high-efficacy weight loss inductors.

Cash-Pay Versus Reimbursement

4.
Payer barriers: Access and reimbursement constraints make pure cash-pay channels a secondary option for long- term patient retention. An endocrinologist at Optum argued on 27 March 2026 that insurance coverage remains the single largest barrier to widespread obesity therapy adoption. Therefore, petrelintide must secure favorable insurance formulary placement because relying solely on direct consumer cash-pay is commercially unsustainable for lifelong therapies.
5.
Employer coverage: Securing employer-backed coverage will require clinical differentiation that justifies the cost of non-incretin options. A former director at Eli Lilly noted on 12 February 2026 that value-based contracts with major US payers are essential to accelerate market penetration. This implies that petrelintide's commercial success depends on convincing payers of its long-term economic benefits.
6.
Contrary evidence: Some experts suggest that direct consumer cash-pay channels may succeed if out-of-pocket costs are reduced. A family medicine physician stated on 5 August 2026 that patient demand is so strong that direct-to-consumer models can thrive if pharmaceutical partners optimize retail pricing structures. However, this model faces headwind because sustained lifelong adherence is highly sensitive to ongoing out-ofpocket expenses.
7.
Price competition: Competition from established players will force down margins in the commercial cash-pay segment. Morgan Stanley notes on 30 September 2026 that Eli Lilly is building a comprehensive direct-toconsumer subscription model to lock in patient loyalty. This competitive threat means petrelintide faces a crowded cash-pay landscape unless Roche leverages its commercial scale to match these aggressive pricing frameworks.

Tolerability Versus Standalone Efficacy

8.
Tolerability advantage: Petrelintide's clinical differentiation relies almost entirely on its exceptional, placebo-like tolerability profile. Jefferies Research reports on 7 October 2026 that the ZUPREME-2 trial showed a gastrointestinal discontinuation rate of only 1.9% for petrelintide compared to 1.7% for placebo. This outstanding persistence profile means that petrelintide can retain patients far longer than highly efficacious but poorly tolerated incretin therapies.
9.
Efficacy deficit: Standalone weight loss from petrelintide is significantly lower than that of competing amylin programs. Jefferies Research notes on 7 October 2026 that petrelintide achieved up to 9.2% weight loss at 28 weeks in type 2 diabetic patients. This is roughly half the weight loss potential of leading agents, meaning petrelintide is unlikely to be a standalone blockbuster without clinical combination strategies.
10.
Eloralintide profile: Eli Lilly's competitive amylin eloralintide delivers superior weight loss but carries a much higher tolerability burden. Morgan Stanley reports on 5 March 2026 that eloralintide achieved up to 20% weight loss in its Phase 2 trial. However, the asset saw high AE-related discontinuation rates, which leaves a significant commercial opening for petrelintide's benign safety profile.
11.
Compliance focus: Long-term treatment persistence is a critical factor for chronic obesity management. An endocrinologist at Optum observed on 27 March 2026 that many patients quietly discontinue GLP-1 therapies due to persistent digestive side effects and do not return. Consequently, having a drug with placebo-like tolerability represents a significant clinical and commercial breakthrough for real-world disease management.

58 Physician preference: Prescribers will choose between efficacy and tolerability based on individual patient phenotypes. A primary care physician noted on 19 February 2026 that patients unable to tolerate the standard GLP-1 class represent an immediate, high-value target for a well-tolerated standalone amylin. Thus,

12.
petrelintide is highly attractive to clinicians because minimizing severe gastrointestinal side effects improves long-term patient compliance.

Clinical Alternatives and Comps

13.
Competitive pipeline: Peer amylin assets are advancing rapidly, threatening to crowd the tolerability-focused market segment. Morgan Stanley highlights on 6 October 2026 that Viking Therapeutics initiated a Phase 1 maintenance study for oral VK2735. This rapid pipeline expansion means that Zealand Pharma must accelerate Phase 3 development to prevent competitors from capturing the first-mover advantage.
14.
Small molecule threats: Oral small-molecule amylin programs present a major threat to injectable peptide therapies. Jefferies Research reports on 9 September 2026 that Structure Therapeutics is developing ACCG- 2671, an oral amylin showing peptide-like weight loss. This oral option could completely disrupt injectable commercial channels due to superior convenience and lower manufacturing costs.
15.
Extended-release options: Next-generation formulations aim to reduce the dosing frequency of amylin analogs to enhance patient convenience. An endocrinologist stated on 3 February 2026 that early short-acting amylin formulations were commercially limited by frequent dosing requirements. Therefore, developing once-weekly or monthly long-acting therapies is critical to maximize long-term patient adherence in real-world clinical settings.
16.
Other side of the story: Standalone weight loss efficacy may ultimately prove secondary if payers prioritize combination therapies that offer both profound weight loss and clean tolerability. An endocrinologist noted on 15 September 2026 that future market differentiation will depend on overall disease reduction and metabolic health benefits. This suggests that petrelintide's commercial success will be decided by its coformulation with Roche's enicepatide rather than standalone maintenance.

Section 3 – Combination Therapy with Roche's Enicepatide

Section summary
  1. The combination of petrelintide and enicepatide offers superior clinical weight-loss synergy.
  2. Specialists highly favor fixed-dose co-formulations to maximize chronic treatment persistence.
  3. Next-generation long-acting technologies like BEPO Star unlock once-quarterly peptide dosing.

Clinical Synergy and ZYNERGY Trial

1.
Biological synergy: Biological pathways suggest that combining amylin and incretin receptor agonists delivers enhanced metabolic control. Roche's investor presentation on 28 September 2026 outlines that amylin and GLP-1/GIP signaling pathways promote satiety through distinct neural pathways. This dual signaling mechanism restores leptin sensitivity and improves insulin regulation without compounding gastrointestinal toxicity. Consequently, the combination can achieve profound weight loss and glycemic control while maintaining placebo-like tolerability.
2.
ZYNERGY design: The clinical evaluation of this promising combination therapy is advancing rapidly into Phase 2 development. Roche's company presentation on 28 September 2026 highlights the upcoming ZYNERGY trial, designed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of combining enicepatide and petrelintide in overweight or obese patients. The trial is scheduled for its first patient in during the fourth quarter of 2026. This timeline confirms that the co-formulation represents a central pillar of Roche's mid-stage metabolic pipeline.

Clinical synergy: Obesity experts express high expectations for combined amylin and incretin treatments due to complementary biological mechanisms. An endocrinologist at Boston Medical Center noted on 24 March 2026 that adding an amylin analog to an existing GLP-1/GIP regimen represents a highly anticipated clinical strategy. This approach targets distinct satiety pathways in the brain stem to prevent the rapid return of hunger. Ultimately, combining these mechanisms could break the clinical weight loss ceiling currently observed with single-agent therapies.

Specialist Preferences for Co-Formulations

5.
Co-formulation convenience: Prescribers heavily favor single-injection combination therapies over multiple daily or weekly injections to ensure compliance. An endocrinologist noted on 3 February 2026 that managing multiinjection regimens is exceptionally difficult for patients and limits real-world adherence. Therefore, a fixeddose co-formulation of petrelintide and enicepatide would significantly enhance patient persistence compared to separate standalone injections. Delivering both active peptides in a single injection is critical to securing high patient compliance and commercial success.
6.
Comparative efficacy: Fixed-dose combinations must deliver clear advantages over established therapies to gain widespread physician adoption and reimbursement. An endocrinologist at Optum observed on 27 March 2026 that a combination therapy must demonstrate superior efficacy or fewer side effects to avoid being classified as a generic alternative. Payers will refuse premium reimbursement unless clinical data show a distinct benefit over standalone GLP-1s. Consequently, the ZYNERGY trial must prove unambiguous therapeutic benefits to satisfy payers and secure market share.
7.
Contrary evidence: Some experts caution that fixed-dose combinations present significant clinical challenges due to the inability to adjust individual peptide ratios for patient-specific responses. A former director at Zealand Pharma noted on 7 February 2025 that patients often respond differently to the synergistic side effects of combined GLP-1 and amylin agents. Without the clinical flexibility to titrate each molecule independently, fixed-dose co-formulations might trigger unmanageable gastrointestinal adverse events during dose escalation.
8.
Pricing pressure: Even highly efficacious combination therapies face steep commercial headwind if they cannot secure broad insurance coverage. An endocrinologist at Coastal Endocrinology stated on 8 October 2025 that insurance coverage is highly inconsistent, with roughly half of non-diabetic obesity patients facing immediate coverage denials. This access barrier means that even a clinically superior petrelintide co-formulation could see its commercial potential severely restricted. Therefore, Roche must design aggressive pricing and contracting strategies to overcome payer reluctance.

European Clinical Biotech Long-Acting Technologies

9.
Long-acting shift: European clinical biotechs are pioneering next-generation long-acting technologies to address chronic treatment non-adherence and differentiate their pipelines. Jefferies Research highlights on 14 July 2026 that the obesity and psychiatry sectors are experiencing a major shift toward sustained-release injectables. French biotech MedinCell utilizes its proprietary BEPO technology to enable controlled drug release over several months from a single bioresorbable subcutaneous deposit. This technology aims to improve real- world treatment adherence significantly compared to daily oral medications.
10.
BEPO Star: Sustained formulation of complex peptides like GLP-1s requires innovative polymer chemistry to maintain stable therapeutic concentrations. Jefferies Research reports on 13 May 2026 that MedinCell's next-generation BEPO Star technology utilizes star-shaped branched copolymers to achieve steady release of large peptides. This technology overcomes the narrow therapeutic windows and high initial burst release challenges that historically limited peptide long-acting injectables. Delivering a steady, controlled release represents a step-change in long-acting peptide delivery profiles.
11.
Quarterly dosing: Sustained-release technologies could transform the competitive obesity landscape by extending treatment intervals to several months. Jefferies Research notes on 16 June 2026 that MedinCell identifies once-quarterly subcutaneous dosing as the commercial sweet spot for chronic obesity therapies. Formulating GLP-1 or amylin peptides using BEPO Star can completely bypass the patient compliance issues associated with weekly injections. This ultra-long-acting dosing profile would meaningfully differentiate next-generation obesity therapies from weekly competitors.
12.
Partnership validation: Securing high-value pharmaceutical partnerships validates the commercial scalability of European long-acting drug delivery platforms. MedinCell's annual report published on 16 June 2026 highlights their strategic partnership with AbbVie, which includes up to $1.90 billion in potential milestone payments to co-develop six long-acting products. This massive collaboration provides substantial nondilutive capital to fund ongoing technology optimization. Such partnerships demonstrate that established pharmaceutical companies value sustained-release platforms to protect franchises from generic competition.
13.
Competitive platforms: Other European drug delivery specialists are developing competitive technologies to enable stable co-formulations of metabolic peptides. Adocia's annual report of 9 May 2025 highlights its BioChaperone CagriSema technology, which offers a stable, single-chamber combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide. This formulation provides clear commercial and manufacturing advantages over standard dualchamber delivery pens. Thus, Zealand and Roche face a highly competitive European technology landscape focused on optimizing peptide co-formulations.
14.
Other side of the story: While long-acting injectables and combination therapies offer clear compliance benefits, emerging oral therapies could completely disrupt the obesity market structure. An endocrinologist at Columbia University noted on 27 March 2026 that potent oral dual-agonists could capture significant market share due to superior patient convenience. If highly convenient oral formulations can match the efficacy of injectables, patient and prescriber preferences will shift away from subcutaneous therapies. Consequently, the commercial success of Roche's combo is highly vulnerable to rapid oral pipeline advances.

This extended timeline creates a multi-year visibility gap during which competitive dynamics, payer policies, and physician prescribing habits may shift substantially. The current evidence relies heavily on Phase 2 proof-of-concept data showing petrelintide's exceptional tolerability but limited standalone efficacy, leaving open the fundamental question of whether the 9.2% weight loss profile will translate into meaningful commercial adoption once competing assets with superior efficacy profiles enter the market.

A second critical gap concerns payer reimbursement policies specifically for non-incretin amylin therapies positioned as maintenance treatments rather than first-line weight-loss agents. Endocrinologists consistently emphasize that insurance coverage represents the largest barrier to obesity therapy adoption, and payers will refuse premium reimbursement unless clinical data show distinct benefits over standalone GLP-1s. The current evidence does not contain detailed actuarial analyses, health economics modeling, or formal payer interviews that would clarify whether insurance plans will cover petrelintide as a step-down maintenance therapy after initial GLP-1 induction, or whether they will demand head-to-head comparative effectiveness data before granting formulary access. Without this information, projections of market penetration in the maintenance segment remain speculative rather than evidence-based. The third major uncertainty revolves around real-world patient persistence and treatment patterns beyond controlled clinical trial settings. Furthermore, there is insufficient data on whether patients who discontinue high-efficacy therapies due to tolerability issues actively seek alternative maintenance options or simply exit pharmacological treatment altogether. Understanding this behavioral dynamic is essential to sizing the addressable maintenance market for petrelintide. Closing this gap would require longitudinal real-world evidence studies tracking patient treatment journeys, prescriber decision-making at the point of GLP-1 discontinuation, and patient-reported willingness to continue therapy with better-tolerated alternatives.

The current evidence lacks detailed Phase 2 or Phase 3 timelines for these competitive oral programs, making it impossible to assess whether petrelintide will face a crowded market by the time it launches in 2030 or whether it will enjoy a window of differentiated positioning.

Resolving this uncertainty would require comprehensive competitive intelligence tracking the clinical progress, regulatory interactions, and expected approval timelines of all oral and long-acting injectable competitors in the amylin and broader obesity space. The fifth information gap relates to pricing strategies and net revenue realization for obesity therapies positioned on tolerability rather than maximal efficacy.

Payers emphasize that long-term adherence data and fair pricing structures are the ultimate drivers of sustained coverage, and favorable formulary tier positioning depends on lower real-world cost wastage. However, the evidence does not contain specific pricing benchmarks, discount structures, or value-based contracting frameworks that Roche and Zealand plan to implement. Without clarity on whether petrelintide will be priced at parity with GLP-1 therapies, at a premium justified by superior tolerability, or at a discount to capture maintenance volume, peak sales projections remain highly uncertain.

Closing this gap would require detailed pricing strategy disclosures from Roche, benchmark analyses of maintenance therapy pricing in other chronic disease categories, and scenario modeling of payer willingness-topay under different clinical value propositions. Finally, there is limited visibility into Roche's internal resource prioritization and portfolio decision-making criteria. While Roche has publicly stated its ambition to become a top three player in obesity and committed substantial upfront capital, the evidence does not reveal the performance thresholds that would trigger accelerated investment versus deprioritization.

If ZYNERGY combination data or early Phase 3 readouts show suboptimal weight loss even with dual-agonist synergy, it remains unclear whether Roche would continue full commercial buildout or shift resources toward alternative metabolic assets. Resolving this uncertainty would require access to Roche's internal strategic planning documents, portfolio review criteria, and contingency scenarios for petrelintide underperformance. - Conference Each of these gaps can be systematically closed through specific research activities: completion and publication of the ZUPREME Phase 3 trials, formal health economics and outcomes research with major payers, real-world evidence studies of GLP-1 discontinuation patterns, competitive pipeline tracking, transparent pricing strategy disclosure, and ongoing monitoring of Roche's strategic communications regarding the metabolic portfolio.

Until these critical data points emerge, the resolution of the Key Question regarding petrelintide's commercial path remains medium confidence rather than definitively answerable.
