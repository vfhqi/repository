EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Materiality of Internal and External Change

Dassault Aviation maintains an exceptionally stable, long-tenured executive team that prioritises internal promotion but continues to face criticism for minimal financial market communication. Both Chairman and CEO Éric Trappier and COO Loïk Segalen have served in their respective roles since 2013, with executive terms set to expire at the 2027 General Meeting. Minimal communication from C-suite management exists with the public market, creating a persistent information gap for external investors. Externally, resilient business aviation departures up 4% YTD through May 2026 support steady demand for high-end corporate travel, while global business aviation flights increased 4% YTD through May 2026, outpacing commercial airline passenger growth. However, supply chain issues directly delayed Falcon deliveries below the group's annual target, as Tier-3 suppliers struggle with raw materials and timely deliveries. France and Germany officially cancelled the joint FCAS sixth-generation fighter jet program, representing a material strategic setback for future defense collaboration. The net internal change is minimal and stability-focused, while external forces exhibit high materiality driven by supply disruptions and defense budget expansion.

Recent Earnings Delivery and Beat Miss Profile

Consolidated adjusted revenues reached €7,420M in FY 2025, representing a 19.10% year-on-year increase, with a revenue beat of +€409M (+5.84%) against consensus. Adjusted operating margin reached 8.60% in FY25, expanding from 8.30% in FY24, while adjusted EPS reached €13.60 compared to consensus of €12.39. Rafale deliveries of 26 units beat guidance of 25, while Falcon deliveries of 37 units missed guidance of 40 units due to supply chain constraints.

Available cash stood at €9.44bn in 2025, representing a pristine, debt-free capital structure, with positive FCF of €1.60bn driven by immense advance payments on export Rafale contracts. H1 2025 revenues of €2,847M missed consensus of €3,334M by -14.60%, with a €67M French tax surcharge cited as a one-off item. The earnings trajectory demonstrates strong full-year outperformance offset by persistent first-half seasonal weakness and civil delivery shortfalls.

Forward Guidance Credibility and Management Archetype

Management guided to adjusted net sales in the €8.5 billion range for FY26, with structural assumptions including the delivery of 40 Falcon business jets and 28 Rafale fighter jets. Management upgraded its original FY25 revenue guidance from the €6.5 billion range to over €7.0 billion late in the year, with final FY25 net sales of €7.42 billion outperforming the upgraded target by 5.84%. The historical tracking demonstrates a sandbagger management posture that sets low baseline expectations to ensure consistent financial delivery.

Long-term EBIT is highly sensitive to the US dollar, with a 10 cent EUR/USD shift impacting operating income by €60-70 million, while short-term transaction exposure is mitigated by forward currency contracts with an EBIT sensitivity of €3-5 million per 1 cent change in the hedge rate. The guidance incorporates ongoing upstream supply chain bottlenecks that are expected to last for another 12 to 24 months. Management credibility is high based on consistent sandbagging patterns and multi-year beat rates, though guidance remains hostage to unresolved supply chain execution risk.
