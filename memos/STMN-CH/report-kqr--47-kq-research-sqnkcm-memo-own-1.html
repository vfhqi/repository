Section 3 – Swiss Franc Macro Trends and Forecast Accuracy (2015-2029)

Section summary
  1. Structural inflation differentials keep the Swiss franc on a long-term nominal appreciation path, repeatedly defying historical consensus forecasts.
  2. Forward bank forecasts expect near-term franc strength to persist through 2027 before global monetary policy normalization prompts a gradual retreat.

Historical Exchange Rate Shocks

1.
Unexpected surge: The Swiss National Bank shocked global financial markets in January 2015 by unexpectedly abandoning its long-standing floor of 1.20 francs per euro. Commonwealth Bank research noted that this sudden policy shift caused the trade-weighted Swiss franc to appreciate by 17% in overnight trading. This rapid appreciation immediately threatened Swiss export competitiveness and pushed domestic inflation deeper into negative territory.
2.
Inaccurate forecasts: Professional forecasters failed to anticipate this monumental structural change in Switzerland's currency regime. Historical data from the KOF Consensus Forecast showed that economists predicted a rate of 1.20 francs per euro just one month prior to the January 2015 decision. This unexpected policy shift resulted in a massive 20% forecast error immediately after the currency peg was abandoned.
3.
Volatile stabilization: The Swiss franc subsequently entered a period of volatile trading before stabilizing around parity against the euro. News reports from late 2025 indicated that the euro-franc exchange rate fluctuated widely between 0.92 and 0.97 throughout the year. By early 2026, the dollar-franc rate broke below 0.78, which pushed the trade-weighted franc toward new all-time highs.

Historical Foreign Exchange Interventions

4.
Active monetary tightening: The Swiss National Bank actively used foreign exchange sales as a key policy tool to combat domestic inflation during the global energy shock. The central bank disclosed that it sold CHF 161 bn of foreign currency between the second half of 2022 and the end of 2023. This substantial intervention program actively drove franc strength to dampen imported consumer price inflation within the Swiss economy.
5.
Shift to buying: The central bank eventually reversed its intervention strategy as global inflation pressures began to subside and the franc strengthened significantly. ING research reported that the central bank purchased CHF 728 mm in foreign exchange during the third quarter of 2024. This pivot marked a clear transition from active currency selling to selective buying to prevent excessive nominal franc appreciation against major trading partners.
