Section 3 – Industry Structure + Concentration + Value Chain Economics

Section summary
  1. the Nordic banking market is characterized by a highly efficient, digitally mature structure that keeps operational costs low relative to European peers
  2. market concentration remains domestically high but highly fragmented on a pan-Nordic basis, allowing localized dominant players to defend shares
  3. competitor conduct is defined by intense mortgage and corporate margin pressure despite constructive macro and policy rate stabilization

Nordic banking market landscape

1.
Domestically concentrated structure: The Nordic banking market is highly concentrated domestically but remains increasingly diversified at group level.
2.
Efficient operating models: Nordic retail banks operate with exceptional cost-efficiency, consistently achieving lower cost-to-income ratios than broader European peers.
3.
Subdued credit growth: Credit expansion has been rather slow since the pandemic, showing low single-digit volume growth across household lending segments.
4.
Favorable macro backdrop: Long-term economic indicators are constructive, as GDP growth expectations for the Nordics are projected to outperform the Eurozone.
5.
Consolidating sector drivers: Ongoing consolidation is heavily propelled by escalating regulatory complexity and reporting requirements which disproportionately burden smaller regional entities.

Concentration and market shares

6.
Swedish loan concentration: Sweden remains domestically concentrated with Handelsbanken capturing 19%, Swedbank holding 18%, and SEB possessing 13% of total loans.
7.
Swedish deposit dominance: Retail and corporate funding pools are similarly concentrated, with Swedbank holding 16% and SEB capturing 15% of Swedish deposits.
8.
Norwegian market leadership: DNB commands the Norwegian loan market with a dominant 27% market share of loans, followed by the SpareBank 1 Alliance at 23%
9.
Danish loan fragmentation: Denmark displays structural mortgage concentration, as Nykredit Realkredit dominates the loan market with a massive 35% market share.
10.
Fragmented Finnish mortgages: Supplementary mortgage guarantees in Finland are concentrated, with OP Pohjola controlling a 39.2% share versus Nordea at 29.6%
11.
Accelerating merger activity: Mid-sized savings banks in Norway are actively consolidating, with Sandnes Sparebank and Hjelmeland Sparebank merging to realize vital scale benefits.

Competitive conduct and margin dynamics

12.
Recovering Swedish spreads: Swedish banks are experiencing margin relief, as average realized mortgage spreads recovered to 1.51% in mid-2026.
13.
Intense mortgage discounts: High price transparency in Sweden drives aggressive discounts on list rates, strengthening retail borrower bargaining power.
14.
Subpar mortgage returns: High competitive pressure has depressed Norwegian mortgage profitability to a low 5.7% ROE, below targeted returns.
15.
Danish pricing disadvantage: Non-Totalkredit lenders consistently lose market share due to Totalkredit offering structurally lower administration margins on residential mortgage loans.
16.
Externally funded bonuses: Totalkredit utilizes customer bonuses of 0.25% funded externally by Nykredit, enabling partner banks to price aggressively.
17.
Growing corporate lending: Nordic corporate credit activity is accelerating, with corporate loan pipelines strengthening across major banks as macro uncertainties begin to resolve.
18.
Active digital switching: High digital sophistication across Swedish retail segments facilitates rapid customer switching behavior and intensifies price-based competition.
19.
Other side of the story: unmitigated pricing wars on mortgages could permanently depress core net interest margins if smaller players keep using excess capital to buy unprofitable growth.
