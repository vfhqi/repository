One point for Richard before the questions. Hexatronic is held, as a Core Investment, at 4.74% of the fund, so the questions are weighted to whether to add, hold or cut and to what would break the case. The memo's case is two cases joined at the first-quarter 2027 report (late April 2027): a high-probability six months in which the company beats a thin, company-set consensus twice against easy comparisons (Q4 2025 at a 7.2% margin; Q1 2025's cold winter at 8.6%), and a lower-probability, negatively skewed twelve months after that, when North American growth meets comparisons of +44% and +51%, Data Center's third-quarter timing benefit reverses and glass and connector supply caps growth. Central value SEK 55 at end-2027 (42% above the last close), probability-weighted about SEK 47 (21% above), range about SEK 26 to 65. The third-quarter earnings summary called the quarter a material positive (sales 11% and EPS 37% above the four-contributor consensus; organic growth 17%; margin 11.3%), with three caveats the call can test: the margin test was passed on mix and timing rather than on price pass-through; supply, not demand, is now the binding constraint; and the cycle, not the corporate change, wrote most of the quarter. The memo puts its tripwires outside the company (Corning, Prysmian, hyperscaler capital expenditure, spot fibre prices), so the call's job is to find out how management itself reads the refill, the Data Center level and the supply cap.

A second point: what this call can and cannot give. Patrik Johannesson is investor relations. He can explain the third quarter's components, definitions, the order book, the cues already given, the supply comments, the acquisition practice and the timetable for the open appointments. Strategy, pay and the targets are better kept for the chief executive or the new chief financial officer; those questions are marked "or save for management". Our files hold no offer of a later meeting with management; it is worth asking for one.

What the call should settle, and why

The case in one line: a cyclical niche specialist in the second year of an upswing, with a lower cost base and a better mix than in its last cycle, whose next two reports are bounded and likely beats, and whose second half of 2027 either proves a Fiber Solutions margin floor near 10% through hard comparisons or reverts to its own 2023 template. Six judgements decide that.

  • Judgement 1, the North American refill. North American sales rose 80% in the third quarter to SEK 1,190m, 48% of the group. The memo's base is +12% in 2027, front-loaded. If management reads the surge as end use (new builds, middle-mile links to data centres, BEAD from 2027), the base holds; if any part was customers restocking, a 20% fall from the second quarter of 2027 costs SEK 0.58 to 0.65 of 2027 EPS (2027 EPS about SEK 2.1 to 2.2, down 22% to 24%).
  • Judgement 2, the Data Center level. SEK 692m in the third quarter (+44% organic, 16.9% margin) against SEK 538m in the second; management expects the fourth quarter to normalise toward the first-half pattern. Our base is SEK 550m in the fourth quarter and SEK 2,640m in 2027 (+15% organic). Each 10 points of organic growth is SEK 0.15 to 0.20 of EPS; a fourth quarter below SEK 500m would signal a hyperscaler pause, above SEK 600m would lift 2027 toward the upside scenario (EPS about SEK 3.2).
  • Judgement 3, the Fiber Solutions margin and price. 9.4% in the third quarter, from 5.4% a year earlier, on volume. The memo reads the recovery as half structural (the cost base) and half cyclical (absorption against a price headwind in United States HDPE duct). Each 100 basis points is SEK 52m of EBITA, SEK 0.17 of EPS; a stall at 8.7% costs SEK 0.12, a slip to 7% costs SEK 0.42.
  • Judgement 4, supply as the binding constraint. Glass fibre and selected connectors "were beginning to restrict growth", backlogs rising, the order book flat at four months through 17% organic growth, working capital out SEK 205m in nine months, cash conversion 72%. If the cap means revenue deferred, working capital and conversion are the cost; if it means revenue lost to integrated rivals (Corning, Amphenol), it is a volume risk the memo's base case does not carry.
  • Judgement 5, the open seats. The chief financial officer moved to run Fiber Solutions on 1-Nov-2026 with an external search; Data Center has an acting head; the deputy chief executive who built the acquisition programme resigned in June. Both permanent appointments by the fourth-quarter report (5-Feb-2027) remove the largest internal risk; no names by then would, alongside Harsh Environment and supply, make a third problem at once.
  • Judgement 6, the margin ceiling. LTIP 2026 vests on an average adjusted EBITA margin of 9.8% to 11.1% over 2026 to 2028, against the 12% target; our path averages 10.8%. If management stands by 12% with Harsh Environment at 15%, our 2028 margin (11.3%) is low; if the incentive range is the real plan, the 2028 targets will be met only with acquisitions, and FY2029 consensus (SEK 4.18, one broker) comes down toward SEK 3.6.

Order of play. Ask the Data Center components (A1, A2) and the North American read (A3) first, then supply (A4) and the Fiber Solutions margin and price (A5, A6), then the lines below EBITA (A9). Then the appointments (B6.1), Corning (B2.1) and BEAD (B1.1). Leave pay, the targets and disclosure to the end, or for management.

Range A: scope for guidance and sell-side upgrades or cuts over the next four quarters (the fourth quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2027)

A1 ★ Data Center in the fourth quarter. "You said Data Center activity should normalise toward the first-half pattern in the fourth quarter. Is our reading right that this means sales nearer SEK 540 to 580 million than the SEK 692 million of the third quarter, and does the margin fall back toward the first-half 15.5% as well?"
- Why: our base is SEK 550m; the one broker, Redeye, has SEK 582m. Each 10 points of Data Center organic growth is SEK 220 to 265m of revenue, SEK 45 to 65m of EBITA and SEK 0.15 to 0.20 of EPS. The memo's tripwires are SEK 500m and SEK 600m.
- Tell: "back to the first-half run-rate, underlying momentum unchanged" = our base and a likely beat on 5-Feb-2027; "lower, as some projects completed" = the lumpiness reading of the earnings summary and the first test of the hyperscaler channel; "the third quarter is closer to the new level" = the upside scenario.

A2 ★ What made the third quarter's Data Center beat. "How much of Data Center's third-quarter sales and profit came from one or two large projects, for example at Communication Zone, and how much was product timing that reverses?"
- Why: about three quarters of the third quarter's EBITA beat against the broker came from Data Center; the memo notes that one Communication Zone project drove much of it and that project concentration is not disclosed. Services are 39% of segment sales, so the incremental margin is only 15% to 25%.
- Tell: "spread across many customers" = the SEK 550m to 650m quarterly range is a base, not a peak; "one or two projects" = the third quarter was a peak and the market's fourth-quarter bar should sit nearer SEK 550m.

A3 ★ North America: end use or refill. "North American sales rose 80% and you reversed the planned downsizing at Clinton. How do you read how much of that was customers building and how much was customers or distributors restocking, and how should we think about growth as the comparisons reach +44% and +51% in the middle of 2027?"
- Why: judgement 1, the largest in the memo: base +12% in 2027 on about SEK 2.5bn of North American Fiber Solutions; a 20% fall from the second quarter of 2027 is SEK 175 to 195m of EBITA and SEK 0.58 to 0.65 of EPS, and Clinton would be under-absorbed again. The memo puts that adverse case at 20% to 25% over eighteen months.
- Tell: "direct sales under frame agreements, no distributor stock to speak of" = the channel-check reading, growth stays positive; any mention of customers building inventory ahead of price rises or supply shortages = the refill reading, and the cut risk from mid-2027 rises.

A4 ★ What the supply constraint has cost. "In the third quarter you said glass fibre and selected connectors were beginning to restrict growth. Was any revenue lost or only deferred, how long are lead times now, and when do you expect the new suppliers you are validating to deliver?"
- Why: the memo puts a glass or connector delivery failure at 10% to 15% over eighteen months, worth a quarter's SEK 150 to 200m of revenue; the earnings summary says the case has no reading in which demand is right and the revenue does not arrive. The shortage binds hardest in bend-insensitive data-centre fibre and multi-fibre connectors.
- Tell: "deferred, the order book is building" = working capital and conversion take the strain, earnings hold; "some orders went elsewhere" = the integrated rivals take the allocation, a volume risk to 2027; "validation complete by mid-2027" = the memo's upside scenario.

A5 ★ The Fiber Solutions margin in the fourth quarter. "Fiber Solutions earned 9.4% in the third quarter. With higher fibre and transport costs now coming through and the usual winter seasonality, how should we think about the fourth-quarter margin, and is our reading right that the improvement came from volume rather than price?"
- Why: our fourth-quarter base has Fiber Solutions above 9% against Redeye's 8.0%; the memo's fourth-quarter test is above or below 9% (the earnings summary sets an alarm at a group adjusted gross margin below 38%). Each 100 basis points is SEK 52m of EBITA, SEK 0.17 of EPS.
- Tell: "seasonally lower but well above last year's" = the cost-base floor of 8% to 9% holds and the 5-Feb-2027 beat is likely; "cost increases arrive before our price increases" = the earnings summary's first reading: the third quarter was the quarter before the pressure.

A6 ★ Price pass-through. "How do the glass-linked surcharges and index-linked contracts work: what share of Fiber Solutions sales reprices with a lag, how long is the lag, and have customers accepted the increases you announced?"
- Why: the memo models pass-through with a one-quarter lag and calls pricing power nominal: real pricing exists only in defence connectors, subsea and, while the shortage lasts, data-centre hardware (15% to 20% of sales), worth at most 50 to 100 basis points of group gross margin in 2026 to 2027. The adjusted gross margin was 39.8% in the third quarter, from 37.5% in the second half of 2025.
- Tell: "most contracts index within a quarter" = the margin is protected; "we raise prices case by case" = the gross margin falls before it recovers; any sign that scarcity lets the company price data-centre, subsea and defence orders up = the positive form of the supply reading.

A7 The adjusted gross margin. "The adjusted gross margin of 39.8% in the third quarter: how much came from the Data Center mix and the submarine shipments, which you say continue only through the fourth quarter?"
- Why: the earnings summary argues the margin test was passed on mix and timing; the Q4 2024 level was 41.4%. Each point of gross margin on about SEK 2.2bn of quarterly sales is about SEK 22m of EBITA a quarter.
- Tell: "mostly mix" = expect it to fall back toward 38% in the first half of 2027 without a change to the case; "mostly price and cost" = the margin has a structural element the memo does not credit.

A8 The order book. "The order book is about four months of sales, unchanged from the second quarter. How much of it is the long-dated submarine work, and has the Fiber Solutions part lengthened or shortened since the summer?"
- Why: the order book does not separate Fiber Solutions and is skewed by submarine orders (Redeye, July 2026), so it can misstate the near-term FTTH and North American trend. It is the only forward indicator the company publishes.
- Tell: "Fiber Solutions cover up" = demand still ahead of supply into 2027; "flat, mostly submarine" = less visibility than the four months suggests.

A9 ★ The lines below EBITA. "What should we model for the tax rate in 2027, after 29.6% in the third quarter and 26.7% for nine months, and for net interest? And how do you expect contingent consideration on JOWO and Superior to move, after the SEK 28 million revaluation in the third quarter?"
- Why: our inputs are a 26% tax rate and net financial items of SEK (105)m in 2027; each point of tax is SEK 0.04 of EPS. Earn-out revaluations and items affecting comparability add SEK 0.10 to 0.20 a year of reported-EPS noise with a negative skew, enough to turn an in-line quarter into a reported miss. The earnings summary flags "no cushion below EBITA" as live inside the beat.
- Tell: "about 25% to 26% as profit shifts to the United States" = our input; "nearer 28% to 30%" = 2027 EPS SEK 0.08 to 0.16 lower; "further earn-out charges likely as the units outperform" = reported EPS below consensus even on an EBITA beat.

A10 Working capital and cash at the year-end. "Working capital took SEK 205 million in nine months and conversion was 72% in the third quarter. How much is inventory built against supply shortages, and how much should reverse at the year-end?"
- Why: each SEK 1bn of growth absorbs SEK 230 to 260m; our free cash flow after leases is SEK 455m in 2026 and SEK 420m in 2027 (68% of net income), and close to zero after SEK 400m of acquisitions in 2027. The earnings summary's cash-conversion threshold is 80%.
- Tell: "safety stock of glass and connectors, partly reversing" = timing; "receivables from the North American growth" = conversion stays low while growth lasts.

A11 The first quarter of 2027. "Last year's first quarter, SEK 1,698 million at an 8.6% margin, was held back by a cold winter. How much of that do you regard as weather that should not recur?"
- Why: our base has the first quarter of 2027 at SEK 2,090m (+23%) at 10.1%, with EBITA up about 45%; Redeye has SEK 2,038m and SEK 217m. The memo puts the odds of beating the bar at about 65%.
- Tell: "a normal winter gives back most of it" = the second beat is likely; "Europe will stay weak regardless" = less of the base is easy.

A12 The krona. "With a stronger krona likely after the Riksbank's November decision, is our reading right that, because production is local, a 10% stronger krona takes about 9% off reported sales but only about 2% off EBITA?"
- Why: our sensitivity is revenue (9%) and EPS (3%, SEK 0.08) for a 10% move; translation took 9 points off reported sales in the first quarter of 2026. We carry (2%) translation in 2027 revenue.
- Tell: "yes, natural hedge" = a reported-sales headline risk, not an earnings one; "larger effect on profit" = the sensitivity is too small.

A13 The company-compiled consensus. "How many analysts contribute to the consensus you publish before each report, and do their EPS figures include the amortisation of acquired intangibles?"
- Why: the memo calls the bar thin (four contributors, one visible broker on FactSet) and every beat is measured against it; our consensus-basis and model-basis EPS differ by SEK 0.35 to 0.45 a year.
- Tell: "four, reported EPS" = our basis is right; more contributors = a broader revision base for 2027.

Range B: the key risks, optionality and drivers of the case

B1. Demand: United States carriers, BEAD and hyperscalers

B1.1 ★ BEAD timing. "When do you expect the first BEAD-funded orders to reach you, which states are furthest advanced for you, and is your United States duct BABA-compliant today while cable waits for the South Carolina line?"

Louisiana, Indiana and Pennsylvania are furthest advanced in our files.
- Tell: "first orders in the first half of 2027" = a new leg arrives as the refill comparisons harden, cushioning judgement 1; "2028" = the mid-2027 gap stays open.

B1.2 Customer exposure in the United States. "Your largest customer is below 4% of sales. How much of North American Fiber Solutions comes from private-equity-backed builders, and have any customers changed their 2027 build plans?"
- Why: about 80% of United States fibre construction is private; the memo names Brightspeed's 2027 refinancing (it spent USD 5.6bn against a USD 3bn budget) and AT&T's USD 1bn lower 2027 to 2028 capital expenditure as early warnings. Late 2025's 26% North American fall was visible only when reported.
- Tell: "broad base, plans intact" = the channel holds; any named deferral = the adverse case moves closer.

B1.3 Hyperscaler financing. "Have you seen any data-centre customer slow or defer a project since the summer, given the financing strains in the artificial intelligence build?"
- Why: the memo puts a hyperscaler pause or financing event at 15% to 20% over eighteen months (EBITA down 10% to 20%) and treats it as correlated with the United States carrier channel through long rates; Data Center growth of 5% instead of 15% costs SEK 0.18 of EPS.
- Tell: "no change, pipeline growing" = keeps the base; "projects pushed into 2027" = the fourth-quarter level is below SEK 550m.

B1.4 Re-cabling and services. "How much of Data Center's sales is repeat work in existing halls, such as re-cabling or day-two services, rather than new builds?"
- Why: the memo's counter-case is that re-cabling every two to three years could be a recurring base nobody has measured; proof of it would move the memo's central multiple toward the 75th percentile (17.6 times).
- Tell: a meaningful recurring share = a higher-quality Data Center than the memo credits.

B2. Supply and Corning

B2.1 ★ Corning as supplier and competitor. "Corning is both your main glass supplier and a growing competitor in connectivity. How secure is your allocation for 2027, on what terms, and are you losing any bids to Corning's own products?"
- Why: five glass makers hold about 67% of preform capacity; Corning holds 50% to 65% of United States optical fibre and wins 50% to 60% of bids (earnings summary); spot fibre prices rose five-fold in China and 3.6 times in Europe since mid-2025. The memo calls Hexatronic a volume seller in the wrong layer of the value chain.
- Tell: "multi-year agreements with volume commitments" = supply defers revenue, it does not lose it; "allocation quarter by quarter" = the integrated rivals set the pace of 2027 growth.

B2.2 The South Carolina cable line. "When does the South Carolina cable line start production, and what share of your United States cable will it supply in 2027?"
- Why: capital expenditure is SEK 330m in our 2027 (the subsea and cable lines); four previous capacity projects slipped (Clinton 2022, South Carolina 2023, Ogden 2024, the cable line to 2027). Local cable makes the United States offer BABA-eligible beyond ducts.
- Tell: "on time in 2027" = the BEAD leg is fully eligible; another slip = the fifth.

B2.3 Input costs in 2027. "Standard telecom fibre has fallen from about USD 17 to 18 to USD 11 to 13 per fibre kilometre while data-centre fibre stays tight. How do you expect your input costs to move in 2027, and how quickly does a fall pass back to customers?"
- Why: the memo expects falling input costs in 2027 to 2028 to give a brief gross-margin tailwind and then return it, with the end of scarcity around 2028 as Chinese, Corning and Prysmian capacity lands.
- Tell: "we keep part of the benefit for a few quarters" = upside to the 2027 gross margin.

B3. Competition and pricing

B3.1 ★ United States duct pricing. "Orbia has spoken of overcapacity in United States HDPE conduit, and Southwire and Cantex are adding capacity. Has duct pricing in the United States fallen this year, and how does your offer hold its price?"

- Tell: "prices stable, our systems sell on installation savings" = blown-fibre lock-in protects the margin; "some pressure on plain duct" = the memo's reading, the floor is 8% to 9%, not 10%.

B3.2 Europe. "European Fiber Solutions fell 9% in the third quarter, after falls of 31% and 10%. Where do you expect it to settle, and have European duct prices stabilised?"
- Why: our base has Europe (3%) in 2027; European microducts are oversupplied since 2022, and a second European consolidation (SEK 100 to 150m of one-off costs, SEK 50 to 100m of savings) is the memo's downside response.
- Tell: "close to the bottom" = Europe stops subtracting; "more capacity to take out" = one-off costs in 2027.

B4. Harsh Environment and subsea

The permit slipped from late 2025 to late 2026 and is pending; a one-year slip puts that SEK 100m at risk.

B4.2 The Harsh Environment margin. "Harsh Environment earned a record 11.0% in the third quarter against a 15% target. What closes the gap: defence connectors, subsea volume, or a recovery in oil and gas?"
- Why: our Harsh Environment margin is 12.0% in 2027 and 13.0% in 2028; the group 12% target needs this segment at 15%. Amphenol's harsh-environment margin is about 30%. Oil and gas dynamic-cable orders are deferred.
- Tell: "subsea volume from 2028" = the gap closes late; "defence pricing now" = upside to 2027.

B5.1 ★ The acquisition pipeline and price. "You have bought at 4.7 to 6.4 times EBITDA. With United States installation businesses trading at about 7 to 10 times, can you keep buying at 5 to 7 times, and who leads the work since the deputy chief executive's departure?"

B5.2 Earn-outs. "How much of the JOWO (up to EUR 7.6 million) and Superior (up to USD 3 million) earn-outs do you now expect to pay, and when?"

- Tell: "fully provided now" = less reported-EPS noise in 2027.

B5.3 Equity, dividends and buybacks. Or save for management. "After the SEK 600 million placing in May, what would lead you to issue equity again, and when could a dividend or buyback come back into the policy?"
- Why: we carry a 30% chance of a further directed issue over four years (a SEK 1bn issue at the last close adds about 26m shares, 12%) and no dividend before 2030; net debt falls toward SEK 0.5bn by 2029.
- Tell: "acquisitions first, no issue needed at this pace" = our base; "a capital return once leverage is well below 1 times" = a 2028 to 2029 catalyst.

B6. People and incentives

B6.1 ★ The chief financial officer and Head of Data Center. "Where are the searches for a permanent chief financial officer and Head of Data Center, and do you expect both in place by the fourth-quarter report on 5 February?"

B6.2 Internal audit and controls. Or save for management. "With no internal audit function and the chief financial officer seat open, how are the acquired units' controls reviewed?"

B7. Margins, capital expenditure and the 2028 targets

B7.1 Depreciation and the margin. "Capital expenditure has run below depreciation. When the subsea and cable lines start, how much will depreciation rise?"

B8. Disclosure

B8.1 ★ Order intake and the price-volume split. "Would you consider publishing order intake, a Fiber Solutions order book separate from submarine, or the split of price and volume in Fiber Solutions?"
