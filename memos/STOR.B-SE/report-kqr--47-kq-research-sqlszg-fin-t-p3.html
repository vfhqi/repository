Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Storskogen's complex feedback loops dictate whether its acquisition-led business model can sustainably compound earnings or if leverage constraints will again halt growth.

Defining the

1.
Key Question Critical investigation: "which reinforcing or balancing feedback loops are at work in this business model, such that dynamics at play today create additional scope for further positive or negative dynamics in the near and medium term (reinforcing feedback loops), or the opposite (balancing loops)?"
2.
Dynamic earnings impact: Resuming acquisitions could raise the EPS growth rate significantly, while prolonged deleveraging or organic decay would compress valuations and depress future earnings. DNB Carnegie notes that M&A is the main driver of earnings revisions over the coming 6 to 12 months. Understanding these feedback loops is critical because the sell-side does not normally reflect uncompleted acquisitions in consensus estimates.
3.
Baseline quantitative profile: DNB Carnegie's October 2026 forecast projects adjusted EPS to rise from SEK 1.12 in 2026e to SEK 1.39 in 2028e. This trajectory assumes net debt to EBITDA leverage falls from 2.7x in 2025 to 1.7x by 2028e. If the positive compounding loop accelerates without a leverage reset, actual EPS could substantially exceed current consensus estimates.

Related Financial Dynamics

4.
Acquisition multiples constraints: The viability of the positive loop depends on the multiple arbitrage between public valuation and private acquisitions. Storskogen previously faced severe distress when leverage peaked at 4.35x, forcing a halt to M&A. High interest rates and crowded competition now make discipline at the stated ceiling essential for maintaining the debt path.
5.
Governance and control risks: The absence of an internal audit function across Storskogen's 114 business units raises the risk of control failures. A past governance failure at Tungdykargruppen resulted in a SEK 30m charge, demonstrating how growth can outpace head-office capacity. A rising unit count could compromise decentralized oversight and control and lead to further financial impairments.
6.
Decaying organic performance: Organic growth is another critical variable that can balance or reinforce these loops. DNB Carnegie's historical analysis shows that Storskogen's organic sales growth was negative 3.2% in 2025 before stabilizing. Positive organic growth is vital because it relieves pressure on leverage and allows Storskogen to generate internal cash for acquisitions.

7.

While Lifco maintained a self-funded acquisition loop with low leverage for a decade, other peers like Sdiptech required active deleveraging. Evaluating these peers provides a benchmark to judge whether Storskogen's decentralized model can withstand higher interest rates over the next 18 months.
8.
Amplified cyclical exposure: Macrocylical dynamics introduce another feedback loop through capacity utilization and order intake. DNB Carnegie warns that more than half of the group is exposed to industrial production niches. This exposure means that construction-related weakness in the Services division could intensify domestic price competition and drag down group margins during economic downturns.

Section 2 – The Positive Acquisition Loop: Cash Generation and Peer Precedents

Section summary
  1. Storskogen's strong operational cash generation of SEK 2,451 million in 2025 supports the restart of its positive acquisition loop.
  2. Peer precedents demonstrate that disciplined leverage management and low acquisition multiples are critical to maintaining long-term compounding.

Cash Flow and Conversion Targets

1.
Strong cash generation: Storskogen reported a robust cash flow from operating activities of SEK 2,451 million in 2025, demonstrating the fundamental strength of its diversified portfolio across trade, industry, and services. This cash performance reflects continued operational discipline and execution across the group's business units during a seasonally challenging environment. Consequently, the group has successfully transitioned into a new phase where it can resume its acquisition agenda and drive long-term earnings growth.
2.
High cash conversion: Storskogen achieved an adjusted cash conversion of 74 percent in 2025, which comfortably exceeds its long-term financial target of above 70 percent. This solid cash generation provides the group with substantial strategic flexibility and allows it to fund bolt-on acquisitions without relying on dilutive equity. Unless working capital requirements normalize more aggressively than expected, this high cash generation will continue to provide strategic flexibility to accelerate M&A and support future EPS growth.
3.
Disciplined entry multiples: Storskogen's management has reiterated that they remain committed to acquiring target companies at multiples of 7x EBITA or below. This disciplined ceiling compares very favourably with some of its larger compounder peers that often pay higher entry multiples for quality niche businesses. Maintaining this conservative discipline is vital because it ensures that every incremental acquisition will sustainably compound future earnings per share.

Leverage Path and Consensus Mechanics

4.
Conservative leverage targets: Storskogen maintained an interest-bearing net debt to adjusted EBITDA leverage ratio of 2.3x at year-end 2025, which is in the lower part of its target range of 2.0x to 3.0x. Furthermore, the chief financial officer stated an ambition to keep this leverage ratio below 2.5x during the Q1 2026 call, demonstrating strong balance sheet discipline. This capital structure cushion means the positive acquisition loop can run for several years before leverage limits are breached.
5.
Muted consensus expectations: Sell-side analysts at DNB Carnegie highlight that acquisitions are not normally reflected in consensus earnings expectations because brokers only add announced or completed deals to their forecasts. This cautious practice means that consensus EPS estimates tend to level off in the outer years of forecasts as prior deals pool out. Therefore, restarting the positive compounding loop creates substantial potential for positive earnings per share surprises over the next 18 months.

This historic track record demonstrates that a decentralized model can sustainably compound cash flows if head-office oversight remains highly disciplined and avoids complex central integrations.

This deleveraging has allowed Sdiptech to resume its acquisition engine and support a gradual earnings recovery.

This steady deal deployment will add at least EUR 70 million of EBITA, which will directly boost future earnings per share.

Addtech's earnings mud-through strategy and Indutrade's decentralized model allowed both firms to grow sales and offset organic headwinds through M&A. This organizational pressure requires increasing decentralization of the acquisition process so that capital allocators in business areas manage the pipeline. Without these structural changes, the acquisition pace will level off because the central M&A functions become a severe bottleneck for future expansion.

Swedish serial acquirers typically achieve this by reinvesting operating cash flows into niche subsidiaries that generate high return on capital employed. This reinvestment strategy means that Storskogen's ability to consistently identify profitable niches will determine its long-term return on incremental capital.

This outperformance is driven by systematic M&A execution rather than large, complex integration programs. Consequently, Storskogen's focus on small and medium-sized niche businesses reduces transaction execution risk and supports steady, non-cyclical compounding.

Section 3 – The Negative Feedback Loop: Intangibles, Liabilities and Control Gaps

Section summary
  1. Rising intangible amortization and substantial minority put option liabilities act as structural cash drags on Storskogen's capital compounding.
  2. The absence of a central internal audit function raises operational oversight risks as the group's decentralized business unit count expands.

Intangibles and Cash Commitments

1.
Escalating non-cash expenses: Acquisition-related amortization of intangibles rose to SEK 175 million in the second quarter of 2026. This rising non-cash charge directly reduces reported operating profit because each new transaction adds depreciable customer relationships and technology. Consequently, Storskogen's reported earnings will continue to face structural downward pressure from M&A unless organic expansion accelerates.
2.
Heavy put option liabilities: Storskogen's outstanding minority put option liabilities reached SEK 1,740 million at mid-2026. This balance sheet commitment represents a substantial increase from the SEK 1,473 million reported at year-end 2025. Because these options grant minority shareholders the annual right to exit, they represent significant cash obligations that restrict future acquisition headroom.
3.
Growing contingent considerations: The group's total liability for unpaid contingent considerations grew to SEK 80 million by June 2026. These earnout structures are typically measured at fair value using a discount rate of 11.4 percent. Although management utilizes earn-outs to align entrepreneurial incentives, these obligations deplete available free cash flow upon operational milestones.

Balance Sheet Risks and

4.
Impairments Elevated goodwill concentration: Goodwill accounted for approximately 78 percent of the total purchase price in combinations during early 2026. Out of SEK 220 million in total transaction value, SEK 171 million was allocated to goodwill. This high concentration indicates that Storskogen is paying substantial premiums for future synergy expectations.
5.
Massive historic impairments: Storskogen recorded a non-cash impairment of SEK 920 million in the second quarter of 2024. This charge resulted from the strategic divestment of nine unprofitable business units to M Industrial Invest AB. The write-down included SEK 550 million of goodwill, proving that historical acquisition assumptions can decay under macroeconomic stress.

Corporate Governance and Control Failures

6.
Missing audit oversight: Storskogen lacks an independent central internal audit function across its decentralized operating subsidiaries. The Board's 2025 corporate governance report explicitly argued that decentralized self-assessments are sufficient. However, relying on local management self-evaluations could overlook critical internal control deficiencies during rapid expansion phases.
7.
Contrary evidence: The Board's structured internal control framework, which is built on COSO standards, successfully utilizes 33 standardized key controls to manage financial reporting risks across its smaller business units. This proactive control mapping suggests that Storskogen can adequately mitigate operational oversight risks without a costly centralized internal audit department.
8.
Precedent control failure: Storskogen experienced a significant control failure in 2020 related to its acquisition of Tungdykargruppen. Misleading seller information in the transaction process led to significant financial shortcomings and a SEK 30 million charge. This historical incident confirms that rapid growth can overwhelm diligence capacity unless head-office monitoring is tight.

Vintage Organic Performance

9.
Decaying legacy earnings: Organic EBITA growth for Storskogen's legacy acquisitions has shown signs of structural decay during economic downturns. The group's organic EBITA growth fell to negative 4 percent in the second quarter of 2026. This operational decay suggests that earnings from the 2020 to 2022 vintages are failing to maintain their post-acquisition profitability levels.
10.
Segment cyclical pressure: The performance of acquired business units remains highly vulnerable to cyclical contractions in end-markets. For example, construction-exposed companies within the Services and Trade areas faced intense margin compression throughout 2025. These persistent headwinds imply that acquired earnings are highly dependent on macroeconomic stability.
11.
Other side of the story: While rising intangible amortization, minority put option liabilities, and governance gaps pose structural risks, Storskogen's aggressive portfolio cleaning has successfully weeded out underperforming assets. The divestment of nine unprofitable units in 2024 raised the group's adjusted EBITA margin by 0.7 percentage points. Consequently, the restructured portfolio of 113 business units is now better positioned to deliver resilient organic earnings compounding.

Closing multiple arbitrage: While broker research assumes Storskogen can buy niche units at a disciplined 7x EBITA ceiling, expert insights paint a more competitive picture. Experts warn that Scandinavian serial acquirer multiples have inflated to 8x to 10x EBITDA, meaning that multiple arbitrage has structurally closed in crowded Nordic markets. If Storskogen is forced to pay higher entry multiples to maintain its deal pipeline, the positive compounding loop will decay rapidly and weigh on equity valuations.
4.
Competitor platform bidding: Intense competition from larger, well-funded serial acquisition platforms threatens to bid up private asset prices across Europe and restrict deal flow. Expert insights indicate that Scandinavian players are increasingly forced to look abroad due to crowded domestic M&A markets. If competitor bidding forces Storskogen to abandon its strict entry pricing, incremental earnings growth will decelerate and underperform sell-side models.
5.
Compromised capital returns: Paying higher multiples for target companies directly compresses the return on capital employed for incremental acquisitions. If entry multiples rise toward peer averages of 10x EV/EBITA, the group's reinvestment rate of return will fall. Under these conditions, the acquisition engine will fail to generate double-digit growth without taking on excessive leverage, which would severely damage long-term EPS compounding.

Disruptive Players and Rating Triggers

6.
Restrictive credit rating: Credit rating agencies possess the structural power to disrupt Storskogen's recovery trajectory regardless of management's optimism. S&P maintains a BB rating with a negative outlook on Storskogen, with downgrades threatened if FFO to debt remains below 20 percent. If a downgrade occurs, it would increase future refinancing costs and restrict access to debt markets, directly lowering net income.
7.
Vendor loan vulnerabilities: The complex divestment of nine business units to M Industrial Invest AB in August 2024 exposes Storskogen to substantial counterparty risk. Storskogen converted prior loans into a SEK 300 million NewCo loan, which carries ten-year terms and capitalized interest. If NewCo fails to generate cash, unrecoverable vendor loan defaults would trigger major balance sheet write-downs and depress reported earnings per share.
8.
Restricting bank covenants: Syndicated lenders and credit providers have the power to enforce restrictive financial covenants that can halt the acquisition engine. Storskogen's facilities agreements restrict distributions, disposals, and additional debt if leverage exceeds 3.5x. If cyclical headwinds in Services depress EBITDA, debt covenants would restrict operations and force dilutive equity raises that degrade EPS.

Early Indicators of Structural Break

9.
Operational growth decay: The clearest early indicator of a structural break in the business model is prolonged negative organic EBITA growth. Storskogen reported negative 4 percent organic EBITA growth in the second quarter of 2026, indicating underlying portfolio decay. If this decay continues, the group will rely on expensive debt rather than cash flow to fund M&A, triggering downward estimate revisions.
10.
Oversight and control failures: A rising business unit count combined with the absence of an internal audit function represents a major operational vulnerability. Historical control failures, such as the SEK 30 million Tungdykargruppen charge in 2020, show that rapid growth can overwhelm head-office capacity. If further control failures occur, investor confidence would collapse and prevent future bond issuances, halting the compounding loop entirely.
11.
Deal volume deceleration: Volatility in deal inflows serves as an early warning sign that the pipeline is deteriorating or competition is too fierce. Management noted deal inflow volatility during the first quarter of 2026, which indicates a slower M&A pace. If deal volumes remain low, Storskogen will fail to meet its growth targets regardless of available liquidity, causing consensus downgrades.
