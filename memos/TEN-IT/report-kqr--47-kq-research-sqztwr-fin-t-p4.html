Effective Tax Rate and Argentine Peso Devaluation

2.
Deferred tax accounting anomalies: Tenaris's effective tax rate is heavily impacted by non-cash deferred tax adjustments required under IFRS. Tenaris applies the liability method to recognize deferred income tax on temporary differences between the tax bases of assets and their financial statement carrying amounts. This methodology forces the company to recognize deferred tax gains or losses from changes in local tax bases for subsidiaries using a functional currency different from their local currency. Consequently, these non-cash deferred tax charges can create major fluctuations in the reported quarterly effective tax rate even though they do not result in any physical cash tax payments.

As of June 30, 2026, Tenaris held a net short Argentine peso exposure of approximately $14.2 million. This exposure has been aggressively reduced from a net short peso exposure of $28.1 million on June 30, 2025, which helps insulate the company's bottom line.

These statutory payments will continue to act as a modest drag on consolidated net income.
