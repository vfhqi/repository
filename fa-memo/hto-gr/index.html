The reasoning behind the inputs

Segments: revenue and margin by segment, where the company reports them

OTE reports one, essentially Greek, operation since the Romanian exit (98.3% of FY2025 revenue from Greece, 99.9% in euro). It discloses revenue by line (mobile service, retail fixed, wholesale, System Solutions, other) but no segment margins: the interim report states operating assets are managed on a group basis and segments are evaluated on net margin. The split below is my estimate (EUR m), anchored on disclosed growth rates, Deutsche Bank's national wholesale figure and FactSet segment consensus; totals are exact to the inputs, lines are accurate to about plus or minus EUR 30m. The operating drivers behind the inputs

  • Revenue. FY2026: nine months actual EUR 2,598m; fourth quarter about EUR 884m (-3.5%: transit about EUR -33m, System Solutions about EUR -15m against a fourth quarter of 2025 that beat on public ICT, mobile service +2%). FY2027 -4.1%: the final EUR 130m of transit (company guidance), System Solutions -7% after the EU Recovery and Resilience Facility ends in December 2026 (management "same levels"; a Uni Systems expert expects public IT demand -20% to -30%), national wholesale -6.6%, mobile service +2.5% with the price step, retail fixed +1.0%. FY2028 to FY2030: core service about +1.3% a year, national wholesale -9% to -10% a year as the Vodafone-PPC venture fills, System Solutions -2% then +2%.
  • EBITDA bridge (EUR m; FY2027 / 2028 / 2029 / 2030): price step +24 / +21 / +20 / +20 (about 3.5% from about February 2027 on about two million postpaid mobile and fixed contracts, probability-weighted for a 25% to 35% chance of no step or a cap; then about 2.5% to 2.7% as Greek inflation falls); core volume and mix +10 / +8 / +8 / +8 (postpaid migration, fibre, pay-TV, net of prepaid erosion); national wholesale -13 / -18 / -16 / -14; System Solutions -6 / -1 / +1 / +1; net cost +6 / +6 / +8 / +9 (voluntary-exit payback, digital and care automation, copper switch-off from 2028, less wage, energy and content inflation). EBITDA growth +1.4% / +1.1% / +1.4% / +1.6%. The margin rise (40.5% in FY2025 to 45.2% in FY2030) is about 3 points mix (zero-margin transit and low-margin ICT leaving) and about 1.5 points operating.
  • Quarterly phasing (Part 3). EBITDA growth is weakest in the first half of 2027 (System Solutions laps +76% and +52%; the step is only partly in) and about +2% in the second half; brokers'-basis quarterly EBITDA about EUR 373m (fourth quarter of 2026), then about 348m, 355m, 387m, 381m and 350m.
  • D&A is flat: FactSet EBITDA less EBIT has been EUR 142m to 147m every quarter since early 2025; capital spending of about EUR 600m is only slightly above D&A and falls to EUR 540m; the 2027 spectrum renewal (about EUR 78m on a longer term against EUR 119m over 15 years) lowers spectrum amortisation. D&A includes depreciation of leased assets.
  • Net financial items include lease interest; they rise about EUR 7m in FY2027 after the EUR 500m 0.875% bond was replaced by EUR 350m at 3.853% (to September 2029) and cash; the EUR 100m facility is undrawn.
  • Tax 23.5%: Greek statutory 22%; nine-month 2026 reported figures imply about 24%. The Romanian-exit tax gain was booked in FY2025; its cash arrives in FY2026 (cash tax about EUR 51m in 2026 against about EUR 155m to 170m in 2027, Morgan Stanley), so the model's tax line, which is the profit and loss charge, overstates FY2026 cash tax by about EUR 145m.
  • Dividend per share is the dividend declared for the year and paid the following July: about two-thirds of a distribution of about 100% of the prior year's underlying free cash flow (policy 70% to 100%, at least half in cash dividends; 2025 and 2026 distributions were 66% to 67% dividends). The 2027 distribution is about EUR 590m (DPS EUR 1.02 on about 381m shares), with most of the FY2026 tax windfall retained against the 2027 spectrum payment, as the chief executive said in November 2025.
  • Share count. No scrip, no material dilution (executive equity is largely phantom or parent shares), so the non-buyback change is zero. Buybacks (Part 2) are about one-third of each distribution, phased across calendar years; at EUR 19.5 to 22.5 a share they retire 9m to 10m shares a year. How to read these figures
