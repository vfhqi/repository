EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (BLUF)

Antin Infrastructure Partners operates as Europe's largest pure-play infrastructure manager with €33bn in assets under management, generating highly predictable revenues through three distinct fund strategies–Flagship, Mid Cap, and NextGen–though faces mounting execution risks from leadership transitions and fund lifecycle stepdowns that threaten near-term fee stability. The platform maintains exceptional financial strength with industryleading 1.34% management fee rates, a 55% EBITDA margin, and a flawless €368m net cash position with zero debt, yet delivers this profitability through an aggressive 114% dividend payout ratio that could prove unsustainable if fundraising cycles elongate past 2027.

Antin captures high-margin value at the GP platform tier of the infrastructure value chain, charging premium 1.5% fees during investment periods and 20% carried interest above an 8% hurdle, but its pure-play positioning exposes the firm to severe margin compression if unlisted infrastructure fundraising cycles extend, as it lacks the multi-strategy revenue diversification of larger peers who can cross-subsidize operations through private credit or real estate fees.

Business model resilience and structural vulnerabilities

Revenue generation remains highly predictable with over 95% derived from contracted long-term management fees, insulating the platform from near-term market volatility. The Flagship strategy has delivered top-quartile performance with fund sizes scaling from €1.1bn in Fund I to €10.2bn in Fund V, achieving an impressive 2.5x realized gross multiple and 22% gross IRR. Geographic diversification has successfully reduced European investor concentration from 93% in Fund I to 41% in Fund V, securing a more resilient global capital base. Operating margins declined from 73% in 2020 to 55% in 2025 due to strategic hiring ahead of fund launches and scale-ups, representing near-term profitability pressure.

Flagship Fund V closed at €10.2bn in late 2024, representing the largest infrastructure fund globally to close that year and holding a 30% market share of largecap raises. The company distributed €127.2m in stable dividends for 2025 at a 114% payout ratio, though an earlier-than-expected step-down in Mid Cap I will shave €10m from management fees while massive leadership turnover heightens execution and team stability risks.

Financial profile and peer positioning

Antin generated an effective management fee rate of 1.34% in 2025, remaining stable compared with 1.33% in 2024, demonstrating resilient pricing power. Bridgepoint recorded a lower management fee margin on Fee- Paying AUM of 1.17% in H1 2026, down from 1.18% the previous year, reflecting credit strategy dilution. Petershill Partners reported a blended net management fee rate of 1.22% in 1H 2025, declining from 1.35% in 1H 2024 due to capital realizations.

Antin's FPAUM stood at €22.0bn at the end of 2025, up only 1.6% year-onyear, reflecting a temporary lull in active fundraising. Underlying EBITDA fell to €161.7m in 2025, down 13.4% from €186.9m in 2024, with margins declining to 55% from historical peaks of 73% in 2020. Antin ended 2025 with €367.9m in cash and cash equivalents, carrying zero bank borrowings or gross financial debt, representing a unique capital position.

The firm distributed €127.2m in stable dividends for 2025, translating to €0.71 per share and representing a massive 114% dividend payout ratio on underlying net income.

Competitive landscape and consolidation threats

Brookfield leads global infrastructure fundraising with $103.9bn raised between January 2020 and December 2024, while Antin holds the ninth position globally and second in Europe with $28.8bn raised. BlackRock's GIP division ranks second with $86.0bn raised, followed by KKR at $81.6bn and Macquarie at $77.1bn, though Antin represents a highly scarce stand-alone asset as one of the few scaled pure-play infrastructure managers remaining.

Every transaction is governed by the five-point "Antin Infrastructure Test" to prevent style drift, generating an outstanding 2.5x realized gross multiple and 22% gross IRR. BlackRock acquired Global Infrastructure Partners for $12.5bn at an extreme 25-29x fee-related earnings multiple, while CVC acquired DIF for €1bn, demonstrating unprecedented consolidation activity. Large institutional limited partners are directly competing with general partners by bidding on greenfield infrastructure concessions, while major pension funds increasingly build internal direct-investment desks to bypass fee structures.

Antin's stand-alone pure-play model risks structural marginalization if mega-platforms leverage their massive distribution networks to lock up LP allocations.

Customer concentration and fundraising execution risks

Antin's customer base comprises over 300 world-class institutional investors geographically distributed across Europe, North America, the Middle East, and Asia. Pension funds represent the largest customer type at 46% of total capital committed, highly attracted to long-term stable cash flows of value-add infrastructure. Flagship Fund V closed in late 2024 at €10.2bn, exceeding its €10.0bn target and representing a 56% step-up compared to Flagship Fund IV.

Antin maintains an industry-leading effective management fee margin of 1.34% of feepaying AUM, stable compared to 1.33% reported in 2024. Antin's customer base is highly concentrated, with the two largest active funds accounting for approximately 70% of fee-earning AUM, exposing the firm to fundraising delays. Challenging market conditions have elongated fundraising timelines, pushing the launch of Flagship Fund VI to mid-2027 and delaying the next major leg of management fee growth out to 2028.

Operator quality and foundation durability

Alain Rauscher maintains exceptional strategic continuity and team resourcing, positioning Antin as Europe's largest pure-play infrastructure manager with zero corporate debt and an 18-year track record protected by a proprietary 5-point test generating top-quartile realized returns. Antin has built a well-invested platform with 255 professionals across offices in Paris, London, New York, Singapore, and Seoul, including 38 dedicated inhouse specialists in legal, tax, financing, and sustainability. Antin has delivered a top-quartile realized investment track record spanning 18 years since 2007, generating an outstanding 2.5x realized gross multiple and 22.00% gross IRR. Antin benefits from deep LP customer lock-in via closed-end fund structures with 10-year contract lifecycles, achieving re-up rates of approximately 85% across successive fund generations. Antin commands industry-leading pricing power, sustaining a blended management fee rate of 1.34% of fee-paying assets and charging 1.50% on committed capital during active investment periods. Antin's operator strength and pure-play model are highly vulnerable to prolonged fundraising cycles; any delay in launching Flagship Fund VI past mid-2027 will trigger severe fee-step downs as active funds transition to invested capital at cost, while lack of strategy diversification prevents offsetting infrastructure fundraising lulls with credit or real estate fees.

Structural growth tailwinds and paradigm sensitivity

Unlisted infrastructure is expected to sustain a strong 11.00% asset under management CAGR through 2028, benefiting from growing allocations in high inflation periods. Long-term growth is supported by an estimated global infrastructure investment gap of $15.00trn through 2040, with stretched post-pandemic government budgets mechanically increasing reliance on private capital. The global energy transition requires massive capital spending that governments cannot fund, with Antin's NextGen strategy deploying €4.40bn to energy transition and circular economy solutions. Real asset portfolios are structurally resilient in high inflation regimes due to built-in contract indexation, with 45.00% of Antin's portfolio enjoying direct contractual inflation protection. Higher interest rates compress project valuations, widen bid-ask spreads, and slow exit volumes, while rising rates increase financing costs for leveraged assets necessitating higher equity co-investment sizing. Unhedged high-for-longer interest rates could compress exit multiples and delay realizations, while a prolonged fundraising slowdown past 2027 exposes Antin's monoline platform to severe fee-step downs as active funds transition to invested cost without timely successor fund fee activations.

The central investment tension centers on whether Antin's elite fee pricing power, flawless balance sheet, and exceptional historical returns can offset the mounting structural risks of monoline fund concentration, elongated fundraising cycles, and aggressive consolidation by diversified mega-managers. The platform's 55% EBITDA margin and 1.34% fee rate represent best-in-class profitability among listed alternatives peers, yet the 114% dividend payout ratio and 70% fee-earning asset concentration in two flagship funds expose severe cash generation vulnerability if Flagship Fund VI activation delays past mid-2027.

While Antin's proprietary investment framework and deep institutional relationships create durable competitive moats, the firm's pure-play positioning prevents it from cross-subsidizing operations during infrastructure fundraising lulls, leaving it structurally exposed to margin compression that diversified peers like Bridgepoint and EQT can absorb through private credit and real estate fee streams.

Capital Allocation Tier: Limited Partners (LPs) act as the foundational capital suppliers by allocating portioned resources to unlisted infrastructure funds to achieve asset-liability matching. institutional allocators target stable, inflation-hedged yields and long-term downside protection
2.
GP Platform Tier: Antin operates as the General Partner (GP) manager, pooling LP capital into closed-end fund structures to originate and manage assets. The GP platform captures high-margin contracted fees and performance-linked carried interest
3.
Portfolio Asset Tier: Capital is deployed directly into operating infrastructure assets that provide essential public services under concession or regulatory frameworks. These assets generate highly predictable, inflation-linked cash flows
4.
Special Purpose Vehicles: Individual acquisitions are structured as ring-fenced, non-recourse Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) to insulate the parent fund from debt liabilities. utilize project finance debt to fund development capital expenditures
5.
Co-Investment Channels: Large LPs increasingly demand direct co-investment rights alongside the main fund to reduce their overall blended fee burden. GPs utilize these fee-free co-investments as strategic sweetners to secure massive anchor fund commitments
6.
Retail Evergreen Wrappers: Retail wealth platforms bundle underlying institutional assets into semi-liquid evergreen wrappers. These retail products carry additional intermediation fee layers of 100-200bps on top of the standard GP fees
