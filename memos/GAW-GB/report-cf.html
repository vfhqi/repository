Appendix

Plausible problems to monitor

The financial research reveals several critical early warning signs that warrant monitoring despite Games Workshop's strong overall performance trajectory. Declining like-for-like sales at established UK and US retail stores during H1 2026 reflect extremely high prior-year comparisons from previous major hobby launches, yet this retail channel weakness contrasts sharply with the robust 26% Trade channel growth observed in the same period.

The deceleration in mature retail stores signals potential saturation in core Western markets and raises questions about the sustainability of the direct-to-consumer model when compared to specialty retail peers who typically maintain more stable store productivity metrics. Hold-rated sell-side analysts highlight that growth is increasingly price-driven, with potential risks to underlying volume expansion, which presents a structural vulnerability distinct from peer leisure goods companies that demonstrate more balanced volume-price contributions.

The group raises average retail prices by 4% to 5% annually, with the October 3.5% price increase supporting core gross margin progress despite US tariff headwinds, yet expert commentary suggests this pricing strategy may be approaching natural limits. Independent hobby retailers report that third-party paint manufacturers have captured 35% to 40% market share, indicating customer willingness to substitute away from premium-priced Games Workshop accessories when viable alternatives exist.

This substitution behavior, while currently confined to ancillary products, could extend to core miniatures if economic pressures intensify or if competitor quality continues improving. Management estimated that new US import tariffs could impact group profit before tax by approximately £12 million in FY26, with tariff headwinds reducing H1 2026 gross margins by 180 basis points, creating a structural cost headwind that significantly exceeds typical leisure goods manufacturers who maintain more diversified global manufacturing footprints.

The company's concentrated Nottingham production base exposes it to disproportionate regulatory and geopolitical risks compared to multinational toy and specialty retail peers who have already completed supply chain regionalization. Profitability has been periodically impacted by unhedged global carriage cost inflation and rising international shipping freight rates, and unhedged raw material and freight costs could spike simultaneously, representing a dual-volatility exposure that other UK-listed consumer discretionary companies typically mitigate through active hedging programs.

Licensing income fell to £16.0 million as Games Workshop lapped Space Marine 2 comparisons, meeting the minimum management guidance of at least £16 million, demonstrating the inherent lumpiness of the licensing revenue stream that will create challenging year-over-year comparisons throughout FY27. While the Amazon transmedia partnership offers long-term optionality, Jefferies maintains a base-case release timeline of 2028 for the initial TV production run, reflecting the lengthy development process, meaning near-term licensing growth will depend entirely on video game releases that historically exhibit high variance in commercial success.

This licensing volatility contrasts with branded toy manufacturers like Hasbro or Mattel who maintain more diversified and predictable licensing portfolios across multiple entertainment franchises. Finally, experts describe Nottingham headquarters and store staff as intensely dedicated, evangelistic hobby cultists, yet extreme reliance on long-tenured insiders may create cognitive blindspots if external supply-chain shocks disrupt Nottingham.

The promote-from-within culture, while preserving institutional knowledge, reduces exposure to external best practices that competitor organizations routinely import through lateral senior hires. This insularity becomes particularly concerning when combined with cash allocation for IT increased from c.3% to c.5% of core revenue to execute the Systems Improvement Plan, suggesting the company is addressing legacy technology debt that may have accumulated precisely because long-tenured management lacked urgency to modernize systems that "worked well enough." The IT modernization project creates execution risk during a period of simultaneous warehouse transitions and manufacturing expansion, presenting a multi-front operational challenge that peer organizations typically sequence more conservatively.
