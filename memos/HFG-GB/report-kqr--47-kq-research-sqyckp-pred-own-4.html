The Core Investment Question

1.

Section 2 – FDA Export Restrictions on Foppen's Greek Smoked-Salmon Plant

Section summary
  1. The Greek smoked-salmon plant remains locked under FDA export restrictions after Hilton Food Group received regulatory feedback requiring four additional issues to be resolved.
  2. Management has shifted production to the Netherlands to maintain key customer supply, but the associated mitigation costs are diluting overall seafood margins.

Ongoing FDA Import Alert Status

1.
Persistent regulatory barriers: Foppen's Greek smoked-salmon plant faces ongoing United States Food and Drug Administration export restrictions following a Listeria contamination incident. Hilton Food Group first identified Listeria monocytogenes in certain products in late 2025, which led to enhanced US regulatory controls and shipment suspensions. Consequently, management was forced to relocate smoked salmon production to its Netherlands facility to maintain supply. This ongoing regulatory barrier continues to prevent profitable exports from Greece to the lucrative North American market.
2.
Initial product recall: The regulatory crisis originated when Foppen Seafood issued a voluntary recall of its Norwegian Smoked Salmon Slices on June 25, 2024. The Food and Drug Administration published the recall notice after routine testing of lot 412 revealed Listeria monocytogenes contamination. The affected toast- sized packages had been distributed to Kroger and Payless supermarkets across 15 US states. This biological contamination event triggered immediate US import-alert actions that severely disrupted the Greek plant's export status.
3.
Substantial financial damage: Hilton Food Group incurred massive adjusting items of £27.6m during 2025 as a direct consequence of the Foppen contamination. According to the Hilton 2025 annual report, this charge included £18.4m for inventory impairment and £9.2m for temporary Dutch production relocation and airfreight costs. These heavy mitigation expenses depressed the group's seafood margins and severely impacted statutory profits.
4.
Escalating interim costs: The financial drag continued into 2026, with Hilton Food Group recording £7.0m of Foppen-related exceptional costs in the first half of the year. Management reported that these costs comprised £3.0m in production inefficiencies, £2.1m in airfreight logistics, and £1.2m in inventory write-offs following an unrelated third-party warehouse fire in Los Angeles. These recurring regulatory and operational overheads turned seafood operations unprofitable, causing an adjusted operating loss of £2.2m.

While management remains hopeful, the broker emphasizes that Foppen and Seachill remain material headwinds for the group for the foreseeable future.

FDA Regulatory Mechanisms and Timelines

8.
Automatic port detentions: The FDA uses Import Alert 16-120 to place foreign seafood manufacturers on detention without physical examination when they violate seafood safety regulations. This administrative mechanism allows the agency to hold incoming shipments automatically at US ports of entry without testing each one, shifting the compliance burden entirely to the foreign processor. Consequently, import alerts are materially harder to reverse than standard warning letters because the foreign facility must provide rigorous scientific evidence of ongoing control.
9.
Protracted compliance processes: Eden F&C received an official warning letter in May 2025 reminding them of the severe consequences of seafood HACCP violations, including potential placement on Import Alert 16-120. Historical precedents show that foreign facilities placed on these detention lists often remain blocked for months or years while attempting to remediate underlying structural issues. This long-term disruption means that seafood exporters typically suffer severe margin dilution and lost shelf presence while their US import pathways remain restricted.

The import alert restricted the distribution of 11 actively distributed products into the US, forcing the company to launch a multi-year remediation plan. Although Viatris engaged independent third-party experts to accelerate reinspection preparations, the restrictions remained active well into 2025, demonstrating the unforgiving timeline of US regulatory clearing processes.
