APM Mini-Memo: SCHOTT Pharma

The one-line view. A locked-in maker of drug vials, syringes and cartridges whose revenue has turned up again at 17.7 times earnings; the case depends on obesity-drug cartridges filling new capacity faster than idle polymer syringe lines dilute the margin.

What the company does

SCHOTT Pharma makes the containers that injectable drugs are sold in: vials and ampoules are 73 per cent of revenue, prefillable syringes and cartridges the other 27 per cent. In the year to September 2025 the company earned 280 million euros of operating profit before depreciation, a 28.4 per cent margin, on revenue of 986 million euros, and the market values it at 3.4 billion euros.

Entrenched. Each container is registered in the customer's drug approval, so switching supplier takes six to nine months, and the container costs under one per cent of the drug, so SCHOTT Pharma charges 10 to 30 per cent more than peers and 97 per cent of its revenue repeats. The company leads polymer syringes with 70 per cent of the market and is third in glass syringes behind Becton Dickinson and Stevanato. The Carl-Zeiss-Stiftung owns 77 per cent through the parent SCHOTT AG, which also supplies the glass tubing, so the shares are thinly traded.

A plausible investment thesis

Recovering. The story is a two-year air pocket passing in a business that is otherwise an annuity. Vaccine work fell from 10 per cent of sales to about 5 per cent and left polymer syringe lines idle, one large glass syringe customer cut its orders, and in December 2025 management cut its medium-term targets to revenue growth of 6 to 8 per cent and a margin approaching 30 per cent by the 2029 financial year. Since then the customer dispute has been settled, guidance for 2026 was raised in July to 5 to 6 per cent growth with a 27 to 28 per cent margin, and the third quarter grew 8.3 per cent at constant currency against expectations of about 3.5 per cent, led by 11 per cent growth in vials.

Upgrading mix. Beneath the recovery runs the shift from bulk glass to sterile ready-to-fill formats, which sell for five to ten times the price and are already 57 per cent of revenue against a target above 60 per cent. The largest volume driver is cartridges for obesity and diabetes injections: over one billion euros is contracted through 2030 with the two leading developers, adding up to five points a year to growth at an estimated peak margin of 35 per cent.

To earnings. Consensus has earnings per share growing about 12.5 per cent a year as revenue grows 6 to 8 per cent and the margin climbs back towards 30 per cent. Cash is already better: nine-month free cash flow rose 49 per cent to 58.9 million euros and net debt is 106 million euros, so the balance sheet does not constrain the capacity programme. The drivers to track are the syringe division's margin, the high-value share of revenue, and how fast the new cartridge capacity in Hungary and Pennsylvania fills.

The Target Setup is 'CfC Clearing in HQ low-cyclicality business on Q1/Q2 multiple, leading to reversion to normal': the concern was idle polymer lines, a customer dispute and repeated guidance cuts, the business is high quality and barely cyclical, and the multiple sits at the 40th percentile of its own range. A 'Demand-driven EPSU' combines with it if the cartridge contracts fill the new lines. The case does not yet match that setup crisply, because revenue has turned and the margin has not.

Case Riskiness and simplicity

Medium. Two things must go right: obesity-drug cartridge volumes must arrive on contract and fill the new lines, and the polymer syringe lines must find new work, in mental health and aesthetics drugs, or at least stop shrinking. The main risk is that the contracts protect against a customer switching supplier, not against a customer ordering less, which is what happened in the first half of 2026: only about 35 million euros of prepayments stand behind the billion euros contracted, and take-or-pay terms have already been renegotiated. Obesity pills threaten the injection volumes beneath the cartridges, with Bernstein expecting pills to take half the market by 2028, although injections were still 90 per cent of American prescriptions in September 2026, and Stevanato wins the largest new syringe contracts.

Watch. The syringe division's margin fell to 29.4 per cent in the third quarter from over 36 per cent a year earlier, and the group margin to 26.8 per cent from 32.3 per cent; the case turns when that division's margin stops falling. The tests are the full-year results, the guidance for 2027 and the polymer share of sales. Management credibility is the swing: the chief executive, finance director and syringe division head all changed within about a year and guidance has been cut several times since the listing, yet the 2025 margin beat the guide.

Business quality

High. The quality is not disputed in our files: regulatory lock-in, an oligopoly in which the top three hold over 60 per cent of core containment, customers who prefer zero defects to savings, and a 28.4 per cent margin against Stevanato's 25.1 per cent and Gerresheimer's 16.8 per cent. Demand grows with biologics, which are 78 per cent of injectable approvals, in a market expanding 9 per cent a year. The weaknesses are capital intensity, with over 800 million euros invested from 2020 to 2025, and a parent that is both the key supplier and the controlling owner.

Plausible returns and valuation

At 21.75 euros on 6 November the shares trade at 17.7 times earnings for the next two years, the 40th percentile of their own range, and 17 per cent below the 52-week high after a 72 per cent rise in the six months to October. The weekly TSR/Valuation rating of 6 November is B, a valuation reading only. The weekly routine's plausible return of 29 October is 30.1 per cent a year on average, 36.2 per cent over 18 months and 24.0 per cent over three years, from consensus earnings growth of 12.5 per cent and the multiple moving from 17.7 to 23.3 times.

B, with A reachable. My own read is more cautious: earnings growth and the small dividend alone give about 13 per cent a year, a C against your bar, and the rest needs the multiple to re-rate as the margin turns, which would lift the return to B or A. The shares stand at a 30 per cent discount to Stevanato, and the Sector Digest judged that the format shift could let SCHOTT Pharma be valued nearer a medical device maker. Broker targets run from 17 to 23 euros.

Value chain and thematics: the pros and the cons

For. Approved capacity is the scarce asset in this Sector, and SCHOTT Pharma owns it in the format that is gaining: ready-to-use cartridges are under 5 per cent penetrated, European sterile manufacturing rules push sterilisation onto the packaging supplier, and the Sector Digest judged the format shift the Sector's biggest story for SCHOTT Pharma. The Pennsylvania expansion tripled high-value vial capacity with 38 million dollars of federal funding and hedges American tariffs on medical glass. The weekly Thematic Fit/Momentum rating of 6 November is C, on the environment only.

Against. When a medicine reaches more patients, suppliers gain volume rather than price, and the Sector Digest expects the container shortage premium to end around the close of 2027. A Researcher's report of 25 October warns that synchronised expansion by SCHOTT Pharma, Stevanato and Gerresheimer could leave the industry up to 20 per cent overcapacity by 2030, and bulk glass makers have already conceded price cuts of 5 to 10 per cent.
