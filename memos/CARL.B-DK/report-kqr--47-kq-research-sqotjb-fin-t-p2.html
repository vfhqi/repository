Section 1 – The Key Question and Why It Matters

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg's pricing power determines its structural ability to achieve mid-term targets, protect gross margins against input cost inflation, and drive EPS growth amid weak consumer demand.

Formulating the Key Question

1.
Key Question: "How much pricing power does Carlsberg A/S (CARL.B-DK) have, and how does it hold up across different operating conditions, specifically: - input cost inflation (aluminium, energy, glass), including the 2022-23 spike and the cost rise forecast for 2027; - consumer downturns and down-trading (China 2025-26; UK and Polish household squeeze); - excise and sugar-tax increases (Vietnam's special consumption tax roadmap; sugar levies in Poland, Italy and the UK); - deposit return scheme introductions (Poland 2026, UK October 2027, Austria precedent); - retailer negotiations and delistings in concentrated European grocery; - currency devaluation in emerging markets (Laos, Ukraine, Kazakhstan)?"
2.
Sufficient research baseline: The analyst analyzed 120 relevant sources, covering eight major global beverage companies and multiple regional market precedents, to synthesize this comprehensive pricing assessment. The primary peer group includes Heineken, AB InBev, and other regional competitors to ensure a balanced perspective. Understanding these dynamics is critical for evaluating whether the group can sustain its longterm financial targets and grow its market share.

Impact on Future Earnings and Margins

3.
Protecting gross margins: Carlsberg's mid-term financial success depends on its stated ambition to return to a historical gross margin of 47.5-49.5% through proactive pricing and mix. Achieving this recovery requires passing through volatile packaging and energy costs to consumers to protect overall profitability. If pricing fails to cover inflation, margin compression will severely drag on operating leverage and cash flow. Consequently, pricing execution directly dictates whether the company can meet its core profitability and margin targets.
4.
Driving EPS acceleration: Pricing power acts as the primary catalyst for Carlsberg's projected earnings per share growth trajectory over the next 18 months. Deutsche Bank predicts that EPS will grow at double-digit rates, reaching DKK 71.52 in 2027 and DKK 79.07 in 2028. This growth remains highly sensitive to volume retention during price hikes, especially in challenging European retail environments. Ultimately, solid pricing execution prevents earnings downgrades and underpins the group's compounding algorithm in volatile markets.

Valuation Risks and Industry Themes

5.
Mitigating valuation discounts: Successfully proving structural pricing resilience is essential for Carlsberg to close its persistent valuation discount relative to staples which sits at a 17% discount. Jefferies notes that while the brewer has strong EPS momentum, its multiple has historically contracted due to market skepticism. Sustained pricing power would validate the group's multi-beverage model and spark a major rerating. Without it, the stock risks de-rating further to historical lows as operating leverage deteriorates.
6.
Consolidation and premiumisation: Carlsberg's pricing power is deeply intertwined with the broader industry themes of global premiumisation and category consolidation. Jefferies notes that global brewers must rely on premiumisation and revenue growth management because historical cost-cutting and consolidation levers are largely exhausted. Carlsberg's strategic pivot into soft drinks through the Britvic acquisition improves its strategic health and pricing leverage. This diversification helps insulate the group from structural declines in mature beer markets across Western Europe.
7.
Other side of the story: Skeptics argue that Carlsberg's challenger position means its pricing power is highly nominal and dilutes volumes. Jefferies highlights that Carlsberg is a challenger in 66% of its markets, meaning its growth must be actively won rather than defended. If pricing hikes trigger severe down-trading or volume losses in competitive regions, the top-line algorithm will fail to deliver. Therefore, aggressive pricing might protect short-term margins but structurally impair long-term market share and growth.

Section 2 – Pricing Power Against Input Cost Inflation and the 2027 Outlook

Section summary
  1. Carlsberg's robust pricing framework enables the group to mitigate severe packaging and energy inflation by executing targeted revenue management.
  2. Emerging margin headwinds in 2027 will test Carlsberg's strategic target to price in line with local inflation while defending absolute profitability.

Raw Material Inflation Impacts

1.
Volatile raw materials: Carlsberg faces significant cost pressure from its packaging-heavy COGS basket because packaging averages 40% to 50% of brewing expenses, according to our analysis of three major companies. Jefferies' report of 13 April 2026 highlights that raw materials represent 25% of COGS while direct utilities comprise a mid-single-digit percentage. Across 15 relevant sources, this exposure means fluctuating commodity prices directly dictate the gross margin trajectory, with two past cases showing severe lag impacts. Consequently, packaging and energy volatility remains the primary driver of margin uncertainty over the next 18 months.
2.
Understated energy exposure: Brewers are highly vulnerable to energy shocks because both aluminium smelting and glass manufacturing are extremely electricity-intensive and natural gas-intensive processes. Jefferies' research on 13 April 2026 outlines that direct utilities and indirect energy costs embedded in glass packaging represent the major risk channels for Carlsberg. This means that spot energy fluctuations will eventually bleed into the cost base as legacy hedges roll off. Therefore, energy shocks will heavily compress EPS unless retail price increases are executed with minimal lags.
3.
Manageable cost sensitivity: Carlsberg requires precise pricing adjustments to protect its operating profit when energy costs experience sudden market spikes. Jefferies' note on 4 March 2026 estimates that a 10% increase in energy prices requires a corresponding pricing increase of approximately 1% to protect EBIT. This benchmark helps the sell-side evaluate whether management's commercial adjustments are mathematically sufficient to cover cost increases. Unless Carlsberg achieves this pricing offset, rising power expenses will directly drag on the company's operating margin.

Testing the 2027 Outlook

4.
Ambitious pricing targets: Carlsberg intends to implement aggressive revenue growth management policies to neutralize the expected commodity cost increases of 2027. Bernstein's note of 9 September 2026 reveals that Carlsberg plans to price in line with local CPI while covering over 80% of input inflation through advanced revenue management. This ambitious claim suggests that the group is moving away from flat nominal pricing toward active real pricing. Successful execution of this strategy will protect the group's gross margins and accelerate EPS growth.

Benchmarking Against Peers

7.
Peer pricing variations: Heineken has adopted a more cautious pricing stance in Europe compared to its aggressive strategy during the peak inflation of 2022-2023. Jefferies' analysis on 14 September 2026 reveals that Heineken priced up to 160% of CPI during the cost spike, but subsequently moderated to roughly 80% of CPI. This pricing pivot was designed to win volume back but ultimately left absolute margins vulnerable to lingering cost pressures. This case demonstrates that pricing below inflation protects volume share but postpones necessary operating margin recovery across mature markets.
8.
Competitor pricing pressure: Heineken's recent commercial execution reflects a deliberate margin sacrifice in Europe to rebuild brand affordability and volume momentum. Jefferies' report of 28 October 2026 indicates that Heineken's constant-geography price/mix in Europe was only 0.1% as off-trade growth outpaced the on-trade. Management's broader guidance suggests pricing increases will remain broadly equivalent to 70% to 90% of CPI to avoid alienating consumers. This cautious competitor pricing means Carlsberg faces intense competitive mainstream pressure if it tries to price strictly in line with inflation.
9.
Structured revenue compounder: Anheuser-Busch InBev demonstrates a resilient revenue per hectolitre growth algorithm that has consistently supported its long-term margin progression across multiple inflationary cycles. Bernstein's note of 4 August 2025 emphasizes that the global brewer targets mid-single-digit top-line growth through real pricing, defined as price-mix growth in excess of local inflation. This strategy has allowed the firm to steadily recover from the input cost storms that compressed its margins by approximately 450 basis points. This structural pricing capability proves that premiumisation and disciplined pricing can successfully offset severe packaging headwinds over time.

Historical Pricing Precedents

10.
Historical margin recovery: Carlsberg's historical performance during the 2022-23 input cost crisis reveals that real pricing lags often create temporary margin compression. Carlsberg's 2023 annual report explains that price increases taken during those years were insufficient to maintain gross margins. This led to a 100 basis point gross margin decline to 44.6% in 2023 as packaging and logistics costs outpaced pricing. This past failure highlights that Carlsberg's EPS remains highly vulnerable to short-term pricing execution delays during rapid inflationary cycles.
11.
Positive margin inflection: Carlsberg demonstrated its ability to restore gross profitability in 2024 as raw material headwinds began to normalize. Carlsberg's presentation on 1 October 2025 showed that gross margin recovered from 44.6% in 2023 to 45.8% in 2024. This recovery was driven by robust revenue per hectolitre growth alongside successful mitigation of COGS per hectolitre. This positive trend suggests that Carlsberg's structural pricing power can successfully rebuild absolute margins once inflation begins to moderate.
12.
Contrary evidence: Carlsberg failed to fully pass through agricultural commodity and duty spikes during the 2011 cost cycle, which led to significant margin compression. Carlsberg's Q3 2011 conference call confirmed that while pricing compensated for absolute input costs, the gross profit margin declined because organic COGS per hectolitre surged by 9%. This structural drag was worsened by price leadership that led to a 250 basis point volume market share loss in Q3 2011. This precedent shows that aggressive pricing to cover inflation can severely impair volume share and contract relative margins.

Future Outlook and Risks

13.
Projected growth divergence: Skepticism remains among major brokers regarding Carlsberg's ability to achieve its ambitious 2027 organic targets without suffering severe volume erosion. Jefferies' report of 29 October 2026 projects Carlsberg's organic EBIT growth at only 4.3% for 2027, well below the consensus forecast of 5.7%. This discrepancy stems from expectations of a weak consumer environment and aggressive mainstream competitor discounting in Europe. This conservative outlook indicates that Carlsberg's pricing power may face headwinds if consumer demand remains depressed.
14.
Other side of the story: Carlsberg's ability to cover input inflation in 2027 could be structurally limited by intense retail resistance and competitive mainstream price cuts. Carlsberg's 2023 annual report highlights that retailer resistance to accepting price increases on consumer goods poses a persistent operational risk. If major competitors like Heineken continue to price below inflation to protect market share, Carlsberg's real pricing strategy will trigger unsustainable volume losses. Consequently, Carlsberg may be forced to absorb a portion of 2027 inflation to defend its volume base, dragging down gross margin recovery.

Section 3 – Pricing Under Consumer Downturns and Downtrading

Section summary
  1. Consumer downturns across China, the United Kingdom, and Poland are testing Carlsberg's pricing resilience as volumes shift toward lower-margin segments and mainstream price competition intensifies.
  2. High-end premium portfolios like 1664 Blanc are experiencing cyclical pressure in China, while diversified multi-beverage categories and local power brands help stabilize overall revenue per hectolitre.

Regional Price Mix Performance

1.
Positive pricing trajectory: Carlsberg's global commercial execution has delivered resilient organic top-line performance despite widespread macroeconomic headwinds across its core geographic territories. The group verified organic revenue per hectolitre growth of 0.8% in the first quarter, which subsequently expanded to a positive 1.0% during the first half of the fiscal year. This upward pricing momentum continued into the third quarter with a reported organic revenue per hectolitre rise of 3.0%. This steady progression demonstrates that targeted revenue growth management can sustain top-line metrics even when global consumer sentiment remains under pressure.
2.
Deconstructed top-line growth: Carlsberg's third-quarter revenue growth was supported by a combination of nominal price increases and category adjustments rather than volume acceleration. Morgan Stanley points out that the 3.0% organic revenue growth was led by a 3.0% increase in revenue per hectolitre, while organic volumes remained practically flat at 0.3%. This positive pricing was driven by premium category shifts and country mix, which successfully offset severe mainstream beer volume weakness across Europe and China. This means that Carlsberg's current top-line expansion relies heavily on pricing actions to compensate for underlying volume stagnation.
3.
Diverse regional pricing: The pricing dynamics achieved by Carlsberg in the third quarter varied significantly across its three primary operating regions. The group reported that organic revenue per hectolitre grew by 2% in Western Europe, 2% in Asia, and a strong 4% in Central and Eastern Europe and India. Deutsche Bank argues that Western Europe and Eastern Europe delivered ahead of consensus, whereas Asia top-line growth remained weak due to the Chinese consumer slowdown. These regional variations highlight that emerging market premiumisation and multi-beverage execution are critical to maintaining group-wide pricing leverage.

Headwinds in China and East

4.
Subdued Chinese demand: The ongoing consumer downturn in China has severely impacted volume performance and pressured premium pricing strategies across the brewing industry. Jefferies reports that Carlsberg's thirdquarter organic volumes in Asia fell by 6.7%, driven primarily by a near 10% volume drop in China due to bad weather and inventory destocking. Although revenue per hectolitre in Asia managed to rise by 2%, the severe consumer slowdown in China limits visibility on a premiumisation recovery over the next 18 months. This geographic concentration means that Carlsberg's mid-term targets remain highly sensitive to Chinese macroeconomic volatility.

Consequently, short-term volume pressure in China may simply reflect cyclical weather volatility rather than a structural decline in Carlsberg's premium pricing power.
