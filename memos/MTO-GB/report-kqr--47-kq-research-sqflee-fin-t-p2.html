EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The central question of how much pricing power MITIE possesses across different operating conditions can be partly answered with medium-to-high confidence based on the assembled evidence. MITIE has demonstrated strong historical wage inflation recovery, with pricing actions recovering 97% of wage inflation in fiscal year 2024, declining slightly to 95% in fiscal year 2025 and 94% in fiscal year 2026. However, the company achieved only a seventy-six percent recovery of the statutory National Insurance rise, with gross costs of forty-nine million pounds offset by only thirty-seven million pounds in pricing recoveries.

This incomplete recovery of legislative cost changes represents the most significant structural constraint on MITIE's pricing power, particularly because approximately ten percent of MITIE's contracts lack indexation clauses entirely, leaving these portfolios vulnerable to rapid margin dilution during inflationary spikes.

The evidence reveals sharp distinctions between MITIE's pricing dynamics across different business segments. In high-margin compliance services, specialized peers including PTSG and Churches Fire achieve gross margins between 30% and 45%, compared to MITIE's reported gross margin of 11.2% in fiscal year 2025. MITIE's acquisition of Marlowe in August 2025 for approximately 350 million pounds positions the company as a leader in the fast-growing 7.6 billion pound UK compliance market, where tighter fire safety regulations and environmental mandates support recurring, non-discretionary compliance revenues that help providers maintain firm pricing structures.

This regulatory tailwind enables true above-inflation price increases in compliance verticals. Conversely, traditional facilities management maintenance contracts face far greater pricing pressure, particularly at renewal points where MITIE lost the Defra contract on price and the Rolls-Royce contract to a global bid, directly impacting fiscal year 2025 earnings.
