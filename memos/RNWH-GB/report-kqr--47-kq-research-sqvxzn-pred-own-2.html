EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Renew Holdings plc operates under framework agreements with 10 of the 12 major UK water utilities, positioning the group to capture a substantial share of the Ofwat AMP8 allowance for 2025 to 2030. The company's opex-led engineering model translates non-discretionary water infrastructure spending into highly visible EPS growth through subsidiaries including Envolve, Browne, Seymour, and Edwards Diving Services. Total committed investment will double to £104bn in AMP8, with new infrastructure funding rising to £45bn from £11bn in AMP7. Yorkshire Water received a record £8.3 billion investment allowance and spent £989.8 million in the first year of AMP8. Pennon upgraded its capital investment plan to approximately £3.6 billion, representing an increase of around £1 billion compared to the original AMP8 business plan. Affinity Water's AMP8 delivery plan involves £2.3 billion of total expenditure, including a £900 million capital enhancement scheme.

The Water Industry National Environment Programme grew to nearly £24 billion in PR24 from a historical £5.40 billion baseline, with WINEP funding legally protected under statutory environmental obligations. Ofwat's AMP8 allowance includes £178 million specifically for base maintenance of existing assets, ensuring critical daily repair tasks cannot be deferred.

Pennon's capital investment of £643.6 million was slightly lower than anticipated due to reprofiling of capital spend to ensure robust modeling before physical works, temporarily delaying construction billing for engineering providers. Yorkshire Water reported a £142 million capital cost variance against allowances, with 90% driven by timing delays in project delivery, deferring revenues into later years.

Thames Water is seeking £2.4 billion in emergency funding to increase its super-senior facility to £5.4 billion and continue operating until autumn 2027. South East Water's senior secured debt was downgraded to subinvestment grade rating of Ba1, making affordable debt financing extremely difficult. However, Renew maintained net cash of £10.6 million with trade receivables rising to £240.90 million, demonstrating strong financial resilience.
