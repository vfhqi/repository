Section 1 – Brief company description and history 9 findings

Section summary
  1. AddLife represents a high-potential Life Science compounder transitioning from a deleveraging phase to an accelerated M&A cycle as operational improvements take hold. Business Profile: AddLife represents a leading independent European partner in the Life Science industry that operates as a distributor, advisor, and strategic partner across 30 European countries. The Group provides value-added medical and laboratory products to both the private and public sectors, establishing marketleading positions in selected niches.
1.
Operational Scale: The Group manages approximately 85 operating subsidiaries and employs roughly 2,300 people to deliver customized medical and laboratory solutions. This extensive European network allows AddLife to access larger markets and secure robust relationships with global suppliers
2.
Decentralized Strategy: Operating through a highly decentralized business model, local subsidiaries retain full responsibility for their commercial operations while being guided by the Group's financial targets. This structure combines the extensive resources of a large corporation with the flexibility and rapid decisionmaking of local entrepreneurs
3.
Segment Classification: The business is divided into two core areas: Labtech, consisting of Biomedical and Research and Diagnostics, and Medtech, comprising Hospital and Homecare. While Labtech serves hospital laboratories and pharmaceutical companies, Medtech mainly targets publicly funded healthcare and social care
4.
Product Composition: Distribution represents the vast majority of AddLife's commercial footprint, with own- manufactured products, primarily restricted to assembly, accounting for only 10% of net sales. This lowcapital distribution model helps protect cash conversion, reducing the heavy capital requirements seen in manufacturing-intensive peers
5.
Underlying Growth: Over the last three years, AddLife faced significant headwinds from elevated leverage and post-pandemic demand declines that temporarily muted its core M&A engine during 2022 and 2023. This leverage-driven pause in acquisitions dampened its historical profit momentum as the company prioritised balance sheet repair. Operational Recovery: Management drove tangible operational progress during 2024 and 2025,

delivering 15% adjusted EBITA growth in 2024 followed by 10% organic adjusted EBITA growth in 2025. These improvement initiatives, alongside active product pruning, helped rebuild investor confidence ahead of the 2026 earnings season.

6.
Peer Comparison - Lifco: In comparison, peer compounder Lifco has sustained a highly profitable model, growing its EBITA at an 18.5% CAGR between 2006 and 2024 while expanding its margins to 23%. Lifco's highly disciplined strategy involves redeploying growing free cash flows into numerous small-to-medium acquisitions
7.
Peer Comparison - Indutrade: Meanwhile, peer compounder Indutrade experienced stagnated profits during 2024 and 2025 due to industrial weakness, yet expects to maintain its long-term track record of >10% EPS growth. However, Indutrade's ability to sustain this long-term expansion is heavily contingent on organic EBITA growth accelerating in the coming periods. Peer Comparison - Addtech: Peer compounder Addtech has demonstrated exceptional long-term resilience,

achieving a 20% EBITA CAGR from 2002 to 2024 and recently restructuring to accelerate its M&A scalability. This strategic restructuring is expected to reignite organic revenue growth and scale up acquisition volumes across continental Europe.

8.
Share Price Performance: AddLife's absolute share price performance reflected a turnaround, rising 10.98% over the last six months despite remaining down 3.54% over the preceding 12 months. Relative to other compounders, this recovery indicates that the market is beginning to price in its operational improvements. Forecast Upgrades: Over the last six months, sell-side analysts upgraded AddLife from Hold to BUY in February 2026 as net debt fell to a supportive 2.2x net debt to EBITDA by the end of 2025. This upgrade marked a turning point, signaling that the company had regained the financial capacity to resume its acquisitive growth path
