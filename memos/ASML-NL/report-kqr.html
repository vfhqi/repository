Section 3 – Backlog and Capacity Outlook

Section summary
  1. ASML's order backlog has expanded significantly, driven by customer down payments and robust demand for lithography systems.
  2. Planned capacity expansions for extreme ultraviolet systems remain tight, forcing the company to explore additional options to satisfy customer requirements.
  3. Technological transitions to next-generation overlay metrology and larger photomask platforms are expected to bolster the company's long-term pricing power.

Inferred Backlog Trajectory

1.
Significant backlog expansion: ASML's order backlog reached an estimated record high of approximately €56 bn in the third quarter of 2026. This inferred figure represents a substantial 44% increase from the last officially disclosed backlog of €38.8 bn at the end of 2025. Bernstein Research calculated this expansion based on the company's surging contract liabilities. This momentum suggests that the current lithography upcycle will extend well into 2028 and support robust future earnings.
2.
High prepayment ratio: ASML's balance sheet reflects strong customer commitments with contract liabilities surging to €24.6 bn in the third quarter of 2026. Bernstein Research established that the order backlog typically runs at 2.2 times these liabilities. This relationship implies that the company collects a substantial 35% to 40% of system value upfront as downpayments from customers. These metrics indicate that 60% of the projected 2028 extreme ultraviolet revenue is already covered by secured orders.
3.
Robust order visibility: ASML's non-current contract liabilities grew by approximately €5 bn to €6 bn sequentially in the third quarter of 2026. Morgan Stanley reported that this surge strongly correlates with customer downpayments for future tools. This rise indicates healthy order intake and improves visibility for shipments scheduled through 2028. This trend reinforces expectations for sustained long-term earnings growth despite near-term macro uncertainties.
4.
Vulnerable backlog shipping: ASML experienced a challenging period in early 2025 when its order book-to-bill ratio fell below 1.0. Redburn Atlantic noted that shipping out of the existing €34 bn backlog helped meet revenue targets but failed to support the share price. This historical precedent suggests that sustained new order momentum is crucial to justify premium valuations. Investors must monitor whether future bookings keep pace with shipments to prevent multiple contraction.

Capacity Expansion and

5.
Commitments Tight supply targets: ASML originally planned to expand its annual capacity for low-NA extreme ultraviolet systems to 110 units by 2028. Roger Dassen, the Chief Financial Officer, confirmed that the company targets a 30% capacity increase in 2027 and investigated a similar ramp for 2028. However, management acknowledged that these planned levels will not fully meet customer demand over this timeframe. This supply deficit is prompting the company to seek alternative ways to boost output.
6.
Upgraded production targets: ASML is currently investigating methods to expand its 2028 capacity beyond the previously guided 30% baseline. Huatai Research reported that raising the expansion pace above 30% implies a significant upward revision from the original target of 110 units. This effort is driven by customers who expect chip supply to remain insufficient through 2028 and are accelerating their own fab plans. Such capacity revisions would significantly boost long-term earnings per share if successfully executed.
7.
High order quality: ASML actively validates customer commitments before allocating shipping slots to prevent tool underutilization. 117 118 - Earnings Calls - Earnings Calls 120 121 122 CCB International reported that the company cross-references orders with customer cleanroom readiness and fab construction schedules. Furthermore, each order carries a substantial downpayment and an exit cost to deter cancellations. 8.
Contrary evidence: Jefferies warned on October 14, 2026, that industry supply tightness could ease significantly by 2028 due to massive global capacity additions. The broker noted that if capacity catches up with demand, the substantial gross margin upside from software upgrade sales could start to reverse. Consequently, earnings projections for 2028 remain highly sensitive to overall wafer-fabequipment spending trends.

Technical Upgrades and Pricing Power

9.
Progressive productivity enhancements: ASML and TSMC announced an initiative on September 8, 2026, to transition the industry to 12-inch photomasks for High-NA EUV. This large-format technology is designed to enable a greater than 40% productivity increase for dualmask exposures. Samsung also joined this collaborative platform to support its own planned High-NA adoption in memory by 2028. This shift removes stitching constraints and lowers long-term chipmaking costs for leading customers.
10.
Extended transition timelines: TSMC expects to utilize High-NA EUV in high-volume manufacturing by 2030, when the technology achieves optimal maturity. The collaborative initiative targets establishing a 12-inch mask pilot line by 2031, with full node production slated for 2033. Deutsche Bank noted that this transition timeline reduces adoption risks by allowing the mask ecosystem to mature gradually. This delayed insertion strategy prevents near-term capital expenditure spikes for major customers.
11.
Next-generation process control: ASML shipped its first next-generation overlay metrology system, the Yieldstar 550, to support advanced process nodes. This tool provides post-exposure measurement to enable real-time feedback and wafer-by-wafer adjustments. Jefferies reported that the system improves productivity by 60% for 14-angstrom logic and 1D DRAM applications. This technological edge strengthens ASML's holistic lithography portfolio and reinforces its long-term pricing power.
12.
Strong competitive positioning: ASML's expansion into the metrology space allows it to capture a larger share of the overall process control market. Bernstein Research reported that KLA remains the market leader in overlay metrology with a 64% share, while ASML holds approximately 36%. However, ASML's unique ability to bundle lithography tools with metrology poses a structural threat to competitors. This integration enables customers to optimize yields, supporting steady average selling price premiums of 3% to 7% for holistic solutions.
13.
Broadening mask infrastructure: ASML's transition to advanced masks is triggering significant research and development spending across its equipment supply chain. SUSS Microtec focused its 2026 development on cleaning platforms specifically designed for High-NA EUV photomasks. Additionally, Veeco Instruments is expanding its deposition systems to support larger-format mask blanks and improve yield. This collective ecosystem preparation ensures that ASML's technical upgrades are fully supported by manufacturing infrastructure.
14.
Other side of the story: While customer visibility continues to improve, the backlog is vulnerable to cleanroom readiness and installation delays at client fabs. TSMC's plan to delay High-NA insertion until 2030 demonstrates that leading-edge customers remain highly sensitive to steep system costs. 142 143 146 148 149 10Q - Earnings Calls Morgan Stanley reported that if cleanroom constraints or supply chain bottlenecks delay actual tool shipments, near-term capacity additions may fail to satisfy optimistic earnings projections. Consequently, near-term relative share performance could remain constrained until the 2028 capacity framework is formally resolved.
